Sharemarket dips but ends week higher despite risks

Australian shares have limped across the finish line but notched a second straight week of gains, despite facing a slew of domestic and global risks.

The S&P/ASX200 fell 44.3 points on Friday, down 0.49 per cent, to 9020.8, as the broader All Ordinaries lost 60.5 points, or 0.66 per cent, to 9144.7.

The top-200 was still up 1.2 one per cent since Monday and has risen for five of the past six weeks, despite the Reserve Bank hiking the official cash rate to 4.85 per cent on Tuesday, its highest level since 2008.

The Australian dollar is buying 69 US cents, down from 70.09 US cents on Thursday at 5pm.

Mining stocks eased from Thursday’s surge, tracking tumbling US tech stocks as traders mulled the US midterms’ likely impact on the artificial intelligence build-out that has supported 2026 stock prices and driven Wall Street’s bull market.

Miners fell taking heavyweights like BHP (down 2.34 per cent), Rio Tinto (-2.03 per cent) and Fortescue (-2 per cent) lower. Gold miners also slumped, with Northern Star sliding 1.12 per cent and Evolution 2.64 per cent lower.

Technology stocks fell on the back of the meltdown on Wall Street’s Nasdaq index. Megaport initially tumbled 9.5 per cent but clawed back to end the day down 3.89 per cent. Codan closed 2.9 per cent lower. But Xero and Australia’s most valuable tech company WiseTech retreated by less than 1 per cent.

Most financial stocks helped lift the bourse with small gains across the board. Commonwealth Bank added 0.73 per cent, Westpac rose 1.06 per cent, National Australia Bank jumped 2.67 per cent and ANZ added 0.6 per cent. Macquarie Group slumped 3.69 per cent as it was hit with a $100 million capital penalty by financial regulator APRA, saying it breached capital requirements for its non-bank businesses. The wealth giant also reported half-year profits of $2.48 billion.

Energy stocks were largely stable as oil prices advanced overnight after a steep drop in the previous session.

In the US, the S&P 500 fell 4.4 per cent for its worst drop since President Donald Trump shook markets worldwide in April 2025 with his “Liberation Day” tariffs. The wipeout more than erased the market’s gain from the day before, which was its best in three months. The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped more than 1800 points, and the Nasdaq composite tumbled 5.5 per cent. In all, about $US3.2 trillion ($4.6 trillion) was wiped off Wall Street.

AI stocks worldwide felt pressure as worries increase about possible moratoriums on data centres following Democratic victories in the US midterm elections.

Trump blamed Democrats for the heavy losses in Americans’ 401(k) portfolios – similar to Australia’s superannuation system – as the selloff in technology and artificial intelligence-related stocks deepened after his Republican Party suffered widespread losses in the midterm elections.
“Such a sad thing to watch!” the president said in a post on his Truth Social platform after the close. “It took us so much work to get us to a level that no one has ever seen before, and then this.”

The nationwide victories by Democrats raised the possibility that governments could force pauses in construction of more data centres. That would mean fewer purchases of AI chips, not to mention electricity and other products and services that are getting vacuumed up in the AI frenzy and driving much of the US economy’s growth.

Nvidia fell 9.3 per cent and was by far the heaviest weight on the S&P 500, a day after dropping 3.2 per cent. Advanced Micro Devices tumbled 17.3 per cent. CoreWeave, a company that rents access to AI chips to customers over the cloud, fell 9.8 per cent.

AI chip stocks are some of Wall Street’s most influential because they’ve become some of the largest. Stock prices tend to follow where corporate profits and interest rates go over the long term, depending on them much more than on who controls what in Washington.
