NC officials launch inquiries into 31 gas stations over elevated prices

North Carolina officials are investigating 31 gas stations after consumers reported elevated gas prices, the state Department of Justice said Friday.

The investigations are the first following a push by Attorney General Jeff Jackson to crack down on stations suspected of pocketing savings from the temporary suspension of the state’s 41-cent-per-gallon gas tax.

Jackson this month called on consumers to report gas stations suspected of not passing along the savings to consumers. More gas stations are expected to receive inquiries next week. 

“Any gas station not passing their tax savings onto customers is violating the law, and we are reviewing every single complaint we receive,” Jackson said in a statement. “Escalation letters have started going out and we are expecting a prompt explanation from each gas station we contact.” 

Jackson’s office said it has asked the 31 stations for explanations about their pricing practices, but noted that receiving an inquiry letter doesn’t mean a station has been found to have violated the law. Jackson previously said his office will take into account gas stations’ explanations for pricing differences, factoring in things such as transportation costs and wholesale prices when considering possible penalties.

Lawmakers this month approved a GOP-backed measure to suspend the state's tax to provide consumers relief from high fuel prices. Democratic Gov. Josh Stein signed the bill. The suspension runs through Nov. 30.

Shortly after the suspension went into effect, Jackson announced a new program — NC Fuel Check — that allows consumers to report stations that are charging significantly more than nearby competitors.

The new law doesn’t require gas stations to lower prices. And North Carolina's price-gouging law applies only during declared emergencies or qualifying market disruptions. Neither has been declared for the gas tax suspension. 

But the state has other rules against unfair and deceptive trade practices. Jackson, a Democrat, warned earlier this month that stations found in violation of those rules could face civil penalties of up to $5,000 per violation.

The state’s gas tax is collected from fuel suppliers before gasoline reaches stations. Drivers were expected to see elevated prices in the early days of the suspension as retailers sold inventory purchased before the suspension took effect. Prices have dropped as more stations have restocked with tax-free fuel. 

A gallon of regular unleaded currently averages about $3.41 per gallon in North Carolina, according to AAA. That’s down 16.7% from a month ago, around the time lawmakers introduced the gas-tax suspension bill.
