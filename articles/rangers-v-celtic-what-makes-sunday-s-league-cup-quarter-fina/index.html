Rangers v Celtic: What makes Sunday's League Cup quarter-final crucial?

TheTools · 13.09.2026 05:45 · source · permalink
  • Published
    9 hours ago

No away fans, calamities in Europe and Derek McInnes' Old Firm debut as a manager.

In Glasgow, every Old Firm match is big, but going into Sunday there is a sense this game could be seismic.

Rangers legend Graeme Souness says the Premier Sports Cup quarter-final is an opportunity for both sides to "put a marker down" as they meet for the first time this season.

But what can we expect as Rangers and Celtic face off (12:00 BST) at Ibrox?

Rangers or Celtic - who are favourites?

Celtic have won all six of their Scottish Premiership matches so far this season with 14 goals scored and just three conceded, but that does not quite tell the full story.

They squeezed past Dundee at home in their first game of the season, but that was followed by rampant wins at Kilmarnock and Dundee United.

Things looked even better when they put three goals past LASK without response in the first leg of their Champions League play-off, but what occurred the following week was a capitulation of the highest order.

Callum McGregor's early goal in Austria had Celtic four goals to the good and heading for the league phase, but LASK mounted the comeback of all comebacks to win 5-1 on the night and 5-4 on aggregate.

While the ship has steadied since then and Celtic have won all four of their following league matches, a narrow victory at St Johnstone in midweek was far from convincing, albeit manager Martin O'Neill had made significant changes to his starting line-up.

Previewing Sunday's derby encounter, Celtic great Scott Brown explained the importance of a fast start in this fixture.

"As a Celtic player, you want to silence that crowd early doors," he said.

"You want to make sure that you keep the ball, you dictate, and if Celtic get an early goal it becomes a nervous Ibrox.

"The big [derby] is always the first game."

For Rangers, domestically, it's been a different story. They began the new season with a draw at Dundee United and a defeat to Hibernian, leaving McInnes winless in his first two Premiership matches as manager.

But a rampant League Cup win against St Mirren, followed by four league wins on the bounce, and things look a bit rosier heading into Sunday's game at Ibrox.

While things have improved domestically, McInnes' men faced similar embarrassment to their city rivals on the European stage.

After finishing third in the league last term, they entered the Europa League third qualifying round.

Rangers were beaten 2-1 by Polish outfit Jagiellonia Bialystok in the first leg and their 1-1 draw at home meant they dropped into the Conference League play-offs.

The first leg of that tie was played in Glasgow, where McInnes' men were 1-0 winners against Czech side Jablonec, who ultimately won on penalties in the return leg.

Why are there no away fans at Ibrox?

Earlier this month, it was confirmed there would be no away supporters at any SPFL matches between the city rivals this season.

The "temporary arrangement" was reached following talks between the Glasgow clubs and the SPFL and follows the Scottish FA's independent review into events surrounding last term's Scottish Cup quarter-final at Ibrox, which was marred by unrest and pitch incursions from both sets of fans.

About 8,000 visiting fans, a figure that was much larger than previous away allocations, had been allowed into Ibrox back in March to witness Celtic's penalty shootout win.

The disorder that followed prompted the SFA to force both Celtic and Rangers to play their next home Scottish Cup ties behind closed doors.

For this weekend's League Cup clash, Rangers had initially proposed an allocation of 300 supporters, but Celtic rejected the offer and in light of the SPFL's ruling, there will be no away fans inside Ibrox on Sunday.

What are the managers saying?

Rangers manager Derek McInnes: "I think we've got to bring the crowd with us.

"You're not going to play Celtic and dominate the game for 90 minutes. We'll have spells, they'll have spells, it's just the way it is.

"I think it's important that we tap into having that 50,000 behind us and be behind the players, support them, have their backs, not be on their backs and really make it as tough for Celtic as possible.

"There's been a lot of frustration over a period of time at the club and it's up to us to try and show that we're going to deliver something a bit different."

Celtic manager Martin O'Neill: "I suppose if you get in front in the game, that makes them anxious.

"Talking about getting in front and getting in front are two different things. The crowd will be right behind their team, which you would expect.

"We just do our best. We know that we're up against a very fine side. We're up against a crowd that can make a difference.

"I'd be delighted to be celebrating something in the game."

Commentator's notes

  • Celtic have won the past six League Cup meetings between the clubs since losing the final to Rangers in 2011.

  • Celtic's last League Cup win at Ibrox was in the groups in 1973. They also beat Rangers in the semis that season but have never won a League Cup knockout tie in Govan.

  • Celtic have only won four of their previous 14 games against Rangers at Ibrox.

  • Rangers have only failed to get to the semis once in the past decade.

  • Celtic haven't lost at the quarter-final stage since 2009, when Hearts beat them 1-0 at Celtic Park thanks to a Michael Stewart penalty - current coach Stephen McManus was sent off late in the tie.

  • O'Neill's record at Ibrox: played 11, won six, drawn one, lost four.

  • McInnes' record against Celtic: played 61, won 10, drawn four, lost 47.
