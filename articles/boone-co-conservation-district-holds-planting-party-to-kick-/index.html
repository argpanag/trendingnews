Boone Co. Conservation District holds planting party to kick off fall

TheTools · 26.09.2026 18:23 · source · permalink

BELVIDERE, Ill. (Rockford News First) - Dozens of people came together Saturday to celebrate the start of the fall season and plant native shrubs and trees.

Boone County Conservation District held the free event from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m., Sept. 26, welcoming community members to the LIB Conservation Area at 603 N. Appleton Rd. in Belvidere.

Participants spent time helping plant trees and other native plants to work towards creating a healthy habitat. There were also nature-themed crafts offered, as well as treats from the Edward’s Apple Orchard.

This work is part of a larger project meant to improve Belvidere’s urban tree canopy and funded by a grant from the Morton Arboretum’s Chicago Region Trees Initiative. That project works to provide high-quality urban woodland along the Kishwaukee River and is expected to be complete by 2028.

“We want to provide the community with opportunities to recreate in a high-quality, diverse, and native riparian woodland and enjoy the natural areas of Boone County,” said Frank Rabbit, Director of Natural Resources of the Boone County Conservation District, in a news release. “Transforming an overgrown riparian habitat with little ecological value to an ecologically healthy woodland with native shrubs and trees allows us to do just that.”

Formed in 1964, the Boone County Conservation District was the first of its kind in Illinois and now manages more than 4,400 acres of prairies, woodland and wetlands. Visit the district’s website to get involved.
