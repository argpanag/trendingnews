Tyson Fury vs Anthony Joshua: Paris Fury clarifies fighter did not have schizophrenia diagnosis

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:34 · source · permalink

Tyson Fury revealed his schizophrenia diagnosis in a new Netflix documentary

  • Published
    26 November 2026, 18:54 GMT

Paris Fury, the wife of Tyson Fury, says the boxer has never received a diagnosis of schizophrenia.

In clips promoting the release of a forthcoming Netflix documentary, the heavyweight fighter spoke of his mental health struggles in the months after he beat Wladimir Klitschko to become world champion in 2015.

He detailed hearing voices in his head, while wife Paris said he had "mild schizophrenia" .

Netflix has now edited the film ahead of its release on Friday, 27 November.

In a statement, Paris Fury said: "I want to clarify a statement I made in the original version of the Tyson Fury documentary.

"I recalled a conversation from many years ago when discussing Tyson's condition and I used the word 'schizophrenic'. While Tyson did show some symptoms associated with schizophrenia, he doesn't have this condition and has never received a diagnosis."

She added: "Mental health is complex, and it isn't always easy to find the right words, especially when you're living it with someone you love. I'm so proud of Tyson for really opening up in this documentary and want to say thank you for all the support we've received as a family."

A spokesperson for Netflix told BBC Sport: "Following new information shared by the family relating to Tyson's official mental health diagnosis, we have worked alongside them to update the documentary accordingly ahead of launch."

It comes after the British Boxing Board of Control said it was looking into Tyson Fury's comments about his schizophrenia diagnosis but was "fairly confident" the British heavyweight will be cleared to fight Anthony Joshua next month.

Fury, 38, is due to face Joshua at Cardiff's Principality Stadium on 11 December, with the ringwalk expected in the early hours of 12 December.

"We are in the process of dealing with the matter but if there are any medical issues we wouldn't let anyone box - it doesn't matter if it's Tyson Fury or not, it could be anybody," Robert Smith, the board's general secretary, told BBC Sport.

"I'm fairly confident he will be absolutely fine. We're dealing with it now and we'll have an answer very shortly."

Under regulation 8.1(b) of the British Boxing Board of Control's 2026 rules and regulations, external, schizophrenia is among the conditions that "may preclude" a boxer from obtaining a first licence, while for an existing licence holder the board "may take such action as in its absolute discretion it sees fit".

Smith would not say what the process would involve over the next few days, saying "it's a medical matter".

He said all boxers on the card will undergo medical checks, adding: "If our doctors are satisfied he's fit to box, he will box."

Originally, the documentary featured the Furys recalling his admission to hospital in June 2016 with a suspected heart attack.

Doctors said he had suffered an anxiety attack. He was diagnosed with schizophrenia after he left hospital, according to the film.

The NHS describes schizophrenia as a form of psychosis, and symptoms can include hallucinations, delusions and muddled thoughts and speech.

"Words can't explain the darkness I was in," Fury said.

The heavyweight, who struggled with drug and alcohol addiction after beating Klitschko, says a Halloween night out in 2017 prompted him to return to training, calling that a "turning point" after being "embarrassed by how I looked".

He went on to beat Deontay Wilder in 2020 to become world heavyweight champion for a second time.
