Brazil Issues Red Storm Warning as Winds Over 100 Km/h Threaten Southern States

TheTools · 07.11.2026 19:07 · source · permalink

Table of Contents

Brazil has issued its highest-level severe storm warning for November 7, 2026, as dangerous thunderstorms threaten parts of Paraná, Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul. The country’s National Institute of Meteorology, INMET, has classified the weather threat as red-level “great danger,” warning of extremely heavy rain, destructive winds and hail that could disrupt road travel and regional transport.

The official warning is valid from midnight until 11:59 p.m. local time on Saturday, November 7.

INMET warns that rainfall could exceed 60 millimetres in one hour or 100 millimetres in a single day, while wind gusts may surpass 100 kilometres per hour.

The alert also identifies a significant risk of fallen trees, flooding, electricity outages, structural damage and disruption to road transport.

Red Alert Covers Paraná, Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul

The highest-level warning includes western and southwestern Paraná, western Santa Catarina and several regions of Rio Grande do Sul.

Areas listed by INMET include western Paraná, southwestern Paraná, southern and central Paraná, western Santa Catarina, northeastern Rio Grande do Sul and parts of the Serra region.

The warning does not mean that every city in the three states will experience identical weather conditions.

Severe thunderstorms can produce highly localised damage, with powerful winds and intense rainfall affecting individual municipalities while neighbouring areas experience less significant conditions.

INMET advises residents and travellers to seek shelter during dangerous storms and avoid exposed outdoor locations.

Road Travel Faces Risk From Flooding and Fallen Trees

Road transport is among the sectors most exposed to the forecast conditions.

Heavy rainfall can reduce visibility and create dangerous driving conditions, while fallen trees and debris may obstruct roads.

Visitors travelling between cities in southern Brazil should check official highway information before departure, particularly when planning long-distance journeys through rural or mountainous areas.

Tourists travelling to regional destinations, national parks or countryside accommodation may face additional difficulties if access roads are affected by flooding or storm damage.

However, the November 7 warning is a forecast of potential disruption rather than confirmation that specific highways have closed.

Airports Could Face Weather-Related Delays

The red-level weather warning comes just three days after a tornado damaged Chapecó Airport in Santa Catarina on November 4.

That earlier storm brought winds reaching 126 kilometres per hour, damaging terminal infrastructure and forcing the suspension of flight operations while airport authorities assessed the damage.

The November 7 warning concerns another period of dangerous weather and should not be confused with the earlier tornado.

Strong crosswinds, thunderstorms, lightning and heavy rainfall can cause temporary airport delays, diversions or ground-handling restrictions.

Regional airports serving southern Brazil may therefore require additional operational precautions if severe storms develop near their facilities.

Nevertheless, INMET’s warning alone does not establish that Curitiba, Florianópolis, Porto Alegre or other major airports have closed.

Passengers should check flight status directly with their airlines and departure airports.

What Travellers Should Do During Brazil’s Storm Warning

INMET has urged people in affected areas to take precautions against dangerous weather conditions.

Travellers should avoid unnecessary outdoor activities during severe thunderstorms and stay away from trees, power lines and structures that could be damaged by high winds.

Motorists should avoid driving through flooded roads, particularly where water depth and road conditions cannot be assessed safely.

In Brazil, Civil Defence can be contacted on 199, while the Fire Department emergency number is 193.

The red warning is currently valid through November 7. Any extension into November 8 would require a new or updated official meteorological advisory.

Travellers should continue monitoring INMET and local Civil Defence announcements for information about actual storm damage, road closures and transport disruption.
