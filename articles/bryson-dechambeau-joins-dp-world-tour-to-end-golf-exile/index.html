Bryson DeChambeau joins DP World Tour to end golf exile

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:53 · source · permalink

Exclusive: Two-time major winner reenters the mainstream and is now guaranteed entry to every event after submitting forms before deadline

Bryson DeChambeau has joined the DP World Tour for the 2027 season as he prepares for a year-long suspension from the American circuit.

In a huge coup for Wentworth, sources have revealed that the two-time major winner submitted the forms in the hours before Sunday’s deadline and is now part of the elite category one membership bracket that is guaranteed entry to every event.

When the demise of LIV Golf was confirmed in a bankruptcy court earlier this month, DeChambeau was offered a free route back to the traditional tours because of the exemption he earned with his success at the 2024 US Open, where he famously beat Rory McIlroy by a shot.

It is expected that, along with fellow LIV player Jon Rahm, DeChambeau will be forced to sit out next year’s campaign on the PGA Tour until they are welcomed back. However, there were fears that DeChambeau could concentrate on his social media channels instead and attempt to become the first “YouTube” golfer to be competitive in the majors without playing on a Tour.

The Californian has more than 10 million followers and subscribers on platforms ranging from YouTube to Instagram and insisted that this highly unlikeable marriage could work out. “That’s an incredibly viable option, I’ll tell you that,” DeChambeau said earlier this year.

Yet after a statement issued last week in which he suggested a measure of contrition, DeChambeau – who disappointed the game at large at the Open in July with his entitled behaviour in arguing with officials over a clear two-shot penalty – has reentered the mainstream and so avoided being frozen out of the professional sport.

“Arnold Palmer said no player is bigger than the game, and I want to acknowledge that, along the way, I’ve said some things that didn’t help,” DeChambeau said. “Whatever comes next, I’ll approach it with humility and respect for the people who carried this game long before me. My passion has been to grow this game.”

DeChambeau had already committed to play in next month’s Nedbank Golf Challenge – the DP World Tour event that takes place in Sun City, South Africa – and, with a personal schedule expected to consist of at least a dozen events it is anticipated that he will start the new chapter of his colourful career in the Hero Dubai Desert Classic in January.

That $9m tournament will also feature Rahm and another ex-LIV golfer in Tyrrell Hatton, as well as Tour stalwarts such as McIlroy and Tommy Fleetwood. If the PGA Tour does stick to its rules and sanction the LIV players with a 12-month ban, this could be a thrilling taste of the high-quality fields to come on the DP World Tour.

McIlroy, who won an eighth order of merit, has vowed to play more on his home circuit if, with the added competition, he has to pull out all the stops to collect a record ninth crown and despite having his issues with DeChambeau, last week said the 33-year-old would be a “big positive”.

“I think a name like Bryson DeChambeau and everything he brings with him, good and bad, he brings eyeballs, which is a good thing for tournaments,” McIlroy added.

Wentworth HQ is remaining tight-lipped, but is expected to publish a list of the new members later on Tuesday. As well as DeChambeau, Telegraph Sport has learnt that it could also include a few Ryder Cup legends in Lee Westwood and Ian Poulter, as well as Sergio García.

The Tour is renegotiating its “strategic alliance” with their American counterparts – the PGA Tour pumped in more than £60m in the latest financial year and is intent on gaining more favourable terms – this influx of golfing heavyweights could hardly be better timed.
