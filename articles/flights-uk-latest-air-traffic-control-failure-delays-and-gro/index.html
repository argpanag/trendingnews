Flights UK latest: Air traffic control failure delays and grounds flights

TheTools · 21.09.2026 10:29 · source · permalink

Key moments

Dozens of flights have been cancelled and hundreds more delayed in a fresh air traffic control meltdown.

Around a dozen British Airways and 50 EasyJet flights have been cancelled while data from Flight Radar suggests that nearly 200 flights have been delayed across the country.

Martin Rolfe, the chief executive of National Air Traffic Services (Nats), is now facing fresh calls to quit after the fourth meltdown on his watch.

Manchester, Glasgow and both Belfast airports were worst affected by the “technical issue” across central England, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

Nats has apologised and insisted that it was unrelated to the previous air control incident earlier this month.

Ryanair: ‘Nats chief must go’

Ryanair has renewed calls for Martin Rolfe, Nats boss, to resign or be sacked after two significant technical failures within a month.

An airline spokesman said: “Ryanair today today called on failed Nats CEO Martin Rolfe to resign immediately, or for Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander to remove him, after yet another Nats’ flight data processing system at Prestwick failed again this morning, less than 2 weeks after the same system collapsed in NATS’ London Centre on 8 Sept and disrupted more than 2,000 flights across the UK and Europe.”

Passengers face 10-hour delays

Amy Curtis, 26, was booked on an EasyJet flight from Edinburgh to Bristol that was scheduled to take off at 9.55am. However the airline’s app was telling her that it will not take off until 8.34pm this evening.

Asked what she thought about the air control system going down again, she gave a rueful laugh and said: “It’s not great. I’ve not seen anybody to speak to. It’s not very helpful.”

Ms Curtis, who was in Edinburgh for the weekend seeing friends, said she did not know what to do for the next nine hours and estimated she would not get home until after midnight.

Nats: Delays caused by ‘connectivity issue’

Nats has insisted the “connectivity” problem it faced this morning had nothing to do with the technical issue it faced earlier this month.

The air traffic control firm said: “Early this morning we experienced a technical issue at our Prestwick centre in Scotland. To maintain safety, air traffic regulations were applied to manage traffic demand while our engineers investigated.

“This was a connectivity issue with part of our systems network, specific to the operation in Scotland. It is nothing to do with the problem we had earlier this month.”

It added: “We apologise to passengers for the disruption and thank them for their patience. Passengers should continue to check with their airline for the latest information on their flight.”

Nats insiders are unable to explain what a “connectivity issue” amounts to in practice, the Telegraph understands.

‘I’m going to miss Snow Patrol’

Simon, 40, said he was “frustrated” by the delays.

He was scheduled for the 9.50am flight from Belfast to London Luton, which has now been delayed to 12.55. He arrived at the airport at 8.30am, 10 minutes after being notified of the delay.

He is getting the flight to see a Snow Patrol concert this evening but is now “worried” he might miss it.

He told The Telegraph: “I understand there’s an issue with air traffic control but there’s been no contact from the airline, no one has come to speak to us, it’s extremely frustrating and has negatively impacted my thoughts on EasyJet.

“It’s really frustrating, these things happen but the fact there’s no response to it at all – we’ve spent hours in the airport with no contact, no refreshments, nothing.

“The airport’s crowded, there’s a vibe of people being frustrated but everyone is calm and relaxed. I think the fact there are still planes leaving is keeping people calm. I’m worried I might miss my concert.”

Delayed flights begin to take off

Passengers on a flight from Manchester Airport to Orlando, Florida, “clapped” when it was finally announced their flight would take off.

Lisa, from Bury, was expecting to cancel her evening plans on the trip with her husband for their 10-year wedding anniversary when they received an update saying the plane was ready to take off.

She told Manchester Evening News: “We were meant to leave at 9.15am, which got delayed to 9.40am overnight. After we boarded we got told there was a two hour delay, then we got told we wouldn’t be leaving until 1.50pm.

“There were a lot of unhappy passengers and a lot of groaning. We had plans tonight which we were preparing to cancel.

“Luckily we’ve just had an update saying they’re ready to go. Everyone clapped when they found out.”

She added: “The staff were really good, they started some entertainment and brought round some water. The captain offered passengers to come and speak to him while we were delayed. Everyone just seems relieved to be departing.”

Nats apologises for delays

Air traffic control firm Nats has apologised for today’s flight disruption caused by an issue in its Prestwick centre and said it is working with airports and airlines to “minimise any further disruption”.

The firm said: “Passengers should still check the status of their flight with their airline. We apologise for the disruption.”

Passengers stuck on runways

Passengers stuck on runways are being taken off planes.

Lewis Fraser, from Inverness, hoped to make a connection flight from Heathrow to Chicago, but is stuck on the Glasgow Airport runway.

“We boarded the flight as normal but were immediately told we were delayed,” he told the BBC.

“Some passengers have left the plane in the last few minutes. I’m on the 8am to Heathrow and due to fly to Chicago for work at 12.15pm but that now looks unlikely.

“They’ve given us tea and snacks. The latest update was around half an hour ago to say we might be waiting another hour and 45 minutes.”
