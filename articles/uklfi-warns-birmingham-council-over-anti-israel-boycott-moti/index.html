UKLFI Warns Birmingham Council Over Anti-Israel Boycott Motion

TheTools · 02.11.2026 11:36 · source · permalink

UK Lawyers for Israel (UKLFI) has urged Birmingham City Council to withdraw or postpone a motion advocating BDS against Israel, warning that the proposals risk breaching the law and increasing hostility towards Jewish residents.

The motion, proposed by Councillors Nosheen Khalid and Jamil Khan, is due to be debated on 3 November 2026. It proposes new ethical procurement principles, reviews of pension fund investments and banking relationships, and representations to the Foreign Secretary concerning Israel.

In a letter dated 1 November, UKLFI warned that the proposals raise serious legal and equality concerns, despite the accompanying Council report stating that there are no legal, financial, procurement or equality implications.

UKLFI pointed out that section 17 of the Local Government Act 1988 restricts politically motivated procurement boycotts. The Procurement Act 2023 also prevents suppliers from being excluded simply because they conduct business in or with an occupied territory.

UKLFI further warned that the West Midlands Pension Fund must make investment decisions in the financial interests of its members and beneficiaries, rather than pursue political objectives. Government guidance issued in June 2026 prohibits the exclusion of investments in particular countries or companies.

UKLFI also challenged the motion’s reliance on false UN reports and the International Court of Justice’s 2024 Advisory Opinion, which is based on such reports and, in any case, does not determine the legality of individual procurement or investment decisions by English councils.

Particular concern was raised about the potential impact on Birmingham’s Jewish community. UKLFI warned that anti-Israel boycott and divestment campaigns  mobilise and normalise hostility towards Jews and that the Council must consider these consequences under its public sector equality duty.

A UKLFI spokesperson said:

“Birmingham City Council cannot lawfully use public contracts, pension funds and banking arrangements to pursue political campaigns.

“It is particularly disturbing that a motion which could increase hostility towards Jewish residents is being presented as having no equality implications.

“Councillors must put the law, the interests of pension fund members and community relations ahead of performative political gestures.”

UKLFI has called for the motion to be withdrawn or deferred until councillors receive proper legal and equality advice. It has requested that its letter be circulated to all councillors before the debate.
