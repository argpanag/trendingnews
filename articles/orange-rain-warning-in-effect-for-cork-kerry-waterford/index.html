Orange rain warning in effect for Cork, Kerry, Waterford

A Status Orange rain warning is now in effect in counties Cork, Kerry and Waterford and will remain in place until 5am.

Met Éireann has forecast "intense rain" and warned of the risk of flooding due to fallen leaves and blocked drains.

A separate Status Orange rain warning for Dublin, Wexford and Wicklow will be in operation from 2am until 8am.

A Status Yellow rain and wind warning for Leinster and Munster came into effect at 7pm yesterday and will be in effect until 10am.

The National Directorate for Fire and Emergency Management has advised of the potential for flooding and difficult travelling conditions due to the arrival of Storm Austen.

Households are advised to secure any outdoor items such as bins or garden furniture and to ensure access to essential supplies. Motorists should exercise caution and avoid driving through flooded roads.

Local authorities made preparations throughout yesterday for heavy rain and wind associated with the storm.

Met Éireann said there could be disruption and delays to public transport, flooding and very difficult travelling conditions as the weather system spreads eastwards across the country.

Met Éireann said river levels are expected to rise across Cork, Kerry, Waterford, Tipperary, Kilkenny, Carlow, Wexford, Wicklow, Kildare, Laois, Offaly, Dublin and parts of the surrounding counties, potentially resulting in rivers exceeding their banks and flooding is expected.

Met Éireann Senior Forecaster Gerry Murphy said the potential for flooding with leaves and blocked drains is "substantial".

"From my flood forecasting colleagues, the water levels in some areas are not quite as high as perhaps some times during the winter... because we are in the autumn still and we have had a dry summer," he said.

Mr Murphy said it will be wet everywhere overnight because of the Status Yellow rain warning for all counties, but areas in south and east under the Orange warning will be worst affected.

"You could be talking anywhere between 40mm and 60mm of rain in those areas with an orange rain warning, which means flooding will be likely," he said.

"Flooding is likely, and that flooding is likely to be mainly surface flooding."

Mr Murphy said Friday will be a blustery day and he warned weather conditions will be bad for those getting to work and school tomorrow.

"The heaviest rain is overnight along the east coast from 2am until 8am. For rush hour, there is likely to be floods on many of the roads, and it will be a difficult enough morning in the east and in the south as well."

Mr Murphy said there will be "no real let up" from the rain over the weekend and blustery conditions are expected for Sunday's Dublin marathon.

'Not a night for driving'

In Cork, sandbags and pumps were deployed as the county comes under rain warnings, with people being urged not to travel unless absolutely necessary.

Cork County Council's Director of Roads, Transportation and area Services Padraig Barrett said: "It'll be a bad night, not a night for driving, not a night for going out.

"So I'd urge people that have to go out to exercise caution, and that caution should continue into tomorrow morning."

He said preparations are being made for flooding but he is hoping it will not happen.

"We've set up all our flood operation plans right around the county. Sandbags have been deployed. Pumps have been deployed where necessary to non-flooding locations. But the messaging to the public to secure loose items in their gardens and on their buildings, and to exercise caution when driving."

A spokesperson for Cork County Council has said preparations in accordance with the East Cork MD Flood Action Plan are in progress in advance of the storm.

They include inspections of and cleaning of key drainage assets, and the distribution of Sandbags in key locations and flood prone areas.

They added that while river levels are currently low in East Cork, over 1,000 sandbags have been made available for use in Midleton.

The Midleton flood pump has also been mobilised at the lower end of the Main Street, while Baby's Walk will be closed tomorrow.

In Kerry, the County Council has advised road users and members of the public to expect disruption and possible flooding tonight and tomorrow morning.

A spokesperson for the council said motorists can expect surface water and flooding, as well as the possibility of fallen trees and debris on many routes.

Council crews are on standby to deal with any problems which arise, while the council's emergency number will be available for the duration of the weather warning.

Sandbags are being distributed to areas which are vulnerable to flooding and any weather-related issues can be reported to 066 7183588. Kerry County Council is also monitoring the weather forecasts for the weekend and will issue further advisories in due course.

Senior Engineer with Dublin City Council Roy O'Connor said Storm Austen is expected to bring less rain to the capital than in other parts of the country, but there is still a possibility of localised flooding.

He said defence barriers will be put up along the Dodder river, and crews will monitor it throughout the night, but as the storm is arriving a low tide the river is not expected to flood.

Speaking on RTÉ's Morning Ireland, Mr O'Connor said that tomorrow morning "is probably when most people will see potentially a little bit of debris and so forth on the ground".

Bus Éireann, meanwhile, is advising its customers to check its website for updates if they are intending on taking one of their services over the coming days.

The bus operator's Chief Customer Officer, Allen Parker said: "All Bus Éireann services are scheduled to operate as normal, however if any are impacted by the stormy weather, we will post details online as quickly as possible to keep our customers informed.

"The best approach would be to check service updates on our website before leaving home, to minimise any potential disruption," Mr Parker added.

Storm Austen was named by the UK Met Office, as the UK will see the worst impacts of the storm in relation to winds.

The National Severe Weather and Flood Coordination Group met yesterday and said local authorities had activated their severe weather assessment teams in advance of the storm.

"They, along with other stakeholders, are actively preparing for all eventualities and will be ready to respond in any affected areas where needed," the group added.

In a statement, Head of the National Directorate for Fire and Emergency Management Keith Leonard said: "There is the potential for heavy rainfall, particularly in southern and eastern coastal counties.

"Localised flooding is possible, so road users should not attempt to drive through flooded roads.

"Trees remain in full leaf at this time of year, making them more vulnerable to strong winds – motorists should be alert for fallen trees, debris and difficult driving conditions, particularly during Thursday night and Friday's morning commute.

"If you need to drive during the next few days, allow extra time for essential travel."

Met Éireann, the UK Met Office and the meteorological service in the Netherlands, KNMI, launched the list of names for the 2026/27 storm season, with each of the national partners contributing seven names, according to the forecaster.

The name Austen was submitted by Met Éireann.

Austen H Nagle was the first director of the Irish Meteorological Service and worked in the British Meteorological Office from 1927 to 1936.

He later took up a senior post in the United States Weather Bureau.
