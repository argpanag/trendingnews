Next generation: Shamrock Gaels keeping it local

TheTools · 07.10.2026 12:46 · source · permalink

Riverstown, a small village in the east of County Sligo.

A locality from where the local GAA club, Shamrock Gaels, draws some of its base. Pressing matters this week are Sunday's senior county final at Markievicz Park, where the 'townies' St Mary's are the opposition.

Gaels are the defending champions, looking to win the Owen B Hunt for just the fourth time.

The mention of Riverstown, population 394, as of the 2022 census, also provides a link to the now infamous Lansdowne Road riots and, more satisfyingly, to that joyous evening for the Republic of Ireland in the Puskas Arena last November.

The Kenny name resonates well beyond the idyllic village and the trickling River Arrow.

Johnny Snr cut a dash for Sligo Rovers during the 90s, as well as for Galway United, Finn Harps and Newry Town. With the Bit O'Red he won an FAI Cup and found the net against Club Brugge in the old Cup Winners Cup.

And then in 1995, Kenny was one of two home-based players, the other being Shamrock Rovers' John Toal, who were asked to train with the Ireland squad under Jack Charlton, in advance of the friendly with England. A chance to see the pros up front for a player who never went full-time. Kenny was present in the ground as the English started their own demolition job on a stadium that was creaking.

And then involvement with the other code. Success with Shamrock Gaels as the club won the senior title in 1990 and also lining out for county. After his football days were over, Kenny enjoyed more days out with Gaels, playing junior football up until the age of 53.

The next generation. Six sons, all sporty, with three of them, Ciarán, Patrick and Niall, on the starting XV that ended Gaels' 33-year wait for county honours after a replay last October.

And then there's Johnny Jnr, who was close to finding the net in Budapest minutes before Troy Parrott sent the country into raptures and onwards to a World Cup play-off. Young Kenny, described by Gaels secretary Paul Brennan as "extremely grounded" - without the "I've made it big" vibes - played Gaelic football up until his mid-teens before the lure of soccer took hold.

The 23-year-old, like his father, began his playing days with local side Arrow Harps, before spells at Sligo Rovers, Celtic, Shamrock Rovers and Bolton Wanderers. He's now with Preston North End in the English Championship.

No doubt he will be keeping a close eye from across the water on how things will unfold at Markievicz Park. Can the Gaels do it again? Can the next generation, as exemplified by the Kennys, deliver again?

It is the Shamrock Gaels way.

Brennan, who is part of the extended squad, was speaking to RTÉ Sport ahead of the game.

"Our last period of success was in the early 90s and a lot of the families involved nowadays are children of those players," he said.

"A lot of the surnames are the same in terms of Deignans, Quinns and Kennys. All of their parents would have playing in the early 1990s. So there are good links between the periods. One of things that we are really proud of and it was the same last year, every single player on our panel has played Under-12 football for the club.

"We have no players that have moved or integrated into the area; it's just not a place where people move to or migrate to. It's unusual in today's world to have that. Others clubs will have the odd family move into the area.

"And the result of it all is that we are bucking the trend in relation to demographics. We have two teams at some age grades at girls level, where as other clubs in Sligo are looking at amalgamation. We are one of three clubs in Sligo that fields three men's teams. And we have two adult senior teams in ladies."

That sense of community and its importance, perhaps a tokenistic view when when referring to the GAA, is very much at the heart of what Shamrock Gaels are about, a club that was founded in 1972 after the amalgamation of the two clubs Sooey and Knockalassa.

"Our motto, and it's part of our crest now is: 'Our Community, Our Family, Our Club," Brennan stated.

"It's not something we implemented because it sounds good, it's the heart of everything we do. We are community driven and have a lot of initiatives through our healthy club sub-committee which are aimed at integrating every member of the community.

"We have 'Tea in the Park' on a Monday where elderly members go for a walk around Riverstown and have a cup of tea afterwards.

"Retired, pensioners, many of those with no real involvement in the club are doing something that keeps them a part of the community. We run supporters' buses to our adult games and to some county games. That brings a lot of goodwill, looking after the elderly members.

"We also have underage coaching for children with additional needs. We are a one-club model going back a few years and the ladies team have a county junior final to look forward to on Saturday." (Which they duly won).

A lot going on off the pitch and now much to be excited about on it. Gaels boast county players David Quinn, Lee Deignan, Shane Deignan, Evan Lyons and Conor Sheridan in their ranks. The quintet will be key to their hopes in defending their crown, as will the Kenny clan, described by Brennan as "athletic, skilful and talented footballers".

St Mary's are celebrating their 50th anniversary this year. A club that enjoyed success early on by way of county and provincial honours and where former All-Star Barnes Murphy was a driving force.

Their current coach is Mark Breheny, a Yeats County stalwart and a teaching colleague of Brennan in Summerhill College.

Lie of the land then ahead of the showdown?

"They are coming into it with better all-round form than we are," was Brennan's summation of the opposition, who also won the senior league crown earlier in the year.

"We started off the championship not in the best fashion; two draws in our first two group-stage games. That put us under a bit of pressure. We weren't happy with our first-half performance in the semi-final against St Molaise Gaels but we got a crucial goal just before half-time which gave us a bit of breathing space.

"We got a few two-pointers in a row from David Quinn, and that and allowed us to go on and win comfortably in the end. We weren't expecting a win (19 points in the end) of that size against St Molaise Gaels.

"St Mary's had been favourites at a few different stages during the year. We're expecting a close game; they have a very potent forward line and a lot of players on their squad would have inter-county experience.

"Their defence has improved a lot this year. Similar to ourselves they a good blend of youth and experience. Mark is in charge, so no shortage of experience and know-how. We had narrow victory under lights in the semi-final last year, so it should be a good game for the neutrals."
