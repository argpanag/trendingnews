Chelsea: Remarkable Truth of Lamine Camara Transfer Collapse Amid Liverpool Contact

TheTools · 06.09.2026 18:47 · source · permalink

Deadline day saw Monaco almost sell Lamine Camara and Folarin Balogun, yet in a dramatic twist neither player ended up leaving the club.

GMS understands Monaco's preference was to sell Balogun and keep Camara, but the French club were forced to pivot as the 11pm deadline approached.

Balogun's Everton medical had complications, leading the Toffees to reconsider the deal, and even try for Manchester United's Joshua Zirkzee at the eleventh hour. However, after making some extra medical checks, Everton were eventually able to renegotiate the £40m package and decided to proceed, only for Balogun to block the move.

While all this was ongoing, Monaco decided to sell Camara to Chelsea for €55m. It followed rejected bids of €40m, €45m and €50m.

Chelsea were led to believe the decision to sanction Camara's exit was not tied to Balogun. And the club were left furious when Monaco pulled the plug with just 30 minutes left of the window.

Monaco CEO Thiago Scuro did so because he believed Balogun's switch to Everton was back on. And he had good reason to do so given both clubs had signed the paperwork, only for US striker Balogun to get cold feet.

"We had five minutes to spare [to finalise the Balogun deal]," said Scuro. "I signed the contract. Everton signed the contract. At the last moment, the player decided not to sign.

"Balogun’s allegation is, 'I’m not feeling well because of the way the club treated me. I’m not feeling well because Everton’s medical department raised doubts about my future or my health. I have nothing [no medical issues], so I’m not comfortable signing a long contract here'. And that’s how we finished our transfer window."

By the time Monaco realised Balogun had refused to join Everton, unhappy at the concerns raised over his fitness, it was too late for Chelsea to sign Camara. This was because Scuro refused to sign a deal sheet which would have bought more time for the Senegalese midfielder to complete his move to Stamford Bridge. Everton were equally as disappointed as Chelsea, with sources alleging Monaco were slack at providing the requisite medical records which could have expedited a deal.

As all this unfolded, Camara was kept almost entirely in the dark. And although Scuro claimed Camara was content to stay at Monaco, GMS understands this is not reflective of what the 22-year-old midfielder actually told him.

Camara had already spoken to Chelsea boss Xabi Alonso on August 28 and expressed a firm desire to join Chelsea. Then, on deadline day, Camara told Scuro he would remain professional and trained as normal, but it was also made clear he wanted to leave for Chelsea if a satisfactory offer came.

When Chelsea bid €50m, which was rejected on the morning of deadline day, Camara asked again to leave. Scuro told him the club wanted €70m and ideally wished not to sell him at all.

And when Balogun's Everton move became in doubt, Camara was informed he could depart with terms in place with Chelsea for his €55m transfer. Camara had also verbally agreed a six-year contract with a one-year option.

Camara had no reason to think the transfer wouldn't happen. Yet GMS understands Scuro then called Camara at around 10.30pm, 30 minutes before the window shut, to tell him the Chelsea transfer was off. The player was told he would get a full explanation the next day.

Sources say Scuro then refused to answer calls as those close to Camara tried to establish whether the Chelsea move could be saved. Scuro argued his actions were just "part of the game" in a press conference on September 2.

Thiago Scuro Fights Back After Criticism

He said: "What happened was that around 22:00, Everton realised that, 'OK, there is no problem [with Balogun]. We are ready to do it'," said Scuro. "As soon as we got the information, we came back to Chelsea and thought, 'OK, we have our deal there [with Everton]. There is no deal on [with Chelsea].' It’s also fair to do this. It’s also part of the game.

"Because of this, we become the bad guys in the room. People say 'Monaco is not fair, Monaco is not right.' It's too much."

Chelsea sources say the club was left angered by Monaco's conduct, and at no point were they led to believe Camara's transfer would be cancelled if Balogun ended up at Everton.

Chelsea also had time to cancel Enzo Fernandez's £125m joint-British transfer record move to Manchester City but chose not to, having given their word to the player he could leave for the Etihad Stadium.

The handling of the Camara situation has led to a broken relationship between Chelsea and Monaco, and there is no guarantee the Blues will return the player in 2027.

Liverpool are another Premier League club to watch. Sources have told GMS that senior Liverpool figures made a series of calls, including on deadline day, to inform Camara he was high in their thinking for 2027 if he didn't join Chelsea.

Liverpool never intended to add another midfielder following the departure of Curtis Jones to Inter. The only way they would have bid for Camara over the summer is if Alexis Mac Allister had departed.

Camara still dreams of making a move to the Premier League and leaving Monaco in January is not discounted.
