7 Movies To Watch Before 'Avengers Endgame: Encore'

TheTools · 22.09.2026 19:19 · source · permalink

Before Avengers: Doomsday finally comes out this December, Marvel Studios has decided to do yet another re-release of Avengers: Endgame titled Avengers Endgame: Encore. With three minutes of Avengers: Doomsday content—only for IMAX and Infinity Vision viewers, for some weird reason, and only a small sneak peek for non-premium viewers—people are definitely popping out to see this project before the next big event film.

Avengers: Endgame premiered all the way back in 2019, meaning many audience members may not have seen it in quite a few years. With this in mind, there may be some catching up to do. So, before Avengers Endgame: Encore hits theaters, there are about seven movies that people should give a watch before re-experiencing this iconic project. These films are all important, entertaining, and a joy to return to before this new re-release of one of the highest-grossing movies of all time.

'Iron Man' (2008)

Iron Man is the one that started it all, giving Marvel Studios the confidence to keep moving forward with their movies and showing that heroes outside of Spider-Man could be a big success. It's impossible to say that one can watch the finale of the Infinity Saga of the MCU without watching the one that kickstarted it. With Tony Stark (Robert Downey Jr.) being one of the leading men of Avengers: Endgame, seeing his origin is quite important.

Not to mention, when Stark makes his final stand in the big climax, he makes a direct reference to the big ending of the MCU's debut. Being the major sendoff for the one and only Armored Avenger, it's pretty dang essential to make sure one remembers the Iron Man plot, so the events of Avengers Endgame: Encore can hit even harder. It's also a great experience to go from the very beginning of an 11-year character arc, all the way to the end, seeing just how far a character as flawed as Tony Stark has come.

'The Avengers' (2012)

The first time The Avengers came together in 2012 proved not only to Marvel Studios, but to audiences, that a cinematic universe like the MCU could actually work. Avengers: Endgame acts as the sendoff for almost the entire original team of Avengers, so it only makes sense that one would need to watch their first bout in The Avengers before seeing them part again in their encore. Given how much of Avengers: Endgame focuses on these original six Avengers, as they're some of the only heroes left on the planet post-Snap.

The Avengers also sets the standard for what a good Avengers adventure should look like moving forward. It was the perfect starting point to this series, and, at the end of the day, it'd feel wrong to watch the upcoming film without seeing where they all came together in the first place. Origins are important, and this 2012 blockbuster is the iconic beginning of these heroes' story together. It's nostalgic to see this group of extraordinary people become Earth's Mightiest Heroes before almost all of them leave in Avengers: Endgame.

'Guardians of the Galaxy' (2014)

While it may be a movie in The Avengers franchise, the space-based Marvel team known as the Guardians of the Galaxy is absolutely crucial to the story, given their connection to Thanos (Josh Brolin). To really understand the connection between the team and the main villain, fans need to revisit the likes of Guardians of the Galaxy, which acts as a set-up not just for the group, but for the connection between Gamora (Zoe Saldaña), Nebula (Karen Gillan), Drax (Dave Bautista), and Thanos.

Guardians of the Galaxy is also hilarious, and always worth a rewatch—one of the most rewatchable MCU movies, easily. James Gunn's vision for the Guardians is excellent and does an incredible job setting up what's needed for the space-side of the MCU, while making a simply great movie. Before this one, audiences had never really gotten the chance to explore the space side of this already expansive cinematic universe, so it's clear why Guardians of the Galaxy is so important. It's hard to beat this movie, so not only is it essential to see this movie before Avengers Endgame: Encore, but it's just a great flick to return to, period.

'Avengers: Age of Ultron' (2015)

The sequel to The Avengers had a lot to live up to after the mass success of the first movie. Such a high bar put a lot of pressure on Joss Whedon, and while it didn't do great things for Avengers: Age of Ultron, the movie didn't end up being terrible, per se. However, the sequel did struggle quite a bit with the meddling for future setup for things like the Infinity Stones. While it's still an entertaining movie, Age of Ultron struggles with an overstuffed plot. Who could forget the completely unnecessary scene of Thor (Chris Hemsworth) getting visions in the pool?

This, however, does lead to Avengers: Age of Ultron being far more essential to Avengers: Endgame. The exposition of the Infinity Stones is important, as well as the introduction of Vision (Paul Bettany). Wanda (Elizabeth Olsen) and Pietro Maximoff (Aaron Taylor-Johnson) are also incredibly important for the Infinity Saga finale. Vision and Wanda are both vital to the end of the Infinity Saga. Wanda's story up until the finale leads to her having an absolute bloodlust for Thanos, leading to her almost killing him on the last battlefield. Not to forget, Vision is the reason that the original Thanos—not the one from the past—got the Mind Stone in the first place and had the chance to snap the universe out of existence to make Avengers: Endgame possible.

'Captain America: Civil War' (2016)

Often considered an "Avengers 2.5," Captain America: Civil War is a huge moment in the MCU timeline. Its consequenceswere a major factor in The Avengers losing to Thanos, leading directly to Avengers: Endgame. The events were also patched up and led to some strong full-circle moments within the iconic 2019 project, being the first time the likes of Tony Stark and Steve Rogers (Chris Evans) see each other since the end of this third Captain America journey.

Captain America: Civil War also introduces two big MCU characters that return in Avengers: Endgame: Black Panther (Chadwick Boseman) and Spider-Man (Tom Holland). There is so much important content within this trilogy-capper—leading to the "Avengers 2.5" claims—and seeing it makes this entry in The Avengers franchise all the more satisfying. The war between the Avengers present on Earth is intense, filled with tension, and has a plethora of interpersonal drama.

'Avengers: Infinity War' (2018)

To say one should see Avengers Endgame: Encore without watching Avengers: Infinity War is an absolute disservice to the story of both films as a whole. At the end of the day, these two movies are a two-part story that needs to be consumed as one experience. The only reason Avengers: Endgame happens is the direct ramifications of the 2018 war for the Infinity Stones. Avengers: Infinity War is also commonly considered the best Avengers film, thanks to how stellar the execution of it is.

Directors Joe Russo and Anthony Russo came together to juggle a huge handful of characters at once—each getting their own time to shine at some point, multiple stories running simultaneously, and spectacle-filled action sequences. Somehow, they managed to do all of this with prowess, while keeping Thanos as the main protagonist, pushing the story forward and weaving the plots together. This third Avengers movie is one of the best MCU projects to date, so it's not only a necessary watch for Avengers Endgame: Encore, but a very entertaining one, too.

'Captain Marvel' (2019)

Despite not being the most beloved Marvel Cinematic Universe project, Captain Marvel is an important movie pre-Avengers Endgame: Encore because she kind of just... shows up. Without knowing Carol Danvers' (Brie Larson) connection to Nick Fury (Samuel L. Jackson), her appearance in the 2019 hit feels very random. So, it's pretty important for audiences to remind themselves of her story before she slides into the battlefield.

She's the one who goes into space to find Tony Stark, Nebula, and Rocket Raccoon (Bradley Cooper), making her very important to the plot. Not to forget, in the final battle, she's the one who comes in at the final hour to save the heroes from Thanos' attack on the battlefield, single-handedly destroying his ship before going toe-to-toe with him in a mano-a-mano fight. So, while her screen time in the 2019 flick isn't long, she's still vital to the plot.
