The biggest entertainment event of the year isn’t a movie — it’s ‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ | CNN Business

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:54 · source · permalink

A version of this story appeared in CNN’s Reliable Sources newsletter. To get it in your inbox, sign up for free here.

This week’s launch of “Grand Theft Auto VI” is arguably the biggest media and entertainment event of the year.

Some might even go further, since gamers have been waiting so long for Thursday’s release — the last “Grand Theft Auto” game came out in 2013 — and FOMO is kicking in among the general public.

About 15 million copies of “GTA VI” have already been preordered, according to Sensor Tower data. Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss H. Zelnick told investors last week that the sales are at “unprecedented levels.”

But if you’re not one of those gamers, you might not grasp why this is such a big business and cultural story. So I asked a couple of veteran video game industry journalists to put it into perspective.

Peer Schneider, EVP and co-founder of IGN Entertainment, said, “It’s not a question of whether GTA VI will be a hit; it’s a question of just how big it’ll be.”

“The last game in the series, GTA V, famously made a billion bucks in just three days — faster than any movie in history,” Schneider said. Based on preorder figures, “GTA VI” has already easily cleared that milestone.

The production costs are another scale, too. Even the biggest Hollywood blockbusters typically cost a few hundred million dollars to make; estimates for “GTA VI” exceed $1 billion.

Schneider pointed out that other video game publishers, and even movie studios, have “cleared out of the way,” choosing not to release many new titles starting Thursday because “everyone who is into video games will play ‘GTA VI’ and will have time for little else.”

“With so many paid and free-to-play games vying for people’s attention, time — not money — is the most precious commodity for gamers,” he added.

This moment is decades in the making. Stephen Totilo, who has covered the industry for 20 years and now publishes the video game newsletter Game File, pointed out that “GTA” has been a premier franchise for a quarter-century.

“From 2001 through 2013, GTA’s developers at Rockstar Games released five big games in the series, plus a slew of spin-offs,” he said. But since then, “Rockstar has released no new GTA games at all.” Not since the first year of Barack Obama’s second term. So there’s a lot of pent-up demand.

Rockstar could afford to take its time, though, because “GTA V” never stopped being a blockbuster. The game has sold nearly 235 million copies worldwide, according to Take-Two, and its online multiplayer mode has kept players coming back — and spending money — for more than a decade. An entire generation of gamers has grown up in the interim: Kids who were in kindergarten when “GTA V” came out are now in college.

Rockstar, a subsidiary of Take-Two, targeted a 2025 launch date but repeatedly delayed it, further raising the stakes for this week.

Retailers, broadband providers and fast-food chains have all tried to tap into the launch excitement with promotions and ad campaigns.

It’s about more than stealing cars

“It might be hard for some non-gamers to understand the appeal of ‘GTA,’” Totilo acknowledged. “They might even be unnerved by it, if they remember headlines from the early 2000s about the violence in the games.”

“On one level, they’re just a more complex take on Pac-Man,” Totilo said. “At their core, they’re games about chasing and being chased, except there are no ghosts, fruits nor simple grids,” he said. “Instead, they play out in fictionalized versions of New York, Los Angeles or GTA VI’s Vice City riff on Miami, with the player in control of the crooks and the police in hot pursuit.”

Rockstar “has added a love story this time,” he noted: “You play as both members of a criminal couple. Think of GTA VI as an interactive Bonnie and Clyde, where you control the dramatic, violent lives of two robbers in love.”

Some of this gameplay was previewed in a first-of-its-kind trailer that Netflix streamed around the world in August.

Totilo also emphasized the open-ended nature of the “GTA” franchise. “Rockstar creates meticulously detailed cities full of streets and alleys to explore, populates them with virtual citizens for players to meet, records scores of radio stations — some with music, but talk radio, too — to tune into. They fill the map with activities to try out, then set players loose.”

And loose doesn’t always mean violent: Totilo said he spent part of a recent “GTA” night delivering virtual pizzas in his virtual city.

Schneider said that for IGN, a decades-old video gaming website, “a new game from Rockstar means a sustained traffic spike for multiple years as people come to us for review coverage, guides, and game maps.”

“We’ll all be awake at 3:00 am, publishing content, when GTA VI releases in New Zealand — the first place it’ll be available in the world,” he said. “We expect many gamers to change their console settings and spoof their place of residence just to play a few precious hours earlier.”
