Knicks Ticket Prices Are Soaring After Last Season’s Championship

TheTools · 20.10.2026 09:02 · source · permalink

Advertisement

Supported by

Knicks tickets have never been cheap, but prices for the first game alone are soaring after last season’s championship — more than a trip to Paris.

This summer, Ankit Kakkad, a New York Knicks season-ticket holder, thought it was a great idea to sell his ticket to Game 4 of the N.B.A. finals for about $28,000. The money would cover his Knicks season tickets for about the next two and a half seasons.

He felt even better about the decision when the team went down by 29 points. But as the Knicks clawed back in the second half, regret crept in. It escalated as the team pulled off one of the greatest comebacks in league history, with a last-second tip-in by OG Anunoby.

“If I’m being rational, absolutely it was the right move to make,” he said recently.

But Mr. Kakkad, 42, is not rational. He is a Knicks fan. He had believed every year of his life that the Knicks would win a championship. He believes last year’s Knicks team was perhaps the greatest team in N.B.A. history.

This kind of enthusiasm is part of what drives the sports ticket market.

Knicks tickets have never been cheap, but their championship last season means that now a single ticket can cost more than a vacation in Paris, or a month’s rent in a midsize American city. The team’s home, Madison Square Garden, is the most expensive place to watch a game in the N.B.A. Prices for tickets this season, which starts on Tuesday, are averaging nearly twice what they did last season on the secondary market, including on sites like StubHub and SeatGeek.

The average price for the Knicks season opener on Tuesday is $1,894, according to the ticket reselling site TickPick, the highest average price ever for a regular-season N.B.A. game. Many tickets were going for more than $5,000.

“My impression is that people are resigned to the fact that it’s New York City and it’s the Knicks, and the Knicks are winning and the ticket prices are just going to be high,” said Jonathan Macri, who hosts a podcast called “Knicks Film School.” He said he had never seen as much demand for Knicks tickets as during the past few years, during the team’s ascent.
