Opposition calls for answers on whistleblower allegations

TheTools · 20.09.2026 13:40 · source · permalink

Opposition politicians have renewed their calls for both the former justice minister Helen McEntee and current Minister for Justice Jim O'Callaghan to answer questions before the Dáil on the handling of allegations by garda whistleblowers.

Ms McEntee, who currently serves as Minister for Foreign Affairs and Defence, yesterday insisted she had answered questions on the controversy already when she took Leaders Questions in the Dáil on Thursday.

The former justice minister said she was not sure she could add anything further thereby ruling out a specific appearance before the Dáil on the matter.

Mr O'Callaghan has meanwhile rejected calls for a public inquiry into the handling of protected disclosures within An Garda Síochána following this week's RTÉ Investigates programme.

Senior Coalition figures have also insisted that protected disclosures legislation precludes Ms McEntee from discussing how she treated the whistleblowers' concerns but garda whistleblowers Dr Donal Corcoran and Detective Superintendent Brian O'Reilly have objected to that interpretation.

The vast majority of the Opposition believe Ms McEntee and Mr O'Callaghan should make themselves available to TDs for questioning but it is unclear whether any party is willing to trigger a motion of no confidence in Ms McEntee in particular in order to bring the fall-out from the latest garda whistleblower revelations into the Dáil chamber for certain.

"Minister McEntee should have answered the questions when she had the opportunity in the Chamber on Thursday. We need to get to the bottom of who knew what and what went on," said Sinn Féin Waterford TD Conor D. McGuinness on RTE's The Week in Politics.

He added that he felt for ordinary gardaí and that the ministers should be asked about the processes of protective disclosures, culture and leadership within An Garda Síochána.

Speaking on the same programme, Social Democrats Justice Spokesperson Gary Gannon pointed out that the legislation on protective disclosures is designed to protect the whistleblower, not the Government or senior garda management from accountability in the Dáil.

"Public confidence in An Garda Síochána is crumbling, and we want to understand the nature of that and how it can be protected as well as the responsibility," said the Dublin Central TD.

He added that Ms McEntee and Mr O'Callaghan needed to come into the Dáil together this coming week to insert confidence back into the system.

However, the Minister for Education Hildegarde Naughton has repeated the Coalition's assertion that Ms McEntee has said as much as she can legally on the matter.

"Minister McEntee and all ministers are precluded from commenting on protected disclosures. It is an independent process; that is clear in statute. The Legislation was brought forward in 2014 and amended in 2022," said the Galway West TD to RTÉ's The Week in Politics.

Speaking during Leaders' Questions in the Dáil on Thursday, the Labour Party's Alan Kelly accused Ms McEntee - when she was justice minister - of having "acted without legal authority" by having "revealed Brian O'Reilly's identity, thereby depriving him of the legal protections he was entitled to and exposing the nature of his allegations".

RTÉ Investigates reported that two people who handled protected disclosures made by garda whistleblowers have themselves blown the whistle, alleging that reforms introduced to protect those who speak out have been undermined by senior management.

Deputy Kelly was taking issue with the minister's decision to direct that the investigation of Brian O'Reilly's protected disclosure "be removed from your Secretary General and be redirected to An Garda Siochána, thereby usurping the OPDC statutory direction, which is their role".

However, Ms McEntee insisted on Thursday and again on several occasions during a media event yesterday that she had "adhered to the law at all times" when it came to protected disclosures during her time at the Department of Justice.

She also told reporters yesterday that she was not considering her position as minister over the controversy.

Two of the whistleblowers who featured in Wednesday's broadcast, Dr Corcoran and Det Supt O'Reilly, have said there must be an independent investigation into the concerns they and others have raised about the protected disclosure system within An Garda Síochána.

Revelations 'tip of the iceberg' - Former AGSI general secretary

Antoinette Cunningham, former General Secretary of the Association of Garda Sergeants and Inspectors (AGSI) has said that she was not shocked or surprised by what was revealed in the RTÉ Investigates programme on protected disclosures not being handled properly in An Garda Síochána.

She told RTÉ's This Week that what was shown in the programme might only be the "tip of the iceberg" in terms of how disclosures were handled by An Garda Síochána.

She said that people make protected disclosures because they recognise that something is wrong and when it is not handled properly, it demoralises people and they suffer victimisation and penalisation as a result.

"That is just wrong," she said.

Ms Cunningham said it was a shocking indictment that those in the office of garda protected disclosures feel they themselves have to make protected disclosures because of issues they have identified.

She added that some of the people that she represented during her time in the AGSI made protected disclosures and were not happy about how their disclosure was handled by An Garda Síochána at the time.

She said that she spoke to some people this week regarding their issues, and in one case, the identity of the complainant was disclosed which she said is a serious breach, but none of those she spoke to featured on the RTÉ Investigates programme.

Ms Cunningham added that in some cases, the issues raised in a protected disclosure may have been handled properly through investigation, but the legislation and policy on protected disclosure was not adhered to or that aspect may not be handled in the way it should have been.

She said that gardaí deserve a tribunal that can get to the root of the issues and that independence is key when it comes to something as serious as this.

"There are matters that need to be looked into," she said, adding that An Garda Síochána cannot be entitled to "correct its own homework" when it is something as serious as this.
