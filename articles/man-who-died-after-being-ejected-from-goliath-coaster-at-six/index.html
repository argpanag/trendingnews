AP: At least 6 other riders have died on roller coasters in the US since 2003

A man who fell to his death from a roller coaster at an Atlanta-area amusement park was not the first to die on such a ride in recent years.

Authorities have not released information about how 42-year-old Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem was thrown from Goliath at Six Flags Over Georgia on Monday. Several people also were injured.

Here’s a look at other fatalities and injuries on US roller coasters:

Six Flags Magic Mountain

The park in Southern California announced last month that it was retiring its famous X2 roller coaster following lawsuits alleging riders suffered serious brain injuries. The park settled one wrongful-death lawsuit with the family of a 22-year-old rider who was rushed to a hospital with severe brain bleeding in 2022. He died a day later. Six Flags Entertainment Corp. said the ride had consistently passed safety tests.

Epic Universe

A medical examiner concluded last year that a man who was unresponsive after riding a roller coaster at the Universal Orlando Resort park died from blunt impact injuries. Joshua Stephany, the medical examiner for the Orlando area, ruled the death an accident.

Texas Giant

In 2013, a woman riding a roller coaster in Texas died after she was thrown from her seat, fell 75 feet and struck a metal beam. Witnesses said the woman expressed concern about the Texas Giant roller coaster’s safety bar not completely engaging as the ride was starting.

Darien Lake

A US Army veteran who lost both legs in Iraq was killed in 2011 after he was thrown from a roller coaster at Darien Lake Theme Park & Resort in upstate New York. Investigators said operators did not follow rules posted at the ride’s entrance, which require that riders have both legs.

Dixie Landin’

A 21-year-old rider fell to her death from a roller coaster at the amusement park in Louisiana in 2010. A state investigation could not determine a cause.

Disneyland

In 2003, a locomotive broke loose from a train on the Big Thunder Mountain Railroad roller coaster in California, killing a man and injuring multiple other riders. The car derailed after a guide wheel fell off. State investigators found the wheel was not tightened according to Disney’s standards and a safety wire was not installed.

Who is responsible for making sure amusement park rides are safe?

The federal government has no regulation over amusement park rides, rather that responsibility is left to the states.

Mobile amusement rides, like those at a carnival, are regulated by the Consumer Product Safety Commission, but “fixed-site rides,” like the ones at Six Flags, are outside of the commission’s jurisdiction.

This came after Congress passed the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1981, limiting the commission’s oversight only to rides not permanently fixed to the site and leaving it to states to regulate amusement park rides.

There is no uniformity across the country over which state regulator is in charge of inspecting amusement park rides. For example, the Texas Department of Insurance takes the lead that state, whereas in California, it falls under the state Department of Industrial Relations.

This web of state-by-state regulations have received backlash, especially by Sen. Ed Markey, who has repeatedly introduced legislation to return the oversight of amusement park rides to the CPSC, and calling the current legislation a “loophole” in safety regulations.

The six flags that shaped Texas — and inspired an amusement park

The first Six Flags amusement park, Six Flags Over Texas, opened its gates in 1961 after real estate developer Angus Wynne Jr. envisioned the park as a temporary use for the land before developing it into an industrial complex.

The park was designed to celebrate Texas’ history, and its name refers to the nations whose flags had flown over the state: Spain, France, Mexico, Texas, the United States and the Confederacy.

It wasn’t until 2017 that Six Flags decided to stop flying the Confederate Flag over its amusement parks, The Washington Post reported.

The flags represent the “changes, complexities, and richness found throughout the state’s history,” according to the Bullock Texas State History Museum, but fail to recognize the long-standing history of Indigenous people who dominated the region.

Wynne Jr. later sold Six Flags and the company was eventually expanded. Today, Six Flags is the largest regional theme park company in the world.

What are the requirements to become a roller coaster operator?

Six Flags says various teams perform extensive maintenance and inspections to keep its rides running safely. But what qualifications do the employees operating the rides need?

Six Flags currently lists various ride operator positions throughout the country, with similar requirements and qualifications across locations.

Applicants must be at least 16 years old, with hourly pay ranging from $12 in Texas to $17.50 in California.

A current listing for a rides team member in Austell, Georgia, a role with similar qualifications and responsibilities to a ride operator, pays $11.50 per hour.

Some of the daily functions of an operator include performing daily equipment checks before the ride opens to the public and confidently operating rides “using controls, signals, and communication.”

Operators are also responsible for pressing buttons and switches to start the rides and ensuring restraints have been checked and secured, according to several of the postings.

Ride operators must also pass ride certification tests with 100% accuracy.

Six Flags says its rides are engineered with safety systems and that each attraction has unique requirements.

The park says weight and height requirements, among other restrictions, are posted at each attraction, but “guests are responsible for making informed decisions about whether a ride is appropriate for them.”

But several current job postings suggest that operators are responsible for enforcing rider restrictions and measuring for height requirements.

Expert: Goliath manufacturer has “stellar record,” but ride has no secondary restraints

The Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia is manufactured by Bolliger & Mabillard, a company with a “stellar” safety record, according to Brian Avery, a professor at the University of Florida who specializes in ride safety and risk management. But, notably, the ride does not include a secondary safety restraint for riders, Avery said.

The “scalloped-like bucket seat” on Goliath features a waist restraint that sits on top of the thigh and part of the waist, Avery explained. The ride does not have an over-the-shoulder harness like some other roller coasters, he said.

“It’s really a design consideration,” Avery said, explaining that during the ride’s development designers typically do a “hazard containment assessment,” to determine the best mechanism to restrain the occupants while experiencing the coaster’s speed and transition forces.

Still, a secondary restraint is something Avery believes should be considered. “If you have your primary fail, what are you left with? Yeah, nothing,” he said. “And it’s an impossible feat to stay contained if it did fail, because of the forces, the speed.”

But roller coasters are designed “to flirt with the edge of danger,” Avery says. “We want to feel the thrill of it, and some of that is you go airborne, some of it is you’re getting sucked back in your seat. So it really is what people are chasing.”

“There are inherent risks associated with amusement rides and devices, and we can’t escape that. But those risks should be identified. They should inform the public, and there should be measures to mitigate or eliminate those,” he added.

What we know — and don't know — about the incident at Six Flags Over Georgia

We’ve been bringing you updates after a man died at Six Flags Over Georgia when he fell from a roller coaster yesterday.

Here’s what we know, and don’t know, about the incident so far:

What we know

  • Who died in the incident: The man who died Monday has been identified by police as 42-year-old Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem of Atlanta.
  • Where the incident happened: Kariem died after falling off the Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia near Atlanta. The ride has a top speed of 70 mph and a top height of 200 feet, according to Six Flags.
  • What witnesses saw: Onlookers at the park described seeing Kariem fall out of the coaster car and hit it three times before falling onto the roof of a haunted house attraction below.
  • How Six Flags has responded: Six Flags has closed nine rides across its parks after the death, a spokesperson for the company said yesterday. The park is on weekend operations and is scheduled to open on Friday, the spokesperson said.
  • How the ride’s manufacturer responded: The manufacturer of the roller coaster, Bolliger & Mabillard, called yesterday’s events “tragic.” The company said it is “actively investigating” the incident but had no further comment until it has “a better understanding of what transpired.”

What we don’t know

  • How Kariem fell out of the coaster: What caused Kariem to fall off the roller coaster remains unclear. Investigators continue to examine the circumstances surrounding the incident, the Cobb County Police Department’s Major Crimes Unit said.
  • What the cause of death was: The cause of Kariem’s death has not been disclosed. The Cobb County Medical Examiner’s Office said it was “unable to share information during ongoing investigations,” adding that “all information will be verified and released by the Cobb County Police Department.”
  • Who was injured: Several people were injured in the incident, Cobb County Board of Commissioners Chairwoman Lisa Cupid said. Exactly how many people were injured, how severely they were harmed and their identities have not been released.

Six Flags financial problems started well before tragic death

Six Flags has been struggling with financial losses and a weak stock performance for years.

The company hasn’t recorded a profitable quarter since the summer of 2024, following the completion of an $8 billion merger with rival Cedar Fair. Six Flags reported a $203 million net loss during its most recent quarter, more than double the nearly $100 million loss it saw a year earlier.

Six Flags reports its next earnings for the summer months in early November. Wall Street analysts forecast a return to profitability.

The company has been shedding some of its parks to help with costs, selling seven before the start of the 2026 season. It also closed one combination Maryland amusement park and waterpark at the end of last season.

Safety issues also aren’t helping its finances. Those pose a risk for the company’s ability to get adequate and affordable liability insurance, the company warned in its annual financial filing at the start of this year.

“Although the company carries liability insurance to cover possible incidents, coverage may not be adequate to cover liabilities (and) the company may not be able to obtain coverage at commercially reasonable rates,” it warned.

Six Flags (FUN) shares are down 23% so far this year, even before Tuesday’s plunge. The stock is nearly 80% lower since its merger with Cedar Fair closed in July 2024.

Six Flags Over Georgia has been open for almost 60 years

Six Flags Over Georgia, the amusement park where a man died after falling off a roller coaster yesterday, has been open for nearly 60 years, according to CNN affiliate WSB-TV.

The park opened in the summer of 1967, according to the outlet. Years later, in 1973, the “Great American Scream Machine” roller coaster opened there. At the time, it was the “world’s tallest, longest and fastest roller coaster,” according to Six Flags Over Georgia.

Now home to 45 roller coasters, the park welcomes about 3 million guests annually, CNN affiliate WXIA-TV reported earlier this year.

In July, two people were seriously injured in the park after a tree fell on visitors during a storm, WXIA-TV reported.

Fights and a shooting at Six Flags Over Georgia during its opening weekend in 2024 also prompted officials to call a public safety meeting, according to the outlet.

Every ride at its parks “undergoes extensive inspection and maintenance procedures,” according to Six Flags.

Daily inspections are undertaken by park teams, while third parties, including state ride inspectors, insurance inspectors, and engineering experts, also regularly evaluate the rides, it says.

Six Flags loses about $170 million in market value following roller coaster tragedy

Shares of Six Flags Entertainment sank 14% to their lowest level since 2009 on Tuesday after a deadly roller coaster incident forced the theme park operator to temporarily close some rides across the country.

The sell-off erases about $170 million in market value from Six Flags, a company that was already facing significant financial pressure.

Six Flags announced Monday it has temporarily shut down nine rides at various parks after a man fell from the Goliath coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia.

Police have not said how the man died or how many people were hurt.

Six Flags, which trades under the ticker symbol “FUN,” has lost nearly half its value over the past year. Shares fell Tuesday to the lowest intraday level since December 2009. The company had a market value of $1.22 billion at Monday’s closing bell but that has declined to less than $1.1 billion.

Six Flags is backed by NFL star Travis Kelce, the husband of Taylor Swift. The company announced Kelce as a brand ambassador in March.

Kelce teamed up with activist hedge fund Jana Partners in October 2025 to buy a 9% stake in Six Flags.

Last month, Jana Partners called on Six Flags to immediately hire an investment bank to explore a sale, The Wall Street Journal reported.
