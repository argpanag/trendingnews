Preview: War of words tees up fiery Aviva Test

TheTools · 20.11.2026 14:48 · source · permalink

Finally, eventually, it's time to focus on the rugby.

After five days of press conferences and disciplinary hearings, of hip-drops and knee-drops, we have a game to cover.

Remarkably, Ireland and South Africa could be doing it all over again next week, depending on how this weekend goes. Popcorn at the ready.

If Andy Farrell's side beat the Springboks this evening, and New Zealand and France also win their games with England and Argentina, then it will be Ireland against South Africa once again at Twickenham on Nations Championship finals weekend.

A rematch next week could even be on the cards if South Africa win this evening, providing Ireland claim a losing bonus-point, and England get nothing from their meeting with the All Blacks.

Winning the Nations Championship would be something major for Andy Farrell's Ireland to use as a World Cup springboard, but with France expected to lock in first place in the northern table this weekend, it looks highly unlikely Ireland will be contesting the overall final on Sunday week in London.

A top-two finish in the table would at least guarantee Ireland another Test against either the All Blacks or Springboks next week, and with less than a year to go until the Rugby World Cup, an extra game against top quality opposition would be invaluable to finish off 2026.

If Ireland are to finish the year in style, it starts this week.

They appeared to be clicking into gear in their first game of the month against Argentina, before their performance regressed against Fiji last week, with Farrell admitting the 43-15 scoreline "flattered them".

While Ireland have won three of the five meetings of these sides in the Farrell-Erasmus era, they have lost two of the last three, the most recent of which was a 24-13 defeat at the Aviva Stadium last year. Just like the Fiji game, the scoreline flattered Ireland.

Ireland's discipline capitulated against the world champions, with James Ryan shown a 20-minute red card, and Andrew Porter, Paddy McCarthy, Jack Crowley and Sam Prendergast all shown yellow cards, with the home side giving up 20 penalties, six of which came at the scrum.

Both Ireland's discipline and scrum have improved since then, but neither are yet a strength.

Through five games of this Nations Championship, their penalty count of 8.8 per game is just about below the average, but the position where they give up those penalties remains a concern.

Of the 44 penalties conceded, 13 of those have been in their own 22, and they have been unable to shake their habit of giving up penalties in flurries when they are defending their own line, which has seen Sam Illo and Tadhg Beirne spend time in the sin-bin across the last fortnight.

When you face the Springboks, there is almost an acceptance that you'll be dealing with some scrum penalties, given their dominance at the setpiece, but if Ireland remain disciplined and controlled round the park, they can prevent it from being a major factor.

"Trying to keep 15 on the pitch would be helpful," defence coach Simon Easterby said this week, when asked what they key is to beating South Africa.

"Making sure you are as disciplined as you can be, not to give them access points.

"We need to make sure that we're better in lots of areas, but like I said at the start, making sure we're disciplined and avoiding the avoidable penalties and avoiding the opportunities that we give them access to will go a long way for us to be in the fight in the end."

Given the hammering South Africa inflicted on Ireland last year, the Springboks have somehow managed to come to Dublin feeling aggrieved.

Their main grievance is the six-game suspension of their head coach, with Erasmus banned after an explosive press conference on Monday afternoon, while there has also been uproar in the world champions' camp that Ireland's Sam Prendergast avoided suspension following a hip-drop tackle in their win against Fiji last week.

Erasmus (below) described the tackle as "terrible" and worthy of "an immediate red card" on Monday afternoon, before declaring the Ireland out-half would escape a ban due to a "technicality". It turns out the technicality is that it was not deemed to be a red card offence.

With Ireland out to prove a point following last year's defeat, and the Springboks believing the world is out to get them, there could be fireworks early on at the Aviva.

Even without grievances, the world champions have shown themselves to start games hot. Through five games of the Nations Championship, South Africa have outscored their opponents 67-7 in the first 20 minutes of games, while they have won the 20 minutes after half time 47-0.

If Ireland need inspiration, then they should look to their 25-24 win in Durban in 2024.

While that game will forever be remembered for Ciarán Frawley's late drop-goals and a miracle Irish comeback, their win was built on a ferocious opening quarter of physicality which gave them a 10-point head start.

Farrell has rolled the dice with his team selection, taking a page straight out of the Springboks' playbook by naming Dan Sheehan as his starting number 8.

The Ireland captain moves from the front row to the back row in arguably the most adventurous selection call made by the Ireland head coach in his six years in charge, backing Sheehan's ballcarrying ability and physicality to fit what is needed in this game, while holding Nick Timoney back on the bench.

With Sheehan at number 8, it makes space for Rónan Kelleher to start at hooker, with the 28-year-old viewed as a more natural scrummager. It's worth noting that Kelleher was the starting hooker for Ireland's last two wins in this fixture, in 2023 and 2024.

Sheehan has only ever started one game in the back row, lining up at number 8 in Leinster's URC win against Munster last month, and he insists he won't be overthinking the change of scenery.

"It can't be that complicated. That’s my approach to it, to be honest," he said at yesterday's captain's run.

"Once the ball’s in play, it’s the same thing, it’s just playing rugby.

"We all know each other’s roles and what we expect of everybody is the same thing, really. You’ve got to be able to do everything.

"Obviously, there’s a few details that can get the ball into play, but once the ball’s in play, it’s play on."

Since their 2024 second Test win in Durban, Ireland's record against the big three in rugby makes for sobering reading, with six consecutive defeats to South Africa, New Zealand and France, all six of which have been by double digits.

There have been flashes of the old Ireland in 2026, with hammerings of England and Scotland in the Six Nations, and those performances will have to be matched - if not bettered - if they are to get the job done this week.

Verdict: South Africa
