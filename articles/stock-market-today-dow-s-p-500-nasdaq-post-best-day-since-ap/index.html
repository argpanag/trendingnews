Stocks soared on Monday in their best day since April 2025 as investors cheered Nvidia's (NVDA) board reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO. It marked a major rebound as buyers stepped back in for the holiday-shortened week after a bruising sell-off.

The S&P 500 (^GSPC) rose 4.7% following seven straight days of declines. The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) jumped more than 1,400 points to recover by 3% following its worst week since the COVID-related sell-off in 2020. The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) rallied nearly 6.5%.

Nvidia stock soared 31% after the AI chipmaker announced it has reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately.

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Nvidia's board chair Harvey Jones said.

  • Oatly stock soars after Belgian investor ups stake

    Oatly (OTLY) stock was on track to nearly double in Monday's trading session after Belgian investment company Verlinvest upped its stake in the Swedish oat milk manufacturer.

    Verlinvest purchased the interest from China Resources — a Chinese state-owned conglomerate with which it had a joint venture in Oatly. The purchase brought Verlinvest's stake in Oatly to roughly 42.6%. The investment company also backs brands like Vita Coco, Insomnia Cookies, and Vitaminwater.

    Related to that buyout, on Nov. 20, Oatly confirmed that Verlinvest had issued a tender offer to comply with Swedish law, calling it a technicality. Verlinvest is offering $4.13 per share of the company's American Depositary shares. Oatly's board urged shareholders not to tender their shares.

    Oatly stock surged as much as 108% before paring some gains. Its shares have a short interest of just 2.3%, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence. Shares are down by around 98% since its May 2021 IPO.

  • Nvidia stock's one-day gain rivals top 5 in AI chip giant's history

    Even after pulling back slightly from its morning highs, a 31% jump puts today on pace to be Nvidia's fourth-best single-day performance since its 1999 IPO, and its biggest gain since May 2003. To put the scale in perspective: almost all of Nvidia's other top-ten days occurred between 1999 and 2003, when the entire company was worth under $4 billion.

  • Stock market surge driven by 'mechanical' buying warns strategist, as Nvidia shares spike higher

    Shares of Nvidia (NVDA) surged on Monday following Jensen Huang's surprise reinstatement as CEO, sparking a massive rally across US stocks. Nomura cross-asset strategist Charlie McElligott warns of reading too much into the massive bounce.

  • Goldman Sachs: 3 conditions for the stock market to bottom

    The S&P 500 and Dow are beginning the week in a correction, thanks to last week's sell-off. The big question for investors is whether the market will continue to tank or bounce back.

    In a note on Friday, Goldman Sachs' research team, led by Ben Snider, highlighted three market events that could support the stock market correction bottoming out and equities recovering from here.

    "One catalyst would be news that increases investor confidence in the trajectory of future earnings associated with AI investment and monetization," the Goldman team wrote.

    "Another potential catalyst would be an improvement in the macro backdrop, such as a dovish signal for Fed policy. Third would be a major decline in investor positioning, such as has often marked the bottoms of past drawdowns."

    With these conditions to watch, the team noted that, due to uncertainty surrounding the earnings outlook, there's a wide distribution of near-term outcomes across markets.

Goldman Sachs: 3 conditions for the stock market to bottom

The S&P 500 and Dow are beginning the week in a correction, thanks to last week's sell-off. The big question for investors is whether the market will continue to tank or bounce back.

In a note on Friday, Goldman Sachs' research team, led by Ben Snider, highlighted three market events that could support the stock market correction bottoming out and equities recovering from here.

"One catalyst would be news that increases investor confidence in the trajectory of future earnings associated with AI investment and monetization," the Goldman team wrote.

"Another potential catalyst would be an improvement in the macro backdrop, such as a dovish signal for Fed policy. Third would be a major decline in investor positioning, such as has often marked the bottoms of past drawdowns."

With these conditions to watch, the team noted that, due to uncertainty surrounding the earnings outlook, there's a wide distribution of near-term outcomes across markets.
