João Félix has a European return on his mind – and one club still holds a special place: “I dreamed of playing for them as a child”

TheTools · 27.09.2026 12:32 · source · permalink

After a title-winning season with Al Nassr, the Portugal forward reflects on Atlético, Barcelona, Cristiano Ronaldo and what comes next.

João Félix (26, Viseu, Portugal) is tipped to become Portuguese soccer’s next leading figure. His breakthrough came when he was barely out of his teens, but maturity has been his companion in recent months after several difficult seasons. The decision to take his game to Saudi Arabia, where he was named the best player in the league after winning the title with Al Nassr, has lifted his performances and, above all, his confidence in everything he does. He showed as much against Wales last Thursday, scoring the only goal of the game.

“I want to win a major title with the national team. Spain stopped us at the World Cup. They were the best,” he told AS from Portugal’s training camp. He enjoys playing in Saudi Arabia, but he is keeping an eye on Europe and a possible return. He does not say it outright, but the Portuguese forward dreams of wearing Barcelona’s colors again. Barça was the club he admired as a child, and that remains true today.

The World Cup didn’t go as Portugal had hoped...

No. Naturally, we wanted to go further, but we played Spain, who were the best team at the World Cup. We were in the game, and then they scored that goal. That’s soccer.

Do you think Spain deserved to win the World Cup?

Well, yes. They played well. They defended well, attacked well and had a very clear idea of how they wanted to play and do things. They became champions because they deserved it. Nobody wins a World Cup without deserving it, and they obviously did.

You had a very good season and were named the Saudi league’s best player. Are you happy with how it went?

Yes, yes, of course. Above all, because we became champions after a tough season. It isn’t easy to win the league with Al Nassr. I think they had gone seven years without winning it. At the start, we lost the Super Cup on penalties. We could have won that too, but I’m happy with the season and with becoming champions. I played very well, and I have to be happy about that.

In my view, your performances have improved, as we also saw at the World Cup. I don’t know whether that’s down to the way Al Nassr play or the freedom they give you...

Yes. I feel better as a player. I understand the game and the different moments in a match better. Being 26 isn’t the same as being 20 or 21. I feel much more complete as a player now than I did before. And, of course, playing in my position, in a style that makes use of my abilities for the good of the team, has helped me. That means I can help the team more. As I’ve always said, I believe in myself, and I think I can help the team much more when I play in my position. That’s what happened last year.

Did Atlético Madrid perhaps come too soon for you?

Well, I don’t regret it. Of course I don’t. I had some wonderful, very important moments at Atleti. Naturally, I would have liked things to go better, but that’s soccer. It’s part of growing as a player. It helped me grow and learn, but no, I obviously don’t regret going there so young.

Why weren’t you able to succeed at Atlético? There were times when you played at a very high level...

We won the title one year, and it was a very good season. That’s soccer. Sometimes things work out, and other times they don’t. As I’ve always said, I never stopped trying to do what was best for me and for the team. I tried to help whenever I could, and if things didn’t work out, they didn’t work out. As with everything in life, players have highs and lows. Of course I would have liked more highs than I had at Atlético.

Do you still watch their games? Do you still consider yourself an Atlético supporter?

I watch the games when I have nothing to do at home. I turn on the TV and watch. I like watching certain games and certain teams. I keep up with the results and watch matches when I can. If I have nothing else to do, I’ll watch them.

Did you see the latest derby against Real Madrid?

No, I wasn’t there. What was I doing? I had a few days off and was relaxing on the beach. I was taking it easy. I didn’t watch any soccer.

What memories do you have of Barcelona?

Good ones, very good ones. In the end, we didn’t win anything, but simply playing for Barcelona made me very happy. Obviously, being there on loan isn’t the same as belonging to the club. Everything is different when you’re their player. Anything can happen in a year: you can be doing really well or really badly, you can have highs and lows. But I’m very happy to have worn the Barça shirt. It was a childhood dream for me, my father and my brother. It was an honor.

Real Madrid are the only one of Spain’s big clubs you haven’t played for, then?

I would have been the biggest traitor in soccer! No, no, no. I really enjoyed being at Barcelona. Well, yes, Real Madrid is the one I missed, but I’m proud to have played for Barcelona.

I didn’t see you among the 30 Ballon d’Or nominees. It seems players in Saudi Arabia have to prove more than others, yet a player from the Saudi league has been nominated, and it isn’t you.

Those things aren’t up to me. I don’t know who chooses the nominees, but I wasn’t too concerned about it. I wasn’t on the list, but I wasn’t expecting to be. Honestly, I didn’t even know the list was coming out that day. I’m relaxed about it. In the end, people still don’t watch much of the Saudi league, and it’s a little underrated. Of course it isn’t one of the top five leagues, but it’s a good competition. I’ve had a great season, but I’m relaxed about the nominations.

Do players over there have to prove more?

It seems that way. Maybe if we’d gone further at the World Cup, I might have been there, but we didn’t.

Who’s your favorite?

I’ve always said the Ballon d’Or should go to the player you watch and think, “Wow.” Someone who’s a star and deserves to be remembered in soccer. If there’s a player who makes you turn on the TV and stay glued to it, it’s Lamine. It’s true that Mbappé and Kane have had great seasons, but Lamine is soccer in its purest form. He’s won the World Cup, too. In a World Cup year, winning it is always very important if you want to get closer to the Ballon d’Or.

You played alongside him, didn’t you?

Yes, right when he was starting out. I think he made his debut the year before I arrived, but he wasn’t playing regularly. During my year there, he started playing around halfway through the season and soon became a starter.

Do you see a bit of yourself in him, in that eye-catching way he plays?

Other people can say that, but the truth is that I watch Barça, and of course I watch them a lot because of Lamine. I love the way he plays, the way he takes on a game and the enjoyment he gives us. You watch him and enjoy it. Sitting at home on the couch, you enjoy what he and Barcelona are doing just as much.

But there’s Atlético, and Mourinho’s Real Madrid, too...

Mourinho is Portuguese, but I don’t know him. I’ve never worked with him. Barcelona, Real Madrid and Atlético are teams people like to watch, but of course I watch Barcelona more.

What does it mean to play with Cristiano?

It’s something few people in soccer can say they’ve done. I got to do it with the national team, and now at Al Nassr. It’s an honor, honestly. We grew up watching Cristiano. He was our idol, someone we looked up to, and now I get to share these moments with him. Winning the league last year was especially meaningful. Being able to help Cristiano win the title he had been chasing since he arrived... We have to enjoy it as much as we can because we know it won’t last forever. But while he’s here, we’ll be here too.

Can you imagine setting up his 1,000th goal?

I’ve thought about that. When he’s one goal away, every time I get the ball I’ll look for him to see if I can give him the pass. That goal will go down in soccer history, and being part of it would be a great moment.

What’s left for you to do in soccer? What’s your next goal? What drives you to keep improving?

To get better. I’m very happy at Al Nassr. This will be my second year. I’ll be 27 at the end of the season. I do plan to return to Europe, though perhaps not right away. I don’t know if that will be at the end of this season or the next, but I want to go back, play in the Champions League again and compete there again. It’s always different. Playing in Europe, in the Champions League, against the best teams in the world gives you extra motivation. That’s definitely on my mind.

But you seem very happy at Al Nassr...

I’m very comfortable in Saudi Arabia. I’m happy and enjoying my soccer, but it’s true that I’m also thinking about returning. I don’t know whether it’ll be next year, in two years, three or four, but I’d like to play in European competitions again.

Do you think Portugal can win one of the major international titles?

Of course. I’ve won two Nations Leagues, but winning a European Championship or a World Cup would be incredible too.
