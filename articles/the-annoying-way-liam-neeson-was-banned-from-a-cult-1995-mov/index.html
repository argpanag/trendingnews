The "annoying" way Liam Neeson was banned from a cult 1995 movie

TheTools · 08.09.2026 12:30 · source · permalink

Fresh from a career-best and Academy Award-nominated performance in Steven Spielberg’s Schindler’s List, Liam Neeson would have been well within his rights to believe that his days of tireless auditioning and pounding the pavement in search of work were over.

Unfortunately for him, Hollywood doesn’t work that way. While it’s undeniably true that his name value went up by several notches after playing the title role in a ‘Best Picture’-winning masterpiece, he still wasn’t in a position where he could walk into any role of his choosing, no questions asked.

Neeson found that out the hard way, and the whole thing left a sour taste in his mouth, with the Irishman having cut short his holidays to meet about what he thought was a guaranteed role in an eventual cult favourite, only to discover that his presence had been vetoed, and he wasn’t sure who to blame.

Well, he was, but as it turned out, he blamed the wrong person. For all intents and purposes, the eventual action hero was convinced that a gig in Sam Raimi’s western, The Quick and the Dead, was his for the taking, and while he didn’t name names, you’d have to imagine he was up for Cort, played by Russell Crowe.

As the top-billed name in the ensemble, as well as a co-producer and a key creative driving force who paid out of her own pocket to ensure Leonardo DiCaprio was hired, Sharon Stone was integral in deciding who would surround her in the gun-toting adventure. From where he was sitting, Neeson wasn’t on the list.

“It was the reverse position of what an actor usually finds himself in,” he mused. “Sam wanted me, the writer wanted me, the producers wanted me, Universal wanted me. Sharon Stone apparently didn’t want me.” All that for nothing, and his last-minute rejection managed to piss his wife, Natasha Richardson, off, too.

“The annoying thing was that I was having a holiday with my wife in Thailand, and we were flying out from London, and I was asked if I could fly to Thailand via LA to meet with Sharon,” Neeson explained. “It was a very pleasant meeting. Obviously, the decision had been made. I think Sharon was just going through the motions.”

It was a waste of everyone’s time to have the Taken throat-puncher divert his holiday to pitch up in LA for a chinwag with Stone, although he was under the impression it was a mere formality, only for The Quick and the Dead to make a last-minute pivot and hire the much lesser-known, and presumably cheaper, Crowe instead.

The final sting in the tale was that Stone released a statement through her representatives claiming it wasn’t her decision, but “it was the production that decided a different way.” That didn’t really matter to Neeson, since he missed out on the underrated oater regardless.
