Avis Bills Renter More Than $4000, Claiming He Drove 18,000 Miles in 7 Days

TheTools · 20.10.2026 16:52 · source · permalink

In case you need some help with the math, that distance in that time would require averaging a continuous 107 miles per hour.

Family road trips are usually full of unexpected occurrences. Somebody needs to hit the restroom minutes after leaving the last stop; nobody agrees on where to get lunch; someone constantly wants to know how much longer until they get where they’re going. But according to one Avis bill, a Toronto man's trip also involved the sort of non-stop driving that would interest an endurance racing team, as the company charged him for covering almost 18,000 miles in a week.

When one simply divides the mileage by the time allotted, the plausibility of the charge becomes comical. According to Toronto CityNews, Yuehao Huang rented a vehicle in Montreal on September 11 for a seven-day family trip. He says his phone recorded roughly 1400 km (870 miles) traveled during the journey, including walking. Yet Avis billed him for 28,933 km (17,978 miles), adding about CA $5900 (roughly $4200 at current exchange rates) to a prepaid rental costing more than CA $1000 ($710 USD).

Let's do the math. Seven full days contain 168 hours, so covering the billed distance would require averaging 172 km/h (107 mph) throughout every one of them. That's the average, too; every minute spent parked would require driving faster afterward to make up the difference.

We can look at it in the reverse way, too. Let's say he consistently drive 62 mph, never stopped, and somehow refueled on the fly. It would take roughly 289 hours to reach that mileage—more than 12 days. Allow eight hours a day for sleeping during a seven-day rental, and the required driving average climbs to roughly 258 km/h (161 mph). And again, these figures don't account for eating, sleeping, refueling, or anything else. In other words, nobody needed access to the car's tracking data to realize this was a silly figure.

Still, somehow, the charge apparently survived the initial complaint. Huang told CityNews that not only did his conversation with Avis not lead to recognition of the error, but said his credit card company American Express didn't help out either. Only after the media reached out did Avis reportedly admit there was a data entry error; it also claimed that it apologized, and issued a mileage refund. Consider this one more reminder to snap plenty of photos of the car, the mileage, and anything else pertinent both at the pickup and drop-off of any rental vehicle.
