Predictions, Best Bets & Odds for Michelsen vs Etcheverry

By Eric Rosales in Tennis

Published: September 6, 2026 at 10:33 am EDT

  • Alex Michelsen battles Tomas Martin Etcheverry for a trip to the quarterfinals at the US Open
  • Etcheverry’s reliable serve keeps the matchup competitive, but his outright price seems short for an upset case
  • See my Michelsen vs Etcheverry prediction, best bets, and the latest betting lines below

The Round of 16 at the US Open Men’s Singles brings a compelling matchup as Alex Michelsen takes on Tomas Martin Etcheverry.

Their recent hard-court form is also notably different: Michelsen has won nine of his last 11 matches on the surface across Washington, Montreal, Cincinnati and the US Open, a big reason why he’s the heavy betting favorite Sunday.

Etcheverry, meanwhile, is just 3-3 over his comparable hard-court stretch.

Action gets underway at 2:30pm ET on the Grandstand Court at USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, New York, with ESPN Unlimited carrying the broadcast.

Michelsen vs Etcheverry Prediction and Best Bets

Currently ranked #32 in the world, Argentina’s Etcheverry brings the better official ranking into this match, while Michelsen sits at #46. The more predictive indicators, however, point in the other direction: Tennis Abstract’s Elo ratings, updated August 31, give Michelsen a 1,846 overall Elo and a 1,827 hard-court Elo, compared with Etcheverry’s 1,763 and 1,713, respectively; Tennis Ratio’s 52-week key-stat data also shows Michelsen winning more return games (22.6% to 19.4%) and converting more break points (40.6% to 38.3%).

  • Pick: Michelsen -4.5 Games (-117 at Polymarket)

Michelsen’s price is supported by more than home-court momentum. His hard-court Elo advantage is 113 points, a meaningful gap in a best-of-five match, and his return numbers give him a clear path to separation. He wins 52.3% of return points against first serves and 22.6% of return games, well ahead of Etcheverry’s 48.1% and 19.4%. That pressure should create enough break chances to offset Etcheverry’s respectable service-game win rate.

Etcheverry does have the cleaner serve profile—he wins 82.7% of service games to Michelsen’s 81.6% and commits fewer double faults—but Michelsen saves more break points and owns the superior overall efficiency numbers. With Michelsen already dispatching Brandon Nakashima and Daniel Merida Aguilar in straight sets at this tournament, I am laying the modest 4.5-game spread rather than treating the official ranking as the decisive factor.

  • Pick: Under 38.5 Games (+100 at Polymarket)

The total-games market offers a better complement to the spread than Michelsen’s expensive moneyline. Michelsen has won all three of his US Open matches in straight sets, while Etcheverry has dropped just one set; that combination points to a competitive opening but not necessarily a long match. Michelsen’s 113-point hard-court Elo advantage and superior return numbers give him a strong chance to end sets before they reach tiebreak territory.

Etcheverry’s 82.7% service-game win rate and 73.0% first-serve points won keep the Over live, but Michelsen’s 22.6% return-games-won rate should create enough scoreboard separation to produce a relatively efficient result. At plus money, Under 38.5 is the preferred secondary play, with Michelsen’s straight-set path the key to the wager.
