MotoGP, Miller: “We looked like idiots today, but it was worth a try.” | GPOne

TheTools · 31.10.2026 11:26 · source · permalink

After the disappointment of missing the final race at Phillip Island, another setback came for Jack Miller. During the Sprint race at Sepang, the first drops of rain falling on the track misled the Australian rider who, betting on a change in the weather, returned to the pits earlier than planned. The decision was sound in principle, but it caught the pit crew off guard because the rain bike wasn't ready yet. As a result, Miller’s race ended after just two laps, leaving him deeply disappointed.

“I came in too early. I was in eighteenth place,” Miller explained “So, out of the points. Here, in Malaysia, turns seven to eight and nine to ten are usually the first spots where the rain hits. I felt a few drops on both of them and thought the next lap would be wet, so I pulled into the pits.”

Once in the pits, the horrible surprise.
“Unfortunately, the bike wasn’t ready yet. I spoke with Giacomo Guidotti before the race and asked him to have the other bike ready. In my mind, that meant right away. In his, it meant after four, five laps, so there was a miscommunication. Unfortunately, today we looked like idiots.”

The bike seemed ready.
“I thought so too, but then I saw the bike come back in. The tires hadn’t been changed yet, and I realized it was game over. I apologize for my reaction [ed. towards the team], but, at that moment, when you pull into the pit, you’re still in the race. In my mind, I wouldn’t have made that decision if I hadn’t thought it was valid. I thought it might be a chance to win at that point, given the timing and the situation. Unfortunately, the rain came later, but, like I said, we were in seventeenth place. It was worth a try.”

After all, the weather forecast had called for rain.
“Honestly, I had checked the radar, which said the rain would come after the race. But when I saw the raindrops—and they were big, not small—I thought it was coming sooner,” the Australian concluded.
