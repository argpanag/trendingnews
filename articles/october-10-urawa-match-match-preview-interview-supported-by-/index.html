INTERVIEW October 9, 2026

MATCH PREVIEW & INTERVIEW: October 10 vs. Urawa
supported by PLAUD

  • #Rikizo MATSUHASHI
  • #Tokiwa Kouta
  • #Hirokazu ISHIHARA

The match unfolded as a battle over how Tokyo could overcome Nagoya's man-to-man defense.

Tokyo set the pace early on with an aggressive style. From the moment they won the ball back, multiple players combined in one-touch moves to launch attacks, while Alexander SCHOLZ carried the ball forward from the back line at the right moment and supplied through balls, demonstrating a potent, multi-layered attack.

However, Nagoya subsequently showed their strength in the challenges against Tokyo's possession, and there was a prolonged spell in which Tokyo were unable to develop effective passing combinations in the final third. Despite controlling much of the first half, they also had moments when they allowed clear chances, and the sides went into the break level at 0-0.

In the second half, with the team struggling to break down their opponents as a unit, the No. 33 produced a brilliant individual moment that brought the stadium to its feet. In the 57th minute, he drove through the middle on a long dribble from near his own penalty area, drifted to the right while drawing opposition players with him, and, spotting the instant a diagonal route opened up, fired a right-footed shot into the corner—goal! His first strike since last October gave Tokyo the opening goal.

In the 68th minute, Takahiro KO received his second yellow card and was sent off, but even with a numerical disadvantage, Tokyo did not simply sit back and defend. They pressed aggressively, moved intelligently off the ball to link up their passes, and took control of the game.

They continued to show their fighting spirit throughout the five minutes of second-half stoppage time, seeing out the match through sheer determination and concentration. Demonstrating their ability to close out a win, they recorded their third straight league victory with a clean sheet.

After the international break, the 2026/27 Meiji Yasuda J1 League resumes for the first time in about three weeks. During this period, the team won the 3rd Round in two cup competitions. Including the league matches before the break, they have gone unbeaten in 10 official matches, winning five straight while keeping five consecutive clean sheets—an impressive record.

The first of an eight-match stretch in a packed schedule began with Wednesday's 3rd Round Emperor's Cup JFA 106th Japan Football Championship match against Shonan Bellmare. Dani GOMEZ, making his first start since joining the team, scored a goal, while Kaede SUZUKI made her first competitive start, as fresh faces contributed to the victory. Also not to be overlooked in terms of giving the team momentum was Teruhito NAKAGAWA finding the net for the first time in approximately one year and two months. Despite the scoreline already being well in Tokyo's favor, NAKAGAWA's strike was greeted with great joy by the players, staff, and fans and supporters alike. He has continued to run for the team and play selflessly in pursuit of victory. It was another moment that strengthened the unity of the blue-and-red family.

This week's match against Urawa Reds comes just two days after the Shonan match, so the team will need to recover quickly and absorb the tactical plan in a short period of time. However, there is plenty of confidence that they can compete based on the style they have built up so far. While the focus has been on their run of consecutive wins without conceding, centre-back Hayato Inamura has vowed to keep producing results while continuing the football Rikizo Tokyo has built: “We have been able to embody the idea that attack is the best form of defence, and I want to keep that going. Of course, personally, I am committed to keeping clean sheets, but it is not good to become overly sensitive about conceding, so I want us to focus as a team on how many goals we can score.”

If they cannot score, they keep shooting until they do. If they cannot break through as a unit, they break through with individual quality. If possession play proves difficult, they keep it simple and put the opponent under pressure. They pin opponents back in their own half and keep attacking by immediately winning the ball back whenever they lose it. The style the coach has aimed to build has taken root within the team, and they are increasingly gaining the ability to produce results while adapting to how opponents approach the game. It is proof that they are looking inward while striving for greater heights.

Urawa Reds, meanwhile, appointed Coach CHO Kwi Jae this season. They began with three straight losses, but have since regained form by adding possession play to their hard work from the front and quick vertical attacks—just as Coach Rikizo MATSUHASHI described Urawa's football as “ever-changing.” With powerful attackers out wide, they have successfully turned things around by alternating between a back four and a back three, recording seven wins and one loss in official matches since that three-game losing streak. In particular, Tatsuki SEKO, signed midway through the season, has become the starting point of their incisive attacks as a defensive midfielder, and the battle in midfield looks set to be one of the keys to deciding this week's match.

Kota TOKIWA prefaced his comments by saying, "While bringing the intensity that is one of my strengths," before adding, "Rather than taking it into a physical battle for the ball, I want to get to the second ball one step earlier with quick anticipation and reactions so that it does not become an even contest. From there, I also want to control whether we settle the game down or play fast football." He is looking to gain the upper hand in the mental battle that comes before the physical one and use it to take control of the match.

Without dwelling too much on the fact that this is a run of consecutive matches, each player wants to give everything in the game in front of them and secure victory. By adapting to circumstances, improving their finishing, and advancing one win at a time, they will forge their own future. And in front of what is sure to be a packed crowd at Ajinomoto Stadium, they want to deliver a performance that demonstrates the blue-and-red side's determination to aim even higher. 

Q: With only two days between matches, what have you done to prepare the team for this congested schedule?
A: Nothing different from usual. The preparation period is short, but we have still gone over everything as we normally do.

Q: How does the team view the upcoming run of consecutive matches?
A: As I mentioned briefly at the press conference, it is important not to simply think that we want to win the Emperor's Cup or win in order to advance, but to see it as something we truly need. This is not just about an eight-match stretch; it is a slight difference in mindset when it comes to how we achieve our goals. It is not about wanting something, but whether we act because we believe we need it. To attain it, I want the players to shift their mindset while considering various factors—the team's current situation, whether they start, come off the bench, or begin as game changers. The players expressed that well on the pitch, and I want us to keep it going.

Q: Did the match against Shonan Bellmare two days ago, including the performances of the new players, feel like a game that showed the team’s overall strength had improved?
A: I’m very happy, and above all, everyone delivered results and looked happy. I think it’s wonderful to see that sparkle in their eyes. I believe this applies to anything, but in the end, the sparkle in the eyes of those around you—or in your own eyes—may be what success truly means. In that sense, I wouldn’t call it success just yet, but I think the fact that they are approaching their work with such enthusiasm will undoubtedly have a positive effect.

Q: The goal by Teruhito NAKAGAWA that you also mentioned at the press conference may have given both him and the fans and supporters an opportunity to move forward, wouldn't you say?
A: I think that is because it reflects his contributions to date—not just over the past year, but since joining FC Tokyo, as well as his contributions to the J.League as a whole. He is someone who has continued to work away quietly without showing those feelings on his face, so I was truly happy for him.

Q: With such a short preparation period, how do you feel about turning the focus inward and putting into practice what the team has built up so far?
A: Of course, there are things we can do and things we cannot. I also think that if we delve too deeply into things, our attention gets spread across too many areas and it becomes difficult to concentrate on any one thing. That is why, in terms of where we place our emphasis, we are able to manage every aspect—attack, defense, and set pieces—in today’s session as well as in our daily training, while taking into account the players’ condition and the team’s current situation. So I do feel that we have steadily built up those aspects as well.

Q: What is your impression of Urawa Reds, who have bounced back after three consecutive defeats?
A: They are highly adaptable, with a strong foundation. The coach's style is clearly reflected in the team, and in that sense, I think they are an excellent side.

Q: Against an opponent that deploys speedy players, what are the specific keys for FC Tokyo to bring out its strengths?
A: Of course, while we need to understand our opponents well and match their rhythm when they have the momentum, I think it is important not to play into their hands when we have the momentum. First of all, we want to break down their strengths. They are very aggressive and decisive, so making them hesitate or catching them off guard will be one key to disrupting their coordination. Also, when it comes to quick counters after winning the ball, if we start using the large spaces they create, they will begin to hesitate as well. I think it is important to keep creating those small moments of hesitation and mismatches.

Q: As in the previous match, could taking an early lead, drawing the opposition in and then looking for a second goal be one of the key points?
A: It is about game management, I suppose. I think it is one of our strengths, and perhaps something that can be said of the J.League as a whole. Of course, every team is trying different things, but when you are leading, it is important not to give the ball away cheaply to the opposition. If it is still early in the first half, naturally it is also important to keep attacking and look for a second goal. We need to do both: show those qualities ourselves and draw them out from the opposition, while reading the flow of the game properly. At the moment, that means moving the ball to draw the opposition out and then making good use of the space created; or, when the opposition have the ball, drawing them in, setting up a solid block and launching quick counterattacks. Designing those kinds of situations is efficient, and it can also make the opposition second-guess themselves. I want us to make it even more precise.

Kota Tokiwa

Q: We have had tournament-format matches, but the league campaign resumes from here. What is your mindset going into it?
A: Coach Rikizo MATSUHASHI always tells us, “The opponent in front of us is the strongest opponent.” Even when the run of eight consecutive matches was about to begin the other day, he said that rather than focusing on the eight-match stretch, we need to take it one match at a time. I think it is important to fight properly in each and every match.

Q: What is your impression of Urawa Reds, who have bounced back after losing their first three matches of the season?
A: They have many players with individual quality, and I think the new manager's ideas are starting to fit the team well. In addition, they have strengthened the squad with Japanese players who had been playing overseas, so I think it is having a positive effect across the board. The manager's style is more pronounced than it was with Urawa in the past, and they play direct, fast football while also being solid defensively. I feel they are really coming together gradually.

Q: I think the battle in midfield will be a major key to the match. Could you tell us about your role in your position and your determination going into it?
A: Physical intensity is one of my strengths, so while bringing that intensity, rather than taking every contest all the way to a challenge for the ball, I can compete for and win second balls or get there one step before things become an even contest with the opponent. In those situations, I also want to control whether we settle the game down effectively or play at a faster tempo.

Q: What is important in winning the ball before you end up in an even contest with the opponent?
A: I am a player who relies on anticipation, so there is also an instinctive element to it. I can instantly predict where the ball will drop based on its pace and the opponent's body position, so that is where I want to make my move.

Q: How would you like to respond and play against the possibility of the opposition switching between a back three and a back four?
A: Whether they play with three or four at the back, what we need to do ultimately does not change. We want to control possession, and defensively, our top priority is to protect the goal. Closing off the middle and forcing them wide, as well as applying a high press, will not change. We do not know who will step out where, but I think we will be fine as long as everyone fully understands the fundamentals. If I had to name one thing, though, I feel that showing our build-up play against teams with a back three is an area where we can still improve, so I want us to execute that well.

Hirokazu ISHIHARA

Q: It has been some time since you joined Tokyo. How do you feel about the team’s current level of cohesion and your own role within the squad?
A: I feel that, as a team, we are becoming highly polished. We have consistently scored the opening goal, and we have conceded very few goals as well. We were overturned in the opening match against FC Machida Zelvia, but in many of the other matches we have taken the initiative, scored first, and gone on to win. Of course, there have been some matches we should have handled better, but I think the fact that we have been able to show what we want to do in so many matches is something each of us should take confidence from.

Q: Having played in a variety of situations—as a starter, off the bench, and at both right and left back—how do you assess your own progress, and what are the challenges ahead?
A: I think full-backs in this team are expected to contribute a great deal in attack. I have not produced any numbers yet, and I do not think I have contributed enough offensively, so that is an area where I need to do much more. That said, I feel I have been able to adapt well whenever I have been used, regardless of the situation or position. Going forward, I want to increase my contribution in terms of numbers.

Q. How do you feel about the competition for positions and your relationships with high-level rivals such as Sei MUROYA and Yuto NAGATOMO?
A. I think we have each built very good relationships. Since the start of the season, each of us has produced results and earned a place in the next match. Even with many injured players at full-back right now, I feel a responsibility to play. I can never cut corners, and I know that if I do not fully show my strengths, someone could take my position when the injured players return. We each have different strengths, but I believe the fact that we are all bringing them out properly has contributed to the team's good form. Rather than creating any tension, we have all built very positive and strong relationships.

Q: October brings a busy schedule of consecutive matches, and your next game is a J1 League match against Urawa Reds. What will you be focusing on, and how are you approaching the game?
A: I think I will naturally be conscious of it even if I do not try to be. I was there for three and a half seasons, and honestly, I had not really considered leaving. Having so many players I know on the opposing side when playing for a new team may make it difficult in some ways, but they are an opponent we absolutely cannot lose to, so it fires me up. I know the characteristics of the players I will be matched up against, and I think they also know my strengths and weaknesses, so I expect they will come at me hard. I want to prepare so that I can show what I am capable of and make sure I do not lose mentally.

Q: With a close match or a high-scoring battle expected, what do you see as the key points to securing a win?
A: Urawa have been involved in a lot of high-scoring games this season, and I think they have both scored and conceded a lot of goals. Tokyo have been able to score first so far, so I think that opening goal is really important. But even if we score, they have the ability to respond, so it is important to put our bodies on the line in the decisive moments and, above all, not concede. In attack, on the other hand, I think gaps will open up because they press from the front, so it is important not to miss even one of those openings. I think we just need to do what we have been doing up to now.

Q: Please share a message for the fans and supporters ahead of this match, in which the team will be aiming for its fourth straight league win.
A: For me personally, it is a match against my former club, but it is just one match in the league season. Rather than thinking that we absolutely have to win this particular match, I want to approach it simply with the determination to win this one game. I believe that steadily winning one match at a time will lead to another winning streak, so I want to focus on winning the match right in front of us. We will focus on doing what we need to do as Tokyo and winning. I want us all to fight together—the players, staff, fans, and supporters—so that we can win together.
