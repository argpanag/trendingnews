Day 1 of Diamond League Final sees multiple records fall - Canadian Running Magazine

TheTools · 04.09.2026 20:36 · source · permalink

Meeting records, Diamond League records, national records and world leads made for an electric first day in Brussels

Day 1 of the Diamond League Final in Brussels got off to a fast start, and featured a series of tactical races and meeting records as the world’s best athletes battled for titles. Nine Diamond League champions were crowned by the end of the day’s track events, and while there were no world records, the racing did not disappoint.

Meeting records

The women’s 3,000 metres isn’t always the most highly anticipated event of a meeting, but the field in Brussels delivered one of the most exciting races of the season, with all three podium finishers dipping under the previous meeting record. Reigning world champion Faith Cherotich of Kenya and reigning Olympic champion Winfred Yavi of Bahrain, have battled throughout the season, and it was Cherotich who came out on top in the final, winning in a meeting-record 8:51.61. Yavi fell to third in 8:53.54 after Kenya’s Doris Lemngole closed quickly to claim the second spot in the final metres, clocking 8:52.86.

The second record came from Kenya’s Emmanuel Wanyonyi in the men’s 800m. He went out fast and led for most of the race, finishing in a quick 1:41.80. Canadian star Marco Arop was in second for much of the race, but was unable to close the gap over the final 100 metres, ultimately finishing fourth, in 1:43.50.

The final performance of the day brought multiple records. In the men’s 400m, Busang Collen Kebinatshipi of Botswana dominated, setting a new Diamond League record, meeting record and national record in 43.40, capping off a historic, undefeated season. Jereem Richards of Trinidad and Tobago also set a national record for second, in 43.68. The top four runners all dipped under 44 seconds and set PBs.

Tactical races

The women’s 5,000m race had tactics written all over it, with the field moving slowly until the final lap, averaging approximately 70 seconds per lap for much of the race. After sitting in the middle of the pack until 500 metres remained, two-time world indoor champion Frenweyni Hailu of Ethiopia made her move to the front and held on to win, clocking 14:35.20. Two more Ethiopians followed her across the line to complete an Ethiopian sweep. Likina Amebaw finished second, in 14:36.31, while Senayet Getachew was third, in 14:37.09.

Equally tactical, but much faster, was the men’s 1,500m final, and many of the pre-race headliners would leave disappointed. Norway’s Jakob Ingebrigtsen led for much of the race and looked strong before a group of young challengers made their move over the closing 100 metres. Australia’s national record holder, Cameron Myers, 20, took the win in 3:30.14, narrowly edging out Kenyan Phanuel Kipkosgei Koech, 19, who was second in 3:30.18. The oldest podium finisher, the U.S.’s Ethan Strand, 23, outpaced Ingebrigtsen to take third in 3:30.50, while Ingebrigtsen would drop to fourth, in 3:30.52.

Quick and dominant

As she has done throughout the season, St. Lucia’s Julien Alfred dominated the women’s 200 metres. Even after easing up through the finish, Alfred won in a quick 21.79 and was the only athlete in the field to break 22 seconds. Quebec’s Audrey Leduc also lined up in the final, finishing sixth, in 22.95. With the result, Leduc punched her ticket to the World Athletics Ultimate Championships in Budapest on Sept. 11. Whether she accepts the invitation remains to be seen.

The men’s 100m saw four athletes break 10 seconds, but reigning world champion Oblique Seville of Jamaica continued his streak of strong performances, claiming the Diamond League title in a comfortable 9.90.

As expected, in the men’s 110m hurdles, an American trio swept the podium, though the race was slower than many would have predicted, given that no athlete in the field ran under 13 seconds. World-record holder Ja’Kobe Tharp stormed back after a slow start to claim his first Diamond League Final win, in 13.03; Jamal Britt was second, in 13.09, and Trey Cunningham finished third, in 13.22
