Dow drops 1,800 points; Nasdaq tumbles into correction territory on AI tech wreck: Live updates

TheTools · 04.11.2026 23:06 · source · permalink

Stocks plummeted on Thursday, erasing the sharp gains from the rally seen in the wake of the U.S. midterm elections, as investors accelerated their move away from technology stocks on fears of a data-center buildout slowdown.

The Nasdaq Composite declined 5.52% to close at 24,315.00. The tech-heavy index slid into correction territory, falling more than 10% below a record set last month. The S&P 500 dropped 4.36% to end at 7,353.73. The Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 1,833.06 points, or 3.44%, and settled at 51,523.06.

The Dow more than wiped out its advance from Wednesday's massive postelection rally. All three major averages posted their worst day since April 2025.

The small-cap Russell 2000 index decreased 4.45% and also entered a correction.

Stocks related to the artificial intelligence trade, particularly those linked to the chips industry and data centers, slid for a second day. Investors fear that a Democratic sweep of both chambers of Congress as well as victories in key gubernatorial races could decelerate the data center buildout.

For instance, Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont — who caucuses with Democrats — has called for a national data center moratorium. In a post on X Wednesday evening, Sanders wrote that "Americans don't want Big Tech oligarchs wrecking the environment, gutting jobs and threatening humanity's future."

Chipmakers Broadcom and Nvidia respectively fell 10% and 9% Thursday, while software stock Oracle moved lower by almost 7%. Another chip stock, Qualcomm, reported earnings that beat estimates, but the stock dropped 9% on a weaker-than-expected earnings outlook. The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) was down more than 12% for its worst day since the Covid-19 breakout of March 2020.

Shares of neocloud companies also retreated, with CoreWeave losing almost 10%. Nebius and Iren slid about 15% and almost 17%, amid worries about a backlash to the industry in light of the election results in various states.

Investors are "stepping away until they get a better sense of the implications of the midterms around the AI capex cycle in particular, which is the biggest economic tailwind we've had going for us for the last, call it, three years," said Tim Holland, chief investment officer at Orion. "It's about tone and tenor from the party that did really well, incredibly well, at the federal level and at the state level."

Thursday's selling pressure was so intense that it spread to other parts of the market. Shares of industrial giant Caterpillar declined 6%, while financial stock JPMorgan fell 4%. Aerospace name Boeing and athletic apparel giant Nike pulled back 2%.

"Unlike yesterday, you're just seeing that the sell-off in tech, the size of it, is now feeding itself into the broader market," said Keith Lerner, chief investment officer at Truist Wealth. "The rubber band is becoming increasingly stretched."

On Wednesday, the Dow jumped more than 1,500 points to notch its best day since April 2025 after a decisive midterm win for Democrats. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite also popped more than 1% each. Areas related to AI were under pressure, however, while economically-sensitive stocks rose.

U.S. equities sold off significantly during Thursday's session.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 1,833.06 points, or 3.44%, to settle at 51,523.06. The S&P 500 shed 4.36% to 7,353.73, while the Nasdaq Composite lost 5.52% to 24,315.00.

Loop Capital downgraded Dollar Tree to Hold from Buy on Thursday, maintaining its $140 price target after shares rallied more than 20% since early October. 

“We believe our bullish fundamental outlook on Dollar Tree is largely priced in at current levels,” Loop Capital’s analyst Anthony Chukumba said. The downgrade reflects valuation concerns rather than weakening fundamentals. 

Chukumba said Ollie's Bargain Outlet ("Ollie's") provides a more compelling long opportunity for investors seeking exposure to the deep discount retailing space heading into the fast-approaching holiday selling season.

The analyst said high gas prices and tighter household budgets will push more shoppers toward Dollar Tree ahead of the holidays. The retailer’s growing range of products at different prices also continues to lift sales at existing stores.

“All that said,” Chukumba said, “with Dollar Tree stock trading above its historical average next fiscal year P/E multiple, we believe upside potential and downside risk are fairly balanced at current levels.”

As bad as Thursday's stock market washout is, conditions have to get a lot worse before the New York Stock Exchange circuit breakers would kick in and pause trading. At the Thursday low, the S&P bottomed out with a loss of 4.23% before recovering somewhat.

Level 1 and Level 2 Market-Wide Circuit Breakers that halt trading for a minimum of 15 minutes only come into effect between 9:30 am ET and 3:25 pm ET if the S&P 500 drops by 7% or more (Level 1) and 13% (Level 2).

A Level 3 decline of 20% or more can come into effect at any time and halts trading for the rest of the day.

When were the circuit breakers last used? Four times in March 2020, twice before the major Covid lockdowns began to be put in to place on March 15, and twice after. The only other time that the circuit breakers, first introduced in the aftermath of the 1987 stock market crash, were used was once in October, 1997 during the Asian financial crisis.

Jobless claims slipped further last week, hovering around levels not seen since the late 1960s, the Labor Department reported Thursday.

Initial filings for unemployment claims totaled a seasonally adjusted 189,000 for the week ended Oct. 31, down 6,000 from the prior period and below the Dow Jones consensus estimate for 197,000. The total tied for the lowest since July, which was the bottom going back to September 1969.

Continuing claims, which run a week behind, rose 23,000 to 1.72 million.

Productivity posted a stronger-than-expected jump in the third quarter while the portion of output that showed up in worker pay hit a record low, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday.

Nonfarm business sector productivity surged 3.6% for the period, up from 2.1% in the second quarter and well ahead of the Dow Jones consensus estimate for 2.9%. Compensation less productivity, a metric known as unit labor costs, contracted 0.7%.

At the the same, the percentage of output that goes to workers in the form of compensation, known as the labor share, slipped further to 53%, the lowest in a data series that dates back to 1947.

The defense company now expects sales to rise to around 13.2 billion euros ($14.9 billion) in 2026, down from a prior outlook range of between 13.7 billion and 14.2 billion euros. It is expected to have an operating margin of around 18.5%, down from the previously expected 19%.

Rheinmetall said it's revising its 2026 guidance due to delays in the German military's Arminius Boxer project, now expected to be commissioned next year, and setbacks in artillery ammunition acceptance testing.
