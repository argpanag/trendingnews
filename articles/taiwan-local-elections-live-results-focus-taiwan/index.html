Taiwan local elections live results - Focus Taiwan

TheTools · 28.11.2026 08:08 · source · permalink

5:37 p.m.

Two other high-profile races that are expected to be competitive are New Taipei and Taichung.

In New Taipei, which has 3,434,625 eligible voters, KMT candidate Lee Shu-chuan (李四川) currently leads the DPP’s Su Chiao-hui (蘇巧慧) 404,251 to 382,661, per TVBS. SET News has Chiao with a slight lead at 549,990 to Lee’s 548,506.

In Taichung, which has 2,376,502 eligible voters, the DPP’s Ho Hsin-chun (何欣純) is leading the KMT’s Johnny Chiang (江啟臣) 327,902 to 308,246, according to TVBS, while SET News has Ho at 379,890 to Chiang’s 362,220.

5:24 p.m.

The most closely-watched race this election cycle has been Taipei, where Mayor Chiang Wan-an (蔣萬安) of the KMT is seeking reelection against Legislator Puma Shen (沈伯洋) of the DPP.

Taipei has 2,031,431 eligible voters, and if turnout is around 60-65%, that will yield a total of 1,218,000 to 1,320,000 votes.

Currently, TVBS has posted counts of about 563,000 votes, with the KMT's Chiang holding a 44,000 vote lead. SET News has posted counts for about 711,000 million votes, with Chiang leading by about 36,000 votes.

5:19 p.m.

Lienchiang County Magistrate Wang Chung-ming (王忠銘) of the KMT declared victory in his reelection bid at around 5 p.m. Saturday, saying he had defeated independent candidate Tsao Erh-yuan (曹爾元) in a rematch of their 2022 race.

5:09 p.m.

KMT Legislator Chen Yu-jen (陳玉珍) has declared victory in the race for Kinmen County magistrate.

4:57 p.m.

In the 2022 local elections, the Kuomintang won 14 of the 22 mayoral and magistrate races, while the Democratic Progressive Party secured five, the Taiwan People's Party won one, and independent candidates took the remaining two.

4:35 p.m

Taiwan on Saturday also voted in a referendum to abolish the government’s “nuclear-free homeland” energy policy.

The referendum, designated as National Referendum No. 22, asked voters: "Do you support the government using nuclear power to boost the economy and thereby abolishing the 'nuclear-free homeland' energy policy?"

The vote was Taiwan’s fourth national referendum on nuclear power since 2018, and comes after the country completed its nuclear power phase-out in May 2025.

Under the Referendum Act, a proposal passes only if valid votes in favor outnumber votes against the referendum, and account for at least one-quarter of all eligible voters. With roughly 20 million people eligible to vote, the proposal needed at least 5 million affirmative votes to pass.

4:26 p.m.

More than 11,000 local government positions are being contested across 8,906 electoral districts, with 18,244 polling stations set up and more than 300,000 election workers mobilized, according to the Central Election Commission (CEC).

Around 19.58 million people in Taiwan, or about 85 percent of the population, are eligible to vote in the local elections, while about 20 million are eligible to vote in the referendum, which has a lower voting age – 18, compared to 20 for local elections.

4:16 p.m.

Polls in Taiwan closed at 4 p.m. and TV stations are already posting counts of hundreds of thousands of votes. Votes are counted polling station by polling station and then relayed to political parties and TV stations, but it is hard to tell how accurate the numbers shown on TV are. 

4:10 p.m.

Taiwan is holding its nationwide elections for local government offices, such as city and county leaders. Polls closed at 4 p.m. and the complete results are expected before midnight.

(By CNA staff)
