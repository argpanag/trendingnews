Coast Guard Auxiliary, marina owner urge safe boating on Arkansas waterways

by Grace Harvey

LITTLE ROCK, Ark. (KATV) — As Labor Day weekend brings more people to Arkansas lakes and rivers, safety officials are urging boaters to plan ahead and stay alert before heading out on the water.

Blake Sasse with the U.S. Coast Guard Auxiliary said heat, alcohol and long days on the water can create dangerous conditions.

“People have been out on the water for a while in the heat. Some people have been drinking by that point, and that's when we see most of our accidents,” Sasse said.

Officials recommend boaters check the weather, carry enough life jackets for everyone on board and designate a sober operator before leaving the dock.

Jim Jackson of Rockwater Marina said boaters should treat operating a vessel with the same seriousness as driving a vehicle.

“Everybody needs to remember that a boat is not a floating bar,” Jackson said. “You must have a designated driver and somebody who is sober.”

Sasse warned that boating under the influence can bring serious consequences.

“You can lose your license and worse,” he said. “So it’s definitely something to avoid. And you should have a designated boat operator when you’re out on the water that isn’t drinking at all.”

Holiday weekends also bring heavier traffic to the Arkansas River, where boaters share the water with kayakers, canoeists, paddleboarders and people fishing from shore.

Jackson encouraged boaters to slow down and watch for others on the water.

“There’ll be a lot of not only boaters, but there’s also a lot of kayakers and canoeists,” Jackson said. “People need to be extremely careful. Go slower than normal this weekend.”

He also said experienced boaters should be patient with newcomers who may not understand navigation rules or right-of-way guidelines.

“We all need to remember that there’s going to be new people out on the water that don’t necessarily know the rules of navigation,” Jackson said. “We need to be patient. We need to be looking out for those people.”

Officials said preparation, patience and sober driving can help make sure everyone returns home safely after a day on the water.
