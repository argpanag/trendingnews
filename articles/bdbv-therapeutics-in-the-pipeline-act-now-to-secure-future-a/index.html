BDBV therapeutics in the pipeline: Act now to secure future access to successful tools for those who need it most urgently

TheTools · 03.10.2026 06:14 · source · permalink

23 September 2026, Geneva

Background: MSF published today a technical brief titled “Therapeutics for the 2026 Bundibugyo Ebola disease outbreak: access gaps and recommendations” analysing access challenges concerning three Bundibugyo Ebola Virus (BDBV) therapeutic candidates - MBP134, remdesivir, and obeldesivir - that are currently being evaluated through the PARTNERS and EBO-PEP clinical trials in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The three candidates are being developed by Mapp Biopharmaceutical (MBP134) and Gilead Sciences (remdesivir and obeldesivir), with public and philanthropic funding contributing to their development and/or clinical evaluations. The MSF brief highlights that the publicly available information on pricing, licensing, supply, and post-trial access remains very limited despite repeated calls for transparency. Past Ebola experience suggests clinical success does not by itself guarantee equitable access, and may lead again to delays, limited stockpiles, and dependence on ad hoc donations restricting treatment availability.  

The brief is meant to inform pharmaceutical corporations engaged in drug development, policymakers, funders, UN bodies, and civil society about the status of BDBV therapeutic options, key access concerns, and recommendations to ensure equitable access should these candidates prove effective  

Dr Monica Rull, Deputy Executive Director, MSF Access  

“As clinical trials for promising Bundibugyo Ebola Virus therapeutics move forward in the DRC, there is a window of opportunity to ensure that any effective tools are accessible to the people who need them most. Experience from past Ebola outbreaks has shown that scientific progress alone is not enough. Greater transparency and clear planning for financing, stockpiling, and the inclusion of children in development and access strategies are essential to prevent the same delays and inequities from being repeated."  

“If access is considered only after efficacy is proven, future BDBV treatments access may once again be determined by non-transparent commercial decisions by pharmaceutical corporations and reactive last-minute measures. Funders, researchers and policymakers engaged in therapeutics' clinical trials must act now to include the right access conditions in a transparent way.”
