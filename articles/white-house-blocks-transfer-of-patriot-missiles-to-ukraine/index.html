White House blocks transfer of Patriot missiles to Ukraine

TheTools · 20.10.2026 09:54 · source · permalink

Trump administration’s failure to authorise move comes as US president escalates clash with Zelensky

The White House has blocked the transfer of Patriot missiles to Ukraine, US officials have said.

The transfer of interceptor missiles from countries friendly to Ukraine requires the approval of Pete Hegseth, the US defence secretary.

While Mr Hegseth has not formally rejected the deals involving countries other than the US – as the manufacturer, it retains control over the weapons – he has not completed the steps required to approve them, sources told The Washington Post.

A Ukrainian official told the outlet last week that Kyiv had “only a couple” of missiles – vital to intercepting Russian missiles – left in its stockpile.

The Trump administration’s failure to authorise sending the missiles to Kyiv, while it ratified other assistance to the country, came as Donald Trump continued to attack Volodymyr Zelensky and pivoted further in support of Russia.

The US president has cited Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries as the reason for ballooning fuel costs, despite them being widely attributed to his war on Iran.

He has also claimed his Ukrainian counterpart is avoiding ending the war to stay in power, and called for him to be replaced.

On Monday, Mr Trump told Mr Zelensky to “remember” their Oval Office argument at the beginning of his second term, in which he and JD Vance, the vice-president, attacked the Ukrainian leader and ejected him from the White House.

“Zelensky is a man that should’ve had this war settled a long time ago. If he would have listened to me and he remembered the Oval Office, he would have had this settled a long time ago,” Mr Trump told reporters before a visit to Kansas.

“I think it’s time for somebody else to negotiate on behalf of Ukraine.”

He also told reporters: “Russia, frankly, has been fine on Ukraine. They haven’t been doing much.”

Mr Zelensky has criticised the US for failing to provide adequate intelligence, claiming the US gave Kyiv just 60 seconds’ warning before a Russian ballistic missile attack struck the capital on Monday morning.

“Once again, there was a Russian ballistic missile attack in several waves overnight, and once again, there was no warning from our partners,” Mr Zelensky wrote on Telegram, adding that Russia had launched 16 ballistic missiles.

“We have welcomed literally EVERY attempt by our partners to end this war with dignity.

“It is very important to call things by their proper names. We must call the aggressor an aggressor and help Ukraine protect lives.

“When deliveries of missiles for Ukraine’s air defence are blocked, while Russia receives sanctions relief or delays, this is, unfortunately, a denial of reality on the part of our partners.”

Mr Trump has indicated he is ready to meet Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China next month in a summit that could include discussions on ending the Ukraine war.

In an effort to capitalise on Mr Trump’s warm tone towards Russia, the Kremlin suggested the unprecedented meeting, which would exclude Mr Zelensky.

Meanwhile, Steve Witkoff, the US envoy, is to travel to Oslo for peace talks on Tuesday with Mr Zelensky and European leaders, a US official told Axios. Jared Kushner, Mr Trump’s son-in-law, will dial in remotely because he has the flu, according to Axios.

Jonas Gahr Støre, the Norwegian prime minister, as well as E4, EU and Nato representatives, will discuss preparing a peace proposal to present to Russia, before Mr Witkoff heads to Moscow to meet Putin.

Discussions are said to focus on the previous round of talks in Miami and an attempt to put together a Ukraine-European proposal for Mr Witkoff to take to Moscow.

Mr Zelensky said on Tuesday morning that he had spoken to Marco Rubio, the US secretary of state, about “the issues that matter”.

“First, ballistic missile interceptors, which are needed to protect lives. Second, early warning of Russian ballistic missile attacks, which is vital. Third, the situation with Starlink/Starshield, which needs to be restored,” he wrote.

“Of course, all of this comes against the backdrop of our diplomatic efforts to end this horrific war and the constant killing of people.”

The heightened tensions come after Mr Trump agreed to a deal to buy 300,000 tons of Russian diesel, despite sanctions he signed into law on buying fuel from the region.

Mr Zelensky criticised the US president, calling the deal an “investment” in the war.

Ukraine’s Starlink and Starshield internet access was then heavily reduced after Elon Musk approached Mr Trump with the idea. Mr Zelensky said that “people will die” if access to the tech continues to be restricted during Russian strikes.

Russian strikes on Ukraine on Monday killed a one-year-old in a pram alongside two other toddlers and three adults.

The toddler was killed when a drone struck a park in Nova Vodolaha, Kharkiv, at a time when many families had gathered there, Ukrainian officials said.

“Unfortunately, two women and an infant who was in a stroller were killed,” Ivan Vyhivskyi, the Ukrainian minister of internal affairs, wrote on Telegram. At least 14 people including seven children were injured, he said.

According to the United Nations, civilians were killed in Kyiv on 22 out of the 30 days of September, the highest frequency recorded in any month since the start of Russia’s invasion.
