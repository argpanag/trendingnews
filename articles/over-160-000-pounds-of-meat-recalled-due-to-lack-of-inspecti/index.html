Over 160,000 Pounds of Meat Recalled Due to Lack of Inspection, Receives Highest Risk Level From USDA

TheTools · 23.09.2026 17:53 · source · permalink

Aryelle Siclait

Health Reporter/Editor

Star Meat Delivery Inc. of Lucama, North Carolina, is recalling approximately 167,639 pounds of raw pork, beef and goat products, according to a U.S. Department of Agriculture announcement posted Tuesday.

The items were produced without the benefit of federal inspection, meaning they weren’t confirmed to be “safe, wholesome and properly labeled” by the USDA’s Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS).

The recalled meat is also labeled with a false USDA mark of inspection, the announcement added.

This recall has been classified as class I, the highest health risk. This classification refers to “a situation in which there is a reasonable probability that the use of or exposure to a violative product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death,” according to the U.S. Food & Drug Administration.

Which Products Are Being Recalled?

Any meat product produced by Star Meat Delivery Inc. with an inspection and establishment number that reads “EST. 1363” is falsely labeled, according to the announcement. Consider it misbranded and unsafe to eat, the FSIS warned.

The vacuum-sealed raw pork, beef and goat products were produced on various dates before Sept. 22, 2026.

They were packed into cardboard boxes ahead of shipping, and once they arrived at their destinations, may have been relabeled with the respective retailer’s sticker and handling instructions, the announcement said.

Where Was the Recalled Meat Sold?

The recalled raw pork, beef and goat products were shipped to A&D Foods, a distributor in Duluth, Georgia, to then be shipped again to individual retailers and restaurants across the country.

Why Is the Meat Being Recalled?

“Food produced without inspection may contain undeclared allergens, harmful bacteria, or other contaminants that put consumer health and safety at risk,” the recall announcement explained.

The FSIS website adds that, under the Federal Meat Inspection Act, all meat sold commercially that is intended for human consumption must be inspected wholly or in part. This applies to livestock including cattle, sheep, swine and goats.

The FSIS discovered this recalled meat had been falsely labeled and never inspected “during FSIS surveillance activities,” the announcement says.

What to Do If You Purchased Recalled Meat?

If you bought any of these recalled items, do not eat them. The FSIS also urges restaurants and retailers not to sell or serve these products.

Instead, throw them away or return them.

So far, there have been no reports of illness or injury from customers who consumed these products. However, if you are concerned about illness or injury, the announcement says to contact your healthcare provider.
