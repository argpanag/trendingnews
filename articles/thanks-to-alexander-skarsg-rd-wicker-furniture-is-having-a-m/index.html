Thanks to Alexander Skarsgård, Wicker Furniture is Having a Moment

TheTools · 11.11.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

Skarsgård has charmed us before, but this time, as a man quite literally woven from wicker, he's got audiences looking at wicker furniture in an entirely new light. In the newly released comedy/romance movie Wicker, the actor plays a basket-woven partner who listens, respects his wife's wishes and is everything one might hope for in a companion. And perhaps that's precisely why wicker was chosen. With its intricate weave, tactile surface and unmistakable handmade appeal, it has a way of making a room feel warmer, more lived-in and more interesting.

If wicker’s recent moment on screen has brought the material back into focus, its growing presence in interiors is hardly a coincidence.

“I think the renewed interest in wicker is part of a larger shift in how people approach their homes and the materials they choose. After years of polished finishes and manufactured surfaces, there's a growing appreciation for materials that feel natural, tactile and connected to the process of making. Wicker fits into this shift beautifully, adding warmth and texture without
creating too much visual clutter,” reasons Sivan Santhosh, founder of Neytt Homes, whose rugs have featured on global platforms, including the famed Met Gala carpet.

Wicker has long held a place in the hearts of designers and architects alike. More than a material, it is a weaving technique used to craft furniture and accessories from a variety of natural materials, including rattan, cane, willow, bamboo and jute. “There is something quite honest about wicker – you can see the material, understand its texture and appreciate the handwork involved. Its weave creates its own patterns, shadows, and depth, adding visual interest without the need for extra ornamentation. Sometimes, good design is simply about choosing a material that speaks for itself,” says Santhosh.

Long associated with leisurely verandahs, country houses and vintage furniture, wicker has gradually moved beyond its tropical, coastal and traditional associations. The secret to making it work in contemporary spaces lies in geometry and proportion, says Chirag Swain, principal designer of his eponymous design brand. The thickness of the frame, scale of the weave and balance between solid and open surfaces can all influence how a piece comes together, giving the material a more modern expression.

A similar approach works particularly well in urban homes, where subtlety often goes a long way. “In a contemporary apartment, wicker works best when integrated into furniture or architectural elements. A wardrobe with woven panels, a cane-front cabinet or a dining chair with a slim frame can introduce the material subtly. It's the contrast with surrounding materials that makes it interesting,” says Vineeta Dassani, interior designer and creative director of TIS Living.

If you're looking to introduce wicker into your home, start small. Think rustic pendant lights, chandeliers or standing lamps, where woven surfaces can cast interesting patterns of light and shadow. Sculptural installations and murals offer another way to treat the material as an art form, while smaller additions such as a bedside table, tray or dining chair can bring in just the right amount of texture. Greens, rust, terracotta and muted reds can give the material a different visual identity, particularly when introduced through upholstery or furniture elements.

For those willing to make a more pronounced statement, wicker can extend well beyond smaller accents. “Wicker across larger furniture surfaces can establish a visual relationship between the furniture and the surrounding architecture,” suggests Swain. A woven backrest, headboard, desk screen or cabinet front can transform the character of a piece while retaining its functionality. Wardrobe fronts, media consoles and room dividers are equally interesting applications, allowing the weave to become an integral part of the design. A woven partition, for instance, can divide two areas while still allowing light to filter through and maintaining a sense of openness.

When seeking wicker furniture, a well-made chair is a good place to begin “Looking underneath the seat can also reveal a great deal about the construction and finishing,” suggests Dassani. Running your hand over the weave is quite an effective way to gauge wicker quality. “It should feel smooth and consistent, with no loose strands or sharp edges. Buyers should also understand the difference between natural and synthetic wicker, particularly when shopping for outdoor furniture, where UV-resistant synthetic wicker is generally the more practical choice,” advises Nishi Jain, founder and principal designer of House of J.

Since many UAE homes feature expansive glazing, polished stone flooring and clean architectural lines, interiors can sometimes feel cold or visually uniform. “Wicker is a wonderful way to soften these spaces,” Jain says, making it a natural fit for contemporary homes that benefit from a little warmth and textural contrast.

The key to making wicker feel at home in such spaces is to consider what it is paired with. The interplay of different materials brings out wicker's texture and helps it feel right at home in contemporary urban spaces. A stone-topped table with a woven base, for instance, creates an interesting balance of textures. Wood is another natural pairing, although it's best to avoid matching wicker too closely with honey-toned teak, as the combination can feel dated. Instead, opt for walnut, pale ash, or darker wood finishes that allow the weave to stand out. Brass, particularly in an aged or unlacquered finish, makes for a lovely accent, whether through handles, lamps, or subtle furniture details.

However, its placement and upkeep matter, especially in Middle Eastern climates. “Natural cane should be protected from prolonged direct sunlight and kept away from areas where condensation or excessive moisture may affect it. Covered balconies should also be treated as outdoor spaces when considering natural wicker,” cautions Dassani.

Since wicker is a weaving technique rather than a material in itself, the choice of fibre, weave and finish can make all the difference to how it looks and functions in a space. “Different natural fibres vary in their flexibility, strength, texture and ability to respond to their surroundings, making it important to understand the material before deciding how best to use it,” says Santhosh.

The weave itself is equally important. A tighter, more uniform weave or a darker, more treated finish lends a piece a refined, structured look, while a more open, irregular weave in a natural finish feels relaxed and organic.

Beyond its aesthetic appeal, wicker furniture is lightweight, durable and relatively easy to maintain. Regular dusting with a soft brush or vacuum attachment keeps it clean, while excessive moisture and prolonged sunlight should be best avoided. For exposed outdoor settings, make sure you opt for UV-stable synthetic wicker, paired with weather-resistant frames and fabrics. The right choice of materials and finish can make all the difference to the furniture’s longevity.

After all, if a wicker husband can win someone's heart, perhaps a wicker chair, pendant or cabinet deserves a place in your home, too.
