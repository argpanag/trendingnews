US stocks surged on Monday as the AI trade remained in focus ahead of a busy week of earnings and inflation data.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) rose 2.2%, while the S&P 500 (^GSPC) gained 2.3%. The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) climbed about 2.7% after all three indexes posted losses last week.

All eyes were on the artificial intelligence trade following a volatile post-election market shake-up and the revelation that Nvidia's (NVDA) board is opening an investigation into CEO Jensen Huang over his affair.

Several news outlets reported last week that the US believes Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman who is alleged to have been recruited to spy for China's intelligence agency. In a memo to employees, Huang admitted to marital infidelity, stating, "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry."

(^DJI )

Nvidia stock trimmed losses, falling 4% Monday. Broader stock indexes shrugged off the news as they continue to recover from a bruising sell-off last Thursday.

Helping buoy stocks, last week's slide in energy prices continued Monday with Brent Crude Futures (BZ=F) falling to around $91 per barrel.

The biggest swell of third quarter earnings results is in the books, with just a few more weeks in the reporting season. On Monday, Hims and Hers (HIMS), Rocket Lab (RKLB), and Monday.com (MNDY) were among the companies sharing earnings.

The week starts off with light economic data, with wholesale trade data serving as the primary report.

  • October inflation data is expected to be cooler, helped by steadying energy prices

    October inflation data coming on Tuesday is expected to show that inflation cooled somewhat from September's elevated levels but remains sticky, Yahoo Finance's Claire Boston reports:

    Economists surveyed by Bloomberg expect the Consumer Price Index to rise 0.3% in October compared to a month earlier, a far slower pace than September's 0.6% gain. Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, is seen rising 0.2%, down from 0.3% a month earlier.

    October's report is unlikely to include annual inflation figures because the Bureau of Labor Statistics was unable to publish full October 2025 data due to the government shutdown last year. But some economists estimate that inflation accelerated slightly on a year-over-year basis.

  • Musk confirms groundbreaking on $10 billion solar cell manufacturing project in Texas

    Elon Musk appeared to confirm on Monday that groundbreaking has begun for Tesla's $10 billion solar cell manufacturing factory in Texas, less than a week after Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the state's gubernatorial election.

    Writing in response to posts by a widely followed Musk-sphere blogger that claimed to show images of groundbreaking at the proposed factory site, Musk said, "Project Crystal Sun."

    The site, located in Fort Bend County, Texas, is expected to hold a massive solar cell manufacturing complex to make solar panels and other components, per documents filed with the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts. Tesla is looking to have the factory operational by the first quarter of 2029, with a capital investment of $10.1 billion, the company told the Texas Comptroller.

    The update comes as questions swirl around Musk's extensive business ties in Texas after wins by Governor-elect Hinojosa and Senator-elect James Talarico, the latter race in which Musk spent hundreds of millions of dollars on the unsuccessful bid of former state Attorney General Ken Paxton.

    SpaceX has invested more than $3 billion into Starbase and the nearby Starship launch infrastructure, while Tesla has reported construction costs of more than $1 billion for its Gigafactory production site in Austin. In August, SpaceX and Tesla laid out a joint plan to spend $16.8 billion building a "Terafab" semiconductor production facility in Grimes County.

    If both planned projects come to fruition, Musk's infrastructure investments alone in the Lone Star State could total more than $30 billion.

  • Jensen Huang just handed Wall Street its first $4.5 trillion CEO scandal

    Wall Street has priced plenty of CEO scandals. It has never priced one at a company worth $4.5 trillion that makes up nearly 7% of the S&P 500 (^GSPC).

    Nvidia (NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang admitted in a Nov. 8 memo to employees to an affair with Alice Huang, whom US intelligence officials believe worked for China's Ministry of State Security. "I was unfaithful to my wife," he wrote. The board has opened an independent investigation.

    The scale is hard to overstate. At Friday's close, Nvidia was worth about four times the nine biggest companies ever hit by CEO personal-conduct scandals combined, by Yahoo Finance calculations. The largest, Intel (INTC), was worth $249 billion when Brian Krzanich resigned in 2018 over a past consensual relationship with an employee. Nvidia is about 18 times that.

    This is key-person risk — the danger that a company's value rests on one person — at its most extreme. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has run it ever since, with no named successor.

    Adjust for a bigger stock market, and the gap narrows but doesn't close. No earlier scandal-hit company made up more than about 1% of the S&P 500, a mark only Intel in 2018 and HP (HPQ) in 2010 reached. Nvidia is about seven times that.
