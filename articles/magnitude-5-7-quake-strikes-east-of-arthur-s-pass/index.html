Magnitude 5.7 quake strikes east of Arthur's Pass

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:59 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Emergencies

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._v0.KrRTFtoPsaA8nSDloPZ4KejQJr59ANQpD9ULgGwzaM0/emergencies/2644964%2Fmagnitude-5-point-7-quake-strikes-east-of-arthur-s-pass

Two earthquakes jolted residents up and down the country on Tuesday evening.

GeoNet said a magnitude 5.7 tremor struck 15 kilometres southeast of Arthur's Pass at 11.30pm, at a depth of 5km, with strong shaking near the epicentre.

More than 14,000 people reported feeling the quake, some as far south as Invercargill and as far north as Auckland.

Most described the shaking as weak to light.

A second, weaker quake struck a quarter of an hour later, at 11.44pm, in the same spot.

GeoNet said this one was a magnitude 3.2, at a depth of 4km. Just under 1200 people reported having felt it.

One RNZ listener in Christchurch said the first quake "felt deep and distant" and went on for about a minute: "Grandma awake!"

Another in the Christchurch suburb of Redwood described being "restless and shaken yet again".

Maree said the quake was "quite strong" in Fendalton, and described it as the strongest she had felt in the last seven years.

In New Brighton, Alison hadn't yet gone to bed and felt "a gentle rocking motion", while fellow resident Liz said she was "awake and didn't feel a thing".

Colleen said her bed was swaying in Rangiora.

Ilam resident Brian Thomas said his wooden bungalow shook abruptly: "I was instantly awakened, remembering the 2010 quake."

Mike from Westport described it as a "short shake, not strong", while Freda in Avonside said "it was very strong, my house shook and furniture rocked".

In Lyttelton, Chris and partner were both woken from deep sleep. A listener in Karatani, Dunedin, said they could still feel the "rolling motion" an hour later.

The quakes follow an earlier weak tremor on Tuesday, at 12.40pm. The magnitude 3.3 quake's epicentre was 15km southwest of Arthur's Pass, at a depth of 5 km. Only three people reported noticing it.

This week marks a decade since the devastating 7.8 magnitude Kaikōura earthquake struck just after midnight on 14 November 2016.
