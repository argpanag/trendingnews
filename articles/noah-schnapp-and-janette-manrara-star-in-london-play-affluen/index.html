Noah Schnapp and Janette Manrara Star in London Play Affluenza

TheTools · 03.10.2026 19:57 · source · permalink

Actor Noah Schnapp and former Strictly Come Dancing professional Janette Manrara are starring in the world premiere of Andy Sandberg's dark comedic play Affluenza at London's Riverside Studios, which has extended its run through Nov. 28, 2026, following high ticket demand.

The production, which premiered on Sept. 26 as the first of five plays in the venue's Mainstage 26/27 season, is loosely inspired by a true story involving a privileged teenager whose drunk driving incident ends in devastation. Set in the Hamptons, the play examines themes of wealth, moral accountability, and the American justice system when the defense argues the teenager suffered from affluenza.

Alongside Schnapp and Manrara, the six-person cast features Olivier nominee Rupert Young, Juliet Cowan, Wallis Currie-Wood, and Donald Sage Mackay. Sandberg serves as both writer and director for the two-hour-and-fifteen-minute Off-West End production.

The director expressed enthusiasm for the show's reception during its opening phase.

"We’ve been thrilled by the audience’s reaction to Affluenza in previews," said Andy Sandberg, Writer and Director.

He praised the cast and team working on the production.

"The extraordinary cast have brought my characters to life in the most wonderful way, and we’re set for a phenomenal run between now and 28 November," said Andy Sandberg, Writer and Director.

Sandberg noted that adding performances before previews began was an exciting milestone for the creative team.

"We are delighted by the enthusiasm for Affluenza, and adding extra performances before we even begin previews is incredibly exciting," said Andy Sandberg, Writer and Director.

He added that he looks forward to welcoming audiences to Riverside Studios.

"I’m honoured to be working with an extraordinary cast and team on the show, and we can’t wait for audiences to see it," said Andy Sandberg, Writer and Director.

For Manrara, the play marks her non-dancing theatrical debut following her stint playing Roxie Hart in the UK tour of Chicago. She plays three distinct characters in the piece.

"I was a thespian in high school," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She explained how she initially transitioned into dance during her early career.

"I just loved telling stories," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

The performer highlighted what drew her to the project after reading the script.

"It’s such an exciting challenge," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She noted the transition into a non-dance role.

"This is the first time I’m doing something not dance-related. But as soon as I read the script, it totally captured my interest because of the topics it discusses," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Manrara emphasized the societal relevance of the themes explored on stage.

"The play holds up a mirror to society: it covers so many important subjects that are hugely relevant right now," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She elaborated on the specific issues raised throughout the drama.

"Privilege, power dynamics, the justice system, morality, generational consequences – how what you do in your young adult life can really impact on you later – and parents trying to do the right thing for their children. I love plays and films where, the second you’ve seen them, you just have to have a conversation. Affluenza is definitely one of those," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Working alongside experienced stage actors provided a unique learning opportunity for her.

"They’re all so phenomenal – I feel like I’m a competition winner," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She described her approach to absorbing techniques from her co-stars.

"I’m a big sponge. I gain a lot from just watching, so even when I’m not involved with a scene, I try to be in the room," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Manrara shared feedback she received from her co-star following an early scene.

"The first scene I had with Noah, he turned to me after and said ‘I really enjoyed doing that with you’. I think that comes from working with celebrities who were very vulnerable, and thinking about how to make the other person feel comfortable," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Reflecting on her current personal and professional path, she described her evolving career stage.

"reinvention," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She expanded on how motherhood and maturity have influenced her life choices.

"I’m a mother now, I’m turning 43 in November, and Lyra is a toddler. Over the summer I had a massive period of reflection. Women, especially mothers, put so much pressure on themselves to be a certain way – we shouldn’t do this or wear this.

But I still feel very myself. I’m just more aware of who I am and what I value now, so I make better choices than I did at 25. I’m ready to navigate to a different part of me: Janette 3.0," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Regarding changes at Strictly Come Dancing, she noted her instinct about future developments.

"gut instinct," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She clarified her ongoing connection to the show despite departing her presenting duties.

"But I’m hosting the live tour, so I’m still part of the Strictly family, and Aljaž is on it, so we still have Strictly in our house. Really the biggest change is that I won’t be watching on Saturday night because I’ll be on stage!" said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She described her passion for returning to acting after watching major West End plays.

"tap back into the acting part of me. It’s always been there, laying dormant, waiting for the right moment," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She recalled her reaction to seeing productions like Prima Facie and Inter Alia.

"I remember thinking ‘I want this as my next challenge.’ I’ve been working hard, quietly preparing myself for an opportunity like this, and I’m so grateful that Affluenza came along," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Manrara expressed her appreciation for live audience connections.

"There’s nothing like the feeling of live theatre and connecting with an audience," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

She offered advice for theatregoers attending the show.

"Be prepared to go on a wild ride," said Janette Manrara, Actress.

Tickets for Affluenza start between £25 and £28 at Riverside Studios in London. The venue's Mainstage season will continue with Sacrilegious running from Dec. 5, 2026, to Jan. 23, 2027, followed by Expecting from Jan. 30 to March 27, 2027.
