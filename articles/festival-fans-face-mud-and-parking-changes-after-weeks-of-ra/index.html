LICKING COUNTY, Ohio (WSYX) — Rain and mud are not stopping fans from traveling across the globe for the Lost Lands Music Festival in Central Ohio.

Thousands of people from places like Indianapolis, Hawaii and Bowling Green, Kentucky have come to Licking County for the annual event, which continues to draw an international crowd each year. Festivalgoers said they are ready for any weather, packing items like ponchos and rain boots to prepare for the muddy grounds.

For Terra Knotts, who lives on Jacksontown Road near the festival site, Lost Lands is a family tradition. She and her relatives have spent six years helping out by allowing people to park in their front yard.

"This is my daughter. She's one of the drivers for me. It's my son, my daughter, my grandkids. We make it happen for them," said Knotts.

After weeks of steady rain in Licking County starting in August, Knotts said part of her yard is now covered with caution tape to block off flooded areas.

"We usually have it full of cars and I'm having to accommodate so my neighbor opened up her yard for me to help me because the weather has just made it a mess," Knotts said.

This year, she and her husband are giving extra rides to and from the festival entrance so visitors can avoid the mud.

"They're nice people but the weather it's definitely it's made it a mess," she said.

Festivalgoers said mud and rain are just part of the experience.

"Just get ready to be muddy and I don't know maybe a poncho, rain boots," one attendee said.

Another festivalgoer said weather would not affect their plans. "I don't care about the rain. It doesn't matter," the attendee said.

"You just got to take it as it goes. if it rains, it rains. If your favorite artist doesn't show, they don't show. You just gotta be there," another attendee said.

With more rain forecast for the weekend, Knotts said she worries some people may need a tow out of her yard by Sunday. Even with the challenges, she said she will keep opening her property to as many festival fans as possible.

"It's crazy. It is everybody. We just love them. I can't say enough it's just a wonderful place for people to come," Knotts said.
