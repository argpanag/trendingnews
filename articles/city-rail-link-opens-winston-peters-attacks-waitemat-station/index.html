City Rail Link opens: Winston Peters attacks Waitematā station name

TheTools · 11.09.2026 14:59 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

The country’s biggest-ever transport infrastructure project officially opened with fanfare, cheers and, of course, politics.

The Prime Minister, Auckland Mayor and Transport Minister shared similar themes of celebration, collaboration and congratulations for those involved in kicking the City Rail Link into gear.

“It’s just a fantastic thing. A world-class city needs a world-class underground system, and this is what we’ve got with the CRL. It’s really special,” PM Christopher Luxon said.

He described it as a “game changer” that will boost the economy by more than $2 billion.

“People are going to be able to get to where they need to go without getting stuck in traffic. That’s more time they get to spend with their families.

“It’s more time they get socialising, it’s more time they get working. There’ll be fewer cars on the roads ... deliveries will be quicker, fuel use and emissions will be lower.

“And while this may be the end of the project, it’s also the beginning, I think, of an incredibly exciting new chapter for Auckland.”

Chris Bishop recounted how long it had taken and thanked everyone involved, including businesses who faced years of disruption and heartache.

“Their contribution, and it has been a contribution, has helped make this project possible. None of this has been easy,” Bishop said.

“But I am certain that once people jump on the first train, they will recognise it’s worth it.”

In the audience were hundreds who’d worked on the project, politicians of all stripes, school students and local businesses.

After opening his speech with a rendition of Johnny Cash’s Folsom Prison Blues and the lyrics “I hear the train a-comin’, it’s rolling ‘round the bend”, Rail Minister Winston Peters quickly descended into politics.

He promised that from Sunday: “Aucklanders can expect trains turning up and guess what? On time”.

Peters reminded everyone of the Government contribution to the project.

“The fact is without the $2.4 billion in investment, this tunnel would be really nice but a little used asset. Mr Mayor, we hope seriously you are happier now.

“We will not stop advocating for rail as unblocking Auckland. It benefits all of New Zealand. A blocked road is no good for the nation or the economy.”

But he railed against the decision to rename Britomart station Waitematā.

“I have a question for you though. How is it possible that after 100 years we have built the City Rail Link, but there is no Auckland Central station?

“That name follows universal precedent. Go around the rest of the world. There is London central, there is New York central, so why not Auckland Central? Instead we have Waitematā station.

“We’ve got news for you. Waitematā is the harbour, not the station, and people don’t want to catch a train into the sea,” he said, to some laughter.

He questioned why other stations weren’t called Mayoral Drive or Mt Eden.

“Instead, we’ve been tasked with new names. So, thank you Auckland Transport, or should I say ngā mihi.”

That comment received boos from some in the crowd.

“Some of us are not woke, we believe in talking to ordinary people,” Peters responded.

Others in the crowd later pointed out to the Herald there is no New York or London central station.

There is a Grand Central Terminal in New York and in London a number of central train and tube stations – but none are called London Central.

Peters vowed to continue with Government-funded rail corridors, including a fourth main line connecting Avondale and Onehunga and level crossing removals.

None of those projects have been funded.

Auckland Mayor Wayne Brown also got political.

“I have admiration for the tenacity of Len Brown to get this project past the Government,” Brown said, to applause.

“Who then took it off him and entered into contracts that I never would have signed.

“Most of all, responsibility of this falls to my council and the public transport version of AT to make this fully used, pay its way, and trigger a more intensified, efficient city.”

He said “many people have told me to be positive today, but the fact remains this took too long and cost too much and is disappointing”.

The lessons learnt would hopefully be passed on to other mega-projects, Brown said.

Despite the project taking so long that successive Governments and ministers have been involved in it, Labour leader Chris Hipkins and his transport and infrastructure spokespeople were not at the event.

Deputy leader Carmel Sepuloni represented Labour, while Hipkins was in Tauranga at the AIMS Games.

Bishop said: “success has many fathers”, noting all the people there for the ceremony.

The Herald understands only one Labour representative was invited to the event.

“It was kicked off by a National government. It’s been finished by a National Government,” Luxon said.

“There’s been lots of people in between and I think what’s really exciting is it’s finished, and now it’s about the huge opportunity that it represents for the city.”

While there’s no mega-project looming, the next step for rail in Auckland is level crossing removal on the Western Line.

Auckland Transport chief executive Stacey Van Der Putten said it was an important day but acknowledged it had been a “sad week” with the death of a MAGS student near Baldwin Ave station at a level crossing.

Bishop admitted the level crossing removal programme should’ve started earlier.

“There’s a business case that’s underway being led by Auckland Council in relation to that. This is probably a 20-year programme of work.

“As you move in towards the inner west around Mount Albert, Morningside, those are higher priority because you’ve got more traffic volume obviously coming through, and there are safety risks there.

“I don’t want to dwell too much on whether or not we should have got on with them earlier. I’ve publicly said before the answer to that is ‘yes’, because really maximising the benefits of CRL come from sorting out the level crossings.

“I’m reluctant to dwell too much on that because today is about the future and about the, you know, the great achievement of CRL. If we’re honest about it, we should have got on with it sooner.”

The public can use the City Rail Link from early Sunday morning, with the first train departing Ōtāhuhu just before 6am.

Auckland Transport will have 100 “ambassadors” at stations next week to help people navigate their new journeys.

KiwiRail and One Rail will also have extra staff to monitor safety and operational issues.

Van der Putten said the agencies were going into “hypercare” mode.

“We’ll have people observing and picking up things to be able to feedback that we need to fix, and we’ll have extra security, we’ll have extra people everywhere actually.

“We are in this hypercare mode, and there’ll be good vigilance in terms of feeding that back and making changes as necessary.”

Fourteen thousand people have worked on the project.

KiwiRail CEO Peter Reidy told the Herald earlier this week he was confident the system was ready.
