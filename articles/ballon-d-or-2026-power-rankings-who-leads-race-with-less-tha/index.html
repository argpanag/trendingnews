Ballon d’Or 2026 power rankings: Who leads race with less than month to go?

Here’s how the top 10 contenders rank in the race to lift the Ballon d’Or ahead of the ceremony on 25 October

The race for the Ballon d’Or is entering its final stretch with just three weeks until the world’s best player is crowned in London.

Spain’s triumph over Argentina in the final will surely bode well for the likes of 2024 winner Rodri and Lamine Yamal in their pursuit of the individual accolade, who have both played their part in Barcelona’s 100 percent start to the new campaign, hoping it will complement their role in winning football’s most coveted trophy.

Harry Kane remains football purist’s favourite for the Ballon d’Or, however, following his sublime goalscoring campaign with Bayern Munich and after making a series of important interventions at the World Cup with England, too – can he replicate that unparalleled form in the early months of 2026/27 to make team achievements irrelevant come the ceremony on 25 October?

During the World Cup eight-time Ballon d’Or winner Lionel Messi showed that he can still force himself into the conversation at the age of 39, firing eight goals to propel Argentina to the final and briefly take the crown as the competition’s all-time top goalscorer - until he was eclipsed by Kylian Mbappe, while Sir David Beckham has tipped the Argentine to win after inspiring Inter Miami to the Campeones Cup.

Here’s how the top 10 front-runners stack up in The Independent’s Ballon d’Or 2026 power rankings:

10. Pau Cubarsi 🆕

What’s this, a new entry to the power rankings?! Barcelona’s Cubarsi is favourite to bring home the Golden Boy award, but he’s so far been bumped from our list by Vinicius Jr, who squeezed into our top 10 mainly to spare myself some angry emails from those affiliated to Real Madrid and Brazil (I joke, kind of).

The 2024 runner-up had another strong season at the Bernabeu, netting 22 goals in 36 appearances, though was unable to inspire the club to a trophy. And after a below average start to the new term, with just one goal to his name as Real sit six points off the top, he’s been usurped by an El Clasico rival in Cubarsi.

Despite still only being 19, Cubarsi is an established figure at the heart of the Barca and Spain defence, and after picking up the honour of Young Player of the Tournament following his country’s World Cup win, he has carried that impenetrable form back to the Camp Nou. The Blaugrana have won seven from seven, have only been breached five times and look the team to beat this season, not just in Spain but across Europe.

He’s up there among the best defenders on the planet as a teenager, playing a role that usually favours the more experienced; he will only get better. But does that mean Cubarsi become the second player to ever win both the Golden Boy and Ballon d’Or on the same night after Messi? Doubtful.

9. Michael Olise ↔️

Crystal Palace could only keep him in Croydon for so long.

With Bayern Munich and France, Olise has established himself as one of the world’s best attackers, a talent so bright that the German giants did well to ward off interest from the likes of Real Madrid - who no doubt saw the World Cup partnership he formed with Mbappe and want to reunite the pair in the Spanish capital at some stage.

Will his electrifying performances be enough to win the Ballon d’Or? Probably not, this year. But he’s in the conversation, which is a statement in itself.

The jury may also fear what an Olise acceptance speech might look like. If his post-match interview from Bayern’s opening Champions League win over Bodo/Glimt is anything to go by, in which he scored a brace and assisted another, it’ll be like getting blood out of a stone. The perils of the nonchalant gimmick.

8. Erling Haaland ⬇️

On solely his performances, Haaland does not exactly deserve to drop down in these rankings as we hurtle towards the Ballon d’Or ceremony. So let’s start with the good.

In the summer, Haaland was one of the stars of the World Cup and ensured Norway, playing in their first finals since 1998, lived up to their dark-horse status by powering them to the last-eight. On the way, his unrelenting goalscoring exploits humbled Brazil, sending the five-time winners packing. Away from the big-hitting nations, Haaland was far and away the tournament’s standout player, to little surprise.

He has then followed up on a pretty bog-standard season for City - just the 38 goals last year, the fourth season he’s hit 30+ at the Etihad, and a domestic double - by starting the season with seven goals in six appearances. The man can’t and won’t stop scoring.

But, there’s a big but. Voting for the Ballon d’Or closed a few days after City’s guilty verdict was revealed. Currently, every bit of success related that club feels tainted. We feel that would have swayed some on the jury (not all) to vote against Haaland, whose goals are being financed by owners who inflated their own sponsorship income by £830m across eight seasons. He was already an outsider, but his odds have dropped further.

Apologies Erling. Not your fault.

7. Jude Bellingham ⬆️

How was this man’s place on the England plane ever in question?! Thomas Tuchel’s player of the tournament, Bellingham served his critics a serious helping of humble pie by guiding the Three Lions to the elusive World Cup bronze.

That beautiful redemption arc, on its own, got Bellingham onto this list. That’s because the season that preceded the World Cup wasn’t spectacular. Injuries limited him to just 13 goal contributions for Real Madrid in another trophyless campaign.

However, he’s started the new season pretty well, and that keeps him on this list - six goal contributions in seven league games shows he might be returning to his Madrid form of old under Jose Mourinho. He also just bagged four goal contributions in England’s 7-0 thumping of Croatia, the first midfielder to do so in a single match for the Three Lions since David Platt scored four against San Marino in February 1993.

His chances of winning the Ballon d’Or are still low compared to the competition. But as was proved at the World Cup - maybe don’t count him out.

6. Ousmane Dembele ⬆️

Had France won the World Cup, as many expected them to do leading up to that drab semi-final exit, Dembele would have been heavily favoured to secure a second successive Ballon d’Or. The combination of World Cup and Champions League winners’ medals is a bit of a cheat code.

That would’ve been quite the gimme for a player who, let’s be honest, was France’s fourth best attacker at the World Cup. And while, like last year, he ended the season as Paris Saint-Germain’s top scorer with a Ligue 1 and Champions League to boot, he only managed about half of his goalscoring output.

His trophy haul still carries enough weight to be comfortably in the top 10 contenders, but the failure to lift that World Cup title gives him only an outside chance to go back-to-back - and we think that’s fair. His slow start to the new campaign, still goalless in the league at the time of writing, doesn’t help his cause. Sorry Ousmane, but this year shouldn’t be your year.

5. Lionel Messi ↔️

He couldn’t again, could he? Messi’s inclusion on this list encapsulates the significance of the World Cup in deciding the Ballon d’Or winner; he’s not going to take the accolade for his MLS exploits, is he. But fair play to the eight-time winner; at 39, he showed he very much still had it.

Messi’s eight goals propelled Argentina to the World Cup final, where they were rightly defeated by Spain - had he lifted back-to-back titles, he’d probably be towards the top two of this list. After all, he claimed the 2023 award after La Albiceleste’s triumph 10 months earlier, by which time he was on the books at Inter Miami.

Even with his runners-up medal, Indy Sport writers had him as the player of the tournament. That, combined with the jury’s historic love for the Argentinian, means you can’t exactly write him off. Messi’s retirement from international football, announced following the death of his father, Jorge, underlines that this is now his final chance to win another Ballon d’Or.

Beckham has tipped him to do just that after helping Inter Miami to the Campeones Cup. I’m sure the jury will put adequate weight on that...

4. Rodri ↔️

You might not like it, but the weight of the World Cup in the eyes of the Ballon d’Or jury is undeniable. Just ask Luka Modric.

Rodri had a sensational World Cup at the heart of Spain’s midfield, shielding their rock-solid defence while acting as the metronome that took play forward. His influence took him to the Golden Ball ahead of top goalscorers Messi and Mbappe - and in the immediate aftermath, we felt that could be enough to swing the Ballon d’Or in his favour.

That always felt like a bit of an injustice, considering he didn’t have the most spectacular domestic season. Returning from injury with Manchester City, it took him a while to properly find his feet in a campaign that saw Pep Guardiola’s side win the FA Cup and League Cup, but miss out on the Premier League title.

Yes, the World Cup is the World Cup - and whether you agree with the importance of team achievements in an individual award, the reality is that it does matter a hell of a lot. However, with the dust now settled on La Roja’s triumph in New Jersey, and while he has slotted nicely into a Barcelona side that boasts a 100 percent record so far this term, he has only completed two full 90s in seven appearances at the time of writing. That is not the instant “first name on the team-sheet” impact he might have needed to get his push for a second Ballon d’Or over the line.

He also doesn’t seem full of self-belief himself, instead feeling that Barcelona is pushing team-mate Yamal to win the award. "The club has opted for Lamine because they believe he is the player who deserves it most, and that's perfectly legitimate," he said.

3. Kylian Mbappe ⬇️

It feels pretty crazy to think that, at 27, Mbappe still doesn’t have a Ballon d’Or to his name, given he’s been at the top of the game for what feels like forever now.

Once again, he’s very much a frontrunner time around. His Golden Boot at the World Cup will do most of the heavy lifting, with his 10 goals in North America seeing him pass first Miroslav Klose and then Lionel Messi as the tournament’s all-time record goalscorer. Both he and Messi would have felt hard done by not to go home with the Golden Ball.

But lest we forget, despite the toxicity that surrounded his club season with Real Madrid, he didn’t exactly experience a drop-off in performance. He bagged 42 goals in 44 games, all while being hounded at the Bernabeu - and while it wasn’t enough to prevent another trophyless season for Los Blancos, you can’t knock his output.

Mbappe still believes he is the best in the world and under Mourinho, he’s crucially got the new season off to a flyer. He is already in scintillating goalscoring form, bagging eight in eight, and suddenly looks one of the biggest challengers to a certain Englishman for the fabled individual prize.

2. Harry Kane ⬇️

England captain Kane is a frontrunner to win the Ballon d’Or despite failing to set the World Cup alight quite like he managed the Bundesliga in 2025/26. That’s a testament to just how prolific he was last term.

Kane was the top goalscorer in Europe, hitting 61 goals in 51 games across all competitions as he spearheaded Bayern towards the league and cup double; he’s added silverware to his ever-present individual numbers. He was then hardly irrelevant at the World Cup, scoring six goals - including a crucial late brace against DR Congo to spare his country serious embarrassment - as England secured their best World Cup finish since 1966.

As he grows into the new season with four goals in his last three games, the jury have reason to put their classic emphasis on the World Cup and wider team achievement to one side. There is also the significance of the location of the Ballon d’Or ceremony to consider; it will be held in London, to commemorate the 70th anniversary of the very first Ballon d’Or won by England’s Sir Stanley Matthews. Could Kane become just the fifth Englishman to win the prize?

"Of course, now it is out of my hands, it is down to the voters and the people who decide it,” Kane said. “The season I had was my best season by far and we’ll have to wait and see."

For months, he has been the odds-on favourite to take home the Ballon d’Or. But not anymore...

1. Lamine Yamal ⬆️

Yamal will surely win his fair share of Ballon d’Ors across his career, but the 19-year-old may very well end up taking home his first this year, going one better than his runners-up finish in 2025.

He became the first player under 20 to appear at both a European Championship final and a World Cup final - he won both. While his output was pretty modest for his standards, he was influential throughout the tournament for Spain - and that’s after coming into it carrying a hamstring injury.

But unlike team-mate Rodri, who sits below him in these rankings, his club form across the past year enhances his argument to win the award rather than detracts from it. He bagged 24 goals and 18 assists across all competitions for Barcelona in another LaLiga-winning season - hardly mediocre numbers - and has started the new term with eight in eight.

It’s back on Spain duty, though, where Yamal has made the pivotal leapfrog over Kane in the Ballon d’Or race. A Nations League showdown, of all competitions, between the pair at Wembley was won by Yamal, who scored inside two minutes when taking advantage of a Marc Guehi error. Spain went on to win 3-2, with Kane missing a crucial penalty.

Yamal has since bagged four more goal contributions in his next two Spain outings and is now officially the bookies’ favourite with three weeks to go. Rodri, his closest Barcelona rival for the Ballon d’Or, has also endorsed Yamal to win it. Things are looking more and more promising for the teenager.
