Oliver Glasner: I didn’t lie or cheat anybody, I was always honest with Palace

TheTools · 11.10.2026 08:31 · source · permalink

Forest manager visits Selhurst Park for first time since leaving club this summer, but is unsure what sort of reception he will receive

Almost five months after lifting the club’s first European trophy on a balmy night in Leipzig, Oliver Glasner returns to Crystal Palace with uncertainty hanging in the air.

The most successful manager in Palace’s history should, in normal circumstances, be welcomed back as a hero, yet there are many supporters who insist he has tarnished the legacy he left behind.

Along with the Uefa Conference League, he also won the FA Cup and Community Shield during an unprecedented period of success.

Glasner is now the head coach of Nottingham Forest, who have emerged as Palace’s unlikely rivals over the past 18 months following the bitter feud over Europe last season.

Forest replaced Palace in the Europa League after the south London club were found to have breached Uefa’s multi-club ownership rules and the Court of Arbitration for Sport dismissed their appeal.

The moniker for English football’s newest grudge game? “El Cas-ico”.

Enmity between the two clubs has deepened after Forest’s owner Evangelos Marinakis took legal action against Palace over the incendiary banner which was unfurled at Selhurst Park in the league game last August.

Marinakis is not expected to attend Sunday’s match, so Glasner is fully under the spotlight after taking over at Forest in July.

While Steve Parish, the Palace chairman, insists the Austrian deserves a warm reception, the truth is that nobody can really predict how it will go.

“I don’t want to be in the middle of the stories about the game, but I know I will be,” Glasner, sitting in the boardroom at Forest’s training ground this week, says.

“There are two hearts beating in my chest because we had two great years with ups and downs, but of course many more ups.

“What I’d love is that the Palace fans look back at the emotions they had winning the finals at Wembley and Leipzig and how much joy they had. Palace fans will always support their club, but when they look back at the two years when I was the manager we had some great moments together.

“Of course, it was a disappointment not to play in the Europa League. But if somebody had told us 15 months ago that we were going to play Conference League, we would have all hugged each other and said: ‘Hey, wow.’”

The anger of many Palace fans appears to centre on Glasner taking what they perceive as a step sideways to join Forest. There was an expectation that he was leaving Palace for a heavyweight club with Champions League pedigree.

Glasner held talks with a number of clubs across Europe before he was convinced by the ambition of Marinakis during talks in Athens.

“Sometimes unexpected things happen in life and there was no plan for me to be Forest manager. I didn’t lie or cheat anybody, I was always honest with everyone,” he says.

“I told them [Palace] in October that I wouldn’t sign a new contract and of course if I’d told him [Parish] then that I was going to Forest he would have been disappointed.

“I gave them nine months to prepare for life after Oliver Glasner. I could have waited until the last day and signed another contract there, but I thought they deserved the truth and the most time to prepare.”

Glasner is still waiting for lift-off at Forest, with one win, two draws and two defeats from the opening five league matches. Those results have not surprised Glasner, who made almost identical starts at both Palace and Eintracht Frankfurt.

Yet he has detected clear progress and believes the players are fully aligned with a new style of play.

The ultimate ambition is to win trophies and take Forest back into Europe, after the club’s stirring return to Continental competition last season.

In Glasner’s office at home, there is a photograph of him lifting the Europa League trophy on a balcony in Frankfurt’s city centre, and those memories drive him on.

“At Palace we won their first ever trophy and it is always something that will stay for ever,” he says. “This is what I looked for when I considered the potential at Forest. Is it possible to write a chapter that stays in the history books of the club?

“I’m closer to the end of my career than the start and it is the memories that stay.

“I can’t do it alone, it will be with the players, staff and ownership, but that was the reason I came here. There is potential in the whole club, and an ambitious owner who wants to improve.”

Forest are intent on stability this season, and want Glasner to be their manager for many years. Last season they had four different head coaches and it was arguably a minor miracle that the club survived.

While Marinakis does have an unwanted “trigger-happy” reputation, he will argue that all those changes had to be made.

Nuno Espírito Santo effectively talked himself out of a job, following the spectacular breakdown in his relationship with former global head of football Edu Gaspar.

Ange Postecoglou’s appointment was a mistake and underlined by his exit after 39 days. Sean Dyche steadied the ship, but his methods irked the players.

Dyche recently claimed on Gary Neville’s podcast, The Overlap, that he never “lost the dressing room” – but why would Marinakis fly over from Athens for a rare midweek appearance unless there were serious concerns over the situation? Dyche was gone hours after the 0-0 draw with Wolves.

Vitor Pereira performed brilliantly but when Glasner gave Forest the green light on his availability it changed the club’s thinking.

Forest have been following Glasner’s career for four years, from his time at Frankfurt. A coach with a clear style of play, his teams are renowned for organisation, hard pressing and attacking fast and directly.

Glasner usually plays with a vertical 3-4-2-1, with wing-backs playing high. Without the ball they become a compact 5-4-1 and close the middle.

Over the international break, he experimented with a back four in a friendly against Swansea City. He believes that formation will offer flexibility to his team, and allow another attacking player to be named in the starting XI.

One of the primary reasons he accepted the job was the huge potential in the squad. Morgan Gibbs-White returned to the England squad over the break, while Glasner is convinced that £50m signing Liam Delap and James McAtee should be aiming to be included in Thomas Tuchel’s squad for Euro 2028.

Glasner’s track record and collection of medals provide clear evidence that, given time, he will over-achieve and deliver.

Palace are the most recent example of that. Supporters have enjoyed arguably the most successful period in their club’s history, and that is down to the 52-year-old who will be in the opposing technical area this weekend.
