Matthew Hayden rips cricket team over 'disappointing' act towards 557-wicket legend

TheTools · 05.11.2026 14:26 · source · permalink

The Australian cricket team continues to cop backlash after their series defeat.

Aussie cricket legend Matthew Hayden has called out the Australian cricket team's treatment of veteran Nathan Lyon after the South Africa series defeat. Lyon featured in the first and third Test matches against South Africa in the series, but was left out of the second match after Scott Boland was preferred in the bowling line-up.

Boland was the star for Australia in the second Test, despite the defeat, but was dropped for Lyon for the decider in Cape Town. Even though Australia brought Lyon back in with spin expected to play a huge role in Cape Town, captain Pat Cummins won the toss and elected to bowl.

This prompted plenty of backlash and it worked against Australia across the Test match. Australia were embarrassed on the final day as South African offspinner Simon Harmer took six wickets. South Africa won by 176 runs with Australia only managing 111 in the final innings.

There has been plenty of backlash to the series defeat with chief selector George Bailey and coach Andrew McDonald being called out for their decisions. Cummins has also come under pressure as captain following the decision to bowl first.

Matthew Hayden has been surprised veteran spinner Nathan Lyon (pictured) has bounced in and out of the Test side. (Photo by Marco Longari / AFP via Getty Images)

Now Aussie cricket great Hayden has jumped in to defend Lyon. The former opener admitted he is dumbfounded at the treatment of veteran in the side who has 'bounced around' even though he has taken 557 Test wickets.

"In Lyon's case the one thing that I've been really disappointed about from the architect's point of view is that they just keep bouncing him around," he said on SEN Radio. "The bloke's got 550 wickets for God's sake! He is the best (Aussie) off-spinner of all time, and he is sitting out in conditions which I still believe have a place for an off-spin bowler.

"Especially on a fifth-day wicket, which he didn't bowl on in Cape Town. So, it was a crazy decision in my opinion. I think it was a decision that was based on a number of other extenuating circumstances, which he won't say, but it can only be that, because there's no way that there was enough moisture in that wicket to be able to bat last on it."

Nathan Lyon struggles in Cape Town

Lyon did struggle to make the same inroads as Harmer in the third Test, but captain Cummins backed him after the series defeat. "I thought Nathan controlled the game really well," Cummins said.

"There are days where he goes and takes five or six wickets, but one of the big keys to why we love him on our side is that it makes everything else function well. For most of the game, they went at 2.5 (runs) an over. We were really controlling that, and Nathan was the key to that."
