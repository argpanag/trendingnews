Didn’t go down great’: Dolphins’ shock Mad Monday ‘beef’ as superstar’s looming exit revealed

TheTools · 29.10.2026 21:40 · source · permalink

The Dolphins have pulled off one huge retention coup, while another big-name star is reportedly headed for the exit after a Mad Monday incident that “didn’t go down great”.

Superstar centre Herbie Farnworth has consistently been linked with a marquee switch to the Roosters, with the rumblings only growing louder as November 1 emerges.

Farnworth is one of several big-name Dolphins stars who could hit the open market, with Hamiso Tabuai-Fidow and Jack Bostock also without contracts beyond the 2027 season.

According to reporter James Hooper, a group of Dolphins players were said to have fronted Farnworth during the club’s Mad Monday celebrations.

They reportedly pressed the English international on his future, with Hooper claiming he is a certainty to make the move to the powerhouse Tricolours club.

“Herbie Farnworth will sign with the Sydney Roosters once the November 1 deadline is out of the way,” Hooper said on the Rugby League Insider podcast.

“You can take that to the bank, it’s better than bank interest. Herbie will be at the Roosters at the latest in 2028, depending on what happens with the Dolphins once it becomes formalised.

“The reason I raise that is so on the Dolphins Mad Monday, I’ve heard there was beef with Herbie.

“A few of the players got into him and said ‘mate, what’s going on? The BYD ad, repeated speculation, you won’t commit. Are you going or not?’.

“The way I’ve heard it, it didn’t go down great... I don’t think he stuck around.”

Interestingly, Hooper also explained that Farnworth’s contract talks with the Dolphins have been continuously stalled as the free agency deadline nears.

“I’m watching Herbie and it comes to the finals and it was ‘I will leave it in the hands of my management’,” Hooper explained.

“Then in finals it was ‘no, I am just focused on the finals, I will worry about it once the finals are out of the way’.

“Then the finals are out of the way and now it’s ‘oh, I am in England camp for the World Cup, we will get the World Cup out of the way’.

“Well guess what, once the World Cup is out of the way, it’s going to be November 1... so we have bugger all time left.”

The Roosters’ war chest to sign marquee players has only grown in recent days, with the Tricolours making the decision to withdraw a multi-million dollar extension tabled to star halfback Sam Walker.

The 24-year-old’s contract talks were reportedly paused in August due to a disagreement on his salary before he informed his management he wanted to remain at the Roosters.

On Monday, Walker’s camp met with the club, who tabled a a three-year deal worth $1.2 million per-season, with ratchet clauses that could inflate his salary to $1.4 million.

After spending time to mull over the contract tabled, it was reported the Walker camp requested a slight upgrade, angering club supremo Nick Politis.

The club’s brutal contract call followed — and reports now suggest the premiership-winning halfback is at long odds to be a Roosters player in 2027.

However, as Hooper put it, the Roosters could benefit from Farnworth’s contract delays, while also not putting up with Walker who also put off making an immediate call.

“So it’s okay for Herbie to want more time and to get through to November 1, and then the Roosters can take advantage of that and sign the best centre in the competition,” Hooper said.

“But it’s not okay for Sam (Walker) to ask for more time? I can see why Nick (Politis) has taken the position that he has, and a big part of me admires and respects that.

“But I can also see Sam’s side too, I think he probably would have stayed... but the Roosters being the powerful club they are and the way they do like to run things with an iron fist.

“They have just gone ‘nah, you know what, you’ve had long enough’.”

As it stands, the PNG Chiefs loom as the obvious candidate to snap up Walker’s services beyond his current contract, armed with tax free salaries.

However, it was revealed on NRL360 on Wednesday that the star halfback isn’t certain he wants to relocate.

“I spoke to his uncle Shane Walker, who played a lot of NRL games, I am told that he said ‘Sam, this could set you up for life’,” The Courier Mail’s Pete Badel said.

“Like you could go there for three years, get four million tax-free and you never have to work ever again. I know there’s elements of his family that want him to go there.

“However, I’m hearing Sam personally doesn’t want to go there. Ideally, I think his preference is to stay at the Roosters, but it’s not going to happen on the money they’ve offered.”

While Farnworth may be headed for the exit, Tabuai-Fidow is reportedly set to re-sign with the 17th NRL club.

According to Code Sports, Tabuai-Fidow was initially expected to hit the open market until a new deal was struck in recent days to lock down his services.

The Origin star has previously failed to agree to the club’s contract offer but the Dolphins reportedly came back to the table to ensure they keep their superstar No.1 long-term.

Meanwhile, Bostock has been heavily linked with a move to the Dragons, with The Daily Telegraph’s David Riccio tipping the centre to depart the club in 2028.

TITANS’ SECURE RISING STAR

Less than five days before he hits the open market on November 1, highly-rated Gold Coast Titans halfback Zane Harrison is on the verge of confirming his long-term future.

Harrison is one of three halfbacks on the radar of the PNG Chiefs along with Nathan Cleary and Sam Walker but is set to shun the expansion side according to CODE Sports.

Instead, he is expected to ink a three-year extension to remain in Queensland until the conclusion of the 2030 season.

Seen as the club’s long-term No.7, Harrison made his debut during this year’s campaign before playing another ten matches until he went down with a serious hamstring injury.

The expected re-signing also comes after a lengthy contract saga, with reports emerging he could earn anywhere between $650,000 to $900,000 on his next deal.

“Zane wants to win premierships for this club,” the club’s football boss Scott Sattler told The Courier Mail last week.

“Zane is a Gold Coast junior and we would like to think we can offer him an opportunity in the coming years in an environment where he can continue to flourish.

“Zane knows what we think of him as a club. We want to continue helping him become the best player he can be.

“Josh (Hannay, head coach) and the coaching staff have come along at the perfect time for a player of his age.

“There’s a lot of good young halves coming through and Zane is no different. What’s impressive is Zane’s commitment to his craft.

“He is continually wanting to get better and he knows it takes a lot of hard work to make it in the NRL long-term.

“The one thing I know with Zane is he has the work ethic and discipline to succeed in the NRL.”

Retaining Harrison is a massive boost for the Titans, who will lose rookie forward Cooper Bai at the end of the 2027 season.

Rumours have also emerged he could depart earlier than expected.

The PNG Chiefs are the frontrunner to sign him with the Melbourne Storm and Sydney Roosters as options for Bai to link up with in the interim before the Chiefs enter the NRL.
