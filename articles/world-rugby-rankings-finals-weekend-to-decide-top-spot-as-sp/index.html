World Rugby rankings: Finals weekend to decide top spot as Springboks risk major drop, France a historic placing

TheTools · 27.11.2026 08:50 · source · permalink

World Rugby Rankings and an image of Springboks lock Eben Etzebeth.

The inaugural Nations Championship champion will be the number one ranked team in the World Rugby rankings.

South Africa and France will collide at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham on Sunday in the final and if the Springboks emerge victorious, they will end 2026 as the top-ranked team in the world, doing so for the fourth year in a row.

If Fabien Galthie’s charges recreate their efforts and beat South Africa as they did a fortnight ago, France will become the top-ranked team in the world for just the second time since World Rugby introduced the rankings. The last time they did so was back in July 2022, with Les Bleus holding onto that position for just one week.

A defeat of more than 15 points could see the Springboks end the year as low as fifth, but that would require Ireland beating Australia and New Zealand versus England ending in a stalemate.

There is plenty of movement possible in the top 10 of the rankings; with the help of World Rugby, let’s dive into the weekend’s permutations.

Weekend’s World Rugby rankings permutations

If France were to claim a victory by more than 15 points in the final, South Africa would drop to fourth if either England or New Zealand win in Saturday’s third-place final. The Springboks haven’t occupied fourth since August 2023.

A win of any kind would see South Africa retain top spot, ending the year in that position for the seventh time in the last eight years. France will drop to fourth if beaten and could also end the year in fifth if they lose by more than 15 points and the second-place final ends in a draw.

As for the All Blacks, second place is the highest that Dave Rennie’s men can finish the year if they overturn last week’s result, but that would require a France win over the Boks to be paired with an Australian win over Ireland.

New Zealand cannot fall any lower than their current equal-lowest ranking of all time of fifth even if they are beaten for a second weekend running by England. England can climb to second if they again win by more than 15 points, a position that they will cement regardless of the results elsewhere.

Andy Farrell’s men could fall as low as fifth if they lose by more than 15 points to the Wallabies on Friday evening but cannot improve on second place by beating Australia. They can drop a position to third even if they defeat Les Kiss’ charges.

As for Scotland, they will only gain just under a rating point for beating Argentina, which isn’t enough to lift them from sixth. However, they could fall as low as eighth with a loss by more than 15 points and a win for Australia over Ireland. Argentina could go as high as sixth with such a margin of victory.

Eddie Jones’ Japan could drop as low as 13th in defeat to Wales, who can’t climb from ninth place even if they beat the Brave Blossoms. Japan must beat Wales by more than 15 points to become the higher-ranked of the two nations and secure a return to the top 10.

Jake White’s Fiji will move above Wales into ninth if they defeat Italy for the second weekend running and the Welsh fail to beat Japan. A narrow Wales win means Fiji must defeat Italy by more than 15 points for the same outcome.

The Azzurri must win by more than 15 points to return to the top 10 and be the higher-ranked of the two nations – defeat could see Italy drop to 13th.
