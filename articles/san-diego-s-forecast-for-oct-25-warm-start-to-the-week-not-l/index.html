San Diego's Forecast for Oct. 25: Warm start to the week, not lasting long

SAN DIEGO (KGTV) —

We had a clear, sunny day today in San Diego, with the start of our Coastal Flood Warning. 

Unusually high tides are predicted- just Sunday morning, NOAA saw the morning high tide top 8 feet!

Monday is expected to be the warmest day of the week, with afternoon temperatures climbing into the upper 70s along the coast and low 80s across some inland communities.

Despite the pleasant weather, the National Weather Service has issued a Coastal Flood Warning for the entire San Diego County coastline through 1 p.m. Thursday.

Forecasters are tracking exceptionally high tides that could potentially reach near-record levels.

Ocean water levels are expected to run approximately 1 to 1.7 feet above astronomical tide predictions.

While large waves are not expected, the unusually high water levels could allow even smaller waves to push farther inland.

Significant coastal flooding is possible in low-lying beach communities, with some roads, parking lots, walkways and properties at risk of flooding. Shoreline erosion is also possible.

The greatest flooding concerns will occur during the morning high tides each day through Thursday.

Residents and visitors are encouraged to avoid flooded areas and never drive through standing water of unknown depth.

Temperatures begin trending down by Tuesday and Wednesday.

The marine layer is also expected to return, bringing areas of low clouds and patchy fog to coastal communities, especially Tuesday night into Wednesday morning.

Looking ahead to Halloweekend, you might want to fit an umbrella in with your costume.

A developing low-pressure system over the Pacific Northwest will help bring cooler temperatures, increasing clouds and breezier conditions to Southern California beginning Thursday.

Rain chances could return as early as late Thursday night into Friday, although the timing remains uncertain.

Forecast models are also tracking another disturbance carrying subtropical moisture over the Pacific.

If these two systems interact, San Diego could experience a wetter Halloween weekend. However, if they remain separated, rainfall could be limited to isolated light showers.

While confidence is increasing in cooler weather and at least some potential for precipitation, it is still too early to determine exact rainfall totals or which communities will see the most rain.

Anyone planning outdoor Halloween activities should continue monitoring the forecast as the week progresses.

Monday's Highs: 
Coast: 77-81°
Inland: 79-82°
Mountains: 67-75°
Deserts: 84-89°
