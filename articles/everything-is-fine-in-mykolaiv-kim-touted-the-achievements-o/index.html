"Everything is fine in Mykolaiv": Kim touted the achievements of his administration and explained why he left

TheTools · 16.11.2026 09:28 · source · permalink

He expected criticism, but considered this work necessary

  • Українською
  • російською

Ukraine’s Minister for Veterans Affairs, Vitaliy Kim, who previously headed the Mykolaiv Regional Military Administration, agreed to take the new post despite having established a smooth operation in the region and knowing he would face criticism. In an interview with RBC-Ukraine, he explained his motivation and shared his thoughts on whether the head of the ministry must be a veteran.

“For me, this is a matter of social responsibility. Everything is good in Mykolaiv. As they say, people remember my leadership: the guy was in charge, did everything right, things worked out, and people love and respect him. Why would he need this?” Vitaliy Kim explained.

“For me, this is a matter of social responsibility. Everything is good in Mykolaiv. As they say, people remember my leadership: the guy was in charge, did everything right, things worked out, and people love and respect him. Why would he need this?” Vitaliy Kim explained.

He does not see his new appointment as a career promotion. Instead, he describes it as difficult work that does not guarantee public approval. According to the minister, he knew he could face criticism but believed the job needed to be done. He called his decision to head the ministry a personal step in the interests of the state, not his own.

As Kim noted, he gained experience working on veterans’ policy while heading Mykolaiv Region. At the same time, he understands the position of those who believe the Ministry of Veterans Affairs of Ukraine should be headed by someone who has served in the military and holds veteran status.

“I wouldn’t agree that the minister necessarily has to be a veteran. Of the six people on my team, four are veterans,” Kim said.

“I wouldn’t agree that the minister necessarily has to be a veteran. Of the six people on my team, four are veterans,” Kim said.

He believes that a leader’s ability to manage the relevant field and work with subject-matter experts should be the deciding factors in an appointment. The minister also explained that the situation facing veterans matters to him, which is why he accepted the offer to head the ministry.

At the same time, Kim cautioned against expecting every problem in veterans’ policy to be solved. Even successfully addressing most issues, he said, would not mean that none remain unresolved.
