Frontcourt depth powers Gilas Pilipinas win over Syria ahead of Iran showdown

TheTools · 27.11.2026 17:57 · source · permalink

Gilas Pilipinas has spent much of the FIBA Basketball World Cup qualifying cycle trying to establish an identity around its size.

Against Syria on Friday night, it finally got a chance to show what that size can look like when the pieces begin to complement one another.

Philippines overwhelmed Syria 97-58 at Karisma Arena in Seremban, Malaysia, extending its winning streak to three games in the Asian qualifiers for the 2027 World Cup.

What began as an 18-18 game after the opening quarter turned into a mismatch, with Gilas imposing its advantage on the glass with a 60-30 rebounding advantage, finding its perimeter rhythm with 14 made triples and steadily taking away Syria's opportunities to stay competitive.

More importantly, the victory lifted Philippines to 5-4 in Group E, putting it in a three-way tie for third with Iran and Jordan. With a full complement of his bigs for this window, it offered a first look at how Tim Cone can use a frontcourt with considerably more size and skill than most opponents can comfortably match.

Kai Sotto lorded inside the paint

Syria had a problem it could not solve: Kai Sotto was simply too big, too skilled and too comfortable around the basket to be handled in single coverage.

The 7-foot-3 center finished with 18 points and 10 rebounds in just 17 minutes and 40 seconds, consistently taking advantage of the space afforded to him inside.

Whenever Gilas got him the ball in advantageous positions, Syria had very few answers. Leaving him with one defender was practically an invitation to give up a basket, while sending additional help risked opening up opportunities elsewhere on the floor.

That is the luxury Sotto provides because Gilas had the luxury of just sending the entry pass to him at the post rather than going through the whole motion of the triangle offense.

Establishing a deep position, sealing his defender and demanding attention around the rim already puts opposing defenses in difficult situations. His touch around the basket makes those opportunities even harder to defend, which ended up at 77.8% from the field.

There is also a cascading effect. When the defense collapses toward Sotto, Gilas can generate cleaner perimeter looks and create driving lanes for its guards. When opponents stay home on shooters, Sotto gets the opportunity to go to work against an isolated defender.

Against Syria, neither option was particularly appealing.

Boatwright adds another dimension to Cone's triangle offense

Bennie Boatwright's 16 points and 13 rebounds were impressive enough for a debut, but his usage offered a more interesting glimpse into what he can become within Cone's system.

He was not simply stationed around the basket waiting for post touches.

Boatwright received opportunities as a pick-and-roll ball-handler coming off dribble handoffs, giving Gilas another way to initiate offense through its bigger players. That is an interesting wrinkle for a frontcourt player with his combination of size and perimeter skill.

The handoff actions allowed the former San Miguel Beermen import to catch the ball on the move, read the defense, and attack before it could fully establish its coverage. Rather than functioning exclusively as a screener or finisher, he could become the decision-maker himself, forcing defenders to account for his ability to turn the corner, create an advantage or make the next pass.

His touches at the pinch post were particularly interesting in the context of Cone's triangle offense. The pinch post gives a skilled big man an opportunity to become a facilitator, with different options available through handoffs, cuts, backdoor actions and exchanges around the perimeter.

"I just think the more Bennie is with us the more he'll get comfortable and know where his teammates are, know where they like to get the ball, and we'll know more about him as we go along," Cone said after the game.

The 30-year-old's ability to operate from that area also makes it easier to play him alongside Gilas' other bigs.

He does not necessarily need to occupy the same spaces as Sotto, AJ Edu and Quentin Millora-Brown. Instead, he can serve as a shot creator and offensive hub from the elbow while his teammates establish position closer to the basket or move through the weak side.

Millora-Brown might not have produced the kind of box-score numbers that immediately jump off the page with just four points, six rebounds, and three assists, but his contribution was easier to appreciate by watching how Gilas operated whenever he was involved in the action.

The offensive intelligence was particularly useful in initiating dribble handoffs.

'QMB' understood when to become a facilitator, where to position himself in the triangle and how to create opportunities for teammates through relatively simple actions. He did not need to dominate the ball to help the offense function. Sometimes, setting the right screen, delivering the handoff at the right angle or recognizing a defensive opening was enough to generate an advantage.

His defensive work was just as interesting.

Millora-Brown showed good awareness in hedging against ball screens, stepping up to disrupt the ball-handler before recovering toward his original assignment, in particular against Syria's naturalized player D'Angelo Harrison, who just finished with 10 points on 20% shooting.

The timing of those rotations matters. A big who commits too aggressively can leave the roller open, while one who retreats too quickly gives the opposing guard an uncontested path to the basket.

It is the kind of contribution that does not always translate into points, rebounds or assists. But having a big who understands offensive spacing, can initiate handoffs and knows how to execute defensive coverages is valuable, particularly when playing alongside more prominent frontcourt scorers.

Iran will be a much sterner test for Gilas

The 39-point demolition of Syria was an encouraging showcase of Gilas' size, but Iran should offer stiffer competition.

Philippines faces Iran on Sunday, Nov. 29, at 5 p.m. (Philippine time) at Karisma Arena in as well. Both teams are tied at 5-4 after Iran fell to Australia 84-67 in its opening assignment of the fifth window. The winner will have an opportunity to strengthen its position in the race for the top three spots in Group E.

Gilas already has a reference point against Iran, having beaten Team Melli 68-56 in their previous meeting in the third window. But Cone is not expecting a repeat of that performance to come easily due to the return of Benham Yakchali for Iran.

"They have Yakchali and he changes the game for them. He's a tremendous player, tremendous shooter, and another ball-handler,"

"We haven't played against him, so we have to account for him as well."

Yakhchali's return, however, was far quieter than the reputation he brought into the window. The veteran guard had averaged 16.5 points and 5.5 assists in his previous qualifying appearances, but against Australia he played 25 minutes and recorded just three points on 1-of-8 shooting.

Iran did have two reliable scoring outlets in Mohammad Amini and Matin Aghajanpour, who finished with 15 points apiece. But ultimately, the team had not enough scoring creation to manufacture efficient offense, as they only shot 34.4% from the field.

It is expected that Yakchali will remain on top of Gilas' scouting report, as he remains a player who can change the geometry of Iran's offense with his pull-up shooting and ability to initiate the pick-and-roll -- but his performance against Australia showed that Philippines cannot simply assume he will dictate the game.

Scottie Thompson, Dwight Ramos and the rest of Gilas' perimeter defenders will have to make him work for his touches, chase him over screens and keep him from comfortably getting into his pull-up game.

Gilas should not mistake Iran's loss for a lack of threat. Amini and Aghajanpour showed they can shoulder the scoring, and the team forced Australia into 17 turnovers despite losing the possession battle on the glass.

But the result exposed how much Iran needs its perimeter shooting and Yakhchali's scoring to function together.

With both teams entering Sunday's matchup with the same record after Friday's results, Philippines has an opportunity to pressure that backcourt, control the defensive boards and make Iran prove it can generate offense without relying on its veteran guard to rescue every possession.
