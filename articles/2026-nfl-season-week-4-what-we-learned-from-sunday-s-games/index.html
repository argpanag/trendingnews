Jeremy Bergman's takeaways:

  1. Taylor, Colts come through, eventually. Indianapolis looked jet-lagged to kick off its Week 4 trip with London, with Daniel Jones committing a silly fumble on the second play from scrimmage and throwing an ugly interception later in the first half, giving him seven turnovers on the season. But the Colts eventually settled into Sunday's game on the legs of the ever-reliable Jonathan Taylor and overwhelmed the beat-up Commanders to move to an even 2-2. Jones looked rattled under pressure early, throwing off his back foot and dirting more than one busted attempt. His turnover issues persist, and down Alec Pierce and Keenan Allen, he struggled to develop a rapport with any target. But as the Colts fed Taylor, the whole offense took a breath. The running back gained 95 yards and two touchdowns on the ground on Sunday, and now has the most rushing yards in international game history (408). With 20 more carries against Washington, Taylor still paces the NFL in the category with 86 on the season entering Sunday's later slate. The way the Colts have moved the ball through the air through four weeks (poorly), Taylor figures to continue shouldering the load going forward. Overall, Indy hasn't proven it belongs among the cream of the AFC crop, but after two straight wins, the Colts are in the running for the AFC South lead, staying afloat despite not playing their best ball. That's enough in early fall.
  2. Limping out of London. It was a week of carnage for the Washington Commanders. With Jayden Daniels and Rachaad White already sidelined by injuries, Terry McLaurin was a surprise inactive early Sunday after incurring a hamstring injury in practice. Then, less than a quarter in against Indy and after two red-zone drives that ended in field goals, Marcus Mariota, Daniels' backup, aggravated an MCL injury on a sack and was replaced under center by seventh-round rookie Athan Kaliakmanis. The former Rutgers signal-caller was boom or bust for an unrecognizable Commanders offense. In the span of four minutes in the third quarter, he threw a terrible pick in Washington's territory to set up a Colts touchdown, and then launched a shocking 47-yard bomb against Sauce Gardner, resulting in a TD pass to Treylon Burks. A drive later, Kaliakmanis completed another 48-yard pass, this time to Dyami Brown. The rookie showed moxie and fearlessness (18.2% of his attempts were deep plays -- 20-plus yards), but he ultimately was too inefficient to get the job done (13-of-31 for 91 yards save for the two big plays). Kaliakmanis' 9-yard sack on a blind-side nickel blitz, knocking D.C. out of field goal territory, illustrated his inexperience. Not helping matters, Jacory Croskey-Merritt and Austin Ekeler were non-factors on the ground (44 yards on 13 combined rushes). Washington saw some improved play from Daronte Jones' defense, which lost Charles Omenihu to a groin injury, in the first half, but that unit cratered down the stretch, allowing four of Indy's five second-half drives to end in points. After last week's galvanizing upset of Seattle, Washington can only see this trip as a missed opportunity. The good news is its season will begin again when Daniels returns, which could come in Week 5 against the Giants.
  3. Sauce lost? Indy's major trade acquisition during the 2025 season did not pay dividends on Sunday. Sauce Gardner, whom the Colts acquired from the Jets last year for two first-round picks, was picked on twice for big gains by none other than … Athan Kaliakmanis? Gardner was burned by Treylon Burks down the sideline for a 47-yard TD that cut Indy's second-half lead to four points. On his very next play, Gardner was beat across the field by Dyami Brown for a 48-yard gainer. The following quarter, Gardner kept a drive alive with a defensive holding penalty. All in all, not the best day on the pitch for Indy's supposed lockdown CB, especially against an unproven QB and worn-down receiving corps. Gardner surrendered four receptions for 117 yards (his most allowed in his career) on five targets, good for a woeful 20% coverage success rate. The Colts need more from the former first-round pick to justify surrendering such compensation for him.

Next Gen Insight for Colts-Commanders (via NFL Pro): When using play-action, Daniel Jones was 4-of-9 passing for 15 yards, averaging 1.7 yards per attempt, tied for the lowest mark in a game in his career (min. five play-action dropbacks). Three of Jones' four lowest play-action yards per attempt games have come against Washington (1.7 in 2020, 1.7 on Sunday, 2.2 in 2019).

NFL Research: Sunday's game was Jonathan Taylor’s second career international game with multiple touchdowns scored, making him the first player in NFL history to have multiple such games. Taylor previously had three touchdowns for the Colts in Berlin in 2025.

Bobby Kownack's takeaways:

  1. Ravens hold serve after Jackson’s injury. The Ravens had a blowout brewing heading into halftime after a magnificent 64-yard field goal by Tyler Loop pushed the score to 24-7. Lamar Jackson was sporting a 144.2 passer rating with 222 yards and two touchdowns on 15-of-20 passing, while Zay Flowers looked fully back from his hamstring injury, going for 100-plus receiving yards and a score in a first half for the second time this season. The team had already totaled 268 total yards heading into the break. However, Jackson sustained an ankle injury on the play right before Loop’s kick, getting rolled up on by Jermaine Johnson II as the defensive end sacked him. Tyler Huntley took Jackson’s place for the final two frames, and Baltimore wouldn’t score again. Huntley went 8 of 9 for 63 yards, but the offense lacked explosiveness and consistency, instead counting on Anthony Weaver’s defense to keep the Titans at bay. Luckily, the D did, giving Derrick Henry (23 carries for 73 yards and a TD) revenge over his former team. Jackson is believed to be day to day, according to NFL Network Insider Ian Rapoport, but his status will bear watching after how quiet the Ravens went without him.
  2. Ward, Tate show chemistry but must mature quickly. Carnell Tate exploded for the best statistical outing of his young career, collecting nine receptions for 145 yards while serving as Cam Ward’s clear No. 1 target. So obvious was Ward’s affinity for Tate that no other Titans pass catcher had more than one reception entering the fourth quarter. It was promising to see the two hook up for crucial gains with such regularity, but both players also made a number of mental mistakes that must be eliminated moving forward. On the second play of the game, Ward shoveled the ball toward Tony Pollard rather than taking a sack, only for the pigskin to slip through the running back’s hands and into linebacker Trenton Simpson’s. Pollard should likely have made the catch, but Ward still needs to learn he’s sometimes better off eating it on such backyard plays. Case in point, the second-year quarterback later had zero chance to pick up a first down while scrambling on a third-and-10 near the mouth of his own end zone. Surrounded by defenders, he inexplicably pitched it to Tyjae Spears (also with a wall of Ravens in front of him), who had to bat the ball out of bounds. Tate also fumbled trying to pitch to Spears, a little lazily missing the mark far in front of the running back on a hook-and-lateral, and he was stripped for a more traditional fumble at the end of a 33-yard gain in the final minute of the second quarter. Ward and Tate are going through growing pains, but this was also their finest showing together yet.
  3. Ravens defense helps deliver win. It got a little hairy at the end, with Tennessee going 70 yards on seven plays for a touchdown and subsequent two-point conversion to pull within six as 1:12 remained, but by that point Baltimore’s defense had saved the day multiple times. It only made sense the group would eventually break a little, and the Ravens recovered the ensuing onside kick anyway. Baltimore’s defensive unit did an excellent job controlling momentum throughout Sunday’s contest. Trenton Simpson’s interception on the second play of the game set up Baltimore’s opening five-play, 37-yard touchdown drive. Later, in the second quarter, the Ravens made a goal-line stand to prevent Tennessee from knotting the score at 14. Nnamdi Madubuike, in his second game back from last year’s season-ending neck injury, was the hero in that instance, stonewalling Tony Pollard’s wildcat run. That again shifted the tenor of the game, as Lamar Jackson led an impressive 99-yard TD drive to extend the lead. Following Jackson’s departure, Baltimore steeled itself against a Titans offense looking to take advantage during the second half. A first-drive field goal and the touchdown toward the game’s end was all Tennessee would muster. The Ravens held the Titans to 9 yards on their other three second-half possessions -- all three-and-outs -- to compensate for a Baltimore offense suddenly unable to find points.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Titans-Ravens (via NFL Pro): Jeffery Simmons totaled only one pressure on his 32 pass rushes (3.1%), his lowest pressure rate in a game since Week 7, 2025.

NFL Research: Zay Flowers led the Ravens with 118 receiving yards, setting a new Ravens franchise record with his 11th career game with at least 100 receiving yards. His 352 receiving yards so far in 2026 surpassed Steve Smith Sr. in 2015 for the most in a player’s first three games of a season in team history.

Kevin Patra's takeaways:

  1. Patriots pick up massive win in Buffalo. Mike Vrabel’s squad stared down a 1-3 start, but Drake Maye and Co. responded late to pick up a big road win against Josh Allen and the Bills, staying in striking distance at 2-2. It wasn’t a perfect game for Maye, but the maligned quarterback made key plays late to pull off the fourth-quarter comeback. Despite two first-half turnovers, Maye led the Pats on several long scoring drives, while passing for 269 yards on 22-of-37 passing with three touchdowns. The QB also led the Pats with 54 rushing yards on eight scrambles. Maye again missed some throws, had a fumble flop out of his hand when he should have eaten a sack, and was a tad off the mark on an interception. However, he looked more like the QB who made plays last season. His late TD toss was on the money for a ridiculous one-handed Efton Chism III catch to give the Pats the lead late. After the defense forced an Allen INT, New England showed its trust in Maye, calling two pass plays instead of running into the teeth of the D. Maye missed on the first, but hit Hunter Henry for a game-sealing catch-and-run first down to keep the ball away from Buffalo. It was a huge building-block game for Maye and the Pats, who kept themselves in the AFC East race.
  2. Bills' defense continues to struggle. First, let’s point out Keon Coleman's big game. After D.J. Moore exited with an injury, Coleman stepped up. He made an acrobatic tumbling catch in traffic, then got open in the end zone for a big-time TD. Coleman put up 116 yards and a score, the third 100-yard game of his career. Josh Allen’s forced INT late was the type of mistake that has plagued the QB in the past. It was a heave he didn’t need to make with time to at least get into game-tying field goal range. The big issue in Buffalo, however, is Jim Leonard’s defense continuing to get beaten up. It’s a roller-coaster ride for this crew. The Bills generated two turnovers, and Gregory Rousseau is a menace, but Buffalo also forced just two three-and-outs and allowed a Pats offense that has spun its wheels this season to speed for 26 first downs and 405 yards, including four drives of eight or more plays. With a chance to secure the win in the fourth quarter, the D couldn’t get off the field.
  3. Doubs authors best game for New England. Efton Chism made the highlight-reel score, but Romeo Doubs, who has struggled with drops early in his first year with the Pats, made the most of his opportunities. Doubs caught six passes for 58 yards and two TDs, including a nifty one-hander on a fade. The WR did pop up a pass that wound up an INT, but it was a little in front of him, and he was trying to make a play. Sometimes flukes happen. Sunday’s performance was more of what was expected when New England signed him in free agency. Doubs generated separation and was used on more than downfield routes this week. Only one of his nine targets was a tight window throw, and he got open by three yards or more on four plays, per Next Gen Stats.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Patriots-Bills: (via NFL Pro): The Patriots used motion on 75.3% of their plays against the Bills, their highest rate in a game since Week 15 of 2023, after using motion on 56.1% of their plays in Weeks 1-3. New England posted a 49.3% success rate, its highest in a game this season.

NFL Research: The Patriots had season highs with 29 points and 405 total yards in their Week 4 win over the Bills. New England had 26 points in its previous two games combined. The Patriots committed two turnovers in Sunday's win and are 7-4 in games with multiple turnovers since 2025, the most such wins in the NFL.

Dan Parr's takeaways:

  1. Bagent has his moment. Ben Johnson was mum on who would start at quarterback in his public comments leading up to Sunday. He let the intrigue linger through the first play of the game, when Case Keenum and Tyson Bagent took the field, but Keenum’s day was done after he made the first handoff. Bagent threw for a career-high 268 passing yards in his first start in nearly three years, filling in for the injured Caleb Williams. It was a strong showing from the fourth-year player, who did not play last week after spending time in the concussion protocol. Bagent’s lone blemish was throwing behind tight end Colston Loveland on a pass that was picked off by the Jets. He benefited from the dominant showing by the running back duo of D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai, who combined for 204 of the team’s 232 rushing yards. Monangai ran a career-high 30 times for 146 yards and two touchdowns, while Swift had 15 carries for 58 yards and lost a fumble. The Bears simply wore out the Jets, running 89 plays to the Jets’ 32, and did a great job of putting Bagent in manageable situations. Johnson has now led the Bears to wins with different starting quarterbacks in the first four weeks of the season, winning by double-digits with each passer. That’s a statement by the head coach.
  2. Jets snap ignominious streak. While the final score might not show it, New York’s defense held up pretty well on Sunday, holding the Bears to two touchdowns on seven trips to the red zone. Notably, the Jets ended their streak of games without an interception at 20 when Minkah Fitzpatrick picked off a Tyson Bagent pass in the second quarter. In 2025, Aaron Glenn’s crew became the first team to go a full season without an INT since the statistic was first tracked in 1933. Unfortunately, that play was one of the few highlights for Gang Green in Week 4. The offense was down some playmakers and it showed. The Jets were 0 for 7 on third down and had more penalty yards (40) than net yards gained (34) in the first half. There’s a lot to clean up heading into next week’s home game against the suddenly red-hot Cleveland Browns, who have won three in a row.
  3. Bears' defense standing firm. There was plenty of concern about whether Chicago had done enough to improve its defense in the offseason, but through four games, that chatter is mostly quiet. The Jets could muster just 157 yards on Sunday, one week after racking up 335 against the Detroit Lions. Dennis Allen’s D has now held teams to 12 points or fewer in each of the last three games. The Bears have allowed just two TDs on the opposition’s last 27 drives. The trademark of Allen’s defense in Chicago has been taking the ball away, but the Bears managed to shut New York down without forcing a single turnover on Sunday. It certainly helps that the Jets held the ball for just 17:15, compared with 42:45 for the Bears.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Jets-Bears (via NFL Pro): Tyson Bagent completed 25 of 34 passes for 268 yards and an interception on Sunday. Bagent completed 21 of 23 attempts from a clean pocket, for 202 yards (+ 17.3% completion percentage over expected), compared to just 4 of 11 attempts for 66 yards and an interception when pressured.

NFL Research: The Bears outgained the Jets, 492-157, on Sunday. That +335 total yards margin is the best for Chicago since Week 16 of the 1979 season in a 42-6 victory over the St. Louis Cardinals (+343).

Nick Shook's takeaways:

  1. ‎Jaguars win despite sloppy performance. Jacksonville got off to a horrendous start in the details department Sunday, committing seven penalties for 70 yards in the first 17:56 of the game. It reached halftime with 10 penalties committed for 100 yards, an outrageous total for a team with Super Bowl aspirations. Somehow, the Jaguars managed to endure their own mistakes and take a 13-3 lead into halftime. Fortunately, the Jaguars avoided the most destructive mistakes, going without a turnover while forcing two takeaways (two Joe Burrow interceptions) and turning one of them into a touchdown, but as Liam Coen likely knows, these errors took points off the board and allowed the Bengals to stay within striking distance throughout the afternoon. If Jacksonville is going to realize its potential, the errors have to be cleaned up soon. The Jaguars were lucky that Cincinnati committed about as many mistakes that cost the Bengals points, too.
  2. Burrow becomes a roller coaster. It's not often Joe Burrow negatively affects his team's chances of victory with his own actions, but it happened for a second straight week. After fumbling away Cincinnati's last hope of winning in Pittsburgh last week, Burrow made two mistakes that heavily influenced Sunday's outcome, throwing an interception in Cincinnati territory that gave the Jaguars a short field and led to a first-half touchdown. The second was even more damaging, as Burrow rolled to his right and attempted to force a pass under duress to Mitchell Tinsley, instead delivering it to the arms of linebacker Foye Oluokun in a fashion fitting for Christmas, not early October. That mistake ended a Bengals drive that had reached Jacksonville's 10-yard line and appeared destined to end in a go-ahead touchdown. In defense of Burrow, he lost Ja'Marr Chase to a concussion in the first half, taking away his most trusted and productive target and forcing the Bengals to shift their approach. But the mixed results -- Burrow leaned heavily on Dohnte Meyers and Tee Higgins to consistently move the ball -- left the Bengals with minimal time to finally find their stride and mount a comeback attempt, which ultimately fell short.
  3. Jacksonville has established an offensive identity. Through four weeks, the Jaguars are 3-1 and are producing at one of the most predictably successful rates of any team in the NFL. Running back Bhayshul Tuten once again paced the rushing attack, finishing with 73 yards on 17 attempts (after two touchdown runs were called back by holding penalties). Chris Rodriguez Jr. served as the timely spell back (six carries, 35 yards and a fourth-quarter rushing score) and Lawrence handled the rest, completing the majority of his 23 attempts for 227 yards and a first-half touchdown. While the numbers weren't particularly explosive, Jacksonville is playing complementary football that relies on a defense that is better than its reputation suggests, and an offense predicated by Trevor Lawrence's composed leadership and reliable execution. It's not tracking toward the history books, but for yet another week, the formula remained the same: Establish a credible running threat with Tuten, play quality defense and let Lawrence work. It was good enough to secure another win Sunday.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Jaguars-Bengals (via NFL Pro):  Joe Burrow was pressured 20 times (34.5% of dropbacks) against the Jaguars, the most in a game since Week 11 of the 2024 season (Chargers, 24). 

NFL Research: The Jaguars had two takeaways (both interceptions of Joe Burrow) in their Week 4 win at the Bengals. It’s their 13th game with multiple takeaways since the start of the 2025 season, most in the NFL. They have forced 39 total takeaways over the last two seasons, tied with the Bears for the most in the NFL over that span.

Kevin Patra's takeaways:

  1. CeeDee explodes in Cowboys win. The Dallas offense sputtered in the first half, unable to punch it into the end zone. Then CeeDee Lamb took over. The star wideout generated a whopping 17 catches for 189 yards and the game-deciding touchdown – 11/140/1 coming in the final two quarters. Dallas found pay dirt on four of its five drives in the second half, turning the Texans' secondary into mincemeat. No one could cover Lamb, who particularly tortured Kamari Lassiter. Dak Prescott targeted Lamb on 21 of 45 routes, the most targets by any receiver to run 45 or fewer routes in a game during the Next Gen Stats era (since 2016). The Cowboys moved him around the formation. Lamb totaled at least seven receptions and 60 yards when aligned out wide (10 receptions, 128 yards) and in the slot (seven receptions, 61 yards, TD), per NGS. Prescott struggled in the pocket through two quarters, including going 0 for 4 under pressure. However, he was much more comfortable in the second half, dicing up the Texans by seeking out Lamb on every big chance.
  2. Texans' defense can’t get the stop it needed. Nico Collins’ return opened up the offense, with the star wideout going for seven catches for 117 yards and two scores. Collins brought big plays to an offense that lacked them and helped open things up for other receivers. Putting up 30 points should be enough to win games for Houston. Unfortunately, the Texans defense didn’t hold up its end late. DeMeco Ryans' D got tortured by CeeDee Lamb and allowed Javonte Williams to punch in three short-yardage TDs. The front pestered Prescott early, but as the game wore on, the secondary couldn’t hold up, leading to big plays for Dallas. A defense that entered the season looking like the best in the league has been average through four weeks. The Texans dug themselves out of a 0-3 hole last year. This year’s 0-4 record feels miles deeper.
  3. Cowboys D still has strides to make after trade. Joey Porter Jr. played 24 of 52 defensive snaps, per Next Gen Stats, including 19 coverage snaps without allowing a reception on one target. Meanwhile, C.J. Stroud ate up DaRon Bland (104 yards, TD allowed) and Reddy Steward (98 yards). The defensive line couldn’t get pressure for long stretches, an issue that has plagued the D all season. Quinnen Williams did make several big plays in the second half, including a sack and a tackle for loss. Dallas allowed a Houston offense that has struggled this season to gain 397 yards and 7.6 yards per play, including eight pass plays of 20-plus yards. We’ll see whether Christian Parker uses Porter more as a traveler as he gets immersed in the new defense.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Cowboys-Texans (via NFL Pro): Dak Prescott completed 32 of 45 passes for 335 yards and a touchdown in the Cowboys' Week 4 win over the Texans, including five of seven for 116 yards against the blitz. He averaged 16.6 yards per attempt against the blitz, his most in a game since Week 4 of 2024 against the Giants (18.5 yards per attempt). Prescott was pressured just once when blitzed, tied for the Texans' lowest pressure total when blitzing in a game under head coach DeMeco Ryans. Prescott was pressured 13 times overall, taking three sacks and completing just three of nine passes for 14 yards.

Nick Shook's takeaways:

  1. Giants' defense delivers again. A week ago, the Giants appeared poised to win an ugly game amid a downpour in New York when Cam Ward and the Tennessee Titans suddenly found an offensive rhythm and moved into the Giants' red zone in the final minute. That comeback attempt ended with a Ward interception in the end zone, preserving New York's five-point lead and securing its second win of 2026. A similar script unfolded Sunday, with New York once again finding itself in the shadow of its own goal post in the game's final minute, needing a stop to emerge victorious. In that pressure-packed moment, the Giants' defense bowed up again, blanketing Jacoby Brissett's favorite targets (Trey McBride, Michael Wilson), overcoming a third-down defensive pass interference on Deonte Banks that set up the goal-to-go situation, and ending another visitor's comeback hopes with an interception. This time, it was Banks who sealed the win, denying Wilson's hopes of earning an inside release and snagging Brissett's forced pass, returning it 98 yards for a touchdown that mattered only to the final score. New York has plenty to clean up, but the Giants' improvement from one half to the next -- buoyed by a more aggressive blitz strategy -- carried them to another narrow victory. Against better opponents, it likely won't hold, but it's becoming a trend of which the Giants can be proud.
  2. Cardinals sputter in frustrating fashion. Arizona raced out to a 14-3 lead and seemed poised to run away from the Giants with a superior offense that was gashing New York's leaky defense. The key word there was seemed, though, because once New York awoke, Arizona never regained its early advantage. There were certainly opportunities. In a four-possession span, Arizona forced three turnovers (two interceptions and a fumble), but the fourth quarter proved to be nothing more than a bog for the Cardinals' offense, which failed to capitalize on a fumble forced by Mack Wilson, punted three times in the fourth and watched their final (and most promising) opportunity end with three goal line incompletions and a pick-six on fourth down to seal the defeat. Arizona will look back on this one with plenty of regret after letting a winnable game get away and failing to cash in on opportunities afforded by a defense that couldn't get a stop a week earlier. Hopefully rookie coach Mike LaFleur can apply these lessons going forward.
  3. New York receives full Jameis experience. There was legitimate pressure on Jameis Winston entering Sunday's game. After a low-output showing in a 12-7 win over Tennessee last week, New York's front office acquired 2024 first-rounder J.J. McCarthy via trade, supplying John Harbaugh with another option under center if Winston continued to struggle. Winston responded positively, leading four scoring drives in the Giants' first six possessions and delivering some magnificent passes to help the Giants pile up points. In true Winston fashion, though, it came with the typical trappings. Winston threw two remarkably ugly interceptions on consecutive second-half possessions, gifting Arizona two additional opportunities to score and ensuring this game would be close to the end. In the end, Winston did more than enough to help the Giants win, but also did his part to keep things interesting. It seems the combined total will keep his job safe for another week, thanks in large part to New York's goal line stand at the end.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Cardinals-Giants (via NFL Pro): Abdul Carter generated nine pressures on 37 pass rushes against the Cardinals in Week 4, the most pressures by Carter in a game in his career. Eight of Carter's nine pressures came within 2.5 seconds, the most quick pressures recorded in a game this season in part due to a 0.70-second pass rush get-off time (second-fastest in a game for Carter in his career).

NFL Research: The Cardinals and Giants combined for five interceptions and 60 points in Week 4. Jeremy Bergman's takeaways:

  1. Rams finally finish. Los Angeles thoroughly outplayed the banged-up Eagles at a soggy Linc on Sunday, but found itself down 10 points midway through the fourth quarter. Matthew Stafford interceptions, special teams snafus and fourth-down follies were to blame. But one week after blowing multiple leads against the Broncos, the Rams took control of the proceedings in eastern Pennsylvania and changed the narrative. After L.A. stuffed Philly at the 2-yard line as the Eagles attempted to go up three scores, Stafford led the Rams on their best drive of the day, finding Puka Nacua (who is absolutely back from injury, finishing with nine catches for 125 yards) and Tyler Higbee on the boundary to get L.A. into Philly territory. An Eagles late hit penalty and Kyren Williams TD later, the Rams were within striking distance. L.A. then stopped Philadelphia on their 11th straight third down of the day and got the ball back. The recipe was the same: Nacua and Higbee into Eagles territory, then Williams (147 total yards on 26 touches) to salt away the drive. The box score will say L.A. should have run away with this one (414 yards to 172), and perhaps Sean McVay's side should have. But the mettle shown against a franchise that has had their number has to be rewarding for the Rams, as they avoid a 1-3 start and keep pace with the rival 49ers and Seahawks in the NFC West. This team can finish -- at times, in spite of itself.
  2. Philly offense flops again. The Eagles' no-show against Chicago Monday night was apparently no fluke. They were even worse in Week 4. Philadelphia was sluggish from the start on Sunday, but benefitted from good field position, thanks to turnovers and Britain Covey, to get 20 points out of its seven first-half drives. Underneath that production was a hampered unit unable to convert in the crunch. The Eagles finished Sunday zero for 12 on third downs, their most attempts without a conversion since at least 1991, per NFL Research. On four fourth-down attempts, the Eagles converted only one; the other three turnovers-on-downs came at the end of the second half and allowed Los Angeles to get back in the game. All in all, Philly's 172 total yards were its fewest since Week 14, 2014. Jalen Hurts was without top targets DeVonta Smith and Dallas Goedert and failed to connect with any regularity with the remaining rag-tag group of pass catchers, throwing for just 93 yards, his fewest in a game he started and finished.
