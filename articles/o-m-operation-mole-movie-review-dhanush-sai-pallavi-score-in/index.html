A fearless RAW agent goes undercover in Pakistan’s brutal Lyari underworld to dismantle a gangster network... thus read the one-liner of Dhurandhar (2025). Replace Lyari with Red Hills in Chennai, and you almost arrive at the storyline of Dhanush’s latest film O.M: Operation Mole. Directed by Rajkumar Periasamy, who gave us the stirring Amaran, the film follows Thirukumaran (Dhanush), an undercover Special Task Officer.

O.M opens with purpose. Nithya Reddy (Sai Pallavi) gets a rousing introduction as an officer at the helm of things who is in pursuit of an illegal sandalwood smuggling racket, and, in particular, the infamous smuggler ‘Burma’ Karthikeyan aka Siya (Mammootty). The first hour or so breezes past, the highlight of which is the single-shot sequence that introduces the protagonist. When we first see Thiru, he is shooting someone, not with a gun, but with a camera. Cops from Andhra Pradesh have arrived to arrest Karthikeyan, but Thiru would have none of it.

What follows is a fascinatingly shot sequence as he galvanises the whole area against the police. The camera, helmed by Ezhil Arasu, moves restlessly, almost breathlessly, becoming a character in itself as it weaves its way through a sea of people and traffic.

We’re in for a riveting watch, we think.

Mammootty in a still from ‘O.M Operation Mole’ | Photo Credit: Special Arrangement

But O.M quickly slides into predictable territory, as we keep getting bits of information from various quarters. A police sequence in Andhra Pradesh is followed by a video call to France, which is succeeded by a sequence in Javvadhu Hills in Tamil Nadu. The editing resembles what we generally see in films of director Hari – fast, quick cuts – which generally works when you want to cram in a lot of information. But with multiple characters, incidents and such quick editing, O.M is too racy for its own good.

I wish the writing choices were not so convenient. It’s almost like Rajkumar takes us for granted; pray, what explains the lengthy scene involving a major reveal where smuggler Karthikeyan literally does a house tour for Thiru? Or the one where Nithya sneaks in to have a chat with Thiru to discuss something that could have easily been done over the telephone?

This is not to say that O.M is a dampener all the way. There are powerful performances to root for, and with a star cast that boasts multiple National Award-winning acts, we get a full platter. Watch out for Dhanush at the rather emotional pre-intermission point; the man surely knows a thing or two about how to bawl and bare his heart out, as much as he knows how to shake a leg (‘Alaakaa Loova’, ufff). Watch out for howSai Pallavi pulls off all those tough jail sequences in the second half. I wish someone would write a full-fledged actioner featuring her soon. And what about Mammootty, whom we last saw in Tamil cinema in Peranbu? As Karthikeyan, he puts on a solid show, but whether an actor of his calibre was needed for it in the first place is a question that needs to be posed to the makers.

While the filmfeatures a galaxy of established names in the star cast, including Indrans and Naseeruddin Shah, it also has a relatively new entrant in its technical team: music composer Sai Abhyankkar. While his scores here do not match up to his recent work in Baththa and Karuppu, he does provide the mass sequences with a rather unique flavour, with tracks like ‘Veezha Veera’. The lovely ‘Senjudare’ gets wasted due to the fast-paced editing, but ‘Alaakaa Loova’ is bound to have theatre-going audiences on their feet.

Sai Pallavi in a still from ‘O.M Operation Mole’ | Photo Credit: Special Arrangement

I never understood a few things about O.M. Why give the protagonist a family backstory when the limited screen time cannot do justice to it? Why feature a smuggler who is so gullible when it comes to believing people? What do you say about an international smuggling kingpin based in Dubai who wouldn’t even bother to rummage the visitors’ pant pockets for recording devices? Overall, watching the 178-minute film, one gets the feeling the makers shot far more than required and ended up with so much content that they didn’t know what to retain and what to axe.

O.M needed more scenes that could breathe rather than huff and puff, and more heft in its interpersonal relationships. All it would have taken was a couple of solid sequences that allowed Dhanush to build a relationship with Mammootty or, heck, even an intense face-off that would have made us go ‘frame-ah paarunga ji’. That never happens, and for that, O.M will be a missed opportunity for Rajkumar Periasamy and team.

O.M is currently running in theatres
