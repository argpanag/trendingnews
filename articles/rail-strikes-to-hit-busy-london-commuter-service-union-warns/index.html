Rail strikes to hit busy London commuter service, union warns

Strikes are set to take place on a busy London commuter service, a union is warning.

The Transport Salaried Staffs’ Association (TSSA) said the industrial action is “inevitable” unless rail chiefs make concessions in a row over pay and working arrangements.

It warned that the walkouts would have a “real impact” on services to and from London.

The threat of significant disruption is on Great Western Railway (GWR) services after staff backed strike action in a dispute over flexible working arrangements, pay and pensions.

TSSA members who are overwhelmingly supporting the industrial action work in areas including rostering, service delivery and performance strategy.

Crucially, some of these staff play a key role in the location of train crew to ensure services can run as planned.

Separate votes in different areas of the business, such as the finance department, showed strong support for industrial action short of strikes, the union said.

GWR bosses said they are still working to try to avert the walkouts and other industrial action.

The TSSA has not yet set dates for its industrial action.

The strike announcement comes as London faces eight 24-hour strikes on the Tube in November, December, January and February.

The National Union of Rail, Maritime and Transport Workers (RMT) announced a new wave of walkouts in a row with Transport for London (TfL) over a voluntary four-day week for Tube drivers.

GWR runs commuter and long-distance services in and out of London Paddington linking the capital to the South West, Wales and the South.

TSSA general secretary Maryam Eslamdoust said: “Strike action and action short of a strike, which will have a real impact on services at GWR, are now inevitable unless the company addresses these perfectly legitimate demands.

“It’s in no way reasonable for our members to suffer any financial hardship for doing shift work, which in itself runs the increased risk of physical and mental impairment.

“Likewise, for clerical grades, it’s simply not acceptable that they see their workloads increase without that reflecting both grading and pay.

“Our union only uses strike action as a last resort so the ball is very much in GWR’s court.

“We stand ready to get back around the table and talk at any time.”

A GWR spokesperson told The Standard: “We continue to work with colleagues and their trades union representatives to find a mutually beneficial solution to the dispute and avoid damaging strike action."
