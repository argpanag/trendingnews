More job losses loom as 1,800 Aussies a day make mortgage move: 'Cutting costs'

TheTools · 25.10.2026 07:25 · source · permalink

More job losses could be in store for the banking industry as it points to an "increasingly challenging environment" that threatens to reduce the workforce of the sector that employs some 175,000 Australians. The Financial Services Union (FSU) has called for NAB to be more transparent with workers about exactly what jobs will go after announcing it will cut 2,500 roles by Christmas – a move the union labelled "despicable" on Tuesday.

Economist and market strategist Tapas Strickland worked at NAB for more than a decade before leaving the bank last year. A pattern of job cuts at the bank is something he's seen up close over previous cycles.

Speaking to Yahoo Finance, he believes there will "definitely" be more job loses across the banking sector as the 'Big Four' try to cut costs in response to challenges in the economy, as well as other factors including funding expenditure on artificial intelligence.

"ANZ had their first run at it, and now obviously NAB. You'll probably see the same thing from CBA and Westpac," the Moomoo market strategist said.

In September last year, ANZ flagged it would cut headcount by 4,500 roles to reduce the complexity of its workforce, while last week Commonwealth Bank chair Paul O'Malley declined to rule out further job losses for it's more than 50,000 employees due to AI when pressed on the matter at the bank's annual general meeting. The bank has shed about 1,000 roles this year, according to the union.

Speaking on ABC radio this morning, the CEO of the Australian Banking Association, Simon Birmingham, said NAB has been clear about why it needed to cuts jobs, and pointed to challenges in the Australian economy, competition in the banking services sector and a drop in loan activity during the current housing market downturn.

"The challenge in lack of productivity, the challenge in terms of decline in loan activity, and fierce competition … not just for loans, but also for deposits, which of course drives up the cost base on the other side of the ledger, and while good news for consumers … it does create a more difficult operating environment," he said.

More than 1,800 Aussies a day switching their home loan

Birmingham noted that home loan activity has fallen by about 12 per cent to 20 per cent "in different categories" and more Aussies are increasingly working to find the best deal on their home loan borrowing costs.
