Archbishop ‘assaulted by gender-critical teacher’

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:42 · source · permalink

Police are investigating incident involving Enoch Burke and head of the Church of Ireland

The alleged assault of the head of the Church of Ireland by a gender-critical teacher is being investigated by police in Northern Ireland.

Footage appears to show Enoch Burke grabbing Archbishop John McDowell by the arm during a confrontation after a funeral in Armagh on Thursday.

In the footage, Mr Burke, who lost his job after refusing to use a transgender student’s preferred pronouns, accuses the Archbishop of Armagh of remaining “silent” while he was in prison for contempt of court.

While holding the Archbishop’s arm, the teacher says: “I have languished in a prison cell for upholding the Church of Ireland ethos and for refusing to forsake scriptural truth. What do you have to say about it?”

The Archbishop, who is the Primate of All Ireland, is heard saying he is being assaulted and prevented from going about his lawful business, adding: “If you keep assaulting me, I am going to report you to the police.”

After the Archbishop enters his residence, Mr Burke says to camera: “Behind these doors, an Archbishop, the Primate of All Ireland, the one who appoints the board of management [of the school], and he is running away. He will not give an answer.

“He is a traitor to Church of Ireland doctrine, to that Christian belief, man and woman. It is not ambiguous, thank God. He made them male and female but this man is running away. He won’t get away with that. I say today: God will judge him. This cannot be allowed.”

The video comes amid Mr Burke’s long-running legal battle following his dismissal from Wilson’s Hospital School in Co Westmeath.

Mr Burke, an evangelical Christian from Co Mayo, argued that a school request to address a transgender student by their new name and pronoun was unconstitutional and went against his right to express his religious beliefs.

He was dismissed by the school following a disciplinary process and was jailed for contempt of court after flouting orders to stay away from the school.

A police spokesman said: “Police are investigating a report of an assault on a member of the clergy at St Patrick’s Cathedral in Armagh. Police inquiries are ongoing.”

The Church of Ireland accused Mr Burke of haranguing the Archbishop.

A spokesman called the incident “particularly hostile and aggressive”, adding: “No respect was shown for the occasion or the sanctity of the cathedral grounds. Entry on to the See House property, which is a private residence, is a further concern.

“Archbishop McDowell, other members of the Church who were present, and the Church’s authorities, will assist with the PSNI’s enquiries.”
