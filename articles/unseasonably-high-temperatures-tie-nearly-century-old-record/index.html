Unseasonably high temperatures tie nearly century-old record

The last time it was this warm in Chicago on Nov. 20, the Shedd Aquarium and Alder Planetarium had only been open for a few months.

Nearly a century later, the high for Friday tied the record high of 68 degrees set in 1930 — and forecasters are still determining if the record has been broken.

Visitors stroll around Lincoln Park Zoo Friday as temperatures hit 62 degrees.

Pat Nabong/Sun-Times

The National Weather Service noted Friday morning that there was a chance the Chicago area would see record-breaking heat.

“It’s certainly possible, I can’t rule that out,” said Kevin Birk, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service. “We may eclipse that record here this afternoon.”

The stretch of higher temperatures have been driven by a warm pattern that might be influenced by strong El Niño conditions, according to Birk.

Flamingos bask in the sun Friday at Lincoln Park Zoo as temperatures hit 62 degrees.

Pat Nabong/Sun-Times

The unseasonable warmth won’t last much longer, though. Saturday’s high is expected to be 68 degrees before a cold front bringing lower temperatures and a chance of rain moves in Sunday.

“We’re going to see highs back in the 40s, so that’ll be the end of these abnormally warm conditions, at least for the next week.”

Visitors stroll around Lincoln Park Zoo Friday as temperatures hit 62 degrees.

Pat Nabong/Sun-Times

Birk described this week’s temperatures as the “upper echelon” of warmth for mid-to-late November, but autumn-like conditions will return with the cold front.

“I think we’re just going to be going back to what we typically see here in Chicago for the most part,” Birk said.
