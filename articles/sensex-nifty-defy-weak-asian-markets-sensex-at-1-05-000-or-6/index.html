Sensex, Nifty Defy Weak Asian Markets; Sensex At 1,05,000 Or 68,000? Morgan Stanley Reveals 2027 Targets

TheTools · 16.11.2026 10:45 · source · permalink

Indian stock market closed higher on Monday, November 16, with the BSE Sensex gaining over 500 points and the Nifty 50 settling above 25,290. Domestic equities remained positive despite weakness across several major Asian markets, as buying in select sectors, including FMCG and banking, supported the benchmarks. However, broader markets underperformed, with mid-cap and small-cap stocks ending in the red.

Stock Market Today: Sensex Jumps 512 Points, Nifty Above 25,290 Despite Weak Asian Markets

The BSE Sensex closed at 82,104.79, rising 512.34 points or 0.63% from its previous close of 81,592.45. During the session, the index opened at 81,810.98 and touched an intraday high of 82,311.65 before slipping to a low of 81,762.82.

The Nifty 50 ended at 25,291.10, gaining 146.80 points or 0.58% from its previous close of 25,144.30. The index opened at 25,206.15, touched a high of 25,337.60 and recorded an intraday low of 25,172.10.

FMCG, Banking Stocks Lead; Small Caps Underperform

Sectoral performance remained mixed during Monday's trading session. Nifty FMCG and Nifty Bank were among the strongest performers, helping the broader market benchmarks maintain their gains. In contrast, Nifty Auto, Nifty Realty and Nifty Pharma underperformed.

The broader market faced selling pressure despite gains in the headline indices. The Nifty MidCap 100 declined 0.51%, while the Nifty SmallCap 100 fell 1.10%, indicating that the recovery was not spread evenly across market segments.

According to the market breadth data, 4,795 stocks traded during the session, with 1,577 advancing and 2,984 declining, while 234 remained unchanged. A total of 153 stocks touched their 52-week highs, compared with 112 stocks that hit their 52-week lows. Meanwhile, 234 stocks were in the upper circuit and 289 were in the lower circuit.

The divergence between the headline indices and broader markets suggests that buying interest was concentrated in select large-cap stocks, while several mid-cap and small-cap shares continued to face pressure.

Asian Markets Today

Indian equities ended higher even as several major Asian benchmarks recorded losses on Monday. South Korea's Kospi declined 2.95%, marking the steepest fall among the major regional indices cited in market reports. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.51%, while China's CSI 300 slipped 0.99%.

Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index moved against the regional trend, gaining 0.70%. The mixed performance across Asian markets highlighted the difference between domestic buying interest and weakness in several overseas markets.

Sensex At 1,05,000 Or 68,000? Morgan Stanley Reveals Bull And Bear Market Targets For 2027

Looking ahead, Morgan Stanley expects Indian equities to enter a more decisive earnings-led phase and has set a base-case target of 95,000 for the Sensex by December 2027. The target implies an upside of around 16% from the levels cited in its outlook.

The brokerage has also outlined a bull-case target of 1,05,000 and a bear-case target of 68,000, reflecting the potential range of outcomes depending on earnings performance and broader market conditions. Its probability-weighted Sensex target stands at 90,750 for December 2027.

Morgan Stanley's positive outlook rests largely on expectations of stronger corporate earnings. The brokerage expects Sensex earnings to compound at around 18% through FY29, with earnings per share projected at 4,081 in FY27, 4,871 in FY28 and 5,669 in FY29.

Its estimates indicate earnings growth of 18.5% in FY27, 19.4% in FY28 and 16.4% in FY29. For the broader market, it expects earnings growth of 20% in FY27, 23% in FY28 and 20% in FY29.

The brokerage believes that sustained earnings growth could become a key driver for Indian equities in 2027. However, the outlook also depends on companies delivering on profit expectations, particularly as valuations remain above long-term averages in several market segments.

"On the flows front, the week marked continuation of the last week trend. FIIs were aggressive net buyers of Rs. 333.4bn, purchasing in all four sessions and extending their buying streak to a third week after a ten-week selling run. DIIs, in contrast, remained net sellers for the second week by Rs. 241.9bn, selling in all four sessions," said Pabitro Mukherjee, Deputy Vice President-Research, Bajaj Broking.
