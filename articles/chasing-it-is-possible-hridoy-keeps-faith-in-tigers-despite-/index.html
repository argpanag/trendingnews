'Chasing it is possible': Hridoy keeps faith in Tigers despite uphill task

TheTools · 09.11.2026 05:23 · source · permalink

Bangladesh may be staring at a daunting final-day challenge in the second Test against the West Indies, but Tawhid Hridoy believes a strong start could still put the hosts in contention for an unlikely victory at the Sylhet International Cricket Stadium.

West Indies ended the fourth day on 345 for 3 in their second innings on Sunday, stretching their overall lead to 315 runs. Kavem Hodge remained unbeaten on 140, while Amir Jangoo was not out on 20, leaving Bangladesh with plenty to do on the final day.

The hosts had briefly seized the initiative after Hridoy's record-breaking unbeaten 242 helped them post 386 in their first innings, securing a 30-run lead over the visitors' 356. However, the Caribbean batters turned the match on its head on Sunday, with Hodge and debutant Kevin Wickham leading a dominant batting display.

Bangladesh now face the prospect of having to surpass their highest successful Test chase of 215, achieved against West Indies in Grenada in 2009, should the visitors declare without adding to their lead. With seven wickets still in hand, however, the Windies could make the task even more formidable.

Despite the odds, Hridoy has not given up hope of a remarkable turnaround. Asked whether a target of 350-360 would be safe on the improving Sylhet surface, the batter felt Bangladesh could still have a chance if they got their approach right from the outset.

"Look, it will depend on how we start. The wicket still looks good to us. But this is red-ball cricket. If we can get off to a good start, I think chasing it is possible," Hridoy said during the post-day press conference on Sunday.

"However, we cannot say what will happen tomorrow just yet. First, we have to see how many runs they set as a target, and then we will plan accordingly."

Bangladesh's immediate challenge, though, will be to make inroads into the West Indies' remaining batting line-up before turning their attention to the chase. Hodge, who scored just one in the first innings, has already registered his third Test century, while Jangoo is also coming off a hundred in the first innings.

The hosts will also need their spinners to make a significant impact after another frustrating day for the trio of Mehidy Hasan Miraz, Taijul Islam and Nayeem Hasan. Taijul picked up two wickets but conceded 118 runs, while Miraz took one for 58. Nayeem went wicketless in his eight overs.

"Of course, tomorrow is important. But it is not just tomorrow; every day is very important. We will certainly try to do something good," Hridoy said.

Bangladesh trail 1-0 in the two-match series and need a victory in Sylhet to draw level. With the West Indies firmly in control, the hosts will need something special on the final day to keep that hope alive.
