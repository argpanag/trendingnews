Woman Finds Stranger Buried in Grave She Had Reserved for Herself | The Daily Dazzling Dawn

TheTools · 13.11.2026 14:17 · source · permalink

A 69-year-old woman has described her distress after discovering that a stranger had been buried in a cemetery plot she had purchased and reserved for herself near her parents' graves in Lincolnshire, England.

Marie Lane said she experienced sleepless nights after discovering in 2023 that another person had been buried in her reserved plot at Deeping St James Cemetery.

The Ministry of Justice (MoJ) later granted a licence for the body to be exhumed so the burial error could be corrected. Deeping St James Parish Council acknowledged that a mistake had been made and apologised to both families for the distress caused.

Lane said she purchased the exclusive right to be buried in the plot in 2013, choosing the location because it was directly behind her deceased parents' graves.

"I bought a plot to be near to my parents," she told the BBC.

She added that being buried elsewhere would defeat the purpose of reserving the space close to her family.

Lane said she visited the cemetery in early 2023 and was shocked to find that someone had already been buried in the space she had paid for.

She recalled feeling uncertain about what to do when she discovered the burial.

The woman said she spent more than three years seeking a resolution and eventually had no alternative but to request that the body be moved because there was no other suitable space nearby where she could be buried alongside her parents.

According to documents seen by the BBC, Deeping St James Parish Council had confirmed Lane's exclusive burial rights in 2013.

The Ministry of Justice granted a licence earlier in 2026 allowing the body to be exhumed to correct the mistake.

In England, exhuming a body from unconsecrated ground generally requires a licence from the Ministry of Justice. Consent from the deceased person's next of kin is usually required.

However, a letter from the department to the parish council confirmed that the licence had been granted without the family's consent, despite the relatives raising moral and religious objections.

The ministry explained that there was a lack of other possible solutions to rectify the burial error.

The BBC said it had attempted to contact the family of the person who was exhumed.

Lane's husband, Eric Lane, 77, said the situation had had a devastating effect on his wife.

He questioned the parish council's ability to manage cemetery records and expressed concern that a similar mistake could happen again.

He also said his wife no longer felt comfortable visiting the cemjetery because of the distress associated with the incident.

The BBC asked the parish council what steps had been taken to improve record-keeping and prevent similar mistakes. No response to that specific question was reported.

Marie Lane said the experience had changed how she felt about the cemetery and the reserved plot.

She explained that she sympathised with the family of the person who had been mistakenly buried there and understood that the situation would also have caused them considerable distress.

Lane said she had repeatedly asked the parish council to arrange a meeting with the other family because she wanted to speak to them directly. However, she said those requests had been refused.

She also questioned why the matter had taken so long to resolve, saying the process should not have required such a lengthy struggle.

Deeping St James Parish Council acknowledged that an error had occurred and apologised for the distress caused to both families. It confirmed that the burial plot had been returned to Lane.

Despite the exhumation and the return of the plot, Lane said she still felt uncomfortable visiting the cemetery and no longer visited her parents' graves as regularly as before.

The incident has raised concerns about cemetery administration, burial records and the emotional impact that mistakes involving burial arrangements can have on families.
