Headteacher suspends 25 pupils in a week ‘for laughing and zipping up bags’

TheTools · 17.09.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

New interim principal at Frome Community College says punishments are for ‘persistent disruption’

A headteacher has suspended 25 pupils in the first week of term at a school in Somerset.

Dan Jeffries has been accused of running Frome Community College like “a military base” because of the crackdown.

Parents have criticised his methods, claiming their children have been punished for “nervously laughing”, zipping up bags too loudly or looking out of the window.

However, Mr Jeffries has dismissed the criticism, stating that pupils who had been suspended would have been punished because of “persistent disruption”.

He told ITV News: “For someone to be suspended for disruptive behaviour, they’d have to be completely defiant and disrupt us four times, in our policy, before we’d consider it.”

Natalie Morris said her daughter was suspended for “nervously laughing”, adding: “She has special educational needs and disabilities, additional needs and sensory needs. So that was a massive setback for her when she did get suspended.

“She was just nervously laughing because of her surroundings, strange faces... She gets overwhelmed – it’s a new school, new people.”

Ms Morris claimed other pupils had been suspended for reasons including zipping up their bags too loudly, staring out of the window or “being a minute late”.

She said this had created an environment that had affected students’ mental health, with one feeling the need to “hide in the toilets”.

She added: “You want your child to feel safe, you want them to learn. You want the same opportunities as every other child, like everyone else’s kids that come here.

“That’s all we want – we want the kids’ needs to be met and not be treated like it’s a military base.”

Ann-Marie Ramsey, who also has a child with special educational needs and disabilities, said the increased discipline was “crazy”, adding: “If she was a bit fidgety or not concentrating, she would be sent to the reset room where they get disciplined if they’re not doing what the school wants them to do.”

She claimed students were being policed over their “hair and nail colour” and that parents were “not being listened to”.

In its most recent Ofsted visit in 2023, the college was given a rating of “good”.

Mr Jeffries was brought in as the new interim principal during the summer. 

Liz Halliwell, another parent, was less sceptical about his methods, saying: “He has come in and he is making sure that the rules are being followed.”

In response to the criticism, Mr Jeffries said all suspension cases involved “defiance leading to the disruption of the running of the school” and not reasons of “uniform or lateness”.

He said: “I am there to ensure we are inclusive and that we maintain really high standards. We have 1,400 children at the college, we had a total of 20 days’ worth of suspensions last week – that’s with some children receiving more than one day.

“It wasn’t a wide-scale number, it wasn’t a particularly large-scale event. It is safe to say a number of children did find returning to school more complicated, and that does happen some Septembers.

“All children deserve to have interruption-free education and where, we have a very small minority of pupils who are disrupting the learning of others, it’s important that we support as much as we can – but we don’t allow that to affect everyone else’s education.”
