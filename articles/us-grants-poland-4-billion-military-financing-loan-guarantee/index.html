US Grants Poland $4 Billion Military Financing Loan Guarantee

TheTools · 15.09.2026 12:00 · source · permalink

By Arun Bishnoi in News, Poland, USA

The United States has granted Poland a $4 billion Foreign Military Financing (FMF) loan guarantee, raising total U.S. support to nearly $20 billion. The agreement was signed in Warsaw on September 9, and lets Poland borrow funds under a U.S. guarantee to buy American-made weapons and equipment. The deal underscores deep U.S.-Poland defense ties and aims to strengthen NATO’s eastern flank and regional security.

Poland is one of America’s closest allies in Europe and has been rapidly modernizing its armed forces. Its defense spending is among NATO’s highest, and between 2021–2025 Poland was NATO’s largest arms importer. Major purchases have included U.S. F-35 fighters, AH-64 Apache helicopters, Patriot air-defense systems and HIMARS rocket launchers. U.S. officials say these acquisitions improve Poland’s self-defense and interoperability within NATO. Under the FMF program, Congress provides grants and loan guarantees so U.S. partners can buy American equipment.

The $4 billion loan guarantee backs Poland’s foreign-currency borrowing for weapons. If Poland defaults on those loans, the U.S. government would cover the cost. The State Department has not publicized interest rates or repayment terms. Polish officials say this new loan guarantee has “more favourable terms” than earlier ones, since a U.S. grant will subsidize part of the financing costs. As in prior deals, the money will be channelled through Poland’s state development bank (BGK).

In July 2025, the State Department announced a $4 billion FMF guarantee for Poland. U.S. Under Secretary Thomas DiNanno told the Polish press on 12 June that another $4 billion was being offered, bringing the total FMF support to $20 billion. No specific disbursement dates have been given. Poland’s defense minister said the funds will help “accelerate the implementation of existing contracts, including for Apache helicopters”.

The Defense Security Cooperation Agency (DSCA) noted this guarantee “brings total U.S. loan support to over $15 billion in just the past three years,” reflecting Poland’s commitment to NATO’s collective defense. The DSCA said such FMF deals “ensure our allies can defend themselves with reliable, proven systems”. The State Department added that by working together “the United States and Poland are securing the future of NATO, deterring aggression, and standing firm for freedom, sovereignty, and peace in Europe”.

There is some domestic debate. In Poland, right-wing leaders argue that an EU-backed defense loan program (SAFE) could cover Warsaw’s needs and worry that U.S. rules might clash with EU spending requirements. The government counters that SAFE and U.S. FMF support “do not compete; they complement one another”. In the U.S., aid to Poland has broad bipartisan backing as part of NATO solidarity. The agreement clearly signals robust U.S.-Poland security cooperation and helps bolster NATO deterrence against Russia.
