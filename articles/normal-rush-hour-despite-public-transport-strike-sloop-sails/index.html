Normal rush hour despite public transport strike; Sloop sails Amsterdam kids to school

TheTools · 09.09.2026 08:10 · source · permalink

The nationwide public transport strike did not have a major impact on rush hour Wednesday morning. Traffic on the roads looked about the same as usual, and the train stations were largely deserted. Commuters found ways around the strike. In Amsterdam, a sloop boat took children to school.

An ANP reporter saw that Utrecht Central Station was completely empty on Wednesday morning, apart from a few NS Employees. Amsterdam Central Station was also largely deserted. An NS spokesperson told the news wire that the same was true throughout the country.

Inside Amsterdam Central Station, a Spanish couple holding Eurostar tickets to Paris were caught off guard by the strike. “We didn’t receive any notification from the carrier about the strike or the fact that the train isn’t running.” An NS employee helped them rebook their tickets for Thursday. “Nothing is running at all today. The only way to get out of Amsterdam is a €400 flight.” So they have no choice but to spend “another rainy day in Amsterdam.”

The ANWB reported a “fairly average rush hour for a Wednesday morning,” with only “slightly” more traffic in some places. “It looks like a great many people have adjusted their schedules and are working from home. In any case, they aren’t taking to the road en masse,” a spokesperson said.

Rijkswaterstaat also reported that traffic was flowing well on the national roads despite the strike. "With a peak of 206 kilometers of traffic jams on our roads, it is slightly busier today than usual for a Wednesday."

School children and commuters who could not work from home on Wednesday had to make a different plan. In Amsterdam, children from the Noord district crossed the IJ in a small sloop boat to get to school, De Zeeheld primary school near Oude Houthaven. “A colleague has children at this primary school. That’s how the idea came about to improvise a small ferry for the crossing ourselves,” Fons van de Kar of Eco Boats said.

The children and some parents sat on the boat with umbrellas and raincoats, and got soaked from the local downpour. But “without this crossing, the children wouldn’t have made it to school on time,” Van de Kar noted. The skipper limited himself to this single trip.

Reporting by ANP and NL Times
