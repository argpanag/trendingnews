Here’s How T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon Stack Up on 5G

You’d think that having nationwide 5G Standalone (5G SA) coverage means you can actually use it most of the time. Well, not quite. A new report from Opensignal (via Android Authority) finds that T-Mobile users have access to 5G SA 71.2% of the time, compared to 20.2% for Verizon and just 4.4% for AT&T.

T-Mobile got a head start by launching its nationwide 5G SA network in 2020, and it’s clearly paying off. AT&T and Verizon announced their nationwide deployments last year, but they’ve still got some catching up to do.

AT&T Is Faster, but T-Mobile Isn’t Far Behind: Opensignal

According to the report, AT&T delivers the fastest 5G SA download speeds at 344.7Mbps and upload speeds at 22.6Mbps. It also tops Opensignal’s Consistent Quality metric at 88.9%, which measures how often a network can handle everyday activities reliably. Which means if you’re looking at raw performance, AT&T has plenty to brag about.

T-Mobile, however, sees the biggest jump when users switch from 5G non-standalone to 5G SA. Its download speeds improve by 84%, while upload speeds increase by 47%. It also takes the crown for latency at 40.2 milliseconds, narrowly beating AT&T’s 40.6ms. Verizon comes in at 42.9ms.

The good news is that all three carriers see at least an eight-percentage-point improvement in Consistent Quality with 5G SA compared to 5G NSA. In other words, the technology isn’t just marketing fluff. It can make your connection more reliable, alongside improvements in speed and latency.

Still, there’s little point in having a better network if you rarely get to use it. T-Mobile’s lead shows the value of getting 5G SA into customers’ hands early. AT&T and Verizon have made progress, but they’ll need to expand access before more subscribers can reap the benefits.
