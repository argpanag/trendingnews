Historical drama or intimate tragedy for marking Aberfan: why staging The Revlon Girl hits differently | Collin Rugg News

TheTools · 19.10.2026 12:04 · source · permalink

Focusing on the quiet aftermath of tragedy rather than the catastrophic collapse itself is precisely why Neil Anthony Docking’s play The Revlon Girl resonates so powerfully. Marking 60 years to the day since the Aberfan disaster, Vision Theatre Company will open a poignant stage production at Boulevard Theatre in Milford Haven on Wednesday, October 21, 2026. Instead of attempting to dramatise the physical event, the play looks behind closed doors at the personal grief and survival of the bereaved mothers left behind.

On October 21, 1966, a colliery spoil tip collapsed above the Welsh mining village of Aberfan, claiming the lives of 116 children and 28 adults. The Milford Haven production provides audiences from across Pembrokeshire and across Wales a space to reflect on the immense loss and the persistent, intergenerational impact felt across mining communities.

Beyond the colliery disaster: inside the hotel room

Many historical dramatisations rely on sensational spectacles to capture catastrophe, yet theatrical storytelling often finds its deepest emotional truth in restraint. Set eight months after the disaster, The Revlon Girl brings audiences into a private room above a local Welsh hotel where a small circle of bereaved mothers meet in secret.

Struggling with profound guilt and the crushing weight of public mourning, the women take the quiet, daring step of inviting a beauty consultant from cosmetic firm Revlon to give them tips on makeup. Through tears, unexpected laughter, and harsh honesty, the narrative explores the complexity of survivor guilt and the guilt of simply wanting to feel human again.

“An opportunity to remember them, to honour their families, and to recognise the community whose lives were forever changed.”

Grief, laughter, and reclaiming everyday life

A widespread misconception surrounding profound historical tragedies is that communal remembrance must remain solemn and entirely detached from everyday domestic life. Docking’s text actively counters this assumption by showing that humour and grief coexist side by side even in the darkest circumstances.

By shifting focus from the political fallout and engineering failures to the emotional reality inside a hotel room, the production addresses the universal human impulse to connect and rebuild when normal life has vanished.

Event details and schedule at Boulevard Theatre

The commemorative anniversary gala opens on October 21, with proceedings beginning promptly in the early evening. A collective silence will anchor the night before the live performance gets underway.

Directed by creative director Drew Baker, the production ensures that the anniversary serves not only as a historical marker for Wales, but also as an artistic forum on community healing. Audiences unable to attend the gala can catch the play across its subsequent evening performances through Saturday, October 24.
