I’m A Celebrity 2026 – Who is Mariella Frostrup?

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:04 · source · permalink

The 26th series of I’m a Celebrity… Get Me Out of Here! returns to our screens, signalling the start of three weeks of Bushtucker Trials and celebrity meltdowns in the Australian jungle.

The celebrities will kiss goodbye to their mobile phones, internet, social media and television as they swap the comforts of home for three weeks of eating animal testicles, being covered in creepy-crawlies and confronting their worst fears.

Former England and Manchester United footballer Wayne Rooney is the biggest name and early favourite to be crowned the 2026 King of the Jungle – and go one better than his wife Coleen, who finished runner-up in 2024.

But who are the celebrities taking part in the 26th series of I’m a Celebrity, once again hosted by Ant McPartlin and Declan Donnelly?

Mariella Frostrup

Mariella Frostrup is a Norwegian-born British journalist, broadcaster, author and campaigner who has become one of the most recognisable voices in British media over a career spanning more than four decades.

Born in Oslo in 1962, Frostrup moved to Ireland with her family when she was six. Her Norwegian father was a journalist who worked as foreign editor of The Irish Times, while her Scottish mother was an artist. Her father died aged just 44, when Frostrup was 15.

She later moved to London and began working in music PR, promoting acts including Dire Straits, UB40, Bananarama and the Boomtown Rats. She was also involved in the landmark Band Aid and Live Aid charity initiatives.

Frostrup has written for almost every major British newspaper, including The Times, The Sunday Times, Daily Mail, Mail on Sunday and The Observer, as well as magazines such as Harper’s Bazaar, Vanity Fair and Family Traveller.

For almost 20 years until 2021, she was The Observer’s agony aunt, offering advice to readers on relationships, family matters and personal dilemmas.

Her name has become synonymous with arts and literature, having presented some of the UK’s leading books programmes and cultural shows while serving on the judging panels of prestigious literary awards.

She presented BBC Radio 4’s Open Book, interviewing leading authors and publishers and reviewing new fiction and non-fiction titles. Her other broadcasting credits include BBC Radio 2’s The Green Room and The Book Show on Sky Arts.

Frostrup has also judged the Booker Prize and the Orange Prize for Fiction, while becoming the first non-elected member of the Royal Academy’s Council to serve a full six-year term.

She has made cameo appearances in popular comedy programmes including Absolutely Fabulous and Coupling, and presented BBC Panorama investigations examining reality television and the housing crisis.

In 2008, Frostrup received an honorary Doctor of Letters degree from Nottingham Trent University in recognition of her contribution and commitment to journalism and broadcasting.

Her distinctive voice has become one of her trademarks. In 2005, she was voted the sexiest female voice on television, while her voice has also been used for announcements in lifts on the London Overground.

Frostrup has become a passionate advocate for gender equality and women’s rights. She has served as Save the Children’s Gender Ambassador and was a founder trustee of The GREAT Initiative, which campaigns for gender equality.

She is also a patron of The Shark Trust, a longstanding supporter of Comic Relief and an advocate for environmental conservation.

In recent years, Frostrup has become particularly prominent in campaigning for greater awareness of menopause and the challenges it can create for women in the workplace.In 2024, she was appointed the UK Government’s Menopause Employment Ambassador.

Her first marriage, from 1979 to 1984, was to Richard Jobson, lead singer of Scottish punk rock band Skids. She later met human rights lawyer Jason McCue during a charity trek in Nepal, and the couple married two years later. They live in Somerset and have two children together.
