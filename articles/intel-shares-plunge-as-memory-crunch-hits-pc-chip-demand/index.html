Intel Shares Plunge as Memory Crunch Hits PC Chip Demand

TheTools · 29.10.2026 21:48 · source · permalink

Third-Quarter PC Chip Revenue Falls 5%, Missing Expectations Surging HBM Demand Drives Up Prices for PC Memory Weaker PC Processor Demand to Carry Into Fourth Quarter

SILICON VALLEY — Intel, one of the world's leading chipmakers, posted weaker results in its personal computer business. As the three largest memory makers — Samsung Electronics (005930.KS), SK hynix (000660.KS) and Micron — shifted production lines toward high-bandwidth memory (HBM) to meet artificial intelligence demand, PC memory became harder to secure. That squeezed PC manufacturers and sharply reduced demand for Intel's PC processors.

Intel said on the 29th that third-quarter revenue rose 21% from a year earlier to $16.51 billion. The figure matched the $16.5 billion consensus compiled by the London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG). Earnings per share came in at 52 cents, beating the 41 cents forecast by brokerage analysts.

Results diverged sharply by segment. The data center and AI unit posted revenue of $7.3 billion, up 78%, while the client computing group, which makes chips for personal computers, saw revenue fall 5% to $8.1 billion, below expectations. Intel makes both CPUs for AI servers and CPUs for PCs.

The stark gap between the two businesses comes down to memory. Major memory plants worldwide have concentrated on HBM production, reducing supply of PC memory and sending prices soaring. According to Counterpoint Research, global PC shipments fell 4% from a year earlier in the second quarter as prices for memory and other components jumped. Bloomberg said demand for Intel processors rose with the buildout of data center infrastructure, but at the same time a supply shortage emerged in memory chips made by Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Micron. That ultimately pushed up prices for laptops and desktops and led to weaker demand for the Intel chips used in those products, the news agency said.

With the PC slump expected to continue into the fourth quarter, Intel guided for revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion. That falls short of the $17.1 billion analyst consensus compiled by LSEG. Bloomberg's survey also showed an average analyst estimate of $17 billion, with some brokerages having expected more than $18 billion.

Intel expects the PC market downturn to last at least through the first half of 2027. Bloomberg reported that the market is projected to contract 12% in 2026 and shrink a further 10% next year.

Intel's foundry unit, which makes chips on contract for other companies, posted revenue of $6.7 billion, up 58% from a year earlier. Intel is focused on winning customers to challenge the dominance of Taiwan's TSMC in foundry services.

On disappointment over the results, Intel shares plunged 17.14% in after-hours trading as of 5:17 p.m. Eastern time.
