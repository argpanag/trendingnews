Ackerman goes where others fear to tread

TheTools · 25.11.2026 12:20 · source · permalink

Sharing a field with Marques Ackerman is challenging, whether you’re a teammate or an opponent. Why it should be difficult for the latter isn’t difficult to understand — what’s not to like about a lusty left-hander lashing the ball to all parts.

But Ackerman also makes demands on those who bat with him. He and Zubayr Hamza completed a stand of 96 in Centurion on Tuesday, day two of the second Test against Bangladesh. Ackerman scored 57 off 68 in the partnership, Hamza 27 off 61. Then Ackerman shared 74 with Tony de Zorzi. Shared is a strong word for it: Ackerman hammered 67 off 37. De Zorzi? Seven off 41.

In mitigation, De Zorzi — who is batting in a match for the first time since September 24 because of a hamstring injury — was hobbled by cramp for much of his innings. Even so, the difference between De Zorzi and his tanned terrier of a partner was stark.

While De Zorzi languished at the crease, trying to find his feet and everything else after two months on the sidelines, Ackerman dashed and darted about the place with sure-footed confidence and middled the ball with outrageous frequency.

De Zorzi and Ackerman, both Gauteng kids, have played with and against each other from under-13 level. But Tuesday was, of course, their first dance in a Test. Here’s hoping De Zorzi was forewarned by Aiden Markram and Temba Bavuma about what to expect.

Against Australia at Newlands in October, Ackerman faced two fewer balls than Markram but scored 34 more runs than him in their stand of 158. Batting with Bavuma in the second innings of the same match, Ackerman hit 47 off 57 in the partnership. His captain made 17 off 68.

“If he can face 100 balls he’s going to get a hundred 10 times out of 10,” Ryan Rickelton said last Tuesday about Ackerman. Rickelton is a smart fella, but he had inside information: Ackerman had scored 157 off 164 the previous day in the first Test against Bangladesh at the Wanderers.

Almost a month earlier, in that game against the Aussies in Cape Town, Ackerman squandered the chance to make his first Test century in only his second innings by charging Nathan Lyon and being stumped for 95. He had faced six deliveries fewer than a hundred.

Ackerman completed an effort of 150 off 138 on Tuesday that exhausted his opponents and batting partners alike. So Rickelton remains right: bowl a hundred balls at Ackerman and he will hurt you harder than others might.

Others like De Zorzi, who batted for a touch under four hours and faced 137 balls for his 66. Batting with Ackerman looks bloody hard. Is it?

“It’s always going to look difficult for me compared to Ackers,” De Zorzi told a press conference. “He is free-flowing, he’s batting incredibly well, and he’s taking the game forward. He’s being himself and he’s putting the team in winning positions. All his hard work is coming to fruition now, so I’m very happy for him.

“Batting with him is similar to batting with Sauce [Markram]. The opposition are more worried about him and I’m fighting my own demons in my head. So I’ve just got to get off strike and then they [Markram or Ackerman] will entertain everyone.

“Everyone’s obviously fixated on them, and rightly so. It’s easy — you get them on strike. You just have to make sure you don’t get sucked in trying to match them.”

Consider that South Africa’s other century-maker on Tuesday, Wiaan Mulder, took 177 deliveries to get there. His strike rate, 56.17, was almost half Ackerman’s 108.59. Mulder’s prize for all that toil is that he is second only to West Indies great George Headley in converting 50s into 100s. Headley turned all of his first half-dozen half-centuries into hundreds. Mulder has done so five times out of six. On top of that, he had to keep De Zorzi on the straight and narrow during their partnership of 92.

“I was cramping everywhere,” De Zorzi said. “It started in my hands, which are important when you're batting. Then it started going to other places. Luckily, Wessie [Mulder], who’s a dad now, was calm and said, ‘Stay calm, stay present, keep breathing.’ I’m very grateful to him for his words because they kept me in the moment.

“When you’re batting you have lots of thoughts that come in. And when your body starts fighting against you it’s tougher to fight those thoughts. My only real strength in my batting is being able to fight my mind and being able not to give up on myself. That’s all I try to do, really.”

It’s difficult to imagine Ackerman, given his current form and consequent success, digging deeply enough to mine words of gold like that. You can sing when you’re winning, but you learn the most when times are tough.

The underarching subplot to all that is South Africa have run the visitors ragged. Already in a bad way after crashing to 91 in little more than the equivalent of two sessions on Monday, Bangladesh will drag themselves into the third day 403 runs behind having spent 115 overs in the field. South Africa are likely to keep them out there a while longer. As De Zorzi could tell us, Test cricket takes no prisoners.
