Princess of Wales wears Diana’s favourite tiara for the Oman state banquet

TheTools · 21.10.2026 14:58 · source · permalink

Catherine accessorised with the Lover’s Knot headpiece and Camilla donned one favoured by Queen Elizabeth II

The Queen and the Princess of Wales pulled out all the stops on Tuesday evening for the state banquet at Windsor Castle in honour of the Sultan of Oman, continuing the charm offensive that began this morning.

The royal women were hosting the leader alongside their husbands, King Charles and Prince William, with the Princess Royal also in attendance. It is part of a four-day trip by the Sultan to the UK, the first from the nation since 1982. Back then, the previous Sultan met with the late Queen Elizabeth II and Diana, Princess of Wales, among others.

In honour of the occasion, the Princess of Wales wore a sleeveless buttercup-yellow gown by Elie Saab, which featured an elegant cape overlay. The latter detail has become a favourite of the Princess in recent years, and she also wore dresses with cape detail for the German and French state banquets in December and July 2025 respectively.

Unsurprisingly, Catherine accessorised with the Lover’s Knot tiara, her go-to for most royal occasions that require a tiara. Originally commissioned by Queen Mary in 1913, it became closely associated with the late Diana, Princess of Wales, and has been worn by the current Princess of Wales since 2015. Completing her look was more glitz: earrings and a bracelet from the late Queen’s collection, and a beaded clutch bag. The evening was also an opportunity to wear the King Charles II Royal Family Order and GCVO Sash and Star, two of her royal honours.

Meanwhile, Queen Camilla opted for a purple full-length gown with long, gently fluted sleeves – purple is one of her favoured hues for such occasions. The dress features beaded embroidery on the bottom of the skirt, which dazzled when catching the light. She chose the Girls of Great Britain and Ireland tiara, one of the late Queen Elizabeth II’s favourites, and which she wore during a visit to Oman in 1979. As it is not one of Camilla’s usual tiaras, it’s clear she is nodding to this past history through her choice.

This is the second state banquet to be held this year, with the first taking place in March for Nigeria’s president, Bola Ahmed Tinubu. On that occasion, the Princess of Wales wore a long-sleeved, high-neck emerald gown by Singaporean designer Andrew Gn, widely considered to be a nod to Nigeria’s flag.

The Princess of Wales has honed a tried-and-tested formula when it comes to state banquets, almost always wearing long-sleeved gowns, often with beading, sequin or lace detail to differentiate them from her usual black-tie looks. The colour is usually chosen to nod to the visiting dignitary’s home nation – hence why she wore burgundy red for French president Emmanual Macron’s visit last year and Prussian blue for German leader Frank-Walter Steinmeier. For the latter, she also had a rare opportunity to switch up her tiara choice in homage to the visitor, instead wearing the rarely seen Oriental Circlet tiara, which was designed by Queen Victoria’s German husband, Prince Albert.

When it comes to which designers to wear, she usually turns to those she trusts the most, including Sarah Burton, Jenny Packham and Phillippa Lepley. While royal tours are an occasion for wearing local designers, state banquets seem to be more often an opportunity to champion home-grown talent.

On Tuesday morning, the Queen and the Princess of Wales, both well versed in the art of soft diplomacy, showed just how powerful a joint effort can be as they greeted the Sultan of Oman at Windsor Castle.

With the Queen in vibrant poppy red and the Princess in a deep green, they represented the two dominant colours in the Omani flag: a classic way to pay homage to a visiting royal on an official state visit.

Catherine wore a Sarah Burton for Alexander McQueen coat that she’s owned since at least 2022, and last wore in public on St Patrick’s Day in 2025. Underneath, she wore a black turtleneck with a frilled collar. She completed her look with a Juliette Botterill hat, coordinating Gianvito Rossi suede courts and a Chanel bag – a classic black flap with gold hardware that’s been a part of her collection since 2023.

The pièce de résistance was her Prince of Wales feathers diamond and emerald brooch – a piece which has been worn by others who have held the title of Princess of Wales since 1863. Originally it was given to Princess Alexandra of Denmark from the “Ladies of North Wales” when she married the Prince of Wales (the future King Edward VII). It does more than coordinate; it exhibits her status as the wife of the next in line to the throne.

Images of the Princess later, viewing a special exhibition of items from the Royal Collection relating to Oman, show the sleek, dark-green belted midi dress worn underneath, a £260 style by the American designer Norma Kamali.

Queen Camilla’s coat and matching dress were made, meanwhile, from a wool crepe by Fiona Clare, her go-to couturier, and teamed with a Philip Treacy hat.

She went all-out with the jewellery too, completing her look with a ruby and diamond flower brooch, one of a pair known as the Queen Mother’s Cartier floral clip brooches. This set, which dates back to the 1940s, was on display at the Victoria and Albert Museum’s Cartier exhibition last year.

Both Camilla and Catherine’s ensembles for this formal engagement could be likened to uniforms for the jobs they are performing. Style is a factor, naturally, but everything they choose to wear becomes powerfully symbolic on a state visit such as this.
