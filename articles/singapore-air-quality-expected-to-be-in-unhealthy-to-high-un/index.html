Singapore air quality expected to be in ‘unhealthy’ to ‘high unhealthy’ range on Oct 11

SINGAPORE: The 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) is forecast to be in the unhealthy to high unhealthy range for the next 24 hours, said the National Environment Agency (NEA).

Showers were observed over several parts of Singapore on Saturday, NEA noted in its media advisory on Saturday (Oct 10) evening.

"Moderate to dense smoke haze from southern Sumatra was observed to drift towards Singapore," it said, adding that smoke haze was also observed in many parts of Kalimantan.

While some showers are forecast over Singapore and parts of the surrounding region, dry conditions are expected to continue over parts of southern Sumatra and Kalimantan, said the agency.

"Under the prevailing east-southeasterly or southerly winds, smoke haze may continue to drift towards Singapore," it added.

The 24-hr PSI for Singapore is thus forecast to be in the unhealthy to high unhealthy range. 

Air quality across Singapore remained in the unhealthy range on Saturday, although readings had eased overnight after reaching new highs for this year's haze season on Friday.

The central region registered a PSI of 192 at 5pm on Friday, the highest reading of this year's haze season.

As at 9pm, the 24-hour PSI ranged from 119 to 165 across the country.

The 1-hour PM2.5 in the south fell into the normal band at 8pm, followed by the east at 9pm.

Readings in the other regions are in the elevated band, ranging from 79 to 98.

Earlier, the 1-hour PM2.5 reading in the central region jumped into the high band with a reading of 151 at 3pm before falling back into the elevated band.

NEA warned on Friday that the 24-hour PSI could exceed 200 and enter the Very Unhealthy range, as smoke haze from southern Sumatra and Kalimantan continued to be blown towards Singapore.

Singapore’s Haze Task Force, comprising 28 government agencies, has been convened and a series of measures are ready to be rolled out should air quality deteriorate further.

If the PSI exceeds 200, N95 masks will be distributed to eligible Singaporeans, while air-conditioned haze shelters at community clubs, as well as selected residents' committee and residents' network centres, will be set up.

Preschools must stop outdoor and strenuous indoor activities when air quality is Very Unhealthy, while outdoor sporting activities may be modified or suspended. Employers have also been advised to limit outdoor work.

Schools may be closed if the next day’s 24-hour PSI is forecast to exceed 300 and enter the Hazardous range.

Any decision to close schools will be announced by around 8pm the day before through schools and the Ministry of Education’s official channels, Education Minister Desmond Lee said on Saturday. 

"We will continue to monitor the situation closely each day and review whether schools should stay open or be closed the next day," he said in a social media post.

The haze comes as the Formula 1 Singapore Grand Prix weekend continues at Marina Bay, with the first sprint race in Singapore taking place on Saturday. Organisers have said contingency measures are in place should air quality worsen.
