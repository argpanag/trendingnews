Iisalo adds to NBA versus Europe debate, rejects culture shock

TheTools · 17.11.2026 05:57 · source · permalink

Giannis Askounis

Make Your Preferred Basketball Source.

In a discussion drifting away from the Memphis Grizzlies, 44-year-old Tuomas Iisalo proudly revisits Europe and praises assistant Julius Thomas

By Johnny Askounis/ info@eurohoops.net

After the Memphis Grizzlies beat the Washington Wizards at Capital One Arena on Monday, head coach Tuomas Iisalo spent much of his postgame press conference talking about Europe rather than his team improving to 10–4 in the 2026–27 NBA Regular Season.

Asked whether moving from Europe to the NBA made his job harder, the Finnish tactician rejected that idea. “I never felt that way,” he said. “Coming over because I didn’t come to a head coaching role. I came to be an assistant coach and wanted to do the best in that job and in helping the head coach in that situation, Taylor Jenkins, have the best possible success.”

Injuries, not culture shock, caused the real problems. “It was more a learning experience,” continued Iisalo. “Then last year, the season overall, I thought we were very unlucky with the injuries. Starting in the beginning, and we’ve talked about it a lot that a lot of our interactions are based on the point guard and the center, and I think we had like one or two players that were healthy in a rotation of six players, and that put some monkey wrenches in our development. And then once we got going, there was another iteration of the team and whatnot. You always learn the most from those most difficult seasons. And that’s the challenges, even though in the moment, you don’t necessarily feel like, ‘Oh man, this is great that I’m going through this’, or that we are going through this, but in the end, the deep end is the best teacher.”

Comparing European teamwork and NBA talent

Questions then shifted to a debate sparked by EuroLeague players, including Guerschon Yabusele, claiming Europe’s top clubs could beat plenty of NBA teams. Having led successful programs in Germany and France, Iisalo pointed out that the two styles are hard to measure side by side.

“It’s very difficult to compare because the court is different, the rules are different, game time is different, also the amount of games that are played,” explained the Helsinki native. “If you would put NBA teams on schedules that play two to three times a week instead of four times a week, I think it would also look a lot different. And that discussion is also ongoing in the NBA about the amount of games, but I don’t think there’s any question that the best players in the world are in the NBA. That’s very clear. But at the same time, I’m very proud of European basketball. I know how high-level it is, especially how well the teams execute as a collective. And I think you have great examples also in the NBA of teams moving in that direction and being really very much like more than the sum of the parts.”

Iisalo also praised his German assistant Julius Thomas, who joined Memphis this season.

“He gives a lot,” he said. “I got to know him when he was a very young coach for Dragons Rhondorf in the German third league and was our farm team head coach, and I spent a couple of days with him when I took over the Bonn program and thought he was a very promising young coach. And in the beginning, he mostly set up the video and mopped on the baseline, and now he’s an assistant coach in the NBA. So that says, I think, everything you need to know about what I think about him and his development. He works mostly on the offensive side. He has very high standards and is a very, very fast learner, and a really well-rounded coach already at a young age.”

Memphis now heads to Rocket Arena to play the Cleveland Cavaliers on Wednesday. On Thursday, the club returns to FedExForum to host the Golden State Warriors.
