Lake Mead barrel murder victim identified more than 4 years after remains found

LAS VEGAS (KSNV) — The remains of a homicide victim found inside a barrel at Lake Mead in May 2022 have been identified, according to the Clark County coroner’s office.

The coroner’s office worked with the Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department’s Homicide Unit to identify the man, whose name has not yet been publicly released.

Local law enforcement and Clark County officials are expected to provide additional details about the identification and ongoing investigation.

Body found inside barrel at Lake Mead in 2022

The discovery dates back to May 1, 2022, when visitors at Hemenway Harbor at Lake Mead found a barrel containing human remains that had surfaced as water levels at the lake declined.

Investigators determined the remains belonged to an adult male who was believed to have been between 27 and 61 years old at the time of his death.

Authorities believe the man was shot in the back of the head sometime between the late 1970s and early 1980s before his body was placed inside the barrel and dumped into the lake.

The discovery prompted widespread attention, with investigators working to determine the victim’s identity and what led to his death decades earlier.

DNA and clothing helped investigators

In an interview with News 3 in June 2024, Clark County Coroner Melanie Rouse discussed the challenges investigators faced in identifying the victim.

Rouse said generating DNA profiles that could be compared against available records was an important step in the identification process.

“We could have the best investigation ever with the most solid information, but if we don't have anything to compare it to and there is not already a profile already created for that individual for us to make a comparison then we can’t make an I.D.,” Rouse said.

Despite the passage of decades, the victim’s skeletal remains were largely intact, along with his clothing and personal belongings. Investigators used those details, along with assessments from specialists, to help develop a profile of the man.

Items recovered included a short-sleeved button-down shirt, jeans with a 46-inch waist and 30-inch inseam, blue-and-white size 11 Trax tennis shoes and a silver Timex watch.

“We are very lucky that the condition of the clothing and the condition of the remains were in the state that they were,” Rouse said.

Rouse said specialists also helped estimate the victim’s age and height at the time of his death.

Possible organized crime connection remains unclear

Geoff Schumacher, Vice President of Exhibits and Programs at the Mob Museum says the circumstances surrounding the killing have drawn comparisons to the 1976 murder of Johnny Roselli, a former mobster with ties to Las Vegas who was killed in Florida and found inside a barrel in Biscayne Bay.

However, investigators have not publicly established a connection between the Lake Mead case and organized crime.

For more than four years, the Clark County Coroner’s Office and local and federal law enforcement agencies have examined the available evidence and pursued leads in an effort to identify the victim and solve the decades-old homicide.

Rouse previously emphasized the importance of continuing to review evidence as new information becomes available.

“Sometimes cases just take longer while that information becomes available. But we’re always willing to rerun it, to continue to look and to continue to work on these cases,” she said.

Investigation continues after identification

Although authorities have now identified the victim, the investigation remains ongoing as detectives work to determine who was responsible for his death.

Clark County officials and local law enforcement are expected to provide additional updates on the case Thursday afternoon.
