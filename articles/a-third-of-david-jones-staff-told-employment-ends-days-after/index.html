A third of David Jones staff told employment ends days after Christmas

TheTools · 20.11.2026 06:28 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Retail Industry

Topic:Retail Industry

More than 1,600 David Jones staff will lose their jobs after Christmas. (ABC News: Fletch Yeung)

In short: 

Nearly 1,700 David Jones employees have been served their redundancy notices today.

Staff were told December 30 will be their last day, as the union seeks to find them alternative employment.

What's next?

Those made redundant are expected to receive their entitlements, but for customers delivery options such as click and collect are unavailable.

A total of 1,689 David Jones employees have been served their redundancy notices today.

Staff were informed their employment will officially terminate on December 30.

The union, the SDA, says it will be working overtime to help members find alternative employment.

"We will leave no stone unturned in our efforts to find alternative employment for our members, and are grateful that already a number of companies have come forward seeking the skills and experience our DJs members have to offer," NSW secretary Bernie Smith said.

The SDA says it is communicating directly with members and has established a dedicated help desk to ensure what David Jones is offering redundant employees is in line with their financial and legal entitlements.

The iconic retailer went into voluntary administration earlier this month.

Receiver David McGrath and Joseph Hansell of FTI Consulting confirmed it would close 19 of the retailer's 38 stores across Australia and New Zealand on November 11.

In a letter to suppliers, David Jones said: "We expect the affected stores to continue trading through the peak seasonal period, with closure dates anticipated to occur later in the year."

Friday's news confirms staff will be asked to leave a day before New Year's Eve.

"There is never a good time for a company to go under, but it is even more stressful for our affected members as it occurs in the very shadow of Christmas, normally a happy and relaxed moment in an otherwise busy year," Mr Smith said.

"We once again appeal to DJs shoppers to treat shop floor staff with decency and sympathy at this most stressful time."

Retail analyst Gary Mortimer described the news as "deeply disappointing".

"While employees should receive their entitlements, that provides little comfort to workers and families now facing an uncertain future just weeks before Christmas," Professor Mortimer said.

Earlier this week ABS data showed a broader surge in Australia's unemployment rate, and that was before the David Jones redundancies.

"With Australia's unemployment rate now sitting at 4.9 per cent, the loss of around 1,500 positions will place even more pressure on the labour market and add to the number of Australians seeking work," Professor Mortimer said.

"My thoughts are with the affected employees, many of whom have dedicated years of service to the business and are now confronting a difficult holiday period."

There has been a surge in online orders since the receivership was announced and the David Jones website warns that "deliveries may take longer than usual".

Common delivery options such as express, same-day, Saturday delivery, and click and collect are unavailable.

The ABC has confirmed David Jones is up to date with superannuation payments to its 6,000 employees, in line with new pay-day laws that came into effect in July.
