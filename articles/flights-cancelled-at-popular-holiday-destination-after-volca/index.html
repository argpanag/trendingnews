Flights cancelled at popular holiday destination after volcano erupts

TheTools · 15.09.2026 08:24 · source · permalink

Flights have been cancelled at a popular holiday destination after a famous volcano erupted, causing headaches for tourists.

Located on the east coast of Sicily, Italy, in the Metropolitan City of Catania, Mount Etna is situated between the cities of Messina and Catania. It is the tallest active volcano in Europe.

On Monday morning (September 14), the volcano erupted, leading to travel disruption across the region. Catania Airport was forced to suspend flights for safety reasons due to the volcanic activity.

A 4km high eruption cloud was seen above Mount Etna following its eruption. It comes following reports of ‘continuous ash emissions’ coming from the volcano's Northeast Crater last week.

A red flight alert (VONA, Volcano Observatory Notice for Aviation) was issued by INGV Etneo Observatory in response to the news. This meant that any aircraft bound for Catania Airport had to be redirected.

While the warning was initially in place to 4pm on Monday, it was extended into Tuesday morning (September 15). Catania Airport is Italy's fifth-busiest airport by passenger traffic, with around 40,000 passengers travelling via it every day.

Shortly before 6am local time on Tuesday, plumes of black smoke could be seen rising from the mountain once again. Due to this, the flight suspension was extended to 10am.

Catania Airport said on social media: “Passengers are kindly advised to check the status of their flight with their airline before travelling to the airport. Further updates will follow.”

Around 150 flights were cancelled due to the volcano, just one week after UK airports experienced issues of their own. The travel chaos comes after a five-day shutdown of the Sicilian aviation hub last month.

In what marked the longest emergency closure at the airport since 2002, it came during the peak Ferragosto holiday. Airport operator SAC said that 36 per cent of arrivals scheduled between 6 and 12 August were cancelled, while more than 600 flights were diverted to alternative destinations including Palermo and Trapani.

According to Italy's Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology, Mount Etna is experiencing ‘variable-rate degassing’. It is known for its frequent emissions and ash activity.

The institute said in a statement: "A rapid evolution of the ongoing phenomena towards more energetic activity with the formation of eruption columns, ash clouds, and collapse of portions of the summit cones with the formation of pyroclastic flows cannot be ruled out.”t
