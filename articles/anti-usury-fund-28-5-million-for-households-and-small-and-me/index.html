The funding made available by the Ministry of Economy and Finance to the Anti-Usury Fund to prevent recourse to the illegal lending circuit loan sharking. The funds will be used to provide guarantees on bank loans and direct financing, up to a maximum of 40,000 euros, to households and SMEs for whom access to credit is difficult. There are 67 organisations – including credit guarantee consortia, associations and foundations – operating throughout the country, providing counselling and support to people in financial difficulty and facilitating access to credit by issuing guarantees to banks. The Interministerial Commission, established and chaired by the Treasury Department, is responsible for determining the allocation of resources. The criteria taken into consideration include the organisations’ actual capacity to facilitate access to credit, the incidence of usury in their areas of operation, and the efficiency of their management in utilising resources.

The procedure

The full list of associations, foundations and credit guarantee consortia authorised to operate in the granting of loans guaranteed by the Fund is available on the Treasury Department’s website. As stated on the dedicated page of the Ministry’s website, clicking on ‘Households’ brings up a region-by-region list of the associations and foundations you can contact. If, on the other hand, you select the ‘Businesses’ option, you will be taken to the list of credit guarantee consortia operating in the area, including those not authorised to disburse loans direct loans. Information on how to register as a managing body for the Fund is also available on the Treasury’s website: on the ‘Regulations’ page, under the ‘Circulars’ heading, you will find fillable forms. For further clarification, please contact the dedicated email address: segreteria.antiusura.dt@mef.gov.it.

The Fund

The Fund, which is partly financed by administrative penalties imposed for breaches anti-money laundering and foreign exchange regulations, celebrates its 30th anniversary this year. Established in March 1996, under Law 108, it was reformed with the adoption of the Ministerial Decree of 27 February 2026, which provides for an expansion of the operations eligible for financing and the entities eligible for guarantees, that is, those entities that meet the legal and economic requirements to obtain a guarantee. Among the other changes introduced are the creation of synergies with the regulations governing the SME Fund and an overall simplification and rationalisation of the regulatory framework.

Credit guarantee consortia already authorised under the previous system may continue to issue guarantees using resources from the Fund until 31 December this year. They must also register in accordance with the new rules by that date. Associations and foundations will have until 26 February 2027 to comply, but in the meantime they may continue to operate under the previous regulations.
