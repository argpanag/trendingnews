The Jorge Jesus Era Begins With Portugal

There is a new face on the bench, but the expectations remain exactly the same.

Portugal returns to the UEFA Nations League as defending champion and will begin its campaign against Wales at the Estádio José Alvalade, a match that will also mark the highly anticipated debut of Jorge Jesus as national team head coach.

The experienced manager took charge following the departure of Roberto Martínez after the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and his first squad has maintained much of the core that has represented Portugal in recent years.

The mission could not be clearer: defend the title.

Portugal won the 2024/25 edition of the UEFA Nations League, its second title in the history of the competition, and now shares Group A4 with Wales, Norway and Denmark.

The schedule will be particularly demanding. After this match, the Portuguese will visit Norway on September 27 and Denmark on October 1 before returning home to face the Norwegians again on October 4.


Cristiano Ronaldo Will Start Against Wales

One of Jorge Jesus' first major decisions has already been made.

Cristiano Ronaldo will start against Wales, as long as there are no last-minute issues.

The Portuguese coach all but confirmed the captain's place in the starting lineup during his pre-match press conference.

"If nothing unusual happens, Cris will start tomorrow," Jesus said.

The decision comes just days after the new head coach made it clear that Cristiano Ronaldo's enormous historical importance does not guarantee him every minute with Portugal.

Jesus considers the forward a symbol of the national team, but he has also insisted that his performances will determine how much he plays.

For this Thursday, however, there are no doubts.

At 41 years old, Cristiano Ronaldo will begin another edition of the UEFA Nations League as captain and the main attacking reference of a team that also features stars such as Bruno Fernandes, Bernardo Silva, Vitinha, João Neves, Rúben Dias and Nuno Mendes.

Wales Returns to the Nations League Elite

The challenge for Wales will be enormous.

The team coached by Craig Bellamy is back in League A after earning promotion in the previous edition of the UEFA Nations League, where it completed its campaign without a defeat.

Now, the level of difficulty increases considerably.

The Dragons will have to begin on the road against the defending champion before traveling to Denmark for their second match of the group.

Bellamy called up 28 players for this international window, although injuries ruled out defenders Joe Rodon and Dylan Lawlor. On the other hand, Harry Wilson, Jordan James and Ben Cabango are back, while Cian Ashford received his first senior national team call-up.

It will also be a reunion between two national teams that have not faced each other since one of the most important matches in their recent history.

Remembering Euro 2016

The last time Portugal and Wales faced each other was in the semifinals of Euro 2016.

That night in Lyon ended with a 2-0 victory for the Portuguese.

Cristiano Ronaldo opened the scoring early in the second half before assisting Nani for the goal that sealed the victory and sent Portugal into the final.

Days later, the Portuguese defeated France to lift the first major trophy in their history.

More than a decade later, Cristiano Ronaldo is still here.

This time the stage will be Lisbon and the competition will be different, but Portugal once again begins a European tournament with the goal of finishing it by lifting another trophy.
