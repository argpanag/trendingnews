Khoslas disclose full list of LPs as they close Seahawks purchase

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:20 · source · permalink

Billionaire tech investor Vinod Khosla and his family closed their group’s $9.6 billion acquisition of the Super Bowl champion Seahawks on Thursday from the estate of Paul Allen, and for the first time disclosed their full list of co-investors.

They did not disclose individual stake sizes, but the following list is in descending order of the size of their stakes, and when two LPs have the same stake, they are listed alphabetically.

Private equity firms Sixth Street and the Carlyle Group/Dynasty Equity collaboration each hold 3%, according to sources.

  • Former Sequoia Capital Managing Partner Mark Stevens, who is already an LP with the Golden State Warriors.
  • The Aramburuzabala family. The Aramburuzabalas, of whom the most prominent member is Tesalia Capital founder María Asunción Aramburuzabala, are the descendants of the founder of Grupo Modelo, which makes Corona, Pacifico and Modelo beers.
  • Val Blavatnik, son of DAZN owner Len Blavatnik and special adviser at Access Technology Ventures, his father’s firm.
  • Sheryl Sokoloff, wife of Jonathan Sokoloff, co-managing partner of private equity firm Leonard Green & Partners.
  • Gonzalo Hevia Baillères, a tech entrepreneur and member of the family behind the powerful Mexican business conglomerate Grupo BAL.
  • Jesse van der Werf, a Canadian entrepreneur and CEO of Heavy Metal Equipment & Rentals.
  • Samir Kaul, managing director of Khosla Ventures
  • Sunny Gupta, cofounder and CEO of tech company Apptio.
  • Nader Naini, managing partner of Frazier Healthcare Partners.
  • Penny Pritzker, an entrepreneur and former U.S. secretary of commerce
  • Carlyle/Dynasty Equity group
  • Sixth Street

Andalusian Sports Advisors served as adviser to the Khosla family and the new ownership group. Sullivan & Cromwell LLP served as legal counsel.

LPs have no formal role in running the team, but sometimes they are considered future buyers of majority stakes in other teams. For example, Khosla acquired 3.1% of the 49ers in 2025 before buying the Seahawks this year.
