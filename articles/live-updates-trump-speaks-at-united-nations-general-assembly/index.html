US proposes $5 billion investment in fund to repair energy infrastructure in Middle East

The Trump administration is proposing a $5 billion US government investment in a new Middle East fund that would repair and boost energy infrastructure damaged in the Iran war and reduce the region’s reliance on the Strait of Hormuz, according to documents reviewed by CNN.

President Donald Trump is expected to discuss the proposal with the leaders of Gulf nations later today in New York, three sources told CNN. The proposal itself began circulating last week, sources said.

The US willingness to invest in repairs and new building for the region is a signal of how devastating the war has been for the region in terms of widespread damage to infrastructure, and the upending of energy exports.

The six-page proposal is titled the Partnership for Allies Construction and Trust (PACT) and sets out a vision for a $50 billion strategic fund that will back the construction of pipelines, refineries, ports and other major infrastructure needs.

The proposal says that participating countries will contribute investments, but it is unclear which countries will actually put money in. Some Gulf nations are planning to support it, but are not planning an investment at this time, two regional sources told CNN. They were skeptical that the effort would drive an end to the conflict which has recently expanded with the Iranian-backed Houthis engaged in aggressive strikes in the Red Sea and in Saudi Arabia.

The proposal also lists global sovereign wealth funds and firms as “able to invest in individual deals,” listing KKR, Blackstone, KBIC and Global Infrastructure Partners.

The proposal includes a map with some of the new infrastructure proposed, and a timeline stating that initial project announcements would come in January. The proposal was first reported by the Wall Street Journal.

The details of the final proposal could change given the initial documents reviewed by CNN were marked as a draft.

CNN has reached out to the White House for comment.

Trump set to address Iran, cartels and Cuba in UN General Assembly speech, excerpts show

President Donald Trump plans to address Iran, cartels in the western hemisphere and the regime in Cuba in a wide-ranging US speech before world leaders at the UN General Assembly that will center on America’s power in the world, according to excerpts of his speech obtained by CNN.

The president will paint himself himself as someone who has acted to confront conflicts around the world and a leader who has brought global peace, despite his war in Iran causing significant instability in the region and a global energy shortage.

“While others have talked, I have acted. While others have spoken of peace, I have made it. While others have ignored threats, I have confronted them,” his written remarks say. “ I have used it to make America the most powerful country in the world.”

Trump will specifically address the US war with Iran and condemn the country’s former Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, for his actions before the US first launched strikes in Tehran in February.

“In two weeks, we will mark the third anniversary of the October 7th attack in Israel,” Trump is expected to say. “Iran’s Supreme Leader celebrated that massacre and called it a ‘service to humanity.’ The same regime slaughtered over 72,000 of its own citizens this year.”

“Just imagine if such a vile regime were ever empowered to carry out large-scale terror attacks from behind a nuclear shield,” Trump will say, using that as justification for launching the war with Iran. “This was the reality we had to confront — a reality that far too many preferred to ignore,”

The president will also thank the UN Security Council for voting to pass a resolution condemning Iran’s attacks on commercial shipping.

Additionally, Trump will focus some of his remarks on cartels, calling them “the ISIS of the Western Hemisphere.” And he plans to criticize the Cuban regime, claiming they are working in coordination with “left-wing radicals and Communist networks here in the United States.” He specifically claims they have “cultivated ties” to the Communist Party USA, Antifa, and the Democratic Socialists of America.

Rubio says US is looking into potential AI hotline with China

The United States is looking into a potential hotline with China that would allow the two countries to warn each other about dangerous AI incidents, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday.

His comments come after CNN reported last week that a partially AI-generated intelligence report that turned out to be false almost led to military action that could have caused a war between Washington and Beijing.

The top US diplomat said the idea as “nascent” and “a concept” and noted it may not be ready in time for Chinese leader Xi Jinping’s visit on Thursday.

“It’s not a bad idea. It’s one we’ll explore with the Chinese, among others,” Rubio said in an interview with “Fox & Friends.”

Rubio said AI must be addressed globally because “you can’t build a border around AI.”

The top US diplomat said the technology has “huge benefits,” but “it has some potential harm as well that we’re all concerned about.”

“We’re all struggling” with how to develop guardrails given AI’s rapid advancements, Rubio noted.

“What the president has said is yes, if there’s harm that comes from AI, we have to protect our country from it. But we cannot afford to not be at the leading edge of AI because it’s going to happen,” he continued.

President Donald Trump has railed against concerns around AI, but said on Saturday he plans to create an “AI Force” and name an AI czar.

Brazilian president begins address at UN General Assembly

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has started his address to the UN General Assembly in New York.

Lula’s remarks come at a consequential time for his country, which has general elections in October.

The vote is in the hands of a polarized electorate, which will decide between a field of candidates that includes incumbent leader Lula and Flavio Bolsonaro, a son of firebrand former president Jair Bolsonaro, who was charged with leading an attempted coup in 2022.

Lula previously governed Brazil for two consecutive terms between 2003 and 2010 and returned to power in 2022 after facing corruption allegations that led to his imprisonment. The sentences were annulled by Brazil’s Supreme Court.

Brazil has also been on the receiving end of some of President Donald Trump’s economic tariffs, as the US accused Lula’s government of engaging in “unfair” trade practices.

In an interview with CNN’s Christiane Amanpour on Monday, Lula said Trump and the US government “cannot impose their rules” on other countries.

“He has the right to govern. But he doesn’t have the right to govern other countries. He doesn’t have the right to impose his will. He has to have the willingness to negotiate,” Lula said of Trump.

“Fault lines in our world are widening,” outgoing UN chief warns in opening address

UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres has just delivered his opening remarks to kick off today’s General Assembly speeches, warning the “fault lines in our world are widening.”

“Cracks have become canyons,” he told world leaders. “Geopolitical divides have opened and inequalities have intensified.”

Guterres pointed to the potential dangers of artificial intelligence in his speech, amid growing concerns over the controversial technology, as well as the dangers of climate change.

“Humans are increasingly handing power to machines, with artificial agents going rogue,” he said. AI autonomous weapons are going from “science fiction to military reality,” he added.

“Military power alone cannot secure peace,” he said, using his speech to highlight the plight of civilians in conflicts in the Middle East, Sudan, Ukraine and other nations. The General Assembly starts today ahead of Guterres’ exit from his role at year-end.

US will live up to "commitments" with Saudi Arabia amid Houthi escalation, Rubio says

More from US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who said the US will “live up to our commitments” with Saudi Arabia amid an escalation in the region from the Iran-backed Houthis.

“We are working to see if this thing can be fixed but ultimately, if it comes down to it, the United States will always stand with Saudi Arabia and support them because we have an agreement with them to do so,” Rubio told “Fox and Friends” today. He is in New York for the UN General Assembly.

“I can’t discuss in this setting or any public setting all the conversations that are ongoing with Saudi Arabia but suffice it to say that we will live up to our commitments to them and have been,” Rubio said.

Some context: His comments come as the US has yet to take kinetic military action against the Yemeni rebel group, despite requests from the Saudis, but have provided intelligence and targeting support. President Donald Trump, who will meet with Gulf leaders later today, has publicly downplayed the notion of US direct military support.

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Monday he had agreed to provide “defensive air-to-air refueling” for Saudi jets for a limited period, according to Reuters.

The Houthis have made significant gains in recent weeks and have launched strikes into Saudi Arabia.

Rubio noted on Fox that it’s “a dangerous conflict.”

“It’s not just the Houthis, Kataib Hezbollah out of Iraq, which is an Iranian proxy, also fired into Saudi Arabia, and it has shut down their main pipeline. That pipeline needs to get restarted,” he said.

Rubio defends Iran war amid high oil prices and ahead of UNGA meeting

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio defended the war against Iran amid high oil prices and claimed the amount of oil transiting through the Strait of Hormuz is “increasing dramatically.”

The top US diplomat acknowledged that the prices are “very high” and “anyone who’s concerned about prices itself is not wrong,” but argued that the prices “would be double” if Iran had a nuclear weapon. Rubio has repeatedly said the US is fighting the war to prevent Tehran from having a nuclear weapon.

Rubio said the US military had “worked very hard” to clear the Strait of Hormuz of mines and said the “enormous majority” of ships have been able to transit the critical waterway.

He also attributed price increases to the attack on Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline.

“The Houthis attacked a Saudi pipeline, and the Saudis had to shut down that pipeline, so there’s still oil in the system, but the markets are reacting to the expectation that there will not be as much Saudi oil in the future,” Rubio said.

“It’s not Iran directly, it’s the Houthis, but the Houthis are an Iranian proxy,” he said.

Trump told advisers he'd like to meet Iranian officials if "conditions are right"

There is also some skepticism about whether the officials present in New York have the confidence of Tehran’s Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei, who is viewed as the ultimate decision maker, the official said.

Waltz on Wednesday told CNN’s Kaitlan Collins that Trump “is always open” to dialogue.

“That’s a question I wish you could ask the Iranians is, will they sit down directly with us? And who is speaking for the ayatollah? That’s been a continuous issue,” Waltz said.

Iran attending UNGA in New York, even as peace with US remains elusive

A slimmed-down Iranian delegation is in New York this week for the United Nations General Assembly, as US President Donald Trump touts the possibility of “very big things” ahead amid the war in the Middle East.

A founding member of the UN, Iran has maintained a presence at the global diplomatic event for decades, led most recently by the country’s president, Masoud Pezeshkian, and his predecessor, Ebrahim Raisi.

But the presence of Iran’s delegation this year, on the home turf of a country with which it remains at war, is unprecedented in recent years.

Which Iranian officials are allowed to attend?

The Trump administration has said it will allow Iran’s “core delegation,” which will include Pezeshkian, to come to New York.

This year’s delegation will be smaller than during the 2025 event, months before the US-Iran war started, a State Department spokesperson said Thursday.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi arrived in New York yesterday, according to state media, while Pezeshkian is expected to travel to the US today. The exact makeup of Iran’s wider delegation is not yet known.

No US strikes on Iran have been publicly reported for more than two weeks.

Despite the lull, Trump indicated that major decisions on Iran may be approaching — a statement that risks aggravating tensions between the two nations ahead of the diplomatic gathering.

The Palestinian Authority has been banned from entering the US again. Here's more about it

The US has, for a second consecutive year, banned officials from the Palestinian Authority (PA) from entering the country for the United Nations General Assembly. PA President Mahmoud Abbas will once again have to speak at this week’s event virtually.

The US State Department said last week that it would not be issuing visas to PA officials, citing “ongoing activities that undermine the prospects for peace” in what it called the “Israeli-Palestinian Conflict.”

Among these activities are attempts to “internationalize” the conflict, including through the International Criminal Court and International Court of Justice, as well as “continuing to pay stipends to terrorists and to glorify acts of terrorism and terrorists in public pronouncements and school textbooks,” the State Department said.

The Palestinian Foreign Ministry called the US’ actions “unjustified,” saying that they undermine “efforts to rebuild trust, strengthen Palestinian-American relations, and advance the two-state solution.”

The Palestinian Authority was formed in 1993. Though it is supposed to govern over the West Bank and Gaza, in recent decades, the militant group Hamas has run the Gaza Strip, according to the Council on Foreign Relations (CFR).

The PA is headquartered in the West Bank, operating from the city of Ramallah. It is comprised of several Palestinian factions, but excludes militant groups such as Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad, CFR says.

Abbas is deeply unpopular among Palestinians. He has led the PA since the death of Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat in 2004 and has clung to power, despite being seen by critics as lacking democratic legitimacy.

In a 2025 letter, Abbas condemned the Hamas-led October 7, 2023 attack on Israel and pledged to hold elections and reform the PA, according to excerpts shared with CNN.

CNN’s Ibrahim Dahman, Nadeen Ebrahim and Abeer Salman contributed to this reporting.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said a new drone agreement is ready to be signed as he arrived in New York for the first day of the United Nations General Assembly, underscoring Kyiv’s push to strengthen its defense capabilities as the war with Russia grinds on.

He did not specify which nations would be involved in the drone deal.

He said Ukraine is also seeking to strengthen its air defenses and secure sufficient financing from partners.

UK PM Andy Burnham to hold high-stakes first meeting with Trump

Britain’s newly installed Prime Minister Andy Burnham will hold a high-stakes first meeting with US President Donald Trump today on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly.

While the two men have spoken on the phone previously, their first face-to-face meeting marks an important diplomatic test for the center-left Burnham, who roundly criticized Trump before he took office. That, combined with Trump’s notorious unpredictability and habit of wading into sensitive political issues like immigration, the prospect of Irish reunification or whether Britain should open new oil and gas fields in the North Sea, makes this a potentially tricky meeting.

Still, Burnham assured reporters Monday that he trusted Trump personally, adding that the “special relationship” between the US and UK remained strong.

“When you come into this job, you realize how deep this relationship is,” he said, according to Britain’s PA News Agency. “We trust our allies. Of course we do.”

Burnham was previously the mayor of Manchester, a city in northern England, and he has tempered his tone towards Trump since becoming prime minister two months ago. He even brushed off Trump belittling his previous job as “the mayor of a town.”

“Now the important thing is to focus on the moment you are in,” Burnham said. “And I did say to the president in our first phone call, I said, ‘Mr. President, Manchester isn’t some town,’ I said, ‘This is Manchester.’

“And so yes, the right thing to do is to have a laugh about it, isn’t it, and move beyond it.”

Before the meeting, Downing Street emphasized the defense cooperation between London and Washington, announcing that the UK successfully fired a US torpedo from a British-made undersea drone, the first time either country had fired a torpedo from an uncrewed, underwater vessel.
