WHAT IS AN INVEST DURING HURRICANE SEASON?

Few places are more vulnerable than the area around Cape Hatteras in the Outer Banks, where a decades-long battle against coastal erosion has reached a crisis point, with 32 homes collapsing into the ocean since 2020. 

A mandatory evacuation is in effect on Hatteras Island as storm conditions are expected to worsen, with the potential for significant ocean overwash.

The main concern for the Outer Banks region will be prolonged periods of wind gusts over 40 mph, fueling moderate coastal flooding and destructive waves.

Parts of North Carolina Highway 12, the primary route through the Outer Banks, are closed due to flooding. The highway is frequently overwashed during coastal storms.

The worst of the flooding will stretch from the Outer Banks into Chesapeake Bay and up through Ocean City, Maryland, where water levels are expected to rise more than two feet above normal during high tide.

WHAT IS COASTAL EROSION AND WHY IS IT DANGEROUS?

The highest waves and greatest coastal flooding risk are expected during afternoon and evening high tides, which occur between 3 and 7 p.m., depending on the location.

According to the FOX Forecast Center, the worst impacts will last through late Wednesday evening and into Thursday.

Coastal flood alerts are active across coastal North Carolina and Virginia.

93L could develop into Tropical Storm or Subtropical Storm Josephine

Meanwhile, the nor'easter is being given medium odds of becoming Tropical Storm or Subtropical Storm Josephine over the next two days.

Tropical systems are mainly powered by warm ocean water, with thunderstorms around a defined center, while a subtropical storm gets its energy both from warm water, as well as the clash between warm and cold air, similar to a typical low pressure storm that generates thunderstorms.

WHAT IS A TROPICAL STORM?

"As the nor'easter begins to turn out to sea late tomorrow, it will still be over unusually warm water," FOX Weather Hurricane Specialist Bryan Norcross said.

"It will be a robust storm at that time, and the fronts that originally powered it – the contrast of unseasonably cold and unusually warm air – will be fading away."

The system is eventually expected to track farther into the open Atlantic toward Newfoundland, Canada, at the end of the week.
