Major change at Bunnings, Target and Big W as retailers embrace 60-minute trend

TheTools · 20.10.2026 04:05 · source · permalink

Customers can now have non-grocery items delivered to their door in under an hour. But Professor Nitika Garg warned that there are some downsides to be cautious about before shopping.

A growing number of big-name Australian retailers are now available on delivery apps, promising to deliver items to customers' doors within 60 minutes.

Big W became the latest to join the trend this week when its products rolled out on DoorDash, joining Target and Bunnings, which are on Uber Eats.

Some might wonder why anyone would need a Target T-shirt delivered in an hour, but UNSW consumer expert Professor Nitika Garg told Yahoo Lifestyle why retailers are going down this route.

"Customers are choosing where they'd rather spend their time," she said.

"Do you want to spend time going to Bunnings or Big W, or would you rather spend time with the family or relaxing?"

She rejected the idea that it came down to laziness or consumer over consumption.

Even though these retailers already have in-house home delivery offers, offering 60-minute delivery is more for people who are really time poor and in a crunch.

Bunnings' move makes the most sense, as it can save tradespeople from leaving the job site to dip into the hardware store for a screw or tool.

Sydney tradie Connor Wood told Yahoo Lifestyle he's had to duck to Bunnings multiple times in a day, and said 60-minute delivery would be a lifesaver in some scenarios.

Tradie Connor Wood said having items delivered to site through Uber Eats would be a game-changer.

But there's an everyday need that might explain why Target and Big W have joined the fold.

If you've just smashed a few dinner plates and have people coming over in two hours, you might not be able to dash to the store, possibly with the kids in tow, to replace them.

Nitika added that Aussies might be far more familiar with Uber Eats or Doordash.

"The brands are trying to leverage how consumers might not want to learn the delivery mechanism for each individual retail store, even if they offer delivery," she said.

"So the brands get more business and the consumers get more convenience."

The cost of convenience as Bunnings, Target, Big W join delivery apps

Retail delivery has been getting faster and faster in recent years. What might normally have taken a week to arrive at your door, can now get there either the same day you pay or the following day.

A 2026 Australia Post report found half of Aussies now expect this type of service when the item is urgent or tied to an occasion.

Gen Z (49 per cent) and Millennial (46 per cent) shoppers far prefer speed over certainty that it will arrive compared to Gen X (30 per cent) and Baby Boomers (13 per cent).

But speed isn't free.

UNSW consumer expert Professor Nitika Garg said there is a big cost for convenience.

Not only do you have to pay for 60-minute delivery, but the products can be more expensive than the normal shelf price, as that happens when you're buying supermarket items through these platforms.

"Customers need to keep that in mind and ask themselves at what point does it become not feasible?" Nitika told Yahoo Lifestyle.

"You want to check that you're not getting a double hit in terms of paying the delivery charge, service fee, and inflated prices," Nitika said.

But she added that people who are in a crunch will usually pay the premium to the cost of convenience.

What does Bunnings, Target, Big W offer on delivery apps?

Bunnings promises items can arrive in less than 60 minutes, and more than 30,000 products are available to purchase through Uber Eats.

The offer, which launched in February, was originally available at 15 stores, but the plan is to roll it out across Australia and New Zealand.

Target became Australia's first fashion retailer to jump on the trend at the start of October, and claimed 25,000 items can be selected through the same app.

Aussies can now get tens of thousands of items delivered to their door in an hour thanks to a new trend.

"It complements, rather than replaces, our existing channels and gives customers another option alongside shopping in-store or through Target's existing online offer," Bernard Wilson, chief customer officer for Kmart and Target, told Yahoo Australia.

Target products on Uber Eats will be listed at the same price as they are found in store, and will be available at select stores before an expected national rollout.

Big W has listed 156 stores on DoorDash, and said 35,000 items can be purchased, with two-thirds reportedly costing less than $20.

These retailers believe the service will especially come in handy during the busy Christmas period.
