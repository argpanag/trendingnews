A century of rivalry renewed as Black Sticks host fired-up India

TheTools · 19.11.2026 03:04 · source · permalink

While cricket might be dominating the sporting conversation between New Zealand and India this month, there's another sport that’s celebrating a century of competition between the two countries.

One hundred years after an Indian hockey team first toured New Zealand, the Black Sticks Men are preparing to renew their rivalry in their upcoming three-match series.

The games will be held at Auckland’s Lloyd Elsmore Park, the opener taking place on Thursday, before a weekend double-header on Saturday and Sunday.

All of the games will be aired live and free on TVNZ+.

The series has been aimed at “celebrating the history and friendship” between the two nations, with a focus on emerging players and creating a “vibrant and multicultural” occasion.

But they’re certain to be a competitive edge for the visitors, after the Black Sticks stunned the eight-time Olympic champions at their own World Cup back in 2023.

Captain Nic Woods admits they are bracing for a backlash from the tourists.

New Zealand play India in a three-match series with a unique historical edge. (Source: 1News)

“I think they do want to get one back over us,” Woods told 1News.

“It will be pretty feisty I’d assume. Even though it’s a friendly, no match out there is going to be easygoing.”

India’s army hockey side became the first India sports team to tour New Zealand back in 1926.

One of the selectors of the host team that year was Sidney Holland, who would go on to become a two-term Prime Minister of New Zealand, taking office from 1949 to 1957.

The hockey and cricket teams are two of eight Indian representative sides touring New Zealand as part of the century festivities.

Their volleyball and lawn bowls teams are also in the mix, while the India football team also just wrapped up their two-match series against the All Whites, losing both matches convincingly.

Even India's rugby bosses are here, with former halfback turned Bollywood star Rahul Bose now on a sevens mission.

“For the last 70 years we've only had one team that's made it to the Olympics, that's hockey,” Bose said.

“(Rugby sevens) would like to be the next team in eight years to be in the Olympics.”

NZ Head of Sport Diplomacy Peter Miskimmin says the initiative has been beneficial for both countries.

“We know India’s very keen to be a powerhouse of world sport,” Miskimmin said.

“They’re having the next Commonwealth Games, they're bidding for the next Olympics in 2036.

“They look to little old New Zealand and and go ‘gee you're pretty good at sport, we can come and learn from you’.

“But equally the commercialisation of sport in India that we in New Zealand can learn from.”
