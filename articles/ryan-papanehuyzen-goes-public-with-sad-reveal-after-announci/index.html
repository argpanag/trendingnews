Ryan Papanehuyzen goes public with sad reveal after announcing NRL retirement

TheTools · 17.09.2026 01:12 · source · permalink

The Melbourne Storm champion has lifted the lid on his decision and a regret he has.

Former Melbourne Storm player Ryan Papenhuyzen has opened up on a sad regret he holds about the manner in which he told his teammates of his NRL retirement. The 28-year-old stunned the NRL world at the end of last season when he announced he was quitting the sport.

In January he confirmed that he's retired and won't play NRL ever again. Papenhuyzen has since embarked on a career as a professional golf caddie and will be part of Channel 7's broadcast team for the Rugby League World Cup in October and November.

Ryan Papenhuyzen has opened up on his decision to retire from NRL. Image: Getty/Channel 9

The Melbourne Storm champion made an appearance on the 'Freddy and the Eighth' podcast on Wednesday with Andrew Johns, Brad Fittler and Danika Mason. Nine months after deciding to retire, he admitted some regrets about the way he told his teammates of his decision.

Ryan Papenhuyzen has been playing a lot of golf and has become a pro caddie. (Photo by Mark Brake/Getty Images)

Papenhuyzen said he only told the Storm after the grand final that he was walking away because he didn't want it to be a distraction. He initially only wanted to have a 12-month break before coming to a more definitive decision, but quickly realised he wanted to retire permanently.

"It was more like I wanted to have a year away from footy and see how I feel," he said. "I sit here now and i'm pretty content with my decision (to retire permanently).

"I probably didn't go about it the best way - I messaged the group (of players instead of speaking face-to-face). If I could have my time back I would probably go about it a bit differently."

Ryan Papenhuyzen won't be returning to NRL

Papenhuyzen has continually stated he has no desire to return to professional rugby league. And he reiterated that point on Wednesday when asked if he still has any drive to compete.

"I think my drive has gone into different areas now," he said. "It served its purpose for footy and I got a lot out of it and met some great people and had some good opportunities. But I'm looking at the next path now and really enjoying that."

He confessed he misses the excitement of playing finals footy, but doesn't miss the hard work and constant training that goes into making the finals. The 2020 Clive Churchill Medallist said he hasn't fully committed to what he wants to do next and is keeping his options open on what he described as a "gap year".
