Can PLTR Stock Live Up To Its Multiple?

TheTools · 28.08.2026 23:48 · source · permalink

This is a hyper-growth chapter for Palantir Technologies. The company now provides a complete sovereign AI stack, with its AIP platform as the core engine for government and commercial clients.

This explosive U.S. growth is creating immense demand that strains the company’s ability to scale. In response, management is now actively searching for technically competent partners to help meet this demand.

That’s the story the market is currently paying 147.9x trailing earnings for. Has it taken the multiple too far, or is the growth implied by today’s price reasonable? Let’s unpack below.

Before we get into the math behind that valuation, PLTR‘s current numbers are worth keeping in mind as a reference point:

LTM refers to last twelve months.

What The Price Is Asking For

To defend PLTR’s $446.2B market cap over the next 7 years, three things have to play out. The multiple settles from today’s 147.9x toward 28.8x, the multiple a mature, scaled software franchise typically settles into. Margins land near 35%, set near the midpoint of the company’s own margin cycle, since its peak clears mature peers but its through-cycle average sits well below that peak. And revenue compounds from $6.2B today to $44.3B at maturity, supporting $15.5B of annual net income. That last line works out to a required revenue CAGR of 33%, below the 79% the business is currently running.

Is This Realistic?

Growth is being driven by accelerating adoption in the U.S. commercial segment. Existing customers are also expanding their use from the Foundry platform to the full Ontology and AI stack. The company is also entering the market for fine-tuning models, expanding its role beyond orchestration.

The company’s current growth and margins are at a cyclical peak, far above its historical average. Management has already signaled a significant expense ramp for the coming quarter due to new hiring. This spending, combined with a heavy reliance on the booming U.S. market, creates risk of margin reversion if growth moderates.

Today’s price needs less growth than the business is currently delivering. But that current pace is a cyclical peak, not a sustainable rate. In a normal year, the bar is meaningfully higher.

The challenge is finding enough competent partners to scale its U.S. boom before cyclical pressures cause margins to revert.
