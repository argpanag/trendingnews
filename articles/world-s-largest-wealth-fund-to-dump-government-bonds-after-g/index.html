World’s largest wealth fund to dump government bonds after global sell-off

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:17 · source · permalink

The world’s largest sovereign wealth fund is planning to sell off around £90bn of government bonds amid fears of a growing crisis in public finances.

Norway’s state pension fund, which is worth 21.8tn kroner (£1.7tn), said it was considering cutting the proportion of government debt held in its bond portfolio from 70pc to 50pc.

The proposal from Norges Bank Investment Management, which manages the fund, would reduce its government bond holdings by around £90bn.

Bonds have dropped sharply as governments borrow vast sums on financial markets, with major economies including the US, France and Japan all seeing their debt load outweigh GDP.

Norges said in a letter to Norway’s ministry of finance that it wanted to put the money freed up by selling bonds into riskier assets to boost returns over time.

Bosses said: “A government share of 50pc will be sufficient to cover the liquidity needs, including in periods of turbulence in financial markets.

“The remaining part of the bond index should provide exposure to more sources of risk premiums.”

The Norwegian Government Pension Fund Global, known as the “oil fund”, holds around 1.5pc of all listed companies, making it the world’s largest single investor.

Its decision to shift away from government debt in its bond portfolio risks pushing up borrowing costs for taxpayers, as lower demand will allow debt investors to call for higher returns.

Government borrowing costs surged earlier this week amid a major sell-off by investors, with the yield on benchmark 10-year UK gilts hitting the highest level since 2007.

The Treasury offered buyers of inflation-linked debt the highest returns since 2001 during an auction on Thursday.

Bond markets are expected to remain on edge before Andy Burnham’s first Budget next month and as central banks face pressure to increase interest rates over the Iran war.

Norway’s oil fund, which began investing in 1998, proposed keeping its total proportion of UK debt at about 4.2pc.

However, it plans a sharp reduction in its holdings of US debt from 34.1pc of its government bond portfolio to 21.9pc.

The US 10-year Treasury yield, considered a global benchmark for the cost of borrowing, rose above 4.75pc for the first time since January 2025 on Monday.

On top of concerns about rising inflation, bond investors must also contend with soaring government debt levels, which topped $40tn (£30tn) in the US for the first time last month.

Nicolas Bickel, head of investment at Edmond de Rothschild, said the impact could be significant because of “the message it sends about the value of holding Treasury notes in a long-term investment portfolio”.

He added: “Inflation and fiscal policy environments have changed, and this will continue to affect long-term interest rates, which we expect to remain higher for a longer period of time.”

Kevin Thozet, a member of the investment committee at Carmignac, said Norges Bank was unlikely to rapidly sell its US Treasury bonds, and would instead run down the portfolio in stages.

He added: “The signal is strong and reflective of a deep-seated trend. For more than five years, developed sovereign bond markets (and notably in the US) have been delivering very poor returns.”
