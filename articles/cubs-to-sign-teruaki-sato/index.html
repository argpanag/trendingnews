Cubs To Sign Teruaki Sato

TheTools · 24.11.2026 17:17 · source · permalink

The Cubs have agreed to a deal with slugger Teruaki Sato, reports Jeff Passan of ESPN. It’s a six-year, $120MM pact. Michael Cerami of Bleacher Nation was the first to report that the two sides were nearing an agreement. Sato is represented by VC Sports Group.

Chicago has dropped $240MM in the span of 12 hours on two massive additions. The club signed left-hander Trevor Rogers last night, and now locks up one of the top bats in the free agent market. Sato won the Triple Crown in NPB last season, slashing .306/.383/.609 with 39 home runs. The 27-year-old is expected to play corner outfield for the Cubs, notes Passan.

Sato has mostly played third base with the Hanshin Tigers, but he does have experience in the outfield. He’s made 44 starts in right field over the past two seasons. The Cubs entered the offseason with multiple voids to fill alongside Pete Crow-Armstrong with longtime outfielders Seiya Suzuki and Ian Happ hitting free agency.

Suzuki and Randy Arozarena were the only hitters ranked above Sato in our Top 50 Free Agents list. The two-time Central League MVP has taken his game to another level over the past two years, piling up 79 homers and 208 RBI. He scored 100 runs for the first time in his career this past season. Sato has had a strikeout rate around 27% in each of the last three seasons. The swing-and-miss is the main concern as he transitions to MLB, though the issue isn’t as severe as it was for fellow NPB import Munetaka Murakami, who was able to hit the ground running with the White Sox.

Sato will join the top offense in the game. The Cubs comfortably paced the league in scoring this past season. His addition, plus the losses of Suzuki and Happ, will shift the balance of the lineup. The lefty-swinging Sato will replace a right-handed bat in Suzuki. He should be near the top of the order with fellow lefties Crow-Armstrong and Michael Busch. Righties Alex Bregman and Nico Hoerner likely slot somewhere between the lefty contingent.

The final cost will be more than the reported $120MM. Chicago will also owe a posting fee to Hanshin. Based on the size of Sato’s deal, the number heading to the Tigers will be around $20MM.

The internal replacements for Suzuki and Happ include Pedro Ramirez, Matt Shaw, and Kevin Alcantara. With Sato aboard, the Cubs will have to lean on that trio for just one spot in the outfield. The club could also look for another addition. Shaw got a brief look as the everyday right fielder to begin 2026 with Suzuki tending to an injury. He faded to a backup role once Suzuki was healthy. Ramirez was solid in his first taste of the majors, but the majority of his opportunities came in the infield. He spent a total of one inning on the grass.

The Cubs also need to fill at-bats at DH. The team rotated through options at that spot, with no player getting more than 170 plate appearances there. Suzuki, Michael Conforto, and Moises Ballesteros led the roster in DH reps. None of them are in Chicago anymore.

The Blue Jays, Padres, Phillies, Diamondbacks, Yankees, Rangers, and Tigers were known to have at least some level of interest in Sato. Those teams will have to head back to the drawing board to find an impact bat. Suzuki and Arozarena are the top prizes left. Brandon Lowe, Luis Arraez, Jazz Chisholm Jr., and Happ are in the next tier. The corner infield market is relatively thin beyond Ty France. Alec Bohm, Mauricio Dubon, and Rhys Hoskins are among the top remaining options for teams.
