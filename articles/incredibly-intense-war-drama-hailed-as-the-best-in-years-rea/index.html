"Incredibly intense" war drama hailed as 'the best in years' reaches number one on Netflix

TheTools · 26.11.2026 16:56 · source · permalink

It's been compared to Saving Private Ryan.

A new war film starring Masters of the Universe's Nicholas Galitzine and It actor Bill Skarsgård has been climbing the streaming charts, landing at number one on Netflix.

Inspired by the real events leading up to the Battle of Okinawa during World War II, The Mosquito Bowl follows four American college football players who enlist in the Marines during the global conflict.

Galitzine plays John McLaughry alongside Skarsgård as Bob Bauman, with Smile 2 star Ray Nicholson and Jay Kelly's Tom Francis taking on the roles of Tony Butkovich and Dave Schreiner, respectively.

The quartet enlists following Pearl Harbor in 1941, with the film's title referring to a football game they play on Christmas Eve 1944, just before the Battle of Okinawa would claim lives on either side.

Directed by Peter Berg, who co-wrote the movie with Mark L Smith, The Mosquito Bowl is based on the 2022 book The Mosquito Bowl: A Game of Life and Death in World War II by Buzz Bissinger.

What to Read Next

Currently the most-watched film on Netflix UK, following a limited cinema release on these shores, The Mosquito Bowl has been hailed as "the best war film in years" by a fan on IMDb.

"The combat sequences are outstanding - raw and brutal", they wrote, also noting that the performances are "solid".

Another viewer labelled The Mosquito Bowl an "incredibly intense movie" and likened it to Steven Spielberg's Saving Private Ryan.

"The combat is visceral and impressively staged, while Nicholas Galitzine, Bill Skarsgård, and Ray Nicholson deliver strong performances that give the story real emotional weight", wrote someone else.

The film fared less well on Rotten Tomatoes, where it has a meagre critic score of 38% from 53 reviews - a sharp contrast with an audience rating of 90% on the same aggregator.

The Observer called it a "dispiriting viewing experience" due to the graphic nature of some scenes and the "sludgy, desaturated cinematography that casts a corpse-like pallor over everything".

Meanwhile, Empire praised it as "a beautifully shot account of American brotherhood" whose depiction of war "shocks like an ice bath".

The Mosquito Bowl is streaming now on Netflix.
