Pie firm crumbles owing $5.8m

TheTools · 25.09.2026 07:24 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Collapsed West Auckland pie distributor the Westie Food Group owes creditors $5.8 million, with receivers warning a shortfall in the millions is likely.

Receivers Stephen Keen and Stephen White of Teneo were appointed by BNZ to Westie Food Group (WFG) on July 15 after the company defaulted on the terms of its loan agreement.

Teneo’s first report into the Avondale-based company said it had been involved in the pie trade.

“The company operated as an Auckland-based distributor of chilled pies, savouries and ready-to-eat convenience foods, supplying about 700 supermarkets throughout New Zealand,” the report said.

The company’s best-known product was Westie Pies.

The report noted WFG only marketed and distributed the pies, with an “independent third-party manufacturer operating from the same premises” responsible for baking and packaging.

Receivers said WFG failed after “experiencing cashflow issues, and its creditor position was worsening”.

Receivers withheld the value of company assets – aside from noting two company vehicles had been sold yielding $35,294 – but said there would likely be a shortfall to creditors owed a total of $5.9m.

Secured creditors were owed $2.2m, including $1.2m due to appointer BNZ.

Preferential creditors – employees and Inland Revenue – were owed $337,985.

Unsecured creditors were listed as being owed $3.3m, and were warned a full write-off was likely.

Receivers anticipated being able to settle all sums owed to preferential creditors, and part-repayment to BNZ.

“However, as the asset realisation process is not complete, it is not possible to provide an estimate on the level of funds available,” receivers said.

“Distribution to other [unsecured] creditors are not envisaged at this stage.”

The collapse of WFG occurred at the same time a related food company also ran into solvency issues.

On July 15, the same day receivers were appointed to WFG, liquidators were also appointed to Good Vibes Company.

WFG and Good Vibes share director and shareholder Stefan Crooks.

Crooks was listed as the managing director of WFG.

Stefan Crooks did not respond to Herald requests for comment.

Good Vibes was incorporated in 1992 and traded as a school lunch manufacturer. It also operated several school canteens.

Good Vibes had been a provider for the controversial Healthy School Lunches programme until December 2025.

Last year Labour MP Carmel Sepuloni said Crooks had been “one of the big Auckland lunch in school providers” but had lost much of his business with recent changes in government policy, and “65 people from Westie Pies were laid off as a result of the cancelling of their contract”.

Liquidators Steven Khov and Kieran Jones’ first report into Good Vibes reported creditors were owed more than $8m – including $2.3m owed to BNZ.
