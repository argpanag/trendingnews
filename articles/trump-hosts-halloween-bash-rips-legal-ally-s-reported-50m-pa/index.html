Trump hosts Halloween bash, rips legal ally's reported $50M payment

TheTools · 01.11.2026 21:06 · source · permalink

The jungle-theme gala at Mar-a-Lago kicked off the winter social season at the Palm Beach club on Saturday after Trump spoke at six tele-rallies.

  • President Donald Trump attended a jungle-themed Halloween gala at his Mar-a-Lago club.
  • Trump criticized a former attorney for receiving $50 million to secure a pardon for a Venezuelan billionaire.
  • He also commented on reports of U.S. surveillance of Nvidia's CEO, Jensen Huang.
  • Before the party, Trump held political rallies and "tele-rallies" and touted economic investments in Florida.

An extravagant Halloween gala was sandwiched between political rallies and campaign speeches during President Donald Trump's 30th stay at Mar-a-Lago this term.

The jungle-theme gala at the Palm Beach club kicked off its winter social season. Videos and images on social media showed the ballroom location decorated with emerald floodlights and leafy table centerpieces. Attendees sported cheetah-, leopard- and tiger-print dinner jackets, with others in a variety of safari and other costumes.

Trump joined the party following six 30-minute scheduled "tele-rallies" ahead of the Tuesday, Nov. 3, midterm elections and told reporters he was “feeling good” about GOP U.S. Senate candidates in Michigan, Alaska and Florida, where Republican incumbent Ashley Moody is at the top of the ballot.

Trump, wearing his branded dark blue suit, white shirt and red tie, jovially told reporters he chose to dress as "Donald Trump" for the Halloween party at Mar-a-Lago.

The president, however, bristled about the revelation that one of his former attorneys, Alina Habba, is reported to have pocketed $50 million after securing a pardon for a Venezuelan billionaire indicted in 2022 on bribery charges. Julio Herrera Velutini pleaded guilty to a campaign-finance violation in 2025 before Trump pardoned him.

The president criticized Habba, who served as a U.S. attorney in New Jersey for a brief time in the administration, in speaking with reporters at the Halloween gala.

“It's one thing for a person to get paid legal fees or whatever. She's a lawyer," he said. "I thought it was totally obnoxious and outrageous, and it's not acceptable."

The disclosure follows months of polls showing government corruption in his second presidential admninistration as a surging issue for many voters in the Nov. 3 midterm election.

The revelation also follows Trump's admission he gave a pass to failed Brazilian presidential candidates Flavio Bolsonaro last month to enter the United States, despite Brazilian courts ordering Bolsonaro to surrender his passport in light of a criminal investigation.

Trump, though, spoke favorably of another corporate America ally under an unflattering spotlight. Jensen Huang, the president and CEO of the powerful artificial intelligence chipmaker Nvidia, has been reportedly surveilled by U.S. authorities because of concerns he could be close to someone who might be a Chinese intelligence asset.

“He's a great, brilliant person, and we want to protect our brilliant people. When it comes to chips, there's never been anybody like him," Trump said of Huang, who traveled with the president to a summit in China in May. "So we hope that everything's OK with Jensen. I have a lot of respect for him."

The president began his Halloween with a round of golf at his Trump International Golf Club, not far from his Winter White House. Trump had arrived in the Palm Beaches on Oct. 30 after holding a rally in Daytona Beach.

There, he touted billions of investments that he said his administration is bringing to his adopted home state, including a Johnson & Johnson distribution center in Jacksonville, an expansion to Blue Origin's rocket park at Cape Canaveral and Williams International's aircraft engine factory in Okaloosa County.

Trump also lauded what he counted as 88 "all-time record highs" in the stock market, a boon to Florida retirees and others with 401(k) and other investment accounts.

The president also reminded the audience that his administration removed taxes on tips, which favors the legions of Sunshine State service-industry workers.

Going forward, he said the federal agencies Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac in 2027 "will be investing $40 billion in Florida mortgages to help" as many as 117,000 Floridians purchase homes, while the U.S. Department of Labor "will be providing Florida with $40 million in federal grants to support nearly 10,000 brand new apprenticeships."

The president departed Palm Beach on Sunday, Nov. 1, the final day of early voting in Florida. So far, more than 5.1 million people in the state have voted across the state, including 350,000 in Palm Beach County. 

Trump, who cast an in-person early ballot on Oct. 24, was scheduled to headline rallies in Kansas and Iowa before returning to Washington.
