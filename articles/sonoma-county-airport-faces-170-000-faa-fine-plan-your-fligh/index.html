Sonoma County Airport Faces $170,000 FAA Fine: Plan Your Flight

TheTools · 12.11.2026 20:51 · source · permalink

The FAA proposed a 0,000 fine against Sonoma County Airport over alleged firefighting and wildlife check lapses on March 2025 flights. What it means for you.

The Federal Aviation Administration has proposed a $170,000 civil penalty against Charles M. Schulz Sonoma County Airport in Santa Rosa, California, for allegedly violating aircraft rescue and firefighting and wildlife inspection requirements, according to an FAA announcement released on Nov. 12, 2026. The alleged violations involve two passenger flights in March 2025.

The airport’s emergency staffing had already drawn FAA attention: a 2025 whistleblower complaint led to an investigation and a surprise inspection in which the crew missed a timed response drill on its first try, The Press Democrat reported. For more on flying, see our airlines hub.

  • Airport: Charles M. Schulz Sonoma County Airport (STS).
  • Proposed penalty: $170,000.
  • Allegations: Missing firefighting coverage on two flights; one skipped wildlife check.
  • When: Passenger flights in March 2025.
  • Next step: The airport has 30 days to respond.

The FAA Allegations

The FAA alleges the airport did not provide the aircraft rescue and firefighting personnel and vehicles required by its FAA-approved Airport Certification Manual for two passenger flights, and did not notify the airline that the coverage was missing.

For one of those flights, the airport also allegedly did not inspect the runway for deer and other wildlife before the first flight of the day, as its FAA-approved wildlife hazard management plan requires, the agency said.

The airport has 30 days after receiving the FAA’s enforcement letter to respond. These are proposed penalties for alleged violations, not final findings.

Earlier Staffing Concerns

A whistleblower call to the FAA hotline on June 28, 2025, led to an investigation into whether the airport had enough fire and rescue staff for aircraft emergencies, The Press Democrat reported.

During a surprise inspection on Aug. 6, 2025, the crew missed the three-minute response requirement in a timed runway drill by 1 minute and 26 seconds, then passed on a second attempt, the newspaper said. Airport officials said the airport was safe and met federal staffing standards, and they asked county supervisors to approve two more emergency response positions.

A consultant hired in 2024 had warned that the many duties assigned to operations staff across the airfield could lead to missed response times, according to The Press Democrat.

The newspaper also reported that a fully staffed county fire station at the airport would take about four years to build and cost more than $4 million, which shows why staffing at smaller airports can be hard to expand quickly.

The FAA has not said whether more enforcement is planned, and the airport has not released a public response to the proposed penalty.

Proposed FAA penalties are often settled or reduced after an airport responds, so the final amount could differ from the figure announced.

Flying From Sonoma County

Charles M. Schulz Sonoma County Airport is a small commercial airport serving Santa Rosa and the wine country north of San Francisco, with flights to West Coast cities and a few other destinations.

The proposed fine does not change flight schedules. Airlines continue to operate, and the FAA action concerns how the airport met its own safety plans on specific days in 2025.

Small airports like this one must have firefighting crews and vehicles on duty for scheduled passenger flights. If coverage is missing, the airport is required to tell the airline, which can then decide how to proceed.

Travelers who prefer more flight options can compare the airport with the larger Bay Area airports, which are about an hour or more away by road depending on traffic.

What It Means for Travelers

Travelers flying in and out of Sonoma County should not expect any change to their flights from the proposed $170,000 penalty, but the case shows the FAA is checking that small airports staff firefighting crews for every passenger flight. Check flights on the airport’s website.
