Ticketmaster Keeps Top NFL Resale Role as League Expands Access for Competitors

TheTools · 21.10.2026 15:49 · source · permalink

Home »

The NFL has reached a new multiyear agreement with Ticketmaster that will significantly expand access to verified resale tickets across competing marketplaces while keeping Ticketmaster in its leading role within the league’s ticketing infrastructure.

The agreement announced Wednesday introduces what the NFL calls “Ticketing 3.0,” an expanded open-distribution model designed to give season-ticket holders broader exposure for their listings and allow fans to shop for more verified tickets across multiple authorized platforms.

Under the new arrangement, Ticketmaster will remain the NFL’s exclusive top-tier, or “platinum,” secondary ticketing partner and continue powering the NFL Ticket Exchange. However, other authorized secondary marketplaces will gain access to season-ticket resale inventory from all 32 NFL teams, a significant departure from the current arrangement in which Ticketmaster has exclusive access to that comprehensive inventory.

The changes were approved by NFL owners Wednesday and are expected to be implemented during the upcoming offseason, according to Sports Business Journal, which reported additional details of the agreement.

“The inventory will get broadcast out across the entire network,” NFL Senior Vice President of Club Business Development Bobby Gallo told SBJ, explaining that the expanded distribution will give season-ticket holders access to a wider pool of potential buyers.

The agreement represents an important change in how NFL resale inventory reaches consumers. Rather than requiring season-ticket holders to list separately across different platforms to reach competing audiences, the league says eligible tickets can be listed once and distributed through multiple participating marketplaces.

For ticket buyers, the arrangement means authorized competitors should have access to a broader selection of verified listings, potentially making it easier to compare options across platforms without encountering substantially different inventory.

SeatGeek, which competed with Ticketmaster for the league’s top secondary ticketing designation, has reportedly reached an agreement in principle to participate at a new “gold” partner level, according to SBJ. The agreement has not yet been finalized.

Other companies currently operating within the NFL’s authorized ticketing network include Sports Illustrated Tickets and Tixr. The league added Tixr to its ticketing network in May, expanding the number of companies eligible to work directly with NFL clubs on ticketing services.

Although Ticketmaster will retain its preferred position, the expanded distribution represents a meaningful opportunity for competing marketplaces to reach NFL customers with inventory that previously was not available to them through the league’s full season-ticket resale feed.

It could also have significant implications for the competition for primary ticketing services.

Ticketmaster currently handles primary ticketing for 26 NFL clubs, while SeatGeek serves the other six. However, a source familiar with the situation told TicketNews that additional NFL teams have expressed interest in switching their primary ticketing operations from Ticketmaster to SeatGeek for the 2027 season. Those potential deals depend on the league’s new ticketing arrangements being finalized, the source said. No additional team contracts have been publicly announced.

The agreement does not directly change the league’s primary ticketing operations for events such as the Super Bowl and most international games.

The NFL has operated an open ticketing network since 2017, allowing approved marketplaces to participate in the sale of authenticated tickets. The new agreement goes further by extending access to inventory that had remained concentrated on Ticketmaster’s platform.

The decision also resolves a closely watched round of negotiations over the NFL’s future ticketing structure.

As TicketNews reported in October 2025, league executives had been evaluating potentially sweeping changes to their ticketing arrangements ahead of the expiration of existing agreements in 2027. Options reportedly under consideration included establishing an NFL-owned ticketing platform, gaining greater control over ticketing data and restructuring the relationship between teams and technology providers.

Ultimately, the league has chosen to maintain Ticketmaster’s central role while expanding opportunities for competitors to participate in secondary ticket distribution.

The revised structure is also expected to generate substantially more fee revenue for the NFL than its previous agreement, Gallo told SBJ. Financial terms were not disclosed, although the new agreement will run longer than the expiring four-year contract.

“We’ve created more value through less exclusivity,” Gallo said in the NFL’s official announcement.

For Ticketmaster, the agreement preserves its position as the league’s principal ticketing technology partner, even as competing marketplaces receive greater access to inventory flowing through that infrastructure.

Ticketmaster President Saumil Mehta said the company is focused on extending the league’s verified-ticketing experience across additional sales channels.

“Fans should be able to easily find and buy verified tickets with confidence,” Mehta said.

The arrangement could create more competition among consumer-facing ticket marketplaces, particularly as companies compete on pricing, fees, customer experience and marketing to attract buyers looking at the same inventory.

However, expanded distribution does not automatically mean lower ticket prices or reduced fees. The NFL has not detailed how transaction fees will work across participating marketplaces or whether the new structure will change the overall cost of purchasing resale tickets.

The distinction between broader marketplace access and control of underlying ticketing technology remains important. While competitors will gain access to more inventory, Ticketmaster will continue operating the NFL Ticket Exchange and retain its preferred partnership status.

The league has not announced a specific date for the new distribution system to become operational or identified which additional marketplaces will participate under the revised partner structure.
