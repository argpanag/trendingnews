Aftermath of earthquake at a location given as Nagqu

BEIJING, Oct 23 (Reuters) - The number of people injured in a magnitude 7 earthquake that struck the southwestern region ‌of China's Tibet has risen to 65, local government ‌said in a social media post on Friday, while the death count remained ​unchanged at 16.

The quake, with an epicentre in Nagqu, a sparsely populated, high-altitude city in northern Tibet, is the strongest to hit China since January 2024.

More than 300 police officers including traffic and ‌community police personnel were ⁠sent to the epicentre to carry out rescue and emergency relief work, the Nagqu police department said ⁠in a separate post on messaging app WeChat early on Friday.

Officers sent to the region checked for damage to homes and helped people ​relocate ​to safer locations, the Nagqu police ​also said in the ‌WeChat post.

A video the police posted on TikTok sister app Douyin showed officers packing supplies in a truck in the dark and inspecting a building and rubble with flashlights.

More than 12,000 people have been safely relocated, state media outlet Xinhua reported.

Videos previously released ‌by local official media showed people leaving ​their homes and seeking shelter outside ​or in their cars ​parked on the street. One resident reported being ‌awakened by the strong quake and ​hearing the house ​squeaking during the tremor.

The China Earthquake Networks Center said in a post that 413 aftershocks had been recorded.

In 2010, a ​magnitude 7.1 tremor ‌struck Yushu in neighbouring Qinghai province, killing at least ​2,698 people and leaving nearly 300 people missing.
