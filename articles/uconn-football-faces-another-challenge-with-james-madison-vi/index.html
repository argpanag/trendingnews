UConn football faces another challenge with James Madison visiting Saturday: How to watch

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:08 · source · permalink

Breaking News

With three games remaining, the UConn football team needs to win two in order to clinch bowl eligibility for a third year in a row and the first under new head coach Jason Candle.

It won’t be easy with James Madison next up on Saturday at Rentschler Field, followed by Old Dominion and a road trip to Wyoming for the season finale, but it isn’t impossible, either.

The Dukes, 8-2, have one of the most explosive offenses in the Group of Six with multiple capable quarterbacks, and an above-average defense that is one of the best in bringing pressure at the line of scrimmage.

UConn has lost five consecutive games in the all-time series with JMU, including a 44-6 whooping on the road in 2023.

“Tough times here in November. This is the part of the season where it begins to become a grind and we’re still challenging our players to make sure that they have a great attitude toward improvement, not approval,” Candle said. “Every program in the country’s got the same challenges and we’ve got to do a great job of making sure we focus on what needs to be focused on, and that’s to continue to improve and get better.”

Turnovers sunk the Huskies as they suffered their second-straight loss last week, falling to Bill Belichick and North Carolina, 38-21, after a pair of interceptions and a lost fumble in the rain from Kalieb Osborne.

JMU won’t make it easy on him, either. The Dukes rank No. 12 nationally with 28 sacks on the year and No. 19 with 61 tackles for loss, with linebacker Nakian Jackson leading the way with 71 tackles and seven TFLs this season. Fellow linebacker JT Hooten has 66 tackles and a team-best 6.0 sacks, while cornerback DJ Kelly has made four interceptions on the year, good for sixth-most nationally.

“I think (Osborne) is doing a great job every game, just improving, getting up. You make mistakes, just keep playing. I think he does a great job responding when things don’t go his way,” said tight end Xavier Watson, who has been one of the top targets with 11 catches for 153 yards and a touchdown this year.

“(James Madison), they try to do a lot of different things to try to confuse you. … That’s something that we’re gonna game plan for and be ready for. I think we’ll do a good job with that.”

In his first year as a starter and seeing consistent playing time, presnap reads and decision making have been part of the learning process for Osborne. But, while there have been mistakes, there have been highlights, too. And he has shown improvement as the season’s gone on. Saturday will reveal how far he and the offense still has to go.

“With the opportunities he’s gotten, the mistakes he’s made, the highlight plays he’s made, all learning tools and all valuable lessons if he’ll just pay attention to the tape,” Candle said. “Sometimes you can get caught in the trap as a coach just sitting there watching the other team (on tape) and you get down in this rabbit hole, you never come out. … Well, watching your own team play probably tells a better story of where things are. You have to really do a great job of making sure that you don’t lose the self scout part of it.”

The Huskies’ defense, No. 99 of 138 in the FBS in terms of total yardage allowed (392.3 yards per game), No. 68 against the run (142.56) and No. 121 against the pass (249.8), will have its hands full Saturday.

James Madison ranks 16th nationally in total yards per game, averaging 455.6, behind a standout running back in George Pettaway, who has missed the Dukes’ last two games with a lower body injury and could potentially return Saturday. His 824 rushing yards on the year rank No. 20 nationally despite missing action. Senior Seth Cromwell (75 carries, 370 yards, three touchdowns) has stepped up in his absence.

Coach Billy Napier has also used two different quarterbacks in JC Evans and Camden Coleman, but it seems as though Evans, who won the starting job out of camp and was injured in Week 1, has returned to the lead role. He has completed 45 of 62 passes (72.6%) on the year for 621 yards, six touchdowns and an interception, while running 47 times for 162 yards.

“Every game’s an opportunity,” said UConn sophomore defensive lineman Zakaih Saez. “We got something to prove every game. … You can’t dwell on the past. The past happened, we can’t go back and change what we did last week. Now we got a new week, another opportunity to get better, some way, somehow, any way you can.”

Candle, who has never had a losing season in 10 years as a college head coach, isn’t allowing himself to look too far forward as far as bowl eligibility goes.

“There’s too many challenges on the schedule to look down the road and try to forecast and foreshadow what that’s going to look like,” he said. “That’s for social media to have fun with. We have a really important game this weekend that we have to win.”

What to know

Site: Rentschler Field, East Hartford

Time: Noon

TV: CBS Sports Network – Chris Lewis, Doug Pederson, Hailey Sutton

Friday morning line: James Madison by 4.5

Radio: UConn Sports Network, Fox Sports 97.9 – Mike Crispino, Wayne Norman, Adam Giardino

Record: UConn: 4-5, James Madison: 8-2

Series: James Madison leads, 5-1.

Last meeting: Nov. 11, 2023 – James Madison 44, UConn 6 at Bridgeforth Stadium in Harrisonburg, Va.
