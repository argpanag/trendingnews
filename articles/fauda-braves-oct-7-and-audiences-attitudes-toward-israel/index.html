‘Fauda’ Braves Oct. 7, and Audiences’ Attitudes Toward Israel

TheTools · 07.09.2026 09:04 · source · permalink

Supported by

The new season of the Israeli hit depicts the Hamas attacks and aftermath. It arrives as Israel’s artists face resistance overseas precisely because of how the country responded to Oct. 7.

Lior Raz, the star and co-creator of “Fauda,” the hit Netflix series about a team of Israeli undercover anti-terrorist operatives, was in Romania for a speaking engagement when he woke up to a phone call from his sister.

“I need you to save me,” she told him. It was the morning of Oct. 7, 2023, and she was calling from a bomb shelter in a kibbutz along the Gaza border.

She eventually made it to safety on her own. But two nights later, Raz and his writing partner, Avi Issacharoff — veterans of a military unit much like the one the series depicts — were among scores of volunteers lined up at a rendezvous point in southern Israel to receive the names and numbers of people in border communities who still needed evacuating.

It had come to this: As rockets from Gaza continued to bombard the area, attackers remained at large and the Israeli government was M.I.A., the two television writers raced around in the dark rescuing strangers in Raz’s SUV like Uber drivers in the apocalypse.

One woman they picked up took one look at Raz and seemed to momentarily forget what was going on around her, Issacharoff recalled: “She says, ‘Oh my God, it’s Doron from “Fauda!”’ and wants a selfie.”

“Fauda” easily could have ended with the release, nine months earlier, of its fourth season, which had closed on a grim shot of its main characters splayed on the ground, badly wounded, after an entirely fictional battle that went bad. The creators deliberately left the ending ambiguous because they weren’t sure if they wanted the series to continue, they said.
