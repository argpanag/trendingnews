  1. 'A truly remarkable woman': NSPCC chief pays tribute to Esther Rantzenpublished at 19:49 BST
    19:49 BST

    Dame Esther Rantzen has left an "extraordinary legacy", NSPCC chief executive Chris Sherwood has said following the broadcaster’s death on Wednesday.

    He says she "helped millions of children" by founding Childline in 1986, a charity that offers a private and confidential helpline for children.

    Sherwood adds her "unwavering determination and tenacity in ensuring children were safe and heard never diminished".

    "Esther was a unique and truly remarkable woman who during her life did incredible things and went to exceptional lengths to ensure children and young people across the UK had somewhere to turn when they were in need of help," he says.

    "We will miss her so much but will ensure her extraordinary legacy and commitment to helping children lives on for many years to come."

  2. Rantzen was a prominent campaigner in the assisted dying debatepublished at 19:44 BST
    19:44 BST

    Rantzen was diagnosed with terminal lung cancer in 2023 and soon became a prominent campaigner in favour of legalising assisted dying - though she accepted she might not be alive to see it become law.

    Revealing she had signed up to Swiss assisted dying clinic Dignitas, Rantzen said she wanted to ensure her family's last memories of her were not "painful because if you watch someone you love having a bad death, that memory obliterates all the happy times".

    But she admitted that if she did decide to have an assisted death at Dignitas that would put "my family and friends in a difficult position because they would want to go with me, and that means that the police might prosecute them".

    Earlier this month, Rantzen shared an update on her health, admitting she had become "too physically fragile" to end her life at the Swiss assisted dying clinic Dignitas.

    "Everything I didn’t want to happen I’m having to go through now," she said. Obviously, I would prefer that my end could be swift and painless and I could ask for help now I’m dying. But I can’t."

    She wrote "this is why I want the law to change and change quickly", by giving the choice of assisted dying to future generations who are terminally ill with six months or less to live.

    "It’s just a question of choice about this most personal issue of all, I don’t want to impose my choice on anyone else, but I believe they should have no right to impose their choice on me."

  3. That's life! - the show that made Esther Rantzen a household namepublished at 19:38 BST
    19:38 BST

    Dame Esther Rantzen got her first on-screen TV appearance on BBC consumer programme Braden's Week, fronted by Canadian presenter Bernard Braden, which she joined as researcher in 1968.

    After Braden departed the BBC in 1972, the show was rebooted a year later, renamed That's Life! and Rantzen was selected as its main presenter.

    Launched in May 1973, it became an almost instant hit.

    Recorded just before transmission on Saturday - and then later Sunday - nights, it was initially a consumer protection programme investigating viewers' complaints about shoddy service and faulty goods.

    Over the years it grew to include more quirky items.

    It initiated the Jobsworth award for over-zealous bureaucrats and council officials, and Heap of the Week, in which failing domestic appliances would be destroyed on screen in a variety of ingenious ways.

    The most memorable moment came in 1988, when Rantzen told the story of Nicholas Winton, the man who had organised the rescue of 669 children from Czechoslovakia, which was occupied in 1939 by Nazi forces.

  4. Dame Esther Rantzen - broadcaster, charity founder, and assisted dying campaignerpublished at 19:15 BST
    19:15 BST

    A broadcaster

    Dame Esther Rantzen presented the BBC show That's Life! for 21 years during its run from 1973 to 1994, a show that blended consumer rights investigations and entertainment.

    She later went on to appear in Strictly Come Dancing, and was inducted into the Royal Television Society's hall of fame in 1997.

    A charity founder

    It was through her work on That's Life! that Rantzen founded Childline in 1986, a charity that offers a private and confidential helpline for children.

    When they set up a helpline for an episode of the BBC television programme on child abuse, the presenter's team was inundated with calls.

    Afterwards, Rantzen realised there was an urgent need for an outlet for vulnerable children to talk about the taboo subject of child abuse.

    Later on in 2013, Rantzen set up another telephone service. The Silver Line helpline provided a line for people aged 55 or older to tackle feelings of loneliness and isolation that many older people experience.

    In 2015, Rantzen was recognised for her charity work when she was made a dame for her services to children and older people.

    An assisted dying campaigner

    Rantzen joined the Swiss assisted dying clinic Dignitas after being diagnosed with terminal lung cancer in 2023, and became a vocal campaigner for the UK's Terminally Ill Adults Bill.

    Rantzen said she had become "too physically fragile" to travel to Dignitas, and said the law around assisted dying - which she called "messy and cruel - must "change and change quickly".

  5. Full statement from family of Esther Rantzenpublished at 19:10 BST
    19:10 BST

    In a statement issued to the Press Association her children Miriam Wilcox, Rebecca Wilcox and Joshua Wilcox said:

    "Our adored mother, Dame Esther Rantzen, died today. Her legacy of compassion and understanding will live on in her charities Childline and the Silverline, and through those of us who were lucky enough to love and be loved by her.

    "We are grateful she is finally at rest with her beloved husband, Desmond Wilcox, but we will miss her immeasurably. She was our sunshine. We will always remember her endless capacity for joy, her ability to comfort every heartache, her beautiful way with words and her expert guidance in all things.

    "She was the most wonderful mother to us three children and a cherished grandmother to her five treasured grandchildren. We have been so lucky for so long, no matter how much time we had with Mum it could be never be enough, but we are so grateful for our lives together.

    "Mum’s work with charities and campaigning, particularly for children, older people, and the terminally ill, meant the world to her so thank you to everyone who has written to her, shared their stories with her over the years and worked alongside her.

    "We hope the humour, ingenuity and audacity she brought to her work may continue to inspire you as it will do us. We kindly ask that we are given privacy and time to grieve."
