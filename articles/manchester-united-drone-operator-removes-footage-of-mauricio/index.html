Manchester United: Drone operator removes footage of Mauricio Pochettino's first training session and apologises

TheTools · 20.11.2026 19:54 · source · permalink

A 12-minute video of Mauricio Pochettino's first training session at Manchester United was leaked online, showing the new head coach doing training drills ahead of Sunday's match against Liverpool, live on Sky; the apologetic drone operator says he has now taken down the footage

Friday 20 November 2026 09:25, UK

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

The drone operator who posted footage of Mauricio Pochettino's first training session at Manchester United on YouTube has said he has now removed the video.

The 12-minute drone footage over United's Carrington training ground showed the the Argentine overseeing various tactical drills with his new team as they prepared for their game against Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday, live on Sky Sports.

CC Overview, who posted the footage, said on Friday morning: "I would like to speak about yesterday's incident. A total error of judgement on my part led to me filming some of the Manchester United training session at Carrington, whilst the flight was legal in terms of drone laws I fully accept it was unethical on my part. My aim was to picture Mauricio Pochettino in his United training kit for the supporters to see for the first time.

"I had wrongly presumed that with it being close to the start of the session that no meaningful training sessions would have started so would have been some harmless warming up shown.

"I also wrongly assumed that the training session would have been open to various media outlets. I would like to apologise to Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino and the players for my actions and sincerely hope those actions do not affect the outcome of any match.

What do we know about the drone operator?

  • The footage was uploaded to YouTube channel CC Overview
  • 'Colin' describes his channel as 'drone footage mainly of construction at football stadia and events affecting our environment.'

"I would also like to state that Liverpool Football Club or any other football club were not involved in my decision to film at Carrington yesterday. The video has been removed from my channel.

"I have spent the past four years building up my YouTube channel and were well regarded in the fields I film and would now like to get back to building up my damaged reputation and making well-received responsible videos. Thank you."

However, the video has now been shared widely across social media. The footage has been viewed over eight million times on reposts from other X accounts and has since been reuploaded by several other YouTube channels, collectively attracting hundreds of thousands of views.

United have signs around the Carrington area warning that the use of drones is "strictly prohibited" and anyone caught "will be reported for prosecution and subject to civil action".

After the video emerged on Thursday evening, CC Overview maintained they had not broken any laws and claimed there were "many other media there".

Sky Sports News understands the club do not plan on taking legal action over the authorised filming of Pochettino's first training session in charge as head coach but will not rule out legal action in the future if further incidents occur to protect the rights of the club and its staff.

Sky Sports News has been told by club sources there was no security breach at the Manchester United training ground yesterday.

The club have acknowledged the drone operator has apologised and took down the footage without any contact from those at United.

Liverpool boss Andoni Iraola also reacted to the footage, saying: "They sent me it yesterday, the footage. But the important part of this is it shouldn't be legal.

"Yesterday you had access to our training, the cameras recorded 15-20 minutes of our training, you know who trained with us, you know who didn't train but it's not a matter of the advantage it's the fact someone can do this.

"There is a reason we train behind closed doors because there are some things you have to respect. I don't think it's nice for any club."

'Pochettino must now protect his players from another story'

Sky Sports News' Danyal Khan:

"This is quite an incredible story… this was a drone operator who flew not necessarily over the training ground or over Carrington, but far enough with a zoom that could really detail the tactical set-up that maybe Pochettino will go with on Sunday or what he's certainly working towards.

"You could also see the personnel who were there, questions around some players' fitness or availability… which ones were in place in his tactics run-through as well.

"This is a huge disappointment and not ideal for this Manchester United team who are trying to build some momentum, find some positive energy going into Pochettino's first game against Liverpool.

"It's just one win in 11 games in all competitions, and they're now having to get through another story and another burden.

"For Pochettino, it is now about protecting this group of players from more negative energy around them.

"Even before this happened, these types of incidents have been a concern for teams across the Premier League and EFL. Other clubs will be very aware and start thinking about how they can protect themselves from this happening to them.

"The footage has now been deleted, but of course it's across all social media platforms and you can't really take it back once it is out there, can you?"

'Difficult to stop this behaviour' claims drone expert

Drone expert Jon Hurndall speaking with Sky Sports News:

"The only way to stop it is to reduce the airspace in the open category, which I guess the operator has been operating in. There's not much they can do. All the signage in the world isn't going to stop the operator from flying over.

"My view on it is that the privacy is there to stop people filming into homes. It's more of a legal debate as to whether filming a training session breaches privacy laws.

"There's lots of different sections of the law and the regulations. This guy seems to be operating in the open category.

"There's different certifications of aircraft. Different weight categories. The fundamentals are that when you're operating in a certain category you can't fly over assemblies of people.

"I don't know if that's the case here, but an assembly of people would be a group who can't move out of the way in the event of failure. On a sporadic training pitch, it's difficult to to say that a rule's been breached. Then once you're up in the air, you're in the realms of airspace. If there is no airspace restriction in that particular area and you're not near an airport, it's pretty difficult to stop that type of behaviour.

"I think the only way to stop it from happening is to increase security. Maybe buy more land because they can only go a maximum 500 metres away from the eyeline of the pilot. They have to remain within the view of the system in the air.

"If that's not a possibility, the only thing is to really restrict the airspace. That's up to the CAA (Civil Aviation Authority) on if they will allow that."
