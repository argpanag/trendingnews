Flutter to cut 1,400 jobs, with 240 Dublin jobs at risk

TheTools · 20.10.2026 12:02 · source · permalink

Getty

Paddy Power owner Flutter Entertainment is to cut around 1,400 jobs in the UK and Ireland as part of a major restructuring after being hit by increases to UK gambling taxes.

It is expected that 240 jobs in Dublin will be at risk, while workers in Leeds will also be impacted.

The gambling giant said the proposed job cuts will impact workers in Leeds and Dublin.

The restructuring will also impact UK and Ireland staff based at Flutter hubs in Porto, Portugal, and Cluj-Napoca, Romania.

It is understood the move will hit around 15% of workers in the UK and Ireland, where Flutter employs around 9,000 staff.

Flutter said the overhaul is part of efforts to mitigate costs following a "significant increase" in the UK gaming taxes from last year's autumn budget.

UK and Ireland boss Kevin Harrington said the group was not able to "fully absorb" the impact without cutting costs.

Plans announced in last year’s UK autumn budget saw the rate of remote gaming duty - the tax on online casino games and slots - increase from 21% to 40% earlier this year.

UK firms will also see a new 25% rate of general betting duty for online bets from April next year, up from 15%, as part of the budget changes.

It comes amid speculation that the British government could increase the rate of machine games duty, which is the tax on fruit machines, roulette machines and other slot machines, in their next budget later this month.

Flutter said its restructuring plan is likely to cost between 80 and 90 million US dollars (€71m - €80m)

Mr Harrington, Flutter UK and Ireland chief executive, said: "The material increase in betting and gaming duties announced in the UK budget last year has fundamentally changed the economic model of the regulated betting and gaming industry in the UK while helping to fuel the unlicensed, unregulated market.

"As we warned a year ago, we cannot fully absorb a rise of this magnitude without reducing our fixed cost base and therefore must restructure our business so that we can best position ourselves for the future," he said.

"We remain confident that we are well placed to benefit from share gains given our position as the largest scale operator in the UK," he added.

Flutter employs 28,500 people globally and as well as Paddy Power its brands include Sky Bet and Betfair.

Flutter is the latest and largest operator to cut jobs as the UK gambling sector grapples with the most significant tax overhaul in its recent history, with other companies warning of hundreds of millions of dollars in lost earnings.

Ladbrokes owner Entain last month outlined plans to cut about 20% of customer care roles, two months after it said it would remove 500 roles to offset the impact of higher taxes.

It also sent a letter to British Prime Minister Andy Burnham to voice concerns over any further tax increases on the sector.
