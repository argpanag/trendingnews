Former Merrillville legal secretary charged in alleged expungement fraud scheme

TheTools · 16.10.2026 22:20 · source · permalink

Authorities said one alleged victim spent a weekend in jail because an expungement had not been completed.

MERRILLVILLE, Ind. (WLS) -- A former Merrillville legal secretary has been charged following a three-year investigation into allegations that people seeking to have their records legally expunged were defrauded out of thousands of dollars.

Heather Ann Textor, of Lowell, was arrested Thursday and is accused of taking money from individuals who believed she was handling their expungement cases. According to Indiana State Police, some victims did not discover the alleged fraud until years later.

"Several of the victims in this case did not realize they were victims until years later," Sgt. Glen Fifield of the Indiana State Police said. "They just assume that their case had been adjudicated, that their criminal history or traffic tickets had been expunged and they weren't."

Textor is charged with fraud, forgery, counterfeiting and theft. In total, she faces 42 felony charges and three misdemeanor charges.

Indiana State Police said investigators began examining the case three years ago.

Authorities said one person spent a weekend in jail because an expungement had not been completed.

"This person had no care for the victims that she had taken advantage of. It was all about her. It was all about her getting money," Fifield said.

Among those identified as victims in the charging documents is Lamar Cheairs, who said he sought assistance in 2023 to clear his record after serving time for mistakes he made when he was younger.

Cheairs said he paid Textor $980 but later learned that no expungement work had been completed when he attempted to legally purchase a firearm about a year later.

"It's really diabolical. Like, because, I would have never thought in a million years that if I go to a lawyer's office, where you're supposed to feel safe and secure about doing whatever you want to do, you know, at that law firm, I went there, and the fact to just get tricked and go through that, and it was it was wild," Cheairs said.

Cheairs said he was eventually able to clear his record and pursue the career he wanted. However, he said the delay disrupted his efforts to move forward with his life and continues to create challenges as he works to support and care for his family.

"I mean, you just you don't do people like that, you know. To just take everybody money, you know, and give them a sense of false hope that everything is fine when, in all actuality, it's not," Cheairs said.

Prosecutors said a partner at the Merrillville law firm where Textor worked cooperated with the investigation and told investigators she had been fired in 2023. Representatives of the law firm declined to comment Friday.

Textor is scheduled to return to court next Tuesday.
