Brewers lead critical Game 3 of the World Series as they try to move toward franchise’s first title | CNN

TheTools · 26.10.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

What we're watching here

  • Game 3 is here: The Rays need a win to make this World Series competitive. The Milwaukee Brewers lead the series 2-0 after winning both games at American Family Field.
  • Brewers take the lead: The Brewers used a couple of soft hits to strike first and take the lead over the Tampa Bay Rays in a critical World Series Game 3. It’s 2-0 in the fourth.
  • Milwaukee in command: Both games of this Fall Classic have come down to the wire, but it’s the National League champions who are fully in control of this series. Of the 57 teams that have taken a 2-0 World Series lead, 46 of them have won the title.
  • Series gets juicy: The series has moved to Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Florida, where the Rays will hope to use home-field advantage to get back in this thing.

Updates

The Tropicana Field crowd comes to life as Peralta works a 1-2-3 inning

Freddy Peralta performed a rare feat in the 4th inning – he retired the Brewers in order.

Peralta struck out Garrett Mitchell and Cooper Pratt, which injected some much-needed life into the crowd in St. Pete.

A groundout from Sal Frelick ended the inning in quick fashion, but the Rays need to find a way to break up the rhythm of Brewers’ starter Robert Gasser and do it quickly.

Score: Brewers 2, Rays 0

Robert Gasser is cruising through the Rays lineup

Through three the Rays are making solid contact off Robert Gasser – two of the outs in that frame were over 100 mph off the bat – but he’s managed to face the minimum and given up just a single hit, back in the first.

In a word: he’s cruising, with credit to the Brewers defense, which has been airtight so far tonight.

Score: Brewers 2, Rays 0

Brewers break through with a pair of runs

Another inning, another rally for the Brewers.

After a quick out, Jackson Chourio laced another single – his second of the game already – and Jake Bauers drew a walk, and Freddy Peralta was right back in the soup.

This time, they cashed in as Christian Yelich hit his second bloop single of the game to bring Chourio in for the first run of the game.

On the very next pitch, William Contreras dropped a single into right field where Victor Mesa, Jr. struggled to field it cleanly, allowing Bauers to score.

Peralta escaped the inning without any further damage, but the Rays once again find themselves in the unenviable position of playing catch-up against the Brewers.

Score: Brewers 2, Rays 0

The Rays go down on 5 pitches in the second

Chandler Simpson keeps the game scoreless and… so do the Rays’ hitters. Tampa’s heart of the order goes down 1-2-3 on just five pitches.

They got, you could say, gassed. (Robert Gasser is pitching.)

The Rays' Chandler Simpson makes the catch of the series

This Brewers offense is just relentless, and once again they got the leadoff man on – this time courtesy of a Freddy Peralta walk to Garrett Mitchell.

But the Rays defense took Peralta off the hook in a big way.

After a strikeout of Cooper Pratt, left fielder Chandler Simpson made about as good of a catch as you’ll ever see to rob Sal Frelick of extra bases and possibly save a run.

Then catcher Nick Fortes caught Mitchell stealing to end the inning and fire up the crowd in Tropicana Field.

Score: Brewers 0, Rays 0

A baserunning mistake ends the Rays' first inning

Chandler Simpson is fast. So fast that he can beat out a would-be double play to wind up on first.

So fast that, once safely at first, you have to assume he’s a guarantee to attempt to steal second.

So fast that, even if the pitcher knows he’ll be running, Simpson can still make it to second before the throw from the catcher.

So fast, apparently, that he’ll actually go a little too far, and wind up tagged out after over-sliding the bag.

Such ends the Rays’ first inning.

Brewers 0, Rays 0

Junior Caminero makes his voice heard

As the Rays watched their four-run lead slip away in Game 2, their 23-year-old slugger, Junior Caminero, was seen on the broadcast yelling emphatically in the dugout.

ESPN wrote about how that moment was indicative of Caminero’s budding leadership on the club. He has become more vocal as the Rays have gone deeper into October, but it’s not an entirely new part of his personality.

Kevin Ibach, the Rays assistant GM and vice president of player personnel, told CNN Sports a story about Caminero: Every year the organization brings some of their top American prospects down to the Dominican Republic, where Caminero is from originally, to help them understand and appreciate where many of their teammates learned the game.

When they did so back in January this year, Caminero insisted on coming out to dinner to spend time with the young prospects.

“Junior’s sitting in the middle of that table, talking to them, asking them questions,” Ibach said. “In English, and that’s the most impressive thing.”

Those are players who likely won’t debut in the majors for several seasons, teenagers now, although Caminero is not much older. It shows a level of commitment to the organization that the Rays are fortunate to have found in someone who is also capable of hitting a ball over 400 feet.

Brewers threaten, but come up empty in the top of the first inning

The dynamo that is the Brewers offense got off the a promising start in the first inning as Jackson Chourio ripped a single into center field.

After Rays starter Freddy Peralta struck out Jake Bauers, Brewers captain Christian Yelich dropped a bloop single down the third base line and Peralta was in a jam three batters into the game.

But Peralta successfully tiptoed through the minefield, inducing a popout from William Contreras followed by groundout from Brice Turang to end the inning.

Score: Brewers 0, Rays 0

We are underway in Game 3

The aforementioned Freddy Peralta toes the rubber and fires the first pitch of Game 3 – a fastball that nearly hits Brewers leadoff hitter Jackson Chuorio.

The entire series could hinge on the result of this game. Tampa needs to win to make sure it gets competitive in this series, whereas the Brewers would be on the verge of winning the championship with a victory.

Nine tense innings await. Here we go!

Freddy Peralta back in familiar form and squaring off against familiar faces

There are two distinct storylines to know about Freddy Peralta, the Rays’ ALCS MVP and Game 3 starter. The confluence of which makes it a little crazy that he’s pitching in the World Series – this year and against the Milwaukee Brewers.

First, Peralta spent a decade in the Brewers’ organization. They acquired him as a minor leaguer and developed him into a multi-time All-Star. He’s been gone for less than a year, traded to the Mets this past January.

“It is a little weird,” Peralta said before Game 2 about now facing his longtime club in the World Series.

The reception from fans in Milwaukee was warm – they cheered him during introductions ahead of Game 1 and even shouted at him in the bullpen later that night: “We still love you!”

The second thing is that the trade to the Mets turned out to be a disaster. Coming off a season with a 2.70 ERA and the most wins in the National League, Peralta was a mess in New York, helping tank their World Series aspirations so thoroughly that they wound up as sellers at the deadline.

What went wrong? “Sometimes I ask the same question myself,” Peralta said.

He was dealt to the Rays in August, where they tinkered with his mechanics and reignited his confidence. Tampa fixed Freddy by treating him “as a king” and he has repaid them this October with an ERA just over 1 in four appearances. It’s a stunning turnaround.

Peralta’s success with the Rays has drawn attention to their longtime pitching coach, Kyle Snyder. The 6-foot-8-inch former mediocre major leaguer is renowned for turning pitchers into the best versions of themselves – many of whom go on to cash in elsewhere. He does it with an embrace of cutting-edge technology and palpable concern for their personal well-being.

“You get the environment right, you get the relationship right. Those things all matter,” Snyder told CNN Sports.

Tropicana Field is World-Series-ready after being shuttered for the 2025 season

The Rays are hosting their first World Series game since 2008, and their home stadium – the quirky Tropicana Field – is ready to meet the moment.

This was no certainty heading into the 2026 season, after “The Trop” sustained significant damage from Hurricane Milton in October 2024. The domed stadium’s fabric roof was ripped to shreds by the powerful winds of the storm.

The Rays spent the entire 2025 season playing their home games at cross-bay George M. Steinbrenner Field, the spring training home of the rival New York Yankees. And it was a race against the clock to get Tropicana Field ready for Opening Day this year.

But here we are, six months later, and the Fall Classic has come to St. Petersburg, and Tropicana Field’s quirky architecture might be just what the Rays need to turn the momentum of this series. The roof of the Trop, which is nearly identical in color to a baseball and criss-crossed by a network of wires and catwalks – all of which are in play – has the potential to turn any game on its head.

Depending on where a ball might strike the roof, it could result in an automatic home run or remain a live ball with the defensive players able to play the ball wherever it falls.

For anyone new to Tampa Bay Rays baseball, buckle up and expect the unexpected.

The Brewers' secret weapon (who you've maybe never heard of)

If you’re not familiar with Milwaukee Brewers’ Game 3 starter Robert Gasser, welcome to the club. This writer had not heard of him before this postseason.

But look at Gasser’s recent stats if you need an introduction.

The 27-year-old southpaw hasn’t surrendered more than one run or three hits in a game since the first of September, a run of nearly 32 innings with just four runs and 16 hits allowed in that stretch.

Not bad for a guy who had only 27 career starts entering the 2026 playoffs.

Gasser has been even more impressive under the bright lights of the postseason this year, allowing a single run across nine innings against the San Diego Padres and Los Angeles Dodgers.

The Sports Equinox is upon us

It’s the one day in 2026 when all four major American sports play at once.

Plus, we get a bonus event just to make this night even better.

October has a good case to be the best sports month as the MLB conducts its postseason, the NBA and NHL get started and the NFL hits full swing. But tonight is the only night that we’ll have all four playing at once.

In the NFL, the Philadelphia Eagles and Dallas Cowboys will face off and there’s a full slate of NBA games. The NHL has a slower night with only three games, but there’s still hockey to be played.

And with Game 5 of the WNBA Finals tipping off shortly in the Bay Area, this sporting equinox is worth all the channel surfing and stream buffering.

Fire up the multiview, it’s going to be a wonderful sports night.

25 years on, the 2001 World Series still resonates

For the first time in 46 years, a sitting US president was about to throw out the first pitch in a World Series game.

Deep in the bowels of Yankee Stadium, George W. Bush was being warmed up by a man wearing a softball mitt. Outside on the diamond, a fake umpire was standing near home plate with a gun and a taser concealed beneath his uniform. Above them, the stadium was rocking, so much so that the picture frames in the offices below were hanging askew.

Nothing was normal on October 30, 2001: The New York Yankees were hosting the Arizona Diamondbacks for the biggest prize in baseball in a city still in shock – just 13 miles away, the debris from the World Trade Center still burned.

But despite all the fear and emotion, it would end up being one of the greatest World Series ever played.

The Brewers are 2 wins away from their first title

There are five MLB teams that have never won a World Series. After this season, there will be four.

That alone imbues this series with compellingly straightforward stakes. For the Rays, it has been 29 years of existence and two failed trips to the Fall Classic, most recently in the neutral-site Covid-19 World Series in 2020. For the Brewers, it’s been 58 years of existence and they haven’t played in the final game of a season since 1982, before many of their current fans were even born.

If the Brewers win tonight, they’ll be one win away from a World Series title – just as they were in 1982.

The Rays, in particular, are often mocked on a national level for their fickle Florida fanbase that would rather enjoy the year-round sun than sit in the necessary traffic to get to games in St. Petersburg.

But when the broadcast cuts to either crowd in a clinching scenario, keep in mind the people who are there are hoping to see history made.

Be the judge: Did Yandy Díaz swing?

It’s one of the toughest judgment calls in sports: When does a hitter go too far on a checked swing?

On the final pitch of Game 2 with the tying run on third, Yandy Díaz clearly thought he held up in time and Aaron Ashby’s curveball should have been ball four. There were 40,000-some-odd folks at American Family Field who will say this was a clear swing and Díaz went too far.

The longtime Ray had to be restrained after the game and guided off the field by coaches and teammates. It’s a really close call – I’m honestly not sure what I think.

You be the judge – should this have been strike three?

It just sort of feels like Milwaukee's year

A sold-out ballpark full of fans willing to pay obscene prices to see their hometown team finally back in the World Series will, indeed, give games a certain aura. Especially if they win. It’s like what a soundtrack does for a movie scene: Subconsciously tell you how to feel.

And the sound of 40,000-plus Midwesterners losing their minds did a lot over the past two nights to make the Brewers seem like the main characters in this World Series.

Recency bias factors, too. We forget how a team can set a home run record one night and get literally no-hit the next (see the Phillies in the World Series in 2022). The Brewers have enjoyed their home field advantage this weekend and the Rays may do the same when the series turns to Tropicana Field on Monday.

But it’s not just the two Ws that make Milwaukee feel inevitable. After the Rays jumped out to a 4-0 lead in the first, we highlighted an utterly absurd statistic. This year the Brewers have a better than .500 after falling behind.

The best team in baseball got that way by beating the other team more often than not when they’re up, and when they’re down. Combined with the best bullpen in the National League and the games go a little something like this: They always have a good chance to come back, but the opposition does not.

Destiny, though, requires more than just dominating stats. It requires the ball bouncing your way and an unexpected hero coming through in a big moment.

On Saturday night that was Sal Frelick.

He was, perhaps, the least likely member of the Brewers lineup to hit a 408-foot home run to straightaway center off a lefty reliever. And so of course that’s exactly what he did, to tie the game and complete the comeback.

Some home runs have their importance underscored by the author taking his time to admire his moon shot and round the base paths, milking every moment. But Frelick scampered out of the box and sped around the bases like he was eager to keep the momentum moving.

The next inning, rookie shortstop Cooper Pratt smacked the go-ahead single. It was his error in the first that had kicked off the Rays’ four-run frame and then there he was: the living embodiment of the Brewers overcoming early failures for ultimate victory.

It’s a potent fallback to winning easily.

Of course, twice now the Rays have ended the game with the tying run on third base – which you a testament to just how well-matched these two teams are. Or could draw your attention to how Tampa has gone 3-20 with runners in scoring position through the first two games of this Fall Classic.

Extremely not Team of Destiny behavior.

Former teammates collide as Gasser and Peralta take center stage in Game 3

The Milwaukee Brewers hand the ball to left-hander Robert Gasser who enters Game 3 with a pristine 1.00 ERA over nine postseason innings.

Standing in the way of a Milwaukee stranglehold is Tampa Bay right-hander Freddy Peralta (2-0, 1.08 ERA this postseason), the ALCS MVP who spent his first eight seasons with the Brewers.

Armed with a blazing fastball, Peralta faces the ultimate test against his former club as the Rays fight to keep their World Series hopes alive.

Catch up on what happened in Game 2

The Milwaukee Brewers defeated the Tampa Bay Rays 5-4 to take Game 2 of the World Series on Saturday at American Family Field.

Saturday’s game proved to be a bit more complex for the Brew Crew, having to erase an early huge deficit to go up 2-0 in the best-of-seven series.

The Rays jumped on Milwaukee’s rookie sensation Logan Henderson, scoring four runs in the first inning, highlighted by Liam Hicks’ two-run home run.

But as the Brewers have proved all season, an early hole doesn’t affect them in the slightest.

Milwaukee clawed back in the bottom of the first, adding two runs to make it a 4-2 game.

After a shaky start to the game, both starting pitchers — Henderson and Drew Rasmussen found their grove until the bottom of the fourth inning when Rays first baseman Jonathan Aranda’s error at first allowed the Brewers to make it a one-run game.

Both teams turned to their respective bullpens, but the tide truly turned in the bottom of the sixth inning.

Rays’ relief pitcher Tyler Wells allowed a moonshot home run to Brewers’ outfielder Sal Frelick to tie the game at 4-4.

With the sold-out Milwaukee faithful behind them, they quickly took the lead the next inning with Cooper Pratt RBI single.

The drama did not stop there.

Brewers reliever Aaron Ashby has proved to be a workhorse for the team all postseason and after getting out of the eighth inning with ease, his ninth inning lift was a bit harder.

A leadoff double by Jonny DeLuca put the tying run in scoring position right away. However, the 28-year-old struck out Victor Mesa Jr, made pinch hitter Kevin Williamson ground out and struck out Yandy Diaz on a controversial check swing.

The Brewers clawed back from four runs down and yet again proved why this team loves playing from behind.

They improved 41-37 when allowing the first run this season, including the postseason and find themselves two games from bringing home the franchise’s first World Series title.
