Kane confident England's strengths can trouble Spain in Nations League opener

TheTools · 26.09.2026 17:14 · source · permalink

The Nations League opener allows the Three Lions to show the value of their style against the international standard-bearers, with Harry Kane pointing to England's physicality, pace and pressing.

Harry Kane commented on the English DNA of football as the Three Lions prepare to play Spain.

The last meeting of the two teams saw England defeated 2-1 by La Roja in the final of the 2024 European Championships.

Spain had won the 2023 Nations League title, and in the two years after that contest, they reached the 2025 Nations League final and lifted the World Cup trophy in July this year by beating Argentina, whom England lost to 2-1 in the semi-final.

Indeed, it was the fallout from that elimination that reignited the conversation about the approach to the game in England.

'We have a lot of strengths'

England went ahead through a goal from Anthony Gordon, but they displayed little initiative in the final half hour of the match.

Lionel Messi moved to the outside of a lowering 5-3-2 formation and wreaked havoc as Enzo Fernández and Lautaro Martínez made good on the pressure by full time.

By contrast, Spain showed the shrewd tactical and technical discipline that defined their run to the final, not conceding a shot on target from the Albiceleste for almost the full two hours of the final.

Kane on England's approach

Kane admitted that La Roja have admirable qualities ingrained in their style, but he also feels that his country can create unique problems for opponents based on their own outlook on the sport.

"It's culture for Spain, playing a certain style from five, six or seven years old until they're professionals. In England it's not so much like that.

"It doesn't mean we can't play under pressure or keep the ball; we still have a lot of amazing technical players - but we have a lot of strengths that they don't have; physicality, pace and power. The way we press and win duels, a lot of Premier League style football in a way. I think at our best we combine both. We're not just a team that play like Spain but we have a lot of strengths that we can showcase."
