Cassandra Wilson, the legendary jazz vocalist, has died at 70.

Her longtime manager Robert Torry confirmed that Wilson passed away on Tuesday at her Jackson, Mississippi home surrounded by family and close friends. No cause of death has been publicly announced.

"It is with profound sadness that we announce the passing of Cassandra Wilson, the Grammy Award-winning and legendary jazz artist," read Torre's statement per WBGO. "Cassandra Wilson transitioned peacefully at home, surrounded by family, close friends, and her manager. Her family and close friends respectfully ask for privacy during this difficult time as they grieve her loss."

MORE BY KELLY CORBETT

Time magazine once named her America's best singer, and for good reason. Wilson won global fame by mixing traditional blues, folk, pop, and country into jazz to create her own unique sound. Over her career, she picked up four Grammy nominations and two wins for Best Jazz Vocal Album: "New Moon Daughter" in 1997 and "Loverly" in 2009.

She also made music history as a lead voice on Wynton Marsalis' "Blood on the Fields," which in 1997 became the first jazz work to win a Pulitzer Prize. In 2022, she received the nation's highest jazz honor when she was named an NEA Jazz Master.

Details on memorial services will be announced later.
