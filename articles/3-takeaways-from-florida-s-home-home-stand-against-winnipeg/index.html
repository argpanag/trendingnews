3 Takeaways From Florida's Home & Home Stand Against Winnipeg

TheTools · 01.11.2026 14:29 · source · permalink

Roughly a month into their season, the Florida Panthers are starting to look as dangerous on the ice as they had on paper over the summer. They weren’t happy with their back-to-back games in Ottawa and Pittsburgh, but now things seem to be clicking. A get-right game against Chicago led into two very solid games against the Winnipeg Jets. Lundell scored his first career hat trick to secure the Cats’ victory in Sunrise. In as competitive a division as the Atlantic has been, the Panthers finding their game quickly has been important; they currently sit third in the division, but there’s only four points between first place and last place. Florida’s offense seems to have found its footing, and a great update on Barkov & Marchand: both players are skating, with Marchand expected back in three weeks and Barkov expected back in four weeks.

Heating Up:

A big sore spot to start the season was Florida’s power play. On paper it should’ve been one of the most dangerous ones in the league, but games aren’t played on paper, and it struggled through the first five games of the season. Sam Reinhart scoring on the power play against Buffalo seems to have kicked it into gear, and it’s starting to get that ‘touch of death’ feel to it. In two games against the Jets the Panthers scored five power play goals coming from Verhaeghe (2), Reinhart (2), and Seth Jones (1) en route to a 5-1 win & 5-2 win. Reinhart is now tied for the league lead in power play goals (6), something that’s not unexpected. He’s been one of the best at scoring on the man advantage since joining the Panthers in the 2021-2022 season, and he’s consistently put up double-digit power play goals since then. After an abysmal start, the PP is now sitting at 24.2% and 11th in the league. The PK, which had been up and down, has settled and is sitting at 84.7% for 7th in the league.

Breakout Year:

For anyone who questioned why Seth Jones was initially picked for Team USA’s Olympic squad, he’s showing why on the ice this season. In 14 games, he’s got 18 points (2G, 16A) and is second on the team in points, trailing only Matthew Tkachuk. He ranks 3rd among defensemen in points production at the moment, and he’s only shooting at 5.4% right now, which is down from 8.0% & 7.0% the two prior seasons. With the production he’s putting up at the moment, he is on pace for a career year in power play assists. If his production keeps this kind of pace, he’ll have a career year in points and will almost certainly be in talks for the Norris. Of course it’s still early, and there’s no guarantee that the pace will stick, but Seth Jones has been nothing short of impressive to start his second full season down in South Florida. An all-situations type player, it’s very obvious why Florida’s season tanked after he went down with an injured collarbone last year. He was a big part of keeping the team in the fight and became one injury too far for the team to overcome. Jones broke the Panthers’ single-month points record by a defenseman previously held by Keith Yandle. Yandle put up 17 points in March of 2018; Jones just posted 18 points during October. Seth Jones and Matthew Tkachuk became the second forward/defenseman teammates in history to register 16 assists each in a single month; Mario Lemieux & Paul Coffey in 1988 with Pittsburgh were the first. When Seth talked with the media following the home game against Winnipeg, he said, “They’re just flowing right now. So, they come and go and I don’t judge my game based on based on points. I judge it based on how smart my plays are, how efficient I am breaking the puck out, and that kind of comes with it. A lot of it’s been power play, and that’s been pretty hot lately.” HC Paul Maurice had high praise for the defensemen, “Seth Jones has been a huge, huge driver and we needed him [to be]. He’s got lots of confidence up top. He’s offensively as strong there is in the league from the blue line.”

Improvement:

With Markstrom down with injury, there’s been an extra workload placed onto Akira Schmid. Schmid looked ok in his first few starts, but the eye test said he wasn’t as strong as Markstrom. Now he’s had three straight starts where there’s been clear improvement in his game, and he’s only allowed 2 or fewer goals. His rebound control has gotten better, and he told the media after the Chicago game that he’d been working on breakaways and shootouts with Tallas. In 8 starts for Florida, Akira has a 5-2-0 record with an .889 SV% and a 2.86 GAA. That save percentage may not look great, but league average SV% according to Hockey Reference is at an .891 with a 2.96 GAA. So Schmid is right around the average numbers-wise at the moment, which is great news for a backup goalie getting a larger workload. Markstrom was seen skating ahead of the Friday night game against the Jets; he should hopefully be back soon. In the meantime, fans should have confidence in Schmid as he grows his game. Seeing the improvement from the start of the season to his last three starts has been great, and his two starts against the Jets have been his best two games of the season.
