'Serious thuggery': Gang member who attacked couple after doing burnouts jailed

TheTools · 25.11.2026 12:23 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Black Power gang member Ztanlee Pira punches a Matangi homeowner in the head, and inset, pulls the finger at NZME during his sentencing in the Hamilton District Court on Wednesday. Inset photo / Belinda Feek

A Black Power gang member who knocked a couple unconscious after doing burnouts on their driveway sat in court with a look of loathing and shaking his head as one of his victims addressed the judge.

“I suffered three fractures to my neck, two brain bleeds, multiple brain contusions and lacerations, and concussion after being knocked unconscious,” said the woman, who still wears a neck brace.

Her husband suffered memory loss, bruising, swelling, cuts, grazes and strains to his left hand and shoulder during Ztanlee Pira’s vicious attack.

But Pira appeared uninterested, even as Judge Noel Cocurullo handed down his sentence, and instead tried to communicate with his partner in the public gallery.

But this wasn’t lost on the judge, who said the 25-year-old would get no discount for remorse.

“I have watched the way, on occasion, you have responded as the victim read her statement.

“You are not genuinely remorseful.”

Pira viciously attacked the two victims while doing burnouts on the driveway of their semi-rural Hamilton home on the evening of February 28 this year.

The couple, whose names were suppressed, were in court to face Pira again.

In her statement, the wife said what happened to her was something she had never “imagined I would experience”.

“I was assaulted in my own driveway.”

She suffered a series of fractures to her neck and spine, severe swelling to her face, cuts and bruises, and a shoulder sprain.

The injuries meant she hadn’t been able to return to work and had regular appointments with her doctor and physiotherapist.

She now struggled to sleep, and her husband being injured added to their trauma.

They were discovered unconscious on their driveway by their son, which had been “enormously distressing”, she said.

“I simply cannot forget what happened,” she said, adding that she now wanted to move on with her life.

About 8.48pm on February 28 Pira was doing burnouts in his Ford Falcon outside the victim’s house in Matangi.

Smoke was pouring out from his tyres and obscured the road.

The victims gestured at Pira to stop and leave, with the wife throwing stones at his car.

But instead of leaving, Pira – along with his two “large” male passengers – drove towards the couple.

He got out and ran at the wife, who put her hands up with open palms in a “submissive” position.

Pira “wound up his right arm” and punched her to the lower jaw/neck area with “considerable force”, before running at her husband and swinging at him.

He managed to duck under the punch, but Pira then pulled him to the ground.

He stepped back and kicked the victim in the face as the victim kneeled face down.

The victim was instantly knocked unconscious.

The wife stepped forward again with her palms up and stood over her unconscious husband.

Pira punched her a second time to the head, again with considerable force, which knocked her unconscious, and she fell flat on to the ground from standing height, unable to break her fall.

Pira and his two associates then fled.

Crown prosecutor James Lewis told the judge there was no information before him to warrant further discounts, after Pira accepted a sentence indication earlier this year.

The court heard that on the day of the incident Pira had been “remembering” the passing of his father but the judge was quick to note that was “10 years earlier”.

“That is my point, sir,” Lewis said. “It’s not something that is exceptional, and we all have to come to terms with at some point.”

Although Pira had attended a restorative justice conference, any remorse was limited as it didn’t meet the threshold of genuine remorse.

However, Pira’s counsel, Jerom Keung, said his client’s father had died 10 years to the day of the offending, when he was aged 14.

In her letter to the court, Pira’s mother explained how much that had affected her son, as he struggled to cope and had a lot of bottled-up anger as a result.

He urged the judge to take into account Pira’s age, trauma, grief and loss, which were all relevant, he said.

In jailing Pira, Judge Cocurullo told him that the victims did absolutely nothing that night “that would in any way shape or form justify your thuggery”.

“Unfortunately for you, but fortunately for them and justice, what you did was caught on good CCTV.

“This notion, as sad as it is, that your father passed a decade ago but somehow is connected to what you did is fabricated by you and an utter nonsense.

“You were doing burnouts on this occasion ... these innocent citizens came out to address that in a non-violent way.

“You were in the wrong, and you seriously attacked them.

“They will be incredibly traumatised by the consequences of your actions.

“They were a serious piece of thuggery without any justification at all.”

Judge Cocurullo took Pira’s eight-year starting point and added 5% for his previous history.

He allowed 18% for his guilty pleas and 5% for attending the restorative justice conference “because the law requires me to”.

He declined to give any discount for youth, nor the impact a jail sentence would have on his 10-month-old baby.

However, he agreed to allow 5% for section 27 (background) issues.

On charges of driving while disqualified, causing sustained loss of traction, and injuring with intent to cause grievous bodily harm and wounding with intent to cause grievous bodily harm, Pira was jailed for six years and 11 months.

Belinda Feek is an Open Justice reporter based in Waikato. She has worked at NZME for 11 years and has been a journalist for 22.
