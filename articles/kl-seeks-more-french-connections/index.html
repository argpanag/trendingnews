KL seeks more French connections

TheTools · 18.11.2026 00:00 · source · permalink
    bookmark

KUALA LUMPUR: Malaysia is seeking greater French investment in key sectors – particularly rare earth processing – as the two countries look to deepen economic ties.

The two nations are also looking at expanding cooperation in semiconductors, artificial intelligence and data centres, alongside defence, security and economic ties.

These were among the topics discussed when Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim held a bilateral meeting with visiting French President Emmanuel Macron at the historic Sultan Abdul Samad Building here yesterday.

The French President, who is on a two-day visit here at Anwar’s invitation, his first visit to the country since assuming office in May 2017, was accorded a state welcome at Dataran Merdeka.

Macron, accompanied by his wife Brigitte Macron, arrived at 9.30am and was received by Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim and his wife, Datin Seri Dr Wan Azizah Wan Ismail.

Also present were both Deputy Prime Ministers, Datuk Seri Dr Ahmad Zahid Hamidi and Datuk Seri Fadillah Yusof.

Macron also inspected a guard of honour comprising three officers and 103 other ranks from the First Battalion of RAMD, commanded by Major Adibadiuezshah Ahmad.

The French President’s visit is expected to open a new chapter in Malaysia-France relations, with both countries seeking to elevate their existing ties to a Strategic Partnership.

The visit builds on the momentum from Anwar’s official trip to France in July 2025, the first by a Malaysian head of government to the country in 15 years.

Speaking ahead of the meeting yesterday, Anwar said cooperation in the rare earth sector must be built on trust, stressing Malaysia’s commitment to expanding processing activities in an environmentally safe manner.

“Rare earth processing is something we are very keen on attracting and expanding and we are doing it in an environmentally safe way,” he said.

Anwar said French investment in Malaysia continued to grow, citing aerospace, defence and semiconductors among the sectors offering opportunities for further cooperation.

Macron also expressed France’s interest in the rare earth sector, describing it as one of the key areas the country wanted to pursue with Malaysia.

According to a Foreign Ministry statement issued on Monday, the visit focuses on efforts to elevate Malaysia-France relations to a Strategic Partnership, covering trade and investment, critical minerals, energy transition, defence and education.

In 2025, total trade between the two countries stood at RM17.78bil (US$4.17bil), with Malaysian exports valued at RM6.05bil (US$1.41bil), mainly comprising electrical and electronic products.

Imports from France totalled RM11.73bil (US$2.76bil), consisting primarily of transport equipment, machinery and chemicals.

France was Malaysia’s 21st-largest trading partner globally and fourth-largest among European countries in 2025.

On education and culture, Anwar highlighted longstanding cooperation between the two countries, including opportunities for Malaysian students to study at French universities.

“We need to continue to support the translation of Malay classics into French, as well as the translation of French classics into Malay,” Bernama quoted him as saying.

Also at the bilateral meeting were Fadillah, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani, Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister Datuk Seri Tiong King Sing, Defence Minister Datuk Seri Mohamed Khaled Nordin and Chief Secretary to the Government Tan Sri Shamsul Azri Abu Bakar.
