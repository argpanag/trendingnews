Live Nation and Unplugs Bring Switchable Earplugs to 60 Concert Venues - That Eric Alper

TheTools · 20.10.2026 20:02 · source · permalink
  • Music

The loudest moment at a concert can be unforgettable. So can the ringing in your ears afterward, although that’s a souvenir nobody should be collecting.

Live Nation is making hearing protection a little more practical for concertgoers, partnering with Unplugs to introduce adjustable earplugs at 60 venues across the United States.

The partnership, announced October 20, includes an equity investment from Live Nation and brings Unplugs’ transparent earplugs directly to fans through participating venues.

The idea is refreshingly simple: protect your ears while keeping the music, conversations and excitement of a live show within reach.

Unplugs feature a mechanical two-mode switch that allows wearers to adjust how much sound reaches their ears without removing the earplugs.

In the closed position, acoustic filters reduce volume while preserving sound clarity. Switch to the open setting, and more sound passes through, making conversations with friends easier.

No charging cables, batteries or smartphone apps are required.

It’s the kind of small design decision that addresses a surprisingly familiar concert problem.

Anyone who has worn earplugs to a show knows the routine. The band finishes a song, your friend says something, and you pull out an earplug to hear them. A few seconds later, the music starts again, and you’re trying to put it back in.

Multiply that by an entire evening of music, and hearing protection can become an inconvenient part of an otherwise brilliant night.

Unplugs wants to make that routine unnecessary.

According to figures shared by Live Nation, three in four fans believe hearing protection should be an essential part of the concert experience.

Yet nearly 60% of concertgoers who have used earplugs at live music events have removed them to speak with friends or other fans.

That gap between wanting protection and actually keeping it in place is central to the company’s approach.

For Unplugs founder Tom Worcester, hearing health has been a lifelong concern.

Worcester has worn hearing aids since he was four years old. Later, while working in the festival industry, he saw how difficult it could be for music fans to balance protecting their hearing with enjoying the full concert experience.

The connection eventually brought him back to someone from his childhood.

Worcester reunited with his former audiologist, Dr. Natan Bauman, and Bauman’s co-inventor Ralph Campagna to develop their mechanical earplug technology for concert audiences.

It’s a fascinating origin story for a product designed around such a common experience.

“We thought hearing protection has had it backwards this whole time,” Worcester says. “Fans deserve to protect their hearing without sacrificing the experience they came for.”

His goal is to give listeners more control over how they experience sound throughout the night.

That flexibility becomes especially useful at festivals, where a day can involve hours of performances, conversations, food breaks and movement between stages.

The same pair of earplugs can remain in place as the environment changes.

For music fans, it’s a practical approach to a subject that’s becoming increasingly important.

Concert sound levels can be intense, particularly during extended performances or when listeners are positioned close to speakers.

Repeated exposure to loud sound can contribute to permanent hearing damage, making protection especially valuable for people who regularly attend concerts.

Musicians, sound engineers, touring crews and venue employees also spend significant portions of their working lives in loud environments.

The new partnership extends beyond the audience.

Unplugs will provide earplugs to venue employees and make them available to artists and touring teams through green room gifting on selected tours.

Fans can purchase the black earplugs at participating Live Nation venues through vending machines, sound guides, merchandise stands and guest services locations.

Importantly, complimentary foam earplugs will continue to be available, ensuring concertgoers still have access to a free hearing protection option.

Tom Allison, Live Nation’s senior vice-president of New Ventures, says the initiative reflects changing attitudes among concert audiences.

“More fans are thinking about their hearing, but they still want to hear the music and talk to their friends,” Allison says.

The rollout also coincides with National Protect Your Hearing Month and National Audiology Awareness Month, both observed in October.

It’s an appropriate moment for an initiative that brings hearing health directly into concert spaces.

Live music is an experience built around sound, and protecting the ability to enjoy it for years to come is an important part of being a fan.

The Unplugs partnership recognizes that concertgoers have different needs throughout an evening, from listening closely to a favourite song to catching up with friends between sets.

A simple switch gives them another way to manage those experiences.

The earplugs are now available for purchase at 60 participating Live Nation venues across the United States.

The best concert memories deserve to last a lifetime. Your hearing deserves the same consideration.
