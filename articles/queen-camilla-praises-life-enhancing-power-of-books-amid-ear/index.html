Queen Camilla has stepped out for a literary event while Charles Spencer's memoir continues to put the monarchy under the spotlight. The Queen used the occasion to highlight the worldwide reach of her Reading Room initiative and its charitable work.

Meanwhile, the memoir has opened a fresh war of words between Spencer and Buckingham Palace, with Diana's brother accusing the King of "gaslighting" him.

Queen Camilla is celebrating the power of books at a particularly eventful moment for the Royal Family, as Charles Spencer's new memoir about Princess Diana fuels fresh controversy involving King Charles III.

Camilla appeared at Chatsworth House in Derbyshire for the Queen's Reading Room festival, where she spoke about her lifelong love of reading and its "life-enhancing power." She founded the charity after her Instagram book club gained traction during the pandemic.

The Queen told guests that what began as a simple effort to share her passion for books has grown into a global community covering more than 180 countries. The organization also donates books to women's refuges and homeless shelters.

Charles Spencer Accuses King Charles Of 'Gaslighting' Him

ZUMAPRESS.com / MEGA

One book in particular is currently commanding attention at the royal court. Diana's brother has used his memoir to make a series of allegations about Charles, prompting a rare response from Buckingham Palace.

The palace declared that "the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason," a statement Spencer did not take kindly to. He accused the King of "gaslighting" him and said the response effectively suggested he had lied without directly denying his account.

Spencer also compared the statement to the Palace's famous "recollections may vary" response following Prince Harry and Meghan Markle's 2021 Oprah Winfrey interview, calling the earlier wording "much more elegant."

Queen Camilla Calls For Togetherness Amid Royal Family Row

ZUMAPRESS.com / MEGA

Queen Camilla also delivered a message about unity during the festival, quoting a proverb about the value of sticking together.

According to The Telegraph, the Queen told attendees she had recently been reminded of the saying, "If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together."

Camilla did not explicitly connect the proverb to the dispute surrounding her husband and Charles Spencer. Still, the message of togetherness landed as the disagreement continued to generate headlines around the Royal Family.

Charles Spencer Stands By Shocking Claim About Diana's Death

At the heart of the escalating dispute is a particularly explosive allegation about the days following Princess Diana's death in 1997.

In "Swan Song: Diana, My Sister," Spencer claims he and the then-Prince Charles argued over arrangements for Diana's funeral. During that exchange, he alleges Charles "hissed" at him, "Rest assured, we'll forget her soon enough."

Spencer has refused to back away from the account. During subsequent interviews, the Earl insisted those were "exactly the words" Charles used and described the alleged remark as "monstrous."

He has also suggested the King is embarrassed about what he allegedly said. Buckingham Palace, meanwhile, has declined to comment further as Spencer continues to promote the memoir and defend his version of events.

Queen Camilla Reportedly 'Nervous' About Prince Harry's Diana Documentary

Francis Dias/NEWSPIX INTERNATIONAL / MEGA

Camilla also reportedly has another reason to be uneasy about the renewed focus on Diana, with Prince Harry said to be developing a documentary about his late mother.

According to reports, the Queen is nervous about what the Duke of Sussex could reveal in the project, particularly given his previous criticism of her. Harry famously branded his stepmother "dangerous" in his memoir, "Spare," and accused her of sacrificing him on her "personal PR altar."

The reported documentary would mark the 30th anniversary of Diana's 1997 death and could once again put her complicated history with Charles and Camilla under a microscope.
