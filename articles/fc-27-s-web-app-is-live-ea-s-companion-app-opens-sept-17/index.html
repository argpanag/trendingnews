FC 27’s Web App Is Live; EA’s Companion App Opens Sept. 17

TheTools · 16.09.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Electronic Arts opened the EA SPORTS FC 27 Web App at 17:00 UTC (1 p.m. ET) on Wednesday, September 16. The mobile Companion App is not due to refresh until 17:00 UTC on Thursday, September 17, according to EA’s launch timetable. Anyone searching for the FC 27 Companion App on Wednesday is therefore a day early, even though the browser-based service is already available.

The two apps let players manage an Ultimate Team club away from the full game. Returning players whose accounts are in good standing can use the Web App to build squads, open eligible rewards and enter the Transfer Market before FC 27’s broad release. EA’s eligibility guide says early access requires an Ultimate Team club created in FC 26, FC 25 or FC 24 on the relevant platform before August 1, 2026.

That is not permanent access on its own. After November 7 at 23:59:50 UTC, users must have logged into FC 27 Ultimate Team at least once on a PC or console to keep using either app. Transfer Market access can also remain locked for individual accounts; EA says access is earned through play and cannot be manually granted by support.

The app rollout is the first live operating test in a tightly sequenced launch. Ultimate Edition players can begin on PC at 23:00 UTC on September 17 and on other platforms at midnight local time on September 18. The worldwide PC release follows at 23:00 UTC on September 24, with other platforms opening at midnight local time on September 25. EA will also release a limited, free-to-download FC 27 Lite on September 25.

For EA’s new owners, the apps matter less as products sold on their own than as an early entry point into the company’s largest recurring-revenue engine. EA’s final public annual report recorded $5.383 billion of live-services and other revenue in fiscal 2026, against $7.531 billion of total revenue. That works out to 71.5%. The filing also says Ultimate Team extra content was material to the business and that a substantial portion came from FC Ultimate Team.

EA is no longer a public stock. PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners completed their acquisition on August 4; holders received $210 in cash per share, trading ceased and EA was set for delisting from Nasdaq. The FC 27 launch now tests the operating assumptions behind that consortium’s roughly $55 billion investment rather than offering a new catalyst for an EA ticker.

A smooth Web App opening is not evidence that FC 27 will meet the buyers’ financial expectations. The apps are free, EA does not disclose their revenue separately, and heavy early traffic can reflect curiosity rather than durable spending. There is also an obvious downside: login failures, blocked Transfer Market access or an unstable Companion refresh could frustrate the returning players EA is trying to bring into the new Ultimate Team season.

The more useful evidence arrives after the full game opens. Engagement across the first Ultimate Team season, demand for paid FC Points and the ability to sustain players beyond launch week will say more about the franchise’s economics. Thursday’s Companion App release is one checkpoint in that process, not the result.
