Six-time MVP holds off championship superstar for No.1 as returning guns, rising stars shake up rankings

TheTools · 18.09.2026 23:59 · source · permalink

The NBL season starts tomorrow night following one of the most historic off-seasons in league history, and before Melbourne United and the Adelaide 36ers tip off the new campaign, it is the perfect time to rank the top 15 players heading into NBL27.

With a host of high-level imports preparing to make their NBL debuts, it seemed unfair to include them on the same list as established stars who have already proven themselves in this league. That is why a separate Top 10 Import Debutants list was created, allowing for a more accurate ranking.

So, who scrapes into the top 15? Who takes the No. 1 spot? Read on below.

Fox Sports Australia has gone through the league to rank the top 15 players heading into the 2026-27 NBL season.

15 | TYRELL HARRISON (BRISBANE)

He is entering his 10th NBL season and is still just 27 years old.

The big man is coming off the best scoring season of his career despite dealing with injury setbacks over the past two seasons.

Harrison averaged 15.0 points, 8.8 rebounds and 1.4 blocks across 25 appearances last season, putting together an impressive individual campaign during what we can all agree was a pretty disastrous year for the franchise.

Now, with a new-look team, head coach and a point guard teammate in Arnas Velička, we could see another level unlocked in Harrison’s game as he pushes to become the best big man in the league.

Don’t be shocked if we see him flirt with 20 points and 10 rebounds per game this season, provided he stays healthy.

14 | TORREY CRAIG (SYDNEY)

He is back for a full NBL season for the first time since the 2016-17 campaign with the Brisbane Bullets.

Craig joined the Kings late last season and suited up for just 15 games, but still walked away an NBL champion after averaging 12.4 points, 6.4 rebounds and 1.1 blocks.

Now, as he prepares to turn 36 in December, the NBL and NBA veteran is primed for another big season alongside star teammates Matthew Dellavedova, Xavier Cooks and Kendric Davis.

Craig put his offensive talents on display with a 26-point performance against Adelaide in Round 18 last season, showcasing exactly why the Kings wanted him back for another year.

He shot a blistering 54.4% from the field and 44% from deep during the regular season, giving Brian Goorjian another dangerous offensive weapon.

Craig looks fit and ready for a big year, one that could even rival his Brisbane tenure, where he averaged close to a double-double with 15.2 points and 8.0 rebounds.

13 | JOSH BANNAN (TASMANIA)

Bannan is a player who probably doesn’t get the recognition he deserves for what he produced last season.

After spending his first two NBL seasons with the Brisbane Bullets, the forward joined the Tasmania JackJumpers and immediately put together a career-best campaign.

He set the franchise record for the most rebounds in a season while finishing second behind JaVale McGee in double-doubles with 14.

Bannan played all 33 games, averaging 15.5 points, 9.6 rebounds and 2.3 assists while shooting 50.1% from the field.

He is a no-fuss player who simply gets the job done. He makes the hard plays and does all the little things that don’t necessarily show up in the box score.

Still just 25 years old, Bannan is nowhere near his ceiling, and we could see another huge leap from the forward this season.

12 | TYLER HARVEY (ILLAWARRA)

No longer an import after becoming an Australian local, Harvey remains one of the best players and pure sharpshooters in the NBL.

Despite the Hawks’ title defence not going to plan last season, Harvey often found himself playing out of position at point guard. Even then, he still averaged 16.9 points, 2.8 rebounds and 3.6 assists while shooting a career-high 38.9% from deep.

He remains one of the most lethal scorers in the league, and the southpaw shooter, still just 33 years old, is preparing for his seventh NBL season.

Alongside Milton Doyle and Kelan Martin, Harvey will be a major piece in Illawarra’s push back towards the top six, while remaining one of the most clutch shooters in the competition.

11 | TARAN ARMSTRONG (TASMANIA)

He has only played two seasons in the NBL, both with the Cairns Taipans, but somehow Armstrong already feels like a veteran.

The Tasmanian made an incredible jump between his first and second seasons, lifting his scoring average from 7.7 points to 17.1.

His second-last game in the league was arguably his best, producing a monster triple-double with 28 points, 10 rebounds and 10 assists.

Now, Armstrong returns to his home state, where he will play in front of friends and family following an overseas stint that saw him spend time alongside NBA legend Stephen Curry and the Golden State Warriors before heading across the globe to Dubai and experiencing EuroLeague basketball.

Still just 23 years old, Armstrong looks noticeably different from the player we last saw in the NBL. He has added muscle, looks quicker and, although he has never been an elite shooter with a career mark of 32.6% from deep, his shot looked much improved throughout the pre-season.

Expectations are high, but just like his demeanour, expect Armstrong to remain calm and measured regardless of who is standing across from him.

10 | XAVIER COOKS (SYDNEY)

He is a three-time NBL champion coming off another solid season with the Kings.

Yes, statistically it was one of the quieter seasons of his career, averaging 13.4 points and 6.9 rebounds, his lowest numbers since 2020-21, but make no mistake, Cooks remains one of the best two-way players in the league.

We also probably have to accept that he isn’t suddenly going to become an elite free-throw shooter, with a career mark of 56.6%. No player is perfect.

What Cooks lacks at the stripe, he more than makes up for with his defensive pressure, versatility and elite finishing around the rim.

He finished sixth in blocks last season at 1.2 per game and shot 63.8% from the field, the third-best mark in the league among players who attempted at least 250 field goals.

Cooks is a former MVP for a reason. Despite the statistical decline, he remains one of the best players in this league.

9 | SAM MENNENGA (NEW ZEALAND)

This is where things start to get incredibly debatable, but after what Mennenga produced last season, he deserves his place inside the top 10.

The reigning Next Gen Award winner took a massive leap during NBL26, establishing himself as one of the emerging stars of the competition.

Across 27 games, Mennenga posted career-highs of 17.7 points, 6.9 rebounds and 1.7 assists while shooting an impressive 53.1% from the field.

His final five games of the regular season gave us a glimpse of just how high his ceiling could be, averaging 25.8 points and 7.0 rebounds.

He has become one of the league’s most dangerous scorers and explosive dunkers, featuring in several of the best highlights from last season.

Still incredibly just 24 years old, Mennenga isn’t close to reaching his ceiling. After re-signing with the Breakers, expect the Kiwi, alongside Parker Jackson-Cartwright, to play a major role in New Zealand’s push for a top-six finish.

8 | JACK MCVEIGH (CAIRNS)

McVeigh is entering his eighth NBL season and his second with the Cairns Taipans after picking up his player option.

He is also one of the most unorthodox stars you’ll see in the league.

McVeigh isn’t overly flashy or athletic, but he has developed into one of the most versatile scorers in the competition.

He is coming off a career season, averaging 21.2 points, 6.3 rebounds and 4.0 assists across 29 games while shooting 49.9% from the field.

McVeigh finished fourth in the league in scoring, with his season highlighted by a ridiculous career-high 47-point performance against the New Zealand Breakers.

Despite Cairns enduring a difficult campaign, McVeigh did everything he could to keep them competitive, and there were several nights where his individual brilliance was enough to drag them across the line.

Now paired with some elite local talent in Keanu Pinder and Reyne Smith, while Next Star Luke Paul and import Jaylon Brown join the roster, McVeigh could reach another level with an improved supporting cast around him.

7 | LUKE TRAVERS (MELBOURNE)

One of Australian basketball’s brightest young stars is back in the NBL.

Travers returns following two seasons in the United States, where he picked up valuable NBA and G League experience, and now rejoins Melbourne United, the club where he produced the best season of his NBL career.

After spending four seasons with the Perth Wildcats, Travers joined United in NBL24 and averaged career-highs of 12.0 points, 7.6 rebounds and 1.0 blocks.

Now a little older, stronger and wiser, Travers has added muscle to his frame and is set to play a major role in United’s championship push alongside some of the league’s best veterans.

Although his outside shot remains the major question mark, with a career three-point percentage of 32.8%, Travers has the potential to be among the league leaders in rebounds and double-doubles.

He can play multiple positions and defend almost anyone put in front of him.

Travers is primed for an All-NBL selection if he reaches the heights many expect from him.

Big season brewing.

6 | ZYLAN CHEATHAM (ADELAIDE)

New Zealand Breakers Cheatham, who averaged 15.2 points per game, was good.

Adelaide 36ers Cheatham averaged fewer points at 11.7 per game, but became a far more complete player and established himself as one of the best two-way stars in the league.

His debut season with the 36ers saw him finish as one of just two players to average a double-double.

He ranked 12th in assists and was the only forward inside that group, finishing the season averaging 11.7 points, 10.2 rebounds and 4.0 assists per game while adding nearly one steal and one block.

The best part of Cheatham’s game, which can also be a little frustrating at times, is just how selfless he is.

He does whatever he can to help his team win, but that also means he doesn’t always look for his own shot. Cheatham attempted just 7.2 field goals per game last season despite converting them at 54.0%.

Now, he is back for another season with largely the same core around him and is primed for a monster campaign as he approaches his 31st birthday in November.

Cheatham will be pushing for an All-NBL selection this season.

Huge season coming.

5 | NATHAN SOBEY (S.E. MELBOURNE)

Incredibly, Sobey produced arguably the best season of his 12-year NBL career last year and now enters his third campaign with the Phoenix.

Across 32 regular-season appearances, Sobey averaged 22.0 points, 3.9 rebounds and 4.7 assists, setting a franchise scoring record in the process.

He then produced a career-high 41-point performance against Adelaide in the playoffs, further proving that age really is just a number.

Now 36, Sobey will attempt to go one step further this season and help guide the Phoenix, alongside head coach Josh King and a well-rounded roster, towards his first NBL championship.

He is a high-volume scorer who, yes, can frustrate fans at times with his shot selection, but he makes up for it with outrageous hustle and determination to see his team succeed.

He is a leader, a veteran and fully deserving of a spot inside the top five.

4 | KRISTIAN DOOLITTLE (PERTH)

Like fine wine, Doolittle seems to be getting better with age and every season he spends in Australia.

The 28-year-old is coming off the best season of his NBL career, averaging 16.6 points, 7.4 rebounds and 3.6 assists, which included a career-high 34.6% shooting from deep.

He is basically a point forward, more than capable of bringing the ball up the court and initiating the offence, something that proved incredibly valuable for a Wildcats team that lacked a traditional player in that role last season.

Now, as he enters his fourth NBL season, Doolittle is primed to establish himself as one of the league’s best two-way players while also emerging as a sneaky candidate for multiple awards, including MVP.

There is no reason why we couldn’t see Doolittle push towards 20 points per game this season while helping Perth chase another championship.

3 | PARKER JACKSON-CARTWRIGHT (NEW ZEALAND)

He may be one of the smallest stars in the league, but he is also one of the quickest.

That is just part of the reason Jackson-Cartwright comes in at No.3.

Entering his fourth season with the Breakers, Jackson-Cartwright is coming off a record-setting campaign after establishing the 40-minute era record for the most assists in a single season with 249.

He is as pure a point guard as you’ll find.

Not only does New Zealand’s offence flow through him as a facilitator, but he remains one of the league’s best scorers, coming off a season where he averaged 17.7 points, 4.5 rebounds and 7.5 assists.

It is unclear when we might finally see the now 31-year-old slow down, but until those signs appear, he remains a top-three player in this league and deserves the respect that comes with it.

2 | KENDRIC DAVIS (SYDNEY)

Flip a coin between the top two.

Depending on who you ask, the order probably changes, but for now, Davis comes in as the second-best player in the league after producing one of the most impressive seasons you’ll see from an import in just his second year in the NBL.

His first season with the Kings saw him crowned an NBL champion and Finals MVP, with a career-high 40-point performance thrown in for good measure.

Across 33 games, Davis averaged 24.4 points, 3.9 rebounds and 6.7 assists while shooting 47.5% from the field.

He is a star. Simple as that.

His Game 3 performance against Adelaide, coming after the Kings suffered a heartbreaking loss, was legendary.

Davis finished with 34 points and 15 assists without committing a single turnover.

Now, he rejoins a Kings roster that looks a little different, but the challenge and ultimate goal remain exactly the same.

Win another championship.

For Davis, this season is brewing as potentially his best yet, and perhaps this time those extra couple of votes finally go his way and he wins his first MVP.

Third time’s the charm, right?

1 | BRYCE COTTON (ADELAIDE)

He is a six-time MVP for a reason.

Cotton is the best import we have ever seen in this league and is coming off another incredible, career-defining season.

Long known as arguably the most dangerous scorer in the NBL, Cotton showed another side of his game last season as a facilitator, producing a league-leading and career-high 7.6 assists per game to go alongside his 25.7 points, which also led the competition.

Now, he is reunited with Trevor Gleeson, with the pair sharing plenty of championship-winning history from their days together in Perth.

That reunion could unlock another level in Cotton.

He remains the man everyone is chasing. Davis is coming, but for now, Cotton sits at the top.

And he will do everything he can to stay there as he chases another MVP award, one that would see him tie the great Andrew Gaze.
