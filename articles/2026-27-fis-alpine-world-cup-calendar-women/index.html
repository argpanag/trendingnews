2026-27 FIS Alpine World Cup Calendar — Women

TheTools · 18.10.2026 07:30 · source · permalink

With one week to go until the 2026-27 FIS Alpine World Cup season kicks off in Sölden, Austria, it is high time we look at the calendar for the season. The 2026-27 campaign will feature 40 women’s races across 20 venues. The circuit travels from the Austrian Alps to North America before returning to Europe’s classic World Cup venues. The season will also include the 2027 FIS Alpine World Ski Championships in Crans-Montana, Switzerland, from February 1-14, before concluding at Sun Valley, Idaho, in March.

This season also marks the 60th anniversary of the Alpine Ski World Cup. In 1966, French journalist Serge Lang, Team France’s coach Honoré Bonnet, and Team USA’s coach Bob Beattie discussed how to make alpine skiing more attractive during the famous Hahnenkamm race in Kitzbühel. Beattie proposed holding World Championships every year rather than every two, while Bonnet and Lang suggested a World Cup system similar to soccer. Their discussions helped lay the foundations for the international circuit, which launched in January 1967 with races in Europe and North America. The first World Cup season ran from January 5 to March 26, 1967, and featured three disciplines: downhill, giant slalom, and slalom. From 1968-69 onward, the season began in December, while super-G was added in 1982.

Athletes to Watch

Mikaela Shiffrin enters the 2026-27 season as the reigning Overall Crystal Globe winner after claiming her sixth career title in 2025-26, tying the women’s record with Austria’s Annemarie Moser-Pröll. The American also won the slalom Crystal Globe for the ninth time, further extending her dominance in the discipline. This season, however, Shiffrin plans to change things up. Rather than concentrating heavily on slalom, she intends to put more emphasis on giant slalom and super-G while racing a reduced slalom schedule. She has described the coming campaign as a season of exploration, seeking new challenges after years of dominating technical racing.

Shiffrin has not officially confirmed her participation at the Sölden season opener but has been spotted on the Rettenbach Glacier, so is expected to compete. She has confirmed that she will skip the opening slaloms at Levi and Gurgl before beginning her slalom campaign at Killington, a venue close to her Vermont roots. Her ability to remain competitive in giant slalom while developing her super-G form could make her an even more versatile contender. Whether the change strengthens her bid for another overall title remains one of the season’s biggest questions.

The comeback story of the season this year is Slovakian Petra Vlhova, who is coming back after a very long absence from the World Cup circuit. Shiffrin’s biggest rival in slalom skiing is finally poised to compete again after suffering a serious knee injury at the 2024 World Cup races in Jasná, Slovakia. The 2022 Olympic Slalom Champion and 2021 Overall Crystal Globe winner has the pedigree to challenge the best when healthy, but returning to the top level after such a long absence is a major task. Her comeback will be less about results and more about rebuilding confidence and regaining race fitness.

Another comeback of the season is Federica Brignone, who still faces challenges from a leg injury suffered in April 2025. The Italian was basically absent for nearly the entire World Cup season but then swept in to claim to Olympic gold medals at the Milan-Cortina Winter Games. She is the 2024-25 Overall Crystal Globe winner and could be serious contender if she can overcome her continued leg pain. Her technical precision and ability to perform under pressure make her one of the most accomplished racers of her generation.

Emma Aicher was one of the breakout stars of 2025-26, finishing second overall behind Shiffrin. The 22-year-old German racer is the only woman on the World Cup circuit who competes across all four disciplines. She has yet to win a giant slalom podium but has been on the podium in the other three disciplines. Speed, however, remains her strongest area, with three super-G victories and two downhill victories thus far. 10 of her 13 career podiums were achieved last season, so if the rising star can build on last season’s strength, she could be a serious contender for the overall season title.

Next up on our watch list is the wonder-from-downunder, Alice Robinson. The Kiwi had burst onto the World Cup scene in 2019 at age 17 after winning the Junior World Championships — an instant qualifier for the World Cup Finals — and finished her debut on the podium just 0.30 seconds behind Shiffrin. Robinson kicked off her first full World Cup season with a victory at the season opener in Sölden, leaving experts speechless. After her incredible debut season, she backed up with another strong season but then failed to podium for two whole seasons. She made strategic changes to her team and returned to her old form in 2023-24. The 24-year-old is, like Aicher, still finding her form and could be a massive contender for the overall season title after winning her first super-G race last season. For the upcoming season she switched ski sponsors, now skiing for HEAD Rebels. If she can build on last year’s performance, the ski is the limit for this power house.

Switzerland’s Camille Rast has been on the World Cup circuit for a decade but unlike Robinson has been slowly building up to her current form. She finished last season in overall third — her career best. She is a strong technical skier and has shown she can podium and win in both slalom and giant slalom. Rast’s consistency gives her a realistic opportunity to challenge for Crystal Globes again. With Shiffrin exploring a broader programme and Vlhova attempting a comeback, the technical races could offer more intriguing matchups than ever.

Meanwhile, Croatian slalom specialist Zrinka Ljutić’s story is more akin to Robinson. Ljutić had a strong 2024-25 season which saw her claim the slalom crystal globe, however last season, the 22-year-old Croatian could not find her form. She finished 27th overall and 23rd in the slalom standings, well below the level she showed when she emerged as one of the sport’s most promising young technical racers. Ljutić’s talent is not in question, but consistency will be essential if she is to return to the podium regularly. The slalom field is deep, and Shiffrin, Rast, and other technical specialists leave little room for mistakes.

The countdown to the 2026-27 FIS Alpine World Cup season is on. With Shiffrin exploring new disciplines, Vlhova targeting a return, Brignone working toward recovery, and Aicher, Robinson, Rast, and Ljutić looking to make their mark, the women’s circuit promises an intriguing season from Sölden to Sun Valley.
