Sooryavanshi explains the thinking behind his Doha blitz | ICC

TheTools · 25.11.2026 09:41 · source · permalink

The 15-year-old says a half-hour call with his idol, Yuvraj Singh helped him through a lean run, before the Doha pitch convinced him to back his own strengths.

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi has said a long phone call with Yuvraj Singh helped steady him before the innings that rewrote the List A record books at the Asia Cup Rising Stars in Doha.

The India A opener made 204 off 53 balls against UAE on Tuesday. It included the fastest double-century in List A cricket, reached in 52 balls, and 28 sixes, the most in a List A innings. Sooryavanshi also set records for the fastest List A century and 150, and his strike rate of 384.90 is the highest for a List A hundred. India A chased down 250 in 14.2 overs.

The innings came after a quiet few months. Sooryavanshi had not passed 45 since the start of September, and he followed 18 off 12 balls in his only Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy game for Bihar with 26 and 11 in this tournament with India A. He admitted this lean run had affected him.

"I played one or two domestic matches. I didn't get any runs," he said.

"I was a bit distracted. I was thinking, 'what's going on'." He said in an interview with the BCCI website.

Before the UAE game, Sooryavanshi said, he spoke to only four people: his father, his childhood coach Manish Ojha, his friend Pratham Jachak, who played alongside him in the Under-19s, and Yuvraj. He said the call with Yuvraj lasted almost half an hour.

"Because I was very tense, I spoke to him openly," Sooryavanshi said.

"'Sir, I am having these problems when I am going out to bat'. He explained everything to me for half-an-hour."

Yuvraj's central message was about the value of a start. "If you bat for 20 balls, the team's score will be different," Sooryavanshi recalled. The advice also went into the technical side of his batting.

"He is my idol. If you talk to your idol, you listen to him carefully, so I listened to him carefully," he said. "He told me a lot of important things - specific things about my batting."

Sooryavanshi then went back to video of his own batting. "I felt I was making a mistake, which I tried to fix yesterday. I tried to do what he told me to do. That helped me."

The composure on show in Doha follows a pattern. At the ICC Under-19 Men's Cricket World Cup in Zimbabwe in February, Sooryavanshi, then 14, made 175 off 80 balls in the final against England in Harare, with 15 fours and 15 sixes.

It was the highest individual score in a World Cup final at any level. India won by 100 runs for a record sixth Under-19 title, and he became the first player to be named both Player of the Final and Player of the Tournament at an Under-19 World Cup.

Sooryavanshi said he had approached his last two matches with a longer-format mindset, and that he began Tuesday's game the same way. The surface changed his plans.

"When I saw the pitch, I didn't think it would help [the bowlers] much," he said. "If I tried to play my shots, the ball was coming on to my bat. So I decided to back my strengths."

The UAE attack was inexperienced, and only one of its five bowlers had played more than ten List A games.

Sooryavanshi, whose career has moved quickly since IPL 2026, pointed to his adaptability.

"I can change my game according to the situation, I have that much control over myself," he said, recalling what he said after being named Player of the Tournament of the Indian Premier League in Ahmedabad.

India A had lost their previous game to Pakistan A, so the win against UAE was needed to keep their campaign on track.
