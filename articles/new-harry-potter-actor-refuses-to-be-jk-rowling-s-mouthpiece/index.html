Actor Paapa Essiedu, who will play Severus Snape in HBO's upcoming "Harry Potter" remake, recently spoke out about the backlash author JK Rowling has continued to face after making problematic comments about feminism and transgender rights. In a new interview, the actor pushed back against the idea that he needed to separate himself from the series entirely because of Rowling's views. "I have been super clear about my position on this topic," he said, adding that he refused to be labeled Rowling's "mouthpiece" despite his affiliation with the upcoming series.

New 'Harry Potter' Actor Pushes Back Against Belief He Has To Speak Out About JK Rowling's Problematic Stances

MEGA

Speaking with Vanity Fair, Essiedu said he wasn't interested in fanning the flame or discussing Rowling's views as his new series approaches. "I'm really keen not to make a kind of 'them and us' dynamic here. I'm not really here to speak about Jo's politics or to be a mouthpiece for her," he said. "I can only be really, really clear about what I believe and be consistent with that."

Essiedu has spoken previously about his beliefs surrounding trans people, signing an open letter disagreeing with the UK Supreme Court's ruling that trans women and men are not legally considered women and men, according to PEOPLE. And if that wasn't enough, he made his position abundantly clear during his latest interview.

'Harry Potter' Star Says Trans People Have The 'Right' To Be Treated With 'Respect'

"I believe, in my bones, that trans people, queer people, people of all creeds and color have got the right to be treated with respect and dignity and safety at all times, whether they're in the workplace, whether they're at home or in the street, whatever," Essiedu said to Vanity Fair. "That's what I believe, and that's the way that I choose to live my life. That's the creed by which I live."

Essiedu will appear as Professor Severus Snape in the upcoming "Harry Potter" remake. While some of the news surrounding the remake has been negative due to Rowling's headline-making comments on trans women and other social issues, Essiedu said the TV series has the chance to do "really beautiful things."

"When I come back to this story, this actual story, I'm thinking about the themes that it explores," he said. "Themes of forgiveness and good triumphing over evil, and of accepting people for who they are and allowing people to really be themselves within a world where anything is possible."

Essiedu isn't the only "Harry Potter" TV star to condemn the famous author ahead of the new series' air date. Bel Powley, who will portray Aunt Petunia Dursley in the upcoming batch of episodes, also condemned the author's previous comments in an interview with The Telegraph.

"I strongly disagree with J.K. Rowling's views on gender. I think that the trans community deserves safety and dignity and respect and total acceptance," she said. "But I love Harry Potter. I grew up with the books. I am from that first generation of readers and, again, I love magic and spookiness."

Don't forget, Rowling first came under fire in 2020 after making posts on X, formerly known as Twitter, that readers viewed as transphobic. "People who menstruate.' I'm sure there used to be a word for those people. Someone help me out. Wumben? Wimpund? Woomud?" she wrote at the time.

JK Rowling Previously Supported A British Researcher Who Was Fired For Making Anti-Trans Comments

A year before that, Rowling faced even more backlash for supporting a British researcher who was fired from the Center for Global Development for making anti-trans comments. The researcher, identified as Maya Forstater, was fired after posting that "men cannot change into women," according to PEOPLE.

Rowling fired back with a public statement of her own, though, writing, "Dress however you please. Call yourself whatever you like. Sleep with any consenting adult who'll have you. Live your best life in peace and security. But force women out of their jobs for stating that sex is real? #IStandWithMaya #ThisIsNotADrill."
