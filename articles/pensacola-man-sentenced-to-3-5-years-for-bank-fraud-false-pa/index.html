Pensacola man sentenced to 3.5 years for bank fraud, false passport, and identity theft

PENSACOLA, Fla. -- A 23-year-old Pensacola man will spend more than three years in federal prison for his role in a bank fraud and identity theft scheme that used stolen identities and fraudulent passport documents to open accounts and cash counterfeit checks, according to federal prosecutors.

Vontavius Jamaal Bradley was sentenced to 3.5 years in federal prison after previously pleading guilty to conspiracy to commit bank fraud, use of a false passport, and aggravated identity theft.

John P. Heekin, United States attorney for the Northern District of Florida, announced the charges and sentence on Tuesday.

According to court documents, Bradley conspired with co-defendants Elizabeth Ann Rogers and Donny Bernard Ross, and others, to open accounts at various banks and credit unions in the Pensacola area using stolen identities.

Once the accounts were opened, the defendants used them to cash stolen, altered, or counterfeit checks, court records say.

Bradley obtained and provided fraudulent identification documents to accomplices, including false U.S. passport cards, according to court documents.

Court records say he also provided fraudulent checks used in the transactions, and the co-defendants split the proceeds.

As part of the scheme, Bradley and his co-defendants obtained and used personal identifying information of real people without their knowledge or authorization, according to court documents.

In addition to the prison sentence, Bradley was ordered to pay $18,264 in restitution to financial institution and identity theft victims.

Rogers and Ross have also pleaded guilty to the same charges and are awaiting sentencing before United States District Judge M. Casey Rodgers.

The case resulted from a joint investigation by the U.S. Department of State’s Diplomatic Security Service, Homeland Security Investigations, the Escambia County Sheriff’s Office, and the U.S. Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration.
