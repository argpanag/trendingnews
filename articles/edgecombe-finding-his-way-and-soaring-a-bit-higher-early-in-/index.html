Edgecombe finding his way and soaring a bit higher early in 2nd Sixers season

TheTools · 07.11.2026 02:39 · source · permalink

VJ Edgecombe is adapting to life in the 7-1 Sixers' starry starting lineup.

Edgecomeb’s 11 first quarter points were fueled by his dunks

In his second season as a Sixer, it appears the beauty of VJ Edgecombe’s game will be blending the moments that make everyone marvel and the nuanced, non-scoring plays that help his team.

Edgecombe threw down four first-half dunks Monday night in the 7-1 Sixers’ win over the Bulls. He posted 21 points on 9-for-12 shooting and five assists.

“We were able to get out in transition,” Edgecombe said. “I’m still just finding ways, trying to play off of everybody. I got a lot of easy ones tonight.”

Edgecombe is in the early stages of adapting to life in a starry starting lineup that now features LeBron James and Jaylen Brown alongside Tyrese Maxey and Joel Embiid.  The Sixers want last year’s Rising Stars MVP to play his natural game, but the gist of Edgecombe’s job has changed.

“I think his role has shifted a little bit for us this year in that one of the things at the top of his priority list is to supply energy,” Sixers head coach Nick Nurse said. “He’s the youngest of the crew. He’s got to have explosive energy at the start of most games. 

“And it’s a little shift for him because he was playing super huge minutes … last year. He doesn’t have the ball in his hands as much, so he’s got to go get it and find it. He’s got to do all the other things like flying in for rebounds. … And he’s probably going to be on the best backcourt player. Him coming out with that kind of spark is what we need.” 

Through seven games, Edgecombe has averaged 12.7 points, 7.1 rebounds, 3.0 assists and 1.1 steals in 33.3 minutes per contest.

He looks on track to be one of the top guard rebounders in the NBA. Edgecombe’s defended high-usage ball handlers ranging from Jalen Brunson to Josh Giddey to Donovan Mitchell. He hadn’t exceeded the 20-point mark before Monday, however, and he started the year a tad cold from three-point range. After going 2 for 4 in each of his last two games, he’s up to 34.3 percent. 

“I don’t get antsy or impatient or anything,” Edgecombe said. “We’re 7-1 and that’s the most important thing. …Tonight I was able to get going a little bit (as a scorer). Once you find that joy out there, just playing basketball … I was kind of hard on myself early because I was missing shots I (usually) make. And that’s not on anybody else but me. 

“It’s fun when you’re winning. I don’t want to be a cancer because I’m not getting the shots I want to get. I’m still learning. I’m in Year 2. I know everybody’s expecting this big leap; they were expecting me to average 20-something. But it’s fine. The most important thing to me is winning and whatever I’ve got to do every night, I go out there and do. Whether that’s rebounding, defending, whatever, I’ll do it.” 

Beyond the points, aspects of Edgecombe’s play are destined to fly under the radar. 

Savvy cuts, sustained defensive attention to detail and old-fashioned box-outs won’t pop up on many highlight tapes. Those areas all matter to the Sixers, though, and Edgecombe certainly seems to be bought in. 

The sample size is tiny, but he’s currently second in the league in points per possession off cuts at 1.80. He’s shot 8 for 9 off cuts, according NBA.com.

“They all require so much attention, so all eyes are on them,” Edgecombe said. “That’s where I can sneak in a little backdoor, sneak in a little cut, just because everyone is focused on them. Everybody’s focused on the ball. If I cut … it might not get me the ball and I might not get the shot, but somebody else might get a shot or it might open up a lane for somebody else. 

“That’s my main thing. There’s going to be possessions where I don’t touch the ball. There’s going to be possessions where I touch the ball. If I can create an advantage for somebody else on my team, that’s the most important thing.”

When the Sixers are sprinting forward in transition, Edgecombe has learned that his teammates are eager to see him shine. And James is as capable as they come at flinging long-distance passes to open men. 

“I told him (at training camp) all he has to do is run and I’ll try to put it on time, on target as much as I can,” James said. “With his athleticism and his speed — and his athleticism above the rim, too — it just allows him to play above and be a recipient of those passes.”

Back at camp, Maxey had shared that Edgecombe “was dunking last year, but he wasn’t dunking like this.” 

He’s building some strong in-game evidence to back up that claim. 

“I must say I think I got a little more on my vert,” Edgecombe said with a laugh. “I think I got a little faster, a little stronger. That was my goal going into the offseason, just making sure I came back as explosive as I could be, as strong as I could be. 

“And obviously, strength also comes with time. I’m only 21, so that comes with time. They’ve got dad strength and all that. I ain’t there yet. I just tried to make sure that I was putting in the work so I could be more explosive.” 

Everyone tends to take notice when Edgecombe shows off that explosiveness.

It’s just not the only thing the Sixers appreciate about their second-year standout. 

“I’ve got great teammates,” he said. “They encourage me every day to keep being myself. … Whatever I can do to help us win, that’s the most important thing to me.”
