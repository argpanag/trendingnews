Judi James previews Celebrity Traitors semi final

TheTools · 30.10.2026 20:36 · source · permalink

/

/

The body language expert makes her predictions for a nervy night at the castle.

The Celebrity Traitors is hitting beast mode as we head into the semi-finals!

Thursday night’s episode had us all on the edge of our seats as comedians James Acaster and Joanne MacNally did not see the funny side of being banished from the castle – with faithful Sharon Rooney murdered.

The tension is becoming almost too much to bear, but luckily you can distract yourself with something lighter over on Paddy Power Games, or see if your numbers pop up with Paddy Power Bingo.

Ahead of a HUGE night, Paddy checks in with body language expert Judi James (below) to get the latest on all the scheming.

Last night’s drama exploded Celebrity Traitors at its very concept and core, meaning a thrilling, iconic head-to-head tonight will be vital to its ongoing success.

A Traitor self-disclosing and quitting over having to show Traitor-y Traits implied the one thing that every celebrity that has ever appeared on the show has gone to massive lengths to deny, which is that it is not just another panto-style gig where villainous behaviours and acts of seismic disloyalty are part of the ‘on stage’ fun. A chilly air of reality blew through the castle last night that would have sent shivers down all the competitor’s spines as well as, alarmingly, the spine of Queen Claudia herself.

The contestants in the celebrity version spend their entire time on the show strenuously denying there is anything genuinely nasty about their skulduggerous and ruthless behaviors.

We need to know they are all lovely folk behind the scenes and the same applies to Claudia, who has to host some otherwise cruel-looking moments.

They need to do this for their career survival. The people they are competing against can be fellow actors, comics on the same circuit, even people who might be responsible for booking them onto their own shows in future.

Reputations can be crushed like a grape overnight if anyone decides to take genuine umbrage over being banished or ‘killed’ by a fellow performer. Remember when Alan Carr ‘killed off’ his reputed ‘best friend’ Paloma Faith? It turned borderline nasty and speculation over a genuine off-screen spat and split rumbled for months after the show was ended.

Unlike the civilian version, nobody on Celebrity Traitors can be allowed to be seen to be taking the game totally seriously, which in turn allows it to be thrilling because the Traitors can act as evil as they like while the Faithfuls can go around accusing each other without upsetting or appearing to genuinely dislike each other.

This is a hugely fragile concept. At the very least they are being asked to do the one thing any performer can do that will be seen as an act of open aggression to a fellow performer, which is to cut their screen time.

Celebrities go on reality shows to boost their careers, plug their own shows and/or resurrect their profiles. A two to three week run on prime-time TV will have the bookings flooding in and the agents rubbing their hands in glee. Banishing a fellow celeb from performing in front of the kind of viewing figures Traitors has can be like a hefty knee to their bank balance.

The proof of this delicate web of essential chumminess can be seen in the tsunami of ‘love’ that appears on every show. All those love hearts chalked on the name boards; all the ‘I adore you’s’ that are yelled as they banish and all the ‘friends for life’ claims are about sustaining the narrative that their traitorous behaviors, performed lies and acts of ‘murder’ aren’t real. They are shoring up their own brand popularity and the sense that the lies and villainy are just panto.

So, when a Traitor self-reveals and quits over a distaste for lying and deception it can leave the other players and even Claudia left feeling vulnerable, like Captain Hook walking off in the middle of the panto because he didn’t like being beastly to Peter Pan. It leaves the rest of the cast hanging.

Traitors provides an outlet for everyone’s secret wish to play act the ‘bad guy’ for a while and, essentially, to lie without guilt or compunction. Watching people openly lying to save their own skin is usually only reserved for court rooms or politics but here the fans get a chance to watch people perform lies openly, without guilt and without fear of real-life recrimination.

The other arc for Traitors is actually one of loyalty among a game where disloyalty can be part of the survival process. After all the skulduggery of the last civilian Traitors there was that cathartic, tear-inducing moment right at the end where Rachel and Stephen did trust each other and work together to win the series and share the prize money and everyone could rest easy in their beds that night knowing the world is a good place after all.

The success of reality shows like Big Brother rested heavily on a similar redemption arc, where despite all the bad behavior in the House, the nice guy tended to win the show, illustrating a moral level of the fans to the fans themselves.

A lot rests on tonights show then and whether it can make James’s reveal as a Traitor look like a temporary blip. Traitor ‘morals’ have a good champion in Maya when it comes to passing the legacy of ruthlessness on to the next generation unharmed. Maya’s day job as a ‘game show’ host runs along similar moral lines meaning she has shown no signals of any guilt about playing the game hard.

Maya gets the format and she has been the one egging her fellow Traitors on to be even more dastardly at times.

Maya should happily carry on the panto villain aspect of Traitoring to the next ‘generation’, although she has been left totally exposed and vulnerable by last night’s round table, meaning she could be swept off the board in an instant. The show badly needs a final moment that builds on what we saw last night: a ‘Holmes and Moriarty’ – sized clash between Maya and Michael Sheen.

MICHAEL SHEEN’S DIGIT OF DOOM, HOW A MELODRAMATIC DIGIT GESTURE GAVE ONE TRAITOR NOWHERE TO HIDE

Michael has emerged as a worthy hero of this series. He played low to avoid an early banishment but he also established himself as such a calm, intelligent and brave strength for the Faithfuls that even when logic appeared to imply he must be a Traitor the rest of the group knew they would feel vulnerable without him there.

He became the wise father figure that nobody wanted to banish and there were powerful body language moments, like his terrifying grimace during the blindfolded ‘make a shield’ task where he thwarted the Traitors by scaring them via body language alone.

His dramatic finger point at the round table last night was straight from Poirot as he suddenly wheeled his entire body round to point the accusing finger in a way that prompted an instant confession. The power of his non-verbal gesture set off a chain of ‘disaster’ that is still set to rumble tonight.

If Michael had used his index finger pointing in the same half-hearted way as all the other Faithfuls have, i.e in a slightly random signal of weaker intent, there might have been a three-Traitor final, but the way he spoke first to the group about his suspicions before suddenly wheeling round to pin his Traitor with all the confident surety of eye contact and a direct, surprise point that seemed to be resilient to any declaration of innocence led to one of the most dramatic moments of the entire series.

Michael’s body language was what prompted the Traitor self-reveal, and he did it in a gesture that Agatha Christie herself would have applauded as it left absolutely no scope for denial. When you suddenly turn to point you shock your ‘victim’, leaving them no scope to apply a performed denial or lie. It was the kind of ‘It’s a fair cop’ moment that you normally only see in movies and plays. One key factor in lying is time. The brain needs to first think of the truth then to suppress it and lastly to create the lie performance and reaction. Michael’s surprise ritual gave no time for any of this and it proved effective and lethal.

MICHAEL’S ‘NICE GUY’ TELL THAT COULD BE HIS DOWNFALL

Michael did show one ‘tell’ that might be a fatal flaw when it comes to unmasking more Traitors though. He is very brave about accusing them but his hand gesture at the prospect of doing so last night suggested he is a nice guy who intrinsically finds no pleasure in the human aspect of doing it in the finale.

He named all three Traitors correctly but as he did so he massaged his hands together in a self-soothe ritual that would imply unease or discomfort. His levels of empathy might make him falter in terms of continuing his full house win by going for the jugular and unmasking the full set of Traitors. He now appears able to do so but he might have to deal with some inner emotional squeamishness, rather than taking any total pleasure in the victory as someone like Ross might have done.

MAYA BUCKLES WITHOUT HER COMFORT EATING PROPS

The show’s villainous heroine is now leaking like a colander and her moment of declaring that ‘That’s what we need to be killing them…banishing them’ moment of verbal reveal as a Traitor showed her ploy of keeping quiet during the big conversations has been a wise one.

Her physical ploy to avoid moments like this and detection in general has largely been dough-based. Maya has been continually stuffing her mouth with food, mainly bread or croissants, to give herself an excuse for not commenting.

Last night she even showed another reason for using food as a prop. When she was under pressure of accusation from Michael in the kitchen, she stood holding up a lump of bread that she was eating, and as she denied any guilt she brandished the bread aloft, level with her face but away from it.

Food tends to have a visually hypnotic effect on the viewer, meaning we tend to succumb to an almost primal urge to stare at it, which made it the perfect deliberate distraction for Maya to use to avoid being stared at straight in the face as she denied any guilt. She might need to dangle a whole bread basket of sourdough tonight though to avoid scrutiny from the remaining Faithfuls.

The proof of this food as a distraction theory can be seen in the queue for the till in the supermarket, where everyone feels impelled to just stare at the shopping in the trolley of the person in front.

MAYA v MICHAEL: FOUR KEY TELLS THAT MAYA SHOWED TO SILENTLY & UNWITTINGLY CONFIRM HER TRAITOR STATUS

If neither of these two ‘big beasts’ of the series make it through to the final, this last dramatic scene would have at least created one of the most memorable moments of the series.

As Maya and Michael stood around the brazier Michael went into full Van Helsing (The vampire hunter from Dracula) mode with his body language and completely destroyed Maya’s aura of leadership self-control.

Michael’s simple technique was a kindly but knowing smile and silent pauses.

His friendly, paternal smile came with a deadly ending though when the warmth died from his cheeks in a denial gesture and his eyes scanned her up and down as though assessing her for the final encounter.

His ‘As we began…’ sounded important and sonorous.

Maya’s first tell came as she ‘blinked first’, looking downward in a cut-off of implied guilt under his gaze.

The second tell came in her hand position. Michael’s were held in a clasp that showed he was warming them in the cold night air. Maya appeared to mirror his pose, but her fingers were entwined in a mesh, which is a signal of a desire to both form a barrier and to avoid conversation.

He made himself look sociable, but her body language suggested no desire to chat. If Maya were a Faithful, she would have been assessing Michael with her eyes in exactly the same way he was assessing her but she showed her guilt and fear by avoiding any engagement either verbal or with any questioning gaze.

Maya’s third tell came as her smile became a ‘fear-sneer’, with her lips closed and pulled down at the corners.

The fourth was when her eyes then performed some dramatic darting, looking up and away into the distance to avoid his scrutinising stare. These tells even ended in a tumbleweed moment where she sloped off rather than walking off with confidence.

Maya will be aware that if she doesn’t murder Michael, she has given her Traitor status away to him so blandly here that he will probably come for her, making Michael vulnerable. But she is also a professional show host who would be aware of the dramatic potential of a finale between herself and Michael, which might possibly tempt her to allow him to live.
