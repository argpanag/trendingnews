Can bipartisan results keep HD 89 blue? Debra Tendrich is banking on it as Cathy Higgins seeks flip

Democratic Rep. Debra Tendrich spent her first term building bipartisan support in Tallahassee to pass far-reaching legislation. Now, she’s hoping to leverage that cross-aisle goodwill into another victory in House District 89.

Standing in the Lake Worth Beach lawmaker’s way is Republican Cathy Higgins, a former Atlantis Mayor, who boasts deep experience in local government, education and business.

Affordability, public education and public safety figure prominently in both candidates’ pitches.

HD 89 covers an inland portion of Palm Beach County including all or parts of Atlantis, Greenacres, Lake Clarke Shores, Lake Worth Beach and Palm Springs.

Democrats hold a 38% share of the electorate, while Republicans and independents account for 29% and 33%, respectively. Voters there belonging to no party or a third party have skewed rightward in recent cycles, though not yet enough for a flip.

Tendrich won her seat in 2024 by nearly 3 percentage points, succeeding term-limited Democratic Rep. David Silvers. That same year, Kamala Harris took HD 89 by roughly a point.

While Tendrich again this year faced no opponent on the Primary ballot, Higgins secured her nomination by winning 67% of the Republican Primary against Jeff Buongiorno, who made news just before the Primary for getting arrested at the Supervisor of Elections’ main office for refusing to move his campaign table.

Tendrich, 40, brings a personal story of perseverance to the race. She fled an abusive relationship in 2012, arriving in Florida with her daughter and little else before rebuilding her life. In 2016, she founded Eat Better Live Better, a nonprofit focused on nutrition education and food assistance.

Her work with struggling families and experience escaping domestic violence helped to shape her legislative agenda.

She passed a domestic violence package this year, with unanimous approval in both chambers of the Legislature, to increase penalties for repeat offenders, expand relocation assistance for victims and allow protective injunctions to address threats against pets and service animals.

The measure, which earned her laurels from multiple advocacy organizations, also strengthened coordination involving military protective orders and established an electronic-monitoring pilot program in Pinellas County.

Tendrich worked on the measure with Rep. Danny Nix and Sen. Alexis Calatayud — both Republicans — anchoring her argument that she can deliver from the minority party through smart collaboration with Republicans.

Other bills she passed supported students with special needs, improved West Palm Beach police and firefighter pensions and established Holocaust Remembrance Day.

But her cross-party work has produced disagreements within her caucus. This year, Tendrich was the co-prime sponsor of a proposal to replace references to the West Bank with the more historical “Judea and Samaria” in state materials and newly acquired schoolbooks.

Supporters described it as recognizing Jewish history — Tendrich is Jewish — while some Democratic opponents questioned the state’s role in renaming foreign territory.

The bill passed the House, with 14 Democrats voting “no,” including House Democratic Leader Fentrice Driskell and House Democratic Leader-designate Christine Hunschofsky. But it died unheard in the Senate.

Tendrich wants another term to address insurance and housing costs, strengthen public schools and teacher pay, expand workforce preparation and improve mental health services, according to an endorsement editorial from the Palm Beach Post.

Higgins, 57, spent more than a decade in local elected service managing municipal priorities before mounting her bid for state office. Born in West Palm Beach and raised in Lake Worth, she served 12 years on the Atlantis City Council, including three as the city’s first woman Mayor.

In private life, she taught dance at the Dreyfoos School of the Arts for 16 years and today directs the dance program at The King’s Academy, according to her campaign website. She also operates valet and security companies with her husband that she says employ nearly 400 people.

Higgins’ platform emphasizes reducing household costs, supporting law enforcement, limiting government spending and managing development to protect infrastructure and green space. On education, she favors competitive teacher salaries, school choice and expanded career and technical programs.

She says her municipal tenure — which included preserving funding reserves, avoiding tax increases, improving emergency communications and helping to develop parks — makes her well prepared to address statewide affordability pressures.

Higgins also cites her lifelong involvement in community service. Her late mother, Gwendolyn James, founded The Soup Kitchen of Palm Beach County, and Higgins herself describes four terms leading a philanthropic education organization’s local chapter and participation in church missions building homes in Mexico.

Higgins has endorsements from Atlantis Mayor Michael LaCoursiere, Vice Mayor Aaron Rinker and Council Treasurer Derek Cooper, along with the business-focused group Associated Industries of Florida.

Tendrich enjoys bipartisan support from dozens of local officials and organizations, including Palm Beach County School Board Chair Karen Brill, State Attorney Alexcia Cox, Public Defender Daniel Eisinger, Tax Collector Anne Gannon, School Board Member and County Commissioner-elect Erica Whitfield, School Board Member Virginia Savietto, County Commissioner Joel Flores, a passel of municipal officials from both sides of the aisle and groups like the Florida AFL-CIO, AFSCME Florida, Florida Fraternal Order of Police, Florida Planned Parenthood, Florida Police Benevolent Association, Florida Professional Firefighters, Florida Realtors PAC, Ruth’s List Florida and The Hispanic Vote.

The Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee (DLCC) has also made Tendrich a defense priority this cycle as Democrats seek to break Republican supermajorities in Tallahassee.

Tendrich raised more than $139,000 through her campaign account and political committee between January 2025 and mid-October. She had about $100,500 remaining just over two weeks before Election Day.

She also received about $28,000 in in-kind aid from the DLCC and Florida Democratic Party for research and staffing costs in addition to about $6,000 worth of in-kind support from others for various campaign-related endeavors.

Higgins, meanwhile, amassed close to $78,000 through her campaign account and political committee, of which she had roughly $52,500 left by Oct. 16.

Another $29,400 in in-kind aid came Higgins’ way from the Republican Party of Florida and Florida House Republican Campaign Committee, earmarked for professional consulting and campaign staff.

Election Day is Nov. 3.
