Second half

Room for improvement for both teams.

Gordon gets us going.

Gervasse Green from Sydney has written in:

“Morning Dan, Serious question: is Suaalii even playing? First half almost done, and not a single mention. No tackle? A scything run maybe? Performed well in the pre-match anthem…?”

He’s on the field. But he’s not done anything.

To be fair, not entirely his fault. Ikitau hasn’t exactly lit the place up either. Australia just can’t get their backs in the game. Is that on Gordon? I’m not sure. Is it on the pack? Not sure either. It’s just been a scrappy affair. McReight and Valetini the only men in gold producing so far. Lonergan is having a decent game to be fair.

Not a great half. But Scotland will feel they’re deserve the lead. I certainly feel they deserve the lead.

Two tries – one apiece – chalked off the record. Scotland scored two, Australia one. There were a million knock-ons and spills.

One of those games I’m afraid. So far, at least.

Half-time: Scotland 12-5 Australia

A stodgy half ends with Gordon kicking the ball out, as he attempted to find Jorgensen on the right wing with a cross-field kick.

In truth, that was pretty scrappy from the Aussies, who won the line-out five metres from the line but didn’t do anything with the ball. They were all bunched so close to each other and the defence held. Gordon tried to pick the lock but overcooked his kick.

40 min: Australia on the ball and they lose it. Rinse and repeat in this game. But there’s Fraser McReight who steps in and gets low, fast and furious and accurate, and he wins a penalty on the floor to secure the scrappy ball. First it was Darge to rip from Potter, but McReight is like a hyena, he just sniffs the opportunity. Line-out five out fort Australia. Can they end the half with points?

39 min: Australia almost find an edge as they from the scrum, left to right, and get it ti Wright. He curves his run but is brought down, just, by a diving Dobie. However, there’s a high shot soon after so Australia kick it out, not quite inside Scotland’s 22, for a line-out late in the half.

38 min: Jordan grabs a brilliant catch in the air to give Scotland go forward. Then a couple of big rumbles on has the team in blue cantering through the heart of the Aussie midfield. M Fagerson the latest to do so but he spills in contact. Not the first time I’ve written that. The ball is greasy, but you feel both sides could be doing more to protect the ball.

35 min: They won’t be denied this time! The maul is such a potent weapon in this sport. All the more reason not to give soft penalties at the scrum.

Oh, but was there an Aussie infringment, possible obstruction, in the build up?

Yes, say the refereees, so NO TRY! The lifters at the line-out obstructed the ball and prevented the Scottish pack from having a fair crack at stopping the maul.

Russell hoofs it clear and the home team keep their advantage.

34 min: Z Fagerson brings the maul down illegally. Will he be shown a card? Not this time, but another penalty in the red zone will surely see someone get their marching orders. In that sequence, as Australia tried to work the space, Williamson smoked Wilson.

31 min: Scrum for Australia after a Scottish spill on halfway. Another error from the home pack, but this time it’s a full penalty. Gordon rakes a brilliant kick to the corner to set up a line-out five out. That really is a superb kick and now a great opportunity to hit back immediately.

TRY! Scotland 12-5 Australia (Dempsey, 28)

The Aussie scores against his former team! No denying his allegiances with that meaty carry from close range. Really composed from Scotland after the maul five out was sacked well by the Wallabies. So Scotland went with the pick and go, pick and go. First Ashman, then Fagerson before Dempsey looked up, spotted a crack in the wall, and burrowed over. Russell this time lands the extras from just left of the sticks.

27 min: Penalty for Scotland after about 10 phases from the line-out. They didn’t go forward much, but for a strong carry from Darge down the opposite end of the pitch. Then Valetini put in a mighty hit on Dempsey that encouraged his mates to come in for the steal, but they did so illegally so Scotland get a chance to kick to the corner, which they do.

25 min: After finding himself in a tangle at the line-out, which his team just about secured, Lonergan hoofs a decent touch finder with his heels touching his own line.

But it’s not gone very far so Scotland will have an opportunity to throw to the line just beyond the Australian 22. They’ll do so without their set piece leader as Cummins leaves the field having taken a nasty fall from the previous line-out. On comes Williamson.

24 min: NO TRY! So close to a stunner from Scotland. Off the line-out, Russell punted across the field, from the centre to the right, and Steyn ran round the covering Wallaby and managed to get a boot on it, kicking it back infield and over the try line where Graham swooped to dot down. But replays showed that the ball had crossed the perimeter of the pitch so it’ll be an Aussie line-out five from their own line.

23 min: There’s a charge down, oh, but Canham is off side! That was close. He saw a chance to get in White’s face as the Scotland 9 readied himself to box from a maul following a line-out, but the Aussie lock was little too early. So now Scotland get a chance to line-out inside Australia’s half.

22 min: Scotland pinch a turnover then make some ground down the right touch. But they’re soon turned over, after som Harlem Globetrotter style rugby with Steyn flinging a behind the back pass. The Aussies scramble and win the turnover on the ground as McReight smokes White, who had a dart, then gets to his feet to make the steal. There is no better jackal in the game.

20 min: Kinghorn catches a tester from Wright. Then Jorgensen fields a box kick. Gordon hoofs to the corner and forces Russell to try a banana kick to find touch. That all ends with a big net gain in territory for Australia. Gordon with the best punt in that sequence. Not necessarily long, but in the corner.

18 min: Lonergan receives Russell’s drop-out clearance and fancies an attempt at a drop-goal. He doesn’t quite catch all of it and misses.

17 min: The scrum takes an age to settle. We need a few goes before a free kick is awarded to Australia. That’s the second time the Scots have been pinned. A kick pins Kinghorn down in his own 22 but a lovely return has Gordon fielding. Potter runs it before the forwards get to work. Lonergan then hoists a box. Jorgensen catches it and Wright rakes a kick to the corner for Potter, but it’s fractionally long and Russell can field it and dot down in the goal area for a drop-out.

15 min: Back on the pitch, Scotland look to run it sooner after conceding. But they spill the ball in contact which means we’ll get an Aussie scrum just beyond their own 22.

Decent start this from both sides. Ball in hand they look threatening. White is having a really good game, zipping it about.

The gainline battle is something. Valetini and Wilson putting in some big hits.

Magic from Harry Potter! Forgive me grabbing the lowest of hanging fruits but that really is a special finish from the winger. Australia’s first bit of possession had Gordon combine with multiple mates before Potter recieved it on the left wing. He still had a load of work to do, stepping off his right and holding off the challenge of two tacklers. He was brought to ground but wriggled and dotted down. Great finish.

The extras are missed from out wide so we’re all level.

TRY! Scotland 5-0 Australia (M Fagerson, 9)

Scotland on the board! Great work, so clinical. From the scrum, they had perpetual go forward. First Jones then darge. Eventually White flung a wuick pass to Fagerson who was loitering on the right wing. He dived low to score in the corner. Russell couldn’t slot the extras, hitting the pole.

8 min: Scotland win the scrum and Jones carries hard from first reciever. They’re pressing, so close….

6 min: The exit was made easy for Australia as Scotland were over eager at the scrum, giving away a free kick. But White is back over the ball after winning a line-out. Russell grubbers ahead and Gordon has to mop up. He’s swamped in the corner and there’s a turnover, Scotland on the ball. Alaalatoa steals it back but he knocks on! Almost from the prop, but that spill means Scotland get a scrum five out in the right corner. Great kick from Russell there, real class and awareness that out Gordon under pressure.

5 min: They look to maul but it splutters, so Ashman peels off and darts himself. They keep the ball but snake across the field, from right to left, without going forward. Eventually they spill it. Great defence from Australia who first halted the maul and then bossed the gainline. Valetini I think with the final hit that dislodged the ball. Scrum to the Wallabies on their own line.

4 min A very kickable penalty is instead hoofed to the corner. Right into the corner so Scotland get a throw five out.

2 min: A bit of kick tennis before White hoists a high ball. Scotland comes away with it as Dempsey, who used to play for Australia, runs it up. Scotland get an advantage as a Wallaby strays off side. Nothing comes of it so they get a penalty bang in front, about 30 metres back.

There’s no shortage of Australian connections in the Scotland camp.

Captain Sione Tuipulotu was born and raised in Melbourne, represented Australia at Under-20 level and played for the Rebels before qualifying for Scotland through his Greenock-born grandmother. She famously flew over from Australia to surprise him and his brother Mosese before the Wallabies’ visit in 2024, then watched Sione score a try in Scotland’s 27-13 victory.

Then there’s Jack Dempsey, born in Sydney and capped 14 times by Australia, including at the 2019 World Cup, before switching allegiance in 2022 through his Scottish grandfather. His Scotland debut? Against Australia, naturally.

Even Gregor Townsend has a connection. He spent two seasons playing club rugby for Warringah in Sydney in the 1990s, where he crossed paths with future Wallabies coach Michael Cheika, then playing for Randwick.

Australia team news

Australia have reshuffled their side following last weekend’s heavy defeat by England, with Carter Gordon returning at fly-half after recovering from a broken leg.

The former rugby league recruit replaces Ben Donaldson and forms a half-back partnership with Ryan Lonergan.

An injury to Isaac Henry sees Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii shift into midfield alongside Len Ikitau, with Harry Potter coming onto the wing. Up front, Rob Valetini returns to the starting side at blindside flanker, while Jeremy Williams moves back into the second row alongside Josh Canham.

Captain Harry Wilson continues at No 8, with Fraser McReight completing a formidable back row. The Wallabies have struggled for consistency throughout the Nations Championship and will be looking to arrest their slide against a Scotland side that has enjoyed the better of this fixture in recent years.

Australia: Tom Wright; Max Jorgensen, Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii, Len Ikitau, Harry Potter; Carter Gordon, Ryan Lonergan; Angus Bell, Billy Pollard, Allan Alaalatoa, Josh Canham, Jeremy Williams, Rob Valetini, Fraser McReight, Harry Wilson (capt).

Replacements: Brandon Paenga-Amosa, Isaac Kailea, Taniela Tupou, Lachlan Shaw, Tom Hooper, Tate McDermott, Ben Donaldson, Filipo Daugunu.

Scotland team news

Gregor Townsend has made just two changes to the side beaten 43-21 by New Zealand last weekend, restoring Blair Kinghorn at full-back and Scotland’s record try-scorer Darcy Graham on the wing.

Kyle Rowe drops out of the squad altogether, while Jamie Dobie moves to the bench. The pack remains unchanged, with Ewan Ashman retaining his place at hooker after scoring a remarkable hat-trick against the All Blacks.

Finn Russell and Ben White combine at half-back for the 28th time, while captain Sione Tuipulotu continues his midfield partnership with Huw Jones.

Newcastle back-rower Ollie Leatherbarrow could make his international debut from a six-two bench. Scotland have won four of their last five meetings with Australia, including a 27-13 victory at Murrayfield in 2024, and will be desperate to maintain that record after another frustrating defeat by the All Blacks.

Scotland: Blair Kinghorn; Kyle Steyn, Huw Jones, Sione Tuipulotu (capt), Darcy Graham; Finn Russell, Ben White; Rory Sutherland, Ewan Ashman, Zander Fagerson, Gregor Brown, Scott Cummings, Matt Fagerson, Rory Darge, Jack Dempsey.

Replacements: Gregor Hiddleston, Pierre Schoeman, Will Hurd, Max Williamson, Josh Bayliss, Ollie Leatherbarrow, Jamie Dobie, Tom Jordan.
