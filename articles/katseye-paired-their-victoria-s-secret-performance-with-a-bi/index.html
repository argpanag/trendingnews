Katseye Paired Their Victoria’s Secret Performance with a Big Announcement

TheTools · 19.10.2026 01:06 · source · permalink

They sang “Pinky Up” during the PINK section of the show

Katseye is the only logical performer to introduce the PINK section of the Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show.

The four girls followed Tate McRae’s performance with “Pinky Up,” the buzzy hit from their latest EP, Wild. The group stepped onto the runway from the stands in matching miniskirts and corsets. Two wore neon pink leggings, while the other two wore sheer black leggings—pop-punk, streetwear-inspired looks that were a perfect fit for the fashion brand’s more youthful section. If the color theory weren’t already clear, they began the performance singing into a selfie camera labeled the “Pink Cam” in the bottom left corner.

Of course, one song wasn’t enough for the evening. The four singers followed “Pinky Up” with the second single on their EP, “Animal.” At the end of the performance, a giant portrait of the girls standing alongside the Victoria’s Secret models descended, featuring the words: “Pink Class of 2026.”

Only four of the original six members attended tonight’s event: Daniela Avanzini, Lara Raj, Megan Skiendiel, and Yoonchae Jeong. Meret Manon Bannerman and Sophia Laforteza are both on temporary hiatuses—Bannerman announced her temporary leave in February 2026, while Laforteza announced hers in February 2026. But the band revealed tonight that she will be returning soon.

“We are so happy that she’s coming back,” Raj told E! News exclusively at the 2026 Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show alongside Megan Skiendiel, Yoonchae Jeong and Daniela Avanzini. “We’re so proud of her. She's really been so brave.”

This performance follows a big summer for Katseye. Not only did Wild drop on August 14, but the girls were also featured in the documentary Katseye: Wild Hearts, which premiered on August 12. The 80-minute film closely follows the singers after their Netflix show, Pop Star Academy: Katseye. Laforteza appears throughout the documentary, including sit-down interviews, while Bannerman appears only in archival footage.
