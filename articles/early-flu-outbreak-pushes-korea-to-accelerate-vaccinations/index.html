Early flu outbreak pushes Korea to accelerate vaccinations

TheTools · 16.09.2026 07:22 · source · permalink

The government is moving up influenza vaccinations for children and older adults as this year's flu outbreak begins earlier than usual and vaccine supplies tighten.

The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) said Wednesday it revised the schedule for the 2026-2027 seasonal influenza National Immunization Program (NIP) after issuing a flu epidemic advisory last Friday.

The agency said it adjusted the schedule after considering the current outbreak and its outlook, vaccine supplies, medical needs and healthcare providers’ readiness. The decision followed a review by the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices and consultations with medical groups and local governments.

Influenza has recently spread rapidly, particularly among school-age children and younger age groups.

In the 36th week of this year, the rate of influenza-like illness was highest among children aged 7-12, at 75.1 cases per 1,000 outpatients. The rate was 49.8 among children aged 1-6, 31.3 among those aged 13-18 and 24.0 among infants under age 1.

Although the rate among people aged 65 and older was relatively low at 8.8 per 1,000 outpatients, the age group accounted for 60.0 percent of all patients hospitalized with influenza.

Children's vaccinations moved up to next Monday, elderly schedule advanced by 3-6 days

Under the revised schedule, NIP influenza vaccinations for children aged six months to 14 years will begin next Monday.

Children requiring one dose were already scheduled to begin vaccination next Monday. Those requiring two doses, however, had originally been scheduled to start Sept. 28. The government moved their start date forward by one week to next Monday.

Vaccinations for pregnant women will begin next Monday as originally scheduled.

The vaccination schedule for older adults will also be moved forward. Vaccinations for people aged 75 and older will begin Oct. 6, six days earlier than the previous Oct. 12 start date. Those aged 70-74 will begin Oct. 12 instead of Oct. 15, while those aged 65-69 will start Oct. 15 instead of Oct. 19.

High-risk groups, including immunocompromised people, can receive vaccinations from Oct. 6 after consulting healthcare professionals, regardless of their age-group schedule.

Long-term care hospitals and facilities will not have a separate vaccination start date and may begin administering shots as soon as vaccines are supplied.

NIP secures 12.33 million doses, but private supplies remain tight

The KDCA said it expects no disruption to the national vaccination program despite the accelerated schedule.

The agency has secured about 12.33 million influenza vaccine doses for the 2026-2027 NIP season and plans to distribute them gradually to healthcare providers in line with the revised schedule.

Overall vaccine supplies, however, are tighter than in previous years.

All three strains recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) for this season’s Northern Hemisphere influenza vaccines — influenza A(H1N1), A(H3N2) and B/Victoria lineage strains — were changed.

Production yields declined as manufacturers worked to optimize cultivation and manufacturing processes for the new strains, reducing Korea’s total supply of domestically produced and imported vaccines by about 4 million doses from last year.

The KDCA said it worked with vaccine manufacturers and importers to secure an additional 370,000 doses for private-sector vaccinations. Still, it acknowledged that private-market supplies remain tighter than in previous years.

Domestic production and distribution schedules were also affected because the WHO supplied the reference standard for the influenza B component needed for vaccine quality testing about a month later than last season.

The KDCA and the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety advanced lot-release approvals by about two weeks from the original schedule, but supplying sufficient quantities before the NIP begins remains challenging.

The KDCA plans to review vaccine supply conditions weekly with manufacturers, importers, distributors and the medical community while pursuing additional production and imports.

“We adjusted the vaccination schedule in response to the early issuance of the influenza epidemic advisory after reviewing vaccine supply conditions and available quantities and taking into account feedback from experts and those in the field,” KDCA Commissioner Lim Seung-kwan said.

Lim added, “We will closely monitor vaccine supplies and vaccination trends and work closely with relevant agencies and the medical community to ensure that the vaccination program proceeds without disruption.”
