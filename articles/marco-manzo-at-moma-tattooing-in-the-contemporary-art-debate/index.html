Marco Manzo at MoMA: Tattooing in the Contemporary Art Debate

The question of whether tattooing can be treated as an artwork is central to the Manifesto of Contemporary Artistic Tattooing presented by Roman artist Marco Manzo on 19 June 2026 at the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York. The manifesto proposes a framework for documenting, certifying and archiving tattoo works to preserve their artistic memory despite the unique challenge that the human body is the artwork's support.

Manzo's appearance at MoMA follows a trajectory that includes the Venice Biennale, MACRO and the Complesso del Vittoriano. His practice spans tattooing, sculpture, photography, radiography, video art and digital technologies.

What was presented

  • Manifesto of Contemporary Artistic Tattooing: a proposal for documentation standards and attribution of authorship and features.
  • Video-art performance using artificial intelligence to animate 66 radiographic plates created by the artist.
  • Works and artist proofs previously exhibited or donated to collections as part of Manzo's ongoing practice.

Why it matters

The project raises practical and theoretical issues for the art world: how museums, archives and collectors can integrate works whose medium is the body. Manzo's proposal offers tools to retain critical and historical traces of tattooed works without erasing the distinctions between tattooing, painting and sculpture.

Implications and future directions

  • Potential protocols for certification and registration of author, images and provenance of tattoo works.
  • Crossovers between visual arts and digital technologies, exemplified by AI-assisted animation of radiographs.
  • New opportunities for collecting, archiving and scholarly research in contemporary art.

Event date: 19 June 2026 (presentation at the Celeste Bartos Theater, MoMA).
