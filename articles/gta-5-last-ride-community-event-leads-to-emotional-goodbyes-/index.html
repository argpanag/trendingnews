GTA 5 Last Ride Community Event Leads to Emotional Goodbyes From Fans After 13 Years of Mayhem

TheTools · 17.11.2026 16:20 · source · permalink

So long, farewell.

Today, gamers around the world are saying their goodbyes to GTA 5, a game that has been with them for as long as 13 years. Its initial release was a massive moment for many; not only was it yet another exciting new, ambitious entry into one of gaming’s most beloved franchises, but it would also be a catalyst for so much more for millions of people.

For me, I was just 12 years old. It was an inappropriate age to be playing such a game, but after following the pre-release hype for months and playing it myself, it had a massive impact on me. It set me on the path that I am on today and inspired me to pursue a career in games media, partially so I could cover GTA 6 when it came out.

And now, we’re here. I have spent literally half of my life chasing this moment. It was a journey of all kinds of ups and downs, but I am hardly alone in that journey. Millions of GTA 5 fans have forged friendships, lost loved ones, and experienced life-altering changes during the game's undeterred longevity.

Every Celebrity in GTA 5 and GTA Online

Take GTA YouTuber TGG as an example. He has 2.32 million subscribers, and his story largely traces back to GTA 5. Although he had dabbled in other GTA games prior to this, 5 was the game that really hooked him. In 9th grade, his dad bought him the game and essentially unlocked a whole new world for him. The sandbox elements within GTA Online were immediately captivating for a young teenager, leading to memories he still holds dearly to this day.

“It was freedom,” he said in reference to what made GTA Online special in its earliest days. “No other game would just drop people in an open world and say, 'Do whatever the f**k you want.'"

But it wasn’t just about blowing stuff up or mowing down NPCs for him. When things weren’t going well at home or in his personal life, GTA Online offered an escape, but one that wasn’t overly-isolating. It was like a virtual third space where he could hang out with people from school and find common ground, allowing him to make new friends with kids he may not have otherwise been able to connect with.

“I think one of the reasons those memories are so special to me was because it helped [me make friends]. Racing with people from school, you end up with a full lobby of 16 people. Maybe you only know three or four of them very well, but that's 10 new people from your school that you're racing with that you can build friendships with. And being able to talk about whatever happened in a race or whatever happened in a mission that you were grinding out together, and having an icebreaker with people at school the next day really helped. I don't want to say [it] helped [me] get through school necessarily, but [it] helped expand my circle.

"And to me, as someone who was always a more quiet kid and wasn't the most popular kid in school, those [moments] were very important to me. And I think looking at it a bit deeper, that's probably the reason that those memories are more special to me because they helped me expand my circle at school when it was probably pretty small.”

TGG eventually spun his love and encyclopedic knowledge of GTA Online into a career. He launched a YouTube channel while he was still in school and took inspiration from another creator known as TmarTn for the kinds of videos he wanted to make. After making over a thousand videos, TGG has become a leading source for GTA news, partially because he is the one breaking it himself. He was invited to Rockstar North earlier this year and given the opportunity to preview the game, allowing him to bring a bunch of exclusive info back to his viewers. It’s a surreal moment.

TmarTn, whose real name is Trevor Martin, is still making GTA videos. He has over 5.69 million subscribers on his most active channel and has made countless videos about the franchise. GTA 6’s launch is a sort of full circle moment for him, as his journey with the franchise largely began with the original Vice City.

“The first one I had was Vice City on PS2. So it's kind of cool because it's this full moment. I was a little kid playing this, worried about the loading screens that were popping up and things like that, trying to hide it from my mom. And now I'm back in Vice City and I'm a grown mid-thirties man and I'm still... It's kind of the same vibe. You know what I mean? It's still just as exciting.”

TGG and TmarTn aren’t the only ones whose love for GTA 5 led to fruitful careers. 31-year-old Adam Butterworth was a fan of the game since day one and it became a staple of his life. Every December, Rockstar adds snow to GTA Online and a bunch of holiday festivities, and experiencing this became a holiday tradition for Butterworth.

“Every Christmas Day me and a few mates would load the game [up], [drive] to the parking lot in the city center, go to the roof, and just chat while trying to push each other off the roof or break the car. It was stupid but it was a good time and as we got older and game time became harder to get among us it became something we would be excited for on Christmas… besides the ungodly amount of food and presents.”

Eventually, his love for GTA 5 turned into something more: a job at Rockstar Games. After bearing the elements in a historic UK snowstorm to attend a job interview, Butterworth scored a gig working in the developer’s QA department. In the lead-up to his first day at the company, he immersed himself even further into GTA 5’s world.

“While waiting for my start date, every day I would do the drive around the map and just think that in a few weeks I would get to work with the team that made this game, a game that meant so much to me.”

A Rockstar community member named Josh, AKA Vinewood Motors, has also managed to turn GTA Online into something more than just a game. After building a sizable social media presence sharing content, Josh began to use his following for good. He has raised over $40,000 for charity as part of a series of GTA-centric Gameblast streams over the years. The annual charity streams have become so significant that Rockstar Games has also supported them.

Still, some people don’t have grand ambitions for GTA Online. Some of them just want somewhere they can spend their time and be social. A 22-year-old GTA 5 fan named Harry told me that he spent over 4,300 hours in the game over the last decade. Not unlike others, it has become a source of core memories with his friends.

“Over the last 11 years, I’ve had so many fond memories doing heists, races, adversary modes, and just running around in freemode. I still remember the rush my friends and I felt after finally completing the Doomsday Heist finale for the first time, after many attempts and a lot of arguing.”

All of those memories have culminated in a special event that players are dubbing “The Last Ride.” This isn’t an official in-game event from Rockstar, it’s one created by the community. Players will drive around San Andreas by looping around its highway before ending up at the airport. They then plan to board a jet and fly off into the east toward Vice City while the sun sets behind them. It’s a way to say goodbye to a place they’ve called home for years.

“My plan for GTA 6 launch night is to drive around Los Santos with my friends and talk about some of our favourite memories,” said Harry. “We’re gonna take our favourite cars, say goodbye to landmarks like the casino, our yachts, apartments, and nightclubs. Then, as soon as the clock strikes midnight, we’re opening GTA 6.”

“I’ll say goodbye to a game that has seen me go from university, to Rockstar, to Remedy [Entertainment], to being a home owner, getting married and becoming a dad,” said Butterworth. “13 years of enjoyment and memories.”

Like many others I spoke to for this story, 22-year-old Brian Berkman got GTA fever at a young age and had to wear down his mom so she’d buy the game for him. Now, as he prepares to leave GTA 5 behind, he’s driving through Los Santos like he’s leaving his hometown behind.

“It sounds a little corny on paper but this is the one singular video game that has stuck around for what feels like my entire life; some people have that with Minecraft but I was admittedly never as big a Minecraft guy as others,” said Berkman. “It’s always been GTA 5 for me even before I owned the game myself, sometimes it feels like I know the in-game map better than my own real-life neighborhood sometimes.

“Even when I’d have nothing to do in the game itself I’d find myself driving around Los Santos blasting Non-Stop-Pop FM feeling like I had my own home away from home, while simultaneously in the comfort of my own home. Everytime I’d think this game was getting too old, Mike Posner 'Cooler Than Me' would come on and all of that would wash away, and I’d feel like I was playing the coolest game in the world. Hearing that come on the radio one last time will have me unreasonably emotional on November 18.”

This will also be a moment for some fans to honor those who didn’t make it to GTA 6. 28-year-old Tobias Johnson plans to use this send off to GTA Online as a way to remember his friend, Noah, who passed away almost a decade ago.

“[This] will be a chance to relive the memories I was able to have with my friends, especially Noah, who I lost at 19 years old in 2017,” said Johnson. “I think about him every day and some of the best memories I have of him are from playing whatever random game mode or race on GTA Online together. The last ride will be symbolic for me as this is kinda just taking that next step in life.

“I’m almost 30 now and while I can always go back to GTA 5, this will be a nice little bit of closure on that time of my life and it will be incredibly cathartic to drive around Los Santos and really soak in those memories of Noah and I one last time.”

While some people will write all of this off as “just a game,” it can’t be stressed enough that it’s not really about that. GTA 5 has been sort of a companion for the last decade and a half. It’s about people having one constant thing during a long period in their lives where they experienced a lot of change. It was something they could routinely come back to because GTA Online is more accessible for casual play than some other games. You can fire up the game one evening and never fire a gun or commit a major felony.

Sometimes, all you want to do is cruise around in a flashy sports car while a radio station that feels like it’s frozen in the early 2010s scores your drive. Maybe you want to go hang out at the casino and gamble fake money away. It’s something you can do by yourself, or with a group of friends. It’s a place where your mind can be free. There’s no skill-based matchmaking to ruin your adrenaline rush. There’s no timer or score limit that brings an end to your fun. As TGG said, it’s freedom to do whatever the f**k you want.

GTA 5 has followed me through half my life. From 7th grade to writing for IGN at 26 years old, this game has been there every step of the way. Every crush, every girlfriend, every break up, every pet, every major life decision. I graduated school, got laid off, made stupid choices, and learned from those stupid choices. GTA 5 was there every time I needed a late night drive or something to blow up. It was there when my friends and I had nothing to play, but wanted to do something passive so we could talk.

As some fans step on to these virtual planes to head to Vice City, they’re not just headed to a new game. They’re embarking on a new chapter of their life. New relationships will be discovered, old ones will be strengthened. Would-be creatives will be inspired to create. Maybe it’s also a chance to feel like a kid again. It’s an opportunity to go back to that time where everything was right in their world, when things were simple and there was a way to escape in a different world. In the unruly chaos of a GTA game, people have found peace and catharsis… and there’s nothing wrong with hoping GTA 6 can restore or even build upon that feeling even more.
