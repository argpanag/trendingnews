Airbus wins Vietravel order for 50 jets, MOU with Vietnam Airlines for A350s

TheTools · 17.09.2026 07:26 · source · permalink

Photo: S5A-0043, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

AIRBUS LOCKS IN VIETRAVEL ORDER FOR 50 JETS

What Happened

Airbus has secured a firm order from Vietravel Airlines for 50 aircraft comprising 20 A220s and 30 A321s, with the first A321 slated for delivery in June 2025. The deal also includes a memorandum of understanding between Vietravel and Vietnam Airlines for five A350-900 widebody jets, with those aircraft scheduled for delivery between 2033 and 2034. The broader order is expected to reach initial deliveries starting in 2029.

Why It Matters

The order demonstrates Airbus's competitive grip on the Southeast Asian market at a time when regional carriers are modernizing fleets to manage operating costs. Single-aisle jets like the A220 and A321 have become central to regional expansion strategies because they deliver fuel efficiency and economics required for high-frequency, thin-route networks typical of growing Southeast Asian markets. The scale of this commitment—50 aircraft to a single operator—signals confidence in the region's capacity for sustained air travel growth.

Who Benefits

Vietravel Airlines gains a modern, homogeneous fleet that improves operational efficiency and reduces maintenance complexity. The carrier, anchored within the T&T Group ecosystem, gains strategic access to long-haul capability through the A350 partnership with Vietnam Airlines while building regional capacity with narrowbodies. Airbus strengthens its foothold in Vietnam and expands its installed base in the region, securing significant future spare parts and support revenue alongside the aircraft sales themselves.

What Happens Next

Vietravel will receive its first A321 this month, marking the beginning of gradual fleet transition over the next decade. The bulk of single-aisle deliveries will arrive from 2029 onward, while the widebody A350s enter service between 2033 and 2034, allowing the carrier to phase in new aircraft alongside retirement of older equipment. Airbus and Vietravel will now work through detailed contracting and configuration finalizations as the order moves from commitment to execution.
