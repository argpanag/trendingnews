Two Cape women accused of robbing man after sex act in A.C. casino hotel room

A Decrease font size. A Reset font size. A Increase font size.

Two Cape May County women are accused of robbing a man after one performed a sex act on him inside an Atlantic City casino hotel room.

Veda Engel and Amanda Crafts each is charged with second-degree counts of theft and conspiracy to commit robbery for the Oct. 9 incident.

Engel, 23, of Cape May, also is charged prostitution, a disorderly persons offense.

Police were called to Harrah’s Atlantic City, where the victim said he met the women at a bar at Caesars, and he exchanged numbers with Engel, who later called him to meet up with the women in their hotel room, according to the affidavits of probable cause obtained by BreakingAC.

Once inside the room, the man said the two women stripped him naked and physically restrained him to the bed, where Engel performed a sexual act, the affidavit states.

Engel then stopped and said, “That’s what you get for $1,250,” according to the complaint.

Crafts, 35, of Wildwood, then held him down as Engel used the facial recognition on his cell phone to make four separate transactions totaling $1,250 via Apply Pay to her account, the affidavits state.

“(The victim) stated that these transactions were made without his consent in exchange for sexual acts,” the detective wrote in the affidavits.

Engel previously was charged with prostitution in December, after she allegedly offer an undercover State Police detective at the Borgata Hotel Casino and Spa sex in exchange for $1,000.

She was released on a summons at that time.

Crafts told the judge at her first appearance last week that she is a teacher. While she did not specify where, her LinkedIn lists her as a computer teacher at Middle Township Middle School.

The school’s website also lists “Amanda Crafts, computers” under “Special Area Departments” on its website.

“I’ve never been to jail,” she told Judge Timothy Maguire at her first appearance via video from the Atlantic County Justice Facility. “I’ve never been arrested before. I was in the wrong place at the wrong time.”

When she learned she was being held pending a detention hearing six days later, Crafts said she was confused.

She told Maguire she has four children and that she did not think she had someone who could take care of them and get then to school while she was in jail.

But when the judge indicated that he believed “that starts the process to see what social services are available,” Crafts spoke up.

“Well, no, what I’m saying is, I don’t know who is going to be responsible for getting them to school for an entire week,” she said.

Crafts ended by saying she would “have to make phone calls and figure it out.”

Engel and Crafts were both freed Thursday, after the state consented to their pretrial release with court check-ins required every other week.

They cannot have any contact with one another or the victim.

Both also must maintain employment, Judge Christine Smith said.

Engel works at SoFresh SoClean Commercial Cleaning in Ocean City, her attorney, Jordan Barbone, said.

“I’m a teacher,” Crafts told Smith at her detention hearing.

“You’re a teacher?” the judge asked.

“I’m a teacher,” Crafts nodded.

“Do you know if you can go back to work?” Smith asked.

“I don’t know,” Crafts said. “I have to make union contact tomorrow when I get released.”

She indicated she has another job that she would be working as well, but did not say what that is.
