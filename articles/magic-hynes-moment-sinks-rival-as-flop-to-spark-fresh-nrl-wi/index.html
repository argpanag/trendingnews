Magic Hynes moment sinks rival as flop to spark fresh NRL wildcard debate — Big Hits

TheTools · 12.09.2026 13:30 · source · permalink

Nicho Hynes stood tall when his side needed him most as the Sharks knocked the Cowboys out of the finals in front of a terrible crowd at Allianz Stadium.

Cronulla will play either the Panthers or Roosters next week after they won 26-16 on Saturday night, with their halfback sealing their spot in the second week of the finals when he fooled the defence to score with 15 minutes to go.

Hynes has been a lightning rod for attention, with his form to determine how far the Sharks go this season.

He was solid without being spectacular for the opening hour as he was reunited with halves partner Braydon Trindall who missed several weeks with a shoulder injury.

Trindall looked in pain at times but got through the contest with Heilum Luki running at him whenever possible.

Cronulla’s halves weren’t able to convert a mountain of possession into first-half dominance, with big man Siosifa Talakai finally breaking the deadlock when he squeezed the ball down.

The hosts had two tries disallowed in the first half but headed to the sheds in front after Sione Katoa scored off a scrum to punish some sloppy defence.

They passed their first test, but the Sharks will have to be infinitely better next week against much stiffer opposition.

THE DEFENCE RESTS

North Queensland’s season is officially over, and their performance on Saturday night will lead to calls for the NRL to introduce a wildcard round next year.

No team that concedes 652 points should play finals footy, especially one that allowed 82 in one game against the Roosters.

To their credit, the Cowboys defended brilliantly in the opening 20 minutes. But they didn’t help themselves with Jake Clifford kicking out on the full, while their usually slick attack never got going.

Losing Coen Hess to a head knock in the first half hurt their go forward, with teams like the Raiders and Storm thinking they could have done more damage if they’d made the finals.

OFF THE DRINK

The Cowboys needed something special from Scott Drinkwater to keep their season alive, but instead he produced one of the worst games of his career as he bid farewell to the club.

Dragons fans wouldn’t have been thrilled with what they saw as Drinkwater put down two bombs and has as many runs as he did errors with 10 minutes to play.

He delivered a quality pass out of dummy-half to set up their first try, but that was the only highlight as another ball was intercepted by Katoa for a Cronulla try eight minutes into the second half.

CROWD CONTROL

The decision to play this at Allianz Stadium didn’t go to plan, with only 17,429 fans turning up for the elimination final.

The Sharks wanted to play it at Ocean Protect Stadium in the heart of the Shire where their passionate fans would have filled the venue.

But 15,000 simply isn’t enough these days for a finals game, especially when the facilities are dated.

It was always going to be a risk moving the game given they were playing an interstate team, but the NRL will be livid with the paltry showing on a perfect Saturday night in Sydney that felt flat from start to finish.
