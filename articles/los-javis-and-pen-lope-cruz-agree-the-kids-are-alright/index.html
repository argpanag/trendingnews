Los Javis and Penélope Cruz Agree the Kids Are Alright

TheTools · 20.11.2026 14:00 · source · permalink

DUO

By Penélope Cruz

Photographed by Danny Lim

Styled by Izaake Souza

Javier Ambrossi wears Shirt Valentino. Jeans and Belt The Society Archive. Javier Calvo wears a Suit and Shirt Valentino. Necklace Isabel Marant.

In Spain, they’re simply Los Javis. Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo started as actors, staged a small Madrid musical that became a film, then built a run of television hits that made them famous at home. Their latest, La Bola Negra, is on another scale entirely. Inspired by an unfinished Federico García Lorca play, the three-era queer drama about love and war won them Best Director at Cannes, set box office records in Spain, and has made them Oscar hopefuls. Few directors get a moment like this. Even fewer get Penélope Cruz, who appears in the film, to interview them about it.

FRIDAY 3:15 PM OCTOBER 9, 2026 LONDON

———

PENÉLOPE CRUZ: I have so many things I want to know, but we should start from the beginning because I’m a tiny bit older than you guys and I want context.

JAVIER AMBROSSI: Do you think so?

CRUZ: A tiny bit. You obviously have a shared obsession with cinema, but how did it start? At home on TV? Renting films? Going to the movies?

JAVIER CALVO: My father used to take me every weekend to the cinema, but also do you remember this DVD collection of Spanish films El País released? It included all the [Pedro] Almodóvar movies, it had—

AMBROSSI: Berlanga.

CALVO: [Luis García]Berlanga, [José Juan]Bigas Luna, [Víctor] Erice. I was obsessed with that collection, and I would watch these films over and over in my room, on my little DVD player.

AMBROSSI: I started with an obsession for books and literature. From the time I was very young, I read every day, every kind of book. That stayed with me a long time before I switched to entertainment and television.

CRUZ: I watched everything. I wasn’t interested in going anywhere. I wanted to watch all the shows and all the movies. Whatever they were showing on television was fine with me. That was the happiest time of my life.

AMBROSSI: We are the same. I learned about entertainment from television before I discovered cinema.

CRUZ: And we all three have a shared crush on Almodóvar. Your experience of discovering him and his world was similar to mine, right?

AMBROSSI: Yes. As a kid, I saw the poster for Kika, the one that had the guy in a tank top. I was flushed, looking at that poster. So even the posters for Pedro’s films made a big impact.

CRUZ: I feel the same way.

CALVO: I went to the movies with my family, and I remember seeing you in Volver. It was shocking to see a reality similar to my own could be portrayed in the movies in such a beautiful, fun way. That was so inspiring for me—the way you talked, the way I recognized my culture and myself in there.

AMBROSSI: I went to a Catholic boys’ school and my mother knew I needed to see other things. Every week, she’d say, “Let’s go to the movies,” and everyone around was like, “Ah, but Spanish movies are not okay,” because I come from a very traditional family. But she was like—

CRUZ: “Let’s go.”

On Javier Calvo Suit, Vest, Shirt and Tie Emporia Armani. On Javier Ambrossi Suit, Shirt and Tie Givenchy by Sarah Burton.

AMBROSSI: “Let’s go, because I know it’s going to wake up something in you.”

CALVO: At a certain point, I discovered Gus Van Sant and other queer directors, but it was hard for me to find queer movies.

AMBROSSI: You had to go to a video store. It’s so funny when people say, “In Spanish cinema, there’s always gay people and civil war.” That’s not true.

CRUZ: And actually there are not so many films about the civil war.

AMBROSSI: No, it’s not true. I don’t know who said that, but some people think it’s a reality. This year, La Bola Negra is the only Spanish movie with a gay leading character.

CRUZ: That is incredible. Aren’t you looking forward to the day when a queer film is made and the first question in every interview is not, “How do you feel that this subject is being talked about?” It’s the same when you work with a female director and the first question is, “How is it different working with a female director?” It makes you realize how modern, important, and necessary the words of Federico García Lorca still are. I think that’s why everyone is connecting so much with your film, because you did something so brave without lecturing anybody.

AMBROSSI: Thank you.

CRUZ: You are reminding us what it is to be on the right side of things. It’s so heartbreaking to still hear that as almost the first question in every interview.

AMBROSSI: I understand why.

CRUZ: I also understand why they would ask, but in 2026, we are still like, “This is a big deal.”

AMBROSSI: I would love for it to be super normal to have leading gay characters, but it isn’t.

CRUZ: In the same way there are not so many female directors.

AMBROSSI: There are not. They can repeat it and repeat it and repeat it, but it’s not true. It’s a big thing now because our rights are being threatened. Fascism is growing. I think art comes when it’s needed, and it’s not a coincidence we’re seeing so many queer stories this year.

CRUZ: You’ve done something very huge. People are going like crazy to the theater to see this film, not only once, but two and three times. If you had done this from a place of, “I’m going to tell you what to think,” they would have rejected it, but what you did is so smart and so deep, and you did it from a point of view of no judgment. I don’t feel like we are giving enough credit to young people. Everyone is saying, “They’re so confused,” but they are seeing much more than we think. And you both knew that in the way you wrote this—you treated them as they deserve to be treated, as a smart generation, aware of the traps they are being served every day through social media. They are aware of the threats to democracy, the threats to freedom, the threats to respect. You are such old souls to have written something like this. You inspired teenagers to skip school on the day of the premiere because they wanted to be the first ones there. In the theater, you can see a 17-year-old and a 90-year-old man or woman hugging, strangers hugging. I don’t know if you realized how powerful it is.

AMBROSSI: I don’t realize yet, but when we did La Mesías, I was very surprised by young people because we were told specifically by everyone, “Young people are not going to watch this.” Everyone was worried because the first two episodes are so challenging, and everyone was like, “Guys, this time young audiences are not going to follow you,” but it ended up being the favorite show of a lot of young people.

On Javier Ambrossi Shirt Carter Bright. Tie The Society Archive. On Javier Calvo Sweater Prada.

CRUZ: That is my hope for the future, that these younger generations will say, “Wait, I also want to be part of this artisanal way of doing things, because the world wants to rob that from me.”

AMBROSSI: It’s happening. Young audiences know they want the real thing. They know better.

CRUZ: They also know so much about politics. We have to encourage them and make them feel seen and heard.

AMBROSSI: La Bola Negra is a movie specifically about Federico García Lorca, who was part of a free generation. He experimented a lot. He was a young, vibrant guy trying to understand what he wanted to do, mixing poetry with naturalism and family drama. Suddenly he wanted surreality, to experiment. Lorca represented young people very well because he was young, and that’s why people are connecting with his spirit.

CRUZ: There is hope for the world.

CALVO: There is hope. It’s beautiful to see young people from all over being interested in Lorca. Having a hit movie that talks about literature is amazing.

AMBROSSI: And about our Spanish literature.

CRUZ: This is like a revolution, and I’m so proud to be part of it because it’s huge and so necessary.

CALVO: You made it huge.

CRUZ: No. I’m very much aware how lucky I am to be part of this film.

AMBROSSI: But you being a part of this gave so much attention to the movie worldwide.

CRUZ: The movie would’ve gotten the attention anyway, because it’s a masterpiece.

AMBROSSI: Without you, it wouldn’t be La Bola Negra.

CRUZ: I know the two of you are very different, but it is amazing how life has put you together. This is not a coincidence.

AMBROSSI: No, it’s not.

CRUZ: You have been together so many years, and even now that you’re not a couple, you’re working together because you are best friends. The level of respect I see you have for each other is so inspiring.

AMBROSSI: Thank you.

CRUZ: It is beautiful to see people can continue to share things when a relationship is over. You told me a little bit about your writing process, the schedule. You wake up really early and exercise before you start writing?

AMBROSSI: Every day.

CRUZ: I would like to spend a day with you both when you are writing. [Laughs]

AMBROSSI: It’s fun.

On Javier Calvo Suit Saint Laurent by Anthony Vaccarello. Hoodie The Society Archive. Shoes John Lobb. On Javier Ambrossi Suit and Shoes Celine. Jacket Canada Goose.

CALVO: There’s a lot of silence, a lot of reading, a lot of thinking. There’s a lot of walking around the house, there’s a lot of music, and there’s a lot of talking.

AMBROSSI: At the beginning, it’s all about researching.

CRUZ: Together, or do you do that separately?

AMBROSSI: Together. I don’t know the words but it’s like “double body” or something. Hyperactive people work very well as couples.

CALVO: With someone next to you.

CRUZ: No, no, no.

AMBROSSI: Is that the body double?

CRUZ: It’s because of ADHD?

AMBROSSI: Yeah. Body doubling.

CRUZ: I didn’t know it was called that.

AMBROSSI: It’s a strategy of productivity for people with ADHD that consists of realizing a task in the presence of another. I think both of us have that. If we are not in the presence of the other, we are not going to work.

CRUZ: I completely understand and relate to that. There’s so many things where I need somebody else there to be sharing that experience.

AMBROSSI: At the beginning of the process, we will be researching. Even though we are not talking, we are reading the same book and then we—

CALVO: We share things.

AMBROSSI: We will share thoughts. Or we’ll have a call with a historian for hours about Granada in the ’30s, then the civil war in the north of Spain for 10 days, and we will travel. But we have to be—

CRUZ: Very organized.

AMBROSSI: Very organized and really looking for research. Research is key in our work as creators. We work very well with reality. We need to anchor the script to something real, and after the research, we go with a structure. That’s where we do different things. I work well with the structures because my mind goes to big things, and I have a sense of the start, the middle, the end. Javi always starts from the beginning.

CALVO: I imagine the movie from the first shot and I go as far as I can, I go back, I try to imagine it again at night.

CRUZ: It’s exhausting. It also happens while I’m sleeping. I shoot my dreams and I do different takes of situations.

CALVO: Oh, wow.

On Javier Ambrossi Shirt Celine. Jacket Carter Bright. Shorts and Socks The Society Archive. Shoes Timberland. On Javier Calvo Coat Canada Goose. Sweater Celine. Pants Tom Ford.

CRUZ: For many years, I shot my dreams. I go back, and back, and back, and back.

AMBROSSI: Take two, three, four.

CRUZ: The person has to react differently, but it’s a live sequence.

AMBROSSI: Oh my god.

CALVO: We need to take a break.

CRUZ: But who cares? We wouldn’t change it for more stability.

AMBROSSI: I think I have something different than you both, but in a way it’s the same. For example, a conflict we always have is Javi will be obsessed with the first line for one month, and I will be building the rest of the movie. I’m always like, “Javi, at some point we need to talk about the next scene,” and he’ll say, “Oh, you’re always thinking about the future.” [Laughs]

CRUZ: I need to know what’s coming after.

CALVO: You’re a visionary. You think in the future and I think of the past.

AMBROSSI: I don’t care about the present or the past. I’m always in the future.

CRUZ: I imagine that writing this, you had to go through a huge crisis of trying to put together three stories in such a genius way.

CALVO: Yeah. At some point I said to Javi, “I don’t want to make this movie anymore.”

AMBROSSI: But you say that in every project.

CRUZ: I didn’t want to ask this, but now I want to.

AMBROSSI: Ask it.

CRUZ: What would you say to somebody who is maybe not going into the theater to see this film because they are afraid you will try to change the way they think?

CALVO: This is a movie about humanity and seeing the other, and acknowledging that the other exists. We shouldn’t be killing each other. I think it’s a really simple thing I hope everyone agrees on. It’s not trying to lecture anyone. It’s an emotional journey about humanity and the importance of life.

AMBROSSI: Yeah. I would say go, give it a try.

CRUZ: And that’s what is happening. Everyone is going!

AMBROSSI: Go to the theater, sit there for the movie, and I’m sure no one in the world will want Carlos, Sebastián, or Alberto to be unhappy.

CRUZ: Exactly.

AMBROSSI: Maybe they will think politically differently. Maybe they won’t agree with us, but I’m sure deep in their hearts, they won’t be able to wish bad things on the characters.

CRUZ: This is why the movie is conquering everyone’s heart. Your mind is so open and you don’t judge through your characters. You don’t judge anybody, which is the most important thing. The audience is very smart and they know that.

CALVO: You are the best interviewer ever.

AMBROSSI: The best sister, not mother.

CRUZ: I tell you, you and I are almost the same age.

AMBROSSI: We are sisters.

CRUZ: And we love each other.

AMBROSSI: Yes. Thank you.

CALVO: Thank you so much.
