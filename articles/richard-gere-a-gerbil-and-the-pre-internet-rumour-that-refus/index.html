Richard Gere, a gerbil and the pre-internet rumour that refuses to die.

TheTools · 26.09.2026 01:21 · source · permalink

Forty years after one of the most absurd, ridiculous and completely unsubstantiated urban legends was born, the Richard Gere gerbil rumour still won't go away.

Case in point: Just recently, the 77-year-old Pretty Woman star and longtime activist called out President Donald Trump for his cuts to foreign aid.

"He has idiots around him who have the same ignorance about him," Gere said.

How did White House Communications Director Steven Cheung respond? He tweeted on X. 

"Not going to listen to a guy who may have put a gerbil up his ass," he wrote. 

Yes, an actual political official used a 1980's myth as a clapback.

So how did this bizarre rumour survive for decades — and why does everyone, from comedians to political staffers, seem to know it?

The Hollywood Reporter has just released a new podcast simply titled Gerbil, hosted by journalist Allen Salkin, to answer that exact question.

Salkin isn't trying to fuel the gossip. He's on a noble mission to unpack why certain rumours are so wildly sticky, where they actually come from and what they reveal about our collective psychology.

This particular legend dates back to the 80s. Gere supposedly landed in an emergency room with a furry friend stuck where the sun doesn't shine. It's important to note, at this point, that there is zero evidence and zero medical record of this actually happening. 

Yet, in a pre-internet world, it spread like wildfire.

It was basically the very first viral meme decades before social media existed. Everyone suddenly had a cousin or a friend of a friend who was a nurse or a staff member at the hospital when it happened. 

So, where did a story this rogue actually come from? Well, Salkin interviewed sex expert Dan Savage on the podcast, who pointed out that the rumour was deeply rooted in the era's homophobia. 

After Gere starred in American Gigolo, he became a massive sex symbol. The era — late 70s and 80s — was awash with rumours about closeted celebrities. People's homophobic anxieties were easily channelled into such wild, perverted tales.

Beyond that, urban legends like this stick because people want to believe Hollywood has a dark, weird underbelly. If a story is strange enough, people assume no one could possibly make it up. 

Plus, because it started in a slower, pre-internet era, the myth had time to really bake into our subconscious.

Today, a weird celebrity rumour lasts about 10 minutes before 14 new ones replace it by lunchtime. We're constantly being inundated with celebrity gossip and drama that may or may not have any legs. The news cycle is oversaturated and we are desensitised.

But back then, stories stuck for life. Alongside the ones about Marilyn Manson removing a rib or Walt Disney being cryogenically frozen, this Richard Gere rumour has certainly had a lifespan. 

Gere has basically ignored it for four decades, practising a strict policy of not giving ridiculous rumours oxygen. The closest he ever came to addressing it on record was during an interview with the legendary Barbara Walters, who tactfully asked if he knew why people tried to "smear" him.

His very Dalai Lama-esque response? 

"Does any of this bother me personally? No. People have their own motivations for doing crazy things... If I'm a cow and someone says I'm a zebra, it doesn't make me a zebra."

(Or, you know... a gerbil.)
