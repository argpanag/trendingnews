The Alternative Premier League Table No 2: Expected Goals Against – Sunderland’s miserly start

TheTools · 05.09.2026 16:38 · source · permalink

Eight Sunderland players have combined to block nine shots so far this season NurPhoto/Getty Images

The Athletic has live coverage of every game in the Premier League today.

This Premier League season, The Athletic will dive into the various dimensions within expected goals (xG) to spot some of those trends in our weekly Expected Premier League Tables.

Having looked at the xG created by all 20 teams after Matchday 1, this week we will be diving into xG against across the first two matchdays in comparison to their average xGA per game in 2025-26. The same caveat applies – it is far too early to draw conclusions from this data, so this is just to spot trends that may be of interest for later in the campaign.

 

Arsenal have built on their league-best defensive record from last season with another excellent defensive start to 2026-27. Across two matches, they have allowed just 11 shots, with nine of those worth an xG of 0.04 or fewer. Only one – from Coventry City’s Jack Rudoni, worth 0.15xG – has been on target and was easily saved by David Raya.

Having won the league while averaging 56 per cent possession in 2025-26, they have wielded marginally more control of the ball with 62 per cent. Arsenal’s ability to limit teams, though, is still down to their excellent organization without the ball.

Sunderland are right behind Arsenal for shots on target allowed with just three out of a total of 21. Two of those led to goals in the defeat to Ipswich Town – both from errors – with Emersonn’s strike into an empty goal worth 0.77xG.

Fulham’s Josh King missed a 0.45xG shot over the weekend, while eight Sunderland players have combined to block nine shots, with only Nordi Mukiele managing two.

Rivals Newcastle United lead the league in this category with 21 blocked shots from the 44 total that they have faced (joint most with Ipswich). Lewis Hall has thrown himself in the way of five shots, including one off the goal-line in the dying moments against Tottenham Hotspur. No player has prevented more opposition xG from blocks than Hall’s 0.66.

Mathias Jaissle’s side have preferred to sit deep, inviting teams onto them, and have allowed five shots worth 0.2xG or more but conceded just once from those efforts. Lukas Hornicek has saved one of those, with the other three efforts being blocked.

Beginning the season against Liverpool at home and Spurs away has a part to play in that, and Newcastle’s approach could change with Bournemouth, Leeds United, Millwall (EFL Cup) and Hull City up next.

Ipswich have allowed the most xG after two matches at 5.4, while letting in six goals. The quantity of shots allowed is a major reason, with 44 in total, 15 of which have been on target. They have only blocked two of the efforts on target, frequently giving goalkeeper Kjell Scherpen too much to do.

Scherpen made six saves at the weekend against Manchester United and prevented 0.7 open-play goals (four conceded excluding the own goal from 4.7 expected goals on target) but still conceded five times. Across the last three completed league seasons, only eight goalkeepers have faced more shots in a game than the 33 he faced against United.

Manchester United found their cutting edge in the second half against Ipswich, but there will be a few concerns at the other end. They have allowed only 17 shots (third fewest behind Arsenal and Manchester City) but nine of those have been on target with an xG per shot of 0.181, the worst mark in the league. Six of the shots on target have been worth 0.2xG or more, with four of those ending in goals.

It is a departure from Michael Carrick’s 17 matches in charge last season when they allowed an xG per shot of 0.105 and conceded 18 goals, the fifth-fewest in that stretch, where they also averaged 49 per cent possession.

That has increased to 66 per cent against promoted Hull and Ipswich, but so has their vulnerability on the break, which requires attention.

Hull City have had a dream start with two wins in two. They have allowed 32 shots, the sixth-worst mark, but just eight of those have been on target. Goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis kept out a 0.36xG Bryan Mbeumo effort against Manchester United before denying Taiwo Awoniyi’s part-unintentional 0.72xG shot with a sprawling save at the weekend.

Sergej Jakirovic’s side have been hard to break down and, importantly, are one of only four teams yet to make an error leading to a shot alongside Arsenal, Brentford and Leeds United.

Only three teams have allowed fewer shots than Brentford’s 21. That is the least they have managed in their opening two games of a season since being promoted in 2021, while the six shots on target allowed is the joint fewest.

Keith Andrews’ side have let in only one shot worth over 0.15xG – that was Dominic Calvert-Lewin’s equaliser, worth 0.92xG, for Leeds over the weekend.

Brentford’s attacking performance has stood out, with only Manchester United (6.6) generating more xG across two matches than their 5.6, while they beat Birmingham City 6-1 in the Carabao Cup’s second round. But they have been very good defensively too.

Only Arsenal (0.05) have allowed a lower xG per shot than Leeds’ 0.07, with Daniel Farke building on the defensive foundations that saw them concede only 24 times in 20 games between January 1 and the end of the season.

Their opponents have racked up 35 shots in two matches but had just eight on target. Impressively, all eight have been worth less than 0.1xG, including Kevin Schade’s goal at Elland Road. James Trafford has faced just 1.1 expected goals on target (which measures the quality of a shot after it is taken rather than before), the fewest behind Raya (0.04).

Only three players have blocked more shots than summer arrival Tarik Muharemovic’s four too.

Nottingham Forest follow Leeds for the least xG per shot allowed at 0.09. Unfortunately, they have also conceded from two low-quality chances in the form of Victor Munoz’s superb, albeit deflected, equaliser at the weekend (worth 0.06xG) and Anton Stach’s free kick on Matchday 1 (0.04xG).

Forest have generally been solid, with only Manchester City (61 per cent) and Sunderland (57 per cent) winning a higher share of aerial duels than their 56 per cent. They may have only a point from their first two games, but it is a solid start on the defensive end.

Manchester City have had a more measured start defensively to 2026-27, conceding 1.3xG across two matches after allowing 1.2 per game on average last season as Pep Guardiola embraced a more direct, vertical style. Enzo Maresca has brought more composure to their in-possession play, with City enjoying 69 per cent possession, an increase on the 53 per cent average from the final two league games of 2025-26.

Of the 14 shots they have conceded, only Marcus Tavernier’s goal for Bournemouth (0.34xG) and Eddie Nketiah’s miss (0.31xG), were worth more than 0.11xG.

Maresca’s former side Chelsea, meanwhile, have kicked up a gear in attack under Xabi Alonso with a more direct style that has led to seven goals while averaging just 33 per cent possession, the second lowest after Hull (30 per cent).

The primary concern has been their dead-ball defending, with two of their goals conceded against Brighton & Hove Albion at the weekend coming from set pieces. Chelsea have let their opponents have the ball, but their pressing is still a work in progress, resulting in teams being able to create quality openings against them.

They have allowed 29 shots, 12 of which have been on target, with Emiliano Martinez and Robert Sanchez forced into four saves apiece in a game each.

Brighton have allowed the second-highest proportion of shots from inside the box at 74 per cent, contributing to a high xG per shot of 0.14 for their opponents. They have allowed only six shots on target, but four of those have ended in goals, with Romeo Lavia, Joao Pedro and Pedro Neto all finishing off quality chances worth 0.32xG or more.

Fabian Hurzeler’s side have been a little careless too, with no team making more errors leading to a shot than their five (including Lavia’s goal at the weekend).

Errors have also been a concern for Aston Villa, who have made four leading to a shot including two goals, both in the 4-0 defeat to Brighton. Their opponents’ xG per shot of 0.172 is the second-worst in the league behind those of Manchester United.

The performance against Arsenal, however, should encourage Unai Emery. Villa conceded only seven shots, four of which were worth 0.08xG or less, but Bukayo Saka converted the best chance (worth 0.57xG).

Spurs have conceded the second-most shots after two games, behind Ipswich and Newcastle (44 each) with 37, 10 of which have been on target. Set pieces have been a concern for them too, with Brentford’s Michael Kayode and Newcastle’s Yoane Wissa scoring from the second phase of corners.

The bigger issue, though, has been the ease with which teams have bypassed their press. Brentford played through the spaces Spurs left in midfield for the opening goal of their 3-0 win, while Newcastle frequently targeted the wings with direct play to generate opportunities.

Teething issues are to be expected with Roberto De Zerbi attempting to implement his tactics with a team that has undergone plenty of change, but they need results too, starting with their visit to Nottingham Forest this weekend.

The same applies to Frank Lampard’s Coventry City, who have allowed more direct attacks (10) than anyone else and conceded twice from fast breaks. They have faced only 26 shots (eight on target), and their bigger issues are arguably at the other end, but being harder to break down might be the better route to prevent a freefall with Manchester City and Brighton up next.

Fulham have similarly struggled to cope with quick attacking play, conceding 1.38xG from fast breaks across their first two matches. Playing Chelsea in their first game was a factor, but half of that xG total (0.7) came from Sunderland striker Wilson Isidor’s shot at the weekend, which hit the post.

Across the previous three league seasons, Fulham ranked 20th out of 25 teams for xG conceded from fast breaks per 90 at 0.12. But Alvaro Arbeloa has brought a more expansive style of play which they will need to adapt to, especially while defending crosses with Fulham’s opponents completing a league-high 50 per cent of open-play cross attempts.

Everton have allowed five shots from fast breaks, more than any other team, with Alex Scott’s goal for Bournemouth at the weekend coming from that situation too. On Matchday 1, Crystal Palace striker Jean-Philippe Mateta missed two great chances, blasting over from within the six-yard box and hitting the post from a cross.

David Moyes’ side have been targeted out wide, especially on their right side, where midfielder Merlin Rohl has moonlighted at right back following Jake O’Brien’s departure. The hope will be for deadline-day arrival Ainsley Maitland-Niles and Jake O’Brien, who stayed despite speculation over his future, to provide better cover.

Palace have conceded 34 shots, of which 14 have been on target. While the average xG of these shots is only 0.098, the high number on target suggests both Everton and Manchester City were able to get clear shots away at Dean Henderson's goal. Limiting the volume of these shots and getting more bodies in the way will likely be crucial if Pierre Sage's side are to turn their form around.

Bournemouth find themselves in a similar position. Their 26 shots allowed across two matches follow their trend from the past two seasons when they averaged 12.8 shots against per 90. Worryingly, their opponents this season have managed to get exactly half of those shots on target, up from 35 per cent across 2023-24 and 2024-25.

Three of those have been worth 0.49xG or more, but have all been saved by Djordje Petrovic. He made two excellent point-blank saves to deny Thierno Barry – one from his own mistake and one from a team-mate's error – while easily dealing with a weak-footed Erling Haaland shot on Matchday 1.

Marco Rose’s side have had issues playing out from the back, which has often given their defenders less time to recover in case of turnovers. Bournemouth have blocked just four shots, the joint lowest among teams to concede 18 or more shots in their first two matches.

Liverpool are grappling with the demands of Andoni Iraola’s philosophy, and that has meant playing at a higher tempo. Their direct speed (the pace at which the ball is moved towards the opposition goal, measured in metres per second) across two matches stands at 1.6 m/s, up from an average of 1.2 across the last two seasons under Arne Slot.

They have conceded eight big chances across two matches, three of which have led to goals for Anthony Elanga, Dan Ndoye and Morgan Gibbs-White. Lapses in concentration – like from the throw-in that led to the penalty conceded against Forest – have played their part too.

The high press that Iraola set up at Bournemouth is still taking shape at Liverpool, with just three high turnovers forced in two matches. They have been snappier in the midfield third, though, with only Manchester United (52) winning the ball back more times than their 46.
