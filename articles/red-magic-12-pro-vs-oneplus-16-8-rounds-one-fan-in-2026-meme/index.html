TL;DR

  • Both run the same 2nm Snapdragon chip. Red Magic starts at $1,045 (CNY 6,999), OnePlus at $895.
  • Red Magic adds a 25,500rpm fan, liquid cooling, 700Hz shoulder triggers and a headphone jack.
  • OnePlus has the bigger 9,000mAh battery, a 3x zoom lens, a 200MP main camera and IP69K.
  • Red Magic charges faster: 120W wired and 80W wireless, against OnePlus's 100W and 50W.
  • Red Magic plans a global launch. OnePlus no longer launches new phones in the US or Europe.

Three days after OnePlus launched its 9,000mAh flagship, RedMagic answered on October 15 with a gaming phone that runs the same chip, adds a built-in cooling fan, and costs $149 more at the starting tier. It’s the clearest test this fall of a simple question: does a phone built only for games beat a flagship that games well but does everything else too? Here’s how the Red Magic 12 Pro+ and OnePlus 16 compare across eight rounds, and who should pick which one.

Price and Availability: $149 Apart in China, Worlds Apart Everywhere Else

Both phones launched in China first, and the price ladders sit close together.

Red Magic also has a Golden Saga limited edition with 24GB of RAM and 1TB of storage, but it hasn’t priced it yet. OnePlus opens regular sales in China on October 31.

The base gap is $149, but it shrinks to just $75 once you step up to 16GB of RAM. That’s the tier most gamers should look at anyway, because the extra memory keeps more apps and game data loaded in the background.

Now for the part a spec sheet won’t tell you. OnePlus stopped launching new phones in North America and Europe this summer, so buying a OnePlus 16 in the US means importing it without a local warranty. RedMagic is going the other way. Its global launch details are due on November 3, and the phone will likely be sold outside China as the RedMagic 12 Pro. That Pro+ name mix-up trips up a lot of buyers every year. Last spring’s global 11S Pro started at $849, so we’d expect the new model to land at that price or a little higher.

Red Magic wins this round for most of our readers, simply because it’s the one with a path to your country. If you live in China, OnePlus is the better deal at the base tier.

Design: Two Gaming Looks, Only One Built Like a Tank

These two phones are almost the same size, and the Red Magic is only 3g to 6g heavier. What surprised us is that Red Magic fits a working fan into a body just 8.6mm thick, which is slimmer than last year’s 11 Pro+ at 8.9mm.

Red Magic’s look is pure gaming: a transparent back that shows off the cooling hardware, RGB lighting, and 700Hz shoulder triggers. Those are touch-sensitive zones on the frame that act like the L and R buttons on a controller. The touch sampling rate, 700Hz here, is how many times per second the phone checks for your finger, so higher means faster response.

OnePlus is the tougher phone. IP ratings are dust-and-water scores. IP68 means the phone survives being dunked, IP69 covers hot, high-pressure water jets, and IP69K is the industrial version of that jet test. The OnePlus 16 carries all of them.

Red Magic also keeps a 3.5mm headphone jack, which almost no flagship has anymore. If you play competitive shooters with wired earbuds to avoid Bluetooth lag, that matters more than it sounds.

Call it a draw. Red Magic wins for gamers who want triggers and a jack. OnePlus wins for anyone who wants a phone that survives a rough life.

Display: Red Magic Hides the Camera, OnePlus Treats Your Eyes Better

Refresh rate is how many times per second the screen updates, so a higher number means smoother motion. Both phones hit 185Hz in supported games, so that part is a tie.

Red Magic’s big trick is the true full screen. The selfie camera sits under the display, and the bezels are just 0.96mm thick. In landscape games, nothing cuts into the picture. That’s a real advantage when an enemy hides in the top corner.

OnePlus counters with a sharper panel, about 450 pixels per inch against roughly 431 on Red Magic, and its 165Hz mode works across the whole system, not just in a few apps. It also has faster PWM dimming. PWM (pulse-width modulation) is how OLED screens dim by flickering very fast, and a higher rate is usually gentler on people who get headaches from flicker.

Nits measure brightness. Red Magic’s 1,900-nit full-screen figure is close to OnePlus’s 2,000 nits, so outdoor reading should feel similar.

OnePlus wins narrowly. The under-display camera is clever, but OnePlus’s screen is sharper and easier on sensitive eyes for hours of use.

Performance: Same Chip, Red Magic Squeezes Out More

Both phones use the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6, a 2nm chip with two main cores at 5.01GHz and an Adreno 850 GPU (graphics processor). A smaller nanometer number generally means more speed for less heat.

Geekbench is a benchmark app that scores how fast a chip finishes everyday and heavy tasks. In the Red Magic’s pre-launch Geekbench run, it came out about 5% faster in single-core and 6% faster in multi-core. Same silicon, different results. That usually comes down to cooling and how hard each brand lets the chip run before slowing it down. These are single runs, not a controlled lab test, so treat the gap as a direction rather than a final verdict.

Memory helps too. Red Magic uses LPDDR5T RAM rated up to 10,667Mbps, one of the fastest phone memory types you can get right now. Both use UFS 4.1 storage. The OnePlus 16 scored about 5.38 million on AnTuTu v11, a popular all-round benchmark, while Red Magic hasn’t posted an AnTuTu result yet.

Each brand adds helper chips. Red Magic has its RedCore R5 gaming chip and a G1 display chip for frame boosting. OnePlus uses a trio: P4 for display processing, G3 for network, and T3 for touch.

Red Magic wins. A 5% gap won’t change how Instagram opens, but in long games it’s a hint of more headroom.

Cooling: A Real Fan vs a Smarter Heat Sink

This is the round that defines the matchup.

Red Magic’s ICE cooling system combines an active fan spinning at 25,500rpm with a liquid-cooling loop and extra thermal layers. The fan is sealed to IPX8, so it’s built to survive water. Gaming phones are getting fans for a reason. 2nm chips run hot enough that passive cooling struggles over long sessions, and even Honor is adding one to its next gaming phone.

OnePlus uses a passive Glacier system: a 3D vapor chamber that it says moves heat 40% better than before, a new thermal gel, and graphite rated at 2,100W/m·K. A vapor chamber is a flat metal pocket of liquid that spreads heat away from the chip. OnePlus says Delta Force averaged 185fps over three hours in its lab.

Here’s how we read it. A vapor chamber can only spread heat into the phone’s body. A fan actually pushes hot air out. That’s why fan phones usually hold their top speed longer before thermal throttling, which is when a chip slows itself down to avoid overheating. You’ll feel throttling as sudden frame drops halfway through a match.

The trade-offs are real, though. A fan makes a faint whir, uses a little battery, and can pull in dust through its vents over time.

Red Magic wins. If you play for an hour or more at a time, a fan is the single best upgrade a phone can have.

Cameras: Not a Close Fight

The OnePlus 16 wins this almost everywhere. Its main sensor is larger, and sensor size matters more than megapixels for low-light shots. It adds a 3x periscope zoom, a folded lens that zooms without a thick bump. Red Magic has no zoom lens at all, so anything past 2x is just a crop.

Selfies are the bigger gap. Under-display cameras have to shoot through the screen’s pixels, so they look softer and hazier than a normal front camera. Red Magic’s 16MP sensor is fine for video calls. OnePlus’s 50MP autofocus camera is in another league.

OIS (optical image stabilization) physically moves the lens to cancel hand shake. Both main cameras have it.

OnePlus wins, easily. If photos matter to you even a little, this round should weigh heavily.

Battery and Charging: OnePlus Lasts Longer, Red Magic Refills Faster

mAh (milliamp-hours) is how much charge a battery holds. OnePlus has 600mAh more, about 7%. It uses a silicon-carbon cell, which mixes silicon into the battery so it stores more energy without getting thicker.

Red Magic hits back on speed. 120W wired and 80W wireless are both faster than OnePlus’s 100W and 50W. Red Magic’s capacity also jumped from 7,500mAh on the 11 Pro+, so the gap is much smaller than it was a year ago.

Both support bypass charging, which sends power straight to the phone instead of the battery while you play plugged in. It cuts heat and slows battery wear, which matters on a gaming phone.

One thing to keep in mind: the fan draws a little power. In long sessions, we’d expect OnePlus’s bigger battery and fanless design to stretch further.

OnePlus wins narrowly. More capacity beats faster refills for most days. If you charge on a wireless pad at your desk, Red Magic flips that.

Software and Extras: Two Very Different Promises

The Red Magic runs RedMagic OS 12 on Android 17. It’s built around a game space with performance sliders, fan controls, and trigger mapping. The phone can also add generated frames and upscale supported games to 2K at 185Hz. Frame generation inserts computer-made frames between real ones so games look smoother.

The OnePlus 16 runs ColorOS 17 in China. OnePlus is retiring OxygenOS and moving its phones to the same ColorOS software Oppo uses, which is polished but busier than what longtime fans are used to.

Both have ultrasonic fingerprint sensors, which read your finger with sound waves and work with wet hands, plus IR blasters for controlling TVs. We haven’t seen a long-term update promise for either model yet, so check before you buy if you plan to keep the phone for years.

Draw. Red Magic’s software is better for gaming. OnePlus’s is better for everything else.

So, Which One Should You Buy?

We went through both spec sheets, benchmark data, and company claims line by line. Here’s where we landed.

Final tally: Red Magic 3, OnePlus 3, two draws.

Get the Red Magic 12 Pro+ if you play for long stretches, use shoulder triggers or wired earbuds, want a screen with nothing cutting into it, or live outside China and want local support.

From our side, the OnePlus 16 is the better all-round phone, and it’s cheaper. But the Red Magic 12 Pro+ is the better gaming phone, and for most readers outside China it’s also the only one of the two they can walk into a store and buy. That’s a stronger argument than any benchmark.

FAQs

Is there a cheaper gaming phone with a fan?

Yes, but it means stepping down a chip generation. The iQOO Neo 12 runs last year’s flagship chip with a cooling fan, a 9,000mAh battery, and a 185Hz screen, and it should cost well below either phone here.

Will OnePlus release a cheaper version of the OnePlus 16?

It looks likely. The OnePlus Ace 7 leak looks like a flagship and is widely expected to become the OnePlus 16R in India, trading the top chip for a much lower price.

Do phone cooling fans wear out?

They can, slowly. Fans have moving parts and vents that can collect dust, so performance may dip after years of heavy use. Most fan phones let you turn the fan off for light tasks, which saves battery and wear.

Which phones have the longest battery life right now?

Phones with 8,000mAh to 10,000mAh silicon-carbon cells now lead, mostly from Chinese brands. 

Should I wait for Black Friday to buy a gaming phone?

If you can wait, probably. Red Magic often runs launch discounts, and Black Friday phone deals can knock real money off last-generation models, though memory costs have kept some prices from dropping much this year.
