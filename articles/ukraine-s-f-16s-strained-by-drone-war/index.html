Ukraine is using American-made F-16 fighter jets to shoot down Russia's jet-powered drones, but the aircraft are flying so frequently that they risk breaking down, reports The New York Times.

Nearly half of Ukraine's F-16 fleet — about 25 aircraft — are grounded for maintenance, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said this month.

Zelenskyy said Kyiv is asking Western allies to move more maintenance work to Ukraine. The U.S. has awarded a contract worth up to $235.5 million to Belgium's Sabena Aerospace Engineering for maintenance of Ukrainian F-16s.

Ukraine's Foreign Ministry disputed a Wall Street Journal report that more than half of the F-16 fleet was grounded.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Georgiy Tykhy acknowledged that some parts for Ukraine's older F-16s are no longer manufactured and are difficult to obtain, but rejected reports that the fleet's readiness was catastrophically low.

Zelenskyy asked Belgium's King Philippe and Prime Minister Bart De Wever for additional F-16 fighter jets and ammunition during meetings last week as Russia continued attacking Ukraine's energy infrastructure.

Belgium will deliver 10 F-16s to Ukraine by the end of the year, De Wever said after meeting Zelenskyy on the sidelines of the Oct. 15 European Union summit in Brussels. The figure is up from the three jets Zelenskyy announced Sept. 29.

De Wever said Ukraine urgently needs combat-ready fighter jets and described the F-16 as one of the few weapons capable of effectively countering Russia's jet-powered drones.

The two leaders also discussed ammunition for the aircraft, including rounds manufactured by Belgian defense company FN Herstal.

Belgium first pledged 30 F-16s to Ukraine in May 2024, with deliveries scheduled through 2028. Transfers were delayed because Belgium had not received enough U.S.-made F-35s to replace its aging fighter fleet, but De Wever said the new aircraft are beginning to arrive.

Retired Air Force Lt. Gen. David A. Deptula, head of the Washington-area Mitchell Institute for Aerospace Studies, said Ukraine was "forced to use a relatively small fighter fleet day after day as a substitute for an adequately resourced, layered air defense system."

Using F-16s in that role, he said, left Ukrainian forces "consuming a finite resource faster than they should."

"It's not a sustainable long-term proposition," Deptula said.

Ukraine's use of F-16s has been successful.

Ukrainian F-16s accounted for "a significant portion" of the 1,900 jet-powered drones intercepted last month out of 3,500 launched by Russia, Col. Yurii Ihnat said in an interview.
