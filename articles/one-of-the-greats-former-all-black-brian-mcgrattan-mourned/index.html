‘One of the greats’: Former All Black Brian McGrattan mourned

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:48 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Tributes are being paid to “one of the greats” after former All Blacks prop Brian McGrattan died aged 66.

An All Blacks profile described the 1.9m, 105kg prop as an “immensely powerful scrummager”.

His hometown club, Marist St Pats in Wellington, said it was “deeply saddened” to learn of the death of its former captain and “club great”.

“Brian was a proud member of the Red Machine and one of the outstanding forwards to wear the Marist St Pats jersey,” the club said.

“He was one of the greats who helped shape Marist St Pats.”

Known as “Grats”, McGrattan captained the club’s Under-19 side in 1976 and made several appearances for its Jubilee Cup-winning senior side in 1978.

The club described McGrattan as a “powerful and uncompromising prop” who became a regular in the side that won the Swindale Shield and Jubilee Cup in 1979 and began a 42-match winning streak.

“His achievements at the highest level were built on the foundations of club rugby,” his club said.

In 1983, McGrattan became the club’s fifth All Black, one of 13 new All Blacks selected for the tour of England and Scotland under captain Stu Wilson.

McGrattan toured Australia and Fiji in 1984 and played both tests in Argentina in 1985, packing down alongside Hika Reid and Steve McDowell.

In 1986 he helped the “Baby Blacks” beat France 18-9 at Lancaster Park in Christchurch. He played his final test against Australia at Athletic Park in Wellington later that year. He made 23 appearances for New Zealand, including six tests.

McGrattan joined Hamilton Marist in 1987 and played twice for Waikato that season, against North Harbour and King Country.

Club vice-president Bernard Gittings said McGrattan was ahead of his time. He took up weight training early and would pick up the ball and run with it.

“He was a bit of a star when he was at the club,” Gittings said.

Gittings said the prop was always willing to help younger players improve.

Hamilton Marist coach Paul Martin remembered facing McGrattan in his first game for Marist. Martin, himself a prop, said McGrattan taught him a lesson that day.

McGrattan returned to Wellington in 1988 and captained Marist St Pats to the Swindale Shield and Jubilee Cup double. It was his fifth Jubilee Cup title with the club.

By the time he retired in 1992, he had played 105 games for Wellington A.

“Our thoughts and deepest sympathies are with Brian’s family, friends, former teammates and everyone who had the privilege of knowing him,” Marist St Pats said.

“Rest easy, Brian. Thank you for everything you gave to our club and our game.”
