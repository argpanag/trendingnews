Here are the top American finishers in the Bank of America Chicago Marathon

The 2026 Bank of America Chicago Marathon kicked off Sunday in Grant Park, and several Americans were giving it their all to make history

The 2026 Bank of America Chicago Marathon could be a record-setting day in more ways than one, and American competitors had big days on the streets of the city.

In the women’s open division, Emma Grace Hurley set a new American debut record as she crossed the line in 2:20:48, finishing in seventh place overall in her first-ever marathon.

Grace Hurley is no stranger to the Chicago running scene, as she smashed the American record in the 8K distance during March’s Shamrock Shuffle with a time of 24 minutes and 29 seconds.

In the men’s wheelchair race, Aaron Pike was the top American finisher, crossing the line in 1:30:17 to finish in fifth place unofficially. Miguel Jimenez Vergara was the second-best American finisher in the race, finishing in ninth place overall.

Marcel Hug of Switzerland won the race, setting a new unofficial course record with a time of 1:21:34.

On the women’s side of things, Switzerland’s Catherine Debrunner set an unofficial world record of 1:33:16, while Tatyana McFadden of the United States finished with a time of 1:42:01, good for second place overall and tops among her countrywomen.

In the men’s open race, a three-way battle between Americans unfolded on Sunday, with Vincent Mauri racing out to an early lead. Charles Hicks, who has the second-fastest time ever recorded by an American man in the marathon, ended up catching up to him around the 35K mark, and Clayton Young was also running in the same pack.

In the end it was Young who took home the top spot among Americans, finishing seventh overall with an unofficial time of 2:05:23, just 40 seconds shy of the American record at the marathon distance.

Hicks ended up finishing in eighth place overall and second among the American contingent. Mauri finished in 2:06:46.

Those spots are important both from a bragging rights perspective and from a financial perspective. The top American finishers in the men’s and women’s open divisions each collect a cool $15,000 bonus, with top-five finishers in those groups all receiving payouts.

Second place American finishers walk away with $12,000, with prizes of $10,000, $7,000 and $5,000 also up for grabs.
