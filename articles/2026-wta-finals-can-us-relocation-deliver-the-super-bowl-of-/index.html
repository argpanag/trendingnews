2026 WTA Finals: Can US relocation deliver the ‘Super Bowl of tennis’ after Saudi exit? - SportsPro

TheTools · 06.11.2026 16:20 · source · permalink

Getty Images

The WTA Finals remains the most lucrative event in women’s tennis outside the four Grand Slams.

This year’s traditional curtain closer will take place at a temporary home at Indian Wells Tennis Garden in California, with the world’s best eight singles players and doubles teams competing for a total prize pot of US$10 million.

The venue change follows the WTA’s decision to end its three-year hosting agreement with Riyadh a year early, reportedly due to concerns over the war in the Middle East. The Saudi deal was controversial from that start due to the country’s alleged human rights abuses and treatment of women, but it was undeniably lucrative.

It means the WTA begins a new commercial chapter this Sunday, with the tour welcoming new sponsors and eager to strengthen its blue ribband event.

The host 

Despite being the WTA’s marquee event, the WTA Finals has endured a nomadic existence. Indeed, Indian Wells is the seventh different venue to host it since 2018.

As the home to the BNP Paribas Open each spring, the Tennis Garden was a logical short-term solution.

WTA chair Valerie Camillo said: “From its world-class facilities and passionate fan base to its proven ability to deliver premier tennis events, the venue offers everything needed to showcase the very best of women’s tennis.”

Matches will take place between November 8 and 15 in Stadium 1, which seats over 16,000 fans and is the second-largest outdoor tennis stadium in the world. It also marks the Finals’ first return to Southern California since 2005.

From 2027 to 2029, the WTA Finals will then settle in Charlotte at Spectrum Center, home of the National Basketball Association’s (NBA) Charlotte Hornets. The city is also set to become WTA’s new headquarters, bringing more stability to its showpiece.

However, sharing the venue with an NBA franchise comes with its compromises. The tournament is expected to be shortened from eight days to five, creating a more compressed schedule that could require finalists to compete on consecutive days, prompting concerns around player welfare.

Financial challenges and commercial opportunities

The return to the US coincides with a difficult financial period for the WTA. In September, The Telegraph reported that the organisation is forecasting a US$23 million operating loss in 2026, underlining the economic impact of its departure from Saudi Arabia.

The tour is also preparing for the potential conclusion of CVC Capital Partner’s US$150 million investment, with the final annual payment due in 2027. This pressure is compounded by the WTA’s pursuit of increased player compensation and greater prize-money parity.

Despite this financial strain, WTA continues to attract strong partners.

Last month, yoghurt brand FAGE was unveiled as title partner of the next four editions of the WTA Finals. While the financial terms were not disclosed, Teodora Ivanova, chief business officer of WTA Ventures, described the agreement as “a very attractive partnership for both sides”.

Just this week, the WTA also announced its first official merchandise collaboration with Hello Kitty, which will debut at Indian Wells and is designed to give fans new ways to engage with the sport.

Whilst questions persist over the financial sustainability of the tour, there is a sense that women’s tennis is only scratching the surface of its commercial potential.

Prize money

This years’ prize fund is US$10 million with US$8 million allocated to the singles draw and US$2 million set aside for doubles, a reduction of US$5.5 million from Riyadh last year.

An undefeated singles champion can still earn US$3.37 million, including participation fees, and an undefeated doubles pair will take home US$725,000.

The 2025 edition delivered a historic payday for Elena Rybakina, who earned US$5.235 million after defeating Aryna Sabalenka in the final. It was the largest ever single prize money payout in women’s sport and a record across both the men’s and women’s professional tennis tours.

Broadcast strategy

There are some upsides to the move, however. Relocation to the US – one of tennis’s most commercially developed territories and the world’s biggest sports media market – could significantly strengthen the tournament’s broadcast proposition.

Domestically, Tennis Channel is primed to capitalise on the venue change after extending its exclusive US rights agreement with the WTA in 2025 for a further six years.

Hosting the event in California allows matches to fill the prime-time evening slot, which will give viewing figures a boost. The atmosphere should also be a vast improvement on Riyadh, where sparse crowds detracted from the in-venue and on-screen experience.

In the UK, the new time zone means early-hours viewing for fans watching on Sky Sports, which holds exclusive UK rights to both the ATP and WTA Tours through 2028. Despite this, Sky remains crucial to the WTA’s broadcast strategy and its ambitions to grow the game.

According to The Guardian, women accounted for 60 per cent of Sky Tennis’ audience in 2025, compared with 31 per cent across its wider sports portfolio. This is an extremely attractive figure for advertisers looking to reach female consumers on linear TV or streaming platforms such as Sky Go and NOW.

Elsewhere, the WTA benefits from a broad international distribution network. TSN and RDS carry coverage in Canada, while Stan Sport and beIN Sports broadcast the tour in Australia. In Brazil, CazéTV holds exclusive rights through 2030, while Chinese coverage is provided by Tencent and Migu following rights renewals earlier this year. Across several major European markets, including France, Spain and Germany, coverage is available through Eurosport, and Italian viewers can watch on SuperTennis TV.

Aside from traditional television, WTA and TikTok have just launched a global partnership, centred on player storytelling and creator content. As part of this new agreement, the 2026 WTA Finals draw ceremony was livestreamed on TikTok for the first time on Tuesday this week.

Although the partnership does not include live match rights, it provides the WTA with an additional direct to consumer channel and an opportunity to engage a younger audience by bringing fans closer to the action.

With the move to Charlotte on the horizon, greater locational certainty will allow commercial and broadcast partners to plan further ahead and subsequently increase their investment.

The bigger picture

The WTA Finals arrives in California during a period of intense transition and financial unease.

This said, the return to the US presents an opportunity to build on greater exposure in one of tennis’s most important commercial markets, along with a new title sponsor in FAGE.

Progress on the long-discussed merger between the WTA and its male counterpart, the Association of Tennis ­Profes­sionals (ATP) Tour had been expected this year. However, talks regarding this historic union have been suspended indefinitely as the women’s game braces itself for a decline in its financial fortunes, at least in the short term. 

Despite the mounting financial pressures, Camillo has openly stated her ambition to transform the Finals into the “Super Bowl of women’s sports”, reinforcing the organisations belief in the event’s potential.

Ivanova also said: “We have an operating machine that can sustain this growth and the cost base that is required to produce this growth, and so we feel very confident about the future.”

The WTA’s challenge now is turning that confidence into sustainable commercial growth for the Finals and tour as a whole.
