Oilers notebook: Messier rages against Babcock hire; Frederic remains an enigma

On ESPN’s NHL season preview show, Messier said he was “absolutely shocked” they brought in Babcock because of the stories Messier has heard about Babcock’s treatment of players on past teams.

“I’m absolutely shocked from the stories I’ve heard from very good, reputable character people about the way they were treated,” said Messier, arguably the greatest leader in NHL history over his 1,756 games, starting with 851 here and his five Cups with the Oilers before getting one as Rangers’ captain.

Nobody bleeds Oiler orange and blue more than Messier.

Babcock, hired to a multi-year contract over the summer, has acknowledged he has made players uncomfortable, saying he could have been better, pledging to do better when introduced as the new coach here. But, Messier, never a tap-dancer, always saying what’s on his mind, emphatically voiced his disapproval on the ESPN show.

Time will tell how the Babcock experiment works. It’s very early, 10 days into camp.

So far, the Oiler leaders Connor McDavid, Leon Draisaitl and Zach Hyman are fully on board, liking what they’ve seen from Babcock. He’s pushed the players in the practices and drills, trying to get to know a new team. He looks like he’s enjoying himself, and he’s been his usual quoteable.

McDavid and Co. badly want their first Cup, want to be led.

What does Babcock, who has a Cup from his days in Detroit, crave?

“I don’t look at what I want (down the road). As a team, I just want them getting better every day. Yesterday (Monday) I didn’t like what I saw from the group. Today we got a way better. It’s process, process, process. That’s what we’re focusing on,” said Babcock. “We’ll work at getting the players feeling good about themselves and we’ll go from there.”

Where does Trent Frederic fit?

Nobody has to rebound more than Frederic after last season’s awful four goals and seven points in 74 games. He could be on a quasi-third line to start the season with centre Jason Dickinson and Kasperi Kapanen, although Alex Formenton’s clear speed advantage may push Frederic down to the fourth line.

Right now, Frederic remains an enigma.

We keep thinking Frederic can be a major contributor, starting Year 2 of that eight-year $30.8 million deal but in his first exhibition, he was lukewarm in Winnipeg Tuesday. Right now, Colton Dach, Babcock’s favourite player, is doing the things Frederic must do, although from the centre slot: hitting hard and often, using his size, lots of arriving at opposing players in ill-humour.

Frederic will be on the team somewhere, but he’s had a vanilla camp.

“I just want Freddy to be Freddy, have some jump, get in on the fore-check. Play a simple physical game and feel good about himself. If we can get him back, he can be an important piece here,” said Babcock.

Long Latvia journey for Tralmaks

Latvian winger Eduards Tralmaks, who played for his country in the Olympics with three goals in four games, is bound for Bakersfield. But, Tralmaks, who played four years at U of Maine before embarking on a minor-league journey, is a definite call-up possibility winger during the year with his bull-like strength in tight quarters. His skating is average but his strength grades out very highly in board battles.

You have to wonder why Tralmaks, 29, would ever want to sign a one-year free-agent deal here with clearly a crowded NHL field at forward rather than, say, Calgary with their small Flames’ forward group. But Tralmaks, who has played 154 AHL games and two seasons in Kladno, the team Jaromir Jagr partly owns, took the plunge with the Oilers.

“I played in Winnipeg (Tuesday) and hopefully it’s not the last game,” said Tralmaks, who played in Grand Rapids last season and has been in Boston’s minor-league system too.

“I’ve proven myself on the Latvian national team and at a lot of different levels, close to where I want to be (NHL). I think I’m a good depth (NHL) piece. That’s how I see myself. I can pick up the trash and put it into the net.”

The Latvian Tralmaks wears No. 34 in honour of his countryman and former Oiler forward Sergei Zholtok. He died at 31 of a heart attack in the dressing room after a Nov. 3, 2004, game in Minsk during the NHL lockout.

“I looked up to him growing up, which is why I wear his number. But my teammates at the Olympics, Zemgus Girgensons and Teddy Blueger … they’re the top names I look at, too.”

Zholtok played 588 NHL games, 37 for the Oilers in 2001-2002 with Doug Weight, Ryan Smyth and Todd Marchant as teammates. The Oilers had acquired him from Montreal for Chad Kilger.

This ‘n that

  • Defenceman Shakir Mukhamadullin had an uneven first Oilers game in Winnipeg, alongside likely Bakersfield veteran Josh Brown. Babcock likes his ranginess long arms in battles, but the jury’s out on the Darnell Nurse trade piece. “There’s eight guys battling for spots (Mukhamadullin, Ty Emberson and Spencer Stastney in the 6-7-8 holes) and we’ll figure it out. He’s big, he can skate and like a lot of guys who arrive in the NHL, you arrive as a skill guy, then you look at the league and figure out where do I fit in. He’ll have to be a penalty-killer, a box-out, a compete guy,” said Babcock, who likes D men who are more trees than shrubs.”
  • The Oilers reduced their camp by five, sending defencemen Beau Akey, Samuel Sjolund and Zach Bookman and forwards Brady Stonehouse and Simon Tassy to Bakersfield. Akey, 21, the Oilers’ 2023 second-round draft, is far down the organizational ladder. He didn’t get an exhibition game. He’s behind Atro Leppanen, Damien Carfagna, Tomas Cibulka, Josh Brown, Riley Stillman and possibly Spencer Stastney if he’s sent down to the farm squad and clears waivers. Tassy, Bookman and Sjolund are on AHL contracts, not Oiler two-way deals.
  • The Oilers may well have to loan one of their minor league goalies to another organization for playing time because they have four chairs (AHL and ECHL) and five bodies—Matt Tomkins, Connor Ungar, Samuel Jonsson, Nathaniel Day and Eemil Vinni.
  • Local product Trey Fix-Wolansky, who signed with Canucks, hoping a weaker NHL team might be his ticket to regular NHL employment after 26 NHL games in Columbus and Rangers, unfortunately didn’t last until the Oiler-Canucks exhibition Thursday. He’s part of huge group of guys on NHL waivers. “I think he can do it (play in NHL) but he needs the right opportunity,” said Perry Pearn, who has had the one-time Oil Kings’ winger at his 3-on-3 pro camp and likes his skill level. “He’s a bit in between. He’s not exactly a top-six NHL player. I don’t think he’s proven to teams he’s a good enough defensive player to be a bottom-six player.” Pearn switched gears to talk about another ex-WHLer and 3-on-3 camper Parker Kelly from these parts (Camrose), who has morphed into a nice role-playing piece in Colorado after playing junior in Prince Albert and undrafted. “Parker isn’t a way better player but his intensity and willingness to do things away from the puck may be a little higher (than Fix-Wolansky),” said Pearn. Kelly had 21 goals last season for the Avs.
  • Former Oilers first-rounder Reid Schaefer is on the bubble in Nashville with fellow forwards Joakim Kemell and Adam Edstrom to stay on the roster in Nashville. The Preds surprisingly put feisty ex-first-round Sharks’ draft pick forward Ozzy Wiesblatt, 24, on waivers Wednesday. Wiesblatt, who played 40 games last season, hit Leon Draisaitl with a hard, hit early in a March 15 game at Rogers Place, which KO’d Draisaitl with a leg injury for a month. Wiesblatt, 24, is one of the few guys on the waiver wire who might intrigue another NHL team.
  • Ex-Oiler farmhand defenceman Cam Dineen, who played three years in Bakersfield and four NHL games for the club, signed with Flyers as a free agent in July but didn’t last long in Philly’s camp. Also on waivers.
  • Islanders defenceman Ethan Bear, the one-time Oiler who looked like a surefire No. 5 NHL D in his early days here, cleared waivers Wednesday. He hasn’t been the same since he got a shoulder injury playing in the worlds.
