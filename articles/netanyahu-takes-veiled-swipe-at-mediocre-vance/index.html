Netanyahu takes veiled swipe at ‘mediocre’ Vance

TheTools · 27.10.2026 14:15 · source · permalink

Israeli prime minister’s apparent dismissal of US vice-president came after he refused to endorse him for re-election

Benjamin Netanyahu appeared to take a swipe at “mediocre” JD Vance after the US vice-president refused to endorse him.

In a post on X on Sunday, the Israeli prime minister shared an AI-generated image of a nuclear attack on Tel Aviv.

The accompanying text, which he attributed to Yuval Bloomberg, a conservative Israeli commentator, said: “We have another two years of Trump to finish the work in Iran, and there’s no telling what will come after that – whether it’s Kamala Harris or some other mediocre figure.”

Mr Vance is tipped as the favourite to run as the Republican candidate in the 2028 US presidential election, suggesting he is one of the “mediocre” figures referred to.

Donald Trump recently endorsed Mr Netanyahu, who is facing a tight race in the first nationwide election in Israel since the Oct 7, 2023 attacks.

Asked last week whether he would also endorse the Israeli prime minister, Mr Vance declined, saying he was focusing on the midterm elections next month.

“I endorse American candidates, and that’s where I’m focused on – the midterms,” he told Mark Halperin, the political journalist, in an interview on Air Force Two. “I’m not anti-endorsing him, I’m not pro-endorsing him.”

Mr Vance reportedly opposed Mr Trump’s decision to launch America’s strikes on Iran, which were supported by Israel.

The war with Iran dismayed many of the Maga movement’s most ardent supporters, including Mr Vance’s friend Tucker Carlson, the former Fox News anchor.

Mr Carlson, who now hosts a popular conservative podcast and is credited with helping to return Mr Trump to the White House, had a very public rift with the US president, particularly over the conflict, which he described as “absolutely disgusting and evil”.

The former Fox anchor has been repeatedly accused of anti-Semitism, including over his decision to interview Nick Fuentes, a far-Right commentator often described as a white supremacist, on his show.

Mr Vance is seen by many as the probable Republican nominee for 2028 and has reportedly tried to allay concerns within the party about his views on Israel.

Semafor reported on Monday that he had tried to reassure pro-Israel Republicans that he would simply like a relationship with Israel that is “more explicitly guided by US interests”.

On Joe Rogan’s podcast earlier this year, Mr Vance said he was a “guy advocating for a normal relationship with a normal country”.

In his post on X on Sunday, Mr Netanyahu suggested there were no guarantees for what Israel’s relationship with the US would look like after the end of the Trump administration.

He echoed Mr Trump’s insistence that the ultimate goal of the war with Iran was to ensure that the country did not have access to nuclear weapons.

The next two years, he said, were “the window of time we have left to complete the necessary work to ensure our physical existence for the coming years”.
