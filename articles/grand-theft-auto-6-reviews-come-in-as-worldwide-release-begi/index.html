  2. GTA 6 is 'the game equivalent of a Christopher Nolan film'published at 14:57 GMT
    14:57 GMT

    Victoriya Holland
    BBC World Service

    Reece Reilly is a New Zealand-based gamer and podcaster, and interviews game developers and industry figures for his show Kiwi Talkz.

    He’s been playing GTA 6 for over two hours already, and has high praise for the look and feel of the game.

    "The graphics and the art direction, especially are astounding," he tells World Business Express. "I've never seen anything like this in a game. The voice direction is amazing. The music is great. It just feels so good.

    "You can see where all the investment with the time, the money and the resources. It's like the equivalent of Christopher Nolan, I guess, being able to swing for the fences because he has the resources to do it."

  3. 'That's something I've never really seen before in a video game' - Australian gamerpublished at 14:41 GMT
    14:41 GMT

    Simon Atkinson
    Reporting from Sydney

    Spoiler alert: This post contains a few details about the game.

    It’s 01:00 here in Sydney. Having crashed Andrew Shelley’s apartment for a couple of hours, we’ll leave him to play well into the early hours.

    But what are his first impressions?

    "The thing that's striking me the most is the visuals. It looks stunning and I'm just enjoying kind of how different it feels to previous games, but also with some level of familiarity," he says.

    "I saw a storm for the first time. It looks incredible. The way it kind of envelopes the whole map and feels like a weather system is moving in and it's all localised. That's something I've never really seen before in a video game."

    "Maybe I could have been a little more stealthy and survived a little longer without having to shoot. Maybe one day I'll try a pacifist run through the game where I won't have to kill anyone."

  4. I reviewed GTA 3 25 years ago, when the developers hit a winning formulapublished at 14:26 GMT
    14:26 GMT

    Daniel Coles
    UK digital editor

    It was 25 years ago this month that I reviewed GTA 3 for the BBC.

    This was the breakthrough 3D version of the game that propelled its humour, violence and open-world freedom to mainstream attention.

    I called the gameplay a "bloodbath" - it came just weeks after the 9/11 attacks.

    But it was also clear to me and millions of new gamers with their shiny PS2 consoles that the Edinburgh developers had hit a winning formula.

    I thought they might be on GTA 15 by now. But I reckoned without the soaring costs of game development.

    It's remarkable how, like James Bond, it has stayed so beloved by its patient fans - but could it still be around in another 25 years?

  6. What do other less favourable reviews say?published at 14:01 GMT
    14:01 GMT

    Joe Coughlan
    Live editor

    Reviews have been largely positive for the latest game in the Grand Theft Auto series.

    The lowest-scoring review so far is from ScreenHub.

    The Australia-based publication gave the game four stars out of five, praising it as "lovingly rendered" but saying large sections are "boring and too limited".

    Gaming news outlet Noisy Pixel also rated the game nine out of 10, calling the protagonists "compelling" but describing the story's pacing as "uneven". It said the system for police responding to criminal actions could feel "unpredictable".

  8. GTA 6 holds its 99 score as more reviews come inpublished at 13:12 GMT
    13:12 GMT

    Tom Gerken
    Technology reporter

    Earlier this morning, I told you GTA 6 had launched to a near-perfect score from critics.

    According to Metacritic, which aggregates reviews, the game has a 99 out of 100.

    About 50 more reviews have come in since I wrote that, and the score is unchanged. In fact, things might be looking even better.

    Metacritic's overall list, external now puts GTA 6 above Ocarina of Time in its rankings - though it's unclear whether that's because it has a higher average score or if it's simply alphabetical.

    I've asked Metacritic to confirm whether GTA is now officially the best.

    But even if the games are tied at the top, here's something interesting: Zelda got its score back when there weren't many online reviewers. In fact, it scored 99 based on just 22 critic reviews.

    GTA 6 has done it based on 146.

  9. Gaming the media - how previous GTA releases stirred up fears of violencepublished at 13:01 GMT
    13:01 GMT

    Imran Rahman-Jones
    Technology reporter

    Though it’s 18-rated like its predecessors, this may be the first GTA launch where headlines aren’t dominated by concerns about violence.

    Earlier releases were criticised for allegedly glamourising crime, but the controversy was often fuelled by the game’s own creators.

    "The marketing team decided, well you know what, let’s play up this kind of bad boy thing a little bit," the first game’s writer and publicist Brian Baglow told Witness History in 2015.

    The strategy generated huge publicity. Newspapers and even Parliament debated whether it would corrupt the nation’s children.

    "We just did things that we felt were fun," the game’s co-creator Dave Jones told the BBC’s Tech Now programme.

    "We never at any point kind of thought, should we do that? Is it controversial? We never thought that way."

  18. Pretending to be in New Zealand, booking leave and buying consoles - the lengths fans are going topublished at 11:30 GMT
    11:30 GMT

    Laura Cress
    Technology reporter

    When Victoria Evans filed a leave request at work, the reason she gave was “GTA 6”. She told the BBC her boss "doesn't know what GTA 6 is".

    It found GTA was mentioned in 4.6% of employees’ time-off requests.

    It said an estimated 83,000 days had been booked off for the game throughout November and December.

    Booking leave is only one way fans are making sure they’re ready for the big day.

    Others are buying a new console to play it on or even pretending they’re halfway around the world to get early access.
