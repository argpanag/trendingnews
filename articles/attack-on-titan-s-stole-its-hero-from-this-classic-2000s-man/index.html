Attack on Titan's Stole Its Hero From This Classic 2000s Manga

TheTools · 10.10.2026 23:45 · source · permalink

Attack on Titan has been one of the biggest manga franchises since its debut on September 9, 2009. The story of Eren Yeager's quest to avenge his mother's death and uncover the mysteries beyond the walls captivated readers until the manga's conclusion in 2021. Thanks to its anime adaptation, the franchise has become an international phenomenon, spawning live-action adaptations, video games, and more.

However, Attack on Titan's finale had already happened in 2005, in a way, starring a very different protagonist with remarkably similar motivations. Enter Lucy from the infamous manga Elfen Lied. Long before Eren Yeager attempted to reshape the world through mass slaughter, Lucy's powers and hatred of humanity had already brought her to the brink of annihilating the human race, and while Eren may be more famous, her story was so much better.

The circumstances of Eren and Lucy's upbringings are initially very different, but their lives begin to converge after specific, devastating events reshape their worldviews. Many would point to his mother's death as the defining moment in Eren's life, but the first cracks appeared earlier.

The murder of the Ackermans and Eren's decision to kill one of the men responsible while saving Mikasa marked his first encounter with the brutal violence of the world beyond the walls of his childhood. Eren was able to save someone he was willing to kill to protect, but Lucy wasn't so lucky.

After her puppy was brutally beaten to death with a rock in front of her, Lucy unleashed her Diclonius vectors for the first time, slaughtering the children responsible and the girl who had betrayed her trust by revealing the puppy's hiding place. Unlike Eren, however, Lucy's destructive potential was also tied to the mysterious DNA Voice, an influence connected to the Diclonius that would later push her toward violence and the extermination of humanity.

For Eren, his mother's death became another formative tragedy, sending him down a path of revenge that eventually echoed Lucy's hatred of humanity. While Eren gained military training and gradually learned the truth about his people's suffering, Lucy struggled with a conflicting desire for acceptance and love despite her growing distrust of humans.

The DNA Voice's influence further complicated Lucy's struggle, as its demands for destruction clashed with the part of her that still wanted human connection. Throughout their stories, both characters grow close to the people around them.

Beyond Lucy's interactions with Kouta's friends and other Diclonius, there is Kouta himself, whom she had cared for since they were children. Even though Kouta no longer remembered her, Lucy never forgot him. Eren, meanwhile, grew close to his fellow soldiers and formed lasting bonds with his squadmates.

This is why both of their actions at the ends of their journeys were viewed as heartbreaking by some readers and validating by others. Both characters commit horrific acts while believing they are fighting for a future where the people they love can live without fear. Their choices leave readers questioning whether the suffering they endured can ever justify the destruction they ultimately bring upon others.

ElfenLiedReachedAttackonTitan'sEndingYearsEarlier

Both Eldians and Diclonius face discrimination because of their potential to be dangerous. This persecution shapes Eren and Lucy, fueling their resentment toward a world that fears and mistreats them. While Lucy experiences this hatred from childhood, Eren initially sees himself as an ordinary human, until he discovers the truth about the Eldians and the prejudice they face beyond the walls.

This contributes to his eventual decision to protect his friends and the people of Paradis, even if it means destroying those he considers a threat. One of the most infamous aspects of Attack on Titan is its ending, in which Eren uses the Rumbling to kill 80 percent of humanity.

His actions stem from a complicated combination of his desire for freedom, his determination to protect his friends, and his knowledge of a future he struggles to escape. However, Eren isn't the first anime protagonist to threaten humanity on such a scale, as Lucy reaches a similar point during the final arc of Elfen Lied, titled “Farewell, Lucy,” after Kouta is shot while protecting her, despite having just learned that she murdered his father and sister.

Throughout the Elfen Lied manga, Lucy struggles against the influence of the DNA Voice, which urges her to exterminate humanity and expand the Diclonius population by infecting men with their DNA. As her vectors grow more powerful, her ability to cause destruction threatens humanity, while the strain begins to take a physical toll on her body.

However, Lucy's love for Kouta conflicts with the Voice's destructive influence, and she uses her remaining power to heal him before the Voice takes control of her body and continues its rampage against her will. Eren and Lucy's endings parallel each other, as both characters threaten humanity on a massive scale and are ultimately stopped by someone they love.

Their stories approach accountability differently, with Eren's final conversation with Armin revealing not only his desire to make his friends into heroes, but also his selfish impulses and relentless pursuit of freedom. Lucy, meanwhile, attempts to take responsibility for her past by apologizing to Kouta for murdering his family and expressing regret for the suffering she has caused.

Although both characters are ultimately stopped by someone they love, their final interactions reveal another important difference. Eren deliberately distances himself from Mikasa and his other friends, partly to make it easier for them to stop him and emerge as humanity's heroes.

Lucy, by contrast, apologizes to Kouta and promises not to kill again if he allows her to remain with him, expressing her long-standing desire to be accepted despite the atrocities she has committed. Eren gives his friends a chance at a future, but he cannot guarantee lasting peace for Paradis.

Lucy's ending offers a more intimate form of emotional closure, as her attempt to save Kouta gives her a final moment of selflessness amid the devastation she has caused. Although Lucy's fate is tragic, the epilogue leaves open the possibility of a new beginning through two horned girls who appear to be connected to her, offering a glimmer of hope that contrasts the doomed distant future that Eren's actions were unable to prevent, and that reveals his actions were all pointless.
