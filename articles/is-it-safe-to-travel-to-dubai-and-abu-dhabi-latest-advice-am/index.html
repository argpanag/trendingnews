Is it safe to travel to Dubai and Abu Dhabi? Latest advice amid regional conflict

Holidaymakers may feel uncertain about travelling to the Middle East, including Dubai and Abu Dhabi, amid strikes in the region.

Earlier in the year, missiles and drones hit Dubai, and a series of explosions were reported in Dubai and Abu Dhabi as the war between America and Iran intensified.

Since June, the UK’s Foreign Office (FCDO) has removed its recommendation against “all but essential travel” to the UAE, and it has not put in place any new travel recommendations.

The country, popular among tourists for its ultra-modern cities, was subject to this travel advisory for months following the outbreak of war between Iran and the US in February.

Here’s the latest travel advice for the UAE, plus all the key questions and answers.

Is it safe to travel to the UAE?

The FCDO no longer advises against travel to the UAE, but warns that “the situation in the Middle East remains unpredictable”. This still stands as of 1 October.

What is the government's advice?

The FCDO is still displaying advice for travellers amid ongoing regional tensions.

It says that British nationals should read its crisis abroad document, follow advice from local authorities and sign up to FCDO travel advice email alerts.

It also advises that if conflict resumes, they should stay away from areas around security or military facilities, keep departure plans and travel documents up to date and stay indoors if they are advised to take shelter.

“Since 8 July there have been strikes and retaliatory attacks by Iran in a number of locations across the region against US military and civilian infrastructure. There is a risk of more attacks and unforeseen escalation in the region,” the FCDO says.

“Before the ceasefire, the Iranian regime had stated its intention to target locations associated with the US and Israel, including organisations, businesses, facilities and institutions.”

It adds: “British nationals currently in the Middle East should be prepared for possible flight cancellations, periodic airspace closures and potential travel disruptions.”

Are flights to Dubai and Abu Dhabi running?

As of 1 October, commercial flights between the UK and the UAE are running as normal; however, several carriers, including British Airways and Virgin Atlantic, have cancelled or temporarily suspended routes in the region.

British Airways will recommence Dubai flights on 3 November, but won’t start up flights to Abu Dhabi until winter 2027. Virgin Atlantic will also not restart its Dubai flights until winter 2027.

Under air passengers’ rights rules, travellers are entitled to be flown to their destination as soon as possible on any airline that has seats available in the event their flight is cancelled.

Can I book a holiday to the UAE?

As the FCDO no longer warns against non-essential travel to the UAE, your travel insurance is likely to be valid if you decide to book a trip. Your travel insurance is at risk of being invalidated if you travel against advice from the FCDO. However, it is important to check individual policies.

Package holidays are likely to start operating as usual. There are currently no grounds for travellers to expect a refund if they cancel, nor to claim the money back through travel insurance.

If FCDO advice changes to advising against all travel, you can cancel a package holiday without penalty for a full refund.
