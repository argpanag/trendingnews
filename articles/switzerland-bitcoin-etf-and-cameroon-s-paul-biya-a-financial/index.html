Switzerland Bitcoin ETF Approval Brings New Scrutiny

The Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) approved the country's first publicly available investment fund that invests directly in Bitcoin.

The fund falls under Switzerland's collective investment scheme framework, classified as an alternative investment fund carrying particular risks. Unlike existing Swiss crypto exchange-traded products structured as debt securities, the new vehicle operates under a different regulatory framework.

FINMA granted an exemption from standard diversification requirements, citing Bitcoin's relatively long trading history and substantial trading volume compared with other cryptocurrencies.

Switzerland Bitcoin ETF Approval, FINMA

The regulator also stressed that approval does not eliminate Bitcoin's market risks. Investors could still lose a significant portion of their capital because of price volatility.

Custody arrangements, operational safeguards and transparent risk disclosures remain central to the approval. The fund is expected to begin trading in November, according to its issuer, BIT Asset Management.

The decision raises a question: as crypto becomes more accessible through regulated financial products, how effectively are investors protected against risks associated with digital assets?

Between Switzerland and Cameroon's Paul Biya

Cameroon President Paul Biya returned home on August 20 after spending 73 days abroad, including an extended stay in Geneva, Switzerland. The 93-year-old leader's absence attracted scrutiny over government continuity and political succession in a country he has governed since 1982. His office had described the trip as a short private stay.

Switzerland's appeal to international investors extends beyond crypto. For decades, the country has attracted foreign wealth, including money linked to politically exposed individuals. That history has repeatedly raised questions about whether financial transparency and anti-money-laundering safeguards are sufficient to prevent the country's financial system from being exploited.
