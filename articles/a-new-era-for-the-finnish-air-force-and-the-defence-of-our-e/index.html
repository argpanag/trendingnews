"A new era for the Finnish Air Force and the defence of our entire country" – F-35 fighter jets were ceremoniously unveiled in Rovaniemi

TheTools · 01.10.2026 12:40 · source · permalink

Elmo Eskelinen, Verneri Vuohelainen, Oliver Kraatz 1.10.2026 15.40

The president of Aeronautics for Lockheed Martin Orlando Sanchez Jr. rejoiced over the jets' arrival in Finland.

A long awaited milestone was met on the first of October as the Finnish Air Force presented Finland's first F-35A Lightning II fighter jets to media and other guests in Lapland's Air Wing in Rovaniemi.

"Today a new era has begun for the Finnish Air Force and the defence of our entire country. The new F-35 fighter jets will strengthen our defence capability for decades to come," said the President of Finland, Alexander Stubb.

"The delivery of very capable F-35 fighters and their integration to Finland’s Defence system strengthens both Finland’s and the Alliance’s deterrence and defence," said the Minister of Defence, Antti Häkkänen.

All national media outlets were present at the event in addition to important guests tied to the F-35 purchase. Representatives from Lockheed Martin, the manufacturer of the fighter jets, were also present.

"Finland's first F-35s are home and it was amazing to see them outside," the president of Aeronautics for Lockheed Martin Orlando Sanchez Jr. commented. 

Alongside speeches, the Air Forces Big Band, supported by Linda Lampenius, performed live for guests.

The highlight of the event was a flyby of the new F-35 fighter jets.

Defence capability strengthened for decades to come 

The arrival of the planes begins a new F-35 era which will last over 30 years. The new fleet is to be used up until the 2060s.

The 64 F-35 fighter jets purchased from the U.S Lockheed Martin will replace Finland's current F/A-18 Hornet fleet. The Air Force will use both models of aircraft simultaneously up until the F-35 fleet is in full operational capability by the end of 2030.

"This isn't just about a better fighter jet; it's about an entirely new generation of systems. The F-35 generates a massive amount of data for use by the entire defense system and supports its operations," Stubb says.

The F-35 is a fifth generation multirole fighter jet whose capability is based, among other things, on stealth capabilities and advanced sensors.

The new fighter jets will be utilised to defend Finland's territorial integrity, repel enemy aircraft and support Army and Navy operations. 

"For Finland, the F-35 is not just a new fighter jet, but a comprehensive system that strengthens our ability to conduct aerial defense and strike operations, counter enemy air defense, and gather intelligence," says the Commander of the Finnish Air Force Maj. Gen. Timo Herranen.

The first planes, JF-510 and JF-512, arrived in Rovaniemi on the 18th of September. Aircraft JF-511, from the same batch, was kept in Arizona due to an avionics malfunction. Regardless, this third aircraft will arrive in Finland by the end of autumn. 

Training is well underway

The F-35s system capabilities will be developed in phases as aircraft deliveries and personnel training progress.

"My assessment is that it is not only this ultimate fighting machine. We also need the committed and qualified people operating the machine which is one part of the air defence as a system," says Häkkänen.

The first eight jets manufactured for Finland are currently in the use of Finnish pilots and staff for initial training in the United States.

The first training groups have already graduated, and the training will continue in the U.S until early 2028.

In Finland, training for flight technical personnel will begin in Rovaniemi in late 2026, and pilot training will begin in January 2027.

The training is led by Finnish instructors who were trained in the U.S, in collaboration with American, Norwegian, and Danish flight instructors and experts.

The Lapland Air Wing is scheduled to achieve initial operational capability with the F-35s in early 2028. By that point, the Air Command will operate exclusively with F-35 aircraft.

The Karelian Air Wing's first F-35s will enter service in 2028.

Benefits for every branch of the military 

The purchase of these new F-35 jets are not only a replacement for the older F/A-18 Hornets, but will also provide the Defence Forces with a substantially increased performance ability. 

"The F-35 multirole fighter jets are described, with reason, as quarterbacks of the battlefield," says the Air Force's F-35 Chief Procurement Officer, Col. (Ret.) Henrik Elo.

In particular, the information capacity of the F-35 has developed significantly, according to Elo, 
compared to previous generations. 

"The advanced and versatile sensors of the jet facilitate the detection and recognition of new targets. Sensor fusion combines data collected from multiple sources for the pilot so that they can focus on what is most important: decision making and combat control." 

The same information is also shared to other systems connected to the same network, whether they are on land, at sea, in the sky or in space.

"In the future, the Army and the Navy will be provided with better situational awareness by JF, which will allow them to defend civil and military targets through joint operation more efficiently than before," Elo elaborates.

JF (Joint Fighters) is the Finnish model code for the F-35 jets. According to Elo, it perfectly illustrates what the role of the new jets will be. 

Finnish defence is further strengthened by the fact that, by the year 2035, there will be more than 750 F-35 jets in Europe. 

"Joint operation capability with other F-35 user nations will facilitate an unprecedented level of defence efficiency in all of Europe. Thanks to its network capabilities, the jets will not only strengthen aerial defence but also our total defence capability with our allies."

"The deterrent power of these jets clearly reduces the chances of ending up in a war. Regardless, If we were to end up at war, The performance capability of the F-35s would be a crucial factor in maintaining our independence."

F-35 firepower in only increasing 

The development of unmanned systems, like long-range drones seen in Ukraine, will not reduce the role of fighter jets in warfare, says Elo.

"The assumption that something small and cheap could replace the performance capabilities of a fighter jet is baseless. These drones have nothing to do with achieving and maintaining aerial superiority."

Throughout the war in Ukraine, drone strikes have developed very quickly and have become one of the most notable aspects of the war. 

However, every war is different, and according to Elo, Ukraine's situation cannot be compared to Finland's situation in which the country has an ability to achieve aerial superiority and maintain it with the aid of allies.

"The war in Ukraine is an example of what happens when neither side in a war can gain control of the skies. The war becomes lengthy, casualties increase for both sides, and still there is no solution in sight."

Elo continues that, according to experts, the war in Ukraine has in no way shown that the significance of fighter jets has decreased, if anything it has only increased. 

"Many countries have examined the possibility of using high-end unmanned systems in aerial warfare. All research has resulted in the same outcome. Unmanned systems are not capable of replacing manned fighter jets. They only work to reinforce them. In practice, this means that in the future, nations will continue to intensively work on developing their next-generation fighter jets."

According to Elo, multirole fighter jet development is moving alongside high-end unmanned systems, allowing decisions and use of fire to still be controlled by humans. 

"High-end unmanned systems which are currently in development will increase the firepower of F-35s and will diversify their capability to undertake different tasks."
