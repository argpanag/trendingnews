Arsenal return to Highbury after 20 years for Carabao Cup clash

TheTools · 27.10.2026 16:39 · source · permalink

Mikel Arteta’s side are preparing for an unusual away trip, with a piece of Arsenal history adding an extra layer of intrigue.

For the first time in over 20 years, Arsenal are returning to Highbury in Tuesday’s Carabao Cup clash against League Two team Fleetwood Town.

For Gunners fans of a certain vintage, Arsenal Stadium, popularly known as Highbury after the London district in which it was located, will forever be the club’s real home.

Between September 1913 and May 2006, the current Premier League champions played at Highbury, famous for its striking Art Deco architecture, famously tight and immaculate pitch, and iconic clock.

Much to the dismay of many connected with the club, Arsenal began building a new stadium, the Emirates Stadium, in 2004 after attempts to expand Highbury were blocked, causing a reduction in capacity and less matchday revenue.

At the time of its closure, Highbury had a capacity of under 38,500, while almost 61,000 fans can fill the Emirates.

Arsenal’s other Highbury

On Tuesday, Arsenal won’t, however, be returning to their old ground, which has been redeveloped as a luxury apartment complex known as Highbury Square. The complex retains the shape of the stadium, while the East and West Stands, which are Grade II-listed buildings, remained as part of the development. In the United Kingdom, Grade II is a status granted to structures of special architectural or historic interest that are legally protected under statutory heritage lists.

Instead, Mikel Arteta’s team will be visiting Highbury Stadium in Fleetwood, which is not quite as old as the ‘other’ Highbury and definitely not as big.

Opened in 1939, Fleetwood’s Highbury has a capacity of a little over 5,300, which means the Gunners’ Premier League superstars will be out of their comfort zones. The ground is named after Highbury Avenue, one of its adjacent streets.

Fleetwood’s very different Highbury

Fleetwood, who are based in the northwest of England, were promoted to the English Football League for the first time in their history in 2012, starting in League Two, England’s fourth tier.

They spent 10 seasons in League One, the third tier, between 2014 and 2024 but are now in their third year back in League Two.

The Fishermen, as they are nicknamed, are playing in the fourth round of the Carabao Cup for the first time and have never faced Arsenal before.
