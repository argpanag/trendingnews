  4. A sombre mood among Republicans - and a muted Trumppublished at 08:35 GMT
    08:35 GMT

    Bernd Debusmann Jr
    White House reporter in Washington DC

    It's hard to overstate how upset many Republicans are feeling in the wake of what has turned into a stunning defeat for the party at the polls.

    Most Republican acquaintances and contacts I spoke to during the day yesterday expected losses, but were cautiously optimistic that perhaps they'd be able to maintain the House of Representatives and eke out victories in races that they expected to be tight.

    "This was worse than I thought," one Republican staffer texted me not long ago. "It's a disaster."

    The coming hours and days, another Republican contact told me, will involve "a lot of soul searching" to determine what went wrong.

    A surprisingly muted President Trump earlier laid the blame squarely on the candidates themselves, even if he acknowledged that high petrol prices played a role.

    But inside the party, some senior figures are reportedly also already pinning the blame on officials that pushed Trump to go to war in Iran. Others have suggested Trump is to blame for his party's woes.

    "The president said going into in that the election was going to be a referendum on him [and] act like he's on the ballot," conservative commentator Ben Shapiro said on his podcast. "I think that, you know, the president was kind of on the ballot."

    Trump has no public events on his schedule for Wednesday, although it's likely we will hear from him on Truth Social or through further media interviews.

  5. Ron Johnson: 'Probably true' that Trump hurt Republican midterm candidatespublished at 08:21 GMT
    08:21 GMT

    Republican Senator Ron Johnson, who has represented Wisconsin since 2011 and was not up for re-election in the midterms, has described the evening for his party as "not a good one, not by a long shot".

    He tells Radio 4's Today programme that the war in Iran, and particularly the resulting surge in fuel costs, continue to "weigh heavily on people's minds".

    Asked if Donald Trump is responsible for the Republican Party's losses, Johnson says midterms are "always going to be a referendum on the incumbent administration".

    "President Trump did tell the electorate to pretend his name was on the ballot - apparently they took him at his word and this has been the result," Johnson adds.

    Will the results of the midterms have consequences for the Kremlin?
