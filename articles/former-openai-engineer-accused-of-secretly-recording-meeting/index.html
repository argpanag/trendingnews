Former OpenAI engineer accused of secretly recording meetings may have obtained trade secrets

TheTools · 24.11.2026 17:17 · source · permalink

AI-translated article with transliterated names. Read original Chinese article here.

US prosecutors said a former engineer accused of planting a listening device in an executive meeting room at artificial intelligence company OpenAI had likely obtained company trade secrets, including confidential documents concerning a strategic partnership between OpenAI and another company.

Bloomberg reported that US prosecutors filed documents in federal court on Monday (23 November), asking a judge to reject the latest bail application by Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi Arabian former OpenAI engineer. Prosecutors said the investigation was ongoing, but they feared he might flee before trial, given his relatively weak ties to the US and considerable resources and connections in Saudi Arabia.

Prosecutors said investigators had found hundreds of audio files on Alfozan’s personal computer, including recordings of dozens of internal OpenAI meetings or conversations in September this year. Many may involve trade secrets.

Investigators found that Alfozan appeared to have carefully organised the recordings and some artificial intelligence-generated transcripts by date, renaming the recordings according to their contents. He is also believed to have uploaded some recordings to the cloud-based file-sharing platform Dropbox.

Prosecutors also found several photographs on his phone. One appeared to show a term sheet for a strategic partnership between OpenAI and another company, marked “confidential”.

Prosecutors noted that while Alfozan was secretly recording, organising and transcribing meetings covering matters such as OpenAI’s partnerships and technological capabilities, he was also in frequent contact with several people linked to the Saudi government.

Alfozan, who holds a US green card, denies stealing OpenAI’s trade secrets for Saudi Arabia and has pleaded not guilty to wiretapping charges.

His lawyers said in a court filing last Friday (20 November) that Alfozan had no special or secret ties to the Saudi government and had actively cooperated with investigators throughout, including allowing them to examine his phone.

Alfozan joined OpenAI in July 2025. In October this year, OpenAI employees discovered a sophisticated recording device behind a television in an executive meeting room. Alfozan was subsequently arrested and dismissed by the company that same day.

Alfozan is seeking release on US$1 million (about S$1.29 million) bail. A hearing is scheduled for Tuesday (24 November) in San Francisco.
