Antonelli - the boy 'born in a garage' who became world champion | Межа

TheTools · 01.11.2026 22:35 · source · permalink

Photo source: BBC Sport

Kimi Antonelli became F1’s youngest world champion after dominating the series in his second season. His father Marco first spotted his son’s potential when Kimi was 10.

This is an updated version of an article first published in August 2026.

This is reported by BBC.

Marco Antonelli was at the Adria circuit in north-east Italy with his Lamborghini sports car team while his 10-year-old son Kimi was competing in a karting race. At the end of the day, Marco, a former driver, asked him if he wanted to try the Lamborghini.

Marco strapped them both into the car, with the belts extended around them. He handled the throttle and brakes; Kimi controlled the rest.

“I couldn’t see the track because he was in front of me with a helmet on,” Marco tells BBC Sport. “I could only see beside me the marker panels before each corner – 100m, 50m. “We started to drive, step by step, faster, faster, faster. In the end, I pushed like I was alone and I arrived at the braking point at the end of the straight, 320km/h, braked very hard and at the limit. “The car had a lot of oversteer. Kimi controlled the oversteer perfectly, then I went on the throttle, Kimi finished the oversteer, changed gear, and went. “I said: ‘OK, stop, because it has now become dangerous.’ But when I saw the video, 10 years old, on my leg, oversteer, everything perfect, I said: ‘Maybe he has something special.’”

– Marco Antonelli

Kimi turned 20 in August. Now in his second F1 season, he has dominated the series and become its youngest world champion. His father says Kimi was “born in a garage”.

“Kimi from 15 days old was in the garage and heard the sound of the engine and the smell of the exhaust,” Marco says. He and his wife Veronica worked together on their racing team business.

When Kimi was about three, Marco brought home a simulator he had bought for guests to try at the Bologna Motor Show. Kimi wanted to use it every evening, sitting on his father’s leg with the controls divided between them as they had been in the Lamborghini seven years later.

“I remember one night I started to fall asleep because I had been at work very early and we had a crash because I didn’t brake when we arrived at the end of the straight,” Marco says. “And Kimi said: ‘Dad, dad, no sleep, no sleep. I have to drive.’”

– Marco Antonelli

Marco did not initially want his son to become a racing driver, having struggled to advance his own career. In the 1990s, he came close to becoming a works driver in the DTM touring car series, then a major hit with audiences across Europe. He tested for Alfa Romeo, but the team boss left and the opportunity disappeared. A later chance with BMW, alongside Roberto Ravaglia and Steve Soper, also fell through.

“Sometimes you are fast but not in the right position to become a professional driver,” Marco says. “I suffered a lot for it,” he says. “And, of course, I was scared that my son [would] relive the same situation. At the beginning, I was very stressed about the passion my son had for motorsport.”
