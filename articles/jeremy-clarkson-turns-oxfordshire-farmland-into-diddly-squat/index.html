Jeremy Clarkson Turns Oxfordshire Farmland Into Diddly Squat Farm

TheTools · 22.11.2026 21:11 · source · permalink

British television presenter Jeremy Clarkson transformed farmland near Chipping Norton, Oxfordshire, into Diddly Squat Farm after taking over its agricultural operations in 2019. He purchased the property in 2008 for a reported £4.25 million, initially leaving a local contractor responsible for cultivating the land before documenting his own farming experience in the Amazon Prime series Clarkson's Farm.

The property had previously formed part of the Sarsden estate and included Curdle Hill Farm. For approximately 11 years, a contractor from a nearby village cultivated barley, rapeseed and wheat before retiring in 2019.

Clarkson explained the original arrangement in an interview with Farmers Weekly journalist Johann Tasker, published June 29, 2021.

"I bought the farm in 2008 and it was contract farmed by a chap in the village," Clarkson said.

The purchase had first become public through Clarkson's Sunday Times column on November 8, 2009, in which he announced his acquisition.

"I have bought a farm," Clarkson wrote.

He also discussed his financial motivations for purchasing agricultural land in the column.

"Land is a better investment than any bank can offer," Clarkson wrote.

Another consideration involved inheritance and taxation.

"The Government doesn't get any of my money when I die," he wrote.

However, Clarkson also highlighted a more personal attraction to owning farmland.

"I can now have a quad bike. I have always loved the idea of such things," he wrote.

The reported purchase price remains disputed. The £4.25 million figure originated in a headline published by The Times in 2013, but other publications have provided different amounts and acquisition details.

The Daily Mail has associated the same £4.25 million price with a 2012 purchase of the 312-acre Curdle Hill Farm. Another account from the publication refers to Clarkson acquiring a 1,000-acre property in 2008. Woman & Home, citing the Daily Mail, gives a different figure of £4.45 million.

Consequently, 2008 remains the recurring acquisition year in accounts of Clarkson's ownership, while the exact purchase price has not been consistently established.

According to Countryfile, the property covers approximately 1,000 acres, equivalent to 400 hectares. Clarkson also described the farm's scale when explaining the television project's origins.

"And I thought we could do wheat, barley, [oilseed] rape on a perfectly straightforward, ordinary 1,000-acre farm. And that's where the idea came from," Clarkson said.

Clarkson said existing farming television programs influenced his approach because he believed they did not adequately represent everyday agricultural work.

"So I realised nobody had a clue what farming was really about," he said.

He wanted the series to distinguish itself from the comedic stunts associated with his previous television work on The Grand Tour.

"I wanted to show people who aren't farmers what farmers really do," Clarkson said.

The first season of Clarkson's Farm premiered in 2021, following Clarkson as local farmers and agricultural specialists introduced him to the practical challenges of running the property.

After taking responsibility for the operation, Clarkson named the property Diddly Squat, referring to its limited productivity. Local farm worker Kaleb Cooper, who appears in the series, had previously worked under the farm's former manager, Howard Pauling.

Today, the farm produces barley, rapeseed, wheat, potatoes and echium, while also harvesting blackberries. Its livestock includes sheep, pigs and chickens.

The farm shop sells vegetables grown on the property and sourdough bread made using flour produced from its own wheat. Clarkson established the shop after an unusually successful harvest left him with an unexpected surplus of 40 tonnes of potatoes.

He subsequently introduced beehives for honey production and expanded the shop's offerings to include apple juice and milk supplied by a local dairy. The business later expanded with the opening of a pub called The Farmer's Dog.
