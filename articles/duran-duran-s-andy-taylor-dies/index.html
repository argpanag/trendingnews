Duran Duran's Andy Taylor dies

TheTools · 25.11.2026 21:48 · source · permalink
  • Buddy Iahn
  • November 25, 2026
  • Pop

The guitarist passed away following prostate cancer

In a joint statement from his family and former Duran Duran bandmates, it was announced today (Wed, Nov 25th) that guitarist Andy Taylor, beloved husband, father, and grandfather, has died following a long and courageous battle with prostate cancer. He was 65. Andy passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by his family, on Monday, November 23rd.

“To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad, and the proudest grandfather. He was the rock of our family. For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first. Nothing made him happier than having us all together, and no matter how far music took him, his heart was always at home,” Taylor’s wife, Tracey, shares.

“Andy faced the last few years with so much courage and determination. He never gave up, fighting until the very end, never losing his love of life. The kindness and support shown to him by fans from all over the world throughout his illness brought him immense comfort and strength, and for that we will always be grateful.

“Andy knew just how deeply he was loved, and we take great comfort in that. We will miss him every single day.”

Duran Duran adds, “Andy was there at the very beginning of our story. From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music. His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.

“It was great to make music with him again when the original five reunited in the early 2000s, and that time together, both on stage and in the studio, meant a tremendous amount to all of us. When we were inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, Andy was too unwell to join us, but his words that night reminded everyone of his strength and the bond we shared.

“Andy faced his illness with remarkable courage, honesty and grace.

“Our thoughts are with his wife Tracey, his children Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew, and grandson Albie. They all loved him deeply and we ask that their privacy be respected at this very difficult time. In lieu of flowers, the family has asked that donations be made in Andy’s name to Prostate Cancer UK: prostatecanceruk.org.

“With love from Simon, John, Nick and Roger.”

Taylor publicly disclosed his stage 4 prostate cancer diagnosis in November 2022 via a letter read by frontman Simon Le Bon at Duran Duran’s Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction ceremony, which Taylor was too ill to attend. Despite undergoing targeted radiotherapy treatments that extended his life for several years, he succumbed to the illness.

Taylor joined the classic lineup in 1980, bringing a muscular, hard-rock edge that balanced Nick Rhodes’ synthesizers and John Taylor’s funk basslines. His guitar work anchored global hits across their first three studio albums—Duran Duran in 1981, Rio in 1982, and Seven and the Ragged Tiger in 1983—as well as the 1985 James Bond theme “A View to a Kill.”

Following Duran Duran’s initial 1985 hiatus, Taylor co-founded the rock-funk supergroup The Power Station with bassist John Taylor, singer Robert Palmer, and Chic drummer Tony Thompson, scoring major hits with “Some Like It Hot” and a cover of T. Rex’s “Get It On (Bang a Gong)”. He launched a solo career with Thunder in 1987 and worked extensively as a producer and session guitarist for artists including Rod Stewart and Belinda Carlisle.

Taylor reunited with Duran Duran’s original five-piece lineup in the early 2000s, recording the 2004 album Astronaut and touring worldwide before departing again in 2006. In 2008, he published his memoir, Wild Boy: My Life in Duran Duran.
