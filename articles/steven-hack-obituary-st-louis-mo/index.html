Steven Hack Obituary - St. Louis, MO

TheTools · 25.11.2026 22:19 · source · permalink

Steven Jay Hack, 68, passed away November 19, 2026, in Los Angeles. He was preceded in death by his parents, Wilbert and Claire Sue Hack and his brother, Rick. Steven was deeply loved and cherished by his family. A loving brother to Karen (Larry) Melnick, and the late Richard (Claire) Hack. Loving nephew of the late Beverly and Fred Stuhlman, Sheila Jacobson, the late Warren Jacobson, Ron (Peggy) Hack and Sam (Marilyn) Hack. Loving uncle to Katherine (Hank) Woodward, Michael (Amy) Melnick, Will (Corinne) Hack, and Autumn Hack. A loving great uncle to Mendel Hack, Ryan and Anna Woodward and Lincoln and Bowyn Melnick. Steven was also a dear nephew, cousin, friend, and colleague to many.

Steven was born in St. Louis, MO. Growing up he followed his big sister around learning tap dance moves and soon found his passion for dance and theatre. He performed in many productions at the JCCA, directed by Lynn Cohen. After graduating from Parkway North HS, he studied acting at California Institute of the Arts (CalArts) and the American Academy of Dramatic Arts (A.A.D.A.). When a chance to dance in the movie “Grease” presented itself in 1978, he was off to Hollywood. Steven can be seen doing back flips in the state fair scene with Olivia Newton John and John Travolta singing and dancing to “We Go Together!”

Steven moved to New York and made his Off Broadway debut in “Coolest Cat in Town.” He went on to appear in several Broadway productions, including being an original cast member of the Andrew Lloyd Webber musical “Cats” for five years and the Broadway and national touring productions of “A Chorus Line.” Steven and was with the company when it returned to Broadway for its celebratory and record-breaking 3,389th Broadway performance on September 29, 1983. Other credits include, “The Odd Couple” with Nathan Lane, “South Pacific” with Reba McEntire, “Paint Your Wagon” at the Geffen Theater and the Los Angeles production of “Counselor-at-Law” alongside John Rubinstein which received an LA Ovation Award. Theatre, especially musical theater, was Steven’s “happy place.” He was respected and loved in his theater community.

Following his Broadway career, Steven headed back to Los Angeles where he added many television and film credits to his already impressive resume. Comedies, dramas, sit-coms, horror, sci fi. He did it all. “Bones,” “American Horror Story,” “Criminal Minds,” “Perry Mason,” “Seinfeld,” “Amsterdam,” “Everybody Loves Raymond”, “General Hospital,” “Days of our Lives,” “Scrubs,” “Thor: The Dark World,” “Ratched,” “Fantastic 4” to name just a few. He was proud to have made the transition from being a Broadway song-and-dance guy to being a working film and television actor.

Steven loved that his dad, Willie, took videos of his performances all the way back to the JCCA and was so proud to have his parents see him perform on Broadway, prior to his dad’s passing. His autobiographical one-man stage show, “Willie’s Son,” to honor his late father, was nominated for both the Los Angeles Ovation Award and the Valley Theatre League’s ADA Award.

Steven was a life-long student. In addition to studying at CalArts and the American Academy of Dramatic Arts, he continued to take classes over the years to hone his craft. He was also a teacher, imparting practical business skills to creatives. In addition, he had a graphics design business to help fellow actors and was a wiz with Photoshop and other online tools.

Steven was a proud member of the Academy of Television Arts & Sciences and regularly attended screenings in Los Angeles. He was also a founding member of Interact Theatre Company and a member of Los Angeles’ Musical Theatre Guild.

Steven brought so much happiness into this world through his kind, giving, talented way. He was much beloved and admired both personally and professionally.

In announcing his death, Interact Theatre Company artistic director Barry Heins and executive director Walter Ware III wrote, “As we remember Steven, we celebrate not only his many contributions to our company, but also the joy of knowing and working with him. He will always hold a special place in Interact’s history and in the hearts of those who knew him.”

“Remember me with smiles and laughter, for if you remember me only with tears, you don’t remember me at all.” Love always, Steven.

A Graveside Funeral Service will be held Wednesday, December 2, 12 Noon at Chesed Shel Emeth Cemetery, 650 White Rd. Chesterfield, MO 63017.

Donations in Steven’s honor may be made to Broadway Cares/Equity Fights AIDS (BC/EFA) https://broadwaycares.org and the Entertainment Community Fund (formerly The Actors Fund).
