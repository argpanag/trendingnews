Small ball vs. Guards Ball when the Rays and Guardians meet in the ALCS

A pair of low-payroll teams with similar styles will face off in the AL Championship Series.

It’s small ball vs. “Guards Ball” when the Tampa Bay Rays and the Cleveland Guardians open their best-of-seven series Monday night at Tropicana Field.

Two-time All-Star right-hander Drew Rasmussen takes the mound for Tampa Bay after dominating the New York Yankees in the opener of the AL Division Series.

Lefty Foster Griffin will start for Cleveland, which worked out late Sunday after beating the Chicago White Sox in Game 5 of its ALDS.

The Rays won an AL-best 98 games in the regular season, swept New York in the ALDS and seek the third pennant in the franchise’s 29-season history.

Cleveland won 85 games, fell behind Chicago in the ALDS and became only the 11th team to overcome an 0-2 deficit in a best-of-five playoff series.

“I don’t think it’s really set in yet,” Guardians manager Stephen Vogt said. “But we’re here. The guys are excited. We’re prepared. We’re ready to go. We’re playing some really good baseball right now."

The Rays-Yankees matchup was David vs. Goliath in terms of payroll because New York opened the season with MLB’s third highest at $302.8 million and Tampa Bay ranked 26th at $90.5 million.

But the Rays are facing a team now that spent even less. The Guardians were last in the majors at $75.5 million.

Both teams emphasize contact at the plate, speed on the bases, strong fundamentals, solid pitching and doing whatever it takes to win.

Tampa Bay led the AL with a .258 batting average and had the fewest strikeouts in the majors. The Guardians only hit .239, didn’t have a hitter with more than 18 homers and had the fifth-fewest strikeouts in the league.

The two clubs bolstered their rosters at the trade deadline. Tampa Bay got starter Freddy Peralta, catcher Liam Hicks and reliever Tyler Wells.

Cleveland acquired Griffin, outfielder Jo Adell, and first baseman Nathaniel Lowe.

“I do think there’s more similarities than not,” Rays manager Kevin Cash said Sunday. “I feel Cleveland does mirror us or we mirror them. And I say that with the utmost respect to them, the way they develop pitching, the way they think about the game."

Rasmussen took a no-hit bid into the eighth inning of the ALDS opener, finishing with 10 strikeouts in a 1-0 victory. He was 16-5 with a 2.67 ERA and led the AL with a 0.91 WHIP. Rasmussen has been one of baseball’s best pitchers since an internal brace procedure in July 2023 that forced him to miss more than a year.

“I could really care less where people would rank me,” Rasmussen said. “I’m going to come in every single day, be as prepared as possible and I’m going to go out there and compete to the best of my abilities and do my best to execute and give our team the best chance to win.

Griffin was 3-3 with a 5.19 ERA in nine starts for Cleveland after the trade. The 31-year-old lefty was 12-3 went a 3.06 ERA with the Nationals after spending the previous three seasons with the Yomiuri Giants of Japan's Central League.

Griffin had seven strikeouts and gave up one run in 2 2/3 innings of relief in Game 3 against the White Sox.

“They got some slug for sure, and also just have some really good just baseball knowledge-type players: get a guy over less than two outs, knowing when to hit the sac fly, put the ball on the ground in certain situations," Griffin said. "Have some speed, as well. More of a complete team instead of just a 1-through-9, trying to hit you for a home run or slug."

Junior Caminero batted .276 with 42 homers and 101 RBIs and was 3 for 10 with one double and walked twice vs. the Yankees. Jonathan Aranda hit a career-best 22 homers in the regular season and was 4 for 12 in the ALDS with a homer in the opener.

Yandy Diaz (.293, 22, 85) and Chandler Simpson (.306, 48 steals) are integral parts of the lineup.

Chase DeLauter led the Guardians with a .287 average to go with 16 homers. Steven Kwan hit .282 and Angel Martinez led the team with 18 homers. Martinez wasn’t in the starting lineup in Game 5 vs. Chicago after going 0 for 15 and striking out eight times in the first four games. Jose Ramírez batted .235 in the regular season but homered twice against the White Sox, including a two-run drive in the 2-0 win in Game 5.

The Rays won their previous appearances in seven games, against Boston in 2008 and the Houston in 2020. They lost the World Series both times, to Philadelphia and the Los Angeles Dodgers.

Cleveland is 3-3, including a five-game series loss to the Yankees in 2024, and have not won the World Series since in 1948 — the longest drought in the major leagues.

One of the most memorable special teams performances in Jacksonville Jaguars history belongs to a 335-pound defensive tackle.

DaVon Hamilton stepped in for All-Pro long snapper Ross Matiscik on Sunday and delivered his best snap of the game on the final play, leading to Cam Little’s 57-yard field goal that gave Jacksonville a 19-17 victory against Philadelphia in London.

“He put it right on the button,” coach Liam Coen said. “I’m like, ‘Oh, man, here we go.’ Competitive greatness. Being at your best when your best is required.”

Hamilton was the unsung hero of an unlikely victory.

Matiscik injured his left shoulder early in the game, and the Jaguars turned to Hamilton, who practices snapping a few times every week. Hamilton snapped eight times against the Eagles — four punts, two extra points and two field goals.

Three of them were high, forcing 6-foot-5 punter Logan Cooke to make some impressive grabs and leading to one missed extra point. But Hamilton was money when it mattered most.

“Stepping up for your team and helping them win games, it’s a big moment,” Hamilton said. “Whatever I can do I’ll do it to help my team win games.”

Hamilton called it a fun experience and added that he’ll be fine if he never has to do it again.

The Jaguars (4-1) flew in veteran long snapper Jacob Bobenmoyer on Monday to replace Matiscik before Sunday’s game against Houston (1-4) at iconic Wembley Stadium. Bobenmoyer previously played for Las Vegas (2023-25) and Denver (2020-22).

“We’ll get somebody in here to be able to come in and immediately get those op times better and get the operation cleaner,” Coen said.

Hamilton, though, will be on standby.

A third-round draft pick in 2020, Hamilton stepped in for Matiscik last year at Tennessee but didn’t face nearly the same kind of pressure as he did against Philadelphia. The Jaguars beat the Titans comfortably, 25-3.

Jacksonville’s run defense is the best in the NFL. The Jaguars allowed 22 yards on the ground against Philadelphia a week after giving up 25 at Cincinnati. It marked the seventh time in the Super Bowl era that a team surrendered 25 yards or fewer in consecutive games and the first since Baltimore in 2019.

The Jaguars also became the second team in the Super Bowl era to not allow an opposing player to rush for 40 or more yards in its first five games, joining the 1969 Dallas Cowboys.

Penalties were a problem for the second consecutive week. The Jaguars were flagged 13 times for 100 yards against the Eagles, giving them 26 penalties for 225 yards over the last two games. They also had two offsetting penalties on the opening kickoff, which nullified a 90-yard touchdown and ended up with backup linebacker Bryan Thomas Jr. getting ejected.

Parker Washington bounced back from his worst game in more than a year with one of the best of his career. Washington caught six passes for 124 yards and two touchdowns. He finished with the second-most yards in his four NFL seasons.

Washington scored on a 51-yard catch and run in the first quarter and made a diving catch in the end zone with 3:26 to play that sparked Jacksonville’s comeback.

He has seven TDs in his past nine games, raising his value amid contract negotiations.

Jacksonville’s offensive line had its hands full trying to block Jalen Carter, who finished with two sacks and two forced fumbles. The unit also was flagged three times for holding – all on rookie guard Emmanuel Pregnon – and called for two false starts. Throw in four delay-of-game penalties, including one in which Lawrence expressed frustration with center Robert Hainsey, and it was a mostly forgettable afternoon for the position group.

Left tackle Anton Harrison had tests Monday on his injured right knee, and Coen said he’s “a little stiff.”

“We’ll have to be patient with if he’s not able to practice what that looks like and go from there,” Coen added. “We’ll have to see what the MRI looks like. We’re at the mercy of it right now.”

26 — Number of points Jacksonville allowed in the fourth quarter at Houston last year, resulting in a 36-29 loss. There’s little doubt it will come up this week.

Jaguars will try to beat Houston for just the fourth time in their past 17 meetings.
