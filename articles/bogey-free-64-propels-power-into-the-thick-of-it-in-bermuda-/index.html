Séamus Power cut a frustrated figure as he walked off the 18th green at Port Royal Golf Club on day one of the Butterfield Bermuda Championship, but less than 24 hours later he was all smiles as he rolled in a short birdie putt to take a share of the early lead.

After getting to seven-under through 14 in round one, a sloppy finish saw him find hazards on back-to-back holes and miss short birdie putts on the following two to card a five-under 66. He parred the opening three holes in round two after starting on the 10th, but then rolled in a 20-footer for birdie on the par-3 13th to open his birdie account.

Two more back-to-back on 17 and 18 got him to the turn at three-under, and then he made another pair of back-to-back birdies on three and four – the first, a 13-footer on the short par-3 and the second a 12-footer after landing a 150-yard approach just over the pin.

Clearly feeling it with the putter, he rolled in another from 14 feet on the par-4 sixth, and though he couldn’t find the hole from long range on either seven or eight, he cleaned up for par on both and then clipped a wedge to kick-in distance on the last to move to -12 and complete a bogey-free round that lifted to the top of the leaderboard with the afternoon wave yet to go out.

“Yeah, it was really good, and honestly it was one of those rounds that didn’t really get off to the best start,” he said. “I had long chances on 10, 11 and 12 and knocked in a really good putt after a good 5-iron on 13 [and that] kind of settled me down a little bit, and then [I] got it going around the turn.

“It was one of those rounds that was really good and it could have been better. So it was one of those funny days of golf, but overall anytime you’re bogey free, especially when it’s some breeze, a lot of good things have happened, and yeah, I feel very good after my first two days.”

Despite those couple of short misses at the tail-end of round one, Power has gained over five strokes on the field with the putter, and he feels that that’s been his biggest strength this season and if he can continue to roll the ball like he has been doing, then he thinks he’ll have a good chance over the weekend.

“Yeah, putting’s been strong all year,” he said. “I don’t know, I think I was maybe 11th on Tour coming into this week on strokes gained, so it’s been very good. I found something like towards the fall last year and really had to kind of carry on this year, and I’ve had some tournaments this year where it’s really gotten hot and it makes such a difference in golf.

All the way back to Torrey Pines the first two days, same kind of thing, you know, where everything is OK but then you knock in a bunch of putts and everything looks great.

“So it was a lot of that for the first two days. I probably didn’t hit it quite as well as I would have liked, but it’s pretty close and I feel pretty comfortable with my long game and then if you’re knocking in some putts you’ve always got a chance.”

Power’s last PGA Tour victory came at this course and tournament back in 2022, and in addition to that victory, he’s yet to miss a cut in seven appearances at Port Royal and contended on the final day in 2025 before a late slip saw him fall just outside the top 10.

Yeah, I’ve always enjoyed it. My first time coming here, I actually — honestly, I always tell people this, I thought it was much closer to the Caribbean so I was kind of blown away, it was a totally different vibe than I was expecting,” he laughed.

“It’s beautiful, the colours of the houses, the colour of the water, all that kind of stuff. I just kind of fell in love with the place pretty early on and always felt comfortable on the golf course for whatever reason. I’ve putted well here quite a few times, obviously continued that this week so far, and I think just feel comfortable with the place. Like the hotel, like the whole kind of vibe of the whole place, so it helps. You’re standing on the first tee and you just feel a little more comfortable than you do on other weeks.”

The two-time PGA Tour winner entered the week ranked 103rd in the FedExCup Fall Rankings, with the top 100 at the end of the season retaining full playing privileges, and though he’s managed to play close to a full schedule in 2026 with conditional status, he admits that regaining full status remains his number-one priority over the closing stretch of the season.

“This year I was in the 111-to-125 category, which ended up being fine – I think the only tournament I missed outside the elevated [events] was Phoenix. So played a full schedule, but it was the unknowns and not knowing where I was going to get in and when I was going to get in was the tough part.

“So for me, trying to get back into that top-100, get yourself back to full status, and then I just feel like you’re in a better more comfortable spot starting off in January. So like that’s going to be the big goal.

“Obviously always trying to get a win, but this time of year, I mean the win kind of takes care of both. So that top-100 is really going to be a big thing. And a couple finishes have been great so far, pushed me a lot further away from that 125 and inside that — obviously you still have the 125, 110 and 100, but the way I’m playing I really feel like I’m good enough to be in the top-100, so I just have to put the results on paper.”
