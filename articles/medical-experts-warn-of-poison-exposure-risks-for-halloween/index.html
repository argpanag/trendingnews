Medical experts warn of poison exposure risks for Halloween

TheTools · 28.10.2026 18:09 · source · permalink

ROCHESTER, N.Y. - All of the fun Halloween costumes, festive decor and tasty treats can create more risks for children. The Upstate New York Poison Center says 35% of its cases involve kids 5 years old or younger.

"There's lots that we can do for hosting parties or going to parties, prior to trick-or-treating, to keep our family safe. If people are using glow sticks or glow products, be sure to remind smaller children to not put them in their mouths. And should a glow product burst and there is some exposure to mouth or eyes, you can call us at the Upstate New York Poison Center," said Mary Beth Dreyer, health education program manager for the Upstate New York Poison Center.

Dreyer says glowsticks, face paint, button batteries, medication and cannabis are all poison concerns around Halloween.

"If people are using face paint, I'd suggest taking a look at it. Reading the label. Be sure it's not expired," Dreyer said. "Consider using it, or testing it out, on a smaller patch of skin to be sure that there's not a skin reaction. And again, keep it away from young children's eyes, unless it's specifically made for eyes, like eye makeup.

"There are also things like button batteries that are in products this time of year. Families are using those lights instead of a candle in their pumpkin, or they’re an accessory for a costume that has a button battery, or just a decoration that has a button battery in it. Be sure that it's secure in that product. And then the loose or the spare button batteries, store those up and out of sight and reach of smaller children."

Dreyer has tips for Halloween lovers looking to host a gathering this season, too.

"So many poisons can be prevented with proper storage and proper use. Being sure to read labels, reading directions. If someone's coming to your house, give them a place to store their bags or their purse that's up and out of reach of smaller children that are curious. Most poisoning exposures can be prevented," she said. "Any time you have medicine, either over-the-counter or prescription medicine in your house, cannabis in your house, alcohol in their house, regardless of if it's Halloween or not, it's a good idea to keep those up, stored, locked, and safe, for unintentional exposures."

Potential symptoms of an exposure can vary, depending on the person, product, how it’s exposed, and the gravity of the exposure. The American Red Cross says some symptoms could include dizziness, trouble breathing, abnormal skin color and maybe a strange smell on someone’s breath. Opened or spilled containers are a clear sign to look out for, too.

However, with children, family knows them best. Even if kids are not verbally expressing something’s wrong, don’t hesitate to reach out for guidance if you’re worried there’s been a potential exposure.

"Even if they think a poison exposure happened, you don't have to wait for a symptom to appear. But if they think or suspect that a small children ate something that they shouldn't have, they can call us with any questions," Dreyer said.

The Upstate New York Poison Center can be reached at 1-800-222-1222.
