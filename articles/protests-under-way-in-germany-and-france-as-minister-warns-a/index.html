320,000 attend protests in France, according to government

Turnout at the protests in France against the cost of living stood at 320,000 nationwide, the interior ministry said, lower than the 410,000 seen the previous Saturday and 550,000 on October 17 when riots erupted across the country.

Demonstrations were largely peaceful through the afternoon, though police fired tear gas in Lyon, France’s third-largest city, and Toulouse in the southwest, while tension also broke out at the end of the march in Marseille, Reuters reported.

Police cancel march in Cologne due to masked protesters

Police in the German city of Cologne have called off the march organised by Gen Z regelt due to some protesters wearing masks and throwing smoke bombs.

“The demonstration will continue as a stationary rally,” a police spokesperson told German DPA news agency.

France interior ministry says 15,000 people taking part in protests

The French interior ministry said about 15,000 people were taking part in demonstrations across the country, adding that the situation remained “generally calm”.

The protests were unfolding “without any notable incidents”, the ministry added.

Police had made 27 arrests by 2pm local time, AFP reported.

So far, police in Germany have reported 2,700 protesters in Berlin, with 1,800 people participating in Munich, and about 1,000 in Frankfurt. Police in Cologne have not provided figures but said it was “significantly” lower than the 15,000 registered by organisers, according to the German newspaper Die Welt.
