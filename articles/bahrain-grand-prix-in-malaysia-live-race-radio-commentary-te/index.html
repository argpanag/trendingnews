Summary

  • Verstappen wins ahead of Antonelli and Hamilton following several overtakes for cars off start as result of divergent tyre choices

  • Russell retires late in race from third, impacting title race

  • Race start delayed by more than hour and a half after heavy rain and confusion over grid order

  • Cars out of position during attempts to form behind safety car because of reported software issues

  • Original grid line-up used for restart

  • Two safety cars following Bottas and Albon retirements

  • Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia held at Sepang circuit near Kuala Lumpur

  • Race rescheduled from April because of Middle East unrest

    The top three are in the cooldown, chatting about this incident-packed grand prix. Max Verstappen says he was surprised to see his rivals on slick tyres at the start, adding it didn't look that wet but there was "so much water on the tarmac".

    It's a shame Sepang won't be on the Formula 1 calendar full time, but it's been a great one-off appearance for Malaysia.

  3. Verstappen wins in Sepangpublished at 11:35 BST
    11:35 BST

    The crowd are serenading race winner Max Verstappen, as Lewis Hamilton gives a hug to his Ferrari team waiting to congratulated him. The seven-time world champion went started on the front row in second, fell all the way down to 17th on his slick tyres but made his way through the field to claim a spot on the podium.

    Charles Leclerc is fourth, Isack Hadjar has to settle for fifth and Oscar Piastri, who didn't get the luck with the change from the virtual safety car to a full safety car at the end, is sixth.

    Liam Lawson is back in the points in seventh and Fernando Alonso will be the toast of Aston Martin in eighth.

    World champion Lando Norris is ninth, but the McLaren driver is under investigation for driving erratically for that moment he went off the track when the safety car was ending.

    Rookie Arvid Lindblad is our final points scorer in 10th, so double points for Racing Bulls.
