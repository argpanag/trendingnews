Wheel Talk Newsletter: In it for the vibes

TheTools · 10.11.2026 19:17 · source · permalink

Members Only

Who is your Tour de France Femmes avec Zwift dream team?

There's no Giro d'Italia route to talk about this week and hardly any women's road cycling news, but it's all popping off on the cyclocross side, thanks to the Marie Schreiber/Lucinda Brand drama at the European Championships. Jonny already covered that in Spin Cycle yesterday, but I will say that I feel for Schreiber. Her road season didn't go to plan, and it would have been the biggest victory of her career (so far).

As one Wheel Talk Discord member pointed out, had the jury followed the opinion of the "wronged rider" (Brand) herself, Schreiber would not have been relegated. Of course, that's not how these things work, but it was the most sullen version of Lucinda we've seen on the top step of a podium. Sidenote: still can't believe Lidl-Trek opened the door for her to leave ... rookie move by management.

First things first: We haven't forgotten about you, Picnic-PostNL

A few weeks ago, on the Wheel Talk podcast, we discussed the situation at Picnic-PostNL and the news that 71 riders and staff had anonymously reported a culture of fear and mismanagement at the Dutch team. In our discussion, Loren Rowney pointed out posts that Molly Weaver, a former rider for the team, wrote back in 2020, after she left the team for Trek-Drops, only to retire in 2018 due to the lasting damage from the Dutch squad she had been a part of for nearly three seasons.

I was able to dig up one of Molly's stories from the old place.

When the news broke at the end of this season, Weaver took to Instagram to say she had called attention to this six years ago to crickets. Only one other former rider has come forward publicly since the story went public: Esmée Peperkamp. But now, Ellen van Dijk has some words to say.

Now, the reason I mentioned our conversation on the Wheel Talk podcast is that we specifically called out the reaction of John Degenkolb. The German rider said that, in both of his stints at the team, he never experienced any treatment in line with the reports.

"Why were voices like mine – and those of many others – left out of the investigation?" he said. "I respect your point of view ... but there are a lot of people who had a different experience than you."

On the podcast, we explained why we disagreed with the timing and statement Degenkolb had, and explained the harm his words have on those who did experience different treatment than him.

Van Dijk, it seems, had a similar experience to Degenkolb while on the team, but in an interview with Cyclingnews, she expressed her situation in a way that didn't minimize how the riders around her may have been treated.

"They have a strong opinion on how to work with riders, and they really believe in their approach. And for them, it's only that way that works," Van Dijk told Cyclingnews.

"There's a lot written about an anxiety culture, and I never experienced it that way. The management wanted you to listen to them, but I was never scared of them. I mean, if I wanted to say something, I would say it. But maybe if you're a younger rider, you can experience that.

"I just didn't experience the anxiety they are writing about, and for me, that's a that's an important nuance to it. Maybe some people did experience that, but I didn’t."

We're still waiting for something to happen within the organization to answer the claims made by former riders and staff, but if team boss Iwan Spekenbrink's latest statements are any indication, it doesn't seem like change is going to happen any time soon (again see Spin Cycle).

One thing we do know: the team only has nine riders signed for next season, all returning with previous contracts. No new signings have been announced yet, and now that it's approaching mid-November, the silence is loud.

Demi Vollering's 2027 will look similar to this year

In an interview, FDJ United-Suez director Lars Boom hinted at Vollering's 2027 schedule and goals, stating that it would look quite similar to that of 2026.

"Well, I think the Tour de France again. And defending her world title," Boom said.

"She will ride some Classics and maybe the Giro again. It's a bit obvious, of course, but I think the Tour and the World Championships will be major milestones again."

It hardly warrants a line in the newsletter, because of course the Tour and Worlds will be her biggest aims. Whether or not she does the Giro is an interesting question mark, given the challenging nature of the parcours.

Did we do a good job with this story?
