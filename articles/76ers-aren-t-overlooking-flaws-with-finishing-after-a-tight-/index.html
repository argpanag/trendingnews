76ers aren't overlooking flaws with finishing after a tight win vs Bucks

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:11 · source · permalink

A win is a win in the NBA. It's hard to find reasons to complain after getting a victory, especially when that dub pushes a team to tie the league's best record, but the Philadelphia 76ers have plenty to fix after Saturday's action against the Milwaukee Bucks.

Welcoming a 1-15 Bucks team to South Philly, the Sixers knew that a loss would make them the laughingstock of the weekend. Being at the top of the Eastern Conference and holding a "superteam" label this season, every shortcoming is under a microscope.

You can bet that squeezing by the one-win Bucks will be another stain that NBA fans discuss, but the 76ers have to be real with themselves after that. Fortunately, it sounds like the head coach, Nick Nurse, isn't looking past it. Following the 132-129 victory, Nurse acknowledged that a win is a win in the NBA, but he dropped an honest assessment to follow.

The Sixers need to be better

"We got to finish a little better," Nurse told reporters, while revealing his message to the team after beating the Bucks. "Building a couple double-digit leads and giving them back a couple times…We should not have let ourselves be in a position to have it go either way.”

You could say the Sixers stole the comeback from the Bucks. Because although Philadelphia managed to finish on top, a missed call by the officials when Joel Embiid clearly stepped out of bounds while it was a one-possession game should have given Milwaukee a chance to get out in front with seconds left to go.

Instead, Embiid went to the line to improve the score to 132-129. The Bucks managed to get the ball to Tyler Herro, who attempted a three, which he made, but a quick replay review revealed that Herro didn't get the shot off in time. The 76ers collected another win.

With a 14-3 record, the 76ers know they still have a lot to work on. Slow starts have them playing second halves digging their way out of early holes. Then, in games like Saturday's, they can't successfully pull away with a big lead without it being a nail-biter, even against the Eastern Conference's worst team.

Outside of simply saying a win is a win, the biggest positive takeaway is the fact that the 76ers aren't saying that first part without keeping it real with themselves. They have to finish better.
