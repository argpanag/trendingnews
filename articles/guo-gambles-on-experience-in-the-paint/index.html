Guo gambles on experience in the paint

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:55 · source · permalink

Without the towering presence of NBA center Yang Hansen, China will have to lean on its veterans against two tough opponents in the Middle East as it attempts to keep its qualification hopes alive for the 2027 FIBA World Cup.

As its qualification journey for next year's FIBA showpiece resumes this week with a doubleheader on the road, the Chinese national team will certainly miss the giant influence of Yang on both ends of the floor when it takes on Saudi Arabia on Thursday at a neutral venue in Doha, and then Lebanon on Monday in Zouk Mikael, a town located 14 kilometers to the north of Beirut.

With Yang busy in the NBA with the Portland Trail Blazers, Team China will have to rely on its returning pillars in the paint, Zhou Qi and Wang Zhelin, two veteran centers who have both been absent from the national roster for some time due to their respective physical issues.

However, they've been called up by head coach Guo Shiqiang ahead of the critical qualifiers, both of which leave little room for error.

Currently sitting on a 5-3 win-loss record, two straight wins over the Saudis and Cedars would keep Team China safely above the top-three qualification cut in Group F, leaving coach Guo's men needing only one more victory in February's closing window at home — against the Cup host Qatar and the Saudis again — to be sure of place at next year's 32-team FIBA marquee tournament, where all the Asian teams will be fiercely vying for the single direct qualification berth at the LA28 Olympics.

The margin is as thin as just one game, though.

After eight games prior to this week, China's neighboring rival South Korea, also a traditional powerhouse at the continental level, trails Guo's team in fourth place in the group with a 4-4 record — separated by a single win — and will edge China out should they eventually finish tied on the same record, thanks to its head-to-head advantage.

Team China's vulnerability in terms of mental strength and perimeter offense, especially when facing moments of extreme pressure in high-stakes games, has already been exploited by the Koreans in its opening two losses last year and remains a concern, even with Zhou and Wang back guarding in the paint.

The lack of finesses and depth on the back court, where 31-year-old, injury-plagued captain Zhao Jiwei remains the only reliable playmaker, and the team's inconsistent 3-point shooting, which allows opponents to stack defenders under the rim, neutralizing China's advantage in size, are pressing issues that coach Guo has to address in the game against the bottom-ranked Saudis before challenging the group's current runner-up Lebanon in front of its wild home crowd.

The collective strength of Team China, anchored by its big men, will be the key to prevailing, said Guo.

"We studied closely how Saudi Arabia and Lebanon play, sharpened the details on both ends of the floor, got everyone on the same page tactically and built up team spirit," said Guo, who's been under fire with critics questioning his tactics after losing to Korea twice and Japan once at home in July.

"We will focus on playing to our style and making the most of our advantages. I hope the players can stay calm, take care of the details and leave everything on the floor in both games."

Without Yang, who helped clinch an 89-87 home win in the dying seconds against Lebanon with a buzzer-beating layup on Aug 31, Guo's tactics of running plays from inside out are expected to be drawn around veteran Zhou, a former NBA player with the Houston Rockets.

The 2.16-meter Beijing Ducks rebounder and rim protector has rejuvenated his game this CBA season, contributing 12.5 points and 7.4 rebounds on average for the three-time league champion so far this term, currently ranking him second among all domestic centers in both stats.

Zhou will be itching to prove his worth on the continental stage, after having suffered back-to-back losses on national duty in his two previous appearances against Korea last year.

Wang, a two-time CBA MVP and the backbone of the Shanghai Sharks 2025-26 championship roster, is back in a Team China jersey for the first time since the 2023 World Cup in Manila, where the squad failed to secure Asia's only direct qualification spot to Paris 2024.

With renewed confidence and improved fitness, Wang, who tops the domestic center rankings in rebounds (9.8) and efficiency (17) in the current CBA season, is expected to help Zhou stay fresh as an equally strong backup.

The Saudis, however, have their own response in the form of "Big Mo" — the versatile 2.08-meter center Mohammed Alsuwailem — and prolific guard Muhammad-Ali Abdur-Rahkman, who led all players as the 2025 Asia Cup's top scorer.

Even being towered over by the twin presence of Zhou and Wang, Alsuwailem's explosive power shouldn't be underrated, as he delivered 31 points and 15 rebounds for an efficiency of 42 in Saudi Arabia's 72-85 near-upset of Korea in the last window.

Having averaged 19.9 points per game to lead the Saudi barrage, Abdur-Rahkman, who currently plays with the Kawasaki Brave Thunders in Japan's B League, is expected to come out all guns blazing as his squad looks to revive its dying hopes of reaching basketball's biggest tournament, being held in the Middle East for the first time.

Coach Guo, however, remains confident in his team's drive to keep the winning streak going and to be able to face whatever challenges present themselves on Thursday.

"We will go all out for two wins and fight for a place at the World Cup, and show the spirit and fight of Chinese basketball," he said.
