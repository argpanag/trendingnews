Joseph Zada Spent a Month Watching “Everything Woody Harrelson Has Ever Done” to Prepare for ‘Hunger Games’ Role

TheTools · 17.11.2026 20:39 · source · permalink

Zada appared alongside 'Hunger Games' stars past and present at the film's NYC premiere.

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping took over New York City on Monday, bringing together casts old and new.

Original franchise stars Jennifer Lawrence, Josh Hutcherson, Woody Harrelson and Elizabeth Banks walked the carpet alongside Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes stars Rachel Zegler and Tom Blyth, along with newcomers Joseph Zada, Mckenna Grace, Whitney Peak, Elle Fanning, Glenn Close, Ralph Fiennes, Jesse Plemons, Kieran Culkin and Maya Hawke.

Sunrise on the Reaping follows the 50th edition of the Games and is centered on a 16-year-old Haymitch Abernathy who is portrayed by Harrelson in the central films. Zada takes on the younger version of Haymitch, and explained to The Hollywood Reporter how much Harrelson and his signature mannerisms impacted his own performance.

“It was kind of like a base layer; I spent a month when all that I did was look at everything Woody has ever done,” Zada told The Hollywood Reporter, “and past that, it was just up to me to bring myself to it because that’s what’s going to be interesting and what you want to see on the screen: the true light of yourself and bringing out all those aspects of me that align with the character.”

Sunrise on the Reaping, the fifth novel in Suzanne Collins’ bestselling series, is also known to be the darkest and most brutal, with Hawke emphasizing, “This story is devastating. The hope that you feel from this story is what you know the future is going to hold. The story itself is not overly hopeful; it’s gut-wrenching.”

As for how they navigated shaking off that material when leaving set, Zada admitted, “I didn’t even try, and I should have tried harder but I didn’t because I just didn’t want to. I wanted to give this every single inch of myself and push myself to the next level, and I’m so glad I did because it paid off and I’m so proud of the film.” Peak, whose character has a romance with Haymitch before he’s drawn into the Games, added, “Joe and I were definitely trying to keep it a little bit lighter between takes just so you can relieve your nervous system a little bit. It’s kind of tough to stay in that place for too long.”

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping hits theaters on Friday.
