“PlayStation 5 gets Jailbroken: Sony in Further Trouble? – The Quad: Student News Service of WCU

TheTools · 25.10.2026 13:04 · source · permalink

ByKelly Baker

Image Credit: Martin Katler(martinkatler) via Unsplash

PlayStation has not had a good year in the court of public opinion, and to make matters even worse for them, some within the gaming public have managed to effectively “jailbreak” their most recent console, the PlayStation 5.

Jailbreaking, to put it in terms that most readers should be able to understand, is like buying a new car from a dealership. You own the car, but the manufacturer designed and built the vehicle, and you can only get repairs done by their official mechanics. However, you could take the new car to a reliable and knowledgeable independent mechanic who could modify the car’s engine, rims, stereo systems, etc. That said, doing so would void the warranty and might be illegal depending on what you install.

Similarly, whenever you buy a gaming console, you own the console, but you can only play the games made for that particular console, and you can only buy games and other applications directly from the company— at least digitally. By jailbreaking a console like the PlayStation 5, you bypass any digital restrictions and can install whatever games or applications you want onto the system.

For as long as gaming consoles have existed, gamers have been modifying them to suit their specific wants. Even if this kind of modification is illegal under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (Title 17, Section 1201), hackers have regardless been trying to crack into the PS5’s systems for years. However, a substantial breakthrough in recent days has given Sony (which founded and owns PlayStation) considerable pause for thought.

On Tuesday, Sept. 30, “Relapse” was posted to the internet via GitHub (a website where developers can store, manage and collaborate on source code), which can be used to jailbreak both the PS5 and the PS5 Pro, up to firmware v13.60. Meaning, if any PS5 or PS5 Pro was not updated to firmware v14.00, the exploit can work.

As Kyle Orland, writing for ArsTechnica, stated in an article: “The so-called Relapse exploit makes use of a long-known WebKit vulnerability in the PS5’s hard-to-access web browser, escalating from there to gain write access to the PS5 kernel and install an ELF loader to streamline the running of arbitrary code. The new method is also a lot more efficient than previous PS5 jailbreak exploits, which could hang the system for 50 minutes per attempt.”

With this recent breakthrough, it has now become easier to jailbreak any PS5 console, rather than trying to find a console that hasn’t been updated in a long time. What’s more, people are now reportedly able to play PlayStation 3 and PlayStation 2 games on the jailbroken PS5 consoles with the use of emulators (legal software that emulates gaming hardware), and even non-PlayStation games.

Again, all of this is still illegal under the DMCA, but considering how tricky it can be to enforce some laws like the DMCA, coupled with human boldness and curiosity and the demand for free games, a small community has continued to push the envelope of what is possible with every game console. This has left companies like Nintendo and Sony to play the part of a gardener pulling up weeds; send a cease-and-desist letter to one group and have the official game console developers issue a new firmware update, only for the cycle to start again with another person trying to jailbreak the updated console.

To rub salt in the wound of Sony’s newest problem, another exploit has come forward called “Overdrive,” an application for newly jailbroken PS5s to download free games and downloadable content onto the console. As Sam Sepiol wrote for Insider Gaming: “The listings for these titles do not display prices; instead, players can quickly see the size of the game and its review rating.” To PlayStation’s credit, they seemed to have caught onto this problem relatively quickly, as Overdrive cannot be accessed at the time of writing. Adam Corsetti, writing for NotebookCheck, stated as much: “The program’s GitHub page also now returns a 404 error. It’s not known what or who prompted the shutdown, but Sony Interactive Entertainment is the likely party.” Unfortunately for Sony, allegedly other clones of Overdrive have since cropped up.

This breakthrough in the realm of jailbreaking gaming consoles doesn’t exist in a vacuum, however, as the rise of AI tools has helped expedite the process. Games such as Grand Theft Auto V, initially released all the back in 2013 for the PS3 and Xbox 360, was able to be played via WebAssembly (a tool to run intensive software inside of a web browser) reportedly with the help of AI, even though Rockstar Games quickly took it down.

Irrespective of whatever the immediate future holds for PlayStation and Sony, the genie cannot be put back into the bottle.
