Medicine overuse : Remedy lies in a combination of efforts - The Tribune

THE Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation’s advisory to curb the indiscriminate and repeated use of painkillers and antibiotics signals the scale of the public health crisis. Curative medicines — to be used only when necessary and under medical supervision — are routinely taken either preventively or for the mildest of infections that the human body can naturally handle. The overuse and misuse of antibiotics place India at the centre of the global antimicrobial resistance crisis. There is an acute lack of awareness of how such practices can contribute to kidney-related complications and other adverse drug reactions. Easy over-the-counter access only partly explains the self-medication culture. Malpractices such as overprescribing and overcharging have fostered a general distrust in the medical system. Financial constraints, cultural beliefs and the unchecked role of chemists acting as informal medical practitioners further compound the problem.

THE TRIBUNE, India’s oldest, daily English-language newspaper, was first published on February 2, 1881, in Lahore (now in Pakistan), and save for 40 days in the immediate aftermath of Partition, has come out every day over the last 145 years. The newspaper is run by a five-member Trust, which is chaired by Shri N N Vohra, former Governor of J&K State (2008-2018); as well as Justice S S Sodhi, former Chief Justice of the Allahabad High Court; Shri Gurbachan Jagat, former Governor of Manipur; Lt Gen. Shamsher Singh Mehta, former Western Army Commander; Shri Paramjit Singh Patwalia, Senior Advocate in the Supreme Court.

THE TRIBUNE is free, objective, and independent. Restraint and moderation, rather than agitational language, are the hallmarks of the paper.

The Tribune has two sister publications, Punjabi Tribune (in Punjabi) and Dainik Tribune (in Hindi).
