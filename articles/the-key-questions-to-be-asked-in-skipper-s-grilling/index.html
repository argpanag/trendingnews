All times AEDT

9.30am | Pat Cummins to speak to media in Sydney

2.30pm | Australia v England ODI

How other teams have ball-tampered

Cummins was asked about what other ball tampering techniques he had heard.

"I've heard superglue on branding and the keepers gloves, I've heard nail polish, layers and layers of it to make it super hard, the Murray Mints …

"I heard some claims a long time ago sand paper was taped between your fingers. These are discussions that have probably happened in every suburban change room as well."

He then clarified he never personally saw them happening, but has heard strong stories of other international teams doing it.

He said the vagueness surrounding what's allowed under the rules in terms of what's allowed and what's not to shine the ball leaves wiggle room.

"In saying that, when you see sandpaper on the screen, that definitely crosses the line."

We’ve also now ticked over the hour mark. Incredible.

‘The whole scale got lost’ in sanctions

It feels like we’re starting to wind down now, which is unsurprising given we’re now more than 50 minutes into the presser.

He's just spoken more about the bans.

“There was a lot of lawlessness going on in that series … it was a crazy series, and I’m glad we haven’t had one since,” he said.

“And (ball tampering) was prevalent. You hear stories of other teams doing it, and when there were instances of people getting caught, it was a slap on the wrist – a small fine, demerit points, whatever.

"I think the whole scale got lost. It's my personal opinion, but their bans were way over the top."

Becky’s advice before this presser

"Be honest and tell the details, and put your washing away," was the advice she gave to him this morning.

“I’ve been with Becky since well before that tour. She’s seen everything. She lives it all with me. She’s just saying ‘show your emotion’ because there are a lot of people hurting.”

How it’s possible the bowlers wouldn’t know

Cummins just gave a really good insight for non-cricket folk about how it’s possible the bowling unit wouldn’t know about ball-tampering.

"It's actually not the bowlers, it's mostly the batters who work on the ball," he said.

"If the ball gets wet, it won't reverse. We're running around getting sweaty, so we don't touch it.

"You see the keeper take it, he gives it to first slip, who throws it all the way to mid-off and they work on it, then give it straight to the bowler."

‘He’s not naïve’

Despite being forced to speak today, he's still backed Khawaja for telling his story.

"He's not naïve, he knew this was going to be a hot topic," he said.

"I respect everyone's right to tell their story, 100 per cent go for it. I'm glad people feel like they can -- it's their story to tell.

"But I hope this is put a line under. We've been consistent. We dealt with this at the time … we had to defend ourselves in 2021, we're defending ourselves now.

"If we have to do this every time … it's pretty ridiculous."

Cummins earlier said he would stand there for as long as it took for all questions to be answered, hoping to not have to ever speak about it again.

We're already at the 40 minute mark.

The ‘most serious’ claim Cummins has taken exception to

Khawaja claimed to be Aussie skipper, you have to be a certain skin colour.

Cummins took particular exception to that.

"Of all things I've read this week, I think that's the most serious," he said.

"To think the job I hold is quarantined from people based on skin colour, that's a big statement. To say that, I think you've got to have evidence. I just don't think that's the case.

“I agree with Uzzie on a lot of things, but when I disagree, I’ll tell him. Him saying to be Australian captain you have to be a certain skin colour, I couldn’t disagree more.”

‘Usman knew more’

Cummins has given the strongest indication he believes Khawaja should have been involved in the investigation and interviewed.

"It looks like it's been sitting heavy on him for a while. It's eight and a half years," he said.

"Again, he's sitting next to Bancroft, so it sounds like he knew a lot more than others in the change room.

"We all took part in that investigation at the time, we told them everything, absolutely everything. An open book.

"So in some ways, maybe that was for us, part of the process to help get over it, and maybe this is his chance (because) he never actually fully got over it at the time."

On video of argument with wife

Cummins was just asked about a video published by The Australian of an argument between him and wife Becky.

"It's half comical. Becky wears her heart on her sleeve … I mean it's all a bit ridiculous, isn't it?

“Like we’re here talking about something that was dealt with eight and a half years ago. Now I’m walking my daughter, one-year-old daughter in a nappy out the front, and I’ve got cameras chasing me and my family? That’s a bit ridiculous.”

Cummins wasn’t a fan of ‘ugly’ culture

Cummins said he wasn't a fan of the culture within the Australian side when he was coming through the ranks.

“I hadn’t played a lot of cricket up until that stage. I was in and out, played a little bit of white ball cricket and hadn’t played a lot of Test cricket,” he said.

"But my memories of coming back into that side, there were parts that I didn't really think was OK. I thought the way we acted on the field was quite abrasive.

"I didn't like some of the way we spoke to umpires, the opposition. I think there was a bit of an arrogance and a bit of an ugliness at times that just didn't really sit well with me as a young guy.

"Now that I'm captain, I've spent a lot of time making sure that's not the case with this current Aussie team, and I think we've been absolutely brilliant since 2018."

Cummins addressing the media in Sydney. Getty Images

Punishments ‘way too heavy-handed’

Cummins also believes the sanctions dished to Smith, Warner and Bancroft were "way too heavy-handed".

"I think the intentions might be sound, and if we're talking about how they were sanctioned and the justice, my personal opinion is it was way too heavy-handed. It was way over the top. I think that's OK to say.

"But to then extrapolate that out into everyone else knew … that's a huge extrapolation and very different to what I think was injustice."
