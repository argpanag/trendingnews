Shnaider battles past Eala for second straight week to make Tokyo semis

TheTools · 30.10.2026 11:25 · source · permalink

In the home stretch of 2026, Diana Shnaider and Alexandra Eala have gifted fans by kicking off what promises to be an electric rivalry between two of the most talented up-and-comers on the WTA Tour Driven by Mercedes-Benz.

It's advantage Shnaider after two thrilling battles in as many weeks. The second round of the BNB Ningbo Open saw the 22-year-old edge a top-quality 7-5, 6-4 tilt. A week later, the pair delivered an even greater blockbuster in the Toray Pan Pacific Open Tennis quarterfinals -- and despite being two points from defeat in the second-set tiebreak, Shnaider came out on top again, advancing 2-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 in 2 hours and 52 minutes.

The pair combined for 88 winners off the ground -- 45 for Eala and 43 for Shnaider -- and both tallied positive ratios of winners to unforced errors. Shnaider's game was increasingly watertight as the match went on: of her 27 unforced errors, only two came in the third set.

"This match deserved to be a final," said Shnaider in her on-court interview. "It was an insane level of tennis from both of us. I didn't believe I would be able to pull that win off, because she played insane. I was just trying to remind myself to keep fighting for every point, and that paid off."

Shnaider advances to her fifth semifinal of 2026, following Adelaide, Roland Garros, Washington and Ningbo. She'll face another fast-rising youngster, 18-year-old Slovak wild card Mia Pohankova. The 2025 Wimbledon junior champion is ranked No. 144 and contesting just her fourth WTA main draw this week, but she backed up her second-round upset of Naomi Osaka by defeating qualifier Alycia Parks 7-6(3), 6-3. Pohankova extended her positive record against Top 100 opposition to 10-6.

Turning points: Eala delivered an opening set of sustained brilliance after falling behind 0-40 in her first service game. She saved all three, setting the tone for the match: the 21-year-old would fend off the first eight break points she faced, and despite repeated pressure was only broken once.

From 1-0 down, Eala ran through five straight games in a blitz of winners -- 14 in total -- from all corners of the court: stab volleys, angled groundstrokes, precise passes and her signature pummeled returns. Appropriately enough, she sealed the set with a forehand winner crosscourt.

Secure your seats for the WTA Finals

After a season-long race, the world's top players converge in the California desert with the WTA's biggest title still to be won.

Improved serving enabled Shnaider to stay with Eala through the second set: having won just 52% of her first-serve points in the opener, she raised that number to 71%. But she was unable to take four break point opportunities at 2-1 and 4-3 -- and at 4-4, Eala appeared on the brink of victory as she struck a terrific backhand winner for a 15-40 lead on Shnaider's serve.

But that game would ultimately tip the momentum in Shnaider's favor. The five-time WTA titlist navigated six deuces and saved four break points -- the first two courtesy of netted Eala forehands, the next two with her own winners. Shnaider ultimately extricated herself from the game with a backhand one-two punch -- and from this juncture, it was Eala doing the chasing.

In the ensuing tiebreak, Eala managed to peg Shnaider back from 5-3 to 5-5 -- but, two points from victory, double faulted. Shnaider sealed the set with an ace, and took her momentum into the decider.

At 1-1 in the third set, Shnaider finally managed to capture the Eala serve -- though she had to fire six clean winners in a single game to do so, denying Eala from 40-0 up and sealing the key break with a return winner. Eala continued to pose a threat, but Shnaider was able to pass the test, coming through tight holds in her final two service games and sealing victory on her first match point with another ace.
