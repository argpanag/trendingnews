Italian Grand Prix: Pierre Gasly beats George Russell to stunning Monza pole

TheTools · 05.09.2026 16:47 · source · permalink

Pierre Gasly's only grand prix victory came at Monza in 2020

  • Published
    5 September 2026, 16:15 BST

Alpine driver Pierre Gasly took a stunning pole position at the Italian Grand Prix.

The Frenchman, whose sole Formula 1 victory came at this race for Red Bull's junior team in 2020, beat Mercedes' George Russell by 0.060 seconds.

McLaren's Oscar Piastri was third with the Ferraris of Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton fourth and fifth despite an upgraded engine.

Red Bull's Max Verstappen took sixth, and McLaren's Lando Norris only ninth after a messy session for the world champion.

Championship leader Kimi Antonelli of Mercedes qualified seventh but will start at the back because of a grid penalty for taking a new engine.

Piastri may lose his third place because he is under investigation for impeding Red Bull's Liam Lawson, which could result in a three-place grid penalty.

Gasly's pole comes a day after he lost his third place from the Monaco Grand Prix because the FIA Court of Appeal reinstated a penalty he was given at the race in June.

Saturday's unexpected performance makes him the first Frenchman to take pole for a grand prix since Jean Alesi for Benetton at this race in 1997.

The result is a hiccup for Russell, who has a golden opportunity to make up ground on team-mate Antonelli, who has a 30-place grid penalty.

Antonelli was employed for much of the session in front of Russell to give him a tow, so to be beaten by an Alpine and be only 0.12secs ahead of Piastri was a disappointment.

Russell said he started his final lap too close to Verstappen, who let Antonellli by before the start of it to avoid running in clean air.

"Going into Parabolica, Max just slammed on the brakes and didn't want to be the lead car," Russell said. "In hindsight, it would probably have been better not trying this, but P2 is a good place to start."

Alpine put a large upgrade on their car at the last race in the Netherlands, where Gasly could qualify only 11th. This result came out of nowhere.

The 30-year-old said: "It was amazing. Thank you to everyone. It was an incredible qualifying. From the first lap in Q1, I felt very good in the car, trying to chip away a bit every single session.

"It felt like a very good lap. It has been nine years trying to chase a pole position and to take my first win here and then my first pole here is amazing."

Russell, who had been a strong favourite for pole in the light of Antonelli's penalty, said: "Firstly, massive congrats to Pierre because that's an incredible performance.

"We were finishing a game of chess 15 minutes before qualifying and he beat me there and I thought, 'well, I will beat him in qualifying.' But here we are. I am disappointed not to be on pole but Pierre was very fast from Q1."

Piastri's result is a boost for the Australian, after a difficult few races, and it came with a time he set on his first flying lap.

He was leading the pack around, with Norris behind him, at the start of the final runs, not an ideal position given the importance of a tow at Monza, and locked up into the first chicane, going straight on into the run-off area.

"The first lap felt quite good," the Australian said. "I tried to see if anyone wanted to go in front out of the pit lane but no-one did.

"It was always going to be difficult. I tried to be as late as possible into the first chicane and locked up so that was the session."

Piastri's mistake also hampered Norris. Now without a slipstream, he was 0.4secs down at the first sector and bled time through the lap. "Without a slipstream, with our straight-line deficiency, it's hard to put something good together," said the Briton.

Ferrari had looked set to be in the mix at the front before qualifying with their new engine that was believed to be worth about 0.1secs per lap.

But the session did not go their way.

Hamilton made it through into the final session by only 0.001secs as Audi's Gabriel Bortoleto just failed to displace him, and let out an expletive over the radio when he saw the narrow escape.

And the red cars were just not fast enough in the final session. Leclerc was fourth on both his runs, while Hamilton, ninth on his first run, improved on his second but ended up 0.007secs behind his team-mate.

Gasly says pole 'exceeded all our expectations'

The top three in qualifying were all powered by Mercedes engines

Pole is an extraordinary story for Gasly, who lives in nearby Milan and spoke in Italian to the crowd in the trackside interviews afterwards.

He has laboured in F1's midfield throughout his career, even when he had his big chance at the Red Bull team in 2019. He was dropped back to the junior team, then called Toro Rosso, after half a season, and has been trying to rebuild his career ever since.

A move to Renault-owned Alpine in 2023 offered him a chance at a factory team, but they have struggled badly through most of his time with the team, and finished last in 2025.

Executive adviser Flavio Briatore, who has caused controversy off track this weekend, persuaded Renault to curtail their F1 engine programme at the end of last year and switch to customer Mercedes power-units.

The move, combined with switching off any effort on their 2025 car so as to be as well prepared as possible for this year's new rules, vaulted them towards the head of the midfield.

A strong start to the season was followed by them falling backwards somewhat as others upgraded their cars.

The new parts in Zandvoort did not have an especially auspicious debut, but Gasly has looked strong all weekend. Even so, not even they thought they were in a position to take a pole on merit in Monza.

"It exceeded all our expectations," Gasly said.
