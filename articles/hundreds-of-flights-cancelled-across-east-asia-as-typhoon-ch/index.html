Hundreds of flights cancelled across East Asia as Typhoon Champi roars toward Taiwan

TheTools · 23.10.2026 14:48 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Taiwan is bracing for powerful Typhoon Champi, which is passing close to the island's east coast with torrential rains and gusts of up to 191 kph (119mph)

  • Taiwanese authorities have urged people to stay away from coasts and mountains, and are evacuating residents on the north-east coast living near a landslide dam at risk of breaching in the storm

  • Hundreds of flights in Taiwan and neighbouring Japan have been cancelled, affecting thousands of passengers

  • At least one person has died in Taiwan after being swept away by waves, according to authorities

  • Champi is the latest in a string of intense typhoons as a Super El Niño heats up the Pacific Ocean and fuels storms

  7. What's in a typhoon name?published at 07:45 BST
    07:45 BST

    Typhoons get their names from a list compiled by the World Meteorological Organization, an agency of the UN.

    The names are submitted by member countries.

    Some, like Dolphin, are in English. Some are in the local language, like Chan-hom (a Laotian tree), Peilou (a migratory bird in Macau) and Nangka (the Malay name for jackfruit).

    Countries like the Philippines give typhoons their own local names because of the sheer number of storms that affect the country every year.

    Filipino meteorologists are referring to Typhoon Champi as Rosal - the local name for the gardenia plant.

    Typhoon names can be recycled for different storms, but are usually retired after they cause serious destruction, like Typhoon Haiyan (or Yolanda in the Philippines), which killed more than 6,000 people in 2013; or Typhoon Fung-wong (Uwan in the Philippines), which killed dozens when it struck the Philippines and Taiwan last November.

  9. What does El Niño have to do with these typhoons?published at 06:59 BST
    06:59 BST

    Earlier we reported that this has been an unusually hectic typhoon season for the region.

    But it's not just typhoons. Recent months have seen a range of extreme weather events around the world.

    • The eastern Pacific is seeing above average hurricane activity
    • Indonesia has been battling its worst wildfire season in more than a decade, as its forests become drier and hotter
    • Reduced monsoon rainfall is threatening agriculture in India

    Much of this is because of an exceptionally strong El Niño - a naturally occurring event that happens once every few years and lasts for around a year.

    During this period, trade winds in the Pacific Ocean can weaken or reverse, allowing warm water to spread eastwards.

    The heat from the ocean lifts global temperatures and changes rainfall patterns.

    The UK Met Office said this is shaping up to be the "largest El Niño in living memory". This means 2027 is likely going to be the world's hottest year on record, the agency said.

  10. While landfall is unclear, hazards remainpublished at 06:41 BST
    06:41 BST

    Weather forecasters may be unsure if Champi will make landfall in Taiwan, but the hazards from the storm, one of the strongest on the planet this year, remain.

    Taiwan's weather agency forecasts that up to 90cm (35in) will fall from Thursday to Saturday, with the north-east coast expected to be the hardest-hit.

    "People should be aware that the strong winds and extremely heavy rain will continue into the first half of tomorrow (Saturday)," Central Weather Administration administrator Lu Kuo-chen says.

    A typhoon has technically made landfall when its centre or eye moves from the sea onto land, according to Taiwan's weather agency.

    Across the Asia region, however, many typhoons have caused widespread damage even when the eye has not made landfall.

    Storms that skirt coastlines are capable of stirring torrential rains and strong winds that cause floods and landslides.

  11. Will Typhoon Champi make landfall in Taiwan?published at 06:24 BST
    06:24 BST

    We reported earlier that Taiwanese authorities were warning people to stay alert as the island braced for Typhoon Champi to make landfall.

    But for now, it remains unclear if the storm will make landfall in Taiwan or just brush past the island's coast as it heads northwards to Japan.

    Forecasts by the meteorological agencies in Taiwan and Japan show that the eye of the storm is expected to skirt past Taiwan's northeastern tip without making landfall.

    But a forecaster from Taiwan's Central Weather Administration also told Reuters that they could not rule out landfall, if the storm "stalls, loops, or wobbles from side to side" as it approaches Taiwan.

  12. Champi batters the Philippines' northern islandspublished at 06:10 BST
    06:10 BST

    As Champi moves closer to Taiwan, it is also stirring heavy rains and strong winds in Batanes, a Philippine island chain located between southern Taiwan and Luzon, the Philippines' main northern island.

    The second-highest in a five-step wind warning system has been raised in the Batanes islands. This means 150km/h winds are expected.

    Photos posted on the local government's Facebook page showed waves crashing into seaside villages and roads closed to traffic. There were no immediate reports of casualties.

    While it is affected by many of the 20 or so tropical storms in the Philippines every year, communities in Batanes are reputed to be more resilient against inclement weather. Many homes are made of thick limestone, engineered to withstand typhoon winds.

    Champi has hovered in waters north-east of the Philippines for days. It is known locally as Typhoon Rosal.

  13. Champi was this year's strongest stormpublished at 05:41 BST
    05:41 BST

    A few days ago, Typhoon Champi officially became the strongest storm of the year.

    On Tuesday afternoon, it attained a maximum sustained wind speed of 185mph (298km/h) as it moved over the Philippine sea - the equivalent of a Category 5 hurricane.

    Typhoon Champi was initially categorised as a "super typhoon", which is when a cyclone's sustained wind speed reaches 240 km/h(150 mph).

    The typhoon appeared to weaken on Wednesday as it continued travelling through the sea.

    But it remains powerful, carrying sustained winds of 155km/h (96mph) near its centre around noon on Friday, according to Taiwan's weather authorities.
