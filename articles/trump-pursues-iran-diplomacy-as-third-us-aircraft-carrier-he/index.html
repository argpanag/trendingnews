Trump pursues Iran diplomacy as third US aircraft carrier heads toward region

TheTools · 09.11.2026 14:42 · source · permalink

NAPLES, Italy — President Donald Trump was slated to meet with Qatar’s prime minister Monday as diplomatic efforts to end the war with Iran continue, even as he leaves open the possibility of crippling military strikes.

Meanwhile, another U.S. aircraft carrier is on its way to the region, where two others are already deployed. USS Theodore Roosevelt departed Singapore on Sunday, transiting the Strait of Malacca toward the Middle East, according to ship spotters.

Trump is scheduled to meet privately with Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani, a key mediator in efforts to end the Iran war.

Vice President JD Vance also is scheduled to meet separately with Al-Thani, who also is Qatar’s foreign minister, according to multiple reports.

Qatar has played a central role in keeping communication between Washington and Tehran open as direct peace talks have stalled, with the war nearing its ninth month.

Fresh off heavy midterm election losses driven in part by opposition to the unpopular war, Trump faces mounting pressure to find a way forward in Iran, even as he weighs renewed strikes and diplomatic proposals being advanced through Qatar and other regional mediators.

On Sunday, Trump again signaled his preference for a diplomatic solution, saying there wasn’t “any reason” to escalate the war with Iran.

“They’re withering away as a country, and they’ll come and they’ll make a deal,” Trump said on the CBS News show “60 Minutes.”

He added that Iran was close to making a deal before the recent U.S. midterm elections, which he said had given Tehran the false impression that domestic opposition to the war would force Washington to make concessions.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio also cautioned against optimism on the negotiating front, saying the countries are not on the verge of a breakthrough.

“Iranian demands are simply not just unacceptable but, in some cases, outrageous and ridiculous and really not realistic,” Rubio said Sunday shortly before leaving for a Group of Seven foreign ministers meeting in Paris.

In regards to the U.S. carrier presence in the Middle East, USS George H.W. Bush and Japan-based USS George Washington have been on duty there since April and August, respectively.

It’s uncertain whether the U.S. will keep three carriers in the region or Roosevelt will relieve one of the others.

The ship’s carrier strike group includes Carrier Air Wing 11, the cruiser USS Chosin, the destroyers USS Paul Hamilton and USS Decatur and more than 5,000 service members.

In addition to Bush and Washington and embarked carrier air wings, at least 15 other Navy warships are operating in the Middle East. All told, there are some 50,000 U.S. forces in the region, according to U.S. Central Command.

Last month, the Defense Department reportedly sent a Patriot missile defense system, renewing concerns that U.S. and Israeli forces were preparing for a large-scale attack against Iran in the coming weeks.

Transfer of the Patriot system is among several recent indicators that the U.S. may be preparing for renewed strikes against Iran.

On Saturday, CENTCOM announced the formation of Task Force Iron Gate, saying it is designed to further cripple the Iranian economy and choke off supplies of weapons, dual-use technologies and the like across Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea.

The group “will further constrain Iran’s ability to finance terrorism, rebuild weapons factories, and replenish their military,” CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said in a statement.

Last month, Cooper said the U.S. maritime blockade in the Arabian Sea had prevented Iran from exporting at least $25 billion in crude oil, which is typically used to finance military operations, proxy groups and terrorism.
