NBA power rankings 2026-27: Championship Tiers, with Knicks, Spurs Thunder in top three spots

TheTools · 12.10.2026 11:30 · source · permalink

The NBC Sports weekly NBA Power Rankings are back, bigger and better than ever. This preseason edition of the rankings puts teams into championship tiers, broken down this way:

• Title Contenders. This is self-explanatory. These are highly talented teams that don’t need things to go perfectly to win it all.
• Second Circle Contenders. Teams that could make a run to the conference finals or NBA Finals, but need to answer bigger questions and/or have no margin for error.
• Playoff teams. Teams poised to have a good season and make the NBA playoffs but are not a threat to win it all.
• Play-in teams. Teams that could make the playoffs but likely have to come out of the play-in first.
• Zone of Shame. These are the teams trying to avoid the bottom three worst records (and the lottery punishments that now come with them). The NBA calls that bottom three the “Relegation Zone,” which is a terrible name — nobody is getting relegated. The Cleveland Charge and Maine Celtics won’t be promoted from the G League to play in the NBA next season. So I’m borrowing a phrase from the great Tom Ziller of Good Morning Basketball and calling it the Zone of Shame. I’m open to other ideas, that’s just the best one I heard.

Final note: I know more than 16 teams are listed as playoff teams and more than 20 are at play-in level or above, this ranking is more about potential and expectations than a hard count. That comes much later in the season.

New York has 10 of its top 12 rotation players back from the team that just won 53 games and an NBA title — they are the most complete team in the East, and the one with the fewest questions. The Knicks kicked the Karl-Anthony Towns/Josh Hart contract extension drama down the road, although Towns wasn’t a fan of that move. Knicks management saw it as a distraction, and now it’s been put off. New York was a good regular season team a season ago (+6.4 net rating, fifth-best in the league) that found a magical groove in the playoffs. Don’t be shocked if we get the good team, but not the magical one, for long stretches of the regular season. Watch the Knicks get their rings and see the first banner in 53 years go up to the rafters in Madison Square Garden on NBC and Peacock on opening night (taking on the 76ers).

In the preseason opener, Mitch Johnson started De’Aaron Fox, Stephon Castle and Dylan Harper together, moving Julian Champagnie to the bench. That’s the kind of thing to try in the preseason, but it might carry over to the regular season. Those three make a powerful defensive force (especially with Victor Wembanyama behind them), but do they have enough shooting, and will the second unit lose too much shot creation? It may not last, but keeping those three guards happy and dialed in is a legit concern for a motivated team coming off an NBA Finals loss. Big game right out of the gate on opening night, the final game in the NBC and Peacock triple-header to tip off the season (9:30 p.m. ET start).

We know how good this team’s starting five is, especially with Jalen Williams back and healthy, but what might really scare teams is how strong the second unit has looked in preseason, led by Jared McCain and Ajay Mitchell. Those two are lighting teams up with movement and scoring. Also worth noting, rookie big Aday Mara has looked pretty good in the preseason, and while he won’t get much run on a deep team like this, if (really, when) they need him for a stretch, he looks like a guy who can help.

A lot of things have to go right — and Joel Embiid’s knees have to hold up — for this team to be a true contender, but I’m giving them the benefit of the doubt to start the season. That said, don’t be surprised if this team gets off to a relatively slow start. We’ve seen this before when “superteams” were assembled: When LeBron James and Chris Bosh joined the Heat in 2010, that team started 9-8 while it found its bearings, but went on to win 58 games and reach the Finals. My prediction for the 76ers: Somewhere between December and February, there will be a four-week stretch where they will look like title contenders, but physically they won’t be able to hold up when it matters most.

Cleveland is going to win a lot of games and it wouldn’t be the least bit surprising if it is the No. 1 seed in the East at the end of the regular season. That is not how the Cavaliers’ season will be judged. After four consecutive years of uninspiring playoff exits — including being swept by the Knicks last May — this feels like a make-or-break season in Cleveland. That said, they locked up Donovan Mitchell and James Harden, then filled the hole at the three with Peyton Watson, so Cleveland can largely run this back for a few years.

The most interesting development in the preseason has been Ausar Thompson maybe developing a bit of a jump shot. He’s not suddenly trying to be a Splash Brother (think more DeMar DeRozan), but in his preseason debut, he went 3-of-3 from beyond 8 feet, including a 20-foot pull-up jumper. Then, on Saturday, he was 0-of-5 in the midrange. A working jumper matters because in the playoffs teams sagged off him and packed the paint (making life even more difficult for Jalen Duren). If Thompson can’t space the floor, it becomes increasingly difficult to play him and Duren together in the playoffs. Hit some mid-range shots, and that spacing suddenly looks better. Also, John Collins is a solid addition, but this team is going to miss everything Tobias Harris brought to the table.

It’s tough to draw too much of a correlation between a couple of preseason games played in an arena next to a casino on another continent and a regular season NBA game, but the Rockets looked promising in their NBA China games. Fred VanVleet is back and looked comfortable, scoring 17 points and adding seven assists in the opener, while much of the offense still ran through Alperen Sengun, who had a triple-double in 22 minutes. Houston remains the team I think is most likely to challenge the Spurs and Thunder in the West.

JJ Reddick may have had to walk this back politically, but he was correct when he said, “I think with this group, probably the first time in my three years here, that I feel like the pieces actually fit together.” This is the best roster they’ve had to maximize Luka Dončić’s style of play. This team should be an elite offense and win a lot of games, but their ceiling ultimately depends on Walker Kessler living up to that contract and becoming a defensive force (expect much more drop coverage and much less switching from the Lakers). Also, this preseason, Ziaire Williams has been a key on-ball defender, a role he could thrive in, plus Jake LaRavia and Sandro Mamukelashvili have looked good.

I am higher on a bounce-back year from Indiana than many pundits. The offense has looked dramatically better and has had a flow, even with a rusty Tyrese Haliburton back in the preseason. It’s a very positive sign. Haliburton has a Steve Nash-like impact on a team and an offense because of his pace and style of play, this team was built to maximize that. However, like all the teams at this level in this ranking, the Pacers have questions to answer. For one, how Haliburton and Ivica Zubac mesh will be one thing to watch early in the season.

This ranking feels a little low for a Boston team that is going to win a lot of regular season games, but this first ranking is focused on title contention, and I don’t believe these Celtics, as constructed, are as serious a postseason threat (I am higher on the potential of the Pacers and Rockets). That said, just having Jayson Tatum back on the court will be welcome, he is a joy to watch. Plus, this team will defend, has depth and athleticism, and will be disciplined under reigning Coach of the Year Joe Mazzulla.

A huge key to the Timberwolves living up to LaMelo Ball-fueled hype is getting a big season from Jaden McDaniels. Particularly on offense. It’s only a couple of preseason games, but he’s played with confidence, and that’s very promising. This preseason the team has lived up to the hype of being incredibly entertaining, Jonathan Kuminga has been fitting in, and Rudy Gobert is adjusting to catching more lobs. A lot of people think this team could challenge the Thunder/Spurs dominance, if it all comes together, they might.

Any team with Nikola Jokić has to be considered a potential serious playoff threat, and Denver’s potential starting/closing five of Jokić, Jamal Murray, Aaron Gordon, Cameron Johnson and Christian Braun is very good — if everyone can stay healthy and on the floor. Particularly Gordon. DeMar DeRozan will provide some scoring punch off the bench, but depth is a genuine concern. That said, if the core five is healthy come the playoffs, the Nuggets are a threat because of Jokić.

Giannis Antetokounmpo is praising Heat Culture and has fully bought in, and when you put the league’s best downhill player in an uptempo offense, good things happen. Miami is going to be a good defensive team, but questions about depth and how the offense looks when the game slows down in the clutch (and playoffs) are legitimate. The Heat will need to prove they deserve a higher ranking, and opening-week games against Minnesota and Detroit can help with that.

This ranking may be too low for the Raptors — if Kawhi Leonard stays healthy (similar to last season), this is a team that could (and maybe should) be a top-four team in the East. I personally am optimistic about their season and think they will climb from this ranking. The only reason they are not there right now is that I have to see it, and I need to see Jakob Poeltl stay healthy for a season. This will be an impressive defensive team, with Scottie Barnes and Leonard on the wing, not to mention Jamal Shead at guard.

Atlanta will be a fearsome defensive team after trading for Lu Dort to come off the bench, joining a roster that already has Dyson Daniels and Nickeil Alexander-Walker on the perimeter. That defense gives them an identity. They have an All-NBA level player in Jalen Johnson to lead the offense. Most importantly, there is a real sense of possibility and opportunity around a team that was tied to — and their ceiling capped by — Trae Young for so many years. That should excite the fan base.

This is a make-or-break-it-up season in Orlando — this team has the third-highest payroll in the NBA and nothing to show for it (this franchise hasn’t made it out of the first round of the playoffs since the animated version of “How to Train Your Dragon” hit theaters). Orlando is running it back, betting on better health from Paolo Banchero, Franz Wagner and Jalen Suggs, and that new coach Sean Sweeney can make all the puzzle pieces fit.

This ranking is based on two things: How good Kyrie Irving looked in his one game in China and Cooper Flagg’s expected growth in his second season. I also like some of their pickups, such as Santi Aldama, drafting Morez Johnson Jr., and taking a flier on a still-young Zaccharie Risacher. That said, there are still a lot of questions to answer, starting with how coach Dusty May will adjust to the NBA game after college.

Count me among the people excited for this Jazz season — I am picking Darryn Peterson to win Rookie of the Year, and I think Utah is going to be a playoff team in the West. But they have a lot to prove. I expect big years from Lauri Markkanen and Jaren Jackson Jr., who flew a little under the radar last season. That said, the loss of Jusuf Nurkic at center to start the season is rough as this team really lacks depth up front (Jackson Jr. may not love playing the five a lot, but will have to early).

The Suns built a new, Dillon Brooks-inspired culture last season and caught the league off guard by playing harder and caring more about each possession than their opponents. I’m less sold that they can replicate that, despite Devon Booker’s greatness. The Suns need Miles Bridges to hit his 3s at a higher rate, and while I like the young front line of Khaman Maluach and Oso Ighodaro, a lot is going to be asked of them. This is a solid team but one poised for a small step back.

Trae Young will get a lot of attention for his passing — he had eight assists in the first quarter in a preseason game against the Pistons over the weekend — but watch them and what stands out is the passing among the big men of Anthony Davis, AJ Dybantsa and Alex Sarr. This is going to be a very entertaining team to watch, how much that leads to winning remains to be seen, but the Wizards will be a League Pass favorite this season.

It’s just good to see Damian Lillard back on an NBA court again — and in Portland (it always felt off to see him in Bucks green). What looked promising this preseason is how well Deni Avdija seemed to fit and play off the Lillard-and-Ja Morant backcourt, and Scoot Henderson had his moments, too. Making that guard rotation work — don’t forget about Jrue Holiday — is new coach Micah Nori, who deserved a shot (and not to instantly be on a lame-duck contract that undercuts his authority). This ranking may be low for a Portland team that will be fun to watch.

It feels like a new era in Chicago. Caleb Wilson is the young star, Matas Buzelis is coming into his own as the potential No. 2 on this team (maybe 1A this season), and there will be plenty of Josh Giddey. There is a lot of changeover. All that change is welcomed and needed, but can lead to a slow start to the season. Add to that Nic Claxton’s hamstring injury, and there are legitimate concerns about the defense. Still, there are reasons for hope in the Windy City, which is a welcome change of pace

The season hasn’t even started and the Clippers are racked by injuries: Brandon Ingram (Achilles), Max Strus (foot), Yanic Konan Niederhauser (foot) and Jordan Miller (shoulder) — and that is not including Bradley Beal, who is closer to a return and may be ready by the start of the season (the others won’t). The Clippers have an interesting young backcourt with Darius Garland and Keaton Wagler, plus some solid veterans in Derrick Jones Jr., Rui Hachimura and Brook Lopez. In a deep West, it’s tough to see this team’s path to a lot of wins.

The Warriors have locked up Stephen Curry, he isn’t going to play anywhere else, and that will keep the fans — and the $$$ — flowing in. They also locked up Brandin Podziemski on a deal that feels like a bit of an overpay to me, but isn’t outrageous. Yaxel Lendeborg has impressed in the preseason. Both Draymond Green and Kristaps Porziņģis are under contract, although Porziņģis is away from the team with a health issue and nobody knows when he will return. That’s kind of the story with the Warriors: When Jimmy Butler gets healthy midseason and if Porziņģis is around, this team can win some games. But how much of that version of the Warriors are we going to see?

This is a bit of a low ranking for an interesting team — I like what the Grizzlies are building with a front line of Cameron Boozer, Cedric Coward and Zach Edey (with Isaiah Stewart off the bench). Boozer is everything that was advertised and more. The Grizzlies have looked good at moments this preseason, and starting Jaylen Wells makes sense for spacing. That said, I’m not sure this team will win a lot of games in a deep West, but we might be sleeping on them as a play-in team.

Trading away fan favorite LaMelo Ball was a bet against Ball, but also a bigger bet on Brandon Miller and Kon Knueppel becoming stars in their own right. They have the freedom now. I like the additions of Coby White at the point to replace Ball and Naz Reid at the four, plus Grayson Allen and SF Royce O’Neale give them some solid veterans to lean on. This team has plenty of shooting and could surprise and move up to become a play-in team.

What are we doing here? Running it back with a 26-win roster and betting that Jamahl Mosley as coach will be the difference? Derik Queen and Jeremiah Fears should improve, and hopefully the Pelicans get better health from Zion Williamson, Herb Jones and Dejounte Murray, but what does that really get them? Free Trey Murphy.

Julius Randle is a floor raiser and Michael Porter Jr. is going to put up points — increasing his trade value before the deadline — all of which means Brooklyn will win more games than it did a season ago. I was high on Mikel Brown Jr. before the draft and remain bullish on him, and Egor Demin showed how much he’s grown at Summer League. All of which is to say, there are reasons for optimism in Brooklyn.

Sacramento will give the keys to Darius Acuff Jr., and he has had some promising preseason moments playing off veteran big man Domantas Sabonis. The real future pairing was supposed to be Acuff and Maxime Raynaud, but the French big man is going to miss the start of the season with a knee injury, but fortunately it was not as bad as it looked when it happened. Ben Simmons is going to draw an outsized portion of headlines, but we haven’t seen enough of him this preseason to have a sense of whether he can really take some of the shot-creation load off Acuff.

This team has some interesting players, including Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel’el Ware, Kasparas Jakučionis and rookie Brayden Burries, and we know Tyler Herro can put up points and will have the green light. I’m a fan of Taylor Jenkins as a coach, but he’s essentially opened a particularly brutal basket on “Chopped” of mismatched ingredients — and a number of those players could be gone at the trade deadline, with new ones in their place. It’s going to be a rebuilding process in Milwaukee, we’ll see which players look like the franchise will keep around for the long term.
