WTA Beijing Best Bets Including Maya Joint vs Sinja Kraus

TheTools · 29.09.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

The WTA Beijing tournament, also known as the China Open, begins. This is the fall 1,000-point tournament that forms the heart of the WTA Asian swing in the back end of the tennis season. Players who might not have done well at the US Open have extra incentive to do well in China as the tennis season winds down, with the WTA Finals a little more than a month away in Indian Wells. There are a lot of stories to follow at this tournament, but the biggest news is the players not competing in China. Notably, Amanda Anisimova, Jessica Pegula, and Marta Kostyuk have withdrawn from the China Open. Alexandra Eala has skipped the event and is therefore inviting the possibility of being fined so that she can play in the Asian Games. With these four high-profile players out, the tournament will open up opportunities for other players. Let’s see who takes advantage over the next week and a half. WTA Beijing promises to be as unpredictable as most of the 2026 WTA tennis season has been. LWOS has our best bets for three matches. Post your best bets and thoughts in the comments.

WTA Beijing

McNally – Yastremska: 23:00 EST

H2H: 0-2

Caty McNally is 12-11 on hardcourt this year. Dayana Yastremska is 6-12. This is the first match since the US Open for both players, so they haven’t played any live matches the past four weeks before starting this WTA Beijing tournament. McNally and Yastremska both felt they needed to take a lot of time off instead of playing any smaller events earlier in September. It does put them in the same basic position. Which player it benefits is hard to tell.

Best Bet to Make

The WTA Beijing betting angle here is not easy. You could have McNally moneyline as a parlay leg, but you’ll get a better price for Yastremska to win one set. Since both players are rusty after four weeks off from the tour, the idea that McNally won’t be locked in seems reasonable. It’s less about trusting Yastremska and more about trusting that McNally won’t be at her best, even if she does win the match.

That being said, betting on Yastremska +1.5 sets is a value bet for sure.

Value bet/ the best odds: Yastremska +1.5 sets @1.65 @betway

Joint – Kraus: 23:00 EST

H2H: 0-0

This is a very interesting WTA Beijing first-round match. Maya Joint had a terrible year until she beat Serena Williams at Wimbledon. Then she started playing better. She is 7-3 in her last 10 matches, and she made the Seoul final. That’s why she’s a very clear favorite over Sinja Kraus, who is only 5-5 in her last 10 matches and has been a lot less consistent than Joint in recent weeks.

Best Bet to Make

While Maya Joint should definitely win this WTA Beijing match, she still has a losing record on the tour this year. She is still 14-15 on hardcourt as well. It’s not as though she is an elite player. She isn’t. She should win the match, but she won’t crush Kraus. As long as the match is fairly close, Kraus is in position to offer a good price for covering the spread.

That being said, betting on Kraus +5.5 games is a value bet for sure.

Value bet/ the best odds: Kraus +5.5 games @1.61 @1xbet

Tjen – Kalinina: 23:00 EST

H2H: 0-0

This WTA Beijing match is priced close to a toss-up. Janice Tjen has nine hardcourt wins this year while Anhelina Kalinina has only four. However, Tjen’s overall hardcourt record this year is 9-15, which is obviously not very good. Kalinina is 32-10 on clay this season but only 4-5 on hardcourt. She recently won two indoor hardcourt matches at the Billie Jean King Cup, but those don’t count toward her official hardcourt record, which is outdoors only.

Best Bet to Make

The lack of outdoor hardcourt wins for Kalinina this year makes it hard to trust her in this WTA Beijing match. Go with Tjen to win the match and maybe consider a pro-Kalinina game spread parlay leg.

That being said, betting on Tjen winning is a value bet for sure.

Value bet/ the best odds: Tjen winning @1.90 @betfair
