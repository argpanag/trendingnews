Texas wins late, Oklahoma wins easily: College football Week 2 oddly specific predictions

TheTools · 12.09.2026 04:31 · source · permalink

College

Football Week 2

Texas hosts Ohio State in perhaps the most impactful nonconference game of the 2026 regular season. Scott Wachter / Imagn Images

Remember when Clemson and Florida State ruled the ACC and believed they deserved a bigger cut of the TV money?

Those two programs probably believed their brands, trophy cases and television ratings would protect them from ever slipping back to the pack. Life can be humbling sometimes.

Point and laugh at the ACC’s former kings if you choose, but overconfidence comes for all of us. I’ve learned that the hard way here at Oddly Specific, where predicting exact scores and yardage totals is an invitation for the universe to make fun of you.

After a 5-4 opening week, I have no business laughing at anybody — not even those terrible predictions some of you made. But I’ll summon the courage — or arrogance — to try again, beginning where we always do: stat-stuffers.

Most passing yards

Oregon-Oklahoma State looked like an elite quarterback matchup before Drew Mestemaker and the Cowboys laid an egg as nearly two-touchdown favorites at Tulsa last week. Dante Moore, meanwhile, opened with a career-high 378 yards and three touchdowns against Boise State — an effort the now sixth-ranked Ducks badly needed to pull out a win at home.

Oklahoma State’s rebuilt roster doesn’t have enough defensive answers to survive four quarters of Moore, who throws for 425 yards and four touchdowns in a 24-point victory.

Most rushing yards

Defending ACC champion Duke made it clear in its 17-3 win over Tulane last week that they’re ready to ride Nate Sheppard as much as necessary. The second-year player from Louisiana ran for 227 yards and two touchdowns on a career-high 35 carries. Illinois, meanwhile, allowed UAB to rush for 194 yards on 48 carries in its 42-23 Week 1 win over the Blazers.

Duke’s muddy passing game should ensure Sheppard another enormous workload. He carries it 30 times for 175 yards and two touchdowns. Illinois survives, 20-17, but doesn’t cover the 6.5-point spread.

Most receiving yards

KJ Duff caught nine passes for 196 yards and three touchdowns and was responsible for 81 percent of starting quarterback Dylan Lonergan’s passing yardage in an embarrassing home loss to UMass last week. Boston College knows the ball is going to Duff on Friday night, but can the Eagles do anything about it? Duff gets peppered with targets from new starter AJ Surace and finishes with 11 catches for 210 yards and two touchdowns, including another score of at least 50 yards.

Who wins? Boston College breaks a 21-21 tie and covers the 3.5-point spread with a late touchdown.

Five big games

No. 11 Oklahoma (-5.5) at Michigan

Michigan needed the replay officials to find one extra second between their couch cushions, allowing Bryce Underwood’s 47-yard Hail Mary to rescue the Wolverines in a one-point win over Western Michigan. Oklahoma’s defense is nasty. The Sooners led the country in both sacks per game (3.5) and tackles for loss per game (9.4) last season and opened 2026 by holding UTEP to 198 yards.

Michigan’s defense will keep the Big House interested, but it won’t stop fans from booing their $12 million quarterback. OU quarterback John Mateer, meanwhile, accounts for three touchdowns, including two in the second half as Oklahoma forces two more Underwood turnovers and holds Michigan out of the end zone until the game has already been decided. OU wins 26-13.

Arizona State at No. 10 Texas A&M (-14)

Kentucky transfer Cutter Boley threw six touchdown passes in his Arizona State debut. But doing that against Morgan State at home is a little different than facing a Mike Elko defense with 100,000-plus angry fans yelling at you in College Station. The Aggies allowed Missouri State only 67 total yards last week — the fewest by an A&M opponent since 1978 — and have won 46 of their last 50 nonconference home games.

The noise and defense rattle Boley into four sacks and his first interception as a Sun Devil. Marcel Reed, meanwhile, accounts for 300-plus yards and three scores, and Texas A&M controls the second half in a 34-16 win.

UCF at Pittsburgh (-7)

UCF’s 73-6 blowout of Bethune-Cookman featured 360 rushing yards and seven rushing touchdowns — scored by six different players. Pitt’s defense is a tad better. The Panthers allowed Miami (Ohio) only 27 rushing yards on 21 carries, while quarterback Mason Heintschel threw seven touchdown passes to seven receivers.

UCF has four starters back from a secondary that helped the Knights rank 22nd against the pass last season. But UCF has lost nine consecutive road games, and its running game won’t provide enough cover once Pitt forces Alonza Barnett III to throw. Heintschel goes for 325 yards and four touchdowns, and the Panthers win 31-23.

Arizona at No. 15 BYU (-7.5)

BYU has won five straight in the series, but four were decided by one possession. Last year’s meeting included a weather delay, a 10-point BYU fourth-quarter comeback and two overtimes before the Cougars escaped 33-27.

All-Big 12 first-team quarterback Noah Fifita keeps this game exciting with three touchdown passes, including one to give the Wildcats a fourth-quarter lead. But Bear Bachmeier answers with a touchdown drive that includes big plays from freshman Legend Glasker, and BYU survives another thriller, 24-21.

No. 1 Ohio State at No. 4 Texas (-1.5)

I predicted Texas would beat Ohio State 27-24 in my preseason prognostications. No reason to switch things up. The Buckeyes have defeated Texas twice since January 2025, but neither game was in Austin, where the Longhorns have won 10 straight and forced at least one turnover in 24 straight.

Jeremiah Smith still gets his — 100-plus receiving yards and a touchdown — but Colin Simmons and the Texas pass rush make Julian Sayin look mortal long enough for Arch Manning to rally the Longhorns in the fourth quarter. Manning accounts for three touchdowns, including the game-winner to Ryan Wingo on fourth down with only seconds to play.

Upset alert

Memphis at Boise State (-8)

The Broncos made an impression at Oregon last week and are 14-1 at home under Spencer Danielson. They’re also celebrating the blue turf’s 40th birthday with special hand-painted helmets. Charles Huff’s team arrives with a defense that had four interceptions in a win against Arkansas State and a running back in Jaylin Carter, who averaged 8.2 yards per carry and scored five touchdowns in the opener.

Carter runs for 150 yards and two touchdowns, and the Tigers win their second heavyweight G6 road game before September is half over. The final score: 35-31.

Week 1 report card

After going 6-12 last season picking upsets, I’m 0-1 to start this season after Florida State couldn’t knock off SMU. Let’s review some of my other predictions.

The good: I said Trinidad Chambliss would throw for 325 yards and three touchdowns in an Ole Miss victory over Louisville. He finished with 336 yards and three touchdown passes — plus another score on the ground — as the Rebels won 41-38 on a last-second field goal. LSU also covered against Clemson as predicted, with Sam Leavitt using his legs to produce 113 rushing yards and two touchdowns in a 51-10 demolition.

The bad: My stat-stuffer candidates all missed their predicted totals — and it wasn’t close. Oklahoma State’s Drew Mestemaker threw for 241 yards instead of 350 in a 24-10 loss to Tulsa. Michigan State’s Cam Edwards rushed for 98 instead of 175. And Florida Atlantic’s Easton Messer caught seven passes for 73 yards instead of 13 for 142. I also picked Cal to beat UCLA.

Just missed: I predicted Michigan State would beat Toledo 30-17. The Spartans won 30-20. I also said Colorado’s Julian Lewis would throw a fourth-quarter touchdown to give the Buffaloes a late lead at Georgia Tech. Lewis did exactly that with 42 seconds remaining, but instead of Justice Haynes answering with the winning touchdown, Colorado blocked Georgia Tech’s last-second field-goal attempt and escaped with a 14-13 win.
