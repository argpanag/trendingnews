GTA IV looks like a new game with RTX Remix, and I didn’t expect Liberty City to feel this alive

TheTools · 18.11.2026 13:30 · source · permalink

Every time I tell someone that I never much cared for Grand Theft Auto V's story or gameplay, I'm met with incredulous looks and gasps. It's only after I tell them that GTA IV is my favorite game of all time that it makes sense. Niko Bellic's story has always stood out as the more mature, grounded, and grittier experience — one that ingrained itself in the heart of my 12-year-old self and never really left.

Since 2008, not many games have come close to replicating that experience for me, and none have succeeded all the way through. Because I've been experimenting with RTX Remix lately and running path-traced versions of Half-Life 2, Max Payne, and Portal, a path-traced Liberty City was the next logical step.

I was curious at best, and yet, with RTX Remix, Grand Theft Auto IV is now luring me to play it start to finish again, even with GTA 6 just a day away at the time of writing.

RTX Remix is basically a time machine for old PC games

It takes surprisingly little to make old games look new

RTX Remix works particularly well with older games built around DirectX 8 and DirectX 9 because it can intercept their dated rendering pipeline and replace much of the lighting with modern ray-traced effects.

These games stand to benefit from physically-based materials, global illumination, reflections, and far more convincing lighting. This isn't just an upscale here — Remix effectively teaches an old game how to render light differently.

I expected getting GTA IV working with it to involve the usual ritual of obscure dependencies and broken compatibility layers, but thankfully, the process for my Complete Edition on Steam was much simpler. I downloaded three files from a single GitHub repository, dropped them together, and pointed the resulting setup at my GTA IV executable. That was essentially it.

That's what makes RTX Remix particularly exciting for old PC games. The technology doing the heavy lifting is extraordinarily complicated, but getting it into a compatible game can be remarkably simple. And once I saw Liberty City running through that path-traced pipeline, I immediately understood why this technology has been catching my attention lately.

Liberty City has never looked this alive

RTX Remix made me see GTA IV differently

I knew RTX Remix would change GTA IV, but the first time I saw its path-traced lighting hit the hood of my car, I had the same reaction I did the first time I saw Cyberpunk 2077's path-traced global illumination. The city suddenly had light coming from somewhere, rather than simply having brighter and darker textures.

The difference is particularly obvious around water. The old rendering gives the waterfront that familiar GTA IV haze, while Remix gives the water, stone, and wooden surfaces enough definition to separate them properly. Even the distant skyline feels less like a backdrop.

Since the RTX compatibility mod for GTA IV turns every single game light — including the sun — into RTX Remix lights, the difference is obvious in every single frame, and Liberty City is better for it.

Then there are the streets. Wet surfaces actually pick up the surrounding lights, cars, and sky, giving Liberty City that slick, rainy-night look I remember from newer games rather than the flat sheen of the original. The Remix mod's tiny change of giving each car two headlights and rear lights each instead of a single centered light makes all the difference in the world, especially when the roads are slick.

Every single place in Liberty City felt new, and since every light source in the game is a remix light in the RTX Remix mod, that checks out. The vanilla game has that unmistakable green-blue cast, but Remix makes the streetlights feel like they are illuminating the roads instead of simply painting them bright.

The Schottler underpass at night floored me too, with deep shadows pooling under the arch while shop glow licks the wet cobbles. This is, for all intents and purposes, still GTA IV; it just looks like the game finally got the rendering engine it deserved.

RTX Remix doesn't need to reinvent GTA IV

It makes the game newer without making it different

The muted colors, grimy streets, overcast skies, and the distinctly cold Liberty City palette are all still very much here. Unlike a DLSS 5 neural rendering layer, RTX Remix refrains from giving a game that "modern title" sheen.

Making an old game prettier, after all, is rather easy. The tough part comes when you have to ensure you don't trample over the art direction that made it so memorable in the first place. Remix mostly leaves the original assets and visual identity alone and lets better lighting do the heavy lifting. The result feels more like a remaster than a reinterpretation.

Coming back to the DLSS 5 comparison, neural rendering can produce some genuinely spectacular results, but I've occasionally found myself wondering whether I'm looking at the original artist's work or an AI interpretation of it.

With GTA IV, I never had that feeling. Niko still looks like Niko, Liberty City still looks like Liberty City, and everything still feels unmistakably 2008. RTX Remix hasn't replaced the game's identity, but it certainly has given that identity some modern lighting to work with.

The makeover comes with a serious performance tax, though

60 FPS on an 18-year-old game feels like a pretty good deal

The catch is performance, because RTX Remix turns GTA IV from around 230 FPS on my 4070 Ti into a heavier workload. With path tracing enabled, I initially hovered around 50–60 FPS, with occasional drops into the low 40s.

Thankfully, I didn't spend time chasing performance. Five minutes of tweaking was enough. Dropping the in-game settings from Ultra to High and switching DLSS from Quality to Balanced gave me a solid 60–65 FPS experience.

One reason this works is that 99% of the meshes in the RTX Remix mod are rendered through RemixAPI, which drastically improves performance and reduces the CPU bottleneck.

Here, though, in a path-traced Liberty City, I'd happily take 60 FPS over 230 FPS. RTX Remix makes GTA IV look so damn good that I'm ready to play it from start to finish again. With GTA 6 arriving, that's saying something.

Maybe I wasn't done with GTA IV after all

It's ridiculous to be getting this excited over a game I've already finished more times than I can reasonably count. GTA IV has been sitting in my Steam library for years, perfectly playable and perfectly familiar. And yet, this little experiment has made Liberty City feel interesting again, now that I have a new reason to look at every single one of its streets once again.

And perhaps that's the best thing RTX Remix can do for games like this. It doesn't need to convince me that GTA IV is better than what came after it. It just reminded me why I loved it in the first place. Grand Theft Auto 6 can take over my evenings tomorrow, but tonight, I have one more drive through Liberty City that I absolutely need to take.
