Trump to meet President at Áras during visit next weekend

TheTools · 03.09.2026 06:58 · source · permalink

Getty

US President Donald Trump is to meet President Catherine Connolly at Áras an Uachtaráin during his planned visit to Ireland next weekend.

He is likely to arrive in Dublin early on the morning of Saturday 12 September, before travelling on Marine One helicopter to the Phoenix Park for meetings with Ms Connolly and Taoiseach Micheál Martin.

Mr Trump will spend Saturday night at his golf resort in Doonbeg for the Irish Open tournament.

The US president's sons Eric and Don Jr will attend the event in Doonbeg next week.

It is understood the brothers will not be travelling with the president.

While a bilateral meeting with Mr Martin at Farmleigh House has long been expected, there had been doubt over whether the US President would meet President Connolly.

It is understood that a draft itinerary for Mr Trump's visit early last month had no scheduled engagement at Áras an Uachtaráin, while a spokesperson for Ms Connolly had said she would consider any request for a meeting she received.

Mr Martin said he does not have any concerns about the meeting between President Connolly and Mr Trump.

Mr Martin said that the meeting wasn't confirmed earlier as it was subject to President Connolly’s schedule.

"Someone doesn’t want to jump ahead and be announcing things in terms of a forthcoming visit because it’s subject to the president’s schedule," he said.

President Connolly insisted during last year's election campaign that she would meet Mr Trump if the opportunity arose, but she was critical of his administration during her St Patrick’s Day message when she said that "the normalisation of war can never be accepted".

He erroneously referred to Ms Connolly as a "he" during a meeting with the Taoiseach in the Oval Office on St Patrick's Day.

The pair's meeting is being described as a brief courtesy call by those with knowledge of the trip, and so is unlikely to replicate the visits of previous US officeholders Barack Obama and Joe Biden to former president Michael D Higgins.

Preparations for the US President's arrival have been under way for several months, although Government sources acknowledge the sequence of events could still change up to the last minute.

His visit is likely to resemble his second state visit to Britain this time last year, during which he largely stayed within the confines of the walls of Windsor Castle, away from protesters.

The Dublin leg of the journey will be largely confined to the Phoenix Park for meetings with President Connolly and Mr Martin, with a planned pit stop to turn the sod at the new US embassy in Ballsbridge likely off the agenda due to security and logistical challenges.

It is understood that Mr Trump will be greeted at Dublin Airport by Tánaiste and Minister for Finance Simon Harris.

RTÉ News understands that an event will also be held at the US Ambassador's residence in the Phoenix Park on Saturday.

Mr Trump is friends with the US Ambassador to Ireland and businessman Ed Walsh.

Details will be confirmed soon but it is believed the event will take place following Mr Trump's meetings with Irish leaders.

Mr Walsh regularly plays golf with Mr Trump and is a member of Trump National Golf Club Bedminster, one of Mr Trump's golf resorts, where he has won the club championship.

The US leader will travel to his Doonbeg Golf resort on Saturday afternoon for the Irish Open.

A senior Government representative may also attend the tournament and surrounding events in a non-official capacity.

Mr Trump will leave Ireland on Sunday evening, 13 September, or early the following day.

Demonstrations at Mr Trump's visit are planned in Dublin and Clare.

People Before Profit TDs Richard Boyd Barrett and Paul Murphy have called on the public to join a protest in Dublin against the visit of Mr Trump, who they called an "imperialist warmonger".

Asked about the prospect of protests against Mr Trump, Mr Martin said: "We're a democracy, people have a right to protest.

"I think what's important... is that they're respectful and they respect the rights of others who participate in the event."

Meanwhile, the former ambassador of Ireland to the US, Dan Mulhall, said he expects a meeting between Mr Trump and Ms Connolly will go well.

Speaking on RTÉ's Today with David McCullagh programme, Mr Mulhall said Mr Trump is quite affable on a one-to-one basis and that President Connolly will realise there is no point having a fight with him.

"Trump has a capacity to get on with people you wouldn't expect him to get on with."

Mr Mulhall said the best outcome from the visit is that it goes off without any major gaffes or incidents.

Mr Mulhall said Mr Trump is under a lot of pressure politically and both sides will be happy if the visit goes well.

"I think both sides will be happy if the visit just goes off smoothly and he goes away happy to have seen some of the best golfers in the world play his golf course," added Mr Mulhall.
