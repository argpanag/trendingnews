Saudi Arabia FA announce investigation after Ruben Neves taunted with Diogo Jota chants

TheTools · 14.09.2026 10:28 · source · permalink

Ronaldo, who plays for Saudi club Al Nassr and is a Portugal international team-mate of Neves and played alongside the late Jota. Alex Pantling - FIFA/FIFA via Getty Images

The Saudi Arabian Football Federation (SAFF) has announced an investigation will be undertaken after Ruben Neves appeared to be taunted by chants about his late friend and team-mate Diogo Jota during a match on Saturday.

Earlier on Sunday his Portugal team-mate Cristiano Ronaldo had called for the Saudi Pro League (SPL) to issue lifetime stadium bans for any fans involved. The condemnation was echoed by Neves’s club side Al Hilal, who released a statement calling the chants “offensive and unacceptable” and said they had filed a complaint with the SAFF.

In turn, the SAFF said the “relevant committees” will take “necessary action” after the incident, which “is inconsistent with the values of Saudi football and the principles of sportsmanship”.

“SAFF and SPL stress that exploiting personal tragedies for the purpose of abuse or provocation is unacceptable behaviour that has no place in Saudi football,” the statement read. “The outcome will be announced upon completion of the relevant process.”

Jota died in July 2025 aged just 28. He and his 25-year-old brother Andre Silva passed away when the car they were in crashed in Zamora, Spain.

Footage widely circulated on social media, filmed from the Al Taawoun Club Stadium stands, shows Al Hilal’s Neves standing over a free-kick close to the home fans when chants of ‘Jota, Jota, Jota’ become audible.

The central midfielder appears to sarcastically applaud supporters, before repeating the actions of pointing to the sky and then making an eye-pointing gesture towards the section of the stand, off camera, from which the chant appears to emanate.

The 29-year-old, walking past media after the match, can be seen briefly telling reporters: “I just hope they don’t go to pray later after what they did.” He did not go into further detail on the incident.

Ronaldo, who plays for Saudi club Al Nassr and is a Portugal international team-mate of Neves and played alongside the late Jota, has called on the behaviour to be punished, adding that those involved “should never be allowed to enter a stadium again.”

Posting on Instagram, Ronaldo wrote: “The league must take action and punish this kind of fan behavior. This is no longer football, and there have to be limits. People who behave like this should never be allowed to enter a stadium again.”

An Al Hilal statement said the behavior “contravenes sporting values ​​and ethics, transcending the boundaries of competition and fan support”.

“The company expressed its absolute rejection of such actions targeting players or their families, or intended to cause personal offense,” it added. “Sporting rivalry, however intense, cannot justify encroaching upon players’ personal or human circumstances, nor does it excuse exploiting feelings of loss as a means for provocation or abuse.”

The Athletic has contacted Neves’ representatives for comment.

Neves and Jota were close both personally and professionally. Born just four months apart, they were team-mates at youth international level for Portugal and then at Porto, before both joining Wolverhampton Wanderers in the Championship in July 2017, aged 20.

The two were Wolves team-mates for three seasons, both families lived in the Compton area of Wolverhampton and, each with young children, socialised together. On the pitch, Jota and Neves helped Wolves rise to the Premier League while each became regulars on international duty with Portugal’s senior squad.

Neves has a tattoo on his calf of himself and Jota embracing, a permanent reminder of their close friendship. The Al Hilal midfielder was a pallbearer at Jota’s funeral.

Jota, who won 49 caps for Portugal and wore the No 21 shirt for his country, had his national shirt number inherited by Neves in August last year. “He had a very close relationship with Diogo and is the ideal person to represent him,” then-Portugal head coach Roberto Martinez said of the decision.

Al Hilal won Saturday’s league game 0-6, with former Arsenal forward Gabriel Martinelli scoring a hat-trick while England international Ollie Watkins netted twice.

Simone Inzaghi’s side are top of the standings with 18 points from their opening seven matches, while Al Tawooun are 17th, one place off the foot of the table.
