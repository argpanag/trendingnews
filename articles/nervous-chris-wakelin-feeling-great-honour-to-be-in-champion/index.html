'Nervous' Chris Wakelin feeling 'great honour' to be in Champion of Champions final | SnookerHQ.com

TheTools · 14.11.2026 06:30 · source · permalink
  • David Caulfield Author

Chris Wakelin moved one win away from securing the biggest title of his career after beating Neil Robertson 6-2 in the Champion of Champions semi-finals on Friday.

Wakelin, a two-time ranking event winner, put in another solid shift to see off the 2015 and 2019 winner of the invitational tournament.

Robertson compiled breaks of 108, 69, and 53 during the opening four frames, but the pair reached the mid-session interval with the scores tied at two frames apiece.

Reigning Scottish Open champion Wakelin subsequently took control after the resumption, compiling runs of 67, 55, 96, and 63 in the last four frames to triumph.

A key moment materialised in the fifth frame, when an easy missed brown from the Australian allowed Wakelin in with an opportunity to move in front.

“If you guys are talking about it, then obviously you guys picked up on it as well,” Wakelin told ITV. “You’ve got to make the most of these opportunities.”

“Neil and I have had some great battles over the last couple of years. I know that I’ve got to play near my best to win.

“In the last frame, I did genuinely feel nervous. That was the first time I felt nervous in a match for quite some time.

“I should have won frames five and six in one visit and made it a little bit easier for myself, but as it is with me sometimes, I just struggle to pot those balls that matter.

“These tournaments like the Masters and the Champion of Champions are massive, massive events. Five years ago, I could never see myself qualifying for these events.

“So to be here in the final of such a prestigious and huge event is a great honour for me.

“It’s difficult to think too far ahead. I try to just stay in the moment and focus on every match that comes my way.

“But to play in the final of an event like this is an exciting time.”

Wakelin, who also knocked out reigning Champion of Champions winner Mark Selby this week, would pocket a career-high sum of £150,000 if he were to go all the way to glory in Sunday’s final.

The 34 year-old will encounter either Kyren Wilson or Shaun Murphy, who will contest the second semi-final fixture on Saturday in Leicester.

Wilson is on a remarkable 14-game unbeaten streak that has seen him pick up back-to-back ranking trophies at the Northern Ireland Open and the International Championship in the last few weeks.

The Kettering cueist is the favourite on current form, but Murphy boasts a far superior head-to-head record from their prior duels with one another.

Indeed, the 2017 Champion of Champions winner has won their last six meetings on the World Snooker Tour, a sequence that includes victories over Wilson in two big finals.

Wakelin will probably be hoping that the match ends late, which could give him a slight edge considering he has a full day of rest before the title-deciding showdown.
