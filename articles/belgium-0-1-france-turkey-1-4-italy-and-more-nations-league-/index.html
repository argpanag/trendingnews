That’s all from me, thanks for reading and for your emails. Our Nations League round-up will be live on the website shortly (and will be put into this blog). Cheers, and roll on tomorrow’s Nations League fixures, including Spain v Croatia, Scotland v Switzerland and Czechia v England.

A word for Michael Kayode. The Brentford right back registered a goal and an assist on his Italy debut. The Azzurri looked completely transformed with him in transition and that long throw is a weapon for anyone. This is a post-Di Lorenzo world, and everyone is just waking up to it.

Fair play to Mancini, he made substantial changes after the defeat to Belgium and it paid off. Next up for Italy? France away on Friday …

Northern Ireland’s Isaac Price speaks to the BBC after the 0-0 draw with Hungary:

I think there is a little bit of frustration, we had chances and they did as well. Bit disappointed to not get a win in front of the home crowd but a draw is not a bad result. I think we are playing well as a team and that is the main thing. A clean sheet is a positive start but there are things we have t work on in the final third.

I think there is a little bit of frustration, we had chances and they did as well. Bit disappointed to not get a win in front of the home crowd but a draw is not a bad result. I think we are playing well as a team and that is the main thing. A clean sheet is a positive start but there are things we have t work on in the final third.

I’m watching that Olise goal back again. His first touch from the pass of Cherki, off the bony bit of his shoulder, was entirely deliberate as it allowed him to sprint forward at pace. After leaving De Cuyper in the dust, the rest was typical Olise: approaching the box, cutting inside, shot faking to keep the Belgium defence and keeper guessing, before curling the actual shot right into the side netting. Lammens had no chance. From a wide-angle replay, some of France’s players starting celebrating before Olise even took the shot. He’s that good.

Full-time: Romania 2-4 Bosnia and Herzegovina

Bosnia and Herzegovina can be very happy with their work. This win following a 0-0 draw in Poland on Friday.

Full-time: Belgium 0-1 France

What a goal to win it from Olise. Belgium had a late chance for a penalty shout, with Newcastle’s Fernandez-Pardo cannoning a ball off Koné in the France area, but play is waved on.

Full-time: Sweden 3-1 Poland

The British-based trio of Nygren, Ayari and Gyokeres secure the win for Graham Potter’s side.

Full-time: Turkey 1-4 Italy

All the damage done in the first half. Italy – and Michael Kayode in particular – were excellent before the break, and Turkey were wasteful in the second half. Absolutely torrential conditions in Bursa.

Full-time: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

An unbeaten start for Northern Ireland after a win and a scrappy draw. Not bad, Michael, not bad.

GOAL! Belgium 0-1 France (Olise 88)

Ooooooohhhh my worrrrrrd!!!! Olise scores a sensational solo goal, starting from his own half, beating three Belgian defenders and sidefooting a trademark finish into the far corner. Olise initially took the ball just before the half-way line on his shoulder, speeding past De Cuyper. After leaving the Brighton man flailing on the floor, Olise races towards the Belgian box, cutting inside Mechele and finishing past Lammens before Tielemans had a chance to get back. The France squad goes crazy in celebration on the sideline, none more so than Zidane, who runs down the touchline – Mourinho-style – kicking a spare ball high into the stands! GOOOOOAL!

84 min: Turkey 1-4 Italy

Turkey have completely dominated the second period but only have one goal – two minutes into the half – to show for it. Mancini will be delighted, his side were enterprising and exciting in the first half and determined and dogged in the second. It is a long way from a perfect performance, but this is something for him to build on.

GOAL! Sweden 3-1 Poland (Gyokeres 72)

I’m a little late in reporting this, apologies, but Gyokeres has surely sealed the victory for Sweden, volleying a third goal after the Arsenal striker was given an acre of space at the back post. Easy finish.

81 min: Belgium 0-0 France

This has been a fabulous game but no goals … yet. Zidane wants to change that and has casually thrown on Olise and Cherki, for Barcola and Akliouche. Olise on the right, Doué now on the left.

“I don’t know if Désiré Doué is playing football or putting on a one-man show, a mesmerizing mix of French mime, ballet, and tap dance,” emails Justin Kavanagh. “But it’s hugely entertaining.”

78 min: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

Price beats Kerkez with a shimmy and tries to arrow a finish inside the near post, but Blackburn keeper Toth is equal to it. A minute later, Northern Ireland captain Shea Charles is a little lucky to stay on the pitch, lunging in and making a clumsy tackle on Milan Laszlo Toth. You have seen many a second booking given for that sort of challenge. The game is going at 100mph. One moment of quality from either side can decide this match.

Huge save from France's Maignan!

74 min: Belgium 0-0 France

France have dominated this second half, but are nearly caught out on the counter attack. Belgium break with speed, with De Ketelaere feeding Lukebakio down the right and the Benfica forward uses his pace to stream away into the France area. Lukebakio tries to poke a finish towards the bottom corner, but Maignan spreads himself and saves with his trailing leg!

71 min: Turkey 1-4 Italy

Not sure you can really be ‘hanging onto’ a 4-1 lead, but Italy are defying definition right now. Turkey are throwing everything at Mancini’s side as they attempt to get back into the game but are being wasteful at the final moment: Akturkoglu and Baris Yilmaz particularly guilty. Scalvini also made an excellent last-ditch sliding tackle to deny Baris Yilmaz.

67 min: Belgium 0-0 France

Speaking of Brighton footballers, here comes Maxim De Cuyper for Belgium, the left-back replacing Seys, who was given a bit of a going over by Doué. France also make a substitution with Dembele replacing Lepaul. The Rennes forward looked lively and linked the play well but snatched at a good chance in the first half. Let’s see what Dembele can do.

GOAL! Sweden 2-1 Poland (Ayari 64)

Ayari is becoming a bit of a superstar, isn’t he?! Sweden take a corner short to the Brighton man, who sends Szymanski to the shops with a delicious feint, before curling a strike into the far corner from the edge of the area. Great goal.

61 min: Belgium 0-0 France

France pushing and probing, now the dominant team in Brussels. The Zidane era has not got off to a rip-roaring start with a 1-0 win in Turkey and no goals here, but Les Bleus have been exciting going forward. Maybe more exciting with what is effectively their second XI than the first XI that played against Turkey?! Doué has been a joke this game, taking on Belgians like he’s playing some sort of video game.

59 min: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

Perhaps buoyed by that fiery end to the first half, Hungary have begun the second half much better than the first. Lukacs and Shafer are much more prominent with the ball for the visitors. Still goalless.

TURKEY GOAL DISALLOWED! It stays 4-1 to Italy!

55 min: Turkey 1-4 Italy

This is a ridiculous decision to deny Turkey a perfectly valid goal. Baris Yilmaz goes clean through but with the ball squelching and bouncing wildly in the downpouring rain, the Galatasaray forward is forced wide. Italy’s defenders recover but can’t stop Yilmaz from squeezing a shot across goal, off the far post and in! The goal is ruled offside by referee Michael Oliver! I think Akturkoglu was penalised for being in the eye-line of Donnarumma, but he was nowhere near the Italy goalkeeper. Shocking decision!

GOAL! Romania 1-4 Bosnia and Herzegovina (Mahmic 55)

Bosnia and Herzegovina have surely sewn up the victory, Mahmic nodding a fourth after a wondrous cross from Bajraktarevic on the right.

GOAL! Turkey 1-4 Italy (Pio Esposito 50)

Game on?! Game off! Pio Esposito immediately restores Italy’s three-goal cushion, heading into an open net after Sandro Tonali’s shot cannons off the post. Manchester United goalkeeper Bayindir (on loan at Celta Vig), who gifted possession to Italy in the first place, was helpless on the ground following Tonali’s shot.

GOAL! Turkey 1-3 Italy (Baris Yilmaz 47)

Turkey start the second half like a train, catching Italy cold! Aydin gets free down the right, reaching the byline and he plays a beautiful low cross that dissects Donnarumma and the Italian defence, with Baris Yilmaz tucking in a tap-in at the back post! Game on?!

Peeeeeeep!

We’re underway again.

“Talk of brothers playing for Italy will always bring to mind the great George Hamilton of RTE and his clarification that “the Baggio brothers, of course, are not related””.

A fiery end to the half in Belfast with Shea Charles clashing with Hungary’s Lukacs just as the whistle went. Szoboszlai is not happy as he trudges off, claiming that there was some sort of misconduct from the Northern Ireland captain.

You’ll notice that Bosnia and Herzegovina are now 3-1 up, Tabakovic pouncing to tuck a rebound away.

GOAL! Sweden 1-1 Poland (Szymanski 43)

Poland are level! A wonderful team goal with Zalewski the instigator. The Atalanta man starts the move with a clever reverse pass, receives the ball back on the byline and cuts the ball back to Szymanski, six yards out, who tucks the ball away!

40 min: Belgium 0-0 France

Belgium are growing into the game. De Bruyne, evergreen, goes close, curling his shot just wide of Maignan’s right-hand post!

“Your description of camera lenses trained on Zidane reminded me of the 2006 documentary/conceptual art film Zidane: A 21st Century Portrait,” emails Peter Oh. “It consists of a full match of moody Zizou-only footage set to a soundtrack by Mogwai.”

Northern Ireland hit the bar!

36 min: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

Pinball in the Hungary box as Atcheson and Shea Charles look to pounce on the loose ball. Patrick Kelly finally gets a shot away, a toe-poke that loops towards goal, but bounces off the crossbar and away. Northern Ireland are are by far the better side, but they really need to make this dominance count for something.

33 min: Romania 1-2 Bosnia and Herzegovina

With so much going on elsewhere, I regret to inform you that I have neglected Romania v Bosnia and Herzegovina. The visitors have a 2-1 lead with Leeds United’s Muharemovic scoring the second, a bullet header from a corner.

30 min: Turkey 0-3 Italy

Turkey have stabilised, somewhat. Arda Guler can’t get into the game for the hosts. With no Hakan Calhanoglu and Kenan Yildiz (both injured), Turkey are struggling for inspiration.

“Here in Naples, I’m watching the Turkey-Italy match with Italian friends after the poor showing against Belgium,” emails Colum Fordham. “I’m impressed by what I’ve seen so far. Mancini has goven Italy some shape. The Esposito brothers are playing with aplomb but I’m really really impressed by Kayode who is doing a fine job in place of the Napoli captain Di Lorenzo. An assist and a goal. And I wish Raspadori was still playing for Napoli. Turkey have good players but no signnof a team. Poor Montella.

25 min: Belgium 0-0 France

Some full-blooded challenges from Belgium, who are hanging on a bit here. Twice France are denied penalty shouts, with Barcola and Doué going down in Belgium’s area, before Seys goes in heavily on Camavinga. Free-kick, from which Akliouche tests Lammens with a testing shot down to his left, the ball bobbles to Doué, then Kalulu, who sees his shot cleared off the line by Ngoy! Belgium are all over the place, with Seys being given a particularly torrid night by Doué.

GOAL! Turkey 0-3 Italy (Kayode 27)

Kayode scores on his debut, as Italy run riot in Turkey. Boos rain down from the stands after Turkey lose the ball on the edge of their own area. Pio Esposito is again the creator as he feeds Kayode – the right-back completely unmarked as he advances again from right-back – and the debutant tucks away a fine finish inside the near post. What a huge improvement from the defeat in Belgium. Not even France looked this accomplished when they played here on Friday.

GOAL! Turkey 0-2 Italy (Frattesi 22)

Frattesi doubles the lead for Mancini’s men! The goal comes at the end of a flowing, one-touch move, as Pio Esposito and Tonali combine on the edge of the keeper before releasing Kayode. The Brentford right back flashes a shot across goal, Bayindir saves but Frattesi is there to tap in the rebound!

18 min: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

Jamie Donley goes down in the area, and there are shouts from the home side for a penalty, but Northern Ireland are penalised for offside. Michael O’Neill’s side are much the stronger in Belfast.

France's Doué hits the bar!

11 min: Belgium 0-0 France

Doué nearly scores a wonderful solo goal! The PSG man has just ridiculous feet, the sort of player who could do the Riverdance inside a phonebox, and he somehow squeezes past Seys and Godts in one outrageous moment, before smashing a shot onto the bar. Lammens was beaten, but Belgium survive.

GOAL! Sweden 1-0 Poland (Nygren 10)

Celtic’s Nygren glances an inswinging cross into the far corner and Graham Potter’s side have the lead!

13 min: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

Hume goes close from long range! A dreadful clearance from Orban lands at the Sunderland full-back’s feet and he drives a bouncing shot just a yard wide. It’s been an even opening 13 minutes to this game, both teams in it.

6 min: Belgium 0-0 France

Belgium v France is slightly behind the others, due to that delayed start. The hosts have made another bright start. They will fancy their chances against this much-changed France XI. Godts is again looking lively.

“I’m guessing ‘WE ARE BELGIUM’ was in English because if it was in the three official languages (Dutch, French and German) then it’d take up three quarters of the stadium, just for that, leaving no room for owt else,” quips Andy Flintoff.

GOAL! Turkey 0-1 Italy (Bastoni 9)

Bastoni scores his fourth goal for Italy in truly bizarre circumstances. It’s absolutely bucketing down rain in Bursa and a low corner squirts across Turkey’s box. Zeki Celik, who plays in Italy for Juventus, should deal with it at the near post, but the defender completely miskicks and Bastoni is well placed on the penalty box to shoot/tackle the ball into the roof of the net! A great start for Mancini and co!

4 min: Turkey 0-0 Italy

Raspadori jinks one way and the other, then fires uncharacteristically wide from a tight angle. I love Raspadori: he lacks an all-round game to be truly elite but normally is deadly with his finishing. The sort of player that would have 20 finishing on Championship manager, but have an otherwise average set of stats.

2 min: Northern Ireland 0-0 Hungary

Looks like Hungary are lining up in a 4-3-2-1, almost a Christmas tree formation. Szoboszlai is one of two players in the No 10 role.

Peeeeeeeep!

We are underway in almost all of the games. Belgium v France is slightly delayed.

Belgium and France are now out of the tunnel. Almost all the photographers are trained on Zinedine Zidane as he takes his place in the dugout, dressed head to toe in dark suit, dark shirt, dark tie. It’s a good look.

A massive tifo, spelling out ‘WE ARE BELGIUM’ (in English?!) adorns the terraces at the King Baudouin Stadium.

The teams are out! We’ll have the anthems now. There really is nothing that holds a candle to Il Canto degli Italiani, in my book.

There is some sort of delay in Brussels, where neither Belgium or France are yet to emerge. Ominously, Sweet Caroline is playing over the tannoy.

Much like France, Italy are almost completely different to XI that started against Belgium. Sandro Tonali and Pio Esposito are the only outfield survivors and the headline news is that the latter’s brother, Sebastiano, is also handed his debut: the Esposito brothers are first to start for the Azzurri since Aldo & Luigi Cevenini in January 1915! Another notable inclusion is Brentford’s Michael Kayode. I can’t believe how long it has taken for Italy to hand the right-back his debut, considering the 22-year-old has been one of the best full-backs in Europe for nearly a year now. But here we are.

The only outfield player to survive from France’s win over Turkey is Maxence Lacroix. Nine changes!. We’re not sure whether that is because of injury or fatigue, or whether that is because Zidane had a withering asssessment of Friday’s win, but it is a much-changed XI for Les Bleus.

Esteban Lepaul will earn his first start and play as the No 9, while Andy Diouf, Jérémy Jacquet, and Lucas Da Cunha all make their France debuts. This is going to be very interesting.

Preamble

Whisper it, but the Nations League is … quite good? It is, at least, a serious upgrade on the international iterations of the past. Jonathan Wilson, in today’s newsletter, described the Nations League as a “palate-cleanser” and “having “a similar role to the Carabao Cup, a competition that is not nothing but is not obviously something either”, which I think is just about right. You can read more of Jonathan’s thoughts right here.

Plenty to go on, then. Belgium v France promises to be a cracker. Belgium were excellent in victory on Friday, Mark van Bommel’s first game in charge. They swatted aside Italy 2-0 in Rome, and did it all without the sidelined Jérémy Doku (although his replacement, PSG’s Mika Godts, was a revelation). France are missing Kylian Mbappé after the Real Madrid was hurt in scoring the only goal of the game in Turkey – Mbappé has since been diagnosed with hyperextension of the posterior capsule of his left knee, and is expected to be out for the rest of the international break. Now coached by Zinedine Zidane, France flickered with quality against Turkey but also looked vulnerable. They are far from the finished article, but there is plenty of time for Zizou to work his magic.

Northern Ireland came away from Georgia on Friday with a dramatic 1-0 win, following Shea Charles’ 99th-minute toe-poke, but tonight is arguably a stiffer test as Hungary and Dominik Szoboszlai arrive in Belfast. Hungary lost at home to Ukraine on Friday and will be determined to bounce back.

Sweden v Poland looks evenly matched, but Turkey v Italy is the pick of the games otherwise. Vincenzo Montella, Turkey’s manager, faces compatriot Roberto Mancini in the battle of the dugouts. The pair briefly played together 30 years ago at Sampdoria, in Mancini’s final season at the club. Together they scored a combined 37 goals in 1996-97 to help Samp qualify for the Uefa Cup. “I believe in him; he has the experience and knowledge to bring Italy back to the top,” said Montella in the lead up to this game. “He is the right person. Our relationship? We don’t socialise, but there’s mutual respect, esteem, and affection after our year at Sampdoria.”

We’ll see if Mancini changes his XI tonight, the one that lost to Belgium looked tired and lacking in ideas.
