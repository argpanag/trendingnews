Tinubu honours Bayelsa boat tragedy victims at Ibadan rally

TheTools · 21.11.2026 15:29 · source · permalink

Vice-President Kashim Shettima welcomes President Bola Tinubu on his arrival at the APC campaign rally in Ibadan, Oyo State, on Saturday. Photo: X/@stanleynkwocha_

President Bola Tinubu on Saturday called for a minute of silence in honour of victims of the boat accident in Bayelsa State during the All Progressives Congress campaign rally in Oyo State.

Tinubu, who expressed sympathy with the bereaved families and the people of Bayelsa, also announced that Vice-President Kashim Shettima would visit the state to commiserate with those affected by the tragedy.

The President made the announcement while addressing supporters at the rally, where he called for prayers for the repose of the victims’ souls and an end to similar incidents.

He said, “When the boat capsized in Bayelsa State, we show solidarity and sympathy. Our condolence goes to the people of Bayelsa State and the bereaved families. Can we have one minute silence?”

He then led the gathering in observing the one-minute silence, after which he prayed for the victims and their families.

“May their souls rest in perfect peace. Give peace and happiness to the people of Bayelsa and prevent the reoccurrence. We will support them,” he said.

Tinubu added that he would be unable to speak at length at the rally.

“The Vice-President will go and condole the families and the people of Bayelsa after this rally. I won’t be able to speak much for today,” he said.

The President had earlier directed that the Ibadan rally be held without much fanfare in honour of the victims of the accident.

The boat accident occurred at about 11am on Friday near the river junction between Oweikorogha and Otuan communities in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State.

The passenger boat was conveying mourners from Ayama to Ukubie community for a burial ceremony when it reportedly developed a mechanical fault and capsized.

The Bayelsa State Police Command confirmed that four people, comprising two adults and two children, died in the incident, while 24 passengers were rescued.

Reacting to the incident, the Bayelsa State Government warned boat operators against overloading, reckless navigation and travelling without life jackets.

The state Commissioner for Information, Orientation and Strategy, Ebiuwou Koku-Obiyai, also urged operators to ensure their boats were in good condition and comply with approved passenger capacity limits.

“Overloading, speeding, recklessness and travelling without life jackets must stop. No journey is worth a life,” she said.
