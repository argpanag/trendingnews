Google is celebrating World Space Week 2026 with an artwork series featuring cosmic images from NASA's James Webb Space Telescope. The featured Doodles spotlight the Rosebud Nebula and the Carina Nebula, honoring breakthroughs in modern astrophotography and international space technology.

Google launched a multi day homepage series which is known as Google Doodle to mark World Space Week 2026. Google is replacing its search logo with high resolution space observations doodle on 6 and 8 October. 

The episodic release of Google doodles features astrophotography captured by NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). Each installment exposes deep celestial nurseries while celebrating international technological advancements across space exploration.

What is World Space Week 2026? 

World Space Week is a global event that runs annually from October 4 to October 10. The official theme for World Space Week 2026 is "Rocket Revolution" designated by the United Nations. 

According to official Google Doodles documentation the interactive feature honors the technology behind awe-inspiring astrophotography. It gives billions of web users a clear window into active astronomical research.

World Space Week 2026 Day 1: The Rosebud Nebula

On Day 1, which fell on October 6, the spotlight was on the Rosebud Nebula. It is a reflection nebula situated at 3300 light years away from Earth. Interstellar dust clouds block energy from over 130 young stars inside the nebula. And this process heats up the surrounding materials which produces a unique pinkish glow resembling a closed flower bud.

With the help of the near-infrared capabilities of the JWST telescope, scientists were able to go through thick stellar curtains that prevented older telescopes from getting clear pictures. The piece shows how real physical gas structures fall down under gravitational pressure to form new solar systems.

World Space Week 2026 Google Doodle Day 2: The Carina Nebula

World Space Week 2026 Google Doodle The Day 2 Google Doodle released on October 8 highlights the gigantic Carina Nebula. This highly active star nursery measures more than 300 light-years across and is one of the biggest such formations visible on our planet. The immense walls of gas are illuminated by the ultraviolet rays of newly-born stars.

"More than a dozen O-type stars live inside, and they are the hottest, most massive, and brightest stars in the universe," noted Google in its official release notes. These huge stellar engines give shape to immense gas clouds in the form of pillars that scientists call 'cosmic cliffs.'

Multiple days are needed for the logo campaign in order to maintain engagement for the entire observance period. Complex astrophysical ideas can be broken down into segments to make them easier for general audiences to understand.
