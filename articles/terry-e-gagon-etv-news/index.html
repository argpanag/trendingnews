Terry E. Gagon (June 20, 1941 – October 25, 2026)

Dr. Terry Ersel Gagon, 85, passed away peacefully, surrounded by family in Atlanta, GA on October 25, 2026. The son of Ersel Loren Gagon (Myton, UT) and Yetta Lukton (Denver, CO), he was born at home on Midsummer Eve, 1941, in the coal mining camp of Hiawatha, Utah, Carbon County, where his father worked as a mechanic in the mine. He spent his first eleven years there, learning to hunt on horseback, fish and camp with his parents, and make the best out of difficult circumstances. When the opportunity arose, the family moved to Price, UT where Terry attended grade school and developed a love of books, science, and music that would carry him from the coal mine to the University of Utah Medical School to a residency at Johns Hopkins and beyond. Throughout his life he never abandoned his origins or his admiration of the people of Price and Salt Lake. He carried the landscape and its larger than life characters with him, recounting stories that delighted and amazed his children and grandchildren.

Forged in the canyons and deserts of Carbon County, Terry’s quiet self-assuredness developed into a career as a renowned and gifted psychiatrist. He started at Walter Reed Army Medical Center where he was called upon by soldiers and generals alike to help navigate the most difficult challenges. He possessed a humility and ability to meet people where they were that made him wise beyond his years. His success as a healer owed a great deal to his humble childhood, a time bathed in legends, hardships and the stories told by his own father, Ersel, a wry son of an Irish sheepherder. Terry wasn’t your typical psychiatrist. He could ride a horse, lasso a lawn dart off a roof, shoot a rifle with dead eye precision, drive a double-clutch truck, navigate an icy mountain road, build just about anything, use any carpenter’s tool, and surprise you with unexpected expertise. You could count on him in troubling times.

Terry loved medicine and was called to the profession after a doctor saved his life with emergency surgery as a young teenager. Without the means to attend college, he achieved the title of National Merit Scholar and was offered several scholarships and appointments. Due to the unwillingness of a parent to receive the financial aid that would have allowed him to leave home, he stayed in Price, attending Carbon College for two years before his good friend Frank Karas kidnapped him and brought him to Salt Lake City to begin a new chapter. Terry found a room at a boarding house, took a job at a smelting plant, and enrolled at the University of Utah, completing his undergraduate degree and his medical degree at the same time on a March of Dimes scholarship.

It was there at the university where he met his first wife Charlene, the mother of his first three children. They moved to Baltimore where Terry worked at Baltimore City Hospital and held a residency at Johns Hopkins. A reservist in the Army, he was called to serve after their first child Genevieve was born, completing basic training in Texas before shipping off to Germany. Coming stateside after three years running the medical clinic in Prüm during the Vietnam war and playing violin in the local orchestra, he returned to the D.C. area to complete his medical residency in psychiatry and begin training as a psychoanalyst. During these years, sons Colin and Jeremy were born. The family moved to Great Falls, VA where Terry continued his army career while moonlighting in a home office he built with wood he harvested from an old barn. On any given weekend he would attend to pressing animal matters like fetching escaped ponies, fending off rabid foxes and raccoons, setting fence, or clearing snakes from the pathway to his psychiatric office. He might work on an old car or help load a horse into a trailer. He and his pack of barking dogs would mow the expansive hilly property with his John Deere mower. He would see patients on weekend mornings, his children instructed to dive into the bushes to avoid contact as they drove up. At night, he would play Beethoven, Rachmaninoff, Grieg and Joplin, filling the house with music. Throughout his life he would exclaim, “My whole life is determined by animals”, but it was equally shaped by music and unlikely intersections of the rural and urbane.

When Terry transferred from Walter Reed to Fort Gordon in Augusta, GA, his life changed dramatically. Deemed a capable leader, he became Chief of Psychiatry at Ford Gordon and found artistic fulfillment as a section violinist in the Augusta Symphony. It was there his first marriage ended and he met his spiritual soul mate, Eleanor Law, marrying in Washington, D.C. on a snowy January day, surrounded by friends and colleagues. They wasted no time in embarking on an adventurous new life, settling in Decatur, GA and soon after, traveling to Peru to welcome their beautiful daughter Molly into the family. He was proud of his children, all of them animal-lovers and accomplished musicians with their father’s sense of humor.

Retiring from the Army as colonel, Terry completed his career with several leadership posts including Clinical Director of GMHI in Atlanta, GA, and Clinical Director of Northwest Georgia Regional Hospital in Rome, GA. During those years, the family returned to Price annually to visit Terry’s father Ersel and revisit favorite pathways through the state. When he retired, the family moved to Draper for a few years while his youngest finished high school. There, he reconnected with long-missed family members and enjoyed the view from the foothills of the Wasatch mountains. With Molly grown and off to college, he and Eleanor made one last migration to the place where their marriage began, Decatur, GA. They survived Covid, cancer, and old age until he fell ill. He was adored by his family and by the many souls and students he inspired, entertained and comforted.

Terry is preceded in death by his father Ersel, mother Yetta and younger brother, Darel Gagon (d. 2026). He is survived by his loving wife Eleanor, children Molly (Nash), Jeremy (Tiffany), Colin (Elizabeth), and Genevieve (Ted), his grandchildren Angus and Lucy, sister-in-law Meryle, cousin Janice Calabrese, and nephews Shane, Mike, and Brad Gagon and their children.
