Former Liberal MP Daryl Maguire jailed for visa fraud

Former Liberal MP Daryl Maguire has been jailed over a lucrative cash-for-visas fraud while he was sitting in the NSW parliament.

Maguire was found guilty in July of conspiring to commit an offence. He was sentenced on Friday morning in the NSW District Court to three years’ prison with a one-year non-parole period.

The 67-year-old, half of the “love circle” that cost Gladys Berejiklian her NSW premiership, conspired to create bogus applications for 10 Chinese nationals as he purported to employ them at businesses in Wagga Wagga in the NSW Riverina region, the trial heard.

The former Wagga Wagga MP, who held the seat for almost two decades, denied committing fraud and claimed he merely helped businesses looking for foreign workers.

Maguire’s associate Maggie Wang, also known as Maggie Logan, was earlier sentenced over her role in the plot.

Maguire identified businesses that could act as purported visa sponsors before introducing them to Wang, the jury heard. Wang would pay business owners $30,000 to $60,000 stuffed into envelopes for their contribution.

A real estate agent, vineyard, accountant and furniture store were some of the firms the imported workers would be employed by for two years. Many migrants never worked at the firms, the court heard.

After a meeting at Sydney Airport in May 2013, the owner of the Wagga Wagga RSL club decided not to partake in the visa proposal after a potential Chinese applicant could not say exactly where he was from in the country.

The business owner was encouraged to lie to immigration officials if they turned up asking where the worker was, the jury was told.

Prosecutors told the jury Maguire feigned ignorance after he was told by one concerned business owner of dodgy cash payments being offered to bring overseas workers to Australia.

“It’s a bit odd they’re paying me cash; what is going on here?” one of the business owners asked him in early 2015.

“I don’t want to know about it,” the court heard that the then Wagga Wagga MP said.

After serving 12 months of his sentence, Maguire will be released from custody on a two-year recognisance bond. He must be of good behaviour for two further years.

Maguire has a history of public controversies.

In August last year, he was sentenced to 10 months’ jail for giving misleading evidence to the corruption watchdog about a multimillion-dollar Sydney property and the $720,000 in commission he expected to receive.

He was swiftly released on bail pending an appeal, which was subsequently successful. In March, his conviction was quashed.

Maguire resigned from politics in August 2018 after public airing of that investigation, including covert phone taps played at an ICAC inquiry.

Two years later, he made headlines over revelations he’d been in a secret “close personal relationship” for at least five years with Berejiklian while she was treasurer and then premier.

Berejiklian is not accused of any wrongdoing.
