The Maple Leafs’ load management schedule for their two goaltenders begins with a 50-50 split. 

While the first three assignments were easy to easy to predict — Sergei Bobrovsky in the season opener against Montreal, Anthony Stolarz on the back-to-back versus the New York Islanders and a return to Bob on two days’ rest to face second division rival with Ottawa — this week was more murky. 

Coach Jim Hiller cleared that up a lot on Tuesday morning, naming Stolarz to face the Nashville Predators in the fourth and final night of the homestand.

Stolarz has the team’s lone win in three one-goal decisions, making 26 saves in the 2-1 score and part of the thinking must have been not to sit him eight days before Thursday in Vegas begins the Leafs’ three-game trip to the U.S. Southwest. 

“We have a bit of a plan, kind of mapped things out,” Hiller said Tuesday morning. “That’s nothing that can’t change (but) I’m not going to go into (how far into the season). I’ll leave it at that.” 

Hiller should have few unknowns in giving Stolarz more work in the early going. He’s 32 and very well adjusted to working around Bobrovsky from their Stanley Cup days in Florida.

Bobrovsky, good for 50-plus starts the past few seasons, knows his own body best at age 38. Hiller hinted the data-centric Leaf hockey department has already delved into what starts make sense for both, as has netminding coach Curtis Sandford.

“Workloads, analytics, there are lots of different things that go into it,” Hiller said. “We have many people working on that and coach Sandford is the obvious point man.”

General manager John Chayka said a few days ago that Bobrovsky wanted to make sure Stolarz was getting some quality starts through the 84-game season, which the boss saw as very unselfish on the future Hall of Famer’s part.

Another factor in the efficient division of labour for Toronto and all NHL clubs is now having a full-time third goalie. He‘s for emergency back-up purposes, but also someone to take the other net when a starter sits the morning skate, as Stolarz did Tuesday, travel with the team and step into a post-practice shooting gallery. For the Leafs, that’s 27-year-old Brendan Bonello from Mississauga. 

“He gets a lot of work,” Hiller agreed. “You can see how the (EBUG) position has evolved, the pre-ice, the post-ice, it was a long time coming. The reason we’re mapping it out is you don’t want to burn anybody out.”
