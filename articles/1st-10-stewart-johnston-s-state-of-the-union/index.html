1st & 10 | Stewart Johnston's State of the Union

TheTools · 15.11.2026 09:05 · source · permalink

CFL Commissioner Stewart Johnston met with the media Friday morning in Calgary; photos and graphics courtesy CFL.a

CALGARY -- DISCLAIMER -- The following story contains details of Canadian Football League commissioner Stewart Johnston's annual state of the union address here at the 113th Grey Cup.

Now, if you're already convinced the league's boss is the devil incarnate, a man who yanks wings off butterflies, chews with his mouth open and is absolutely hell-bent on destroying everything about the CFL with his rule changes, new playoff format and the like, well, what follows likely won't be worth your time.

In fact, we'll give those who are aligned with the above description a few moments here to gather their things and click out of this piece if need be...

Merci beaucoup.

OK, and so for those who remain -- even the cynics and skeptics -- we begin the first edition of our annual offseason 1st & 10 column by pulling out some of the highlights of the CFL's 15th commissioner's annual address for further discussion.

Johnston met with a room full of media for almost 50 minutes this morning at the BMO Centre, opening with a 17-minute address and then fielding questions for the duration.

He spoke clearly and confidently about his vision for the CFL and its potential, hammering home that idea several times and opening his remarks with this:

"After the season we have just experienced, I have never been more confident in the promise of our league. We will continue to think big about our future; not recklessly, not by walking away from the things that make Canadian football distinctly ours but by refusing to put limits on what this league can become.

"... The 'C' in the CFL matters," he added later. "Every game is a Canadian story. Canadian cities, Canadian players, Canadian communities and increasingly Canadian culture."

"I'm thrilled with what we've accomplished in 18 months, and I have never been more optimistic about the future of the Canadian Football League. The opportunity in front of us is enormous."

Here is what jumped out from our spot in the audience...

THE CFL NUMBERS

-Attendance was down one percent from 2025 and television audiences dropped seven percent, with Johnston attributing some of that to the FIFA World Cup and the temporary displacement of B.C. Lions and Toronto Argos from their facilities. Even with the World Cup buzz, he indicated the TV numbers still averaged over 500,000 viewers per game.

-Viewership among 18-24-year-olds grew 16 percent and the four playoff games to date delivered an average audience of 1.1 million -- an eight percent increase over last year.

-Game day revenues increased by two percent, the fourth consecutive year of growth coming out of COVID.

-60 percent of the games were one-score contests into the last three minutes.

-The new play clock timing this year improved the flow and tempo of the game, adding an average of three more offensive plays without increasing the overall duration.

-The CFL set a record for partnership revenue last year and grew it another eight percent in 2026.

-CFL content generated 11 million views on YouTube in 2026 -- up 77 percent year over year; Instagram views reached 45 million -- up 153 percent; TikTok following increased 111 percent.

-Sunday's Grey Cup will be broadcast on TSN, RDS, CTV, Crave in Canada and on DAZN globally.

-The league and clubs contributed $4.6 million to amateur football in Canada.

THE RULE CHANGES DISCUSSION

The league implemented the new 35-second play clock this year, eliminated ties and points on missed field goals or punts that leave the end zone untouched. But it's the changes planned for next year that have generated the most chatter -- and anger -- among some CFL fans.

Next year the field will be shortened from 110 yards to 100 yards with the elimination of the 55-yard line. As well, end zones will shrink from 20 yards to 15 yards so they are universal across the league, and the goal posts will be moved to the back of the end zone.

The CFL will also introduce a new playoff format where eight of nine teams will make postseason.

Johnston on the rationale behind the new format:

"... designed to create more games of consequence in the back half of our season. Teams will be battling to make the playoffs, to host a playoff game and earn a bye to the Grey Cup Semi-Finals.

"That matters because we saw the other side of that this year. Competitive imbalance in the East left too many late-season games without enough at stake."

And on the angst these changes have caused with some fans, the commissioner spoke about the need for the league to embrace change while still celebrating its uniqueness.

"Not everyone is going to agree with every step we make and that is OK," he said. "If you're going to think big occasionally you're going to create debate.

"For more than a century Canadian football has been extraordinarily good at celebrating its history. We should be. This league has magnificent traditions and we so many of them reflected this week in Calgary. But tradition without trajectory is merely history. Tradition without momentum is nostalgia and tradition without vision only looks back. Do not our history to become a ceiling on our ambition. Our story has to give us the confidence to build the future."

"We're not standing still," he said during another chunk of his speech "Since becoming commissioner, I've said we must relentlessly ask ourselves one question: how can we increase the entertainment value of an already great game?

"... We can identify a problem and defend the status quo, or we can identify a problem and try and make better. We are choosing better."

EXPANSION CHATTER

The CFL has been hinting about expansion in this country for eons, way back to the early 1980s and the conditional franchise being awarded to the Halifax-Dartmouth area.

This time -- truly -- it feels like the expansion chatter surrounding Quebec City is real with TSN's Dave Naylor doing some excellent work on that story earlier this week in this piece.

Johnston was reluctant to make concrete statements but did confirm he has been to Quebec City and met with a potential ownership group there.

"It's an outstanding market, it's an outstanding football market," he said. "The vision for a 10th team in Quebec City or anywhere else is to have a successful franchise. It's not a 10th team just to have a 10th team. We need to have a successful franchise that will be a part of a successful CFL.

"We have not commented publicly on how those talks have been progressing other than to say this: we have been engaged with very productive conversations with a local ownership group in Quebec City. We're encouraged by those and we can say Quebec City has emerged as the leading contender for a 10th franchise in the CFL.

"There's still a lot of work to do and when there's something more to report, we'll hand it over to you."

He also indicated there is more interest, too, from outside of Quebec.

"We have a number of conversations going on right now and have been over the last year," Johnston said. "Some very interesting potential ownership groups and some very interesting communities that are interested in talking about the potential of the CFL to come there."

FYI

The CFL's 2027 season will begin earlier -- a schedule should be released in the next few weeks, from what we understand -- and that means other changes as well.

The CFL Draft Combine will be a week earlier, while the draft itself and training camps are also moving up two weeks.

Johnston also said the CFL has no plans to pause its season in 2028 when the Summer Olympics are being held, adding: "We think our league and our teams are strong enough to play through the Olympics. 10 on a team."

The CFL and CFLPA are working with Football Canada to allow CFL players to attend the first professional flag football camp, which will run from November 29-December 5th in California.

Two of 20 players attending will be Blue Bombers in receiver Nic Demski and quarterback Taylor Elgersma.
