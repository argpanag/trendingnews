Wales match report

Here’s Ben Fisher’s report from Cardiff.

Wales were plucky. They ran a lot and showed plenty of heart. But they also showed very little quality, apart from two stunning saves from Ward. What a shame, then, that it was his howler that cost them the match.

Let’s hear from Ethan Ampidu, Wales’ best player tonight:

To be honest, we were sat in half time thinking that if we brought the energy levels up we could take all three points. So dissappointed with that.

We wanted to show who we really are and we did that. At times we were sloppy and gave the ball away.

In November we’ve got two more games. Six points to play for, that’s our aim.

The level that group A comes with, we wanted to show that we belong here.

Greece remain unbeaten in group A2.

They’re on eight points with two wins and two draws. The Netherlands have the same record but have scored two more goals than the Greeks.

Germany under Jurgen Klopp are not firing on all cylinders. They have just one win from four games, including one defeat.

Down in the basement is Serbia who have yet to register a point.

What’s the story with Haaland?

Well, he asked to be subbed off around the 67th minute. Was he limping just before that? I’ll let you make up your mind.

City fans, your week could be getting a whole lot worse…

Speaking of points, what does that mean group A4?

Portugal have a full house with 12 points having all four of their matches.

Denmark are in second with six.

Wales are tied with Norway on three points, but they have an inferior goal difference so sit bottom.

Here’s the Welsh skipper, Ben Davies:

I didn’t feel like there was a whole lot in the game to be honest. A tough way to concede.

We’d have loved to have had more (than their three points from four matches) but we’re playing quality opposition. I’m very proud of the group.

We can’t fault the effort that’s in this group.

Full-time: Wales 0-1 Denmark

Boos ring out around Cardiff as the final whistle sounds.

Spare a thought for Ward. It was his howler that led to the Danish penalty, but let’s not forget that he made two outrageous saves to keep his team in it before then.

Francisco Conceição has been shown a second yellow and is off!

Portugal will have to hold onto this lead with 10 men.

90+1 min: The referee lost his footing in Cardiff, which brings about the biggest cheer from the crowd in this second half. Sums it up.

90 min: A long throw looks like Wales’ best hope here. But another one from the right comes to nothing. They get the chance to hoof a cross from almost the halfway line, but that is easy pickings for the keeper who got a good long sight of it. There’ll be four minutes of added time. They could play for another four hours, I’m not sure Wales would score playing like this.

GOAL! Portugal 2-1 Norway (Goncalo Ramos, 80)

Who needs Ronaldo? With ten minutes left in Porto, Goncalo Ramos finishes off Joao Felix’s assist. Some really neat interplay in the build up with the ball pinging between playters. The dink into the box, the deft touch back and the simple finish. Really neat.

84 min: Getting desperate for Wales now. A long throw from the right comes to nothing. They don’t look like scoring, though their high press – absent before this – has created a change in possession. It’s recycled back but then they lose it again. Just not enough quality on show tonight I’m afraid.

80 min: Ooooh… That was close from Denmark’s keeper. A bouncing ball lost momentum just around the edge of his area. But Hermansen grabbed it on the right side of the line.

Hojlund is off. Bellamy is waving his arms around. He needs a response from his team.

77 min: About ten minutes ago, Haaland was subbed off for Norway (they’re still locked at 1-1 with Portugal). That’s not something that normally happens. Is he injured? Nothing confirmed (or denied) yet. Still, one to keep an eye on.

GOAL! Wales 0-1 Denmark (Hojlund, 75)

A stutter, a delay, and a cool dink into the net after sending Ward the wrong way! Denmark take the lead and their main man celebrates with barely a smile. Ward has had a howler there. Football can be so cruel sometimes.

73 min: Oh Danny Ward! What have you done?! A nightmare for the keeper. A tame pass back is shovelled on and he has no idea that Isaksen is right there, inside his box. Ward tries to make the covering tackle but instead he slams into the Dane and gives away a super soft penalty. Could that be a red card? No. He’ll get the chance to redeem himself.

GOAL! Netherlands 2-1 Serbia (Zirkzee, 72)

Netherlands take the lead! Zirkzee, on as a half-time sub, taps his team ahead. Some neat play around the box had the ball worked to the back post on the left before it was cut back. Zirkzee just couldn’t miss from less than a metre out.

70 min: Denmark break and Hojlund is released down the left. He’s really quick and seems to ghost past the defenders frantically chasing back. He’s on the angle so a shot isn’t on. Instead he fires a low ball across the face of goal. Would have been a tap in. If anyone in white was there.

70 min: Like Germany, Netherlands are bossing the ball, and have had far more attempts at goal, but they can’t break the deadlock with Serbia.

63 min: Johnson, at the back post, lashes over. Brilliance from Williams down the left as he beat Isaksen and delivered a cross from deep. It went over everyone and found Johnson free. It took a while for him to bring the ball under control and perhaps he had more time than he thought. With the ball still coming down he swiped at it but that was always going to be difficult to keep down.

57 min: Germany have had 64% possession, have had 16 attempts at goal to Greece’s two, and have completed almost three times as many passes, but they still can’t break the deadlock.

Meanwhile Wales play their best footy of the night. A long ball switch from Thomas, after brilliant skill, finds Johnson. The winger, running at pace, links with Cullen inside the area who tries to flick the ball on for himself before shooting, but the cover tackle sees it out for a corner.

56 min: WARD MAKES ANOTHER STUNNING SAVE! This one was even better than the first. Brilliant Danish play creates an opportunity for Isaksen on the edge of the area, who times his late run to perfection and meets the dinked cross with his head at pace. He couldn’t have played that better, directing it to the bottom left corner as he was looking at it. But Ward, at full stretch, dived across and tipped it onto the post to keep it out. Brilliant goal keeping.

55 min: Williams darts down the right flank but runs into a corner. So they switch to the left where Thomas whips in a lovely ball at pace. Cullen, inside the area, meets it, but does that come off his shoudler? His back? It’s not his head. At least not enough of it to trouble the keeper. Half a chance goes begging.

GOAL! Rep of Ireland 1-1 Israel (Dor Peretz, 47)

Israel equalise.

50 min: WHAT A SAVE! How has Ward kept Wales level? From about a metre away, Højlund slides in and meets a cross across the ground from the right. He gets it on target – how could he not from there – but the stiff left left leg of Ward kept it out. Great stop!

Just had another look at Kristoffer Ajer’s stunner for Norway.

Not even his teammates could believe it!

45 min: We’re back in Cardiff. Wales need a bit more zip on the ball. Ampadu flies into a tackle on the impressive Daramy close to the touchline. There’s no faulting Wales’ energy off the ball. Time to see a bit of quality on it.

Half-time recap

Just had a whip around and taken stock of the games.

Portugal v Norway
Norway struck first through Kristoffer Ajer, but Portugal answered almost immediately with Joao Cancelo. The quick exchange has turned a game that looked poised to settle into a tactical battle into a more open one.

Netherlands v Serbia
Teun Koopmeiners put the Netherlands ahead, but Serbia responded within four minutes through Mihajlo Cvetković. That makes for a lively first half where the Dutch found a breakthrough, but could not make it stick.

Greece v Germany
Germany thought they had taken the lead through Jonathan Burkardt, only for VAR to rule it out. Greece have had to absorb pressure, with Kostas Tzolakis called into action early; the disallowed goal will feel like a reprieve, but Germany are finding openings and seem to be the more dominant side.

Republic of Ireland v Israel
Troy Parrott’s penalty has given Ireland the lead in this closed-door match in Serbia.

Wales v Denmark
Wales began brightly, with Brennan Johnson getting on the end of a Daniel James cross and the hosts finding space down the left. Denmark grew into it and threatened through Rasmus Højlund and Gustav Isaksen, but Pierre-Emile Højbjerg’s early injury withdrawal has disrupted their midfield. A goalless first half is just about fair.

Only mitigating factor in the penalty shout is that Isaksen did get the shot away just before he was cut down.

But only just.
