O'Prey: Mageean in thoughts during Portaferry win

TheTools · 16.11.2026 21:08 · source · permalink

Portaferry manager Connor O'Prey said that he hopes that their brilliant Ulster SHC semi-final win over Dunloy on Sunday provided a little bit of joy in troubling times for the family of Ciara Mageean.

The 2024 1500m gold medallist died earlier this month at the age of 34 after a battle with bowel cancer. Her loss was particularly felt in her home village in county Down. Mageean played camogie for the Portaferry club and also assisted with the senior hurling team under previous manager Karol Keating.

Sunday's Ulster semi-final victory against the Antrim champions in Armagh was attended by some of Mageean's family, including husband Thomas Moran who she married in October.

Portaferry's story since their sole Ulster title in 2014 has been mostly of near misses against some of the province's big names, but they made a mockery of that with a dominant 1-25 to 1-14 win over the Cuchullains.

For former Down half-forward O'Prey, he hopes the result provided a sliver of light in dark times for the Mageean family.

"It's been emotionally draining and then you're trying to weigh up the balance of trying to do a job professionally from a hurling perspective but, you know, there's the emotional side of things as well," O'Prey told RTÉ's Inside Sport.

"Each of the papers that are contacting you looking for an interview, you know fair play to the players - they've answered all the questions that's been put to them in relation to Ciara and everyone spoke about her in such a wonderful way.

"We're obviously a grieving community at the moment; winning yesterday is not going to do a massive amount for Ciara's family, but they are a great Portaferry hurling family so hopefully they'll take a wee bit of joy in the victory yesterday."

Sleacht Néill await in the Ulster final on 28 November and O'Prey said that Sunday's performance ensures that they will carry plenty of confidence into the showpiece.

"You take away the two goals that we conceded in the first 15 minutes and you're maybe nine points to three (ahead) and you're a bit more comfortable.

"The way we finished the first half, three points on the bounce to get a three-point lead and then the way we finished the second half as well... I was just looking at the stats there this morning, I think we created 42 chances to their 22 and only conceded 2-14.

"You take away the two soft goals, you know, it was a serious defensive effort put in, starting from the corner-forwards really and working its way back.

"We're glad we got the win. I think it's been coming in Ulster and in fairness throughout the championship campaign, we've been putting up really high scores around 28 to 32 points a game and that carried on yesterday, thankfully."
