Shares of Advanced Micro Devices (NASDAQ:AMD) were taking a dive on Thursday morning in response to a short-seller report, spotlighting its business with China.

The news comes a few days after the U.S. slapped tighter chip export restrictions on countries, including those in Southeast Asia, known to funnel chips into China.

As of 11:16 a.m. ET, AMD was down 6.8% on the news.

AMD gets nailed by a short report

Hunterbrook Capital, a short-seller firm, said this morning that AMD has significant exposure to Chinese companies that are subject to U.S. export restrictions.

Hunterbrook found that Chinese server maker IEIT received updated AMD firmware even after it was blacklisted by the U.S., and said that the expanded restrictions could affect AMD's Singapore business and relationships with other Chinese chip companies.

Hunterbrook also said it was short AMD and long a basket of comparable securities.

What it means for AMD

Short-seller reports often receive exaggerated market reactions, but they sometimes uncover real issues facing a company.

Like Nvidia, AMD has sizable exposure to Southeast Asia and China, and it took an $800 million write-down on its MI308 AI chips in April 2025 in response to tighter chip export restrictions.

In 2025, 22.4% of AMD's revenue came from China and Hong Kong. While that doesn't necessarily indicate any wrongdoing, it does make the company exposed to tighter export restrictions and scrutiny over its China business.

Additionally, AMD stock is much more expensive than Nvidia, making it more vulnerable to a crash as high expectations are baked into the stock.

AMD has not responded to the short report, but investors should keep an eye on the issue, as export restrictions will remain a factor regardless of the accuracy of Hunterbrook's claims.
