Hawks duo to be sanctioned for ugly ump act and fans face hefty bans as fresh vision emerges

TheTools · 20.09.2026 05:53 · source · permalink

Two Hawthorn players are reportedly set to face AFL scrutiny over their conduct towards an umpire.

It comes a day after irate Hawthorn fans pelted umpires with bottles as they attempted to leave the MCG after “one of the bravest calls” seen in an AFL final.

The Herald Sun’s Jon Ralph reports senior Hawthorn players James Sicily and Tom Barrass are “looking at” misconduct fines following the aftermath of the heated prelim.

Hawthorn co-captain Sicily and Barrass were seen aggressively approaching field umpire Hayden Meyer after the final siren.

Vision shows Barrass ran from 20 metres away to remonstrate with Meyer, while Sicily appeared to have choice words for the official.

Ralph reports Sicily “in particular used abusive language”.

Earlier on Sunday, veteran journalist Damian Barrett suggested Barrass and Sicily could face scrutiny for their actions.

“Tom Barrass, I feel he’s got at least a question to (answer) from the AFL of what he was doing in that moment,” Barrett said on Nine’s Sunday Footy Show.

“It’s demonstrable body language towards the umpire Hayden Meyer ... Then you see James Sicily also conversing with Meyer post-match; he’s captain ... What he said, I think, is worthy of discussion.”

But Hawks coach Sam Mitchell was unaware of what had unfolded in those immediate minutes.

“I didn’t know that. I haven’t seen that,” he said of the on-field confrontation.

“The umpires have got a very difficult job.

“How they performed in key moments will be reviewed by them in the same way we review how we played in key moments.”

Umpire Jacob Mollison was seen attempting to shield his fellow whistleblowers as they left the field of play on Saturday night, and even tried to point out those responsible to security.

The attack on the men in charge came after field umpire Meyer made the big call to penalise Jarman Impey for holding the ball with less than a minute to play in the preliminary final.

The AFL released a statement on Sunday regarding the looming sanctions for those found to have thrown projectiles at the umpires.

“The AFL and MCC security will work with Vic Pol to identify the bottle throwers directed at the umpires last night,” the league communication reads.

“If identified, they will be banned from games and the stadium for a minimum 5-years.

“The passion for footy is unmatched in Australian sport, however what transpired last night by a number of individuals was dangerous and crossed the line.”

Nine’s Owen Leonard reported Victoria Police had already fined a 40-year-old man from Croydon $418 for throwing a bottle, and he may now face bans from the AFL and MCG.

Three other incidents involving violence are also under investigation with 10 fans evicted by police.

Lion Hugh McCluggage converted with the shot on goal to put Brisbane into next weekend’s AFL decider.

Despite Fox Footy’s experts unanimously agreeing it was “100% the right call”, Hawthorn fans took their frustrations out in disgusting scenes post-match.

“Hawthorn fans have pelted the ground with bottles in the outrage of it all,” Gerard Whateley said.

“There’s 75,000 Hawthorn fans right now that think that they’ve been hard done by,” Garry Lyon added.

While Impey “paid the ultimate price” on the scoreboard, it was the umpires who paid it in the aftermath.

“You never want to see anything thrown at the umpires,” Fox Footy’s Kath Loughnan said.

“For the kids watching on and for the adults, don’t do that!

“You don’t need to throw anything at them!”

“The players have to be brave, the umpires have to be brave in heated moments as well; late in games in big games,” Jonathan Brown added.

“Quite often you see those free kicks not paid.”

When asked specifically about the bottle throwing, Mitchell replied: “I saw that on the ground! What happened there?”

“(Sighs) The emotion of sport is why we love it so much.

“All sports, but ours in particular, the beauty of the game is the emotion that makes you feel and how that expresses itself is really hard.

“I had no idea what I wanted to do at the end of the game. Part of me wanted to break down in tears, part of me wanted to scream with rage. Part of me wanted to throw chairs … so when you’re in the stands, you need to continue to be feeling that emotion because that’s why we love it. But how it manifests itself, we shouldn’t be throwing things on the field and doing those types of things.

“But the passion in the stand and the passion of the game is why we love it so much.

“So I hope for the vast majority, it was felt responsibly.”

Fox Footy’s Gerard Healy, who was calling the game for SEN, described the fan behaviour as “cr*p”.

“This is rubbish,” he said.

“This is cr*p - free kick was there every day of the week. Not sure what they are blowing about.

“That is rubbish from the Hawthorn supporters.”
