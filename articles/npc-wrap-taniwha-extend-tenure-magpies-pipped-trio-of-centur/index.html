NPC wrap: Taniwha extend tenure, Magpies pipped, trio of centurions celebrate

TheTools · 20.09.2026 18:11 · source · permalink

It is going to take something special to wrestle the Ranfurly Shield away from Northland.

With another convincing defence under their belt, after Northland saw off Waikato's challenge in Whangārei 34-17, the prized Log o' Wood now appears destined to spend the summer in the Far North.

The historic tenure now stretches to two defences, following a 20-10 win over Tasman last month.

Blues halfback Sam Nock celebrated 100 games for Northland with a contentious but crucial try, weaving through a splintered maul as Waikato desperately pleaded obstruction.

A spectacular finish by Tepaea Cook-Savage in the corner with three Taniwha converging reduced the margin to 10 and offered the Mooloos some hope.

But when Australian import Lachlan Hooper struck with his second for the holders, the gate was shut as Northland notched their first ever win over Waikato in a shield match.

Their one remaining defence will come against Counties Manukau on 26 September.

While Northland's dream run continues, Hawke's Bay's came to a crushing halt on Sunday against North Harbour.

The Magpies had looked untouchable across their opening six games, but the signs it would not be their day came early as Bailyn Sullivan was sent to the bin for an ugly spear tackle with his side hot on attack.

Brother Zarn kept Hawke's Bay in front for most of the first half after bashing his way through the Harbour defence to score, but the visitors snatched the lead back late with an Israel Leota try on the siren to make it 12-10 at the break.

A lovely assist by Folau Fakatava sent Geoff Cridge over on his century appearance before the dynamic halfback went in himself to establish a 12 point advantage.

However, three quick tries, including a double to the dangerous Kade Banks handed Hawke's Bay their first loss of the season 31-24.

Expect these sides to meet again.

Round seven kicked off with a stunning upset on a sodden Thursday night in Palmerston North, where the Manawatū Turbos edged Taranaki 27-25 courtesy of a last minute penalty.

It was far from the prettiest rugby we saw during round seven, but marked a memorable night for Manawatū.

The 'Naki appeared to have the result sealed when Reece MacDonald got caught upright, and the Bulls swarmed to hold him up. However, instead of forcing the turnover, replacement prop Jarred Proffit inexplicably jumped onto the maul to concede a bizarre penalty with time almost up.

MacDonald stepped up and nailed the penalty kick to steal a famous win.

Down in Dunedin, Otago and Tasman put on a 90-point thriller.

Former All Black Shannon Frizzell marked his return for Tasman with an industrious 40 minutes at number eight, while winger Kyren Taumoefolau took his season try scoring tally to 12.

Both backlines were unencumbered under the roof, with Mako fullback Maloni Kunawave continuing to impress with his electric feet, but it was Otago's offloading enterprise which proved too tough to contain for Tasman.

The hosts produced some dazzling tries in the 57-33 win, the returning Finn Hurley's sensational 50-metre solo effort capping the victory.

In a meeting of two teams battling for momentum in 2026, Auckland survived a scare from Counties Manukau after seemingly having things in control before a Counties comeback at Eden Park.

With such potent attacking threats in their arsenal, Auckland weren't expected to struggle as much as they have this campaign, but things began to click on Saturday with their dynamic back three of Harlyn Saunoa, Xavier Tito-Harris and Rico Simpson running riot in the opening 50 minutes.

However, the Steelers refused to lie down for their cross city brothers, and led by Josh Gray scored three tries on the trot to square the ledger at 33-apiece with 10 to play.

It took a late Cody Vai'i try to secure the much-needed 43-33 win for Auckland - just their second of the season.

Elsewhere, things just keep getting worse for Wellington.

The 2024 champions find themselves winless and at the bottom of the table after another humiliating loss, this time at the hands of a ruthless Canterbury side.

Since losing the shield to Northland in round four, Canterbury have found their rhythm in what is an ominous sign for the competition after their emphatic 66-19 drubbing of the Lions in Christchurch.

The third player to mark 100 NPC appearances at the weekend, Daniel Lienert-Brown celebrated his milestone in bittersweet fashion after he was forced to leave the field with an arm injury in the first half.

Auckland wing Harlyn Saunoa dives in to score against Counties Manukau at Eden Park.

The tone was set after 90 seconds when the Lions handed Canterbury five points on a platter, a shocking overthrow landing right in the arms of Corey Kellow to simply dive over the chalk for the opener.

From there, Canterbury continued the carnage at almost a point a minute, one-test All Black Dallas McLeod dotting down twice while Louis Chapman controlled things masterfully at halfback.

Wrapping up another eventful round, the Stags kept their season alive with their second win, producing a boilover against Bay of Plenty in the deep south.

The Steamers struck first in Invercargill through Seuseu Naitoa Ah Kuoi before Cole Forbes set up Rory van Vugt with a deft cross-field kick for a 12-7 lead.

That would prove the final points for the afternoon for Bay of Plenty, as the Stags piled on six unanswered tries to clinch a comprehensive 43-12 victory.

Southland were led by inspirational skipper Sean Withy who made several damaging breaks, while Mika Muliaina is coming into his own in the 10 jersey.

Despite suffering their first loss, Hawke's Bay keep their spot at the top of the table, with Harbour nipping right at their heels in second.

Northland strengthened their title credentials to hold down third spot, while Otago round out the top four. However, the sleeping giant that is Canterbury appears well and truly awake, and primed for a strong late run.

Try of the week

Otago produced some pearlers in Dunedin against the Mako, but the pick of the pack came courtesy of two magic out the back offloads by Lucas Casey and Jake Te Iwi to send Riley Lucas away for a scorcher.

Commentary call of the week

"Opting for a tap play, we have seen a lot of this in the NPC, and I'm loving it!": Taylah Johnson shows her appreciation for Counties attacking intent.

Rennie's radar

The brutal ball carrier that is Brayden Iose shone in the wet for Manawatu. His versatility and consistency could well have him in All Black conversations.
