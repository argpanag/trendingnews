Ukraine fires 1,336 drones into Russia in one of war's largest attacks, Moscow says

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:12 · source · permalink

The attack appears to be one of the largest-ever overnight barrages by Kyiv.

LONDON -- Russia's Defense Ministry on Monday reported one of the largest Ukrainian overnight drone attacks of the war to date, with Moscow's forces claiming the shooting down of 1,336 Ukrainian craft on Sunday night and Monday morning.

The attack appears to be one of the largest-ever overnight barrages reported by the Russian Defense Ministry, according to ministry statements analyzed by ABC News. The ministry's figures cannot be independently verified.

Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov described the attack as "massive." He told reporters on Monday morning, "Our air defense systems continue to operate very effectively," adding, "The system is functioning, although, unfortunately, no system in the world can provide a 100% guarantee."

The attack comes as President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping prepare to meet later this week at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China.

Moscow was among the main targets of the attack, according to officials in the capital. Mayor Sergey Sobyanin said in posts to Telegram that around 600 drones were launched toward the city in the latest barrage. Most were destroyed far from the capital, Sobyanin said, with 191 craft downed on approach to Moscow.

Elsewhere, reports indicated Ukrainian drone attacks in areas home to major oil refineries in the Rostov and Krasnodar Krai regions in the southwest of the country.

Rostov regional Governor Yuri Slusar said in a post to Telegram that at least three people were wounded in the Azov area. "In total, four industrial facilities in Azov experienced fires," he added.

In Krasnodar, six people were injured when drone debris fell on a home, regional authorities said. A fire was also reported in the village of Ilsky, which is home to a large oil refinery that has previously been targeted by Ukrainian drones.

Ukraine's General Staff said in a statement that its drones hit the Ilsky refinery, which it said is "one of the largest oil refineries in southern Russia, with a capacity to process approximately 6.6 million metric tons of crude oil per year."

"Its location in the south makes the plant particularly important to the military: from here, fuel has the shortest route to Russian forces on the front lines and in occupied Crimea," the General Staff added.

The General Staff said that drones also hit the "Flash Energy LLC oil depot in the city of Azov, Rostov Oblast," describing the refinery as another that is key for frontline Russian logistics.

The Crimean Bridge -- which spans the Kerch Strait connecting Krasnodar Krai in Russia to occupied Crimea -- was also attacked, according to the Moscow-appointed head of occupied Crimea. Sergey Aksenov said in a post to Telegram that traffic on the bridge would be restricted until midday.

Russia's federal air transport agency, Rosaviatsiya, reported significant disruptions all across western Russia. Two of Moscow's four international airports -- Vnukovo and Sheremetyevo -- were among those put under temporary flight restrictions amid the latest attacks.

Airports in Yaroslavl, Kaluga, Nizhny Novgorod, Krasnodar and Sochi also faced restrictions, Rosaviatsiya said.

Russia also continued its long-range strike campaign overnight. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 158 drones, of which 77 were jet-powered, into the country on Sunday night into Monday morning. Defenders downed or suppressed 131 drones, 55 of which were jet-powered, the air force said. Impacts were recorded across 19 locations, it added.

Ukraine's State Emergency Service said in posts to Telegram that the central city of Dnipro was among the targets, where one person was killed and 10 others were injured.

Oleg Kiper, the governor of the southern Odesa region on the Black Sea coast, said the city also came under "massive attack" with damage reported to infrastructure facilities.

Trump and Putin will meet as Russia's ongoing full-scale invasion of its neighbor nears its fifth anniversary.

A round of shuttle diplomacy by Trump's peace envoys -- Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and special envoy Steve Witkoff -- last week saw trilateral U.S.-Ukraine-Russia talks held in Abu Dhabi, but without any apparent diplomatic breakthrough.

Putin and his negotiating teams have shown no willingness to abandon maximalist war goals that would bar Ukraine from NATO membership, cap the size and capability of Kyiv's armed forces and force Ukrainian troops to surrender territory in the east of the country that Moscow's troops have been unable to take by force.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, meanwhile, is calling for a ceasefire along the current contact line -- a proposal repeatedly rebuffed by the Kremlin -- with talks on a final settlement to follow.

ABC News' Morgan Winsor and Tanya Stukalova contributed to this report.
