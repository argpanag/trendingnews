Audi Will Build Its First US Production Facility In Georgia

TheTools · 25.10.2026 12:15 · source · permalink

Without a manufacturing site in America, Audi was left particularly exposed when Trump’s import tariffs came into effect. So, whereas BMW and Mercedes-Benz were able to mitigate the fallout thanks to their local factories, the Ingolstadt-based automaker bore the brunt of these sweeping changes in US trade policy. Sales are dropping precipitously, also affected by slashed EV incentives and the complete absence of hybrids from Audi’s American lineup.

Despite these headwinds, Audi remains committed to serving US customers while bolstering its presence stateside, seeing it as a crucial market for future growth. As such, the company has just announced plans to open its first production facility in the United States, and this was reconfirmed by President Donald Trump yesterday.

Audi Eyes Georgia With A $4 Billion Investment

The German luxury brand will establish its new plant in Peach County, Georgia, planning to invest $4 billion while creating approximately 4,500 jobs. Construction will begin in 2027 at the Middle Georgia Mega Site industrial park, and production is expected to get underway in the early 2030s. When complete, the Georgia site will be one of Audi’s biggest manufacturing facilities worldwide, with an annual capacity of 200,000 vehicles.

It will handle production of the company’s Q7, Q8, and Q9 SUVs, all destined to reach American customers. “As part of our larger U.S. strategy, we are focused on becoming an even more significant player in this market,” said Audi of America President Vito Paladino. “This investment signifies the brand’s commitment to our customers, dealers, employees, and communities across the country.”

Perfect Timing Or Just A Coincidence?

The announcement came just in time to rally support behind Republicans ahead of the midterms, scheduled for Tuesday, November 3. And with Georgia being a key political battleground for both parties in the upcoming elections, this recent development will play a key role in securing crucial votes for Trump’s camp.

But setting up shop in Georgia also makes sense from a business perspective, as the auto industry already has a sizable footprint in the state. For instance, Kia and Hyundai both have manufacturing facilities in Georgia, while Porsche and Mercedes run their US headquarters out of Atlanta. It’s simply a case of agglomeration, making The Peach State a very attractive option for Audi’s US ambitions.

CarBuzz Insight – Why This Matters:

With Audi losing around $1.4 billion due to US import tariffs last year, this investment is a major step toward regaining its momentum. At the same time, Georgian residents will benefit from new employment opportunities thanks to the thousands of jobs Audi is creating. Important political points have also been scored, so this is clearly a big deal for everyone involved.

But you have to wonder whether tariffs will even be a problem anymore by the time the facility is up and running, as we’re still several years away from its inauguration. In any case, it probably won’t hurt for Audi to follow the lead of other German carmakers by establishing its first US plant, since it would make the company far less exposed to trade policy in the future.
