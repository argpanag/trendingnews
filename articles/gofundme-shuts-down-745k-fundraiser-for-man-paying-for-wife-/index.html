UNDATED (WKRC) - GoFundMe reportedly shut down a $745,000 fundraiser for a man paying for his wife's cancer treatment.

According to NewsNation, a GoFundMe campaign benefiting 81-year-old David Johnson, who is paying for his wife's cancer treatment, was shut down by GoFundMe after the company discovered that a “neighbor” operating the fundraiser was Johnson's daughter.

The report states that NewsNation was told by GoFundMe that the company received a tip on Sept. 25 questioning the identity of the woman running the campaign, with that individual identifying herself only as a neighbor named “Erin.”

After the company's Trust & Safety team investigated the tip, which is standard practice, the team reportedly discovered that Johnson's daughter was behind the fundraiser, which had raised nearly $745,000.

During a recorded call, GoFundMe said the organizer was asked if Don and Josephine Johnson were her parents, and she reportedly answered that they were, NewsNation reported.

In a statement to the outlet, GoFundMe said the team “identified factual misrepresentations,” which violated the company's Terms of Service.

When speaking with GoFundMe, the couple's daughter reportedly stated that she invented the “Erin” persona because she had a hunch that her parents' story might connect with more people if it appeared to come from a stranger who had been moved by their story, rather than their own daughter directly asking for help, per NewsNation. She added that she filmed the GoFundMe video herself and maintained that all other details regarding her parents' situation were true.

GoFundMe told NewsNation that it chose to pull the campaign because misrepresenting an organizer's identity undermines donors' ability to know who they are giving to.

“As a result, the fundraiser has been removed and all donors have been refunded. Ensuring GoFundMe is a safe and trusted place to give and receive help is our top priority. Taking action like this allows GoFundMe to protect donors’ generosity and our continued ability to support people who are fundraising to help others,” GoFundMe wrote in a statement to NewsNation, adding that all donors were refunded.

According to the outlet, Johnson started a job as an airport luggage courier three years ago to help pay for his wife's pancreatic cancer treatment.
