Duncan Ferguson Recalls Prison Ordeal and Criticizes Everton Exit

TheTools · 17.10.2026 10:55 · source · permalink

Former Everton striker Duncan Ferguson has reflected on the prison sentence that shaped his relationship with the Merseyside club and criticized Everton's decision to let Dominic Calvert-Lewin leave on a free transfer. In interviews published by The i Paper and Goodison News, Ferguson discussed the death threats he faced during incarceration, the support he received from Everton fans and the club's failure to retain a striker he believes could still have contributed significantly.

Ferguson, who served 44 days in prison for an on-field assault, was 22 when he was incarcerated. He was already on probation for previous offenses, and the conviction became a turning point in his life and his approach to confrontation.

Reflecting on his early years, Ferguson explained that he had struggled physically as a young footballer before developing into a more imposing player. As his profile increased, he found it difficult to walk away from confrontations in public places.

He recalled the aggressive advice that influenced his reactions: "Make sure you throw the first punch."

Ferguson described entering prison with an unexpected sense of excitement, only for the reality of incarceration to become apparent on his first night at Barlinnie in Glasgow. Inmates shouted threats after the doors closed, leaving him fearing for his safety.

He also recalled the moment his physical development improved his prospects in football: "I've got a proper chance now".

The former striker said the threats continued into the following morning, when he had to leave his cell and walk onto the prison landing. Although the initial hostility eventually eased, he said the experience never allowed him to feel completely secure.

Ferguson said the experience also changed his perspective on his behavior. He described himself as a different person today and said he no longer drinks.

His experience behind bars strengthened his connection with Everton after supporters rallied around him during his sentence. Ferguson said the club's fans sent approximately 10,000 letters expressing support and promising to stand by him after his release.

He recalled the admiration he felt when watching Ruud Gullit play before joining Newcastle United, saying: "Wow, this is the level".

Ferguson also discussed his Newcastle career, describing recurring injuries as a major obstacle. He recalled being unable to finish the 1999 FA Cup final after coming off at halftime and praised former teammate Alan Shearer's ability and mentality.

Turning to Everton's recent transfer decisions, Ferguson said the club should have done more to keep Calvert-Lewin, whom he coached as a young player. The England international left Everton when his contract expired at the end of the 2024-25 campaign and subsequently joined Leeds United.

Calvert-Lewin had arrived at Goodison Park from Sheffield United for £1.5 million and scored 71 goals in 273 appearances. According to Goodison News, he has scored 17 Premier League goals since the start of last season, earning a recall to the England senior team during the most recent international break.

Ferguson made his position clear when discussing the departure on The Overlap Daily.

"[Everton] should never have let Dominic go," said Ferguson.

He emphasized that he had backed Calvert-Lewin early in his career and remembered the forward scoring twice in a match Ferguson managed against Chelsea.

"I coached Dominic as a young man and I put him in my team."

Ferguson said the striker's early performances had demonstrated his quality, making Everton's eventual decision particularly frustrating in his view.

"He scored his first two goals in the same game; the game I managed against Chelsea. I put him up front. He's a great player."

Although Ferguson acknowledged that he did not know the full details of the negotiations, he maintained that Everton should have found a way to retain the forward.

"You should never have let him go. But obviously I don't know what's happened behind the scenes."

He argued that the club should have been willing to meet the player's financial demands, even if doing so required a substantial salary commitment.

"If he wanted £200,000 a week, give him £200,000 a week. If he wanted £100,000 a week, give him it."

Ferguson also addressed Everton's current need for a more reliable goalscorer, with the club continuing to seek greater consistency in attack. He believes the position requires improvement despite the effort shown by the current forward.

"[Everton] need a better player up front," said Ferguson.

He tempered that criticism by acknowledging the striker's willingness to keep getting into scoring positions and continue working despite missed opportunities.

"Now, to be fair to the lad up front, he's scoring goals, and he's got a great willingness to embarrass himself, which is a great trait."

Ferguson's comments link his longstanding loyalty to Everton with his concerns about the club's decisions in attack, particularly the departure of Calvert-Lewin, whose recent scoring record has highlighted the value of retaining an established Premier League forward.
