Kim Kardashian Makes A Rare Comment About 'Co-Parenting' With Kanye West's Wife Bianca Censori

TheTools · 30.10.2026 18:29 · source · permalink

Kim Kardashian has an unconventional way of co-parenting with her ex-husband, Kanye West. Instead of communicating directly with the artist, 49, she primarily communicates “through his wife,” Bianca Censori, or his manager.

In the new authorized Kim Kardashian biography, Citizen Kim: The Woman Who Created the Future, author Jonathan Van Meter shares that West is currently based in West Hollywood, but Kardashian, 46, doesn’t know exactly where he is.

“I’ll communicate through his wife. Or through his friend, who is also his manager,” the SKIMS founder told Van Meter, per Page Six, who shared an instance during his interview with Kardashian when she took a call from someone on West’s team about their children, North, Saint, Chicago, and Psalm, visiting West.

“It is challenging co-parenting with someone who comes by every few months, but I’m always like, ‘Oh, Dad’s traveling,’” Kardashian told Van Meter. “I still make up stories and protect them because I want them to think their dad is the greatest. But I invite him over all the time. ‘Do you want to come to dinner? I’ll leave the house. Come and have dinner with the kids in their house so that they can feel comfy. Sleep over for a few nights?’”

Kardashian and West married in 2014 and divorced in 2022. In the years since their split, Kardashian has done her best to co-parent with West despite the fact that they don’t always get along. “I will sweep everything under the rug, all the horrible things he has said about me, so that the kids can see their father,” she said in the book, per People. She also touched on the fact that she constantly worries about him.

“Oh, I worry about him all day. That’s the father of my kids,” she explained. “I’ll never not worry about him. And that’s a big burden of having kids with someone who you don’t necessarily get along with. My kids would never know that. I do everything in my power to forget about all the negative things that happened. I have to be realistic, and my therapist definitely tells me, ‘Your boundaries have to go up,’ but we manage to co-parent without too much conflict.”
