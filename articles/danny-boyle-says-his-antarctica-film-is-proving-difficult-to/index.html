Danny Boyle Says His Antarctica Film Is Proving Difficult To Make; ‘28 Years Later’ Part 3 Remains “In Theory”

TheTools · 29.09.2026 16:51 · source · permalink

Acclaimed filmmaker Danny Boyle recently brought his new film “Ink,” a Rupert Murdoch biopic exploring the media mogal’s moves in Britain (based on a stage play), to the Toronto International Film Festival, and during his time there, he appeared in an interview for the Visionaries panel at TIFF: The Market (the festival’s newly minted film market).

Over that nearly 50-minute conversation at TIFF covering various topics, Boyle detailed his gestating Antarctica movie (that would see him reunited with longtime collabator/screenwriter John Hodge) and expressed the complexities of getting that one into production and also briefly touched upon the potential of that third film in his “28 Years Later” trilogy that he hatched alongside screenwriter Alex Garland (created the rage virus saga starting with “28 Days Later”) and fellow director Nia DaCosta, who helmed “28 Years Later: The Bone Temple.”

“There’s that saying, which is, ‘The only cure for boredom is curiosity, and there is no cure for curiosity.’ And I’m very lucky to be under that at the moment. I just feel that curiosity. That’s exciting. We’re trying to make a film in Antarctica next, which is very, very difficult to do — permissions, money, all that kind of stuff — but it drives you sort of on, really, to do that,” Boyle said. “And, you know, we’ve got a third part, in theory, of ‘28 Years Later’ as well, which is fascinating, with Cillian, another wonderful actor. So, yeah, there’s lots to still feel curious about.”

As previously reported earlier this month, the story is said to focus on “American endurance athlete Colin O’Brady and British polar explorer Louis Rudd, who undertook competing solo journeys across Antarctica in 2018. O’Brady completed his roughly 930-mile trek after 54 days, with Rudd finishing shortly afterward.” We’re still waiting on concrete casting news after Austin Butler and Colin Farrell were rumored for those lead parts (it’s unclear whether they’ll be hired).

The logistics of filming a feature on location in Antarctica would certainly be a daunting task for anyone (only a handful of movies have actually shot there; Kinji Fukasaku‘s Japanese post-apocalypse sci-fi 1980 film “Virus” was one of them), let alone Boyle, but the director isn’t a stranger to filming in harsh environments and seems to have a knack for projects that capitalize on exterior shoots in nature, not just on sound stages. Although we could see Boyle opting for more hospitable icy locations (Canada, Iceland, and other countries have doubled for Antarctica before), we’ll have to wait and see.

There had been a promise when “28 Years Later” was announced that it left the door open for Cillian Murphy‘s (“Oppenheimer”) Jim, the main protagonist of the first film from 2002, to make a grand return, as he was named as producer on the new films and would have a brief scene in “The Bone Temple.” With that, there had been assumptions that the Oscar-winning Irish actor could participate in a bigger way for “28 Years Later 3” (yet to be titled, with Garland expected to write the script). Unclear when that third installment could get going, as Garland is a tad busy at the moment after wrapping on his fantasy film “Elden Ring” at A24, based on the popular video game, with lore contributions by mega-popular author George R.R. Martin (“Game of Thrones” creator).
