Croatian and former Yugoslav football have lost one of their greatest representatives. In Švic, at the age of 92, former captain of Dinamo Zagreb, Yugoslav international, and Olympic champion from Rome in 1960, Željko Matuš, has passed away.

Matuš belonged to a generation that lived through entirely different times in football. In an era when million-dollar contracts were not yet a given, he was one of the most important players of the Zagreb club, while also considering life after his career.

He joined Dinamo in 1954 from Tekstilac Oroslavje. For the Zagreb side, he made 428 appearances, won the Yugoslav championship title in 1958, and secured three cup trophies.

As captain, Matuš led Dinamo in the historic 1962/63 European season, when the Zagreb side advanced all the way to the final of the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup, the predecessor to today’s Europa League. Along the way, they eliminated Porto, Bayern Munich, and Ferencváros, but were ultimately beaten by Valencia in the final.

Even greater success came with the Yugoslavia national team. In 1960, he won a silver medal at the European Championship, and a few months later became an Olympic champion in Rome. In the final against Denmark, he scored in a 3:1 victory. Two years later, he also competed at the World Cup in Chile, where Yugoslavia finished in fourth place.

Matuš, however, did not enter history solely for his football achievements. Dinamo Zagreb recalled an anecdote shared by sports journalist Žarko Susić about the former captain.

At one point, the Dinamo director presented the players with a choice: focus on their studies or on football? Most chose football. Matuš gave a different answer.

"I don’t want to choose. But if I must, I’ll choose studying."

His response was quite unusual for the time, but Matuš later made good on his decision. After leaving Dinamo, he continued his career in Švic, where he became a respected dentist. He spent the final decades of his life in that country.
