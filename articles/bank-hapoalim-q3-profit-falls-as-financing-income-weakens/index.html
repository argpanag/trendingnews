Bank Hapoalim Q3 Profit Falls as Financing Income Weakens

TheTools · 16.11.2026 08:42 · source · permalink

Market News

Table Of Contents

  • Weaker financing income drove the sequential profit decline despite broad credit growth.
  • Higher fee income and lower credit provisions partly offset the financing income shortfall.
  • The special bank tax reduced reported returns while a new buyback extends shareholder payouts.

Bank Hapoalim’s third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to shareholders fell to NIS 2.364 billion from NIS 2.488 billion in the previous quarter, according to results released by the bank on November 16, 2026, as lower interest rates, inflation and capital-market activity reduced financing income. Return on equity was 14.0%.

The year-earlier result included NIS 380 million of net one-time income from an insurance reimbursement. Excluding that item, third-quarter 2025 profit was NIS 2.378 billion, almost level with the latest quarter.

Financing Income Falls Despite Business Growth

Income from regular financing activity declined to NIS 4.819 billion from NIS 5.109 billion sequentially. The bank linked the decrease to lower average interest rates and inflation, as well as lower capital-market volatility and trading volumes. Income from non-regular financing activity fell to NIS 28 million from NIS 325 million, reflecting lower equity-investment income and realized losses on available-for-sale bonds.

Fee income partly offset that weakness, rising to NIS 1.277 billion from NIS 1.145 billion in the previous quarter. The increase included financing-related fee income recognized after a reimbursement from insurers concerning Sale Law guarantee insurance, as well as syndication fees from infrastructure transactions.

Operating and other expenses declined 1.8% sequentially. The provision for credit losses fell to NIS 167 million from NIS 298 million, which the bank attributed to a smaller collective provision reflecting improved risk parameters and more favorable macroeconomic forecasts.

Net credit to the public reached NIS 553.2 billion at September 30, 2026, up 15.4% from a year earlier. The bank said growth was broad-based across operating segments and economic sectors, providing an expanding lending base even as income from financing activity came under pressure.

Tax and Shareholder Returns

The special tax on Israeli banks also weighed on after-tax profitability. Bank Hapoalim estimated its 2026 special tax at approximately NIS 950 million. Excluding the tax effect, third-quarter return on equity was approximately 15.4%.

The capital position included a common equity Tier 1 ratio of 11.62%, above the bank’s 11.0% minimum internal target and the 10.24% regulatory minimum.

The board declared a distribution equal to 50% of quarterly net profit, comprising a NIS 946 million cash dividend payable December 1, 2026, and a NIS 236 million first tranche under a new share-buyback plan. The plan, approved November 15, 2026, authorizes repurchases of up to NIS 1 billion from November 17, 2026, through November 1, 2027, or earlier if the full amount is acquired.

The quarter shows credit expansion, higher fees and lower credit costs partly offsetting weaker financing income. Whether those offsets can continue to match pressure from lower rates and market-related activity is central to subsequent results, while the special tax reduces reported returns as the new buyback extends shareholder distributions.
