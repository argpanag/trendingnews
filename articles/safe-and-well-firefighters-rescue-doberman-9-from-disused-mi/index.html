'Safe and well': firefighters rescue Doberman, 9, from disused mineshaft

TheTools · 16.11.2026 05:01 · source · permalink

Your digital subscription includes access to content from all our websites in your region. Access unlimited content and the digital versions of our print editions - Today's Paper.

It was a lucky escape for Magic the Doberman after falling about six metres down a mineshaft in the Nerrina bushland on Monday.

Magic had been on a walk with her owner when she slipped down the disused shaft off Lofven Street about 9.22am on November 16.

Fire Rescue Victoria trench, confined space and mineshaft rescue experts from Lucas and Ballarat City responded along with CFA crews from Invermay and Glen Park.

On arrival, crews conducted air monitoring to ensure it was safe for them to descend into the shaft to rescue the nine-year-old Doberman.

One firefighter was lowered into the mineshaft with rigging equipment to secure Magic with harnesses and ropes and have her raised back to her relieved owner waiting at the surface.

The incident was declared under control at 10am and Magic was rescued, uninjured, at 11.17am - a little less than two hours after falling into the hole.

"We are happy to report she was uninjured and is now safe and well. Magic was very happy to meet all her new firefighter friends," United Firefighters Union delegate Joe Camilleri said.

Mr Camilleri said it was not uncommon for dogs to fall down old mineshafts in the bushland around Ballarat, but they were usually smaller dog breeds than a Doberman.

"She's a little bit bigger than what we're normally used to rescuing from down there so it took a bit of specialist gear to retrieve the dog, but she was apparently super happy to see her owner and super happy to see the firey when he got down to the bottom of the hole," he said.
