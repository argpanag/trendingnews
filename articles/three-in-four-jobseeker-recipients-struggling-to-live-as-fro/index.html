Three in four JobSeeker recipients struggling to live as frontline services get $4.7 million boost

TheTools · 18.10.2026 10:38 · source · permalink

For many Australians facing financial hardship, turning to government assistance is often in the hope that they won't end up below the poverty line. Unfortunately, that's rarely the case.

The Australian Council of Social Service (ACOSS) has found that there are an estimated 3.7 million people living in poverty in Australia, and around 1.9 million of those are in households reliant on government support payments. That's because, at their current rate, the payments aren't enough to adequately cover an Australian household's needs.

In the spotlight are JobSeeker, Youth Allowance and Parenting Payments, which ACOSS chief Cassandra Goldie said are "so low they are forcing people into poverty".

Anti-Poverty Week report shows which households are struggling

ACOSS and the University of NSW released a new report today, coinciding with the start of Anti-Poverty Week. 

The report found that three in four people in households relying on JobSeeker and Youth Allowance were below the poverty line, which, for a single adult, is $584 a week after housing costs as determined by ACOSS. 

It's not a new problem the country is facing, and it's a growing one at that. The report also highlighted that in 2022-23, half of all people in households relying mainly on income support were living in poverty too. 

The risk was highest in households relying mainly on JobSeeker (76 per cent), followed by Youth Allowance (73 per cent) and Parenting Payment (71 per cent). 

Goldie said this is a "policy choice" the government could — and should — fix.

Specifically, the council is calling on the federal government to lift the JobSeeker, Youth Allowance, and Parenting Payment income support payments to match the Age Pension and Pension Supplement, at least to $618 a week at current rates. 

While the support payments do increase twice a year in line with the indexation cycle, the advocacy group says more needs to change for people left out of the economy, asking for a "real increase" instead. 

"People in households relying on JobSeeker had incomes averaging $331 a week below the poverty line. For Parenting Payment households it was $236 a week," UNSW Scientia Professor Carla Treloar said. 

And with the cost-of-living pressures and dreary economic conditions that Australians are up against, there are concerns this gap will only increase.
