British Airways flight declares emergency off Irish coast due to technical issue

TheTools · 19.11.2026 19:06 · source · permalink

A British Airways flight bound for Montréal was forced to divert over the Irish coast following a technical issue.

Flight BA95, operated by a Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner (registration G-ZBJK), left Heathrow on schedule just before 5:00PM local time.

AirLive reports that during the 7-hour and 30-minute journey to Canada, an hour into the journey, the flight crew chose to stop and return to London. The plane was cruising at an altitude of 38,000 feet before crossing over the Atlantic Ocean.

According to a source the diversion was caused by a technical issue involving the aircraft’s flaps or slats, the high-lift devices on the wings used to control lift and drag during take off and landing. During the return to the UK, the pilots declared a general emergency by transmitting transponder code 7700, securing priority handling from air traffic control.

The aircraft touched down safely on Heathrow’s Runway 27R approximately two hours and 30 minutes after its initial departure. Following the safe landing, British Airways officially cancelled the remainder of the service.

Due to how late it was the airline was unable to source a replacement aircraft or issue a fresh flight crew. AirLive reports affected passengers are being assisted with overnight accommodations and rebooking onto alternative flights to Montréal.
