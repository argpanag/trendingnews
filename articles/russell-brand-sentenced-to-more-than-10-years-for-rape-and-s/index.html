Russell Brand, once a Hollywood film star and award-winning comedian, has just been sentenced to over 10 years in jail for sexual assault and rape crimes he committed two decades ago.

Some of the women in their victim impact statements, read out in court today, said they hadn’t discussed what had happened to them with other people, sometimes for 20 years.

But they had felt emboldened to come forward to police following investigations published by The Times, Sunday Times and Channel 4 in 2023 concerning allegations made by other women.

Met Police since last Thursday have said they are also investigating two other rape allegations against Brand, including one involving a then 16-year-old girl, and have passed on those cases to prosecutors.

He also said that Brand removing the condom without her consent, and ejaculating into her “which led to months of fearing she might have HIV” were “serious further aggravating factors”.

He said those actions “must raise” his sentence punishment by two years above the starting point.

As I reported earlier, the judge reduced the sentence by one year for mitigating factors such as Brand not having any serious prior convictions.

The judge is relaying the sexual assaults Brand committed against the women.

He starts with the attacks on one woman in a pub in London where Brand groped the breast of a fellow party attendee.

“The effect on her had her wandering the streets of London, phoning a friend in the middle of the night, tearful and distressed.”

He outlines another assault, where Brand, visiting a radio station with an entourage launched himself at a woman, groping and kissing her in front of her colleagues and his entourage.

He notes the attack’s impact on her, how it damaged relationships with her male colleagues.

“There was the added humiliation of you assaulting her in front of an audience and the laughter afterwards”.

Judge Bennathan starts with Brand’s climb to fame 20 years ago.

“About 20 years ago, Russell Brand, you were becoming famous. You were clever – and in many people’s eyes, funny and charismatic.

“You had also become a sexual predator,” he says.

“Your status protected you until 2023 when journalists published allegations” about his rapes and sexual abuse of women.

Such public reporting “empowered” other women to come forward to police, says Bennathan.

Judge Bennathan is about to begin his sentencing remarks, having considered the submissions from the prosecutor and Brand’s defence lawyer this morning.
