Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade postponed to Saturday as severe storm nears NYC

TheTools · 25.11.2026 09:39 · source · permalink

The 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade has officially been postponed.

NEW YORK (WABC) -- New York City officials, the NYPD and Macy's have decided to postpone the annual Thanksgiving Day parade.

Macy's released a statement that said, "With severe weather expected on Thanksgiving Day, and out of an abundance of caution, in partnership with the City the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will be postponed."

The parade will now step off on Saturday at 8:30 a.m.

This is the first time the parade has been postponed because of weather. It was canceled from 1942 to 1944 during World War II.

The approaching storm threatened to ground the balloons and drench spectators.

The National Weather Service had already issued a high wind warning before the call was made to move the parade.

The most significant weather-related disruption before this year came in 1971, when high winds grounded all giant balloons. In 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic led to a scaled-back celebration without spectators.

The balloons were deflated Wednesday night, and in a 6 p.m. news conference, Macy's Executive Producer Will Coss confirmed that there will not be a second inflation celebration Friday night, but instead the balloons will be brought back to New Jersey to be prepped for the parade, and the reinflation Friday would not be open to the public.

Coss said the balloons need to be prepared again in a significantly shortened timeframe.

“They have to do back and the be parade readied again, which is a weeks-long process that is going to get condensed to about 24 hours,” he said.

Coss reiterated that Macy's is "heartbroken," but said safety is paramount.

During the evening briefing, Mayor Zohram Mamdani said safety will "always be our top priority."

He said the storm will come with widespread coastal flooding at high tide and wind gusts of 60 mph, well in excess of what is permissible for the balloons.

He acknowledged the disappointment but promised a “truly spectacular “ parade that is also safe.

Unfortunately, Mamdani confirmed he will not be at the parade on Saturday because of his upcoming surgery.

As for security, NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said there is no specific or credible threat, but the department will call in over 1,000 officers to work Saturday.

“By moving the parade to Saturday when weather conditions are much more favorable, we can give New Yorkers the celebration that they’ve been looking forward to without compromising on safety,” Tisch said. 

Tisch also said officers who were going to work Thursday on overtime can now spend the day with the family if they choose and more officers will be scheduled for Saturday.

Meanwhile, spectators who had traveled from around the world were left dumbstruck when Eyewitness News reporter Joelle Garguilo broke the news to them Wednesday afternoon that the parade had been postponed. They were left scrambling to figure out whether they could extend their stay to catch the parade on Saturday.

Among those disappointed by the postponement were the Welsh family from Kansas, who were visiting New York City for the first time and planned to return home Friday.

“I'm very disappointed. We waited, I waited, to go to New York City until they were old enough to experience the parade,” Melissa Welsh said.

Another visitor said, “Very big bummer. My whole life I wanted to come to this. Our nephew is in the parade in a marching band.”
