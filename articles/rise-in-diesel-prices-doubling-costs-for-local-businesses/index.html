Rise in diesel prices doubling costs for local businesses

TheTools · 15.10.2026 00:49 · source · permalink

ALBUQUERQUE, N.M. – Diesel prices in New Mexico have topped $6 a gallon, and businesses that rely on fuel say their costs have more than doubled.

According to AAA, the average price for diesel in New Mexico is sitting at $6.04 a gallon, up from $3.44 during the same time last year. Businesses that cannot stop moving say the higher costs are adding up fast.

The New Mexico Rail Runner Express says it started the year paying $2.46 a gallon and paid $5.53 a gallon in the month of September.

Augusta Meyers, the communications manager for the New Mexico Rail Runner Express, said the train service uses about 1 million gallons of diesel a year. She said September’s diesel bill was just over $524,000 for less than 95,000 miles.

Meyers said ticket sales bring in more than $3 million a year and cover the cost of diesel. She said the Rail Runner does not plan to raise ticket prices or change its schedule right now.

“Diesel used to be cheaper, obviously, and when we were able to negotiate that, now with it going so high, we haven’t been able to,” said Meyers.

Longshot Express owner Frank Lopez said his hauling company has cut back as diesel prices climbed.

“We started out with 12 trucks. Now we’re down to four,” Frank Lopez said. “We used to run anywhere in the country, and now we’ve also been sticking to the Southwest now,” said Lopez.

Lopez opened the doors to their company back in 2003. For the first time in 23 years they are seeing diesel prices at an all time high. He said the business adds a fuel surcharge for customers, but those costs still circle back.

“You try to get back what you can with the customers, but at the end you end up paying for it again. You know, we pass it to the customers a fuel surcharge. It passes to the farmer, the consumer, and it comes right back around, and we end up paying it again anyway. It’s it’s really so it’s it’s more than just diesel,” Lopez said. “We never track our drivers as much, and we started tightening up on their idling. Every every little bit counts. The five cents saving here when you’re adding up 300 gallons, it adds up.”

Meyers said current fares still cover the Rail Runner’s rising diesel costs, and the agency does not plan to charge more for tickets or change service at this point.
