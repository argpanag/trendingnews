Council of Europe Ends Monitoring of Two Cyprus Human Rights Cases

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:12 · source · permalink

POLITIS TEAM

The Council of Europe has closed oversight of two European Court of Human Rights rulings after finding Cyprus fully complied with its obligations.

The Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe has ended its monitoring of Cyprus' compliance with two judgments of the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR), concluding that the Republic had taken all necessary measures to implement the rulings.

The decision concerns the cases Vassiliou and Others v. Cyprus and Zavridou v. Cyprus, in which Cyprus had been found in violation of the right to respect for family life.

According to the Law Office of the Republic, the decision is considered a significant development, as it confirms that Cyprus has fully met its obligations under Article 46 of the European Convention on Human Rights, which requires states to implement ECHR judgments.

Missing person case

The first case involved a Greek Cypriot soldier missing since the 1974 Turkish invasion, whose remains were identified and returned to his family in 2000.

The ECHR found that the right to family life had been violated because relatives were not adequately informed by the Missing Persons Service about the progress of investigations, the likelihood of his death and the location of his burial.

Parental alienation case

The second case concerned the parental alienation of children from their mother.

The court ruled that the authorities had failed to take timely and effective action to enforce custody and care orders issued by the Nicosia Family Court in favour of the mother.

Compliance measures accepted

The Committee of Ministers decided to close supervision of both cases after reviewing reports submitted by the Attorney General's Office and determining that Cyprus had adopted the necessary individual and general measures.

According to the Law Office, those measures included steps to provide redress to the affected individuals, as well as changes to administrative practices and legislative procedures aimed at preventing similar violations in the future.

The cases before the Committee of Ministers were handled on behalf of the Attorney General by government lawyer Thelma Prodromitou.
