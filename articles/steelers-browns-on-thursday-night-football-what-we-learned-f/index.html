Steelers-Browns on 'Thursday Night Football': What We Learned from Cleveland's 27-24 win

TheTools · 02.10.2026 08:09 · source · permalink

REWATCH: Steelers-Browns on NFL+ Premium

FULL BOX SCORE

  1. Watson has Browns in first place. Like him or loathe him, Deshaun Watson led the Browns to a result that their fans had to love on Thursday night. Looking like the best version of himself since his days with the Texans for much of the evening -- and struggling plenty, too -- Watson piloted Cleveland on a game-winning drive that ended with a 56-yard Andre Szmyt field goal. Moments later, Grant Delpit sealed the win and a banner day for the Browns' defense with an interception. When he was at his best, Watson was decisive and accurate, finishing 24-of-33 passing for 268 yards with a 2-yard touchdown pass to Harold Fannin Jr. Watson and Co. sputtered at the onset (back-to-back punts on seven plays) and stumbled a bit at the end (interceptions and two fumbles, one lost in second half). The end result and the impressive play in between are the largest takeaways, though. Joining Brian Sipe (1979), Watson became just the second quarterback to lead game-winning drives in the fourth quarter of three straight Browns games. Entering the night, the Browns, Steelers, Bengals and Ravens were tied for first. Regardless of the results for the latter two on Sunday, Cleveland will end Week 5 in first place. The last time the Browns ended a game week leading the AFC North was when they were 1-0 in 2023. Led by Watson, these Browns are in first place after four games. Who would’ve thunk it?
  2. Steelers lose in ugly fashion. A methodical start and a frenetic finish weren’t enough to erase the ugliness in between for Aaron Rodgers and the Steelers' offense. Grumpy most of the night as he was battered by the Browns pass rush, Rodgers threw for 299 yards and three touchdowns before his second interception ended the game. It was dramatic in the end and showed off some fight from the Steelers, but this wasn’t a pretty loss for Pittsburgh. It was bowling-shoe ugly mostly. Rodgers was sacked five times with Cleveland racking up 10 QB hits. With the Browns readily applying man coverage (73.3% of the time) and Steelers receivers unable to find separation, Pittsburgh failed to scheme options open, and Rodgers rarely dumped it to a short and easy option. Instead, he took too many sacks, got upset and often vented his frustrations. Much of the talk is likely to be video capturing a frustrated Rodgers cussing about a play not getting in fast enough and head coach Mike McCarthy returning his quarterback’s choice words. Rodgers guided the Steelers on a 15-play sojourn that covered 78 yards in nearly 10 minutes on their first drive, ending with a Roman Wilson 12-yard touchdown off a Rodgers pop pass. Thereafter, Pittsburgh was stymied, scoring three points over its next seven drives, until the Steelers’ defense forced a pair of late takeaways. Perhaps most peculiar was why Jaylen Warren, who had 93 yards on 17 carries, wasn’t fed more. There’s plenty of football left this season, but this wasn’t pretty for Pittsburgh.
  3. Browns’ defense, kicking game win the night. Cleveland was excellent, defensively, and its special teams units were outstanding. Denzel Ward’s interception of Rodgers to open the second quarter completely turned the game. The first of three Cleveland takeaways, Ward’s pick came on the drive following the Steelers’ opening-possession march, in which they methodically worked their way to a 7-0 lead. It invigorated the offense, as well, which answered with Browns touchdowns on three straight drives. The three takeaways and the bullying of Rodgers in the backfield were the biggest keys for a Browns defense highlighted by Carson Schwesinger (nine tackles), Sam Williams (sack, four QB pressures) and Isaiah McGuire (three tackles, sack, four QB pressures). Special teams can’t be overlooked, though. Szmyt has been perfect all year, and that continued with the first of his three extra points Thursday to his 56-yard game-winner, his second FG in the win. For the season, he’s now 8 of 8 on three-pointers and 7 for 7 on singles. When points weren’t being produced, punter Corey Bojorquez did his part with a performance that can’t go unmentioned. On four punts, Bojorquez averaged an incredible 62.8 yards, with one downed on the Steelers' 1-yard line and another going 81 yards.
  4. Young talent shows out for Browns. There were flashes aplenty from the Browns’ youthful skill position players, and they played monster roles in the Browns’ offensive surge in the second quarter. Cleveland turned a 7-0 deficit into a 21-7 advantage. Jaleel McLaughlin, the 26-year-old ex-Bronco, began the scoring parade with a 28-yard run in which he juked one defender and ran through another. Rookie wide receiver Denzel Boston is quickly becoming one of the league’s most dangerous deep threats, as his Browns' season-long 60-yard grab set up Quinshon Judkins’ 2-yard touchdown run. 2026 first-round wideout KC Concepcion’s quickness and run-after-catch ability have made him and Boston a nightmare combo. Concepcion, 22, had five catches for 64 yards, while Boston, 22, snagged four grabs for 89 yards. Fannin, 22, had the Browns’ last TD of the game in the second quarter to conclude the scoring surge. Sustaining the offensive output is a work in progress, but the young Browns nucleus has helped the team to an impressive start and underscored that there is more reason for optimism.
  5. Steelers weren’t better defense in this one. T.J. Watt has a penchant for making big plays on big stages. He did so Thursday, credited with a forced fumble and the subsequent recovery late in the third quarter. Robert Spears-Jennings’ interception on the next Browns drive led to the Steelers flirting with a comeback after Rodgers hooked up with Darnell Washington for a score and subsequently sneaked in a two-point conversion. It wasn’t enough, though. Watson was pressured on just 21.6% of his dropbacks, per Next Gen Stats, and sacked twice. That’s not going to get it done. This is a highly paid and high-profile defense with Watt, Jalen Ramsey, Nick Herbig, Keeanu Benton and others. It was hardly terrible on Thursday, but it wasn’t the dominant force it needs to be to take over games and rack up wins. It started well enough, but it was the Browns' defenders who dictated this one, not the Steelers’.

Next Gen Insight for Steelers-Browns (via NFL Pro): Pittsburgh’s defense pressured Deshaun Watson on 21.6% of his dropbacks, the eighth-lowest pressure rate generated by a defense this season. Watson completed 3 of 5 passes for 27 yards with an interception under pressure.

NFL Research: The Browns won their eighth straight Thursday game, extending the longest active streak in the NFL. In doing so, Cleveland snapped first-year Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy’s seven-game winning streak on Thursdays, which extended back to his time with the Cowboys.
