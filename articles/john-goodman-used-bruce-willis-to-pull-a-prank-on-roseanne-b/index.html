Goodman explained how Willis helped him play a prank on Roseanna Barr on the set of Roseanne.

John Goodman is reflecting on his friendship with Bruce Willis.

The Monsters Inc. star discussed his relationship with the Die Hard actor, which began when they were both finding their footing as actors in New York City in the late 1970s. Goodman fondly recalled a prank that Willis helped play on Roseanne Barr when he visited the set of Roseanne during his Moonlighting days.

“Bruce came over to hang out in my dressing room one day, and I surprised Roseanne,” he recalled during a conversation with Josh Horowitz on Happy Sad Confused. “There was a reveal. I was supposed to be in bed, and she comes up and yanks the covers off. So I put Bruce in there. And I think they have it on film. It’s pretty cool.”

Goodman explained that he first met Willis when he began frequenting Cafe Central on the Upper West Side of Manhattan. “It was a hangout spot,” he said. “I got dumped by a girl in ‘78 and my evenings were free. And I was making some nice bank in the commercial industry. I really started scoring, so I had some dough for once in my life. It was like the first money I had. So naturally I wasted it.”

The Righteous Gemstones star set the scene. “I met all these great actors that hung out there, and we would just talk about acting,” he said. “That’s how I got to know Bruce. We’d talk about Three Stooges. Films we liked, performances we liked, actors — and he was hot on the subject. He was in love with acting, and so was I.”

Goodman said that Willis’ friendship continued into his stardom. “We just hit it off,” he said. “And when he made it, anytime I needed to be in Los Angeles, he put me up, and we had a great group of guys, a lot of fun.”

Willis provided a point of connection between Goodman and another legendary star: Tom Cruise, who worked with the Barton Fink actor for the first time in Digger.

“It was weird, we started talking initially, and the only time I’d met him before was 30-plus years ago at Bruce Willis’ house in Malibu,” Goodman said of Cruise. “And I didn’t fit in. I just felt out of place. But he remembers saying hi to me 30-something years ago, and I said, ‘There’s no way you remember that.’ And we were talking about who was there, so he wasn’t lying to me.”

Goodman said that he and Cruise immediately dove into their performances once they joined forces for Digger. “I met him, and we started jumping into it, which is how he prefers to do things,” he said. “And he was a spark plug. He kept me going, and [was] a cheerleader on set. ‘Aren’t we lucky to be here doing this?’ Yeah, we are, even though I’m tired. But yeah, he really kept us going.”
