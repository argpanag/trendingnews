Righto, Barney Ronay’s match report is here…

…which means that’s it from me. Thanks all for your company and comments – sorry I couldn’t get to them all – mazal tov to all the winners, Alexia and Aitch in particular, and peace out.

I’ve no clue how he managed all that without leaking eyeballs; I’m halfway there myself. I guess that’s the kind of composure that gets you 77 goals in 68 appearances, an absolute outrage of a season.

“And then lastly for me it’s just the journey. For any young boy or girl wanting to achieve their dream, anything is possible with hard work when no one else is watching. To go through the ups and downs, to learn with resilience, determination and self-belief … anything is possible.

My journey wasn’t straightforward, I wasn’t the kid who went straight to the top. I had to experience the downs, I had to experience a lot of low moves, I had to fight my way to be the very best right now and i hope that can give you inspiration. Thank you.”

Well said, Aitch/Haitch; beautifully done.

Now he moves to Kate, his wife and best friend. They’ve been through ups and downs; she’s his biggest supporter and wanted this more than he did.

There was an anxious wait to find out, “it’s been horrible for you so here it is, we dun it. Yo always want the best for me, you sacrifice yourself to help me achieve my dreams and I know they’re your dreams as well. It wouldn’t be possible without you, this is trophy that we celebrate, me and you, all our close-knit team so I just want to say i love and celebrate tonight, I love you.”

Finally it’s his kids, past whose bedtime it is; goodness me, imagine Harry Kane being your dad, and yet at the same time, it won’t matter, he’s just their dad.

They’re the reason he does this, they might not appreciate it yet and he brings a few trophies home, but this one’s a special one.

“I can’t wait to see you and celebrate with you, I want to set a good example, being a father but also being a professional, what it means to work hard, what it means to believe in yourself and yeah, I love you very much.” Gosh, he’s giving out a lot of love here and it’s moving to enjoy.

Now it’s on to his parents, who’ve travelled all over watching him play without ever putting pressure on him to become a footballer – “It was always about working hard, it was always about improving, it was always about learning,”, a lesson there for us all. The traits they’ve given him have allowed him to be successful, and he loves them. Ahhh, that’s a lovely tribute, may we all cause our kids to feel that way about us.

His brother-agent Charlie is next – “It doesn’t seem like long ago since we were kicking a ball about in the garden and park, watching football, talking about football, just football mad – “and looking like Scott Dissick,” he neglects to add.

He knows Charlie says he gave him his ability, so thanks him for that, and to have an older brother like that, who travels around the world to watch him, giving up time with his family to support.

Now it’s on to the personal thanks, starting with Dr Alejandro Iloriago Callapo, with whom he’s been working for seven years – since he tore his hamstring pretty much off the bone. He thanks the work that no one sees, giving up time away from family, and the friendship they’ve formed, is a true honour.

Now it’s on to Kelly, Gary and Tom, his parents and brother-in-law. They live their emotions together, they’ve made him feel part of the family since he stepped into their house at 18 and they allow him to do what he does on the pitch through what they do off it.

“Wow, wow wow” begins our Ballon d’Or laurearte, saying it’s lucky he’s got a list. He thanks France Football, all those who voted for him, it was a difficult choice, and his teammates, some of whom he was up against.

He then talks about how his teammates push him, citing Dayot Upamecano, who he faces in training, then sends his best wishes to Vincent Kompany, hoping this has given him a lift.

He moves on to the Bayern suits, musing on what an honour it is to play for the club, then he talks about England, saying it wasn’t exactly what they wanted, but they had a great year, it’s an honour to captain the squad, and Thomas Tuchel also pushed him.

Now it’s on to Spurs, cue wild cheers in the auditorium. He wouldn’t be where he is today without the club, he was there from 11, living his dream, and he thanks the Spurs fans around the world. The support he gets in the ground, on social media – he appreciates it all. He used to be a fan himself and anytime he can give back he’s happy to.

Now it’s on to coaches who’ve helped him –Bradley Allen, Chris Ramsey, John McDermott, Alex Inglethopre, Ryan Mason, who’s one of his best friends, Kenny Jackett, Russell Slade, Mauricio Pochettino, Tim Sherwood, Jose Mourinho Antonio Conte, Roy Hodgson, Garethg Southgate, then Vinny and Thomas all of whom played a key role in his development and without any of them he wouldn’t be here now.

“Harry, Harry, Harry, Harry!” chant the home crowd, but now it’s time to hear from the man himself – if the crowd will let him speak.

The winner of the men's Ballon d'or for 2026 is Harry Kane of Bayern Munich and England

Biggup and hold tight to a machine and a mensch.

OK, here we go … but first, more blather about how tightly contested it is this year, a reflection of lacking quality, rather than quality itself, but here we go…

Now it’s time for R9 to present the men’s award; he’s asked which player he’d play to watch … and he says Luis Figo, pointing at his mate. Honk!

Asked if this is the best of her Ballons d’Or, she says the first one was special. This one though, the way they played last season and won, giving so much happiness to their supporters, after 14 years in Barca, the club she loves, it’s the best way to finish.

Putellas says she’s speechless, them thanks her teammates, talks about the great season that’s been, and says anyone on the shortlist could’ve won it. She had some incredible experiences with her team, they overcame obstacles and injuries, and it’sa great source of pride for her to have been captain.

She thanks Pera, saying he deserves his award as a great coach and person, also the Barca fans, and adds that she’s been going 14 years and there’ll be many more. She’s only seen the management team briefly this evening, but thanks them for being by their side and she hopes they’ll see one another soon; “this prize is for Barcelona”.

She then thanks her international teammates, saying it was an incredible year as they won the Nations league again, citing in particular the semi-final against Sweden, then looks forward to the World Cup in Brazil – they’ll try their best.

Now it’s on to the people in her off-pitch life, her family, her mum, her sister, her friends. She had trouble with injuries and thanks them for the support they gave her, allowing her to enjoy football again.

Then, in English:

“To all the boys and girls in the world, if you want to play football, play football, it doesn’t matter if you’re a boy, a girl,or where you come from. Don’t listen if someone tells you you can’t do it, just do it, believe in yourself – that’s the most important thing, if you have a dream, believe in it, even if, at some point you have to start from zero, and the most important thing, do it with love and passion.”

I’m on my feet!

This is her third time taking it home, and surely no one can argue against her. A total genius and a joy to behold.

“Is this a hydration break?” chortles Wanda Wyporska; well, I’d certainly relish the opportunity to take on board fluids.

OK, decision time: I’d go Putellas and Lamine, but I’ve got a feeling it’ll be Putellas and Kane.

Ahahahaha, it’s time for break; in the auditorium, booing.
