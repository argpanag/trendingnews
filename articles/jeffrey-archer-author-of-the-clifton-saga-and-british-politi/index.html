The British writer and former politician Jeffrey Archer, author of several bestsellers, has died at the age of 86. The news was announced by his publisher HarperCollins, who explained that the novelist passed away on 5 October ‘suddenly and peacefully at home, between writing sessions’.

Author of The Clifton Saga and Cain and Abel, over the course of his career, Archer has sold over 300 million books, spending 14 years and five months on the British Sunday Times bestseller lists and over five years on those of the New York Times.

His writing career spanned five decades and helped him recover from the financial ruin caused by a number of bad investments. His debut novel, Not a Penny More, Not a Penny Less, was published in 1976 and is said to have been inspired by his own experience, when he nearly went bankrupt.

A former Conservative MP who made his fortune during the premiership of Margaret Thatcher, was jailed for perjury in 2001 after being found guilty of lying in a 1987 libel case against the Daily Star.

Whilst he was campaigning to become Mayor of London in 1999, allegations emerged that Archer had lied in court during a libel trial that had taken place more than a decade earlier. He had sued the newspaper that had accused him of paying a prostitute and had been awarded damages of 500,000 pounds.

A jury found that Archer had fabricated an alibi during the trial against the Daily Star, and he was sentenced to four years’ imprisonment. Archer was, however, released early in 2003 after serving just over two years in prison; this did not result in him losing his seat in the House of Lords, as there was no legal procedure in place at the time to remove him from office. He remained in the House until his retirement in 2024.

Before her imprisonment, Archer had sought to amend the laws of succession to guarantee equal rights for the daughters of the royal family. Years later, her efforts inspired the Succession to the Crown Act of 2013, which put an end to the centuries-old practice whereby younger sons took precedence over older daughters in the line of succession. Some of Archer’s literary works deal precisely with themes such as gender bias and inequality.

His latest book, Adam and Eve – the story of a count’s daughter who longed for a son so she could pass on her inheritance, and of a shepherd’s son who longed for a daughter because he knew a son would be sent to war – is due to be published this week, his publisher added, describing Archer as a ‘master storyteller’ whose readers ‘meant everything to him’. Archer is survived by his wife, to whom he was married for 60 years, Mary Archer, their two children, two granddaughters and three grandsons. His personal fortune is estimated to have been around $250 million.
