How the Vikings and 49ers are training for Mexico City’s thin air

TheTools · 20.11.2026 23:37 · source · permalink

The San Francisco 49ers headed for the mountains. The Minnesota Vikings brought thin air indoors. Before meeting Sunday night in Mexico City, two of the NFL’s best teams have taken different routes to prepare for the altitude.

The high-scoring 49ers — No. 1 in the ESPN Power Rankings with an 8-1 record — have spent the week training at the Air Force Academy in Colorado Springs, Colorado, where the practice field sits about 7,250 feet above sea level.

Meanwhile, the Vikings — a defensive powerhouse tied for first in the NFC North at 7-2 — stayed home, using a temporary altitude chamber at their Minnesota practice facility in advance of a Saturday flight to Mexico.

The challenge awaits at Estadio Banorte, better known as Estadio Azteca, about 7,352 feet above sea level — more than 2,000 feet higher than Denver. Reduced oxygen can accelerate fatigue, making preparation another part of the matchup.

The 49ers flew directly from Dallas after their 46-40 victory over the Cowboys last Sunday, repeating the Colorado training approach they used before beating Arizona in Mexico City in 2022.

The 49ers brought cooks, strength and conditioning equipment and rehabilitation resources to Colorado Springs. Their flight to Mexico is also scheduled for Saturday.

Minnesota’s preparations have included cardio workouts in the altitude room over about five weeks, using stationary bikes and a treadmill. Nearly the entire roster has completed at least five sessions, with simulated elevations gradually increasing from 9,000 to 12,000 feet, according to the team.

The 49ers already have one international result this year supporting their approach.

For their season opener in Melbourne, the 49ers arrived a week before kickoff. The Los Angeles Rams prepared at home, treating the trip more like a routine road game, arrived later and got pummeled 27-7.

Sunday’s game in Mexico City, which will kick off locally at 7:20 p.m., ends a four-year NFL absence from Mexico. An anticipated return in 2025 fell through because of renovations ahead of the 2026 World Cup.

Sunday’s game will be Mexico City’s sixth regular-season NFL contest and San Francisco’s third. The league has committed to games there through 2028.

The last Mexico City NFL game was on Nov. 21, 2022, when the 49ers beat the Arizona Cardinals 38-10 before an announced crowd of 78,427 at Estadio Azteca.

Ticket demand for this week has been enormous, with nearly 840,000 fans registering for tickets by early October. Official prices were advertised from 2,108 pesos (US $117) to 11,656 pesos ($649), and the NFL says the roughly 87,000-seat venue is sold out.

EFE reported Friday that resale upper-level end-zone seats on StubHub were listed from US $462 to US $815, while some premium and VIP listings across resale sites reached US $21,000.

As for Sunday’s important NFC matchup, it pits the NFL’s most productive offense against its stingiest scoring defense.

San Francisco, which will be the designated home team, leads the NFL in total offense at 399.8 yards per game and ranks second in scoring at 31.2 points per game. 

49ers quarterback Mark Purdy, a leading MVP candidate, has completed 70.1% of his passes for 2,369 yards, 20 touchdowns and five interceptions. He leads the league at 9 yards per pass attempt and threw for 389 yards and three touchdowns without an interception against the Cowboys.

Running back Christian McCaffrey also had a big game against Dallas, catching four passes for 161 yards and two touchdowns and rushing for 62 yards and another TD. The 49ers outgained the Cowboys 584-439 in total offense.

Minnesota allows a league-low 14 points per game and leads the NFL with 37 sacks. Defensive coordinator Brian Flores’ unit has surrendered just six touchdown passes in nine games and is coming off an eight-sack, four-takeaway performance in a 30-10 victory over Green Bay.

Kirk Cousins will make his second start since the Las Vegas Raiders traded him to Minnesota — and will face San Francisco for the second time in three weeks. He threw three interceptions against the 49ers in his final game with Las Vegas in Week 9.

The 49ers have been the top-selling team on NFL Shop in Mexico for five years, underscoring their popularity in the league’s largest market outside the United States. They are expected to draw a heavily partisan crowd Sunday, giving the game a home-field feel.

Kickoff is at 8:20 p.m. EST. The game will air on NBC and Peacock in the United States, TSN and CTV in Canada, and Canal 5 and ESPN in Mexico. Paid streaming options are also available.
