Elon Musk Says Tesla and SpaceX Would “Probably” Be Better Together

TheTools · 19.11.2026 15:32 · source · permalink

This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.

Elon Musk, who runs both Tesla (NASDAQ:TSLA | TSLA Price Prediction) and SpaceX (NASDAQ:SPCX), was asked on November 18, 2026, whether his two largest companies would be better together. He spoke by video from China at the DealBook Summit. He told Andrew Ross Sorkin that the companies do not need to be together, then replied: “probably yes.”

Elon warned that a combination is not certain and would need Tesla shareholder approval. He later retweeted a summary dated November 19, 2026: “RT @KanekoaTheGreat: Elon Musk predicts 100% of superintelligence compute will be in space, and says Tesla and SpaceX would “probably” be b…”

Both stocks had closed before he spoke. Tesla rose 0.95% to $383.72 and was trading at $386.95 after hours. SpaceX rose 1.27% to $143.50 and was trading at $145.05 after hours.

This places Tesla holders in a difficult position. SpaceX is worth about $1.89 trillion, compared with Tesla’s $1.52 trillion. In any share exchange, SpaceX would therefore be the larger party, and Tesla owners would end up with a smaller slice of a bigger company.

What Musk Committed To

Nothing is signed, and no merger agreement, exchange ratio, structure, or timetable has been disclosed.

On SpaceX’s October 20 earnings call, Musk called a merger a good idea, confirmed that special committees had been formed, and said no terms had been agreed. Special committees are groups of independent directors that negotiate a deal when one person controls both sides.

His newest point concerned pay. He said he should not receive Tesla stock option awards just for combining with SpaceX and that any such award should be linked to Tesla’s own performance. He did not say that any existing award has changed.

Sizing the Overlap for Each Company

Musk pointed to a growing overlap in AI and robotics. Some links are already on paper: a roughly $2 billion Tesla investment in SpaceX equity and a semiconductor research fab at Gigafactory Texas built in partnership with SpaceX.

Tesla is looking for new growth because its third-quarter revenue of $28.046 billion[1] slipped 0.17%, while operating income fell 55.79% and free cash flow fell 61.63%.

SpaceX, by contrast, is growing quickly: third-quarter revenue reached $14.6 billion, net income was $4.4 billion, and AI Solutions & Infrastructure revenue rose 830%. Tesla would bring factories and robots, but SpaceX already has its own AI business.

Conflicts and Divided Attention Before Any Vote

Musk sits on both sides of any deal. That makes the special committees and a Tesla shareholder vote the real safeguards, and regulators would likely look closely, too.

In the same session, he said he felt foolish about spending at least $474 million supporting Republican candidates in the 2026 midterms. He said he would spend less if he could do it again and that he is not MAGA, which shows how much of his money and attention goes to things outside Tesla.

Tesla’s annual meeting is on December 3, 2026, at 3 p.m. Central. Musk, chair Robyn Denholm and JB Straubel are up for re-election.

What Tesla’s Valuation Already Prices In

Tesla trades between its 52-week low of $297.38 and high of $498.83 and is down 14.68% for the year. At a P/E of about 400, holders are paying for autonomy, robots, and a merger premium that no filing supports yet.

That valuation leaves little room for the risks a deal would bring, which would likely leave Tesla owners as the minority in a company led by SpaceX. Musk’s own comments on pay also suggest the merger should not count as a Tesla achievement. SpaceX owns the faster-growing AI and connectivity businesses directly and would be the larger party in any exchange.

Check what has been filed by the December 3 meeting. No agreement or exchange ratio may appear by then. If so, the merger premium in Tesla stock would likely rest only on Musk’s comment that a combination is “probably” better.
