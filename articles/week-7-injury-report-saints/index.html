Week 7 Injury Report (Saints)

TheTools · 25.10.2026 07:24 · source · permalink

Injury Report

The Steelers arrived in Paris, France on Friday morning ahead of Sunday's game against the New Orleans Saints.

The team was scheduled to have a walkthrough at Stade de France upon arrival, but those plans changed due to the weather and the team headed straight to their hotel, where they held media access and did not practice.

Linebacker T.J. Watt appeared on the injury report on Thursday with a calf injury, listed as limited that day. He doesn't have a designation on the game status.

Coach Mike McCarthy was asked if that was a concern for Sunday's game.

"I would say no," said McCarthy. "Frankly we just got off the flight, and we'll work the guys here and see how they respond and tomorrow's practice will answer all those questions."

McCarthy was asked during a Friday press conference if safety DeShon Elliott will be activated from the Reserve/Injured List. Elliot was a full participant all week and is listed as questionable.

"We'll make those decisions officially after tomorrow's practice," said McCarthy.

He did, however, praise Elliott's fight back from his injury and what he means to the team.

"DeShon Elliott, he's one of our leaders, number one," said McCarthy. "You look at what he brings to the locker room. Just to watch him fight back from this injury. He's definitely someone you root for. It's been a real milestone for him to get to this point. It definitely would be huge for him to play again, especially Sunday against the Saints.

"It has been a long road back, but he's a big part of what we're about in Pittsburgh."

The Steelers placed defensive end Sebastian Joseph-Day on the Reserve/Injured List, which means it's time for everyone to step up and do their part on the defensive line, a line that is missing Derrick Harmon as well, also on IR.

"He's a great player," said Benton. "He brought that vet mentality, and he was one of those guys that immediately you can ask him questions, and he'll have answers for you. Just those little tips and tricks to help out the younger guys, and myself included.

"He'll be missed. But it's still that next man up mentality, and we've got to go get the job done.

"That's the name of the job as well. Next man mentality and just always being on your Ps and Qs because you never know when your name could be called. I think the guys are ready to get their name called and play in this game."

Those young players include rookies Gabriel Rubio and Kevin Jobity Jr. Rubio has stepped up in Harmon's absence, while Jobity has yet to make his NFL debut.

"I love our young guys," said McCarthy. "I think it's important when you go all the way back to the spring, the offseason program, training camp, you have to develop these young players because history will show that they're playing in the biggest games of the year, come November, December, and here we are in Paris. We have great confidence in our young players."

Benton said he has been helping them as much as possible, answering any questions they have.

"My big thing is no secrets," said Benton. "I feel like they can ask me anything, and I'll tell them how I play my blocks and how I see it. It might be different from a coach's perspective, but I feel just giving them that ease and let them know that it's going to be cool, they made it to this level for a reason, is a big part of it.

"I feel like both of those guys have been a sponge for us, all the way back to training camp. They weren't afraid to ask questions. I know as a rookie personally, I asked on the side, but they're raising their hand in the meeting rooms and not afraid to be in front and ask those questions. So, I feel from jump, they've been right in that mentality. Then just getting all the details fine-tuned throughout practice and training camp has been amazing to watch."

Rubio has seen more playing time as of late and looks forward to continuing to contribute, even though he hates to see Joesph-Day out.

"It's disappointing that he's down and out. But it's another opportunity for a next man up situation," said rookie Gabriel Rubio. "I said it a couple of weeks ago whenever I was elevated after (Derrick) Harmon's injury. So, we're going to see who's elevated to that spot.

"I think it's definitely a testament to our character. We are definitely a resilient group, and the next guy who is going to be elevated is going to be ready, the coaches trust him for a reason."

Jobity doesn't know what the plan is yet, but he is hoping to get his first opportunity.

"I hope so," said Jobity. "I haven't heard the news yet, but we'll see. I'm ready. Hopefully it's my time too."

With receivers Roman Wilson and Michael Pittman Jr. both on the Reserve/Injured List, and Ben Skowronek already ruled out, the team will have to rely on a variety of options at the position.

Having young guys step up has been part of the plan the last few weeks, and one of those players has been rookie Germie Bernard.

Bernard had 11 receptions for 87 yards, and caught his first NFL touchdown, against the Bucs, with quarterback Aaron Rodgers going to him in key moments.

"I think it's just the trust that we're able to build from the offseason," said Bernard. "He sees the work that I'm willing to put in, to do whatever it takes to help the team win. He sees that I am prepared when the opportunity presents itself. And so, I think that's where the trust comes in."

Bernard scored the game-winning touchdown on a six-yard reception from Rodgers in overtime, for the 26-19 win.

With the injuries at receiver, other than the addition of Marquez Valdes-Scantling to the practice squad earlier this week, the Steelers have stuck with the group they had in the receivers' room instead of looking outside for help.

"It means a lot to us, knowing that we can be trusted by the staff," said Bernard. "That we could be trusted by our quarterback to make plays when it counts. We put in the work and it showed."

Valdes-Scantling, who was signed to the practice squad on Tuesday, was most recently with the Los Angeles Chargers, as well as spending time with the Dallas Cowboys this season. He appeared in three games for the Chargers in 2026, with two receptions for 22 yards.

Valdes-Scantling was signed to the Steelers practice squad in 2025 and elevated to the 53-man roster in mid-December. He appeared in five games for the Steelers, starting one. He had 10 receptions for 80 yards and one touchdown.

Valdes-Scantling was also with the San Francisco 49ers in 2025, signing with their practice squad prior to the start of the 2025 season. He appeared in five games for the 49ers, with four receptions for 40 yards.

For his career he has appeared in 119 games, starting 70. He has 221 receptions for 3,708 yards and 21 touchdowns.

Valdes-Scantling was originally drafted by the Green Bay Packers in the fifth round of the 2018 NFL Draft, the 174th overall selection. He spent four seasons with the Packers (2018-21), as well as playing for the Kansas City Chiefs (2022-23), Buffalo Bills (2024), New Orleans Saints (2024) and 49ers (2025).

McCarthy is familiar with him from their time in Green Bay and is happy to be working with him again.

"Number one, it's great to see him," said McCarthy. "I had him as a youngster when he came out. So, I know he was here last year. You could just see when he walked in the building. Everybody was excited to have him back.

"So, it's a great to have him part of it.

McCarthy was asked if Valdes-Scantling would be available to play on Sunday if needed, but he wasn't ready to commit one way or another.

"He looked good in practice yesterday," said McCarthy. "It was great to see him. It's been a while. We'll see.

"He's off to a good start. It looks like he stepped right in, like he never left."

Valdes-Scantling was able to step in quickly last year when he was signed, connecting with quarterback Aaron Rodgers once again.

His ability to catch on fast is something that doesn't surprise McCarthy.

"He's played in some great systems," said McCarthy. "Number one, you look at his resume and it speaks for itself. He does have a great connection with Aaron. So, I know Aaron was excited to get him back. I'm sure he'll help him make the adjustments that are needed, particularly in the area of terminology."
