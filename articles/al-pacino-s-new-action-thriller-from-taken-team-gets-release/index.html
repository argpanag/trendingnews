Al Pacino's New Action Thriller From 'Taken' Team Gets Release Update

TheTools · 06.11.2026 18:35 · source · permalink

Luc Besson has spent decades running what amounts to the movie business's most reliable second-act factory. He turned a 56-year-old Liam Neeson into an action star with Taken, and the list of actors he's reinvented through EuropaCorp's pulp machinery runs long enough that it's practically a genre of its own. There was reason to think the formula had run dry after the company's financial troubles in the late 2010s, but his Dracula adaptation quietly did solid business for Vertical this year, which may explain why the distributor has come straight back for the next Besson production.

That one happens to put Kiefer Sutherland in a priest's collar and Al Pacino across from him as a mob boss, and American audiences now officially have a path to seeing it. Vertical has acquired North American rights to Father Joe, the Barthélémy Grossmann-directed thriller that premiered out of competition at Venice in September, with a wide theatrical release planned for 2027. The premise is the kind of thing only Besson seems to greenlight with a straight face.

Sutherland plays the title character, a Vietnam veteran and former Special Forces operator who has traded combat for a Little Italy parish in 1990s New York, except he hasn't really. By night, he dismantles the local drug trade with considerable firepower, and his more creative method involves dosing dealers with a poison whose antidote is only dispensed at Sunday mass. That inevitably puts him in the crosshairs of Pacino's crime boss, while Ever Anderson, the daughter of longtime Besson collaborator Milla Jovovich, plays a young woman the priest takes under his protection. Michael Rispoli, Fred Melamed, and Erik Palladino round out the cast. The official synopsis frames it this way:

"In the heart of Manhattan's Little Italy, where mob bosses rule and faith is fading, Father Joe (Sutherland), a man of God unlike any other, wages a secret war – singlehandedly busting crime rings and turning the city's underworld into his congregation. As the stakes rise and the network runs deeper, Joe must forge unlikely alliances, cross sacred lines, and take his war beyond the walls of the church to save the lives no one else can."

Venice reviews were mixed in a fairly predictable way, with critics mostly agreeing the film is ridiculous and splitting on whether that's a feature or a bug. TheWrap memorably dubbed Sutherland's character "Catholic Batman," and the consensus praise landed on the actor's refusal to wink at any of it, which has arguably always been the secret ingredient in Besson's better B-movies. Sutherland is also roughly the age Neeson was when Taken arrived, and it's hard not to read this as a deliberate attempt to run that playbook again. Whether it works commercially probably depends on how Vertical positions it, but the Dracula numbers suggest there's still a theatrical audience for unapologetic Besson pulp. The deal was brokered with EuropaCorp's Brieuc Jonnet, Kinology's Grégoire Melin, and CAA Media Finance, with an exact 2027 date still to come.
