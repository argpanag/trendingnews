FIRST ALERT 7 Forecast for Monday, October 12th, 2026

TheTools · 12.10.2026 13:30 · source · permalink

ODESSA, Texas (KOSA) - FIRST ALERT 7 Forecast for Monday, October 12th, 2026: It’ll be a hot start to the workweek across West Texas and southeast New Mexico with a mixture of blue sky and clouds, along with the chance for isolated thunderstorms later in the day. Changes are ahead with increased rain chances, the potential for heavy rainfall and severe weather and much cooler temperatures by the end of the week.

High temperatures will warm into the 80s and 90s. Midland/Odessa are looking to reach a high temperature of 92 degrees. Winds will range elevated through the day, between 10 and 25 miles per hour with wind gusts 25+ mph possible. Skies will be mostly sunny to partly cloudy through the day. A few showers and thunderstorms will be possible in the afternoon and evening, mainly across western areas. That said, coverage will be rather isolated, so most areas look to remain dry. Brief downpours, frequent lightning, and gusty winds will be possible with the strongest storms.

Coming up tonight, conditions are looking to be cool to mild with early morning lows Tuesday into the 60s and 70s under partly cloudy skies. Isolated showers and thunderstorms remain possible overnight, but most do look to remain dry.

As we head into Tuesday, hot temperatures look to continue with highs remaining in the 90s for most. The chance for heavy rainfall, flooding/flash flooding and severe weather begins Tuesday as well and will likely continue through the end of the workweek. High uncertainty remains regarding rainfall totals and coverage, but locally heavy rainfall is looking increasingly possible with instances of flash flooding throughout the week. Fall-like temperatures are forecast by the weekend behind the storm system, with highs in the 60s and 70s and morning lows in the 40s and 50s. Of course, temperatures will be dependent on how much rainfall occurs through the week, but we are looking ahead to a cooldown by the end of the workweek. Stick with First Alert 7 through the week as details become ironed out and as the system impacts West Texas and southeast New Mexico.
