Stock market rocked as investors dump tech shares; Nasdaq suffers worst day since June: Live updates

TheTools · 29.10.2026 22:04 · source · permalink

The Nasdaq Composite tumbled on Friday, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average outperformed as traders moved out of key stocks related to artificial intelligence and into more defensive areas of the market.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq lost 2.86% and closed at 25,437.00, posting its worst single-day performance since June. The index was also roughly 8% below its recent high. The blue-chip Dow was down 572.25 points, or 1.09%, to end at 51,770.31. The S&P 500 slid 1.82% to 7,580.07.

Selling pressure intensified in afternoon trading as investors took money off the table into month-end and ahead of next week's contentious midterm elections.

Amazon shares lost more than 1% after the company reported earnings and revenue for the third quarter that fell short of Wall Street's expectations. The e-commerce and cloud giant also posted negative free cash flow of $42.4 billion and maintained its 2026 capital expenditures forecast for $220 billion.

Intel, meanwhile, tanked almost 29% after issuing a weak outlook for the current quarter, pointing to a slowdown in PCs. Revenue in the company's client computing and physical artificial intelligence group declined 5% from the year-ago quarter to $8.1 billion.

Sandisk was also down 13%, even as its fiscal first quarter results beat estimates. The data storage company announced an eight-for-one forward stock split.

Chipmakers more broadly were under pressure as well. Advanced Micro Devices dropped close to 11%, while others such as Nvidia and Broadcom fell 4% and 5%, respectively.

As investors rotated out of tech, defensive areas such as consumer staples and healthcare saw gains. Walmart and Coca-Cola each rose more than 1%, keeping the Dow's losses in check. Fellow Dow members Merck and Amgen were also higher.

"The tone has changed a bit, and whether that changes the sustainability of the rotation is still yet to be determined," said Globalt's Keith Buchanan, who noted that this is a "scrutinizing" and "discerning" market. "Last year was hope. This year is: At the end of day, how does this all work in real world terms, like how to finance [it] and who gets the economics. Those questions are much more difficult to answer."

"There are real economy risks, and the market's picking up on that," the senior portfolio manager added.

Adding to the downbeat sentiment, The Financial Times reported, citing sources, that Blackstone told investors Anthropic may not receive an investment-grade rating after its debut. Anthropic's S-1 filing offered some momentum to the AI trade earlier this week, but pressure resumed as Wall Street digested more tech earnings.

On Thursday, Alphabet shares posted their worst day on record after the company missed on earnings and revenue in the third quarter and posted negative free cash flow of $9.07 billion. The Google-parent's slide dragged the Nasdaq to a more than 1% loss in the regular session.

Friday marked the final trading day in October. The month was rocky for the AI trade, with investors paying close attention to companies' spending around the build-out. Hyperscalers Alphabet and Microsoft did not lift their capital expenditures forecasts.

At the same time, neoclouds — the cloud-computing companies that are behind the infrastructure needed for AI datacenters — are facing investor scrutiny over their debt levels. CoreWeave, which has seen a surge in its bond yields, is the poster child of that worry: Shares cratered 37% in October. Nebius plummeted almost 35% on the month.

The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM), which includes AI memory plays Micron and Seagate Technology, is off 20% in October, while the iShares A.I. Innovation and Tech Active ETF (BAI) is down nearly 17%.

Traders wrapped up a losing month, with the Nasdaq dropping 5.3%, its steepest fall since March 2025. The S&P 500 declined 0.9%, while the Dow added 1.7%.

The three leading U.S. indexes fell sharply during Friday's session.

The S&P 500 dropped 1.82% to 7,580.07, while the Nasdaq Composite declined 2.86% to 25,437.00. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 572.25 points, or 1.09%, to 51,770.31.

AstraZeneca beat market expectations with quarterly results published Friday, as Britain's largest drugmaker reaffirmed its full-year and medium-term sales targets, citing a rich pipeline. 

Third-quarter revenue grew 7% at constant exchange rates and came in at $16.2 billion, ahead of FactSet estimates of $16 billion. Core earnings per share were $2.79, and beating expectations of $2.59, up 19% year-on-year.

The beat was driven by double-digit growth in its oncology, respiratory & immunology, and rare diseases businesses, offsetting declining sales from loss of exclusivity for its heart drug Farxiga, AstraZeneca said as it touted its drug candidates yet to hit the market. 

"Our rich series of upcoming readouts in 2027 has a potential of more than twenty billion dollars," CEO Pascal Soriot said in a statement. 

In 2026, AstraZeneca expects to grow revenue by mid-to-high single digits and core profits by low double digits. 

Analysts largely expect the drugmaker to hit its stated goal of generating $80 billion in sales by 2030, despite a July clinical trial flop that would reduce those margins.

France's economy contracted by 0.1% in the third quarter, flash data from statistics agency Insee showed on Friday.

Economists polled by Reuters had been expecting French gross domestic product to rise by 0.1% from the previous quarter.

In the second quarter, France's economy posted no growth. In the first quarter, GDP shrank by 0.2%.

Insee currently projects France will post annual GDP growth of 0.2% in 2026.

A decline in foreign trade dampened growth in the third quarter, with exports declining 1.7% following growth of 2.8% in the previous quarter.

"This decline was driven by a drop in exports of transport equipment (-9.1% after +20.8%), particularly in the aerospace sector," Insee said.

France is currently grappling with nationwide unrest, which began in Paris in late September. The government is also negotiating next year's budget with parliament, with an aim of slashing spending to bring down the country's deficit.

— Chloe Taylor

Binance said Friday that a security incident had an estimated impact of $6.3 billion, with around $3 billion in cryptocurrency assets frozen so far, and pledged to cover all losses.

The world's largest crypto exchange said an initial investigation suggests "the security incident may involve a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions."

The exchange said it would cover all losses using its Secure Asset Fund for Users and company funds, adding that customer funds remained safe.

Binance also temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals, revoked the affected address authorization and enlisted law enforcement and blockchain intelligence firms to trace and recover funds.

— Jenny Lee, Liz Napolitano and Davis Giangiulio

The Bank of Japan declared that inflation in the country was "generally consistent" with its target of 2%, the first time it has made that assessment since adopting the target in 2013.

This comes as the central bank expectedly held rates at 1.25%, still at its highest level since 1995.

The decision was split 8-1, with board member Hajime Takata dissenting. Takata had wanted the bank to hike its policy rate to 1.5%.

A CNBC survey of 16 economists before the decision revealed that all surveyed expect the BOJ to set the stage for a rate hike in December or January next year, as domestic inflation and wages continue to rise.

Thirteen respondents said they expect a terminal rate, or the level at which the BOJ is expected to stop its tightening cycle, of 1.75% to 2% by around mid-2027.

Japan has been grappling with a weak yen and high energy prices that have pushed up inflation.
