French Tennis Star Gaël Monfils Reveals His Unexpected Post-Retirement Plans

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:58 · source · permalink

They do not involve coaching.

Gaël Monfils is one step closer to the end of a storied tennis career. The 40-year-old French tennis star, one of the most beloved entertainers the sport has ever produced, lost in the second round of the U.S. Open on Thursday to American Learner Tien, bringing his Grand Slam career to a close after 201 matches and 131 wins, the most by any Frenchman in major history. Monfils has previously said he plans to retire at the end of the 2026 season, with a final farewell expected on home soil at the Paris Masters.

The retirement plan, however, is probably not what most fans were expecting. When asked about his post-tennis plans, Monfils told reporters in Flushing Meadows that he would be working in finance, and that he had already signed to work in wealth management for a Swiss company. “My next chapter is looking more in an office and working in finance. I will always have a look on tennis because I love the sport, my wife’s playing,” Monfils said, referring to his partner, Ukraine’s Elina Svitolina. “I don’t think next year I will do so much about tennis. I will be around no matter what, for Elina a little bit, but I will be more of a finance guy.”

The Frenchman is actually not the first player on the tour to make the pivot from tennis to finance. Milos Raonic, a Canadian tennis player who previously ranked as high as No. 3 in the world, joined Goldman Sachs in New York since retiring from the sport in January 2026.

Monfils, who reached a career-high ranking of world No. 6 in November 2016 and has won 13 ATP Tour singles titles, arrived at Louis Armstrong Stadium on Thursday for what he knew would be his final Grand Slam match. As it often is, the crowd appeared on his side, despite a homegrown player on the opposing side of the net. “It’s been a long ride,” Monfils told the crowd after the match. “I came here in 2003 for the juniors for the first time, and here I am in 2026. Thank you very much. I felt all the love. I can tell you guys you are my second-best crowd after Paris, so you are up there.”

Monfils and Svitolina already made their mark on the U.S. Open this year when they teamed up in the mixed doubles tournament, their first and last time as an official doubles partnership. They advanced to the semifinals before falling to Belinda Bencic and Flavio Cobolli. “We had zero expectations,” Svitolina told the WSJ. “We understand each other even by [looking] at each other’s reaction. We can find the right words for each other, which is great, and I think very helpful in mixed doubles because everything goes super quick. You have no time to be sad and upset.” Svitolina remains in the singles draw and plays her third-round match tonight against Anna Kalinskaya.
