M5.7 quake strikes Japan's Kumamoto Prefecture, 2 injured

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:34 · source · permalink

An earthquake with a preliminary magnitude of 5.7 hit the southwestern Japan prefecture of Kumamoto early Thursday, injuring two people, authorities said.

The 2:22 a.m. temblor originated in the Amakusa-Ashikita area of the prefecture at a depth of about 10 kilometers and registered lower 5 on the Japanese seismic intensity scale in parts of the prefecture, according to the Japan Meteorological Agency. No tsunami warning was issued.

Kumamoto was struck by a M7.1 temblor on July 28, which registered a maximum 7 on the intensity scale in parts of the prefecture, killing over 30 people. The quake brought extensive damage, including the toppling of a smokestack at a papermill.

The early Thursday quake was the first one in the prefecture registering lower 5 on the intensity scale since Aug. 1. The weather agency said people in the area need to remain on alert for an earthquake of a similar intensity for about a week.

The municipal governments of Minamata and Kuma issued evacuation orders.

Eight Kyushu shinkansen bullet trains between Hakata and Kagoshima-chuo stations were suspended, affecting around 5,600 people, according to JR Kyushu.

Thursday's quake was felt across Japan's southwestern main island of Kyushu as well as in parts of the islands of Shikoku and Honshu, the agency said.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, who is visiting China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum summit, posted on social media that she had been briefed on the temblor, adding she had instructed relevant agencies to grasp the situation and provide appropriate information to the public.
