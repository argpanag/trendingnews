Sheriff's deputy injured by fallen tree while responding to incident in Alabama

A deputy sheriff was injured while responding to a vehicle crash Friday night when a falling tree struck him and trapped the driver, CNN affiliate WALA reported.

A car had struck a tree, causing it to fall and multiple vehicles also hit the tree, Dale County Sheriff Mason Bynum told the outlet.

“An additional tree fell, and fell over one of the vehicles that were involved and trapped that driver and also struck the deputy,” Bynum said.

“He has been transported to a local hospital with minor injuries.”

Isaias brought wild weather to the Gulf Coast, downing trees and power lines as it made landfall as a strong hurricane on Friday night.

The sheriff added that the driver was extricated and is okay.

Bynum urged the public to stay off the roads in what he called a “very dangerous situation.”

CNN has contacted Dale County Sheriff’s Office for comment.

"Reverse storm surge" pulls water out of Mobile Bay

Isaias pushed water away from the head of Mobile Bay late Friday night, dropping water levels at Mobile State Docks to roughly 3 feet below the usual predicted tide, according to a NOAA gauge.

This is called “reverse storm surge.” Strong winds push the water away from the coast rather than toward it.

Water levels have since rebounded, but still remain below the expected tide.

Isaias is no longer a hurricane, but flooding and strong winds remain

The storm continues to weaken and has also lost its tropical characteristics, making it a post-tropical cyclone. It now has sustained winds of 65 mph, according to the National Hurricane Center’s 2 a.m. ET advisory.

The storm is moving north through southern Alabama at 20 mph, spreading strong winds and a flash flood threat farther inland.

All hurricane warnings have been discontinued, but tropical storm and storm surge warnings remain in effect for parts of Florida’s Gulf Coast.

Authorities urge residents to stay off roads as crews assess hurricane damage

Officials in Santa Rosa County, Florida – where more than 81,000 customers are without power – are urging residents to stay indoors as dangerous conditions persist in the wake of Hurricane Isaias.

“PLEASE STAY OFF THE ROADS,” the county’s Emergency Management office said on Facebook. “It is not yet safe to travel due to downed power lines and hazards.”

The Okaloosa Board of County Commissioners said in a late Friday update there were numerous downed trees and power lines, and flooded roadways.

Crews are currently assessing damage and working to clear debris from roadways, officials said, with more teams set to be deployed soon to join the effort.

In Alabama, Dothan-Houston County Emergency Management Agency said in a late Friday update that all Houston County roads were closed until “conditions are deemed safe for travel.”

Recovery work begins after Isaias landfall

Recovery efforts are underway after Hurricane Isaias tore through parts of the Gulf Coast late Friday.

Crews are restoring power to about 13,000 meters across Riviera Utilities’ network in Alabama, and more than 16,000 meters are already restored, the company said on Facebook.

“Some crews are already working where conditions allow. Following engineering assessments, widespread restoration efforts are expected to begin between midnight and 1 a.m. More than 100 additional lineworkers are on-site, with further mutual aid on standby if needed,” it added.

In Florida’s Okaloosa County, preparations are underway to hand out critical supplies to residents from 10 a.m. Saturday, CNN affiliate WALA reported.

Bottled water, tarps and ready meals will be given to those in need at a location to be confirmed once it is safe and accessible, an official told the outlet.

Gulf Shores spared eye of landfall, but damage reported across Alabama

Alabama’s Gulf Shores braced for the eye of Hurricane Isaias and residents breathed a sigh of relief as the storm shifted northeast late Friday.

Earlier, wind and rain reached 60 miles per hour, with gusts so strong it was painful to stand outside, Rosales said.

“Standing out here in the winds were making you unsteady on your feet. But now all of that seems to be beyond us. Which again, big sigh of relief for the people here of Gulf Shores,” she added.

However, the state still suffered damage from the storm.

Daleville DPS reported fallen trees and downed power lines in several locations. Two major roads, Alabama Highway 85 and US Highway 134, were reported to be impassable.

Flash flooding, battered boats and storm surge lash Florida's Gulf Coast

Flash flooding and storm surge are creating dangerous conditions across Florida’s Gulf Coast as Hurricane Isaias is moving inland after making landfall near Destin on Friday night.

In Fort Walton Beach, a flash flood emergency was issued, with vehicles navigating water-covered roads and one truck appearing to turn around after encountering high water.

In nearby Destin, storm surge pushed into the harbor as rough seas battered boats at docks and strong winds brought down trees and utility poles.

Nearly 10 inches of rain falls in just 3 hours in Florida Panhandle

A weather station in Walton County, Florida, measured a staggering 9.67 inches of rain in just three hours Friday night, including just over 4 inches in a single hour, according to a National Weather Service storm report.

This station, 2 miles south of Caney Creek, recorded just over 12 inches in 24 hours.

A flash flood emergency nearby remains in effect until 1:15 a.m. CT for parts of coastal Okaloosa County, including Destin and Fort Walton Beach.

This is yet another example of overwhelming rainfall becoming more frequent in a warming world, as rising global temperatures push weather toward the extremes.

Power outages continue to rise, affecting hundreds of thousands of locations

Hundreds of thousands of customers are still without power in Florida and Alabama as Hurricane Isaias continues to lash the Gulf Coast region with winds and rain after landfall.

The outages have continued to steadily rise over the evening hours.

Here’s an update on how many are in the dark, according to PowerOutage.us:

  • Florida: 379,000 customers
  • Alabama: 218,000 customers
  • Georgia: 12,700 customers

That’s a total of more than 610,000 affected locations, including outages in Mississippi and Louisiana.

Some Florida counties are still seeing outages affect a high percentage of customers, such as Santa Rosa County and Walton County where more than 77% are without power.

Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis said at a news conference shortly after landfall that power crews and personnel from the Florida Department of Transportation will begin their response efforts overnight.

Isaias is weakening, but a dangerous night is still ahead

Isaias’ winds will continue to weaken as it pushes inland tonight, but the danger is far from over. The worst flooding could still be ahead for some areas east of where Isaias made landfall.

Intense rain and flash flooding: Parts of Florida’s Big Bend and southern Georgia face a rare Level 4 of 4 risk of flooding rainfall, particularly into the early morning hours when rainfall rates could reach 3 to 6 inches per hour. Tornadoes embedded in rain bands: Tornadoes are possible this evening across the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend. The threat will spread into southeast Alabama and southwest Georgia overnight. A tornado watch is in effect for these areas until 6 a.m. ET Saturday.
