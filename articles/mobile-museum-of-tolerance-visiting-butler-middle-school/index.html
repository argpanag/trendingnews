Mobile Museum of Tolerance visiting Butler Middle School

TheTools · 22.10.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

LOWELL, Mass. - A ‘classroom on wheels’ is working to end the cycle of hate in society one lesson at a time.

“We're gonna have a field trip here, like in the bus," Maika said. "And we didn't even have to go to the actual place we just – it was like right here.”

A new experience offering lessons about tolerance.

The Mobile Museum of Tolerance is in Lowell, MA this week where kids are learning about the Civil Rights Movement.

“When we were learning a row about Ruby Bridges," Amara said, "I really liked learning about her bravery.”

“Back then," Aaron said, "they were treating people unfairly by their skin colors.”

“I want to add on what Aaron said that like, in the 1960s," Daniel said, "people couldn't go to the same school because of their skin colors.”

Through discussions and videos, middle schoolers enjoyed learning in a new setting.

“If we didn't have this," Heitor said, "we wouldn't know how to treat each other differently.”

“I think the setting and hearing a different voice is always helpful," Social Studies teacher Dante Gerrior said. "A lot of these kids take the bus to school. So to be able to learn on a bus while being at school is pretty interesting.”

The Mobile Museum says their visits are being funded through the Massachusetts Department of Education at no cost to schools.

“We have mobile museums in Hawaii, California, Florida, Illinois, New York and Massachusetts," Theresa Leahy said. "I think the hope is that they take back ideas of promoting tolerance and then moving toward acceptance of other people.”

And while it’s not a traditional bus ride, students at Butler Middle School say they’ll remember this school trip for a long time.

“It was a fun experience for me because I never had this in my lifetime," Aaron said. "Because all the things that we did in the Butler School was all classes, but this was a special moment that we, that we did.”
