Texas Rep. Harrison to Newsmax: No Governor Should Have Unchecked Powers

Texas Republican state Rep. Brian Harrison warned Thursday on Newsmax that Democrat Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa could inherit sweeping emergency powers that effectively allow her to govern as a "dictator," urging outgoing Republican Gov. Greg Abbott to call a special legislative session to restrict those powers before leaving office.

Appearing on "National Report," Harrison said Texas law gives governors extraordinary authority during declared emergencies, allowing them to "modify laws, ignore laws, or create and enforce laws, including criminal laws, on a whim."

"It's absolutely true, and it's shocking," Harrison said.

"Texans, I think all Americans are shocked when they learn that in the state of Texas, any time there's an emergency, and it's the governor who decides when there's an emergency, they inherit effectively all the powers of a dictator," he explained.

"The Constitution and separation of powers are thrown out the window, and the governor of the state of Texas can basically do anything they want by executive action," Harrison continued.

"It is flatly unconstitutional. No governor of either party should have total dictatorial, unchecked executive emergency powers," he added.

Harrison urged Abbott to act while Republicans still control the governor's office, arguing that a special session could prevent Hinojosa from exercising unchecked executive authority during her upcoming four-year term.

"Governor Abbott, you're still the governor. You still have the authority to call us back," Harrison said, urging him to "do something, perhaps historic, on your way out, which is protect Texans by limiting the damage that Governor Hinojosa and the rest of the Democrats can inflict on the state of Texas."

Harrison rejected accusations that his position was politically motivated, pointing out that he introduced legislation four years ago, shortly after Abbott won reelection to a third term.

"I have been wholly consistent," he said, adding that Hinojosa, his colleague in the Texas House, previously expressed support for restricting gubernatorial emergency authority.

"She will have a chance to prove whether she was sincere, that she actually wanted to limit governor's executive powers, or if she was only upset when a Republican had those powers," Harrison said.

He also criticized Abbott's use of emergency authority during the COVID-19 pandemic.

"Greg Abbott, during COVID, people went to jail for violating laws that no member of the legislature ever voted on," Harrison said. "That happened."

Harrison warned that Hinojosa has already signaled plans to exercise emergency authority to intervene in Texas electricity markets immediately after taking office.

"This is not academic. This is not theoretical," he said.

"Gina Hinojosa, governor-elect herself, my colleague in the Texas House, announced within the first 24 hours of her victory that she intends to use her emergency executive powers on the very first day that she is sworn in to meddle and interfere in our Texas electricity markets," added Harrison.

He described those powers as "unconstitutional, draconian and tyrannical," arguing that allowing governors to write or change laws by executive order undermines the legislative branch.

"If the governor can write or change any law, that means the people and their elected representatives in the legislative branch have no power," Harrison said. "So that's untenable."

If Abbott declines to call a special session, Harrison said he would appeal directly to Hinojosa and Democratic Attorney General-elect Nathan Johnson, both of whom he said previously supported limiting emergency powers.

"I'm going to ask them, were you serious?" Harrison said. "Because if you were serious, if you haven't been lying over the last couple of years, then I fully expect y'all to support us in the event that Greg Abbott fails to deliver."

Harrison concluded, "God bless you, and God bless Texas."
