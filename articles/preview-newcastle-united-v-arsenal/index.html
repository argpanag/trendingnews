We look to continue our flying start to the Premier League season when we head north to Newcastle United on Saturday (5.30pm UK), hoping the Magpies fail to clip our wings.

With international football on the back burner until spring, we can now focus on a huge run of fixtures right through the remainder of 2026 as we aim to finish a glorious year top of the pile, starting with the 200th meeting between the Geordies and us.

After five years of battles with Eddie Howe’s side, this contest should feel different with the hosts under new management, and Bruno Guimaraes swapping black and white for red and white. But with a five-point lead to protect, we’ll hope to follow up last season’s vital victory on Tyneside by picking up a sixth-straight league win.

TYNESIDE TROUBLES

Another summer of upheaval at Newcastle saw Howe’s hugely successful five-year tenure come to an end during pre-season, while Anthony Gordon, Sandro Tonali and Bruno Guimaraes also flew the Magpies’ nest. In their place came Al-Ahli boss Matthias Jaissle and £265 million’s worth of young players from across Europe.

A positive start brought a draw with Liverpool before beating Tottenham Hotspur, but Jaissle has since been under scrutiny after claiming just two wins from the next eight league games, leaving his side in the bottom half and the season in the balance.

Damaging 4-1 away losses at Leeds United and Coventry City caused alarm, but their home form is also a major concern. Newcastle have won just four Premier League games at St James’ Park in 2026, and their solitary win this term was a nervy 2-1 triumph over Hull City. Recent defeats to Aston Villa and Everton mean no team has lost more often on their own patch in the division this calendar year, while they drew 1-1 at Fulham before the international break.

WHAT THE MANAGERS SAY

Arteta: "We love travelling. It's what it is. First of all we just want to focus on the game that we have tomorrow, a really tough one, and then we'll go game by game again.

"[This game] will be even more difficult, because they will be even better prepared; they will have even more necessity to do that, and that's what we expect. We always expect the best version from the opponent. That's how we have to prepare for the game, and that's how we're going to approach it."

Jaissle: "I honestly feel privileged to be on the same touchline as [Arteta]. He has achieved something special at Arsenal in the past few years. It is a dream for a young manager coming through to be facing him for the first time, but we are opponents and competitors for those 90 minutes.

"If you face the champions, that is motivation enough. We are playing at home in front of our fans - they can be the twelfth man. The fans can give us so much power and support. This is a great opportunity, a big challenge."

TEAM NEWS

Martin Zubimendi was forced to withdraw from the field during Spain’s 3-2 loss to England during the international break, 

Bukayo Saka remains sidelined with the thigh injury sustained at Bayern Munich earlier in the month, while Max Dowman will complete his three-match ban after sitting this one out. 

Joelinton will miss this match as he needs to see a specialist about a thigh problem. Former Gunner Joe Willock and summer recruit Nico Gonzalez have missed training this week due to illness but should be OK, while backup goalkeeper Ewen Jaouen remains sidelined with a hamstring issue. 

In better news for the hosts, Jacob Ramsey returns from suspension while Tino Livramento was back on the bench at Craven Cottage following a calf layoff.

TALKING TACTICS

Adrian Clarke: In their 4-2-3-1 shape, I expect Newcastle to try to press us ferociously. That is a core principle of Jaissle, but their confidence is lower than it was on our recent visits. The squad are still getting to grips with the new man's demands, and if their press doesn’t click, you can play through them. They have faced 20 shots from fast breaks, more than anybody else this season. 

Guimaraes offered the back four terrific protection, and without his presence they do not look as solid down the spine. New keeper Lukas Hornicek has kept Newcastle in several matches with a series of stellar saves that prevented 4.3 additional goals, but set pieces could be something we target, as only Crystal Palace have conceded more shots from dead-ball situations. 

In attack, they have the third-lowest expected goals tally, averaging just 3.3 shots on target per match, and no Magpie has netted more than twice in the Premier League. Will Osula's pace is worth keeping an eye on, and Yoane Wissa in the No.10 role gets on the end of chances, but scoring consistently is an issue for all their forwards. 

They are at their most dangerous down the left, with Lewis Hall and Harvey Barnes creating 23 chances and 43 crosses combined. No one else in the Magpies squad comes close to matching their numbers. So, Ben White or Jurrien Timber will need the defensive support of Noni Madueke when those two dynamic wide men push us onto the back foot.

FACTS AND STATS

We have won eight of our last 10 Premier League away games, and since losing 1-0 to Newcastle in November 2024, have lost just four times on the road in the league.

We have lost just two of our last 16 league games immediately following an international break (W12 D2). 

We have won more Premier League games against Everton (41) and West Ham (39) than against Newcastle (38), while our 32 clean sheets against the Magpies are the most one side has against another in the competition.

Newcastle have won just one of their last 29 Premier League games against sides starting the day top of the table, and none of their last 18 since a 2-1 victory over Chelsea in December 2014. 

We have won our last four league games by an aggregate score of 5-0. We last won five without conceding a goal in September/October 1987.

We are looking to become only the fifth side to reach 10 wins in as few as 11 games from the start of a Premier League season, after Chelsea in 2005/06, Man City in 2011/12, Man City again in 2017/18, and Liverpool in 2019/20, all of whom went on to win the title.

Newcastle's average of 16.5 shots faced per game is their highest on record (from 2003/04) in a campaign in the competition.

Mikel Arteta’s next win will be his 159th as a Premier League manager. It will take him above Mark Hughes (158) into 10th place for wins by managers in the competition’s history.

Martin Odegaard (110) and Declan Rice (105) are the top two players for line-breaking passes in the final third in the top-flight this season.

Only Exequiel Palacios (68) has won possession more in the Premier League this season than Newcastle’s Lewis Hall (67). Hall leads the division outright for possession won in the defensive third (41).

MATCH OFFICIALS

Chris Kavanagh has been handed this game, the second time we’ve had him in charge of one of our games this season following the 2-1 win against Chelsea. That extended his run to just one defeat in his last 17 Gunners games, which came at Anfield last season, but we have won the subsequent five fixtures, including the pivotal success at West Ham United last May, when he overturned his decision to award the Hammers a late equaliser after a VAR review.

The Magpies have also had a favourable record under the Lancastrian’s watch, winning seven of the last nine, although this is the first time he’s refereeing them this season. Kavanagh has been busy this season, dishing out four red cards in seven games as well as two penalties in his last outing - Manchester United’s 4-4 draw with Aston Villa.

Referee: Chris Kavanagh

Assistants: Dan Cook, Wade Smith

Fourth official: Elliot Bell

VAR: Stuart Attwell

RECENT TYNESIDE TRIPS

The Magpies have lost 38 of their 62 Premier League games against us; their joint-most losses to a side in the competition, along with Liverpool. However, on home turf, they have made life difficult for us, winning four of the last six games.

But that doesn’t include last season, as in September 2025, Nick Woltemade handed the hosts the lead, but late goals from Mikel Merino and Gabriel turned the game on its head to give us a vital early-season win. 

That ended a run of three consecutive losses at St James’ Park, as we were beaten 2-0 in the Carabao Cup semi-final second leg the previous February when Jacob Murphy and Gordon got on the scoresheet. A towering header from Alexander Isak clinched the three points two months earlier, and in 2023/24 Gordon scored in controversial circumstances to clinch another single-goal win.
