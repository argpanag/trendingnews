The matchups that had the Colts oh so close in an overtime loss to the Chiefs

TheTools · 21.09.2026 04:43 · source · permalink
  • The Indianapolis Colts lost to the Kansas City Chiefs 33-30 in overtime.
  • Colts running back Jonathan Taylor rushed for 92 yards and two touchdowns.
  • Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce caught eight passes for 90 yards and a touchdown.

KANSAS CITY, MO — The Colts lost a heartbreaker on Sunday night.

Indianapolis fought all the way to the end of the game, but the Colts came up empty, losing a 33-30 decision on a Harrison Butker field goal after initially tying it in overtime.

Before Butker’s big kick, there were plenty of matchups that led to Indianapolis being in that position, namely from a defense that struggled for the second consecutive week and an offense that couldn’t quite do enough on its final drive to win.

Colts DE Laiatu Latu vs. Chiefs RT Jaylon Moore

Indianapolis desperately needed Latu to break out this season.

Defensive tackle DeForest Buckner is still dangerous, but he is a veteran coming off a neck injury, and Indianapolis has asked Buckner to carry the pass rush for far too long. Latu, who had 8.5 sacks in his second season, was supposed to finally provide the remedy by emerging as a true weapon off the edge.

Latu hasn’t looked like that kind of player two games into the season. Kansas City superstar Patrick Mahomes had plenty of time in the pocket on Sunday night, dropping back to little pressure throughout most of the game.

Buckner recorded a sack for a stop near the end of the first half, but that was almost the entirety of the Indianapolis pass-rush production. When the Colts needed a big stop on third-and-11 in the second half, Latu broke free far too late in the down, allowing Mahomes to launch a 45-yard completion to Tyquan Thornton.

It happened again in overtime. After a Jerry Tillery sack forced Kansas City into a third-and-13, the Chiefs doubled Buckner, and Latu wasn’t able to win his matchup outside, allowing Thornton to beat Kapena Gushiken and Hunter Wohler for a 30-yard completion that got the drive moving.

Colts WR Laquon Treadwell vs. Kansas City’s cornerbacks

With Alec Pierce out, Indianapolis needed somebody to step up at wide receiver as a deep threat.

Treadwell, the 31-year-old who hadn’t caught a pass since the 2023 season, ended up being the player to do it.

The big wide receiver made his first catch in four seasons with a 10-yard grab in the first half, then drew two key pass-interference penalties downfield.

Then Treadwell made the biggest play of the game, getting behind the Kansas City defense for a 48-yard completion that almost spelled disaster. Treadwell caught the ball, then got back up and was hit hard, fumbling what looked like the game away.

After review, it was determined that Treadwell was touched by Chiefs defensive back L’Jarius Sneed, allowing Indianapolis to retain possession and set up a 38-yard field goal.

Colts RB Jonathan Taylor vs. Kansas City’s edges

Taylor’s patience has become legendary, the calling card for one of the most explosive backs in the NFL. While Taylor’s breakaway speed gets the highlights, it’s the patience that sets up those runs, waiting and waiting for the Indianapolis offensive line to clear the holes.

His patience was deadly for most of the night Sunday.

Taylor carried 24 times for 92 yards and scored two touchdowns. Both touchdown runs came as the result of incredible patience. When most running backs attack the outside zone, they press the hole until a cutback lane opens up, then slice back through the opening in the hopes of reaching the second level before it closes.

Taylor operates differently. When he handles the ball on an outside zone, Taylor keeps moving to the edge, biding his time until the Indianapolis offensive line stretches everybody out and clears every defender out of the way, leaving him with a clear path to run to the end zone. The first, a 24-yard touchdown run at the end of the first half that signaled the Colts wouldn’t allow Kansas City to run away with the game, was a beauty, waiting and waiting until a path opened.

The second, a game-tying carry from 5 yards away, looked like more of the same, and it might have been even more impressive because of the compressed area. While there’s not as much space to operate, Taylor applied the same principle, pushing and pressing until the Chiefs defense gave way, allowing him to push into the end zone for a game-tying score.

But Taylor’s early success might have led to a mistake in the red zone in overtime. When the Colts got the ball deep in Kansas City territory, Steichen called back-to-back runs to Taylor that went nowhere, leading to a game-tying field goal that left Kansas City a chance.

Chiefs TE Travis Kelce vs. Colts linebackers

Kelce, the star of so many Kansas City Super Bowl runs, is supposedly past his prime.

The big tight end is 36 years old now, no longer as fast as he once was, a multimedia star who is still able to contribute because of his ability to read defenses and find the holes.

The Colts linebackers almost made Kelce look like he’d turned the clock back Sunday night. Unable to identify clear starters at the position, Indianapolis is rotating four linebackers at the position. Akeem Davis-Gaither is the veteran of the group, an experienced linebacker who has struggled in coverage for several seasons. The other three linebackers — Jaylon Carlies, Bryce Boettcher and CJ Allen — are all seeing their first extended action in the NFL.

Kelce made them look silly in coverage, along with the safeties, a couple of times. Kelce caught eight passes for 90 yards and a touchdown, repeatedly sliding into open spaces and creating yards after the catch to keep the chains moving. Indianapolis does not have a linebacker available who can handle a player like Kelce right now, and it was painfully obvious against a crafty veteran like Kelce.

Colts QB Daniel Jones vs. Kansas City’s blitz

Kansas City’s defensive plan was easy to see.

Chiefs defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo clearly watched the tape. Every time he got Indianapolis into an obvious passing situation, Spagnuolo tried to heat up Daniel Jones with the blitz, sending free rushers at the quarterback regularly.

The strategy worked for Baltimore in the season opener. Unable to find outlets, Jones often got happy feet and missed.

Jones couldn’t push the ball down the field Sunday night. Not without the services of Pierce, who left the game early due to a heel injury.

But he was much better against the blitz, repeatedly finding his hot receiver for completions just before taking a blow. Slot receiver Josh Downs caught seven passes for 72 yards, a tough day that included repeatedly finding holes in the zone in time for Jones to make a throw. Jones was far from prolific; he completed 22 of 31 throws for 210 yards, and there were times that Jones didn’t handle the pressure well.

For the most part, though, Jones battled all day, until he couldn’t find a key receiver on third-and-4 near midfield. Kansas City didn’t blitz that time; Jones had nowhere to go, and he ended up throwing the ball away to avoid a sack.
