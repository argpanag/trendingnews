Clarkson Turns Oxfordshire Farmland Into Diddly Squat Farm

TheTools · 22.11.2026 21:13 · source · permalink

Long before it became the setting for a popular farming television series, a property near Chipping Norton in Oxfordshire operated as conventional agricultural land. Jeremy Clarkson purchased the farm in 2008 for a reported £4.25 million, initially relying on a local contractor to manage its crops until he took over the agricultural operation in 2019.

The property, previously associated with the Sarsden estate and known as Curdle Hill Farm, covers approximately 1,000 acres, or 400 hectares, according to Countryfile. Its original farming operation focused on rotating barley, rapeseed and wheat.

Clarkson explained the arrangement in an interview with Johann Tasker published by Farmers Weekly on June 29, 2021. The former contractor continued managing the land throughout the 11 years following the purchase.

"I bought the farm in 2008 and it was contract farmed by a chap in the village," said Clarkson.

The purchase first attracted newspaper attention in November 2009, when Clarkson mentioned becoming a farm owner in his Sunday Times column. He subsequently explained his financial and personal motivations for acquiring agricultural land.

"I have bought a farm," Clarkson wrote in his November 8, 2009, column.

His explanation included the potential financial advantages of owning agricultural property and his enthusiasm for recreational vehicles.

"Land is a better investment than any bank can offer," Clarkson wrote.

He also highlighted the inheritance-related considerations behind the purchase.

"The Government doesn’t get any of my money when I die," Clarkson wrote.

His most personal motivation involved the opportunity to own and ride an agricultural vehicle.

"I can now have a quad bike. I have always loved the idea of such things," Clarkson wrote.

The reported purchase price, however, remains inconsistent across published accounts. A 2013 Times headline attributed a £4.25 million price to the acquisition, while other reports have associated the same figure with a different purchase date and property description.

The Daily Mail has linked the £4.25 million figure to the 312-acre Curdle Hill Farm in Chadlington, describing its purchase as occurring in 2012. Another report from the publication refers to Clarkson purchasing a 1,000-acre property in 2008. Woman & Home, citing the Daily Mail, gives a different figure of £4.45 million.

Consequently, £4.25 million remains a reported purchase price rather than a definitively established transaction value.

Clarkson's decision to farm the property himself eventually provided the foundation for his Amazon Prime television series, Clarkson's Farm, which premiered in 2021. The program follows his efforts to understand agricultural operations with guidance from experienced local farmers and specialists.

He originally envisioned the production around ordinary agricultural activities rather than exaggerated farming entertainment. The concept centered on demonstrating how a large working farm actually operates.

"And I thought we could do wheat, barley, [oilseed] rape on a perfectly straightforward, ordinary 1,000-acre farm. And that’s where the idea came from," Clarkson said.

He also described his dissatisfaction with the limited representation of everyday agricultural work in existing television programs.

"So I realised nobody had a clue what farming was really about," Clarkson said.

The distinction influenced his approach to the series, particularly its presentation of agricultural work to audiences without farming backgrounds.

"I wanted to show people who aren’t farmers what farmers really do," Clarkson said.

The property subsequently became known as Diddly Squat Farm, a name Clarkson chose to reflect its lack of productivity during his early farming efforts. The expression refers to having virtually nothing.

Local farm worker Kaleb Cooper, who appears in the television series, had previously worked on the property under its former manager, Howard Pauling.

Today, the farm produces barley, rapeseed, wheat, potatoes and echium, while blackberries are also harvested. Its livestock includes sheep, pigs and chickens, according to Countryfile's February 2, 2025, account.

The farm shop developed after an unusually successful potato harvest left Clarkson with 40 tonnes of surplus produce. He subsequently expanded its offerings with honey from purchased beehives, apple juice and milk supplied by a local dairy.

The shop sells vegetables grown on the property and sourdough bread made using flour from its own wheat harvest. Its commercial success was followed by the opening of a pub called The Farmer’s Dog.
