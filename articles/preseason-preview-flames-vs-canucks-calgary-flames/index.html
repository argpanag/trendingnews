Head Coach Ryan Huska is likely to have a much different-looking lineup than the one that suffered a 4-2 setback to the Kraken Sunday evening.

Throughout training camp, the two practice groups have stayed static: one squad with a heavy NHL veteran flavour, the other boasting younger hopefuls and American Hockey League vets. And Huska himself said over the weekend that each group was expected to play one home contest, and one road fixture, before the season starts for real at the 'Dome Oct. 1.

Aydar Suniev earned First Star honours in Sunday's contest thanks to a two-goal performance; counting the Next Gen Showcase game Sept. 14, Suniev has counted all three of Calgary's goals to date in exhibition play. Also encouraging, the play of Sam Honzek against Seattle. The Slovak - who played junior hockey in Vancouver and made his NHL debut against the Canucks in 2024 - was certainly one of the pacier players in Sunday's game, and was arguably unfortunate to have been limited to one assist on the night.

Monday was a scheduled off day for the team, but Tuesday's morning skate at the Scotiabank Saddledome will offer clues as to who's in, and who's out against Vancouver.

But if the overall plan remains true to form, tonight is likely to be one of only two opportunities for the bulk of the Flames' vets to see game action before the points start counting for real. 

A chance to build on chemistry developed over a fast-paced Training Camp, as well as another opportunity for those hopefuls bidding for full-time jobs with the big club to show their mettle against live opposition.

The Other Side

The Canucks arrive in Calgary with a brand-new front office heavy on Vancouver alumni. Daniel and Henrik Sedin signed on as co-Presidents of Hockey Operations, Ryan Johnson was named the new GM, and the team has brought in a new Head Coach in Manny Malhotra, too.

Malhotra, a former Vancouver centreman who was part of the Canucks squad that reached the Stanley Cup Final in 2011, landed his first NHL bench boss job in June after a two-year stint in Abbotsford that saw him lead the AHL Canucks to the Calder Cup in 2025.

Vancouver added several experienced players over the summer including longtime Montreal Canadiens sparkplug Brendan Gallagher, as well as veteran defencemen Jamie Oleksiak and Luke Schenn, the latter of whom is beginning his third stint with the organization. But the big off-ice news (from a player perspective) came earlier this month when Zeev Buium, the No.-12 pick in the 2024 Draft, signed an eight-year, $75-million contract after being acquired mid-year from Minnesota last season.

The Canucks opened their exhibition campaign Saturday with a 4-2 loss at Seattle; Braeden Cootes and former Red Deer Rebels star Arshdeep Bains provided the offence for the visitors.

Player To Watch

We're likely to see defenceman Simon Nemec in Flames silks for the first time this evening. 

The centrepiece of a blockbuster deal between Calgary and New Jersey in June, Nemec has been skating with former Devils teammate Kevin Bahl throughout camp and arrives in Alberta with a new, five-year contract in tow. 

Selected No. 2 overall in the 2022 NHL Draft, Nemec put up 11 goals from the New Jersey blueline last season, including an overtime winner in his last game action on Scotiabank Saddledome ice.
