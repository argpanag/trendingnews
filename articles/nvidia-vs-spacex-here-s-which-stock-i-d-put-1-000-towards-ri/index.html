Nvidia vs. SpaceX: Here's Which Stock I'd Put $1,000 Towards Right Now | The Motley Fool

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:18 · source · permalink

Both AI stocks come with risks, but one has a clear edge over the other.

By Chris Neiger – Nov 23, 2026 at 3:18AM EST

Key Points

  • SpaceX's businesses are accelerating, and the company reported an impressive swing to profitability in its third quarter.
  • Nvidia is all kinds of drama right now, but its revenue more than doubled in its most recent quarter.
  • Nvidia edges out SpaceX for the win because it's highly profitable and its shares are much cheaper.

Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) and SpaceX (SPCX -7.69%) are popular artificial intelligence (AI) stocks and, no doubt, household names right now.

SpaceX continues to expand its rocket launch business, artificial intelligence services, and Starlink internet service. Led by Elon Musk, SpaceX's businesses appear to be on an upward trajectory, though the company's spending is accelerating and profits remain sparse.

Nvidia is still growing like a weed, too, with sales and earnings surging in its recently reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 results. But with co-founder and former CEO Jensen Huang recently resigning after admitting to an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, some wonder what the future holds for this leading AI company.

Which is the better company to put $1,000 toward right now? My bet is on Nvidia stock. Here's why.

Image source: The Motley Fool.

SpaceX rising

SpaceX reported impressive Q3 2026 (which ended Sept. 30) results, including the 161% jump in revenue to $14.6 billion. The soaring sales helped SpaceX swing to a profit, achieving net income of $4.4 billion, up from a loss of $1.1 billion in the year-ago quarter.

SpaceX's three core business segments -- Starlink, Space Launch, and Super Intelligence (what is calls artificial intelligence) -- all expanded in the quarter. Starlink sales were up 69% to $5.2 billion, Space Launch revenue climbed 6% to $1.8 billion, and the company's Super Intelligence was a standout success, soaring 830% to $7.6 billion.

AI growth was certainly the headline for the quarter. The company rents cloud computing capacity to other tech companies and also has its own Grok AI model. The company is spending heavily to expand its AI infrastructure, which is clearly paying off but could continue to weigh on profitability.

Capital expenditures (capex) rose 185% to $19.6 billion in the quarter, with 83% going to AI. And SpaceX's management says AI spending will be between $35 billion and $40 billion in Q4 alone, making a sizable acceleration of spending.

Nvidia's drama

Jensen Huang is currently trying to mount a comeback to the Nvidia CEO role, but it's unlikely to succeed. Nvidia Board Chair Harvey Jones said in the fiscal 2027 Q3 (ended Oct. 25) that the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO."

That's for the better. Despite Huang being instrumental in growing Nvidia into an AI juggernaut, there are serious unanswered questions about whether his actions affected national security and if Nvidia knew that some of its chips were being smuggled into China. In short, Nvidia's drama is far from over even with CFO Colette Kress taking the helm as interim CEO at the company.

Putting all of that aside for a moment, Nvidia actually had a fantastic quarter. The company's sales more than doubled to $115.8 billion, and its earnings popped 111% to $2.68 per share. Nvidia's chips are in high demand as the AI boom surges on, helping the company earn enviable gross margins of nearly 75%.

The company's important data center segment was a standout success, with revenue rising 110% from the year-ago quarter to $107.6 billion.

More growth for the company is likely on the way, too. Nvidia's management said Q4 sales will be $130 billion, rising 90% year over year. And leadership raised fiscal 2028 revenue guidance to 75%, up from its previous guidance of 70%.

Why I'd put $1,000 toward Nvidia over SpaceX right now

Even with all of Nvidia's drama, and given SpaceX's recent growth, I still believe that putting $1,000 toward Nvidia is currently the better move.

The reasons are pretty straightforward. Nvidia is much more profitable than SpaceX and spends far less to grow its business. That doesn't mean SpaceX can't be a good long-term investment, but it puts Nvidia in a much better financial position.

What's more, Nvidia's shares are far cheaper. Nvidia stock has a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of around 10, which is below the tech sector average and far below SpaceX's forward P/E ratio of 49.

Nvidia has a lot of drama to overcome, but its high profitability and its cheaper price tag give it the win in this matchup.
