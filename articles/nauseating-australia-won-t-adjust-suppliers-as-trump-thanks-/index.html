'Nauseating': Australia won't adjust suppliers as Trump thanks Putin for oil

TheTools · 10.10.2026 06:54 · source · permalink

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has described the move as "weak" and a "gift" to the Russian autocrat.

6 min read

Published 10 October 2026 5:08pm

By Jack Revell

Source: SBS News

In brief

  • Australia's energy minister has said there would be no change in the country's oil supply following a controversial US-Russia deal.
  • Trump thanked Putin for the deal which would release 2.25 million barrels of oil to the global market.

Energy Minister Chris Bowen has taken a strong stance against a new US-Russian oil deal that could see millions of barrels released to the global market from the heavily sanctioned nation currently at war with Ukraine.

Bowen was speaking on Saturday after United States President Donald Trump thanked Russian leader Vladimir Putin for the "very successful" deal, which he said would lower fuel prices in the United States ahead of the November midterm elections.

The deal is expected to provide billions of dollars to Russia, which analysts say will likely go directly to funding the Russian war effort that has killed tens of thousands of people and displaced and wounded many more.

"We don't need or want Russian fuel in Australia," Bowen told ABC News.

"We do not want Vladimir Putin to benefit from this war in the Middle East. What the United States does is a matter for them. But we won't be changing our approach."

Global oil prices have surged since March after Trump followed Israel into war with Iran, resulting in the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the vital shipping lane through which a fifth of the world's oil and gas normally travels.

Bowen added that Australia has arrangements in place "best as we possibly can" to ensure that the country does not buy oil originating in Russia.

"That policy is not changing, will not change," he said.

Sanctions against the Russian energy sector have been the centrepiece of a coordinated effort by the European Union, G7, US, and Australia since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Last year, Trump signed an executive order to economically penalise countries still importing Russian fossil fuels.

Deal will release vast quantities of Russian oil

The US Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control has issued a temporary six-month general license authorising the sale, delivery, and import of Russian diesel in the US.

Trump touted the deal to reporters outside the White House on Saturday, praising his Russian counterpart and long-time Western adversary who has been under heavy sanctions since launching an invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

"That is a big deal," Trump said. "That is massive amounts of oil coming into our country."

"I want to thank President Putin, to be honest with you. We have massive amounts of oil coming into our country and it's diesel, which is what we want.

"So, thank you."

The US leader said on social media that the deal would supply diesel, used primarily in agriculture, heating and trucking. 300,000 metric tonnes would be released to the US and global market immediately, with a further 500,000 to be released in November, and 1 million "immediately thereafter".

More oil still would follow, Trump said, depending on the condition of Russian oil refineries, which have been damaged by Ukrainian attacks.

Zelenskyy furious over deal

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy criticised the deal — announced while a Ukrainian delegation was in the US to discuss how to seek a resolution of the war — as a "weak decision on the part of strong partners".

"Gifts to Putin will not bring peace or any benefit to the civilised world. Russia will "repay" the diesel with further terror and perfidy," he said on X.

His comments came after Zelenskyy previously clashed with US Secretary of State, Marco Rubio, who described the Ukraine conflict with Russia as a "stalemate" on a recent trip to Europe.

"We are not in a diplomatic dead end," Zelenskyy said, calling on the US to step up in its support for the embattled country.

"Shut down its entire economy. Shut Russia's mouth — simply stop feeding it with your sales. Impose sanctions on its weapons."

The oil deal was also criticised by US lawmakers, including Trump's fellow Republican, Representative Don Bacon, who said it was time to apply further sanctions than the ones Congress passed just last month, not lift them.

"Now is the time to use those sanctions to squeeze Putin’s war machine, not reward a dictator by putting more money in his hands while he continues targeting and killing Ukrainian civilians," Bacon said on X.

'The aggressor is being rewarded'

Former Australian ambassador to Russia, Peter Tesch, said the deal would do little to improve the cost of fuel in the US but would bring substantial benefit to the Russian economy as a result of Trump's war in the Middle East.

"This is perhaps the more nauseating dimension of this," Tesch told ABC News on Saturday.

"Apart from the potential for up to $300 million to flow into Russia's treasury, which will then of course be directed towards Putin's war of choice against Ukraine."

Tesch said that the deal demonstrated US' contempt for international norms and its willingness to act unilaterally when in its own singular interests.

Further, the deal puts pressure on Zelenskyy not to attack Russian oil infrastructure so that orders can be met. While the new external demand might put pressure on Russia's own domestic oil needs, it was unlikely to seriously undermine Putin's authority, Tesch added.

"They are rewarding the aggressor even as Putin is ramping up his deliberate targeting of civilian infrastructure, apartments, hospitals, energy facilities in Ukraine, as that country is staring down the barrel of another hard winter," he said.

"I hope that President Zelenskyy will continue to enjoy the support not just of Australia, but of European partners and will continue to do what he needs to do with the support of his allies to extract a price for Putin from his war of choice."
