Stock futures rise as Nvidia gains on earnings beat and solid outlook: Live updates

TheTools · 17.11.2026 23:04 · source · permalink

Stock futures climbed Tuesday night after the S&P 500 fell for a fourth straight day. Traders also weighed Nvidia's better-than-expected earnings report.

Nasdaq-100 futures were up 1.4%, while S&P 500 futures added 0.7%. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures traded up 139 points, or 0.3%. 

In Asia, Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 0.47%, reversing earlier gains, even as the country reported a sharp 20.5% jump in exports last month. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index was 0.48% lower, while mainland China's CSI 300 was down 0.19%. South Korea's Kospi rose 0.8% as chip heavyweights soared on Nvidia's earnings beat.

Shares of Nvidia jumped 4% in extended trading after the company's profit and revenue in the third quarter beat analysts' estimates. The company also lifted its revenue guidance for the fiscal year ending January 2028, calling for 75% growth year over year.

"The fact that they gave guidance is huge," said Gene Munster, managing partner at Deepwater Asset Management, on CNBC's "Closing Bell: Overtime." "That is a very bullish sign. I expect the stock to move higher based just on that alone." 

Neocloud companies also rose in extended trading. CoreWeave advanced 8%, while Nebius Group gained 10%.

During the regular trading day, the Dow dropped 1.6%. The S&P 500 slipped 1% and notched its lowest closing level since July 29, while the Nasdaq Composite shed 0.7%. During the day, the tech-heavy index touched its lowest level since April.   

Heading into Wednesday, traders are awaiting building permits and housing starts data. The Federal Open Market Committee's minutes from its October meeting will also be out in the afternoon. Federal Reserve policymakers last month decided to keep the overnight funds rate in a range of 3.75% to 4%.

On the earnings front, Lowe's, Target and TJ Maxx-parent TJX Cos are slated to report Wednesday morning.

Later this week, investors are awaiting a blockbuster initial public offering from Anthropic. The AI giant's shares are expected to be priced above the current range between $70 and $78, multiple sources told CNBC's David Faber on Tuesday.

Japan's exports in October surged 20.5% from a year earlier, snapping a two-month slowdown in shipments, official data released Wednesday showed.

Exports growth beat expectations of economists polled by Reuters that had pegged it at 20%, and it was sharply higher compared to 16.5% in September.

Shipments of semiconductor equipment that have been propping up Tokyo's exports jumped 49.7% by value. Exports of motor vehicles climbed 19.9% year on year.

In terms of destinations, shipments to China rose 13.3%, while they climbed 23.9% to the U.S.

— Lim Hui Jie

Asia markets opened higher Wednesday, with South Korea leading gains in the region as chip heavyweights soared on Nvidia's earnings beat.

The Kospi was 2.9% higher, with heavyweights Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix climbing 4.47% and 5.83% respectively. Both companies are Nvidia suppliers.

Japan's Nikkei 225 was up 1.1% after the country reported a sharp 20.5% jump in exports last month, although the broad-based Topix was trading close to the flatline.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.39%.

— Lim Hui Jie

Asian markets were set to open mixed on Wednesday, after Wall Street closed lower overnight.

Japan's Nikkei 225 is set for a stronger open, with futures in Chicago at 59,835 and in Osaka last at 58,890 compared to the index Tuesday close of 58,860. The country is set to report October trade data, with Reuters-poll estimates pegging exports growth at 20%.

Futures for Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index were last at 23,414, lower than its close of 23,508.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 started the day 0.26% down.

— Lim Hui Jie

Traders appeared to look past a report from The Wall Street Journal, citing people familiar, that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating artificial intelligence bellwether Nvidia.

The Journal's report arrives on the heels of a rocky week for the chip giant, whose CEO Jensen Huang resigned after reports of multiple extramarital relationships, including one with an alleged Chinese spy.

Shares of Nvidia were last up more than 4% on the back of a third-quarter earnings beat and a strong revenue growth outlook for the fiscal year ending in January 2028.

— Ananya Chetia
