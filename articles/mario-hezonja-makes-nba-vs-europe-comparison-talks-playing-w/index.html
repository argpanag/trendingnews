Mario Hezonja says life in the NBA feels easier than in Europe because of the superior organization and travel setup, while playing alongside stars such as James Harden and Donovan Mitchell has pushed him to embrace a smaller, more versatile role after starring for Real Madrid.

Cleveland Cavaliers forward Mario Hezonja says returning to the NBA has shown him just how significant the gap remains between basketball in the United States and Europe.

Asked about the difference in travel and the number of games compared with Europe, Hezonja noted that life has actually felt easier since his return.

"It's easier here. In Europe, they cram in a lot of games, while organizationally it's on a completely different level here," Hezonja told Kos Magazin.

"When you've spent many years in Europe, you start thinking you're really close to the NBA. We played against Dallas in Madrid, and when you see those guys come out, they're completely different bodies."

Hezonja was referring to Real Madrid's preseason meeting with the Dallas Mavericks in October 2023, when the Spanish powerhouse defeated Dallas 127-123.

The 31-year-old went even further when comparing how the two basketball environments operate.

"As far as organization goes, it's not even close, and it probably never will be close to what it is here in the U.S.," Hezonja said. "Now I can really see how far away it is from everything."

He also welcomed the possibility of a new league structure in Europe (NBA Europe), suggesting that NBA involvement could help narrow the gap.

"I'd be happy if some kind of league were created in Europe, but that should be left in their hands because this is really different," he added.

The Croatian forward has also had to make a significant adjustment on the court.

After being one of Real Madrid's primary offensive options, Hezonja now shares the floor with established NBA stars such as Donovan Mitchell and James Harden and has far fewer possessions with the ball in his hands.

He insisted that the change has not been difficult.

"It's not, because there are other things I can contribute with: rebounding, setting screens, assisting," Hezonja said.

"So I focus more on that role instead of asking myself why I'm not the one bringing the ball up or playing pick-and-roll. I focus a lot on the things I can do to help the team."

Over his first 12 games of the 2026-27 season, Hezonja is averaging 7.8 points, 5.4 rebounds, and 1.5 assists per game for Cleveland while shooting 42.2% from the field.

Hezonja returned to the NBA during the summer after spending four seasons with Real Madrid.

Cleveland signed him in late July to a reported one-year, $2.8 million contract, bringing him back to the league for the first time since the 2019-20 season.

During his spell in Madrid, Hezonja won the 2023 EuroLeague title and was named ACB League MVP for the 2025-26 season after averaging 17.5 points and 4.9 rebounds.
