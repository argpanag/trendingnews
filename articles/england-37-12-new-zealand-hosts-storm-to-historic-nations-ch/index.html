Right, thanks for joining Lee earlier and me for a few minutes. Now you just about have time to read Robert’s match report before joining Lee for Ireland v South Africa:

Good night from me.

Robert Kitson has filed his match report:

If Kiwi fans thought last week’s loss to Wales was painful this was arguably as bad in terms of the reputational damage. The home side were 19-0 up after just 36 minutes and had the game all but wrapped up within a couple of minutes of the restart when Ollie Chessum smashed his way over to claim his side’s fourth try. From a margin of victory perspective this was England’s biggest victory over New Zealand in the 121-year-old history of the fixture.

There were even occasions when the fabled silver fern was in some danger of being totally trashed, with almost three-quarters of the game taking place in New Zealand half. If anything it was England who more closely resembled the slick All Black sides of yesteryear, particularly in the first-half when tries from Henry Slade, Jack van Poortvliet and Ben Earl rewarded the hosts’ purposeful approach play and seemingly greater desire.

England paying the penalty for the 45-21 defeat in South Africa, when they failed to get a bonus point, while France did get two when they lost 34-32 to New Zealand.

Guy Hornsby writes: “I know people will say NZ have been poor, and they have, but England didn’t give them a sniff for a half. They controlled possession, were layered in attack and brutal in defence. With Fin Smith, Furbank and Slade, there’s 3 that can play first receiver, and Atkinson followed up the Australia game with another really promising game at 12, where he’s looking settled. We can only beat who is in front of us, and right across the 23, we’ve been really great. It does feel like things are falling into place, and our depth, bar perhaps at hooker, if we get an injury, is looking pretty good. The mix of dynamic attack and power is really coming together. Excellent performance. For the All Blacks? A lot of soul searching, they really didn’t fire much of a shot.”

Hard to disagree.

England have a huge points difference of 159. But any France win over Argentina with a bonus point would knock them down to second. Ireland could beat South Africa … and wind up in Friday’s third-place final.

Sean Fitzpatrick closes the reaction out by laughing ruefully at the possibility of a rematch against England in next weekend’s second-place final. New Zealand have had a chastening few months. Care said they were worse against England than against Wales – and the scoreboard suggests he was right. But was that about England being so good?

Danny Care calls it a masterclass by England and potentially “the most un-All Blacks performance” he has seen from New Zealand.

Ugo Monye says we have seen the team get better and better, and they were comfortable in winning by a record margin … with no over-celebration.

In a shock development, Itoje is looking forward to next week.

“We had a fast start again.” He praises the impact of those coming “off the wood”. It’s a theme.

Now Maro Itoje. “It feels pretty good … very proud of the team, the coaches.”

Genge asserts that the gameplan hasn’t changed since the Six Nations, that it is just about fine-tuning.

Genge talks about the bench, the importance of being in the 23, “You have to be happy for each other … it’s not possible to do it for 80. We’re happy to play whatever the role is as long as we’re winning.”

Ellis Genge can’t hear the questions from Mark Pougatch. When he moves in closer he praises the team for riding New Zealand’s wave successfully.

Danny Care makes a joke about “England to win the World Cup” but calms down to say it was a clinic in attack and defence.

Hello, Philip Cornwall here. While Lee prepares for Ireland v South Africa, I am stepping in for the reaction to another fine England win. After all the flak Steve Borthwick took pre-tournament with suggestions he had lost the dressing room, what a November. The players rejected those claims verbally and have now demonstrated it in fine style.

I’m jumping over to cover Ireland v South Africa and I leave you in Philip Cornwall’s capable hands.

FULL TIME! ENGLAND BEAT THE ALL BLACKS!

A convincing and powerful victory from England over a poor New Zealand.

PENALTY! England 37 - 12 New Zealand (Marcus Smith)

80 mins. Smith slots three to end the match.

79 mins. The ball is soon back in England hands, with Fisilau prominent in the carries moving them up to the 5 metre line. On an advantage Smith chips it in-goal but an All Black hand grounds it. However, we come back for the penalty.

78 mins. The scrum on halfway from the Barrett knock-on takes a few moments to complete, and when it does Marcus Smith boots the ball aimlessly back to NZ.

76 mins. England’s forwards drive into the line with a few carries, but Scott Barrett gets amongst the breakdown to win a holding on penalty. The ball is cleared upfield and on the next possession Chessum rattles Barrett’s torso to send the ball flying from his hands.

74 mins. From a 5m scrum England win a penalty after Pasilio Tosi drops his knee to the grass. The home side opt for the tap and go.

71 mins. A representation of how comfortable England are in this match: Henry Pollock has replaced Murley on the wing.

It’s not a forced move due to injuries and lack of subs, this is a choice by Borthwick.

70 mins. To emphasise the previous point, the NZ scrum has just been marmalised.

68 mins. As you can imagine the All Blacks are forcing it now, with the latest possession being shovelled to the right and into touch in the face of a white wall that remains unhurried and unmoved.

The most worrying thing if you are a New Zealander is how utterly weak they have looked. The energy is low which is likely a confidence thing, but their weak physicality is a real concern.

66 mins. A promising position for NZ after a Roigard half-break around the ruck is ruined by Cowan-Dickie getting on the ball first to win a penalty.

TRY! England 34 - 12 New Zealand (Seb Atkinson)

63 mins. Ellis Genge is off the bench and having a wonderful time running with the ball. His latest punches a hole into the 22 and a calm reset sends the ball to Atkinson to dive over in the right 13 channel.

60 mins. Aumua is on at hooker but the outcome remains the ball being chucked to an England player in the linout. The All Blacks have now lost 40% of their lineouts in this game; there really are no words.

58 mins. NZ are working phases near halfway where their initial pass is always 10 metres from the ruck to first receiver, and the England defenders are absolutely gobbling this maladroit nonsense up. In the end McKenzie has had enough and chips it forward for Barrett to chase, but the ball is gathered by England and that’s that.

PENALTY! England 27 - 12 New Zealand (Marcus Smith)

55 mins. After that brief hiatus England return to the NZ 22, Early carrying strongly once more. They are up to the 5m line with the noise raising and that weirdly appropriated slave spiritual song ringing out from the crowd.

The All Blacks again do enough to spoil the possession at the ruck close to the line, but they were offside amongst it all and Marcus Smith caresses it over.

TRY! England 24 - 12 New Zealand (Beauden Barrett)

52 mins. And just like that, when all looked to be achieving nowt, there is a spring forward from the All Blacks with Roigard breaking the line and then finding Barrett on a fabulous angle from deep to outpace Furbank and race over from 30 metres.

50 mins. Eight phases from NZ near halfway go nowhere as the Cardiff vibe of of one-out runs, slow passes and zero disguise allow England to defend easily,

46 mins. Vaa’i charges down a van Poortvliet kick on the England 5m line, looping the ball high but into the arms of Chessum who carries forward to allow space for a successful clearance this time.

Absolutely nothing going for NZ presently

45 mins. The All Blacks wake up a bit, finding Beauden Barrett out on the right touchline who bunts the ball forward up the line, but the covering defenders mop it up.

TRY! England 24 - 5 New Zealand (Ollie Chessum)

43 mins. From the stolen lineout England move forward quickly before the ball moves left via a lovely looping pass from Fin Smith to Chessum, lurking like a mobile monolith on the wing. He rides a tackle and gets it down.

42 mins. Van Poortvliet puts the ball into touch after receiving the kick-off and Coles gets up in front of Holland to nick Taylor’s lineout throw. Next lineout the same happens but it’s Chessum who steals it.

It appears the “no more mistakes, eh lads?” plea from Rennie at half time has fallen on stony ears.

England’s turn to receive the ball as the second forty of the match begins.
