  4. 'It's nonsense that Palmer isn't comitted to England' - Rogerspublished at 22:22 GMT
    22:22 GMT

    FT: England 2-1 Croatia

    England

    England goalscorer Morgan Rogers, speaking on BBC Radio 5 Live about people saying Cole Palmer is not as committed to his country as he should be: "It's a lie. He wants to be here. He wanted to get called up and he wanted to come and represent his country as he always has done. People just see it as news and say the easy thing.

    "We all know he had a difficult year last year with injuries and I know personally he's been managing them injuries ever since. He was in quite a low place with his injuries last year and didn't feel 100% himself on the pitch, so he wants to be here and he wants to be the best version of himself. Of course he does, who wouldn't? But he also has to look at his body. He wants to play for 10/15 years, he also has to manage himself. If he has to manage himself in the short term for the greater good then he has to do that and you have to fully support it. It has nothing to do with England or not wanting to play with England. It's a nonsense really that people are questioning someone that wants to play for England. You see the joy and smile on his face, how much he loves the game, so I honestly don't get it and he doesn't get it. But it's something he has to deal with but I can assure you he wants to be here and he wants to play for his country."

    On whether he wants to see Palmer in an England shirt: "Of course I do. What a player he is. What a person he is and it's someone you want to see in an England shirt. He's got so much quality, so much ability. We all know what we can do, what he's done, so of course we want to see him in an England shirt and hopefully sooner rather than later."

  6. 'Beyond dreams'published at 22:16 GMT
    22:16 GMT

    FT: England 2-1 Croatia

    England

    England captain Harry Kane, speaking on BBC Radio 5 Live: "Big thanks to everyone who organised the start of the match. To have all them presentations, thank you to Croatia and Luka [Modric] for presenting me with that as well.

    "I'm trying to take everything in. It's an important milestone in England's history but once the whistle goes, the most important thing is the match and trying to win. I've always said all these big milestones that I've had, when you win the game it makes it 10x better and I've been lucky enough to win all of them, especially here at Wembley so of course nice to do it again.

    "It's been a crazy few weeks. It, of course, takes a lot out of you emotionally, physically and mentally and all these things. Of course I'm very good at staying focused on the job and what needs to be done but it's a hard balance because you want to take it in. Breaking the record is a massive thing in the history of our game, seeing the tifo there, having my kids with me, and being presented by all the people was beyond dreams, so of course you have got to take it in. Once the whistle goes and you're on the pitch, you just start focusing on what your job is, pressing and playing the ball, playing assists and goals and that just takes over. It's always been in me that I'm able to switch, and of course now I can enjoy it with my family on the pitch after this."

    On how he was feeling when he was taking the penalty: "Little bit nervous, as always, especially since I missed my last one for Bayern. The next one is always important to bounce back and not get on a run of missing them. I done my usual routine yesterday in training and looking at the goalkeeper. I haven't gone down the middle with a penalty for maybe three or four years. It's been a while since I done that. Maybe 2023 since I last went down the middle. With penalties, you have to keep things fresh, keep things moving. I thought it was a good time to do it, looking at his clips. Thankfully, it paid off today."

  9. 'Everything will be different against Spain'published at 22:06 GMT
    22:06 GMT

    FT: England 2-1 Croatia

    England

    England manager Thomas Tuchel, speaking to ITV: "Good win. We wanted this win desperately and I think we deserved it. I think it was hard work against a good opponent. It took us a while to grow into the game but after 15 minutes I think we did OK, got the goal and got better and more confident and had some good actions and good chances, good half-chances. Got a little bit sloppy at the very end of the first half, started the second half very good, didn't score the second goal, conceded from a set piece, and then showed a good reaction.

    "We got the job done and it was important because it was a special occasion for Harry [Kane] and he scored the winner, so perfect evening."

    On whether England were sloppy when they conceded: "It was maybe only three or four minutes before we gave passes away and especially in a moment when we were clearly the better team, we came with a very strong reaction after the half-time, had a beautiful chance created from Declan Rice and they blocked it on the line. It was like our third goal was coming and we had some easy ball losses in our own half that killed our momentum.

    "An awkward moment in our box can always happen. It's important to react and we did it."

    On how his defence did tonight: "Good. It was demanding for Dan Burn and also for Jarell [Quansah] because they [Croatia] play with a back five and have two number 10s in between the lines.

    "It was a lot of work to do, a lot of communication but they did very well."

    On what will be better against Spain on Sunday: "I don't know. Everything will be different. Completely different scenario, completely different setup, but I'm very positive that we will get what we want."

Harry Kane scored his 92nd goal as he broke the appearance record for England's men's side

Harry Kane celebrated a men's record 126th appearance for England with a goal as they brushed aside Croatia in the Uefa Nations League.

The Bayern Munich striker led the team out in front of huge tifo display celebrating the landmark, before he lifted the Ballon d'Or trophy which he won recently to crown him the world's best player.

Kane, 33, received accolades from Prince William and ex-goalkeeper Peter Shilton - whose record he broke.

But it was a slow start from Thomas Tuchel's side at Wembley and it wasn't until the 24th minute that they found the breakthrough.

Kane was involved as he dropped deep and played a chipped pass to Jude Bellingham who cut the ball back for Morgan Rogers to bundle in from close range.

Croatia grabbed an unlikely equaliser in the 59th minute after an error from centre-back Jarell Quansah, who tried to head the ball back to keeper Jordan Pickford following a corner, but left it short and Petar Sucic nipped in to poke the ball into the net.

But Quansah redeemed himself five minutes later as the defender won a penalty after he was adjudged to have been held and pulled by Ivan Smolcic at an England corner.

After a video assistant referee (VAR) check, Kane confidently put the ball down the middle for his 92nd England goal, with 27 of them spot-kicks.

Substitute James Garner thought he added a third late on but Bellingham was adjudged to have been offside before he teed up the Everton midfielder for a low drive past Dominik Livakovic.

Victory secured England's place in the quarter-finals of the Nations League, which take place next year. They will face Group 3 leaders Spain - who beat the Czech Republic 2-0 - on Sunday knowing a win over the world champions will put them top of the standings.

England analysis: Kane's historic night

The match with Croatia was always going to be about England's record-breaking captain Kane.

Boss Tuchel had warned in the build-up that supporters should not expect the same performance which saw the Three Lions put seven past Croatia in the last international break and he was right as his side underwhelmed

But the result was not the main focus on Thursday night as Ballon d'Or winner Kane got the acclaim he deserved as he continued to stake his claim as his country's greatest ever player.

Even in a performance where England were not at their best, Kane often dropped into midfield - something which has become such a crucial part of his game and highlighted an outrageous range of passing.

Kane spoke earlier in the week about how he has targeted reaching 100 goals as quickly as possible and his penalty put him within eight of another astonishing achievement - and 39 clear of any other England player.

He will be 35 by the time the European Championship comes around in 2028, but his quality and prowess in front of goal shows no sign of diminishing, giving England a shot of ending that long wait for a major trophy.
