On Sunday, the Moto3, Moto2 and MotoGP Grand Prix races will take place at the Misano World Circuit Marco Simoncelli. SPEEDWEEK.com provides an overview of when and where the races will be broadcast.

13.09.2026 - 07:07

This article is an automatically generated English version. The Original Article was published in German.

As well as the official website motogp.com, Sky Sport also broadcasts all 22 race weekends of the MotoGP World Championship, featuring the premier class, Moto2 and Moto3. However, both services are subject to a subscription fee. At the turn of the year from 2023 to 2024, the Austrian private broadcaster ServusTV, part of Red Bull Media House, ceased its linear broadcasting operations in Germany; since then, MotoGP broadcasts have been provided in collaboration with DF1. From 2027, selected races will then be shown on free-to-air television in Germany on RTL.

Advertisement

Twelve selected events from the premier class of motorbike racing will be broadcast live and free of charge on DF1 via linear TV in 2026; digitally, these races can be viewed on the streaming services ServusTV On and Red Bull TV, which are also accessible on any modern TV set. Highlights will be shown for the remaining races.

This weekend’s San Marino Grand Prix is one of the events to be broadcast live in Germany. DF1 will broadcast the races in all classes on Sunday from 10.20 am.

In Austria, ServusTV is providing free coverage of the MotoGP World Championship via its linear and digital channels, including Red Bull TV. On Sunday, ServusTV will broadcast the races in all classes live from 10.20 am. ServusTV On has been on air since Friday.

SRG holds the linear and digital rights for Switzerland and will broadcast on SRF (German), RTS (French) and RSI (Italian). On Sunday, SRF 2 will broadcast the MotoGP race from 1.50 pm. The premier class Grand Prix will also be streamed live on Sunday via the SRF Sport app.

RSI will broadcast live races from all classes on LA2 on Sunday from 10.55 am. RTS2 will show the MotoGP race on Sunday from 1.50 pm.

Due to the allocation of secondary broadcasting rights, coverage may occasionally be available via providers not listed here. Furthermore, given the variety of transmission and reception technologies, as well as the specific choice of provider and its channel line-up, it can only be determined on a case-by-case basis whether MotoGP can be received legally.
