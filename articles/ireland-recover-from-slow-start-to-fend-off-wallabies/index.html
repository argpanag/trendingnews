Ireland recover from slow start to fend off Wallabies

TheTools · 27.11.2026 22:06 · source · permalink

Ireland will head into a World Cup year in good health after they made it a clean sweep for their Nations championship November.

Andy Farrell's side never quite hit the same heights as last week's win against the world champions South Africa, but after surviving an iffy opening quarter they fended off Australia 38-14 at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham.

Their win confirms they finish fifth in the unofficial standings in the first Nations Championship, but the overall ranking mattered far less than the performance, in what was their 11th and final game of 2026.

That performance had a bit of everything.

For 20 minutes Ireland struggled to even touch the ball as some inaccurate passing and a raft of penalties invited the Wallabies onto them, with Les Kiss's side taking a 7-0 lead after Jeremy Williams' try.

It was the splash of water in the face that Ireland needed, with tries for Robbie Henshaw and Nick Timoney, along with two conversions and a penalty from Sam Prendergast nudging them 17-7 ahead at the break.

Rob Baloucoune's try after half time gave them a comfortable buffer, but they had to weather a Wallaby storm for large periods, before James McNabney (below) continued his impressive fledgling Test career with two tries in the final 10 minutes to hammer home victory.

Jacob Stockdale justified his start with an impressive outing on the left wing, while Prendergast's 100% record off the tee saw him kick 13 points, while setting up Baloucoune with a wonderful crossfield kick.

However, there was a return to the setpiece inconsistencies and hair-trigger indiscipline, which will give Farrell food for thought heading into the Six Nations.

It was a brutally sloppy opening from Ireland, with a pair of scrum penalties either side of a passing mix-up between Jamison Gibson-Park and Sam Prendergast, and they were fortunate not to give up a try on 12 minutes when Carlo Tizzano broke into the Irish 22, but the Wallabies fluffed a great opportunity when Tom Wright knocked on.

The stats showed the Wallabies had 90% possession through the opening quarter, but Ireland weren't helping themselves, a rare penalty and lineout in the Australian half was spilled by Baird after they had tried to throw over the top.

The discipline of last week's win was nowhere to be seen, and two quickfire penalties - both against Tadhg Beirne - gave the Australians a tap-and-go opportunity from five metres out on 22 minutes, with Williams burrowing over for a converted try and a 5-0 lead.

They try woke Ireland up, and when they won a midfield penalty shortly after the restart, Prendergast kicked them to the 22, and a clever play off the lineout between Gibson-Park and Rónan Kelleher got them close, before quick hands sent Stuart McCloskey into space, and he put his centre partner Henshaw (above) in for a try to level it at 7-7.

Australia had to rejig their backline when they lost Max Jorgensen to a HIA, and it was no surprise Ireland began to control the game after his departure, getting Jacob Stockdale and Rob Baloucoune more involved, although lineout issues continued to stunt their progress.

On 36 minutes they hit the front, with Stockdale breaking down the left touchline after some fast handling, and when a penalty was awarded, Ireland kicked to the corner and mauled their way over, with Timoney getting the touch down.

Prendergast converted that score and added three more to Ireland's tally right on the stroke of half time with a penalty to make it 17-7, although the Wallabies were lucky to keep 15 players on the field after Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii avoided a yellow card for a deliberate knock-on in the 22.

Stockdale came within a fingernail of extending the lead within seconds of the restart, a chip-and-chase down the left touchline rolling back the years to his 2018 breakout season, but the wing was millimeters from getting to the ball (above).

Fortunately, the third try came seconds later as Ireland went back on the attack, before Furlong's deft draw-back allowed Prendergast flick a crossfield kick to Baloucoune, and after James Ramm bobbled the ball, it fell kindly for the Irish winner to run in and score, with Prendergast maintaining his 100% record off the tee for a 24-7 lead.

The floodgates didn't open, and a penalty against Hugo Keenan led to a sustained period of Australian pressure, which was matched by impressive Irish defence to hold them out on four separate occasions, but eventually the Wallabies broke them down, with Josh Canham's direct line slicing the defence open, and Kalani Thomas following up to score.

The Wallabies were controlling possession and territory, but errors were plaguing them, and when a Gibson-Park kick was chased down by Keenan, the full-back forced Fraser McReight into touch for a 70 metre gain, and the platform for them to kill off the game.

Ireland's scrum had grown into the contest, and on 70 minutes they drove the Wallabies off their own ball to win a penalty under the posts, and from a pick-and-go effort they crashed over for their fourth try, McNabney celebrating his second cap with his first Irish try.

The Ulsterman's debut against Fiji lasted just three minutes due to a concussion, and determined to make up for lost time, the 23-year-old powered over again with the final play of the game, as Prendergast converted to put the finishing touches on the win.
