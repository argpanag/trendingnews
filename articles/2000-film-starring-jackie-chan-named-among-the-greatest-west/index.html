Jackie Chan has made plenty of martial arts movies throughout his career, but one of his most memorable genre mashups took the action star all the way to the American West.

Shanghai Noon, the 2000 action comedy starring Chan and Owen Wilson, has been named among the best Western movies of all time by StudioBinder. The publication ranked the film No. 85 on its 101-film list, placing the unlikely cowboy adventure alongside some of the most influential Westerns in movie history.

The movie may be considerably lighter than many of the classics surrounding it on the list, but that's part of what makes Shanghai Noon such an unusual Western.

Jackie Chan Takes on the Wild West

Directed by Tom Dey, Shanghai Noon follows Chon Wang, a Chinese Imperial Guard who travels to the American West after Princess Pei-Pei, played by Lucy Liu, is kidnapped.

Once in the United States, Chon crosses paths with Roy O'Bannon, a small-time outlaw played by Wilson. The two are unlikely partners, but they eventually team up to rescue the princess and take on the people responsible for her kidnapping.

The movie combines the conventions of a Western with Chan's trademark martial arts action and a buddy-comedy setup. StudioBinder describes it as a Western that successfully brings together two mismatched heroes while mixing satire of Western tropes with martial arts action.

That combination gives Shanghai Noon a very different feel from the more traditional Westerns on StudioBinder's list, including The Searchers, High Noon, Stagecoach, and Unforgiven. Although, according to the IMDb trivia page for the movie, there are several nods to classic Westerns, including Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid and Red Sun.

More than 25 years after its release, Shanghai Noon remains an entertaining reminder that a Western doesn't necessarily need to take itself too seriously to leave an impression on the genre.
