Michael O'Neill targets 'huge achievement' ahead of Ukraine and Hungary fixtures

TheTools · 10.11.2026 11:54 · source · permalink

Michael O'Neill is aware that a "massive carrot" dangles before his Northern Ireland squad as they aim to secure promotion to the Nations League's elite tier.

Following an impressive sequence of results in the previous international window, Northern Ireland head into their final two Group B2 fixtures sitting top of the table, with a crucial group decider against Hungary on the horizon next week.

When O'Neill's team kicked off their four-match run in late September with a 1-0 victory away to Georgia – clinched by Shea Charles' stoppage-time strike – it marked their maiden win in League B of this tournament.

Now, heading into Saturday's home clash with Ukraine, they remain the sole nation in the competition yet to ship a goal after four matches, sitting one point ahead of Hungary and four clear of Ukraine.

First place would guarantee automatic promotion to League A, while runners-up face a play-off against one of the lower-ranked teams from the top tier.

"The players have a massive carrot," O'Neill told the Press Association. "It's easy, I don't think I'm going to have to motivate this group too much.

"They have the opportunity to finish top of the group, the opportunity to go to Nations League A, the highest level of international football we can play at.

"There's a massive carrot for them and it would be a huge achievement if they could achieve that."

Nevertheless, O'Neill is cautious about heaping excessive pressure on his side to claim top spot. During their October meeting with Ukraine in Slovakia, which resulted in a commanding 3-0 victory for Northern Ireland, the starting line-up boasted an average age of merely 22 years.

The squad has grown even younger since then, with Manchester United's 23-year-old keeper Dermot Mee and 20-year-old Eoin Kenny coming in for the departing Bailey Peacock-Farrell, 30, and Dion Charles, 31, in O'Neill's most recent squad announcement.

Should Northern Ireland fail to defeat Ukraine while Hungary avoid defeat in Georgia this weekend, the battle for first place will be decided next Tuesday at Budapest's imposing 67,000-capacity Puskas Arena – a daunting venue for any away side.

"We're in with a chance of winning now. Of course we want to try and win the group and get to the next level," O'Neill stated.

"But we're a young team, and we're a young squad. We won't put that too much of a burden on this group of players. They've given themselves a great opportunity and we'll make sure we do everything possible. Both of these will be tough games.

"Ukraine are a good side, Hungary are a good side, and were in League A in the previous Nations League.

"Ukraine have a lot of good players, an extremely proud nation, and you see that when they come out and play international football.

"We know the opportunity we have, but we also know the challenge is huge as well."

Northern Ireland have been dealt a setback on the injury front, though, with promising Arsenal forward Ceadach O'Neill pulling out of the squad. The 18-year-old made two appearances during the previous international window, bringing his tally to four caps, and subsequently made his Gunners debut in the Carabao Cup defeat at Fleetwood.

"We're naturally disappointed to be without Ceadach for the final two games of the campaign," Michael O'Neill said. "He has been a great asset to the squad in the last international window and everyone here wishes him a swift recovery."
