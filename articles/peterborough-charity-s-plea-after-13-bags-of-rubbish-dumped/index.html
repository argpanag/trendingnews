Peterborough charity's plea after 13 bags of "rubbish" dumped

TheTools · 18.11.2026 12:47 · source · permalink

The charity said 13 bags of unwanted items were left on the doorstep, with five more found dumped in the shop's bin outside

  • Published
    7 hours ago

A Cambridgeshire charity is urging people to put "more thought" into what they donate after bags of rubbish were dumped outside one of its shops.

Deafblind UK's charity shop in Yaxley, near Peterborough, said 13 bags containing unwanted items and household waste were left outside the store, while another five bags were found in the store's bin.

Shop manager Kirsty Lilley said the bags included muddy items, bottles and other rubbish.

"It was really disgusting. It was foul smelling," she said.

"It's a health hazard as well to the volunteers."

The charity, which supports people living with deafness and blindness, relies on volunteers and income from six shops across Cambridgeshire and Peterborough.

It accepts donations including good-quality clothing, shoes and electrical items. It also takes rags if they are clearly labelled.

Kirsty Lilley said the items were mixed with household waste and were muddy

Staff were only able to salvage two bags of items to sell.

Lilley said: "If they had just come to us, and said, 'Can you take rag?', we probably would have said yes if it was bagged properly."

She said the problem of people leaving donations outside shops was becoming more common for a lot of charities.

In their case, she said the issue had worsened since three charity bins were removed from the shopping centre's car park where the store is located.

The charity has urged donors to put more thought into what they donate

Despite the issue, Lilley stressed that donations remained vital to the charity's work.

"We appreciate every single person that donates to us. It's really important," she said.

"But maybe put more thought into them. If you're not sure what we take, give us a ring and we can let you know."
