    Manchester United v Sabah (20:00 BST)

    Simon Stone
    BBC Sport Manchester United reporter

    When Michael Carrick was asked about 15-year-old JJ Gabriel last month, he navigated the narrow path between high praise for a young player with immense talent and the knowledge he will ultimately be the one who decides when it is time for first-team exposure.

    "JJ is one of the boys," he said. "He has obviously got really big talent. He has trained with us quite a bit and he is getting experience all the time. He is physically adapting to each step and we are just continuing his development."

    Ryan Giggs, Manchester United's record appearance holder, was 17 years old when he made his club debut in 1991.

    Like Carrick with Gabriel, Sir Alex Ferguson saw Giggs playing youth football in the flesh, initially in a trial game at the Cliff training ground.

    Giggs can't assess what he looked like, but he knows what it felt like. In an insightful chat with his former team-mate on Rio Ferdinand Presents: The Debrief, external, external, Giggs offered this assessment: "Sometimes you have to be patient. Sometimes it gets to a point where you can't not pick him or have him train in the first team.

    "It is that in-between time where he thinks he should be in the first team squad every week, when Michael [Carrick] sees it a different way.

    "He is probably thinking: 'No, you need to play games. It isn't really going to do you any good travelling here, there and everywhere, getting five to 10 minutes on the pitch."

    Aside from the first game in Helsinki, Gabriel featured in all of United's pre-season trips this summer. Yet he played just once, as a late substitute against Atletico Madrid in Stockholm.

    United have posted plenty of footage of Gabriel training with the first team this season and there is a view he may be given his first-team debut in the Carabao Cup third round, which would make him the club's youngest ever player.

    However, as United have had a tricky start to the season results wise, is it practical for him to play against Premier League opposition Brighton on 16 September?

    Champions League games offer 11 substitute places, rather than nine in domestic competition, so does that mean he could be involved against Sabah on Thursday? Given he did not train with the first team on Wednesday, possibly not.

  4. Carrick explains Shaw's Man Utd training absencepublished at 11:53 BST
    11:53 BST

    Manchester United v Sabah (20:00 BST)

    Simon Stone
    BBC Sport Manchester United reporter

    Debate around Manchester United's left-back position has been raging all summer.

    The fundamental point is that while Luke Shaw started all 38 Premier League games last season, and the first three this, it was going to be stretch to imagine the 31-year-old would navigate through a 60-game campaign.

    Now United head coach Michael Carrick has given an insight into how Shaw played so often.

    Last season, Carrick's team only had two midweek games, neither of which was followed by a weekend fixture.

    Now they play Sabah FK in the Champions League, followed by Manchester City on Sunday, then Brighton in the EFL Cup next Wednesday and finally a trip to Fulham before the next international break.

    There is no point Shaw pushing himself to play tomorrow because it risks his involvement in the other games. So he didn't train.

    "We just gave him an extra recovery day today" said Carrick.

    "We'll see by tomorrow how he is, but it's not an ongoing thing or a plan or anything like that.

    "It was just off the back of the game the other day. So, as normal, each individual has different little things that we manage."

    Matthijs de Ligt's back injury cannot be described as a 'little thing' as it has kept him out for nine months now.

    Like Shaw, he was absent from training despite recent posts on United's social media accounts showing the Netherlands defender in a session.

  6. From England to 13 trophies in five countriespublished at 11:50 BST
    11:50 BST

    Manchester United v Sabah (20:00 BST)

    Dale Johnson
    Football issues correspondent

    Valdas Dambrauskas was working three jobs - at Brentford, London Tigers and as a sports development manager - when the opportunity arose to coach full-time.

    It meant leaving England to return to Lithuania, champions Ekranas appointing him assistant first-team and reserve-team coach at the end of 2010.

    After four years at Ekranas he was named manager of Zalgiris Vilnius, Lithuania's biggest club, where his career really took off.

    Dambrauskas won two league titles, three Lithuanian Cups and two Lithuanian Super Cups across three seasons - including a treble in 2016.

    Then came a series of moves in which Dambrauskas took charge of eight teams over nine years in seven countries.

    First RFS, with whom he won the Latvian Cup, and then Gorica in Croatia.

    A third league title came with Bulgarian side Ludogorets Razgrad, along with another Super Cup and Europa League qualification.

    Dambrauskas then returned to Croatia with Hajduk Split, a time which he described as "absolutely amazing" as the club won the Croatian Cup - their first trophy for 10 years.

    Next up was a spell in Greece with OFI Crete, then Omonia in Cyprus - who qualified for the Conference League - and Diosgyori in Hungary.

    Finally, in the summer of 2025, he arrived at Sabah, the 10th club of his career.

    Dambrauskas completed the league and cup double in his first season, as the club won the title for the first time.

    It took his trophy tally to 13 across five countries.

    Next stop, the Champions League.

  7. The Rwandan eyeing Champions League history against Man Utdpublished at 11:46 BST
    11:46 BST

    Manchester United v Sabah

    Isaac Fanin
    BBC Sports journalist

    Joy-Lance Mickels has been a Manchester United fan since childhood, and on Thursday the Rwanda international will fulfil a lifelong dream.

    The 32-year-old is expected to start up front when Azerbaijani side Sabah travel to the home of the Red Devils in the opening round of the league phase of this year's Champions League (20:00 BST).

    "I watch every [Man Utd] game when I can," he told the BBC World Service.

    "It was the best draw we [Sabah] got in my opinion. It makes it even better because we're going to play in Old Trafford.

    "Manchester United are one of the biggest clubs in the world so it will be a proud moment to step out on to the pitch."

    Mickels is in his second stint with the side based in the town of Masazir, which lies about 16km (10 miles) north-west of the capital Baku.

    He has played an important role in helping the Azerbaijan Premier League champions progress this far on their debut in the competition.

    The forward scored four times in qualifying as the club, founded just nine years ago, navigated four two-legged ties to reach the Champions League proper.

    One of those goals was the strike which ultimately sealed a 6-4 aggregate win over Israeli side Hapoel Be'er Sheva after a 94th-minute goal had forced the play-off tie into extra time.

    "Reaching the Champions League is an historic achievement for Sabah and starting the league phase at Old Trafford makes it even more exciting," Mickels said.

    "At the same time, we're not going there only to enjoy the atmosphere. We want to compete to represent not only our club but Azerbaijan in the best possible way and show we deserve to be there."

  9. Carrick on Shaw, 'magical' Champions League and Sabahpublished at 11:39 BST
    11:39 BST

    Manchester United v Sabah (20:00 BST)

    Manchester United

    Manchester United boss Michael Carrick has been speaking to the media before Thursday's Champions League fixture against Sabah at Old Trafford (kick-off 20:00 BST).

    Here are the key lines from his news conference:

    • Defender Luke Shaw has been given an extra recovery day and they will know by Thursday whether he will be in the squad. Dutch defender Matthijs de Ligt has trained well and is "on good track" to make a return soon but United have to "get the balance right" for the next steps.
    • Slovenia forward Benjamin Sesko is "in a good place" and will be part every game. Carrick added: "It's just a case of when he takes that next step".
    • Carrick said the Champions League is a "magical competition" and where the club wants to be, adding: "When you're out of it, you realise how special it is and when you're in it, you want to make the most of it. It's the best competition to challenge yourself and to push against the best."
    • On progressing in the Champions League, he said: "We're capable of achieving it. It's up to us to take the step of doing that. We've got to be humble enough and realistic enough to know where we're coming from and where we want to get to. We're capable of some really, really good things and especially on our day, we feel that we can go up against anybody and come out on top. It's up to us to bring that over a consistent period and consistency through games. In the end, that gets you the results and hopefully puts us in the stages that you're talking about."
    • Carrick said the Champions League means "an awful lot" and winning it as a player was "the ultimate" and "the best feeling I've had". He added that it would be nice to feel that again one day.
    • On the approach to selecting players: "It's always a balanced view. That's why we're pushing to have as strong a squad as possible to be able to adapt to certain games and certain strengths and different ways of playing and different circumstances. It's about managing the group and having the trust in everyone who's able to play and will play."
    • On Sabah: "I think they're a very well-coached team. You can see that quite clearly, their ideas, the principles that they have that are repeatable. I think they've obviously won a lot of football matches over the last 12 months or so. They're playing with a lot of confidence and you can see the way they play and take the ball and play through the pitch. They have some really good connections and press well as a team. So, we are fully aware of what we're up against and the challenge that we face."

"Reaching the Champions League is an historic achievement for Sabah and starting the league phase at Old Trafford makes it even more exciting," Mickels said.

"At the same time, we're not going there only to enjoy the atmosphere. We want to compete to represent not only our club but Azerbaijan in the best possible way and show we deserve to be there."
