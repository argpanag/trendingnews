Israel election live updates: Netanyahu battles to stay in power as voters head to the polls

TheTools · 27.10.2026 07:36 · source · permalink

Former IDF Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot is the prime minister’s strongest rival in the first nationwide election since the Hamas-led attacks of Oct. 7, 2023.

What to know

  • Israelis head to the polls today for the first nationwide election since the Hamas-led attacks of Oct. 7, 2023, the deadliest day in the country’s history.
  • Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is facing one of the most significant electoral tests of his decades-long political career after three years of war, crisis and global outcry over the country's military campaign in the Gaza Strip.
  • Ahead of the vote, Netanyahu’s Likud party was neck and neck with the Israeli military's former Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot, who has emerged as his strongest rival in a fraught campaign that has centered on the country's security and future.
  • No single party has ever earned a majority in the Knesset, or parliament, in Israel’s history so it is unlikely we'll know a winner when polls close this evening. Negotiations to build a governing coalition will likely need to take place.
  • Netanyahu has staunchly defended his conduct and the war in Gaza, which has left about 74,500 people dead since Oct. 7, and goes into the election facing an arrest warrant from the International Criminal Court. He vehemently rejects a U.N. commission's allegations of perpetrating a genocide in Gaza.

Israelis set to vote in first election since Oct. 7 attacks

Israelis this morning are voting in an election being closely watched in the United States.

Both Netanyahu and Eisenkot claim the future of Israel and its security is at stake. Netanyahu has played up his close ties to President Donald Trump in the final days of the race, and a Netanyahu victory could mean the war in Iran they launched together could intensify again.

How this former general could oust Netanyahu

The candidate with the best chance of ousting Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in Tuesday’s election is understated and, by his own self-deprecating admission, overweight.

But according to his friends and supporters, Gadi Eisenkot’s lack of glamour is exactly what Israel needs.

 Ariel Schalit / AP

“We’re not choosing a Hollywood actor. We’re choosing a prime minister,” said Kobi Marum, who said he’s known Eisenkot for 45 years, since they served together in the military. “They know that he says exactly the truth, no fake news. That’s the man. He’s not charismatic like others, but he’s saying exactly the right thing.”

The last polls before election day have given Eisenkot’s Yashar presumed coalition a lead of about two to six seats over Netanyahu’s ruling coalition, according to three of Israel’s main broadcasters.

What to know about the Israeli elections

Oded Balilty / AP

After three years of navigating war, crisis and global outcry, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is facing one of the most significant electoral tests of his decades-long political career.

As Israelis go to the polls on Tuesday, Netanyahu’s party is neck and neck with former Israel Defense Forces Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot, who has emerged as his strongest rival in a campaign that has revolved around everything from Israel’s security and military conscription to bread-and-butter issues like the economy.

This is the first nationwide election in Israel since the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas-led attacks, the deadliest day in the country’s history.

The attacks, which saw 1,195 people killed and 251 taken into Gaza as hostages, triggered a major security crisis that led to three years of war on multiple fronts. There are few households in Israel that haven’t been affected in some way by the events that have played out since, leaving a sense of profound grief and insecurity that lingers across the Israeli society.
