Alleged irregularities in multibillion-rand antiretroviral (ARV) tender - power987.co.za

TheTools · 20.11.2026 09:52 · source · permalink

The Portfolio Committee on Health has decided to hold a private meeting to examine its next step after the Health Department failed to take action after frequent warnings about a multibillion-rand antiretroviral (ARV) tender.

This comes after a briefing from the Minister of Health, Dr Aaron Motsoaledi and senior officials from the Department of Health on a recent High Court judgment that set aside portions of the ARV tender process after finding deficiencies in the evaluation and awarding of contracts.

Speaking on POWER Drive, ActionSA MP and Member of the Health Portfolio Committee, Dr Kgosi Letlape said that some members of the committee raised concerns regarding this tender in February; however, they were overlooked.

“The issues were not taken seriously. The minister’s response was essentially that the matter would be looked into since he does not handle tenders directly, he said.

Letlape stated that the court gave 120 days for the Department of Health to correct it, and the issues that were raised in court were the very same issues that were raised by the members with the committee.

He added that one of the issues was the element of collusion where prices for two different companies were close to each other and looked like they were cut and pasted.

“There were other issues where the black economic empowerment credentials of the companies were not sound, and most seriously, one of the companies had claimed to have capabilities for local production that did not exist,” Letlape explained.

He concluded that the minister’s response that he doesn’t deal with tenders directly is not satisfactory. They expect him to take accountability and disciplinary action to be taken for those who are involved.

“He is the politician in charge, and he should say I will look into this matter and will come back to you with a concrete answer. Not to say it’s not me; it’s the others. He must take responsibility as the executive officer,” Letlape said.
