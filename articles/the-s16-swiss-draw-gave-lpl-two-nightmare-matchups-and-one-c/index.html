The S16 Swiss Draw Gave LPL Two Nightmare Matchups and One Clean Escape

TheTools · 25.10.2026 01:06 · source · permalink

IG vs GEN and TES vs HLE are the headliners, BLG missed T1, and the first-round BO1 is about burning options, not settling strength.

When the draw ended, two matchups stuck first: IG vs GEN and TES vs HLE. BLG, in Pool 2, did not get T1 from Pool 3. When the screen cut to the next group, someone in the training room folded a slip covered in matchup notes and left it folded. AL drew KC, BLG drew MKOI, DK drew G2. The first Swiss round enforces same-region avoidance and seed-pool crossover. Four China-Korea matches were possible. The draw delivered the two biggest ones.

First-round BO1 distributes risk, burns options, and exposes information. It gives a weak read on strength. For three straight years, two of the eight first-round winners missed the quarterfinals. That stat matters for the later opponent sequence. Teams that have played each other do not meet again in the Swiss stage. IG can use a low-expectation match to remove GEN from its later draw pool. TES gets a cheap test from a position with no baggage. AL gets a specific EWC video assignment. BLG gets the clearest margin for error to fix its slow starts.

IG vs GEN: The Most Lopsided Paper Matchup, the Cleanest Risk Point

GEN is the LCK No. 1 seed. Chovy won his sixth LCK title. Ruler was Finals MVP. IG came out of the Nirvana group, fell to the lower bracket after a 1-3 loss to TES, and beat JDG 3-1 for its ticket. Overseas predictions gave GEN a much higher win rate. Ning and Liu Qingsong made the same point before the match: BO1 has none of BO5’s adjustment room. One early design, one teamfight position, one Herald collision can drag the game somewhere unpredictable.

Mid lane is the center of this match. Rookie and Chovy last met at Worlds in S9, when Rookie’s IG beat Chovy’s GRF. Chovy said before the match: “He was already very strong, and he is a player who operates mainly by trading health in lane; the proportion of health trading is especially high.” That line names the tension: Rookie’s aggressive trading against Chovy’s discipline and snowballing in a BO1. Top lane, TheShy vs Kiin, is another collision. Kiin’s stability is GEN’s base. One unreasonable TheShy engage in a BO1 can crack GEN’s macro script. GEN’s bot lane has had early-laning swings. Ruler’s form has loosened GEN’s old pattern of stable mid and bot advantages with the jungler controlling the map. IG’s JiaQi is at Worlds for the first time. The paper disadvantage is clear. Whether GEN’s bot lane can actually suppress him will be settled on the rift.

After the draw, IG’s whiteboard still had the last ban slot from the JDG qualifier. No one erased it. The analyst printed GEN’s first-fifteen-minute gold curves from its last three BO1s. The paper edges sat under the keyboard.

TES vs HLE: 369’s “Nothing to Lose” and Zeus’s “Winning Is Expected”

369 came back to the training room after a visa issue blocked the original top laner. He had not played an official match in half a year. His rehab was described as “being chased down and killed by a Zed in Emerald,” and his ranked score once hit 0-31. His first match back is against Zeus. 369’s Worlds record against Zeus is three straight losses. Knight said on stream: “Pressure in Emerald is this high, how are you going to play Zeus?” Gumayusi said on stream he was happy to draw TES, then shrugged. The clips make the pressure plain.

Zeka’s line when he saw the draw, “Then Zeus must be under a lot of pressure,” flips the matchup. 369 has no baggage. He was called up in an emergency, has no match rhythm, and faces expectations so low that winning is a surprise and losing is normal. Zeus stands on the other side. Winning is expected. If he fails to dominate lane, or if 369 holds his ground in a few exchanges, public opinion turns on him. That psychology does not change champion stats. It changes BP conservatism, the threshold for early trades, and the coaching staff’s risk appetite. 369 answered before the match: “After I trade blows with Zeus, I’ll be able to tell whether Emerald players or Zeus put more pressure on me.” The calm is the information fog that is hardest to target in a BO1. What champion and lane plan will a man bring after half a year without an official match? The sample barely exists.

Bot lane carries another story. JackeyLove has met Gumayusi at Worlds three years in a row. S14 and S15 both ended in quarterfinal exits. In Gumayusi’s stream, the line “It would be nice to play TES in the quarterfinals” came up. Jin-hyeok hyung replied, “Quarterfinal TES…” The chat spammed, “You’re really bad.” Gumayusi shrugged and said, “What’s wrong with saying it?” HLE is the 2026 MSI champion. Zeus won Finals MVP. The team’s system and teamfight coordination are mature. If TES wants suspense, Tian and Creme seizing early tempo against Kanavi and Zeka matters more than whether the top lane can “hold on.”

When 369’s stream chat spammed “Hanmen Zeus,” he pushed the camera down and moved the unopened bottle of stomach medicine next to the mousepad.

AL vs KC: The No. 1 Seed as a Target, and a Video Already Cued

AL, the LPL No. 1 seed, drew KC. On paper, this is the steadiest draw among the four LPL teams. KC swept AL 2-0 in the EWC knockout stage. Canna’s Rumble broke Flandre’s Ornn in lane and in teamfights. In game two, KC team-wiped AL at the dragon soul fight and ended it. KC jungler Yike said after reaching Swiss: “We have already done it once at EWC, so I think we can do it again.” KC came through the play-in with a 3-0 sweep over MVK. Its hands and champion pool are live on the patch. AL went straight into Swiss, landed later, and has less time to adapt. The No. 1 seed tag makes AL a target. The 0-2 at EWC put the correction list on the table: BP design, response to early pressure, and top-lane survival. If AL cannot answer at draft, its paper strength shrinks in a BO1.

In AL’s training room after landing, the takeout bag was still unopened. The analyst cut KC’s Herald collisions from the two EWC games into a loop. The projector froze five seconds before the dragon soul fight in game two.

BLG vs MKOI: The T1 That Never Came, and the Margin That Must Be Taken

BLG has won all three international meetings against MKOI: S14 Swiss, the 3-1 at 2025 MSI, and 2026 EWC. MKOI is the LEC No. 2 seed. Overseas odds gave MKOI a 5-to-1 payout on a win. The strength gap is clear. This match matters for BLG because a low-cost win would fix its slow start. The short BO1 leaves little time to get into form. If MKOI grabs early tempo, the match can still turn into a slog.

BLG in Pool 2 did not get T1 from Pool 3. When the screen cut away, the BLG analyst pulled the printed MKOI BP sheet from the folder. A room card held down the corner. T1 went to another group. BLG lost one high-traffic match and gained a margin-for-error match it must take.

DK vs G2: The Trouble-Maker in a Short Format

DK and G2 meet for the seventh time internationally. DK leads the map score 8-5, just beat G2 2-0 at EWC, and G2 beat DK in S13 Swiss. DK jungler Lucid said before the match: “I don’t think beating G2 will be very difficult.” Applied to G2, that is the danger. G2’s ability to make trouble in short international formats does not need equal paper strength. Unconventional picks and early designs are the hardest variables to digest in a BO1. If DK cannot build a cushion in lane, G2 can drag the match into chaos.

Closing: After Draw Night, the Teams Start Doing Concrete Things

IG reviews GEN’s bot-lane laning. TES schedules rehab scrims for 369. AL loops KC’s EWC footage. BLG works on its slow start. DK studies G2’s unconventional picks. The first round leaves every team alive. It shapes the option list in later same-record matchups. LPL got four draws: two dangerous but clean, one with a specific counter problem, and one with a clear margin for error.

Two hours after the draw, the schedule was still out. A staffer asked in the group chat, “Is the time set?” No one replied. The iced Americano had melted until water covered the cup wall. The draw screenshot was still on the phone.
