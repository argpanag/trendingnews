Police identify suspect vehicle tied to deadly midtown Toronto home invasion | CBC News

TheTools · 27.11.2026 17:56 · source · permalink

Mayor Olivia Chow says she's 'angry' at rise in home invasions, growing fear among residents in Toronto

Police have identified a suspect vehicle tied to a fatal shooting that took place during a home invasion early Wednesday in midtown Toronto.

The vehicle is described as a four-door sedan, possibly a Honda with dark winter metal rims, Toronto police said in a news release.

Emergency crews responded to reports of a shooting at a residence in the St. Clair Avenue W. and Poplar Plains Road area at around 2:40 a.m., according to police.

An unknown number of suspects forced entry into the residence, police said. One of the suspects was confronted by a resident of the home, shot him and then fled, police said.

The resident was taken to hospital, where he was pronounced dead. He has since been identified as Toronto musician Michael Eckert, 39.

Toronto musician Michael Eckert killed in attempted home invasion, police say

Toronto police Chief Myron Demkiw said Thursday that suspect details are not yet available and investigators still believe the incident was not targeted.

He urged residents to call if they recognize the suspect vehicle or have any information about it. 

Residents in the area are still being asked to check their camera feeds for any evidence, however police have received "incredible co-operation" from several community members already, he said. 

"No detail is too small for us to consider. We'll take it all," he told CBC Radio's Metro Morning.

Tributes continue to roll in for beloved musician

Eckert is being remembered as a talented musician who "always led with his heart," according to his longtime friend Galen Pelley.

"He spoke louder through his music than he ever would off stage or in person," he said, adding Eckert's instrument of choice was the pedal steel guitar.

Eckert was a "true pillar" of the music community and played directly from the heart, said Grammy-winning artist Justin Gray in a post on Facebook. He said the sudden loss is "beyond comprehension" and that the two worked together for over 20 years.

"He meant a lot to our community and our city and the world are dimmer places without him. Soar high, Neon Eagle. You will be dearly missed and fondly remembered," he said.

Michael Eckert was a beloved artist in Toronto's music scene and was expected to play a show on Saturday night, according to tributes rolling in on social media. (Alberta Davis/Facebook)

Eckert performed at the 113th Grey Cup in Calgary as back up for Blue Rodeo this month, Pelley said, adding: "He's gone too soon and that was a real moment for him to flourish."

Blue Rodeo expressed its condolences to Eckert's family and friends and called him "a kind, supportive and sweet person who was loved by so many in our music community."

"He was an absolute wizard on his instrument, deeply accomplished and fearlessly adventurous," the band said in a social media post. "It's always sad when we lose someone in our community, but a senseless, tragic loss of this sort is especially painful."

MP Melissa Lantsman posted on social media platform X that the country needs to have stricter laws against home invasions and harsher penalties.

She also said Eckert was a familiar face in Toronto's music scene and known to many "from the stage, the crowd and the bar after the show." Lantsman said he was expected to play a show on Saturday with The Lizards.

"Michael's loved ones deserve more than condolences. They deserve action. RIP and come see the show Saturday to honour a brilliant musician," she said.

Toronto police say they've had great support from residents for their investigation and are urging other community members to check their cameras for any evidence. (Paul Smith/CBC)

Uptick in home invasions in Toronto: police chief

There has been a rise in home invasion across the city, Demkiw said. Despite that, he said police are working hard to keep residents safe and solve "a lot of very serious crimes."

Demkiw said one solution to reduce the number of incidents is stronger penalties to deter people from considering crimes of that nature.

He said there is no evidence to suggest any connection to last month's violent home invasion in the Bridle Path neighbourhood.

"People need to know that [community safety] is a whole service priority and has been," he said, adding Toronto has an "incredibly" high homicide solve rate.

Mayor says police have city's full support

The city is grieving with Eckert's family, friends and fellow musicians, Toronto Mayor Oliva Chow said at an unrelated event Thursday. She echoed Demkiw's sentiments, saying home invasions have increased in the city and that she'll work with all levels of government "to stop these crimes."

"People are frightened. I have heard it. Neighbours checking their locks. Families lying awake at every sound in the middle of the night," Chow said.

"That fear is real. I feel it too, and I am angry."

She said police have the city's full support to find those responsible.

News made resident feel 'sick to my stomach'

Tara Brown said her daughter's father lives about a block from where the home invasion happened and her daughter, 13, lives at his house half of the time. The issue is "top of mind," she said. The news made her feel "sick to my stomach," she said.

"We all know this is happening but no one is doing anything about it. It's more a feeling of powerlessness," she said. "This is kind of unacceptable that this can happen."

She said she has told her daughter what to do if an intruder comes into the home.

"I have taught her already: If you hear a sound at night, you stay in your room. You never come out," she said. "I think it's pretty disgusting that, as a parent in 2026 in Canada, I have to teach my daughter, when you hear a sound at night, you don't come out because you might get shot."
