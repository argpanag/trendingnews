The Boston Red Sox have placed themselves in an excellent position with only two weeks remaining in the regular season. Boston closed its final extended homestand by defeating the Kansas City Royals, 4-1, on Sunday. The Red Sox won the final two games of the series, improved to 82-68 and moved 14 games above .500.

The Red Sox have 12 games remaining. They will spend the next week on the road before returning to Fenway Park for six games that could determine their postseason seed.

According to ESPN’s playoff projections, Boston has a 99.9% chance to qualify for the postseason. Reaching October appears likely. Determining where the Red Sox will begin the playoffs remains unfinished business.

Red Sox Hold Second American League Wild Card

Boston enters the final two weeks in third place in the American League East. The Tampa Bay Rays lead the division at 90-59, placing the Red Sox 8 1/2 games out of first place. The New York Yankees sit at 86-63, giving them a 4 1/2-game advantage over Boston.

Those records leave the Yankees in possession of the first AL wild card and the Red Sox holding the second.

Boston’s strongest competition for a wild-card berth comes from whichever team fails to win the AL Central. The Chicago White Sox lead that division at 76-73, while the Cleveland Guardians are only a half-game behind at 76-74. The Toronto Blue Jays remain within striking distance at 75-75. The Texas Rangers, who will host Boston’s next three games, are 74-76.

The Red Sox have a six-game advantage over Cleveland and a seven-game lead over Toronto. That is a significant cushion with only 12 games remaining, but it is not yet enough for Boston to relax.

Securing the second wild card would likely send the Red Sox on the road for a best-of-three series against the Yankees. Passing New York would allow Boston to host that series at Fenway Park.

Red Sox Face Six-Game Road Test

Boston’s final road trip begins Tuesday night against Texas. The Rangers are still fighting to remain in the playoff race and cannot afford to lose ground. Patrick Sandoval is scheduled to oppose Cody Bradford in the opener before Jake Bennett faces Jacob deGrom on Wednesday.

Sonny Gray and Kumar Rocker are the expected starters in Thursday’s finale. The Red Sox will then travel to Tampa Bay for three games against the team with the American League’s best record. That series represents the most difficult portion of Boston’s remaining schedule.

Tampa Bay has won 50 of its 75 home games. Boston has been outstanding away from Fenway, however, producing a 44-31 road record. The six-game trip could determine whether the Red Sox enter the final week attempting to catch the Yankees or simply protecting their postseason position.

Boston will have Monday, Sept. 21, off before beginning its final homestand. The Cleveland series carries obvious playoff implications. The Guardians are competing for the AL Central title but could also become one of Boston’s closest wild-card challengers.

The Red Sox will then finish their regular season with three games against the Chicago Cubs.

Boston Still Has Something Significant To Chase

The Red Sox have won seven of their last 10 games and own a plus-88 run differential, the third-best mark in the American League. Their postseason position became stronger over the weekend, but home-field advantage remains a realistic target.

Catching the Yankees would require Boston to remain hot while receiving help from New York’s opponents. Even if the Red Sox cannot close that 4 1/2-game gap, holding the second wild card would prevent them from falling into a more difficult matchup against a division winner.

Boston controls most of what matters.

A strong road trip could remove nearly all remaining postseason uncertainty. It also could turn the final six games at Fenway into an opportunity to improve Boston’s October path rather than a desperate fight to qualify. The Red Sox have spent months working their way into this position. Their final 12 games will decide where that work takes them.
