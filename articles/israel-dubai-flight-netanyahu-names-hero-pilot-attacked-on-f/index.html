  6. Aircraft appears to show tail fin damage after emergency landingpublished at 21:43 BST 30 September
    21:43 BST 30 September

    By Thomas Spencer and Sam Howarth

    A verified image of the Flydubai aircraft after its emergency landing appears to show it has sustained damage to its tail fin.

    BBC Verify has confirmed the image shows the Boeing 737 Max airliner on the ground at Tabuk airport in Saudi Arabia.

    Passengers can also be seen disembarking the plane onto the tarmac.

    When compared to other images of similar aircraft operated by the airline, a section of the plane’s rudder appears to be missing or damaged.

    It is not clear how this damage occurred, but experts say it is likely a result of abnormal stress placed on the plane during the rapid descent and ascent it experienced.

    Marco Chan, an aviation operations lecturer at Kingston University, tells BBC Verify the rudder damage could be explained by attempts to “aggressively” operate it and putting too much stress on an "extremely sensitive" part.

    He adds it’s possible this occurred either during a struggle to control the plane or an attempt to rescue the aircraft from a rapid descent.

  12. No protocol in place to prevent this hijack, former pilot tells BBCpublished at 20:02 BST 30 September
    20:02 BST 30 September

    Former airline captain Emma Henderson tells the BBC an attack like the one in this morning's Israel-bound flight is "very unusual".

    Although protocols have changed since the Germanwings crash in 2015 - when the co-pilot locked the captain out of the cockpit and caused a deadly crash - nothing could have prevented what happened.

    Back in 2015, the European Aviation Safety Agency told airlines to "ensure at least two crew, including at least one qualified pilot, are in the cockpit at all times of the flight or implement other equivalent mitigating measures".

    But this is different, Henderson explains: "It's very difficult to train for a scenario like this, where it seems that there was an intent by one of the pilots to do something with the aircraft that took it away from its safe flight path and then there was an altercation that resulted in violence".

    Asked about damage to the plane, Henderson says it's difficult to tell why the tail rudder is no longer there. But, she adds, if there was a struggle in the cockpit then there could be enough pressure put on those flight control surfaces to damage that and make it break away.

    "That's very much down to the investigation to find out", she says.

    On the plane dropping 17,000 ft in under two minutes, Henderson says it is "definitely not a rate of descent that we'd use as part of a normal flight", adding that you'd only expect such a drop in that timeframe in emergencies.

  14. Emotional passengers describe terrifying ordealpublished at 19:30 BST 30 September
    19:30 BST 30 September

    Gabriela Pomeroy
    Live reporter

    Passengers who were travelling on the Flydubai plane from Dubai to Tel Aviv have made an emotional return to Israel's Ben Gurion airport.

    Here's how the day unfolded.

    The plane was diverted to Saudi Arabia earlier today after one of its pilots stabbed the other pilot, in what Israel's Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has called an attempt to crash the plane. The plane plummeted 17,000ft (5,200m) and several Israeli passengers have been hailed as heroes after overpowering the attacker and taking control of the plane.

    The passengers were greeted at the airport this evening by crowds of onlookers playing music, singing, dancing and waving Israeli flags.

    "Thank God I'm alive," passenger Haim Lazarovich told journalists, adding that "the most terrifying moment in this flight was when the pilot loses control."

    A number of people were "very emotional and crying as they came off the plane," a paramedic from Israel's ambulance service told the BBC.

    Shortly afterwards, Netanyahu posted on social media that he met with one of the passengers, Yaniv, whom the PM said "broke into the cockpit".

    Yaniv explained: "The pilot who was stabbed was simply very close to the door, so it was hard for us to open it." But before the stabbed pilot collapsed he was able to open the door. Yaniv said the attacker was trying to disable the plane.

    Another passenger, Zvika Meness, said he helped overpower the attacker and that he and two others "tied [the attacker] up with his own zip ties and with the multimedia headphones."
