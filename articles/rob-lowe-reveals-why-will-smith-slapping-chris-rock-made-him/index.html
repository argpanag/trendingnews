Rob Lowe Reveals Why Will Smith Slapping Chris Rock Made Him ‘Happy’ - AOL

TheTools · 25.09.2026 22:08 · source · permalink

The 94th Academy Awards featured a controversial incident that went viral around the world and divided fans everywhere, Will Smith slapping Chris Rock. Although some time has passed since the controversy, the viral slap still echoes across the internet. Now, Rob Lowe has spoken about the incident and revealed why he was glad it happened.

Rob Lowe was pleased with Will Smith and Chris Rock’s infamous Oscars moment for this reason

Recently, the American actor appeared on SiriusXM’s Andy Cohen Live during the Wednesday, September 23, 2026, episode. During the conversation, Rob Lowe recalled the controversial slap Will Smith gave Chris Rock at the Academy Awards and explained why he felt pleased about it.

Rob Lowe said, “The worst opening ever. When Will Smith slapped Chris Rock, I was the only person happy. Because I was like, ‘Oh, good. So now that’s the worst moment in Oscar history.’ But it turns out not really. Turns out I still am dragged, dragged every year.”

Talking about the details of the Will Smith and Chris Rock slap controversy, the incident took place during the 94th Academy Awards in March 2022. The reason was that the comedian, who was presenting the award of best documentary feature at the event, made a joke about the short hair of the 58-year-old’s wife, Jada Pinkett Smith. He compared Jada to the actress with a shaved head from the movie G.I. Jane.

Will Smith took offense to the joke, so he got up from his seat, walked onto the stage, and slapped Chris Rock. The Bad Boys star did not stop there. While returning to his seat, he also shouted profanities at the host.

For context, Jada Pinkett Smith was suffering from a condition called alopecia, which was causing her hair to fall out.

Immediately after the incident, Will Smith won the Best Actor Oscar for his film King Richard. He later publicly apologized to Chris Rock and the Academy, but the Academy banned him from attending its events for 10 years.
