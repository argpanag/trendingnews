Twitch CEO Signals GTA VI Multiplayer in 2027, Ahead of Any Rockstar Date

TheTools · 18.09.2026 01:16 · source · permalink

Twitch chief executive Dan Clancy has put a 2027 marker on multiplayer for Grand Theft Auto VI. Rockstar Games has not. For Take-Two investors, that gap is the news: the head of a major distribution and creator platform is speaking from conversations with Rockstar, but the publisher has not supplied a release month, product name or business model that can go into an earnings forecast.

Clancy told Bloomberg that Twitch had spent substantial time discussing Rockstar’s plans and creator engagement. He expects a large audience increase when the base game arrives, followed by “an even bigger one next year when they launch multiplayer.” Bloomberg’s Sept. 17 interview supports the idea that this is more than an outsider’s guess. It still is not a Rockstar announcement.

Rockstar’s current public page lists only the Nov. 19, 2026 launch for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. It does not list multiplayer, a successor to GTA Online or a 2027 date. Even the label “GTA Online 2,” used widely by fans, remains unofficial.

The market did not price this as a confirmed launch

Take-Two shares closed Thursday at $210.62, down $1.19, or 0.56%, on 2.63 million shares. Amazon, Twitch’s parent, finished at $251.19, up $5.23, or 2.13%, on 29.64 million shares. Those are the Sept. 17 regular-session figures from the TTWO and AMZN market records. The opposite directions, and the absence of an official Rockstar release, make it unsafe to attribute either move to Clancy’s remarks.

The useful read-through is about timing. Take-Two’s fiscal 2027 ends in March 2027. A multiplayer release in January, February or March would fall inside the current fiscal year; anything from April onward would land in fiscal 2028. Clancy said “next year,” not which side of that boundary Rockstar is targeting.

A $5.1 billion recurring-spend question

Take-Two’s latest quarterly release guides to $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion of fiscal 2027 net bookings, with Rockstar expected to contribute about 37%. Management expects recurrent consumer spending to stay roughly level with fiscal 2026 and represent 64% of total bookings. Applying that percentage to the guide produces about $5.12 billion to $5.25 billion of recurrent bookings. That calculation is TS2’s, not company guidance for an individual title.

Multiplayer matters because ongoing engagement, virtual currency and add-on purchases can stretch a launch beyond the initial game sale. In Take-Two’s June quarter, recurrent spending accounted for 84% of $1.39 billion in net bookings, even though it declined 1% from a year earlier. The existing Grand Theft Auto series grew 3%. A new online economy could therefore affect the earnings base for years, but Clancy’s comment provides no pricing, take-rate or player-conversion assumptions.

The strongest counterargument is straightforward. Rockstar may be separating its marketing beats without changing any internal schedule, and a partner can speak loosely about “next year.” The omission of multiplayer from the public lineup does not prove a delay. It only means an investor cannot yet tell whether Take-Two’s current $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion guide includes any new multiplayer revenue.

Why Twitch expects a second audience wave

Twitch has unusually concrete evidence for its confidence. Its 2026 State of Gaming report counted more than 8.6 billion hours of gaming content watched from Jan. 1 through Sept. 1. GTA V ranked among the three most-watched games in the United States, France, German-speaking Europe and Japan. Twitch also said the GTA VI community’s response to an August presentation was large enough to disrupt the site.

The platform’s Drops data explains the commercial route. Streams tagged for Drops generated 1.6 billion hours watched, up 46%, while Twitch hosted more than 7,500 campaigns, 20% more than a year earlier. Forty million users claimed Drops, up 15%. A staggered single-player release and later multiplayer launch would give Twitch and Rockstar two separate opportunities for creator campaigns, subscriptions, advertising and rewards rather than one concentrated week.

That opportunity is much harder to value for Amazon. Its second-quarter report discloses North America, International and AWS segments, not Twitch revenue or profit. A surge in hours watched can improve ad inventory and subscriptions without becoming material beside Amazon’s $200.6 billion quarterly sales base.

The next investable evidence must come from Rockstar or Take-Two: a named multiplayer product, a dated window, platform details and an explanation of how it will be sold. Until then, Clancy’s remarks are a well-placed channel check on 2027 demand, not a date investors should hard-code into Take-Two’s model.
