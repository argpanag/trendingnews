SMBC Plans to Consolidate India Operations Under Yes Bank by FY29, Tells RBI

TheTools · 12.11.2026 14:23 · source · permalink

Category: Financial

Highlights

  • SMBC has reportedly told the Reserve Bank of India that its India operations will be consolidated under Yes Bank by FY29.
  • SMBC holds about 24.21% of Yes Bank after acquiring a 20% stake from SBI and seven other banks for ₹13,482 crore.
  • The RBI granted in-principle approval in January 2026 for SMBC to set up a wholly owned subsidiary in India.
  • SMBC currently operates through four Indian branches: New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Bengaluru.
  • The structure, timeline milestones and regulatory approvals for the consolidation have not been detailed in the material reviewed.

Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation (SMBC) has reportedly told the Reserve Bank of India that its operations in the country will be consolidated under Yes Bank Ltd. (NSE: YESBANK) by FY29. The communication, as reported, sets a multi-year horizon for combining the Japanese lender's direct Indian presence with the private sector bank in which it is now the largest shareholder.

The mechanics of the consolidation, including whether it involves a merger, transfer of business or another structure, were not specified in the material reviewed, and the announced plan should not be read as a completed or approved transaction.

How SMBC arrived at its Yes Bank position

SBI and seven other private banks, which had invested during Yes Bank's 2020 reconstruction, sold a 20% stake to SMBC for ₹13,482 crore. SMBC subsequently held about 24.21% of the bank, becoming its largest shareholder, while SBI's holding fell to 10.78%. In March 2020, a consortium led by SBI had taken a 79% stake to stabilise the bank.

The investment marked one of the largest foreign investments in an Indian private bank and a shift in the regulator's approach to foreign ownership of domestic lenders.

The wholly owned subsidiary track

In January 2026, the RBI gave in-principle approval for SMBC to set up a wholly owned subsidiary in India by converting its four existing branches in New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Bengaluru. The RBI said it would consider granting a banking licence under Section 22(1) of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, once SMBC meets the conditions attached to the approval.

SMBC would be the fourth foreign lender to take this route, after DBS Bank India and SBM Bank India, with Emirates NBD receiving similar approval in May 2025. The reported FY29 consolidation under Yes Bank suggests that the subsidiary may be a transitional vehicle, although the filings reviewed do not say so explicitly.

Why the plan matters

Consolidating operations would give Yes Bank access to SMBC's corporate relationships, trade and cross-border business, and could shape the bank's funding profile and growth strategy. SMBC is a wholly owned subsidiary of Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group, which also owns an Indian non-banking finance company, SMFG India Credit.

A combined structure would also raise governance questions, including how overlapping businesses are integrated, how minority shareholders are treated and how regulatory limits on foreign holdings are applied.

Risks and monitorables

Regulatory approvals, shareholder consent, valuation or exchange-ratio questions, and integration complexity are the principal variables. The three-year horizon to FY29 leaves room for changes in plan.

Investors may follow RBI communication, board approvals at Yes Bank, disclosures on the transaction structure and any change in SMBC's shareholding.

Conclusion

SMBC's reported message to the RBI points to a long-term plan to fold its Indian operations into Yes Bank by FY29, building on a 24.21% stake and an in-principle subsidiary approval. The structure, approvals and valuation questions remain open. Formal filings by the bank and the regulator will determine how and when the plan is executed.
