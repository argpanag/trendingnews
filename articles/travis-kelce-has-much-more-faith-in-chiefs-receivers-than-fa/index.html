Travis Kelce has much more faith in Chiefs receivers than fans do

TheTools · 10.09.2026 16:46 · source · permalink

When it comes to the Kansas City Chiefs' wide receiver room, hope is hard to find for many fans. The lack of offseason improvement (or even activity), the lack of development in younger players, the lack of impact overall—it's hard to fault Chiefs Kingdom for largely believing that the team's pass catchers look rather uninspired as a unit.

Don't tell that to Travis Kelce.

Kelce, the Chiefs' legendary tight end, had nothing but high praise for his teammates and their potential for a new season on the latest episode of his uber-popular podcast, New Heights. Together with his brother, Jason, Travis Kelce broke down the upcoming matchup against the Denver Broncos to kick off a new season. In the process, he spoke about the Chiefs' WR corps in ways that are rarely heard.

"I think right now it's we got a stacked, stacked wide receiver room"
Travis Kelce

"I know Xavier Worthy, he's taking that next step. [Rashee] Rice has been proven in this offense, and he's looking as prime and as ready as he's ever been. And then Tyquan Thornton's a tall receiver downfield that made a lot of huge plays for us last year. And you know, there's a handful of others like Nikko [Remigio] and Jalen [Royals]. I mean, I could go on."

The rundown of the Chiefs' depth chart at wideout is quite the optimistic take, but few would know the possibilities as well as Kelce. The front office basically copied and pasted the depth chart from a year ago, which means Kelce has been working alongside the Chiefs' receivers for well over a year, giving him unique insights into what each of them brings to the table.

It's heartening to hear Kelce's description of the Chiefs' wideouts. It's also puzzling. Fans can describe Xavier Worthy as an injury-prone wideout whose projected breakout never happened in 2025. Kelce describes Rice as "prime" and "ready," but Chiefs fans have to wonder if the word "reliable" will ever be an apt descriptor as well.

Kelce is spot-on about Thornton, and yet, as impactful as he was, it's also safe to wonder if the Chiefs will ever open up the playbook for him after largely benching him following a scorching season-opening stretch. But Thornton's usage rates are only one question of many in that lane, since Jalen Royals was also inexplicably benched for the season despite having every reason to see what he can do in a losing campaign.

The lone newcomer is Cyrus Allen, the Chiefs' fifth-round draft choice out of Cincinnati. Most day-three rookies are going to learn for a year in Andy Reid's offense, but Allen was turning heads as early as rookie minicamp, and he followed that up with a strong preseason as well. Kelce is a believer and had glowing words for him, too.

"I think what you're alluding to is the excitement you saw or heard through camp, his playmaking ability, the preseason catches he's had. He's got a lot of explosiveness in terms of his playmaking ability. And he's got a savviness to his routes that you just can't teach. You know, some guys just have a good feel for it, and he's one of those guys, man."

Seeing is believing for most of Chiefs Kingdom when it comes to the receivers. But Kelce has staked his claim early, with a fervent belief that general manager Brett Veach has assembled a group to get things done. The talent is there, on paper, with the sort of young investments that should blossom together with a head coach like Andy Reid and a quarterback like Patrick Mahomes. Of course, Kelce's presence is a big boost as well. Perhaps his faith in them is a part of the equation for success as well.
