Training camp is officially in full swing, and Ryan Huska’s bench is transitioning from summer conditioning drills to real game action. With the league expanding its regular season to an 84-game slate, the exhibition period has been compressed down to four strategic contests. For management, the reduced timeline leaves zero room for slow starts; every shift this week carries real weight for bubble players attempting to secure roster spots before the opener.

Here are the key storylines for the Calgary Flames and their upcoming schedule for the week of Sept. 20-26, 2026.

Early Roster Trimming

Prior to puck drop on the exhibition schedule, the front office began streamlining the depth chart. The club released several junior-eligible prospects back to their respective Western Hockey League (WHL) and Ontario Hockey League (OHL) programs, followed quickly by assigning four players—including forward Carter Wilkie and goaltender Owen Say—to the American Hockey League (AHL) Calgary Wranglers.

These early moves clarify the organization’s immediate priorities. The Flames will be narrowing their focus toward establishing tactical system chemistry among the veteran core, while evaluating a select handful of high-ceiling young forwards.

Health Checks and Lineup Availability

Injury management remains a primary focal point as camp intensity escalates. Veteran playmaker Jonathan Huberdeau continues his recovery process following off-season rehabilitation on his hip. The organization is taking a conservative approach with his timeline, aiming for a potential return near the tail end of the preseason.

On a more positive note, former top prospect Samuel Honzek has been cleared for full contact and is back in uniform. After seeing his previous campaign truncated by a severe lower-body injury, Honzek’s availability provides much-needed middle-six flexibility. His ability to establish a power-forward presence along the boards will be crucial as he competes for a permanent NHL wing position.

High-Stakes Evaluation in Four-Game Tune-Up

Because the preseason is limited to four games, Huska and his coaching staff must evaluate tactical adjustments in real time. The abbreviated schedule forces a condensed trial period for new power-play formations and defensive pairings.

Evaluating line combinations under true game conditions takes precedence over final scores. Expect goaltenders Dustin Wolf and Devin Cooley to split workload duties early to establish their crease rotation ahead of October.

The primary objective over these four games is establishing defensive structure within the neutral zone. Last season, transition defense occasionally broke down due to late tracking from back-checking forwards. The coaching staff has prioritized tighter gap control from the blue line, forcing opponents to dump pucks in rather than entering the zone with speed.

The coaching staff will also be closely watching Zayne Parekh and Simon Nemec as they jockey for position to lead the Flames’ power play.
