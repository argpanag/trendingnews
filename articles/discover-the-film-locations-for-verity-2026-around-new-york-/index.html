Discover the film locations for Verity (2026), around New York in Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens plus Westchester County in Upstate New York.

Home > Films > V > Verity

Sunday November 1st 2026

This well-acted slow-burn mystery is almost a single-location movie, most of the story taking place in the luxurious lakeside home of mega-successful fiction writer Verity Crawford (Anne Hathaway).

Her husband Jeremy (Josh Hartnett) hires relatively unknown writer Lowen Ashleigh (Dakota Johnson) to complete a series of best-selling novels after Verity herself has become mysteriously incapacitated.

This involves Lowen temporarily staying with the couple in their house tucked away in the forests of 'Vermont'. A man sharing an isolated house with his bed-bound wife and an attractive younger woman... what could possibly go wrong?

You probably won't be surprised to learn that the uber-stylish house – though lumbered with a jaggedly ominous silhouette – is not in 'New England' at all.

The opening scenes are set in New York so it makes sense that the production team found an appropriate location not too far away – Westchester, Upstate New York.

It's not a private home but now a wellness retreat, called Beckoning Path, a 1960s architectural landmark set in 27 acres surrounding Cobamong Pond in Armonk, a hamlet in the town of North Castle, Westchester County.

The house was originally designed for the founders of the Dansk homeware brand by their own chief designer Jens Quistgaard, and later repurposed to be a private retreat.

I'm not convinced the row of sinister narrow peaks really communicates warmth and welcome, but I'm no architect.

A gushy television interview with celeb author Verity cuts quickly to Lowen giving a book reading to a sparse and less-than-electrified audience in a New York bookshop.

There's no fakery, this really is Books Are Magic, 225 Smith Street at Butler Street, in the Carroll Gardens neighbourhood of Brooklyn.

On her way to an intriguing meeting with her publishers, Hachette, the bloody spatter from a traffic accident leaves Lowen looking like Carrie at the prom.

Enter Jeremy, a stranger who gallantly whisks her off to be cleaned up, even lending her the shirt off his back.

The bizarre accident was filmed in front of Los Tacos No.1 at the junction of 41st Street and Park Avenue, just south of Grand Central Station in Manhattan.

Now stylishly sporting a man's crisp white shirt, Lowen makes it to her interview at Hachette – and the film uses the publishing house's real offices at 1290 Avenue of the Americas-Sixth Avenue between West 51st and West 52nd Street to the north, near Radio City Music Hall. Coincidentally, Hachette are the publishers of Colleen Hoover, author of the original novel.

That's even the real office interior, though neatly avoiding copyright issues or accusations of product placement by dressing the office with the works of fictitious authors.

Now I don't know if there's an opposite to "spoiler" – maybe improver – but don't waste the rest of the movie wondering how Jeremy set up the bloody accident precisely while Verity was waiting at the crossing light. This really seems to be a million-to-one coincidence and is ever referred to again. Strange and, I found, irritating unless you have a better theory.

Once ensconced in 'Vermont', Lowen learns the story of how Verity and Jeremy met in a bar when he came on to her in a bar with a ridiculously reckless stunt in the alleyway outside.

The bar is Laissez Fair, 10 Theatre Alley off Beekman Street just south of City Hall in Manhattan's Financial District. Described as "a high-end, bespoke underground cocktail lounge and microclub", it's in the basement of the Beekman Hotel, 5 Beekman Street. In the pic above, it's the door with the blue sign on the left of the alley.

When Lowen happens to mention to Jeremy that her birthday has just passed, he insists on taking her to a belated celebration at "the best Bavarian restaurant in Vermont".

In fact, this is German restaurant Zum Stammtisch, 69-46 Myrtle Avenue, Glendale in Queens.

That's it as far as practical locations go, though the mystery continues...
