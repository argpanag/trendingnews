West Ham takeover: Amanda Staveley ‘disappointed’ with failed stake purchase

TheTools · 14.09.2026 10:24 · source · permalink

Daniel Kretinsky has since become the club's largest shareholder Stu Forster/Getty Images

Amanda Staveley has admitted that she is “obviously disappointed” after failing to buy a significant stake in West Ham United, but added that her ambitions for the club are not over.

In a statement released to The Athletic from her firm PCP Capital Partners, Staveley has said for the first time that PCP’s original offer “was for both David Sullivan’s 38.8% stake and Vanessa Gold’s 25.1% stake” in West Ham.

In late July, Staveley, the former co-owner of Newcastle United, reached an agreement with West Ham joint-chair Vanessa Gold to purchase her 25.1% stake. But this led to a pre-emption process, which allowed other shareholders to buy the Gold family stake.

The result of that process was that Daniel Kretinsky became the largest shareholder, increasing his stake from 27% to 46%. Sullivan, who resigned as vice-chairman in June over allegations, which he has called “factually incorrect and entirely false”, made against him, increased his stake from 38.8% to 40%.

“We are obviously disappointed not to have been successful in our bid to acquire the 25% shareholding in West Ham United,” said the PCP statement. “For transparency, our original offer was for both David Sullivan’s 38.8% stake and Vanessa Gold’s 25.1% stake in the Club.

“There was always the potential that the other shareholders would take up their rights under the shareholders’ agreement, which is what they have elected to do.”

While Kretinsky is now the leading figure at West Ham, Staveley and PCP retain their interest in and ambitions for the club. Staveley engaged with the club and the London Stadium over the course of the summer, attending the 4-2 win over Wolves last week, and said in her statement they will be following the new era.

“We will continue to follow this process closely,” said the statement. “Our ambitions for the club remain, and we would be open to the opportunity to work constructively with the existing shareholders in the future subject to us not taking up other opportunities that have been presented to our consortium in the wake of recent events over the past week.”

Staveley was a co-owner of Newcastle United from 2021 to 2024, following the Saudi Public Investment Fund-led takeover.
