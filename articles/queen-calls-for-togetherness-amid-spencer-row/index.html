Queen calls for togetherness amid Spencer row

TheTools · 20.09.2026 21:19 · source · permalink

Her Majesty quotes proverb about importance of unity as Palace says Earl’s controversial memoir betrays ‘clouded reasoning’

The Queen has espoused the need for togetherness amid an ongoing row over damaging allegations in Earl Spencer’s new memoir.

Speaking at her annual book festival in Chatsworth House, Derbyshire, the Queen said she was “recently reminded of a rather beautiful proverb, ‘If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together’”.

Her comments come amid a war of words between Lord Spencer and Buckingham Palace over his forthcoming memoir about Diana, Princess of Wales.

The Queen’s speech at the fourth Queen’s Reading Room Festival followed the late Princess’s younger brother doubling down on Friday night over his claims that the King made disparaging comments about Diana in the days after her death.

Lord Spencer has suggested that the King is embarrassed about the “monstrous” alleged comment in the former’s memoir that the Princess would be forgotten “soon enough”.

In the book, titled Swan Song: Diana, My Sister, Lord Spencer claimed that during a row the pair had over her funeral plans, the then Prince of Wales “hissed” to him on the phone: “Rest assured, we’ll forget her soon enough.”

The incendiary allegation led Buckingham Palace to release a swift and unusual statement accusing the King’s former brother-in-law of “clouded reasoning” and “coloured memory”.

Now, Lord Spencer has used television interviews to double down on his accusations, insisting that he was “telling the truth” and stating that “those are exactly the words he said”.

The Palace has declined to comment further amid the ongoing serialisation of Lord Spencer’s book, being published in the Daily Mail, but the escalating row has threatened to drag the Palace into a back-and-forth.

Despite the allegations, Their Majesties have continued with business as usual, with the Queen appearing in good spirits as she made her usual appearance at the festival for her Covid book club-turned-charity on Saturday afternoon.

Upon arrival at Chatsworth’s South Terrace, the Queen was greeted by a round of applause before she embarked on an extended walk through the grounds with Gyles Brandreth, the broadcaster and her friend.

Brandreth, who is a trustee of the Queen’s Reading Room, said: “The reason we are here is that, a few years ago, Her Majesty thought, ‘I love reading, these are the books I love reading. If I share my books maybe other people will like them too’.

“From that simple idea – the Queen putting online the books she likes the most – has grown this extraordinary following, literally millions of people around the world are now reading books recommended by Her Majesty.”

Representatives from four local book clubs were introduced to the Queen, including the Elm Foundation Book Club, Women on the Road to Healing Book Club, Scathin Book Shop Book Club and ‘Books, Biscuits, and Bifocals’ Book Club.

‘I wanted to share my love of books’

The Queen then unveiled her winter picks, which included Finding Belle by Reeta Chakrabarti, The Picture of Dorian Grey by Oscar Wilde, Whistler by Ann Patchett and Sashenka by Simon Sebag Montefiore.

She also delivered a speech marking six years of her Reading Room, created as a book club in 2020 during the pandemic, and reaffirming her passion for literature.

The Queen said: “I created my Reading Room simply because, like all of us here, I love books and their life-enhancing power and I want to share that love with others.

“In those early days, I had no idea if anyone would be interested, but six years on, we are a global community of millions from over 180 countries, swapping recommendations, undertaking research and donating thousands of books to women’s refuges and homeless shelters.”

She joked that when the club launched, it might have been “called ‘The Long Shot’ by Dee Featist”, but that her “feelings would have been better captured in ‘How Books Can Change Lives’ by Mustafa Hope”.

The Queen also thanked guests for “the incredible support offered to us by a huge number of book lovers: writers, actors, publishers and, above all, readers”.

While at Chatsworth, The Queen met aspiring writers in the Writer’s Room tent and members of local book clubs in The Book Club Tent. She also attended William Sieghart’s Poetry Pharmacy, in which Natascha McElhone, the actress, read for the audience.

Accompanied by hosts Lord and Lady Burlington, the Queen also viewed Chatsworth’s House of Stories exhibition, which has been staged to celebrate the National Year of Reading.

The head of collections at the estate showed Her Majesty a selection of items that are special to Chatsworth, including first and early editions of work by Chaucer, Dickens, Oscar Wilde and Charlotte Brontë.

In the Sculpture Gallery, The Queen attended a reception with literary figures taking part in the festival, including Lee Child, Elif Shafak, Kate Atkinson, and Ken Follett.

Recommended

To mark the fourth Queen’s Reading Room Festival and its return to Chatsworth, details about a forthcoming BBC documentary on Her Majesty’s championing of reading were released.

The Queen will explore the “transformative power of reading” in the BBC Arts programme The Book That Changed My Life, which is scheduled to air next month.

The BBC has said the documentary will follow the Queen as she meets four people whose lives books changed at moments when they most needed them.

“Warm, emotional, and full of hope, the film explores how reading can lift us, open our minds and, sometimes, change the course of a life,” the broadcaster has said.

The details of the show were shared during a festival discussion on the power of book clubs, led by Ann Cleeves, a novelist and crime writer.

During the event, she spoke with Ian Kirby, one of the documentary’s contributors, asking him about his own experience of finding community and hope through reading.

Kirby, from Gateshead, fell into a deep depression after a traumatic incident at home, but turned his life around after joining a local book club and picking up the autobiography of Glenn McCrory, a local boxing hero, which offered a new perspective on his own experiences.

Speaking in the documentary, he says: “That is the beauty of a book. It can give you experiences you’ve never experienced, but they can also be an inspiration.”

The Queen announced the programme during a Clarence House reception celebrating the fifth anniversary of her book club in March.
