All Blacks equal lowest-ever World Ranking; Northern trio close in on Boks

TheTools · 27.11.2026 18:01 · source · permalink

Saturday’s record 37-12 defeat to England in the final round of the pool stages of the Nations Championship has sent New Zealand down to fifth in the World Rugby Men’s Rankings, equalling their lowest-ever position.

New Zealand were last ranked fifth at the end of August 2022, after Argentina inflicted a first-ever home loss on them in Christchurch, 25-18.

A year later, the All Blacks reached the final of Rugby World Cup 2023, and they’ll need a similar turnaround – but in a shorter space of time, with next year’s tournament only being 10 months away – if they are to mount a serious challenge for the Webb Ellis Cup in Australia

Rather than New Zealand, it’s the three strongest teams in the Six Nations who are now breathing down the necks of the Springboks.

Following their 20-17 win over the world champions in Dublin, Ireland have moved up to second on 89.98 points, a gain of two places and 1.28 points. They are 0.47 points ahead of France, who remain in third, but with an improved score of 89.51, after their 45-17 win over Argentina, while England are up one place to fourth, on 89.04 points.

With just 0.74 points separating the Springboks from France, their opponents in next Sunday’s Nations Championship final, the teams are not only playing for the inaugural title but also to finish the year as the world’s No.1 team. Ireland still have a say in that but will get precious few points, if any, for beating the lower-ranked Australia in Friday’s fifth place decider.

The rest of the top 10 is unchanged, but 11th place is now the domain of Japan, who are ranked higher than Italy, following the Azzurri’s home defeat to Fiji.

Meanwhile, Samoa’s forward momentum continues, with a thumping 90-22 Nations Cup win over Spain being rewarded with a two-place rise to 14th. Earlier in the tournament, they were ranked 19th.

The only other major change involves USA, who fall a couple of places to 16th as a result of their 38-10 defeat to fellow Nations Cup champions Georgia.

However, Tonga have swapped places with Spain after they overcame Hong Kong China 55-19, with the Ikale ‘Tahi now 18th and Spain 19th.
