DraftKings EPL DFS Picks for Sunday, Oct. 25

TheTools · 24.10.2026 14:26 · source · permalink

Sunday's four-game DraftKings slate has no runaway favorite and every implied game total above three goals, so the build comes down to who takes the set pieces and who plays the full 90.

Sunday's four-game DraftKings slate is almost the opposite of Saturday's. Every match has an implied total above three goals, but there is no overwhelming favorite to build around. Brentford carry the highest team total at Hull City, while Manchester United-Bournemouth and Liverpool-Brighton offer more obvious fantasy options on both sides. Liverpool-Brighton will probably attract the most attention, though I'm leaning toward the under in that match.

Amad Diallo is the first player into my early builds at $5,500. From there, the main decision is where to spend: Bruno Fernandes has the strongest projected floor, Cody Gakpo occupies a scarce forward spot and Lewis Hall stands well above a weak defender pool. It will be difficult to use all three without accepting questionable value elsewhere.

Lineups remain uncertain. Manchester United's defense is depleted, Brentford are without two defensive midfielders and Crystal Palace played Thursday, when Jorgen Strand Larsen suffered an injury. Liverpool should bring Gakpo, Alexander Isak and Bradley Barcola back into the XI. Set-piece assignments for United, Liverpool, Hull and Brentford also need to be confirmed before lock.

  • 10:00 am: Hull City vs. Brentford

FORWARDS

Amad Diallo (MUN vs BOU, $5,500)

Diallo is likely to be in most lineups, and the reason is simple. A lot of the Manchester United attack is cheap relative to the rest of the slate, and he costs $4,500 less than Bruno Fernandes while going 90 minutes last weekend. The open question is set pieces. If Diallo is splitting corners with Bruno Fernandes, he's an easy click. If not, he's a $5,500 winger in a home game where United are implied for 1.7 goals against a Bournemouth side missing Alex Scott. Either way, at forward, he'll be plenty popular.

Cody Gakpo (LIV vs BHA, $9,000)

Gakpo played 90 minutes in midweek and is projected to start again, which matters at $9,000. Florian Wirtz appears to have moved off corners, potentially leaving Gakpo and Alexis Mac Allister to split them. The risk at this price is a short night. If he's good for 90, he's one of the better forwards on the slate, and if Liverpool rotate him out early, the price won't work.

Mohamed Belloumi (HUL vs BRE, $6,900)

Belloumi is in a better spot than the price suggests. Brentford are missing both of their usual defensive midfielders, so Mamadou Sangare will likely play next to someone less suited for the role, and Hull are at home. Belloumi remains part of Hull's unsettled set-piece mix and has produced useful games when receiving a share, though Regan Slater and Sorba Thomas could also be involved. The one drawback is that Hull are only around 20 percent to win and haven't produced many high floors, mostly because they don't take many corners. At $6,900, I don't mind it.

Alexander Isak ($8,800) is a good tournament option and will be interesting to track given how people handle the Liverpool-Brighton game. Charalampos Kostoulas ($7,700) is in a tough matchup at Liverpool, though he does take shots and draws fouls. Igor Thiago ($6,700) is the Brentford striker in the slate's biggest favorite, but his floor has been low. 

Justin Kluivert ($5,000) is a cheap Bournemouth option if his fitness checks out and he starts again, though Junior Kroupi expects to be an option from the bench. Anan Khalaili ($5,200) should still play 90 minutes for Palace, though Palace's numbers have been poor overall, which limits him. Matias Fernandez-Pardo ($5,100) has been mostly a miss so far and went 56 minutes against Aston Villa, which is not what you want.

MIDFIELDERS

Bruno Fernandes (MUN vs BOU, $10,000)

Fernandes leads the slate and crosses more than six times per 90, so on paper he has the safest floor. As most know at this point, he hasn't been as reliable as last season. Outside of the hat trick against Ipswich Town, his numbers are lackluster. His floor hasn't been the same and he's not on pace to break the Premier League assist record. Manchester United's play overall has been ugly. The decision at midfield is really between Bruno at $10,000 and going down to Pascal Gross or Marcus Tavernier, and I don't have a strong lean since no matchup stands out.

My projections still favor Bruno because they draw from multiple seasons of data, but his current role and Manchester United's performances make him less automatic than the number suggests.

Pascal Gross (BHA at LIV, $8,000)

Gross has 28 set pieces, second on the slate behind Fernandes, and averages more than seven crosses per 90, which gives him one of the best floors on the slate. He is one of the few Brighton players who started Thursday as well, but he should still be a lock for close to 90 minutes. The knock is the matchup. Liverpool are a tough spot, and I'm unsure how many opportunities Brighton will get, though they've been good this season.

Marcus Tavernier (BOU at MUN, $8,400)

Tavernier has 22 set pieces, 5.5 crosses per 90 and 1.6 shots on goal per 90, and with Alex Scott still out, he is the main set-piece taker for Bournemouth, though Kluivert could split them. Manchester United's back line is also in rough shape with all the injuries, which helps. Bournemouth have been hit or miss without Scott and are only around 30 percent to win, so this is a pick that works best if the game opens up.

Alexis Mac Allister (LIV vs BHA, $4,800)

Mac Allister stands out at this price because he could inherit a share of Liverpool's corners now that Wirtz appears to have moved off them. If you want a cheap Liverpool piece without paying $9,000 for Gakpo, this is the way to do it. He's projected to start at home, in a game with a 3.25 total.

Harvey Barnes ($9,200) probably won't be popular, but he plays 85 to 90 minutes and the matchup at Crystal Palace is one of the better ones on the slate, since Palace's defense has been among the worst in the league. Wirtz ($7,300) is off corners and is more of a floor risk, but that also means he won't be popular.

Adam Wharton ($5,600) has the most Palace set pieces (14), and his floor isn't much worse than the more expensive names. Mikkel Damsgaard ($6,600) could handle most of Brentford's set pieces if Mathias Jensen doesn't start. Jensen ($5,400) would likely take them if included, but he may be playing in a deeper role and looks like a 60-minute player. Bryan Mbeumo ($9,600) is playing as the No. 9, so no crosses and no corners, and that makes him a tournament-only play. Yeremy Pino ($5,100) has four goals from five shots on goal, which isn't sustainable, and his floor has been poor even with some sets.

DEFENDERS

Lewis Hall (NEW at CRY, $7,200)

Hall has 24 set pieces, the most of any defender on the slate, and he crosses more than four times per 90, which produces about 10 points in most games. At $7,200, it's a lot to pay, so the real question is where the money goes. Spend on defender or someone like Fernandes. Almost nobody else at defender stands out, so if you have the room, Hall is the one.

Adrien Truffert (BOU at MUN, $5,000)

Truffert crosses nearly five times per 90 over 624 minutes this season, which is a real floor at $5,000. Manchester United's back line is missing several regulars, though Bournemouth are only around 30 percent to win, so don't expect a shutout.

Milos Kerkez (LIV vs BHA, $4,800)

Kerkez crosses about 2.5 times per 90 over 566 minutes, and Liverpool are at home. A shutout is only around 25 percent, so this is about crossing volume at a reasonable price.

Maxim De Cuyper ($7,600) has 17 set pieces, but the price is hard to justify when there are cheaper options. Ryan Giles ($6,100) has already been subbed off early this season and didn't start last game, and Olivier Boscagli ($5,600) and Tyrick Mitchell ($5,800) are also too expensive. Diogo Dalot ($5,200) is a more reasonable Manchester United option if you want that side of the game.

GOALKEEPER

Konstantinos Tzolakis (HUL vs BRE, $4,000)

This is a slate where I'd stay away from Brentford's keeper. Caoimhin Kelleher ($5,600) has the best clean-sheet odds, but Hull are implied for just 1.1 goals, so the price isn't worth it. The cheapest three keepers are where I'm looking: Tzolakis ($4,000), Djordje Petrovic ($4,200) and Bart Verbruggen ($4,500). Tzolakis averages 3.7 saves per 90 and Brentford are implied for 1.9 goals, so the shots should come. Petrovic faces a Manchester United team that has been inconsistent, and Brighton have been better defensively this season, even with Verbruggen at Liverpool.
