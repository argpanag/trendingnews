Over 170,000 Grab Malaysia Driver-Partners Earn Over 5% More Per Minute on the Job Under Effort Based Model

  • Updated earnings model compensates driver-partners who opted in for pickup time and distance from the moment a booking is accepted, without raising passenger fares.
  • Redesigned driver-partner earnings statement gives partners clearer visibility into weekly take-home earnings, bonuses, and incentives.
  • Approach reflects Grab’s focus on driving consistent passenger demand and maximising productive online hours.

PETALING JAYA, 27 November 2026 – More than 170,000 Grab Malaysia driver-partners see an increase in their earnings after opting into the platform’s updated earnings structure, which pays partners for pickup distance and time from the moment a booking is accepted. It underscores its focus on building stable, sustainable income opportunities for driver-partners while keeping rides reliable and affordable for Malaysians.

The updated earning structure was developed over time, testing and adjusting with feedback from driver-partners before opening it to all driver-partners. Results from the rollout show that participating driver-partners earned over 5% more for every minute on the job compared to before. By replacing fixed commissions with a variable Grab Service Fee (GSF) that covers longer pickup efforts, while keeping passenger fares unchanged.

“We hear the driver-partner’s concerns about earnings. Focusing only on a single-trip percentage misses how driver-partners actually build their weekly income. A drive-partner does not earn when driving unpaid to a distant pickup or sitting idle between jobs. By compensating partners for their full effort from booking acceptance and showing their true weekly take-home earnings, we are addressing the factors that impact their earnings, while ensuring Malaysians still get reliable, affordable rides,” said Rashid Shukor, Executive Director, Grab Malaysia.

The flexible variable fee, designed over time, is an improvement intended to deliver long-term benefits to everyone on the platform. When fare and compensation reflect driver-partners’ effort, they will be more likely to accept trips, helping passengers book rides quickly and conveniently. As the service experience continues to improve, passengers will use the service more often, creating more trips and income opportunities for driver-partners. This improved structure and its flexibility has helped better balance supply and demand in the market. This allows Grab to maintain consistent service quality for users and livelihood opportunities for driver-partners, while ensuring the platform remains resilient amid market challenges and changing economic conditions.

Co-created with feedback from the driver-partner community, Grab has also rolled out a new earnings statement giving partners a clearer, itemised view of their take-home earnings. Rather than looking at base fares in isolation, the redesigned layout clearly breaks down job details, earned incentives, bonuses, and weekly adjustments, such as top-ups when actual trip times exceed initial estimates. All passenger tips and earned incentives go 100% to driver-partners.

Alongside fairer compensation, Grab maintains platform guardrails to discourage repeated cancellations, reduce empty travel, and uphold safety standards for all users. This is to keep the marketplace reliable and safe for all users.

“Our goal is to preserve the flexibility that draws people to the gig economy while ensuring fair, transparent working conditions. When we support driver-partners to earn sustainably, we create a healthier marketplace where Malaysians can count on a safe, affordable ride whenever they need one,” concluded Rashid.
