Ferguson apologises after being hit with seven-game ban

TheTools · 24.11.2026 11:48 · source · permalink

Getty

Glentoran manager Duncan Ferguson has apologised for his behaviour that led to a seven-game suspension following a red card in the recent Belfast derby.

Ferguson's unbeaten start since being appointed manager in the Irish League came to an end following a 3-2 loss to Cliftonville at Solitude. The former Scottish international took over at the Oval in October on a multi-year deal in one of the league's most high-profile appointments.

The former Scottish international was given his marching orders by referee Ben McMaster towards the end of the first half in Belfast due to his protestations over Joe Sheridan's opener for Cliftonville.

The one-time Everton and Newcastle striker continued to vent his frustrations and was sent to the stands with Sherdian’s 88th-minute winner securing all three points for the Reds.

Glentoran, who sit fourth in the league, 14 points off big-two rivals Linfield, say they’ll accept the seven-game suspension imposed by the authorities, starting with tonight’s BetMcLean Cup tie against Larne at The Oval.

"I want to apologise to the match officials for my behaviour on Friday night," Ferguson said in a statement.

"I was frustrated by what happened on the pitch and let my emotions get the better of me. I went too far in the way I expressed that frustration towards the officials, and I should have handled the situation better."

"I accept the suspension and I’ll learn from what happened. My focus now is on the players, the staff and the work we’ve got to do together, both on and off the pitch."
