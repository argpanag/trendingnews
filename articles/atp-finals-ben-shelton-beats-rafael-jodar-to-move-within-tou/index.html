ATP Finals: Ben Shelton beats Rafael Jodar to move within touching distance of semi-finals

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:14 · source · permalink

Ben Shelton reached his first Grand Slam final at this year's US Open

  • Published
    3 hours ago

Ben Shelton battled past Rafael Jodar to move within touching distance of a place in the ATP Finals last four.

US Open runner-up Shelton only lost his serve once but still had to fight for just over two hours to beat the fast-rising Spaniard 7-6 (10-8) 7-5.

American Shelton closed out the match with three aces in a row and then wrote "I'll be watching, Arthur" on the camera lens - a reference to the final game in the group.

Shelton will progress to the semi-finals if world number one Alexander Zverev beats France's Arthur Fils later on Thursday.

Fils would need to beat Zverev in straight sets if he is to qualify instead of Shelton.

"I think if he takes Sascha [Zverev] out in straights, I'll say too good and go on holidays," Shelton joked of his good friend Fils.

"I'm always rooting for him [Arthur] and I think he and Sascha will have a great match.

"I'd love to make the semi-finals for the first time. I'll definitely be watching."

Germany's Zverev, unbeaten so far at the season-ending event, is assured of top place in the John McEnroe Group, and will play either Daniil Medvedev or Frances Tiafoe in Saturday's semi-finals.

Shelton did not win a match on his ATP Finals debut last year, but arrived in Turin with a Masters 1000 title and a first Grand Slam final under his belt.

He beat Fils in three entertaining sets in his opening match before being swept aside by Zverev on Tuesday.

Shelton was under pressure against Jodar in the first set, saving two break points and missing a series of routine shots when his opponent was struggling.

However, after taking a tight tie-break, Shelton quickly broke to take control of the third set.

Jodar, competing in his first professional season on the ATP Tour, was strong defensively and wrestled the break back to level at 4-4.

However, he was visibly fatigued and unable to keep Shelton at bay in a series of lengthy rallies, with Shelton breaking for 6-5.

Shelton slipped at the back of court as he served for the match, but recovered quickly to close out victory. He finished with 14 aces to one double fault, having dropped only 11 points behind his first serve.

In the doubles, Britain's Henry Patten and Finnish partner Harri Heliovaara will need to beat French pair Theo Arribage and Albano Olivetti if they are to keep their title defence alive.

Wimbledon champions Patten and Heliovaara have won one and lost one of their three group matches.
