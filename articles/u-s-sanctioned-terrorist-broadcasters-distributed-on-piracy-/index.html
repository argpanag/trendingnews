U.S.-Sanctioned Terrorist Broadcasters Distributed on Piracy Platforms Reaching American Homes, Investigation Finds

TheTools · 12.09.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Digital Citizens Alliance and risk3sixty find Hezbollah's Al-Manar, a U.S.-designated terrorist entity, bundled alongside movies and live sports on 17 of 25 illicit streaming services tested

WASHINGTON, Sept. 10, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Broadcasters controlled by terrorist-designated organizations Hezbollah, Hamas, the Houthis and Kata'ib Hezbollah - collectively responsible for the deaths of hundreds of Americans - are being distributed to American households through illicit piracy streaming services, packaged alongside movies and live sports, according to an investigation by the Digital Citizens Alliance (DCA) and cybersecurity firm risk3sixty.

Researchers found broadcasters affiliated with designated terrorist organizations or sanctioned foreign states on 17 of the 25 piracy platforms it examined. All were accessible over U.S. Internet connections. Al-Manar, Hezbollah's official broadcaster, was most prevalent, appearing on all 17 services carrying such programming. The State Department placed Al-Manar on the State Department's Terrorist Exclusion List in 2004 and the Treasury Department designated it as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist entity in March 2006.

The findings appear in a new report, Hostile Signals: How Illicit Piracy Platforms Give Terrorist-Controlled and Sanctioned Foreign Broadcasters Access to American Homes.

The joint investigation documents how piracy platforms provide organizations associated with terrorist organizations or other U.S. adversaries a commercial distribution system, operating outside U.S. law, that grants them access to paying American subscribers who never went looking for them. Access to the U.S. market is troubling considering the intelligence community's March 2026 assessment that Islamist terrorist groups have shifted toward information operations intended to spread propaganda and to inspire "lone wolf" attacks by individuals with access to the West.

"Piracy platforms give terrorist organizations a back door to access the U.S. market, circumventing two decades of efforts to keep these organizations from being able to broadcast into American homes," said Tom Galvin, Executive Director of the Digital Citizens Alliance.

"Our investigation found that piracy services routinely package terrorist-controlled broadcasters alongside everyday entertainment, creating a distribution channel that operates outside traditional safeguards. When extremist content is embedded in the same interface as sports, movies, and news, it becomes far less visible to those working to identify and disrupt radicalization efforts," said Steven Guris, threat intelligence lead at risk3sixty.
