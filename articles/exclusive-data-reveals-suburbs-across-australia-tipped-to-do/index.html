Exclusive data reveals suburbs across Australia tipped to double in value by 2036 - realestate.com.au

TheTools · 17.10.2026 22:03 · source · permalink

What will your home be worth in 2036? Picture: Getty Images

ANALYSIS

News of the death of Australia’s housing market has been greatly exaggerated, according to an exclusive new data set.

The current housing downturn, which is sparking fear and uncertainty all over the nation, will be temporary, according to the research from realestate.com.au, which shows hundreds of suburbs will experience strong growth over the next five and 10 years.

The data looked at past market performances and a 20 year projection trend- incorporating the current downturn- and projected suburb by suburb median values for houses and units by 2031 and 2036.

The numbers showed growth in most areas of Australia, with some markets primed to perform better than others.

Brisbane home values have risen sharply in recent years.

In Queensland for example, the research uncovered a number of suburbs that could double in value by 2036, despite Brisbane values being currently on a downward trend and 3.9 per cent lower than their March peak.

Realestate.com.au senior economist Angus Moore noted that the state’s recent price falls were minor, when held up to the growth that values had experienced since the Covid pandemic.

“It shows just how remarkable the last six years or so have been in Queensland,” Mr Moore said, adding that cheaper suburbs had outperformed blue chip markets in recent years.

Sydney’s house market has long been the nation’s most expensive, and is often safeguarded against price falls due to scarce housing supply compared to demand.

This data projects that houses worth under $1 million will soon be a thing of the past, while $2 million plus homes will be commonplace all over the greater Sydney area, including the once affordable suburbs in the west.

Sydney property downturns don’t tend to last for long.

“Houses under $1m are already quite rare in most of Sydney,” Mr Moore said.

“We expect (prices) to bottom out next year and … more suburbs will get pushed out (past $1m).”

Realestate.com.au’s projections showed 246 suburbs would have house prices average higher than $2m and 92 suburbs higher than $3m by 2031.

Adelaide property has only recently begun to correct after strong growth in recent years.

The data showed a number of SA suburbs that look likely to continue performing strongly over the next decade.

On current trajectory, some suburbs in the city’s north could rise in value by up to 250 per cent.

Edge Realty director Mike Lao said “a surge of development activity in the wake of Covid – from both local and interstate investors – created a bit of a feeding frenzy.

Adelaide’s affordable suburbs have performed well and are likely to keep doing so. Picture: iStock/BeyondImages

“The heat’s certainly come out of the market … but as long as there is a hunger for properties across the entire market, and the northern suburbs continue to provide a more affordable entry point and an attractive lifestyle, I think homeowners will continue to see good gains over time,” Mr Lao said.

Meanwhile, homeowners in 69 Melbourne suburbs could see six-figure house-value gains by 2031, with cheaper neighbourhoods leading the charge if history repeats.

The realestate.com.au modelling puts five-year growth in those suburbs at 25 per cent or more.

But million-dollar house suburbs could almost double, from 188 in 2026 to 372 by 2036.

Only 23 of Melbourne’s 395 analysed house markets would remain below $1m under that scenario.

Under the separate 10-year scenario, 285 suburbs would gain at least 50 per cent.

Mr Moore said the figures illustrated historical trends, rather than predicting where prices would actually land.

He said Melbourne’s prolonged slump had made the city more affordable than others.

“Over the next sort of five or 10 years, that is going to increasingly look attractive for buyers.”
