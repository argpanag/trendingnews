OpenAI TwitchCon 2026 controversy explained: Why streamers are angry over AI training

TheTools · 18.11.2026 12:24 · source · permalink

Published November 18, 2026

OpenAI’s presence at TwitchCon 2026 in San Diego has ignited fierce criticism from streamers, underscoring growing tensions over artificial intelligence in creative industries.

The company attended the convention, held November 13-15, to promote Codex, its AI-powered coding tool, through a hackathon offering $25,000 in prizes for developers building applications for Twitch streamers.

But the booth drew sharp criticism, with attendees describing it as “tone deaf,” “bland and boring,” and “stupid.”

This backlash is partially fueled by the company's policy introduced in August 2026 that allowed creators to have their videos excluded from the training processes of AI models created by Amazon by default – a feature which required streamers to uncheck it themselves if they wanted to opt out. The issue is that participation should happen with consent explicitly stated, not automatically.

The controversy even resulted in a lawsuit being filed against Twitch and Amazon. In August, a Twitch streamer filed a proposed class-action lawsuit charging Twitch and Amazon with using the content of creators without proper authorization or compensation. The lawsuit claimed that Amazon harvested the content of streamers prior to Twitch officially introducing its opting-out option. These claims were challenged, and the lawsuit does not prove anything about OpenAI using Twitch creators' content.

Some creators acknowledged potential benefits, such as automatic captioning and video clipping, if such tools do not replace human creativity.

Twitch Chief Product Officer Mike Minton declined to discuss past particles due to ongoing litigation, noting the opt-out option represented “a trade-off” the company felt was important to offer.
