U.S.-listed shares of Chinese conglomerate Alibaba Group Holding Ltd. (BABA) were down more than 1% in the overnight session late Sunday, even as the company launched its most advanced artificial intelligence model, Qwen4-Max.

According to a report from The South China Post, the technology and e-commerce giant has stringent cybersecurity guardrails for the model in a bid to balance safety with launching open-source AI models as global concerns over AI-enabled cyberattacks grow.

BABA's New AI Model More Than Halves Task Costs

As per the report, the Hangzhou-based company also introduced dedicated data sets and post-training enhancements to mitigate chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRN) risks.

The launch comes amid concerns about whether open AI models, which Chinese companies prefer, could increase CBRN risks more than the closed-source models from U.S. rivals like OpenAI and Anthropic.

However, Qwen4-Max is a closed model, but the company reaffirmed its open-source strategy by reassuring that smaller-scale, open-weight versions of the Qwen4 series would soon be launched for global developers soon.

The Qwen4-Max comprises 2.4 trillion parameters, the same size as its previous Qwen3.8-Max, although it activates only 71 billion parameters per token, 25% lower than its predecessor.

This lets the new model deliver better reasoning while also lowering inference costs, cutting the average cost per task by 60%, according to the company, which cited an internal benchmark that evaluates model performance on various work tasks.

The model is available via QwenCloud, priced at 7.5 U.S. cents per 1 million tokens.
