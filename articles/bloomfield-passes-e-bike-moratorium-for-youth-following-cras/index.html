Bloomfield passes e-bike moratorium for youth following crash

TheTools · 16.09.2026 11:08 · source · permalink

Bloomfield Township has passed a moratorium on youth using e-bikes and electric scooters in the community following a crash between an e-moto and a truck that critically injured two 13-year-old boys earlier this month.

The township's Board of Trustees voted 6-1 on Monday night to prohibit people under 16 from using e-bikes and scooters on sidewalks for six months. Township police will confiscate the e-bikes and scooters in response to a violation of the moratorium, according to trustees.

The trustees' move comes after the two 13-year-old boys were hit by a truck while riding an e-moto —a high-powered, motor-driven electric two-wheeler — through the sidewalk at Telegraph Road on Sept. 7. Both were in critical condition after the collision, police said.

Both boys were still in the hospital Tuesday. While the parents of the passenger have not relayed his condition to police, the driver of the e-moto was in the ICU Tuesday and has undergone multiple surgeries, said Bloomfield Police Lt. Nick Soley.

"They're both still pretty torn up," said Soley, who added that it will be "a long road" for both of them.

While Police Chief James Gallagher noted it was illegal for the boys to ride an e-moto, he also said his officers have gotten 124 calls for service involving e-bikes since the beginning of the year.

"I'm standing at my kitchen sink looking out my kitchen window, and oh my God — there's a 13-to-15-year-old boy on a motor bike screaming around 15-20 miles per hour in my backyard trying to cut through to the other subdivision," said Bloomfield Township resident Steve Balagna.

"It shouldn’t take a tragedy like (the crash)," Gallagher told the trustees. "We’ve been discussing this all summer, but it’s important right now because we have two young members of our community that are now in the hospital."

Bloomfield Township is the latest Michigan community to crack down on e-bikes and e-motos amid a rising number of accidents and fatalities. At least two communities, Dearborn Heights and South Lyon, have banned them in certain areas.

Michigan law currently allows people to operate e-bikes anywhere that a pedal bike is allowed to operate. Gallagher said this dynamic causes his officers "a lot of problems" and said the State Legislature needs to strengthen state law surrounding e-bikes.

Trustee Neal Barnett said he was in favor of passing a moratorium, and "whatever the consequences are, they are."

"From the Legislature or any legal consequences, I really don’t care," said Barnett. "I’d rather be aggressive and do something to protect our residents."

Clerk Martin Brook cast the lone vote against the moratorium, expressing concern that it would encourage youth to ride e-bikes in the streets.

Gallagher encouraged trustees to amend the moratorium to include a fine against parents whose children violate the ban at a future meeting. He said "creating something that we can hold the parents responsible for" would be an effective way to deter youth from using the electric bikes and scooters.

But because e-bikes are legal to operate in Michigan, Brook said the township needs outreach on the topic.

"We've got to educate. We've got to talk to parents. We've got to follow the law, because they are legal," said Brook.
