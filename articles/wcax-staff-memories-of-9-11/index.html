WCAX staff memories of 9/11

TheTools · 10.09.2026 22:28 · source · permalink

BURLINGTON, Vt. (WCAX) - As Vermont marks the 25th anniversary of the Sept. 11 attacks, WCAX staff who covered the story in 2001 are recalling that day and the moments that followed.

Reporter Adam Sullivan: I was woken up by my roommate. He was working the morning shift at Channel 3 at that time and had watched the second plane hit live.

News Director Roger Garrity: I was working nights. Somebody called me at work and woke me up and said, Are you watching this? I’m like, watching what? And I turned on the TV, and then I scrambled to get in here. I get in around 9:03 to the station, and I see all these people just stunned, looking at the television.

Anchor Darren Perron: I was getting ready in the morning, and Mom kind of barged into my old childhood bedroom and was like, something’s wrong. And I was like, what are you talking about? And she was like, come look at the TV right now. I was like, I’ve got to do something. So, I was close to the Canadian border because I was up in the Northeast Kingdom, you know, visiting family. And so I just immediately got in my car, and I drove up to the border and Derby Line just to see what was going on there. Were they shutting it down? And in fact, they were. It looked almost like nobody knew what the heck was going on. I was like, what’s going on? And they were like, we don’t know.

News Director Roger Garrity: I think people were like, well, like, who’s in charge? Like, you know, because Marselis wasn’t here. And then all of a sudden I had to make all the decisions. We had to say, okay, what is important here? And right away, obviously, air travel was the first thing. We also knew that the Air National Guard had been scrambled. We didn’t know exactly what their role was at that point, but we knew that they were likely, you know, protecting the skies.

Photographer Andy Goodrich: We were set up at the end of the runway. And all of a sudden you just see the head of this soldier in a turret coming around. And this Humvee comes up with a 50-millimeter machine gun and pointed it right at us. And, you know, and I’m like, if he pulls that trigger by accident or on purpose, we’re done. And then they had the police come remove us from the end of the runway. And everybody was treated as a potential terrorist or a potential threat. And then we went to the airport, and they started blocking it off. Then all of a sudden, all at once, the departures changed to cancelled. And we just knew things were never going to be the same again.

Reporter Adam Sullivan: We were already in wall-to-wall national coverage.

Photographer Joe Carroll: The networks were still in command. They were up mostly 24-7. They were exhausted, but it was such a big deal for the country and the world. The world was watching.

News Director Roger Garrity: Every moment, there was some new revelation about what had happened, how severe the damage was, how many people had been hurt or killed. I mean, it continued to unfold over the course of the day. But it was just this uncertainty, like we’re at war, but who are we at war with? You know, there wasn’t really anything to do with it. So it was frustrating for people that this kind of feeling of, I feel afraid in a way that I never have before. Like this veneer of security had been eroded away. And now I also can’t do anything about it. I can’t take out my frustrations.

Photographer Joe Carroll: I went to work with Brian Joyce to find the governor. He just added a little period to the sentence to say that night everything was okay in Vermont. Nobody felt okay. It was our Pearl Harbor.

News Director Roger Garrity: And the other thing that really struck us was how much people wanted to help. The people that turned out to give blood, just long lines. There was no blood needed really, but it was, what can I do?

Photographer Joe Carroll: And we went to Bishop Angell’s service. His brother was killed in one of the planes. Here’s a man trying to hold the flock together and he’s crying.

Reporter Adam Sullivan: A friend of ours worked for Cantor Fitzgerald. He was at the World Trade Center that morning. The most poignant thing I can remember about our drive to New York City was all the American flags. Every single overpass heading into the city was just loaded with flags.

Photographer Joe Carroll: And it was a time we haven’t probably seen since, where the country came together. Our innocence went away. It’s stunning that this could happen in the United States. That this diabolical scheme, which you can hijack four planes and just turn the psyche of the country around in 24 hours. The United States hasn’t been the same since.
