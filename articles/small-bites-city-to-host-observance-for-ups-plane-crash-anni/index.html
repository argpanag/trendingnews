Fighter jet intercepted plane during KY Trump rally, officials confirm

04:05 PM ET, October 21 2026, Lillian Metzmeier 

President Trump hosts rally in Northern Kentucky ahead of midterms

A fighter jet intercepted a plane flying through a restricted airspace over Cincinnati as President Donald Trump addressed the crowd in Highland Heights, according to reporting from the Cincinnati Enquirer.

In an Oct. 17 news release from the North American Aerospace Defense Command, U.S. military officials confirmed a NORAD F-16 fighter aircraft intercepted a general aviation aircraft over Cincinnati at approximately 3 p.m. Central time, as Trump was speaking at a rally in Northern Kentucky.

The aircraft was safely escorted out of the area. In the release, officials said that pilots who violate airspaces with temporary flight restrictions may be met by federal authorities upon landing and subject to further enforcement action.

Greenberg announces citywide observance to mark 1 year after fatal UPS crash

03:48 PM ET, October 21 2026, Lillian Metzmeier 

Louisville will host a citywide observance marking one year since the fatal crash of UPS Flight 2976, Mayor Greenberg announced in an Oct. 21 release.

The event, called "Louisville Remembers Together," will honor the 15 victims killed after the crash, as well as offer support to the families, loved ones and the rest of the Louisville community.

At 5:14 p.m. Nov. 4, places of worship and other institutions across the city are encouraged to ring bells and carillons for one minute, according to the release. At 5:15 p.m., the bells will stop, and the entire community is invited to observe one minute of silence.

City monuments, including Louisville Metro Hall, the Big Four Bridge and the twin spires at Churchill Downs, will be lit yellow during the evening, with the community encouraged to wear yellow.

"Every person who lost a loved one, whose life was changed forever, and who continues to grapple with this tragedy should know that here in Louisville, they will never have to grieve or heal alone," Greenberg said in the release. "Our community is here for you. Wherever you are at 5:14 p.m. on Nov. 4, Louisville will remember together."

A private memorial event will also take place starting 4:30 p.m. at Nov. 4 at Grade A Auto Parts, located at 4568 Melton Ave. Community members unable to attend are encouraged to watch the event live on Louisville MetroTV.

Additional drive-thru voting details announced

03:46 PM ET, October 21 2026, Lillian Metzmeier

Jefferson County Clerk David Yates has announced additional details regarding the county's pilot drive-thru voting program in an Oct. 20 release. The office will provide a drive-thru opportunity at the Kentucky Exposition Center, 937 Phillips Lane, for all precincts and voters Oct. 29-31 between 8 a.m. and 6 p.m., and Nov. 3 between 6 a.m. and 6 p.m., Yates previously announced.

According to the Oct. 20 release, more than 60 election officers and 10 employees from Yates' office will help operate the drive-thru voting location, with multiple law enforcement members providing traffic management and security support.

"This is about keeping pace with the times and continually looking for ways to improve the voting experience," Yates said in the release. "Drive-thru voting offers another convenient option for voters while allowing us to evaluate ways to make the process more efficient and accessible. We're excited to bring this innovative option to Jefferson County and see how it can improve the voting experience."

WDRB+WAVE announce multi-platform newsroom

01:50 PM ET, October 21 2026, Lillian Metzmeier

Months after combining forces, officials at Louisville news stations WDRB+WAVE-TV have announced that more than 100 journalists will come together under one roof to form one of the country's largest multi-platform newsrooms, according to an Oct. 21 report.

Starting Oct. 26, WDRB+WAVE will launch a "local first" news team dedicated to community-first coverage of Kentucky and Indiana. According to the report, the team will dig deeper into every county, town and neighborhood to deliver the most up-to-date coverage for viewers.

"In building WDRB and WAVE, we assembled the largest team of journalists in the region, creating a local news powerhouse dedicated to serving our viewers first and better," General Manager Jay Hiett said in the announcement. "Rooted in transparency, trust and local service, we'll cover every corner of the viewing area. We are committed to telling your stories."

In disaster-prone KY, life-saving infrastructure is at risk, report finds

05:08 AM ET, October 21 2026, Caroline Neal and Connor Giffin

Several areas of Kentucky's critical infrastructure are in urgent need of investment and modernization, the American Society of Civil Engineers found in its latest landmark assessment, including systems responsible for protecting communities from flooding and other disasters.

The report is the ASCE's first for Kentucky since 2019. The state's infrastructure earned a grade of C-minus overall, unchanged from the previous report card. Kentucky earned slightly higher marks in the bridges and roads categories this year, but declined in wastewater. No categories earned an A, reserved for infrastructure in excellent condition and "fit for the future."
