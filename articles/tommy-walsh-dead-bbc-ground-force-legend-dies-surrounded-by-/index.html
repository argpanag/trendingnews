Tommy Walsh dead: BBC Ground Force legend dies surrounded by family - AOL

TheTools · 09.11.2026 15:44 · source · permalink

BBC Ground Force and Homes Under The Hammer legend Tommy Walsh has died at the age of 69 after a long illness. The popular TV presenter died peacefully surrounded by his family. The news was confirmed by his agent Debbie Schiesser who requested privacy for his loved ones. "Tommy died peacefully after a long illness surrounded by his family. As this has only just happened, we would ask everyone to please respect the family's privacy," she said. Prior to her official announcement, his death had been posted on social media by his local football club, the Old Parmiterians FC.

"It is with great sadness that we announce the passing of Tommy Walsh," they shared on X. "A Parmiters boy, he played for the club for many years in goal but made a great career as a TV personality in shows such as Ground Force. He will be sorely missed. RIP Tommy," they concluded the post, which was accompanied by a smiling picture of the presenter.

Tommy first came to prominence as a member of the Ground Force team, when it launched in September 1997, where he worked alongside gardening expert Alan Titchmarsh and presenter Charlie Dimmock.

He went on to enjoy huge TV success, also hosting shows such as Homes Under the Hammer and Flip That House with Tommy Walsh.

His agent didn't confirm what his "long illness" was, but in recent years he has suffered a series of health issues, which he has spoken about quite openly. In 2002, benign lumps were found in his chest.

Two decades later, in 2022, he discovered another cancer, ironically when he was working on a cancer awareness campaign.

At the time, he said: "I went to my GP about an unrelated issue, and while I was there, he discovered this lump in my throat that he was a bit concerned about.

"He said it may be nothing, but that he would rather I went and got it checked, and unfortunately, it turned out to be cancerous. I had to go in straight away and have surgery to remove it. I am now fully recovered and even back playing football!"

Although he recovered, Tommy revealed he was battling lung cancer in late 2023. He shared the news with audience members at a charity event in November, explaining that doctors had discovered a "three-centimetre tumour in the lung" and that he attended an emergency appointment for a CT scan the following day.

In June 2024, Tommy revealed the tumour was "shrinking" thanks to new treatment. "I had to have this new treatment called SABR, and what it is is radiotherapy which targets it to an exact spot without damaging the organs around it. So they used that, and it shrank," he told the Mirror.

"It is now not anywhere else. It is going away. Because it is shrinking, it will shrink down to nothing and disappear. I will then just have to have annual checks. It has worked for me really well."

He went on to express relief that the tumour was found just outside his lung, adding: " They thought it was in the lung and then I would have been in trouble.

"Surgery would have been serious, where they cut you open and take a big lump of bone out of the rib, and then they would have to put a piece of metal mesh in to stop the lung falling out of the hole. So I did not want that but we had that as a backup. The SABR treatment has worked, and I am healthy, so I have not been in any pain."
