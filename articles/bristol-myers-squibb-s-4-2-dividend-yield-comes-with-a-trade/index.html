Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE:BMY) pays $0.63 per share each quarter, up from $0.62 in 2025, for an annualized $2.52. At the latest price of $60.57, the yield is roughly 4.2%. The raise was a small 1.6%, but it marked the 17th consecutive annual increase and the 94th consecutive year of payments. The quarterly rate has rose every year since $0.37 in 2015.

The dividend's future depends on whether the newer drugs that fund the check can grow faster than generics eat away at the older ones.

Raised Guidance Leaves Plenty of Coverage

After second-quarter EPS of $2.04 exceeded the $1.597 estimate, management raised 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance to $6.75-$7.00 from $6.05-$6.35. Measured against that range, the dividend uses only 36% to 37% of earnings. Operating cash flow came in at about $3.4 billion for the quarter. Based on today's share count, one quarter's dividend costs roughly $1.3 billion. The company also paid down $1.2 billion of debt.

"Business development remains a priority, and we are regularly evaluating opportunities in the therapeutic areas we know best, while continuing to return cash to shareholders through our commitment to the dividend."

That was David Elkins on the July earnings call.
