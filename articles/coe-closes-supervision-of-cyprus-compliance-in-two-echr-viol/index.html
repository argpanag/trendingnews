CoE closes supervision of Cyprus’ compliance in two ECHR violation cases

TheTools · 17.11.2026 11:40 · source · permalink

The Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe has closed its supervision of Cyprus’ compliance with the judgments in the cases of Vassiliou and Others v. Cyprus and Zavridou v. Cyprus, adopting final resolutions at its latest meeting in October 2026, according to the Law Office of the Republic of Cyprus.

In a press release on Tuesday, November 17, the Law Office said Cyprus had been found in violation by the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) in both cases over a breach of the right to respect for family life.

“Closing the supervision process is an important decision (resolution) for the country concerned. It means that the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe, as the body responsible for supervising the implementation of ECHR judgments and member states’ compliance with their international obligations, has concluded that the state has taken the necessary measures to comply with the judgment and is therefore ending its supervision of the case,” the press release said.

Compliance, it added, is an international obligation arising from Article 46 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) and is considered an integral part of the rule of law.

The first case concerns a Greek Cypriot soldier who went missing during the 1974 Turkish invasion. His remains were identified and handed over to his family in 2000. The ECtHR found a violation of the right to respect for family life due to the failure of the Missing Persons Service to inform his relatives about progress in the investigation into his disappearance, his possible death and the location of his burial.

The second case concerns the parental alienation of minors from their mother. The ECtHR found a violation of the right to respect for family life because the competent authorities had failed to take prompt and effective measures to facilitate the implementation of custody and care orders issued in the mother's favour by the Nicosia Family Court.

The supervision of the compliance process was closed after the Committee of Ministers was satisfied, on the basis of several Action Reports submitted by the Office of the Attorney General of the Republic, that Cyprus had taken the necessary individual and general measures to comply with the judgments, the statement said.

Individual measures aim to provide redress to the applicants and, as far as possible, restore them to the situation they were in before the violations occurred. General measures seek to prevent similar violations by the state in the future and, in these cases, included targeted measures and changes in administrative practices and legislation.

The resolutions and Action Reports concerning the two cases are available on the website of the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe under the references CM/ResDH(2026)296 and CM/ResDH(2026)297, respectively.

The cases before the Committee of Ministers were handled on behalf of the Attorney General of the Republic by the Counsel of the Republic Thelma Prodromitou.
