Mahmud: We couldn't create pressure against South Africa's tailenders

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:30 · source · permalink

Bangladesh pace bowler Hasan Mahmud admitted that they didn't have any option apart from trying to save the opening Test against South Africa at the Wanderers.

Bangladesh are staring at defeat after another batting collapse on Day 4, slumping to 100/5 in their second innings, still 57 runs behind.

South Africa took a 157-run lead in their first innings while Bangladesh collapsed in the second innings dramatically.

“If I talk about the overall day today, when the day started, there was always a possibility. There was a possibility of Bangladesh winning, they also had a possibility, a draw — all the results were open. Now there is no possibility of Bangladesh winning, now it is a struggle to save the match,” said Hasan on Wednesday (November 18).

“I think when their tailenders were coming in, there was a bit of a run leak at that moment. I mean, we tried to bowl in one place and be disciplined, but somehow it didn't work out. We couldn't create that kind of pressure on the tailenders,” he said.

“Then they got to a good score and when we came back to bat, we also lost a lot of early wickets. But let's see, now we have no option but to save the match,” he said.

“If we can finish (get) two or three partnerships or have a good day tomorrow... I mean, we will definitely try,” he added.

“Personally, I would say that I feel that we can do it. Because when we were down by three runs in the first match, from 20-30, we scored 400 from that position. So I still believe that if we can form a partnership, if we have a good pair, then we can cover up,” he said.

Hasan credited Litton Kumar Das for his record-breaking outing as wicketkeeper, saying he is reaping the benefit of his hard work behind the wicket. By stumping Kagiso Rabada, Litton set a world record for the most dismissals in a Test innings (eight).

Litton overtook the record shared by five wicketkeepers - Wasim Bari, Bob Taylor, Ian Smith, Ridley Jacobs and Joshua Da Silva - with his second stumping of the innings on the fourth morning of the game.

“First of all, I would like to congratulate Litton Dada, the way he is working hard behind the wicket and the way he is supporting the bowlers. So, I always feel good about it. I enjoy his company, and he always tries to be active on the field, tries to talk to everyone and at some point when it comes to decision-making, DRS, he helps a lot. So, I enjoy it,” he added.
