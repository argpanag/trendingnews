Burnham faces Badenoch at PMQs – watch live

TheTools · 14.10.2026 11:42 · source · permalink

Key moments

Andy Burnham is facing Kemi Badenoch at the first PMQs since the party conference break.

The Prime Minister is taking questions from the Tory leader after he accused Russia of interfering in the 2016 Brexit referendum.

On Monday, Mr Burnham was forced to row back from that claim, telling the Commons he had “always respected” the outcome of the Brexit vote.

His comments led to the head of MI5 challenging the suggestion that Russia swayed the outcome, insisting the result was “not in dispute”.

In September, the Labour leader used his conference to open the door to rejoining the EU.

Latest updates

Burnham rules out coalition with Greens

Andy Burnham has accused the Green Party’s policy to define Zionism as a form of racism as anti-Semitic.

The Prime Minister condemned the policy “unequivocally”, which implicitly calls for the abolition of Israel, and said his party would not enter any power-sharing agreements with Zack Polanski’s party while the policy remained in place.

It comes amid speculation that Mr Burnham could call an early election. A recent seat-by-seat poll showed Labour would comfortably be the largest party if an election was held tomorrow but fall 85 seats of a majority.

Mr Burnham said: “It is anti-Semitic, and this side of the house will have nothing to do with it.

“We will always take the right response when it comes to any partnerships we have with other parties.

“We cannot support a partnership with a party that holds a position that has recently been adopted.”
 

Farage calls on Burnham to declare ‘national security emergency’

Nigel Farage has called on Andy Burnham to declare a “national security emergency” over the small boat crossings.

The Reform UK leader said the government deserved praise for proscribing the IRGC but warned “young, unidentified males from Iran” were coming into Britain “every single week”.

The Prime Minister replied: “Could I start by recognising with the charges that have been laid against the individual responsible for the murder of Ann Widdicombe, and indeed also making threats against [Mr Farage].”

He continued: “Can I start by recognising it has been a hard time for him and his family. There is a lot we disagree on, but I defend his right to say it, and I will work hard to secure his safety and every member of this house.

“On the issues related to Iran, obviously we’ve seen the issues at RAF Fairford recently and they have been dealt with in the right way, with the utmost seriousness, by the police and security services.”

Burnham praises Badenoch for challenging English identity

Andy Burnham has praised Kemi Badenoch for challenging the belief that her mixed-race children cannot be English.

In her conference speech last week, the Tory leader said she didn’t want to live in a country run by people who debate whether her mixed-race children are English.

It comes after Suella Braverman, Reform’s education spokesman, said she was not English because it was an ethnicity and could be drawn through someone’s lineage.

Mr Burnham said: “In the spirit of unity with [Mrs Badenoch] I am going to commend her for drawing a line on a decade of division, when she drew a line with the party over there for suggesting that her children were British.

“That is wrong in every single respect. She was brave and she was right to call it out. That is what I want to see more of in the next decade as we go forward.”
 

Badenoch: Why won’t Labour increase defence spending in Budget?

Andy Burnham has refused to answer why he won’t commit to increase defence spending in this month’s Budget.

The Prime Minister has so far refused to commit to increasing defence spending to 3 per cent by 2030, an issue his Chancellor John Healey resigned over when serving as defence secretary.

Asked by Kemi Badenoch why he was delaying the announcement, Mr Burnham said: “I’m meeting President Zelensky, as I said this afternoon, and we will honour our commitments to him and his country, and we will honour our NATO commitments, as I’ve said every time that I have appeared at this dispatch box, and she will have to wait for the Budget.”
 

Burnham: We will bring down welfare bill

Kemi Badenoch has questioned whether Andy Burnham is afraid to cut welfare spending because he doesn’t want to “fight” his backbench MPs.

Mr Burnham said: “I want a system where we give the right support to young people upfront, so that they can be in training, education, or employment from 16 onwards.

“I think they do need better mental health support. I’ve argued for a different education system that gives parity to technical education alongside the university route. And we will bring forward the Milburn report soon.

“We will set out our response to it. We will bring down the welfare bill.”
 

Burnham: Bureaucracy has slowed down approving North Sea drilling

Andy Burnham has said his failure to approve drilling in the North Sea was because of “government process”.

Asked by Kemi Badenoch why he hasn’t approved the Rosebank and Jackdaw sites, the Prime Minister blamed bureaucracy.

He said: “She asks what I’m waiting for. I’ve had to wait for the government process, which was struck down by the courts under the Conservative government, to be fixed because they messed it up, and we inherited that situation.

“So I will take a pragmatic approach on oil and gas. We will set out our decisions shortly.”
 

Burnham: There is incontrovertible evidence of foreign interference

Andy Burnham has refused to say whether he believed raising the issue of Russian interference in the Brexit referendum was a “mistake”.

Refusing to drop the subject, Kemi Badenoch accused the Prime Minister of “digging in” warning him that he “couldn’t fight misinformation with misinformation”.

Asked whether Mr Burnham agreed with the head of MI5 that “talking up Russian interference was a mistake”, he said: “I think it’s important that we are clear-eyed about what happened in the past. 

“There is incontrovertible evidence that outside actors, enemies to this country, have interfered in our democratic processes, and it is inconceivable that that was not taking place across the whole of the last decade.”
 

Badenoch: Do you get bored of ‘putting foot in your mouth?’

Kemi Badenoch has accused Andy Burnham of “trying to unsay” that Russia had influenced the Brexit referendum.

Asking the Prime Minister whether he ever got tired of “putting his foot in his mouth”, Mr Burnham replied: “I am glad that she’s raised my comments because it gives me an opportunity to say to the whole house how I intend to lead this country.

“We have lived through a decade of division, and we know that hostile actors have been seeking to interfere in our democratic process. That is a matter of fact.

“We need to call that out and look straight at it in a way that the party opposite didn’t do.”

Burnham pays tribute to Sir David Amess

Andy Burnham has paid tribute to Sir David Amess following the fifth anniversary of his death.

The veteran Conservative MP was murdered on 15 October 2021 while running a surgery in his constituency.

Before beginning Prime Minister’s Questions, Mr Burnham said: “David was someone of whom I was very fond and worked with very closely on the Health Select Committee when I arrived in this House in 2001.

“We send our deepest condolences to David’s family. There is more work to be done on the security of members of this House, and we will do it.”
 

What did Burnham say about Russia’s Brexit interference?

Andy Burnham has retreated this week from claims of Russian meddling in the Brexit referendum.

In a statement to the Commons on Monday, the Prime Minister said he had “always respected” the outcome of the Brexit vote.

However, he previously told the Rest is Politics podcast with Rory Stewart and Alastair Campbell, the former New Labour spin doctor, that it was important to get “on the record” that Russia had interfered in the referendum.

He said: “I asked the question coming to Downing Street: ‘Well, was there interference in the 2016 referendum from Russia?’ I’ve had the clear answer that, yes, there was.
