Cook visits China, doubles Apple clean energy investment

TheTools · 11.10.2026 08:58 · source · permalink

Apple board chair Tim Cook visited China for the first time since stepping down from the chief executive officer (CEO) post last month. Apple, coinciding with the visit, decided to more than double the size of its investment in a China clean energy fund.

On the 11th, according to China Daily and other state-run media, Chair Cook on the 10th posted photos on China's social media platform Weibo from his visit to Guangzhou, Guangdong province. Cook's public trip to China is the first in about seven months since March.

In Guangzhou, Chair Cook visited the set of the short film "Yingchun," shot with Apple's new smartphone, the iPhone 18 Pro. On site, he met young Chinese director Gu Tianji and Wing Chun practitioner Dai Zilong to observe the filmmaking process using the iPhone camera.

On Weibo, he wrote, "I'm glad to visit China again," and, "Experiencing the allure of Wing Chun through the iPhone 18 Pro lens in Guangzhou was an unforgettable experience."

Apple announced it would expand its committed investment in the China clean energy fund from the previous $100 million (about 134 billion won) to $212 million (about 284 billion won), in line with Chair Cook's visit to China.

The expansion reflects investment commitments from Apple suppliers in China. The fund has already made initial investments in a 260-megawatt (MW) wind power project in the Guangxi Zhuang autonomous region and a 50 MW wind power project in Shandong province.

Chair Cook is also scheduled to attend the 27th annual meeting of the advisory board of Tsinghua University School of Economics and Management, to be held in Beijing on Oct. 13-14. Since 2019, he has served as chair of this board.

This year's advisory board newly welcomed CEO Jensen Huang of Nvidia, CEO Lisa Su of AMD, and senior managing partner Marc Pictet of the Pictet Group of Switzerland.

From 2011 through this year as Apple CEO, Chair Cook placed priority on ties with the Chinese market. Even amid ongoing U.S.-China technology and trade tensions, he visited China regularly and focused on local operations and supply chain management.

This year, in response to rising semiconductor prices, Apple pushed to adopt products from a Chinese memory chipmaker, sparking controversy.

After stepping down as CEO last month, Chair Cook attended a state dinner as Apple's representative during Chinese President Xi Jinping's visit to the United States. At the time, he was seated at the head table with CEO Jensen Huang, CEO Lisa Su, CEO Elon Musk of Tesla, and the U.S. and Chinese first couples.

Even after stepping back from day-to-day management, Chair Cook's participation in the China trip and major external events shows he is continuing to play a role in Apple's China business and external relations.
