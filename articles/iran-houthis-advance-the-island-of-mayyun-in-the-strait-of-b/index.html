Oil: IEA forecasts a fall in consumption of 2.5 million barrels a day in 2026

The International Energy Agency (IEA) has once again revised down its forecasts for global oil demand this year, in light of the recent escalation of the conflict in the Middle East and the latest surge in energy prices. The agency now estimates that consumption in 2026 will fall by 2.5 million barrels a day compared with the previous year, a figure well above the 1.6 million barrel a day decline forecast in August. Crude oil prices have remained well above the levels recorded prior to the US and Israeli attacks on Iran in late February, which triggered a conflict causing damage to oil infrastructure in several Gulf countries. The IEA highlighted in particular the surge in diesel costs due to refining constraints: damage to facilities in the Gulf has in fact drastically reduced supplies of this fuel, which is essential for industry and transport. “For diesel, which accounts for almost 30 per cent of global demand, prices in the United States exceeded the $100-per-barrel mark in early September — 94 per cent higher than pre-war levels — with Europe and Asia following a similar trend,” the IEA stated in its monthly report.

Maritime traffic in the Strait of Hormuz plummets; only seven ships passed through the Strait yesterday

According to preliminary vessel-tracking data published today, the number of ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz fell to seven yesterday, compared with eleven the previous day – a figure well below the ten-year average of 15. According to the data, of the seven vessels, two left and five entered the strait. The figures exclude vessels that may have crossed the strait with their Automatic Identification System (AIS) transponders switched off to avoid detection.

Vance met with US commanders to gather information on Iran

In recent months, the US Vice-President has been in direct contact with the commanders of the US armed forces in the Middle East to obtain first-hand information on the war in Iran. JD Vance, as has emerged from numerous conversations, is concerned about the level of US arms and ammunition stocks and about Iran’s resilience. The Vice-President has incorporated the assessments he has received into his private conversations with Donald Trump to provide the President with all the tools necessary to make his decisions. This is reported by the New York Times, citing several sources, according to whom the information gathered by Vance raises doubts about the nature of the assessments the President receives.
