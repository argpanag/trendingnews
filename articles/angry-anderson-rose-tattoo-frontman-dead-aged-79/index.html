Angry Anderson, Rose Tattoo frontman, dead aged 79

TheTools · 23.09.2026 06:27 · source · permalink
  • Entertainment
  • Celebrity
  • Showbiz

Updated September 23, 2026 - 11.04am. First published at 6.27am

Australian rock legend Angry Anderson has died. He was 79.

His Rose Tattoo bandmates on Wednesday shared that their frontman, born Gary Anderson, had died on Monday.

Australian Rock Legend Angry Anderson has died, he was 79.   Lisa Maree Williams/Getty Images

“It is with great sadness we announce the passing of Gary ‘Angry’ Anderson on the eve of Monday 21 September 2026, surrounded by his loved ones here in Australia,” the statement read.

“Angry was a devoted father, and much-loved member of his friends, bandmates and the wider music and entertainment community.

“His loss will be deeply felt by all those who knew and loved him both here in Australia and overseas.

“The family and band members are asking for privacy at this difficult time and will not be giving interviews.

“We kindly ask that their wishes are respected as they come to terms with this devastating loss and take time they need to grieve.

“We thank everyone for their prays and messages of love and continued support.

“With love, the Anderson family and Rose Tattoo band. Stay young, grow strong.”

Anderson’s band Rose Tattoo announced their frontman passed on Monday September 21. Photo by Matt Williams

The news comes after Anderson was hospitalised following a cardiac arrest in early August.

Anderson’s hospitalisation forced Rose Tattoo to cancel the European leg of their One Last Ride tour.

The band had nine shows remaining with the tour due to wrap up in Busdorf, Germany on August 21.

At the time of cancellation, they revealed Anderson was in hospital but didn’t any provide further details.

“Thank you from the bottom of our hearts for all the love, messages and support you have already sent Angry and the band,” the band said at the time.

Anderson with his Rose Tatoo bandmates Mark Evans, Bob Spender, Mick Arnold and Paul DeMarco.  Instagram/@rosetattoo

It wasn’t until late August that Rose Tattoo shared more about Anderson’s condition.

“We would like to let everyone know that Angry suffered a cardiac arrest and is currently in hospital receiving treatment and the care he needs,” the band said in an August 31 statement.

“At this time, we kindly ask that you please respect the privacy of Angry and his family as they focus on Angry, his treatment and his recovery.

“We appreciate everyone’s understanding and ask that you continue to keep Angry and his family in your thoughts and prayers during the days ahead.”

Barnes family lead tributes

Fellow Aussie rock icon Jimmy Barnes thanked Anderson for taking his son Jackie under his wing in his tribute to “a caring soul”.

“We lost a good man this week,” Jimmy wrote.

“Angry Anderson sang from his heart with power and conviction. But don’t let that tough outside fool you. He was a caring soul who loved his family, cared for his friends and stood up for the downtrodden.

As fellow frontmen of the gritty 1970s and ’80s pub rock era, their bond spanned several decades of Australian music history.

A life of rock’n’roll

Anderson was born on August 5, 1947, in Melbourne, growing up in the suburb of Coburg.

Rose Tattoo had to cancel the final leg of their European tour after Anderson’s hospitalisation.  Instagram/@rosetattoo

He started singing in the early 1970s with groups like Peace Power, Purity and Buster Brown before joining the Rose Tattoo line-up in 1976.

Famous for gritty pub-rock anthems like Rock ‘n’ Roll Outlaw and We Can’t Be Beaten, Anderson and the band moved from the Australian pub scene to mainstream success during the 1980s.

Rose Tattoo’s debut album and hit single Bad Boy for Love received widespread national recognition.

Their next album, Assault and Battery, topped the UK heavy metal charts, catapulting them to international stardom.

Anderson scored a massive global and domestic solo hit in 1987 with his ballad Suddenly, which famously played during Scott and Charlene’s wedding in the soap opera Neighbours.

His career expanded into stage, film, television, in productions including Jesus Christ Super Star and Mad Max Beyond Thunderdome.

Angry’s advocacy

Outside his music and entertainment career, Anderson received a Member of the Order of Australia in 1993 for his tireless advocacy and charity work supporting disadvantaged youth.

He became an active spokesperson for men’s health causes as well as various hospital and children’s charities.

He hosted The Challenge on Channel 9, using his celebrity to galvanise the community to help those in need.

The world might have known Anderson as the tough-as-nails Rose Tattoo frontman but he dedicated his life to the greater good of others.   Lisa Maree Williams/Getty Images

In his 1994 biography, Angry – Scarred for Life, written by Karen Dewey, Anderson shared that he faced a violent family environment and was sexually abused by a family friend from the age of five.

Over the years, Anderson discussed many times how he used his painful upbringing as motivation to break the cycle of abuse.

Having survived addiction and childhood abuse, Anderson became a passionate youth advocate worked on programs tackling youth issues, and served as an ambassador for national child protection campaigns like White Balloon Day to help protect children from harm.

Family heartbreak

Anderson was married to Lindy Michael from 1986 until their divorce in 2002.

Together, they had four children, Roxanne, Galen, Blaine and Liam.

Liam was killed in 2018, he was 26.

He was bashed to death by his best mate Mathew Flame, who was in a state of psychosis after taking drugs over a number of hours.

Flame was charged with murder but found guilty of manslaughter in 2020.

Anderson’s youngest son Liam was tragically killed in 2018, he was just 26.  Instagram

At the time, Anderson shared a powerful victim impact statement: “Words are not enough – there are no words for the cruellest of life’s trials, that we now endure and endure we must.”
