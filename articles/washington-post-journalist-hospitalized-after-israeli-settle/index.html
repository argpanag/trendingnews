Washington Post journalist hospitalized after Israeli settler attack on Palestinian olive harvest in West Bank | CNN Business

TheTools · 16.10.2026 13:10 · source · permalink

A Washington Post photojournalist was hospitalized after Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank attacked her and a group of Palestinians during the annual olive harvest.

Veteran photojournalist Heidi Levine, who was on assignment for The Washington Post, said the group of Palestinians she was with began to retreat when they saw dozens of settlers running toward them.

In a video statement, Levine said settlers kicked her while she was on the ground. “I had my camera bag and my pouch with my passport, and my telephone was on a rope around my neck and they broke that off me, and they broke my cameras off me, and they broke my bag, and they kept kicking me,” said Levine, who estimated the attack lasted 10 minutes. “Just absolute craziness, and I didn’t see anybody trying to stop it.”

“I don’t know how a parent can raise such a monster. They’re not even humans, and yes, they are Jewish terrorists,” Levine added. “And you know what? Sadly, none of them will be prosecuted. I am sure of it.”

The Washington Post said Levine was wearing “visible markings clearly identifying her as a journalist” during the attack. “We are outraged by this unwarranted attack on a journalist doing her job and urge a thorough investigation and follow-through from authorities.”

Video of the attack showed a group of masked settlers, some armed with clubs, hurling stones at a group of Palestinians, some of whom are also throwing stones. In the background, at least five settlers surround Levine and throw her to the ground, beating her as she covers her face with her hands. The settlers appear to rip away Levine’s bag.

Journalist Jasper Nathaniel, who witnessed the attack and helped Levine, told CNN’s Jim Sciutto on Friday that they had watched settlers on a hilltop put on masks and pick up clubs as Palestinians harvested olives below. “I mean, we watched them prepare for this attack,” he said. About an hour into the harvest, he said, the settlers charged down the hill, throwing stones.

Nathaniel said Israeli soldiers then arrived and fired tear gas at Palestinians, forcing him and Levine to retreat. He also described settlers firing guns from the back of a white pickup truck as it raced down the hill.

Levine was caught during the retreat, Nathaniel said. He described seeing five or six settlers surround her and repeatedly beat her with clubs.

At one point, Nathaniel said, an attacker pulled Levine up by her vest while another kicked her back to the ground.

“I was worried that she was going to be killed,” Nathaniel said.

He credited young Palestinian men with helping him reach Levine, saying they “stepped up and provided cover.”

“If those young Palestinian men had not thrown those rocks, Heidi could be dead,” he said. “And I would I would not have been able to get to her.”

Nathaniel, who visited Levine in the hospital after the attack, said she had a broken rib, a broken vertebra and a severe finger injury requiring surgery.

The Palestine Red Crescent Society said it was treating two people after they were beaten by settlers: a 63-year-old woman and a 40-year-old man. The assault is part of a wave of settler attacks at the start of the Palestinian olive harvest that residents reported across the area. Last year’s olive harvest saw a surge of such incidents across the West Bank as settlers attacked Palestinians and prevented them from accessing their land.

Levine was rushed to the Ramallah Government Hospital after the attack, where she was in stable condition, according to the Foreign Press Association (FPA). Photos show Levine’s hand and leg wrapped in bandages as she rests in a hospital bed.

The FPA condemned the attack and called on the head of the Israeli military’s Central Command, which governs operations in the West Bank, to “stop making excuses and do its job of protecting civilians from these violent mobs.”

In a statement, the FPA said, “This is the latest in a long line of violent attacks by Israeli settlers against journalists and other civilians in the occupied West Bank. Yet our repeated calls for action to confront this lawlessness have been ignored by Israeli authorities.”

Additional videos from the scene show a man with a bloodied face after the attack, as well as flame pouring out of a burning car with Palestinian license plates. Another video shows Israeli soldiers blocking the road. One soldier films with his phone; a second hurls what appears to be a smoke grenade as a man begs him, “Please don’t do it!”

Israel Police opened an investigation after receiving reports of the attack. In a statement, police said they “view any act of violence with great severity, particularly when it involves members of the media carrying out their work.”

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said they would examine “all circumstances” around the incident. “Attempts to incite friction between populations undermine security in the area,” the IDF said. “Attacks and violence against innocent civilians are grave and unacceptable acts.”

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who has repeatedly downplayed settler violence as the actions of a rogue group, tried to cast doubt on the attack, saying in a statement on X that “serious questions remain.” He said that Israel “unequivocally condemns violence against anyone,” without issuing any censure of the masked and armed settlers who carried out the attack.

In sharp contrast to Netanyahu, his main rival in the upcoming elections, Gadi Eisenkot, called the attack on Levine “unacceptable” and said these acts “stain an entire nation.”

Israeli President Isaac Herzog said in a statement that he called Levine on Saturday after the Jewish Sabbath ended.

“As President of the State of Israel, I strongly condemn this outrageous and shocking violence, both the terrible attack you endured and other grave incidents of violence, such as what took place today in Taybeh,” Herzog said.

“This weighed heavily on my conscience throughout Shabbat, and I express my deep sorrow and apologize for what you went through. The despicable behavior of the perpetrators of this attack does not represent the citizens or the values of the State of Israel.”

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee condemned the attack in strong terms, calling it a “despicable act of terror carried out by masked cowards” and a “dishonorable act of senseless violence.”

Huckabee called for “a full investigation, arrest, and prosecution to the fullest extent of the law to whoever participated in this.”

Meanwhile, the US State Department said it had reached out to The Washington Post to offer consular assistance. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters Saturday that the Trump administration is “very disturbed by those images and what we’re seeing.”

“We’ve asked the Israelis and continue to ask them to crack down on that stuff,” Rubio said. Asked how the administration squares its discomfort with the attack and Trump’s lifting of sanctions on Israeli settlers early in his current term, Rubio responded that the removal of sanctions is not support for settler violence.

“He doesn’t want to see what’s happening now,” Rubio said of Trump. “Lifting sanctions…does not mean he’s a supporter of what he’s seeing now on television, what he’s reading about.”

Mustafa Barghouti, head of the Palestinian National Initiative, said on X that “gangs of Israeli terrorist settlers” launched an attack on the village of Abu Falah, where Palestinians were preparing to harvest their olives.

“In full view of the cameras, the settlers set olive trees on fire, pelt Palestinian farmers with stones, and fired live ammunition, resorting to every possible form of terror and intimidation to prevent Palestinians from harvesting their own olive trees,” said Barghouti.

CNN’s Dana Karni and Jim Sciutto contributed to this story.
