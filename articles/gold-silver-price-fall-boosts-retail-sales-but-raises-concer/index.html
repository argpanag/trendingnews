The sharp decline in gold and silver prices over the past 15 days has brought relief to jewellery buyers, particularly those planning weddings and purchasing precious metals for gifting, even as it has raised concerns among investors and traders over potential losses.

While jewellers have reported an increase in sales following the decline in prices, industry representatives remain divided over its impact on businesses and the future movement of prices.

According to market rates on Tuesday (November 24, 2026), 24-carat gold was priced at ₹12,780 per gram, down from around ₹18,000 per gram in January 2026. Prices had remained in the range of ₹15,000 to ₹16,000 per gram during September and October. Silver prices have also witnessed a steep decline, falling from around ₹3.5 lakh and ₹4 lakh per kg to ₹1.6 lakh per kg.

Srinivas R., honorary secretary of the Jewellers Association, Bengaluru, said that the gold prices had declined by around 34%, while silver prices had fallen by nearly 60%, creating both opportunities and challenges for different sections of the market.

“The current situation can be analysed in two ways. It is a good time for those who want to purchase gold and silver for weddings or gift them to their loved ones. However, it is definitely not a good time for investors who purchased precious metals earlier, expecting good returns or profits from selling them. They may have to wait for the next two months. As per my prediction, gold prices could decline further to around ₹11,500 per gram,” he said.

T.A. Sharavana, founder and owner of Shree Sai Gold Palace, said that jewellery shops have witnessed an increase in sales over the past 15 days as customers sought to take advantage of the lower prices.

“This is the first time in history that gold prices have risen to ₹18,000 per gram and subsequently fallen to around ₹12,000 within just 10 months. A similar trend has been witnessed in silver prices,” he said.

However, he pointed out that the price correction could adversely affect newly established jewellery businesses, particularly those that had borrowed money from banks and private financiers to set up their shops and purchase inventory.

“Established businesses that have been operating for several years may be able to absorb or compensate for the losses. However, newly opened shops could face difficulties. According to my calculation, gold and silver businesses could incur losses of around 25%, considering the expenditure on employee salaries, electricity bills and other operational costs,” he added.

Disagreeing with the assessment of losses, B. Ramachary, president of the Karnataka State Jewellers Federation (KSJW), said the decline in prices would not necessarily translate into losses for most jewellery businesses.

He said several jewellers would have purchased gold and silver at lower prices in January and February and sold them to customers when rates were significantly higher, ranging between ₹15,000 and ₹18,000 per gram. The profits earned during that period, he said, could offset any losses arising from the current price correction.

Mr. Ramachary also advised those planning to invest in gold and silver to wait until December 9, when the U.S. Federal Reserve is scheduled to review its interest rates.

“Depending on whether interest rates are increased or reduced, investment patterns in gold and silver could change. This could influence demand and prices. With changes in demand and supply, prices are expected to rise by the middle of December,” he said.

Meanwhile, the decline in gold prices is unlikely to have a significant impact on existing gold loan borrowers, said Gopal Krishnan, former president and head of the law department at Axis Bank.

He said fluctuations in gold prices were a regular feature of the market and banks generally accounted for such movements while extending loans against pledged jewellery.

“There will not be a huge impact on gold loans as gold prices keep fluctuating. Banks or private financiers may ask borrowers to clear the outstanding balance or pledge additional gold if the value of the collateral falls significantly. However, banks have not issued any such notices to customers even during previous periods of price decline,” he said.

What influences gold and silver prices?

Several global factors influence the prices of precious metals, including geopolitical tensions, inflation, government debt levels and interest rates.

Mr. Srinivas said governments facing financial pressures could sell portions of their gold reserves to raise funds, potentially increasing supply in the market and exerting downward pressure on prices.

“Rising interest rates in the U.S. could also make interest-bearing bank deposits and other financial instruments more attractive to investors than gold and silver, which do not generate regular returns,” he added.

Silver prices, meanwhile, are influenced not only by investment demand but also by industrial consumption, according to Mr. Srinivas. “Demand from sectors such as solar energy, electric vehicles and data centres plays an important role in determining prices. Efforts by industries to explore alternative materials to silver could also influence its future demand and price movements,” he said.
