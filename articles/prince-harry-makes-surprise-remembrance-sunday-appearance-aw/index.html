Despite previous reports that he'd remain low-key for Remembrance Sunday or keep out of the spotlight altogether, Prince Harry has made an appearance after all.

Prince Harry's Remembrance Sunday appearance

The Duke of Sussex, who served in the military for a decade, was spotted at a small service for the British holiday in Brackley, a small market town in Northamptonshire, where he was able to fly under the radar.

Prince Harry attended a Remembrance Sunday service in the town of Brackley (@ Getty Images)

Per reports from the Daily Mail, Harry was spotted wearing his medals and a poppy while interacting with locals at the Brackley Town Remembrance Service, although there was no sign of his wife Meghan Markle.

An onlooker told the publication: "He was at the memorial service – he was stood next to the pub. He was stood there watching and then afterwards he went into the market and started shaking everyone's hand."

"He was there for the whole service – we were there for 20 or 30 minutes, and he was there before us. He just wanted to show some respect."

The royal Remembrance Sunday service

His appearance was a good couple hours away from the official royal Sunday service for Remembrance, where the Prince and Princess of Wales joined the King and Queen at the Cenotaph in Whitehall.

Following a national two-minute silence to remember those who died in conflict, King Charles, Prince William, Princess Anne and the Duke of Edinburgh laid wreaths alongside senior politicians at the war memorial. 

He did not attend the official royal family's service at the Cenotaph in Whitehall (@ Getty Images)

Some 10,000 veterans took part in the poignant Royal British Legion's march-past, representing 300 different Armed Forces and civilian organizations.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham was also present at the ceremony, saying in an official statement ahead of the service: "I'm incredibly proud to be [honoring] those whose sacrifice continues to protect our way of life to this day."

Remembrance Sunday is a holiday commemorating the military and civilian services of the British and Commonwealth forces (@ Getty Images)

"Remembrance Sunday gives us an opportunity, not only to mark that sacrifice and offer our gratitude, but also to reflect on its significance. To reflect on the selflessness and courage it takes to commit your life to protecting others," he continued. "I want to offer my personal thanks to those who served and those who continue to serve in our Armed Forces communities to this day."

What is Remembrance Sunday?

Remembrance Sunday is commemorated annually on the second Sunday of November in the United Kingdom as a way to honor the services of the British and Commonwealth military, specifically all the military and civilian service people in the two World Wars and subsequent conflicts.

The Prince and Princess of Wales attended beside the King and Queen (@ Getty Images)

In an official post on social media marking the day, William and Catherine's joint message read: "[Honoring] all those who have served, and continue to serve, in defence of our freedom. On Remembrance Sunday, we remember those who gave their lives in service,and reflect on the lasting impact of their sacrifice. We remember them today and always. Lest we forget."
