Barranquilla Airport Braces for 8,800 Arrivals Nov. 20: Plan

TheTools · 19.11.2026 01:14 · source · permalink

Barranquilla's airport expects about 8,800 passengers on Nov. 20 and 125,900 from Nov. 17 to 26 for the Copa Sudamericana final, 25% more than last year.

Barranquilla’s Ernesto Cortissoz airport expects about 8,800 passengers on Friday, Nov. 20, ahead of the Copa Sudamericana final on Nov. 21, up from 5,032 on the same day last year, with about 125,900 passengers from Nov. 17 to 26, 25% more than a year ago, Aviacionline reported from Colombia’s civil aviation authority. Infobae reported the same total.

The airport is opening a renovated international area for the rush. It adds capacity, new immigration filters and more automated Biomig passport lanes, and Migración Colombia is sending 42 officers to Barranquilla over four shifts, Infobae said.

The Busiest Days at Barranquilla

Friday, Nov. 20, is the peak arrival day, and Sunday, Nov. 22, is expected to be the busiest day for international departures, with about 3,800 passengers, according to Aviacionline.

The final at Estadio Metropolitano Roberto Meléndez pits Boca Juniors against Atlético Mineiro, and each club has 12,000 tickets for its fans, the outlet said. Up to 8,500 international passengers are linked to the match, mostly from Brazil and Argentina.

The figures are statistical projections based on historical data, not passengers already carried, the civil aviation authority noted.

Extra Flights for the Final

Barranquilla has no regular nonstop flights to Argentina or Brazil, so most fans connect through Bogotá, Medellín, Panama City or Lima, Aviacionline said.

LATAM plans four to six special flights between Buenos Aires and Barranquilla, and Avianca is running nonstop flights from Buenos Aires plus six extra Bogotá to Barranquilla flights, according to Infobae. For more on Avianca’s plans, see our report on Avianca’s Sudamericana final flights.

Between 12 and 26 private aircraft are also expected, adding to the pressure on the airport’s ramp and terminal.

Domestic demand rises too. About 4,300 extra domestic passengers are expected, and the authority estimates the market needs 1,000 to 4,100 extra seats to keep flights below 95% full on the peak days.

Why the Numbers Jump

Barranquilla normally sees only about 370 passengers a month arriving from Argentina and Brazil with the city as their final stop, Aviacionline said, so the final brings a sudden wave of international visitors to an airport that rarely handles them.

Without the final, the civil aviation authority expected November traffic growth of 5% to 13%. The match pushes the 10-day total about 25% above last year.

About 2,100 Colombians are also expected to travel to Barranquilla for the match, plus about 2,200 international fans who will connect through other Colombian cities, according to the projections.

Fans traveling by road add pressure on the city’s hotels and streets, and Colombian authorities expect more than 1,700 people to cross from Ecuador at the Rumichaca border point, Infobae reported.

Traveling to the Colombian Coast That Weekend

U.S. travelers heading to Barranquilla, Cartagena or Santa Marta between Nov. 19 and 23 should expect fuller flights, higher fares and longer lines at immigration and security.

Migración Colombia expects about 58,000 foreign visitors nationwide for the match, with entries peaking between Nov. 19 and 21, including about 15,000 at Bogotá and about 3,000 at Cartagena, Infobae reported.

If your dates are flexible, arrive before Nov. 19 or after Nov. 23. If not, book connections with extra time, especially when changing planes in Bogotá.

U.S. citizens can enter Colombia as tourists without a visa, but must complete the Check-MIG online form before arrival. Check the current requirements before you fly.

Plan a Barranquilla Trip

Avoid Nov. 20. It is the peak arrival day.

Watch Nov. 22. Departures peak then.

Book connections carefully. Leave extra time in Bogotá.

Complete Check-MIG. Colombia requires it.

Expect fuller flights. Domestic seats are tight.

Book hotels early. Fans fill the city.

If a travel agent plans your Colombia trip, ask them to avoid connecting through Bogotá on Nov. 20 and 22 if possible.
