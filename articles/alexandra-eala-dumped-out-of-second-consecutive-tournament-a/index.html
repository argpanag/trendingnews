Alexandra Eala dumped out of second consecutive tournament against Shnaider as Elena Rybakina withdraws from Pan Pacific Open

TheTools · 30.10.2026 08:09 · source · permalink

 A huge opportunity has arisen for the competitors at the Pan Pacific Open. That was most certainly the case for Alexandra Eala, but for the second tournament on the spin, she has seen her aspirations crumble against Diana Shnaider in a 2-6, 7-6(5), 6-4 loss. 

It was a match dominated by holds after a blistering start by the 21-year-old. Shnaider seized control late on after four crucial break points were saved. She won the second set tiebreak and soon enough seized the initiative in the third, cruising over the finishing line.

Before this occurred, the tournament in Tokyo was hit with a major blow as the World No.1 Elena Rybakina withdrew due to injury. It is the second year on the spin that the Kazakh has pulled out, reaching the semi-finals last year to confirm her spot at the WTA Finals before prioritising that event.

It seems that her health has come first again, and the title charge has abruptly halted along with her Asian swing. In a statement, she said: "Dear fans, I'm very sorry to announce that I've had to withdraw from my next match. Unfortunately, I've been experiencing inflammation and pain in my neck, which has been affecting my ability to play at my best.

"It's a very difficult decision, as I love playing here and always appreciate the incredible support from the fans. I hope to be back again next year. Thank you all for your understanding and support."

It is a massive disappointment for the fans who were waiting for a rematch of the Ningbo Open final against Leylah Fernandez. Rybakina was the champion last time out, but this time the Canadian does not even need to step onto court to get her revenge. She is advanced into the semi-finals without hitting a ball.

Shnaider got the first hold on the board before shooting out into a 0-40 lead. Eala averted the danger winning five points on the spin to get herself back level. This theme of missed break points would be costly for Shnaider later down the line.

For the time being, Eala was in control. She ticked off five games in a row to lead 5-1, breaking twice to muster up a comprehensive advantage. Shnaider ended the rut but was very much on the back foot. Eala wrapped up the first set with no fuss and steamed clear.

Shnaider desperately tried to find a route back into the match, but it was repeatedly blocked off. She led 2-1 as two break points cropped up in deuce. They were both successfully defended by the 21-year-old. That same feat occurred in the eighth game as the holds of serve prolonged.

It was Eala’s turn to hang her head. She led 15-40 with a break moving her 5-4 ahead. Both were squandered, along with two other break points formed later on in a long game. Shnaider breathed a sigh of relief as the second set continued.

A tiebreak was needed to split these two fierce competitors. Shnaider seized the early advantage and led 4-2. Eala struck back and drew level at 5-5. An untimely double fault handed Shnaider with a set point, and she needed no second invitations.

From being two points away from winning, Eala would find herself a break down. She led 40-0 in the third game of the third set, but any sign of a routine hold was extinguished. For the first time since the fifth game of the match, there was a break of serve, and Shnaider’s first of the contest.

Eight of the next nine points went with serve, and Shnaider was three away from victory. She had to wait a brief period as Eala underwent a medical time out with her ankle seemingly the port of call.

Soon enough she was back on court, but the match seemed to be heading in the direction of a routine and sudden conclusion. All Eala could do was force the No.3 seed to serve it out. A break point was not taken in the trend of much of this match, and Shnaider soon created a match winning opportunity, which was converted.

The semi-final lineup is set in stone. Fernandez will play lucky loser Kamilla Rakhimova, while Shnaider will look to avoid a seismic upset against 18-year-old wildcard Mia Pohankova.
