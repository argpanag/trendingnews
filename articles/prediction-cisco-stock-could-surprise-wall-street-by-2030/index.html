Prediction: Cisco Stock Could Surprise Wall Street by 2030

TheTools · 17.10.2026 14:30 · source · permalink

Cisco's CEO is calling it a networking super cycle, and the numbers behind that claim are hard to ignore. But reaching $200 by 2030 requires three specific things to go right, and at least one of them sits outside the…

Price Targets desk. Editor: Vandita Jadeja.

Cisco Systems (NASDAQ:CSCO | CSCO Price Prediction | CSCO Price Prediction) sits in the middle of what its CEO calls a networking super cycle. Last quarter, product revenue rose 24% to $13.46B and networking climbed 28% to $9.79B. AI orders reached $9.30B for fiscal 2026.

Shares are up 43.91% YTD, yet they trade at $108.87 after a 4.2% drop today. Chuck Robbins said it directly: “We believe we’re only at the beginning of this super cycle.” So can Cisco reach $200 by 2030?

What’s Holding Cisco Back Right Now

The stock has lost momentum. Shares fell 7.26% over the past week and 0.71% over the past month. They now trade below the 52-week high of $129.38. Investors are focused on margins.

Non-GAAP gross margin slipped to 66.3% from 68.4% as lower-margin AI hardware took a bigger share of sales. Management also highlighted memory costs and uneven hyperscaler order timing. With a beta of 0.979, Cisco usually tracks the broader market, which makes a sharp one-day drop a clear sign of investor nerves.

Wall Street Sees 25.1% Upside. Our Model Says 19.5%

The consensus target is $136.16, an upside of 25.1%. It rests on 5 Strong Buy, 14 Buy, 10 Hold and 0 Sell ratings. Our one-year base case is $130.14, a 19.5% gain. The scenarios run from $109.27 in the bear case to $138.12 in the bull case, and model confidence is high at 0.9.

In my view, both expectations underestimate Cisco’s earnings. Earnings grew 52.1% year over year, yet only 66% of analysts rate the stock bullish. Ten Holds on a company guiding to $5.05 to $5.11 in fiscal 2027 non-GAAP EPS strike me as too conservative.

Here’s What It Takes for Cisco to Reach $200

To get to $200 from today’s price of $108.87 would require a gain of 83.7%.

With forward EPS of $5.146, a price of $200 implies a forward P/E of 39x. Our base case of $130.14 already implies 26x, so the bold target requires 13x of additional multiple expansion.

Today’s valuation looks steep, but the math shifts over time. If EPS compounds at the 9.23% consensus growth rate through fiscal 2030, it reaches about $6.71, and $200 works out to roughly 30x.

Earnings growth does most of the lifting. An adjustment factor of 1.125 reflects technology sector momentum and strong earnings acceleration, with the five-year bull case already reaching $187.86.

The catalysts keep coming. Cisco expects AI infrastructure revenue of $7.5B in fiscal 2027, roughly double fiscal 2026. Recent releases highlighted Splunk advancements for trusted AI, new agentic collaboration experiences and scalability and openness at OCP 2026.

Robbins added: “The network remains an absolute critical factor as to whether AI deployments succeed or fail.” Cisco is one of several picks-and-shovels names gaining from the data-center expansion (we rounded up seven of these AI infrastructure suppliers, networking included, in a free report here).

A major risk is that hyperscaler AI spending cools before enterprise demand can fill the gap.

Where Cisco Trades Today vs Its Earnings Power

At $108.87, Cisco trades at 21x forward EPS. That’s a reasonable multiple for a company guiding to $72.2B to $73.4B in fiscal 2027 revenue, up from $63.33B in fiscal 2026.

Shares sit between a 52-week low of $67.32 and a high of $129.38. Over 10 years, the stock has returned 383.19%. A 21x multiple on accelerating earnings leaves room for the bull case to play out.

$200 Is a Stretch, But Here’s Why It’s Possible

Hitting $200 by 2030 would take an 83.7% gain. I see it as a stretch, though a reachable one. Three things need to go right. AI infrastructure revenue has to keep growing past $7.5B. Security growth needs to improve to high single digits, as management expects. Operating margins also have to hold near 35% while gross margin slips.

A sharp pullback in hyperscaler capex would undermine the thesis. Returns at this level shouldn’t be expected every year, but we’ve outlined the path for how Cisco could reach $200 in 2030.
