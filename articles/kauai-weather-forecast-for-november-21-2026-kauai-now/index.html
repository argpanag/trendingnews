Synopsis

Breezy to locally strong trade winds will persist into Saturday. Quick passing showers caught up within this trade wind flow will primarily focus along windward and mauka areas. Upper ridging will nose in from the west and, along with a drier air mass settling in over the Central Pacific, assist in producing a few drier days early next week. The trade winds will gradually ease through midweek before a cold front moves through on Thanksgiving day and brings gusty northerly winds.

Discussion

A strong high pressure system to the north will help drive breezy to locally strong trade winds across the state tonight into Saturday. In this flow, low level clouds and showers will be ushered into windward and mauka areas, with some spillover expected into interior and leeward areas due to the stronger trade wind flow and slightly elevated inversion levels. The greatest shower coverage can be expected during the overnight and early morning hours.
The high will gradually weaken and move east-southeast through the weekend and into early next week with weak ridging settling just north of the state on Monday, then sliding over the state by Tuesday night. In response, the trades will gradually weaken. At the same time, a drier airmass will move over the region and upper level ridging will build in from the west, leading to a somewhat drier, weakening trade wind pattern. However, there will be enough low-level moisture to support limited windward and mauka showers. Wednesday is looking like a transition day where the light winds will shift out of the west across the western end of the state and strengthen in advance of an approaching cold front.
According to the latest guidance, a cold front will move down the island chain on Thanksgiving Day, bringing gusty northerly winds and somewhat limited rainfall due to the drier airmass over the area. While both the GFS and ECMWF have been somewhat consistent in showing the front moving through sometime on Thursday into Friday over the last couple of runs, forecast confidence on the timing and strength of this frontal passage remains on the lower side as the models are struggling to resolve the synoptic features in the Pacific at that time range.

Aviation

Breezy trade winds will usher in isolated to scattered showers somewhat enhanced by an upper trough building over the area. VFR conditions will prevail with periods of MVFR over windward and mauka areas.
AIRMET Tango is posted for leeward sections of all islands below 7000 feet due to the breezy trade winds, which is expected to continue over the next couple of days.
AIRMET Sierra has been cancelled as conditions have improved on all islands this afternoon. No other AIRMETS are currently in effect.

Marine

A robust 1033 mb high passing north of the islands will maintain fresh to locally near gale trade winds into tonight, and a Small Craft Advisory (SCA) is in effect for all waters. The high will move southeast and weaken Saturday and Sunday, causing trades to gradually ease and the SCA to eventually be scaled back to the typically windy waters around Big Island and Maui. As the subtropical ridge is displaced southward early next week, winds will decrease further, and the SCA will likely be dropped sometime Monday. The ridge will be pushed near or over the islands on Tuesday, and an approaching front will likely produce increasing southwest winds late Wednesday.
Small surf will prevail along north- and west-facing shores for the next few days followed by much larger swells next week. A tiny north swell will be overlapped by a small north-northwest that will peak on Sunday and decline on Monday. A large northwest swell will build on Tuesday and will push surf along north- and west-facing shores around High Surf Advisory thresholds into Wednesday. An extra large northwest swell is possible on Thanksgiving Day, though a front likely moving down the island chain at the same time could bring fresh northerly winds.
Strong trade winds will maintain rough surf above seasonal average along east-facing shores through Saturday. As trades decline, east shore surf will fall below seasonal average Sunday and will be very small through Wednesday. A small, long-period swell will bring a bump to south shore surf through Saturday, with mainly background level surf expected late Sunday through early next week.

Fire weather

Breezy to locally strong trades are expected through tonight, then will gradually ease through midweek next week. While winds may be a concern through at least Saturday, relative humidities are expected to remain near normal through the weekend. A drier airmass will settle in early next week bringing the relative humidities down, but at the same time, wind speeds will also be on the decline. These conditions will make for a moderate fire weather threat through Sunday and a low to moderate threat early next week. Temperature inversion heights will likely remain between 7,000 to 9,000 feet tonight into Saturday.
