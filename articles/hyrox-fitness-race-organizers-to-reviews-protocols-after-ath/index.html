Hyrox fitness race organizers to reviews protocols after athlete soils herself

WASHINGTON (TNND) — Organizers of a high-endurance fitness race, the Hyrox, said they will review their procedures after the world champion of the event was permitted to continue competing in the race despite having an accident resulting in soiling herself.

Joanna Wietrzyk, the world champion of the Hyrox, became sick while partaking in the age 16-24 elite event in Beijing on Saturday.

Footage online circulated, showing Wietrzyk with fecal matter running down her legs during the race. Part of the race includes completing eight 1km runs and eight workout stations in the least amount of time possible.

Both Hyrox and Wietrzyk, who won the race with a time of 1 hour, 1 minute and 23 seconds, faced heavy criticism from people online. Many pointed out the health risks posed by Wietrzyk's continued participation in the event.

Hyrox stated on its Instagram page early Monday morning, acknowledging the gravity of the situation.

“We ask our athletes to push themselves to the highest levels. At times, they will go beyond what even we thought was possible. That courage, resilience and refusal to give in are precisely what makes elite athletes so extraordinary. Our role is to give them the right framework and the right boundaries within which they can compete, perform and express themselves at their very best," the statement reads.

“Hyrox has clear biocontaminant and medical procedures in place. In this instance, an unforeseen situation arose in which protocols designed for elite competition and mass participation became blurred, creating ambiguity in how they were applied and understood," the organizers continued.

Wietrzyk, the world record holder in the Hyrox women’s solo event, posted a selfie on Instagram from what appeared to be a hospital bed after the race. She later deleted the post.

The Hyrox, an intense indoor fitness competition, attracts around 1.5million competitors annually.
