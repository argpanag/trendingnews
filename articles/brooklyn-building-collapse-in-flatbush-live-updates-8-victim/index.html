Brooklyn building collapse in Flatbush live updates: 8 victims identified, including 2 kids

TheTools · 13.11.2026 00:52 · source · permalink

FLATBUSH, Brooklyn (WABC) -- Eight people are dead and 18 others are hurt after part of a three-story building collapsed into a construction site at the corner of Flatbush and Bedford avenues in Flatbush, Brooklyn, just after 4 p.m. Wednesday, according to the FDNY.

Six bodies were recovered from the rubble, and two people died at the hospital. Two of the dead were construction workers and two were children, Mayor Zohran Mamdani said. On Thursday evening, the NYPD released the names of all eight victims.

The building at 1321 Flatbush Ave., which had a store at street level and apartments upstairs, fell into an open excavation at 2625 Bedford Ave.

The Department of Buildings says the developer had an active permit for excavation and foundation work and has issued a full stop-work order while investigators determine the cause.

NYPD officials released the names of the eight victims who were killed in the Brooklyn building collapse.

The eight were identified as the following:

Nicole Daniela Salvada Rodriguez, 9

Kevin Santiago Reyes Gonzalez, 10

Gabriel Siguencia, 22

Barry Mamadou, 23

Jose Luis Caguana Cosquillo, 26

Edgar Sapon, 26

Juan Daniel Salvada Guzman, 31

Ndiaye Mamadou, 31

Ring camera video captured the moments as a building collapsed in Flatbush, Brooklyn on Wednesday.

Rescue operations have concluded at the collapse site and officials say there are no more victims.

The FDNY has turned the site over to the Department of Buildings and the NYPD.

Earlier, the FDNY said several construction workers were being transported to the hospital for treatment, and firefighters were trying to get a count of all the workers on the job site.

Excavation work had been underway at the construction site, which has been approved for a three-story building, officials said. Plans for a nine-story, mixed-use building there had yet to be approved, officials said.

A stop work order has been issued for the neighboring construction site as search and rescue continues, according to the Department of Buildings (DOB).

The cause of the collapse remains under investigation by the DOB and law enforcement, authorities said.

"We will find out what happened, and we will share that information with New Yorkers," Mamdani said Thursday.

The DOB said its inspectors were at the scene on Thursday assisting first responders with recovery efforts and conducting structural stability inspections of nearby buildings.

In March, the DOB said it issued a stop-work order for the construction site, at 2625 Bedford Ave., after inspectors found demolition was underway without adequate overhead protection installed for the neighboring properties.

During a follow-up in April, the DOB said it found that demolition work had resumed in violation of the active stop-work order, resulting in another violation.

The order was lifted in May after the project team corrected all the violations, according to the DOB.

Mamdani said Thursday that five entities are involved with the construction site and are associated with approximately 100 other sites across New York City.

"Our Department of Buildings is commencing sweeps of those sites today," he said.

Vacate orders are in place for three buildings closest to the incident, located at 1323, 1325 and 1327 Flatbush Ave., Mamdani said.
