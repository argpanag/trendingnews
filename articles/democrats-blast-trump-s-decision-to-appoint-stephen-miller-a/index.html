Several newly elected Democrats and Senator Bernie Sanders have renewed calls for datacenter moratoriums in the days after the US midterm elections. The sweeping Democratic victory, which was buoyed in part by backlash to the tech industry, has added momentum to efforts to slow down the multi-trillion-dollar buildout of the controversial facilities.

On the social media platform X, Sanders posted a call for an immediate national datacenter moratorium this week. “Americans don’t want Big Tech oligarchs wrecking the environment, gutting jobs and threatening humanity’s future,” Sanders said on Wednesday.

Sanders was joined by several Democrats, including Representative Yassamin Ansari of Arizona – a state where Elon Musk’s SpaceX has proposed a datacenter project that would span more than 700 acres.

“Top of the Democratic agenda on day 1 needs to be a pause on advanced AI development, regulation of this out of control industry, and a ban on artificial superintelligence,” Ansari posted Wednesday on X. Musk mocked her post in a retweet, and the two sparred, with Ansari forecasting congressional hearings, telling the centi-billionaire: “See you soon.”

The pushback to the buildout of datacenters, which tech companies need to power the AI products that have become central to their businesses, was one of the most visible issues of the midterms. Opposition to datacenters figured heavily in campaigns across the US, while voters passed dozens of local ballot measures aimed at restricting datacenter projects in their states.

Republican congressman Mark Harris has narrowly won reelection to House in North Carolina’s eighth congressional district, defeating Democrat Colby Watson by just over 5,000 votes.

Democrats have won nine House seats in North Carolina so far, to just one for Republicans, with four races still too close to call, including one in which a Democrat leads a Republican incumbent by just 99 votes.

The elevation of Stephen Miller, the architect of Donald Trump’s brutal and unpopular mass deportation agenda, to White House chief of staff suggests that the president has no intention of rolling back the crackdown, even after voters delivered a sweeping rebuke by electing Democrats to every House seat along the US border with Mexico.

Miller’s appointment has been widely condemned by Democrats, and some Republicans, but the man himself said in a statement that he is “honored beyond words” to run the White House.

“President Trump is the most transformative political leader in American History — and he has single-handedly saved America from those who would destroy it,” Miller said, referring to the incoming Democratic majority in both houses of Congress in typically extreme fashion.

“The mission now is more clear than ever: Fight with all of our soul to support our President and defend this magnificent Republic,” Miller added, in a clear signal that the final two years of Trump’s presidency will pit a maximally combative White House against Congress.

The NAACP’s president, Derrick Johnson, has this to say about the elevation of Stephen Miller, a longtime promoter of white nationalism, to the role of White House chief of staff:

“Stephen Miller belongs nowhere near the Oval Office. He belongs before a judge. He’s a thug who will be remembered as the architect of Trump’s vile and authoritarian regime.”

Pete Hegseth, the embattled former Fox weekend anchor turned US defense secretary who could face an impeachment vote as soon as next week, was quick to praise new White House chief of staff Stephen Miller on social media, posting a meme of the two men together, with the caption “Stand. Fight. Defend the Republic.” Hegseth tagged both Miller and the White House on X, Elon Musk’s rebranded, rightwing Twitter, to make sure they were aware of his post.

Hegseth, who appears to be leading a coordinated social-media campaign to save his job, used the same caption for a previous post on Thursday, which featured images of himself and one of Donald Trump raising his fist moments after surviving an assassination attempt in 2024.

Robert F Kennedy Jr, the health secretary, quickly followed with a post welcoming Miller’s appointment as a supporter of the so-called Make America Health Again movement. “Next to me, Stephen Miller is the strongest MAHA supporter in the administration,” Kennedy wrote. “It’s time to unleash the Kraken.”

As Donald Trump traveled to Cape Canaveral, Florida today, he told reporters that he wants Stephen Miller to “run things well” as the new White House chief of staff.

When asked what prompted Miller’s promotion, the president said that he “wanted to make a change”.

“Susie [Wiles] has done a very good job, Stephen’s been great, and we gotta make moves,” Trump said. “Sometimes in life you have to make moves.”

“Stephen’s been with me from the beginning, he gets it, very good politically … a very good manager,” Trump said on his decision to pick Miller for the top job.

Miller is also traveling with Trump to Florida for the president’s remarks at the Kennedy Space Center later today.

Wiles has taken a 'stranglehold on policy decisions' says ex-adviser to health secretary

Against the backdrop of Susie Wiles’ departure from the White House, it’s worth noting that Calley Means, a former senior adviser to health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr who resigned just weeks before the US midterms, said that Wiles is the most “powerful chief of staff in modern American history” who has “taken a stranglehold on policy decisions”.

Means, an architect’s of the administration’s Make America Healthy Again (Maha) agenda, claimed that Wiles’ former work as a lobbyist at Ballard partners had compromised her support for various policies, particularly his proposals to make children’s diets healthier, in order to still receive financial boosts throughout the election cycle.

“Susie said to me many times: ‘We need money for the midterms, Calley. We need money for the midterms,’” Means said on a two-hour episode of Joe Rogan’s podcast.

“It is absolutely true that [Trump’s] administration has been infiltrated by the very people that he energized voters to say he would get out of DC,” Means told Rogan. “Everything in the political strategy was based on getting enough money for the midterms, not actually doing the things that would then energize voters enough.”

Hakeem Jeffries, poised to be next House speaker, calls for Miller to be 'fired'

Congressman Hakeem Jeffries, who is poised to become the next US House speaker when Democrats retake the majority in the new year, called Stephen Miller “a dangerous xenophobe and reckless extremist who has American blood on his hands”.

Jeffries added: “His [Miller’s] presence in government should be eradicated like a malignant tumor. Fire him today.”
