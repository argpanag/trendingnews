France and Wales rewarded for epic wins in latest World Rugby Men's Rankings | World Rugby

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:55 · source · permalink

France and Wales are the only movers in the top 10 of the World Rugby Men's Rankings after another dramatic weekend of test rugby.

France and Wales both improved their position in the top 10 of the World Rugby Men's Rankings after another dramatic weekend of test rugby across Europe.

Les Bleus set the tone with a nerve-shredding 21-19 victory over South Africa in the Nations Championship on Friday, before Wales caused one of the biggest upsets in test rugby the following day, beating the All Blacks for the first time in 73 years, 35-28 in Cardiff.

South Africa would have surrendered their number one ranking had New Zealand avoided defeat in Cardiff, but Wales' win means the Springboks remain in top spot. 

France's win – their first over the Springboks in Paris in 21 years – has enabled them to move up to third, with Ireland taking their place in fourth, while Wales have climbed one place to ninth.

Outside of the top 10, there was plenty of movement as results from the penultimate round of the World Rugby Nations Cup 2026 took effect.

Samoa and Chile mirrored France and Wales by taking down higher-ranked opponents, with away victories over Portugal and Spain moving them up the rankings.

Having been in danger of dropping out of the top 20 for the first time in history a fortnight ago, Samoa are now 16th, while Chile have climbed one place to 17th.

Meanwhile, USA's emphatic 53-24 win over Romania in Bucharest has earned them 14th place at the expense of Portugal, and the Men's Eagles are now two places off their all-time best of 12th. 

In the 2026-27 Rugby Europe Men's Trophy, Malta's fine debut campaign at that level continued with a third consecutive win, 36-34 at home to Sweden.

The Trophy newcomers are joined by Czechia in enjoying an unbeaten start to the campaign, while Germany picked up a second win, 66-19 against Denmark.  

Czechia lead the competition standings by two points over Malta after labouring to a 31-20 home win against Lithuania, but their ranking remains unchanged at 29th.

However, Germany and Malta have each moved up one place, to 36th and 38th.
