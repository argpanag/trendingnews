‘Outlander: Blood of My Blood’ Season 2 Answers Its Big Cliffhanger

Spoilers below for the first episode of Season 2 of Outlander: Blood of My Blood.

The Season 1 finale of Outlander: Blood of My Blood ended on one of the most perfectly calibrated cliffhangers the franchise has produced. Henry and Julia Beauchamp, Claire's parents played by Jeremy Irvine and Hermione Corfield, stood at the standing stones of Craigh na Dun with their newborn son William, the sound of Mr. Bugg (Terence Rae) and his cronies closing in behind them. Just as Henry is pressing his hand with Julia's against the stone, the screen cuts to black.

Season 2 opens with the resolution of that moment, and naturally, it raises as many questions as it answers. The most pressing unknown has been whether all three of them make it through, if any of them. The ability to time travel has proven to be genetic in Diana Gabaldon's books and on the original Outlander series. That's why both Claire and Brianna can travel as can Brianna's children with Roger. Roger MacKenzie, a descendant of Geillis Duncan through her illegitimate son with Dougal, Buck MacKenzie, can travel, and Buck himself appears in the later seasons having traveled through the stones. If that logic holds, William, as the child of two travelers, almost certainly has the ability.

But whether they have the right currency to make the journey is a different question. Outlander established that gemstones are required to pass safely through the stones, a detail Henry and Julia have almost certainly not been told or figured out yet. They have passed through once, which means they understand the stones are a portal, but the mechanics of how and why still remain mysterious to them.

Henry (Jeremy Irvine) and Julia (Hermione Corfield) try to escape at the stones.Robert Wilson - STARZ

As is revealed within the first few seconds of the Season 2 premiere, not all three of them make it. What transports Henry, in the end, is not a gemstone he brought with him but one that comes to him by accident. In a clever turn by the writers' room, Bugg attacks Henry with a hand blade, stabbing him in the shoulder, and on the handle of that blade is a bright red jewel. Dazed and bleeding, Henry stumbles behind the standing stone, presses his hand against it, and just…falls through.

Irvine notes that the cast and crew filmed several different versions of the scene at the time they filmed the Season 1 finale, and they didn't know which one was going to get chosen until it appeared in the final cut. "I think we did every combination of who runs through the stones, do all of us go through, do none of us go through," Irvine describes to T&C. "It was a nice surprise. But I think I kind of got the idea when I went in for my first costume fitting for Season 2 and it was very much not 1700s clothes." Henry arrives in 1924, a year after he and Julia left. But he awakes in a place called the "Inverness Lunatic Asylum," after being picked up while stumbled along the road talking semi-consciously about the past, bleeding from the stab wound, and identifying himself as Henry Grant (rather than his real name, Beauchamp).

Utterly distraught, Julia is left behind with baby William. Bugg, assuming Henry simply fled and still believing he had kidnapped Julia and William from Lord Lovat (Tony Curran), treats her with the most unexpected kindness and assures her he will bring her back to Castle Leathers. "She's almost back to square one, in terms of where we find her," says Corfield. "She's worked so hard and retained her hope and her positivity in terms of getting back to Claire with Henry, and when then that's all dashed, we find her right at rock bottom."

Showing a steely resolve, Julia manages to hold herself together until Bugg turns away, and then her face crumples. "She's almost out of options and really has to dig deep and put on the facade for Lovat that she's happy to be home and that she was captured by the bladier [Henry], so she really has to crack on with some acting skills," Corfield says.

Davina (Sara Vickers) and Julia back at Castle Leathers.Robert Wilson - STARZ

Revealing how much their relationship has grown since last season, or just how desperately Julia needs allies right now, Julia admits to Davina (Sara Vickers) upon returning to Castle Leathers the truth about where she comes from: the future. Given all of the accusations about witchcraft from last season, Davina might have seemed like the last person to confide in, but Davina appears rather bemused, if not simply relieved to hear the truth. She replies that she surmised as much, calling back to Julia's comment during their first meeting about "King George V" as the current monarch.

"I think she's so desperate not to be lonely anymore. She just wants a friend and a confidante," Corfield says about Julia's decision here. "[Davina] very much believes in the fairy folk and all of those kinds of things, so it is a risk, but I think she trusts Davina enough to take that risk, and [Julia] is at a point where her guard is down a bit, and that puts in her in a vulnerable position."

Henry at the asylum in the 20th century.Robert Wilson - STARZ

Henry's situation in 1924, however, is considerably darker. Even though he has made it back to their own time, he is even less free than Julia is in 1715. Though the doctor initially appears to show some sympathy toward what appears to be severe trauma, recognizing Henry's PTSD from the war, Henry is subjected to electroshock therapy.

"The thing to remember is these were early days—electroshock therapy wasn't something that was very common, so it's easy to look at that doctor figure and ask, 'Is he doing something nefarious,'" says executive producer Maril Davis. "But in reality, he really does think he's helping Henry. He thinks he's the cutting edge of trying to remove these traumatic memories."

One of the episode's most significant twists arrives in the form of his visitor: Henry is a young Reverend Wakefield, the same man who would later adopt Roger MacKenzie as a boy after World War II. Wakefield appears in both the original Outlander series and the book Dragonfly in Amber as a crucial figure in Claire and Frank's lives after the war.

"They live in that time, so it was organic to have them there," showrunner and executive producer Matthew B. Roberts says about the decision to bring the characters back into the story. "It was so seamless with those two characters, and they affect Henry. That's the other part of the equation when you bring back characters is, 'Will they be there, but also how would they affect our characters?' They have to be able to do both, and that was almost perfect. You'll see throughout the season there might be a few more."

His housekeeper, the young Mrs. Graham, visits Henry later in the episode, offers him a biscuit, and gently steers the conversation toward the stones. Much to Henry's surprise, Mrs. Graham believes him when he says he left his family in the past—just as she would one day believe Claire—drawing on the same deep knowledge of Scottish folklore and the pagan traditions of Craigh na Dun that made her such a singular figure in the original series.

"In the asylum, he starts to doubt whether any of that really happened and question whether or not he really has just lost his mind," Irvine says. "So when Mrs. Graham says she believes him, I think it's what makes him believe himself again."
