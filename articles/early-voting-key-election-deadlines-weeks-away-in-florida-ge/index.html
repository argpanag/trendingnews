Early voting, key election deadlines weeks away in Florida, Georgia - What voters need to know now

TheTools · 10.09.2026 20:23 · source · permalink

Vote 2026

Florida and Georgia residents who want to cast a ballot in the midterm general election, with both states having high-profile races for governor and U.S. Senate, have a few more weeks to get registered to vote, with early voting starting soon thereafter.

Key races

Recommended Videos

Both states have a term-limited, Republican governor: Gov. Ron DeSantis in Florida, and Gov. Brian Kemp in Georgia. Republican Byron Donalds and Democrat David Jolly are facing off in the Florida race, with Republican Rick Jackson and Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms in Georgia’s race for governor.

In the U.S. Senate races, Georgia Democrat Jon Ossoff is seeking his second six-year term, facing a challenge from Republican Mike Collins. Florida’s Senate race is actually a special election, because Sen. Marco Rubio became President Donald Trump’s Secretary of State in January 2025. Republican Ashley Moody was appointed to the seat, and is now running in the election to serve the final two years of Rubio’s elected term. Moody faces Jacksonville Democrat Angie Nixon.

Beyond those two races, voters in both states will face choices in statewide cabinet races, U.S. House races, and proposed constitutional amendments, including Florida’s high-profile Amendment 3, which could make sweeping changes to property taxes in the state. Most voters will also see state legislative races on their ballot, and some will also see local races such as county commission and school board.

Registering to vote

In both Florida and Georgia, October 5 is the last day to register to vote, in order to vote in this year’s general election. Both states allow you to register to vote online or by mail. In Florida, you can also register through any tax collector’s office that issues Florida driver licenses.

REGISTRATION INFO: Florida | Georgia

Voting by mail

If you want to vote by mail, you must request a ballot in advance. In Florida, you make the request through your county’s Supervisor of Elections office, and the deadline to make the request is October 22. Georgia voters can request an absentee ballot through the Georgia Secretary of State’s office, and that request must be made by October 23. In Florida, vote-by-mail ballot requests can be made for a specific election or for all elections through the next general election. So, if your last request was made before the November 2024 election, you would need to make a new request for a vote-by-mail ballot.

Both states also offer early, in-person voting. You can vote at any early voting location within your county.

At the polls

On Election Day, November 3, polls will be open from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. in both Florida and Georgia. On Election Day, you must vote at your assigned voting precinct.

Whether you are voting early or on election day, you must show one of the accepted forms of photo identification.

Florida:

  • Florida Driver License
  • Florida ID Card (issued by DHSMV)
  • U.S. Passport
  • Debit or Credit Card
  • Military ID
  • Student ID
  • Retirement Center ID
  • Neighborhood Association ID
  • Public Assistance ID
  • Veteran Health ID (issued by the US Department of Veterans Affairs)
  • Concealed Weapon License (issued pursuant to F.S. 790.06)
  • Employee ID card issued by any branch, department, agency, or entity of the Federal Government, the state, a county, or a municipality.

If you show up without an ID, you can cast a “provisional” ballot, which is then evaluated by a canvassing board that decides if the ballot is eligible.

Georgia:

  • Any valid state or federal government-issued photo ID, including a free ID card issued by your county registrar’s office or the Georgia Department of Driver Services. 
  • A Georgia driver’s license, even if expired
  • Student ID from a Georgia public College or University
  • Valid employee photo ID from any branch, department, agency, or entity of the U.S. Government, Georgia, or any county, municipality, board, authority or other entity of this state 
  • Valid U.S. passport ID 
  • Valid U.S. military photo ID containing a photograph of the voter
  • Valid tribal photo ID containing a photograph of the voter
