Kings guard Ben Simmons tears his Achilles tendon in practice

Ben Simmons tore his right Achilles tendon in practice on Sunday, ending his comeback attempt this season with the Sacramento Kings before the season even started.

The Kings said Simmons hurt himself during a live drill and will need to undergo surgery three days before the season opener on Wednesday night on the road against the Los Angeles Clippers.

“I've faced challenges before that tested me mentally, physically, and emotionally, and I've found a way through every single one of them," Simmons wrote on social media. “This injury is another chapter in my journey, not the end of my story.”

Simmons had signed a one-year deal with Sacramento this summer as he tried to revive his career.

The 30-year-old from Australia is a former No. 1 draft pick, three-time All-Star and Rookie of the Year, but he has struggled to perform and stay healthy in recent years. He sat out last season while dealing with back and leg injuries after ending the 2024-25 season in a reserve role for the Los Angeles Clippers.

Simmons was the first overall pick by Philadelphia in 2016 after playing one season in college at LSU. He sat out his first season with an injured foot but quickly developed into a star player for the 76ers once he returned. He was the Rookie of the Year in 2017-18, was an All-Star the following three seasons, as well as a third-team All-NBA player in 2019-20 and a first-team All-Defense selection in 2019-20 and 2020-21.

But his career hasn’t been the same after sitting out the 2021-22 season when he was dealing with mental health concerns, other physical injuries and was seeking a trade out of Philadelphia.

He was sent to Brooklyn at the 2022 trade deadline but missed the rest of that season with a bad back. He then played 2 1/2 seasons with the Brooklyn before getting cut in February 2025. He finished that season with the Los Angeles Clippers before sitting out last season.

But he never came close to his level of play early in his career in Philadelphia, averaging 5.9 points, 5.8 rebounds and 5.8 assists during his time with the Nets and Clippers.

Sacramento is coming off a 22-60 season and is trying to rebuild around rookie Darius Acuff Jr., who was picked seventh in the draft after a stellar freshman season at Arkansas.

INDIANAPOLIS (AP) — Indianapolis Colts co-owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon sent a clear, concise message in January — make the playoffs or changes would be made.

Six weeks into the season, the pressure is mounting with the Colts holding a 2-4 record after a second-half collapse against previously winless Tennessee.

Indy blew a 13-0 halftime lead by turning the ball over twice in the second half, failing to protect quarterback Daniel Jones and giving up two game-changing plays on special teams.

The result was a crescendo of boos that started midway through the third quarter and grew increasingly louder until the field was cleared. They're likely to continue booing until the results improve.

“We've got to give them something to cheer,” coach Shane Steichen said after the 26-20 loss.

“Like I said, we have got to get a lot of things fixed. We jump out to a lead, we can't, you know, hold on to the lead, We've got to score in the red zone. I thought our defense did a really good job in the first half, but again, turnovers, special teams, a blocked punt, a huge kick return and we had some huge P.I. (pass interference) calls down there. There are a lot of things we have to fix.”

Jones went 22 of 42, was sacked six times and threw two interceptions — both inside the Titans 20-yard line to cost them scoring chances. The turnovers ended the team's 32-game streak without one in the red zone, going back to Week 7 of the 2024 season.

Jonathan Taylor caught two passes for 57 yards, rushed 21 times for 94 yards and tied the score at 20 on a 4-yard TD run with 5:32 to play but also lost his second fumble of the season.

The defense allowed just 64 yards in a scoreless first half, but gave up points on every Titans possession in the second half.

And the special teams units allowed two game-changing plays — Chimere Dike's 58-yard punt return that led to Tennessee's tying score, and a blocked punt that Oluwafemi Oladejo returned 14 yards for a touchdown and a 20-13 lead — before Joey Slye sealed Indy's fate with a tiebreaking 49-yard field goal with 2:03 to go and the game-sealing kick in the final minute.

“I'm sick and tired of the same song and dance every year,” defensive tackle DeForest Buckner said.

Steichen's postgame news conference had a different tone, too.

He spoke with more urgency about finding solutions. He fielded questions about Jones' play, whether he'd make a quarterback change, even midseason staff changes. Steichen wasn't ready to announce or even promise personnel changes yet.

“Yeah, I love Daniel, but we've got to get the turnovers cleaned up,” Steichen said.

Jones concurred.

“I don’t think it’s been consistent enough," he said when asked to evaluate his own play. “I think certainly today wasn’t consistent enough. I thought we did some good things offensively, moved the ball well, and then some bad stuff.”

So after two historically bad defensively performances to open the season followed by two wins — over then-winless Houston and a Washington team missing its starting quarterback — and now last week's double-digit loss at Pittsburgh and Sunday's second-half collapse, the heat is on the Colts.

“Yeah, they’re upset,” Buckner said as he responded to the booing. “We lost to the Tennessee Titans, who were 0-5, and now they’re 1-5 or whatever it is. So, yeah.”
