Cruise ship leaves Cambodia after forcing hundreds of passengers off

TheTools · 04.11.2026 17:01 · source · permalink

SIHANOUKVILLE, Cambodia — After disembarking more than 200 passengers who had paid tens of thousands of dollars to live on board for years, the Villa Vie Odyssey cruise ship departed from Cambodia early Wednesday.

Last week, the vessel’s owner, Florida-based Villa Vie Residences, suspended operations and forced passengers off the ship at the port town of Sihanoukville, saying ‌fuel price increases from the Iran war had made operations prohibitively expensive.

The ship left with no passengers and around 50 crew members, according to Tang Sochetkresna, head of the Sihanoukville tourism department. He said around 100 passengers remained in Cambodia, while the others had largely flown to other destinations in the region.

Passengers unloaded their last belongings from the vessel on Tuesday, and many said they did not expect to return to the ship.

“There are still many things on the ship that I wish I could take with me,” Shirine Thomas, of Wilmington, North Carolina, said shortly after collecting some final items.

Mary Ann Demsar, one of the passengers who was kicked off The Odyssey, told NBC News she is still in Cambodia and watched the ship leave the port overnight Tuesday with other disembarked passengers.

She said that while a group of former passengers is still in Sihanoukville, many have decided to continue their travels to other places in the region. For now, though, she said she is staying put in Southeast Asia, continuing her travels in hopes that The Odyssey sets sail again.

“Everyone is making the best of the situation. If the ship could start up again without the management that got us into this mess in the first place, I would love to be part of that adventure,” she said.

Demsar added that she has heard rumors that the ship is headed to Singapore, where passengers will be allowed to retrieve their things.

Dwight Greene heard the same rumor. He told NBC News that he and his wife, Sheila, left The Odyssey about a month ago when fuel issues started to arise.

“We saw the writing on the wall,” he said.

Now, they are on another cruise, enjoying the region. But they have plans to meet The Odyssey in Singapore to collect the rest of their belongings from their cabin.

Mikael Petterson, founder and CEO of Villa Vie Residences, did not respond to an NBC News request for comment Tuesday about where the ship was heading.

The Villa Vie Odyssey had been cruising the world for at least 755 days as one of only a handful of ships that offered passengers the opportunity to rent or buy long-term or even permanent ​residences on board.

The ship’s final destination was unconfirmed. Some passengers and maritime tracking websites indicated the ship was bound for Singapore. Villa Vie Residences did not respond to emails or phone calls seeking comment.
