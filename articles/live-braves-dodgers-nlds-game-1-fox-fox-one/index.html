LIVE: Braves-Dodgers NLDS Game 1 (FOX/FOX One)

TheTools · 03.10.2026 20:47 · source · permalink

LOS ANGELES -- The last time the Braves and Dodgers met in the postseason, a trip to the World Series was on the line.

The Braves ousted the Dodgers in the 2021 NL Championship Series, going on to win the fourth championship in franchise history. But since then, Atlanta has not made it past the Division Series round, while L.A. is the defending back-to-back champion.

  • NLDS Game 2, presented by Booking.com: Sunday, 8 p.m. ET/5 p.m. PT on FS1/FOX Deportes/FOX One

The Dodgers' three-peat bid will begin in earnest when they take on the Braves in the 2026 NL Division Series starting this Saturday afternoon at Dodger Stadium, with triple-digit heat in the forecast.

  • Dodgers Postseason Tickets are On Sale Now!

During the regular season, the Braves outplayed the Dodgers, winning five of their six meetings and outscoring them 26-15. But the postseason is a different beast, and a rested L.A. club will meet an Atlanta team that had to play hard to eliminate Philadelphia from the best-of-three Wild Card Series.

  • Braves Postseason Tickets are On Sale Now!

When is the game and how can I watch it?

First pitch for Game 1 is set for 4 p.m. ET/1 p.m. PT on Saturday at Dodger Stadium. It will be broadcast on FOX, FOX Deportes and FOX ONE.

Who are the starting pitchers? 

Braves: Dylan Dodd
Key stat: 2.35 ERA over final 34 appearances

Dodd will make his first career postseason appearance while serving as Atlanta’s latest opener. He will attempt to duplicate the success Ray Kerr had when he tossed 3 1/3 scoreless innings while serving as the opener against the Phillies in Game 3 of the NL Wild Card Series on Thursday. Dodd isn’t a traditional left-handed specialist. He limited right-handed hitters to a .564 OPS and left-handed hitters to a .623 OPS this year. 

Dodgers: Tarik Skubal
In somewhat of a surprise move, the Dodgers will be starting Skubal for Game 1 instead of Yoshinobu Yamamoto.

Skubal has a career 2.04 ERA in the playoffs through six starts with the Tigers, including a 14-strikeout performance against the Guardians in the Wild Card Series last year.

Yamamoto had already established himself as a big-game pitcher long before arriving in the Majors, but he took it to another level last postseason when he tossed consecutive complete games in the NLCS and the World Series before closing out the winner-take-all Game 7 on zero days' rest. Yamamoto taking the ball in Game 1 would be the only way to have him available for a potential Game 5 on his usual rest.

How will the bullpens line up after the starter?

Braves: Didier Fuentes might be the only reliever who isn’t available. He pitched three consecutive days during the Wild Card Series. Robert Suarez has surrendered an extra-base hit in each of the three appearances he’s had since his right elbow inflammation forced him to the injured list in June. But his velocity was up and he looked more like himself while throwing two-thirds of an inning Thursday. He’ll likely get some high-leverage spots, especially with Dylan Lee struggling most of the second half and into the postseason.  

Dodgers: L.A. may not have a set closer this postseason, but Tanner Scott, Roki Sasaki and Edwin Díaz all seem to be viable ninth-inning options. Righty reliever Edgardo Henriquez, whose regular season ended early due to a lower back strain, was activated for the NLDS, bolstering the Dodgers' already solid mix of leverage arms.

Any injuries of note?

Braves: RHP Bryce Elder (right knee surgery) and LHP Martín Pérez (lower back discomfort) will not be available during this series. Both could be available if the Braves advance to the NLCS.

Dodgers: World Series hero Will Klein (right elbow discomfort) has faced hitters, but he has not pitched in a game since July 31 and is viewed as more of a depth option. 

Who is hot and who is not?

Braves: Olson, Albies and Harris all homered in Thursday’s win over the Phillies. Harris went 5-for-11 in the Wild Card Series. Baldwin went 2-for-13 and Acuña was 1-for-9. 

Dodgers: In September, Betts (1.112 OPS) and Teoscar Hernández (.965 OPS) were two of the Dodgers' hottest hitters. Tucker (.890 OPS) had his best month by far in his disappointing first season in L.A., perhaps getting hot at the right time.

Freeman dealt with right patellar tendinitis early last month, and he has slugged just .355 since the beginning of July. Ohtani recorded a .666 OPS in his past 29 games, when his biceps and knee injuries flared up and impacted his performance.

Anything else fans might want to know?
• The Dodgers are bidding for the fifth three-peat in World Series history, and the first since the 1998-2000 Yankees. They would be the first National League team to pull off the feat.

• The Braves and Dodgers will be meeting in the postseason for the sixth time. Atlanta was victorious in the 1996 NLDS and the 2021 NLCS. Los Angeles won the 2013 NLDS, 2018 NLDS and 2020 NLCS.
