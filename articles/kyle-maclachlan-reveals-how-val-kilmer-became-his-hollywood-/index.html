  • MacLachlan viewed Kilmer as a "cosmic alternative self" due to their professional overlap in films like Dune, Top Gun, and The Doors.
  • Despite initial doubts about working together, MacLachlan and Kilmer's friendship extended beyond the set, with Kilmer even advising MacLachlan on career decisions.

Kyle MacLachlan has opened up about the unusual path that led to his friendship with Val Kilmer. Long before the actors became close, they repeatedly found themselves circling some of the same major Hollywood roles. In his memoir, Fictional Selves, MacLachlan explains how that professional overlap eventually made Kilmer feel almost like another version of himself.

Kyle MacLachlan recalls his unexpected friendship with Val Kilmer

MacLachlan writes in Fictional Selves that he first heard "slight rumblings" about Val Kilmer while filmmakers considered actors for Paul Atreides in Dune. He ultimately landed the role in David Lynch's 1984 film.

Their careers crossed again when MacLachlan auditioned for Top Gun. He went up for Nick "Goose" Bradshaw, while Kilmer later became one of the film's defining stars as Tom "Iceman" Kazansky.

MacLachlan admitted that Top Gun never felt like a natural fit for him. He recalled realizing director Tony Scott wanted a different type of energy, one that he did not believe he could convincingly deliver.

The pair finally worked together in 1991's The Doors. MacLachlan played keyboardist Ray Manzarek, while Kilmer took on Jim Morrison. That collaboration changed their relationship.

MacLachlan wrote that he felt drawn to Kilmer's mysterious personality and his distance from Hollywood culture. Because they had spent years competing for similar opportunities, he began viewing Kilmer as a kind of "cosmic alternative self."

Their Val Kilmer friendship soon extended beyond the set. When MacLachlan later considered starring in The Trial, he asked Kilmer for advice. Kilmer strongly encouraged him to take the project, which sent MacLachlan to Prague for a difficult six-day-a-week shoot.

MacLachlan later joked that the exhausting production occasionally left him "silently cursing" Kilmer for convincing him to accept it. Despite that memory, his description of Kilmer remains deeply affectionate. He remembered the late actor as creative, well-read, and interested in poetry, music, and painting.

For him, their shared history made the friendship unusual from the start. Dune, Top Gun, and The Doors repeatedly connected their careers before friendship finally connected the men themselves.
