  3. Stewart's talent was clear before he broke through as an actorpublished at 21:16 GMT
    21:16 GMT

    The son of a textile worker mother and a father who was a regimental sergeant major in the British army, Patrick Stewart began his career performing in amateur dramatics in his home town of Mirfield, as well as alongside Brian Blessed in Calder Valley summer theatre workshops.

    His talent as a teenager was clear, according to a local newspaper account - on display at the exhibition - of his last performance before he left for Bristol in 1957 when he was 17.

    "As has now come to be expected, he played his part with considerable ease and was clearly audible," wrote the Mirfield Reporter's theatre critic in a lukewarm review of the local drama club's production of Ronald Jeans' comedy Young Wives' Tale.

    Despite the initial rejections after leaving theatre school, work was soon flowing Sir Patrick's way.

    He landed his first professional role playing Tom Morgan in Treasure Island at Lincoln's Theatre Royal in 1959 before working at the Sheffield Playhouse for 18 months.

  15. From Yorkshire to Hollywood, Stewart held many rolespublished at 20:16 GMT
    20:16 GMT

    Patrick Hewes Stewart was born in Mirfield in the West Riding of Yorkshire on 13 July 1940.

    His mother Gladys was a textile worker. She was active in amateur dramatics and encouraged her three sons to take part.

    His father was a former Sergeant Major in the Parachute Regiment whose experience during the wartime evacuation of Dunkirk had shredded his mental health.

    "My father was a very potent individual," Stewart later recalled, "it was said that when he strode on to the parade ground, birds stopped singing."

    But Alfred Stewart had become a quick-tempered man, suffering from what would now be called post traumatic stress.

    He was prone to drink and often took out his frustrations on his family - although Patrick himself was never the target of his fists.

    Young Patrick's love of drama came at school when one of his teachers introduced him to Shakespeare and encouraged him to perform.

    His first public performance was in a local church production of Cinderella.

    He left school at 15 and eventually got a job as a reporter and obituary writer on the Dewsbury Reporter.

    He also threw himself enthusiastically into the flourishing local theatre. "I used to recite Shakespeare from memory," he said. "I just learned all these big speeches."

    20:12 GMT

    While many actors are typecast, Sir Patrick Stewart moved effortlessly from Shakespeare and Dickens to the command of the USS Enterprise and his famous wheelchair in X-Men.

    In a career that spanned seven decades, Sir Patrick won multiple Emmys and Golden Globes, a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and a knighthood from Queen Elizabeth II.

    One of the world's most versatile actors, he mastered both comedy and tragedy, science-fiction and historical drama - despite an entertainment industry's traditional prejudice against shiny, bald heads.

  17. Stewart 'explored the human condition in all its poetic complexity' - statementpublished at 20:11 GMT
    20:11 GMT

    Patrick Stewart's death was announced in a post shared on his Instagram account:

    “We announce with great sorrow the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer’s Disease and Lewy Body Dementia.

    “He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayers, tears, laughter and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to all of us.

    “We will forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face, an enduring reminder of his invincible grace.”

    “A son of Mirfield in the West Riding of Yorkshire, he travelled from the Royal Shakespeare Company to an equally acclaimed career spanning six decades on Broadway, the silver screen and television.

    “His sonorous voice and singular talent will forever be synonymous with indelible performances in every genre. He explored the human condition in all its poetic complexity and brought hope not only to the far reaches of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, but into science fiction’s most iconic final frontier aboard the Starship Enterprise.

    “His professional accomplishments were humbly matched by his philanthropy and activism on behalf of human rights, survivors of domestic violence, global refugees, veterans’ mental health services, and animal welfare, particularly his cherished foster pitties. He was a Knight, Chancellor, motor sport enthusiast, and lifelong Huddersfield Football fan. He will be remembered for the dignity, compassion and humanity he brought to every role – in his art and life."

    The statement finishes with a quote from his memoir: “Like me, Jean-Luc has lived an unusual life that has taken him far beyond the bounds of normal human experience. But he is also someone who has remained true to his earthbound roots, for they are what gave him his identity. In the end, he feels the call of home, as do I.”
