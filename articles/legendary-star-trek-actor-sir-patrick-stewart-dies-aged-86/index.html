Legendary Star Trek actor Sir Patrick Stewart dies aged 86

TheTools · 30.10.2026 01:51 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Legendary Star Trek and stage actor Sir Patrick Stewart has died aged 86

  • Tributes have poured in, with Sir Ian McKellen describing him as a man "to admire and, in my case, love"

  • Star Trek co-stars have also praised the actor - William Shatner said Stewart "stood for the best of us" while George Takei called him an "irreplaceable legend"

  • Stewart's award-winning career spanned seven decades and, along with his time on screens, it included starring roles on West End and Broadway

  • The Royal Shakespeare Company, where the actor spent his early days on stage, describes him as a "colossus" and fans in Los Angeles are gathering around his star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame

  • Stewart died at his home in Los Angeles on Wednesday, according to a statement shared on his social media

  • His appeal crossed generations, and younger fans would have discovered his work on streaming platforms, writes our culture correspondent Noor Nanji

Legendary Star Trek and stage actor Sir Patrick Stewart has died aged 86

Tributes have poured in, with Sir Ian McKellen describing him as a man "to admire and, in my case, love"

Star Trek co-stars have also praised the actor - William Shatner said Stewart "stood for the best of us" while George Takei called him an "irreplaceable legend"

Stewart's award-winning career spanned seven decades and, along with his time on screens, it included starring roles on West End and Broadway

The Royal Shakespeare Company, where the actor spent his early days on stage, describes him as a "colossus" and fans in Los Angeles are gathering around his star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame

Stewart died at his home in Los Angeles on Wednesday, according to a statement shared on his social media

His appeal crossed generations, and younger fans would have discovered his work on streaming platforms, writes our culture correspondent Noor Nanji

  1. He brought a 'tenderness to his characters', says the Academypublished at 01:41 GMT
    01:41 GMT

    "There was something extraordinary about the way Sir Patrick Stewart could make even the most powerful characters feel profoundly human," the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences wrote in a tribute post on social media.

    The statement from the Academy, which presents The Oscars, said "Sir Patrick Stewart brought a tenderness to his characters that made us feel we knew them".

    "Behind that unmistakable voice was an actor who understood that even the greatest heroes could be uncertain, that strength could exist alongside vulnerability, and that compassion was never a weakness," the post read.

    While Sir Patrick never won an Oscar he took home numerous awards, including a Grammy and two Laurence Olivier Awards.

  2. 'A man to rely on, to admire and in my case, love' - Sir Ian McKellen's tribute to Stewartpublished at 01:03 GMT
    01:03 GMT

    Sir Ian McKellen, one of Britain's greatest actors, has shared a tribute to Patrick Stewart on social media.

    He recalls his "first strong memory" of Stewart as the moment he told him he was considering taking a role in the Star Trek series. McKellen advised against it, warning him to "think of his stage career".

    "Well, he took the risk, and thank goodness for us all that he did..." McKellen later starred alongside Stewart in the X-Men series. The pair also appeared together in a 2009 theatre production of Waiting for Godot.

    The two actors were close friends. Stewart played Professor X, while McKellen portrayed Magneto in the X-Men films, but the pair had known each other since before Stewart's television breakthrough in Star Trek.

    Sir Ian said Sir Patrick “never forsook his origins: and the proudest achievement of the most respected and beloved actor of his generation on either side of the Atlantic, was his appointment as Chancellor of Huddersfield University in Yorkshire”.

    Stewart "told you what he thought and felt," McKellen wrote. "He wore his heart on his sleeve, which made him a man to rely on, to admire and, in my case, love."

    The two frequently expressed their affection for each other during red carpet interviews. McKellen also officiated Stewart's wedding in 2013.

  3. 'I will always and forever be an actor' - Stewart said in Julypublished at 00:44 GMT
    00:44 GMT

    Patrick Stewart said in a recent interview that he had no intention of ever "retiring" from acting, and that he has always loved to be challenged by new work.

    "As any actor my age will tell you, there are fewer and fewer available roles as we get older," he told TV Insider in a July interview, external to mark the 60th anniversary of the Star Trek series.

    "That being said, I still continue to receive interesting offers and I love being challenged by new work, like voicing a pig’s head in Riz Ahmed‘s brilliant new series Bait," he said.

    "I can’t imagine ever officially 'retiring.' I’ll always and forever be an actor."

  5. 'What an actor,' says Ricky Gervais on Stewartpublished at 00:09 GMT
    00:09 GMT

    Tributes have poured in following the death of British actor Patrick Stewart, celebrated for his decades-long Shakespearean stage career and iconic roles in the Star Trek and X-Men franchises.

    Stewart, 86, died at his Los Angeles home on Wednesday, after what his family described as a “brief and poignant journey” with Alzheimer’s disease and dementia. His death was announced Thursday in a statement posted on his Instagram account.

    In a social media post, comedian Ricky Gervais, who guest starred in the television series Extras, describes him as a "legend".

    Sharing a video of the pair giggling in a set of bloopers for the series, the star comedian writes "What an actor."

    "I still can’t believe he agreed to do this scene. And he didn’t even mind when I ruined every take," he adds.

    23:53 GMT 29 October

    Huddersfield Town, Patrick Stewart's football team, has said it is "deeply saddened" by his passing and has praised the late actor as a "lifelong supporter".

    "He always wanted to talk about Huddersfield Town Football Club more than he ever wanted to talk about Star Trek," he says.

    The actress Dame Harriet Walter - who knew Sir Patrick Stewart for more than 40 years - has told BBC Newsnight "the world's going to be very poor without him".

    "I've never heard a bad word against him. And I think the world's going to be very poor without him."

  20. Stewart's talent was clear before he broke through as an actorpublished at 21:16 GMT 29 October
    21:16 GMT 29 October

    The son of a textile worker mother and a father who was a regimental sergeant major in the British army, Patrick Stewart began his career performing in amateur dramatics in his home town of Mirfield, as well as alongside Brian Blessed in Calder Valley summer theatre workshops.

    His talent as a teenager was clear, according to a local newspaper account - on display at the exhibition - of his last performance before he left for Bristol in 1957 when he was 17.

    "As has now come to be expected, he played his part with considerable ease and was clearly audible," wrote the Mirfield Reporter's theatre critic in a lukewarm review of the local drama club's production of Ronald Jeans' comedy Young Wives' Tale.

    Despite the initial rejections after leaving theatre school, work was soon flowing Sir Patrick's way.

    He landed his first professional role playing Tom Morgan in Treasure Island at Lincoln's Theatre Royal in 1959 before working at the Sheffield Playhouse for 18 months.

He landed his first professional role playing Tom Morgan in Treasure Island at Lincoln's Theatre Royal in 1959 before working at the Sheffield Playhouse for 18 months.
