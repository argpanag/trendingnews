Is Monness Wrong to Give Up on Strategy? Wall Street Still Sees $233

TheTools · 20.11.2026 17:32 · source · permalink

@ipopba from Getty Images Pro via Canva

Key Takeaways

  • Monness Crespi downgraded Strategy to Sell from Neutral on Nov. 18 with a $65 target, with the stock already 56% below where it closed a year earlier.
  • The firm’s case rests on bitcoin, Strategy’s leverage, and the dilution from selling stock to buy back its preferred and pay its dividends.
  • Wall Street’s $233 average target has barely moved since Sept. 30. MARA Holdings has been selling bitcoin to cut its debt.
  • Monness has the stronger case until bitcoin climbs back above the $75,452 Strategy has paid per coin on average and its STRC preferred trades at $100 again.

Wall Street’s average price target on Strategy (MSTR) sat 158% above the stock on Nov. 18. That day, Monness Crespi downgraded Strategy to Sell from Neutral and cut its target to $65 from $100, 28% below the $90.32 close. The stock fell another 4.6% to $86.13 on Nov. 19.

MARA Holdings (MARA), another big bitcoin holder, sold bitcoin after the third quarter to repay $750 million of bitcoin-backed debt. Strategy is still selling stock to keep its preferred near par.

Bitcoin Went the Wrong Way for Strategy

On Oct. 29, Strategy’s third-quarter release booked a $20.91 billion gain on its bitcoin, and Founder and Executive Chairman Michael Saylor said, “We’re at the beginning of the next bitcoin bull market.” On Nov. 11, MSCI said it will drop Strategy from its indexes at the Nov. 30 close. By Nov. 16, bitcoin had slid below $65,000, under the $75,452 a coin Strategy had paid on average as of Nov. 15.

Monness Is Betting on More Dilution

This is Gala’s second Sell on the stock. He cut it in April 2025 and moved back to Neutral last November, saying his Sell thesis had played out. Now he’s calling bitcoin, Strategy’s leverage, and the premium the stock carries over its coins. He expects investors to pay less for bitcoin-per-share growth as Strategy leans on new stock to fund STRC buybacks and preferred dividends.

Strategy’s Nov. 16 filing backs that up. From Nov. 9 to 15, it sold $244.7 million of common stock and put $206.3 million of it toward STRC buybacks. Only $33.4 million went into bitcoin, for 485 coins.

Does $233 Still Add Up?

The Street’s average target has fallen from $517 a year earlier to $233 on Nov. 16.

Almost all of that cut came before the latest slide, though. On Sept. 30, the average target was $229.87 with the stock at $153.09. The other targets run from $165 to $435, so Monness’s $65 sits far below even the lowest.

Earnings estimates don’t help much, since Strategy’s profit tracks bitcoin. Analysts expect a loss in 2026 and a profit in 2027.

Each mostly hinges on where bitcoin ends the year.

Monness Has the Better Argument on Strategy

Strategy’s USD reserve held $4.75 billion on Nov. 15 for preferred dividends and interest, and when bitcoin rose 43% in the third quarter, the stock rose 76%. I don’t see $233 working without a bitcoin rebound, and the dilution Monness worries about already shows up in Strategy’s filings: its bitcoin per assumed diluted share fell 3.7% in 2026 through Oct. 25. MARA chose to shrink its bitcoin pile. Strategy’s choice is costing common holders bitcoin per share.

Two things would change my mind, first, bitcoin back above $75,452, and second, STRC trading at $100 without stock-funded buybacks. The next test comes sooner, when MSCI’s removal takes effect at the Nov. 30 close.
