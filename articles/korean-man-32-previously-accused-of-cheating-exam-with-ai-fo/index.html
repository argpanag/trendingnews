Korean man, 32, previously accused of cheating exam with AI, found dead on day he had to prove his skills

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:48 · source · permalink

The man was the first perfect scorer on Korea's Law Education Eligibility Test.

November 20, 2026, 12:51 PM

Top photo from mimiminu_official/Youtube and Canva

A Korean man who became the first person to achieve a perfect score on his national law exam died on the day he was supposed to publicly verify his skills.

Lee Jin Woo was found dead of cardiac arrest in a motel in eastern Seoul on Nov. 19, after he failed to turn up to the verification site, after being embroiled in an AI cheating scandal, The Chosun Daily reported.

The 32-year-old had volunteered to verify his abilities, and he was supposed to show his skills in real time by livestreaming himself solving questions from the College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) Korean Language section.

Suspicions emerged after the top scorer in the 2026 national tax accountant exam had admitted to using AI on Nov. 15, The Straits Times reported.

Cheating scandal

CSAT subjects are graded from one to nine, with one being the highest score.

Questions about Lee's scores emerged after it was revealed that Lee had gotten grades six to eight in his first CSAT in 2013, reported SBS News.

He also got a grade seven in Korean on the 2018 CSAT shortly after he was discharged from the military.

Allegations of AI use erupted after evidence was found of him having shifted written answers onto the LEET test paper, reported The Chosun Daily.

He refuted the claims and pledged to clear his name by livestreaming himself taking the exam.

The livestream brought in more than 400,000 viewers, but Lee failed to turn up to the location at 8am as previously announced, reported SBS News.

Detectives mobilised to locate him

According to The Chosun Daily, which cited sources familiar with the matter, Lee rented a motel room on the evening of Nov. 17 and briefly returned home around 6am on Nov. 18 before disappearing.

After reports were filed expressing concern for Lee's safety, police officers were dispatched at 8:42am on Nov. 19.

40 per cent of Seoul-area detectives were mobilised to find Lee, stated The Chosun Daily.

At around 1:10pm, local authorities found his body, which had already undergone rigor mortis.

A memo torn out of a notebook with about five lines written on it was found in the motel room

It reportedly read: "My body and mind are exhausted."

The note did not contain content admitting to or apologising for his alleged exam cheating, reported The Chosun Daily.

Educational background

According to SBS News, Lee claimed he underwent cognitive changes after experiencing a business failure and losing his smartphone while walking tens of kilometres around Korea.

He was admitted to Korea University's Department of Political Science and International Relations at the age of 30 in 2024, after passing the 2024 CSAT.

Lee took the CSAT again in 2025 to enrol in Seoul National University's dentistry department in 2026. Notably, he only got one question wrong on last year's CSAT.

In 2026, he took the Law Education Eligibility Test (LEET) and scored full marks.

He then published a book on his study methods called "Brain Accelerator" and gave lectures to LEET hopefuls.

Lee had previously been implicated in multiple crimes. In January 2022, he was sentenced to eight months in prison and suspended for two years for intentionally causing an accident and extorting 1 million Korean won (S$969).

During his probation period, he was sentenced to an additional six months in prison for extorting 2.42 million Korean won (S$2,343) from five people using a similar method.

Seoul police said they plan to continue investigations "to confirm the substantive truth" as there is a possibility of Lee having accomplices in the exam cheating.
