‘Stop the delays’: Wong, counterparts blast Israel over Gaza humanitarian crisis

Australia has united with Canada, Italy and the United Kingdom to urge Israel to allow a rapid expansion of aid and essential supplies into Gaza, as cold weather and heavy rains exacerbate an already desperate humanitarian situation.

Images of flooded tent cities and reports of young Palestinian children dying in recent days have sparked renewed international attention on Gaza a year after a supposed ceasefire between Israel and Hamas came into effect.

Foreign Minister Penny Wong and her three counterparts said in a statement on Monday that “the humanitarian situation on the ground is dire” as winter takes hold in Gaza.

“People in Gaza desperately need shelter and humanitarian assistance. Yet 150,000 tents and other vital supplies are being held at the border and prevented from entering Gaza,” the foreign ministers said.

“This situation is entirely reversible – and there is still time to act before the winter fully sets in.”

The foreign ministers called for the Israeli government “to stop the delays now and allow immediate, rapid and unimpeded humanitarian access throughout Gaza”.

“The crossings must be opened, and the tents allowed in,” they said. “Dual-use items necessary for humanitarian operations and early recovery must be granted entry before it is too late.”

The Israeli embassy in Canberra said that more than 85,000 tents and 8000 trucks carrying winter supplies had entered Gaza as part of a winter preparation program.

“More than 820 requests covering tens of thousands of trucks carrying winter-related supplies have been approved, although many have yet to be brought into Gaza by the organisations responsible for their delivery,” the embassy said.

“While Israel facilitates the entry of aid, Hamas continues to loot and divert humanitarian supplies, interfere with deliveries and exploit assistance intended for Gaza’s civilians. This must not be overlooked when assessing the humanitarian situation.”

The fresh international focus on Gaza comes as Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu tries to remain in office despite his chief rival, Gadi Eisenkot, receiving a mandate to try to form a governing coalition following last month’s elections.

The European Union earlier called for Israel to allow more supplies to enter Gaza, declaring that severe flooding and cold weather “are further aggravating the living conditions of an already exhausted population, exacerbating unsafe conditions and human suffering”.

Israel’s foreign ministry responded by labelling the European statement “completely unbalanced” and saying Hamas, which governs much of the strip, must be held responsible for refusing to disarm and cede control of Gaza.

Asmahan Abdalraheem, a 25-year-old Palestinian accounting graduate in Gaza, said: “Winter is coming and we are drowning in tents.”

Three children aged between four and eight were killed with their mother when a dune collapsed on their tent after heavy rainfall in Deir al-Balah, in central Gaza, the United Nations children’s fund UNICEF reported last week.

“Families are living without the most basic conditions for their safety, health and dignity,” UNICEF executive director Catherine Russell said.

“More children could die without proper shelter, working water and sanitation systems, and the equipment needed to restore essential services. The worst of winter is yet to come.”

An estimated 65 per cent of Gaza’s population, about 1.4 million people, live in tents or makeshift shelters, according to the UN’s aid co-ordination office.

Greens leader David Shoebridge said that supposedly “harsh words” from Wong would have no effect in changing Israel’s behaviour.

“Israel has had complete contempt for the escalating language from the Albanese government,” he said.

“We know what actually works is putting pressure on countries like Israel: it is harsh sanctions focused on making a material difference.”

Former Labor cabinet minister Ed Husic said Wong had made an important statement, but stronger action was needed against the Netanyahu government.

Husic said he was stunned to hear from Australian doctors working in Gaza that they were having to reuse syringes and don’t have enough anaesthetic to treat child patients.

The Board of Peace, created by US President Donald Trump, last week called on Israel to allow the entry of essential materials for temporary shelter, water and sanitation infrastructure, waste management, drainage and sewage removal.

The Board of Peace said the threat posed by cold and unsafe conditions represented “an immediate humanitarian emergency”, labelling Israel’s efforts to allow the entry of supplies into Gaza “insufficient” and calling for Hamas to disarm.

An Israeli air strike on Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza last week killed six people and attracted renewed international criticism.

Israel said it was targeting a top Hamas brigade commander who is believed to have survived the strike.
