(WSET) — It’s that highly debated time of year again.

It’s almost time to set your clocks back an hour for daylight saving time.

Some folks want more sunshine after dinner, but others want to see the sun before heading to work.

It’s a trade-off lawmakers face as they consider whether to end the clock change for good.

“Oh, it’s so frustrating. I feel like I have to go to sleep immediately," said one person.

Brighter mornings and darker nights are near.

“And then you get off at five after five, and you get home, and it’s dark. I’m at eight. You ready to go to bed," said another.

The clocks fall back this Sunday, but a proposed federal law says it’s time to stop changing them altogether.

What if we stop changing our clocks?

Permanent daylight saving time would mean brighter evenings but darker winter mornings.

Permanent standard time would mean brighter mornings but earlier sunsets.

The Sunshine Protection Act would make daylight saving time permanent.

“There’s not going to be any way to make everyone happy on this," said Professor of Economics at Virginia Tech Sheryl Ball.

Ball says research isn’t clear-cut.

Some people could benefit from the change.

“I think I definitely prefer, like, waking up when it’s dark outside to have, like, a brighter evening," one person said.

Others could see downsides.

“Definitely a brighter morning, 100%. You know, with blackout curtains. But with the light coming in just even a little bit, it makes it a lot easier to get up and go to work," said another.

Congress has already taken a step toward ending the twice-a-year time change, with the bill now in the Senate. Time is ticking for lawmakers.

For now, the clocks will still change this Sunday.
