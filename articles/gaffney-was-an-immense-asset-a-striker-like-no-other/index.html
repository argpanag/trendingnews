Gaffney was an immense asset - a striker like no other

TheTools · 03.09.2026 11:22 · source · permalink

I was halfway through writing a column about tomorrow's FAI Cup game between St Patrick’s Athletic and Bohemians when an announcement came across my screen that made me change direction.

Rory Gaffney had announced his retirement.

The Pat's-Bohs game can wait. But to summarise - it’s a big game for both sides!

Players are forgotten quickly enough in football and I think when someone like Rory finishes playing it’s important that a few words are written about the player he was, because his contribution to Shamrock Rovers over the last number of years went far beyond a goals column on a stats page.

Watching Rory play at times actually made me jealous.

The biggest reason being that we were probably completely different types of strikers and there wasn’t a chance I was capable of doing some of the things he could do.

I first remember properly playing against him in England in November 2019. Swindon against Walsall in League Two.

We both scored.

Mine was my old reliable - arriving in the box and scoring from close range. Rory’s was completely different. A brave header where he attacked a ball knowing the goalkeeper was coming the opposite direction with fists raised and there was every chance he could have been decapitated in the process.

But strangely enough it wasn’t his goal I remembered most afterwards.

I remember thinking he was everywhere.

I was a striker who wanted to stay high. Play on the shoulder. Keep myself between the width of the posts as much as possible. I wasn’t particularly interested in dropping into midfield and getting involved in general play - the main reason being, I was brutal at it.

Give me as few touches as possible and hopefully one of them ended with a shot.

Rory was a completely different animal.

He would roam into all sorts of areas, his work-rate was incredibly difficult to match and above everything else he was extremely comfortable in possession regardless of where he received the ball.

That became such an important part of Shamrock Rovers’ success when he returned home in 2020.

Rory was never going to be judged as one of those traditional prolific number nines scoring 25 goals every season. He scored 50 goals during his six seasons with Rovers, a very respectable return, but that number almost misses the point of what he brought to Stephen Bradley’s team.

His reliability in possession was probably more important.

Play a difficult ball into him and more often than not it stuck. He could receive it with a defender behind him, protect it, bring someone else into play and allow Rovers to move 20 yards further up the pitch.

He was equally comfortable dropping into midfield to receive possession and effectively becoming another midfielder.

That movement complemented the players around him perfectly along with confusing defenders on not knowing whether to stick or go in after him.

Danny Mandroiu, Johnny Kenny, Darragh Burns, Neil Farrugia and Josh Honohan are just some of the players Rovers have had willing and able to make runs beyond the opposition defence.

When Rory came short those players could go long.

It was a perfect mixture: reliability on the ball through Gaffney, and speed running away through those around him.

I always felt controlling a game became considerably easier for Shamrock Rovers when Rory was leading the line.

That became particularly noticeable in Europe.

European football often involves periods where an Irish side is under pressure and simply needs to keep the football for 30 seconds. Win a foul. Allow the defence to step out. Allow midfielders to get themselves higher up the pitch.

Having a centre-forward capable of receiving a difficult pass with someone climbing all over him can completely change the momentum of a game.

Rory could do that.

We have seen the results Rovers achieved in Europe during his time there and while those nights will naturally be remembered for goals, saves and dramatic moments, players like Gaffney are often the ones allowing a team to function in the first place.

I don’t know Rory personally but everything I have been told about him inside Shamrock Rovers matches the player we watched on the pitch.

Professional. Reliable. Always doing the right things. Always looking after himself and continually putting himself in the best possible physical condition.

The last couple of seasons have unfortunately been disrupted by injuries, something that can eventually catch up with any ageing striker whose game has demanded such enormous physical output.

But he retires with an incredible body of work behind him.

Five league titles with Shamrock Rovers, an FAI Cup, the PFAI Player of the Year award in 2022, promotions in England and Ireland and a career spanning nearly two decades.

Not bad for a striker whose value was never properly explained simply by counting his goals.

Football moves ridiculously quickly.

By this weekend another game will dominate the conversation and by next season new strikers will arrive and supporters will debate their strengths and weaknesses.

That’s why days like yesterday matter.

Rory Gaffney deserves to be remembered as one of the most important players in one of the most successful League of Ireland teams of the modern era.

A striker like no other really.

I certainly couldn’t play like him.

And I hope whatever comes next is every bit as successful as the career he leaves behind.
