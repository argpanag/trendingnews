'Red alert' as volcano erupts in European holiday hotspot and flights halted

TheTools · 26.09.2026 15:25 · source · permalink

Flights have been suspended at Catania Airport as Mount Etna sends a 4km-high volcanic ash cloud into the sky.

Ash is being emitted from Etna’s north-east crater and dispersing southwards, while a violent storm also battered Catania throughout the night.

The airport’s Operational Coordination Unit has closed sector C1 of the airspace and imposed a restriction of zero landings and zero take-offs. A red VONA aviation warning — the highest alert level on a four-point scale — is also in force.

Ten flights had already been diverted before operations were suspended, with five sent to Palermo, three to Trapani and two to Comiso. A Volotea flight between Catania and Verona was also able to depart from Comiso instead.

Earlier restrictions had limited Catania Airport to just four landings per hour, compared with 12 during normal operations, before flights were halted altogether.

Key Events

'All take-offs and landings banned'

Two sections of airspace have reportedly been closed due to ash emitted by Etna, sectors C1 and B3. These are sectors above the volcano, local media reports.

A total ban on all takeoffs and landings was announced by SAC, the company that manages Fontanarossa airport.

Passengers have been told to check the status of their flights with the airline before going to the airport. It has not been announced whether scheduled flights will be diverted to other Sicilian airports.

The ash is coming from the northeast crater, where an eruption of lava is taking place as ash rises 4km into the air above, sparking a red warning from Italy’s National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology.

Second eruption in two weeks

Catania Airport was also partially closed two weeks ago, on Monday September 14, after a similar red alert was issued.

On that occasion SAC, the company that manages the airport, said arrivals would be suspended due to volcanic ash in the atmosphere.

Experts at the Etna Volcano Observatory have issued a red VONA (Volcano Observatory Notice to Aviation) alert for the ash cloud, which reportedly rose 4km into the air.

There was also "frequent explosive activity at the summit craters," the experts warned, with ash clouds, pyroclastic debris and lava at the summit.

Volcanic ash poses huge risk for planes

Boris Behncke, a researcher at the National Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology’s Etna Observatory, said: "Volcanic ash can be a great risk for aircraft.

"It can be sucked into the motors, and force them to stall. This is why everyone is so very cautious with aircraft when there is a volcanic ash emission", reports The Independent.

Floods hit Catania as flights remain grounded

The area has also been hit by a heavy downpour, forcing the rest of the city to a standstill.

Catania Airport is now closed for both inbound and outbound flights due to activity on Mount Etna.

The volcano eruptd several times a year. In 2021, it erupted 11 times. It has already erupted a handful of times in the past several weeks.

Flights now suspended until 12pm Sunday

Catania Airport has announced all flights have been suspended until at least 12pm on Sunday due to Mount Etna's activity.

In a statement, the airport said: "Following volcanic activity and weather conditions, inbound and outbound flight operations at Fontanarossa Airport have been suspended until 12:00 PM tomorrow, Sunday, September 27.

"Passengers are requested to check the status of their flight with their airline before heading to the airport.

"Updates will follow."

Etna a popular tourism hotspot

Mount Etna is popular for holidaymakers and tourists, with many going on to hike the huge volcano.

It rises 3350 metres above sea level and sits along the east coast of Sicily.

For those wanting to hike it, the Italian Civil Serviced advises to be mindful of its adverse territory.

It says: "Etna reaches an altitude of 3350m, therefore hikers must keep in mind that, although the Sicilian climate is particularly mild, going up in altitude one encounters high mountain conditions, with much lower temperatures and frequent gusts of wind. Moreover, the passage from the sea level to the high mountain within a few kilometers, determines sudden meteorological changes due to the currents of humid air coming from the sea which meet with the masses of cold air. For those who intend to make the excursion, it is therefore advisable to dress with hiking shoes, windbreaker and cap to protect themselves from the sun and to bring with them water and food.

"Before hiking it is also essential to find out about the state of activity of the volcano and the risks to which you expose yourself. For the excursions it is therefore advised to turn to the authorized guides, unless you have a good and continuously updated knowledge of the territory and of the dangers connected to the volcanic activity.

"Also, it is necessary to consider that the maps available on the market may not be updated: the frequent eruptive phases in fact change continuously and deeply the morphology of the places, creating new cones and rough lava fields."

Where is Mount Etna?

Etna is one of Europe's most active volcanoes.

It is situated along the east coast of Sicily, spanning 1250km2 and bordered by Nebrodi, Peloritani and the alluvial plain of the river Simento.

Mount Etna in Sicily when it erupted on September 14(Image: Mourad Allili/SIPA/Shutterstock)

The volcano also sits around 45 kilometres south from the Catania Airport.

Airport regularly forced to close due to Mount Etna

It's not the first time activity from the volancao has forced the airport to suspend travel.

Even in recent weeks, Catania Airport has been forced to close. On August 10, all in-bound flights were cancelled for hours after volcanic ash disrupted the airspace.

Another ash cloud earlier this month also grounded flights at the airport.

Restriction on plane landings at airport

According to local news La Sicilia, airport operations has imposed a restriction of four landings per hour at Catania airport.

There are normally 12 arrivals landing every hour when the airprt is fully operating, it adds.

The advisory is in effect due to Mount Etna's recent activity. The Vona (Volcano Observatory Notice for Aviation) issued a red alert, the highest on a scale four, due to the eruption of an ash cloud estimated at four kilometres high.

Flights delayed and cancelled

The live departures board from Catania Airport shows several flights have been delayed or cancelled today.

Meanwhile, arrivals have been cancelled entirely with diversions to Palermo, Trapani and Comiso.

Catania Airport statement

In a statement online, the airport has said: "Following volcanic activity and weather conditions, inbound flight operations at Fontanarossa Airport have been suspended, and temporary limitations on departures are in place until 4:00 PM today, Saturday, September 26.

"Passengers are requested to check the status of their flight with their airline before heading to the airport.

"Updates will follow."

Catania Airport suspends incoming flights due to volcanic ash

In a statement, the company that manages Catania Airport has said "all incoming flight activities are suspended until 4:00PM" after activity was recorded from Mount Etna.

This is "due to the release of volcanic ash into the atmostphere".

According to local reports, the volcanic particles erupted to a height of around four kilometers, as per INGV measurements. The intense emmission was recorded from the northeast crater.

Mount Etna ash cloud forces flights to stay grounded

A four-kilometer-high eruptive cloud from Mount Etna has been recorded in Sicily, prompting a red VONA alert for travel at Catania Airport.

The volcano's activity is affecting Sicilian air traffic, with flights currently being diverted to Palermo, Trapani and Comiso.
