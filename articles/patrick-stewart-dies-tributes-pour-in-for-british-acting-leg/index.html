Patrick Stewart, titan of stage and screen, dies aged 86

Patrick Stewart, the acclaimed British stage and screen actor renowned for both Shakespeare and sci-fi, has died. He was 86.

The Star Trek: the Next Generation and X-Men star died on Wednesday at his home in Los Angeles after “a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia”, said Kelly Bush Novak, his longtime spokesperson, to the Hollywood Reporter.

“He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to us all,” she wrote. “We will forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face, an enduring reminder of his invincible grace.”

LeVar Burton: 'He was family'

LeVar Burton, who played Geordi in Star Trek told the Guardian:

He was family. There is no other context in which to hold him that makes sense. I am lucky to have known and loved so many of the facets of a soul so rich in talent and generous of heart. Of all the dames and knights I’ve known, he was deep down the silliest.

Ian McKellen on his longtime friend

Longtime friend and co-star Ian McKellen has written a touching tribute to his friend, saying that:

Before everything else, Patrick was defined by being born and bred in Yorkshire, the proud county in the north of England which he loved to the end. You could hear it in his accented voice and in the stern honesty with which he told you what he thought and felt. Yorkshiremen have no truck with half-truths or avoidance of big issues. He wore his heart on his sleeve, which made him a man to rely on, to admire and in my case, love.

We first met as ambitious young actors with the Royal Shakespeare Company half a century ago but we weren’t in the same productions. My first strong memory was a few years later, meeting him in the street in central London when he told me about an offer to take over a leading part in ‘Star Trek’.

‘But Patrick think of your stage career in the UK. Do you really want to risk—getting lost in Hollywood for years and years?’. Well, he took the risk and thank goodness for us all that he did for not just his devoted Trekkies but film and theatre audiences, who marvelled at his using international celebrity to star in a string of cinema successes (‘X-Men’ of course) and in great classical plays by Shakespeare, Ibsen, Arthur Miller, Peter Shaffer, Pinter and Beckett at Stratford-upon-Avon, in London’s West End and on Broadway.

Patrick Stewart had a lifelong love for dogs, and in his later years, he and his wife Sunny fostered several rescue dogs — particularly pit bulls, after he fell in love with a neighbour’s pibble Sadie.

He campaigned with the ASPCA against dog fighting and “made it so” Jean-Luc Picard’s dog was a rescued pit bull in Star Trek: Picard, saying they were “the sweetest, most empathic, most sensitive creatures that I’ve ever known”.

In 2021 the ASPCA awarded him its Pit Bull Advocate and Protector award; today the organisation has remembered him as “our longtime friend and devoted animal advocate”.

“We are deeply grateful for Patrick’s friendship, compassion and the meaningful impact he made for animals throughout his life. His dedication to giving a voice to vulnerable animals will continue to inspire us and so many others.”

Actors on their favourite anecdotes of Patrick Stewart to mark his 80th birthday, six years go. Among them: Ian McKellen on when he married Stewart to Sunny, “his mischievous, utterly reliable wife.”

The actor and Stewart’s “Lady Jane” and “Robin Hood: Men in Tights” co-star, Cary Elwes, says:

My heart is seriously heavy today. We have lost the marvelous #PatrickStewart. I first met Patrick on the set of Lady Jane back in 1984 and fell in love with him immediately. Not only was amiable and kind with a great sense of humor, he was incredible to work with. His command of the screen with his sheer presence and that unmistakable voice was a sight to behold. Almost a decade later when Mel and I were discussing who should play King Richard in Men in Tights, I told him it has to be Patrick. Fortunately for us he accepted and once again shared his joy and talent with us all. My now shattered heart goes out to Sunny, his children, friends and fans, of which I will always be one. Good night, sweet Prince.

'Privilege of a lifetime' says Star Trek co-star

Marina Sirtis, who played Deanna in Star Trek: The Next Generation, told the Guardian:

It is impossible for me to imagine the world without Patrick in it. It was the privilege of a lifetime to work with him, and having him as a friend was one of the greatest blessings of my life. My heart goes out to Sunny and the rest of his family.

English actor Malcolm McDowell, writes:

I’ve known Patrick since we were both members of the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1965. He was always a massive talent but had to come to the United States to make his talent appreciated by a larger audience. He was wonderful as Picard, which always slightly amused him. But it also granted him the freedom to do work that he wanted to do. Most memorably would be his Macbeth. He will be missed by all his colleagues and fans around the world. It was a pleasure to have known and worked with him. RIP

Anson Mount, who played Captain Pike in the TV series Star Trek: Strange New Worlds, wrote:

I am mid-flight right now and I have just received the news that Sir Patrick Stewart has left us. Modern technology is both an impressive and an extremely cold thing. Not a close one, mind you, but it was a friendship, an “acquaintanceship” perhaps, that spanned more than half my life.
