At least 30 dead after two trains crash in Spain

TheTools · 28.10.2026 08:50 · source · permalink

Getty

At least 30 people were killed when two high-speed trains crashed in Spain, the second deadly accident in less than a year on the line connecting Madrid to the southern Andalusia region, regional authorities said.

The ⁠Renfe and Iryo trains crashed just after 9am local time (8am Irish time, causing two Iryo wagons to derail, a spokesperson for the Castilla La Mancha region said, adding that the cause was not yet clear.

Footage from regional Castilla-La Mancha Television showed a derailed train with at least one carriage remaining on an elevated section of track while twisted metal, broken panels and wreckage could be seen strewn across an embankment beneath the line.

Miguel, a passenger travelling with several colleagues in the rear car of one of the trains, described to COPE radio the moment of the accident.

"We heard a massive roar, a truly enormous thud that went on for 10 seconds, as if the train were about to derail. Through the right-hand window, we could see a wrecked train - I imagine it must have been the other one," he said.

Castilla-la Mancha regional government said the death toll had risen to 30 from 10 after earlier warning it could rise.

Castilla-La Mancha region spokesperson Esther Padilla said 147 people had been travelling on the Iryo train and 397 on the Renfe train.

The collision comes nine months after an accident between high-speed trains belonging to the same operators on the same line that killed 46 people.

That crash in January, which occurred after a train derailed and smashed into an oncoming train near Adamuz, in Cordoba province, triggered a debate about the level of investment in railway maintenance in recent years.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said he was "shocked" at news of the crash and added in a post on X that he offered his "affection, solidarity, and support to all the victims and their families".
