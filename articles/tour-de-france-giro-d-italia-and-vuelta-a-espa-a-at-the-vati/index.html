Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and Vuelta a España at the Vatican: The Holy Father Will Meet with the Cycling Community - ZENIT - English

TheTools · 19.11.2026 23:19 · source · permalink

The Holy Father will meet with representatives of the world of cycling at the Vatican Photo: ANSA

A representative group of 100 distinguished guests will attend, including legendary figures and current champions of men’s and women’s cycling, including para cycling, as well as institutional figures from various Federations and organisers of the three men’s and women’s Grand Tours (Tour de France, Giro d’Italia and La Vuelta Ciclista a España).

(ZENIT News / Roma, 11.19.2026).- The Holy Father will meet with representatives of the world of cycling at the Vatican, on the occasion  of an event promoted by the Dicastery for Culture and Education in collaboration with the Union  Cycliste Internationale (UCI) on the morning of 23 November 2026.

A representative group of 100 distinguished guests will attend, including legendary figures and  current champions of men’s and women’s cycling, including para cycling, as well as institutional  figures from various Federations and organisers of the three men’s and women’s Grand Tours (Tour  de France, Giro d’Italia and La Vuelta Ciclista a España), together with organisers of other races held  across all five continents.

Among the prominent former and current cyclists attending will be:

  • Eddy Merckx (BEL), winner of five Tour de France titles, five Giro d’Italia titles, one Vuelta a  España title and three UCI Road World Championship titles;
  • Demi Vollering (NED), winner of all three Grand Tours, reigning UCI World Champion and  ranked number one in the UCI World Rankings;
  • Bernard Hinault (FRA), winner of multiple editions of the three Grand Tours, including five  Tour de France titles, and 1980 UCI Road World Champion;
  • Paul Seixas (FRA), a 20-year-old French cyclist considered one of the most promising talents  in international cycling;
  • Jeannie Longo (FRA), five-time UCI Road World Champion and Olympic gold medallist in  1996;
  • Greg LeMond (USA), two-time UCI Road World Champion and the first American to win the  Tour de France, which he won three times;
  • Annemiek van Vleuten (NED), winner of all three Grand Tours, four-time UCI World  Champion in the individual time trial and road race disciplines, and Olympic time trial  champion in 2020;
  • Ricardo Ten Argilés (ESP), multiple UCI Para-cycling World Champion and Paralympic  Champion;
  • Vincenzo Nibali (ITA), winner of all three Grand Tours;
  • Mark Cavendish (GBR), record holder for the most Tour de France stage victories (35) and  2011 UCI Road World Champion;
  • Francesco Moser (ITA), 1977 UCI Road World Champion;
  • Christopher Froome (GBR), winner of all three Grand Tours;
  • Miguel Indurain (ESP), record holder for five consecutive Tour de France victories, 1995 UCI  World Champion in the individual time trial and 1996 Olympic Champion;
  • Elisa Longo Borghini (ITA), fifteen-time Italian Champion, reigning national champion in  both the road race and individual time trial disciplines, and two-time winner of the Giro  d’Italia;
  • Anna Kolyzhuk (UKR), a rider with the UCI Women’s Continental WCC Team, who suffered  a severe spinal injury following a crash in Italy in the summer of 2026;
  • Mariana Pajón (COL), two-time Olympic Champion in BMX Racing and multiple UCI World  Champion.

In view of the event, UCI President, David Lappartient, has highlighted the longstanding historical  roots between cycling and the Catholic Church, from races organised by parish communities and  cycling teams inspired by Gospel values, to the various meetings with Popes throughout the  twentieth century and the recent passage of the Giro d’Italia through the Vatican during the 2025  Jubilee. President Lappartient described the meeting as an opportunity to strengthen cycling’s  commitment, as a sport, to promoting peace and intercultural dialogue among peoples.

Cardinal José Tolentino de Mendonça, Prefect of the Dicastery for Culture and Education, noted that  this meeting follows in the footsteps of Pope Leo XIV’s recent message on sport, contained in the  letter “Life in Abundance” (La vita in abbondanza). Cycling, the Cardinal emphasised, today  represents a cultural experience within our society and a genuine school of values at the service of  humanity.

At the conclusion of the audience, a brief ceremony will take place, during which the UCI will  symbolically donate a number of bicycles to Caritas Rome. This initiative has been organised by  Athletica Vaticana, the Vatican’s multi-sport association, which is also a member of the UCI.
