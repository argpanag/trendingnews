Houthis warn not to fly in Saudi air space

The Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis says it has struck the Red Sea port of Hodeidah ‌to destroy Houthi capabilities, as the militants warn airlines to avoid Saudi airspace, pledging new air strikes within hours.

The developments on Friday, a day after ‌the group struck the tallest skyscraper in Riyadh, signalled a worsening of a conflict that has brought a humanitarian crisis to Yemen and severe disruption to Saudi Arabia, in parallel with the US-led war against Iran.

Hodeidah is the main port bringing food and other supplies into Houthi-controlled northern Yemen. The coalition had earlier warned civilians to evacuate the area.

The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed operational capabilities at the port that were used in smuggling ‌operations and the transport ‌of weapons and ballistic ⁠missiles. The port remains fully operational and the flow of goods will continue, according to Yemen's ​Information Minister.

There was no immediate comment from the Houthis, who had earlier said they would hit Red Sea ports if the Saudis carried out their own threat to strike the Yemeni port.

"We warn all employees, workers, and those in the vicinity of (Saudi Arabia's Red Sea) ports to evacuate them immediately and stay away. We also urge all shipping companies to immediately evacuate their ships from these ⁠ports," Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement.

Airlines should avoid Saudi ‌airspace "because it will ​be the scene of our operations in the coming hours," Saree said, making an exception for airspace near the Muslim holy cities of Mecca and ​Medina. He also ‌said the group would target a power station at Shuqaiq in southern Saudi Arabia.

The Saudi-led coalition also said it carried out ​an operation in Yemen's capital Sanaa targeting ballistic missile and drone capabilities and that operations in Saada and Taiz were ongoing.

Since fighting resumed in Yemen in July in parallel with the wider Iran war, the Houthis have rapidly advanced in Yemen, seizing territory from Yemen's ​internationally ​recognised government and its allies, who are backed by Saudi ​Arabia.

The Saudis have carried out air strikes on Yemen in support of ‌their allies on the ground, while the Houthis have struck Saudi oil targets, airports and office towers.
