Swiss Darts Trophy: Michael van Gerwen's nightmare year goes from bad to worse as he exits tournament while Stephen Bunting is dethroned

TheTools · 11.10.2026 01:43 · source · permalink

Mensur Suljovic celebrates a fourth straight victory over Michael van Gerwen to continue his charge on Swiss soil; you can watch the Grand Slam of Darts in Wolverhampton live on Sky Sports from November 14-22

Saturday 10 October 2026 23:04, UK

Michael van Gerwen's nightmare year went from bad to worse as he was dumped out of the Swiss Darts Trophy in his opening match by Mensur Suljovic 6-4 on Saturday night.

Suljovic led 5-2 before Van Gerwen threatened a comeback but three missed darts to force a decider allowed Suljovic to take advantage.

It was Suljovic's fourth straight win over Van Gerwen and the fifth time in six tournaments that the Dutchman has exited after one match.

Luke Humphries, Gian van Veen and Jonny Clayton all advanced to Finals Day as ten seeds crashed out on a remarkable day of action in Basel.

Newly crowned World Grand Prix champion Humphries was among the headline acts on Day Two at the St. Jakobshalle, where he prevailed against Kevin Doets.

Despite racing 3-0 ahead with 122 and 121 checkouts, Humphries was forced to survive a furious fightback from Doets, defying the Dutchman's 105.64 average to triumph in a breathless decider.

"I was really pleased with that performance," reflected Humphries, who averaged almost 103 and pinned 67% of his attempts at double.

"I felt like I was the better player for most of the game, but Kevin was really strong at the end there, so I had to really work my socks off."

European Champion Van Veen also impressed on Swiss soil, producing a hat-trick of ton-plus checkouts to whitewash a shell-shocked Niko Springer in just under ten minutes.

Clayton, meanwhile, stole the show with an irrepressible display against Maik Kuivenhoven, averaging 108.81 and landing two bullseye finishes to kick off his title bid in style.

The Welshman will take on Rob Cross for a place in the quarter-finals, after the former world champion halted Luke Woodhouse's mid-match fightback to close out a battling 6-4 victory.

There will be a new champion crowned at the St Jakobshalle on Sunday evening, as former champions Stephen Bunting and Martin Schindler crashed out on a day of upsets in Basel.

Swedish star Jeffrey de Graaf put in a clinical display of doubling to account for reigning champion Bunting, while Schindler was swept aside by Tom Bissell, who will feature at Finals Day for the first time in his European Tour career.

Dirk van Duijvenbode recorded a fifth straight win against James Wade to end the left-hander's bid for back-to-back European Tour titles, and a resurgent Michael Smith now awaits the Dutchman in the last 16.

The 2023 world champion delivered one of his best performances of the year to seal a 6-4 against Jermaine Wattimena, converting consecutive 116, 124 and 144 checkouts in the process.

Smith averaged just shy of 103 to cap off a quality display, which also preserves his hopes of qualifying for the Machineseeker European Championship later this month.
