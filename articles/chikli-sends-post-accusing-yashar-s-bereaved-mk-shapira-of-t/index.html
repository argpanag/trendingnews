Chikli sends post accusing Yashar's bereaved MK Shapira of tearing Israel apart | The Jerusalem Post

TheTools · 22.11.2026 18:29 · source · permalink

The now-deleted post, written by social media user Netanel Glesner, accused Shapira of choosing "to stand alongside those who give encouragement to her son's murderers."

Diaspora Minister Amichai Chikli (Likud) defended sending a social media post attacking bereaved Yashar MK Shira Shapira on Sunday, after Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot condemned the comparison between Shapira and her fallen son as crossing a "moral and ethical red line."

Shapira said on Saturday night that the minister had sent her a post accusing her of "tearing apart the nation and siding with haters of Israel." The now-deleted post, written by social media user Netanel Glesner, contrasted Aner's actions during the October 7 attack with his mother's political activity.

"Aner Shapira stood with his own body against absolute evil. With unimaginable courage and extraordinary self-sacrifice, he risked his life to save others from the human monsters who came to murder Jews," Glesner wrote.

He accused Shapira of choosing "to stand alongside those who give encouragement to her son's murderers."

"She is complicit in tearing the country apart from within and endangering its very existence, but she does so with statesmanship, matter-of-factness, and a measured tone. Exactly as she promised from the Knesset podium. Because apparently, it is possible to bring about destruction politely, too," Glasner wrote.

He concluded: "Mother versus son. Wretchedness and irresponsibility on a historic scale, versus courage and self-sacrifice on a historic scale."

Shapira described the accusation as "particularly cruel" given her status as a bereaved mother.

"The least we owe him [her son] is to not forget how to be human beings," she wrote.

Eisenkot: Chickli crossed 'moral and ethical red line'

Eisenkot condemned the comparison, accusing Chikli of using the memory of a fallen son against his mother.

"To pit a bereaved mother against her heroic son, who fell defending the country, and accuse her of 'preferring Israel's enemies over her own brothers' is crossing a moral and ethical red line that cannot be met with silence," Eisenkot wrote on X/Twitter.

"The fact that a minister in the Israeli government chooses to personally send this ugly poison to Shira Shapira, the mother of the late Aner, who gave his life with extraordinary courage to save others, demonstrates a complete loss of direction and a profound disconnect from the most basic values of respect, gratitude, and humanity."

Eisenkot said political disagreements were legitimate but should not come at the expense of bereaved families.

"We can and must engage in political debate, but turning bereaved families into punching bags and making cynical and cruel use of the memory of the fallen is an act of baseness that has no place in the State of Israel," he wrote.

"The State of Israel owes bereaved families far more than this. The very least required of the country's leadership is not to forget how to be human beings."

Chikli: Post was 'too harsh,' but reflected public sentiment

Chikli said he had considerable personal and professional respect for Shapira and had welcomed her entry into politics, but was deeply disturbed by what he described as her willingness to cooperate with Arab political factions.

"I know MK Shira Shapira personally and professionally, and beyond the broader national context, I have tremendous affection and appreciation for her. I was glad when she entered public life," he said.

"Along with this appreciation came very high expectations," Chikli said. "Like many people in Israel, I was filled with sorrow and shock at her decision to join forces with those who support the terrorists who carried out October 7."

Chikli said the exchange followed a controversy over a cover published by the religious weekly Olam Katan, titled "Rais al-Knesset." Shapira had criticized the publication, writing that "whoever chooses to publish such a cover in a religious Shabbat newsletter distributed in synagogues needs to engage in serious soul-searching."

Her response, Chikli said, suggested that she did not fully understand the depth of opposition to political cooperation with the Joint List.

"I considered writing a public response to her, but decided against it," he said.

Instead, Chikli said he had read a column by journalist Kalman Liebskind in Makor Rishon on Saturday that questioned Shapira's political position in light of her son's actions during the October 7 massacre.

Chikli said he later saw Glasner's post and forwarded it to Shapira in a private exchange, making clear he did not intend to share it publicly.

"On Saturday night, I saw a post by Netanel Glesner. It was very harsh, too harsh in my opinion," Chikli said.

"I sent her the post in a private exchange, while emphasizing that I had no intention of sharing it. In my view, the post was excessive, but I think it is important to understand that there is profound shock over her decision to support such a coalition."

"The goal was to reflect the feelings within the national camp," he explained.

"I do not identify with the style of that post, and if I did, I would have shared it myself. The technology exists."
