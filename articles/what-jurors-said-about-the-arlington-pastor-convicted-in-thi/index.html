What jurors said about the Arlington pastor convicted in this fraud case

TheTools · 21.11.2026 01:15 · source · permalink

A jury returns a guilty verdict on wire fraud charges; sentencing is scheduled for March 4, 2027.

A federal jury found an Arlington pastor guilty of orchestrating a real estate and investment fraud scheme that impacted more than 70 victims in the United States, Colombia, Mexico, and the Dominican Republic, according to the U.S. Attorney's Office.

After a four-day trial before U.S. District Judge Mark T. Pittman, the jury found 53-year-old Richard Reinaldo Garcia, a pastor at several churches in Arlington, guilty on Thursday of one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and three counts of wire fraud.

“This defendant didn't just take money from his victims,” said Ryan Raybould, U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of Texas. “He abused personal relationships, exploited the trust people placed in him as a pastor and used false promises to convince victims in the U.S. and abroad to hand over their hard-earned savings. The jury saw through those lies. Today's verdict delivers accountability for those victims and sends a clear message: fraudsters who exploit trust to steal from others will be prosecuted and held responsible.”

According to evidence presented at trial, from at least August 2021 through April 2025, Garcia and others solicited funds from investors for supposed real estate projects involving the purchase and resale of churches, as well as for promoting Christian music concerts.

Garcia promised substantial returns; however, he diverted investor funds to cover personal expenses and his businesses. When victims asked about missing profits, Garcia would often falsely claim those profits had been reinvested in other projects.

“The defendant's scheme betrayed the trust of his congregants and others and used deception to lure them into making investments in his fraudulent projects. This guilty verdict reflects the extent to which the defendant jeopardized the victims' financial security,” said FBI Dallas Special Agent in Charge R. Joseph Rothrock. “The FBI remains committed to working alongside our law enforcement partners to protect the public from fraudsters who seek to enrich only themselves.”

Evidence presented at trial showed that, while carrying out the scheme, Garcia served as a pastor and used his position to encourage congregation members to contribute both money and labor to church-owned real estate projects. The properties were remodeled and later sold for profit, while Garcia solicited additional investments from victims.

Evidence also showed that Garcia controlled several accounts linked to church-related entities, including Ministerio Gracia, Iglesia Gracia de Texas, Gracia Church of Texas, and A Pesar de Todo, LLC.

Garcia instructed victims to wire money to those accounts and record the payments as donations to Gracia Church, even though the funds were intended for investment opportunities connected to the fraudulent scheme.

Prosecutors also presented evidence showing Garcia personally benefited from the fraud. From about 2023 through October 2025, Garcia directed more than $200,000 per year into his personal bank accounts, drove a 2023 Mercedes electric SUV, and lived in a Mansfield property that sold for more than $1.6 million.

According to court documents and evidence presented at trial, victims collectively lost more than $6 million as a result of Garcia’s scheme.

Garcia faces up to 20 years in federal prison on each count. Sentencing is scheduled for March 4, 2027.
