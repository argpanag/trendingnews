Melvin Benn: '"We host the Picnic" ... That's a pretty cool thing to be able to say' - Laois Today

TheTools · 03.09.2026 05:01 · source · permalink
  • Lifestyle
  • Electric Picnic

Electric Picnic director Melvin Benn says that a legacy of the festival being in Laois for more than 20 years is that “for any Stradbally person, wherever they go, they’re able to say, ‘we host the Picnic’.”

“That’s a pretty cool thing to be able to say,” he said when speaking to the media on Sunday afternoon at the festival.

“We work really closely with the local residents. And, you know, the local residents, we give money to the local residents and to the residents committee, et cetera.

“And they spend it as they see fit in that sense.”

When put to him that there was no permanent tangible legacy of an event that brings 80,000 people annually to the area, he didn’t disagree.

“That’s right. That’s absolutely true. And I’ve offered to work with a couple of local people and I’ve said, you know, I’ll match fund what you raise in terms of putting things into public facilities.

“And so far that hasn’t materialised. But I’m not here just to be a donation pot.

“I’m putting the responsibility (back on the residents). They’re not here just to put their hands in a bucket and take.

“But if they raise some money, you know, and there’s been a big discussion around a local community centre, and I’ve said I’ll match fund it.”

With tickets set to go on sale for next year’s festival, he says that the prices will remain unchanged – and that it will remain at 80,000.

He did clarify, however, that the 80,000 is tickets sold – children, performers, crew and staff are on top of that figure.

The particularly Irish feel to this year’s festival – which saw the likes of Amble, Fontaines DC, the Mary Wallopers, the Saw Doctors and Van Morrison all appear on the Main Stage – he says is reflective of the music scene in general at the moment.

“The music line-up has to reflect, you know, what the current sort of popularity of popular music is on the radio stations, for instance, or on Spotify, etc.

“So it’s got a very heavy Irish emphasis this year, because that’s appropriate.

“The Irish acts at the moment are dominating the UK and Ireland, there’s no question about that.

“They’re getting out there into the USA as well, and into Europe. I mean, you know, the Irish acts that are now going into Italy and doing great shows, things like that.

“You know, there’s a huge popularity of the Irish acts at the moment, and that, the Picnic always reflects what the huge popularity of the current time is.

“If that continues to be Irish, then that will continue to be at what’s on the stages. We will always want to be giving new and emerging Irish acts the platform to grow, because the Picnic has done that, there’s no question about that.

“But we don’t choose them just because they’re Irish, we choose them because they’re great.”
