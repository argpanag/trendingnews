Singapore and Malaysia shrouded in toxic haze from months-long Indonesia wildfires

TheTools · 09.10.2026 01:56 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Wildfires have been burning throughout parts of Indonesia for weeks, sending smoke across the country and into neighbouring Malaysia, Singapore and the Philippines

  • This year's wildfire season is one of Indonesia's worst in years, and is primarily caused by illegal slash-and-burn practices used by farmers and companies as a cheap method to clear vegetation

  • Between July and August, Indonesia's Health Ministry recorded over 50,000 cases of respiratory infections linked to land and forest fires.

  • Experts say that a so called "super" El Niño this year has exacerbated drought conditions in parts of Indonesia

  • In Malaysia, the prolonged haze crisis has led to school closures and the cancellation of some outdoor events

  • But Singapore, which has seen days of "unhealthy" air, is proceeding with the F1 Grand Prix, which will take place in the city-state over the next three days

Wildfires have been burning throughout parts of Indonesia for weeks, sending smoke across the country and into neighbouring Malaysia, Singapore and the Philippines

This year's wildfire season is one of Indonesia's worst in years, and is primarily caused by illegal slash-and-burn practices used by farmers and companies as a cheap method to clear vegetation

Between July and August, Indonesia's Health Ministry recorded over 50,000 cases of respiratory infections linked to land and forest fires.

Experts say that a so called "super" El Niño this year has exacerbated drought conditions in parts of Indonesia

In Malaysia, the prolonged haze crisis has led to school closures and the cancellation of some outdoor events

But Singapore, which has seen days of "unhealthy" air, is proceeding with the F1 Grand Prix, which will take place in the city-state over the next three days

Live Reporting

Edited by Gavin Butler with reporting from Osmond Chia in Singapore and Liza Tambunan and BBC Indonesia in Jakarta

  1. Singapore air in the "unhealthy range"published at 02:25 BST
    02:25 BST

    The air in Singapore entered the "unhealthy range" at the start of September for the first time since 2023, Singapore's National Environment Agency said.

    The haze is a result of recent dry conditions in the region and hotspots over parts of Indonesia, the agency said.

    Since then, the local government has issued regular advisories, urging the public to stay hydrated and reduce outdoor activities.

    Schools have been told to reduce prolonged outdoor activities while some have also been supplied with air purifiers to use in the classroom.

    01:43 BST

    Every year Indonesia sees wildfires primarily caused by illegal slash-and-burn practices, which are used by farmers and companies as a cheap method to clear vegetation.

    But recent dry weather caused by a "super El Niño" has exacerbated the problem, particularly in parts of Indonesia's Sumatra and Kalimantan, resulting in a widespread haze that winds have carried across the region.

Every year Indonesia sees wildfires primarily caused by illegal slash-and-burn practices, which are used by farmers and companies as a cheap method to clear vegetation.
