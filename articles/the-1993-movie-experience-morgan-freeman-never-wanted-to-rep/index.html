The 1993 movie experience Morgan Freeman never wanted to repeat

TheTools · 06.09.2026 19:15 · source · permalink

The relationship between an actor and a director is dependent on a rare brand of chemistry.

Ultimately reliant on one another, neither can do their best work unless the other is equally as committed. Given this, it makes sense, then, that many actors, including Olivia Wilde and Clint Eastwood, have sought out opportunities to direct, eventually turning it into fully fledged and decorated careers… For some, however, directing is something they try once and have no desire to revisit – Morgan Freeman is one of those people.

In 1993, Freeman decided to turn his hand to directing with the feature film Bopha!. Set during apartheid in South Africa, the film follows Micah Mangena, a Black policeman working for the racist government, whose son becomes involved with an anti-apartheid movement… As the two find themselves on opposing sides of the political conflict, Micah is forced to confront the growing divide between his duty and his family.

Freeman’s involvement with the film came about in a slightly unusual way. He had originally been approached to play the lead role of Micah, but was not interested. The producers, however, were determined to have him involved. Rather than walking away altogether, Freeman was offered the opportunity to direct the film instead, eventually casting Danny Glover in the role he had turned down.

“I was approached to play the lead role,” he told Interview Magazine. “And was offered the directorial ship as a sop. I didn’t want the lead role, but I thought it’d be a good opportunity for me to try directing.”

It was Freeman’s first and, as it turned out, only feature film as a director. While the experience itself was largely positive, there were certain aspects of the process that left him less than enamoured with the idea of doing it again. “It was a wonderful experience on all fronts,” he recalled. “The one thing that wasn’t quite so wonderful was having to deal with the studio heads sitting and making decisions thousands of miles away.”

For all his frustrations, Freeman found that he actually enjoyed the directing process, though. The problem was that he didn’t enjoy what it demanded of his time. “I enjoy directing, but I’m too lazy for directing. It takes so much time,” he explained. Rather than describing directing as particularly difficult, Freeman saw the problem as being one of sheer commitment. “It’s not hard work. It really isn’t. It’s just time-consuming.”

That became particularly apparent after Bopha! had consumed a year of his life. By committing himself to the film, Freeman had effectively removed himself from other opportunities for an extended period, something he wasn’t particularly keen to repeat.

“Directing is not a career shift I’m trying to make,” he said, concluding, “Having done it, I’ve satisfied myself that I can. If it falls to me to do it, I can. Or if a project comes my way that I want to do myself, I know I can. But I’m basically too lazy to direct. It’s a year out of your life. A year. In a year, I can do any number of things. Two movies and six months’ vacation.”

So, while the experience proved to be a one-off, Freeman had still proved to himself that he could direct. Bopha! had given him a valuable experience behind the camera, and with that, he closed the chapter.
