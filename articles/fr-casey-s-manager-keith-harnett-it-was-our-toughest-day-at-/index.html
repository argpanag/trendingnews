Fr Casey’s manager Keith Harnett: 'It was our toughest day at the office this year by far'

TheTools · 22.11.2026 18:27 · source · permalink

TOUGH DAY: Nemo Rangers manager Robbie O'Dwyer and Fr Casey's manager Keith Harnett shake hands after the match. Pic: ©INPHO/David Ribeiro.

Fr Casey’s manager Keith Harnett was left to reflect on their “toughest day at the office by far” as Nemo Rangers swept them away in a matter of minutes.

The Limerick champions slotted the first point, but Nemo would reel off 3-9 without reply before their next score.

Three quickfire goals, in the sixth, eighth, and 11th minutes, were painful concessions, with the latter seeing full-back Seán Kilbridge sin-binned.

“It was our toughest day at the office this year by far,” said Harnett. “When you come out of Limerick, the standards obviously rise and sure that's evident in inter-county football.

“We knew coming down that it would be a stiffer test and stiffer opposition and they proved it there.

“A couple of things went against us and it was hard to climb back that lead.” After scoring the opening point and trading blows with Nemo for the first five minutes, the game got away from Fr Casey’s in no time.

“We'd focused on having a sharp start and fair play to the boys, we did it. Daniel Daly on the ball there straight away and scored, which was huge. From the line, we couldn't have been happier,” said Harnett.

“But it was 3-something without reply there. There was a black card thrown into the middle of that, so we were down to 14 men and it was hard to counteract.

“We refocused at half-time. We just said to the boys, we’ll try to put up a good performance and initially we did.” They outscored Nemo after the break, 0-10 to 0-7, restoring some respectability to the scoreboard.

“The players themselves knew that they weren't at the pitch of where they should have been,” said Harnett.

“We stepped it up a gear and improved in patches that we were able to compete with them. But again, you'll reflect back to the first half. Them three fast goals, a couple of points, and down to 14 men, it was going to be hard to claw back that kind of a lead.” Harnett insisted the 13-point defeat won’t detract from a year where Fr Casey’s claimed their first Limerick title for two decades.

“It definitely doesn't dampen it. We’d great support here and it was a great year. We got what we wanted. When we set out at the start of the year, it was a county medal.

“A lot of them boys have been chasing it, like John Riordan, Daly, Adrian (Enright). They've been a long couple of years trying to get that and we got that this year, so it's a huge monkey off our backs. Hopefully we'll get back next year and get going again.”
