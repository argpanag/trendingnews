Today we will see Taylor Fritz begin his home-slam bid against teenager Darwin Blanch and Coco Gauff look to continue her hard-court momentum against Zeynep Sonmez, while Alexander Zverev takes his newfound Grand Slam pedigree into a clash with Lorenzo Sonego at the 2026 US Open.

US Open Day 3 Predictions: Today's Best Bets

Coco Gauff vs. Zeynep Sonmez

There's no history between these two, so this is a pure form study, and Gauff's form is the stronger story by some distance.

She is unbeaten in six and recently lifted the Cincinnati title without dropping more than a single set across six matches, and I think that kind of run tells you everything about where her game is heading into a slam she's already won once.

Sonmez has a genuinely good story of her own. the Turkish player has  climbed from outside the world's top 150 to a career-high 48, and her she is coming off from a Wimbledon third round. But her hard-court form this summer has been thin, just three wins from three events, and I believe that gap shows up quickly against someone serving as well as Gauff is right now.

We say: Gauff in straight sets (1/5 with Bet365)

Elena Rybakina vs. Thea Frodin

Frodin is a name worth remembering, a 17-year-old making her slam debut off the back of a Wimbledon Juniors last-16 run. I think her ceiling is real, but this feels a year or two early for the step up.

Rybakina hasn't gone deep at Flushing before, but I believe her broader 2026 season speaks for itself. She's beaten some of the tour's best on the way to an Australian Open crown and her summer hard-court form has held up too, she's reached the last four in Toronto and the last eight in Cincinnati since Wimbledon. She's winning over 81 percent of her service games this year, and I believe a hard-hitting, high-percentage server like that is about as tough a first assignment as New York could have handed an 18-year-old 

We say: Rybakina in straight sets (2/9 with Betfred)

Taylor Fritz vs. Darwin Blanch

Blanch has real weapons, that serve produced ten aces in his last outing even in defeat, so I wouldn't write him off entirely. But he's dropped his last seven matches and is ranked outside the top 240, and I think that run of results is the bigger story here than any single shot in his locker.

Fritz has a genuinely open draw with two of the game's biggest names missing from his side of it, and I believe that's exactly the kind of opportunity a 28-year-old in his position knows not to waste. I don't see Blanch's inexperience holding up for three sets against someone with this much incentive to make an early statement.

We say: Fritz in three (8/15 with Betfred)

Alexander Zverev vs. Lorenzo Sonego

Zverev's head-to-head advantage here is stark, six wins from six with 12 of 14 sets won, and I think that dominance matters even more given how thin Sonego's Flushing history actually is, he's managed only a trio of match wins here across nine trips to New York. That 27 percent clip lags miles behind his form at the other three majors.

Zverev's own year has been transformed by that maiden Roland Garros title, and his run of first-round survival in New York stretches all the way back to his tour debut here. I do think Sonego's ball-striking is capable of nicking a set here, he's shown he can trouble bigger names on his day, but I believe Zverev's calm and his newfound slam pedigree get the job done inside four.

We say: Zverev in four (5/2 with Betfred)
