Intel plunges 20% as weak Q4 forecast overshadows earnings beat

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:43 · source · permalink
  • INTC
    -28.81%

Investing.com — Intel (NASDAQ: INTC) shares plunged 20% Thursday after the chipmaker forecast fourth-quarter earnings and revenue well below Wall Street expectations, overshadowing a stronger-than-expected third-quarter profit and surging demand for data-center processors.

Intel reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $0.52 per share, beating analyst estimates of $0.40. Revenue rose 21% year over year to $16.5 billion, slightly above consensus of $16.48 billion.

The outlook was considerably weaker. Intel forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion, below analyst expectations of $17.08 billion. At the midpoint, the range implies a shortfall of about 9%. Adjusted earnings are expected to reach $0.25 per share, compared with consensus of $0.44.

The guidance comes as Intel faces contrasting trends across its businesses, with strong demand for server CPUs helping offset weakness in the PC market.

"Despite near-term headwinds across the PC industry, demand for our CPUs remains robust," Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan said, adding that the results reflected improving execution, expanded supply and progress in process technology.

Chief Financial Officer David Zinsner said Intel expects PC market weakness to continue through at least the first half of next year.

Revenue from Intel's Client Computing and Physical AI Group fell 5% year over year to $8.1 billion in the third quarter, reflecting the industry slowdown. By contrast, revenue from its Data Center and AI segment surged 78% to $7.3 billion, marking the strongest data-center revenue growth in 15 years, according to Zinsner.

Intel's profitability outlook also disappointed. Adjusted gross margin expanded to 44.1% in the third quarter from 40% a year earlier, but the company expects the metric to decline to 38% in the fourth quarter.

Intel Foundry revenue increased 58% year over year to $6.7 billion. The company generated $5.1 billion in operating cash flow during the quarter, while investors continue to assess whether its manufacturing business can attract enough external customers to support its long-term investment plans.

Updates on foundry customer commitments, manufacturing capacity and Intel's next-generation process roadmap could provide further clues about the company's turnaround when management discusses the results on its earnings call.
