Staff Predictions: Florida State Seminoles vs. Miami (FL)

TheTools · 16.10.2026 12:00 · source · permalink
  • Florida State Football

FSU looks to avoid 14th straight loss in games away from Doak

The Florida State Seminoles (3-3, 1-2) are in North Dade for their annual rivalry game against the 3rd-ranked Miami (FL) Hurricanes at Hard Rock Stadium on Saturday. Kickoff is scheduled for 7:00 p.m., and ESPN will televise the game.

Miami (5-0, 3-0) leads the all-time series 37-33, while FSU holds a 21-19 edge in games played at Miami. The Seminoles are 13-9 in the series since Miami joined the ACC before the 2004 season but have lost the last two games, including a 28-22 loss last season in Tallahassee. FSU’s last victory over UM came in 2023, a 27-20 win at Doak, marking the Noles third win in a row against Miami (FL).

UM’s Mario Cristobal is in his 5th season and has a 40-19 record in Miami and a 102-79 overall career record as a head coach. Miami opened its 2026 ACC slate with three straight road game wins over Stanford, Wake Forest, and Clemson and is one of only three teams with three Power-4 road wins. Miami is coming off a bye week after a 41-13 win over Clemson on the road last time out on Oct. 3.

UM, led by mercenary QB Darian Mensah, who was purchased away from Duke via the transfer portal, has thrown 15 touchdowns with no interceptions. Mark Fletcher Jr. is their rushing leader, averaging 76.75 yards per game with 5 touchdowns, followed by freshman Javian Mallory, who averages 57.8 yards per game and has scored 3 TD’s.

Receiver Malachi Toney, a name Seminole fans will get tired of hearing, is Mensah’s favorite target, catching 39 passes with a 15.49 average and 6 touchdowns. Cooper Barkate, who was part of the Mensah portal purchase package from Duke, is 2nd on the team with 19 receptions with a 12.68 average per game and 4 touchdowns.

Overall, 14 players have caught passes from UM quarterbacks this season, 21 different players have gained 10-or-more scrimmage yards, and 11 different players have 100+ scrimmage yards this year.

Miami’s defense is ranked 1st in points allowed (9.8), 1st against the run (42.6), 5th in total defense (249.6), 69th against the pass, T-25th in red zone defense, and 8th in sacks per game. Former FSU kicker Jake Weinberg is perfect in his 5 field goal attempts.

Florida State will try to avoid its 14th straight loss in games away from Doak after coming off an embarrassing performance at Louisville, when after jumping out to a 14-0 lead, the Cardinals outscored FSU 44-6 the rest of the way to their 44-20 win.

Florida State’s offense is ranked 56th in the nation in total offense, 62nd in scoring, 43rd in rushing, 80th in passing, and 73rd in red-zone offense.

After giving up three touchdown runs of 40-plus yards (97, 44, 68) and allowing Louisville to rack up 537 total yards, FSU’s defense now ranks 122nd in the nation, giving up 410 yards/game, 114th in passing defense (244 yards/game), 100th in third-down percentage allowing opponents to convert 41% of the time on third-down attempts, and T-120th in first-down defense allowing their opponents 129 first downs this season.

Florida State is currently trending as a 21.5-point underdog, with the over/under set at 56.5 points, according to FanDuel.

I’ve been around for a while. I think that’s why Frank stopped by my cabana at the racquet club a few weeks ago and asked me to be a guest picker. I haven’t been around long enough to see us shut out the canes in back-to-back years, like we did in 1963 and 1964. In the old days of athletics, when the coaches got mad at us for lack of effort or discipline or who-knows-what, they’d enact the universal but dreaded punishment of “wind sprints.” For the uninitiated, as punishment, you’re compelled to do a long, full-effort sprint, followed by a short bit of rest, then do it all again. And again. And again. I don’t know what the Canes’ skill players have done to warrant this type of treatment from Tony White, but I think this is what we’re going to see his defense force them into this weekend: wind sprints, several of them, much to our dismay.

You never know exactly what you’re going to get in rivalry games, though. I think this one will be closer than it should be, and whatever they do to us, we just have to remember to pay them back in full, even if it takes more than 20 years.
