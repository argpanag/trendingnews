What we learned from F1's first visit to the Madring

TheTools · 14.09.2026 23:48 · source · permalink

Plenty of talking points emerged as the Madring made its debut on the Formula 1 calendar.

The addition of a new circuit brings with it adrenaline, curiosity and a sense of trepidation as no one quite knows how its debut will play out – and the Madring certainly delivered on all of the above. Here are the biggest lessons we’ve taken away from the Spanish Grand Prix weekend…

How the drivers tackled a new challenge

There are so many factors that can influence the success of a driver’s race, from unpredictable changes in the weather to split-second strategy calls, but for most of the season, they can at least rely on the consistency of track layouts they’ve learned by heart over the years.

They spend time in the simulator until making a corner comes down to muscle memory, and while they were all able to use the same method of preparation for the Madring, nothing compares to racing around it in real life.

The feel of the freshly laid surface, testing just how close to the walls they could get, taking in the indescribable energy of the Spanish capital… they were only able to experience all of this fully when FP1 began on Friday afternoon.

Home favourite Fernando Alonso called it “very technical” and “attractive”, while some drew comparisons to Jeddah’s high speeds and narrow sections. Like the Saudi Arabian track, the Madring quickly showed its bite, with Arvid Lindblad, Lewis Hamilton and Ollie Bearman all caught out in Free Practice.

Once everyone found the initial limits, they began to push them further and further and the lap times didn’t lie, plummeting by over 2s between FP1 and Lando Norris’ magnificent final effort in Qualifying.

Tyre management was king

It’s well documented that the better a driver can manage the tyres, the more likely they are to do well in a race. Extending a stint opens up greater strategic options, while limiting degradation ensures more grip and, consequently, better stability on track.

Of course, that’s easier said than done, as many discovered at the Madring. George Russell explained after Qualifying that “as the track has got faster, I’ve been struggling to get the most out of the tyres”, which he thought was why he couldn’t fight for pole position and wound up sixth in the session.

Moving onto the the Grand Prix, Nico Hulkenberg scored for just the third time this season as Audi continue to close the gap to other midfield teams like Racing Bulls and Alpine, with the German identifying that “managing the tyres and making sure you get to the end” made the difference on a day when the three squads showed “identical pace”.

Someone else who seemed to thrive in the tyre department – at least until a poorly timed Virtual Safety Car unravelled his race – was McLaren’s Norris, who admitted that he was proud of his performance despite missing out on victory.

He said: “The way I drive, the way I can look after the tyres and get lap time out of it, it's giving me a lot of opportunities and I can do a better job than the others.”

La Monumental lived up to its name

Speaking of tyre management, few corners are as punishing on the Pirelli rubber as La Monumental, which – look away now if you hate puns – was undoubtedly a monumental feature of the circuit.

Standing at 550m long, flanked by up to 45,000 passionate fans, and with a banking gradient of up to 24%, this arena-style section was a treat to watch as drivers accelerated through – even if some weren’t particularly happy with the traffic.

The variety of racing lines caused tempers to flare, especially as La Monumental led to a thrilling blind crest, making the courage required to send it around there even more admirable.

Another twist in the title fight

It’s not often that you hear an F1 driver – some of the most ambitious, ruthless athletes in the world – concede that one of their biggest rivals deserved victory more than them, but that’s exactly what happened in Madrid.

Kimi Antonelli clinched his eighth win of the season to extend his advantage in the Drivers’ Championship to a colossal 81 points, and although he celebrated, he also said: “It’s good to win, but it doesn’t feel as good because I think Lando today deserved to win. But overall, you know, we’ll take it.”

It wasn’t exactly a fair review of his performance, which saw the 20-year-old start in second place before he got ahead of Norris during the pit stops. He then inherited the lead from Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc, who gambled on a long opening stint before finally changing his tyres with fewer than 10 laps to go.

Like most races, it was far from straightforward and Antonelli had to resist challenges from both Norris and Max Verstappen in order to reap the reward of his most comfortable position so far in the standings.

Perhaps the biggest revelation came from his team mate Russell, who shared his belief that he’s no longer “in the fight for the drivers’ title” after taking P5 at the Madring. Mercedes boss Toto Wolff didn’t quite agree, emphasising that anything could happen over the remaining rounds, and what looks like “a nice buffer” can rapidly shrink after a couple of crashes or retirements.

With Norris on a fantastic run of form, Ferrari consistently sticking close by, and Russell enjoying minimal pressure on his shoulders, the battle to be crowned the 2026 champion still rages on.
