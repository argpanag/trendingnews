The Boston Red Sox received some encouraging recognition Wednesday when Major League Baseball announced its 2026 Gold Glove Award finalists. Three Boston players made the list, giving the organization something positive to celebrate following another disappointing postseason ending.

But the announcement also raises an interesting question about the direction of the franchise.

Third baseman Caleb Durbin, center fielder Ceddanne Rafaela and right fielder Wilyer Abreu were all named American League finalists at their respective positions. Their recognition reinforces something the Red Sox already understand: Boston has assembled several exceptional defensive players.

The challenge for chief baseball officer Craig Breslow is determining how to improve the offense without sacrificing one of the roster's greatest strengths. That's a more complicated assignment than simply finding another productive hitter.

Three Finalists, Three Different Decisions

Abreu's defensive credentials are particularly impressive. The right fielder has already won consecutive Gold Gloves and is attempting to capture his third straight award. He finished the season with 28 defensive runs saved, leading all major league outfielders, and recorded 13 assists from right field.

Those numbers help explain why Abreu remains such an important part of Boston's future. His ability to prevent runs changes the value he brings to the lineup. A hitter doesn't necessarily need to produce enormous offensive numbers to become an essential contributor when he consistently takes hits away from opponents.

Rafaela presents a similar situation in center field. The 2025 Gold Glove winner recorded 21 defensive runs saved this season and led American League center fielders with eight assists. His combination of range, athleticism and defensive instincts gives Boston a valuable presence at one of baseball's most demanding positions.

Durbin, meanwhile, earned his first Gold Glove finalist selection after recording 13 defensive runs saved at third base. His recognition is particularly interesting because it comes as the Red Sox prepare to evaluate their infield options for 2027.

Boston has several areas it could address this winter, but replacing a dependable defender isn't necessarily the easiest way to improve the roster.

Craig Breslow Can't Ignore the Offensive Problem

The Red Sox didn't see their season end because they lacked defensive talent. Boston's offense struggled to generate consistent production during its American League Wild Card Series defeat against the New York Yankees. The Red Sox were eliminated in two games, leaving another offseason filled with questions about how the organization can take the next step.

Breslow acknowledged during the team's end-of-season discussions that improving the offense would be an important priority.

That's understandable.

The Red Sox need to become more dangerous against quality pitching, particularly in games where scoring opportunities are limited.

However, adding offense becomes more complicated when several existing starters provide exceptional defensive value.

A potential acquisition might offer more power or a higher offensive ceiling, but what happens if that player represents a significant downgrade defensively? Would the additional runs scored outweigh the runs Boston could surrender by weakening its fielding? Those questions aren't always easy to answer.

They become particularly important when a team already has multiple Gold Glove-caliber defenders occupying everyday positions.

Boston's Best Solution Might Not Involve Replacing Anyone

The Red Sox don't necessarily need to choose between excellent defense and a productive offense. Their best path could involve improving the lineup around players such as Abreu, Rafaela and Durbin rather than treating those players as obstacles to offensive improvement.

That approach would allow Boston to preserve the defensive foundation that has already produced encouraging results. It would also place greater responsibility on Breslow to identify upgrades at positions where the organization has more flexibility.

The Red Sox must evaluate potential free agents, trade possibilities and internal development options before deciding how aggressively to reshape the roster. There is also the question of how much additional offense Boston can generate from players already under team control.

Improvement doesn't always require a major acquisition. Sometimes it comes from younger players becoming more consistent, veterans returning to form or a lineup functioning more effectively as a group. Those possibilities deserve consideration alongside the pursuit of outside talent.

Gold Glove Recognition Should Influence Boston's Winter

Individual defensive awards won't erase the frustration of Boston's early postseason exit. The Red Sox still need to improve, and the organization cannot approach another offseason assuming its existing roster will automatically take the next step.

But Wednesday's announcement should serve as an important reminder of what Boston already possesses.

Three players earning Gold Glove finalist recognition is a meaningful achievement. Abreu and Rafaela have established themselves among the league's most accomplished defenders, while Durbin has joined them in receiving recognition for his work at third base.

That defensive foundation provides something Breslow can build around. The challenge is finding enough offensive improvement to complement it.

Boston needs more production when facing the American League's strongest opponents. It also needs to avoid weakening the defense that has helped keep the team competitive.

The Red Sox have spent years searching for the right combination of young talent, established veterans and dependable contributors. This winter, Breslow's most important decisions might not involve determining which players need to leave. They might involve finding the right offensive additions without disrupting what Boston already does exceptionally well.
