Generate $50,000 in Passive Income With the 5 Highest Yielding S&P 500 Stocks

TheTools · 16.10.2026 12:55 · source · permalink

When paychecks stop, a portfolio has to pull its own weight, and the 10-year Treasury now sets a brutally high bar for any dividend stock trying to justify its place in a retirement account.

Retirement changes the math on a portfolio. During the working years, a 10% drop is a paper loss you can wait out. Once the paychecks stop, the question becomes simpler and more urgent: how much cash does this money throw off every year without selling a single share? For many retirees, that number decides whether a nest egg lasts.

The benchmark has become harder. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.27% as of October 6, 2026, so any dividend stock now has to justify its yield against a risk-free alternative. The trade-off is growth. A Treasury coupon stays fixed, while a company that raises its dividend year after year can push its income stream past inflation.

Putting $200,000 into each of five well-known S&P 500 dividend payers, for a total of $1,000,000, would generate about $50,921 of dividend income per year, topping the $50,000 mark. A $200,000 stake in each name would produce the following, ordered from lowest forward yield to highest. Each yield below is the annualized forward dividend divided by the current share price.

Philip Morris

  • Forward Yield: 3.22%
  • Shares for $200,000: 1,006.95
  • Annual Passive Income: ~$6,444

Philip Morris International (NYSE:PM | PM Price Prediction) sells Marlboro outside the U.S. and is building one of the largest smoke-free portfolios in the industry through IQOS heated tobacco, ZYN nicotine pouches and VEEV e-vapor. Revenue rose 10.4% in the second quarter to $11.19 billion, and the FDA granted modified-risk authorization to 20 ZYN variants on June 30, 2026.

Its yield is the lowest in this group because the market prices it for growth. The stock has risen 27.04% year to date to $198.62, and that rally pushes the yield down. Coverage is solid. The quarterly dividend rose to $1.60 from $1.47, and the $6.40 forward payout equals roughly 76% to 77% of raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $8.26 to $8.41.

Kinder Morgan

  • Forward Yield: 3.69%
  • Shares for $200,000: 6,203.47
  • Annual Passive Income: ~$7,382

Kinder Morgan (NYSE:KMI) runs one of North America’s largest natural gas pipeline networks, including Tennessee Gas Pipeline and El Paso Natural Gas. It is a C-corp, which means IRA holders face no UBTI issue. Pipelines earn fee-based revenue under long-term contracts and need little reinvestment once built, so most cash flows directly to shareholders.

Management says it can fund its $9.6 billion backlog “almost completely with our internally generated cash flow while continuing to pay a solid and growing dividend.” In the first half of the year, operating cash flow reached $3.45 billion, while dividends paid totaled $1.315 billion. The $1.19 annualized dividend equals about 87.5% of the original $1.36 adjusted EPS target, and the company is tracking more than 12% above that target. Leverage of 3.6x and a Moody’s upgrade to Baa1 give the balance sheet extra room.

Realty Income

  • Forward Yield: 6.09%
  • Shares for $200,000: 3,739.72
  • Annual Passive Income: ~$12,184

Realty Income (NYSE:O) is the largest net-lease REIT, with more than 15,600 properties and occupancy of 98.8%. Tenants pay taxes, insurance, and maintenance, giving the company regular rent checks. As a REIT, it must distribute at least 90% of its taxable income, and it pays that income monthly.

The ultra-high-yield reading partly reflects a 12.25% slide in the share price over the past month as Treasury yields rose. On the measure that fits a REIT, the payout looks comfortable: the $3.258 forward dividend equals about 73% of 2026 AFFO guidance of $4.44 to $4.45 per share. The company has now raised its dividend for 115 consecutive quarters and carries an ‘A’ rating from Fitch.

Verizon

  • Forward Yield: 6.16%
  • Shares for $200,000: 4,355.40
  • Annual Passive Income: ~$12,326

The largest U.S. wireless carrier, Verizon Communications (NYSE:VZ) now runs a much larger fiber network after closing its Frontier acquisition on January 20, 2026. The yield is high because telecom is capital-intensive, revenue growth is slow, and the company carries $136.5 billion in unsecured debt.

Earnings coverage is the strongest in this group. The $2.83 annualized dividend equals roughly 56% to 57% of raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $4.99 to $5.04. On the July call, CFO Tony Skiadas said, “The dividend is still ironclad for us,” and 2026 marked Verizon’s 20th consecutive year of dividend increases. Verizon also expanded its buyback authorization to up to $4.5 billion.

Altria

  • Forward Yield: 6.29%
  • Shares for $200,000: 2,834.47
  • Annual Passive Income: ~$12,585

Altria (NYSE:MO) is the largest U.S. tobacco company. Marlboro anchors its smokeable division, which brought in $4.66 billion of second-quarter revenue, and Copenhagen, Skoal, and more! make up its oral tobacco business. The yield tops this group because cigarette volumes are in secular decline, and investors demand more income to own a shrinking business that returns most of its cash to shareholders.

Earnings still cover the payout. The company raised its quarterly dividend to $1.11 from $1.06, and the $4.44 forward rate equals about 78% to 79% of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.61 to $5.72. Investors should watch for erosion: second-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.48 missed expectations, and Marlboro’s retail share slipped to 39.5%. Altria returned about $3.9 billion to shareholders in the first half and has $665 million left on its buyback.

Five Positions, One Income Stream Above $50,000

Together, these five positions would generate $50,921 in annual passive income from a $1,000,000 investment, with a combined yield of 5.09%. Altria contributes $12,585, Verizon adds $12,326, Realty Income provides $12,184, Kinder Morgan supplies $7,382, and Philip Morris rounds out the group with $6,444.

The payment calendar matters as much as the total. Realty Income pays every month, and the four quarterly payers spread their checks across different weeks, so cash arrives on a regular schedule that maps neatly onto monthly living expenses. That consistency allows a retiree to budget around dividends rather than market timing, and all five companies have raised their payouts within the past year.
