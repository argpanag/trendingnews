Jazz look to end 12-game skid against surging Clippers

TheTools · 18.11.2026 03:26 · source · permalink

Darius Garland paces Los Angeles after taking three of four, while Utah pursues its first road victory of the season minus key starters Lauri Markkanen and Jaren Jackson Jr.

The Utah Jazz face a stiff challenge to snap their 12-game losing streak on Tuesday night when they visit the Los Angeles Clippers in Inglewood, Calif.

Los Angeles is coming off a 113-105 home win over the Charlotte Hornets on Sunday and has won three of four games since starting the season at 1-7. The Clippers have won back-to-back games, including a victory over the Oklahoma City Thunder on Friday, for the first time this year.

"I think the Oklahoma City win was a big confidence booster for us," Clippers guard Darius Garland said. "So, coming into this game (against Charlotte) and finishing it pretty well, I think that was another step over to some good success."

Garland finished with 27 points and seven assists, and will be relied on again as the Clippers seek to continue their first winning streak of the season while trying to extend Utah's six-game road losing streak.

"The team we have, everybody is pulling for each other," Clippers coach Tyronn Lue said. "When our starters don't have it, when they don't have a lot of pop, we can bring our guys off the bench and they got a lot of energy, play the right way, enjoy playing together."

That was apparent Sunday when the Clippers rallied out of a 13-point hole to win.

"We ended up getting some stops after the timeout that T-Lue called," Garland said. "(We) got some stops, got out in transition against some easy buckets and that's what started getting us going."

Utah is hoping to pick up its first win away from the Delta Center this season, but its roster situation isn't helping. The Jazz are missing key starters Lauri Markkanen and Jaren Jackson Jr.

"It's my responsibility to get the guys playing the right way. We are seeking some more consistency," Jazz

coach Will Hardy said. "We need more consistency in a lot of different areas. We got a lot of young players who are taking the court, and the thing that I like is I think their competitiveness has been really hard, and that's where it's all going to start."

Though many players are getting more time than usual, being forced to play their youthful players like rookie Darryn Peterson and second-year Ace Bailey along with reserves from the end of the bench to fill

minutes has led to inconsistent and sloppy play at times.

"We've had some ball security issues at the end of the game, and I think those things have got to turn around," Hardy said. "We've got to dig deep at the end of the game and not get into fatigue, whether that's physical fatigue, emotional fatigue, mental fatigue, but we've got to fight through because that's obviously when you feel pressure and that's when the physicality can go up."

Veteran Mo Bamba has been a pleasant surprise for the Jazz as of late. He scored 26 points, including going 6-for-6 from 3-point range, in his first start on Sunday against San Antonio.

"Mo and I have talked previously about some opportunities for him to pick and pop and shoot as we move forward in the season, and (Sunday) was one of those," Hardy said. "We wouldn't expect that level every night, but I think the space he gave us was great."
