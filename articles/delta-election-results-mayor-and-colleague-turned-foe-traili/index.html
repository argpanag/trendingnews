The race for Delta mayor was one of the big question marks Saturday night.

Would two-term mayor George Harvie prevail, or would his former colleague Dylan Kruger upset him?

There was another possibly — that they’d split their vote, allowing a third candidate, Melissa Granum, to claim a victory.

There was quite a buzz at the Kruger party, which was quite posh and held in the ballroom of the Cascades Casino in Delta. It filled up just after 8 p.m.

But the first result coming in was a shocker — Granum had leapt to an early lead, with 1,414 votes (56 per cent) as opposed to 630 (25 per cent) for Kruger and 460 for Harvie.

The second result was closer — Granum led with 3,861 (47 per cent) to 2,592 for Kruger (31 per cent). Harvie looked in trouble, a distant third with 1,752 (21 per cent).

The Delta website said the early results were from the advance and mail in votes. But Granum continued to lead as more results came in, with 50 per cent at 9 p.m.

The story in Delta in recent years has been the split between two-term mayor Harvie and Kruger, who was elected as a part of Harvie’s Achieving For Delta party.

Kruger had once been seen as Harvie’s probable heir, but in 2024 Kruger led a faction of four councillors from within Harvie’s own party that voted to strip Harvie of his role on Metro Vancouver’s board, where he had been chair.

Harvie responded by kicking the four councillors out of the party.

They both ran for mayor this year, as the heads of two parties: Harvie with Achieving For Delta, Kruger with One Delta.

Granum led a third party, Delta First, decrying the “dysfunction at the council table” caused by Harvie and Kruger’s split.

There are several big issues in Delta that the incoming mayor and council will have to deal with, including the type and pace of development, the replacement tunnel or bridge for the Massey Tunnel, and negotiating with the federal and provincial governments over the Pacific Link oil pipeline that will pass through Delta.
