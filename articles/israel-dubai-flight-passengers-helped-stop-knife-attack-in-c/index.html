  3. Young men stormed cockpit and subdued attacker, passenger tells Israeli mediapublished at 14:04 BST
    14:04 BST

    A passenger on the flight has told Israeli newspaper Haaretz that the plane lost control and began to "dive" about an hour before the scheduled landing.

    Dalia Shpigel told the paper that "suddenly there were terrible screams throughout the plane" before "three to five guys burst into the cockpit" and subdued the attacker and began treating the injured pilot.

    "We realised that the situation was stabilising and continued the flight. Gradually, information began to emerge about what had happened in the cockpit," Shpigel said.

    She said the "young men" then took charge and began tending to passengers as the plane altered its course to land in Saudi Arabia.

    "Right now, we're all here in the terminal, and medical teams are examining us," she added.

  4. Incident comes at febrile time in Israel as elections approachpublished at 13:52 BST
    13:52 BST

    Sebastian Usher
    Global affairs correspondent

    The incident on the Flydubai flight comes at an even more febrile time than usual in Israel, with tightly contested elections just weeks away.

    Whatever the facts turn out to be, it's bound to be used on all sides in the campaign.

    The far-right Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir has, as expected, fired the first salvo, denouncing it as an attempted act of terrorism that shows that Israel must fight until all its enemies are defeated.

    Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is likely to send the same message. On Tuesday, he posted a video from an airbase warning that there were indications that Israel's enemies would mount an attack ahead of the elections.

    One of his main opposition rivals, Yair Lapid has moved quickly to say that nothing in what Netanyahu said remotely resembled what has just happened. Another party leader, Avigdor Liebrman, has accused the government's supporters of trying to turn the incident into "political capital".

    And in the midst of this, the air route between Israel and the UAE - which has been one of the fruits of Emiratis signing up to the Abraham Accords - has been put in doubt.

    Transportation minister Miri Regev is reported to have demanded that all Flydubai flights should be suspended.

    13:43 BST

    The diverted Israel-bound Flydubai airplane is one of the airline's 10 daily flight flights from Dubai to Tel Aviv.

    The airline's first flights depart Dubai at 05:15 local time (02:00 BST), with the last one setting off at 19:15.

    Ten flights also depart from Tel Aviv headed to Dubai each day, with the last one leaving Israel at 22:50 local time (20:50 BST).

    The majority of direct flights on this route are chartered by Flydubai and scheduled to take three hours and 25 minutes. Other airlines, such as Arkia and Israir, also have flights on the route but they are not as frequent.

    Israel and the United Arab Emirates (UAE) established diplomatic relations in 2020 under a US-brokered deal known as the Abraham Accords. The deal resulted in the normalisation of relations between Israel and the UAE. Saudi Arabian airspace was blocked to Israeli air traffic before the deal.

  10. Plane’s sudden altitude drop likely not accidental, expert sayspublished at 13:03 BST
    13:03 BST

    By Joshua Cheetham

    BBC Verify has shared flight-tracking data with aviation experts to better understand what may have happened onboard the Flydubai plane, after it experienced an extreme fall and then rapid fluctuation in altitude in such a short period of time.

    The data on Flightradar24 shows the aircraft initially plunged by about 17,000ft (5,200m), climbed 5,000ft and then fell another 7,000ft to settle into a relatively stable altitude of 15,000ft. All of this took place over the course of less than five minutes.

    Marco Chan, a senior lecturer in aviation operations at Kingston University, says that while sudden drops in altitude can be caused by incidents like rapid decompression, the plane having transmitted an emergency code for “unlawful interference” suggests the drop was not accidental or down to a technical fault.

    While it is still unclear what happened on board, Chan adds that the sudden changes in altitude may have been caused by the pilots jostling for control of the yoke - a wheel-like device that controls the plane’s steering.

  18. Plane plummeted by 17,000ft in two minutes, data showspublished at 10:47 BST
    10:47 BST

    By Paul Brown

    The flight bound for Tel Aviv dropped extremely rapidly over Saudi Arabia just over two hours after take-off from Dubai, flight-tracking data from Flightradar24 examined by BBC Verify shows.

    Just before 05:21 UTC (06:21 BST; 08:21 local time) the plane was cruising at a recorded altitude of 34,000 feet (10,400m).

    In less than two minutes it had descended more than 17,000 feet (5,200m).

    An emergency signal was issued from the plane about 10 minutes later and the available data shows significant ongoing fluctuations in altitude throughout the rest of the flight to Tabuk airport in Saudi Arabia.

Captain and first officer injured, says Saudi airportpublished at 11:49 BST
11:49 BSTBreaking

The company that owns Tabuk airport in Saudi Arabia - where the plane was diverted - has just put out a statement.

It says both the captain and first officer on the Flydubai plane sustained injuries and were taken to the hospital. It does not state the cause of the injuries.

The airport's air traffic control centre received a distress call from the Flydubai plane at 08:44 local time, the statement says, during which the plane requested an emergency landing

The aircaft landed safely around an hour after, the statement adds.

We're getting reports that a recovery flight is on its way to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, to pick up passengers from the diverted Israel-bound flight.

Plane plummeted by 17,000ft in two minutes, data showspublished at 10:47 BST
10:47 BST

By Paul Brown

The flight bound for Tel Aviv dropped extremely rapidly over Saudi Arabia just over two hours after take-off from Dubai, flight-tracking data from Flightradar24 examined by BBC Verify shows.

Just before 05:21 UTC (06:21 BST; 08:21 local time) the plane was cruising at a recorded altitude of 34,000 feet (10,400m).

In less than two minutes it had descended more than 17,000 feet (5,200m).

An emergency signal was issued from the plane about 10 minutes later and the available data shows significant ongoing fluctuations in altitude throughout the rest of the flight to Tabuk airport in Saudi Arabia.
