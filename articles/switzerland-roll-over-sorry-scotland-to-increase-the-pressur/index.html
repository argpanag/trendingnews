Switzerland roll over sorry Scotland to increase the pressure on Pocognoli

TheTools · 16.11.2026 21:53 · source · permalink

A match that should have been about 103 was instead about four. Andy Robertson is due acclaim for becoming the most capped player in Scotland’s history but how the full-back will wish the moment had arrived in different circumstances.

A second-string Switzerland team pulled Scotland apart, raising further and significant questions about the suitability of Sébastien Pocognoli to carry out the job his paymasters boldly entrusted him with. Scottish FA giddiness about Pocognoli as the successor to Steve Clarke, a hugely successful leader of the national team, is looking increasingly ridiculous. “I don’t think you’ve got time in international football,” said Robertson as dust settled here. Ouch.

This was a horrible night for Pocognoli, who is under serious pressure just three months into his tenure. Scotland’s players looked a disillusioned and disorganised lot. Pocognoli has never previously managed in international football, which renders it reasonable to ask his qualifications to improve this grim scenario. Scotland have finished a distant third in a favourable Nations League section. Ramifications exist for the Euro 2028 qualifying draw in December. Pocognoli is taking a pounding in the court of public opinion.

“The score is painful,” said Pocognoli. “I see a lot of good things in the way that we try to play. The result is heavy but the players gave everything. They enjoy playing like this.” An eye test suggests otherwise. Scotland are overly generous in defence, lame in midfield and powder-puff in attack. It is quite the mix.

Pocognoli’s demeanour during pre-match media duties suggested he was irritated by the early questioning of his approach. The Belgian is unwilling to deviate from the deployment of three centre-backs whether Scotland are at home to North Macedonia or away to Switzerland. While the prospect of facing a highly impressive Swiss team would ordinarily make Scots gulp, there was cause for hope in St Gallen. Not only had Switzerland already won Group B1 but they were in makeshift form.

Switzerland’s lack of cohesion was apparent during an opening spell where the visitors looked decent. A flurry of set pieces and a John McGinn half volley that dipped over Yvon Mvogo’s crossbar emphasised Scottish attacking intent. On a difficult pitch, Switzerland’s first 20 minutes lacked purpose. As good as things were to get for Scotland, alas.

There appeared no real danger as Zachary Athekame fired in a hopeful cross from the Swiss right. Scotland had an abundance of cover inside their own penalty area. The problem was that one of those charged with clearing lines, Lewis Ferguson, instead poked the ball past Angus Gunn. No own goals are pretty; this one was especially ugly. Ferguson had needlessly panicked.

Scotland attempted an immediate response as Aaron Hickey headed wide. The Brentford man had to be withdrawn before the interval, apparently damaging his left hamstring when making a defensive intervention.

Hickey, whose career has been seriously disrupted by injury, was understandably distraught. Although Switzerland were by now in control, McGinn spurned a glorious opportunity five minutes before the interval when heading straight at Mvogo. Adrian Bajrami spared McGinn’s blushes with an even worse miss at the opposite end before the teams headed for their orange wedges. The half had been humdrum in nature.

Ferguson shanking the ball out for a Switzerland corner set the tone for a spell where the game was put firmly out of Scotland’s reach. Samuel Ballet burst forward from midfield before playing a one-two with Remo Freuler and slamming towards the Scotland net. The ball was heading in before Winsley Boteli made certain, with the video assistant referee dismissing Scotland’s appeals for offside. The ease with which Ballet evaded those in navy blue was as striking as the space Freuler found himself in. Scotland’s structure, once a staple, was nonexistent. Five defenders and two deep lying midfielders were being gleefully ignored by the rampaging Swiss.

Number three was another Scotland horror show. Nathan Patterson was dispossessed by Boteli. The forward found Alvyn Sanches, who angled a shot across Gunn. So, so easy. Gunn’s positioning was questionable.

Scott McTominay, again peripheral, slammed over when it looked easier to notch a Scotland consolation. Scottish fans jeered after one sideways pass too many in defence. Many headed for the exit as Zeki Amdouni’s shot deflected off Luke Graham and beyond Gunn.

A year ago, Scotland were celebrating World Cup qualification on the most epic of Hampden Park nights as Denmark were vanquished. It is so difficult to square that occasion with 90 minutes in St Gallen. Scotland look a team in ghastly regression.
