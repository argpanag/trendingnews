Phillies vs. Braves NL Wild Card Series Game 3 FAQ, lineups (8 ET, NBC/Peacock)

TheTools · 02.10.2026 01:26 · source · permalink

ATLANTA – After the Braves stunned the Phillies with their come-from-behind win in Game 1 of the National League Wild Card Series, Philadelphia returned the favor in Game 2.

In a series that is shaping up to be another instant classic between the bitter NL East rivals, it now all comes down to Game 3 on Thursday. A Phillies win would set up an NLDS rematch from last year against the Dodgers, while the Braves hope to punch their ticket to the NLDS for the first time since 2023.

“Just win a game,” said Phillies infielder Alec Bohm, who hit the game-winning homer in the Phillies’ 4-3 extra-inning victory in Game 2. “ … I think as much as we want to enjoy this for a little bit, it's the same as we did [after Game 1]: Turn the page, and move on to tomorrow and show up ready to do the same thing.”

So, what could the Phillies and Braves possibly have in store for their winner-take-all showdown?

While that’s the biggest question looming over the National League Wild Card Series, it’s far from the only one. Here’s everything you need to know about Game 3:

When is the game and how can I watch it?

The game will begin at 8 p.m. ET and air on NBC and Peacock.

The Wild Card Series will be available in Spanish on Universo, while Univision Radio will provide Spanish language audio coverage.  ESPN Radio will provide live national coverage of all 2026 MLB postseason games, beginning with the Wild Card Series.

Who are the starting pitchers? 

Phillies: RHP Aaron Nola

The Phillies will start Aaron Nola in Game 3. Nola had a 4.50 ERA in three starts against Atlanta this season.

It wouldn’t be a surprise to see ace Zack Wheeler enter the game in relief.

Left-hander Jesús Luzardo, who started Game 1, and right-hander Andrew Painter, who pitched three innings in relief in Game 2, both said they would be available, too.

Braves: LHP Ray Kerr 

Grant Holmes will attempt to handle the bulk of the innings, but the Braves are going with southpaw Ray Kerr as the opener. He'll be tasked with handling Bryce Harper and Kyle Schwarber in the first inning. Kerr posted a 1.47 ERA over 18 1/3 innings in nine appearances going back to his Aug. 17 season debut. Left-handed hitters have gone 4-for-23 (.174) against him with one extra-base hit, a double.

How will the bullpens line up after the starter?

Phillies: Mattingly said it’s “all hands on deck,” although closer Jhoan Duran wasn’t available in Game 2. Mattingly said he hoped he’d have Duran in Game 3. But other than Cristopher Sánchez, who started Game 2, probably every pitcher on the Phillies’ staff will be available in some capacity in Game 3. That’s everybody from Wheeler to Luzardo to Painter.

Braves: Closer Raisel Iglesias and his top setup men Didier Fuentes and Dylan Lee both pitched in the first two games of this series. Each should be available in this winner-take-all game. Robert Suarez hasn’t been utilized yet. He missed three months with an elbow ailment before making two appearances last week. But manager Walt Weiss may have no other choice but to turn to the right-hander, who posted a 0.56 ERA in 31 appearances before his elbow became a problem in June.

Any injuries of note?

Phillies: Luis Arraez is not on the roster for this series after badly spraining his left ankle on Thursday. 

Braves: OF Lane Thomas could be available during the NLDS. Starting pitchers Bryce Elder (arthroscopic knee surgery), Reynaldo López (right shoulder inflammation) and Martín Pérez (lower back discomfort) may also be available in future rounds.  

Who is hot and who is not?

Phillies: Home runs often come in bunches for Schwarber and Harper. Did their back-to-back homers in the eighth inning in Game 2 get them going? Bohm is 3-for-8 with one double and one home run in the series. After struggling all season, Realmuto has two hits and two RBIs this series.

Braves: Harris is 4-for-8 through the first two games and Dubón is 3-for-9. Acuña is 0-for-6. Austin Riley’s game-winning home run in Game 1 stands as Atlanta’s only homer thus far.

Anything else fans might want to know?

The Phillies are 1-3 in franchise history in win-or-go-home postseason games. They won Game 5 of the 1980 NLCS. They lost Game 7 of the 2023 NLCS, Game 5 of the 2011 NLDS and Game 5 of the 1981 NLDS.
