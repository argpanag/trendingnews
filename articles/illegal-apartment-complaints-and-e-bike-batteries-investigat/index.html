Illegal apartment complaints and e-bike batteries investigated after Brooklyn fire kills 6

TheTools · 06.11.2026 10:04 · source · permalink

SUNSET PARK, Brooklyn (WABC) -- A search of city building records following the fatal fire in Sunset Park found multiple complaints about alleged illegal construction at the home, including claims that the basement had been converted into an apartment and that units on the first and second floors had been divided into three apartments each.

According to the records, inspectors visited the building several times but closed the investigations after they were unable to gain access.

Fire marshals continued Friday to investigate the cause of the blaze as questions mounted about the property's history and whether enforcement efforts could have prevented the tragedy.

Records showed inspectors visited the building in September and October 2025 and again in February and August 2026, but the cases were closed because no one answered the door. Officials said inspectors are not allowed to force entry.

"After multiple unsuccessful inspection attempts," said a department spokesman, "we closed out the complaints... our inspectors do not have the legal authority to use physical force."

Firefighters arrived at the home in about three minutes and encountered heavy fire conditions, with flames visible from every window. At least 11 people lived in the house, and several residents were trapped. Six people died in the fire.

Neighbor Tim DeGaray said the loss has shaken the community.

"Of course it's tragic. I feel sorry for the people that we lost."

Investigators were also examining whether lithium-ion batteries may have played a role in the fire. What appeared to be a scooter and possibly an e-bike were removed from the debris Thursday. It remained unclear Friday whether their batteries sparked the fire or contributed to its intensity.

Eight people have been killed in lithium-ion battery fires in New York City this year, not including the Sunset Park fire. Despite legislation approved by the City Council, concerns remain about enforcement and safety measures.

City Council Speaker Julie Menin has called an emergency hearing later this month.

"We need to find out how much are the agencies doing. Are there gaps in enforcement that need to be improved upon? So the point of the hearing—and it's really an emergency hearing—is to get some answers and to really look at what else needs to be done."

Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the administration continues to consider additional steps to address lithium-ion battery risks.

"It continues to be a major source of concern and a priority of focus for our administration, whether it's exploring additional legislation or it's exploring additional policy that we can make of the administrative angle. This is something that we're talking about every single day."

Fire marshals were continuing their investigation Friday as officials worked to determine the cause of the blaze.
