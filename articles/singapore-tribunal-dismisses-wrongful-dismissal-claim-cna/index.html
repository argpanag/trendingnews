Singapore tribunal dismisses wrongful dismissal claim — CNA

TheTools · 30.09.2026 10:49 · source · permalink

Singapore’s employment tribunal dismissed a former employee’s claim of wrongful dismissal after she failed to meet the requirements of a performance improvement plan. The woman worked as a senior social media specialist, and after completing her probation period, her duties were expanded to include creative functions.

As Channel NewsAsia reports, the woman began working at the company on January 8, 2024, and completed a two-month probation period. In March of that year, she agreed to take on additional creative tasks as part of a broader company restructuring. At the same time, the employee said that she had agreed on the condition that she would receive proper training and mentorship.

Performance improvement plan

In November 2025, the employee was placed on an approximately three-month performance improvement plan. The document identified four areas for improvement: creative ideation and video production, production and task execution, photography and styling, and content planning. The first three areas concerned her new creative duties.

In March 2026, the company assessed that the employee had failed to meet the plan’s requirements. She was offered the option of considering a transfer to another position or resigning; if she declined both options, she was to be dismissed. At the end of March, the woman submitted her resignation, and the company agreed to shorten her notice period so that she could start a new job.

Tribunal’s findings

In June 2026, the former employee filed a claim with the tribunal, initially seeking S$20,000 in compensation and later reducing the amount to S$4,000, the equivalent of her monthly salary. She said she had been dismissed because of difficulties in performing creative functions that she allegedly had not been assigned under her employment contract.

Tribunal magistrate Kevin Ho Hin Tat found that the woman had not resigned voluntarily, but saw no grounds to consider the dismissal as having occurred without just cause. The judge noted that the employee had agreed to the new functions and performed them for a prolonged period. The company’s documents also contained assessments of her work and provided for resources and support under the plan. The tribunal added that the woman would have had difficulty substantiating compensation, as she had been paid for her notice period and there was effectively no gap between her old and new jobs.
