Five unsolved Norwich crime cases that detectives are still investigating

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:42 · source · permalink

Some of Norwich's most horrific crime cases remain open decades after they shocked the city.

Detectives are continuing to appeal for information that could finally bring answers to victims and their families.

Michelle Bettles (Image: Norfolk Constabulary)

Michelle Bettles

Among the most high-profile cases is the murder of Michelle Bettles, 22, who was last seen in Norwich's red-light district during Easter weekend in March 2002.

CCTV captured her walking along St Benedict's Street on the evening of March 28, while witnesses later reported seeing her in the city centre.

Her body was discovered two days later in woodland near Scarning, close to Dereham, around 20 miles from where she was last seen.

She had been strangled and her killer has never been identified.

Kellie Pratt (Image: Norfolk Constabulary)

Kellie Pratt

Another case that remains unsolved is the disappearance of Kellie Pratt, 28, from Norwich.

Kellie was last seen outside the Rose Inn at the junction of Queen's Road and City Road on June 11, 2000.

She was working in the city's red-light area and was reportedly talking on her mobile phone before vanishing.

Despite a major investigation involving hundreds of witness statements, neither Kellie nor her phone has ever been found.

Julie Cooper (Image: Norfolk Constabulary)

Julie Cooper

Police are also still searching for answers over the brutal attack on Julie Cooper in August 2000.

The 28-year-old was walking to work near Martineau Lane and Europa Way when she was attacked and robbed.

She suffered life-changing injuries from a knife or similar weapon but survived.

Extensive enquiries, including national television appeals, failed to identify the culprit.

Natalie Pearman (Image: Norfolk Constabulary)

Natalie Jane Pearman

Natalie Jane Pearman, 16, was found in a lay-by on Ringland Road in November 1992.

The teenager had been working in Norwich's red-light district and died from asphyxia.

Detectives obtained a DNA profile believed to belong to her killer and have interviewed thousands of people, but no match has ever been found.

Susan Margaret Long

The oldest unsolved case is the killing of Susan Margaret Long, 18, from Aylsham.

She was found murdered on Burgh Road in March 1970 after returning from a night out in Norwich.

Advances in DNA technology have led to repeated reviews of the case, but the identity of her killer remains unknown.

Police have stressed that all of the investigations remain active and are urging anyone with information, no matter how insignificant it may seem, to come forward.
