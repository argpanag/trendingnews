New Porsche Mission S Videos Offer a Clue About the Engine Porsche Won’t Talk About

TheTools · 18.10.2026 14:28 · source · permalink

There has been considerable hype surrounding the Porsche Mission S, a super sports car unveiled on October 15 that sits above the brand's famed 911 and takes its design cues from multiple models. However, Porsche did not reveal the car's engine, power output, transmission, or performance figures.

MotorBiscuit recently reported on speculation that the Porsche Mission S could be a petrol-powered car capable of revving to a whopping 8,000 RPM, as hinted at in its teaser, and that it could be powered by a flat-eight engine. Now, a report by Motor1, which features multiple clips capturing the Mission S's engine note, provides further clues that the car could indeed feature such an engine, adding weight to the speculation surrounding Porsche's mysterious vision model.

The flat-eight engine could be part of a hybrid powertrain, as the report suggests, and if the rumors are true, the Mission S would become the first Porsche to feature this engine. The other bits on the car, though, have been inspired by other Porsche models.

Porsche Mission S. Image Credit: Porsche

The round headlights and pronounced fenders recall the 911, while the shut lines running into the lights salute the G model. The flat front and the rear design treatment draw inspiration from the GT1. The roofline recalls the 959, here carried all the way to the tail. Cooling takes its cue from the 917's radiator fan, and the wheels reinterpret the 928's dial pattern with a focus on low weight.

Beyond the borrowed details, the shape is defined by a mid-mounted engine, which allows the classic flyline. A single line wraps around the body, linking the front and rear lights. Muscular surfaces sit above it, while the zone below shows off technical parts and lightweight construction. A fully integrated active rear wing completes the look.

Tobias Sühlmann, Vice President Style at Porsche, described the Mission S as the result of a forward-looking, holistic design strategy. There are three guiding terms for the design: authentic, consequent, and timeless. Sühlmann said Porsche will soon translate this direction into its future product portfolio.

Porsche Mission S. Image Credit: Porsche

Inside, the cabin takes the opposite approach to today's screen-heavy trend. There is no permanent central display and no passenger screen. Instead, a retractable MagSafe-compatible holder lets the driver use their own smartphone for navigation. The My Porsche App also lets owners choose historically inspired gauge faces.

What remains is built around the driver: five round instruments, with an analogue rev counter. Real switches, push-pull dashboard controls, and a reworked Sport Chrono element sit alongside digital feedback. The interior architecture is based on the 550 Spyder and 356, with two lines running through the dashboard and doors to create the feeling of sitting deep within the car.
