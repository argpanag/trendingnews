    21:06 BST

    Three people have been killed in a Russian drone attack on the western Ukrainian city of Ivano-Frankivsk this evening, mayor Ruslan Martsynkiv says.

    According to him, they were all members of the military. He does not specify what exactly was hit, but adds that civilian infrastructure was damaged.

  2. Trump's diesel deal is an about-face on sanctions signed last monthpublished at 20:50 BST
    20:50 BST

    Trump's recently-announced deal to import Russian diesel into the US is a sharp reversal from the Russian sanctions bill he signed into law just last month.

    Trump said on Friday that Russia had agreed to immediately release 300,000 tonnes of diesel to US and global markets, followed by more down the line.

    But the deal he signed in September, named after the late Senator Lindsey Graham, targets Russia's oil and gas revenues that have helped fuel its war in Ukraine.

    Specifically, the law allows Trump to impose a 100% tariff on the top five purchasers of Russian oil and gas, with an exception for countries that import less than 15% of their natural gas from Russia and are working to reduce their dependency.

    The bill also includes sanctions against Russian President Vladimir Putin, senior figures within the Russian government, banks and other financial institutions, as well as Russia's fleet of tankers that have been used to circumvent existing sanctions.

  3. Bipartisan group of senators says Russia deal 'runs directly counter' to US lawpublished at 20:44 BST
    20:44 BST

    Following earlier comments from Republican Senator Thom Tillis, a bipartisan group of senators has questioned the legality of Donald Trump's deal to import Russian oil into the US.

    The group, consisting of three Democrats and four Republicans, including Tillis, makes reference to a bill passed by Congress in 2022 to ban the import of Russian oil, saying it was done "to prevent American dollars from financing Putin’s brutal war against Ukraine".

    The statement adds: "The US should not import oil from Russia until it ends its aggression against Ukraine and ceases threats against our Nato allies."

    The group says the president's decision to allow Russian diesel imports into the country "runs directly counter to that law", adding the administration failed to consult with Congress "or submit any explanation to justify its decision as required by statute".

    “The United States should not provide Putin with a financial lifeline while Russia continues to kill Ukrainian civilians. The Administration must immediately reverse course."

  13. Trump said Putin had agreed to 'immediately' supply US with dieselpublished at 18:33 BST
    18:33 BST

    Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky and US President Donald Trump had earlier came to blows over Trump's announcement of a deal to import Russian diesel into the US.

    Here's what we know about that deal:

    Trump said on Truth Social that Putin had agreed to release 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately "to the American and global marketplace" followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and a further one million after that.

    It is understood that the US Treasury is suspending sanctions on Russian diesel exports until 7 April as part of the deal, but other assets like those in American banks are still frozen.

    Trump wrote on Truth Social that a further three million tonnes of Russian diesel would be delivered "within a short period", dependent on the condition of its refineries, which have been attacked by Ukraine.

    Putin later said that Russia, which introduced a ban on diesel exports earlier this year, was willing to supply both the US and global markets with oil and petroleum products, but his statement did not specify amounts.

    Analysts have questioned whether Russia is capable of providing that quantity of diesel and the extent to which doing so will ease prices for American consumers.

  14. Diesel prices heaping pressure on Trump as US midterms approachpublished at 18:23 BST
    18:23 BST

    Fuel prices have soared since the start of the war in Iran, with diesel now exceeding $6 per gallon in the US.

  16. Whether out of impatience or strategy, Trump's announcements prompt a scramblepublished at 17:49 BST
    17:49 BST

    Anthony Zurcher
    North America correspondent

    Donald Trump’s announcement that he has brokered an energy ceasefire agreement between Russia and Ukraine seemingly came as a surprise to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky.

    “Ukraine supports the ceasefire, and it is important that Russia support it as well,” Zelensky posted on X. “We are awaiting details from the American side.”

    It’s a familiar move for the American president – announcing a deal before it is clear it has been finalised. On several occasions, he has heralded Middle East ceasefires and sweeping peace agreements in the region before all sides had fully agreed.

    Whether it is impatience on Trump’s part or a savvy negotiating tactic to push a deal over the finish line, the result tends to be a scramble by the affected parties to respond.

    Trump has blamed the war in Ukraine for driving up global fuel costs and shown a willingness to pressure Zelensky and work with Russia’s Vladimir Putin to bring those prices down.

    If this agreement sticks - and it translates into a dip at the petrol pump in America - it could provide Trump with a tangible success as November’s midterm US congressional elections approach.

    Neither outcome is assured, however, as previous Russia-Ukraine ceasefires have proven elusive and Trump’s other efforts to lower fuel costs in the US have been largely ineffective.
