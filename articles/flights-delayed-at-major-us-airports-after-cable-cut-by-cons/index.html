Flights delayed at major US airports after cable cut by construction workers

TheTools · 22.09.2026 14:30 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Flights are delayed at major US airports after construction workers cut a cable needed for air traffic control, a top aviation official says

  • Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy said a crew working for a railway operator accidentally cut the cable in New Jersey, impacting airports in New York and Philadelphia among others

  • Ground stops have been ordered at airports in New Jersey, while Duffy says flights are resuming at New York's LaGuardia and Philadelphia - though the airports are still experiencing heavy delays

  • Telecommunications company Verizon, which the cable belongs to, told US media it is working to repair the cable "as quickly as possible"

  • The east coast disruption comes ahead of the United Nations General Assembly in New York City on Tuesday

  1. Flights grounded after mistaken cable-cutting brings down air traffic control systempublished at 22:41 BST 21 September
    22:41 BST 21 September

    Freya Scott-Turner
    Live reporter

    It's been a manic Monday for multiple airports across the northeastern US, after an equipment issue delayed hundreds of flights. Major airports in New York, Philadelphia, Boston and New Jersey were among those affected.

    The problem? A malfunction of a system which guides planes during take-off and landing, called the Terminal Radar Approach Control System.

    Aviation administrator Bryan Bedford said that a circuit had failed, and the system went down because a backup cable had been damaged by a construction crew in New Jersey.

    The travel chaos has come on the eve of the United Nationals General Assembly, which will see more than 130 officials from around the world jet into New York.

    There are signs of improvement, with flights starting to resume at New York's LaGuardia Airport and Philadelphia Airport, but delays remain.

    Duffy said the incident "underscores the need for additional funding" to modernise "ageing infrastructure".

    Meanwhile, telecommunications company Verizon said it was "actively working" to repair the cable restore connectivity, but Bedford warned of a lengthy repair process that could take as long as 13 hours.

  3. New Jersey rail service says its construction crew accidentally cut cablepublished at 22:33 BST 21 September
    22:33 BST 21 September

    As we've been reporting, the air traffic control issues started today when a construction crew accidentally severed a fibre cable needed for communication.

    US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy initially said a crew working for railway company Amtrak was behind the incident.

    The BBC's US partner CBS News obtained a statement from NJ Transit, a different train operator based in the state of New Jersey, which says its crews accidentally damaged the cable.

    "At approximately 9:45 a.m. today, a construction crew working on the Delco Lead project between New Brunswick and North Brunswick accidentally severed a fiber-optic cable while working at the site," the statement says.

    NJ Transit adds that its crews were several feet away from marked indicators that should have shown the location of the cables. It says it's working to determine who placed those markings, as well as the other circumstances around the incident.

  4. Can I get a refund if my flight was delayed?published at 22:10 BST 21 September
    22:10 BST 21 September

    In the event of delays or cancellations, the US Department of Transportation (DOT) sets out on its website when consumers are eligible.

    For cancelled flights:

    • You are entitled to a refund "if the airline cancelled a flight, regardless of the reason"
    • It adds that if the airline offers travel credits, vouchers, or other forms of compensation in lieu of a refund, it "must also let you know that you are entitled to a refund"

    For delayed flights:

    • You can get a refund if the airline "significantly delays" or "significantly changes" a flight
    • "Significant" delays or changes include arriving at a domestic destination more than three hours late, or at an international destination more than six hours late
    • But crucially, the DOT says you cannot claim a refund if you still travel on the delayed flight or on an alternative one offered by the airline Although you may be able to claim compensation for amenities

  5. Telecomms company 'actively working' to repair damaged air traffic control cablepublished at 21:51 BST 21 September
    21:51 BST 21 September

    US telecommunications company Verizon says it is "actively working" to repair the backup cable needed for air traffic control. We heard earlier that damage to the cable is the cause of the ongoing flight disruption that's hit multiple airports across the US.

    In a statement shared with US media, Verizon says it "bears no responsibility" for the incident.

    “We have a fiber cable running adjacent to an Amtrak rail line in New Jersey," it says. "Construction contractors working in the area dug up and cut our cable."

    "Verizon's facilities were fully functional up to that point," it notes, adding that it is "actively working to repair the damaged cable and restore connectivity as quickly as possible".

    US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has said that a construction crew working for national rail operator Amtrak cut the cable accidentally.

    21:36 BST 21 September

    Newark Liberty International Airport in New Jersey has been the most heavily impacted by today's air traffic control disruptions, according to flight-tracking website FlightAware.

  8. Average delays of over three hours at Philadelphia airportpublished at 21:07 BST 21 September
    21:07 BST 21 September

    The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has an airspace monitor which shows how various airports are being affected by ground stops and delays.

    It lists three airports as currently experiencing ground delays due to "equipment outage" - this was the reason for the disruption given by John F Kennedy airport earlier. These are:

    • Departures to John F Kennedy International are delayed by an average of 72 minutes
    • As LaGuardia airport, delays are averaging 44 minutes
    • Ground delays at Philadelphia international airport are averaging 212 minutes

    The site also shows delays averaging 105 minutes for departures to Boston Logan international airport - which had also reported disruption earlier - but it confirms that this is due to weather conditions rather than equipment.

  15. Issue caused by fibre cable severed during construction, US aviation official sayspublished at 19:27 BST 21 September
    19:27 BST 21 September

    US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and aviation administrator Bryan Bedford just briefly spoke to reporters after an unrelated event.

    Bedford confirmed that the malfunction was related to the Terminal Radar Approach Control System, which guides planes during takeoff and landing. He added that a "circuit failed", which then led to the discovery of a backup fibre circuit which had been damaged by construction crews in New Jersey.

    He said that to repair the backup cable would take around 13 hours.

    "It's massive, I'm told," Bedford said.

    He added that the cut fibre line is around 600ft (183m) long, and that it was inadvertently damaged during "construction".

    "Somebody dug it up," Bedford says.

    Fixing the issue requires turning everything off - "sanitising the airspace" - and making sure that all flights are safe. So far, around 90 flights have been diverted to other regions, he estimated, saying he didn't know about the number of total cancellations.

    Duffy added that these problems "are not new to us" and called on Congress to provide more cash to update the system.

  16. Flight disruption coincides with report into 'vulnerable' US air traffic control systemspublished at 19:11 BST 21 September
    19:11 BST 21 September

    This flight disruption comes on the same day that a new report from a government watchdog, external points to vulnerabilities in US aircraft communications.

    The Government Accountability Office (GAO) says that Federal Aviation Authority's (FAA) air traffic and communications systems are "vulnerable to cyber threats", including spectrum interference, spoofing and jamming which the report said could disrupt communications between aircrafts and ground control.

    It adds that the FAA "hasn't sufficiently addressed" the threats, laying out several issues including a lack of real-time monitoring and detection software and a failure to complete risk assessments.

    The FAA has not stated that a cyber attack is the cause of the ongoing disruption. US media are currently citing an issue with "frequencies" at Philadelphia air traffic control.
