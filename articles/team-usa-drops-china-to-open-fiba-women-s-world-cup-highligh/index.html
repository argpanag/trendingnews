Team USA drops China to open FIBA Women's World Cup: Highlights, score

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:37 · source · permalink

Team USA's bid to win a fifth consecutive FIBA Women’s World Cup gold medal is off the good start after a convincing a 94-61 win over China on Friday, Sept. 4 in Berlin, Germany.

The Americans opened the World Cup clicking on all cylinders, led mainly by its young core. Four players finished in double figures, including Caitlin Clark, who had a double (14 points, 11 assists), Rhyne Howard, who had 14 points, and Paige Bueckers, who scored all 14 of her points in the second half.

"I think the 'young and turnt' crew, they did really well. They just kind of took what they wanted. They were the aggressors. They were confident, and they came in when their number was called," Breanna Stewart said. "That's what this team needs. It's like, no matter what your minutes are gonna be, go out and play hard. Nobody's gonna do it for you. We have to do it."

The veterans of the group, like Stewart (seven points, seven rebounds) and Jackie Young (14 points), got the momentum started, but the younger players ran away with it in the second half, pushing the lead to 30-plus points. The ball movement was crucial down the stretch, and every player saw minutes Friday as Team USA collectively unleashed six blocks and racked up nine steals.

"I thought it was great team effort for us," Team USA coach Kara Lawson said.

"China presented a lot of problems for us. Their size in the frontcourt, clearly, is a matchup problem for I think every team in the world, and I thought we did a pretty good job managing that. And they just got a lot of contributions from our players, and I thought Caitlin (Clark) was terrific, coming off the bench, changing the tempo of the game. Her playmaking, 11 assists. I thought Breanna Stewart's leadership and defensive communication was excellent. I thought Rhyne Howard and Jackie Young in the first half --- We just had a lot of different people in those moments step up and that's what we need. We're at our best when we have a lot of different people contributing."

What are the differences between FIBA and the WNBA?

Here's what's different about FIBA play versus what you may see in the WNBA:

  • The 3-point line is closer in FIBA competition, measuring at 20 feet and eight inches from the basket, while the WNBA’s line is 22 feet and 1.75 inches from the hoop.
  • Like in college, each player is only allowed five personal fouls instead of six for WNBA play.
  • There is no defensive three-second rule in FIBA play. Expect to see more zone defense than you might in the WNBA.
  • The ball can be touched or swatted away after it hits the rim, whereas that would result in a goaltending violation in the WNBA.
  • Only coaches can call timeouts in FIBA play.
  • The court size is slightly more compact in FIBA play, measuring 2.13 feet shorter in length and .79 feet shorter in width than the WNBA.
  • The game ball, made by Molten, has 12 panels and provides an extra unique grip. The WNBA uses an eight-panel ball made by Wilson.

Wings’ Li Yueru will not play for China due to lost passport 

When Team USA plays against China in their FIBA Women’s World Cup opener on Friday, China will be without a key player. 

Li Yueru, who plays for the Dallas Wings, revealed in a statement posted on her Instagram account her passport was lost in the mail, preventing her from traveling to Berlin, Germany, to join the Chinese national team for the World Cup. 

Yueru had previously played for China at the 2021 and 2024 Olympics, and in the 2022 World Cup where the Chinese captured the silver medal. This time around, her team will have to compete without her. Read Mitchell Northam’s full story here.

A'ja Wilson, Kelsey Plum out of World Cup 

The U.S. women’s national team roster originally featured four-time WNBA MVP A'ja Wilson and five-time All-Star Kelsey Plum, but both were ruled out four days before the World Cup began due to "health reasons." Wilson was the MVP of the 2022 World Cup, which was Team USA's fourth consecutive time capturing the World Cup.  

"We'll miss being around them ... We wish them both a speedy recovery,” Kara Lawson said on Sept. 1. “But we don't have time to be worried about who's not here." 

Lawson says team will focus on chemistry and building with the players on the floor. A pair of young Washington Mystics in Sonia Citron and Kiki Iriafen will replace Wilson and Plum. 

Team USA could face 'most difficult' FIBA Women's World Cup yet 

Team USA coach Kara Lawson isn't sugarcoating the fact winning 2026 FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup gold will be challenging. 

The U.S. women have largely dominated their international competition winning eight straight Olympic gold medals and nine of the last 12 World Cups dating back to 1979. The field is getting better and Team USA could have an uphill battle in this World Cup, which begins Friday, Sept. 4. 

"It's going to be the most difficult World Cup to win," Team USA coach Kara Lawson said on Tuesday, Sept. 1. "There's no question that the world is improving at a rapid rate. It's so exciting to see ... now when you look at rosters, there's a number of just high quality players. I think the depth of these rosters is really strong." 

The 2026 FIBA World Cup field is made up of 16 countries, divided into four different groups. The entirety of Team USA's roster is made up of WNBA players, while France and Australia's national team aren't far behind with eight WNBA players each. Spain has five WNBA players, while Germany and Belgium each have four. More details here.

How does the FIBA Women’s World Cup work? 

This year’s tournament — Germany’s second time hosting — will feature 16 teams, four more than the 2022 World Cup, but the same number the competition had between 1990 and 2018. 

Similar to soccer’s FIFA World Cup, the basketball version put on by FIBA features a preliminary round-robin group stage round. There are four groups each featuring four teams. The second- and third-place teams will advance to a Round of 12, while the first-place team from each group will advance directly to the quarterfinals. Including the third-place game, the tournament will feature a total of 36 matchups.
