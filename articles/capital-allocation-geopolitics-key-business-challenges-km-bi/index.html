Kumar Mangalam Birla, Chairman, Aditya Birla Group

Capital allocation and geopolitical developments are the two major challenges for businesses operating across multiple countries, but investments must happen despite global uncertainties, Aditya Birla Group Chairman Kumar Mangalam Birla has said.

Businesses cannot afford to put growth on hold despite mounting demands on capital and disruptions across the world, as sustained expansion was essential to remain competitive, Birla said at the Bloomberg New Economy Forum on Tuesday.

“You can’t stop growing... For me, capital allocation has been a challenge because you have a whole lot of new demands on your capital, which we know about, but at the same time. You (have to) keep growing. You either grow or you go down,” he said.

Birla said the group had continued investing in both existing and new businesses despite the uncertain global environment, underlining the need to balance competing investment priorities while sustaining growth.

Geopolitical developments pose another significant challenge, particularly for companies with operations spanning multiple countries, he said. Events unfolding in one part of the world can have repercussions across businesses and markets elsewhere, making it difficult to anticipate their full impact.

“We are a global organisation, and when I read about a significant global event in the newspapers, I can be sure that it will impact us somewhere in the business, somewhere in the world, which I may not even sort of understand the full impact of when I’m reading it in the papers.”

Birla stressed the importance of building resilient businesses while ensuring employees remain engaged and equipped to navigate an increasingly interconnected global environment.

He said companies needed to encourage collaboration and learning among employees, while keeping their focus on strengthening the underlying competitiveness of individual businesses.

“At the end of the day, it’s people who make the difference, and I think keeping them going and keeping them enthused and energetic is an equally important task,” he said.

He also highlighted cost competitiveness and scale as key pillars of the group’s manufacturing strategy, saying businesses needed to build structural advantages to withstand global competition and uncertainty.

“I think it’s about building the inherent strength of the business. So, for example, in all our manufacturing businesses, we’d be in the bottom decile of the cost curve globally. So, amongst the 10 per cent lowest-cost producers, it’s about scale,” he added.
