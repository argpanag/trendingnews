Gathering place escapes the tax man, clears its debt

TheTools · 05.10.2026 18:09 · source · permalink

The Gathering Place announced it has paid off its debt to the Canada Revenue Agency.

Less than two years ago, that debt of about $465,000 threatened to close North Bay’s community soup kitchen.

Through that time, Gathering Place officials note that the doors stayed open and not a single weekday lunch was missed.

“This debt was the anchor around our neck,” said Peter Gregory, Chair of The Gathering Place Board of Directors, in a release.

“Today it’s gone. We went back to where we came from, the grassroots, and that means volunteers, partners and neighbours. Rebuilding the community’s trust without skipping one lunch has been an incredible task. I can’t thank our team, our board leadership and the many partners who came to the table enough. Because of them, the people we serve did not go without.”

Where it started

Gathering Place officials say the rising food costs, record demand and the high cost of running North Bay’s winter warming centre pushed The Gathering Place into crisis in 2024.

The Turnaround

The board and staff say they were forced to make hard choices to protect the one thing that mattered most, the daily lunch.

That included cutting operating costs nearly in half, from about $1.05 million to $544,000 in a single year. They were also able to reduce wages and benefits by 60 per cent, and ended warming centre operations. 

They also scaled back breakfast, dinner, take-away meals and outreach programs so every resource could go to the core lunch service, and replaced paid services with partnerships and bartering wherever possible, from IT support to garbage collection.

They negotiated and honoured a repayment plan with the CRA, restructured the charity’s financing, and secured taxpayer relief on interest and penalties and kept the traditional lunch service running every weekday throughout, without interruption. 

Community Support

Gathering Place officials say the government funding through the District of Nipissing Social Services Administration Board fell from about $257,000 to $50,000 with the end of the warming centre program.

"North Bay filled much of the gap: donations and fundraising rose 18 per cent, to almost $461,000. Coldest Night of the Year, Soup’s On and the Davedi Club spaghetti dinner all played their part," the release stated.  

Over the past year, The Gathering Place also rescued about 182,000 pounds of surplus food from area grocers and partners that would otherwise have gone to landfill. It shares that food with youth centres, shelters, group homes, and church and student food banks across the city.

“On behalf of the North Bay Food Bank, congratulations to Peter, the board, the staff and every volunteer at The Gathering Place,” said Louise Lowe, Chair of the North Bay Food Bank.

“What they’ve done is remarkable. They carried a crushing debt, made the tough decisions and kept feeding people every single day. Hunger in our community isn’t going away, and we’re proud to stand beside them. We’re stronger together.”

The Future

With the CRA debt behind it, The Gathering Place says it is focused on long-term stability and on continuing to serve the growing number of North Bay residents facing food insecurity. It welcomes donations, volunteers and new community partners.

“We invite everyone to come by at lunchtime, see the work being done here, and meet the people who make it happen,” said Gregory.

Donations can be made at thegatheringplacenorthbay.ca or in person at 1181 Cassells Street in North Bay.
