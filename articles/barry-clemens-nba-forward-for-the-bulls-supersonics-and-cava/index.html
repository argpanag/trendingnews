Barry Clemens, NBA Forward for the Bulls, SuperSonics and Cavaliers, Dies at 83 - That Eric Alper

TheTools · 28.10.2026 17:06 · source · permalink
  • Music

Madison Square Garden hosted a very different kind of showdown in the spring of 1973, a one-on-one tournament between NBA players broadcast on national television, and a steady, hard-working forward from small-town Ohio battled all the way to the championship game. Barry Clemens, who carved out an 11-season NBA career with the New York Knicks, Chicago Bulls, Seattle SuperSonics, Cleveland Cavaliers and Portland Trail Blazers, has died at 83, with his passing announced on October 27, 2026.

John Barry Clemens was born on May 1, 1943, in Dayton, Ohio, and starred at Xenia High School before heading to Ohio Wesleyan University. Standing six feet six inches, he became one of the finest players in the Ohio Athletic Conference, earning OAC Player of the Year honours as a senior in 1965.

The New York Knicks selected Clemens in the third round of the 1965 NBA Draft, 19th overall, and he spent his rookie season in New York before joining the brand new Chicago Bulls through the 1966 expansion draft. In Chicago, he enjoyed the best scoring seasons of his career, averaging a career-high 9.3 points and 4.8 rebounds per game in 1967 to 1968 and appearing in the Bulls’ first playoff run.

Clemens moved on to the Seattle SuperSonics in 1969, playing three seasons there and posting his best shooting year in 1971 to 1972, when he made more than 52% of his field goals and 84% of his free throws. He then spent two seasons with the Cleveland Cavaliers, and on May 6, 1973, he reached the final of ABC’s one-on-one NBA tournament at Madison Square Garden, where he fell to Portland All-Star guard Geoff Petrie. He finished his career with the Trail Blazers from 1974 to 1976.

Across 790 regular season games, Clemens scored 5,312 points and grabbed 2,532 rebounds, earning a reputation as a dependable, versatile forward who contributed wherever he was needed. In 2009, he was inducted into the Ohio Basketball Hall of Fame.

Barry Clemens leaves behind a career built on persistence and teamwork, carried from the gyms of Xenia and Ohio Wesleyan to five NBA cities over more than a decade. The small-college star who once went toe to toe with the league’s best at Madison Square Garden proved that heart and hustle could earn a lasting place in the game, and Ohio basketball fans will always count him among their own.
