The 2007 role that scared Sandra Bullock out of acting for years

TheTools · 14.10.2026 16:15 · source · permalink

Sandra Bullock has faced a lot of difficulties in the film industry: sharing a movie set with Donald Trump on Two Weeks Notice, for example, can’t have been easy. But her “hardest time ever” came with a role in 2007 that spooked her out of the business for a couple of years.

As she told CineSnob, “I had the hardest time I ever had working” on the set of Premonition, a supernatural psychological thriller directed by Mennan Yapo. In it, she plays a depressed housewife whose husband has died in a car accident. A day after the life-shattering news, she wakes up to find him alive and well in the house. This unspools a complex plot of mourning, cruelty, and pain.

Should you sit down to watch the movie this Halloween, you’d find yourself preoccupied for an hour and 50 minutes. Bullock, on the other hand, spent three months in a cycle of psychological trauma, shooting the film at odd hours, in an odd sequence, and at an odd place.

She explained, “You know, films shoot out of sequence anyway, which is nerve-racking. Every day, you’re looking at the scene going, ‘We’re shooting the end at the beginning. We’re shooting the middle at the end.’ You always have to go back and see what happens before the scene you’re shooting.”

Bullock continued, “Then, on top of that, the days are out of order, and you have to get yourself to an emotional state of grief every single day for 12 hours a day for three months in Shreveport, Louisiana, with no flight back home in this house – in this cocoon – it was really hard for me. That’s probably why I didn’t work for a year after it. But I’m thankful for it now.”

Of course, Bullock knew her performance had to be nuanced and bold enough to act as the gravitational core of the movie, as the film dealt with far more than a love story between two people. Rather, she became the symbolic conduit for the atrophying American dream.

As she said elsewhere in the interview, the film is “a love story, but it’s also shattering the American dream. The American dream becomes a nightmare because it doesn’t fit everyone. It’s one of those things that I think several people have encountered in life… When it’s too late, and you think, ‘Why didn’t I do something to make life better? Why didn’t I do anything?'”

Following the traumatising project, Bullock cocooned herself away and came back swinging two years later with the roaring comedy The Proposal, the tear-jerker The Blind Side, and yet another rom-com, All About Steve.

Thankfully, all the hard work Bullock put into Premonition paid off at the box office, with the film pulling in $84.1million worldwide against a modest $20m production budget. The reviews don’t quite speak to the same level of success, tanking with an alarming 8% Rotten Tomatoes score. In this instance, we should let the money do the talking.
