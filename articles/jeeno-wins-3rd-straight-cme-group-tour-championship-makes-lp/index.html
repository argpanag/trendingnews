Jeeno wins 3rd straight CME Group Tour Championship, makes LPGA history

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:48 · source · permalink

PUBLISHED : 23 Nov 2026 at 09:10

WRITER: Reuters

Jeeno Thitikul of ​Thailand made LPGA Tour ⁠history on Sunday by winning ⁠the CME Group Tour Championship for ​an unprecedented third consecutive year.

Jeeno opened the final round with an eagle and fired a bogey-free, 7-under- par 65 to overtake 54-hole leader A ​Lim Kim of South Korea, capture the LPGA's season finale in ‌Naples, Florida, and lift the Race to the CME Globe trophy for the second year running.

Jeeno won the $4 million (136 million baht) first prize, the largest winner's check in women's professional golf. In doing so, she passed LPGA legend Annika Sorenstam as the tour's all-time money leader - at the ripe old age of 23.

The World No. 2 finished 24-under 264 at Tiburon Golf Club. She went 26 ​under par last year and 22 under ⁠the year before that. So it tracked that she had one request for tournament higher-ups.

"I have no words. The only word I have to (CME Group CEO Terry Duffy) is just, ‘Don't change the course till I retire. Don't change it,'" Jeeno said at the trophy presentation. "I mean, it's ‌just me and the course. I think we're just really best friends. I told (course reporter Amy Rogers) earlier in the week that I think (Tiburon) is my best friend so far on the tour."

Jeeno has won 12 times on the LPGA Tour. A major is all that's missing from her resume at this early point in her ⁠career.

She thanked her family, including her grandfather who got her started in the sport, for their support as she worked towards the summit of women's golf.

"We overcome the obstacle, we overcome the trouble until the time that all people see the success, but behind the scenes it's not that easy to come here on the LPGA Tour," Jeeno said. "A lot of talented players. But I mean, my family is my everything."

Jeeno won by two strokes over Japan's Aki Iwai, who also ​shot a final-round 65. World No. 1 Nelly Korda made a late push, but she finished alone in third at 20 under following a 68.

Jeeno eagled the par-5 first hole and was 4 under for her round when she made the turn. Meanwhile, ​the ‌door opened when Kim, playing one group behind in the final pairing, made a mess of No. 1 and carded a double-bogey 7.

Jeeno built her lead with birdies at Nos. 10 and 14. She two-putted for birdie at the par-5 ​17th ⁠to double her edge over Iwai.

Korda, meanwhile, had birdies at Nos. 9, 10, 11 and 14 to draw within three of Thitikul with three to play. She settled for par at the par-3 16th, then her hopes ⁠of a come-from-behind championship win deflated when her short birdie putt at No. 17 lipped out hard.

Asked what she would take away from her efforts this week, Korda replied, "Just the grind".

"You know, still being able to compete when I didn't even really feel like I really had my trust in like a B or C game," she said. "So just proud ⁠of the way that I fought this week to kind of give myself a chance. It's really hard to go ​up against Jeeno out here. I feel like she just has this place figured out.

Korda has yet to win the CME Group Tour Championship, two hours from her hometown of Bradenton, Florida.

"Yeah, maybe one day," she said.

Iwai, 24, stayed in close pursuit of Jeeno with a bogey-free round to cap off one of her best seasons yet.

"Doing well and everything ‌fun," Iwai said. "Yeah, so this year feel ⁠like nearly, almost, but always next week, always and then next ​year could be better. I will do my best."

Kim rallied for an even-par 72 and finished where she started the day, 18 under, part of a tie for seventh.
