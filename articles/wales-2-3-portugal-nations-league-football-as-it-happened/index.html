Ben Fisher was in Cardiff tonight, and here’s his report. Thanks for reading this MBM.

Craig Bellamy speaks to the BBC. “We knew we were going to have to suffer … I thought we suffered a little bit too much at times … our intensity wasn’t there … I can understand that … we have a lot of players who don’t play week in, week out to play such a high-tempo game in a short space of time … it’s not ideal … there was moments where it worked really well … really good moments … mistakes … against lesser teams you’d get away with it … at both ends, the last bit has really cost us in this group.”

The final Group A4 table

What drama in the other game in Group A4. Erling Haaland completed Norway’s comeback from two goals down against Denmark with a winner from the penalty spot in the sixth minute of added time! That 3-2 win in Copenhagen gives Norway the advantage in the head-to-head with the Danes, and they leapfrog their way into the quarter-finals! More heartbreak for Denmark, whose hopes were dashed during injury time in World Cup qualifying too; like Wales, they go into the relegation play-offs.

Wales captain Ben Davies talks to the BBC. “We’ve shown a lot of character … good effort … we’ve come up a little short from the results perspective but there’s a lot to take from this … we have to feel confident because we’re playing well … the boys up top can be so dangerous … [Wales’ style] comes with risks sometimes … I’d rather play this way, go toe-to-toe with these teams and have a go … we weren’t a million miles off [the other teams in the group] as the table suggests … we put some very good performances in, I just wish there had been better results.”

Harry Wilson speaks to the BBC. “For large parts we played really well again … we had to suffer … they’re a great team … full of world stars … we look back on [Portugal’s] goals, we’re disappointed … but for large parts we were really good … we knew this group would be tough … a lot of the games we played really well … for large spells we’ve been the better team … we have to take confidence [going into the play-offs] … we’re going toe-to-toe with these teams … our shape is good.”

For the second time in four days, Wales make a big contribution to an entertaining game only to come away with nothing. Sorba Thomas was their star performer, scoring a fine goal and causing no end of problems down the left, pretty much from first kick until last. Harry Wilson was his usual elegant self, also. And while Wales finish Group A4 in last spot, they’ve still got a relegation play-off in March and a chance to retain their League A status. And they’re good to watch, always giving fans bang for their buck. Portugal meanwhile take their first positive step forward into the new CR7-free landscape.

As Portugal walk off, Bruno Fernandes gets the attention of a cameraman and directs him to the captain’s armband as worn by João Cancelo. Now then, what’s that about? And then Jorge Jesus loses his temper with his entire team, because they’ve not gone over to the travelling support to thank them. The boss goes over and does the job himself.

FULL TIME: Wales 2-3 Portugal

The ten men hold on! CF7 wins it for Portugal!

90 min +3: Wilson, who has been lively as ever, works his way infield from the right, swivels, and drags a low drive wide right. Diogo Costa had it covered. That’s probably that.

90 min +2: Thomas crosses low from the left. Roberts shoots. It’s a bit of a scuff, and blocked easily.

90 min +1: Geovany Quenda comes on for Bruno ‘BF99’ Fernandes.

90 min: Thomas sends the corner long. Cabango cushions a header down at the far post. Roberts, facing the wrong way, improvises a scorpion flick that loops towards the top left. It’s on target, but there’s no pace, and it’s an easy claim for the keeper. There will be three additional minutes.

89 min: Thomas, who has been excellent all evening, curls dangerously from the left. Roberts competes at the far post, forcing Veiga into eyebrowing out for a corner. That’s sent in from the right, and earns another corner which will come in from the left. Thomas to take.

87 min: A little pocket of space for Wilson, just outside the D. He takes a touch and flips a cute sidefoot inches wide of the left-hand post. Had that curled in a bit more, it’s not clear if Diogo Costa was getting there.

86 min: Meanwhile in Copenhagen, Martin Ødegaard has equalised for Norway. Denmark’s two-goal lead wiped out!

84 min: Koumas barges down the right and cuts back, the ball eventually finding its way to Thomas on the edge of the D. He’s got more time than he thinks, and skelps a wild shot miles over the bar. That was a decent chance.

83 min: Koumas comes on for Colwill.

82 min: Wales are pushing Portugal back a wee bit here, albeit without creating much. “When Bruno Fernandes is not arguing a decision against his team, you know your case is a pretty thin one,” notes David Bowen. “Absolute numbskull behaviour from Pedro Neto.”

80 min: Thomas advances once more down the left. His cross is flicked clear by Renato Veiga, then mopped up by Fernandes on the other flank, who clears and on the follow-through accidentally catches Roberts in his special area.

78 min: Forbs, he of the new font, dribbles in from the right again. No way through this time. “I get where krishnamoorthy v (8 min) is coming from, but as I still wear a Portugal-shaped scar from 2016, I hope Portugal don’t get one of those wins today,” writes Matt Dony. “Yes, the stakes are substantially lower, and it won’t change anything in the grand scheme of things, but it would certainly make me feel a little better to get something out of a game with Portugal.”

76 min: One minute and 59 seconds passed between Neto’s first and second yellow cards. Both challenges were oranges, too, the first a rake down the back of Sheehan’s leg, the second a laughable aquaplane into Johnson. No idea what the Antonio Rattín tribute act was all about.

RED CARD: Neto (Portugal)

74 min: Neto, pointlessly booked a couple of minutes ago, slides in recklessly on Johnson. He’s late, misses the ball, catches his man, and that’s a clear yellow. Off he goes, and he leaves in what Flann O’Brien would describe as “that lofty vehicle, high dudgeon”. A lot of moaning, and it needs the combined diplomatic skills of Bruno Fernandes (!) and Craig Bellamy (!!!) to get him down the tunnel. What a carry-on.

73 min: Neto is booked for clipping Sheehan. Then another chance for Forbs, which is launched miles over the bar from 12 yards.

72 min: The first one having worked pretty well, Portugal make another double change. Off go Vitinha and Dalot, on come Alberto Costa and Ruben Neves.

GOAL! Wales 2-3 Portugal (Forbs 70)

Cabango doesn’t deal with a long ball down the middle, heading it sideways but not out. Forbs picks up possession on the right wing, glides infield, past the flailing Davies, and whistles an unstoppable low shot into the bottom left. Go CF7! CRwho?

68 min: Thomas nearly zips past Dalot down the left but settles for the corner. The set piece is hit long, and Cabango, on the right-hand corner of the six-yard box, aims a downward header towards the bottom right. Costa only just manages to adjust his dive and scoop off the line and around the post in time. What a save! Nothing comes of the second corner. How on earth is this still 2-2?

66 min: Davies and Colwill make a royal horlicks of a one-two just outside the Wales box. Two miscontrols followed by both men falling over each other in the silent-movie style. Neto takes the ball and breaks into the box down the right, before bobbling a shot across Ward and wide left.

65 mix: João Félix tries to chop his way past Sheehan just by the Wales D and is chopped down for his trouble. Portugal don’t get the free kick, though, and they’re fuming.

64 min: Wales make a double change. Brooks and James make way for Johnson and, making his debut as a 29-year-old, the MK Usurpers striker Aaron Collins.

62 min: Sheehan is booked for a tug on João Félix. Meanwhile the heavens open in biblical style. It is hosing down.

61 min: Wilson has another go at finding the top-left corner with a curler from distance. High and wide again, but not by much.

60 min: Portugal make a double change, replacing Ramos and Trincão with João Félix and Carlos ‘CF7’ Forbs.

59 min: Pedro Neto turns on the jets to blaze past three red shirts down the inside-right channel. He reaches the Wales area and lashes a low drive across Ward and off the base of the left-hand post. So close to a sensational solo effort!

58 min: Wilson grooves into space down the inside-right channel and takes aim for the top left. Always going over, but close enough to get big portions of the crowd going.

56 min: Colwill dinks a pass down the left touchline to release Thomas into acres. Thomas reaches the edge of the box and rolls infield for James, who aims a curler intended for the bottom-left corner. Dalot blocks. Thomas is having a lot of joy on this left wing.

55 min: More of the Portuguese patience.

53 min: Trincão goes into the book for a cynical tug on the shirt of James.

51 min: Ramos advances down the left and is crudely stopped in his tracks by Cabango, who hasn’t taken long to go into the book. That was clumsy, and Ramos was lucky Cabango didn’t connect knee on fruitbowl. Nothing comes of the resulting free kick.

50 min: Fernandes elegantly spins into space down the left but can’t find Ramos on the penalty spot. Wales nearly opened up there.

49 min: It’s all Portugal in terms of possession. Again. They’re pinging the ball around with a bit more pace, in the manner of a team who have been given the half-time what-for.

47 min: Fernandes cuts the corner back up the right touchline … nowhere near any of his team-mates. Very strange, and that’s released the pressure on Wales in extremely embarrassing fashion. The home fans enjoyed that.

46 min: Colwill is dispossessed a tad too easily and Portugal flow forward. Neto wins a corner off Sheehan. Fernandes to take from the right.

Portugal get the second half started. Wales have made one change: Cabango is on for Rodon, reportedly a precautionary measure.

Half-time entertainment. Wales fans! Get your Joe Jordan-infused schadenfreude here …

… but we’re a broad church at Guardian sport, and we want Scotland fans to have something enjoyable to read too.

Meanwhile in the other game in A4, it’s a half-time score of Denmark 2-1 Norway. Pierre-Emile Højbjerg and Rasmus Højlund put the hosts two up in short order in Copenhagen, but Erling Haaland scored his 67th goal for Norway on his 61st appearance to reduce the arrears. As things stand, this group will end Portugal, Denmark, Norway, Wales.

HALF TIME: Wales 2-2 Portgual

… nothing occurs. Turns out we did have enough added time for a goal or two, though. Both teams came so close. This is great.

45 min +3: … and now Wilson comes again, cutting in from the right and curling towards the bottom left. Costa is forced to turn around for a corner. From which …

45 min +2: So here’s the first half in microcosm. Rodon is skinned by Dalot down the left. Ramos meets the cross with a diving header that Davies bravely blocks with his trouser arrangement. The ball breaks to Wilson in the centre circle. Wilson spots Costa out of his box and goes for goal. His long-range rake beats Costa, but just clears the crossbar and lands on the top of the net! So close to an all-timer!

45 min +1: Portugal pass it around patiently and professionally, the spoilsports.

45 min: There will be two additional minutes. Plenty of time for another goal each.

GOAL! Wales 2-2 Portugal (Neto 43)

Trincão dribbles purposefully down the left and crosses long. The ball sails over Dasilva, and Neto can’t miss when the ball drops to him, a couple of yards out. Bing bong.

42 min: Portugal should have gone 2-1 up; instead they’re now 2-1 down. This is fun.

GOAL! Wales 2-1 Portugal (Thomas 40)

Neto dribbles in from the right. He tees up Neves on the penalty spot. Neves shoots. Blocked. Ward claims. Wales counter immediately, and Thomas is sent racing into space down the inside-left channel. He enters the box, steps across the line of Neto so the Portugal man can’t make a tackle or intercept, opens his body, and steers into the bottom right! Bing bong!
