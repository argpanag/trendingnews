WTA Beijing Day 6 Predictions Including Gauff vs Sun

TheTools · 04.10.2026 21:47 · source · permalink

The fourth round at WTA Beijing promises even more entertaining tennis, with a number of the world’s best set to take to the courts in the Chinese capitalm including Donna Vekic vs Iga Swiatek. As always, we at LWOT will offer predictions for every match on the slate, but who will book their spot in the last eight?

WTA Beijing Day 6 Predictions

Jelena Ostapenko vs Elise Mertens

Head-to-head: Ostapenko 1-0 Mertens

Jelena Ostapenko doesn’t do quiet, and her second-round clash with Elise Mertens won’t be either. The Latvian arrives fresh from a nerve-jangling escape against Paula Badosa, clawing back from 5-3 down in both sets to win 7-6 (5) 7-5. That’s the Ostapenko special: swing hard, live with the chaos, and trust the firepower to bail her out when the margins get tight.

With a 1-0 head-to-head lead, albeit from a meeting back in 2019, she’ll fancy her chances of dictating from the first ball, because if she gets the first strike in, Mertens is usually left scrambling.

But Mertens thrives on a rival’s wobble. The Belgian came through her own rollercoaster against Katie Volynets, racing through the opening set 6-0 before watching a 4-1 second-set lead evaporate, then steadying late to win the decider 7-5. Neither woman looked flawless last time out, which makes this a contest of who tightens up less. Expect Ostapenko to go for the lines and Mertens to make her prove it, one extra ball at a time. If the Latvian’s unforced errors stay in check, she should edge it.

Prediction: Ostapenko in 3

Belinda Bencic vs Ann Li

Head-to-head: Li 1-0 Bencic

Belinda Bencic’s opener at WTA Beijing was a match of two halves. The Swiss dismantled Anastasia Zakharova in the first set, then had to dig deep when Zakharova found her range in the second and served to stay in the contest. Bencic broke straight back and took control of the tiebreak to close out a 6-1 7-6 win. It was a timely result, too, as it was her first match since a first-round exit to Yulia Putintseva at the US Open.

Next up is a tricky one in Ann Li. The American was in cruise control against Ashlyn Krueger, rattling off eight straight games from 4-2 down to lead by a set and 4-0, and she saved eight of nine break points along the way. Li also holds a 1-0 head-to-head edge after beating Bencic in straight sets at Flushing Meadows last year. She is dangerous on this surface and has a habit of unsettling higher-ranked players, so don’t expect an easy ride. Still, Bencic’s cleaner, more aggressive play should be enough to see her through in what could be a closer contest.

Prediction: Bencic in 3

Elina Svitolina vs Maria Sakkari

Head-to-head: Svitolina 3-2 Sakkari

Maria Sakkari arrives in the last 16 in ruthless form. The Greek eased past Storm Hunter 6-2 6-2, reeling off eight straight games, and looks to have shaken off the disappointment of her Singapore quarter-final loss to Talia Gibson. Her forehand is firing, and her serve is holding up, so she’ll fancy her chances against anyone left in the draw.

Elina Svitolina’s route was far bumpier. The Ukrainian dropped the first set 6-1 against Katerina Siniakova before rallying for a 1-6 6-4 6-2 win in just over two hours. She leads the head-to-head 3-2, though it’s level at 1-1 on outdoor hard courts, and all their previous completed meetings went the distance. Expect another tight contest. If Svitolina can avoid another slow start, her consistency and defensive resilience should be enough to edge it, but Sakkari has the firepower to make her work for every point.

Prediction: Svitolina in 3

Coco Gauff vs Sun Xinran

Head-to-head: first meeting

Coco Gauff is through, but it wasn’t comfortable. In her first match since the US Open semi-final loss to Elena Rybakina, the American was broken four times and hit five double faults before pulling away in the decider to beat Camila Osorio 7-6 (4) 4-6 6-2 in two hours and 18 minutes. It was far from her best, but it was enough, and the third set showed the level she can still find when it matters. With a second title of the season in her sights after Cincinnati, Gauff will want a cleaner, sharper performance next time out.

Standing in her way is a 16-year-old with nothing to lose. Sun Xinran shocked Cristina Bucsa 6-4, 7-5, coming back from a second-set breakdown for her first top-50 win. That also made her the first player born in 2010 to reach the third round of a WTA 1000 event. Sun has the momentum and the freedom, but Gauff has the experience, the weapons and the motivation to bounce back after a scrappy opener.

Expect the teenager to swing big and get some early looks, but the likeliest outcome is Gauff tightening up her serve and moving into the last 16.

Prediction: Gauff in 2
