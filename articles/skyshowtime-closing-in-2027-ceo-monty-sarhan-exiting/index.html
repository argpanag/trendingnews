SkyShowtime Closing In 2027; CEO Monty Sarhan Exiting

TheTools · 19.11.2026 10:20 · source · permalink

SkyShowtime‘s lifespan is officially coming to an end.

The European streaming service will close its doors at the end of 2027, with CEO Monty Sarhan leaving as part of the move. SkyShowtime’s CFO, Mark Neil, will become interim General Manager as the company winds down.

Staff were informed of the official decision to close in an internal note from the board of directors this week.

The writing had been on the wall for the Comcast and Skydance-backed service since it emerged two months ago that a strategic review was underway, with options including winding down on the table.

It’s understood shareholders were concerned that SkyShowtime had not turned a profit despite a huge spend on content. Last year, Deadline revealed had invested at least $1B into the service, but it had remained solidly in the red, and ongoing speculation this year suggested that had not changed.

Watch on Deadline

The board appeared to confirm this in its letter to staff. “Since SkyShowtime’s launch, the streaming landscape has evolved significantly,” it read. “Major global platforms have continued to expand their scale and invest heavily in content, technology, and sport.

“These competitive dynamics have made it increasingly challenging for regional players like SkyShowtime to achieve the scale needed for long-term success. Despite the excellent work and dedication of this team, the shareholders have determined that winding down the business is the most appropriate path forward.”

SkyShowtime was launched in 2022 out of Amsterdam as a JV streaming service between Paramount, then ViacomCBS, and Comcast as a way of entering European territories that didn’t have access to their own streamers Paramount+ or Peacock. It combines shows from those two streamers plus Sky Studios and local originals, and has subscribers across 22 European markets.

However, the service is operating in a different landscape to its pandemic era roots, with Paramount now sitting inside the same Skydance tent as Warner Bros. Discovery and Comcast splitting its entertainment services up, with cable networks business Versant now separate to NBCUniversal. UK pay TV business Sky handles Comcast’s investment in SkyShowtime.

Here’s the letter from the board in full.

Dear SkyShowtime Employees,

In September, we shared that SkyShowtime’s shareholders had initiated a review of strategic options, followed by the start of employee consultations in October. That review has now concluded, and the shareholders have made the decision to wind down SkyShowtime’s operations in late 2027.

Since SkyShowtime’s launch, the streaming landscape has evolved significantly. Major global platforms have continued to expand their scale and invest heavily in content, technology, and sport. These competitive dynamics have made it increasingly challenging for regional players like SkyShowtime to achieve the scale needed for long-term success. Despite the excellent work and dedication of this team, the shareholders have determined that winding down the business is the most appropriate path forward.

After over four years leading the SkyShowtime business, Monty Sarhan has agreed with the Board that it is an appropriate time to exit the company. We thank Monty for his commitment and contributions to SkyShowtime’s journey. We are also grateful to Mark Neil, who has agreed to serve as Interim General Manager, overseeing the wind-down operating period.

We are committed to supporting everyone at SkyShowtime throughout this process and sharing information as decisions are implemented. In the meantime, SkyShowtime continues to operate as normal for our members. Employee consultations remain ongoing in accordance with local requirements, and timing and process may vary by country.

We want to thank each of you for your hard work, professionalism, and commitment to SkyShowtime and its members. We are grateful for your contributions and will keep you informed as we move forward.

The SkyShowtime Board
