Important advice issued to anyone disposing of pumpkins after Halloween

TheTools · 01.11.2026 08:56 · source · permalink

Halloween has drawn to a close and many of us will be taking down decorations as Bonfire Night approaches – with Christmas not far behind. If you’re wondering what to do with leftover pumpkins, there are a few important things to know.

While it might seem harmless to leave pumpkins outside to “biodegrade”, charities warn this can actually harm wildlife. The best option is to chop them up and put them in your food waste bin (or compost where appropriate), rather than dumping them in parks, fields or woods.

Epping Forest City of London, a charity working to protect 8,000 acres of ancient woodland in the UK, pleaded with locals: “Once ths spooky season is over, please don’t leave your pumpkins in Epping Forest.

“It might seem like a harmless way to give wildlife an autumn treat, but pumpkins aren’t a natural part of our native wildlife's diet. Pumpkins can make hedgehogs very poorly, and leaving food in the Forest for birds, foxes, badgers and deer can contribute to the spread of disease.”

The charity added: "There’s another reason to take your pumpkin home, too. Carved pumpkins can contain traces of paint, glitter, candles or other decorations, which can pose a risk if animals eat them."

It pointed out how dangerous it is to "introduce leftover food" into natural environments that have their own habitats. For example, pumpkins that are not native and don't belong in the ecosystem.

Plus, dumping old pumpkins in woodland, parks or countryside areas is legally regarded as fly-tipping, which can result in an immediate on-the-spot Fixed Penalty Notice of up to £5,000 following recent government clampdowns on waste crime.

Should the offence be considered serious enough to be heard in a Magistrates' Court, the financial penalty can rise dramatically to a maximum fine of £50,000, alongside a possible 12-month jail term.

Concluding the informative post, the charity added: "So after Halloween, please put your pumpkin in your food waste, rather than leaving it in the forest."

It seems the warning was necessary, as local residents took to Facebook to say they'd already "observed several" pumpkins in the forests.

If you do see one while you're out for a stroll, it is worth clearing it out of forests and green spaces if you are able to do so. After all, something much scarier than Halloween is the thought of harming our beloved British wildlife!

How to properly dispose of a pumpkin

Pumpkins go off quickly once carved, so bin them if you notice any mould, sour smells, slime or fruit flies. You should check your local council guidelines to see what the best disposal method is in your area.

Some councils want pumpkins in food waste caddies, while others allow them in garden waste bins. Meanwhile, some ask residents to put them in general waste if they don’t provide food/garden waste services.

Pumpkins are great for compost bins because they break down fast. Just make sure you chop them down into smaller chunks and mix with brown material to reduce the amount of moisture.

Can you eat leftover pumpkins?

If they are carved, this is generally a bad idea because they go rotten quickly. But if you have any uncarved pumpkins, you're in luck.

While the big carving pumpkins can be watery and less sweet than smaller culinary squash, they do have some tasty uses. The gourds are good in soup, pumpkin curry, mashed, roasted or pureed, just to name a few.

With any leftover seeds, scoop them out, rinse them, toss with oil and seasonings, then bake in the oven. They make for a tasty and nutritious snack.
