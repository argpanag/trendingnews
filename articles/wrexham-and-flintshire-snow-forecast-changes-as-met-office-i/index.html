Wrexham and Flintshire snow forecast changes as Met Office issues latest update

TheTools · 16.11.2026 09:20 · source · permalink

The chances of widespread snow across Wrexham and Flintshire appear to have reduced as forecasters update their predictions for the region.

Snow had been expected to move into North Wales on Monday evening (November 16), with earlier Met Office forecast maps suggesting it could spread across large parts of Wrexham and Flintshire by around 9pm.

Some areas had even been shown under heavier snowfall, with wintry conditions forecast to continue into the early hours of Tuesday morning.

However, the latest forecast now paints a different picture.

The Met Office's UK forecast, updated at 4am on Monday, continues to warn that rain and snow are on the way, but now says snow is expected mainly over northern hills as a band of persistent rain moves in.

This means snow has not disappeared from the forecast altogether, particularly over higher ground, but the latest predictions suggest rain is increasingly likely for lower-lying parts of Wrexham and Flintshire.

In Mold, temperatures are forecast to reach around 6C during Monday before falling to around 3C during the evening.

The chance of precipitation rises sharply after dark, reaching 70 per cent at 8pm, 90 per cent at 9pm and at least 95 per cent from 10pm.

It is a similar picture in Connah's Quay, where temperatures are expected to remain around 3C to 4C overnight, with heavy rain forecast for Tuesday.

There is a greater chance of wintry conditions around Rhosllanerchrugog, where temperatures are forecast to fall to around 2C to 3C tonight as the chance of precipitation increases significantly.

The local forecast gives a minimum temperature of around 1C.

The change comes after forecasts over the weekend indicated snow could begin moving into parts of North Wales from around 6pm on Monday.

Earlier maps showed the wintry weather spreading through the evening across parts of Gwynedd, Conwy, Denbighshire, Wrexham and Flintshire, before the heaviest snowfall moved northwards overnight.

While the threat of widespread low-level snow now appears less pronounced, colder conditions remain part of the outlook.

The Met Office says forecasting snow can be particularly difficult because relatively small changes in temperature or wind direction can determine whether precipitation reaches the ground as rain, sleet or snow.

For Wrexham and Flintshire, the latest forecast points towards a wet Monday night for many, with the greatest chance of wintry conditions likely to be over higher ground rather than widespread snow at lower levels.
