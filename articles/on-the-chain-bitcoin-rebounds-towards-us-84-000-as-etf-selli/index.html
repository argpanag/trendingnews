Bitcoin has rebounded towards US$84,000 as cryptocurrency markets recover from last week's sell-off, with easing geopolitical concerns and a return to positive exchange-traded fund flows helping stabilise sentiment.

The world's largest cryptocurrency was trading around US$83,738 at the latest check, up about 1.0% on the session after moving between US$82,738 and US$84,062.

Bitcoin has recovered from lows around US$80,400 reached last week but remains well below the US$86,000-plus levels seen at the beginning of October.

The rebound follows an improvement in risk sentiment after concerns about an immediate escalation between the United States and Iran eased, taking some pressure off oil prices and the US dollar.

Heavy selling pressure

Bitcoin had come under heavy selling pressure as rising energy prices and Treasury yields revived fears that inflation could remain elevated and keep US interest rates higher for longer.

Institutional flows are also showing tentative signs of stabilising.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs attracted around US$21 million of net inflows in the latest completed trading session, ending two consecutive days of substantial withdrawals.

Those previous sessions saw approximately US$729 million pulled from Bitcoin ETFs, including nearly US$485 million in a single day.

The return to positive territory is modest compared with the billions of dollars that flowed into Bitcoin funds during late September, but it removes one source of immediate selling pressure.

BlackRock's IBIT accounted for the largest positive flow in the latest session, while Fidelity continued to experience a small withdrawal.

The broader cryptocurrency market has also moved higher.

Other cryptos

Ethereum was trading around US$2,532, up about 1% over 24 hours as it continued its recovery from levels below US$2,500 last week.

Solana was around US$111, gaining approximately 0.8%, although it remains sharply below the US$120-plus levels reached earlier in October.

XRP was comparatively subdued at around US$1.40, trading close to flat over the latest 24-hour period and remaining below the US$1.50 level it held at the beginning of last week.

Despite the recovery, Bitcoin remains around one-third below its October 2025 record above US$126,000 and continues to trade largely in response to macroeconomic developments.

US Treasury yields, the dollar, oil prices and expectations for Federal Reserve policy have increasingly replaced crypto-specific catalysts as the dominant short-term influences on the market.

For Bitcoin, the first challenge is reclaiming the US$84,000-US$85,000 region.
