Max Verstappen says George Russell 'so far below his level' after poking fun at rival for major error during Las Vegas GP

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:57 · source · permalink

Red Bull's Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix and hit out at George Russell after the Mercedes driver said "I'm not going accept somebody to screw me over" after the pair collided in final practice; Russell crashed during Sunday's race and finished down in ninth

Sunday 22 November 2026 07:53, UK

Max Verstappen says George Russell is "so far below my level" in a scathing response to the Mercedes driver after winning the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Verstappen claimed his fourth win of the season after a stunning defensive drive while Russell crashed in the latter stages and finished ninth, having battled Verstappen for the win earlier.

The pair collided in final practice on Saturday with Verstappen warned for "driving erratically" in the incident but Russell criticised the Red Bull driver.

"There's nothing in the rules to say you cannot overtake someone who is starting a lap but when you do this, you are screwing this person over and he does this week in, week out with many drivers and everybody accepts it," he told Sky Italy.

"I'm not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they're bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody's got to stand up to that guy."

Verstappen was asked about the comments in the post-race press conference following his third Las Vegas Grand Prix victory and hit back at Russell.

He said: "The thing is, he speaks a lot next to the track. And honestly, for me, it doesn't really bother me because he's so far below my level that it doesn't really make any sense for me anyway to try and challenge that. So, it doesn't bother me.

"And today, karma is a b****. It was honestly fantastic when I looked in my mirror and I saw what was happening [when Russell crashed]. I was like, 'fantastic, my day is only getting better.'

"And of course, when I then crossed the line in first, my day was complete."

Verstappen: Finish would have been tight without Antonelli issue

The battery-sensitive power units and significant slipstream effect created an unusual style of racing, with the field tightly bunched and the leader unable to build a meaningful gap.

Verstappen, however, remained consistently in the top two and revealed that Mercedes pair Russell and Kimi Antonelli had worked out they could pass the Dutchman into Turn 14, potentially setting themselves up to win the race on the final lap.

However, Antonelli dropped from second to fifth on the penultimate lap due to a deployment issue, allowing Verstappen to take a relatively comfortable victory ahead of McLaren duo Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri.

"You put a lot of scenarios in your head and unfortunately with Kimi something went wrong on his side, so we never really got that fight to the line because I'm pretty sure it was going to be a fight to the line," Verstappen told Sky Sports F1.

"We did well. I wanted to try and stay in the lead instead of dropping back in the pack and making sure we always had the deployment correct to re-overtake when I would be overtake into Turn 14.

"And at the same time, making sure you could be late on the brakes, sending it up the inside or outside, just defending that leading position."

McLaren's double podiums means they have cut their deficit to Red Bull in the battle for third in the Constructors' Championship to 17 points with two rounds remaining in Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

Verstappen said: "We've had a lot of strong races and some races where we have to learn still and do better but that's fine. That's what we are trying to do heading into next year.

"At the same time, there's still a big battle for us for P3 in the Constructors' Championship. In that sense today was not a good day, even with the win, so we have a fight on our hands in the last two rounds."
