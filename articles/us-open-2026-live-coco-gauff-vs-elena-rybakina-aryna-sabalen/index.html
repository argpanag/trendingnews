  2. Can 'different mindset' carry Gauff to final?published at 01:58 BST
    01:58 BST

    Gauff v Rybakina

    Former US Open champion Gauff will have the home crowd's backing tonight at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

    The 22-year-old American, who won the Flushing Meadows title in 2023 but suffered fourth-round exits in the following two years, said she has a different perspective to this year's tournament.

    "Sometimes you're just so focused on the now, now, now, because you want it now, and obviously you want to take advantage of your opportunities," she said.

    "In the grand scheme of things, [I'm] definitely sitting here in a different mindset and perspective than I was last year.

    "It inspires me a little bit more to keep working, as I see how much I have improved in a year."

    Gauff leads the head-to-head 2-1, but Australian Open champion Elena Rybakina fought back from a set down to triumph in last month's Canadian Open semi-finals.

    "I'm going to have some aces hit on me and some winners hit on me, but I'm going to keep fighting and give it my all. That's all I can do," Gauff said.

    Gauff v Rybakina

    By reaching the semi-finals, second seed Elena Rybakina will become the new world number one on Monday - ending Aryna Sabalenka's 99-week reign at the top.

    But the second seed has described the top ranking as "just a number" and stressed that her focus remains focused on winning her third Grand Slam title.

    "It's never quite the same if you become world number one having lost a Grand Slam final or semi-final just beforehand, so she will be trying to cap off an absolutely perfect couple of weeks by winning the title," tennis correspondent Russell Fuller said on BBC Radio 5 Live.

  7. 'I couldn't fail in front of all of those legends'published at 01:50 BST
    01:50 BST

    Sabalenka 7-5 6-2 Pegula

    Aryna Sabalenka, speaking on court after beating Jessica Pegula to reach the US Open final: "No complaints [about her performance], I am just super happy that I was able to pull off such good tennis.

    "Last time I played against Jessica, it was almost impossible for me to get the win so I am super happy to play such tennis, in front of you all. Thank you for the experience."

    To the crowd: "You guys are the best and your respect for me is priceless, I know you wanted her [Pegula] to win but thank you for being so respectful to me.

    "I was so desperate to win, I wanted it badly and I was just trying to talk to myself. Look at my box, I couldn't fail in front of all of those legends, beautiful girls and my team."

    On her rituals before a match: "I do have rituals, I have my favourite breakfast and dinner that has to be done. Just because I have my ritual, they really squeezed me in, my routine means a lot for me."

    Hewett/Reid 4-6 6-3 10-8 Fernandez/Oda

    British duo Alfie Hewett and Gordon Reid have just reached the final of the men's wheelchair doubles, beating Gustavo Fernandez and Tokito Oda in a match tie-break.

    Hewett and Reid are going for a sixth US Open doubles title together as a partnership. They'll face Spain's Martin de la Puente and Dutch player Ruben Spaargaren in the final.

    Hewett, the second seed, defeated American Casey Ratzlaff 6-2 6-0 to reach the last four.
