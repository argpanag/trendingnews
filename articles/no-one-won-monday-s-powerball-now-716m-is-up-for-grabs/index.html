Here’s what a winner would actually take home, plus the odds and the biggest jackpots ever won.

Someone could win an estimated $716 million in Wednesday's Powerball drawing after no ticket matched all six numbers on Monday.

The jackpot has gone for more than 30 drawings without a winner, since Aug. 12, when a single ticket sold in Illinois won $1.04 billion. 

Monday still produced a couple of big winners. Two tickets, one sold in Florida and one in Georgia, matched all five white balls and won $1 million each.

What are the odds of winning Powerball?

The odds of winning a Powerball jackpot are 1 in 292.2 million.

To put that into perspective, the National Weather Service estimates the odds of being struck by lightning in a given year at about 1 in 1.2 million. That means you're roughly 240 times more likely to be struck than to win the jackpot with a single ticket.

A ticket is far more likely to win a smaller prize. The overall odds of winning any prize are 1 in 24.9.

What are the biggest Powerball wins?

The most recent jackpot winner, who chose to remain anonymous, won $1.04 billion on Aug. 12, the eighth-largest Powerball prize since the game began in 1992. Illinois law allows lottery winners of over $250,000 to keep their identities hidden. 

The largest jackpot ever won, $2.04 billion, went to Edwin Castro, who bought the winning ticket at Joe's Service Center in Altadena, Calif., in 2022. Castro has generally kept a low profile, but the Wall Street Journal reported in 2025 that he spent about $10 million on 15 lots in his hometown after devastating wildfires in January 2025.

How much could the $716 million Powerball jackpot winner actually take home?

There are two ways for potential winners to claim a jackpot — either in a one-time lump-sum payment or an annuity paid out over 29 years. A jackpot winner who selects the annuity will receive one immediate payment followed by 29 annual payments that increase by 5% each year, the Powerball website states. However, both options are subject to federal and jurisdictional taxes.

The $716 million figure is for the annuity option, paid in yearly installments. The lump sum cash value is $307.2 million, according to the Powerball website.

Lump sum winners will then face a 24% federal withholding tax plus additional federal taxes from being in the top tax bracket, bringing the total to as high as 37%. A lump-sum winner would be left with roughly $193.5 million before state taxes.
