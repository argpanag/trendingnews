Realistic returns after reported trade buzz around Blue Jays' Clement, Valenzuela, Lukes and Smith

TheTools · 16.11.2026 22:00 · source · permalink

The Toronto Blue Jays haven't made a major signing yet (as of Monday, Nov. 16) but the rumors on the trade market are starting to heat up. Between Mitch Bannon of The Athletic and MLB Trade Rumors, it's being reported that Brandon Valenzuela, Ernie Clement, Nathan Lukes and Josh Smith are names that teams are either asking about, or who could be pushed out to make room for alternative additions.

They are looking for an infield upgrade with Jazz Chisholm Jr., Brandon Lowe or Ketel Marte being mentioned. They are also chasing an outfield power bat like Seiya Suzuki,
and want bullpen help. But among those four Blue Jays mentioned a likely return would be something along the lines of a cost-controlled reliever, a near-ready prospect or a useful bench piece that complements what they already have.

Can the Blue Jays fix some of their holes by trading Ernie Clement, Brandon Valenzuela, Josh Smith or Nathan Lukes?

What is being said about Clement and Smith

Clement hit .182 with a .442 OPS and one home run over 51 games after the All-Star break. His defense slipped too, with -2 Defensive Runs Saved at second base and -5 Outs Above Average. Clement is out of options and projected for a $7.6-million salary in arbitration.

Meantime, Smith is the least likely of the four to move, but similar to Clement he could find himself in a roster squeeze. He hit .184/.276/.408 as a Blue Jay but Sportsnet's Ben Nicholson-Smith reports Smith played well enough to put himself in the club's 2027 plans. He's projected for $3.7 million in arbitration.

Bannon says, “Any legitimate infield addition makes it hard for Ernie Clement and Josh Smith to stick on the roster, as only one could slide into a utility role...but if they’re later displaced by a signing or trade, plenty of teams could pounce on a versatile infielder — the Dodgers and Detroit Tigers, for example.”

Dodgers

There are two targets that stand out for the Blue Jays from the Dodgers. Ronan Kopp is a 6-foot-7 lefty who struck out 51 batters in 46.1 innings at Triple-A but also walked 43 and posted a 6.80 ERA, so he's more of a lottery ticket than a sure thing.

Zach Ehrhard (OF, No. 15 prospect) hit .291/.400/.472 with 20 home runs and 28 steals at Triple-A. MLB.com projects him on the Dodgers' 2027 bench, which could make him harder to pry loose.

Tigers

The Tigers have several left handed pitching options in their bullpen for Toronto to look at. Drew Sommers is a 26-year-old who posted a 3.63 ERA and a 2.57 FIP with 62 strikeouts in 39.2 innings. There's also lefty reliever Brant Hurter who could be a very useful return. He posted a 3.06 ERA, and .189 BAA in 35.1 innings pitched.

What is being said about Valenzuela

The 26-year-old had an encouraging debut season, getting into 91 games and hitting .222/.307/.372. He also drew strong grades for his glovework and was worth 2.1 fWAR. Bannon notes the Reds have shown interest in the past. The Blue Jays could keep him behind Alejandro Kirk, or they could flip him for a controllable arm and then find a backup on the free-agent market.

If the Reds, who are losing catcher Tyler Stephenson to free agency, are interested in Valenzuela there are some names for Toronto to consider. Spencer Steer works. The right-handed utility man hit .238 with 16 home runs in 98 games. That's more power than Smith and Clement currently bring to the table.

Carlos Jorge is a prospect to watch. The 23-year-old left-handed-hitting outfielder hit .308/.376/.427 with 33 stolen bases between Double-A and Triple-A before a September call-up, and he's regarded as an elite defender in center field. The Blue Jays have talked about potentially finding someone to compliment Brett Bateman at that position, though they'd ideally like a right-handed hitter. Still, this skill set is tough to pass over.

What is being said about Nathan Lukes

With Anthony Santander and Addison Barger due back from injury, Lukes could be the odd man out. Lukes hit a respectable .277/.327/.387 with 10 home runs and 41 RBI in 125 games. But he's 32, and he's much better against right-handers (.744 OPS) than lefties (.493).

As for who could want him, the Phillies, Diamondbacks and Astros are targeting left-handed bats in the outfield. They probably want more slug than Lukes offers, but he would come cheap.

Astros

Miguel Ullola is an interesting arm. The 24-year-old right-hander was moved to the bullpen this year where he struck out nearly 12 batters per nine in the minors, while his fastball touched 97.6 mph.

Alimber Santa posted a 1.42 ERA in 19 Triple-A innings before his call-up. In his first 10 games where he pitched 15.1 innings, Santa posted a 1.17 with a 3.95 FIP.

Both are right-handed options though, but based on the way the Blue Jays' pitching depth was depleted last year, getting guys with MLB experience will always be an asset.

Phillies

Carson DeMartini could be a name to keep in mind. The 23-year-old left-handed hitter has yet to play above Double-A but has the kind of profile the Blue Jays covet. The 2B/3B prospect posted a walk rate of 19.6% with a .429 OBP last season.

Diamondbacks

Mitch Bratt (Diamondbacks No. 12 prospect ) reached the majors in 2026 and would be the ideal target. The lefty struck out 30 big league batters in his first 10 starts (44 innings pitched) to begin his career.

By themselves these four names aren't fetching blockbuster deals, but could be very useful in any move the Blue Jays make to solidify the building blocks to reaching contender status again.
