Quentin Tarantino's New "Swashbuckling" Project Officially Confirms Premiere Date

TheTools · 12.10.2026 19:42 · source · permalink

There are few people in Hollywood as publicly obsessed with movies as Quentin Tarantino. From Pulp Fiction to Kill Bill to Inglourious Basterds and beyond, his oeuvre can be read as one long tribute to the tropes, trends, and aesthetics that have defined Hollywood movies over the past 100 years, and his devotees wouldn't have it any other way.

That's why it's a bit surprising that Tarantino's next project won't be a movie, but a stage play. Set in Europe in the 1830s, The Popinjay Cavalier is a "rambunctious tale of deception and disguise...inspired by the grand adventure epics and sparkling romances of stage and screen," according to the official description. It follows "Rupert Sebastian Thomas Jr., the world’s greatest actor, as he heads to Paris to perform for the illustrious Comédie-Française. But after a few ill-advised words about the French land him in hot water, Rupert the Magnificent’s triumphant trip across the Channel takes a decidedly more dangerous turn."

Tarantino chose Borat star Sacha Baron Cohen to play the leading man in this comedy, which Variety reports will hold previews starting February 23, 2027 at the Phoenix Theatre in London's West End. Opening night is set for March 18. The Popinjay Cavalier will run for three months, closing on June 13. You can get tickets on the play's website.

"I’m keen with anticipation to give the West End a great night out," Tarantino said in a short statement. Sacha Baron Cohen released a longer statement of the kind you might expect from the man who just starred in Ali G: Who Iz I?

Quentin called, I said yes before he even got to say his first ‘motherf—er’. When the best director in the world calls, you don’t ask questions. I am beyond delighted that we will be losing our West End virginity together, I have made him promise not to be gentle.

Although Cohen is best known for his comedic faux-documentaries, he's also an accomplished dramatic actor who received an Oscar nomination for his role in The Trial of the Chicago 7. He should have the range to do whatever Tarantino asks of him in The Popinjay Cavalier.

It sounds like Tarantino had a good time on set, if this joint statement from producers Tom Rothman and Sonia Friedman is anything to go by: "To see Quentin in action in a rehearsal room is extraordinary," they said. "His delight in working with actors, fencers, acrobats, musicians and our creative team is infectious, and there is such pleasure in hearing his delicious language come alive and discovering what his world can become on stage."

As for when Tarantino will ever return to direct another movie, it's unclear. He pulled the plug on his film The Movie Critic during pre-production, since he wasn't "excited" enough by the script. By Tarantino's count, The Movie Critic would have been his tenth film, and he vowed to stop making movies after directing 10. Perhaps directing theater is a way around that rule.

In the meantime, other directors and producers are picking up his slack. Sony is working on a crossover movie between Django Unchained with Zorro. Meanwhile, Tarantino wrote the script for The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, a follow-up to his 2019 film Once Upon a Time in Hollywood starring Brad Pitt in the title role. David Fincher directed the film, which will screen in select cinemas on November 25 before becoming available to stream on Netflix on December 23.
