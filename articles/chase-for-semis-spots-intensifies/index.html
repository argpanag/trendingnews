Chase for semis spots intensifies

TheTools · 27.11.2026 22:01 · source · permalink
  • UAAP Final Four Race
  • UAAP Season 89 men’s basketball
  • La Salle vs FEU basketball

JACOB Cortez is expected to power the La Salle Green Archers to a twice-to-beat advantage in the Final Four of the UAAP Season 89 men’s basketball tournament.

Photograph by Joey Sanchez Mendoza for DAILY TRIBUNE

Defending champion De La Salle University and Far Eastern University (FEU) meet in an all-important clash to book the third Final Four seat as the University Athletic Association of the Philippines (UAAP) Season 89 men’s basketball tournament elimination round comes to a close today at the Smart Araneta Coliseum.

Both squads carry similar 8-5 win-loss records in third but University of Santo Tomas (UST) is lurking behind at 8-6, waiting for the loser in a playoff for the fourth and last ticket to the next round.

So, the 2:30 p.m. clash is a must-win for the Green Archers and Tamaraws, or they risk a dangerous sudden-death match against the Growling Tigers.

The winner will not only secure a semis berth but also vie for the Final Four twice-to-beat advantage in a playoff against the loser between University of the Philippines and National University in the 5 p.m. main game.

Playoffs for the No. 4 semis spot and the last semis incentive are on Wednesday at the Philsports Arena in Pasig City.

FEU is on a two-game roll with huge wins over semis-bound Fighting Maroons and Bulldogs.

The Tamaraws are now just a win away from ending a four-season Final Four drought and first playoffs entry under head coach Sean Chambers.

“It’s been a three-year process. We’re not there yet, but we like where we’re heading,” Chambers said of his now mature core.

“Now, we’ve put ourselves in a good situation of controlling our own destiny. That game on Saturday is still a must-win. At the end of the day, in order for us to belong, we have to beat NU, La Salle, UP, to show that we’re actually getting to the level of respect that we’re trying to get at FEU and try to bring back the glory days,” the third-year mentor added.

Veteran guard Jorick Bautista has been stepping up in his leadership role for the past few games. Chambers expects the same from his court general to replicate FEU’s 90-78 win over La Salle in the first round.

“I’ve actually gotten mad at Jorick because I’ve honestly told Jorick sometimes I think he’s too nice. Sometimes when my other guys, Janrey (Pasaol) and Kirby (Mongcopa), are playing out of the system, he needs to be the voice on the court to let them know this is not okay,” he said.

“But the expectations of where we’re at right now, I’m beyond grateful.”
