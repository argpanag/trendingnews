Preview: Utah Jazz End Back-to-Back With Matchup Against Lakers  | Utah Jazz

TheTools · 19.11.2026 03:00 · source · permalink

The Utah Jazz are spending a few days in the City of Angels.

Utah began a back-to-back set Tuesday night with a matchup against the LA Clippers. Led by hot shot-making from the likes of Brice Sensabaugh (40 points) and Keyonte George (30 points, 11 assists), the Jazz got back in the win column with a 132-107 victory. Utah dished out a season-high 35 assists and knocked down 20 3-pointers, but it was also stingy defense in the fourth quarter that helped push the visitors to a much-needed win. 

“Tonight’s game is a culmination of the team’s work and them sticking together and improving every day,” Jazz head coach Will Hardy said Tuesday night. I’m really proud of the team… the fabric of the locker room is in a really good place right now.” 

It will be a quick turnaround for Will Hardy’s squad, as they will take on the Los Angeles Lakers Wednesday night. The Lakers will also be on the second night of a back-to-back, having hosted the Charlotte Hornets on Tuesday.

The Lakers have thrived out of the gates to begin the season, racing to a 13-2 record that has been fueled in large part by the league’s top-ranked offense. Luka Dončić has dominated in the early going, averaging 39 points and 7.8 assists per game heading into Tuesday’s action. Although Austin Reaves has missed time as of late, he has provided Dončić with an effective running mate (24.7 points and 4.7 assists per game), while former Jazzman Walker Kessler has helped LA’s interior presence (14.5 points, 10.9 rebounds, and 2.2 blocks per game).

The Lakers won the first meeting between the two teams 132-123 back on Oct. 25. 

Here are three keys to Wednesday’s game.

The possession battle 

The Lakers have several key strengths and weaknesses as it relates to the possession game.

Los Angeles has thrived defensively when it comes to forcing turnovers, leading the league in opponent turnover percentage (18.7 percent). However, on the other end, the Lakers have struggled to take care of the ball, ranking 25th in their own turnover percentage.

Meanwhile, on the boards, the Lakers thrive on the offensive glass (32 percent offensive rebound percentage, good for seventh in the league) but have struggled to end defensive possessions (29th in defensive rebound percentage), which could create opportunities for Utah.

The Jazz have struggled in the possession game as a result of ball security issues and a lack of consistent production on the offensive glass, but Wednesday’s game could provide some opportunities for Hardy’s squad. 

The 3-point line 

As expected with a Dončić-led team, the Lakers have had success from deep. 

Los Angeles ranks 12th in frequency of 3-point attempts (41 percent) but fourth in percentage at 38.7 percent. In the first meeting, the Lakers went 20-of-47 from downtown, which helped them claw out of an early hole to gain a road victory. 

However, there will be opportunities for Utah’s shooters to try and find a rhythm Wednesday night. Just over 44 percent of opponent shot attempts have come from three against the Lakers this season, the third-highest frequency. Teams have also connected on 36.9 percent of those looks, which places Los Angeles in the bottom eight of the association.

In a matchup against a high-powered offense, the Jazz will need to be aggressive from three Wednesday night. In Tuesday's win over the Clippers, Utah knocked down 20-of-38 (52.6 percent), which fueled a large chunk of the offensive production.

The transition game 

What makes a matchup against the Lakers offense so dangerous is that JJ Redick’s squad thrives in both the halfcourt and in transition.

Los Angeles owns the league’s top-ranked halfcourt offense yet also ranks fifth in points per transition play. The Lakers also look to run often, ranking sixth in the frequency of possessions that begin with a transition play. As Los Angeles continues to force a ton of turnovers, easier scoring opportunities on the fast break become available to further push the offense forward.

In the first meeting back in October, the Jazz turned it over 17 times and saw themselves outscored 18-4 on the fast break. A repeat performance would spell trouble Wednesday night.

Wednesday’s game tips off at 8 p.m. MT. Tune in to the pregame show with Alema Harrington and Mike Smith beginning at 7:30 p.m. MT on Jazz+!
