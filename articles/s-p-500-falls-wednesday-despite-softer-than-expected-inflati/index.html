S&P 500 falls Wednesday despite softer-than-expected inflation data; index posts losing September: Live updates

TheTools · 29.09.2026 22:04 · source · permalink

The S&P 500 slid on Wednesday, the final day of September, even after new U.S. economic data showed inflation slowed last month.

The broad-market index dropped 0.25%, giving up its gains from earlier in the trading day and closing at 7,651.54. The S&P 500 was up as much as nearly 0.7% at one point during the session. The Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.24% to end at 26,861.06. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.86%, or 443.87 points, to 50,906.05.

The personal consumption expenditures price index for August increased at an annual rate of 3.4%, down from 3.7% the month prior. Economists surveyed by Dow Jones had been looking for inflation to remain steady at 3.7%. Even more encouraging was core PCE, excluding food and energy, which rose 3% year on year, down from 3.3% the month before and also lower than economists had forecast.

The 10-year Treasury ended Wednesday's trading higher on the day, last up about 4 basis points at 5.298%. The benchmark yield surpassed 5.3% at its session high. The 30-year Treasury yield was last up nearly 5 basis points to 5.642%.

"I'm not seeing that inflation expectations are really blowing out, so I think it's really the growth number [that traders are eyeing]... you've got a fairly hot Q3 Atlanta Fed GDP expectation [and] we got a hotter Q2 GDP expectation, and so that's probably pushing out longer-term real rate opportunities," Rob Haworth, senior investment strategy director at U.S. Bank Asset Management Group, told CNBC.

The market turned its attention to the jobs reports, which is slated to come out on Friday. Non-farm payrolls are expected to have risen by 84,000 in September. That's about half of those added in August on a seasonally adjusted basis.

Odds of a Fed rate hike eased on Wednesday. The CME Group's FedWatch tool shows traders are pricing in a 35% chance of a quarter-point rate hike next month. That's down from about 51% a day ago. Traders are still expecting another hike in December despite the light inflation data Wednesday.

"While today's inflation data is somewhat better than expected, strong labor and GDP data suggest the print is unlikely to derail consensus expectations for another rate hike before the end of the year," Adam Hetts, global head of multi-asset and portfolio manager at Janus Henderson Investors, said Wednesday in an email.

"It's still 75% odds of three hikes by the time we get to the middle of next year, so the market doesn't think this is quite behind us yet, but it's maybe not as urgent an issue as as they feared," U.S. Bank Asset Management Group's Haworth said.

Strong private sector data offset the light inflation report with ADP saying 90,000 jobs were added in September, much better than the 68,000 consensus estimate from economists.

Wednesday marked the end of the month and the third quarter. In September, the S&P 500 dropped 0.5%, while the Dow fell 4.3%. The Nasdaq outperformed, rising 1.9% during the month. In the third quarter, the S&P 500 advanced 2%, while the Nasdaq gained 2.5%. The Dow underperformed and lost 2.7% on the quarter.

The S&P 500 ended the session lower on Wednesday, completely erasing its gains from earlier in the session.

The broad-market index closed down 0.25% at 7,651.54. The Nasdaq Composite finished 0.24% higher at 26,861.06. The Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 0.86% to settle at 50,906.05.

Barclays downgraded Mobileye to Equal Weight from Overweight, cutting its price target to $9 from $14, citing limited progress securing larger advanced driver-assistance wins and growing uncertainty around competition and execution.

Analyst Dan Levy said Mobileye's near-term results have remained resilient, but the bigger proof points needed to support its longer-term growth story is yet to materialize.

"While we appreciate that near-term results have been resilient, we see a number of overhangs that are likely to persist, thus limiting the potential for multiple re-rating. Specifically, alongside the lack of larger advanced content wins, China pressure is expanding internationally amidst the sharp rise of exports, questions have emerged around the opportunities with VW, and MBLY's CEO transition adds uncertainty at a strategic crossroads," Levy said in his note.

Mobileye shares closed $7.57 on Tuesday.

Michael Burry, the investor famous for shorting the housing market prior to the Great Financial Crisis, took to social media on Tuesday to blast the frontier algorithm companies at the heart of the AI boom.

"For the benefit of humanity, the markets should tank hard and prevent the OpenAI and Anthropic IPOs," he wrote in a Tuesday post on X.

Anthropic is likely to push its IPO until after the November midterm elections, according to Reuters. It's not clear when OpenAI will go public as the company says it's dealing with product safety concerns.

In another Tuesday post, Burry said Anthropic and OpenAI are poised to destroy trillions of dollars' worth of invested capital

"These are companies that are going suck up and then destrop [sic] TRILLIONS of dollars of capital, and that will be the least of the damage they do," he wrote.

Burry is making active financial bets against the AI boom, according to his recent newsletter posts. He's been shorting names like Micron and Palantir. This week, he closed out some of his short positions and replaced them with put options, giving him additional leverage for his bets while also moving up the implied time horizon for when he thinks the trade could sour.

Some of his moves this were to shrink his tax liability, he said, but it was because he thinks "the bubble in AI may burst sooner than later."

Crude oil prices rallied in September after a drone attack on Saudi Arabia's critical East-West pipeline raised fears of another major supply disruption.

Brent crude futures, the international benchmark, have gained more than 14% this month to trade near $104 per barrel. U.S. crude is up more than 6% to trade above $91 per barrel.

U.S. crude is trading at a steeper discount to Brent than usual at least in part due to talk of a potential diesel export ban, analysts say. An export ban would likely force U.S. refiners to cut production and demand less crude.

The Saudis closed the pipeline on Sept. 10 after it sustained damage in the attack which was launched from Iraq. However, export loadings at the Red Sea port of Yanbu have jumped in a sign that the pipeline is operating again.

Even with inflation still well above the Federal Reserve's 2% target, Goldman Sachs thinks the central bank will raise benchmark interest rates again in December and then stop.

Fresh data Wednesday from the Fed's preferred gauge showed core inflation in August running at a 3% annual rate — elevated, but considerably lower than the July rate and one Goldman economists think will hold through the end of the year.

New York Fed President John Williams on Tuesday said the Fed can afford to be patient before hiking again. With both the data and influential central banker's comments in mind, "we now think that an October hike is unlikely," Goldman chief economist Jan Hatzius said in a note.

"We are pushing back the second hike in our forecast to December, and we see a strong chance that the [Federal Open Market Committee] will ultimately conclude that additional rate hikes are unnecessary," he added.

The call is well out of consensus. Even with traders also pricing in the next hike in December, they also see a total of three or four increases ahead over the next 12 months or so, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool.

—Jeff Cox

China's factory activity returned to growth in September as a deepening economic malaise prompts policymakers to ramp up stimulus steps and bolster growth.

The official manufacturing purchasing managers' index rose to 50.1 from 49.8 in August, National Bureau of Statistics data showed Wednesday, in line with analysts' forecast in a Reuters poll.

The modest expansion was driven by accelerated activity in equipment and high-tech-related manufacturing, as well as consumer industries, according to NBS chief statistician Huo Lihui.

The non-manufacturing PMI also returned to expansionary territory, climbing to 50.2, as business activity picked up in the services sector, and reached their highest level this year in the construction sector.

Manufacturers in the country have benefited from the AI hardware boom, while weak consumer demand at home has been a major worry, with higher energy costs owed to the Middle East war weighing on margins.

—Anniek Bao
