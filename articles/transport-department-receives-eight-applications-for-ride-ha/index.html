TD receives eight applications for ride-hailing service licences with positive response

The Transport Department (TD) announced today (October 30) that the application period for ride-hailing service licences ended at noon today. A total of eight applications were received during the application period, reflecting a positive response from the industry.

An assessment committee formed by representatives from the Transport and Logistics Bureau, the Digital Policy Office and the TD will assess the applications received in detail. The assessment will cover ride-hailing vehicle fleet management, booking platform services, service commencement plans, service commitments, customer service, experience in operating ride-hailing platform services, financial capability and capital investment, as well as detailed plans for implementation of due diligence to ensure that vehicle owners and drivers comply with the owner-driver binding requirement, and for encouraging ride-hailing vehicle drivers on their platforms to provide more ride-hailing trips. The assessment committee will make recommendations to the Commissioner for Transport, with a view to announcing a list of qualified platforms within this year.

A spokesman for the TD said, "The Government is committed to enhancing personalised point-to-point transport services as a whole, providing passengers with more diverse travel choices that are safe, lawful and compliant with regulatory requirements, while fostering a healthy competitive environment and promoting the long-term, healthy and sustainable development of the industry. We will carefully assess each application to ensure that licensed platforms have the necessary operational and financial capabilities to provide safe, reliable and efficient ride-hailing services sustainably."

The TD will also announce details of applications for ride-hailing vehicle driving permits and vehicle permits in due course to tie in with the operation of licensed platforms.

Hong Kong Cybersecurity Attack and Defence Drill 2026 successfully concluded

The Hong Kong Cybersecurity Attack and Defence Drill 2026, spearheaded by the Digital Policy Office (DPO) and co-organised by the Office of the Commissioner of Critical Infrastructure (Computer-system Security) of the Security Bureau, the Cyber Security and Technology Crime Bureau of the Hong Kong Police Force, the Hong Kong Internet Registration Corporation Limited, Cyberport, the Hong Kong Cybersecurity Professional Association, and the Hong Kong Institute of Information Technology, concluded successfully last night (October 31). The drill employed practical combat scenarios to identify system vulnerabilities, and test the defence mechanism and incident response capabilities of participating departments and organisations, thereby enhancing Hong Kong's overall cybersecurity protection and resilience.

As the drill enters its third edition, this year's Hong Kong Cybersecurity Attack and Defence Drill has expanded further in scale. A total of 51 defending teams (also known as Blue Teams) participated, covering designated information systems across 28 government departments, 23 public organisations and newly introduced private enterprises. The 23 attacking teams (also known as Red Teams) comprised representatives from 15 Hong Kong and Mainland cybersecurity professional organisations, four local tertiary institutions, three attacking teams recommended by the Cyberspace Administration of Guangdong Province, and one attacking team deployed by the DPO. This composition fully demonstrates the exchange and collaboration between Guangdong and Hong Kong in cybersecurity, as well as their joint efforts in cultivating cybersecurity talent.

Acting Deputy Commissioner (Digital Infrastructure), Ms Cari Wu, witnessed the successful conclusion of this year's drill at the event venue last night (October 31) alongside representatives of co-organisers. She expressed gratitude to all co-organisers, the Red Teams, the Blue Teams, the referee panel, and all supporting staff. She encouraged all participating units to continue their close exchanges in the cybersecurity field to jointly build a robust and high level cybersecurity shield for Hong Kong.

The drill, spanning three days for a total of 60 hours, commenced on October 29. The Red Teams, acting as attackers, launched simulated cyberattacks in a strictly controlled environment to examine the Blue Teams' cybersecurity defense capabilities. The drill enabled participating teams to accumulate practical experience in handling various types of cybersecurity threats. All participating systems and services remained in normal operation during the drill. Reports of the drill will be provided to the relevant government departments, public organisations, and private enterprises for reference and follow-up actions.

In addition, representatives from over 80 different organisations attended the venue as observers to understand the process of the attack and defence drill, exchange practical experience, and prepare for participation in upcoming drills. The drill has further consolidated the strengths of the Government, the cybersecurity industry, and all sectors of the community to build together a secure cyber environment in Hong Kong.
