President Tharman hosts lunch for Japan’s Princess Aiko during Singapore visit

TheTools · 18.11.2026 21:15 · source · permalink

AI-translated article with transliterated names. Read original Chinese article here.

When Japan’s Princess Aiko visited the Singapore Pavilion at the Osaka Expo last year, she wrote down her wish for “world peace”. Singapore President Tharman Shanmugaratnam said this resonated deeply with the vast majority of people in Singapore, Japan and around the world. Sustaining peace, however, requires all parties to consistently uphold international law and foster mutual understanding and cooperation. Singapore and Japan will work together towards a secure, peaceful and prosperous future.

To mark the 60th anniversary of diplomatic relations between Singapore and Japan, President Tharman invited Princess Aiko, the only child of Emperor Naruhito and Empress Masako, to visit Singapore. He hosted a welcome lunch for her at the Istana Villa on Wednesday (18 November).

In his toast, President Tharman said the friendship between Singapore and Japan had grown ever stronger. In 1970, Princess Aiko’s grandparents, Emperor Emeritus Akihito and Empress Emerita Michiko, visited Singapore as Japan’s crown prince and crown princess and planted two King Sago Palms at the Japanese Garden in Jurong Lake. When they returned on a state visit in 2006 to mark the 40th anniversary of diplomatic relations, they saw how the King Sago Palms had flourished. They serve as “a quiet symbol of how our bilateral relationship had developed and flourished”, said the President.

He said Japanese companies were among the earliest foreign investors in Singapore in the 1960s. Besides creating jobs, they helped Singapore acquire skills and build its own capabilities, enabling it to climb the economic ladder. Many Singaporean entrepreneurs first made their mark as suppliers to Japanese multinationals before venturing overseas. Singapore is now Japan’s third-largest source of foreign direct investment.

The Japan-Singapore Economic Partnership Agreement (JSEPA), signed in 2002, was Japan’s first bilateral free trade agreement and Singapore’s first with a major trading partner.

Singapore’s Japanese community is the world’s fourth largest among cities

More than two decades on, President Tharman said, the two countries would continue to deepen cooperation in innovation, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and digital infrastructure, the green economy and other emerging areas, in a spirit of openness and deep integration.

In March this year, Singapore and Japan announced an upgrade of bilateral ties to a strategic partnership. Beyond cooperation in the economy, technology, sustainability, security and education, he said the two countries could also share their experiences in addressing ageing populations.

“The strength of our friendship is clearly seen in the interest our peoples take in each other’s countries and cultures. Singapore is home to an established and vibrant Japanese community, whose members have enriched our society through their contributions to business, the arts, and our culinary scene. Singapore has the fourth-largest community of overseas Japanese among all cities globally,” he said. Meanwhile, Singaporean visitors to Japan exceeded 720,000 last year, an increase of more than 50% from 2019, before COVID-19.

The President said Princess Aiko’s visit not only commemorated the 60th anniversary of diplomatic relations, but also represented a new generation carrying forward the friendship between the two countries.

Princess Aiko: Remember the suffering of the Second World War and the wisdom of those who fostered today’s mutual understanding

In her remarks, Princess Aiko thanked President Tharman for inviting her to Singapore and for his warm hospitality in hosting the lunch.

She said that when looking back on the journey between the two countries, it must not be forgotten that lives were lost and various hardships were endured in Singapore during the Second World War. “Reflecting on the past and bearing in mind that the very foundation of friendship and mutual understanding we enjoy today is built upon the devotion and wisdom demonstrated by our predecessors, I sincerely hope that the bonds between our two countries will deepen further.”

Princess Aiko also said she hoped her visit would help the younger generation carry forward the friendship built by earlier generations and contribute to further exchanges.

Princess Aiko began her five-day visit to Singapore on Tuesday (17 November). She will also meet Deputy Prime Minister and Minister for Energy and Trade and Industry (Trade) Gan Kim Yong, who is serving as Acting Prime Minister, and Senior Minister Lee Hsien Loong. This is her second official overseas visit, following her trip to Laos in November last year.

Singapore established diplomatic relations with Japan on 26 April 1966. Over the years, Singapore’s leaders and Japan’s imperial family have maintained ties and exchanged visits to mark significant anniversaries of diplomatic relations.
