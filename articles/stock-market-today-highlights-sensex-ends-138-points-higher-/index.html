Stock Market Today Highlights: Sensex ends 138 points higher; Nifty settles above 23,450 - The Times of India

TheTools · 11.09.2026 00:59 · source · permalink

Market benchmark indices Sensex and Nifty ended higher on Thursday, snapping three days of decline, on fag-end buying in blue-chip HDFC Bank and Axis Bank.

However, elevated crude oil prices above USD 100-per-barrel mark, amid persistent geopolitical tensions, kept risk appetite subdued throughout the day.

The 30-share BSE Sensex advanced 138.36 points, or 0.19 per cent, to settle at 74,902.59 on last minute buying. During the day, it hit a high of 74,910.96 and a low of 74,598.47.

The 50-share NSE Nifty edged higher by 46.30 points, or 0.20 per cent, to end at 23,477.80. Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, jumped 1.19 per cent to USD 102.2 per barrel.

Rupee depreciated 25 paise to 95.33 against the US dollar in early trade as Brent crude price crossed the USD 100 dollar mark sharply increasing concerns over India's import bill.

Forex traders said elevated crude oil prices and persistent dollar demand also weighed on investor sentiments. At the interbank foreign exchange market, the rupee opened at 95.15 against the US dollar, then lost ground to touch 95.33 against the American currency, registering a loss of 25 paise from its previous close.

On Wednesday, the rupee slumped 34 paise to close at 95.08 against the American currency.

Market benchmark indices Sensex and Nifty were trading on a flat note in early deals on Thursday as elevated crude oil prices above USD 100-per-barrel mark, amid persistent geopolitical tensions, kept risk appetite subdued.

The 30-share BSE Sensex was marginally up 38.07 points to 74,809.95 in early trade. The 50-share NSE Nifty advanced 9.85 points to 23,439.20. Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, traded 0.20 per cent lower at USD 101-per-barrel.

Asian stocks fell on Thursday as the escalation in the Middle East kept oil prices above $100 a barrel, adding to investor concerns ahead of US inflation data that could influence the Federal Reserve's monetary policy.

MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan fell 1%, while Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's KOSPI dropped more than 1%.

Oil prices held firm on Thursday after Brent crude breached $100 a barrel, as traders braced for deeper supply disruptions following the largest wave of attacks on shipping since the six-month-old Iran-US conflict began.

Brent crude futures rose 0.1% to $101.34 a barrel by 0107 GMT, while US West Texas Intermediate crude gained 0.5% to $96.55 a barrel.

Brent breached the $100 mark on Wednesday and settled at its highest level since late May after Iran and the United States struck tankers in the biggest wave of attacks on shipping since the war began. The escalation has raised concerns that energy supply disruptions from the Middle East could worsen.

Benchmark stock indices fell for the third straight session on Wednesday, with the Sensex plunging 813 points to a nearly three-month low and the Nifty slipping below 23,450 as escalating West Asia tensions pushed crude oil prices above $100 a barrel.

The 30-share BSE Sensex tumbled 813.35 points, or 1.08%, to close at 74,764.23, its lowest closing level in nearly three months. The NSE Nifty declined 203.60 points, or 0.86%, to settle at 23,431.50, its lowest close since June 11.

Selling was led by IT, FMCG, financial services and oil & gas stocks, as investors remained cautious amid rising geopolitical tensions and higher crude prices.

Among Sensex stocks, HCL Tech was the biggest loser, falling 4.55%, followed by Infosys (-4.43%), Tech Mahindra (-3.87%) and TCS (-2.26%). HDFC Bank and Hindustan Unilever were also among the major laggards.

On the other hand, Adani Ports rose 3.67% to lead the gainers. Tata Steel, Trent and NTPC also ended higher.

Wall Street stocks fell on Wednesday as a fresh escalation in the US-Iran conflict pushed crude oil prices back above $100 a barrel, raising concerns over inflation and interest rates. The S&P 500 fell 0.3%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 327 points, or 0.6%. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.5%.

The sell-off was broad-based, with retailers among the biggest losers. Amazon fell 1.8% and Starbucks declined 1.4%. Most S&P 500 sectors ended lower, while energy stocks gained, with Exxon Mobil rising 2.6% and Chevron adding 2%.

Oil prices were at the centre of the market action after the US destroyed five Iranian tankers on Tuesday, further escalating the conflict. The war has largely halted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a key route that handled about a fifth of global oil supplies before the conflict.

President Donald Trump said Wednesday that the Iran war was likely to keep oil prices elevated at least through the US midterm elections in November.

The surge in crude has added to already elevated inflation. US gasoline prices have risen about 32% from a year ago to $4.22 a gallon, while diesel prices have also reached record levels.

Higher fuel costs can directly squeeze household budgets and indirectly push up the prices of goods by increasing transportation and production costs. Investors are now awaiting fresh US inflation data, with the Producer Price Index (PPI) due Thursday and the Consumer Price Index (CPI) scheduled for Friday.
