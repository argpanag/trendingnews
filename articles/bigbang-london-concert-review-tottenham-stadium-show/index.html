BIGBANG London Concert Review: Tottenham Stadium Show

TheTools · 28.09.2026 00:00 · source · permalink

This review is based on press accounts of the Sept. 26 concert at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, not on direct observation.

BIGBANG's London stop opened with "Fantastic Baby" and spent the next two hours making the case that a 20-year-old boy band can still fill a football stadium. It mostly made the case. It did not do it cleanly.

The show, per NME and allkpop, ran on the trio of G-Dragon, Taeyang and Daesung. It was the London leg of the group's world tour, its first large-scale run in roughly nine years, following Goyang, Oakland, East Rutherford and a sold-out Paris. The set was built from the songs the stadium came for: "Fantastic Baby," "Bang Bang Bang," "Loser," "Haru Haru," "Lies" and, from the group's latest single, "BiiiG." NME also lists "Bad Boy" and "Sober."

The production matched the scale. Allkpop describes a live band, professional dancers and large visual displays. NME adds fire effects and choreography-heavy staging, and singles out G-Dragon's oversized bright red fluffy hat as the night's signature image. The show was divided into sections so each member had a solo stretch. NME says those stretches covered pop, trot, R&B and rap-pop, which gave Daesung's playful stage presence and Taeyang's vocals room away from the group hits, while allkpop credits G-Dragon with the most distinctive charisma.

The structure is the show's strongest choice. A stadium concert built only from anthems can tire the room, and the solo blocks break it up. Spread across pop, trot, R&B and rap-pop, they let each member work in a different register.

The weaknesses were technical and atmospheric. NME reported that the audio mix in the first section was muddy, with vocals occasionally buried under the band. For a show marketed on live singing, that is a real fault, and a costly one for a group whose case rests on live performance as much as on nostalgia. The tour is billed as the 2026-2027 World Tour "XX: COSMOS," so the same mix will be judged again at every stop after London. NME also described crowd energy as frequently more muted than expected. The stadium was nearly full, with longtime fans and newcomers, but the reception did not always match the production.

The emotional peak came from an absence. T.O.P. did not perform, but his verse on the closer "Still Life" was played in recorded form, which NME called an emotional tribute. Allkpop notes the ballad's quiet register served as a contrast with the louder songs before it. Ending on a track that acknowledges the missing member is honest, and it avoids pretending the lineup is what it once was.

The members spoke about the anniversary in plain terms. Taeyang told the crowd, per NME, "Reaching our 20th anniversary is like a miracle to me." G-Dragon, discussing the future, said: "I've been trying to figure out my masterplan to bring our golden days back." The second line is the more telling one. It concedes that the golden days are, for now, behind the group.

Allkpop reports longtime VIP fans sang along throughout, and STARNEWS cited a BBC line that "the kings of K-pop have returned." NME's own headline was more measured, saying that 20 years in, the group still has plenty to offer. The headline and the review body agree on the substance. This was a celebration almost fit for royalty, in NME's words, with the "almost" carrying weight.

BIGBANG proved its songs still work at stadium scale, and the solo-block format was the smartest decision on the bill. The mix problems and the flat crowd kept it from more.
