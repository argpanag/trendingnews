36 Years Later, Robert De Niro's Greatest Gangster Line Is Still Unbeaten In Crime Movie History

TheTools · 17.10.2026 12:01 · source · permalink

Crime movies are mostly propelled by violence and daring stunts. However, some of the greatest characters in the genre deliver lines that are just as mind-blowing and inventive as the crimes. Robert De Niro’s Goodfellas character, James "Jimmy" Conway, has a quote like this. Speaking to his protégé Henry Hill, Jimmy says something that leaves no doubt about the importance of loyalty and codes in the Mafia.

Martin Scorsese directed Goodfellas, which was released on Sept. 19, 1990, in the U.S. 36 years later, its appeal hasn’t faded. The film received six Oscar nominations at the 63rd Academy Awards in 1991 (with Joe Pesci winning Best Supporting Actor) and is considered a high point in both Scorsese's and De Niro’s careers. De Niro’s character is loosely based on Lucchese crime family associate James "Jimmy the Gent" Burke, who is widely linked to the 1978 Lufthansa heist. His words are still powerful.

Robert De Niro's Memorable 'Goodfellas' Line Foreshadows a Big Moment

In the first act of the Robert De Niro-led gangster movie, Irish-American teenager Henry Hill becomes fascinated by the lifestyle of the local Mafia members. As he starts doing small tasks for them, he becomes close to De Niro’s Jimmy. When he grows up, he's given heavier responsibilities, and, as fate would have it, he gets arrested.

In court, Henry doesn't mention any names and refuses to admit the existence of the Mafia. Once he is released, Jimmy welcomes him with pride rather than disappointment about his arrest. He gives him cash as a graduation present and praises him for taking his first pinch (his first arrest) as a man. Then comes the iconic line. He says:

"Never rat on your friends, and always keep your mouth shut."

"Never rat on your friends, and always keep your mouth shut."

The line echoes omertà, which, according to The Mob Museum, is the traditional Cosa Nostra code of silence associated with Sicily and southern Italy, which was carried into some Italian‑American organized‑crime groups. It reminds members never to cooperate with the police or acknowledge the Mafia's existence to outsiders. It also warns against betrayal, including siding with other enemy factions. "Ratting" is punishable by death.

The line stands not only because it’s a guide for survival but also because of its foreshadowing nature. For the most part, Henry keeps Jimmy’s words in the back of his mind and stays loyal. However, when he's pushed into a corner, he finally folds. After the Lufthansa heist fallout, Henry becomes an FBI informant, testifies in court, and sends many of his associates to prison. He enters witness protection and starts living, in his own words, as an “average nobody.”

Robert De Niro’s Line Is One of the Best in the Genre

Robert De Niro’s Goodfellas line is even more iconic because it's so relevant. Sure, most people remember Joe Pesci’s "Funny how?" or Ray Liotta's opening line, ”I always wanted to be a gangster!" However, De Niro’s line could be inserted into most iconic gangster movies and TV shows, and it would make sense. That’s because the fear of betrayal is a recurring theme in criminal circles. As tough as the criminals are, they understand that their lives would be ruined if the authorities (or enemies) investigated and someone from the inside gave detailed information.

For example, during Christopher’s initiation ceremony in The Sopranos, Tony Soprano has the young mobster hold a burning saint card, rub his hands, and say the line, "May I burn in hell if I betray my friends!" This is a nice little mob movie reference and a paraphrasing of Robert De Niro’s line. It reminds us once again that ratting shall never be tolerated. Unlike Henry, we see several characters suffer consequences, notably Big Pussy (a key member of the DiMeo crime family) and Christopher’s lover, Adriana. Both are killed after their cooperation with the authorities becomes known. Big Pussy's crew executes him, and members of Christopher's circle murder Adriana after uncovering her role as an FBI informant.

Interestingly, De Niro’s quote doesn’t appear in Nicolas Pileggi's book, Wiseguy​​​​​​, on which the movie is based. In the novel, there is a celebration after Henry’s release, but the specific line doesn't appear. Martin Scorsese reportedly encouraged actors to improvise several scenes and lines (via ScreenRant) to make the themes bolder and the events more cinematic. Still, it remains unclear whether De Niro originated the line or it appeared in the shooting script.

No matter the source, those who appreciate crime films will forever be grateful for its inclusion.
