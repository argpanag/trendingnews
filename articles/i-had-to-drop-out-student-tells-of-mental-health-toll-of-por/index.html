‘I had to drop out’: Student tells of mental health toll of Portobello PE course dispute

TheTools · 14.11.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

Those affected include registered teachers who enrolled at Portobello to upskill and add another subject to their registration, as well as students now undertaking expensive professional master of education (PME) programmes elsewhere. File picture

"I had to drop out of teaching for my mental health. It was an awful situation," said one student who decided to "clean the slate" and walk away from her dream of becoming a PE teacher amid uncertainty over a qualification she pursued at a private college.

The Irish Examiner has now been contacted by more than two dozen students who enrolled in courses at Portobello Institute in recent years. Most enrolled from 2022 onwards with the intention of becoming PE teachers.

However, their qualifications from Portobello Institute have continued to fall short of the Teaching Council's curricular subject requirements for physical education. As previously reported by the Irish Examiner, efforts to resolve the issue have so far been unsuccessful.

Those affected include registered teachers who enrolled at Portobello to upskill and add another subject to their registration, as well as students now undertaking expensive professional master of education (PME) programmes elsewhere.

Others undertook several qualifications with the private college, paying between €12,000 and €20,000, but remain unable to register with the Teaching Council as qualified teachers as they had intended.

In a recent Dáil debate, Social Democrats education spokesperson Jen Cummins raised the issue with further and higher education minister James Lawless. "There have been complaints in the media, and rightly so," she said. 

"There is a lot of fear out there and a lot of money has been spent. It is a stark warning to students to watch out in respect of what they are being advertised." 

Mr Lawless said: "I agree with the deputy that students should be very careful with what they are signing up to. They must ensure that any offering is watertight before they enter into that contract." 

He told the Dáil: "I advise those students to consider seeking legal advice as to what options may be open to them. It would appear to me that they have a strong ground for grievance based on what has since transpired." 

'No one helped me'

However, the student who has told her story to the Irish Examiner said she did seek legal advice. "I’ve tried that. I did that step. I reached out to TDs, no one helped me.”

She first came across the Portobello graduate diploma after searching online for the micro-modules she needed to qualify as a PE teacher. The course, which had fees of €5,850, "popped up" in her search.

Portobello's website previously described the diploma as a "pathway to becoming a PE teacher to gain entry onto a PME or [Portobello’s own] MSc Physical Education, PGCEI & QTS Route”.

Portobello sent her an email in November 2023 saying the course was “designed to give graduates the credits in physical education at undergraduate level to progress towards a career in PE teaching”.

It added: “You have the choice to progress to our MSc PE, PGCEi & QTS AO or to apply to University of Limerick to complete their PME in PE.” 

She began the graduate diploma course with Portobello in autumn 2024. Embarking on her studies, she stepped back from a managerial position in work and had the support of her mother and father.

“I feel like I wasted their money, they work very hard. They wanted to support me, and help me through it. I stopped working full time, and they supported me. I paid for my travel up and down, my equipment, my books, but they handled the fees. They were really good to do that.

 “On my first day, at orientation they had a big PowerPoint — ‘Your pathway to teaching’ —and they had UL there on the screen. They had us all in a room, they said we were the biggest cohort they ever had for PE.” 

Registration issues

In October 2024, the woman first learned of the registration issues faced by prior graduates when a source outside of Portobello Institute gave her a “heads up”. She felt the initial response by the college was to brush it off: “'There’s a bit of trouble with the Teaching Council, but don’t worry we’re sorting it.'”

“In December, January 2025, we all got a bit stressed out. They were like ‘listen, there’s a few gaps in the course, we’re going to give ye extra modules’.” 

However, by the time she finished the graduate diploma in May 2025, the extra modules had not been rolled out.

She applied to the PME in the University of Limerick as planned and her interview went well. In June 2025, she received correspondence from UL to let her know it was unable to offer her a place on the programme. 

“After consultation with the Teaching Council, the course director regrets to inform you of this decision,” it stated. 

When contacted previously by the Irish Examiner, a spokesperson for UL said queries would be best placed with the Teaching Council. 

“UL has engaged proactively with the Teaching Council around this. UL has not recruited students coming from that route since becoming aware that there was a registration issue. It would not be appropriate for UL to comment on the offerings of other institutions.” 

Hibernia College concerns

The student was also offered a place with Hibernia College, which introduced a PE PME for the first time in September 2025. 

Hibernia did not respond to a request for comment, but in a previous statement to the Irish Examiner, it said it is aware of concerns. “We fully support the efforts by relevant stakeholders to provide clear and timely information on this matter, and we hope a resolution can be reached as quickly as possible in the interests of all those affected.” 

As this student began her studies with Hibernia in 2025, she still had no certainty in regards to the additional modules, and whether they would be enough to satisfy registration requirements. A PME with Hibernia costs just under €16,000.

As she approached the end of an extended cooling off period, she faced a choice of either spending two years studying, paying further substantial fees, while not knowing if she would still encounter difficulties qualifying and registering, or exiting the course. 

“I chose to leave. I didn’t even know if Portobello would give us extra modules. I think I lost €500 in registration fees with Hibernia, but they gave me the rest back.” 

She added: “When we found out we might not be accredited, I thought, ‘I’m not doing a two-year Masters, spending over €20,000, and not being able to teach at the end of it’. I had to take a full year out, because I dropped out. I couldn’t do it.”

Formally withdrawing from the course, she wrote to Hibernia to say: “This has been an incredibly difficult decision for me to make, as I was fully committed to pursuing this qualification and building a future in teaching. However, due to the ongoing situation with Portobello Institute and the uncertainty surrounding the programme’s recognition and accreditation, I feel I have no choice but to step back at this time.”

She continued: "Leaving the course deeply saddens me, as teaching was something I am truly passionate about. Nonetheless, I must prioritise my mental health and well-being during this challenging period.” 

Seeking legal advice

In late 2025, the woman sought legal advice. A solicitor acting on her behalf went on to send two letters to Portobello Institute outlining her complaints.

Portobello responded to both, rejecting these “entirely”. 

In the letters, the college offered no further remedy, outside of the opportunity to take part in a “supplementary certificate course” available at no cost to all past diploma students.

These additional modules, intended to address shortfalls identified by the Teaching Council, were held this year. Issues with the graduate diploma first came to the attention of the Irish Examiner  when education minister Hildegarde Naughton confirmed the completion of these has not resulted in the registration requirements being achieved in applicants assessed to date by the Teaching Council. 

This student declined to take part in the additional modules when they were offered to her. By this time, she had already withdrawn from her studies.

“They said they did nothing wrong,” the student said this week. 

The only other recourse she was left with was to issue court proceedings to sue the college. She was advised, as with all court proceedings, of the risk of losing and having costs awarded against her.

“I can’t take that risk unfortunately," she said.

"They probably have a very good legal team, and then I’m just me.” 

She has since enrolled on another course, and is doing well with her new college, but has ultimately given up on teaching.

She points to how Portobello knew of issues faced by the students who started the course before her. “They would have known that but they still decided to run my cohort, and the cohort after me. Why would they do that, and why would they keep the course the same?”

Portobello Institute response

In response to queries, Portobello Institute said: “The University of Limerick does accept Portobello graduates onto its postgraduate teacher education programmes and has done so each year.

“An unsuccessful individual application should not be taken as evidence that Portobello qualifications are generally excluded from consideration by UL.” 

It said the student’s “acceptance onto Hibernia College's PME demonstrates that her Portobello Institute qualification provided a pathway to postgraduate teacher education, consistent with what Portobello communicated about the programme from the outset.” 

The student subsequently withdrew from Hibernia before completing the PME qualification required as part of the teacher registration process, it said.

“We acknowledge the distress she describes and do not seek to diminish her experience. Equally, it is important that the circumstances are presented in their entirety.” 

The student “successfully progressed to the next stage of her intended teaching pathway but did not complete that stage", according to the statement from Portobello.

“Consequently, her experience cannot, in itself, establish that completion of the pathway would have resulted in a refusal of registration by the Teaching Council.”

In a timeline provided by the college, it said graduates first notified it of Teaching Council registration issues between April and October 2024. It said it "repeatedly requested" a meeting with the Teaching Council between February and May 2025. 

In May 2025, it suspended its intake of new students on the graduate diploma in PE pending clarification of Teaching Council requirements.

A spokesperson for the Teaching Council said it "has a statutory responsibility to uphold professional standards and to ensure that all applicants meet the published curricular subject requirements for registration. Registration requirements apply equally and fairly to all applicants and cannot be waived."
