JJ Gabriel asks to quit Manchester United

TheTools · 15.09.2026 18:52 · source · permalink

Exclusive: Fifteen-year-old, nicknamed ‘Kid Messi’, has sent formal notice of intention to cancel his registration with immediate effect

JJ Gabriel, the 15-year-old prodigy nicknamed “Kid Messi”, has unexpectedly asked to quit Manchester United.

In a move that has shocked United and is almost unprecedented, Gabriel – whose full name is Joseph Junior Andreou Gabriel – has sent formal notice that he wishes to cancel his registration at the club with immediate effect to become a free agent.

It is understood that Gabriel is not in line to join another club immediately, but has decided he wants to leave United – the club he has played for since the age of 11.

Clubs cannot sign players to professional contracts until the age of 17 or scholarship agreements until they turn 16, which Gabriel does not do until October 6. United can challenge Gabriel’s request to cancel his registration.

Premier League rules state that: “During your child’s registration period, their registration may only be cancelled if everyone agrees (the club, player and their parent/carer).”

The rules add: “If the parties don’t agree, then any person involved can also ask the Premier League to provide a binding decision on a termination request.”

It remains to be seen whether United refuse Gabriel’s request, but the club are not thought to see the situation as irretrievable yet. United had planned to include the youngster in the first-team squad for Wednesday night’s Carabao Cup tie against Brighton.

News of Gabriel’s desire to leave is a surprising development and raises serious questions about the running of United, who have traditionally been English football’s most successful talent factory.

Gabriel’s request to cancel his registration could not come at a worse time for United co-owners Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazers, following the Manchester derby defeat by 10-man City and large-scale fan protests against them.

Last week, Gabriel shot to prominence outside Old Trafford by scoring a hat-trick against Sabah in the Uefa Youth League, having last season become the youngest winner of United’s Jimmy Murphy young-player-of-the-year award.

Ryan Giggs, a former winner of the Jimmy Murphy award, described Gabriel as a “generational talent” and likened his development to that of Wayne Rooney.

Gabriel has been nicknamed “Kid Messi” after going viral on social media and a source at one of United’s rivals told Telegraph Sport: “This would be like Barcelona losing Messi before he had played for them. It would be huge.”

Gabriel’s social media accounts were wiped of all mention of United the day after his hat-trick against Sabah and the club were informed this week that he no longer wishes to play for them.

Manchester City tried to snatch Gabriel away from United last year and the teenager has also been linked with Arsenal, Real Madrid and Bayern Munich.

Gabriel scored more than 20 goals for United’s under-18 team last season and had been expected to make his first-team debut for the club this term.

Wonderkid’s shock request a damning indictment of Ratcliffe and Glazers

The history of Manchester United has been built on the club’s ability to develop some of the country’s greatest young footballers, a legacy which will make JJ Gabriel’s desire to leave even harder to swallow.

Not many 15-year-olds command the sort of attention Gabriel has so early in his career, but this is the prize talent in youth football. United thought they had already done the hard part by seeing off interest from Manchester City, but somehow English football’s biggest club are in danger of letting the country’s brightest young talent slip through their fingers.

Maybe it would have been easier to rationalise if Gabriel had joined City last year. Then he would have been another academy talent to defect from one rival to another. For him to decide he wants to go it alone is almost unprecedented and gives United plenty to chew over.

Able to play as a striker, a left-sided forward or an attacking midfielder, Gabriel last season became the youngest player to feature for United’s under-18s team, at 14, going on to win the inaugural Premier League Under-18s player-of-the-year award.

The potential departure of Gabriel is a damning indictment of United under co-owners Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazers, who are already the subject of large fan protests and will now come under more fire.

Jason Wilcox, the United director of football, also has difficult questions to answer over why such a talented young player would rather place his career in temporary limbo rather than stay at United.

Gabriel would have his pick of interested clubs before his 16th birthday on October 6. City have already tried and failed to sign him once, Arsenal have shown an interest and news of his talent has travelled as far as Real Madrid and Bayern Munich.

All of the world’s top agencies and intermediaries will now be scrambling to sign Gabriel, who already has a sponsorship deal with Nike.

There will inevitably be assumptions and accusations aimed at those close to Gabriel. But, ultimately, his career path and what he goes on to achieve will determine whether such a bold request is in his best interests.

United’s return to the Champions League was a cause for celebration for the club, but the 4-0 victory over Sabah last week may well be remembered as a sliding-doors moment.

Rather than following the example of Arsenal, who included Max Dowman in the club’s 25-man Champions League squad last season, United did not put Gabriel on the club’s A list, which means he cannot play in Europe.

A home match against such lowly opposition would have offered United the perfect opportunity to give Old Trafford the first glance of Gabriel, just as Dowman – aged 15 years and 308 days – appeared as a substitute in Arsenal’s 3-0 win over Slavia Prague last season.

Gabriel sent social media wild with a hat-trick in United’s Uefa Youth League victory stroll against Sabah on the same day as the first-team’s success. But even a couple of minutes for Michael Carrick’s side would have been cherished more fondly by the player and his family.

United’s final league-phase game in the Champions League, away at Villarreal on January 27, could easily be a dead rubber, yet Gabriel would still not have been eligible to play after being left off the A list.

It was a day after the first team’s victory over Sabah and his own hat-trick that Gabriel’s social media accounts were wiped of all mention of United and less than a week later the club were informed of his desire to cancel his registration.

Born in Enfield, London, Gabriel joined United as an 11-year-old, when his family moved to the Manchester area. Some of those who know Gabriel describe him as a quiet character who is only interested in football.

The teenager had been close to moving across Manchester to City last year after Hugo Viana, their director of football, led a charm offensive to try to snatch him from United. Viana was able to point to recent academy successes such as Phil Foden and Nico O’Reilly as evidence that Gabriel would be given a path to the first team.

But, ultimately, United’s history, the size of the club, and the fear that he could be farmed around City’s stable of sister clubs convinced Gabriel – then aged 14 – to stay put.

He was promoted three years above his age level and spent last season playing for United’s Under-18s, where his development raced far ahead of his years.

There is recognition that a 15-year-old cannot be guaranteed first-team starts or dictate a club’s strategy over youth development. But, increasingly, it is a case of if you are good enough then you are old enough in football. Giggs will be as shocked as supporters that the teenager’s competitive debut in senior football may not be in the red of United.

Speaking about Gabriel in an interview with Rio Ferdinand, Giggs had said: “It’s sort of a generational talent. There’s no point in going out on loan because he’s at the best place and he’d be dipping in and out of the first team. So that experience is invaluable. You know, not only the games and the training, but actually travelling with the team, seeing the expectation of Manchester United...the focus is so huge on United. You need to get used to that.”

Gabriel bounced between training with the first team and the under-21s at the start of this season, without ever being completely sure which group of players he would be working with until he received a message on his phone telling him who to join.

Arsenal handed debuts to Dowman and Ethan Nwaneri as 15-year-olds. Dowman scored in the Premier League last season to prove that even the biggest clubs can let special talents off the leash every so often.

Dowman played for Arsenal against Tottenham Hotspur, Newcastle United and Villarreal in pre-season before the 2025-26 season and made his Premier League debut against Leeds United last August. Gabriel earned a place in United’s pre-season squad this summer but was given only 13 minutes, which included time added on for stoppages, against Atlético Madrid.

To Gabriel, United’s messaging may have seemed mixed. On one hand, they fought to stop him joining City and gave the impression that good performances would be rewarded with promotions. But, on the other, he was treated with kid gloves and wondered if he was as highly rated as he and his family had been told.

A place on the bench for the first home game of the season against Ipswich Town would have sent a strong message to Gabriel that he was at least within touching distance of first-team minutes. It would also have encouraged him that his dream of making his first-team debut in the Premier League was on course to become reality.

The Uefa Youth League hat-trick created a clamour for Gabriel to be included in United’s Carabao Cup third-round tie against Brighton, but by then it was already too late. United’s boy wonder had decided he would rather take charge of his own destiny.
