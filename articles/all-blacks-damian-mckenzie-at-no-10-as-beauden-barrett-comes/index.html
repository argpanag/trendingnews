All Blacks: Damian McKenzie at No.10 as Beauden Barrett comes back in from the cold

TheTools · 08.10.2026 15:27 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Sport

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._Q.VKhexyL5oal5uCV3AUQmI1EOdryHi3hh73RdibV20lY/sport/1791509%2Fall-blacks-damian-mckenzie-at-no-10-as-beauden-barrett-comes-back-in-from-the-cold

Dave Rennie's best-laid plans for the first Bledisloe Cup test against Australia on Saturday have had to be quickly torn up following Richie Mo'unga's injury, with Damian McKenzie moved from fullback to No.10 and Beauden Barrett given a rare start.

Most observers assumed Barrett would start at first-five following Mo'unga's calf issue, floated by RNZ on Wednesday and which was confirmed this morning, but instead Rennie has opted to start Barrett at fullback and move McKenzie a little closer to the action in the absence of the injured Ruben Love.

Still, it represents a significant comeback for Barrett, who was not involved in the first six tests of the year and whose future with the All Blacks appeared tenuous even last month.

After not featuring in the three Nations Championship tests in July, Barrett played a bit-part role in the recent "Greatest Rivalry" series - only coming off the bench in the final test in Baltimore.

Scott Barrett will start for the All Blacks in only his third match in nearly 12 months.

Brett Phibbs / www.photosport.nz

Mo'unga, who also had a calf issue on his return to New Zealand from three years in Japan, will also miss the return test in Sydney a week on Saturday.

Rennie's other option would have been to start the uncapped Josh Jacomb at first-five but, with so much at stake on Saturday - the All Blacks' unbeaten record at their fortress stretches back 53 tests to 1994 and a victory will lock up the Bledisloe for another year - the selectors have gone with experience over youth.

Barrett will be joined in the starting line-up by brothers Scott and Jordie.

Scott Barrett, selected in the second row with Josh Lord, is playing his first test (and only third match in total) since November last year, with Jordie Barrett named in the midfield as expected alongside Quinn Tupaea, who has recovered from a knee problem which ruled him out of the fourth test against the Springboks.

Other notable selections include Shannon Frizell's inclusion as loose forward cover on the reserves bench for what will be his first test for the All Blacks since the 2023 World Cup final.

Tupou Vaa'i has been selected at blindside flanker, with Wallace Sititi starting at No.8 in the absence of the injured Ardie Savea, and Peter Lakai named at openside flanker.

Leroy Carter has been named on the left wing, with Will Jordan on the right, as expected. Cam Roigard will start at halfback inside McKenzie.

Ethan de Groot, skipper Codie Taylor and Fletcher Newell make up the front row, with Anton Segner also named as loose forward cover on the bench.

Shannon Frizell's last match for the All Blacks was the Rugby World Cup grand final defeat to South Africa in Paris in 2023.

Andrew Cornaga/www.photosport.nz

The All Blacks have no specialist back-up lock, but Vaa'i is likely to move to the second row in favour of Scott Barrett in the second half, assuming the former captain finds the going difficult after such a long break from the game due to his sabbatical and recent back surgery.

Rieko Ioane and Josh Moorby make up the back reserves, along with back-up halfback Kyle Preston, further pointers to Rennie's belief that the test will be played at a high tempo.

"The Bledisloe Cup means a huge amount to this group," Rennie said in a statement.

"There's a lot of history and pride tied up in it, and every Test against the Wallabies carries real weight for us.

"We have huge respect for this Australian side and the way they have been playing. We know we'll need to be at our best.

"We've been on the road for the last eight games, so the boys are rapt to be home and really looking forward to running out at Eden Park one last time this year."
