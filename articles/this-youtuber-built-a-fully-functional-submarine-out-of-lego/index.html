This YouTuber Built a Fully Functional Submarine Out of LEGO, and What It Did Underwater Is Insane

TheTools · 07.09.2026 15:36 · source · permalink

For the past several years, the Brick Experiment Channel has been on a relentless DIY quest to build the ultimate, fully functional radio-controlled submarine using as many LEGO Technic parts as possible. With the highly anticipated release of "Submarine 5.0," the creator has delivered a true masterclass in custom engineering, showcasing radical upgrades to the vessel's hydrodynamics, electronics, and depth control.

Through meticulous trial and error, the latest 2.95 kg acrylic craft successfully conquered both a swimming pool and a fast-flowing river—all while introducing two groundbreaking new features.

To improve stability and control, the creator completely overhauled the submarine's electronics. Previous versions relied on a Raspberry Pi Zero, but Version 5.0 swaps that out for a highly efficient Arduino Nano ESP32 microcontroller.

This hardware change drastically streamlines the sub's boot time, simplifies the internal wiring, and minimizes power consumption to maximize the 9V Lego battery box.

Paired with a new 6-axis gyroscope/accelerometer, an absolute pressure sensor, and a dedicated 40MHz radio receiver, the sub now boasts highly accurate proportional controls and a massive range boost that works flawlessly underwater.

The Mechanics: Pump Ballasts and Moving Weights

The most impressive engineering feats inside Submarine 5.0 are the brand-new depth and pitch control mechanisms.

Instead of relying on a rigid syringe piston to draw in water like previous models, the new sub uses a Kamoer NKP peristaltic pump—cleverly modified with Lego parts—to fill a 50 ml animal IV bag acting as the ballast. Because a bag can dynamically shift its shape, it frees up critical internal volume.

And the builder needed every millimeter of that extra space for the sub's coolest new feature: a sliding weight for pitch control. By putting 1.35 kg of dense tungsten pellets on a motorized internal track, the creator can slide the vessel's center of gravity forward or backward. This tilts the nose up or down, allowing the submarine to dive or surface incredibly quickly using forward momentum, drastically improving its underwater maneuverability.

Cramming the new pump, the Arduino board, and the sliding tungsten weight into a 250x120mm acrylic tube is an absolute masterclass in spatial planning.

To top it all off, Version 5.0 finally ditches the flat-front profile of its predecessors. Previous iterations suffered from a blunt design that caused the craft to aggressively veer sideways when pushed to top speed. By capping the acrylic hull with a smooth 12cm rounded plastic nose, the new vessel cuts through the water with excellent hydrodynamic stability, completing a flawless 1.5-meter-deep test run in a local river.
