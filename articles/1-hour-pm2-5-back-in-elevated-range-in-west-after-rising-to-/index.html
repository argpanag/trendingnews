1-hour PM2.5 back in elevated range in west after rising to high band

Singapore

The 24-hour Pollutant Standard Index (PSI) readings were also in the unhealthy range across Singapore.

SINGAPORE: Hazy conditions persisted across Singapore on Friday (Oct 9), with the 1-hour PM2.5 reading in the west dipping back into the elevated range after entering the high range at 8am. 

At 9am, readings ranged from 84 in the north to 137 in the central and southern regions. The east recorded a reading of 114, while the west registered a reading of 126, down from 152 an hour earlier.

The 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) reading in central Singapore jumped to 170 at 9am, the highest reading recorded in the country this year. Readings across the other regions were also in the unhealthy range, ranging from 115 to 164.

For those planning immediate outdoor activities, NEA advises checking the hourly PM2.5 levels.

The 24-hour PSI forecast and corresponding health advisories should be used in planning next day outdoor activities.

When 1-hour PM2.5 levels are elevated, healthy people are advised to reduce strenuous outdoor activity for the next hour, while the elderly, pregnant women, children and people with chronic heart or lung conditions should avoid such activity.

The 24-hour PSI is Singapore's indicator of daily air quality and forms the basis of NEA's daily health advisories. It is calculated using six air pollutants - PM2.5, PM10, ozone, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide and carbon monoxide - and reflects the average air quality of the past 24 hours.

The 1-hour PM2.5 reading, on the other hand, is the hourly average of the PM2.5 pollutant levels. PM2.5 is fine particulate matter with a diameter of 2.5 micrometres or shorter, and is the dominant pollutant during haze that causes harm to health.

A 24-hour PSI reading is considered "unhealthy" when it is in the 101 to 200 range, "very unhealthy" when it is within the 201-300 range and "hazardous" when it goes over 300.

The hourly PM2.5 reading is classified in four bands: normal, elevated, high and very high. It is considered elevated when it ranges from 56 to 150. The "high" band ranges from 151 to 250, and readings above that are classified as "very high".

In its Thursday evening advisory, the National Environment Agency (NEA) said that moderate to dense smoke plumes from southern and central Sumatra and West Kalimantan were observed to drift towards Singapore.

Dry conditions are expected to persist over the southern parts of Sumatra and parts of Kalimantan.

Under prevailing southeasterly or southerly winds, smoke haze may continue to drift towards Singapore, NEA said, adding that the 24-hour PSI for Singapore is forecast to be in the high moderate to mid unhealthy range.
