Scott Barrett back in action with Taranaki after 10-month absence

TheTools · 23.09.2026 11:08 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Scott Barrett will return to rugby after a 10-month break when he starts for Taranaki against Hawke’s Bay in the National Provincial Championship on Sunday.

It will be the 32-year-old’s first game for the Bulls since 2021 and his first senior game in 2026 after he took a non-playing sabbatical following the All Blacks’ victory over Ireland in November 2025.

He was expected to return for the Crusaders earlier this year but was ruled out with a back injury that required surgery in May.

That meant he missed the All Blacks’ Nations Championships fixtures in July and their South Africa tour.

“I’m really excited about the opportunity to play against Hawke’s Bay this Sunday,” Barrett said.

“It was a wee while ago, that I used to sit up there and watch the older brothers play and, yeah, can’t wait to pull on the amber and black again,” he said while the team was training at Stadium Taranaki.

Barrett was All Blacks captain under former coach Scott Robertson, but under new coach Dave Rennie, Ardie Savea has assumed that post.

However, Savea dislocated his shoulder in the third test against South Africa, likely leaving him out of the squad for the remainder of this year’s fixtures and opening the door for Barrett to return as captain.

Barrett’s next opportunity to play for the All Blacks comes in October when they play two Bledisloe Cup tests against Australia before three Nations Championship fixtures in Europe in November against Scotland, Wales and England.

In his return to the NPC, the 32-year-old will form a second-row pair with Josh Lord, with fellow All Black Josh Jacomb starting in the No 10 jersey.

Taranaki will look to build on their 55-19 victory over Southland last week against table toppers Hawke’s Bay.

The Magpies will be hoping to bounce back from recent poor form, losing to North Harbour and Bay of Plenty.

Taranaki sit seventh on the ladder with 24 points, eight behind Hawke’s Bay, who are level with Northland and Otago.
