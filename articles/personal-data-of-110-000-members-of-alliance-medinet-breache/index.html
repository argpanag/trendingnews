SINGAPORE – The personal data of about 110,000 members of healthcare services provider Alliance Medinet has been compromised in a cyber incident involving unauthorised access to its database.

Details affected potentially include members’ full names, personal identification numbers, dates of birth, contact information, policy details and employment information.

In a bourse filing on Oct 24, parent company Alliance Healthcare Group said that the bank account information of seven members in the database and one member’s medical diagnosis might have been compromised.

The data breach involved a third party’s unauthorised access to Alliance Medinet’s system containing its membership database, the parent company added.

Investigations are ongoing, Alliance Healthcare Group said. It added that the affected members are being notified by Alliance Medinet and/or its partners and relevant clients.

Alliance Medinet – the corporate healthcare arm of Alliance Healthcare Group – specialises in managed healthcare services, offering personalised corporate healthcare solutions and third-party administration services, according to the subsidiary’s website.

The subsidiary was established in 2005 and has a network of more than 2,000 panel clinics across Singapore, it said on its website.

In response to media queries, the authorities said they are aware of the incident.

The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore said it is monitoring developments closely, while the Personal Data Protection Commission said it is investigating the incident.

Cigna, Great Eastern, HSBC Life, Income Insurance, Singlife and Tokio Marine, which Alliance Medinet provides services to, are among those affected by the data breach.

In a media statement on Oct 24, Tokio Marine said the personal data of 6,103 members may have been compromised, though no bank account details or medical information were affected.

Great Eastern stated on its website that the bank information of three members may have been compromised, while Cigna said the health data of one individual was compromised.

Additionally, Income Insurance stated that one corporate account involving about 70 individuals was affected, while Singlife reported that 323 customers under its employee benefits group health insurance may have been affected. Both insurers said in separate statements on Oct 24 that there is currently no indication that any medical information was affected.

Income Insurance, Singlife and Tokio Marine said they were notified on Oct 22 by Alliance Medinet of the breach.

The insurers said their systems remain uncompromised and that policyholders may continue accessing policy benefits and submitting claims as usual.

They added that affected individuals and the relevant authorities were informed of the breach as investigations continue with Alliance Medinet.

In the Oct 24 bourse filing, Alliance Healthcare Group said: “Upon discovery, the team acted immediately to contain the incident and engaged independent cybersecurity and forensic specialists to support a formal investigation and further strengthen our IT systems, processes and security controls.

“We have also put in place 24-hour network monitoring and Dark Web monitoring systems.”

The incident was reported to the Personal Data Protection Commission of Singapore on Oct 21, the group said.

A fuller update will be provided in due course, Alliance Healthcare Group said, adding that the rest of the group is not affected by the incident.

“Protecting the information entrusted to us is a responsibility we take very seriously, and we remain committed to working closely with all parties to resolve the matter,” Alliance Healthcare Group said.

“The company and Medinet regret the inconvenience this incident has caused.”

The Straits Times has contacted the affected insurers for more information.
