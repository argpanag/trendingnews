Drama in the dugout an intriguing subplot of Glentoran’s trip to Cliftonville

TheTools · 19.09.2026 13:51 · source · permalink

While the 20th anniversary of Northern Ireland’s famous 3-2 win over Spain at Windsor Park has just passed by, another visit to Belfast by the Spanish more than four years earlier provides an interesting vignette into the meeting of Cliftonville and Glentoran this weekend.

A 5-0 thumping at the hands of Spain in April 2002 came a few weeks after current Reds boss Jim Magilton won his final international cap.

Although he might not have known it at the time, the Lenadoon man last pulled on the Northern Ireland for the goalless draw against Liechtenstein in March 27 2002.

He was named in the squad for the Spain match three week later alongside the man he will face in the visiting dugout Paddy McCourt.

However, although both were named in the same Northern Ireland squad, they never played together as Magilton had to pull out of the squad for the game which saw McCourt win his first cap as a 77th minute substitute for Keith Gillespie.

The end of one’s international career coincided with the beginning of the other’s.

McCourt would go on to win 18 international caps across a 13-year period and although his career did quite overlap with Magilton’s, the two men have one thing in common: neither of them want to be the next Glentoran manager.

After Declan Devine left the Oval hot seat earlier this week following a poor start to the season, Glentoran immediately set their sights on Magilton.

However, the former Ipswich midfielder rejected the club’s approach, opting to remain at Solitude.

The east Belfast club have moved to a full-time model under owner Ali Pour and have made some high-profile signings, such as the return of Conor McMenamin to the Oval at the end of the summer.

However, Magilton could not be tempted away from Cliftonville who themselves are on the verge of major investment.

The recent green light given to Canadian consortium Toronto Investment Group (TIG) means the north Belfast club stand to benefit from an outlay of £3m over the next three years.

The stability of Magilton at the helm is believed to have been a key motivating factor for the TIG investors.

Meanwhile, McCourt will continue in the dugout for Glentoran’s trip to Solitude on Saturday alongside former Reds winger Tim McCann.

Former Derry City and Celtic player McCourt took charge of the team for Tuesday night’s narrow loss to leaders Coleraine, but said in the wake of that game that he isn’t interested in that becoming a permanent arrangement.

“The club are undertaking a process now to appoint a manager, Paul [Millar] asked me on Sunday night would I stay around and help Tim [McCann] out until that process happens,” McCourt told BBC Sport NI.

“Once that happens we’ll have another conversation but under no circumstances am I trying to audition for the job, I’ve no interest in the job. I feel absolutely wholly responsible for where we are.”

On the back of a battling display against Coleraine, a win over Cliftonville would certainly lift the mood around the club.

The Reds lost to champions Larne on Tuesday but sit five points ahead of the Glens in fourth place.

Glentoran were winners on their last league visit to Solitude in April and knocked the Reds out of the League Cup at the quarter-final stage last season.

Midfielder James Douglas believes they can draw on those results and build on their performance against Coleraine when they cross the city on Saturday afternoon.

“We had some excellent results against Cliftonville last season, particularly at Solitude, but they will count for nothing when the game begins,” he said.

“Cliftonville lost to Larne on Tuesday night, but they will probably look at our recent results and feel this is a good time to play us. We have to make sure it isn’t and show them what we are capable of.

“The Coleraine performance has to be a marker for us. That level must become the minimum standard going forward.

“We are the only people who can turn this around, and the only way we will do that is through hard work. There is a real determination within the squad and nobody is feeling sorry for themselves.

“We have to remain resilient and stick together. We are extremely grateful for the support the fans continue to give us, and I know they will travel in numbers again on Saturday. It is up to us to repay that faith.”
