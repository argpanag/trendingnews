Wealth in a Poor Man’s Game: Arman Tsarukyan Can Buy Anything Except the One Thing He Wants | Deadspin.com

TheTools · 20.09.2026 00:51 · source · permalink

MMA tells the same origin story over and over. A kid with nothing finds a gym, fights his way out, and the belt becomes the way to feed a family. Arman Tsarukyan has never fit that script. He had the cars, the watches and the travel long before the UFC started paying him real money.

What he does not have is a UFC title. On Saturday, September 19, at UFC 331 in Los Angeles, the No. 2 ranked lightweight fights Mauricio Ruffy in a five-round co-main event that could finally put him back in front of the belt he has been chasing for seven years.

Not the Usual Fighter Backstory

Tsarukyan was born on October 11, 1996, in Akhalkalaki, a small Georgian town near the Armenian border, into an Armenian family. He is the middle of three children, with an older brother and a younger sister. When he was three, the family moved to Russia looking for better opportunities and eventually settled in Khabarovsk, in the country's Far East.

His father, Nairi Tsarukyan, built a successful construction business there, with Arman's mother, Anahit Hakobyan, also involved in the family company. That business is the source of the lifestyle fans see on Arman's Instagram: real estate, sports cars, luxury resorts. After his 2020 win over Davi Ramos, his father reportedly rewarded him with a new BMW M5.

The Part of the Story People Skip

The family money was not there from day one. Tsarukyan has said the first years in Russia were rough: no house, a father working all day, and a stretch where the family slept in a car before another family helped them get on their feet.

Once the business took off, nothing came free either. By his own account, 11-year-old Arman was earning 500 rubles an hour working for his father because he wanted a moped and was told to pay for it himself. Years later he tried the family company full time and lasted three months. The hours were brutal, the sleep schedule was worse, and he decided a life in sport was the easier road.

Hockey First, Fighting Second

Growing up in Khabarovsk gave him options most future fighters never had. He started in freestyle wrestling and did well, then fell hard for ice hockey, spending roughly a decade in the sport and playing for the junior team of Hockey Club Amur. Even then, wrestling never left. He was going to freestyle sessions before hockey practice.

Around 17 he walked away from hockey for good and went all in on combat sports, competing in wrestling, grappling and pankration before turning pro in MMA at 18. By that age he was already driving cars most people twice his age could not afford, which makes what came next more interesting, not less.

Thrown in the Deep End

Tsarukyan was knocked out in just his second professional fight. A comfortable kid with a safety net could have called it a day. He went on a long winning streak instead and earned a UFC contract.

His debut came in April 2019, on short notice, at 22 years old, against a rising Dagestani lightweight named Islam Makhachev. Tsarukyan lost a decision but pushed Makhachev harder than almost anyone has, and the two took home Fight of the Night. Makhachev went on to become a two-division champion. Tsarukyan went on to become the man nobody at 155 pounds wants to be matched with.

10-1 Since, and Still Waiting

Since that debut he is 10-1 in the UFC, with the only loss a close five-round decision to Mateusz Gamrot in 2022. He has not lost since. The run includes a first-round knockout of Beneil Dariush, a split decision over former champion Charles Oliveira at UFC 300, and a second-round arm-triangle submission of Dan Hooker in Qatar last November.

The title shot did come once. Tsarukyan was booked to rematch Makhachev for the lightweight belt at UFC 311 in January 2025, and a back injury forced him out shortly before the event. He has been climbing back ever since, and the division moved on without him. Ilia Topuria took over the belt, Justin Gaethje and Paddy Pimblett fought for an interim title in January, and in June Gaethje stopped Topuria at UFC Freedom 250 on the White House lawn to become undisputed champion.

Tsarukyan filled the gaps by competing anyway. He has been a regular in Real American Freestyle wrestling, beating Urijah Faber in April before dropping a narrow match to Colby Covington in July, and he picked up a quick submission in a grappling main event in Armenia on August 1.

Rich, But Not the Way the Internet Thinks

The online version of the story has his father worth nine figures. Tsarukyan has pushed back on that more than once. He has described the company as a construction business run by a smart, hard-working man, not a billionaire's empire, and he put his own situation plainly to Ariel Helwani: "I cannot buy private jet. I cannot buy private yacht."

He is not apologetic about enjoying what he does have. His father's advice, he has said, was simple: "If you have money, enjoy it." The fast cars make sense at 29 in a way they will not at 50.

But the point he keeps returning to during UFC 331 fight week is the one that defines his career. "Money cannot buy a UFC title," he said on the UFC's Countdown show, adding that he would trade everything he owns for the belt.

What Saturday Means

Ruffy (14-2, 5-1 UFC) stepped in after Charles Oliveira withdrew from the card following the death of his Chute Boxe teammate Allan Nascimento. The Brazilian is ranked No. 10, trains alongside Alexander Volkanovski, and carries the kind of counter-striking timing that can end a fight in one exchange. He has also said the UFC told him the winner gets the next title shot: "That's the agreement with the UFC."

For Tsarukyan (23-3, 10-2 UFC), that makes this a fight with far more to lose than to gain on paper. He is the heavy favorite, the higher-ranked man, and the one who has already earned a title shot once. A win likely sets up Gaethje in 2027. A loss to a short-notice replacement sends him back down a ladder he has spent four years climbing.

Most fighters are chasing the money. Arman Tsarukyan turns 30 next month with the money already handled, fighting for the one thing on his list that cannot be bought.
