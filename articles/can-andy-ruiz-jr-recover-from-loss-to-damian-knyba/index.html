Can Andy Ruiz Jr recover from loss to Damian Knyba?

TheTools · 05.09.2026 04:36 · source · permalink
  • Boxing News

Andy Ruiz Jr thinks there’s still something left after another poor performance

Andy Ruiz Jr has had four “comeback fights” since 2021, and you could argue that he’s looked worse every time out, culminating in a loss tonight to Damian Knyba.

But Ruiz (35-3-1, 22 KO) says he still wants to come back again, and he’d like to do a rematch with Knyba (18-1, 11 KO), who will probably be trying to parlay this victory into a bigger fight than once again facing an ice cold Ruiz.

“I don’t know, I think it was just him holding,” Ruiz said. “Every time I tried to come in, he’d hold me really tight, and I just couldn’t let my combinations go. But he played a good game plan, but I’d love to run it back and do things a lot different.”

“It was just a long layoff, and it was a great opponent,” Ruiz continued. “I didn’t want to take an easy fight, so that’s why I took Damian. He’s a big guy. But we’ve got to take risks. … I’ve still got it in me, man. I’ve just got to stay active and I want to fight again.”

Two judges, Glenn Feldman and Kevin Morgan, scored the fight close at 114-113, meaning the seventh round knockdown Ruiz suffered ultimately made the difference, as the official scores go. Judge Tony Lundy had it 117-110, which seemed more in line with what we saw. Bad Left Hook unofficially had the fight 118-109 for Knyba, who may not have done anything special, really, but he dictated nearly the entire fight against a slow, lethargic Ruiz who just looked like he’s lost what used to make him a daunting opponent.

It has to be said, too, that Knyba did hold. He effectively used that to keep that tempo on his side, but it would be hard to blame the bulk of Ruiz’s loss on Knyba’s holding. It certainly wasn’t “just” that, or “just” the long layoff. Ruiz showed occasional signs of life in this fight, but also he looked like an aging, rusty fighter who has not truly been in the game for nearly seven years now.

You also can’t really blame Ruiz for wanting to believe that the old version of himself is still in there somewhere.

Do you think Ruiz, who is nearing his 37th birthday, really has another serious run at a heavyweight title in him, or should he consider stepping away from the sport for real this time?
