Dow loses 100 points, S&P 500 snaps 3-day advance as Alphabet and Caterpillar tank: Live updates

TheTools · 28.10.2026 22:06 · source · permalink

Stocks were under pressure Thursday, weighed down by the latest batch of corporate earnings.

The Nasdaq Composite shed 1.38% to end at 26,184.72, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.3% to close at 7,720.39 and snap a three-day advance. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 132.54 points, or 0.25%, to 52,342.56.

Alphabet lost 12% after it missed expectations on adjusted earnings per share and revenue in the third quarter. The Google parent posted negative free cash flow of $9.07 billion. The company kept its full-year capital expenditures forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion.

Fellow Dow member Caterpillar slid more than 9% for its worst day since March 2020. While the industrial giant reported better-than-expected revenue and earnings for the third quarter, sales in its construction industries business came in slightly below estimates.

Microsoft was also down, reversing course from its initial advance after growth in the Windows maker's Azure cloud infrastructure business helped earnings and revenue to beat analyst estimates in its fiscal first quarter ending in September, according to LSEG. The stock ended the session marginally lower.

Neoclouds pulled back as Microsoft's lower-than-expected capital spending commitment pointed to infrastructure partners taking longer to deliver data center capacity. Shares of CoreWeave dropped about 5%, while Nebius declined more than 5%.

The results come amid heightened scrutiny of the artificial intelligence trade. AI stocks, such as the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) and the Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM), have been through a rough patch for most of October, only to perk up in recent days. On Thursday, both funds were lower, with SOXX down more than 1% and DRAM falling more than 4%.

"I think we're in a better, more stable place in October and heading into November in the tech trade than we were in August and September. But that said, I think investors are becoming much more selective," said Art Hogan, chief market strategist at B. Riley Wealth Management. "I think we're in an environment where there's less concern and negativity over tech writ large, but I think specifically, you can certainly get announcements from individual players that will move the needle."

On Wednesday, the Federal Reserve decided to hold steady on rates, leaving the central bank's benchmark interest rate between 3.75% and 4%. The decision to keep policy unchanged was widely expected after the Federal Open Market Committee raised rates a quarter-percentage point in September.

This time, Fed policymakers said that inflation "remains elevated," but also that they are awaiting the "accumulation of more information" before taking further steps. The personal consumption expenditures price index — the Fed's preferred measure of inflation — on Thursday showed that the pace of price increases accelerated in September, with the monthly figure in line with expectations, while the annual rate came in just below Wall Street estimates.

The three leading U.S. indexes ended Thursday's session in the red.

The S&P 500 shed 0.3% to finish at 7,720.39, while the Nasdaq Composite pulled back 1.38% to 26,184.72. The Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 132.54 points, or 0.25%, to 52,342.56.

— Sean Conlon

Seven out of the 11 S&P 500 sectors were higher on Thursday afternoon, with real estate and healthcare taking the lead.

Real estate was up 2.1%, while healthcare rose 1.9%. Those two were followed by consumer staples, financials and utilities, all of which were higher by more than 1%.

In contrast, communication services and information technology bogged down the index, with the former dropping more than 6% and the latter sliding more than 1%.

The broad market S&P 500 was last down 0.6%.

The company earned an adjusted $7.20 per share on revenue of $20.68 billion. Analysts polled by LSEG expected a profit of $7.08 per share on revenue of $20.55 billion. However, sales for its construction industries business came in slightly below estimates.

Shares of Airbus rose as much as 2.3% after the aircraft maker posted a mixed quarter in which it missed on sales but beat on profits. 

The company reported revenue of 17.5 billion euros ($19.5 billion), missing FactSet expectations of 18.1 billion euros. Adjusted earnings before interest and tax amounted to just under 1.8 billion euros, ahead of expectations of 1.75 billion euros. 

Airbus faces significant challenges ramping up aircraft production to meet strong demand for fuel-efficient planes, with supply chain constraints, particularly Pratt & Whitney engine shortages, and quality issues slowing deliveries. 

The company on Wednesday reaffirmed its target of 870 aircraft deliveries this year, which would surpass its 2019 record of 863. Meeting the target would require 323 deliveries in the fourth quarter. 

Despite the heavily backloaded schedule, Airbus CFO Thomas Toepfer told CNBC he was confident it would meet its target, supported by improving supply chain conditions.

"The good thing is, in terms of supply chain, that has really become better than it was a year or two years ago," Toepfer said, adding that Airbus had the materials it needed in its factories to meet fourth-quarter deliveries.

- Elsa Ohlen

London-listed shares of Shell were 3% higher at an all-time high in morning trade after the oil major reported better-than-expected profit and record refining margins in the third quarter.

Adjusted earnings, the company's measure of net profit, rose to $10.81 billion from $5.43 billion a year ago, ahead of the $9.92 billion forecast in an LSEG-compiled consensus.

Elevated commodity prices and global tightness in refined products meant the company also announced hiked its quarterly share buyback program to $5 billion from £3 billion.

— Jenni Reid

German automaker Volkswagen recorded a more-than $6 billion loss in its third quarter, amid Chinese market weakness and U.S. tariff costs.

The Porsche and Audi owner on Thursday posted an operating loss of nearly 5.9 billion euros ($6.6 billion), while sales revenue also tanked 6.3% to 75.3 billion euros.

The company recorded 8.7 billion euros in special items, mostly write-downs, with 6.4 billion euros related to Porsche Goodwill and about 1 billion euros on assets in China, as well as costs tied to the planned sales of its Osnabrück plant.

"The nine-month results make one thing clear: We cannot afford to lose any time and must act quickly and decisively," Volkswagen's Chief Financial and Chief Operating Officer Arno Antlitz said in the report.

Antlitz said the results were affected by costly U.S. tariffs, a shrinking market in China, and a significant increase in competitive pressure in Europe.

"After nine months, our operating return on sales before special items stands at 3.8 percent. That is too low to continue investing strongly in our future," he added.

The company has forged ahead with its restructuring program, with announcements this year that it plans to slash 100,000 jobs in total.

Volkswagen was trading below the flatline after the opening bell and is down 34% so far this year.

Volkswagen is forecasting group sales revenue in 2026 of around 315 billion euros for the full year.

— Sawdah Bhaimiya

Danish Brewer Carlsberg reported a decline in beer sales on Thursday, as consumers across "all generations" moderated their alcohol habits, its CEO told CNBC.

The beverage giant reported that its third-quarter volume grew 5% overall, with soft drinks up 9% and alcohol-free brews rising 11%. However, its premium beer sales declined 3%. The company said revenue grew 5% to 25.3 billion Swedish Kronor ($2.5 billion).

"People are not leaving alcohol, but they are moderating within the category. So they are switching more between alcoholic and non-alcoholic products," Carlsberg's CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Thursday.

"What we are seeing is that all generations are moderating... we all talk about the young generations here, but actually, it's you and me."

Carlsberg also saw a 6.7% volume decline in Asia, which Aarup-Andersen attributed to a "subdued consumer" in China as well as severe weather across the summer, which impacted beer sales. Aarup-Andersen also said that the European consumer is sensitive to costs and looking for better value.

"Beer and generally beverages is a very resilient category...this is not a luxury good. People see it as an everyday staple," he said. "But of course there is a price sensitivity, especially in lower mainstream... and across Europe we see affordability as a real issue."

The company maintained its full-year forecast with operating profit expected to grow between 4% and 6%.

— Sawdah Bhaimiya

French carmaker Renault is maintaining its full-year outlook on Thursday despite seeing a 6% drop in third-quarter car sales.

The company reported Thursday that third-quarter group sales fell to 497,799 vehicles due to a "backdrop of fierce competition," while auto revenues stayed flat at 9.8 billion euros ($10.9 billion). The company attributed declining sales to challenging conditions in Turkey and Latin America, particularly Argentina.

Despite this, revenues rose 1.5% to 11.6 billion euros as sales of electric vehicles jumped 84.7% year-on-year and accounted for just over a quarter of passenger cars sold. The company's shares have declined 25.6% since the beginning of the year.

"Renault Group delivered resilient Q3 revenue, once again demonstrating the strength and agility of our operating model," Renault's chief financial officer, Duncan Minto, said in the report.

"This agility was particularly evident in Q3 as we successfully adapted to Europe's fast-shifting energy mix, leveraging our dual EV and hybrid strategy along with the efficiency of our manufacturing & supply chain setup."

— Sawdah Bhaimiya

South Korean tech giant Samsung Electronics on Thursday reported third-quarter operating profit that is broadly in line with analysts' expectations, as demand for advanced memory chips used in artificial intelligence applications continued to grow.

Samsung's revenue rose 126.88% year-on-year and 13.85% from the previous quarter, while operating profit rose 783.11% from a year earlier.

During the third quarter, Samsung Electronics and five of its affiliates announced a combined $1 billion investment in KKR-backed Helix Digital Infrastructure. Samsung Electronics will invest $500 million, with the remaining amount coming from its affiliates.

Samsung has also announced a partnership with French AI startup Mistral AI to use AI technologies in its semiconductor operations, including chip design and manufacturing. The company also led Mistral's Series D funding round.
