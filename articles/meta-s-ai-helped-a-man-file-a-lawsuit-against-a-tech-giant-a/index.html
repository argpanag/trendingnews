Meta's AI helped a man file a lawsuit against a tech giant after it caused him to lose access to Facebook

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:33 · source · permalink

An American businessman has filed a $75,000 lawsuit against Meta after the platform's automated security systems allegedly mistook its own AI assistant for a hacker and blocked its workers. However, instead of hiring lawyers, the man entrusted the preparation of the lawsuit to the very artificial intelligence that caused the ban.

An American businessman has filed a $75,000 lawsuit against Meta after the platform's automated security systems allegedly mistook its own AI assistant for a hacker and blocked its workers. However, instead of hiring lawyers, the man entrusted the preparation of the lawsuit to the very artificial intelligence that caused the ban.

How an AI assistant caused the block

Entrepreneur Joe Thomas, owner of Kong Koatings Epoxy Countertops, has been heavily using Meta’s Muse AI agent (which he named Baby Mia after his granddaughter) to run his business. The assistant has been writing emails, creating ads, and generating videos for the company’s Facebook pages, Dexerto reports .

When Thomas gave Baby Mia direct access to his business accounts, Meta’s security algorithms recognized the agent’s activity as suspicious and disabled the pages. The blocking lasted for about a week, causing the entrepreneur to lose a stream of new customers from Facebook advertising.

The bureaucratic onslaught of artificial intelligence

According to Thomas, he did not write a single letter himself - the entire fight for restoring access was taken on by an AI assistant. During the dispute, Baby Mia sent more than 100 emails to various departments and officials, including:

  • to the FBI, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC);

  • to the Better Business Bureau (BBB), the California Attorney General, and a number of attorneys;

  • to congressmen and Senator Tim Scott, and also sent a fax to Donald Trump's office.

Eventually, the appeals reached Senator Scott's office. They prepared a special release of data to help the entrepreneur get his accounts back — the document officially listed Baby Mia as the AI ​​assistant who should receive updates on the case. After the senator's office intervened, a Meta employee contacted Thomas, and access to the pages was restored.

$75,000 lawsuit without attorney fees

Despite the accounts being unblocked, Joe Thomas filed a lawsuit in Oconee County, South Carolina, on November 16. The complaint alleges that the week-long outage cost his business at least $25,000 in lost revenue, and the total amount of damages claimed is $75,000. The entrepreneur noted that he personally only signed the documents, while the entire text of the lawsuit was prepared by artificial intelligence.

“I couldn’t have written all this myself, I wouldn’t even know what to do. Now people like me get a chance to defend themselves in court without spending thousands of dollars when big tech companies attack us. Before AI, no one in my position would even think of suing,” the entrepreneur explained.

The case is still ongoing, and it is currently unknown whether Thomas will be able to recover compensation from Meta.
