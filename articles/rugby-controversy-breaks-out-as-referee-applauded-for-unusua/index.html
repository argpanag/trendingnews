Rugby controversy breaks out as referee applauded for unusual red card after 15 minutes - AOL

TheTools · 19.10.2026 12:39 · source · permalink

Referee Hollie Davidson has been widely commended for her firm and resolute response after issuing Toulouse player Rodrigue Neti with a rare permanent red card just 15 minutes into the Champions Cup fixture against Exeter. Davidson intervened following Neti's hit on Ross Vintcent with a tucked shoulder to the head in an incident the Premier Sports commentators labelled as "horrible". The poor tackle attempt prompted audible reactions from the spectators, with Davidson immediately requesting that the TMO replays of the incident.

Slow-motion footage offered no kinder perspective, with Davidson determining there were no mitigating circumstances whatsoever.

Following the implementation of 20-minute red cards, match officials have frequently faced criticism for selecting the easier route by issuing only yellow cards for severe incidents and referring decisions for assessment by the off-field bunker team.

This has led to certain players receiving only 20-minute red cards for serious incidents, instead of their side being reduced to 14 for the remainder of the contest.

Davidson had seen sufficient evidence, however, methodically working through her assessment with assistance from her officials before explaining: "It's fist down and shoulder tucked. Therefore, we apply no mitigation to our process. We have a player who is upright that makes direct contact [to the head] with a tucked arm and high degree of danger. It is a permanent red card."

Neti was notified of the outcome, offering an apology to Vintcent before leaving the pitch.

Premier Sports analyst Ian Madigan commented: "As Hollie Davidson explained it, Neti is always illegal here. That's the type of tackle we want to get out of rugby. It's really dangerous.

"I thought she went through the process really well and made it clear. It would have been easy to give a yellow and leave the decision to the bunker."

Benjamin Kayser added: "The more you look at the replay, the worse it gets. That has to be taken out of the game. It is a clear red card.

"There were a lot of difficult decisions in that game. I thought she got most of them spot on. When you have to issue a red card in a home ground in front of 33,000 people it is a big consequence. She did it with a lot of good explanation and a lot of kindness."

Fans were equally impressed to witness a referee taking decisive action, with Davidson having previously demonstrated similar authority during this summer's Fiji versus England fixture.

One supporter on social media commented: "Some proper refereeing from Hollie Davidson; dangerous, no mitigation, not referring it to the bunker. Permanent red. Excellent to see a ref stand by their conviction."

Another wrote: "Well done to Hollie Davidson calling it as it is! No mitigation and no being swayed from the home crowd.

"Well played Hollie Davidson. Some refs would have hidden behind a 20-minute red card there, but a permanent red absolutely spot-on call," remarked another.

The ruling proved pivotal in the match, which developed into a thriller as Exeter came agonisingly close to making history before losing43-40. Henry Slade failed to convert a final-minute penalty that would have levelled the scores in a contest that saw the lead change hands seven times.
