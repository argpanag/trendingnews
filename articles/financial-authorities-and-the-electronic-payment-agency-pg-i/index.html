Financial authorities and the electronic payment agency (PG) industry are pushing for a "preemptive remedy" plan in which operators pay the damage to consumers first when fraudulent payments occur due to hacking and account theft. Amid growing concerns over personal information leakage due to the recent hacking of the financial sector, which is believed to have used artificial intelligence (AI), attention is being paid to whether consumer protection against possible secondary financial damage will be prepared in the future.

According to the financial sector on the 15th, the Financial Supervisory Service recently delivered an agenda to the "Online Fraudulent Payment Response Consultative Group" asking financial companies, payment operators, shopping malls, and platforms to consider ways to remedy financial consumers in the event of financial damage caused by online fraudulent payments. The consultative body was also found to have begun reviewing related measures. If the discussion proceeds as scheduled, there is a high possibility that a certain level of review opinions will be organized within this year.

The online fraudulent payment response council is a public-private joint organization involving financial authorities, the Financial Security Service, the Korea Fintech Industry Association, and major PG companies and experts to strengthen the safety and consumer protection of online payments. The discussion was reportedly put on the table of financial authorities at the 4th Financial Consumer Protection Advisory Committee presided over by Financial Supervisory Service Chairman Lee Chan-jin, with some advisors saying, "In the event of an online fraudulent payment accident, it is necessary to review the scope of consumer responsibility and the operator's duty to lead the way."

In particular, advisors pointed out that online fraudulent payments need to be adjusted in advance, as there have been cases in which victims suffered significant losses due to lack of clear liability standards in the early stages of voice phishing. The financial authorities said, "We will deliver our advisory opinions to the online fraudulent payment response consultative body that is currently operating so that the relevant contents can be faithfully reflected in the discussion process for preparing standard practice guidelines."

The authorities and the industry are considering pioneering measures to reduce the problem of victims taking losses while it takes time to cover up responsibility in the event of a fraudulent payment accident. According to the Bank of Korea, the average daily amount of electronic payment agency usage has steadily increased from 1.529 trillion won in 2022 to 1.2897 trillion won in 2023, 1.4238 trillion won in 2024 and 1.5542 trillion won in 2025. In the first half of this year, it increased 12.6% year-on-year to KRW 1.7234 trillion.

As the amount of use increases, consumer damage can also increase in the event of fraudulent payments. Financial authorities are also said to have raised a sense of problem that "responsibility standards must be prepared before the damage begins in earnest."

What financial authorities are paying attention to is the case of the United States and the United Kingdom. U.S. Regulation E requires a financial company to temporarily deposit the dispute amount into a consumer account if it fails to complete the investigation within 10 business days when a consumer reports an electronic transfer that has not been approved. If the investigation later confirms that it is a normal transaction, the amount can be recovered.

The UK goes one step further and requires payment service providers to refund the damage and restore the account by the next business day after recognizing the fact in the event of an unauthorized payment transaction. From 2024, a mandatory repayment system has been introduced even for frauds in which victims were deceived by fraudsters and transferred money directly. If certain requirements are met, in principle, the payment business operator repays the damage within five business days.

In Korea, there is a great concern about secondary financial damage due to the theft of personal information in the future due to a series of hacking attacks in the financial sector using AI tools. In fact, hacking and account theft are the typical types of fraudulent payment accidents prescribed by financial authorities. This is the case of stealing existing member accounts of shopping malls and platforms such as "Credential Stuffing," which collects a large amount of IDs and passwords from the outside and attempts to log in by indiscriminately substituting them as automation tools. Credential stuffing is also an attack technique used in the hacking of the AI-initiated financial sector.

An official from the financial sector said, "If the attacker secures additional personal information and simple payment methods, it can lead to damage in the form of purchasing gift certificates or products with high currency and converting them into cash," adding, "It is a problem linked to secondary consumer damage that may occur after information leakage."

Attackers try to cash in by directly joining shopping malls and platforms with personal information acquired by phishing, smishing, and malicious codes. In addition, there are cases in which customer information and payment method information are inquired and stolen after unauthorized access with administrative authority by exploiting the security vulnerabilities of the system.

However, there are also issues that must be overcome in order to introduce a pioneering system. As several operators, including banks, credit card companies, PG companies, and shopping malls, are involved in the transaction process, the responsible entity may vary depending on the cause of the accident. Regulations on cases where consumers have intentional or gross negligence are also needed.

Equity with other crime victims is also an issue. In the case of lease fraud, the government recently introduced a minimum guarantee system that guarantees at least one-third of the lease deposit, but there was constant noise during the discussion. Voice phishing is also not a full-scale relief for consumers by returning the remaining damages after suspending the payment of fraudulent accounts.
