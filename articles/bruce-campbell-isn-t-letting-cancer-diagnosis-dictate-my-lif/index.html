Bruce Campbell isn't letting cancer diagnosis 'dictate my life any more than it has to'

by Elizabeth Stanton

Bruce Campbell is doing well with his recent cancer diagnosis, “considering.”

“It's one of those considering conversations,” he told PauseRewind’s Elizabeth Stanton. “When nothing is symptomatic, I don't even know that I have anything. There's no nagging anything, but it's the whack-a-mole. You know, sh-t happens, you whack it. We're gonna wait for something else to whack. So we're in between whacks, and I hope to go to 18 cities without any issues.”

Campbell is set to embark on an 18-city tour with his new film, “Ernie & Emma,” which he wrote, produced, directed, and starred in. The tour kicks off September 4 at the Alamo Drafthouse in Los Angeles. Campbell will be doing Q&As at each stop and the film will continue to play at each location for a full week.

Promoting the film has been “healing” for Campbell, who revealed he was diagnosed with cancer earlier this year.

“The mental state is important for health-challenged people, and there's nothing more uplifting for me [than to] go show these people a movie I made with my wife, free from the studio system, free from any encumbrance whatsoever, and we were able to get it into theaters,” Campbell said. “So there's a huge joy that comes with that, a relief, a gratitude from all the fans who are showing up, that wouldn't necessarily race out to see ‘Ernie & Emma.’ I think they'll see it out of curiosity. But you know, it has been something that I'm very grateful for the ability to be able to do this.”

He continued, “When I'm going to these events, there is no cancer. There's none. I don't think about it. I don't reference it. Sometimes people bring it up, and I go, ‘Oh, right, right, that. Okay, sure.’”

The 68-year-old explained his diagnosis is more of a “chronic condition” and he’s “living with it.”

“I’ve actually never been busier in between appointments. It's like I'm out of here, dude. I've actually moved some appointments around because I'm like, well, I'm not back till the following week, so I can't come in and see you then because I don't want this thing to dictate my life any more than it has to,” Campbell said.

“I'll go do the thing. I won't ignore it, and I'm a good little patientbut I have to do other stuff, and I have to get back to what I do, which is interacting with people, making movies, and cracking jokes,” he said.
