Will Željko Mitrović try to fly into the arms of the students?

TheTools · 01.11.2026 21:07 · source · permalink

Society

Judging by past experience, Željko Mitrović's business principle is to make arrangements with every authority. Judging by the previous activity of the Student Movement, it will not succeed this time

Among the most memorable scenes from the immediate aftermath of October 5, 2000 is when Nenad Canak, then the leader of the League of Social Democrats of Vojvodina, played the piano at TV Pink.

At that point, Pink was considered television, which just days before Chunk's performance of "My Way" was a symbol of the sinister media system they had spawned Slobodan Milosevic and Mirjana Marković.

Nothing was left to chance, a Zeljko Mitrovic managed to establish a dichotomy: scantily clad female dancers swirled around Chanko sitting at the white piano. It was clear that he was sending a message that Pink would keep kitsch culture as the light motif of his editorial policy, but also that his previous media work under the post-Milošević authorities would be boiled and centrifuged at 90 degrees, with softener, and white as snow.

Before Chunk's performance on October 5th, Pink interrupted the program for two hours. When the broadcast resumed, the program was opened by Dragoljub Đuričić's drummers, and then he addressed the viewers Mladjan Dinkic, one of the prominent representatives of DOS. It was a clear message that this television will adapt to the new political reality or it will not exist.

The opportunism and tenacity of Pink at that moment were not just a coincidence of happy circumstances, because Pink was not created as a media project that accidentally established good relations with the authorities. From the very beginning, he was closely connected with the political and business environment of the nineties.

"Open letters" to Vučić's dissidents

Changing sides after the Fifth of October did not mean that Pink became an independent media, nor that Mitrović decided to reconsider his own role during the nineties.

Already on October 27, 2000, he sold a part of Pink to the Austrian company Mitsui, justifying the move by the need for fresh capital and the expansion of international connections. That deal caused serious public suspicion. NUNS assessed the transaction as possible money laundering, while certain organizations warned that the entry of foreign capital could serve as a protection against possible review of previous business. Mitrović rejected such accusations.

In the following years, he maintained good relations with various centers of political power, and made sure not to hold grudges Zoran Djindjic, not later Boris Tadić.

A new turn occurred with the coming to power of the Serbian Progressive Party in 2012, during which it experienced a real political and propaganda boom.

Pink gradually became one of the most important media mainstays Aleksandar Vučić. The information program acquired a distinctly one-sided political, practically propaganda function. On TV Pink, confrontations with the opposition, critics of the government and unfit public figures have become everyday.

Mitrović's "open letters" to Vučić's dissidents are particularly well-known, and were, to put it mildly, on the verge of good taste.

Much darker - some Mitrovic critics consider it even pernicious - was the "addition" that Pink published about Oliver Ivanović.

A love story about Vučić and the gum factory

Mitrović did not hide his support for Vučić's new government. In September 2012, in an interview for Kurir, he spoke about the positive signs given by the new government and the expectations that the political environment in the country will change.

He was even more explicit in December 2018, as a guest in the morning program of his own television, when he stated that he does not see any problems in the current government and that Pink supports its policies. He described Aleksandar Vučić as a politician that Serbia has never had and, in his opinion, will not have for the next 200 years.

His explanation of why he fell in love with Vučić is particularly interesting. Mitrović said that Pink had a tax debt in 2013 and that he asked the then First Deputy Prime Minister for a certain type of concession. According to him, Vučić gave him 30 days to settle his obligations, with a warning that otherwise the company would go bankrupt. Mitrović claimed that because of this he had to sell ten percent of Pink and the chewing gum factory, but he interpreted this very action as proof that everyone is treated equally in Vučić's Serbia.

This is how an unusual political love story was born, in which the owner of the television fell in love with the president because he did not see through his fingers.

What followed was an uninterrupted campaign of glorifying the character and deeds of Aleksandar Vučić and attacking his political opponents.

Can the year 2000 be repeated?

Current political events once again raise the question of how closely Pink is tied to one political group and whether Mitrović could repeat the move from October 2000.

It is indicative that on October 12, 2026, his open letter to Aleksandar Vučić was read on the show Hit Tweet. In it, Mitrović wished the president success in the elections, calling him a friend and telling him that his stability and firmness are a prerequisite for Serbia's progress and future. He ended with the signature: "Your comrade and friend forever Željko Mitrović, aka Žekspir".

A few days after the victory of "Student List", on October 29, Mitrović announced that he was disbanding the ensembles of the private Odeon Theater, which left 150 people out of work, and he also announced the dismissal of approximately a thousand employees in other companies of his business group.

Of course, the reduction of the business system does not prove that Mitrović has started to change his political course by storming the theater where the real or pretend audience were Vučić's followers. Economic problems can have completely different causes.

However, the little time that passed between the political upheaval and the announcement of a major restructuring of his empire can hardly go unnoticed.

Just a few days before the announcement of the layoffs, Mitrović publicly expressed his unreserved loyalty to Vučić. For now, therefore, there is no evidence that he has already begun to approach the new government, but the history of Pink shows that such a possibility should not be dismissed in advance.

And visibly, under the urgently changed tone of pink news programs, in which representatives of the Student List and "opposition leader" Aleksandar Vučić come to the fore, suddenly divided into equal parts. For now.

Judging by past experience, Vučić could soon be denied the opportunity to continue poisoning the public through Pink's channels and to face "revolutionary justice" on that television, because Mitrović's basic business model is arranging with the current government. Any.

Judging by the previous activity of the Studnet movement, it will not succeed this time.
