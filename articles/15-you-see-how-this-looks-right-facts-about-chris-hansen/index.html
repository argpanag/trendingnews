15 ‘You See How This Looks, Right?’ Facts About Chris Hansen

TheTools · 10.10.2026 00:37 · source · permalink

Chris Hansen became one of television's most recognizable figures every time he calmly walked into a room, offered someone a seat, and asked his iconic question. His work on To Catch a Predator turned him into a household name, but there is considerably more to Hansen than the catchphrase and the confrontations that made him famous. His career has taken some genuinely strange turns, including unusual side projects, internet fame, financial troubles, and a surprisingly complicated relationship with his own pop culture image. With a new movie about his most famous work now bringing him back into the spotlight, some details about the real Chris Hansen are worth revisiting.

He Decided to Become a Journalist at 14

Hansen has said that watching the FBI and police investigation into Jimmy Hoffa's disappearance when he was 14 made him want to become a journalist.

He Was Already Reporting Before Graduating College

While still a senior at Michigan State University, Hansen landed a reporting job at WILX-TV in Lansing, Michigan, in 1981.

He Has Eight Emmys

Long before becoming famous for "Have a seat," Hansen won eight Emmy Awards for investigative reporting, breaking-news coverage, and other journalism.

He Investigated Airport Security Before Catching Predators

One of his earlier undercover investigations exposed security problems at U.S. airports and prompted an investigation by the Federal Aviation Administration.

The Simpsons Turned Him Into a Cartoon Character

In 2006, Hansen actually voiced himself in an episode of The Simpsons called "Loan-a Lisa."

30 Rock Also Used the Real Chris Hansen

The same year, Hansen appeared as himself on 30 Rock in the episode "The Break-Up," meaning he briefly existed in the worlds of both NBC comedy and The Simpsons.

South Park Made Him Cool to His Own Kids

After South Park parodied him in 2007, Hansen said his sons and their friends suddenly thought he was cool.

His Cameo Video Helped Inspire a Movie About Him

Director Lance Oppenheim received a personalized $40 Cameo from Hansen in 2024, became fascinated with Hansen's strange internet persona, and that video eventually helped inspire Primetime.

He Used the Cameo to Advertise Himself

During that supposedly personal video, Hansen congratulated Oppenheim on his documentary, told him to "have a seat," and then proceeded to promote his own true-crime shows on TruBlu.

The Same Cameo Apparently Costs More Now

Oppenheim noted that the Hansen Cameo he bought for $40 had risen to $140 by the time he was talking about it publicly.

He Turned His Own Catchphrase Into a Business Tool

Hansen now uses "have a seat" in personalized videos, social media appearances, and promotional material, turning the phrase that made him famous into part of his ongoing persona.

He Started His Own True-Crime Streaming Service

Hansen co-founded TruBlu, a streaming platform launched in 2022 that features true-crime programming and his own investigative series.
