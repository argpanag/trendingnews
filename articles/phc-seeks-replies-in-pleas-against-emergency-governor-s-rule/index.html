PHC seeks replies in pleas against emergency, governor’s rule

TheTools · 20.10.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

PESHAWAR: The Peshawar High Court (PHC) on Tuesday issued notices to the federal government, the Attorney General for Pakistan, Advocate General of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and other relevant authorities, seeking their replies to petitions challenging the imposition of governor’s rule and emergency in the province.

A two-member bench comprising Justice Ijaz Anwar and Justice Faheem Wali heard the petitions filed by Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan; Tehreek-e-Tahafuz-e-Aiyeen Pakistan through Mahmood Khan Achakzai; advocate Yasir Khattak; Akhtar Jan Mengal; advocate Qaiser Shah; the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Bar Council through Babar Khan Yousafzai and Fazli Wahid Khan and Shandana Gulzar, among others.

The court adjourned the hearing until November 10 and said it would issue a detailed order later.

During the proceedings, Barrister Gohar told the court that governor’s rule and emergency had been imposed in the province under the pretext of terrorism, despite the PTI’s consistent support for the federal government in the fight against militancy. He submitted that dozens of party lawmakers and workers had been picked up, offices of district development advisory committees (DDACs) had been demolished, and signboards installed at development projects completed by the previous provincial government had been removed or destroyed. He contended that raids were being conducted at the residences of PTI lawmakers and that party members were being harassed. He asked the court to intervene and restrain the authorities from taking such actions, warning that the measures could deepen political divisions and fuel public resentment.

Barrister Gohar also told the court that deputy commissioners, district police officers and officials of the Advocate General’s Office had been transferred on October 11.

Barrister Sameer Khosa argued that the circumstances did not warrant the imposition of governor’s rule, maintaining that the provincial government had been functioning and that the situation was not beyond its control. He pointed out that the provincial chief minister and assembly speaker were reportedly missing, with their whereabouts unknown, and questioned the justification for sidelining the elected provincial leadership.

Responding to a question from Justice Ijaz Anwar about whether the petitioners had sought the production or recovery of the missing officials, Khosa said that this relief had been specifically requested.

Khosa referred to the Supreme Court’s judgment in the Pir Sabir Shah case, arguing that even if an emergency were imposed, the chief minister and provincial cabinet should continue to function. He maintained that in the present case, the chief minister, cabinet and advocate general had been rendered ineffective despite the provincial assembly and elected government remaining in place and functioning.

PTI secretary general Salman Akram Raja also submitted that governor’s rule and emergency had been imposed without fulfilling the constitutional and legal requirements. He asked the court to declare the measures unlawful and restore the provincial assembly.

Advocate Moazam Butt challenged the legality of the measures, arguing that the constitutional requirements for imposing emergency had not been met. He questioned the absence of governor’s report cited as the basis for the decision and contended that the provincial assembly’s approval was required under the relevant constitutional provisions. He argued that the provincial apex committee was the appropriate forum for addressing the deteriorating security situation.

Qaiser Shah, another petitioner, clarified that his petition did not challenge the imposition of emergency in 12 districts where, he acknowledged, the security situation was serious and beyond the provincial government’s effective control. He maintained that while emergency measures in the 12 districts were justified, imposing governor’s rule across the province was unlawful.

He said security forces were fighting militants and suffering casualties in those districts, where attacks on law enforcement personnel had continued. However, he argued that the situation in other districts, including Peshawar, Nowshera and Mardan, was comparatively stable and that the provincial administration and law enforcement agencies were functioning normally.

Justice Ijaz Anwar said the court had heard the petitioners and would issue notices to the federal government, the Attorney General for Pakistan, Advocate General of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and other relevant authorities.
