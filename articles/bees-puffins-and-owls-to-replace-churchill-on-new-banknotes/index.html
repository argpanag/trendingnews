Bees, puffins and owls to replace Churchill on new banknotes

TheTools · 08.10.2026 07:07 · source · permalink

Wartime prime minister and other historic figures removed after being deemed ‘elitist and divisive’

Bumblebees, puffins, barn owls and hedgehogs will feature on the Bank of England’s next series of banknotes, replacing Winston Churchill and other British historic figures.

The new banknotes will see the wartime prime minister removed from the £5 note when they are released over the next few years.

The Bank received more than 478,000 responses to a public consultation on which creatures should appear on the new notes.

However, the Bank has also faced criticism for its decision to axe sterling notes featuring portraits of historic figures.

Robert Jenrick, the Treasury spokesman for Reform, called the new banknotes series “ridiculous”.

He said: “The Bank should be focused on getting inflation down, not erasing our history with this nonsense.”

Churchill currently appears on the reverse of the £5 note; writer Jane Austen on the £10, JMW Turner, the 19th century painter, on the £20; and Alan Turing, the Second World War codebreaker, on the £50.

The Bank has argued that new notes with fresh designs are required to include updated security features and keep ahead of fraudsters.

Rhys Phillips, the Bank’s incoming chief cashier, said the new notes would combine “cutting-edge security features and materials science”.

However, research obtained by The Telegraph under the Freedom of Information Act found that the Bank had removed historic figures such as Churchill from banknotes after being told by focus groups they were “elitist and divisive”.

A report commissioned by the Bank revealed focus group participants had said the notable Britons were “contentious and not representative of the UK’s cultural and natural diversity”.

The report said respondents felt the figures represented “a backward-looking vision of the UK that carries too great a risk of division and controversy”.

The Bank began printing sterling banknotes featuring historic figures in 1970.

William Shakespeare was the first to feature on a British banknote, aside from a monarch, when he was depicted on the £20 note more than 50 years ago.

Churchill first appeared on the £5 note in 2016.

The Bank of England spent £22,500 on public consultations about which animals to feature on the banknotes, according to figures obtained under the Freedom of Information Act.

As part of the Bank’s month-long consultation, the public was asked to choose from a shortlist of 18 native animals including mammals, birds, amphibians, insects and fish.

The buff-tailed bumblebee was the most popular creature, receiving 360,399 nominations from the public.

After the survey, the Bank selected bumblebees, puffins, barn owls and hedgehogs to appear as the main images on the notes, alongside a portrait of the King.

Other creatures that appeared on the shortlist but were not as popular included the Atlantic salmon, the bottlenose dolphin and white-tailed eagle.

The banknote series will also feature nature imagery as part of their new design, with the possibility of some of the other shortlisted animals also being included, according to the Bank.

It has yet to be decided which of the winning animals will feature on each of the new £5, £10, £20 and £50 notes.
