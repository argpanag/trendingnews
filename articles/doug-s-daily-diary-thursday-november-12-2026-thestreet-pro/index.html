Boockvar on Nvidia, Yen, Rally Setup?

The following was written by Peter Boockvar:

Who was it that said ‘Truth is stranger than fiction’?/A few other things of note

A hugely successful company doesn’t have to stop being one just because its founder CEO is no longer there if the right people are in place and the best products that customers want are made. That said, it never really is the same. We watch from here the extent competitors take market share (AMD and Broadcom are trading up premarket) and wonder what comes of future investments and financing that Nvidia makes with so many of its partners. As of Q2 (and we’ll get updated figure next week), the company had $99 billion of equity investments in other companies which does not include all the vendor financing and lease guarantees they’ve made on the part of others.

From a contrarian standpoint, the more sour mood heading into this week was the perfect setup for a rally. Yesterday, Investors Intelligence survey said the number of Bulls fell to just 37 from 42.6 while Bears jumped to 24.1 from 18.5. Both are back to early April levels when markets at the time responded to the spike in oil prices and stock market selloff in March. The balance expecting a Correction remained the same at 38.9. Assume those figures capture sentiment as of last Friday. Today, AAII said Bulls fell by 3.3 pts to 30.8, the lowest since July and around the levels seen in March. Those Bulls went to Neutral as Bears declined too, by 4.4 pts to 41.4. The CNN Fear/Greed index is at 37 (Fear) vs 19 (Extreme Fear) one week ago.

With almost nothing else of big importance going on today and certainly difficult to compare anywhere close in relevance to what is going on with Jensen Huang, I’ll go over one earnings calls and a snippet from a release today.

From Brightview Holdings, a stock we own and one that you’ve likely never heard of. They are the largest commercial landscaping company in the country with about 75% of their business land maintenance and the balance new development and whose latter division was subject to a slowdown in residential community construction and which hit the stock by almost 14% yesterday:

“The slowdown in development was driven by three key factors. A sharpening decline in the residential construction end market, delays on several large jobs and delayed bid awards as customers navigate a volatile macro environment underpinned by higher interest rates and inflation. Since 2024, our development revenue related to residential projects has softened in line with the broader slowdown in residential construction.”

Yeti is down premarket after reporting earnings as revenues missed and mentioned this in their release, “Looking ahead, we anticipate the consumer environment will remain cautious and put pressure on demand in Q4.”

Japan said its October PPI jumped by 7.1% y/o/y, above the estimate of 6.5% and up from 6.8% in September. This is another example of a country seeing PPI running well ahead of CPI. Higher energy prices were certainly a key factor and have since pulled back this month as we know but the breadth of price increases were widespread.

As of now, the JGB market is pricing in an 82% chance of a December rate increase from the BoJ to 1.50%.

With the yen testing 160 again vs the US dollar, out comes Atsushi Mimura, the currency official at the Ministry of Finance who said “I have a strong impression that the currency moves over the past few days have been too fast. It’s too early to forget what happened this summer.” This was followed by a government spokesperson who said “I believe the yen being undervalued is a problem” and “The Ministry of Finance will continue to closely communicate with the US Treasury to maintain order in the currency markets.”

More Tales From Nvidia: How Jensen Huang Does Anything Is How He Does Everything (Issue #276!)

* In our series, “More Tales,”  we frequently questioned things like “roundtripping” (that the consensus freely accepted) and other aggressive company policy — now we know there was a reason to question Jensen Huang’s judgment on many levels (including the well-known and vast AI circular financing)

* With the benefit of hindsight (and the failure of investors to recall Jensen’s past aggressive use of accounting and his SEC violations of accounting and disclosure rules), most now recognize how Jensen Huang does anything is how Jensen Huang does everything 

* The company’s Board and investors have to now assess the most recent radius of his control of the company with regard to investments and other positions (like roundtripping) he has taken.

*  However, complicating matters is that the judgment of the Board must also be questioned if Jensen Huang’s affair was a secret that was only known to them — they are vulnerable and a wholesale change has more than a non-trivial possibility
