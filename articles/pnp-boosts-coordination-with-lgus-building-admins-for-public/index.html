PNP boosts coordination with LGUs, building admins for public safety

TheTools · 27.11.2026 06:36 · source · permalink

NATIONAL

by

Christopher Lloyd Caliwan

Nov 27, 2026

2:36 PM GMT+8

(PNP and DILG Infographic)

MANILA – The Philippine National Police (PNP) called for stronger coordination among the organization, local government units (LGUs), and private building administrators to identify safety hazards that could put lives at risk, following a fatal vehicle plunge in Makati City.


In a statement on Friday, PNP Chief Gen. Jose Melencio C. Nartatez Jr. said police units must work closely with local authorities and private establishments to address potential dangers in public spaces and prevent similar incidents.


“Our responsibility to protect lives does not end with preventing and solving crimes. Our police commanders should strengthen coordination with LGUs, building administrators, and concerned agencies to identify potential hazards, address safety concerns, and ensure that threats to public safety are brought to the attention of the proper authorities,” Nartatez said.


His directive came after a man died when the sedan he was driving fell from a parking building onto Jimenez Street in Barangay San Lorenzo, Makati City, on Nov. 26. The impact scattered debris as far as 15 to 20 meters from the vehicle, although no bystanders were injured.


The PNP Scene of the Crime Operatives and other authorities responded to the scene while Makati police began investigating the circumstances surrounding the incident.


Nartatez directed the Makati City Police to conduct a thorough investigation and establish the circumstances that led to the vehicle's fall. Investigators are expected to determine whether negligence or possible criminal liability was involved.


The PNP Chief stressed that public safety requires cooperation from motorists and private establishment operators, particularly those responsible for facilities used by large numbers of people.


“Public safety is a shared responsibility, and every decision made behind the wheel or in managing an establishment can have consequences for other people's lives. We urge motorists to exercise caution and property administrators to uphold safety standards because protecting lives must always take precedence,” he said. (PNA)
