Lawson earns top 10 start in Mexico despite penalty for Verstappen clash

TheTools · 31.10.2026 22:26 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Liam Lawson has earned a top 10 start for Formula One’s Mexican Grand Prix, despite carrying a one-place grid penalty into qualifying.

After posting the eighth-fastest mark in the final stage of qualifying (Q3) at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, a one-place grid drop sees the Kiwi start ninth in Monday morning’s race, after his practice clash with Red Bull’s Max Verstappen.

Regardless, at a time where his future is still to be decided, qualifying in the top 10 is another tick in the Kiwi’s column as he attempts to lead Racing Bulls to fifth in the constructors championship.

Lawson’s best mark of 1m 17.029s was 0.515s off the pace set by Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton, who claimed pole position for the first time this season, 0.009s clear of Mercedes’ George Russell.

Hamilton is the oldest driver to secure a pole position since Mario Andrettti in 1982.

Both he and Russell have the mathematical chance to claim the drivers championship this year, however that remains highly unlikely as Mercedes’ Kimi Antonelli closes in on the title.

Despite qualifying fifth, 0.193s off Hamilton, Antonelli only needs to finish ninth or higher to become the youngest champion in the history of the sport. Antonelli’s hopes will be boosted by Verstappen’s penalty for the Lawson incident, after the former world champion qualified fourth.

Even after posting the fastest times in both Friday practice sessions, Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc could only manage the third-fastest time in qualifying.

Meanwhile, Lawson’s teammate Arvid Lindblad was ninth, 0.088s back from the Kiwi, despite getting an extra practice session on his teammate after Racing Bulls fielded Red Bull rookie Nikola Tsolov the weekend’s opener.

The Mexican Grand Prix begins at 9am on Monday morning (NZT).

Taking to track in Q1, Lawson’s first attempt at setting a lap time was wiped out when Aston Martin’s Fernando Alonso stopped on track, to trigger a red flag that ended the Kiwi’s hope of posting his opening mark.

Once the session resumed, the Kiwi’s first effort of 1m 18.107s saw him temporarily in the top five. And even as he improved to 1m 18.097s, Lawson dropped down the order to advance as the 12th fastest car, albeit safe from elimination by 0.533s.

Into Q2, Lawson improved again to post a lap of 1m 17.628s to sit inside the top 10, but not safe as other cars began to post faster laps behind him. With pressure on to advance, the Kiwi went even better once again, posting a lap of 1m 17.207s that saw the end of McLaren’s Oscar Piastri, as a moral win for New Zealand over Australia.

Needing to finish ninth or higher to earn a top 10 start when the lights go out on Monday, Lawson’s first effort in Q3, 1m 17.357s, was slower than he managed to escape Q2, as Racing Bulls ran their drivers on a used set of tyres.

And staring at a 10th place start, Lawson timing his best effort at the end of Q3 saw him clear not just Lindblad, but Alpine’s Pierre Gasly too, to put himself on course for a 14th grand prix points finish of the year.
