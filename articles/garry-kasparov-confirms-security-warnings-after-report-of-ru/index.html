Garry Kasparov Confirms Security Warnings After Report Of Russian Assassination Plot

TheTools · 01.10.2026 16:08 · source · permalink

The 13th World Chess Champion Garry Kasparov says security services in the U.S. warned him and a fellow Russian opposition figure that their lives were in danger and that they should take precautions. His statement comes one day after a report by Spanish newspaper El Mundo that he was a target of an alleged Russian assassination plot.

In a post published Thursday, the former world champion said neither he nor Ivan Tyutrin, another targeted opposition figure, had been told explicitly that they were among the people whose names are withheld in a U.S. indictment. He said he had taken precautions and would continue to do so.

"But I will not stop and I will not hide," Kasparov wrote, pledging to continue his opposition to Russian President Vladimir Putin. He shared the article in a post on his X account:

Spain's second-largest newspaper reported on Wednesday, citing a source close to Kasparov, that a network linked to Russian intelligence had been planning attacks on Kasparov and other exiled opponents of Putin. It reported that the plans to kill former Russian lawmaker Ilya Ponomarev in the United States were further advanced.

The report comes after the U.S. Justice Department on September 15 revealed that five people had been charged over alleged plots to surveil and kill Russian dissidents and carry out attacks in Europe. U.S. prosecutors have not publicly named Kasparov or Tyutrin as targets in that case.

Their indictment refers to a dissident in the United States as “Victim-1,” whom El Mundo identifies as Ponomarev. According to prosecutors, a person recruited by the network photographed and filmed locations associated with Victim-1. Two of the defendants then offered that person $40,000 to carry out the killing. The person refused and was asked whether someone else could do it. The five defendants remain at large, and the charges have yet to be proven in court.

According to El Mundo, two targets were to be killed in the United States, while another potential victim was targeted in Vilnius, Lithuania.

63-year-old Kasparov was the world chess champion from 1985 to 2000. Although he retired from chess in 2005, he remains involved in the game through his relationship with the Saint Louis Chess Club. There, he recently played a Chess960 match against GM Veselin Topalov, eventually losing 16-8.

In recent years, he has become known as one of Putin's most prominent critics. In 2012, he was arrested outside a Moscow court, where he was reportedly subject to police brutality. He left Russia in 2013 and later co-founded the Free Russia Forum, a gathering of Russian opposition figures in Lithuania. Russia designated him a "foreign agent" in 2022, added him to its list of "terrorists and extremists" in 2024, and ordered his arrest in absentia later that year. Russian state-controlled news agency Tass reported in May that Kasparov had been placed on an international wanted list the previous December.
