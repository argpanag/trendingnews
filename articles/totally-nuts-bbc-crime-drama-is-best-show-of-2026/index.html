'Totally nuts' BBC crime drama is 'best show of 2026'

TheTools · 02.11.2026 18:33 · source · permalink

The darkly comic Irish crime drama is an instant hit with fans.

BBC viewers have found themselves immediately hooked by a 'totally nuts' series. Some have even declared that the new show is the best show of the year, just a day after its release.

Tall Tales and Murder debuted in its native Ireland a couple of months ago and now UK-based viewers get to see what all the fuss is about. While the first episode is set to air on Tuesday (November 3) at 10.40pm on BBC One, prospective viewers don't need to wait around.

That is because the entire series is already available to stream on iPlayer. As a result, it is possible to binge watch the six-part thriller in just a couple of evenings.

The show was actually co-created by Chris Addison, who used to be a regular comedian panellist on Mock the Week. He also starred and wrote episodes of The Thick of It before turning his attentions behind the camera.

He helmed a number of episodes of US sitcom Veep, which is thought of as an American version of The Thick of It. He also co-created comedy Breeders starring Martin Freeman and has teamed up with Irish writer Stuart Carolan for this new project.

Addison shared his excitement about its BBC release on social media. He described the series himself as "bananas" and "bonkers".

Tall Tales and Murder is actually loosely based on the Dublin 'trilogy' series of novels written by Caimh McDonnell. According to its premise, an anonymous patient awakes from a long coma in a nursing home in Dublin, and claims to be a gangster who knows the location of buried treasure. The series also follows unconventional nurse Bridget, who works the night shift at the care home.

She reads the old folk bedtime horror stories, arranges late-night dances and euthanises them if they ask nicely. Into this secret world comes Paul, a vulnerable young man stuck stealing cars to repay a debt to crime boss Ma Baker.

Paul is desperate to stay out of prison and needs a legitimate job to keep him safe, so he calls on his guardian angel - an irascible detective called Bunny Ryan. Bunny uses his personal history with the matron of Bridget’s nursing home to pull in a favour - a favour that will cost him dear.

On Bridget and Paul’s first shift together, the mysterious comatose patient known only as ‘Anonymous’ wakes up and reveals he is a long-lost gangster with a buried hoard of Celtic gold. Could this treasure be the answer to all of Bridget and Paul’s troubles? Will they risk everything to find it?

The series is yet to receive a significant number of critic reviews but fans have wasted no time in sharing their recommendations. One posted online and said: "This show is a black comedy. Brilliant and a breath of fresh air. Likeable and loveable characters involved in crime with a 'Crime Ma'.

"I love Bridget with her wee book about her patients and a lovable AOD. Paul and Bunny hard hapless and loyal to their women. I hope there a second series as great fun, totally nuts."

Another added: "Refreshingly original, competently funny and a very likable cast. I didn't know what to expect going in and oh boy was I pleasantly surprised. The humour is on point, the story fun and entertaining and the both lead actors are amazing with a great cast all around. Definitely on my list of best shows of the year."

Someone else said: "This was a rollercoaster of a adventure so many ups and downs tied to the characters. Fabulous by all involved in creating this. Well done to the actors, thank you for making this so enjoyable."

Tall Tales and Murder airs Tuesday at 10.40pm on BBC One and streaming on iPlayer.
