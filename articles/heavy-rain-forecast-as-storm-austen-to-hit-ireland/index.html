Heavy rain forecast as Storm Austen to hit Ireland

TheTools · 21.10.2026 00:01 · source · permalink

Getty

A Status Yellow rain warning for Ireland will be in effect from tomorrow evening as the first named storm of the season hits the country.

Storm Austen was named by the UK Met Office, as the UK will see the worst impacts of the storm in relation to winds.

Met Éireann said heavy rain associated with the storm will spread eastwards throughout tomorrow and into Friday morning, which could impact outdoor events, lead to difficult travelling conditions and localised flooding.

The warning will be in effect from 7pm tomorrow until 8am on Friday.

The forecaster has also issued a Status Orange - Marine Storm warning from Wicklow Head to Dungarvan to Valentia, on the Irish Sea South of Anglesey, with north to northwest winds reaching storm force 10 at times.

This warning will be in operation from 9pm tomorrow night until 7am on Friday.

Ailís Hankinson, operational meteorologist with Met Éireann, said "it is possible that there may be updates" to the warning associated with Storm Austen "over the coming days".

"It looks like the south of Ireland will feel the worst impacts and as a result, there may be upgraded warnings," she said.

Ms Hankinson said rain will hit the southwest of Ireland first "and will make its way across Ireland, based on the latest model that we have".

She added that by 8am on Friday, people can expect rainfall associated with the storm to gradually clear, with blustery showers following for the remainder of the day.

Met Éireann, the UK Met Office and the meteorological service in the Netherlands, KNMI, launched the list of names for the 2026/27 storm season, with each of the national partners contributing seven names, according to the forecaster.

The name Austen was submitted by Met Éireann.

Austen H Nagle was the first director of Irish Meteorological Service and worked in the British Meteorological Office from 1927 to 1936.

He later took up a senior post in the United States Weather Bureau.
