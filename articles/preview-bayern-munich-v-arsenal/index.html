Preview: Bayern Munich v Arsenal

TheTools · 21.10.2026 19:16 · source · permalink

A mouthwatering third Champions League match of the season awaits as we head to Germany to take on Bayern Munich at the Allianz Arena.

We've taken maximum points from our opening two league-phase matches, thanks to a 1-0 win at Napoli and a 3-0 home win over Lille, as we look to extend our run to 15 straight wins at this stage of the competition.

Bayern boast a formidable home record in this competition, having lost just one of their past 31 European ties at the Allianz. However, across their last 23 European matches, the only team to beat them by more than one goal is us, a 3-1 win when we last faced off in November 2025.

BAYERN BUILDING

Bayern come into this game still unbeaten in the Bundesliga after six games, but they haven't had things all their own way. A 5-1 win against Stuttgart opened their title defence, but they then drew 0-0 with Schalke. They scraped past newly promoted Elversberg but then seemed back to normal after a 7-0 trouncing of Union Berlin when Michael Olise hit a treble.

However, they had to come back twice to salvage a point against Augsburg, but on Saturday they registered another big win, beating Leipzig 6-1 with Diaz bagging a hat-trick. Progress has also been made to the second round of the German Cup after a 4-1 win at second-tier Osnabruck.

The quest to try and clinch a first Champions League title in seven years began with a 5-0 win over Bodo/Glimt, but last week saw them stunned by Viking, firstly falling 2-0 behind, and after coming back to take A 3-2 lead, conceded in the last minute to leave Norway with just a point.

WHAT THE MANAGERS SAY

Arteta: "I'm really looking forward to the game, this is the stage, this is the kind of team, this is the kind of coach, this is the kind of club, the stadium that you want to be involved in. So we have prepared for the game with lots of enthusiasm, and really thrive to come here and win the game.

"We know the kind of game we’re going to play, I think both teams are very clear on that, and we experienced that at the Emirates, and it’s going to be a very similar one in many moments, especially we have a lot of connections and positiveness in and around the team, so yeah, very excited about it."

Assistant manager, Aaron Danks: “Vincent has a virus. It's important that he rests. He's at home resting. We take it day by day. We're in constant communication. For me, not much changes. I plan the training sessions every day. Obviously we miss Vincent, but we have a strong coaching team, and we know what needs to be done. We need to stay calm and keep the players' energy high so they are ready.

"The key will be decided on small margins. We have to be prepared for every aspect of the game. We trust the process and the work we have done with the players in the last couple of seasons. We want them to play fearless and express themselves."

TEAM NEWS

Mikel Arteta confirmed that Martin Zubimendi will be involved after recovering from an injury he picked up after our win over Leeds. Piero Hincapie was in open training on the eve of the clash, doing individual work at the Sobha Realty Training Centre. He will miss this match but Mikel is hopeful he can return for our Premier League match with Everton.

William Saliba and Christos Tzolis are still making their way back from their respective injuries.

As for our hosts, Bayern will be without Michael Olise through suspension after he was sent off in last week's draw at Viking. Kompany has been battling illness, meaning he did not take the pre-match press conference, but is expected to be on the touchline for the clash.

Goalkeeper Manuel Neuer, who has played against us on 11 previous occasions, is a doubt with a foot injury. Attacker Jamal Musiala will miss the clash with a thigh injury, while captain Joshua Kimmich missed open training for personal reasons but could feature tomorrow.

TALKING TACTICS

Adrian Clarke: Bayern’s strength is to control matches with pass-and-move football, rotating positions seamlessly. Having netted 25 goals in their last six matches, they have plenty of firepower, but Olise and Musiala's absences do weaken them. In their 4-2-3-1 shape, deciding how to cope with Harry Kane’s positioning may be our biggest challenge. When the England captain drops into midfield, will one of our centre-backs follow him, or will he be passed onto a teammate? How we communicate in those moments will be crucial.

Out wide Luis Diaz’s pace and directness will test us down our right side. Because the Colombian often joins in as a second striker, Ezri Konsa will often be responsible for handling him. If he starts, whiz kid Lennart Karl, who scored against us last season, is a clever No.10, who we cannot afford to leave too much space for.

The weakness is in their off-the-ball work. The German champions like to play an expansive style, which does mean they leave themselves open at times. Keeping clean sheets in just three of their 10 games so far, opponents do tend to get chances. Minnows Viking scored from a corner, a counter-attack, and from a far-post header in that shock result.

Tactically, we must balance between pragmatism and adventure in this clash of the titans. You can’t go toe to toe with Bayern, but equally it would be risky to soak up waves of pressure too. Arteta will trust his players to defend stoutly, and picking the right times to commit bodies forward will be crucial to our chances of winning.

FACTS AND STATS

We have won each of our last 14 Champions League group-stage matches, and since we returned to the competition in 2023, only PSG (28) and Real Madrid (28) have won more games overall than us (27, level with Bayern Munich).

We are unbeaten in our last eight away matches in the Champions League (W6 D2), keeping five clean sheets in the run; this is already our longest unbeaten run on the road in the competition.

In our last meeting with Bayern in November 2025, the Germans had just eight shots, 14 touches in the opposition box and an expected goals total of 0.65 – all three of which remain Bayern’s lowest in a Champions League match under Vincent Kompany.

Through the opening two matchdays in the Champions League this season, the two players with the most line-breaking passes in the final third are Martin Odegaard (39) and Joshua Kimmich (33).

Four of Bukayo Saka’s 14 Champions League goals have come away from home, two of these were at Real Madrid and PSG. Only two players have ever scored away at all three of Bayern Munich, Real Madrid and PSG in the competition – Didier Drogba and Erling Haaland.

Bayern have lost just one of their last 31 home matches in the Champions League (W24 D6) and are unbeaten in their last eight since a 1-2 defeat to Inter Milan in the 2024/25 quarter-final, winning seven of their eight since then.

MATCH OFFICIALS

Italian referee Davide Massa is the man with the whistle for our trip to the Allianz Arena. The 45-year-old has taken charge of five of our European matches over the years, the most recent in 2025/26 when we demolished Atletico Madrid 4-0 in last season's league phase. 

He last took charge of a match in Germany in September 2019, when Bukayo Saka scored his first Gunners goal in a 3-0 win over Eintracht Frankfurt. Compatriots Filippo Meli and Alessio Berti will be his assistants. Fabio Maresca will be the fourth official, and Marco Di Bello will be on VAR duty.

Referee: Davide Massa (ITA)

Assistants: Filippo Meli, Alessio Berti (ITA)

Fourth official: Fabio Maresca (ITA)

VAR: Marco Di Bello (ITA)

Assistant VAR: Carlos del Cerro Grande (ESP)

MUNICH MEMORIES

We have faced Bayern competitively 15 times in our history, all in the Champions League. The first meeting was in December 2000 when we were pegged back from 2-0 ahead to draw 2-2 in the second group stage, before losing 1-0 in March 2000 in Munich.

Bayern have knocked us out of the competition on five occasions, four of which came in the round of 16 stage. The first came in 2004/05 when, despite a 1-0 win at Highbury, we lost 3-2 on aggregate. A 2-0 success at the Allianz Arena wasn’t enough to overturn a 3-1 loss in the first leg in 2013, while we also bowed out to the Germans in 2014 and 2017. In more recent times, we were beaten in the 2023/24 quarter-finals, where a Joshua Kimmich goal secured a 1-0 win for Bayern in Bavaria after a 2-2 draw in N5.

Our other meetings came in the group stage in 2015/16, when we won 2-0 at Emirates Stadium thanks to goals from Olivier Giroud and Mesut Ozil, while another victory was secured in last season's league phase when we triumphed 3-1 thanks to goals from Jurrien Timber, Noni Madueke and Gabriel Martinelli.
