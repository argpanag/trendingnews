Senator sheds new light on Pentagon’s controversial Canada military cooperation memo

New Pentagon limits on Canada are aimed mainly at U.S. military officials attending think tank gatherings and social functions, not at military operations or intelligence sharing, a Republican senator said Saturday.

Canadian Defense Minister David McGuinty, speaking at the same time, described military cooperation as “business as usual.”

The remarks come after The Washington Post reported on Friday that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth circulated a classified memorandum last week placing new restrictions on military cooperation with Canada.

The report emerged as relations between the longtime allies have been strained by U.S. President Donald Trump's trade war and his repeated threats to make Canada the 51st U.S. state.

Restrictions imposed in 2025 on participation in think tank events had already barred Pentagon officials from attending the Halifax International Security Forum.

Previous administrations, both Republican and Democratic, sent senior U.S. military officials, including defense secretaries, to the event.

Republican Sen. Mike Rounds said the latest guidance appeared consistent with existing practices. He said it was not expected to affect joint military operations or intelligence sharing.

Rounds was speaking at the Halifax International Security Forum, where he said his assessment was based on information from the delegation's Pentagon liaison.

“They’re basically limiting some social engagements, and they’re asking people to get permission before they come in and do think tanks and so forth,” he said.

“We don’t expect any real changes over the way it has been going for the last year or a year and a half,” he said.

Rounds said the disclosure was unfortunate as Washington sought to reassure Canada and other allies of its commitment to longstanding security relationships.

Canadian Defense Minister McGuinty said he had not seen the document and had detected no change in military cooperation.

“I’ve seen no evidence of any kind of change. I’ve heard about the existence of a document. I’ve not seen the document,” McGuinty told reporters.

He cited Canadian vessels working alongside American ships to intercept drug trafficking in the Caribbean.

“So that cooperation remains ongoing. And as far as we’re concerned, it’s business as usual,” he said.

Democratic Sen. Tim Kaine called the directive's tone “bad” and “unnecessary,” while Sen. Chris Coons said it risked worsening relations with a close ally.

“The United States should be doing nothing to further aggravate some of the distance and division between the United States and Canada,” Coons said.
