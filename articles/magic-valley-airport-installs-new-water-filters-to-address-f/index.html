Magic Valley Airport installs new water filters to address 'forever chemicals' detected in drinking water

Following reported elevated levels of polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), also known as forever chemicals, in the Magic Valley Regional Airport drinking water, the city announced it has installed a new filtration system and is exploring options to obtain a new well.

RELATED:

According to a consumer notice issued by the airport, a water sample collected Aug. 26 detected perfluorooctanoic acid, or PFOA, at 9.83 parts per trillion and perfluoro octane sulfonate, or PFOS, at 5.35 parts per trillion. The federal drinking water limit for both substances is 4.0 parts per trillion.

PFAS are a group of manufactured substances that can persist in the environment and accumulate in the human body over time. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says exposure to the chemicals may be linked to developmental and immune system effects, elevated cholesterol and some cancers.

Airport officials had emphasized the results apply only to the airport's drinking water well and are not related to the City of Twin Falls municipal water system. As a temporary fix, officials provided bottled water and told travelers not to drink the water.

The city said in a Friday, Oct. 16, news release the new filters were installed at drinking water sources throughout the airport to reduce PFAS levels under the federal limit.

Airport officials previously said they are releasing the information now, even though initial PFAS monitoring is not required until April 2027 and compliance with federal drinking water standards is not required until April 2029.

Residents seeking further clarification on the city's water system are urged by city officials to contact airport administration at 208-733-5215.
