Summary

    Stefan Borson, football finance expert, speaking to 5 Live: "I think in simple terms there's no question Man City have been confident about the outcome.   20. 'This hasn't been an easy case for the Premier League'published at 18:20 BST
    18:20 BST

    BBC Radio 5 Live

    Football finance expert Dr Tom Bason, speaking to BBC Radio 5 Live: "The charges are grouped into two areas - the charges that they effectively weren't honest in financial reporting, and the second is that they obstructed the investigation. It's the first bucket, the first 80 charges or so, that will hurt City.

    "The PSR regulations allowed clubs to lose £105m over three years. Part of the argument the people made was that City were dressing up money from their owners, inflated sponsorship deals. That the money actually came from the owners.

    "The allegations are that City were able to skirt around FFP regulations by claiming that money given to them by their owners was actually revenue.

    "These companies that were sponsoring City are multinational corporations. The Premier League is a pretty small company within the UK - does it have the power to compel these companies, like Etihad Airways, to give evidence. Does it have the power to get the Email chains from those people?

    "It's one thing people in the pub saying 'of course City did not', it's another to prove it in a court of law.

    "What we have seen from the Premier League when dealing with cases involving other clubs like Everton and Chelsea, is that they are prepared to be more lenient with clubs who don't try to obstruct the investigation and who give evidence.

    "That one of the charges is that City were trying to obstruct the investigation, before the charges came out, suggests this hasn't been an easy case for the Premier League."
