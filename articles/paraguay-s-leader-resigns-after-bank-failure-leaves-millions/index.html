Paraguay’s Leader Resigns After Bank Failure Leaves Millions Without Savings

TheTools · 05.11.2026 21:40 · source · permalink

Advertisement

Supported by

The president of the South American nation, Santiago Peña, has apologized for failing to prevent its worst financial crisis in decades.

Paraguay’s president announced his resignation on Thursday after a major bank with close ties to his administration went under, leaving half the country’s population of six million without access to their money.

In a video statement posted online at 1:16 p.m., President Santiago Peña, a conservative economist, said he was stepping down in the interest of stability. He described the decision as “one of the hardest moments of my life.”

“I want to apologize to the people if I ever failed you,” said Mr. Peña, who was facing an impeachment vote in the lower house of Congress in connection with the bank failure for what lawmakers called a “systematic dereliction of duty.”

“It was never my intention to betray the trust you placed in me,” Mr. Peña said.

Minutes after his announcement, lawmakers voted unanimously to proceed with impeachment.

The dramatic downfall of Mr. Peña, 47, who was elected in 2023, has plunged a die-hard South American ally of the Trump administration into deep political uncertainty.

It also threatens to frighten investors away from a poor and corruption-plagued nation that had recently won plaudits for reforms. The economy grew by 6.6 percent in 2025, the fastest for any Latin American nation save oil-rich Guyana.
