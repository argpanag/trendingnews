Ben Jacobs: Three managers who could replace Michael Carrick at Man United

Manchester United Back Michael Carrick Amid Growing Pressure Over Old Trafford Start

Manchester United are not moving towards an immediate managerial change, despite a deeply underwhelming opening to the season that has intensified scrutiny on Michael Carrick. Credit to Ben Jacobs on the Market Madness podcast, whose reporting makes clear that, for now, the club’s stance is one of support rather than panic.

That point matters. In an environment where speculation can quickly outpace reality, United’s current thinking appears more measured than some of the noise would suggest. Carrick has overseen a disappointing start, performances have fallen short, and the wider mood around Old Trafford has shifted sharply from early optimism. Even so, the club are not believed to be preparing a September dismissal.

There is frustration, certainly. United’s summer spending reached €222.7 million, and the expectation was that investment would bring greater clarity, a more convincing style and stronger early results. Instead, the team have looked laboured and inconsistent. Their league return has been poor, and the collapse against Brighton in the Carabao Cup added another layer of concern.

The 1-1 draw with Fulham only deepened that sense of drift. Gary Neville described part of the display as “the worst 15 minutes I’ve ever seen,” a line that captured the exasperation around a side still searching for cohesion. From a football perspective, that is the biggest issue. Results can wobble early in a campaign, but when structure, control and confidence are missing at the same time, questions inevitably land at the head coach’s door.

Michael Carrick retains backing for now

Jacobs’ information is that Carrick remains supported through the international break. As he explained, “The situation with Michael Carrick is Man Utd continue to back him through the international break,” and, “That’s not my understanding at this stage. There’s still support for Carrick. All well-run clubs have to succession plan, but we’re not in a position where there’ll be a knee-jerk reaction to the current form as of now.”

That distinction between backing a manager and planning for alternatives is an important one. Clubs operating at elite level do not wait for a crisis before mapping out potential next steps. It would be normal for United to maintain an active picture of the market, particularly after recent experiences in the dugout. That does not automatically mean a change is close.

Reports from Italy have suggested Antonio Conte has been approached. The current understanding, however, is that those claims are premature. United may be monitoring multiple options, but there is no indication from this update that Conte is being lined up as an imminent appointment.

Manchester United succession planning already underway

Even while Carrick keeps the confidence of the hierarchy, the broader context cannot be ignored. United have, in recent years, publicly supported managers before changing direction later. That history means every difficult run creates a fresh wave of uncertainty, whether fair or not.

Jacobs put it plainly: “But it’s a results-driven business, and of course, Man Utd, with both Ruben Amorim and Erik ten Hag, backed the managers only to sack them shortly after. So I would expect Man Utd to be well prepared, and there’s certainly plenty of candidates out there.”

The names mentioned are significant. Julian Nagelsmann is currently without a club. Simone Inzaghi is working at Al-Hilal, though his longer-term situation may yet become relevant. Jacobs also highlighted Eddie Howe as a free agent and someone previously put forward by Dan Ashworth during his time at United. Those are heavyweight profiles, each bringing a different tactical and cultural proposition.

What that list really underlines is that United would have credible options if they eventually choose to act. Planning for that scenario is prudent, particularly with a demanding run ahead and little margin for further regression.

Old Trafford fixtures could define next phase

The next block of matches now takes on added significance. United face Tottenham at home before trips to Atletico Madrid, Leeds and Como. In practical terms, this is likely to be the period that shapes the internal conversation most clearly.

If performances sharpen and points follow, Carrick can steady the picture and buy the time required to build. If the same issues persist, the pressure will intensify and succession planning may become more active. Jacobs noted, “It’s expected that we’ll have this kind of speculation, but as of now, Man Utd want to continue with Michael Carrick, and that means that after the international break, he’ll have an important run of fixtures as we head towards the Christmas period.”

For now, Manchester United are holding their nerve. Carrick stays in place, the hierarchy are watching closely, and the shortlist work expected at top clubs continues quietly in the background.

Our View

From a frustrated Manchester United supporter’s perspective, this all sounds painfully familiar. We are told there is support, told there is a process, told the club are calm, and yet every week the football looks short of ideas, short of intensity and short of standards. Spending €222.7 million and then producing displays like that is impossible to dress up.

Carrick may deserve time, but supporters also deserve evidence that things are moving in the right direction. Right now, where is the identity? Where is the authority in midfield? Where is the reaction when games start slipping? Too often, United look like a team waiting for something to happen rather than forcing the issue themselves.

The talk of succession planning will only grow because fans have seen this cycle before. Back the manager publicly, let the form drag on, then act when the season already has damage done. If the club genuinely believe Carrick is the right man, then the next run of games has to show a proper response. Not vague improvement, not one decent half, but real control and real progress.

If that does not arrive soon, supporters will ask a simple question. What exactly was the plan behind all that spending in the first place?
