Laois councillors back call for major reform of inheritance tax system - Laois Today

TheTools · 01.10.2026 11:00 · source · permalink
  • News
  • Council

Laois County Council has backed a call for the Government to reform Ireland’s inheritance tax system amid concerns that the current rules can leave nieces, nephews, siblings and unmarried partners facing significantly larger tax bills.

Fine Gael Cllr Conor Bergin brought a motion before the Council calling on the Minister for Finance, as part of Budget 2027, to introduce a single universal lifetime tax-free inheritance threshold for all beneficiaries.

The proposal would replace the existing Capital Acquisitions Tax (CAT) system in which tax-free thresholds differ depending on the relationship between the person giving the gift or inheritance and the beneficiary.

Cllr Bergin, whose motion was seconded by Fine Gael colleague Cllr John King, said it was an issue he encountered regularly through his professional work and one he felt strongly about.

Under the current system, the Group A threshold is €400,000, principally applying to inheritances from parents to children.

The Group B threshold is €40,000 and covers relationships including siblings and nieces and nephews, while the Group C threshold is €20,000 for other relationships.

CAT is generally charged at 33% on taxable gifts or inheritances above the relevant lifetime threshold.

Cllr Bergin argued that the tenfold difference between the Group A and Group B thresholds could have major consequences for people without children who wished to leave their estate to a niece or nephew.

He gave the example of someone whose niece or nephew had effectively become like a son or daughter to them and had helped care for them in later life.

“Under the current threshold, the niece or nephew is capped at €40,000. Anything above that, the State takes a third,” he said.

“These are assets that people have built up over their lifetime, their home, they might have paid a mortgage, they’ve paid tax on all their earnings while they’ve earned them.”

Cllr Bergin stressed that he was not calling for inheritance tax to be abolished, but questioned whether determining the tax-free allowance primarily by family relationship continued to reflect modern families.

“Families don’t always fit neatly into categories,” he said.

“People who don’t have children, people whose closest relationship could be a niece or nephew, brother or sister, people who have taken on parental roles in the extended family – the State shouldn’t ignore those realities.”

He said he supported the campaign for a single universal lifetime tax-free threshold, which would allow a person to leave assets up to a specified lifetime amount to a beneficiary without the tax-free allowance being determined by their relationship.

Cllr Bergin referenced the work of the End Discrimination in Inheritance Tax (EDIT) campaign and said he had discussed the issue with campaigners including Fiona Dunne in Laois.

He also highlighted the position of unmarried couples, saying cohabiting partners can fall within the €20,000 Group C threshold unless an exemption applies.

“This could be the family home that they’re both living in,” he said.

“People are caught with a huge tax bill. There is a fairer, simpler system that can be done.”

Cllr Bergin said he had raised the issue with Government and within Fine Gael and wanted Laois County Council to add its voice ahead of Budget 2027.

“If it’s not this year, it’s another year lost,” he said. “The more people, the more families that will be caught in this trap every year.”

However, Fianna Fáil Cllr Elva Kelly pointed to recent comments from Minister for Public Expenditure Jack Chambers which suggest the type of fundamental reform sought in the motion is unlikely to happen in Budget 2027.

Speaking earlier this month, Minister Chambers said there was scope for some inheritance tax changes, but ruled out equalising the existing categories. He said doing so would cost “hundreds of millions of euro” and was beyond what was available within the Budget’s tax package.

“There are particular campaigns who want us to equalise different categories,” Minister Chambers said earlier in September. “That has a tax expenditure of hundreds of millions of euro, which isn’t possible.”

He has instead indicated that the Government is considering adjustments to the existing inheritance tax thresholds within the resources available in Budget 2027.

Fianna Fáil Cllr John Joe Fennelly also contributed to the discussion and argued that the current arrangements discriminated against aunts and uncles who had no children of their own but wished to leave their assets to nieces or nephews.

The motion received the backing of the Council and will now add Laois County Council’s voice to calls for reform of the Capital Acquisitions Tax system ahead of the Budget.
