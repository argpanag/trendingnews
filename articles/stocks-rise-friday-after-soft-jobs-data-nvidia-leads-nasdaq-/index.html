Stocks rise Friday after soft jobs data, Nvidia leads Nasdaq to intraday record

TheTools · 01.10.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Stocks rose Friday following a surprisingly weak jobs report that raised hopes the Federal Reserve will hold rates steady in October.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 250 points, or 0.5%, to close at 51,176.46. The S&P 500 gained 0.7%, rising 56.27 to 7,722.72, while the Nasdaq Composite climbed 1.2%, adding 319.27 to 27,190.86, hitting an all-time high earlier in the day before pulling back as Treasury yields reversed course.

Despite Friday's rally, the Dow was down for the week by about 1.3%. The S&P was little changed, off 0.3%, while the Nasdaq was up 0.6% on the week, with Friday marking its third straight advance.

Treasury yields initially fell Friday following the September employment report, but later rebounded, pushing U.S. equities off their highs for the session.

Tech stocks rallied as risk-taking returned on Wall Street, with shares of Nvidia hitting an all-time high for the first time since May, though it eventually closed below its previous record close. CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks and AMD also all rose to all-time highs. Shares of AMD also rose nearly 3%.

Last month's nonfarm payrolls report showed the U.S. economy added 29,000 jobs last month, with unemployment rising to 4.2%. The Dow Jones consensus called for jobs growth of 84,000 and for the unemployment rate to hold steady at 4.1%.

"This is the exact kind of number the market wanted from a labor standpoint," said Phil Blancato, chief market strategist at Osaic. "Not too hot, not too cold ... not overly great, and not weakening."

That number made traders reassess the next move for the Federal Reserve. Fed funds futures trading suggests a 77% likelihood the central bank will stand pat at this month's meeting, according to the CME FedWatch Tool.

Saira Malik, chief investment officer at Nuveen, told CNBC's "Squawk Box" that the report may give the market support before earnings season kicks off, which she thinks will be "very strong."

"So, I think this could be the start of the Santa Claus rally that we've all been hoping for," she said.

Oil prices pulled back, supporting equities, following a report that European nation states are considering a release of strategic fuel reserves following pressure from the Trump administration.

Investors are coming off a modestly higher session Thursday, to start off the month of October. However, the Dow and the S&P 500 were headed for weekly losses amid a global bond rout, while the Nasdaq was the only major average on pace to end the week with gains.

— CNBC's Jeff Cox contributed to this report

The three major averages rose on Friday following softer-than-expected jobs data.

The S&P 500 advanced 0.49% to 7,722.93. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.19% to end at 27,190.86, while the Dow Industrials rose 250.88 points, or 0.49%, to close at 51,177.44.

Friday's rally put the Nasdaq Composite on track for another winning week.

The technology-heavy index jumped more than 1% in Friday trading, bringing its weekly gain to 0.5%. If that holds, it would mark the Nasdaq's third positive week in a row.

The Nasdaq is the only major U.S. stock index on track to finish in the green. The Dow and S&P 500 have slid about 1.2% and 0.2%, respectively, in the period.

— Alex Harring

The Dow Jones Industrial Average on Friday was pacing to end the week in the red, despite Friday's 0.5% advance.

The blue-chip index as of late Friday trading was down more than 1% for the week. It's the Dow's fourth negative week in the last five, with its positive one in that time period coming last week when it finished up just under 0.3%.

— Davis Giangiulio

Citi raised its price target on shares of bitcoin treasury firm Strategy to $240 per share from $136 per share, implying about 34% upside potential.

The change reflects a more bullish outlook for bitcoin over the next year and a higher valuation of Strategy's bitcoin holdings as a result, Citi analyst Peter Christiansen said in a note Friday.

The bank also maintained its buy rating on Strategy, though Christiansen noted investors should take a more cautious view of Strategy's valuation due to the company's growing but "dilutive" cash reserve, which could reduce the benefit of its bitcoin holdings.

Strategy shares were up 3% in early morning trading.

— Tanaya Macheel

Nike shares fell 6% at the open after tanking 10% in the premarket after the global sportswear apparel giant delivered a mixed first quarter of fiscal 2027 that showed its turnaround timeline is further than expected.

The company reported weak trends in its key China market, despite growth in its core North America segment.

While Nike plans $2.5 billion in cost savings by 2031, management said it expects revenues to decline in the high single digits in 2027.

The stock is down 46% for the year.

October marks the fourth birthday of the global bull market, one where the future trajectory is still to the upside, according to UBS.

"Over the past four years, global stock markets have reached one record high after another because corporate earnings are growing faster than most analysts expected. What many initially interpreted as a short-term recovery has proved to be a structural upswing with global reach," Burkhard Varnholt, senior financial market adviser at UBS Switzerland, wrote Friday.

"We therefore see the real story of this bull market as less about prices and more about an economic revival," Varnholt added.

— Sarah Min

Tesla deliveries in the third quarter beat Wall Street's expectations on Friday, coming at 486,532 versus 461,100 expected.

Deliveries eased from 497,099 in the year-ago period but climbed up from the second quarter's 480,126 delivered vehicles.

Shares were up in morning trading on Friday.

– Tobias Burns

The Grayscale Zcash ETF (ZCSH) is up more than 60% in the past month, bringing its year-to-date gain to about 253%, thanks to renewed interest in privacy as speculative capital this year has rotated into AI.

Zcash (ZEC) itself – up more than 170% this year compared to bitcoin, which is stagnant on the year – was designed to keep users' financial transactions completely private. It launched in 2016, but the privacy narrative eventually faded as privacy coins became a compliance headache for regulators and exchanges.

Crypto's mainstream investment case has become increasingly institutionalized and transparent, while AI has made large-scale financial surveillance and transaction analysis more powerful. The combination of the two has allowed the privacy narrative to gain traction and lead to the launch of the Grayscale Zcash ETF in August.

By comparison, the popular iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT) is down about 1% this year and the Bitwise 10 Crypto Index ETF (BITW), which tracks the 10 largest cryptocurrencies, is down 5%. Meanwhile, the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF has gained 12% and the Invesco QQQ is up nearly 22%.

— Tanaya Macheel

Nvidia popped on Friday, briefly hitting an all-time intraday high.

Shares of the semiconductor firm rose more than 3% to as much as $237.88 on the day. At its peak, the stock traded at its highest intraday level ever.

Nvidia's shares began trading on the public market in January 1999.

— Liz Napolitano

Revisions in the September jobs report for July and August are making those months look weaker for job creation.

July was marked down by 31,000 to mark a net job loss of 10,000 on the month, the Labor Department reported Friday. August was marked down by 29,000 for a new total of 133,000 compared to the initial robust report of 162,000.

"Revisions made both July and August look weaker, too. Over the past 3 months, we've an average of a mere +17k per month," economist Justin Wolfers wrote on social media.

– Tobias Burns

Shares for Seagate Technology and Western Digital slipped over 10% and 8% respectively after Toshiba announced plans to double its production capacity for hard disk drives used in data centers, according to a Nikkei report.

The Japanese company plans to invest $380 million to expand facilities in the Philippines, signaling Toshiba's first major hard disk drives investment in about five years, Nikkei reported.

Morgan Stanley put Nvidia back atop its list of semiconductor stocks on Friday, touting the company's range of customers and the importance of graphics processing units (GPUs) to agentic artificial intelligence.

"While 90% of investor conversations are around the biggest hyperscalers and frontier model companies, half of the business - probably the faster growing half - is a broader range of AI model companies, Neoclouds, sovereign entities, ODMs and OEMs, and enterprise customers," Joseph Moore at Morgan Stanley wrote in a Friday note to clients.

Moore has an overweight rating on the stock with price target of $300 and an "industry view" listed as "attractive."

With its numerous backstopping and vendor agreements during the AI boom, Nvidia has faced criticism for being involved in circular financing during the AI buildout, which interlinks Nvidia's investments with its eventual revenue streams.

– Tobias Burns

Oil prices fell more than 2% on Friday morning, extending losses from earlier in the session, following reports of a potential European move to release diesel and crude stocks to ease a global supply shortage

International benchmark Brent crude futures with December expiry traded 2.5% lower at $99.78 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures with November expiry fell nearly 3.8% to $89.40.

It comes shortly after Reuters reported that European Union member states were discussing a French proposal to release additional diesel reserves following pressure from the Trump administration.

Moderna will replace Warner Bros. Discovery in the tech-heavy blue-chip Nasdaq 100 index after the biotech's shares have risen nearly sixfold over the past year.

The company, which became a household name after it developed one of the first vaccines for Covid-19, surprised the Street last month when an experimental personalized cancer vaccine co-developed with Merck showed positive initial results in its first-ever late-stage trial.

Meanwhile, Warner Bros. is in the process of merging with Paramount Skydance. Paramount has said the merger will close on Oct. 6.

Approximately $1.1 million of the nearly $388 million stolen from crypto exchange Bitget in last week's cyberattack has been frozen, as the platform continues efforts to trace and recover the assets.

Frozen assets had not necessarily been returned to the exchange, CEO Gracy Chen told CNBC in an email interview. She did not disclose how much had been recovered.

Speaking on CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Wednesday, Chen said she was "not expecting to recover a lot of funds," citing the limited recovery from previous cryptocurrency exchange hacks. However, "exchanges have a responsibility to demonstrate how they protect users, particularly when something goes wrong," she said.

U.S. Treasury yields rose on Friday, reversing some of the previous session's declines as longer-dated borrowing costs remained near multi-decade highs.

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed 1.8 basis points to 5.252%, while the 30-year Treasury yield rose 2.6 basis points to 5.629%.

Treasury yields had eased Thursday after their recent surge, with investors continuing to weigh the outlook for the U.S.-Iran conflict and its potential implications for inflation and interest rates. Yields move inversely to prices.

—Lee Ying Shan

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has proposed new rules that would make it easier for investment advisers and regulated funds to hold cryptocurrencies on behalf of clients, as U.S. regulators push ahead with writing crypto rules after a sweeping legislation stalled in Congress.

The proposal, announced Thursday stateside, would establish a tailored framework governing how registered investment advisers, investment companies and business development companies hold custody of crypto assets.

The changes are aimed at modernizing decades-old custody requirements and removing regulatory barriers that the SEC says have limited advisers' ability to offer crypto-related investments.

Under the proposed rules, crypto assets could be held in self-custody under "certain circumstances," while state trust companies could also serve as custodians for crypto assets belonging to clients and regulated funds.

—Lee Ying Shan

Oil extended gains Friday, as traders continue to assess Mideast developments following a report the U.S. is sending a third aircraft carrier strike group to the Middle East.

Futures for international benchmark Brent crude for December delivery gained 0.33% to $102.65 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures for June advanced 0.17% at $93.03 per barrel.

According to the Wall Street Journal, Marine Corps ships and up to 10,000 additional troops are also being deployed to the region. The forces are expected to arrive by the end of November, WSJ said, citing officials.

Oil prices could go even higher, as tensions between the U.S. and Iran continue to simmer. "From a trading standpoint, you're unlikely to get a $15 drop in crude oil. It's a higher chance you get a $15 move up," said Simon Lack, portfolio manager of Catalyst Energy Infrastructure Fund on Thursday.

"The Iranians, even though all of their offensive capabilities are taken out, they seem to find ways to fire missiles, and they could start hitting infrastructure again along the Persian Gulf," Lack added.

—Justina Lee
