Anthony Davis’ 44 points upstaged by Wizards’ woes: ‘It’s everything right now’

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:30 · source · permalink

Anthony Davis returned to the lineup Monday and scored 44 points on 16-of-21 shooting with 13 rebounds. Rick Osentoski / Imagn Images

DETROIT — What’s wrong with the Washington Wizards’ defense right now?

Anthony Davis knows the answer.

“Everything,” he said Monday night, after the Wizards lost their fourth consecutive game, falling to the Detroit Pistons 122-111 at Little Caesars Arena. “We’re not rebounding. We’re giving up open 3s. We’re fouling. We’re not guarding the ball. So, it’s everything. Transition. It’s everything right now.”

Davis returned to the lineup after missing Washington’s previous two games because of a right ankle sprain. He played his best game as a member of the team, scoring 44 points on 16-of-21 shooting and collecting 13 rebounds.

Although Davis was correct to cite defense as the primary reason for the four-game losing streak, the Wizards’ problems Monday started on the offensive end. The Pistons have one of the league’s most handsy defenses, and they overwhelmed the Wizards at the outset with their physicality. The Wizards committed six turnovers during the first 4 minutes and 36 seconds, and the Pistons converted those miscues into 7 points.

“We just didn’t value the ball,” Wizards coach Brian Keefe said. “That led them to get out and get easy baskets in transition. I think we did make some improvement for the game, but not consistent enough to get it done. A lot of lessons we can learn from that.”

Washington started its season 4-3, with victories over the Milwaukee Bucks, Chicago Bulls, Charlotte Hornets and Orlando Magic. But over its last four games, Davis’ squad has faced stronger competition, losing a pair of games to the Atlanta Hawks and enduring a 39-point loss at the Oklahoma City Thunder before Monday night’s defeat in Detroit.

During the four-game losing streak, the Wizards’ opponents have made 48.4 percent of their shots from the field and averaged 13.5 offensive rebounds.

If anyone on the Wizards’ roster understands defense, it’s Davis, a power forward/center who has made the NBA All-Defensive First Team three times and the All-Defensive Second Team twice during his decorated career.

The Wizards added Davis to their roster in February in a trade with the Dallas Mavericks, hoping he and point guard Trae Young, who was acquired in January in a trade with the Hawks, would provide the on-court experience and off-court leadership their youth-laden team needs.

Perhaps the urgency to improve the defense that Davis expressed Monday night will filter down to the rest of the roster.

When Davis was asked about his 44-point performance, he answered: “It doesn’t matter. We lost.”
