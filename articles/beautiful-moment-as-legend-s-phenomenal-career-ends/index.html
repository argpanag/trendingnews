Zverev drops second set

The world No.2 has been dragged into another dogfight after having his serve broken to lose the second set 6-4.

Zverev had a break point of his own at 4-4 but was unable to convert. There’s been warning signs for the past 20 minutes or so, with the German struggling to get out of second gear.

Having already played one match that went well into the early hours of the morning this tournament, Zverev is playing like he has “a hangover”, according to Jim Courier, and is level at one set apiece at 11.15pm New York time. If this one goes to five sets, he could be on the court well beyond midnight again.

Sluggish Zverev a ‘head scratcher’

This match is still in the German superstar’s control but Jim Courier and his co-commentators are struggling to figure out why the world No.2 isn’t shifting out of first gear and dominating.

Quentin Halys is serving at around 50 per cent first serves in for the second set, yet Zverev hasn’t stepped up his return game and, at 4-4, is yet to find a break.

Courier has been critical of Zverev but playing “devil’s advocate” wondered if the German was suffering a sort of “hangover” from his marathon first round match against Italian Lorenzo Sonego. He won that match 6-4 in the fifth and it finished well after midnight, with Zverev not getting to bed until 5am.

Courier said that sometimes when you have a late finishing match such as that one, you can feel “jet-lagged” and it takes several days to start feeling normal again.

Courier stunned by Alcaraz return

Jim Courier is commentating on the Alexander Zverev match at the moment and he just made a big prediction for the way the rest of the men’s tournament will unfold.

Zverev may be the top seed by virtue of Jannik Sinner’s injury-related absence but according to Courier, Alcaraz has separated himself from the field in his first two matches after returning from a lengthy layoff due to a wrist injury.

The Spanish superstar has dropped to world No.3 and is the No.2 seed for this tournament by virtue of his stint away from the court but Courier has been astounded by the ease at which he’s found a high level on his return.

Carlos Alcaraz is in action against Portugal’s Jaime Faria. AP Photo/Adam Hunger

“It’s Alcaraz versus the field at this stage of the tournament,” Courier said, while also criticising Zverev’s “passive” performance so far against Frenchman Quentin Halys.

Zverev leads 6-4, 2-3, with the second set still on serve, but has looked a shadow of the aggressive baseliner who won the French Open this year to break through for his first major title.

Alcaraz has dropped just one set in his two wins so far this week, and is on the opposite side of the draw to Zverev, meaning the pair won’t meet before a possible final between the two top seeds.

Rybakina has won the first set over Spain’s Jessica Maneiro with ease, 6-2. She looks fierce on the court and her serve is just too powerful for Maneiro.

But in the end, Zverev came out on top and looks to be comfortable on the court.

20yo eliminates French veteran

After two decades, Gael Monfils has officially played his last match in the grand slam arena, falling to a straight-set defeat against America’s Learner Tien.

Tien was too powerful for the retiring star in the second round at Flushing Meadows, recording a 6-3, 6-4, 6-3 victory.

The 20-year-old did not celebrate his victory without applauding the career his opponent has had, with the pair embracing and sharing a few words at the net.

It was a touching moment for all to watch between the rising star and the veteran.

Gael Monfils, left, of France, embraces Learner Tien AP Photo/Adam Hunger

Tien revealed post-match the exact words he shared with Monfils at the net.

“I told him when we played in Montreal, ‘I hope we get to play one more time’,” he revealed.

“I know we didn’t have many opportunities left. I’m super happy it happened here.

“Sorry for drop shotting you so much tonight,” he added, laughing.

Monfils, who was sitting on his player’s bench listening to the speech, laughed, shaking his head.

Star’s message to influencers amid rising issues in stands

Influencers are starting to take over the stands at the US Open - and many traditional fans of the sport, as well as some players, are not happy about it.

There has been a notable number of pauses in crucial matches due to chair umpires having to inform influencers to stop making noise while capturing content out of the respect of those competing in the match.

Naomi Osaka is one of those who had a brief pause in her first-round match as influencers drawing attention away from the court while posing for photos.

While no player has denied that the rise of influencers has helped the sport reach a new demographic, Osaka wants those figures to respect the sport for what it is.

“If you come to a sporting event, you have to respect the sport,” she said.

“I feel like it’s common courtesy to read up on the sport, especially the etiquette.

“It could be a good thing for people to make the sport of tennis popular to their specific audience. But I think it has to be managed a lot better, especially in the situation like in my first round match.

“Overall there are huge positives (from influencers being in the crowd). You want people to continue to grow the sport. I just think it has to be in a way that everyone knows the rules and the etiquette.”
