De Cuyper and Brighton pile on pain for Manchester United with comeback

TheTools · 16.09.2026 21:18 · source · permalink

Across four days Manchester United have lost the 199th derby against Manchester City, were told by JJ Gabriel that he wants to leave, and now they have been knocked out of the Carabao Cup by Brighton.

Capitulating after being 2-0 up may be the pick of these dizzying blows, as the prolonged boos at the final whistle suggested, and doing so ends a proud 50-year record: the last time the same lead at Old Trafford ended in defeat was Tottenham’s 3-2 win in September 1976.

When Marcus Rashford skated in with the chance to take the tie to 3-3 late on, there was no surprise he hit Jason Steele rather than the net.

Towards the end, Matheus Cunha was thrown on to join Bruno Fernandes, also a late substitute, illustrating how desperate Michael Carrick was. Yet Charalampos Kostoulas went close to scoring a second for Brighton, as United could not find a route to cancelling out Maxim De Cuyper’s 69th-minute strike that proved the winner.

After taking only four points from 12 in the league, serious questions will now be asked about where Carrick’s team are heading; they have to beat Fulham on Sunday to start correcting a dismal start to the season. The manager already looks haunted and he talked afterwards of not caring about being under pressure.

Carrick said the injustice suffered against City on Sunday would give his side an edge and Shea Lacey provided one when decorating a full debut with the sweetest of goals. The 19-year-old shimmied in Brighton’s area, beating Jaouen Hadjam before firing beyond a helpless Jason Steele. As impressive here was Benjamin Sesko’s hold-up work in the buildup as this allowed red shirts to flood forward, Youri Tielemans feeding Lacey.

This was eight minutes in and less than 120 seconds later Mason Mount doubled United’s advantage. Rashford’s pace and drive took him clear along the left, again inside the area. A trailing Steele boot saved the No 9’s effort but the ball bounced into Mount’s path and he drove home, via the turf, for a first goal since a win at Wolves last December.

Lacey’s strike drew a Carrick grin and delight from Mount, and you wondered how Gabriel – earmarked for the squad in this game, with the chance to become United’s youngest player – felt.

Brighton had lost only one of their previous five visits here and a flying start to the league campaign has Fabian Hürzeler’s team in fifth. Yet the 5-0 hammering of Coventry on Sunday already seemed distant as United continued to dominate.

Olivier Boscagli hit a corner straight out, summing up the visitors’ start, before Matt O’Riley forced Ayden Heaven into an error that led to another. This presaged a Seagulls renaissance that featured a lightning Malick Yalcouyé break but, clear in on the debutant Karl Darlow’s goal, the Ivorian spooned wide. The No 35 lacked the sharpness evident in a Rashford nutmeg of Chema Andrés which embarrassed the Brighton man and when Pascal Gross blasted over this was one more chance spurned by Hürzeler’s men.

But then came a goal – and real hope – for Brighton. Hadjam’s long left-right diagonal cut United’s rearguard clean out, and the slumbering Heaven allowed Kostoulas to scamper free, the striker beating Darlow through his legs.

Carrick, having beaten only Ipswich in the league and Sabah in Europe so far this season, needed a win, and as United (not under him) exited at the earliest opportunity in both domestic cups last season, progressing to at least a second tie in one of them would be welcome.

A wild Tielemans long-range shot that ballooned over failed to restore United’s two-goal lead but at least the home team were tapping the ball about, operating in Brighton territory, moving the visitors around.

A Sesko burst from halfway had the onrushing Steele hacking away just in time. United were in control. But the question was: could they maintain this and register the type of mature, accomplished display that would show an evolution in Carrick’s team? Sesko, in yards of space, dawdling and being dispossessed when a simple ball would have fed Lacey, suggested not.

A loose Rashford pass aimed for Sesko hinted at the same. More promising was a pass for Noussair Mazraoui as he drove into the area along the left channel, as was the subsequent corner that created an opening Tielemans narrowly missed.

Carabao Cup roundup: Fleetwood handed win over Blades

A 57th-minute Rhys Norrington-Davies own goal sent Fleetwood into the last 16 of the Carabao Cup for the first time as the League Two club stunned their Championship opponents, Sheffield United, 1-0 at Highbury, before pulling a home tie against Arsenal out in the draw.

Matt Lawlor’s side, unbeaten now in nine games in all competitions, had another Danny Andrew effort ruled out for offside and twice hit the woodwork in a dominant second-half display. It was a searching left-wing cross from Andrew that Norrington-Davies nodded beyond Michael Cooper to give the fourth-tier club a historic victory.

Tom Cannon did get the ball in the net for Chris Wilder’s visitors who lined up with only four starting survivors from last Friday’s 1-0 league defeat by Wolves. But a flag for offside was raised before the striker slid the ball into the net.

United started positively and Ollie Arblaster had a goalbound effort blocked after only 36 seconds. But Mark Helm forced Cooper into a seventh-minute save before the former United forward Ched Evans put his follow-up header narrowly wide.

Jay Lynch denied Joe Rothwell as the visitors attacked again but Cooper was the busier, saving from Lewis McCann and Crispin McLean.

Helm’s dipping right-foot effort from 18 yards grazed the top netting as Fleetwood finished the first half in the ascendancy and they deserved their break from Norrington-Davies’ own goal.

United finally came to life in the last 10 minutes with Lynch brilliantly denied Sydie Peck and Ben Brereton Díaz, while Cannon saw a goalbound strike superbly blocked.

Everton’s forgotten man Charly Alcaraz stepped up when his side needed some inspiration to score the only goal in the win over Wolves three minutes after coming on. The Argentinian had played just 14 minutes all season but belied his lack of match sharpness with a quick switch of the ball from one foot to the other to guide a left-footed shot past the former Everton goalkeeper João Virgínia in the 80th minute.

It also represented a minor success for Tyler Dibling, whose career has not really taken off since his £35m move from Southampton just over a year ago, as he claimed the assist at the end his most encouraging performance for the club. Everton's reward is a home tie against Newcastle. Press Association

But then calamity. Diogo Dalot’s hoofed clearance failed to find Sesko as Luka Vuskovic headed to Gross; Brighton’s captain made room and netted with ease. Even worse followed. Brighton raided along the right, Kostoulas’s cross pinged off Mazraoui’s backside, and there was De Cuyper to finish.

On 72 minutes Carrick played his ace, introducing Fernandes for Mount, then followed up with Cunha. But United remained toothless.
