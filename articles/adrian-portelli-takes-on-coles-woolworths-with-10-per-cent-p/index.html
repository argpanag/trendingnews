Adrian Portelli takes on Coles, Woolworths with 10 per cent price-beat

TheTools · 14.10.2026 06:01 · source · permalink

Adrian Portelli has launched his first LMCT+ supermarket at Deer Park, challenging Coles and Woolworths with a Bunnings-style 10 per cent price-beat offer.

Adrian Portelli has opened his first supermarket in Melbourne and challenged Coles and Woolworths with a Bunnings-style 10 per cent price-beat pledge.

The billionaire unveiled the new policy after the opening of his LMCT+ supermarket at Deer Park, in Melbourne’s west, on Tuesday.

Under the offer, shoppers who find an identical product cheaper at Coles or Woolworths can ask LMCT+ to beat the rival’s regular shelf price by 10 per cent.

The competing product must be in stock, available in store and not on promotion.

The move borrows a familiar concept from hardware giant Bunnings, but takes it into the weekly grocery shop as Australian households continue hunting for cheaper food.

Mr Portelli said he was already looking to shake things up after his first day in the supermarket business.

“It’s day one of the supermarket and it’s fair to say that I’m bored,” he said in a video shared with followers.

“I’m coming in absolutely swinging, it’s time to stop playing games.”

The billionaire said the policy would apply across the thousands of products on his supermarket’s shelves, provided shoppers met the comparison requirements.

The new LMCT+ supermarket has opened in Melbourne’s west, with Mr Portelli flagging plans to expand the business across Victoria despite the buying power of major rivals. Picture: LMCT+

He acknowledged Coles and Woolworths had pricing advantages across some products but said he was prepared to challenge them.

“Come and challenge me,” he said.

Customers who identify a qualifying cheaper price have been told to approach the supermarket’s front counter to have it adjusted.

The offer closely resembles the 10 per cent price-beat guarantee offered by Bunnings, although there are some important differences.

Bunnings extends its policy to eligible like-for-like products, including comparable alternatives to its exclusive brands, while Mr Portelli has specified identical items.

Bunnings also accepts eligible online comparisons, while the LMCT+ offer announced by Mr Portelli is restricted to regular in-store prices.

That distinction could matter when comparing home-brand groceries, different pack sizes and heavily discounted weekly specials.

There is another consideration for shoppers.

Adrian Portelli and Jordan Tabakman at the new Deer Park supermarket, where customers need a $99 annual membership to access discounted groceries and fuel. Picture: LMCT+

Access to LMCT+ grocery discounts requires a membership, with the company’s Everyday Saver option advertised at $99 annually.

That is about $1.90 a week before any savings are made.

For example, beating a $5 supermarket product by 10 per cent would reduce the price to $4.50, saving a shopper 50c.

It would take 198 purchases delivering that same 50c saving to recover the annual membership cost through groceries alone.

However, the membership also covers LMCT+ petrol discounts, meaning customers who already use the company’s fuel stations could save across both businesses.

LMCT+ Supermarket banana display Bananas were advertised at $3.99 a kilogram at the LMCT+ supermarket, but shoppers hoping to claim its new price-beat offer must find identical products cheaper elsewhere. Picture: LMCT+

The new Deer Park store opened at the former IGA supermarket on Hatchlands Drive, attracting shoppers eager to see how its prices compared with the major chains.

Opening-day comparisons found some groceries were already cheaper at LMCT+.

A dozen caged eggs cost $4.20 compared with $6.05 at Coles and $5.70 at Woolworths, while Weet-Bix was priced at $4.60 compared with $5 at both chains.

But the comparison also identified more expensive items.

Watermelons were advertised at $2 a kilogram at Portelli’s supermarket, which has promised to beat eligible regular shelf prices at Coles and Woolworths by 10 per cent. Picture: LMCT+

Pork belly was listed at $24.31 a kilogram at LMCT+, compared with $14 at Coles, although the report did not establish whether the products were identical and eligible for the new guarantee.

Mr Portelli has acknowledged the challenges of competing with established supermarket operators, which have significantly greater buying power.

He told followers that some Coles and Woolworths prices would inevitably be difficult to beat across the thousands of products stocked by his business.

However, the businessman said he was confident enough in his supermarket model to pursue a broader rollout across Victoria.

His latest venture follows LMCT+‘s move into discounted petrol, with the company seeking to expand its membership business into everyday household spending.
