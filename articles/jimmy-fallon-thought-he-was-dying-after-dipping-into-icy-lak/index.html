Jimmy Fallon thought he was dying after dipping into icy Lake Michigan for Chicago's Polar Plunge

Jimmy Fallon thought he was dying after dipping into the icy waters of Lake Michigan during Chicago's Polar Plunge a few years back, he told NBC Chicago's Stefan Holt during a one-on-one chat this week.

"I got out and I heard bagpipes, and that's what Irish people hear when they die," the host of NBC's The Tonight Show said.

Holt traveled to New York City this week to talk with Fallon about a range of topics, from roller coasters to pizza and even a story about a day the comedian was in Chicago that he thought could be his last.

Fallon told Holt that back in March 2014 he came to Chicago to participate in the Polar Plunge, an annual Special Olympics fundraiser event.

"That was the scariest thing that ever happened to me in my entire lifetime," he said.

Footage from his plunge into the lake shows it was a frosty season, with large banks of snow and ice visible on the beach near where participants stepped into the water.

Fallon did the plunge in his suit, and his expression walking out of the freezing water gives a clear picture of how he was feeling.

The bag pipes also weren't part of his imagination -- they were the Shannon Rovers Irish Pipe Band, a staple of the annual event.

"I saw the other side," he said. "That's how it ended for me. I froze to death in Lake Michigan."
