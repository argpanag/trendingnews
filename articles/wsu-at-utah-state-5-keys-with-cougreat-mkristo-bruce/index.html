WSU at Utah State: 5 Keys with CouGreat Mkristo Bruce

TheTools · 10.10.2026 00:35 · source · permalink

Cougars need a sign of life from listless offense

SUSTAIN DRIVES. There is no simpler way to put it. CouGreat defensive end Mkristo Bruce says Washington State needs to out together some productive drives to turn the ship around after a 1-4 start and poor offensive showing heading into Friday's tilt at Utah State.

The Cougars tonight kick off their Pac-12 road opener Friday at 6 p.m. in Logan, Utah, on The CW and the game largely depends on whether or not the Cougars' offense can awake from a five-week hibernation and take some pressure off of the defense. 

Utah State is expected to be at the bottom of the league throughout the year, making this a near must-win game for Wazzu.

Here are Bruce's five keys for the Cougars to get conference win No. 1 in the Kirby Moore era.

1. SIMPLIFY THE OFFENSE TO THE MOST BASIC DEGREE

The offense needs things to be as easy as possible right now. That doesn't mean vanilla. But it does mean making life easy on Caden Pinnick, or whoever the starting QB may be. Wazzu doesn't want to run a plain offense, but it also can't be overly complex.

Get the QB moving a little bit and instead of selling out for the home run play, take the four and five yards play when they're there. Do that, and you will get into a rhythm and push the ball up the field.

Bruce's thoughts: "Here's what I'd like to see. Get the running backs involved early. Give Pinnick some easy completions, quick throws, screens, and opportunities to build confidence. Help that line out instead of constantly putting them in obvious passing situations where defenses can pin their ears back. Get Pinnick in positions to be successful. Bootlegs, crossing patterns, quick outs. Get him some momentum and confidence."

2. SUSTAIN DRIVES AND LET THE DEFENSE BREATHE

It's not just that the Cougars aren't scoring, it's how inefficient their offensive drives are. WSU is regularly going three-and-out, and it's not a coincidence that the defense has given up 47 points in fourth quarters this season.

They start to wear down and the opposing offense takes advantage. A big reason for that is just how long they have to be on the field. Let the defense rest and sustain drives not just with time of possession, but with successful plays and points. Give the defense a boost when they retake the field, especially with the high elevation potentially playing a factor.

Bruce's thoughts: "We've consistently been playing from behind, and that completely changes how you can play defense. We're asking these guys to go out, get a stop, get the ball back, and then three plays later they're right back on the field. I can tell you how frustrating that is. You barely get a chance to catch your breath before you're being asked to do it all over again. We have to fix our time of possession and sustain drives offensively.

"Give our defense an opportunity to play with a lead for once. Let those guys pin their ears back, get aggressive, and put pressure on the quarterback instead of constantly playing from behind. To me, the best thing we can do for this defense right now is figure out how to keep our offense on the football field. You can't keep asking your defense to bail you out when you're not giving them time to recover. I'm not asking for 80-yard touchdown drives every possession. Give me a couple of six, seven, eight-play drives that flip field position.

3. SET THE EDGES

The defense has largely been good for the Cougars this season, but letting quarterbacks escape has been an issue. Wazzu has to do a better job of not just getting to the quarterback, but finishing the play with a sack or forcing a throw away.

The escape lane up the middle has been a constant issue, and one that needs to be corrected. And when running backs look to kick it outside, the Cougar defensive ends and 'backers have to set the edge better so that they can't get into the open field. 

Bruce's thoughts: "This one hits home for me as a DE, and I know how important setting that edge is. I look at guys like Linus Zunk and Matyus McLain, and I love the effort and aggressiveness they bring. McLain had two tackles for loss and looked strong vs Fresno State. Zunk was active and flying around. But as a defensive end, you can't get so caught up trying to make a play that you lose contain. Sometimes your biggest contribution is forcing the running back and QB inside to your help.

"That's where guys like Keith Brown and Isaiah Hung come into play at linebacker. Everybody has to trust each other and handle their responsibilities. If your ends get too far upfield or lose outside leverage, you're creating escape lanes for the quarterback and opportunities for those big runs."

4. STAY AHEAD OF THE STICKS TO EARN THE OPPORTUNITY TO RUN

It's been the biggest separator in Wazzu's offensive success and failure. When the Cougars set themselves up on third down, they are able to keep the chains moving. When it becomes third-and-long, it turns into desperation. They're not succeeding enough on first and second down and that is torpedoing the offense.

Wazzu has sparsely used its running backs over the last two weeks, but part of that is because the Cougars are always playing catch-up. Penalties, sacks, negative plays. You name it, and it's probably derailed a drive as of late.

Bruce's thoughts: "You have to sustain drives to create opportunities for your running backs. When you're constantly going three-and-out, there are not enough offensive snaps to establish a running game. I'm all for getting our backs more involved, but we have to fix our first-down efficiency, stay ahead of the chains, and convert on third down. It's not just about calling more running plays. It's about earning more opportunities to run the football.

"It's frustrating seeing this offense struggle week after week. We're just not used to seeing this kind of offensive production in Pullman. Everybody wants to see us run the ball and I'm all for it, but we've got to look at the bigger picture. We're converting just 27 percent on all of our third downs this season And against Fresno State we had maybe nine first downs and 180ish yards of total offense and 30 yards rushing. That's not going to get it done. For me it starts with winning on first and second down."

5. AVOID THE MASSIVE NEGATIVE PLAYS

There were multiple instances vs. Fresno State where a drive essentially ended before it even started because of a mistake. Taking a sack instead of throwing it away, missing a block, taking a penalty. WSU is hurting itself over and over again.

Forget trying to get to second-and-three, start by at least not making it worse than second-and-10. Because when you get into second-and-20, it's pretty much been game over.

Bruce's thoughts: "We've got to eliminate negative plays, protect our quarterback, and put ourselves in manageable third-down situations. When you're constantly behind the chains and can't sustain drives, it's tough to establish any kind of offensive identity. I know these guys are working hard, and I believe in this program, but something has to change. The answer to this question is Moore taking over the offense."
