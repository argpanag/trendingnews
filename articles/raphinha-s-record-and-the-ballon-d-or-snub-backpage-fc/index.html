Raphinha's Record, and the Ballon d'Or Snub | BackPage FC

TheTools · 10.09.2026 12:45 · source · permalink

Eight goals in five games and a record standing since 1950, from a position he only plays because Atletico refused to sell Julian Alvarez.

On 9 September 2026, Raphinha scored twice against Feyenoord and became the first player in Barcelona's entire history to score in each of the club's opening five fixtures of a season across competitions.

Two against Elche. One away at Athletic Bilbao. Two against Rayo Vallecano. One at Valencia. Two against Feyenoord. Eight goals in five games, from a player who was signed as a right winger, converted into a left winger, and is now playing centre-forward.

He was not nominated for the 2026 Ballon d'Or.

Those two facts sitting beside each other would be strange enough on their own. What makes this genuinely remarkable is the pattern underneath them, and almost nobody is tracing it: this is the second time in three years that a transfer Barcelona failed to complete has rebuilt Raphinha's career.

The Record, and How Far Back It Goes

Two separate records are involved here and they are frequently blurred together.

In La Liga alone, only two players had ever scored in Barcelona's opening five league games of a season: César Rodríguez in 1950-51 and Zlatan Ibrahimović in 2009-10. Across all competitions, including Europe, nobody had ever managed it. Not Messi. Not Suárez. Not Ronaldinho. Raphinha is the first.

The Camp Nou responded to his substitution against Feyenoord by chanting "Raphinha capitán," which is its own detail. He is Barcelona's newly appointed club captain, leading a side he was expected to be phased out of eighteen months ago.

Why Replacing Him Became Club Policy

Barcelona signed Raphinha from Leeds United in 2022 after 17 goals in 67 Premier League games. Leeds' record is on our Leeds United transfer records page.

His first two seasons in Catalonia were unremarkable enough that replacing him stopped being speculation and became policy. The problem was positional as much as anything to do with form. Lamine Yamal had taken ownership of the right wing, which left only the left flank available, and Barcelona spent two consecutive summers trying to buy someone specifically to play there.

That someone was Nico Williams.

The Nico Williams Saga, Twice

This is where the story gets genuinely unusual, because Barcelona did not fail once. They failed in successive years.

The first attempt came after Euro 2024 and collapsed. The second came in 2025, and it went considerably further: Barcelona agreed personal terms, and president Joan Laporta publicly confirmed the club was prepared to pay Williams's €58 million release clause.

On 4 July 2025, Athletic Club announced that Williams had signed a new contract instead. A ten-year deal running to 2035, extending his existing contract by eight years and raising his release clause by more than 50%. Barcelona had been rejected for the second summer running, and the mechanism that killed it was a contract extension used defensively, the same instrument we examined in our piece on how Atlético built a €500 million wall around Julián Álvarez and in our piece on how release clauses actually work. Set a clause low and it becomes a ceiling. Raise it far enough and it becomes a veto.

The Private Message That Ended It in One Evening

The reason for the collapse is barely reported anywhere, and it is the best detail in the entire saga.

SPORT reported that an unnamed Barcelona staff member privately messaged Williams, urging him to cut ties with his agent Félix Tainta and sign with a representative closer to the club, naming Jorge Mendes or Pini Zahavi as examples.

Williams and his family were furious. He and Tainta finalised the Athletic extension that same evening.

A club attempting to reorganise a player's representation lost the player entirely, within hours. And the man who benefited was the one Barcelona had spent two years trying to replace.

Barcelona chased Nico Williams across two summers, agreed personal terms, publicly committed to paying his release clause, and then lost him in a single evening because a staff member told him to sack his agent. Raphinha responded to that failure by scoring 34 goals, finishing as the Champions League's joint-top scorer, and winning Barcelona's first ever domestic treble.

The 34-Goal Answer

The 2024-25 season is the pivot point of Raphinha's career, and the numbers do not need decoration.

He played 57 of a possible 60 matches. He scored 34 goals and provided 18 assists. Barcelona won La Liga, the Copa del Rey and the Supercopa, the club's first ever domestic treble, and he was named La Liga's Best Player of the season.

The Champions League figures are the ones that survive any argument about context. Thirteen goals as joint-top scorer alongside Serhou Guirassy. Only Messi's 14 in 2011-12 has ever bettered it for Barcelona in a single European campaign. And 21 goal contributions in one Champions League season, equalling Cristiano Ronaldo's 2013-14 record.

Across Europe's top five leagues that season, only Mohamed Salah recorded more goal involvements, 57 to Raphinha's 56.

He finished fifth in the 2025 Ballon d'Or, behind Ousmane Dembélé, Lamine Yamal, Vitinha and Salah. Twelve months earlier he had not made the thirty-man shortlist at all.

The Season That Complicates the Story

Honesty requires the dip, because 2025-26 was not a repeat.

Injuries and squad competition reduced Raphinha to 13 La Liga goals in 22 appearances, with a single goal across six Champions League matches. Part of the competition came from Marcus Rashford, on loan from Manchester United and playing in the same areas.

That loan has its own afterlife worth knowing about. Barcelona held a €30 million option on Rashford payable in three instalments through 2028, and let it expire on 15 June 2026 without triggering it, having already spent €70 million on Anthony Gordon in the same position. We covered what that cost United in our piece on why they were outspent by all three promoted clubs, where a lapsed option on an academy graduate carrying no book value removed pure profit from their squad cost calculation. United's record is on our Manchester United transfer records page.

So the player crowding Raphinha out of minutes is gone, and gone in a way that hurt his parent club considerably more than it hurt Barcelona.

Why He Is Playing Centre-Forward At All

Now the second failed transfer, and it is the reason for the record.

Barcelona entered this summer needing a striker. Robert Lewandowski left. So did Ferran Torres, sold to Paris Saint-Germain for under €50 million, and that sale was not really about the striker position at all. It generated roughly €40 million of net Financial Fair Play headroom and removed around €21 million a year from Barcelona's squad cost, which is what allowed them to register Rodri from Manchester City. We traced that entire chain in our piece on how Barcelona actually funded the Rodri transfer.

Deco's plan to replace both forwards was Julián Álvarez. Atlético Madrid refused to negotiate at any price, enforced by a release clause reported at between €400 million and €500 million that applies only to Spanish clubs, while Álvarez remained available to buyers outside Spain at roughly €150 million. That refusal traces directly back to what Barcelona did to Atlético with Antoine Griezmann in 2019, which we set out in full in the Álvarez piece.

Barcelona's fallback was Gabriel Jesus, signed from Arsenal for €10 million plus add-ons, roughly a fifth of the £45 million Arsenal paid Manchester City in 2022. We covered his arrival, and the Guinness World Record he brought with him, in our piece on the record nobody mentioned.

So Barcelona sold one striker to fund a midfielder, lost another to retirement age, were refused the replacement they wanted, and bought a low-cost squad option instead. The centre-forward position was left open by accounting and by a rival club's grudge, and Raphinha moved into it.

The Pattern, Stated Plainly

Line the two events up and the shape is unmistakable.

Failed transfer one: Barcelona try to sign Nico Williams to replace Raphinha on the left wing. Twice. They fail. Raphinha responds with 34 goals, a domestic treble, the Champions League's joint-top scorer award and fifth in the Ballon d'Or.

Failed transfer two: Barcelona try to sign Julián Álvarez to play centre-forward. Atlético refuse. Raphinha moves to centre-forward and sets a club record that had stood, in one form, since 1950.

Both times, the club's recruitment department identified a position it wanted upgraded. Both times, the market refused to cooperate. Both times, the player already in the building answered it.

The 2026 Ballon d'Or Snub

Which brings us to the omission that will define his season regardless of what he scores.

Raphinha was not nominated for the 2026 Ballon d'Or. That is the third consecutive year an award body has either left him out or ranked him below what his output suggests: excluded from the thirty-man shortlist in 2024, fifth in 2025 after a 34-goal treble-winning season, and now not nominated at all.

He has form for responding publicly. When he was left out of the 2025 FIFPRO World XI, he answered with seventeen consecutive Instagram stories.

The awards conversation itself has been unusually contested this cycle, as we set out in our piece on the Ballon d'Or case built around the World Cup, where the argument turned on what the award actually measures: cumulative output, a single tournament, or reputation accumulated over time. Raphinha's case sits awkwardly against all three. His best season is behind him by one year, his following season was interrupted by injury, and his current form is five games old.

The Caveat Worth Stating

One qualifying factor deserves flagging before anyone declares this a permanent transformation.

Brazil exited the 2026 World Cup early, which gave Raphinha a summer break that most of his direct competitors did not get. ESPN identifies that rest as part of why he has started this season at this level. Five games is a small sample, and a well-rested 29-year-old outperforming tournament-fatigued rivals in September is a familiar football pattern that often corrects by November.

None of which changes the record, or the fact that he set it from a position he had never played, in a team that spent two years trying to replace him and one summer trying to buy someone to stand in front of him.

What This Actually Says About Recruitment

There is a broader point here that goes beyond one player.

Barcelona's recruitment department has been right about the shape of its squad twice and wrong about the solution twice. It correctly identified that the left wing needed elite output and that the centre-forward position needed replacing. It was wrong that Raphinha could not provide the first, and wrong that the second required a €150 million signing.

Modern squad building is increasingly constrained by the financial architecture we have documented across this window, from Squad Cost Ratio replacing PSR in England to La Liga's own 1:1 registration rule that forced the Ferran Torres sale. Under those constraints, the cheapest available upgrade is frequently a player you already employ, in a position you have not tried him in.

Barcelona did not discover that deliberately. They discovered it because Athletic Club raised a release clause, Atlético Madrid held a grudge from 2019, and a staff member sent a message he should not have sent.

Raphinha has scored eight goals in five games as a consequence, and nobody has nominated him for anything.
