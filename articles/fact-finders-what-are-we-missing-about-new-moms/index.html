FACT FINDERS: What are we missing about new moms?

TheTools · 24.09.2026 14:33 · source · permalink

TUCSON, Ariz. (13 News) - In the wake of high-profile cases sparking national conversations around maternal mental health, local experts are emphasizing a crucial message: mothers cannot thrive in isolation.

On a recent episode of the Fact Finders podcast, host Brooke Wagner sat down with licensed marriage and family therapist Sherry Dawson, president of the Tucson Perinatal Mental Health Coalition, to examine the spectrum of postpartum challenges—from common anxiety to rare medical emergencies—and why asking “How is the mom?” needs to become the norm.

Dawson highlighted that while the mild “baby blues” typically resolve within two to three weeks after birth, persistent struggles often signal postpartum depression or anxiety, which affects approximately one in seven mothers. Dawson noted that severe postpartum psychosis remains extremely rare—occurring in roughly one in 1,000 births—and requires immediate emergency medical evaluation rather than rest.

A key factor in protecting a mother’s mental health is reviving the traditional “village” around her. Dawson pointed out that the first and most effective intervention for struggling mothers often begins with basic needs, such as two nights of uninterrupted sleep, adequate nutrition, and family members taking over daily chores so mothers can rest.

The Tucson Perinatal Mental Health Coalition offers community resource directories, provider referrals, and educational events to connect families with help across Southern Arizona. For local support, visit tucsonpostpartum.com or call the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-943-5746 (1-833-9-HELP4M).
