Private parking firms told to cap fines for motorists

TheTools · 21.10.2026 09:19 · source · permalink

Operators will lose the power to pursue drivers through the courts if they do not abide by a new code of practice to be placed before parliament on Tuesday

Private parking firms will have to abide by tough new rules or lose the power to pursue drivers through the courts under a government pledge to tackle everyday annoyances.

Operators will be banned from automatically charging motorists for entering a car park without paying and will have to give them at least ten minutes to leave after their session has expired. 

The rules will also cap the maximum fine that an operator can charge a motorist for parking without a ticket at £100.

Motorists will also face a maximum fine of £20 on appeal if they make a mistake like entering their number plate incorrectly on a ticket machine. If a machine is out of order, then operators will not be able to issue fines at all.

The number of private car parks has mushroomed in recent years, from about 10,000 in 2012 to almost 50,000 today. At the same time the number of fines issued has increased to almost 14.4 million a year — a 148 per cent increase since 2019.

Under the new rules, drivers will be given a guaranteed five minutes to decide whether to park and ten minutes to leave once their session ends.

Charges will also be scrapped on appeal for mistakes like stopping briefly to check directions, in the case of breakdowns or broken ticket machines.   

For small slip-ups, like forgetting a parking permit, entering the wrong registration or forgetting to confirm it in the machine when parking, drivers will face a maximum charge of £20 on appeal. 

The changes have been welcomed by motoring organisations, but they said ministers could have gone further.

“While this isn’t quite the Christmas present we wanted for drivers, it should hopefully save many the stress of having to challenge unfair tickets,” Simon Williams, the RAC’s head of policy, said.

“We’ve long called for the banning of debt recovery fees being added on top of parking charge notices and a single independent appeals body to replace the two industry-run ones, but neither have been included, which is disappointing.” 

Jack Cousens, head of roads policy at the AA, said: “After seven long years, drivers finally have something which could offer them better protections against the worst private parking operators. For too long, many private parking operators have aggressively attacked drivers to the point where many simply pay up if they receive a charge from one on the doorstep.

“Ultimately, we need to stop charges being issued in the first place, especially where the driver has done nothing wrong.”

Alison Tooze, chief policy officer at the British Parking Association, said the group welcomed the new code “as an important step forward for motorists, communities and responsible parking operators”. 

She added: “Overall, the code strikes the right balance between stronger protections for motorists and effective parking management, helping keep communities, businesses and local economies moving.”
