2027 Chevrolet Silverado 1500 is Pricier But Offers Better Value

TheTools · 18.09.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

With Canadian sales at a six-year high, the Chevrolet Silverado is the fifth most popular vehicle in the entire auto industry. The company expects great things from the next-generation 2027 model.

At a General Motors media event this week, we learned that production will officially begin around mid-October on a small scale, before ramping up to full capacity early next year. The first units are therefore expected to arrive at dealerships in late November and December.

Today, we can finally share more details about packaging and pricing—with inevitable hikes compared to the 2026 model. As a reminder, the Canadian lineup for the 2027 Chevrolet Silverado 1500 comprises seven trim levels: Work Truck (WT), Custom, Z71, Custom Trail Boss, Trail Boss, High Country and ZR2. The LT and RST trims have been discontinued. The same three cab configurations (single, double, crew) and bed lengths (5.8 feet, 6.6 feet, 8 feet) are still available, though not all will be ready at launch.

Let’s start at the bottom of the lineup. The WT model sees its MSRP go up from $48,999 to $52,999. However, you have to consider everything that now comes standard in addition to the new design: a TurboMax four-cylinder engine delivering 350 hp and 465 lb-ft of torque (increases of 40 hp and 25 lb-ft), 10-speed automatic transmission (replacing the 8-speed unit), 12,000-lb capacity trailer hitch, 11.6-inch instrument cluster and 16.3-inch touchscreen (up from 3.5 and 7 inches, respectively), steering-wheel-mounted audio controls, telescopic steering column, integrated trailer brake controller and more.

There are also additional safety and driver assistance technologies, such as rear cross-traffic alert with braking, front and rear parking assist, safety alert seat, adaptive cruise control, lane keeping assist and pre-collision braking.

Chevrolet expects the new Z71 trim to become the most popular choice among Canadian customers. Starting at $67,199—$2,300 more expensive than the current LT 4x4 or just $700 above the RST 4x4—it comes standard with the previously optional Z71 package (hill descent control, high-capacity air filter, dual exhaust, two-speed transfer case, skid plates, etc.). The off-road tires grow from 32 to 34 inches, and the truck also includes trailer hitch guidance with hitch view, wheelhouse liners, a sliding rear window, universal garage door opener and ambient lighting.

At the top of the lineup, the MSRP of the Silverado 1500 High Country increases by $7,000, while the ZR2 climbs into six-figure territory when you include $3,571 freight, PDI and other fees. The former now features 22-inch wheels (up from 20 inches), continuously variable damping suspension, head-up display, dual wireless chargers, 11.5-inch front-passenger screen, sunroof and Multi-Flex centre console, not to mention a higher level of interior refinement along with Super Cruise technology.

As for the ZR2, it gains 35-inch mud-terrain tires (up from 33 inches), larger fuel tank, underbody camera, massaging seats, Super Cruise and several other extras we mentioned for the High Country. By the way, it undercuts the Ford F-150 Raptor by approximately $12,000.
