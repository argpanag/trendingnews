Your Past Gaming Hours Are Now a Ticket to Buying the New PS5 Pro - AOL

TheTools · 01.10.2026 02:00 · source · permalink

PlayStation has quietly introduced a new requirement for people hoping to get their hands on a PS5 Pro in Japan, and it could make things considerably harder for scalpers.

Sony is changing the way it handles some PS5 Pro sales by requiring prospective buyers to prove they have actually spent time playing PlayStation games. The new system is aimed at giving regular players a better shot at purchasing the console instead of seeing limited stock scooped up by resellers.

And there’s a catch: You can’t simply create a PlayStation account and enter the lottery.

PlayStation Wants Buyers to Prove They Actually Play Games

Under the new system, applicants need to have logged at least 60 hours of gameplay on a PS4 or PS5 during the previous two years.

That requirement makes the system considerably different from a typical console sale or lottery. Instead of simply getting in line early, prospective buyers need an established history with PlayStation hardware.

The measure is designed to make it more difficult for people who are primarily interested in purchasing PS5 Pro consoles to immediately turn around and resell them. Sony has also placed other requirements on the lottery, including restrictions involving PlayStation Network accounts.

The PS5 Pro Has Become a Target for Resellers

Scalpers have become a persistent problem whenever highly sought-after gaming hardware is released in limited quantities.

Advertisement

The basic strategy is simple: Buy a product while it is difficult to find, then resell it at a higher price while demand remains high. Automated purchasing tools can make that process even more difficult for regular shoppers, particularly when inventory sells out quickly.

Sony’s new approach takes a different route by making the buyer’s history part of the equation. Someone who has spent dozens of hours playing on a PlayStation console is much more likely to be an established player than someone who created an account specifically to purchase hardware for resale.

It doesn’t completely eliminate the possibility of reselling, but it creates another hurdle for people trying to exploit limited inventory.

The 60-hour requirement is particularly interesting because Sony isn’t asking buyers to prove they own a specific game or subscribe to a particular service. Instead, the company is looking at how much time the account has spent playing games on PS4 and PS5.

For longtime PlayStation players, 60 hours over two years is relatively modest. For someone attempting to buy a console simply because it can be resold, however, the requirement creates an obstacle that can’t easily be overcome at the last minute.

The system also means that having a PlayStation account alone isn’t necessarily enough to qualify.

For anyone who has spent the past few years building up hundreds of hours in their PlayStation library, though, all that gaming time may suddenly have another benefit: proving you’re actually a PlayStation player when it’s time to buy a PS5 Pro.
