Worker denied time off to attend festival sues for discrimination

TheTools · 23.09.2026 16:25 · source · permalink

Marketing executive claims employer violated ‘philosophical belief’ that attending music event promoted ‘human flourishing’

A marketing executive sued for “philosophical belief” discrimination because she was not granted time off work to attend a rock music festival.

Sarah Sadler alleged that she had been discriminated against when bosses rejected her annual leave request to attend the 2000trees music festival in Gloucestershire, which she said was of “spiritual significance” to her.

At an employment tribunal, the 49-year-old argued that she held a “protected philosophical belief” that attending a music festival promoted “human flourishing”.

Mrs Sadler, who was a marketing executive at the disability charity ARTHOUSE Unlimited, said she had a “genuine belief in the importance of community, mutual support, authenticity, inclusion and shared responsibility” and that festivals created those environments.

But the charity told Mrs Sadler she could not have annual leave to attend the event because it could not find cover.

An employment tribunal in Reading dismissed her case, with a judge saying her belief was not protected by equality laws and was more akin to “a lifestyle choice”.

Mrs Sadler, from Surrey, also brought claims of disability discrimination related to how she was treated after emergency surgery for cancer, but lost that claim too.

The tribunal heard that she started working at ARTHOUSE Unlimited in June 2023 but was dismissed in June 2024.

While working for the charity, bosses were aware that she had previously had stage three bowel cancer, for which she underwent bowel surgery and chemotherapy, and suffered from PTSD.

In December 2023, Mrs Sadler asked to have annual leave for three days in July the following year to go to the 2000trees festival.

The line-up included rock bands Don Broco and The Gaslight Anthem. It also featured Bob Vylan, the rap duo who caused controversy at last year’s Glastonbury Festival with their “death to the IDF” chant.

Mrs Sadler was told that she would not be able to take those days off because she was already taking several lengthy periods of leave and the charity would not be able to secure cover for those dates.

The tribunal heard that in January 2024, Mrs Sadler underwent emergency gallbladder surgery and was off work until the next month because she had a “flu-like” illness afterwards.

She alleged that she was pressured to return as soon as possible and not supported when she wanted to work from home, but the tribunal dismissed these allegations.

In February 2024, Mrs Sadler complained about not being allowed time off in an email to bosses. She wrote: “To deny a leave request for a festival as referred to in the correspondence could be considered to be indirect discrimination and prejudicial. The festival is an annual gathering which is of deep cultural significance to me. (Equality Act 2010)”.

She raised an unsuccessful formal grievance over the issue and how she was treated after her surgery. She told the charity she wanted a financial settlement, which was refused, and she was fired after she did not turn up to a meeting to discuss how to move forward.

Employment Judge Andrew Gumbiti-Zimuto said: “[Mrs Sadler] seeks to advance a belief about human flourishing, namely that people flourish through participation in enduring communities characterised by inclusion, reciprocity, creativity and mutual responsibility.

“[She] presents this proposition as a guiding principle by which [she] seeks to live rather than as a mere opinion.

“However, the evidence relied upon to demonstrate that belief centres almost entirely upon attendance at a festival and participation in a particular social community.”

The judge added that her being refused holiday “had nothing to do with her philosophical belief” and that it was an “operational” decision.
