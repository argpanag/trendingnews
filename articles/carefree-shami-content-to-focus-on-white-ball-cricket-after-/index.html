'Carefree' Shami content to focus on white-ball cricket after Test retirement

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:54 · source · permalink

Fast bowler Mohammed Shami has taken his mind off making a comeback to India's white-ball squads, and is instead focusing on playing for his domestic side Bengal, and in the IPL. Shami retired from Test cricket earlier this month with "no regrets", and even though he will continue to feature in both white-ball formats in domestic cricket, he said for now he is only looking at "enjoying" the game.

"I have stopped counting all these things," Shami said in Mumbai when asked if the 2027 ODI World Cup or the 2028 T20 World Cup were on his mind. "Now I am focussing on my game. Whatever they (the selectors) will decide will be better. But from my side, the only thing is to keep continuing, to stay in rhythm, and keep playing at least for the state and the IPL."

Shami, whose last international appearance was in the Champions Trophy 2025, played the first leg of the Ranji Trophy at the start of the domestic season before announcing his Test retirement. He then turned out in the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy (SMAT) until Bengal were knocked out after losing to Rajasthan on Thursday at the Wankhede Stadium. Shami managed just four wickets in SMAT at an average of 43 after taking 14 wickets in three red-ball games at a much better average of 14. Shami said now that he has retired from Tests, he could afford to "pick and choose" games for the rest of the season.

"As long as you like cricket, as long as you enjoy it, you should play," Shami said in reply to what was keeping him going in domestic cricket. "That is my motivation, that I'm still enjoying it, and I hope some of the boys learn or pick up something while playing for the state because experience matters a lot. This is my belief that it doesn't matter where you're playing and what level, you should transfer something or the other [to the youngsters].

"There is no connection [of me playing in this tournament] with retirement. I have already said that I will continue playing white-ball cricket. And if needed, I will also play red-ball [cricket] for fitness. So, it is not a new experience. It is the same routine that I have been doing for the past 15-20 years. Nothing new."

When asked he if he was going to feature in the 50-over Vijay Hazare Trophy next month, Shami said, "Sure, yaar. But the option is that now I can pick and choose which games to play and which ones to leave. There is no pressure at all. But hopefully, it goes well, and I stay fit till the next season and keep playing well."

Shami, 36, might still continue to play Ranji Trophy games from time to time and said he was still feeling fit. Why did he give up hopes of making a Test comeback then?

"It felt like the right thing, so I did it," he said of his retirement. "I'm moving well, playing well, so I'll play white-ball now bindaas (carefree). It's not good for the body to take too much load. Now when people don't like my skills [in red-ball cricket], then I'm going to play the white-ball game.

"There is no regret [of retirement]. Whatever is written in your destiny, you will get, and I'm happy with whatever I got. Now I'll keep playing till whatever is in my destiny and enjoy it."

Shami also said one of the reasons for turning up in the domestic circuit was to pass on any valuable experience to the younger players in the Bengal side.

"Overall, it was good because there were a lot of youngsters in our team," Shami said of his experience of playing in the SMAT. "We got to see a lot of young talent. Hopefully, we'll get to see new faces from this tournament in the IPL auction; there was a lot of new talent and skills. Hopefully, some new guys will come this season."

Once the domestic season ends, Shami's next big assignment will be IPL 2027, for which he was traded from Lucknow Super Giants (LSG) to Rajasthan Royals (RR). One of the RR players who continues to make waves in the white-ball formats wherever he goes is Shami's future team-mate Vaibhav Sooryvanshi.

The 15-year-old batter most recently bashed a number of List-A records with his staggering 204 off 53 balls for India A against UAE in Doha.

"That innings was beyond imagination; he played it like a video game," Shami said. "You can't even imagine that the team is chasing 200-250 runs, and the game ends in 14-15 overs. But we have seen his hitting range before. And I hope that his ability, and how he enjoys hitting in the zones, hope he continues to do that for very long and keeps performing for the team."
