Mining sell-off hits ASX as traders await AI catalysts

The Australian sharemarket fell on Monday, led by a sell-off in the mining sector and as traders weighed Nvidia’s leadership crisis and Anthropic’s upcoming public offering amid lingering artificial intelligence jitters.

The S&P/ASX 200 closed down 37.5 points, or 0.4 per cent, to 9044.7 as four of the 11 sectors finished in the red. Aside from a drop in mining stocks, the rest of the index eked out modest advances, with real estate and technology in the lead.

Investors took their cues from a weak Friday session on Wall Street, where Nvidia’s shares extended losses after chief executive Jensen Huang resigned over an affair with a woman suspected of spying for China. The Financial Times has since reported that Huang is considering a return amid Silicon Valley pressure on the AI giant’s board.

The leadership crisis comes at an awkward juncture, with Nvidia due to report its third-quarter results later this week. AI market scrutiny is also set to intensify around Anthropic’s initial public offering, scheduled for Wednesday (Thursday AEDT).

On the ASX, market direction was primarily dictated by weaker commodity prices. Iron ore extended its retreat below $US100 a tonne and copper fell to a four-month low after weak Chinese economic growth data dampened the demand outlook for the commodities and appetite for risk.
