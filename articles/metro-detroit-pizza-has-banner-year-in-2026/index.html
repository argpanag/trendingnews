Metro Detroit pizza has banner year in 2026

TheTools · 01.11.2026 09:06 · source · permalink

The year started off with some precarious news for the industry as pizza popularity has reportedly slipped nationwide, but local businesses prevailed, making 2026 a hot one for independent pizzerias.

We stood in lines. We pulled cheese. We chewed crust.

Some of the biggest pizza news of the year was about the buzz on new places. Amelia Street Pizza in Hazel Park opened in June with long lines and daily sell-outs. Months later, there are still lines out the door for the large, round pies with a "half moon" of freshly grated pecorino cheese on the crust.

Over in Macomb County, pizza fans are raving about Crust Punk, a carry-out pizzeria that opened in June in Harrison Township. Like Amelia Street, they don't offer modifications; you take what they have. Their dough undergoes a five-day cold fermentation, then the pizza is offered as one of seven 16-inch signature pies.

This month, Birmingham became home to the second location of Saginaw's New York-style slice shop Old World Pizzeria. Here, you get big oven-baked pizza with cup-and-char pepperoni either by the slice (reheated in the oven to order) or a whole 20-inch pie.

Downriver, Paul Laginess, owner of longstanding Allen Park pizzeria Major Tomato, expanded his business by opening a live music venue next door called Ground Control. Also Downriver, we saw the former Frank's pizzeria space on Biddle transform briefly into a Tru Pizza location (a gluten-free pizza franchise). That closed, and the Pizza Bar opened earlier this month with round and square pies for carryout or dine-in.

In other expansion news, Grandma Bob's is planning a second location in Detroit. This one will be in West Village, where the Marrow butcher shop and restaurant was. Saroki's Crispy Chicken & Pizza opened its 22nd location in Commerce in 2026, and has plans to open more locations in Troy, Redford, Brownstown, Plymouth and Lansing.

Then there's the rising star that is Detroit-style pizza.

Michigan-based pizza chain Domino's came out with a two-slice, Detroit-style square in August, following Hungry Howie's addition of a Detroit-style pizza last September.

Buddy's Pizza celebrated its 80th anniversary this year with new owners, as a group of investors purchased the Detroit brand from CapitalSpring LLC, which has invested in Buddy's since 2018. The new owners said they will focus on Buddy’s customer experience, product quality and brand reputation after the chain has struggled with gaining traction outside of southeast Michigan, closing its last west Michigan location in January in Grand Rapids. They also want to grow Buddy’s frozen pizza offerings in grocery stores nationally.

One of the best of the new-school Detroit-style pizzerias, Michigan & Trumbull this month got a nod from Food & Wine magazine, which named their Long Way Home pie as one of the best 27 pizzas in America.

The biggest Detroit-style pizzeria debut of 2026 was the August opening of Unc's in Capitol Park in downtown Detroit. The owners of nearby Dime Store, a beloved destination for breakfast, brunch and lunch, teamed up with Tony Sacco of Mootz Pizzeria + Bar for this casual, sit-down bar and restaurant, which also does carryout.

"It's been great," said pizza consultant Joe Maino of the year in pizza. He has worked with many Detroit-style pizza brands, including Unc's. "I truly think that Michigan and Metro Detroit is the pizza capital of America without a doubt. Yes, New York has so many pizzerias ... as far as pizza culture, New York reigns supreme; there's nothing like that. But as far as how much people eat pizza, Michigan seems to be up there."

Maino recently competed at the Pizza Expo in Columbus, put on by industry magazine Pizza Today. He took home first place for the Midwest division for Best Pepperoni Slice, and won second place overall in that category.

"I knew I made a good pizza. I needed nice shredded cheese on there, but I needed fresh mozzarella on top, baked on top," he said of his winning pizza, which he sliced into six squares to serve to the judges. "I dolloped the sauce on there real nice, and I parbaked the crust so it was thoroughly baked through. It was a large Detroit-style, but I made it thinner so it was more like a grandma-pizza-Detroit-style hybrid. I finished it with a blend of hard cheeses that I seasoned with oregano, chili flake and garlic and dusted that over top."

Maino said if there's any downside to the pizza landscape this year, it's high prices.

"Pizza is so expensive now, but when you don't have a liquor license, people are trying to make a living and pay their employees good wages based off just pizza, it's gonna be more expensive," he said. "And they are using good ingredients, which do come at a premium."
