Weekend Box Office: The Hunger Games Dominates with $120 Million Debut

TheTools · 23.11.2026 21:01 · source · permalink

Weekend Box Office

Sunrise on the Reaping is the fifth film in the franchise to open over $100 million and will likely outgross Godzilla Minus Zero, The Cat in the Hat, and Ebenezer combined.

By Erik Childress | Published November 22, 2026

TAGGED AS: Box Office, movies, news

The holiday box office got its first true shot of adrenaline this weekend as the sixth film in a nearly $3 billion franchise had the fifth-best opening of 2026. By the end of Wednesday on Thanksgiving Eve the latest in Suzanne Collins’ series will likely have outgrossed Godzilla Minus Zero, The Cat in the Hat, and Ebenezer combined. By next weekend it will have already surpassed the first prequel in these series and will be looking for a second-weekend victory.

King of the Crop: The Hunger Games Dominates with $120 Million Debut

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is the sixth film in the franchise and its second prequel. The previous one, The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, did respectfully enough, grossing $166.3 million domestic and over $347 million worldwide (not counting international re-releases). That was a fraction of what the Jennifer Lawrence-led films made, ranging between $281-425 million domestic and over $2.96 billion combined worldwide. Perhaps the mere suggestion of Lawrence’s return sparked greater interest in Sunrise on the Reaping, because the prequel just grossed $120 million (more than even Mockingjay Part 2) and became the fifth film in the franchise to open with $100+ million.

Up until 2022, Catching Fire had the highest November opening ever with $158 million. It was beaten by Black Panther: Wakanda Forever’s $181.3 million. Sunrise on the Reaping is the 16th November release to open with nine digits and ranks 12th on that all-time list. That hasn’t always translated into a $300 million finish. In fact, six of those openers came up short, including three Twilights, two Harry Potters, and the aforementioned Mockingjay Part 2. Reaping is also the most expensive of the Hunger Games films at $200 million, compared to the $160 million of, again, Mockingjay Deux, and the mere $78 million budget of the first film. The lowest-grossing global total among all of November’s $100+ million openers was last year’s Wicked: For Good, which finished with over $525 million. Mockingjay Part 2 was second lowest with a still massive $652.9 million. Either number would be good news for Lionsgate. As of this weekend, Reaping’s global total is at $225 million.

Tales of the Top 10: I Play Rocky Expands Wide and Lands in Second

After two weeks of limited release, Amazon/MGM is getting Peter Farrelly’s I Play Rocky out there nationwide for audiences. The film made $337,465 in seven theaters its first weekend. That went up to $493,707 in 29 theaters in weekend two. Now in 2,477 theaters, the film jumped to $9.3 million, and it has potential to be one of the season’s sleepers after getting an A+ Cinemascore. Only three other films this year got the grade, two of them among the faith-based crowd with I Can Only Imagine 2 and A Great Awakening and the other for Baz Luhrmann’s EPiC: Elvis Presley In Concert. I Play Rocky certainly is a religious experience for some, so to say it fits in with the barrage of faith-centric films to receive the top grade over the years is not all that off-base. With over $10 million in the bank, let’s see how it performs over the holiday before committing to estimates.

Ti West’s $38 million adaptation of Charles Dickens’ classic isn’t doing too bad, all things considered. Johnny Depp’s wide return to the big screen in Ebenezer fell 59% to $7.7 million in its sophomore weekend to give it a 10-day total of $32.5 million. Back in 2019, the war film Midway had an $8.5 million pre-Thanksgiving weekend and $34.8 million banked. Ebenezer is looking at a finish between $50-60 million domestic. Globally the film is at $78 million; when it passes $100 million, it should be more than enough to get this one in the win column for Paramount.

Warner Bros.’ Dr. Seuss adaptation The Cat in the Hat has been out 17 days and still has not passed $50 million. That’s hardly a milestone for the $86 million animated production which hasn’t even hit that number yet. Another $6.8 million this weekend gives it $47.1 million on the domestic side and $70 million globally. The film is following a similar path to the animated version, which kicked off the holiday season back in 2006. Flushed Away had $48.5 million after a $6.5 million third weekend. Even with Thanksgiving coming up (Hexed is going to get more attention), The Cat in the Hat is looking at a domestic finish in the $65-70 million region. That’s another theatrical loser for the studio. Dune: Part Three can’t get here soon enough for them.

Godzilla Minus Zero fell very quickly and continues to do so. The GKids release made $2.6 million in its third weekend and has made $54.5 million in 17 days after a $30.6 million opening. Paramount’s Street Fighter, by comparison, was up to $53.4 million after a $3.65 million third weekend and a $30.7 million start. As we said last week, the big difference is Minus Zero cost roughly $18 million and Street Fighter $82 million. The math does itself. Also, keep reading to see where it now ranks on the all-time domestic list for non-English live-action releases. Spoiler: it’s pretty high.

The documentary You Can See Everything finished its sixth weekend with $2.5 million; it now sits at $93.4 million after 38 days of release. The weekend was slightly less than The Social Network’s $3.4 million, but Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s film is still over $8 million ahead of that film’s $84.8 million haul in the same period. Everything remains headed to a finish between $100-105 million.

Ghost Soldier does not have the pay-it-forward power of Sound of Freedom, nor is it looking like much of a winner for Sony with $2.3 million in its second weekend. The $18 million Jim Caviezel actioner that opened on Veteran’s Day has made $12.4 million in 12 days and could be out of the top 10 by this Wednesday.

Martin McDonough’s Wild Horse Nine has held quite nicely since its release earlier this month. Another $2.5 million in its third weekend gives it $17.9 million domestic and over $39 million worldwide. His last film, The Banshees of Inisherin, grossed over $49 million globally. Laika’s Wildwood also managed to hang on for another week with $2.29 million, bringing its domestic total to $52.3 million. Globally the $100 million production has only passed $75 million.

Earlier this year, Dhurandhar: The Revenge spent three weeks in the top 10 and made $26.1 million after 17 days and finished with $28.1 million and nearly $153 million worldwide. Ramayana: Rise of a Legend is estimated to finish its 17th day with $28.4 million. The Dhurandhar sequel never cracked 1,000 theaters while Ramayana began in 1,433 with its release from Sony and dropped to only 681 this week but still cracked the top 10. The film has made over $183 million globally to date. Ramayana is now one of the 10 highest non-English live-action domestic releases ever. Here is that list, which also features the aforementioned Godzilla Minus Zero:

The Passion of the Christ ($370.2 million), Crouching Tiger Hidden Dragon ($128.0 million), Life Is Beautiful ($57.2 million), Godzilla Minus One ($57.1 million), Godzilla Minus Zero ($54.5 million), Parasite ($53.3 million), Instructions Not Included ($44.4 million), Pan’s Labyrinth ($37.6 million), Amelie ($33.2 million), Ramayana: Rise of a Legend ($28.4 million), Dhurandhar: The Revenge ($28.1 million), Jet Li’s Fearless ($24.6 million), Il Postino ($21.8 million), Like Water for Chocolate ($21.6 million), La Cage aux Folles ($20.4 million), Baahubali 2: The Conclusion ($20.1 million)

Beyond the Top 10: How to Rob a Bank Comes Up Short

David Leitch’s How to Rob a Bank isn’t a Fall Guy-type of loser, but its numbers are still bad. The $60 million production is down to just $1.68 million in its second weekend, falling out of the top 10, and it has grossed just $8.6 million in 10 days. The revolution to eat the rich just did not develop around these heisting influencers.

Getting one last shoutout is Warner Bros. and DC’s Clayface, making $1.3 million to bring its domestic total up to $66.2 million. With $126 million in the bank globally, one could justify it as a minor success. It will pass Supergirl’s global numbers. Maybe even Shazam!: Fury of the Gods’ $134 million might be a question, although being in that vicinity with the second and third film out of the DC Gunn gate is likely one of the many reasons regime change came so quickly over there.

Angel Studios’ Drummer Boy made $1.2 million to bring its total to $18.8 million, doubling up its $9.4 million opening weekend; something neither The Brink of War nor their remake of Angel and the Badman could do despite starting with less than $3 million each.

Checking in on some of the big platform releases right now, we have Jordan Firstman’s Club Kid making $1.39 million in just 229 theaters, bringing its total to $3.39 million. The A24 release’s estimated $6,069 per-theater-average is the 10th best for a film this year in its third weekend. The numbers are similar, very similar, to 2010’s Blue Valentine, which made $1.38 million in its third weekend moving into 230 theaters (a $6,029 PTA) and was up to $2.84 million after 17 days. It finished its run with $9.7 million. 

James Gray’s Paper Tiger expanded momentarily by Neon this weekend to 28 theaters (from six last week). It grossed $336,000 to bring its 10-day total to $622,000. Its $12,000 estimated PTA ranks 14th among second-week releases this year. Club Kid ranked second. Neon also has Andrey Zvyagintsev’s Minotaur, which won the Grand Prix at Cannes this year. They put it out in three theaters this weekend, where it grossed $97,000.

Focus released Elsinore with one of this year’s Oscar favorites, Andrew Scott, into six theaters, where it grossed $240,000. That equates to their fifth-best per-theater average since the pandemic after Asteroid City, The Phoenician Scheme, Bugonia, and Didi. Sumerian released one of this year’s hugely acclaimed Sundance films, Josephine, with Channing Tatum. In four theaters the film grossed $84,000 and will expand on Dec. 4. The $21,000 per-theater average is the company’s best to date after releases of Queen of the Ring, Mile End Kicks, and The Incomer. Netflix’s La Bola Negra made $200,000 this weekend to bring the film over $4 million. The film will debut on the streaming service on Dec. 2. Finally, Paul Thomas Anderson’s film of Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall grossed $162,000 in three theaters. A $54,000 PTA would rank 12th on the year.

On The Vine: Focker-in-Law and Hexed Lead Thanksgiving Releases

Thanksgiving audiences will have their choices this week starting with the annual Disney offering over the holiday with the witch story Hexed, from the directors of Moana 2, which broke records just a couple years ago. Comedy has had two sequels break $100 million this year, and Universal is hoping Focker-in-Law will be the fourth film in that series to do it. Netflix is going to try and Glass Onion the collaboration between David Fincher and Quentin Tarantino. The sequel to Once Upon a Time In Hollywood, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth with Brad Pitt, opens in theaters over the holiday before hitting the streaming service on Dec. 23. Also opening with Finn Wittrock and Alexandra Daddario is Hershey, the story of the candy empire’s beginning. Finally, Neon opens the latest from Drive My Car director Ryûsuke Hamaguchi.
