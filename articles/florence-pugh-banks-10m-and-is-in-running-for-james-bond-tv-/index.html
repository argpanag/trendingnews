Florence Pugh banks £10m and is in running for James Bond TV series

TheTools · 05.09.2026 00:44 · source · permalink

Florence Pugh has reportedly banked almost £10m and is in the running to be cast as an iconic character in a new James Bond TV series.

The 30-year-old star from Oxford has enjoyed her most successful year yet, with major roles in Marvel films, the Dune franchise and Spider-Man: Brand New Day helping boost her earnings.

Accounts for her company, Flo Pug Ltd, reportedly show she earned around £6 million in the 12 months to August 2025, adding to approximately £4 million already held by the business, according to The Sun.

A movie insider told the newspaper: "She already has a reputation for being one of the ­hardest-working stars in the business.

"These figures prove Florence is now one of the most sought-after stars in Tinseltown too, with earnings that are growing massively year on year.

"It makes her one of the most successful British actresses of recent years, with the potential to earn even more now she is in the frame to be joining the new Bond franchise universe."

The actress has built an impressive catalogue of work over the past decade, appearing in dozens of film and television productions.

Among her recent credits are Dune: Part Two, Thunderbolts and Avengers: Doomsday, while she is also set to star alongside Tom Holland in Spider-Man: Brand New Day.

The latest reports come amid speculation that Pugh could be involved in a new James Bond spin-off project.

It has previously been reported that Amazon's Prime Video service is developing The Moneypenny Diaries, a television series based on Samantha Weinberg's novels written under the pseudonym Kate Westbrook.

The books explore the backstory of Miss Moneypenny, the iconic MI6 secretary who featured throughout the James Bond franchise.

While no official casting announcement has been made, reports suggest Pugh is among those being linked with the project as Amazon continues to expand the Bond universe following its acquisition of the 007 rights.
