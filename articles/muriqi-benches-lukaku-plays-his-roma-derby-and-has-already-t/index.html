Muriqi benches Lukaku, plays his Roma 'derby' and has already troubled Gasp

TheTools · 10.09.2026 17:35 · source · permalink

champions

A flop at Lazio (though his only goals in Italy came against Gian Piero Gasperini's Atalanta, then coached by the Giallorossi's then-manager), the Kosovan striker has now displaced Romelu Lukaku at Fenerbahce and is scoring decisive goals.

It's almost a derby, if not quite. Not least because in his three appearances against Roma (two of which were very brief), Vedat Muriqi never made an impact. This was largely true throughout his Italian spell with Lazio. Yet, the player who was considered almost a flop in Italy first found a new lease of life in Spain, at Mallorca, and is now set to repeat that success in Istanbul, specifically at Fenerbahce. It was there, incidentally, that he made his breakthrough in the 2019/20 season, scoring 17 goals (and providing 7 assists) in 37 appearances. That performance convinced Claudio Lotito to invest around €20 million in the Kosovan striker, an investment that ultimately proved to be an authentic flop. Fortunately for Lazio, the Turkish club invested €15 million this summer to re-sign him, allowing the Biancocelesti to recoup €7 million, as they had retained a 45 per cent sell-on clause when he was sold to Mallorca. 

The Revival

Tonight, Muriqi will lead Fenerbahce's attack in their clash with Roma, a match of immense importance for the Turkish side, who have been absent from Europe's most prestigious competition for 18 years. He returned to Turkey after his revival in Spain, where last season he scored 23 goals, resulting in La Liga's top scorer behind Mbappé. While those goals weren't enough to save Mallorca from relegation, they certainly put him back in the international spotlight. And at Fenerbahce, a team aiming to surprise, Muriqi has started brightly: three goals in seven games, including the decisive strike in Lyon, which played a clear and crucial role in securing the Turks' qualification for the League Phase. He has since added two league goals against Konyaspor and Samunspor, contributing to two of Kartal's side's four Super Lig victories so far. But Muriqi is so vital and central to Fenerbahce that he can afford the luxury of benching a giant like Romelu Lukaku, who is effectively his understudy in the Turkish 4-2-3-1 formation. Perhaps this alone is enough to understand the true extent of his resurgence and his rediscovered brilliance. In Italy, the Kosovan striker scored only two goals in 49 appearances, both, incidentally, against Gian Piero Gasperini's Atalanta – one in the league and one in the Coppa Italia. This, too, might be a warning for the Giallorossi to heed. Muriqi poses a real threat in the Champions League encounter, and he must be treated as such.
