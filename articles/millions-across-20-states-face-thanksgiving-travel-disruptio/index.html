Millions across 20+ states face Thanksgiving travel disruptions as powerful storms take aim at both coasts

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:54 · source · permalink

Storms fire up in the West on Tuesday, and in the East on Wednesday. Storms are likely to cause major travel disruptions at major airports.

Storms on both coasts could blast millions of travelers before, during and after Thanksgiving

Storms are expected to threaten Thanksgiving travel for millions across major corridors and transit hubs on both coasts. Another storm system will break off the relentless atmospheric river pattern and slam the Pacific Northwest and Northern California beginning Tuesday. Meanwhile, another coastal storm is expected to form off the Southeast coast late Wednesday before soaking parts of the East Coast on Thanksgiving Day.

THE TAKEAWAY 

  • Storms ramp up in the West on Tuesday, bringing widespread rain, strong winds and even snow.
  • In the East, storms will start sometime on Wednesday, likely to cause major travel delays from Florida to Maine.
  • The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade could face delays or disruptions if winds are too strong.

We're less than a week from Thanksgiving and forecast models are focusing in on two major storms that could disrupt travel plans for millions of people on both coasts. 

On the West Coast, impacts will begin Tuesday, stretching from the Pacific Northwest all the way through California.

Meanwhile in the East, another coastal storm is expected to form off the Southeast coast late Wednesday and could cause widespread impacts from Florida to Maine on Thanksgiving Day itself and beyond.

West Coast storms

As is the trend for the West lately, a hyperactive jet stream is firing up once again, aiming relentless storms at the region. 

With these storms, low-lying areas of the Pacific Northwest and California should expect widespread downpours, while higher elevations are forecast to get smacked with heavy snow.

On the first day of storms, Tuesday, heavy rain is likely to trigger a flash flooding threat due to the recent excess moisture the West has endured in recent weeks.

The flooding threat is expected to escalate, especially in places like the waterlogged parts of California.

In addition to the widespread rain, gusty winds and King Tides will be a problem. 

While the King Tides won't likely contribute to travel troubles, they are forecast to be some of the strongest and largest King Tides of the year in the West.

Coastal flooding and beach erosion could become a major issue for those living right on the coast. 

The gusty winds plus heavy rain could cause travel delays and potential cancellations at major airport hubs like Seattle-Tacoma International Airport and San Francisco International Airport.

As the week continues, storm after storm will batter the West with rain, wind and snow in the mountains. 

The FOX Forecast Center said the Sierra Nevada, Rockies and Cascade mountains will all see feet of snow. 

East Coast storms 

Meanwhile, in the East, storms are expected to power up along the Southeast coast sometime Wednesday. 

As Thanksgiving festivities begin on Thursday, this large storm sytem is likely to impact more than 20 states from Florida all the way to Maine.

Heavy rain and damaging, gusty winds will ramp up from the Mid-Atlantic into the Northeast throughout the day Thursday.

These conditions could be a problem for the iconic Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, which has a history of rough weather.

Sustained winds must remain below 23 mph and wind gusts must remain below 34 mph.

Widespread travel delays will be possible and persist through the weekend.

For millions of travelers rushing home Friday through Sunday, the biggest threat will be whether these disturbances rapidly intensify into major storm systems, the FOX Forecast Center said.

As the area of low pressure moves up the East Coast, it will usher in a blast of much colder air, sparking the threat of accumulating snow near the Great Lakes, interior Northeast and higher elevations of New England.

The FOX Forecast Center said it's too early to predict snow totals and how strong winds will be.
