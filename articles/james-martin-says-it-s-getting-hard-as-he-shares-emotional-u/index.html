James Martin says ‘it’s getting hard’ as he shares emotional update

TheTools · 05.09.2026 06:30 · source · permalink

Celebrity chef James Martin is one of the most recognisable and successful chefs in the country and has made a name for himself as a restaurateur and presenter. He has also launched restaurants while appearing on television programmes bearing his own name in the title.

James has been on television for around three decades on programmes such as ITV’s Saturday Morning, James Martin’s Midweek Menu and Ready, Steady, Cook.

However, just like fellow celebrity chef Tom Kerridge, he is not immune from the ravages of the economic battering the UK’s hospitality sector has been on the receiving end of in recent years.

As the cost-of-operating crisis has worsened, James has spoken about the toll it has taken not just on him, but on his venues and his staff.

Speaking to The Bolton News about the struggle independents face as he celebrated the city’s Food and Drink Festival, he said: “It's not getting any easier, don't get me wrong, with everything costing and everything else spiralling. So, it's only more relevant now than ever to support that because it's getting bloody hard.”

This isn’t the only time James has addressed the stresses of the hospitality business, as he says those economic challenges have brought his staff members closer together with a greater sense of camaraderie.

Speaking to The Yorkshire Post, he disclosed: “I’ve worked in every one of my sites over the last four days.

“We were lucky, we were full, we did 170 covers last night. Without that team, you can’t do it. It’s made you much more close knit as a team. We were always close anyway, but it's made you a lot closer knit.”

Speaking to the same publication, he detailed how energy costs were proving particularly punishing for businesses like his.

He said: “Energy costs have gone through the roof, we are the highest-rated VAT in Europe in terms of hospitality. Nobody’s listening, all these hospitality businesses are going bust and you’ve got one million youngsters unemployed.”

Whilst James and others have been calling for change, the Labour government has started to respond with the recent announcement of the £10m Hospitality Grant Scheme, which will reportedly aim to help entrepreneurs start new hospitality businesses and try to fill more holes on the high street.

In response, co-chair of the Hospitality Sector Council Jane Riordan said: “The flexible funding will help support innovative projects that boost productivity and sustainability, create opportunities for young people and those furthest from the labour market, and deliver the shared priorities of the Hospitality Sector Council and the wider sector.”

James Martin returns to UK screens this morning at 9.30am on ITV with James Martin’s Saturday Morning.
