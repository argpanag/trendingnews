NFL Power Rankings, Week 3: Bengals, Chiefs climb; Bears, Buccaneers plummet

TheTools · 24.09.2026 14:47 · source · permalink

Week 2 reminds us that Week 1 can be misleading. Week 2 also doubles the evidence available to us, which can bring some early clarity to the Power Rankings.

Then again, when upsets get involved, things get even more complicated. The Browns won the Baker Mayfield Bowl over the Buccaneers after a two-hour weather delay. The Saints completed their second comeback effort of the season to take down a Week 1 darling in Baltimore. And the Rams' five-alarm fire wasn't so serious after all.

With two weeks in the books, let's sort things out.

NOTE: Up/down arrows below reflect movement from the Week 2 edition of the Power Rankings.

Who needs Sam Darnold when you have Drew Lock?! Jokes aside, Lock was excellent in his first start in place of Darnold, dicing up the Cardinals with a fearless style of play that should both endear him to Seahawks fans and help them worry less about the absence of their starting quarterback. Pairing Lock and Co. with the Dark Side defense worked incredibly well for one week and keeps the defending champs atop the Power Rankings.

Joe Brady’s promotion from offensive coordinator to head coach has elevated Buffalo’s offense from a productive and versatile group to an incredibly diverse, efficient and explosive attack that looks like a juggernaut through two weeks. Josh Allen is playing the best football of his career and he’s on an early track to win his second MVP award. If I trusted the Bills’ defense a bit more, I’d move them up to No. 1.

It’s understandable if you didn’t buy into what Cincinnati's defense was serving in Week 1. Sunday’s performance in Houston, however, was undeniable. Dexter Lawrence has transformed this Bengals front, and everyone is benefitting. Cincy is legitimate on both sides of the ball, winning with big plays offensively and consistently stifling opposing offenses. It’s only two weeks, but I see a complete team built for the long haul.

It took just two games for everyone to learn that Patrick Mahomes is back. Mixed with the Chiefs’ newfound running excellence -- thanks in large part to the addition of Kenneth Walker III -- Kansas City possesses its most dynamic offense since the early years of its current run of success. That should worry the rest of the AFC.

Kansas City Chiefs move up 6 spots to No. 4 in Week 3 Power Rankings.

Through one half of football against the Saints on Sunday, the Ravens looked like they were headed toward another one-sided victory. Somewhere along the way, though, they lost their identity, going away from Derrick Henry and losing their ability to move the ball at will. It was only Week 2, so it was an ideal time for them to suffer a setback, but Jesse Minter’s crew needs to identify and apply the lessons learned from the minor collapse.

Denver's Jekyll-and-Hyde offense is back, but that's better than what Sean Payton's team produced in Week 1. The Broncos withstood early challenges presented by the Jaguars, came together defensively to stay within striking range -- their third quarter goal-line stand swung the momentum in their favor -- and woke up offensively with enough time to complete the comeback. Much like last season, I’d like to see that type of output happen earlier in a game, but at least Denver was able to get Jaylen Waddle going.

Folks, Brock Purdy is dealing. Defenses have not been able to stop the 26-year-old in the first two weeks. Purdy is at the peak of his powers and completely comfortable running the show, dicing up the opposition and adding his own flair along the way. Quietly, the 49ers are dunking on the doubters, looking like one of the better teams in the NFL through two games.

Watch the best plays from San Francisco 49ers quarterback Brock Purdy’s 3-touchdown game against the Miami Dolphins in Week 2 of the 2026 NFL season.

Without Myles Garrett and Puka Nacua, the Rams entered Week 2 with less fanfare and a lingering, bitter taste in their mouths from the season-opening loss in Australia. Both absences didn’t end up mattering much. Matthew Stafford and Davante Adams rekindled their productive connection, lighting up New York’s defense and running away with a win over a Giants team that lost Jaxson Dart on Drive No. 1.

It finally happened: The Jaguars lost a regular-season game. They hadn’t dropped one since Week 10 of last season and appeared to be on track to notch yet another victory on Sunday. Ultimately, though, the Jags failed to finish the job, leaving the door open for the Broncos to mount a comeback. Trevor Lawrence’s excellence finally declined, too, and he had nowhere to turn in the late stages at Denver, resulting in a humbling outcome on the road.

The Eagles found themselves in a four-quarter battle with a scrappy Titans team that gave them everything they could handle. Philadelphia’s championship culture prevailed, though. Outside of a couple of interceptions (one ugly, the other unfortunate), Jalen Hurts was excellent as a playmaker, propelling an offense that needed him and DeVonta Smith to carry the squad to a road win. There are no freebies in this league, and a lesser team loses in Nashville on Sunday.

I know the history and likelihood of a team starting 0-2 and reaching the playoffs. For those needing a reminder, just 12.8% of clubs beginning a campaign with that record have made the postseason since 1990. I also think the Texans may have landed the worst draw of any NFL team in the first two weeks. Houston competed with both Buffalo and Cincinnati, and C.J. Stroud looks more like the quarterback we’ve hoped to see. That said, you can't score six points and expect to win.

After a frustrating open to the season, Dallas rediscovered its offensive form against Washington on Sunday. Dak Prescott displayed the same pinpoint passing and fantastic rapport with CeeDee Lamb that helped propel the Cowboys to a No. 2 finish in yards per game last season. There are still concerns about the defense, but for now, the 'Boys will enjoy their first win of 2026.

Dallas Cowboys move up 5 spots to No. 12 in Week 3 of Power Rankings.

Detroit’s offense is capable of hanging with most everyone. The opposite is true of the Lions’ defense, at least until the unit gets healthier in the back end. With a leaky secondary existing as a major liability, the Lions can’t realistically expect to beat the best, but their fight (and Jared Goff’s second-half performance) in Thursday night’s loss to Buffalo shows they’re still an above-average team with clear flaws.

The Saints are a sneaky-tough bunch. New Orleans’ defense managed to limit Baltimore’s powerful offense after getting punched in the mouth early on Sunday. Tyler Shough once again showcased his clutch gene, propelling a 21-3 run to close the game, with the second-year signal-caller taking a brutal hit at the goal line on his fourth-down sneak to score the game-winning touchdown. He’s tough as nails and fits perfectly as the face of the upstart Saints.

Gregg Rosenthal and Jourdan Rodrigue recap the Week 2 matchup between the Saints and the Ravens.

A Carson Wentz-led offense is never going to strike fear into the hearts of opponents, but Brian Flores’ defense sure does. For a second straight week, Flores’ blitz-happy unit carried Minnesota to victory, wreaking havoc in rainy Chicago and forcing timely takeaways that allowed the Vikings to win one of the ugliest games we’ll see all season. As the saying goes, defense wins championships. And until a team solves Flores’ scheme, Minnesota will command respect on a weekly basis.

The NFL’s highest-scoring offense in Week 1 finished with just three points in Sunday's rain-soaked loss to the Vikings. The Bears' Week 2 experience was a nightmare that became even worse when they lost Caleb Williams to a hamstring injury. It sounds like the star quarterback avoided total disaster, but as we saw in the final minutes of Sunday’s game, if Caleb isn’t available or is physically limited, the Bears’ ceiling plunges.

Call it a fortunate draw if you must, but the Panthers still had to play quality football against a division rival in Atlanta to emerge victorious on Sunday. They did that and then some, enjoying significant contributions from key defensive additions (including one of the best days you'll ever see from a linebacker in Devin Lloyd) while Bryce Young continued to perform at a remarkably encouraging level. The Panthers won’t play Cooper Rush and the Falcons every week, but they certainly took care of business in Week 2.

It’s strange to have the Patriots stuck in the bottom half of the rankings after a win, but it’s also jarring to see New England’s offense struggle to find a consistent pulse. Drake Maye isn’t playing as poorly as his stat line suggests and still dropped a few dimes against the Steelers, but he’s also not operating comfortably in the offense, which lost right guard Mike Onwenu to an ankle injury on Sunday. If Maye’s not right, Sundays are going to be a chore for this group until he improves. The defense deserves a hat tip, though; it reduced Pittsburgh's offense to rubble in Week 2.

Through two weeks, we’ve certainly learned the Colts have problems defensively. We also know Jonathan Taylor remains an incredibly effective playmaker, and Daniel Jones has provided enough good tape for us to believe they’re fine at quarterback. Receiver, however, is a massive concern, especially with Alec Pierce (heel) out for several weeks. And while the Colts deserve credit for taking the Chiefs to overtime, Indianapolis' clear weaknesses are likely to bog down the team throughout the season.

The Packers still lack a running game and are a sloppy operation. On Sunday, Green Bay lost 133 yards on 14 penalties and finished with 199 total net yards. Somehow, the Pack still managed to take the Jets to overtime, where Green Bay forced a three-and-out and finished off New York with a walk-off field goal. The most honest Packers fans will admit Green Bay didn’t play a winning brand of football, which is a highly concerning sign for a team expected to compete for the NFC North crown. Congratulations on the win, though.

The good vibes that followed the Giants from their Week 1 win over the Cowboys dissipated quickly on Monday night in Inglewood. Jaxson Dart’s early exit due to a knee injury took the air out of New York’s offense, and the defense's inability to contain Davante Adams -- in a game in which the Rams did not have Puka Nacua -- reflected poorly on a unit that kept Dallas in check a week earlier. This is a week-to-week league, and Giants fans were reminded of that on Monday.

The Raiders are 2-0! They aren’t anywhere near perfection, but they’re undefeated after two weeks and winning in new coach Klint Kubiak’s intended fashion: playing opportunistic, scrappy defense and capitalizing on opponents' mistakes. The best part: They’re doing it with names you might have never heard before. Cody White scored two receiving touchdowns on Sunday. Hezekiah Masses picked off Justin Herbert twice. Furthermore, Kirk Cousins is out there slinging the pill with no fear! I’m not saying the Raiders are going to the playoffs, but they’re certainly fun to watch.

In the second half against the Buccaneers, the Browns enjoyed adequate quarterback play, experienced the thrill of a long, game-tying touchdown pass and capped off a methodical scoring drive with a go-ahead TD on a well-designed and properly executed play. The defense showed up, too, after getting torched in Week 1. I know it must have seemed rather foreign to Browns fans, but on Sunday in Tampa, Cleveland was the better team. We’ll see if this lasts for more than a week.

A crisis of confidence is bubbling beneath the Buccaneers offense, a unit that lacks an identity and is led by a quarterback who looks uncomfortable operating in new coordinator Zac Robinson’s scheme. Baker Mayfield has been bad for two straight weeks, and the defense isn’t living up to the hype. The Bucs should be embarrassed by their loss to the Browns. We’ll see if it spurs improvements or leads to more of the same.

Gregg Rosenthal and Jourdan Rodrigue recap the Week 2 matchup between the Browns and the Buccaneers.

I want to believe in the Jets. I love watching Geno Smith run the offense, and it’s truly enjoyable to see New York compete like a legitimate NFL team. That was the case for much of Sunday, but the franchise's lack of recent success was apparent with Aaron Glenn's questionable clock management late in the game. The Jets failed to put away a Packers team that had no business fighting its way back for an overtime win. New York still needs to learn how to close.

It’s difficult to reconcile the dichotomy that currently defines the Steelers. Their defensive front is one of the most complete in football and has caused major issues for the first two offenses it has faced, yet their offense remains abysmal. Mike McCarthy’s arrival permitted Aaron Rodgers to play more aggressively, but Pittsburgh's offensive line has regressed, the running game is nonexistent and DK Metcalf has been one of the least reliable receivers in football through two weeks. Add in Rodgers’ age and general lack of mobility under pressure (SEE: his two fumbles in Sunday’s defeat), and you have an inept unit. It’s unfair to ask the defense to carry the Steelers on a weekly basis.

It’s not a huge jump for the Titans, ascending to No. 27, but they deserve a ton of credit for battling with the Eagles to the final seconds of Sunday’s loss. Tennessee looked lifeless offensively in Week 1 and appeared destined to wander aimlessly into the abyss this season, but Robert Saleh’s team found a pulse in Week 2 and nearly pulled off a massive upset, riding a high effort level and timely defensive plays to a respectable performance. The Titans are not nearly as bad as they looked in the opener.

It’s fair for Commanders fans to ask the football gods why they deserve such punishment. Jayden Daniels’ latest elbow injury throws Washington back into a familiar place of doom and gloom, which is especially disappointing when considering how hard the rebuilt defense has played for Dan Quinn through two weeks.

I find it hard to believe that, after two weeks, the Chargers are here, below the Browns, Jets, Titans and Commanders. And yet, they haven’t played better than any of those teams to this point. Justin Herbert still rips a few beautiful throws per game, but there’s no telling whether his teammate on the receiving end will actually hang on to the ball. The mistakes and sloppiness on both sides -- pre-snap penalties, dropped passes, fumbles, blown coverages -- are too frequent, and this team looks nothing like one coached by a Harbaugh. Los Angeles was in position to win Sunday, and ended up losing by 12 to a Las Vegas team that won three games last year. There’s plenty to correct and a brutal schedule ahead for the Bolts, whose 0-2 start might only be the beginning of their problems.

The good vibes from a Week 1 upset on the road vanished when the reigning champs came to town and suffocated the Cardinals’ offense, limiting Arizona to 151 yards in a lopsided game. The Cards didn’t put up much of a fight one week after battling to a victory.

The nightmarish quarterback situation in Atlanta has handcuffed the entire operation and dropped the Falcons into laughingstock territory. The hope is that Michael Penix Jr. can improve the offense (and team) in his return to action on Thursday night, but after what we’ve seen in these first two games, I’m not sure Penix will be able to lift the Falcons enough to be anything more than somewhat respectable.

The Dolphins' margin for error is so slim. Consider Sunday’s game against the 49ers, in which 21 of Miami’s first 26 offensive snaps occurred in San Francisco territory. Typically, that kind of game script produces an early lead, but the Fins put just three points on the board in that span. They have to be nearly perfect to compete with upper-tier teams like the Niners. If they don’t perform at that level, the low-ceiling offense is simply going to fall short of keeping up with more talented opponents.
