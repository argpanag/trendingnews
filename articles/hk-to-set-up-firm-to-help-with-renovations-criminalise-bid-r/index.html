HK to set up firm to help with renovations, criminalise bid-rigging

TheTools · 29.10.2026 10:30 · source · permalink

The Hong Kong government has announced plans to set up a company to provide property owners with building renovation support, as part of reforms introduced following the release of an investigation report into the deadly Tai Po fire.

Leading a press conference with the city’s top officials on Thursday, Chief Executive John Lee said authorities would establish a “building management support bureau.”

Financial Secretary Paul Chan said authorities were recruiting a chief executive and other personnel for the body, which will be a non-statutory firm, with the aim of registering the company by the end of the year.

The company will be held by the Financial Secretary Incorporated, a corporation that owns companies set up to handle government projects.

“The… bureau will, in accordance with the government’s strategies, formulate and update building renovation measures, and will offer property owners all-new building renovation services at a cost,” Chan said in Cantonese.

The government will inject HK$300 million into the company. HK$100 million will be initial start-up funding, while HK$200 million will be used to provide subsidies for property owners using its services, the finance chief added.

The company will take on some of the work of the Urban Renewal Authority, a statutory body set up by the government that also assists building owners with tenders, repairs and other services.

Bid-rigging criminalised

Thursday’s press conference came as a report from a government-appointed independent committee was released following months of hearings, during which officials from various departments testified about fire safety and renovation matters linked to the Wang Fuk Court blaze.

The housing estate was undergoing large-scale renovation work when the fire, which claimed 168 lives, broke out.

The public hearings earlier this year found that substandard scaffolding nets were used at the site, which caused the rapid spread of the fire, and pointed to potential bid-rigging, links to competing contractors, and “rubber-stamp inspections.”

Chief Secretary Eric Chan said on Thursday that authorities would propose criminalising bid-rigging under the Competition Ordinance. Offenders would face maximum penalties of 10 years’ imprisonment, or a fine of HK$10 million.

The fine can also be specific to the offence, at a maximum of three times the gains from the crime or 50 per cent of the annual revenue earned for each year of the offence.

Currently, bid-rigging is only a civil offence under the Competition Ordinance. Chan said that in cases where there may not be enough evidence for a criminal prosecution, the civil route would remain, with penalties proposed to be increased to a maximum fine of HK$8 million, up to two times the gains from the offence, or up to 30 per cent of annual revenue for a cap of three years of the offence.

Secretary for Security Chris Tang said authorities were setting up a joint task force, which he would lead, to tackle bid-rigging.

“The report showed that there are people with suspect backgrounds, including triad members, who manipulated tender results and conspired with consultants, professionals and contractors,” he said in Cantonese.

The task force will include officers from the police’s Organised Crime and Triad Bureau, as well as representatives from the Independent Commission Against Corruption and the Competition Commission.

Authorities are also mulling the addition of bid-rigging to the list of specified offences under the Organised and Serious Crimes Ordinance, Tang said, which would give law enforcement powers related to the freezing of assets and demanding certain information.
