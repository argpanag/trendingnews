Magic learn more about Paolo Banchero’s right ankle injury as team hits road

TheTools · 24.10.2026 16:45 · source · permalink

Breaking News

Magic forward Paolo Banchero believed he avoided a major injury during Friday’s game against the Timberwolves, but further testing revealed more information about his sore right ankle.

An MRI confirmed he suffered a low-grade right high ankle sprain, the Orlando Sentinel confirmed Saturday.

Banchero, who recorded 14 points, six rebounds and five assists during Orlando’s 115-93 loss to Minnesota at Kia Center, won’t be available when the Magic travel to New York on Sunday, and he’ll continue to undergo further testing and evaluation, the Sentinel was told.

While an official timeline wasn’t shared by the team when it confirmed the injury Saturday, most athletes who sustain a high ankle sprain often take 4-6 weeks to return to play, depending upon the severity of the injury, according to Washington University Orthopedics. The Magic said Banchero’s status will be updated as appropriate.

Prior to undergoing further testing, Banchero spoke to the Sentinel in the locker room following Friday’s loss about the injury while mentioning his past experience with ankle sprains.

“It was a play in the first quarter where Desmond (Bane) fell into me, kind of rolled up on my ankle and tweaked it to the inside,” Banchero said. “And then I kept playing. And then I came out of halftime and there was that play in the third where I jumped, felt it, got up, asked to come out, came back in, and then felt it again. And I was just like, ‘Yeah, I’m not going to push it any farther.’ But I feel pretty confident it’s nothing too major. I think I just tweaked it to the inside.”

“Most of all my ankle sprains have always been to the outside, so this is kind of foreign to me,” Banchero added. “So a little scary in that way. But doctors looked at it. Nothing’s structurally wrong. I can like move and walk fine. But definitely going to go get it looked at just to make sure everything’s good structurally. But I’m pretty confident that it’s not anything major or serious.”

Banchero’s injury is just the latest for a Magic team that’s already been without Anthony Black (right ankle sprain) and Noah Penda (partially torn right hamstring) to start the season. Orlando has also been without Jonathan Isaac (left knee injury management) and rookie Izaiyah Nelson (left ankle injury recovery).

Of the bunch, it’s Black who appears closest to a return. The Magic upgraded him to questionable on the team’s initial injury report Saturday afternoon for Sunday’s game against the Knicks. In addition to Banchero, Orlando ruled out Isaac, Penda and Nelson.

Following an 0-2 start with losses at home to Atlanta and Minnesota, the Magic hit the road for a three-game trip that starts at Madison Square Garden and ends with two games in three nights Wednesday and Friday at Toronto.

“You can’t overreact,” Banchero said about the slow start. “Obviously two frustrating losses, but it’s a long season. So, you can’t overreact. Same way if we would be 2-0, you know? You can’t overreact to two games.”

Other 0-2 NBA teams as of Friday night included Sunday’s opponent, the defending champion Knicks, as well as the Heat, Spurs, Warriors, Kings, Bucks and the Pelicans.

In order for the Magic to find a win on the road, they’ll need to be far more efficient from 3-point range, take better care of the ball and clean up the glass.

The Timberwolves on Friday night held a 22-8 advantage on offensive rebounds and 17-6 edge in second-chance points. As Minnesota shot 38% from beyond the arc (14-for-37), Orlando ended at 21% from distance (8-for-39).

“Yeah I think (it was) kind of a weird game,” Banchero said. “We did a good job on their stars, but I think the other guys killed us on the glass and second-chance opportunities. I think they got up a lot more shots than we did, which just gives them a lot more opportunity to score the ball.”

“And I just think they out hustled us,” he added. “It’s on me and the rest of the guys to play harder and to limit them on the offensive glass.”

Magic coach Sean Sweeney shared his perspective about Minnesota’s ability to crash the glass, an effort that was led by Timberwolves forward Joan Beringer, who grabbed seven offensive boards.

“They had multiple guys with three or more,” Sweeney said. “So, it’s probably a collective effort there in terms of going to get the ball. But we’ve got to hit guys a little bit more, too.”

Banchero shared Orlando’s mindset heading on the road.

“Just got to get a win, get on the right track,” he said. “We’ve got a tough road trip so we’ve just got to stick together and get some wins.”
