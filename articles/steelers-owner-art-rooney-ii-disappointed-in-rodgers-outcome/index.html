Steelers owner Art Rooney II 'disappointed' in Rodgers outcome, backs McCarthy

TheTools · 27.11.2026 18:02 · source · permalink

PITTSBURGH -- A day after Aaron Rodgers unleashed a firestorm of accusations about Mike McCarthy's fitness to coach, Pittsburgh Steelers owner and president Art Rooney II said in a statement to ESPN on Thursday that he has "100% confidence in Mike McCarthy" while also telling a newspaper that he supported moving on from Rodgers.

Rooney told the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette that "insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization. When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that."

The stunning decision to bench and release Rodgers was prompted in part by the quarterback's demands to make changes to the playcalling structure.

Rooney told the Post-Gazette that he "expects" McCarthy to "be our coach this season, next season and beyond." He reiterated that sentiment to ESPN.

"I am disappointed in where this went with Aaron Rodgers, but I have 100% confidence in Mike McCarthy," Rooney said in his statement. "And I have tremendous confidence in Mason Rudolph and the other players in our locker room.

"Decisions were made on what we believe is in the best interest of the Pittsburgh Steelers. Our main focus right now is preparing for tomorrow's game."

Rodgers made a litany of accusations in a 34-minute interview on "The Pat McAfee Show" on Wednesday that included McCarthy sleeping in meetings, missing meetings and breaking down a huddle at halftime of last week's loss to the Philadelphia Eagles with "1, 2, 3, Packers" -- an incident McCarthy said was an "embarrassing moment."

Rooney told the Post-Gazette he doesn't have any concerns about McCarthy's health.

"Mike's as healthy as the rest of us," Rooney said. "Health was not an issue. I'm not sure where that part comes."

In Wednesday's interview, Rodgers said he found out from teammate Pat Freiermuth that he was being benched in favor of Rudolph and would be inactive for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos and hadn't heard the news from McCarthy.

McCarthy responded to the allegations when he met with the media later Wednesday, saying the Steelers front office was "very in tune with everything that goes on in football operations as far as my job responsibility."

Rooney said in his statement to ESPN that he has "responsibility for ensuring the team and entire organization meets our standards."

The Steelers waived Rodgers shortly after the interview Wednesday. He officially cleared waivers Thursday, making him a free agent.
