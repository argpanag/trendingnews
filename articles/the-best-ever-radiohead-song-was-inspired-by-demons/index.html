The 'best ever' Radiohead song was inspired by demons

TheTools · 16.09.2026 16:05 · source · permalink

Jack Black did a pretty good job at summing up the versatility present within the music of Radiohead. 

“If you want concept,” he said, “You go OK Computer. But if you wanna rock — if you want straight-up fuckin’ songs — you go The Bends. The first few listens, I was like, ‘I don’t understand…. My brain’s not computing….’ Then it clicked in: ‘Ohhhh, I see! It’s the best band in the history of rock!‘”

He only cites two albums from the band, but in his brief praise, he surmises why people are so drawn to the band. These aren’t a group who have ever been stagnant in their creativity, as with every album they have always tried something new and exciting. That means changing up genres, becoming more experimental, or weaving narratives and concepts throughout their records.

With such a diverse catalogue of music, choosing the best songs that the band ever made is a near impossible task, but it’s one that Far Out was pretty happy to take on. Making the cut were the likes of ‘How To Disappear Completely’, ‘Kid A’ and ‘Subterranean Homesick Alien’; however, the track that we crowned as the greatest that Radiohead have ever put out was ‘Paranoid Android’. 

Leaning on the conceptual side of the band that Black previously cited, ‘Paranoid Android’ is a great window into the layered structure that is OK Computer. It’s a six-and-a-half minute journey through an interrogation on modern living. Throughout the song, Thom Yorke is essentially able to give you feelings of anxiety and loneliness, despite being in a flustering environment. The worry on this song is palpable, and it highlights just how good Radiohead are at instilling feeling into both their songs and listeners. 

So, what inspired this track? Well, that anxiety you pick up on throughout the song is very much a reflection of the circumstances which gave rise to ‘Paranoid Android’. One night in Los Angeles, Radiohead lead singer Thom Yorke decided to go on a bar crawl, and in doing so, he found himself in the middle of a group of fans, bar-goers, coke users, and all things in between, each of which wanted to talk to him. When he got back to his bed that night, the anxiety he felt within those different bars really started to wear him down, as he found himself in the negativity surrounding the whole event.

His sleep was haunted by images of those people, and those people were now presenting themselves as demons. “The people I saw that night were just like demons from another planet,” he said, “Everyone was trying to get something out of me. I felt like my own self was collapsing in the presence of it, but I also felt completely, utterly part of it, like it was all going to come crashing down any minute.”

‘Paranoid Android’ presents the paranoia that Yorke felt within that moment over music which accentuates that sense of hopelessness. The words and the instrumentation go so well together that it’s hard to believe they weren’t created at the same time. Yorke said that they had had the sound for a long time, but he just didn’t know what the track was about. It wasn’t until he went to those bars and was exposed to those demons that the meaning of ‘Paranoid Android’ was clear.

“We’d been rehearsing the song for months, but the lyrics came to me at five o’clock that morning,” said Yorke, “I was trying to sleep when I literally heard these voices that wouldn’t leave me alone. They were the voices of the people I’d heard in the bar. It turned out to be a notorious, coke-fiend place, but I didn’t know that. Basically it’s just about chaos, chaos, utter f–king chaos.”
