Apple Owes $5.7 Billion for Infringement of Haptics Patents (2)

TheTools · 25.09.2026 23:30 · source · permalink

Apple Inc. owes over $5.7 billion for infringing patents belonging to Taction Technology Inc. that cover vibration-based technology incorporated in models of iPhones and Apple Watches, a federal jury in San Diego found Friday.

Jurors in the US District Court for the Southern District of California spent parts of two days before determining that Apple infringed claims from two Taction haptics patents — US Patent Nos. 10,659,885 and 10,820,117. They determined, however, that the infringement wasn’t willful.

The suit targeted Apple’s Taptic Engine, which the company says provides users of it products with tactile feedback to simulate actions, like clicks.

“We’re happy the jury found for Taction and vindicated its patent rights,” said Taction’s lawyer, Tigran Guledjian of Quinn Emanuel. “Taction waited five and a half years for this case to get to trial, so it was a long time coming.”

In a statement, Apple thanked the jury but said it strongly disagreed with “today’s verdict and the damages awarded, which are entirely unsupported by the facts.”

The company said the “Taptic Engine is fundamentally different from Taction’s technology, which Taction’s own testing of Apple’s products confirmed during trial.”

“Apple does not use Taction’s technology, and we will appeal,” it said.

Discovery in the case revealed that Taction received financing for the case from Gronostaj Investments LLC and Kenosha Investments LP, which was identified in separate litigation as an indirect subsidiary of Burford Capital Ltd.

The litigation has been fierce, including Apple’s efforts to shine a light on Taction’s connections to Burford.

Taction sued Apple in 2021 for infringing the patents, which both describe “tactile transducers that produce bass frequency vibrations” perceptible by touch.

Apple initially convinced the judge to toss the case in 2023. But the US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit resurrected the suit last year, ruling that the dismissal turned on an unwritten requirement that improperly prevented Taction from using certain pieces of expert testimony.

Apple is represented by Fish & Richardson; Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe; Walker Stevens Cannom LLP; and Goldman Ismail Tomaselli Brennan & Baum LLP. Kenosha and Gronostaj are represented by Singer Cashman LLP.

The case is Taction Tech. Inc. v. Apple Inc., S.D. Cal., No. 3:21-cv-00812, verdict 9/25/26.
