Jetour launching hardcore SUV and ute!

TheTools · 26.11.2026 01:00 · source · permalink

China's Jetour has fired a warning shot at Australia's biggest automotive brands, confirming plans to launch seven new models by the end of 2028, including a hardcore off-road SUV, a dual-cab ute and a fully electric compact SUV.

The Chery-owned brand officially unveiled its Australian operation in Sydney, confirming it would launch in March 2027 with two SUVs, the Jetour 10 and Jetour 20, known internationally as the T1 and T2.

Both are SUVs with an off-road bent, with the 10 arriving with a choice of ICE or PHEV powertrains, and the bigger, tougher-looking 20 arriving as a punchy PHEV producing as much as 445kW in total.

But that's just the beginning, with the brand confirming its Toyota LandCruiser-rivalling Jetour 700 and its dual-cab ute sibling will arrive in the final quarter of 2027, with another three models to follow in 2028.

Jetour Australia Managing Director Johnny Fang also revealed the brand would target a price window of between $50,000 and $100,000 across its entire model range, giving some indication of where its incoming vehicles will be positioned.

"In the fourth quarter of 2027, we plan to introduce (the) Jetour 700 and Jetour 700 pick-up," he said.

"These two models will take us into a premium end of our range and give our dealers an opportunity to serve a totally different group of buyers."

The pick-up, known internationally as the 700P, will give Jetour a challenger to the likes of the Ford Ranger, Toyota HiLux and BYD Shark 6, though it is understood to be a targeting a more premium space than any of them. The 700 large SUV will take aim at models like Toyota LandCruiser, Nissan Patrol and Lexus LX.

The 700 family pack a PHEV powertrain that pairs a 2.0-litre turbo-petrol engine and dual electric motors to produce 665kW and 1135Nm.

Fang confirmed three further vehicles would follow in 2028, including a compact battery-electric SUV, although he stopped short of naming the other two models.

Jetour says its Australian launch has been in the works since June 2025, when it began researching local buyers and their vehicle requirements.

The company established its Australian subsidiary in March 2026, began testing vehicles on local roads in June and started building its Australian team in August.

Fang described Australia as the brand's most important and demanding right-hand-drive market, with the local testing program designed to ensure its vehicles suit Australian conditions.

"A long stretch of highway, a suburban school road and a weekend away each ask different things from our vehicles," he said. "Our job is to bring what we learn on these roads to our engineers."

Jetour has also appointed its first group of Australian dealer partners, with 11 dealerships and dealer groups announced at the Sydney event.

The brand is also hoping to seperate itself from the growing number of Chinese manufacturers entering Australia through its 'Travel+' philosophy, which extends beyond the vehicles themselves to include accessories, off-road experiences and owner communities.

Peter Warren Automotive CEO Andrew Doyle, whose business will sell Jetour vehicles in Australia, said that positioning could help the newcomer stand out in an increasingly crowded market.

"I think it will give people a reason to choose a Jetour beyond the price," Doyle said.

"We see room in Australia for an SUV that offers, obviously, value and is built around how people live and want to use their cars."

Jetour, which was established in 2018, says it has already sold more than 2.6 million vehicles globally in more than 100 countries.

Australian pricing and specifications for the Jetour 10 and Jetour 20 are expected closer to their March launch.
