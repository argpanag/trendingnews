Promising football player from Halifax dies at U.S. boarding school

TheTools · 14.11.2026 21:12 · source · permalink

Marcellus Young Casario was a recruit for the NCAA No.1-ranked Georgia Bulldogs

A promising young football player from Halifax died following a medical emergency on Friday, announced a prominent boarding school in Georgia.

Marcellus Young Casario, 17, was a six-foot-six, 260-pound defensive lineman at Rabun Gap-Nacoochee School, an independent boarding and day school two hours outside of Atlanta.

Young Casario quickly became a cornerstone of the school’s community and distinguished himself with strong faith, was a natural leader, loyal friend, and standout student-athlete, according to a Rabun Gap statement on Saturday.

“Our entire community is heartbroken at the loss of Marcellus Young Casario. Marcellus was a beloved boarding student and tragically passed away yesterday after a sudden medical emergency. Known affectionately as Mar by his coaches, friends, and teammates, he was loved across campus,” reads the school’s statement on Saturday.

“Guided by his strong faith in God, Mar was an active member of our campus Christian Student Initiative and lived out his faith by encouraging others. His life was anchored by the scripture he shared on his Instagram, Psalm 73:26: ‘My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.'”

Star player committed to top NCAA team

In June, Young Casario committed to the Georgia Bulldogs, the No. 1-ranked football team in the NCAA.

“Mar was excited to see his longtime dream of becoming a Division I college football player come true. He was looking forward to graduating from Rabun Gap this December and enrolling at UGA in January. His passion for football began at an early age, inspired by his father and grandfather, who helped foster his love for the game,” read the school’s statement.

Rabun Gap head coach Derek White said Young Casario was everything a coach could want in a player.

“Mar truly embodied our mission to ‘love one another.’ He was known for his relentless dedication, and his journey as a student-athlete was marked by hard work and a true brotherhood with his teammates,” said White.

Heartfelt tribute from teammate

Rabun Gap teammate Jay Schell paid tribute to Young Casario in a social media post on X.

“This (Friday) morning I lost my brother unexpectedly. I am at a loss for words. He was such a great teammate and friend to me,” said Schell. “He had such a bright future ahead of him at UGA. I know for a fact he is with the Lord today.

“It’s so eye-opening for someone to be with you one second and gone the next. Please pray for his family. You don’t really understand what you have until it’s gone. I will always miss and love my friend, brother, and teammate. Every time I step on the field from this day forward it will be in honor of my brother and great friend Mar. Love you, man!”

‘Tremendous void’ in the community

Bob Ahrens, Rabun Gap’s Head of School, said the loss of Young Casario is a “tremendous void” in the close-knit community.

“Mar brought light, faith, and character to everything he did,” said Ahrens. “Our community is mourning, and our hearts and prayers are with his family in Halifax, his teammates, and his friends as we navigate this unimaginable loss together.”

The cause of death is unknown at this time.

Young Casario left Nova Scotia at 14 for St. Andrew’s College, a boarding school in Aurora, Ontario, then moved to the United States to play high school football.

According to multiple reports, Young Casario was rated as the No. 70 defensive lineman in the United States in the recently released 247Sports Composite rankings.

Young Casario had 14 tackles, three sacks, eight quarterback pressures, three pass deflections and a fumble recovery for Rabun Gap this season.

The playoff game on Friday night between Rabun Gap and Cannon School was postponed.
