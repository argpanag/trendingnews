With both firms being chip companies, their narrative is similar on a fundamental level but differs in the specifics. Starting from Intel, the debate is about whether the firm will be able to carve out a larger portion of the AI pie for itself, given that its strengths lie with CPUs as opposed to GPUs, which are powering the current AI wave. Additionally, it also depends on the firm's foundry business, which is aiming to compete with Taiwan's TSMC in the global contract chip manufacturing industry.On the AI front, Intel's second quarter came with good news as the firm's data center and AI business (DCAI) which captures the impact of CPU sales to data centers, saw revenue jump by 59% annually to touch $6.3 billion. Additionally, the foundry business's revenue jumped by 31% to $5.8 billion to indicate that the firm was firing well on both fronts.Yet, the chip business is quite capital intensive and Intel has to invest if it's to carve out a space for itself in the industry. The impact of focusing on the growth is clear as well, since first half capex sat at $6.1 billion while management outlined that full year capex would sit at $20 billion. Not to mention, foundry operating losses were $2 billion and the firm recorded a $11 billion GAAP net loss in the quarter primarily due to a $12.53 billion non-cash mark-to-market charge tied to escrowed shares under the U.S. CHIPS Act agreement.Looking at Micron, while for Intel the debate is about the role of CPUs in the agentic AI space, for Micron, it's about the sustainability of demand and the potential of high prices to remain as mega players SK hynix and Samsung bring additional capacity online and China's CXMT pushes to produce more memory chips. Cramer's tweet about Micron is unsurprising, as he had commented in August that US would "rue" the day it let it in the markets. (NASDAQ:SKHY) In.Starting from the bullish view, Micron's core data center revenue surged by 10 times to touch $18 billin in its fiscal Q4, gross margin touched 87% and management outlined that 75% of fiscal year 2027 were already committed. The third bit is particularly relevant, as it raises the chance of order shifts if Micron is unable to bring additional capacity online - a costly endeavor which could lead to tailwinds in case of a memory price downturn. FY26 capex sat at $27 billion while the shares are down by 27% since their June peak.As for the valuation, Intel's forward P/E of 63 over Micron's 7 means that the former is very richly valued. As for Intel, the picture is opposite as aggressive growth over little immediate earnings output is priced in.
