How a 1994 Seinfeld Episode Comically Disrupted the Macy's Thanksgiving Parade

TheTools · 25.11.2026 12:22 · source · permalink

With the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade celebrating its 100th edition this year, I thought it was the perfect time to revisit one of the funniest fictional mishaps involving the beloved holiday tradition.

On Nov. 17, 1994, NBC aired a Thanksgiving-themed episode of Seinfeld that brought together cowboy boots, a celebrity-owned car, a radio contest and a giant Woody Woodpecker balloon—with disastrous results.

The Season 6 episode, titled "The Mom & Pop Store," remains an alternative to more traditional Thanksgiving television specials. It also features two familiar Hollywood faces: Bryan Cranston and Jon Voight.

How Seinfeld Turned the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade into a Comedy of Errors

In the episode, Elaine (Julia Louis-Dreyfus) helps her boss, Mr. Pitt (Ian Abercrombie), fulfill a childhood dream of participating in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade.

Thanks to her impressive knowledge of big band music, Elaine wins a radio contest that allows Mr. Pitt to become one of the handlers of the giant Woody Woodpecker balloon.

Meanwhile, Jerry (Jerry Seinfeld) finds himself stuck wearing cowboy boots after Kramer (Michael Richards) takes his sneakers to a neighborhood shoe repair shop. An unfortunate fall leaves Jerry with a dental problem, prompting him to attend a holiday party hosted by dentist Tim Whatley (Cranston).

In Seinfeld fashion, the seemingly unrelated storylines collide. While getting his injured tooth examined at the party, Jerry accidentally knocks a miniature Empire State Building trophy out of a window, puncturing the Woody Woodpecker balloon below.

Poor Mr. Pitt's long-awaited moment in the spotlight doesn't exactly go as planned. Jerry and Kramer later watch the balloon deflate on television as Mr. Pitt struggles to keep it afloat.

The Thanksgiving Episode Also Introduced a Future Emmy-Winning Star

The episode is notable for another reason that has nothing to do with Thanksgiving.

It marked Cranston's first appearance as dentist Tim Whatley, a recurring character who would appear throughout the sitcom's run. Years before becoming known for his celebrated performances in Malcolm in the Middle and Breaking Bad, Cranston was making audiences laugh as Jerry's dentist.

The episode also features an appearance by Oscar winner Voight, playing himself in a memorable subplot involving George Costanza (Jason Alexander) and a used convertible.

More than three decades later, "The Mom & Pop Store" is a reminder of what made Seinfeld so entertaining: its ability to turn everyday inconveniences—and even one of America's most cherished holiday traditions—into wonderfully absurd comedy.
