'Grave concern' – Jude Dobson and neighbours fight proposed aged-care home on their street

TheTools · 23.09.2026 23:33 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

In this edition of Property Insider: neighbours including Jude Dobson oppose a developer’s aged-care project on their Mt Eden street; Auckland Transport’s big lease deal; Albert St’s Barclay Suites management changes; the country’s biggest electrical wholesaler celebrates 75 years in business; and there is opposition to Calder Stewart’s plans at Hornby.

Writer, researcher, presenter and director Jude Dobson is among a group of residents fighting a planned three-level aged-care home in her upmarket street on a rise in Auckland’s Mt Eden.

Dobson and her husband Graeme Dobson, with other neighbours, are expressing concern about the effects of a 29-person home at 19 Landscape Rd being proposed by Eddie Wang’s Wang Commercial.

“As immediately adjoining neighbours, we would like to express our grave concern with the development, both with respect to neighbouring residential character, as well as the actual and potential effect on the residential amenity of immediately adjoining properties,” their letter to Auckland Council said.

Demolishing an old home there would erode street character and the design, bulk and form of the new building was completely out of keeping with the area, the letter said.

Traffic effects, visual dominance, shading and impact on adjoining properties were other negatives cited.

The submission claims two neighbours were not told what was really planned. One of the two was the Dobsons.

“The applicant has deliberately kept his intended plan of a supported residential care facility from these two neighbours,” the submission said.

Graeme Dobson said the neighbours were hiring a lawyer.

“This area has a character overlay under the Auckland Unitary Plan so it is restricted more than your average leafy street in the suburbs,” he said.

The Dobsons wrote the letter of opposition with neighbours Peggy Lowe, Bonnie and Timothy Heath, Grant and Rhona Simmons and Derek and Judy Jones.

Asked by the Herald about the dispute on Friday, Dobson said she was clearing airport customs and was reluctant to speak.

Asked if she had a lawyer, she said she had “100%” got assistance to make her views known about the proposal.

“There’s a process to follow.”

Auckland Council has notified the application from Wang Commercial.

Wang wants consent to demolish an existing older home at 19 Landscape Rd.

It wants to develop a “purpose-built supported residential care facility for a maximum of 29 residents, five staff and one night nurse”.

People have until October 12 to submit on the plan, notified on a limited basis.

Big lease deal

Auckland Council and Auckland Transport have leased nine floors of 99 Albert St, which has just been sold by SkyCity Entertainment Group to Mainland Capital and Russell Property Group.

Anita Furniss, Auckland Council’s corporate support services general manager, announced the move.

“We have negotiated a very attractive 14-year deal at 99 Albert St which will give us greater certainty over accommodation costs, a good deal on operating costs and fitout and excellent proximity for our transport-focused staff,” she said in an email.

Financial modelling forecasts long-term cost avoidance of at least $14.2 million during 15 years, she said.

How Auckland Transport will get on with attempts to sub-lease space at its existing glass-clad headquarters is unknown.

Auckland Transport is now at 20 Viaduct Harbour Ave on the waterfront and its lease does not end until 2029.

“Managing this lease may include looking at subleasing options,” the statement issued this month said.

Barclay Suites changes

The Australian arm of the New York Stock Exchange-listed Travel + Leisure Co. has bought some units and the management rights to the 115-unit Barclay Suites at 70-74 Albert St, Auckland.

That is the eighth property the company has in New Zealand, a statement from international operations president and managing director Barry Robinson said.

“Auckland remains one of New Zealand’s most visited markets and a strategically important tourism destination. Recent data shows guest nights increased by 5% last year, while Auckland Airport welcomed 2.4 million international arrivals in the 12 months to March,” Robinson said.

Travel + Leisure Co. intends to refurbish the property as part of its repositioning and, upon completion, the apartments will join the Travel + Leisure Co. network of vacation club properties.

A spokesman in Australia said a multimillion-dollar refurbishment would see the Travel + Leisure Co. apartments redesigned.

A contemporary colour scheme, new appliances, furniture, beds, soft furnishings and electrical fixtures and fittings are planned.

Flooring will be modernised with wood panels in the living spaces and kitchens, while the bedrooms will be re-carpeted.

Storage improvements and bathroom upgrades will be made across the apartments. The project will also see the creation of a more friendly and inviting reception space, with a warm colour scheme, more open welcome desk space, brand new furniture and signage.

Bayleys said the property had been sold for $7.8m as a freehold going concern.

In 2024, the Herald reported how the City Rail Link was banned from closing a street outside Barclay Suites after management went to court to demand maintenance of guest and vehicle access so the business could function.

Barclay Management (2013) instructed Bronwyn Carruthers, KC, to take matters to court.

Judge Melinda Dickey said in the Environment Court she found that the City Rail Link’s consent or designation did not authorise it to shut that street.

“City Rail Link Limited is prohibited from closing the lower Albert St slip lane,” she ruled two years ago.

J.A. Russell celebrates 75 years

New Zealand’s biggest electrical wholesaler is the privately owned J.A. Russell, a national 80-branch chain.

It celebrated 75 years this month.

A party for about 90 people was held at the New Zealand International Convention Centre on Friday, September 11.

The business was founded in 1951 by Jack Spooner, Albert Hopkins and Russell Williamson. They combined their first names into J.A. Russell.

“Today, we have 80 branches across the country and operate under two brands: J.A. Russell and Radcliffe Electrical. We are the largest New Zealand-owned electrical wholesaler in the country,” the business said.

It said it employs more than 800 staff, including more than 100 sales reps on the road. “We are passionate about the electrical industry and sincerely value our relationships with both our customers and suppliers.”

Opposition to Calder Stewart

Calder Stewart’s plans to expand its logistics and warehouse holdings at Hornby, Christchurch aren’t delighting everyone.

Ben Niven and Moa Metals in the area said the company was trying to rezone all the residential land on Main South Road between the Islington Tavern and Marshes Rd to heavy industrial.

“These are all private properties that they are trying to rezone. This is so they can build heavy industrial buildings 20m behind our houses. It’s an attempt to muzzle us from complaining about noise/smell/terrible living conditions,” Niven said in an email.

Ben Stewart of Calder Stewart said PPC19 was a publicly notified Christchurch City Council planning process.

Residents have full rights to make submissions and appear at the hearing.

“Importantly, the inclusion of the privately owned Main South Road properties within the plan-change area was requested by the council, not by Calder Stewart. Calder Stewart owns only 10.6ha of the 23ha covered by the proposed change,” he said in an email.

The plan change did not transfer ownership, require anyone to sell or give Calder Stewart any rights over land it does not own, he said.

The proposed 20m building setback would remain until 2036, Stewart said.

But Niven was not placated.

“I’m against Calder Stewart trying to increase profits at the expense of working-class people’s dreams to own a quarter-acre section to live peacefully,” he said.
