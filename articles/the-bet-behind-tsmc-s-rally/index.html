The Bet Behind TSMC's Rally

If you only skimmed the headlines about Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing over the past year, the story might look straightforward. Investors who held Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing over the past year are up 56.3%, including dividends. Yet the news flow mixed record Q3 2026 profits and aggressive U.S. expansion plans with worries about Chinese competition, U.S. politics, and heavy AI spending. If you had to decide back in November 2025, what should those crosscurrents have told you to focus on?

This theme extends beyond Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing. See which of 53 AI infrastructure stocks may still merit a closer look.

The Two Stories Investors Had To Weigh On Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing

The shares cost NT$1,450 at the start of the period, which left Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing sitting between two very different stories about what came next.

On the optimistic side, one camp saw a Fair Value of NT$1,800, a price that would only make sense if AI demand, advanced packaging and rapid Arizona expansion supported stronger margins and long run revenue growth than consensus assumed.

The cautious view pointed to a Fair Value of NT$1,100, reflecting worries that overseas fabs, higher regulatory and sustainability costs, and customer concentration could steadily erode profitability and leave earnings more fragile.

What The Results Changed For Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing

The clearest data point was Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing’s Q3 2026 report. Revenue moved from NT$989,918.3m in Q3 2025 to NT$1,494,243.0m, while net income rose from NT$452,301.4m to NT$763,064.0m and net margin widened from 45.7% to 51.1%. Record profit tied to AI demand and higher capital plans for U.S. fabs supported the optimistic case, although overseas spending still kept the cautious story alive. Overall, the evidence cut both ways.

The hinge assumption here was whether AI driven demand and advanced nodes would outweigh margin pressure from global expansion. When you assess another chip producer, track both net margin and capital expenditure plans together to see if higher investment is being matched by stronger profitability or simply eating into returns.

What Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing’s Price Already Reflects

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing now trades at NT$2,170, after a 56.3% gain over the past year. The current Narrative’s Fair Value sits above that level, which reflects the author’s view rather than a confirmed truth about what the shares are worth.

According to steingar, an investor in the Simply Wall St Community who wrote the Narrative, the key debate is whether buyers today are fully crediting Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing for its role as critical digital infrastructure rather than a cyclical chip producer. The argument is that today’s price still does not credit the durability of advanced node leadership and associated cash generation.

"The market prices TSMC as a cyclical manufacturer with transient pricing power. The financial reality supports pricing it as irreplaceable infrastructure with compounding economics."

Where TSMC’s Demand Echoes Out

Watching Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing, you are really watching AI hunger for compute. That same appetite highlights a second, related story.

Chip designers focused on accelerated computing try to turn raw power into usable AI systems. They build platforms that combine processors, networking and software in one package.

Large cloud groups and AI specialists use those platforms to create AI factories. The aim is to move from single chips toward entire computing plants.

If that approach holds, your TSMC research may share space with another question. How far can AI spending stretch when clients buy whole factories instead of parts?
