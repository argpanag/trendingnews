Fantasy Football 2026 Sleeper WRs to Target in Updated Flex Rankings

TheTools · 07.09.2026 15:29 · source · permalink

Boom-or-bust outcomes are somewhat unavoidable when it comes to a sleeper receiver in fantasy football.

If they had a higher baseline, then they probably wouldn't qualify as sleepers. The same holds true if they didn't offer rather significant growth potential.

So, a bit of risk-taking is a must here, but that's true any time it comes to chasing potential. NFL teams don't stake a Super Bowl run around a solid and safe group of players, and fantasy managers would be similarly denied of their championship dreams if they took the same approach.

The risk factor comes from the fact these are largely dart throws, but the good news is those tosses shouldn't cost you a lot. To up your odds of striking a Bull's-eye, we'll spotlight two of our top sleeper targets at receiver after updating our flex rankings.

Josh Downs, WR, Indianapolis Colts

With the amount of sleeper hype surrounding Downs, you might think the helium would've floated him out of sleeper range by now. That just hasn't happened. His draft cost has climbed a tad (real-time ADP: 101.6, per FantasyPros), but nowhere near the point of significantly impacting your profit potential.

He has a history of making the most of his opportunities. Across his first three NFL seasons, 198 of his 293 targets were caught. The one inconsistency in his profile has been volume. It felt like a big target spike might be coming last season, and then he wound up seeing a career-low 88 targets in 16 contests.

Maybe we'll all be duped again, but it sure feels like all arrows are pointing toward that spike still coming—just a season later than expected. Michael Pittman Jr. is gone, Alec Pierce only recently returned to full-practice duties after offseason ankle surgery and Keenan Allen, a 34-year-old, only joined this team last month.

Downs has proved a potent option in the slot, but his downfield responsibilities could trend way up. And if they do, he could be remembered as one of the biggest fantasy steals of the season.

Deebo Samuel, WR, San Francisco 49ers

While the fantasy community is collectively (and understandably) fawning over 49ers rookie De'Zhaun Stribling, there hasn't been nearly enough digital ink spilled over Samuel's prospects upon his return to San Francisco.

The same availability concerns surrounding other aging or unavailable playmakers propping up Stribling should apply to Samuel, too. In fact, the veteran might be even better positioned to seize upon these chances, since he can slot both at receiver, where Ricky Pearsall is out, Christian Kirk is on injured reserve and Mike Evans is having trouble staying on the field, and at running back, where 30-year-old Christian McCaffrey is coming off of a 450-touch season.

The 49ers lost their yards-after-catch magic last season, which Samuel spent with the Washington Commanders. Just like the drafting of Stribling, the return of Samuel feels like a direct attempt to remedy this problem.

Throw in the fact that coach Kyle Shanahan has historically slow-played rookie receivers, and one could make the argument that Samuel's outlook is even more favorable than Stribling's. But since Samuel isn't being hyped the same way, he can be had much later in drafts (real-time ADP: 143.4 to Stribling's 102.3).
