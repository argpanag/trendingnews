Sen Chris Van Hollen (D-Md.) endorsed then-Prince George's County Executive Angela Alsobrooks (D) in 2023 in the Democratic primary for the U.S. Senate seat Alsobrooks now holds. (Photo by Danielle E. Gaines/Maryland Matters)

Maryland Democratic Sens. Angela Alsobrooks and Chris Van Hollen remain professional and cordial with one another, despite Alsobrooks’ announcement this week that she will support Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) over Van Hollen in the race to lead the Democrats in that chamber in January.

Not everyone is being as cordial.

Groups including Progressive Maryland, Charm City Indivisible and CAIR (Council on American-Islamic Relations) Action Maryland plan to hold rallies Monday afternoon outside of three Alsobrooks’ district offices in Prince George’s and Montgomery counties and in Baltimore City.

“Rally organizers say her endorsement should be guided by the needs and demands of Maryland voters – not agreements made in Washington,” according to a statement released Friday.

A representative with Alsobrooks’ office declined to comment Friday.

Since Alsobrooks’ went public Wednesday in backing Schumer, criticism of her decision has spread on social media. Some voiced their disdain at Elizabeth Booker Houston, a lawyer and one of the biggest Democratic influencers, when she said people who back Van Hollen (like her) need to stop spreading disinformation “about Black women in politics.”

“Even though I disagree with Sen. Alsbrooks and her support of Chuck Schumer, threatening and harassing her and her staff is not the way to go,” Booker Houston said in her Instagram post Thursday.

Van Hollen released a statement Thursday and said he respects Alsobrooks’ decision, and called for people to lower the temperature.

“These discussions among our party are critical to move our democracy and our caucus forward. And above all, they must be based in mutual respect and civility – there is no space in this discussion for vitriol or hostility,” Van Hollen’s statement said. “Regardless of the outcome, Senator Alsobrooks and I will continue to respect one another and work together on behalf of Marylanders every day.”

Clock’s ticking on transition

In a little more than two weeks, the state’s five new county executives will drop “-elect” from their titles at their swearings-in, which all take place on Dec. 7. Which means their transition teams, or new staff in some cases, have a lot to do and not much time to do it.

Montgomery County Executive-elect Will Jawando announced his transition team a day after polls closed, and we haven’t heard from Democratic Wicomico County Executive-elect Ernest Davis. But two others named their teams this week and one started naming her administration. All were a mix of current and former state and local officials, educators, community leaders and more.

Anne Arundel

County Executive-elect Allison Pickard has already started assembling her administration, naming Emily Hollis, who worked as deputy chief of staff in the comptroller’s office, as the county’s deputy chief administrative officer for government operations.

Jordan Bellamy, a former director of strategic communications with the Bellamy Genn Group and also worked as a lobbyist, will be Pickard’s director of communications and Lawrence Schultz will be the county’s new fire chief on Jan. 4, replacing Chief Trisha Wolford, who is leaving after eight years.

Pickard also named her chief of staff, Ben Smith, who chaired the public affairs practice at Funk and Bolton.

At least two Baltimore County government employees will move south: D’Andrea Walker as chief administrative officer, the role she had in Baltimore County; and Tucker Cavanagh as deputy chief administrative officer for land use and infrastructure. Cavanagh had a similar title for economic development and infrastructure in Baltimore County.

Current Anne Arundel County Police Chief Amal Awad will remain as head of the force.

Pickard said the appointees have “the range and depth of experience to hit the ground running on Day One. With a shared commitment to building a modern, responsive, and accountable County Government that delivers results, strengthens public services, and creates opportunities for residents in every community. We are ready to meet the moment.”

Baltimore County

County Executive-elect Julian Jones has three co-chairs for his transition team: Towson University President Mark Ginsberg; Craig Thompson, an attorney and chair of the Maryland Stadium Authority; and Sharonne Bonardi, executive director with the Federation of Tax Administrators. Freeman A. Hrabowski III, president emeritus of University of Maryland, Baltimore County, will serve as an honorary co-chair.

Topical groups will include Baltimore City State’s Attorney Ivan Bates leading a public safety committee and former Baltimore City Councilman Eric Costello, a vice chair on the Maryland Economic Development Corp., will lead a committee on technology and government innovation.

Former Maryland State Education Association President Cheryl Bost will lead a group dealing with education, while Stacey Rodgers, acting secretary for the Maryland Department of Human Services, will serve as leader on a committee focused on health and human services.

“The people of Baltimore County work hard for their families, and they deserve a government that works just as hard for them,” Jones wrote Monday on his X page. “That’s why I’ve invited these accomplished leaders to help lead my transition and inform how we deliver a standard of excellence in our public services.”

Howard County

Howard County Executive-elect Vanessa Atterbeary, the first Black woman elected to the position, will get some help from county government veterans as well as some folks from her three terms as a state delegate.

She tapped outgoing Howard County Council Chair Opel Jones and Ken Ulman, the former Howard County executive and former Maryland Democratic Party, as her transition team co-chairs. (It’s a busy time for Ulman, whom Gov. Wes Moore named chair of the Maryland Entertainment Industry Council last month.)

Sen. Guy Guzzone (D-Howard), the chair of the influential Senate Budget and Taxation Committee, will be an honorary co-chair. Guzzone and Ulman both served on outgoing Executive Calvin Ball’s transition team for his first term in 2018.

Committees include public safety, education and one labeled “students,” led by high school student Gavin Falcón. The environment committee will led by Environment Secretary Serena McIllwain, who is stepping down at the end of the year. A Planning and Infrastructure Committee will be led by Alex Hughes, then-chief of staff for former House Speakers Adrienne Jones and the late Michael Busch.

“Brainpower, strategic vision, a deep understanding of local and state government, strong relationships and a real commitment to getting things done is what this group brings to the table,” Atterbeary said in a statement.

New kids on the committee

As long as we’re looking at the calendar, the start of the 2027 General Assembly is a little more than seven weeks away and House and Senate leaders were busy this week setting up committees and filling leadership posts.

House Speaker Joseline Peña-Melnyk, who announced leadership posts and committee chairs Wednesday, continued that work Thursday with committee assignments for the incoming freshmen.Peña-Melnyk welcomed the new delegate, who she said “bring a wealth of experience, fresh perspectives, and a strong commitment to public service.”

“They are joining us at a consequential time for our state, with difficult decisions ahead and important work to be done,” she said in a statement released by her office. “I am confident that each of them will bring valuable insight and energy to the work ahead as we serve the people of Maryland,” Peña-Melnyk said.

The freshman assignments are:

The Appropriations Committee will get Dels.-elect Abena Affum-McAllister (D-Charles), Dan Duggan (R-Garrett and Allegany) and C.L. Marshall III (R-Lower Shore).

 Dels.-elect Spencer Dixon (D-Anne Arundel), Josh Hastings (D-Wicomico), Molly McKee-Seabrook (D-Prince George’s) and Matt Post (D-Montgomery) landed on the Economic Matters Committee.

The Environment and Transportation Committee will get Dels.-elect Chanel Branch (D-Baltimore City), Amy R. Brooks (D-Howard), Jerry D. Donald (D-Frederick), Robin Harvey (D-Baltimore County) and Steve Whisler (R-Frederick and Carroll).

Dels.-elect Chuck Cook (D-Anne Arundel), Tracy Gant (D-Prince George’s), Charles Murphy III (R-Baltimore County), Jake Taylor (R-Harford) and LaTasha Ward (D-Prince George’s) head to the Government, Labor and Elections Committee.

Dels.-elect Adrienne Mathis (D-St. Mary’s), Steve Patten (R-Carroll) and Tiffany Welch (D-Baltimore City) head to the Health Committee.

The Judiciary Committee gets Dels.-elect Derek Howell (R-Cecil), Mike Jacobs (R-Anne Arundel), Keenon James (D-Prince George’s) and Christa Tichy (D-Montgomery).

The Ways and Means Committee will get Dels.-elect Andrew Duck (D-Frederick) and Ryan Turner (D-Baltimore City).

Peña-Melnyk also thanked the departing delegates for their “dedication to public service, commitment to their constituents, and contributions to the institution. Their leadership and service have helped shape the House of Delegates and have left a lasting impact on the communities they represented,” according to the statement from her office.
