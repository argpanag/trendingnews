Ronaldo’s €850 million brand raises questions about Portugal’s global appeal

TheTools · 17.11.2026 21:36 · source · permalink

Experts warn that Cristiano Ronaldo’s departure could affect the national team’s global audience.

By Sidsel Senius , in News, World, Sport · 16 Nov 2026, 19:03 · 1 Comments

Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from Portugal’s national team after 23 years, having made 234 appearances and scored 146 goals.

According to The Guardian, Ronaldo’s departure could also affect the national team’s international visibility and commercial appeal.

Daniel Sá, executive director of the Portuguese Institute of Marketing Administration (IPAM), estimates Ronaldo’s personal brand to be worth €850 million, Executive Digest reports.

“According to our most recent assessment, his brand alone is worth 850 million euros,” Sá said.

He believes Portugal will need to find ways to retain international viewers who primarily followed the national team because of Ronaldo.

Former teammates assess Ronaldo’s departure

Leonel Pontes, technical director of Shanghai Shenhua, who previously worked with Ronaldo at Sporting and with Portugal’s national team, said the player’s popularity remains significant in China.

“When I tell people here in China that I’m Portuguese, the first thing they talk about is Cristiano,” Pontes told The Guardian.

Pontes also criticised how Ronaldo’s departure was handled, suggesting that both the player and the national team coach made mistakes and that Portugal missed an opportunity to organise a more appropriate farewell.

Former Portugal international Petit, who played alongside Ronaldo between 2003 and 2008, believes other players must now assume greater responsibility.

Portugal’s current squad includes Vitinha, Bruno Fernandes, João Neves, Nuno Mendes and Diogo Costa.

Petit highlighted Ronaldo’s ability to decide matches through goals, free kicks and set pieces, while emphasising that Portugal has players competing at major European clubs who can take on leadership roles.

Ronaldo’s international achievements

Ronaldo was part of the Portugal squad that reached the final of Euro 2004, where the team lost to Greece.

He subsequently captained Portugal to victory at Euro 2016, securing the country’s first European Championship title.

During the final against France, Ronaldo was forced to leave the pitch through injury. Portugal went on to win the match.

Former international Maniche, who played alongside Ronaldo during Euro 2004, attributed the forward’s longevity to his competitive mentality and ability to respond to setbacks.

Pontes also highlighted Ronaldo’s commitment to international duty, recalling occasions when he played despite injuries or fitness concerns.

Dany Mota, who worked with Ronaldo at Juventus and with the Portuguese national team, described his demanding training routine, including additional gym sessions and physical preparation outside regular team training.

Ronaldo continues club career

Despite ending his international career, Ronaldo continues to play for Al-Nassr in Saudi Arabia.

The forward is also pursuing his ambition of reaching 1,000 career goals.

Portugal will now continue with a squad featuring several established international players, while facing the potential commercial and sporting consequences of Ronaldo’s departure.
