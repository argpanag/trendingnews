15 Record Store Day Releases Actually Worth Getting on Black Friday

TheTools · 06.10.2026 20:26 · source · permalink

Record Store Day Black Friday—an extension of the annual celebration of independent music shops that has since turned into a bloated, navel-gazing mess hurting the exact stores it claims to support—is returning on November 27. RSD was founded in 2008 as a way to help drive customers to then-struggling indie retailers (and entice vinyl-loving cranks like myself), but in recent years has become a clearinghouse for increasingly ridiculous rarities; some notable stores have even refused to participate over profitability and distribution concerns. While the 2027 edition likely won’t solve the well-documented issues the event continues to face, there are still gems worth braving a horde of Sombr fans to purchase. Below, the Pitchfork staff has compiled a list of the best ones amid the nuclear-grade cringe:

  • David Axelrod: David Axelrod
  • Frank Black: Teenager Of The Year
  • Pi'erre Bourne: The Life of Pi'erre 4
  • Fischerspooner: #1
  • Funkadelic: Live at Meadowbrook 1971
  • Vince Guaraldi Trio: A Charlie Brown Christmas (Advent Calendar Edition)
  • Carly Rae Jepsen: Day and Night (Continued)
  • Lil Wayne: No Ceilings
  • Joni Mitchell: Live at Carnegie Hall - 1972
  • Stevie Nicks: Bella Donna
  • Bruce Springsteen & The E Street Band: Live at Tower Theater Philly 1975
  • Masayoshi Takanaka: Super Selection
  • Tom Verlaine: Live in NYC 1996 - 2006
  • Ween: Shinola, Vol. 2
  • Bill Withers: Bill Withers in Concert - Live at the BBC, 1974
