The Curious Case of Sidy Cissoko at Shooting Guard

TheTools · 14.11.2026 11:09 · source · permalink
  • Blazers Analysis

How a relatively unknown player became big in Portland.

Portland Trail Blazers Head Coach Micah Nori made the prudent decision to change his starting lineup earlier this week. After five games replacing Ja Morant in the starting five, Scoot Henderson was returned to the bench to make way for his former G League Ignite teammate Sidy Cissoko.

Henderson has been a disappointment this season. There’s no way of getting around it. He’s been turnover-prone, inefficient, and seemingly lost for extended stretches. Though the former number three overall pick has been serviceable on defense, he’s also logged unnecessary fouls through the early part of the campaign.

As someone who has long been a Cissoko supporter, I was still relatively surprised when the Frenchman replaced Henderson. I and others have advocated for Jrue Holiday as Lillard’s optimal backcourt teammate given his experience and well-rounded game.

But after seeing it play out, admittedly through only three games, there’s no mistaking the improvement accompanying Cissoko’s temporary promotion.

I say temporary because whether we like it or not, Ja Morant will almost certainly return to his role as starting point guard once he’s recovered from his hamstring injury. I’ve already written about my dissatisfaction with the Morant-Lillard backcourt, but it was working a heck of a lot better than the Lillard-Henderson pairing.

Below we discuss the various impacts Cissoko’s starting role has had on the team.

Forward Depth Lacking

The biggest drawback of putting Cissoko in the backcourt is the consequent depletion of the roster’s already-shallow forward rotation, which up until recently he had been supporting.

Outside of Cissoko, Portland’s only true forwards are Toumani Camara and Deni Avdija. Jrue Holiday fills any further void. With Cissoko spending time at shooting guard, the Blazers get even smaller in three-guard lineup.

The Blazers might make a move for frontcourt depth at February’s NBA Trade Deadline. Before they get there they’ll have to hold on over the next three months.

Henderson Check-In

I’ve not been shy about my concerns over Henderson’s game and lack of star qualities. After showing some progression late last season, his game has somehow devolved to where he’s performing like he did as a rookie. He looks out of his element, a deer in the headlights.

On the season, Henderson is averaging 8.4 points on a dismal 31.5 percent from the field and 22.7 percent from three, to go with 2.1 boards and 1.5 assits in 22.0 minutes. Among combo guards, he ranks in the second percentile in points per shot attempt and three-point percentage, seventh percentile in assist rate. He’s also dead last in effective field goal percentage.

Henderson is too small to start at shooting guard on a team with a defensively deficient point guard. He’s not a bad defender but this team needs size in the backcourt to improve on its 11th-best defensive ranking.

The overarching issue with Henderson is he’s almost through his rookie-scale deal and we still don’t know what he is. People may argue he’s currently the same age Lillard was when he entered the league. My rebuttal is four years of exposure to NBA-level play has done little to grow consistent production.

Nori replacing Henderson with someone like Cissoko is far from a glowing endorsement. It does not bode well for him when he hits the rarely lucrative restricted free agency.

Holiday’s Sixth Man Case

As mentioned, keeping Holiday with the bench unit wouldn’t have been my first instinct but it does open an opportunity for the veteran to add another trophy to his cabinet. The 36-year-old is thriving in a reserve role, registering 12.8 points on 48.5 percent from the field, 42.4 percent from three and 89.3 percent from the free throw line. He’s also putting up 3.4 boards, 4.0 assists and 1.0 steals in 26.2 minutes.

For some comparison, last year’s Sixth Man of the Year winner, Keldon Johnson of the San Antonio Spurs, averaged 13.2 points on 51.9 percent from the field, 36.3 percent from three and 79.4 percent from the free throw line. He also registered 5.4 boards, 1.4 assists and 0.6 steals in 23.3 minutes.

Among wings, Holiday ranks third in midrange shooting, fourth in corner threes and points per shot attempt, and sixth in effective field goal percentage. If he’s able to maintain this level of production and efficiency, Holiday will demand consideration for this year’s Sixth Man of the Year prize.

While I’d prefer him in the starting lineup, he appears to be playing a key role running the second unit, also serving in key minutes at the end of games when required.

Cissoko’s Effectiveness

Sidy Cissoko has been incredibly effective in the three games he’s played, averaging 13.0 points on 38.1 percent from three, 3.0 rebounds and 1.3 assists in 29.3 minutes.

But it’s more than just counting stats. Whenever he’s on the floor, it feels like the intensity of the game shifts a few levels. For someone discarded by three NBA teams before starting his Blazers tenure as a two-way player, Cissoko has managed to grow his game exponentially in just 21 short months.

The way Cissoko approaches the game is something you can’t help but marvel at. The ferocity and enthusiasm are unrelenting. He’s also improved his offensive output, able to hit an open three pointers while driving to the rim and finishing with assertiveness. He’s always possessed an above-average passing ability and can dribble the floor but the 22-year-old second round pick is clearly showing a propensity to continue growing his game.

I’m not sure Lillard has shared a backcourt in Portland with a player of Cissoko’s size and length since Wesley Matthews. Obviously Cissoko and Matthews feature different skillsets, but the former’s ability to defend at a high level and at the point of attack, while boasting an ability to hit open threes, has proven to be a winner in the past.

Cissoko may not have Matthews’ shooting stroke, but he appears to add things to his game as his career progresses.

Conclusion

I applaud Coach Nori’s decisiveness. Scoot Henderson needed to be sent back to the bench because he wasn’t doing this team any favors. While Holiday will always be the best option next to Lillard, Cissoko also makes sense thanks to his size and defense.

Holiday is carving out a nice little narrative as a Sixth Man of the Year candidate. If he maintains production, he could win the prize.

The only real problem starting Cissoko at the two is the depletion of the roster’s shallow forward positions. But the debate might be moot if Ja Morant can return to the court soon.
