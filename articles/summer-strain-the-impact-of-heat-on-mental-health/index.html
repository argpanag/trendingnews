Summer strain: The impact of heat on mental health

TheTools · 03.10.2026 20:51 · source · permalink
  • On Air
  • Saturday Morning with Jack Tame

We’ve been hearing lots about the upcoming El Nino and how this might lead to a hotter summer for us, and how Europe has had massive heatwaves this year. Most of us are aware of the physical dangers of extreme heat, such as heat exhaustion and heat stroke.

However, researchers are now finding that heat can also affect mental health – with even a small increase in temperature associated with increases in mental health-related problems. Specifically, people can experience more:  

  • Irritability  
  • Anxiety  
  • Depression  
  • Sleep problems  
  • Worsening symptoms of existing mental illness  
  • Increased use of mental health services  

However, not everyone is affected equally. Those more at risk include:  

  • People with caregiving responsibilities as looking after children, elderly relatives, or someone with health problems can increase emotional strain and reduce the ability to look after one's own needs.  
  • People taking certain medications, particularly those that interfere with the body’s ability to regulate temperature including antidepressants and some heart medications.  
  • People who have increased heat sensitivity including pregnant mums, women in menopause and perimenopause, and people who are sleep deprived (e.g., new parents!). Also, those people who are more exposed to heat such as people who work outdoors, those with poorly ventilated homes, or who work around machinery.  

So what helps protect mental health during heat and what we can do to prepare?  

  • Having strong family and community connections help people cope practically and emotionally. Supportive relationships can provide:  
    • Reminders to stay hydrated  
    • Help accessing cooler places  
    • Emotional reassurance  
    • Monitoring of vulnerable individuals  
  • Access to green and blue spaces (parks, gardens, bodies of water). These environments may provide not only physical cooling benefits but also psychological benefits e.g., increased ability to enjoy being outside, more socialising and receiving support from other people.  
  • Social participation. Activities such as visiting friends, participating in community groups, and maintaining social contact helped protect against depression during heatwaves. Often during extreme heat people become isolated as they stay inside in an effort to remain cool so keeping up social participation can reduce this loneliness and depression.  
  • Reminders to stay hydrated  
  • Help accessing cooler places  
  • Emotional reassurance  
  • Monitoring of vulnerable individuals  

It's important to start thinking about and preparing for summer heat now, to protect both your physical and mental health. It will benefit you and your family, but also wider society if we can reduce pressure on Emergency Depts and medical services. Start thinking about who in your world might be vulnerable over summer. Make some plans ahead of time about keeping connected – try and include trips to blue and green spaces when you connect. Go and buy some fans now and beat the rush. Don’t wait till the middle of summer when we’re all suffering!
