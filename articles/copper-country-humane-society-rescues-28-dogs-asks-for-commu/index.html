Copper Country Humane Society rescues 28 dogs, asks for community help

TheTools · 07.11.2026 00:19 · source · permalink

HOUGHTON, Mich. (WLUC) - In the Copper Country, an animal shelter that rescued dozens of pets is now looking after them.

The Copper Country Humane Society is now caring for 28 dogs rescued from a situation earlier this week that it called ‘overwhelming’. We spoke with CCHS Shelter Manager Becki Clouthier in a Friday phone call about the situation. She says the shelter received several complaint calls about a Baraga County man living with the dogs.

“And the living situation was not pleasant for the dogs or the man in question,” Clouthier said. “It turns out it started with just a couple of dogs. They ended up reproducing and then more and more and more, and he just didn’t have the money to do that, the vetting. So, he agreed to surrender them all. It was obvious he loved his dogs; it was just very overwhelming for him.”

Clouthier thanks the Keweenaw Bay Indian Community Tribal Police, staff, volunteers and the former owner for helping the process go smoothly. The shelter says two mother dogs with newborn puppies are included in the count. Clouthier notes they all appear to be healthy but need medications and treatment before adoption.

“We haven’t been able to do much with them yet because they are scared, and they’re not very well-socialized,” Clouthier continued. “They’re nervous, and they’ll bite right now, so we’re just taking it slow, letting them decompress. Our vet and vet tech are coming out on Monday to help us get them vaccinated and do checks on all of them, figure out whether they’re male or female, set up surgery dates to get them spayed and neutered.”

However, the shelter says caring for and providing for these dogs will cost thousands of dollars. The shelter is now asking the community to help support the dogs as it works to find them homes. This can be through monetary donations, dog toys, and flat sheets.

“They love soft food, so that is one thing I’m going to add,” Clouthier added. “Canned food, and things like that. They seem to be eating that very well.”

Clouthier says you can drop off items at the shelter directly or donate through the shelter’s website. The shelter is located at 501 Nara Park Road. Clouthier also says due to the high influx of dogs and cats, the shelter cannot take in any more pets until further notice.
