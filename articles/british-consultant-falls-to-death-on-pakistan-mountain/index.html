British consultant falls to death on Pakistan mountain

TheTools · 03.09.2026 20:43 · source · permalink

Body found in ravine after Alexander Harris failed to arrive at hotel

The body of a British consultant who went missing on a hike in Pakistan has been found.

Alexander Harris, 30, scaled the 13,000ft Musa Ka Musala mountain in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province on Saturday with his friend Alun David Cooper Cledwyn.

The pair took photographs at the summit and then took separate routes down the mountain, police said.

Mr Harris, a dual British and Irish citizen from Birmingham, failed to return to the hotel, prompting a search operation.

Khalid Iqbal, the Mansehra deputy police commissioner, told the BBC that the body had been found on a path in a forested ravine after an extensive search.

Mr Iqbal said it was suspected that Mr Harris had slipped and fallen after it began raining.

The area where the body was found includes narrow ravines, dangerous mountain tracks and steep drops, and is particularly treacherous in wet weather.

Between 250 and 300 police officers, shepherds familiar with the terrain and other members of the public had searched for Mr Harris on foot and with drones.

Mr Harris, who lived in Islamabad, was an experienced trekker who had climbed Musa Ka Musala before, and frequently travelled to different locations in the region to hike.

Mr Harris and Mr Cledwyn were staying in Siran Valley and began the climb without informing the authorities or obtaining the relevant paperwork, officials said.

Mr Iqbal said: “The hotel had offered him a guide and police security, but he said that he knew the area and did not need any help.”

‘Cared deeply for Pakistan’

Mr Harris worked for Adam Smith International, the global aid and development consultancy, in Pakistan.

A spokesman for the company said: “He cared deeply for Pakistan, its people, its nature, and learned Urdu during his time there.

“We are deeply grateful to the hundreds of people who joined the search effort, including local residents, volunteers, police, rescue teams, forest department, members of Alex’s trail-running community and the government of Pakistan that supported the operation.

“They searched tirelessly in difficult conditions, often in heavy rain, fog and poor visibility, over steep and challenging terrain.

“Our thoughts are with Alex’s family, friends and colleagues at this incredibly difficult time. We remain focused on supporting his family.”

Mr Harris was a keen runner and his running group posted multiple pleas for information while he was missing.

Breaking the news, Islamabad Run With Us wrote on Instagram: “It is with deep sadness that we share the news that Alex’s body has been found in Paracha jungle.

“After days of search and rescue efforts, this is a heartbreaking outcome for his family, friends and everyone who was hoping and praying for his safe return.”

A Foreign Office spokesman said: “We are supporting the family of a British man who has died in Pakistan and are in contact with the local authorities.”
