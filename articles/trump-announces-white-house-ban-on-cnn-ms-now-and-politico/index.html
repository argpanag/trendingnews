Trump announces White House ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico

TheTools · 18.09.2026 19:28 · source · permalink

  • Topic:World Politics

Topic:World Politics

Donald Trump frequently clashes with journalists over critical questions and stories. (Reuters: Evelyn Hockstein)

US President Donald Trump says he has banned three prominent media outlets from the White House, in an extraordinary escalation of his attacks on the American press.

Cable networks CNN and MS NOW and political newsletter Politico have been banned effective immediately, with more to follow, Mr Trump wrote on Truth Social.

He said the ban was "a result of their constant 'reporting' FAKE NEWS!"

"Media Outlets shouldn't be able to constantly write or report FICTION and LIES when they're covering the President of the United States, the Trump Administration, or the United States of America," he wrote.

Later, he told reporters in the Oval Office: "There is something wrong with a country that allows people to write purposely negative stories."

It was not clear how the ban would be enforced. After the post was published on Friday afternoon, local time, staff from the three outlets were still working at the White House.

CNN said the ban, if it went ahead, would be an "illegal assault" on its constitutionally protected right to report without government interference.

Bruce Brown, the president of the US Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, said the ban was "flatly unconstitutional".

"The First Amendment is clear that once the White House invites in some journalists, it can't ban others because it doesn't like their reporting,"

"This is textbook viewpoint discrimination and will be quickly struck down by the courts if challenged."

Regular clashes

Across his two terms as president, Mr Trump has developed a hostile relationship with journalists and media outlets who report critically on his administration.

However, he regularly invites reporters into the Oval Office for impromptu press conferences, and only in rare cases have reporters or outlets been banned.

Last year, the Associated Press news agency was excluded from some Trump events when it refused to follow his order to change the name of the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America.

In 2018, the White House cancelled the media accreditation of CNN reporter Jim Acosta after a verbal clash during a press conference. A judge temporarily overturned the decision before the White House backed down and reinstated the accreditation.

More commonly, Mr Trump launches lawsuits against outlets over stories he disputes or disagrees with.

In 2022, he sought hundreds of millions of dollars in damages from CNN over reports that referred to his baseless claims of election fraud as a "big lie". A Trump-appointed judge later found the comments were "repugnant" but not defamatory, and dismissed the case.

Last year, he sued the Wall Street Journal, which broke a story about a 2003 birthday greeting to sex offender Jeffrey Epstein that appeared to come from Mr Trump. A judge dismissed the case in April, but Mr Trump later refiled.

He also filed a $US15 billion ($21 billion) suit against the New York Times and four of its reporters over a series of articles, including a 2024 editorial that said he was unfit for office. A judge threw out the case four days later.

New York Times publisher AG Sulzberger said Mr Trump had been more hostile towards the press than any other modern president.

"He accuses reporters of treason, a crime punishable by death," Mr Sulzberger wrote in an editorial published earlier on Friday, local time.

"He personally sues news organisations when their coverage displeases him. He directs his administration to use the levers of government power to investigate, subpoena and prosecute journalists for doing their jobs."
