Feds say Stellantis owes 'hundreds of millions' in subsidies, company offers $24M | CBC News

TheTools · 13.11.2026 14:13 · source · permalink

Government issued company a notice of default of a 2022 federal funding agreement

Automaker Stellantis says it will pay back $24 million in federal subsidies after it announced plans to sell its Brampton, Ont., assembly plant — but Industry Minister Mélanie Joly says the company owes far more than what it is offering.

The company announced Monday that it plans to sell the Brampton facility to armoured vehicle manufacturer Roshel Inc., which the federal government says is in violation of a 2022 agreement that put up millions in subsidies to help retool the plant.

In a statement on Thursday, Stellantis Canada CEO Trevor Longley said the company will return the subsidies to the government to the tune of $24 million.

"We recognize the disappointment and uncertainty surrounding the future of the facility. While our decision to sell the plant represents a change from the original plans for Brampton, the agreement we have advanced with a Canadian manufacturer provides a path to return the facility to productive industrial use," the statement reads.

The amount conflicts with the government's characterization of what Stellantis owes.

"Stellantis is on the hook for hundreds of millions of dollars," Joly told reporters following a Thursday meeting with representatives from the automaker, the Ontario government and the union representing the plant's workers.

When pressed, Joly didn't specify a number, simply replying that "Stellantis knows how much it owes." The government's own records previously showed that roughly $220 million worth of subsidies were given to the company to help upgrade its plants across the province.

'I'm fed up': Joly says Stellantis's planned sale of Brampton plant breached contract

Asked for a response to Stellantis's suggestion that it owes $24 million, Joly's office said the minister's earlier statements stand.

Joly wrote to Longley on Thursday saying the government is terminating a resolution process and notifying the automaker that it is in default of a 2022 government funding agreement.

"Patience has its limits. To be frank, I'm fed up. I'm fed up and Canadians are fed up and workers are fed up," Joly said after Thursday's meeting. She also said the government plans to meet with Stellantis's global CEO.

The minister's letter to Stellantis suggests that the company has 30 days to take corrective action or the government could seek to terminate the 2022 agreement and recoup its subsidies.

Did Stellantis violate its deals with the feds? Funding contract provided exceptions around Brampton

Stellantis had been engaged with the government to find a possible alternative for the Brampton facility after the company announced last year that it wouldn't be going ahead with plans to build the Jeep Compass at that plant.

The government had signed an agreement to provide subsidies to the automaker to retool the plant to produce the Jeep model. Joly's letter says the planned sale to Roshel indicates Stellantis is not interested in finding a satisfactory solution.

"I must conclude that Stellantis is no longer diligently working in good faith to prepare a satisfactory plan under the Resolution Process. Accordingly, the Resolution Process is terminated," the letter reads.

Union says government should've acted sooner

Joly had promised nearly a year ago that the government would serve a default notice to Stellantis.

Lana Payne — the national president of Unifor, the union representing the workers at the plant — said the federal government should've acted sooner.

"We have the sale of a plant and everybody in the country says they didn't want to have the sale of a plant. Yet here we are, the plant has been sold," Payne told reporters following Thursday's meeting.

"Why could not something have been done last week? Or the week before? Or the week before that?"

Stellantis auto plant sale is 'last possible outcome that we would have wanted': union

Stellantis and Roshel signed a memorandum of understanding regarding the potential sale in September. The automaker announced on Monday that it intends to go ahead with the sale.

When pressed during Thursday's news conference, Joly indicated that the government will be unable to block the sale.

More than 2,000 workers at Stellantis's Brampton plant have been out of work since the company halted operations in 2023 to retool the facility for the Jeep Compass.

In the deal announced by the Trudeau government in 2022, Ottawa said it would inject up to $529 million in Stellantis plants in Brampton and Windsor, Ont., to help them retool to increase production of electric and gas-powered vehicles.

Stellantis paused the retooling plan at the Brampton plant in early 2025, announcing in October of that year that the Jeep Compass would be built at the Belvidere Assembly Plant in Illinois instead.

Part of the deal with Roshel that was announced on Monday could see Stellantis lease some of the property back.

Stellantis says there are still about 1,800 workers from the Brampton plant on layoff. Since 2023, those workers have continued to receive 70 per cent of their pay, as well as benefits.

'What was the betrayal?' Carney clarifies stance on Stellantis plant deal

Last week, Unifor demanded a meeting of all stakeholders, including the Ontario government, Stellantis and the federal government.

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Thursday that his government had been informed of "some aspects" of Stellantis's plan to sell the auto plant to Roshel before the deal was announced earlier this week.

The prime minister made the comments a day after reports that his office and Industry Minister Mélanie Joly's office had been aware for months of the details of what was going to be made public.

Joly's office has insisted that the minister "had no part in negotiating the deal with Stellantis or Roshel" and didn't meet with the armoured vehicle manufacturer.

Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre has called on Carney to remove Joly as minister over the plant sale. He reiterated that call on Thursday and said all the details of the 2022 agreement should be made public.

"Why not release the deal, Mr. Carney? Your government handed over our money. Canadians deserve to know how much they paid and what they got in return," he said during a news conference in Calgary.

Corrections

  • A previous version of this story suggested that Stellantis had indicated it owes the government up to $220 million in subsidies. In fact, that estimate is from the government's public accounts.
