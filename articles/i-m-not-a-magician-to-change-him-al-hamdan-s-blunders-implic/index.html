I'm not a magician to change him: Al-Hamdan's blunders implicate Donis, so who is to blame?

TheTools · 24.09.2026 07:02 · source · permalink

Al-Nassr's striker failed in the first task

Even the most pessimistic did not expect the declining level shown by Abdullah Al-Hamdan, the Saudi Arabia striker, on the night of the win over Kuwait, by a single unanswered goal, yesterday Wednesday, in the opening matches of the 27th Arabian Gulf Cup.

Al-Hamdan started in the first team but never turned up in the way required. He squandered two clear chances and dealt poorly with several attacks, caught out by his positioning or too afraid to make a decision.

Al-Hamdan lasted 83 minutes, but he struggled to make the most of several attacks. He squandered a clear-cut chance at the start, latching onto a magical pass from Humam Al-Hammami that sent him one-on-one with the Kuwait goalkeeper. Rather than shoot, he wasted it in bizarre fashion with his left foot.

More passes came his way that demanded proper runs, holding the ball up and keeping defenders at bay. He managed none of it, spurning several easy chances for "the Falcons".

Donis had seen enough after 83 minutes. Off came Al-Hamdan, on came Firas Al-Buraikan, and the Al-Nassr star trudged away to criticism and jeers from the stands.

Al-Buraikan, by contrast, made an instant impression. He burst into life with constant movement inside and outside the box, drifting to the left flank and opening space for runners from deep to attack in behind.

Greece's Georgios Donis, coach of the Saudi Arabia national team, defended Al-Hamdan's poor form in the press conference, saying: "He sometimes plays and at other times he does not play."

He went on: "I am not a magician to change what Al-Hamdan is going through with his club, but he remains an important player for us, and he certainly gives us an added dimension and strength."

Al-Hamdan can't escape blame for the string of chances he squandered and his poor decision-making in some attacks. But there's another way to look at it.

His strength lies in playing directly behind the striker, as a second forward, breaking into the penalty area and exploiting the spaces between the two centre-backs when the first striker moves out of the box.

That's Al-Hamdan's value, and it sets him apart from others, specifically from his spell with Al-Nassr last season until now. He's a mobile player who runs into spaces well, and he threatens opponents' goals with powerful strikes, as he did in the recent match between Al-Nassr and Al-Khaleej.

Relying on him as a striker? That's an unproductive idea, and it won't give the Saudi national team the strength they need. Abdullah Al-Salem, Firas Al-Buraikan and Saleh Al-Shehri, for example, are names capable of playing in the main striker position.

Dionis should have recognised this and made a change, whether by dropping deeper in play or bringing on a striker, and adjusting the shape by returning Al-Hamdan as a second forward.
