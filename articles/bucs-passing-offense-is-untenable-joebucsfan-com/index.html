Bucs Passing Offense Is "Untenable" - JoeBucsFan.com

TheTools · 14.09.2026 04:02 · source · permalink

Baker Mayfield throws one of his many passes behind the line of scrimmage yesterday.

There is a reason for that.

Ben Solak of ESPN tore apart Bucs offensive coordinator Zac Robinson and his game plan yesterday because it was so “passive,” in Solak’s words.

In short, the Bucs, on 42 percent of their passes, threw behind the line of scrimmage.

Solak was outraged at the lack of aggression.

This number isn’t even as high as it could have been in the Buccaneers’ 33-27 loss to the Bengals. Before Mayfield started pushing the ball down the field for multiple scores in the fourth quarter, more than half of his targets were behind the line of scrimmage.

This is concerning. No offense wants to live behind the line this often, and certainly not the Buccaneers, who have a deep pass-catching core featuring Emeka Egbuka, Chris Godwin Jr., Ted Hurst III and Jalen McMillan (who was inactive). The Bengals played plenty of zone coverage (65% of dropbacks) but not a ridiculous amount. It shouldn’t have been this hard to get the ball down the field.

Baker did a good job scrambling when downfield routes weren’t available and used his running backs to stay ahead of the sticks — Bucky Irving caught seven passes on the day. But the Buccaneers under new offensive coordinator Zac Robinson cannot become this passive of a passing attack — it’s untenable in the modern NFL.

Joe doesn’t disagree, but Joe did a wee bit of research. Someone by the handle “Oliver G” on the USA Today/Gannett outfit Niners Wire wrote this summer about the screen game — both on offense and defending screens.

Using FTN Fantasy data (the same outfit as the inventor of DVOA, Aaron Schatz, is on staff), the Bengals last year had the worst screen pass defense in the league, posting a -0.560 EPA per play on 46 attempts.

The Bengals may have changed their defensive line a lot but they still have Al Golden as defensive coordinator, who was the architect of this lousy screen pass defense last year.

So armed with this intel, Joe can see why the Bucs wanted to dink and dunk the Bengals to death.

But Solak is right: The Bucs were built to throw down the field. Only clowns, third-rate coaches and egomaniacs don’t use the talent they have in an offense that best fits their skill sets.
