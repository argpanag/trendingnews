Grand Theft Auto VI is scheduled to release on November 19, and cybercriminals are using the wait to target gamers. According to cybersecurity firm Netcraft, several websites are offering supposedly leaked copies, early access, Android and iOS versions, and free game keys. Netcraft examined GTA VI-themed domains first spotted between July 1 and October 27, 2026. Some of these sites deliver malware, while others ask users for cryptocurrency, personal details or survey completions.

Fake leaked builds carry trojans

Many of these sites use the name of CyberLeek, the anonymous leaker who began posting GTA VI gameplay clips on August 18. Only videos and images were made public, and no playable build was shared. Netcraft counted 121 newly registered domains carrying the CyberLeek name, 100 of which appeared in August.

One such site, gta6cyberleek[.]com, served a file named GTAVI.exe, which 34 of 68 antivirus engines on VirusTotal flagged, mostly as a trojan. Another, cyberleek[.]click, offered a launcher detected as malicious by 18 of 62 engines. After installation, users were asked to pay $100 (around Rs 8,800) in cryptocurrency to activate an account.

Early access, mobile versions and free keys

Netcraft also found sites selling early access and PC copies, though Rockstar Games has announced the title only for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. A site called gta6[.]exposed listed the game at $199 for any platform, including PC, and accepted payment in Bitcoin, Monero, Litecoin or Solana.

Other pages advertise GTA VI APK files for Android and instruct users to allow installation from unknown sources, even though no mobile version has been announced. Sites such as mygta6[.]icu and gta6keycodes[.]com promise free pre-order codes but lock them behind surveys, app installs and sign-ups.

How to stay safe

Users should buy GTA VI only from the official PlayStation and Xbox stores or authorised retailers. Avoid downloading launchers or APK files from unknown websites, paying cryptocurrency for game access, or completing offers to unlock codes.

Netcraft expects scammers to shift focus after launch to free in-game currency, Shark Cards, money glitches and fake support pages. Gamers should not enter Rockstar, PlayStation or Xbox account credentials on third-party websites.
