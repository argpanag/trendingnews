Nvidia beats on results and guidance in first earnings report since Huang's exit: Live updates

TheTools · 17.11.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

This is CNBC's live coverage of Nvidia's third-quarter 2027 earnings.

Nvidia beat on profit and revenue after the bell on Tuesday and issued strong guidance, in the chipmaker's first earnings report since co-founder Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation last week as CEO. The stock rose about 3% in extended trading.

Here's how the company did compared to analysts' estimates, according to LSEG:

Revenue more than doubled from $57 billion a year earlier, Nvidia said, a sign of continued strength in its core data center business. The company said revenue this quarter will expand by roughly 90% to about $130 billion, topping the $125.1 billion average analyst estimate, according to LSEG.

Finance chief Colette Kress is used to addressing Wall Street, but never like this. She's now functioning as interim CEO, meaning her responsibilities go well beyond the key numbers. Employees, investors, analysts and customers are eager to hear her first public remarks since last week's dramatic shake-up.

The earnings call is scheduled to begin at 5 p.m. ET.

Nvidia's stock is down 24% this month and on Friday wrapped up its sixth straight weekly drop, the longest losing streak since 2012. The stock has dropped the first two days of this week and is trading at its lowest since July 2025. In addition to the Huang saga, the company is dealing with broader concerns about an AI slowdown and a political shift in Washington, D.C., following this month's midterm elections.

The biggest cloud for Nvidia is around its China business after Huang's reported affair with a businesswoman who's allegedly a Chinese spy. The Commerce Department on Monday announced a change that would prevent Nvidia's chips from being rented to Chinese companies entirely, putting revenue at risk.

Nvidia isn't backing down from the $150 billion share repurchase plan it announced in September.

The company said it spent $28.1 billion on buybacks during the quarter.

— Kif Leswing

Investors have been closely watching Nvidia's gross margin, which has narrowed in recent quarters as the company has piled up supply commitments to build its chips.

But Nvidia beat Wall Street expectations for the critical metric on Tuesday, with non-GAAP gross margin coming in at 74.9%, beating a Street Account estimate of 74%.

Nvidia said.that the sequentially declining gross margins was because it is ramping up production of its Rubin GPUs.

— Kif Leswing

Kress said in a statement that the company's roadmap is on track.

"Our momentum is real. NVIDIA is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people," Kress is quotes as saying. "Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater."

— Kif Leswing

Nvidia's most important business is its data center revenue, which includes the GPU systems it ships to hyperscalers and other data center providers.

Nvidia said it had data center revenue of $107.6 billion, higher than the Street Account estimate of $101.87 billion.

Data center revenue more than doubled from a year ago.

— Kif Leswing

One of the big concerns in the AI trade has been the health of the neoclouds, which rely on the capital markets to fund their expansion and are now facing higher interest rates for future debt.

At least for now, there are no signs of a slowdown. In CoreWeave's earnings report last week, the company said revenue jumped 168% from a year earlier and forecast $13 billion to $13.3 billion in full-year sales, which would be up more than 150% from 2025.

Nebius followed last week by reporting 571% revenue growth.

— Ari Levy

Huang's departure leaves a void of semiconductor engineering experience on Nvidia's 10-person board of directors, which is made up primarily of financial and business executives.

"I think it's important to have someone on the board who is steeped in experience in high tech," chip analyst Patrick Moorhead wrote in a message to CNBC. "The challenge with bringing somebody in with semiconductor experience is that it brings up a lot of conflicts of interest." 

Moorhead said the company could turn to someone who's retired.

Harvey Jones, who has served on Nvidia's board since the company's founding in 1993 and is now board chair, has technical experience that predates his current position managing Square Wave Ventures. Jones served as CEO of chip design company Synopsys and co-founded Daisy Systems and Tensilica.

When Jones stepped down as Synopsys CEO in 1994, Nvidia hadn't announced its first chip yet.

Mark Stevens, another longtime board member, held technical roles at Intel and Hughes Aircraft before moving into venture capital.

Nvidia board members collectively have a median of 24 years since their last operating roles, according to industry podcast host Phil McKinney. That's the highest among companies that he tracks, followed by AMD, whose board members have a median of 11 years since holding an operating role.

— Katie Tarasov

With Nvidia three-fourths of the way through fiscal 2027, most analysts say that, from a financial perspective, the real impact of Huang's departure won't be felt until the end of fiscal 2028. That's because contracted revenue through next fiscal year is largely in the books.

Nvidia is expected to show revenue growth of 90% in fiscal 2027, followed by expansion the following year of 70%, when sales will top $690 billion, according to LSEG's consensus analyst estimate. Analysts expect earnings per share to climb to $15.73 from $9.28 this year.

Beyond that, there's plenty of uncertainty.

— Ari Levy

After publishing a letter on Friday urging the board to "pursue a slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia" that could include bringing Huang back, Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner told CNBC on Monday that he has confidence in Kress.

"Jensen was an extreme visionary in the industry, nobody questions that," Gerstner told CNBC's Scott Wapner. "I think the company is very capable of making the trains run on time day to day."

— Ari Levy

Nvidia's big hyperscaler customers — Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft — all reported quarterly results last month, and mostly kept their guidance for capital expenditures in check.

Alphabet maintained its full-year capex forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion, while Amazon stuck with $220 billion. Meta narrowed the range for its full-year guidance to $135 billion to $145 billion, lifting the bottom of the range by $5 billion. Microsoft reduced its capex forecast for the year to $170 billion from $175 billion

— Ari Levy

Nvidia employees are posting publicly about Huang's exit, with at least one senior research engineer writing that it led to his own decision to leave.

"Jensen's vision and the engineering culture he shaped were important reasons I wanted to stay, Shiqin "Darrell" Dai, a robotics engineer in Shanghai, wrote on social media over the weekend. "His departure led me to decide that my own chapter here should also come to a close."

Dai told CNBC in a message Monday that he is "still in a process of leaving."

Jane Polak Scowcraft posted on LinkedIn on Sunday, "This has been the most challenging week of my career. Jensen was more than just a CEO."

Scowcraft, a senior director of generative AI data strategy, said she is "proud, and still a little in awe, that I get to work here." She called Huang "a champion of AI, open source and open data, and everyone that worked at NVIDIA."

— Katie Tarasov

OpenAI, one of Nvidia's largest customers, announced two new AI models on Tuesday: GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol.

GPT-6.2 Astra is OpenAI's most advanced model for "complex professional work," while GPT-6.2 Sol is its most "versatile model for everyday work," the company said. 

"Both models deliver better results on demanding tasks with fewer output tokens, so you can get more done within your usage limits," OpenAI said in a blog post.

The model releases come as the safety and security practices at OpenAI are facing intense scrutiny. California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued the company on Monday for allegedly endangering the public by developing advanced AI models without adequate safeguards and monitoring.

In July, OpenAI disclosed that its models escaped containment and breached the company Hugging Face, which rattled industry researchers and prompted OpenAI to pledge to invest more in safety. The company pulled the release of GPT-6.1 Astra in September after determining that it did not meet adequate safety standards.

OpenAI said Tuesday that it's deploying GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol with its "strongest safeguards to date." The company said it has strengthened the system it uses to monitor models, and built more controls into its model development and training.

—Ashley Capoot

Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said Tuesday he expects to keep doing business with Nvidia, as the chipmaker grapples with the fallout from former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation.

"I don't foresee a day where we're not partners with Nvidia," Jassy said in an interview with CNBC's Scott Wapner. "I think they have amazing products. I think we're gonna always have customers who want to run Nvidia in our infrastructure."

Jassy, who spoke on the sidelines of Semafor's "Silicon Valley and The World" conference, said he has "deep respect" for Jensen Huang and Nvidia.

"I know they're, you know, I don't know all the details of what's happened, but I know they're talking through it, and we'll see what happens," Jassy said.

Amazon is one of Nvidia's largest customers, deploying the company's high-end chips across its sprawling data center footprint. The companies deepened their partnership in August, with Amazon committing to buy 2 million Nvidia graphics processing units, just a few months after it ordered 1 million Nvidia chips.

At the same time, Amazon has been investing heavily in building its own silicon, including its Trainium AI chips, which are designed to be a direct competitor to Nvidia's popular Blackwell.

Last month, Jassy spoke bullishly of Amazon's in-house chips business, and said the company plans to make its Trainium chips externally available for the first time beginning in "late 2027."

"We're gonna have customers who want our silicon, we're going to have customers that want Nvidia silicon, and there may be others too," Jassy said.

— Annie Palmer

With the recent stock slump, Nvidia's price-to-earnings ratio for the next 12 months has dropped to 10.84, according to FactSet. That's by far the lowest among the tech megacaps and is roughly one-third AMD's forward P/E ratio of 30.4.

— Ari Levy

China's Ministry of Commerce said Tuesday it "firmly opposes" the U.S. government's move to tighten export controls on advanced chips.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security on Monday issued an interim rule that would require data center operators in countries including Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand to obtain a U.S. government license before exporting advanced AI chips.

The rule seeks to crack down on companies that have circumvented U.S. export controls by remotely accessing AI infrastructure from third countries.

U.S. agencies confirmed Monday that they initiated an investigation into Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner that is suspected of helping to ship advanced AI chips to China, violating U.S. export controls. Megaspeed was once led by Alice Huang, who had an affair with former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and who is alleged to have contact with Chinese intelligence.

The draft regulation "disrupts the stability of global semiconductor industrial and supply chains," a commerce ministry spokesperson said in a statement, translated by CNBC.

"China is strongly dissatisfied with and firmly opposes this," the spokesperson said.

China threatened to take countermeasures if the U.S. doesn't "return to the correct track" of "consultation and cooperation."

"If the US side insists on going its own way, China will take countermeasures and firmly safeguard its national sovereignty, security, and development interests," the spokesperson said.

— Annie Palmer

Huang issued his first statement on Tuesday since resigning as CEO, and he signaled a willingness to return to Nvidia.

"If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO," Huang said.

Sources told CNBC Monday that the board is not considering bringing Huang back as CEO. The board is focused on the national security investigation, a person familiar with the matter said.

Huang said he made "serious mistakes" in his personal life and "created problems" for Nvidia's board that he deeply regrets, according to the statement. He added that he is "committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation."

—Ashley Capoot

While Tuesday's earnings report is a big event for Nvidia and the AI industry, attention will quickly be turning to Thursday, when Anthropic is looking to hit the Nasdaq in a landmark IPO.

Anthropic is likely to price above the current range of $70 to $78, multiple sources told CNBC's David Faber on Tuesday.

Nvidia, like a number of its tech peers, has a sizable stake in Anthropic, and is pouring in more money at the IPO price.

Anthropic is also a heavy user of Nvidia's chips through deals it has with SpaceX, Nscale, Lambda and other cloud providers.

In its prospectus, Anthropic calls out Nvidia, AMD, Google and Amazon as its chip providers.

— Ari Levy

As the company powering the AI boom, Nvidia's earnings have been viewed by some in the industry as a proxy for overall AI demand.

Huang said in August that "demand was accelerating." Kress echoed the point, saying, "Incredibly, we are seeing demand acceleration even at our scale."

But some investors are worried about how long Nvidia's commitments to shareholders, customers and partners will last, and are eager for an update on demand signals.

Morgan Stanley analysts cut their estimates for calendar 2027 and 2028 data center sales by about 10% on the Huang news saying, "faced with some uncertainty and multiple downgrades, we would rather err on the side of being too conservative."

Nvidia also has made significant commitments to secure the supply it needs to build chips.

In its most recent filing, Nvidia said it committed to $92 billion in supply and capacity for the rest of this fiscal year, $87 billion in the fiscal year ending January 2028, and $88 billion the following year.

— Kif Leswing

Nvidia announced Tuesday that its board has established a committee overseeing national security and export controls, as the company faces growing scrutiny around its business dealings in southeast Asia and China.

The committee will be responsible for board-level oversight of Nvidia's national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management, the company said.

Former CEO Jensen Huang's resignation has highlighted Nvidia's relationships with Asian partners and heightened concerns that China could circumvent export controls by renting Nvidia's advanced chips through middleman companies.

Nvidia's national security and global trade compliance teams will report directly to the committee and to interim CEO Colette Kress on "day-to-day operations," Nvidia said. The committee will meet "at least quarterly," the company added.

"NVIDIA has both a responsibility and an opportunity to lead the industry in addressing these challenges," Harvey C. Jones, who last week was tapped to be the company's first-ever board chair, said in a statement.

Jones said Nvidia is committed to working with government and industry partners on developing a "robust framework" to "safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation."

— Annie Palmer

Shares of Nvidia tried to rebound at the opening bell, after days of steep declines amid the fallout from Huang's resignation. Shares traded largely flat Tuesday morning.

Nvidia's stock fell 4% on Monday, closing at its lowest level since July 1, 2025.

— Caleigh Keating

The Justice and Commerce Departments announced late Monday that they are investigating Megaspeed, the Singaporean computing company at the center of the Nvidia spy scandal.

In a joint release posted on X, the agencies said they began probing Megaspeed in 2024 "for potential export control violations" after detecting suspicious activity.

The announcement comes as Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has faced growing criticism that he didn't do enough to prevent possible diversion of Nvidia's advanced chips into China, violating U.S. export rules.

Megaspeed was once led by Alice Huang, the woman with whom former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had an affair and who is reportedly believed by U.S. intelligence to be a Chinese spy.

Investigators in May 2025 requested records from Nvidia concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed. In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list, the agencies said.

That month, agents from the Bureau of Industry and Security, along with Singaporean law enforcement officials, conducted checks at Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang.

"Following this enforcement action, the Department of Justice took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed," the statement said.

— Annie Palmer

During the AI boom, Huang has been deeply involved in creating a new class of companies called neoclouds, which competed with Nvidia's hyperscaler customers.

Huang put a lot of importance on supporting the neoclouds, saying over the summer that their business would help drive Nvidia's growth going forward.

But Nvidia is more than just a supplier of chips to that market. It's also a major investor and financier in the space, and Huang has been integral in deciding which companies got chips first. In August, Huang announced a program on CNBC to help the ecosystem raise financing from a group of major financial firms.

It's unclear whether Kress has the same ability to rally Wall Street or to engage in Huang's level of creative dealmaking.

— Kif Leswing

Nvidia has insisted in recent quarters that it gets essentially no data center sales from China, but Huang's involvement with a Megaspeed executive has highlighted a nuance that could end up being critical.

Sales to some data centers, especially in Southeast Asia, may have been primarily intended for rentals to Chinese companies. That's what some in Washington allege about Megaspeed.

Culper Research, a short-selling research firm that published a note on Nvidia's "China problem" in May, alleges that 20% of Nvidia's fiscal 2026 compute revenue was "driven by China."

The Department of Commerce moved to close the rental loophole on Monday, putting that kind of revenue at risk.

Nvidia has denied any physical diversion, and said it has no evidence that any such activity has taken place. But that was before last week's scandal.

In a statement on Sunday, a Megaspeed representative said the cloud provider is conducting a thorough internal investigation.

— Kif Leswing

Following Huang's admission of an extramarital affair on Nov. 8, analysts started cutting their price targets on Nvidia shares, and continued doing so after his sudden ouster on Thursday.

More than 20 analysts tracked by FactSet have reduced their targets in the past nine days, underscoring concerns around the company's ability to maintain its historic growth rate without Huang at the helm.

"We believe ex-CEO Jensen was viewed as the global dealmaker 'AI banker', and key innovator," analysts at Mizuho wrote in a report on Sunday. They lowered their price target to $220 from $250, due in part to their view that the change at the top "adds a 6-7-month management overhang."

— Ari Levy

Many in the tech world, including Elon Musk, have called for Huang to return as Nvidia CEO. President Trump said he'd be open to it.

But Huang currently has no role with Nvidia, either as CEO or board member, and the board appears unmoved by outside pressure, so far at least. Nvidia has hired law firm Wachtell Lipton to lead a probe into Huang's conduct and whether it compromised national security or company secrets.

— Kif Leswing
