Cllr Bobbie Dove demands action as BCP faces deepening financial crisis

TheTools · 02.11.2026 07:35 · source · permalink

BCP Council leaders have been warned to match their assurances with action as the authority battles a severe financial crisis.

Cllr Bobbie Dove, deputy group leader for the Conservatives on BCP Council, said residents needed real accountability as services came under growing pressure.

Cllr Dove said: "We all need confidence that those leading BCP Council are doing everything possible to protect our communities from its financial crisis," adding that "the situation is far too serious for there to be a gap between public statements and tangible action".

Cllr Dove said the political row masked the human cost.

She said: "Families depending on council services, vulnerable people needing support and residents concerned about the future of their local facilities."

Cllr Dove accused the administration of making "endless excuses and blame directed elsewhere" for three years, adding that "recognising national pressures does not absolve the leadership of accountability for its own decisions".

With the budget papers due for scrutiny in January, Cllr Dove warned the council had until the end of November to understand its finances and produce firm proposals.

Cllr Dove warned: "Every day lost, every week lost, leaves less time to secure support, examine alternatives and protect residents from damaging and irreversible consequences," adding that "'too little, too late' is a devastating verdict when it is our communities paying the price".

Cllr Dove called on council leader Millie Earl and finance chief Mike Cox to step up, saying: "This is when the positions of council leader and deputy leader must mean something: taking responsibility."

The warning follows a Bournemouth Echo report published on October 31.

Despite claims of extensive lobbying—including Cllr Cox saying in September, "We've been shouting, screaming, lobbying, whatever you want to call it"—Freedom of Information requests showed leaders held not a single meeting with Treasury or Local Government ministers between July 2024 and September 2026.

Formal requests for meetings were also virtually non-existent, despite Leader Millie Earl saying the authority had warned central government about the crisis for years.

The council's opposition leader, Cllr John Beesley, has called for correspondence with ministers to be published and demanded answers about the lobbying claims.

Bournemouth East MP Tom Hayes criticised the council for failing to engage directly, saying local people "pay a heavy price" when leaders do not pursue every opportunity.

BCP Council faces a £380 million Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) deficit by March 2028 and an in-year financial pressure of more than £32 million.

Defending the administration, Cllr Cox said council leaders had been "open and transparent" in writing to ministers and would "welcome the opportunity to meet" to secure fair funding.
