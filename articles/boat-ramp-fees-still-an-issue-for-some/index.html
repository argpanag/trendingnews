Boat ramp fees still an issue for some

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:06 · source · permalink

Diane McCarthy

Builder Mark Bonne has been raising issues with Whakatāne District Council about the introduction of fees to the publicly owned boat ramps since they were announced.

He said the council should have put something in place for people who could not use an online portal before the fees came in.

“I was going out [fishing] with my grandfather when I was three years old.

If he was still alive, he would be fuming. He wouldn't even know how to switch a cellphone on.

"Why should someone be penalised just because they're not tech savvy? That's not fair."

For him, the main issue isn't the level of the fee, but the reason for its introduction and how it was introduced.

"I have a real issue with a private parking company being brought in to manage a publicly owned asset. I want transparency around who owns the company, how the contract was awarded and why the council couldn't manage this service itself," Bonne said.

“It’s not about the $20.”

He said some of the upgrades to the Whakatāne boat ramp were unnecessary and had caused the increased harbour costs.

If the council needed to introduce fees for its boat ramps, it should have managed it in-house, he said.

"It didn't have to be outsourced. Parking wardens could have managed it.

"I have a problem with the outsourcing of these things. It means the rich just get richer.

He said there should have been more transparency from the beginning, including a public tender process.

"There was no contract document that was drawn up by these managers to say, 'this is what we're going to propose because these are the benefits'."

The Beacon put some of the questions raised by Bonne and others to the council’s ports manager, Mark Read:

Q. These boat ramps were built through the hard work and generosity of local volunteers and have been maintained through ratepayer and community contributions. Why should a private company owned in Australia now be taking a share of the money collected from these public facilities?

A. The council introduced boat ramp fees so users contribute towards the operation, maintenance and improvement of boat ramps, rather than these costs falling solely on ratepayers. As with other electronic payment systems, there are costs to administer the service, and council engaged Stellar to provide the specialist system used to manage permits, payments and compliance. Intellico, trading as Stellar Parking Systems, is a New Zealand registered company.

Reporter's note: Intellico has a 40 percent Australian shareholding.

Q. There is concern the fees could deter boaties from other districts coming to, and spending money, in our district. How many boats from outside the district used the boat ramp before fees were instated? How does the council intend to keep track of this?

A. The council recognises the importance visiting boaties have for the local economy. While comprehensive historical data is not available, information from Whakatāne Coastguard, Whakatāne Sportfishing Club and Census data indicated a conservative estimate of around 8500 non-resident users a year at the Whakatāne Boat Ramp. The new fees system will give the council better data on non-resident. Over 800 local resident permits have been issued to date, with over 100 of those in the last week alone. More than 120 day passes were purchased in October. We expect day-pass use to increase significantly over the summer months.

Q. What percentage of fees or fines does the private company retain and what percentage is paid to council?

A. The arrangement is a profit-sharing model, with Stellar receiving a percentage of the revenue collected. The percentage and payment terms are withheld under section 7(2)(h) of the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987, to enable council to carry out commercial activities without prejudice or disadvantage.

Reporter's note: Councillors were told at a public meeting in June that $17 from every $20 fee would be paid to council. It is not known if this figure remains current.

Q. How much does the council spend on boat ramp repairs and maintenance?

A. The council does not separately record the cost of boat ramp repairs and maintenance. Two contracts covering repairs, maintenance and cleaning across all ports assets currently cost around $230,000 a year, with boat ramp clearing and cleaning, particularly following significant weather events, making up the largest portion of this expenditure. The council is transitioning some of these services in-house to reduce costs.

Q. Why is the Harbour Fund not being used for this repair and maintenance?

A. Harbour leases generated approximately $2.6 million in lease revenue in 2026. This income helps fund both the operational and capital requirements of the wider ports and harbour activities. Council's ability to grow harbour lease income is constrained, as many ground lease agreements only allow rent reviews every 14 years. As a result, revenue growth does not always keep pace with increasing costs.

Q. How much of the money received from the ePermit system is being reinvested into boat ramp repairs and maintenance?

A Revenue collected through boat ramp fees is recognised as income within the ports and harbour activity and is not separately ring-fenced for individual maintenance or repair projects. All revenue collected from boat ramp fees remains within the ports and harbour activity and will be used to support harbour-related operating, including

boat ramp maintenance, repairs, cleaning and other associated operational costs.

Q. Why has the council not publicly provided a transparent financial breakdown?

A. This information is withheld under section 7(2)(h) of the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987, to enable the council to carry out commercial activities without prejudice or disadvantage.

Q. Why should accessing a public boat ramp require people to navigate an online system operated by a private company?

A. Anyone who needs to sign up for a local resident permit but does not have access to a computer or smartphone can come into the service centre on Commerce Street for assistance, or contact the council on 07 306 0500 or [email protected] for help.
