Malaysia and Korea Win Trios Gold at the 2026 Asian Championships

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:28 · source · permalink

Malaysia's men and Korea's women won the trios titles at the 28th Asian Tenpin Bowling Championships in Doha on Wednesday.

Syafiq Ridhwan, Izz Naqiuddin Putera and Timmy Tan led a Malaysian one-two in the men's event with 4,193 pins, while Baek Seungja, Paek Yedam and Nam Hyebin beat Malaysia to women's gold by a single pin, 3,931 to 3,930.

The Asian Championships are the Asian Bowling Federation's flagship event for national teams, with medals in singles, doubles, trios, team of 5 and the Masters. It runs from November 12 to 22 at the 30-lane Qatar Bowling Federation Bowling Center, with 168 bowlers from 18 countries.

Trios was bowled over six games in two blocks of three, and every game also counts towards All Events, which decides the Masters field and qualification for the 2027 IBF World Championships in Korea.

Malaysia's Syafiq Ridhwan, Izz Naqiuddin Putera and Timmy Tan finished 79 pins claer of their own teammates in another Malaysia's trios team, who took silver with 4,114.

This was Malaysia's fourth straight Asian trios title. Syafiq also won trios in Bangkok in 2024, alongside Rafiq Ismail and Hafiz Zainuddin.

Korea took bronze with 4,083, 31 pins short of silver. Ryou Hojun, Han Jaehyeon and Kang Myungjin edged their own teammates Lee Jungsoo, Noh Youngdo and Kim Seongtak, who missed the podium by 29 pins with 4,054.

Korea won women's trios with 3,931 after a 684 in the sixth and final game. Malaysia led by 62 pins after five games, but shot 621 in the last game and finished on 3,930, one pin short.

The Korean trio of Baek Seungja, Paek Yedam and Nam Hyebin had led after the first block with 2,066, with Baek bowling 736 over the opening three games. Malaysia, with singles champion Gillian Lim, Faten Najihah and Sin Li Jane, overtook them in the second block before Korea's closing game turned the result around.

Another Malaysian trio won bronze with 3,862: Adelia Nur Irwan, Nur Syazwani Sahar and Nora Lyana Nastasia. Adelia and Nur Syazwani had already won women's doubles gold earlier in the week. Singapore, the defending champions, finished fourth (3,760) and fifth (3,715).

Where does the medal table stand?

Malaysia lead with 8 medals after six of the ten events: 3 gold, 4 silver and 1 bronze. Their golds came from Gillian Lim in women's singles, women's doubles and men's trios.

Korea are second with 3 medals: gold in women's trios, with bronze medals from Baek Seungja in singles and the men's trios. Hong Kong won men's doubles through Michael Mak and Tony Wong, and Kuwait's Abdulrazzaq Mohammad won men's singles.

Who leads All Events and the race for the World Championships?

Rafiq Ismail leads the men's All Events standings with a 246.07 average, more than 15 pins a game clear of Hong Kong's Michael Mak (230.93). Korea's Baek Seungja leads the women at 224.20, just ahead of her teammate Nam Hyebin (223.73) and Malaysia's Nur Syazwani Sahar (223.61).

All Events runs over 24 games, six each in singles, doubles, trios and team of 5. The top 16 men and top 16 women reach the round-robin Masters on November 21 and 22, and the top three in each go on to the stepladder finals for the last two gold medals.

The All Events pinfall of each country's top five bowlers also decides the places at the 2027 IBF World Championships in Korea, as our Asian Championships preview explained.

The team of 5 is bowled on Thursday and Friday, November 19 and 20, over two blocks of three games, with medals after six games.

The championships began with IBF president Sheikh Talal re-elected as head of the Asian Bowling Federation on November 12.
