The squads

Australia squad: Mitchell Marsh (c), Xavier Bartlett, Mahli Beardman, Alex Carey, Cooper Connolly, Jack Edwards, Cameron Green, Travis Head, Josh Inglis, Spencer Johnson, Riley Meredith, Ollie Peake, Matthew Renshaw, Liam Scott, Adam Zampa

Australia squad: Mitchell Marsh (c), Xavier Bartlett, Mahli Beardman, Alex Carey, Cooper Connolly, Jack Edwards, Cameron Green, Travis Head, Josh Inglis, Spencer Johnson, Riley Meredith, Ollie Peake, Matthew Renshaw, Liam Scott, Adam Zampa

Riley Meredith was added to Australia's squad prior to the second ODI and came straight into the team in Adelaide with rising star Mahli Beardman rested following his scintillating six-wicket debut. The Australian camp has indicated Beardman will be back in the XI for the series decider in Hobart, meaning one of the other quicks – Xavier Bartlett, Spencer Johnson, Meredith or allrounder Jack Edwards will drop out of the side.

Aussie fans have caught a glimpse of the nation's next generation of pace bowlers in the two ODIs so far, with all-format stars Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc and Josh Hazlewood sitting out the series as they prepare for this summer's four Tests against New Zealand. Nathan Ellis is still recovering from a side strain suffered during September's 1-2 ODI series loss to South Africa and has now also been ruled out of the five T20s against England that follow Wednesday's 50-over clash at Bellerive Oval. And with World Cup spots up for grabs in less than 12 months from now, Australia's new-look attack has plenty to play for.

Australia's top order will be out to make amends in the third ODI after collapsing to 4-29 to begin their big run chase in Adelaide. Matthew Renshaw and Cameron Green both hit half-centuries but were left with too much to do. Travis Head is yet to fire in this series after being dismissed for a duck and seven in the first two matches, while No.3 Cooper Connolly (27 and 0) got a start in the series opener but then missed out on Sunday.

Young gun Ollie Peake, only 20 years old but already with eight ODIs to his name, has been left on the bench so far alongside reserve gloveman Alex Carey, but both could come into contention if Australia decide to make a change to their batting line-up in Hobart. Likewise with their allrounders; Green appears to be playing as a batter only in this series, with Edwards getting the nod over Liam Scott in the first two ODIs.

England squad: Harry Brook (c), Jofra Archer, Gus Atkinson, Sonny Baker, Tom Banton, James Coles, Jordan Cox, Henry Crocombe, Sam Curran, Liam Dawson, Ben Duckett, Eddie Jack, Will Jacks, Jamie Overton, Adil Rashid, Joe Root

England squad: Harry Brook (c), Jofra Archer, Gus Atkinson, Sonny Baker, Tom Banton, James Coles, Jordan Cox, Henry Crocombe, Sam Curran, Liam Dawson, Ben Duckett, Eddie Jack, Will Jacks, Jamie Overton, Adil Rashid, Joe Root

England's batters bounced back emphatically in Adelaide, posting a record 355 on Aussie soil after being rolled for 131 in Perth. Skipper Harry Brook has lost the toss both times and said he would have bowled first on each occasion, but will he stick with setting a total if he calls correctly in Hobart given their success in the previous match?

Tom Banton led the way in Adelaide with his second ODI hundred in his past seven innings, and opening partner Ben Duckett is surely due to fire again after posting back-to-back centuries of his own to end last month's tri-series in Pakistan. England's top seven of Joe Root, Brook, wicketkeeper Jordan Cox in the absence of the injured Jos Buttler, Sam Curran and Will Jacks have been relatively stable of late, with Duckett, Brook and Curran the only ones not to get going in the two matches so far after Root, Cox and Jacks all scored half-centuries on Sunday.

Left-arm spinner Liam Dawson has been preferred over veteran leggie Adil Rashid, while young speedster Sonny Baker got his opportunity in Adelaide after Jamie Overton was omitted. Henry Crocombe is England's other emerging quick that is waiting in the wings. The tourists were hit by injury in the lead up to the ODI series with young gun Jacob Bethell (knee) and quick Josh Tongue (toe) sent home. Allrounder James Coles and seamer Eddie Jack, both already part of England's T20 squad, were called in as replacements.

Mahli Beardman is set to come back into the side after being managed for the second ODI. Could Australia now rest Spencer Johnson given he too has just returned from a prolonged back injury?

And is 20-year-old batter Ollie Peake a chance to play his first international on home soil after Australia's batting woes in Adelaide? England have found their winning formula and will be confident of getting the job done with the same XI in the series decider.

This is a big game for Mitch Marsh's Australia in the context of their recent ODI form, having won just one of their four series this year. They suffered 1-2 defeats to Pakistan and Bangladesh in June, and South Africa in late September, with their only victory a 3-0 sweep of Zimbabwe earlier that month. Australia have form over England on their side though, having won 11 of the past 15 ODI matches between the two sides.

England are on the cusp of their first ODI series win over Australia since 2018 when Eoin Morgan's side were at the peak of their powers en route to their maiden 50-over World Cup win the following year. They've won all three of their bilateral ODI series in 2026 with a 2-1 series triumph against Sri Lanka in Colombo in January followed by 2-1 wins over both India and Sri Lanka again at home. They'll be hoping to put their bottom-place finish in last month's tri-series against Pakistan and Sri Lanka behind them by returning to winning ways in Australia.

Players to watch

Travis Head (Australia): Hasn't got going at all in either match so far and chopped onto his stumps for the eighth time in his past 16 innings in last Friday's Perth opener. But the left-hander scored a sublime hundred when he last batted at Bellerive Oval against England in the 2021-22 Ashes.

Ben Duckett (England): Another left-handed opener who has fallen cheaply in both innings so far, with a pair of nines in Perth and Adelaide. Duckett came into the series on the back of hitting 346 runs at 86.5 in a tri-series in Pakistan, which included knocks of 105 (against Pakistan) and 167 (against Sri Lanka) in England's final two matches. His opening partnership with Tom Banton has also been a feature of England's ODI cricket this year, with the pair averaging 60 in nine innings since coming together at the top of the order in September. Australia's Travis Head and Mitch Marsh are just behind them too, with an ODI first-wicket partnership average of 59.62 in eight innings this year.

Rapid stats

  • Australia have won the decisive men's ODI against England when these sides have gone into the final match of a bilateral series tied on the past seven such occasions.

  • England's win by 135 runs in the second men's ODI of the current series snapped a four-game losing streak in the format against Australia in Australia; they will be looking to win back-to-back games against the Aussies in Australia for the first time since a three-game winning streak in January 2018.

  • Australia's loss by 135 runs in Adelaide was their third biggest men's ODI defeat in terms of runs in matches in Australia – 206 runs v New Zealand (January 1986) and 164 runs against West Indies (January 1987).

  • England have gone onto win the decisive men's ODI when going into the final match of a tied bilateral series on the past three such occasions; the last time they failed to do so was against West Indies in November 2024.

  • Australia have won all three of their previous men's ODIs against England at Bellerive Oval, including a successful chase of 304 in their most recent encounter at the ground (January 2015); it was the second-highest winning run chase by any team at the venue (India – 321 in February 2012).

  • Teams have scored at 6.23 runs per over in men's ODIs at Bellerive Oval since the start of 2015, the highest rate of any ground in Australia to have hosted more than two matches in that span.

  • Australia have recorded a 50 per cent win rate in men's ODIs so far in 2026 (7 wins, 7 losses); they will be looking to avoid going below 50 per cent for consecutive years (40 per cent in 2025); they had recorded a win rate of at least 60 per cent in four previous years from 2021 to 2024.

  • Josh Inglis (Australia) has taken 17 catches in men's ODIs in 2026, the most by any player to not have dropped a chance; he has also made four stumpings this year.

  • Tom Banton (England) has scored 50-plus runs in six of his past eight men's ODI innings including two centuries – 126 against Sri Lanka (September 27, 2026) and 121 against Australia (on Sunday).

  • Matthew Renshaw (Australia) scored 122 against Tasmania in his most recent List-A match in Hobart for Queensland (December 2024), his second-highest score in the format (156no against South Australia in November 2021).
