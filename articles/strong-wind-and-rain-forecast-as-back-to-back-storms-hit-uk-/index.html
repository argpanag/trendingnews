    20:18 BST 23 October

    Tara Mewawalla
    Live reporter

    Storm Austen caused major disruption throughout the day. Trains have been cancelled, trees felled, buildings damaged and homes left without power.

    The storm made landfall in south-west England overnight and tracked eastwards throughout the day.

    Jersey saw gusts of up to 84mph (135km/h) while in Brighton, on the south coast, part of the iconic West Pier - an abandoned structure that first opened in 1866 - collapsed into the sea.

    National Grid confirmed this afternoon it had restored power to more than 60,000 homes who were left without due to the storm. However, there are thousands still affected by power cuts.

    Tonight, yellow rain warnings are in place for parts of southern Scotland until 23:59 BST. Yellow wind warnings for large swathes of south-east England, North West England, Wales and the West Midlands will end at 21:00.

    Austen - the first named storm of the season - is easing, but a second storm is brewing.

    After a brief respite from the heavy rain and winds, Storm Boelo will bring heavy and persistent rain spreading across the country throughout Saturday.

    Boelo is also forecast to bring more strong winds across the country over the weekend. During the days, a yellow wind warning will be in place for vast swathes of Scotland, northern England and Northern Ireland. This will become an amber warning in Scotland from 21:00 on Saturday.

    We are ending our live coverage now but you can keep up to date with the latest on Storm Austen and Storm Boelo in our news story.

  4. Storm Austen clearing, Storm Boelo not far behindpublished at 19:12 BST 23 October
    19:12 BST 23 October

    Ben Rich
    Lead Weather Presenter

    Storm Austen is clearing eastwards and the winds will slowly ease overnight.

    The remaining Met Office yellow wind warning in parts of England and Wales is due to expire at 21:00 BST.

    Heavy rain will continue in places during the evening, especially in eastern Scotland where a Met Office yellow rain warning is in force until 23:59.

    This will also clear and parts of the UK will see a zone of clear, chilly weather for at least part of the night.

    This essentially marks the separation between Storm Austen and Storm Boelo.

    However, the gap won’t last long. Rain will arrive in western parts of the UK by early Saturday morning – the first sign of Boelo’s arrival.

    That rain will spread across the country during the day, becoming heavy and persistent.

    Winds will pick up again with gales around many coasts - peaking overnight on Saturday and into Sunday with the Met Office amber wind warning covering almost all of Scotland, the far north of Northern Ireland and some northern parts of England.

  15. Storm Austen hits force 10 in Jersey with 84mph gustspublished at 17:08 BST 23 October
    17:08 BST 23 October

    Storm Austen reached force 10 in Jersey with wind gusts of up to 84mph (135km/h) overnight, according to Jersey Met.

    Force 10 is a measure on the Beaufort wind force scale where trees can be uprooted and buildings damaged.

    Paul Aked, head of Jersey Met, says there were gusts in Guernsey up to 76mph (122km/h), which is severe gale force nine.

    Storm Boelo is forecast to track much further north than the Channel Islands, leaving Jersey and Guernsey on the fringes of the system, he says.

    Jersey Met's forecast indicates further unsettled weather is expected in the coming days.

Storm Austen hits force 10 in Jersey with 84mph gustspublished at 17:08 BST 23 October
17:08 BST 23 October

A tree felled by Storm Austen crushes a wall in Gorey, Jersey

Storm Austen reached force 10 in Jersey with wind gusts of up to 84mph (135km/h) overnight, according to Jersey Met.

Force 10 is a measure on the Beaufort wind force scale where trees can be uprooted and buildings damaged.

Paul Aked, head of Jersey Met, says there were gusts in Guernsey up to 76mph (122km/h), which is severe gale force nine.

Storm Boelo is forecast to track much further north than the Channel Islands, leaving Jersey and Guernsey on the fringes of the system, he says.

Jersey Met's forecast indicates further unsettled weather is expected in the coming days.

Travel disruption continues as services issue warnings of weekend delayspublished at 17:01 BST 23 October
17:01 BST 23 October

Storm Austen is disrupting travel across parts of the country as we enter rush hour.

National Rail says routes across England, Wales and Scotland have been impacted, and travel today and over the weekend. While there are no warnings in place for Monday, it says some disruption is still possible depending on the impact of Storms Austen and Boelo.

Great Western Railway warns that there are multiple cancellations to the network, including between Penzance and Taunton.

Some services to Exeter St Davids, Barnstaple, Truro, Falmouth Docks, Yeovil Pen Mill, Basingstoke and Reading have been cancelled due to trees blocking the railways.

CrossCountry services in the Bournemouth area have restarted after a tree blocked the line, though the rail provider advises services may restart from Southampton Central. A different tree is still blocking the line between Lydney and Gloucester.

Scotrail says due to reports of high water levels on the tracks in the Schotts area, Edinburgh and Glasgow Central services are suspended.

Transport for Scotland has advised passengers to avoid all travel tomorrow unless necessary.

Airports in England's south-west and the Channel Islands are also experiencing disruptions.

Thousands lose power and part of Brighton's West Pier collapses as Storm Austen whips UKpublished at 16:17 BST 23 October
16:17 BST 23 October

A yellow warning for rain is in place in parts of Scotland, while yellow wind warnings stay in place in south-east England and parts of Wales until 17:00, and in south and east England and northern Wales until 21:00

Storm Austen is causing widespread disruption in parts of the UK, leaving thousands of homes without power and travel plans disrupted.

In Brighton, part of the iconic West Pier - an abandoned structure that first opened in 1866 - has collapsed. Stephen Royle, who lives opposite, says the waves rolling in were "enormous" and he is "shocked" by how much of the pier ended up in the sea.

National Grid says almost 18,000 properties have been affected by power cuts today, including 13,000 in Cornwall.

Hannah Dawson-Turk says she and her family had to be evacuated from a holiday park in Perranporth, Cornwall, yesterday afternoon due to the weather, adding "the night before last, it sounded like our caravan was going to be ripped apart".

Earlier today, a wind speed of 84mph was recorded at Jersey Airport, causing trees and other debris to fall and block roads, the infrastructure department said.

National Rail has advised that many routes are travelling at slower speeds, with delays and cancellations possible, while ferry operators Stena Line and Wightlink have cancelled services - more travel advice here.

As we move into Saturday, Storm Boelo - which is travelling higher up in the northern parts of the country - is forecast to bring more strong winds and heavy rain in parts.

The Met Office has put in place an amber warning for wind on Saturday and Sunday to cover parts of Scotland, northern England and Northern Ireland, while a lower yellow warning for wind also covers other parts.

In Wales, the National Grid reports almost 2,000 homes are without power between Milford Haven, in the west, and Usk, in the east.

It says the biggest power cut is in Cardigan, where more than 500 homes are affected.

In north Wales, SP Energy reports power cuts at homes in Pwllheli, Bontnewydd, Llanidloes, Bodelwyddan, Kinmel Bay and Prestatyn.

Two yellow warnings for wind, external are in place, with one ending at 17:00 BST and another at 21:00 in some areas. Periods of heavy rain are also expected.

Injuries and danger to life from flying debris are possible, with drivers and those using public transport asked to plan ahead, the Met Office advises.
