Ex-wife of Tommy Robinson sentenced over £396k loan

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:38 · source · permalink

The ex-wife of political activist Tommy Robinson has been handed a suspended jail sentence after she failed to give liquidators information about a £396,630 loan owed to her company.

Jenna Lennon, 41, from Bedfordshire, pleaded guilty to one charge of failing to deliver up books and records about her company, Hope & Pride Limited, between 22 September 2023 and 1 December 2025.

The lack of information about the loan meant liquidators were unable to verify the "explanations and figures" around it.

The mother-of-three had claimed she was too dependent on an accountant and was "disorganised with public records", her solicitor advocate David Rosen told Southwark Crown Court on Thursday.

Hope & Pride Limited, of which Lennon was the director, had gone into creditors' voluntary liquidation – meaning she had started to close it down.

Judge Tony Baumgartner sentenced her to six months' imprisonment, suspended for a year.

Lennon was also ordered to complete 100 hours of unpaid work and pay £16,000 of prosecution costs by 28 May.

Lennon and Tommy Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, divorced in February 2021 - and Robinson declared himself bankrupt in March 2021.

In a statement, the Insolvency Service said liquidators had repeatedly requested the company's books and records from Lennon, but she did not provide them.

It said her failure to provide "sufficient records" meant liquidators "could not verify the nature and source of more than £1.1m paid into Hope & Pride Limited's bank accounts".

An Insolvency Service spokesperson said: "Without adequate records, liquidators can be prevented from establishing what happened to company money, what is owed to creditors and whether funds were used for legitimate business purposes.

"Lennon's failure left significant questions about Hope & Pride Limited's financial affairs unanswered.

"Her seven-year director ban and this criminal prosecution demonstrate the consequences directors can face when they fail to meet their legal obligations."

The judge told Lennon: "The obligation of an officer of a company to deliver up all the books or papers in his custody, or under his control, belonging to [the] company is an important one."

"I am not asked to sentence you on the basis that you engaged in fraudulent trading, that you misappropriated company money, or that you personally benefited by any particular amount – I make no such findings."
