Leclerc ‘embarrassed’ for Ferrari after Hamilton clash

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:57 · source · permalink

Charles Leclerc received a 10-second time penalty after colliding with Ferrari team mate Lewis Hamilton during the Las Vegas Grand Prix, dropping him to 10th place.

Charles Leclerc has admitted he felt "embarrassed" for Ferrari after his collision with team mate Lewis Hamilton at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, with the Monegasque accepting the 10-second time penalty he received for the incident.

The Ferrari pair came together at Turn 7 on Lap 38 as Leclerc attempted to make a move up the inside of Hamilton, who was battling a McLaren ahead. The stewards deemed Leclerc wholly to blame for the collision, ruling that he had not earned the right to the corner after approaching from a "considerable distance" behind his team mate.

The penalty proved costly for Leclerc, who crossed the line in sixth place but dropped to 10th in the final classification after the 10 seconds were added to his race time. Hamilton, meanwhile, finished seventh after serving a separate 10-second penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage during an earlier battle with Lando Norris.

Speaking to F1 TV after the race, Leclerc said: "At the end of the day, we have stewards who are trying their best, trying to understand who is at fault whenever there is a clash. Today, I was considered to be fault, and I took a 10-second penalty. I'm not going to argue that. I will look back at it.

"It felt like there was plenty of space on the inside of Turn 7 because of the fight with Lewis and the McLaren in front, and I thought that it was definitely safe to be on the inside there.

"Unfortunately, I don't think Lewis, with the angle of his car, saw me in the mirrors and the gap that was once there at the apex was not so much there. But not to blame Lewis, I think he didn't see me, and I just feel very bad for the team again."

The incident comes just weeks after the Scuderia pairing clashed during the Mexico City Grand Prix, where Leclerc received a five-second penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage over Hamilton during their opening-lap battle.

Asked about the latest contact between the two, Leclerc reflected on the discussions that had taken place following Mexico City and explained why he felt the incidents were different.

"We've had discussions, and I think the discussions have gone very well," he explained. "But today, I don't think it's similar to Mexico, where we probably overdrove, both of us, and kind of went over the limit.

"That was maybe more of a racing incident, but it's still not acceptable to have a clash between cars. Again, I'll accept the blame for whatever comes with this incident because, at the end, I took the penalty. But I'll look back at it and analyse it."

Speaking separately to Sky Sports F1, Leclerc was also asked whether Ferrari needed to introduce stricter rules between their drivers following another on-track clash.

"I just felt quite embarrassed for the team in general," he admitted. "I will review."
