Push for $1,900 annual tax for Aussie homeowners as property market slows

TheTools · 19.10.2026 23:28 · source · permalink

A federal MP has called for Australian states to wean themselves off a reliance of stamp duty as the housing slowdown sees state governments face billions less in revenue to provide services. And the federal government should assist in paving the way for a difficult transition towards an ongoing land tax.

That's the assertion from Independent NSW MP Allegra Spender who is the latest to call for property to be taxed on a more consistent, annual basis rather than a lump sum stamp duty at the point of sale. It comes as stamp duty revenue in her home state has fallen by nearly a quarter in recent months.

"The sharp drop in stamp duty shows the folly of relying on a highly cyclical tax to pay for state-delivered essential services," Spender argued.

"States should now set out a transition to a more stable, less distortive land tax and the feds should back them," she posted online Tuesday.

It's something NSW partially implemented at the start of 2023, under former premier Dominic Perrottet which allowed first home buyers to choose between paying stamp duty or an annual land tax of $400 per year, plus 0.3 per cent of the land value. For a property with land value of $500,000, that equates to an annual land tax of $1,900 (as opposed to a more than $40,000 stamp duty).

"NSW Labor inherited a modest plan on that transition that they could have built on," Spender said.

"Instead they dropped it for short-term political gain."

The perennial debate has been forced back into the spotlight as states face a warning from economists of subdued stamp duty revenue into the near term future.

Data from Revenue NSW showed that the government collected $631.1 million from residential property transactions in August, down 24 per cent from a year earlier as sale volumes dropped 18 per cent. September was similar, dropping 23 per cent, to $663.7 million for the month.

Economists from UBS say a 20 per cent decline in stamp duty will leave state budgets $30 billion worse off in the next four years, the AFR reported this week.

Stamp duty 'punishes' some

Independent housing economist Cameron Kusher, like most economists, supports the notion of an annual land tax for property owners. It would mean people who move more frequently would be better off, he told Yahoo Finance.

"Some people move on a regular basis, [and] every time they move, they're essentially punished by having to pay stamp duty," he said.
