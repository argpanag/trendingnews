There are a raft of changes being introduced next month by both federal and state authorities. From credit card surcharge bans to NDIS and aged care support, these will affect Australians all over the country. 

Many banks and businesses have already started introducing changes for their customers, while others won't commence until next Thursday, October 1. While most will take place nationwide, some are only relevant to particular states. 

Here's a breakdown of the upcoming October 1 changes.

Card surcharge ban and interchange fees 

From next Thursday, consumers will no longer face surcharges when making a card payment. 

When paying at a merchant, the surcharges for EFTPOS, Mastercard and Visa will no longer be able to be passed on at the point of purchase, which the RBA says currently cost consumers $1.2 billion each year. 

To counteract absorbing these higher costs, some businesses are considering offering discounts for cash payments. Alternatively, some say they may only accept cash or Pay ID instead. If they're not already, other businesses will choose to increase costs on their goods and services instead. 

Along with the surcharge ban, the cap on the interchange fees paid by businesses to banks will be reduced. Interchange fees are charged by payment service providers to merchants and, upon October 1, will be lowered from 0.8 per cent to 0.3 percent of the value of the transaction. 

But for customers, that means credit card rewards are chaning.

Credit card rewards

With major monetary losses set to hit banks from these fee changes, many are taking action to find that revenue elsewhere. 

Most commonly, banks have cut down or completely eliminated the credit card perks and rewards they offer to cardholders. 

"For some people, this will be a major change that may push them to a different card or to not have a credit card at all," Canstar's data insights director Sally Tindall recently told Yahoo Finance. 

Primarily on the chopping block are the complimentary travel insurance offerings that come with a majority of credit cards, although some banks have also scrapped their reward points partnerships.

NDIS funding

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) is adjusting the budget allocations in participant plans for both social, economic and community participation supports, and improved daily living skill supports.
