Minimum wage France 2026 per hour: the 1 December rise, what counts and how to claim unpaid pay

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:43 · source · permalink

“Minimum wage France 2026 per hour” is a question many people type before taking a job in France, and the answer is about to change. Since 1 June 2026, the French minimum wage, the salaire minimum interprofessionnel de croissance or SMIC, has stood at €12.31 gross per hour in mainland France, under an arrêté (ministerial order) of 22 May 2026. On 9 November 2026, after a meeting with unions and employers’ organisations, the Prime Minister announced that the SMIC would rise by 1.6% from 1 December 2026, bringing the net monthly amount for a full-time job to €1,501.6, according to the government’s announcement on info.gouv.fr. The official labour-law portal adds that the decree setting the new figure will be published “soon” (Code du travail numérique, news item on the December 2026 rise).

This guide is written for people who work in France without being French: British, Irish, American, Canadian or Australian employees, people on a working holiday, international students with a side job, posted workers and young people starting out in hotels, restaurants or shops. It explains how much the SMIC is, how the December rise works legally, who is entitled to it, what an employer may count towards it, and what to do if your payslip shows less. Each rule is linked to the official French text or to the court decision that applies it, quoted in French and translated in brackets. The exact December figure will be the one published in the Journal officiel, France’s official gazette, so this article separates what is already law from what has only been announced.

I. Minimum wage France 2026 per hour: how much is the SMIC and who is entitled to it?

A. France minimum wage per hour and per month: €12.31 today, the 1 December 2026 rise and how the rate is set

The SMIC is not a recommendation. The Labour Code (Code du travail) gives it two purposes. Article L3231-2 states: « Le salaire minimum de croissance assure aux salariés dont les rémunérations sont les plus faibles : 1° La garantie de leur pouvoir d’achat ; 2° Une participation au développement économique de la nation. » (The growth-linked minimum wage guarantees to the lowest-paid employees: 1° protection of their purchasing power; 2° a share in the economic development of the nation.)

The Code itself contains no euro amount. Article L3231-7 provides: « Le taux du salaire minimum de croissance est fixé par voie réglementaire à l’issue d’une procédure déterminée par décret. » (The rate of the minimum wage is set by regulation at the end of a procedure laid down by decree.) That is why the figure you see in guides always comes from a decree or an order, never from the Code.

Three mechanisms move the rate. First, an annual review: the purchasing-power share is ensured « par la fixation du salaire minimum de croissance, chaque année avec effet au 1er janvier » (by setting the minimum wage every year with effect from 1 January), under Article L3231-6. Second, an automatic increase linked to inflation. Article L3231-5 reads: « Lorsque l’indice national des prix à la consommation atteint un niveau correspondant à une hausse d’au moins 2 % par rapport à l’indice constaté lors de l’établissement du salaire minimum de croissance immédiatement antérieur, le salaire minimum de croissance est relevé dans la même proportion à compter du premier jour du mois qui suit la publication de l’indice entraînant ce relèvement. » (When the national consumer price index reaches a level corresponding to a rise of at least 2% compared with the index recorded when the previous minimum wage was set, the minimum wage is raised in the same proportion from the first day of the month following publication of the index that triggers the increase.) Third, a discretionary increase during the year: under Article L3231-10, « En cours d’année, le salaire minimum de croissance peut être porté, par voie réglementaire, à un niveau supérieur » (during the year, the minimum wage may be raised by regulation to a higher level) than the one produced by the automatic mechanism.

The rate in force today comes from the order of 22 May 2026 on the increase of the minimum wage, published in the Journal officiel. Its first article provides that from 1 June 2026, in mainland France and in most overseas territories, the hourly amount is « porté à 12,31 € l’heure » (raised to €12.31 per hour), and that in Mayotte it is set at €9.56 per hour. Its preamble refers to Article L3231-5 and to the consumer price index for April 2026, which is the automatic mechanism described above. The same order sets the minimum garanti, a separate reference figure used among other things to value meals provided by the employer, at €4.35 from 1 June 2026. The public administration website service-public.fr gives the corresponding amounts: €12.31 gross per hour, €1,867.02 gross per month and €22,404.20 gross per year for a full-time job, with an indicative net monthly figure of €1,477.93 after employee social contributions.

How do you get from an hourly rate to a monthly one? Article L3242-1 provides that for a job equal to the legal working week, « la rémunération mensuelle due au salarié se calcule en multipliant la rémunération horaire par les 52/12 de la durée légale hebdomadaire » (the monthly pay due to the employee is calculated by multiplying the hourly pay by 52/12 of the legal weekly working time). With a 35-hour week, that gives about 151.67 hours a month, hence the gross monthly figure of €1,867.02. Part-time employees are entitled to the same hourly rate for each hour worked; their monthly pay is lower only because they work fewer hours.

What will change on 1 December 2026? The government has announced a 1.6% rise and a net monthly amount of €1,501.6. It also states that, with this rise, the SMIC will have increased by 5.3% over 2026. Arithmetically, 1.6% applied to €12.31 gives about €12.51 gross per hour, and applied to €1,867.02 about €1,896.89 gross per month. These are calculations, not official figures: the legally binding rate will be the one written in the decree. As of 22 November 2026, the Journal officiel issues published between 6 and 22 November 2026 that we checked contained no text setting the new amount; the only text with the minimum wage in its title that we found there, a decree of 19 November 2026, amends the rules of the group of experts on the minimum wage. Until the new text is published and takes effect, €12.31 remains the rate an employer must respect. If the decree confirms the announced date, the new rate will be owed for hours worked from 1 December 2026, and the December payslip will be the first one to check.

Two more questions often appear in searches. “Minimum wage France after tax”: the €1,477.93 net figure is calculated after social contributions only; French income tax is then withheld at source at a personal rate, and the payslip must show that deduction. “Is the minimum wage higher in Paris?”: no, the order of 22 May 2026 sets one rate for mainland France, with a separate rate for Mayotte. A collective agreement covering your sector can, however, set a higher minimum for your job, which brings us to who is entitled to what.

B. Minimum wage in France for foreigners, under-18s, apprentices and international students

Nationality plays no part in the SMIC. The book of the Labour Code on wages opens with a short rule in Article L3211-1: « Les dispositions du présent livre sont applicables aux employeurs de droit privé et à leurs salariés. » (The provisions of this book apply to private-law employers and their employees.) Article L3231-1 extends the SMIC rules to staff of certain public bodies employed under private law. A British, American or Australian employee hired by a French restaurant, ski resort, shop or company is therefore entitled to the same minimum as a French colleague. What changes from one situation to another is the right to work and, for some groups, the way the minimum is calculated.

Your contract can be in French and you still have rights over its language. Article L1221-3 states: « Lorsque le salarié est étranger et le contrat constaté par écrit, une traduction du contrat est rédigée, à la demande du salarié, dans la langue de ce dernier. » (Where the employee is a foreign national and the contract is in writing, a translation of the contract is drawn up, at the employee’s request, in the employee’s language.) The same article adds: « En cas de discordance entre les deux textes, seul le texte rédigé dans la langue du salarié étranger peut être invoqué contre ce dernier. » (If the two texts differ, only the text in the foreign employee’s language can be relied on against that employee.) Asking for a translation is therefore a useful first step when the hourly rate or the working time is unclear.

Workers posted to France by a company established abroad are covered too. Article L1262-4 provides that « L’employeur détachant temporairement un salarié sur le territoire national lui garantit l’égalité de traitement avec les salariés employés par les entreprises de la même branche d’activité établies sur le territoire national » (an employer temporarily posting an employee to France guarantees equal treatment with employees of companies in the same sector established in France). Among the listed matters is remuneration, including « paiement du salaire, y compris les majorations pour les heures supplémentaires » (payment of wages, including overtime premiums). A posting contract governed by another country’s law does not allow pay below the French minimum for the work done in France.

What if you were employed without a valid work permit? French law does not let the employer keep the wages. Article L8252-2 gives the foreign worker, for the period of illegal employment, a right « Au paiement du salaire et des accessoires de celui-ci, conformément aux dispositions légales, conventionnelles et aux stipulations contractuelles applicables à son emploi » (to payment of wages and related sums in accordance with the legal, collective-agreement and contractual provisions applicable to the job), and adds: « A défaut de preuve contraire, les sommes dues au salarié correspondent à une relation de travail présumée d’une durée de trois mois. » (Unless the contrary is proven, the sums owed correspond to an employment relationship presumed to have lasted three months.) The same article provides a lump-sum payment of three months’ wages when the relationship ends, unless other rules give more.

“Minimum wage France under 18” is a real search because young workers are treated differently. Article D3231-3 provides a reduction of 20% before the age of 17 and 10% between 17 and 18, and adds: « Cet abattement est supprimé pour les jeunes travailleurs justifiant de six mois de pratique professionnelle dans la branche d’activité dont ils relèvent. » (This reduction is removed for young workers who can show six months’ professional experience in their sector.) Applied to €12.31, the reduction gives about €9.85 per hour before 17 and about €11.08 between 17 and 18. On 4 September 2026, the Bourges Court of Appeal applied this rule to a teenager whose apprenticeship contract had been declared void: he could claim, for the hours he had worked, « au paiement des salaires sur la base du SMIC ou du salaire minimum conventionnel s’il est plus favorable, avec les abattements tenant à l’âge prévus par l’article D. 3231-3 du code du travail » (payment of wages on the basis of the SMIC or of the collective-agreement minimum if it is more favourable, with the age-related reductions provided by Article D3231-3) (CA Bourges, 4 September 2026, no. 25/00792).

Apprentices have their own scale. Article D6222-26 sets their minimum as a percentage of the SMIC depending on age and year of the contract: for example 27% in the first year for those aged 16 to 17, 43% in the first year for those aged 18 to 20, 53% in the first year for those aged 21 to 25 (or the collective-agreement minimum if higher), and 100% for those aged 26 and over. Because these are percentages, each increase of the SMIC, including the one announced for December, automatically raises the apprentice’s minimum.

“France minimum wage for international students” is the last frequent question. Students are paid like any other employee; the residence permit only limits the number of hours. Article L422-1 of the Code governing entry and residence of foreign nationals (CESEDA) states that the student card « donne droit à l’exercice, à titre accessoire, d’une activité professionnelle salariée dans la limite de 60 % de la durée de travail annuelle » (entitles its holder to work as an employee, on an ancillary basis, within the limit of 60% of annual working time). The Labour Code translates this into hours: under Article R5221-26, the student « est autorisé à exercer une activité salariée, à titre accessoire, dans la limite d’une durée annuelle de travail égale à 964 heures » (is authorised to work as an employee, on an ancillary basis, up to 964 hours per year). Exceeding the limit puts the residence permit at risk. On 20 November 2025, the Lyon Administrative Court of Appeal held that this limitation « lui est opposable notamment lors d’une demande de renouvellement de son titre de séjour » (can be held against the student in particular when applying to renew the residence permit), and upheld a refusal to renew a student permit where the student worked more than the 60% limit (CAA Lyon, 20 November 2025, no. 24LY02845). Keeping a record of your hours protects both your wages and your residence file.

II. Paid below the minimum wage in France: how to check your payslip and claim unpaid pay

A. What counts towards the SMIC on a French payslip (bulletin de paie)

The comparison is made per hour of actual work, not on the total paid. Article D3231-6 provides: « Le salaire horaire à prendre en considération pour l’application de l’article D. 3231-5 est celui qui correspond à une heure de travail effectif compte tenu des avantages en nature et des majorations diverses ayant le caractère de fait d’un complément de salaire. » (The hourly wage to be taken into account is the one corresponding to one hour of actual work, including benefits in kind and the various additional payments which in fact amount to a supplement to wages.) It then lists exclusions: « Sont exclues les sommes versées à titre de remboursement de frais, les majorations pour heures supplémentaires prévues par la loi et la prime de transport. » (Excluded are sums paid as reimbursement of expenses, statutory overtime premiums and the transport allowance.) When the contractual rate falls below the SMIC, Article D3231-5 requires the employer to pay « un complément calculé de façon à porter leur rémunération au montant de ce salaire minimum de croissance » (a supplement calculated so as to bring their pay up to the minimum wage).

The courts have drawn the line case by case, with one guiding question: is the sum paid in return for work? Paid breaks are the classic example. Under Article L3121-2, « Le temps nécessaire à la restauration ainsi que les temps consacrés aux pauses sont considérés comme du temps de travail effectif lorsque les critères définis à l’article L. 3121-1 sont réunis. » (Time needed for meals and break time count as actual working time when the criteria of Article L3121-1 are met.) When the employee is free during the break, the payment for it cannot be used to reach the minimum. In a case about a food company, the Cour de cassation held that where breaks were not working time, « les primes les rémunérant, qui ne sont pas la contrepartie du travail et dont la détermination dépend de facteurs généraux sur lesquels les salariés n’influent pas, sont exclues du salaire devant être comparé au SMIC » (the bonuses paying for them, which are not paid in return for work and depend on general factors that employees do not influence, are excluded from the wage to be compared with the SMIC) (Cass. soc., 13 July 2010, no. 09-42.890). It reached the same result for supermarket staff in 2012 (Cass. soc., 21 March 2012, no. 10-21.737).

By contrast, a bonus linked to output counts. In a 2015 case, the Court overturned a decision that had left a production bonus out of the calculation, because the bonus depended on the tonnage the employee helped to produce: « la prime était déterminée en fonction du tonnage produit auquel participait le salarié, de sorte qu’elle constituait la contrepartie d’un travail » (the bonus was determined according to the tonnage produced with the employee’s participation, so that it was paid in return for work) (Cass. soc., 4 February 2015, no. 13-18.523). The fact that a bonus is paid every month is not enough on its own: in 2021, the Court criticised judges who had included a monthly “productivity bonus” without explaining how it was paid in return for, or on the occasion of, the work (Cass. soc., 8 September 2021, no. 20-16.120).

Seniority bonuses are the opposite case. In a 2009 claim brought by a saleswoman seeking a top-up “to reach the SMIC”, the Court ruled, on the basis of the collective agreement of her sector, that « la prime d’ancienneté conventionnelle, qui n’est pas versée en contrepartie du travail, ne peut être prise en compte pour la détermination du minimum conventionnel garanti » (the seniority bonus provided by the collective agreement, which is not paid in return for work, cannot be taken into account to determine the guaranteed collective-agreement minimum), whereas her 2% commission on sales was directly linked to her work and had to be counted (Cass. soc., 6 May 2009, no. 07-44.207). Note that this ruling concerns the collective-agreement minimum, which is a separate floor from the SMIC.

Benefits in kind are part of the comparison, which matters for seasonal staff who are housed or fed. In 2022, ruling on store managers working under a special status, the Court held that « il doit être tenu compte, pour déterminer si les gérants ont été remplis de leurs droits au regard du SMIC, de l’avantage en nature que constitue ce logement » (account must be taken, in determining whether the managers received what they were owed under the SMIC, of the benefit in kind represented by that housing) (Cass. soc., 22 June 2022, no. 20-20.273). The valuation is regulated. For meals, Article D3231-10 provides that, unless a collective agreement says otherwise, « la nourriture est évaluée par journée à deux fois le minimum garanti ou, pour un seul repas, à une fois ce minimum » (food is valued at twice the guaranteed minimum per day or, for a single meal, at once that minimum). With the minimum garanti at €4.35 since 1 June 2026, that is €8.70 per day or €4.35 per meal. In hotels, cafés and restaurants, Article D3231-13 provides that the food so calculated « n’entre en compte que pour la moitié de sa valeur » (is only counted at half its value). Under Article D3231-9, where pay habitually includes food, housing or other benefits in kind, the cash minimum is the SMIC less the amounts fixed to value those benefits.

Other items regularly cause disputes. Meal vouchers do not count towards a collective-agreement minimum: « les sommes consacrées par l’employeur pour l’acquisition par le salarié de titres-restaurant n’étant pas versées en contrepartie du travail, elles n’entrent pas dans le calcul de la rémunération à comparer avec le salaire minimum conventionnel » (the sums spent by the employer on meal vouchers for the employee are not paid in return for work and do not enter into the pay compared with the collective-agreement minimum) (Cass. soc., 3 July 2019, no. 17-18.210). For a thirteenth-month payment, the Court held, again for a collective-agreement minimum, that « si le treizième mois fait partie des éléments de rémunération à prendre en compte dans la comparaison avec le salaire minimum conventionnel, en l’absence de disposition conventionnelle contraire, son montant ne doit être pris en compte que pour le mois où il a été effectivement versé » (although the thirteenth month is part of the pay compared with the collective-agreement minimum, unless the agreement provides otherwise, it is only counted for the month in which it is actually paid) (Cass. soc., 14 November 2018, no. 17-22.539). In practice, an annual bonus does not cure eleven months of pay below the floor. As for tips, the decisions we read for this article do not address whether tips handed directly by customers count towards the SMIC; the list in Article D3231-6 does not mention them, so this point should be checked against your sector’s agreement and your own payslips.

The SMIC and your sector’s minimum are two different floors. Collective agreements set their own pay scales, and Article L3231-3 prohibits indexing them on the SMIC: « Sont interdites, dans les conventions ou accords collectifs de travail, les clauses comportant des indexations sur le salaire minimum de croissance » (clauses indexing pay on the minimum wage are prohibited in collective agreements). Scales therefore lag behind when the SMIC rises. The official labour-law portal points out that, after the December rise, more sectors may have scales below the SMIC, and that in that case the employer must pay the SMIC without waiting for a new sector agreement (Code du travail numérique). Your entitlement is whichever floor is higher for your hours.

To check all this, use your payslip. Article L3243-2 provides: « Lors du paiement du salaire, l’employeur remet aux personnes mentionnées à l’article L. 3243-1 une pièce justificative dite bulletin de paie. » (When paying wages, the employer gives the employee a supporting document called a payslip.) Article R3243-1 lists what it must show, including « La période et le nombre d’heures de travail auxquels se rapporte le salaire en distinguant, s’il y a lieu, les heures payées au taux normal et celles qui comportent une majoration » (the period and the number of working hours covered by the wage, distinguishing where relevant between hours paid at the normal rate and those carrying a premium), the gross pay, the employer’s sector agreement and your classification. Take the base pay plus the items that count, divide by the hours actually worked that month, and compare the result with €12.31, or with the new rate for hours worked from 1 December 2026 once the decree applies.

B. How to claim unpaid minimum wage in France: evidence, deadline and the labour court

Most minimum-wage disputes are really disputes about hours: the hourly rate looks right, but the hours worked were higher than the hours paid. French law shares the burden of proof. Article L3171-4 provides that in such a dispute « l’employeur fournit au juge les éléments de nature à justifier les horaires effectivement réalisés par le salarié » (the employer provides the judge with evidence of the hours actually worked by the employee). The Cour de cassation has explained the employee’s part: « il appartient au salarié de présenter, à l’appui de sa demande, des éléments suffisamment précis quant aux heures non rémunérées qu’il prétend avoir accomplies afin de permettre à l’employeur, qui assure le contrôle des heures de travail effectuées, d’y répondre utilement en produisant ses propres éléments » (the employee must present sufficiently precise evidence of the unpaid hours claimed, so that the employer, who is responsible for monitoring working time, can respond by producing its own evidence) (Cass. soc., 20 January 2021, no. 19-21.755, a hotel and restaurant case). In practice, keep a daily log of start and finish times, rota screenshots, messages asking you to stay late, and your payslips.

Then act within the limitation period, the prescription. Article L3245-1 provides: « L’action en paiement ou en répétition du salaire se prescrit par trois ans à compter du jour où celui qui l’exerce a connu ou aurait dû connaître les faits lui permettant de l’exercer. » (An action for payment or recovery of wages is time-barred after three years from the day on which the claimant knew or should have known the facts enabling the claim.) It continues: « La demande peut porter sur les sommes dues au titre des trois dernières années à compter de ce jour ou, lorsque le contrat de travail est rompu, sur les sommes dues au titre des trois années précédant la rupture du contrat. » (The claim may cover sums owed for the last three years from that day or, where the contract has ended, sums owed for the three years before it ended.) The Cour de cassation has specified when the period starts: « le délai de prescription des salaires court à compter de la date à laquelle la créance salariale est devenue exigible » (the limitation period for wages runs from the date on which the wage claim became payable), and « Pour les salariés payés au mois, la date d’exigibilité du salaire correspond à la date habituelle du paiement des salaires en vigueur dans l’entreprise » (for employees paid monthly, the date on which wages become payable is the usual pay date in the company) (Cass. soc., 24 January 2024, no. 22-21.387). Each month of underpayment therefore has its own three-year clock, starting from that month’s pay date.

Before going to court, a written request to the employer, with your own calculation and copies of the payslips, often settles the matter, especially around a SMIC increase when payroll software has not been updated. If it does not, the claim goes to the conseil de prud’hommes, the French labour court; the firm’s employment law team in Paris works on minimum-wage and back-pay disputes of this kind. Leaving France does not prevent a claim. Article R1412-1 designates the court for the place where the work is performed, and adds: « Le salarié peut également saisir les conseils de prud’hommes du lieu où l’engagement a été contracté ou celui du lieu où l’employeur est établi. » (The employee may also bring the case before the labour court for the place where the contract was concluded or where the employer is established.)

When the debt is obvious, there is a faster route than a full hearing: the référé, an urgent procedure. Article R1455-7 provides: « Dans le cas où l’existence de l’obligation n’est pas sérieusement contestable, la formation de référé peut accorder une provision au créancier » (where the existence of the obligation cannot seriously be disputed, the urgent-applications bench may award an interim payment to the creditor). The form of the order matters. In October 2025, the Cour de cassation quashed an order in which a labour court had awarded unpaid wages outright, holding: « En statuant ainsi, en allouant un rappel de salaire et non une provision, le conseil de prud’hommes, qui a excédé ses pouvoirs, a violé le texte susvisé. » (By awarding back pay rather than an interim payment, the labour court exceeded its powers and breached the text.) It then ordered the employer to pay the same amount as an interim payment (Cass. soc., 8 October 2025, no. 24-17.646). An urgent application should therefore ask for a provision, an interim payment on account.

Paying below the minimum is also an offence. Article R3233-1 punishes with a fifth-class fine the fact of paying wages below the SMIC or below the monthly minimum, and specifies: « L’amende est appliquée autant de fois qu’il y a de salariés rémunérés dans des conditions illégales. » (The fine is applied as many times as there are employees paid unlawfully.) This criminal side does not replace your own claim for the money: the wages owed are recovered through the labour court, and the three-year limitation period described above applies to that claim.

Finally, keep the December rise in view. If the decree sets the new rate as announced, check the first payslip covering hours worked from 1 December 2026: the hourly rate, the number of hours, and any benefits in kind deducted. A shortfall of a few cents per hour, repeated over months and multiplied by overtime and paid holidays, adds up. A posting from abroad, a seasonal contract with housing or a student permit close to its hours limit each call for a closer reading of the contract and of every payslip, because the items that count towards the minimum differ from one situation to the next.

Conclusion

The minimum wage in France in 2026 is €12.31 gross per hour, or €1,867.02 gross per month for 35 hours a week, under the order of 22 May 2026. The government has announced a 1.6% rise from 1 December 2026, about €1,501.6 net per month by its own figures, but the exact hourly rate will be the one set by the decree, which had not appeared in the issues of the Journal officiel we checked up to 22 November 2026. The rule applies to every private-sector employee in France, whatever their nationality, including posted workers and people employed without a permit, with reductions only for under-18s without six months’ experience and specific scales for apprentices. International students earn the same hourly minimum; their permit limits their hours to 964 a year. When checking a payslip, count base pay, output-related bonuses and benefits in kind valued as the Code provides, but leave out expenses, statutory overtime premiums, the transport allowance and payments for breaks during which you were free. If the result is below the floor, gather your evidence of hours, write to the employer and, if needed, bring the claim before the labour court within three years of each pay date.
