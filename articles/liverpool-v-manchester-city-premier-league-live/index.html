Enzo Maresca’s first public display since Manchester City were found guilty on virtually all counts of breaching Premier League financial rules was controlled – apart from the sole, and telling, slip-up of his very first answer.

In Thursday’s pre-emptive PR move, the club had Maresca tell their channels that he was aware of the “noise” around City. Fast forward 24 hours, and to a packed media conference of 45 attendees – around a threefold increase on the norm – at the Etihad Campus, the Italian was asked if this was dismissive of the anger felt towards the club’s cheating. “I understand the reason why most of you are here but the only thing I can say is that there is a process ongoing and in this moment I completely trust the club,” he replied.

This correspondent’s radar bleeped at the use of “in this moment” and, when the microphone was passed, Maresca was quizzed if this meant he may change his mind. “Sorry, that was the wrong answer. I completely trust the club in this moment, in the future, always.” But there was no denying the initial response and how this points to these shaky times for all at City.

Preamble

Of all the gin joints in the all the towns in all the world …

Manchester City’s first game after being found guilty of more than a hundred Premier League charges could have been anywhere: the Etihad, the Gtech Stadium, Portman Road, even Priestfield in the Carabao Cup. Instead, the great scriptwriter in the sky has decided it should be at Anfield – home of Liverpool, their biggest modern rivals and the team most affected by City’s success in the last 15 years. The atmosphere is always febrile when City visit Anfield; today, it will be off the charts.

Under normal circumstances, this would be a good time for City to play Liverpool. They have won five out of five in the Premier League, whereas Liverpool are still learning to speak Andoni Iraola’s coaching language and will be without both Alexander Isak and Cody Gakpo.

You don’t need a PhD in rocket science to know these are not normal circumstances. To many people, this isn’t so much a football match as a public shaming. City will try to focus exclusively on the football – partly because they believe they have irrefutable proof of their innocence, partly because anyone who wants to finish above Arsenal this season needs every point they can get.

Nobody, not even Biff Tannen, knows what’s going to happen over the next few hours. The only certainty is that there will be banners, banter and bile. In almost 140 years of English league football, there has never been a game like this.
