    14:21 BST

    Chelsea v Hull City (15:00 BST)

    Nizaar Kinsella
    BBC Sport Chelsea reporter

    Xabi Alonso left his pre-game news conference in a subdued mood and wanting to conduct a "deeper analysis".

    That was despite Chelsea producing an impressive comeback to beat Leeds United 6-3 at Stamford Bridge in the Carabao Cup on Wednesday.

    It came after Alonso made four changes at half-time, introducing Cole Palmer, who scored twice, and Morgan Rogers, who provided four assists.

    There are now questions over whether the Blues' fringe players are close to the level of the club's established stars after a poor first-half display.

    However, the broader picture remains positive at Stamford Bridge, with Chelsea scoring 16 goals and conceding 10 in the opening five matches of Alonso's tenure.

    It is clear the Spaniard is still implementing his system and Chelsea have at times lacked control in possession. Most importantly, though, he has won four of his first five matches in charge.

    Chelsea appear to be an improvement on last season's side, although there is still significant room for refinement.

    14:19 BST

    Chelsea v Hull City (15:00 BST)

    Former Chelsea manager Mauricio Pochettino made headlines this week when he claimed the Blues should be stripped of the 2016-17 Premier League title.

    Pochettino was managing Spurs, who finished second that season, then - and thinks they should be awarded the title.

    Chelsea were fined £10.75m in March after being charged by the Premier League for historical breaches of competition rules.

    The club also had a six-point deduction "set aside" on appeal.

    Pochettino, who managed Chelsea in 2023-24, said: "We [Spurs] should win one title, one Premier League. We should have been awarded it.

    "[Chelsea] were punished, they admitted, so that's it? The title should be for the team that was second.

    "It's true that we didn't compete with the same rules. It's completely fair to say that we didn't compete before the start of the season with the same card."

  6. Postpublished at 14:12 BST
    14:12 BST

    Bournemouth v Brentford (15:00 BST)

    Sophie Brown
    BBC Sport

    The Cherries have led in all three of their league games this season, yet failed to win any of them.

    Two points from three matches so far probably isn't a fair reflection on how Marco Rose's side have played, but they have developed a habit of crumbling late on in games. Four of the five goals they have conceded have been after the 84th minute, with two of those coming in stoppage time.

    In contrast, all of the four league goals scored by Bournemouth have come in the first half. Against Newcastle last weekend, they had 13 shots in the first half, with five on target; after the break, they had four shots, none of which were on target.

    "In the second half, we dropped a bit in everything," admitted head coach Rose after Bournemouth were pegged back from 2-0 up by an 88th-minute equaliser.

    They started to put that right in midweek, with three second-half goals from a much rotated line-up giving them a 4-0 League Cup win over Lincoln City, which was Rose's first competitive win in charge.

    But now Bournemouth need to transfer that to the Premier League, where they have lost seven points from winning positions this season, more than any other team – and 29 since the start of 2025-26, the joint-highest in the division.

    14:07 BST

    Bournemouth v Brentford (15:00 BST)

    Former Bournemouth defender Joe Partington believes Eli Junior Kroupi has received "rightful recognition" after being nominated for the 2026 Ballon d'Or young talent of the year award.

    The 20-year-old, who is sidelined with a broken foot, scored 13 goals in 35 appearances last season.

    Partington said on BBC Radio Solent's Cherries Unpicked podcast: "We've had some exceptionally talented young players at this football club, but never one who has been involved in this.

    "He did perform exceptionally well last season. Bournemouth really are missing him at the moment, there is a big hole in the team. They are missing him being a slight maverick, being a bit more creative and being able to take risks.

    "Bournemouth feel a bit safe in their playing style at the moment, and he is a bums-off-seats kind of player. When he gets the ball in and around the box, people expect him to beat the goalkeeper or hit the target.

    "It is rightful recognition for him to be included in those top 10 best young players in the word. Will he win the award? I would say the likelihood is probably not, but it is great confirmation Bournemouth's recruitment team continue to hit the mark.

    "Hopefully he is able to overcome this foot injury now and recapture the form that put him in this position in the first place."

  10. Glasner the difference maker?published at 14:03 BST
    14:03 BST

    Aston Villa v Nottingham Forest (15:00 BST)

    Noel Sliney
    BBC Sport

    While Forest haven’t beaten Aston Villa away since 1994 and are winless after their opening three Premier League matches for the first time, Oliver Glasner might view this as the ideal fixture to bring his first victory in charge.

    Glasner is kryptonite to Villa and Unai Emery, with the Austrian unbeaten in all seven meetings and with an aggregate scoreline of 19-4 in his favour – all while at Crystal Palace.

    That sequence includes two victories at Villa Park during a 41-match home spell in which the Birmingham side were otherwise unbeaten.

    Glasner has only ever enjoyed one longer unbeaten run against a manager, and he’s never fared better versus a counterpart without ever losing. Similarly, it’s the longest streak Emery has ever endured against another manager without winning.

    The wing-back system that Glasner implemented at Palace and now Forest stretches Aston Villa’s narrow set-up, while quick and willing runners on the counter-attack leave Emery’s side vulnerable defensively.

    Forest are not fully firing yet under Glasner but the Austrian has expressed his satisfaction with the club’s dealings in the summer transfer window as he configures the squad to his blueprint.

  11. Villa spark?published at 14:01 BST
    14:01 BST

    Aston Villa v Nottingham Forest (15:00 BST)

    Noel Sliney
    BBC Sport

    Villa’s slow start to the current campaign has prompted comparisons with the previous one, when they didn’t score until their fifth league game but then ground out a hard-earned European victory against limited opponents.

    That sparked a remarkable run of 17 wins in 19 matches. The difference this season is that, aside from the abysmal opening defeat by Brighton, Villa are at least showing an identity they lacked during the early malaise of 2025-26.

    Their defensive structure has been swiftly remedied by Unai Emery, while a gradual improvement in the team’s attacking play was accelerated against Brugge on Tuesday night.

    Expensive summer signings Ibrahim Mbaye and Ballon d’Or Young Talent of the Year nominee Johan Manzambi are still to come into the side too – both could make their debuts on Saturday as Villa seek to avoid becoming the first team in English league history to go goalless in their opening four games in successive seasons.

    The Villans are on a run of six successive Premier League home wins against Nottingham Forest and dismantled the visitors 4-0 to reach the Europa League final when the sides last met four months ago. However, six of Villa’s starting XI that night, and nine of the 16 players used, are no longer at the club.

  13. 'It is going to take time to come together'published at 13:56 BST
    13:56 BST

    Aston Villa v Nottingham Forest (15:00 BST)

    Former Nottingham Forest winger Paul Anderson says Oliver Glasner "has the personnel" for his wing-back system but thinks it will take time to fully click.

    "I've been coaching for over five years now and I love the formation," he told BBC Radio Nottingham's Shut Up And Show More Football podcast.

    "It allows you to outnumber an opposition team anywhere on the pitch.

    "You've got to have the personnel to play it, though. You've got to have intelligent players that can drop in and work off each other, and your wing-backs have to be fit.

    "Nottingham Forest have very strong centre-backs at the minute.

    "There is always going to be a question mark over Murillo's fitness, but if he is fit and at 100% then this formation suits him perfectly. The same goes for Nikola Milenkovic - it is perfect for him.

    "It also suits the midfield. It is a formation which is going to allow Morgan Gibbs-White and James McAtee more space in there.

    "So they definitely have players in the squad who can do a job in this formation, it is just going to take time to come together."

Aston Villa v Nottingham Forest (15:00 BST)

BBC Sport

While Forest haven’t beaten Aston Villa away since 1994 and are winless after their opening three Premier League matches for the first time, Oliver Glasner might view this as the ideal fixture to bring his first victory in charge.

Glasner is kryptonite to Villa and Unai Emery, with the Austrian unbeaten in all seven meetings and with an aggregate scoreline of 19-4 in his favour – all while at Crystal Palace.

That sequence includes two victories at Villa Park during a 41-match home spell in which the Birmingham side were otherwise unbeaten.

Glasner has only ever enjoyed one longer unbeaten run against a manager, and he’s never fared better versus a counterpart without ever losing. Similarly, it’s the longest streak Emery has ever endured against another manager without winning.

The wing-back system that Glasner implemented at Palace and now Forest stretches Aston Villa’s narrow set-up, while quick and willing runners on the counter-attack leave Emery’s side vulnerable defensively.

Forest are not fully firing yet under Glasner but the Austrian has expressed his satisfaction with the club’s dealings in the summer transfer window as he configures the squad to his blueprint.
