More mosquitoes with West Nile virus found in Lowell

TheTools · 10.09.2026 20:24 · source · permalink

18-block area in Centralville to be sprayed to reduce risk

  • City of Lowell
    A map of the 18 streets in Centralville that will be sprayed after mosquitoes tested positive for West Nile virus Sept. 10, 2026. (Courtesy City of Lowell)
  • This image provided by the USDA Agricultural Research Service shows a closeup of a mosquito on human skin. (USDA Agricultural Research Service via AP)

LOWELL — The city issued a community advisory Wednesday after the Massachusetts Department of Public Health notified Lowell’s Health Department that West Nile virus had been detected in mosquitoes collected in the Centralville neighborhood.

“As a preventative measure, the City has requested street-level mosquito spraying in the area where the [mosquitoes were] identified,” the city notice said.

In response to the positive pool, the Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project will conduct street-level spraying Thursday evening.

A truck-mounted, ultra-low volume spraying will treat an 18-street area, including 18th, 19th and 20th, Beacon, Billings, Boynton, Bridge, By, Dana, Eustis, Hildreth, Jacques, Ludlam, Richardson and Willard streets and Aiken, Barker and Whitney avenues from 8 to 11 p.m. Thursday.

The area borders the McAuliffe Elementary and Robinson Middle schools.

Residents in current and potential spray areas are advised to close street-facing windows and keep pets indoors between sunset and midnight, remain indoors during the spraying and for 15-20 minutes afterward, and turn off “outside air” controls on older window-unit air conditioners.

The notice said there was no need for special precautions as any residue will have evaporated within 24 hours, but recommended that residents with gardens wash vegetables before eating them.

West Nile virus is commonly transmitted to humans by the bite of an infected mosquito and can cause illness ranging from a mild fever to more serious disease like encephalitis or meningitis.

“It’s important to note that spraying can reduce but not eliminate the threat of mosquito-borne illness in the areas that are sprayed,” the CMMCP said. “That’s why it’s important for individuals to continue to take personal precautions against mosquito bites, both before and after any spraying is conducted.”

Residents can help reduce their risk of WNV by using insect repellent, avoiding peak mosquito activity from dusk to dawn and removing standing water around their homes.

“One basic fact that is constant for all mosquito species – stagnant water is required for the maturation cycle,” CMMCP said. “By removing, covering or draining containers, you can disrupt this process and reduce or eliminate mosquito annoyance and the potential for disease transmission from certain species in your own area.”

Wearing long sleeves and pants when outdoor can help reduce mosquito bites, and residents can mosquito-proof their homes by installing and repairing window screens.

The city of Lowell and the town of Chelmsford had their first pool of mosquitoes test positive for West Nile virus during a scheduled sampling conducted by the Massachusetts Department of Public Health last week.

In Lowell, the positive mosquito was found in the city’s Sacred Heart neighborhood; in Chelmsford, it was from the North Chelmsford area.

CMMCP was created by the Legislature in 1973, and currently consists of 44 cities and towns in Central Massachusetts, from both Worcester and Middlesex counties, covering nearly 810 square miles.

The last DPH update listed Lowell as moderate risk for WNV.

Information about WNV, reports of current and historical WNV virus activity in the state and risk-level maps of impacted areas can be found on the DPH website at mass.gov/dph/mosquito.
