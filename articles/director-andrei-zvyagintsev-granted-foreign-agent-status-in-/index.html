Director Andrei Zvyagintsev Granted 'Foreign Agent' Status in Russia

TheTools · 19.09.2026 09:41 · source · permalink

The Ministry of Justice of Russia has included director Andrei Zvyagintsev in the register of 'foreign agents'. His name appeared in the latest update of the list published by the agency. Zvyagintsev is one of the most renowned Russian directors of recent decades. His films have repeatedly received awards at major international film festivals, including Cannes and Venice, and have also been recognized with a Golden Globe and other prestigious cinematic awards. In 2021, the director left Russia for Germany for treatment. After the onset of the full-scale war of Russia against Ukraine, he publicly condemned the war and stated that he had no intention of returning to the country. Since 2022, Zvyagintsev has been living in France. This year, his film 'Minotaur', created in collaboration with French producers, won the Grand Prix at the Cannes Film Festival. During the award ceremony, the director addressed Russian President Vladimir Putin in Russian, calling for an end to the war. Later, France nominated 'Minotaur' for the Oscar as its national candidate. What does the status of 'foreign agent' mean? In Russia, individuals and organizations included in the relevant register are required to comply with a number of additional requirements related to reporting and labeling published materials. Violations of these requirements are subject to penalties. The Ministry of Justice of Russia traditionally does not provide detailed explanations for the reasons for inclusion in the register. In several previous cases, the agency cited public statements against the war as grounds, however, such wording is absent in the announcement regarding Zvyagintsev. Thus, the director has joined the list of prominent cultural figures who have been granted 'foreign agent' status in Russia in recent years.
