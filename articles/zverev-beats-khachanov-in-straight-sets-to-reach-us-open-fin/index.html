Bryan Armen Graham has buildup and live updates from Flushing Meadows …

Nobody could have argued had Karen Khachanov won that third set. The 30-year-old played his best tennis during it – and barely lost a point on serve – but still fell short of reaching his first grand slam final. Zverev, though, advances in that understated, very effective way of his.

“Hopefully I can not make the American dream come true on Sunday,” says Alexander Zverev in his on-court interview. He hasn’t dropped a set since the second round and will face either Francis Tiafoe or Ben Shelton in the final.

After the second round at 3am, me and my team were in the dressing room actually laughing our ass off how bad I was playing. Moving forward I was playing much better tennis. I have improved a little bit, otherwise I wouldn’t be in the US Open, I think.

I can’t wait for Sunday. It’s going to be incredibly special, Francis and Ben are crowd favourites here in New York. Tonight is going to be an epic semi-final, I’m looking forward to watching it. Hopefully I can not make the American dream come true on Sunday.

Zverev v Khachanov could of course go on beyond that 7pm timing – that is the nature of five-set tennis. The incoming Tennis Australia CEO, Andrew Abdo, suggested last month that tennis should rethink it’s best-of-five format for men’s grand slam matches to attract a younger audience.

Andrew Lawrence has, thankfully for us, launched to the defence of longer epics …

In tennis, a match that stretches on for hours and hours isn’t a drag. It’s an obvious marker of quality, an implied reason to tune in. And if engagement is the name of the game, the drama of a fifth act that holds the potential to maintain an audience, capture a new one and – in the case of the Shelton-Alcaraz quarter – extend the primetime viewing window on the West Coast should be an incentive for those committed to growing the game to embrace the five-setter even tighter, not abolish it.

Siniakova and Townsend win US Open women's doubles

Before we get into the buildup for the men’s singles, Katerina Siniakova and Taylor Townsend have just been crowned women’s doubles champions at Flushing Meadows after coming back to beat Robin Montgomery and Ashlyn Krueger 5-7 6-0 6-2 in the final on Arthur Ashe.

The No 1 seeds have completed the career grand slam after winning Wimbledon in 2024, the Australian Open in 2025 and the French Open earlier this year.

Preamble

It has been smooth sailing for Alexander Zverev for the past few days at the US Open. The No 1 seed had to fight his way through two five-setters to start the tournament but hasn’t dropped a set since, easing through to the verge of his third grand slam final in succession. The unseeded Karen Khachanov was out of sorts earlier this year, dropping to No 50 in the world, but has found real consistency in the last fortnight. He returns to this stage for the first time since the 2022 semis and could today reach his first grand slam final.

These two will be happy to head out on Arthur Ashe Stadium nice and early, giving them a bit of an advantage over Francis Tiafoe and Ben Shelton, who follow in tonight’s American primetime slot, ahead of Sunday’s final. It is a poignant day in New York on the 25th anniversary of 9/11 and Flushing Meadows will pay tribute to the victims of the attacks.
