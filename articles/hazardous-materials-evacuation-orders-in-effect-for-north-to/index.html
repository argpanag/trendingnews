Hazardous Materials Evacuation Orders in Effect for North Toledo

TheTools · 22.10.2026 14:58 · source · permalink

Last updated: October 22, 10:35 a.m. This page will be updated as new information is available.

Evacuation Order Revised

The evacuation zone has been reduced to 1/4 mile radius around 1266 Progress Ave (MC Tank Terminal). Read more about how to return home safely after evacuation.

What happened?

At 10:48 p.m. on Tuesday, Oct. 20, Toledo Fire & Rescue ordered everyone within a 1-mile radius of 1266 Progress Ave (MC Tank Terminal) to evacuate. because of a hazardous materials release.

At 4:24 a.m. on Wednesday, Oct. 21, Toledo Fire & Rescue ordered everyone within a 3-mile radius to shelter in place.

At 5:00 p.m. on Wednesday, Oct. 21, Toledo Fire & Rescue adjusted the evacuation zone to a 1/2 mile radius and lifted the shelter-in-place order.

At 10:30 a.m. on Thursday, Oct. 22, Toledo Fire & Rescue adjusted the evacuation zone to a 1/4 mile radius.

Toledo Fire & Rescue hazardous materials personnel are working with federal, state and local partners to monitor conditions, coordinate mitigation efforts, and evaluate next steps.

Resources

What chemical was released?

The chemical released is glycidyl methacrylate, an irritant that can cause burning of the eyes, nose, mouth, and skin. If exposure occurs, shower or rinse the impacted area. Symptoms of overexposure include headache, dizziness, tiredness, nausea and vomiting. Please contact a medical professional if you believe you're having a reaction to exposure.

Am I in the evacuation zone?

The zone is a 1/4-mile radius around 1266 Progress Ave. If you live or work within 1/4 mile of that address, evacuate now.

Am I in the shelter in place zone?

The shelter-in-place order has been lifted.

Enter you address in the map below to see if you fall in the reduced evacuation zone or view a static image.

There’s fog and haze where I am. Is that the chemical plume?

Dense fog and very little wind are affecting visibility across Toledo. You can’t tell fog from the release by looking, so please don’t rely on how the air looks. If you are outside the evacuation or shelter-in-place zones and smell an unusual odor, please stay indoors, close windows and doors, and turn off ventilation that draws in outside air.

I can smell a chemical odor. Should I be concerned?

Smelling an odor doesn't always mean toxic levels of exposure. If you are outside the evacuation or shelter-in-place zones and smell an unusual odor, please stay indoors, close windows and doors, and turn off ventilation that draws in outside air.

I'm feeling unwell. What should I do?

  • For minor symptoms, such as mild eye, nose, throat, or skin irritation, shower or rinse the impacted area and contact a medical professional.
  • For serious symptoms, such as difficulty breathing, nausea and vomiting, dizziness, or confusion, go to the emergency room or call 911.
  • If you're looking for health-related guidance, you can contact the Toledo Lucas-County Health Department at 419-213-4100.

Is there any air monitoring data available?

The US EPA is overseeing air monitoring at several locations around the perimeter of the evacuation zone. Additional testing will be done later around the perimeter of the shelter-in-place zone. Results will be shared as soon as possible.

Can I drive through the area? Is the Interstate closed?

The interstate is still open. Anyone driving through should roll up their windows and recirculate the air inside the vehicle. Roads are closed surrounding the area of evacuation.

Can I take my boat into waterways in the area?

Coast Guard advises compliance with prescribed evacuation and shelter-in-place zones while operating on water at this time due to potential exposure through the air.

Is the tap water safe?

Yes. The water source is not contaminated. All treatment for tap water takes place inside and is distributed through pressurized water lines.

Who should I call for more information?

Call 911 only for emergencies. Engage Toledo will not be able to provide more information than what has been released. More information will be available here as soon as possible.

Where can I get official updates?

City of Toledo website, Toledo Fire & Rescue social media, and Lucas County EMA alerts.

What is the impact to City services?

City services return to normal operations on Thursday, October 22.
