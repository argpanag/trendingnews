Patient collapses, needs CPR in Waikato ED waiting room

TheTools · 09.09.2026 20:46 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

A patient collapsed and needed CPR while at Waikato Hospital’s Emergency Department waiting room last month.

The patient survived after intervention from staff, but the incident has raised questions about why the patient wasn’t put into a bed on arrival at the ED.

“The lack of beds meant it [the patient collapsing] happened in the waiting room. There were no beds to put her on when she arrived,” a source told the Herald.

The patient collapsed after arriving on the evening of August 26, according to the source.

It follows the death of Waikato father-of-five, Brent Horsburgh.

As first revealed by the Herald, Horsburgh collapsed and died in an ED toilet cubicle on June 30 after waiting more than nine hours for care.

Horsburgh was found unresponsive in the toilet by another patient, and despite staff performing CPR, he couldn’t be revived.

A “rapid review” of the incident found doctors and nurses were off sick at the time of his death.

Asked whether the latest incident was being investigated, Health New Zealand wouldn’t say, but the agency confirmed a patient suffered a medical event last month 40 minutes after arriving at the ED.

“Staff responded immediately and provided care. The patient is recovering well. We do not believe staffing levels contributed to this event,” Waikato group operations director Stephanie Doe told the Herald.

Doe said the patient was “triaged appropriately”, but she would not say which triage category the patient was given.

When any patient arrives at an ED in New Zealand, they’re given a triage score ranging from one to five which stipulates how sick the patient is, and how quickly they should be seen.

Those triaged as category one should be seen immediately as they’re considered to have an “immediately life-threatening” condition, those triaged at category three have a “potentially life-threatening” condition and should be seen in 30 minutes, while those classified as category five are less urgent and should be seen within two hours.

The Herald’s source said the patient was triaged as category three which means they should have been seen in 30 minutes.

HNZ would not respond to questions about whether the patient was seen in the recommended timeframe before collapsing, nor would HNZ respond to a question about whether the patient should have been placed in a bed on arrival, or if spare beds were not available due to the hospital being at capacity.

Doe said like many EDs around the country, Waikato had seen a surge in patients arriving with influenza, RSV and other respiratory illnesses this winter.

More staff were also off work because of sickness.

“During August the Waikato Hospital ED had an average of 266 presentations a day, compared with 256 a day during the same period last year. We have also seen peaks of up to 299 presentations in a single day,” she said.

Because the hospital has occasionally been operating at capacity, Doe said both bed and staff numbers had been increased.
