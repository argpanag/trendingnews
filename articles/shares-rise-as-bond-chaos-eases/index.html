Shares rise as bond chaos eases

TheTools · 04.09.2026 08:45 · source · permalink

The global bond rout is cooling, having approached ‘event horizon’ territory where bonds stop being hedges and instead drive negative feedback loops back to stocks. A pullback in crude prices offered relief, but the pressure remains intense with investors still reluctant to hold onto long-duration debt of any kind. 

The yield on the UK 30-year gilt approached a fresh 18-year high of 5.944 per cent before some cooling in oil prices saw the move retrace a bit. The benchmark 10-year yield rolled over about 10bps from a high at 5.234 per cent to around 5.13 per cent this morning. 

US yields also moved lower after peaking. The two-year Treasury tested just above 4.419 per cent Wednesday, nearly matching the intraday high of early 2025. The 10-year dropped back to 4.77 per cent early Thursday after an intraday high of 4.818 per cent. As a result, the implied odds for the Federal Reserve to hike this month moved down from 70 per cent to 60 per cent.

The risks for bonds still seemed skewed to the downside (ie higher yields), as it does not feel like we are at the level where investors will want to step in front of this wildfire.

This morning the FTSE is showing some relief and rising 0.2 per cent, with Frankfurt a little higher. Wall Street snapped a three-day losing streak, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both rising almost half a per cent yesterday. Among stock movers, Snowflake shares leapt after hours on growth in its AI coding agent. Broadcom shares fell despite strong earnings. There was some disappointing revenue forecast for the current quarter, but the longer-term outlook is very strong, bolstering its challenge to Nvidia.

Comments from the US government helped ease concerns in bond markets as energy secretary Chris Wright said 17mn barrels of oil had transited the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, which, if true, would be the highest level passing the waterway on a single day since the war started. Even if the Strait is not fully open, such a high figure also suggests Iran isn’t in control of it. 

China also said it would work with Middle East countries to ensure crude keeps flowing safely. Later on, President Donald Trump said renewed hostilities with Iran would not continue for “too long.” There is pressure on him to cool things down in time for the midterms. Brent crude hit $97 yesterday but is below $95 this morning. European gas prices have also cooled a touch.

Meanwhile Andy Burnham, facing his first PMQs, sought to reassure the markets and said the government was “grounded in fiscal responsibility” and that Britain would cut its deficit faster than any other country in the G7.

On the whole, it sounded like he was neither ruling out more borrowing nor more taxes. The ‘big spending vibes, small spending commitments’ phase of his premiership cannot survive contact with the enemy, which in this case is bond vigilantes. The best thing the government can do is send a clear signal that it will tackle the supply-side reform required to get the economy moving and more productive. Bond vigilantes would like this – it’s more credible than tinkering with taxes and vibing about welfare spending cuts on the one hand and raising defence spending on the other.

US hiring has slowed, which helped push down the odds for the Fed to raise rates in two weeks. Private companies added 38,000 jobs in August, according to ADP, which is down from 46,000 and below the forecast of 47,000. It makes it the slowest month since January and comes ahead of Friday’s key non-farm payroll figures for last month.

By Neil Wilson, investor strategist at Saxo UK
