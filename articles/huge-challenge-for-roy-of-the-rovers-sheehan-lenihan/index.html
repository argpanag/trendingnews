Huge challenge for 'Roy of the Rovers' Sheehan - Lenihan

TheTools · 20.11.2026 09:24 · source · permalink

Donal Lenihan says Ireland's "Roy of the Rovers" Dan Sheehan will shoulder a huge responsibility against South Africa at Aviva Stadium tomorrow after being picked to start at number 8.

The world champions are in town for a blockbuster Nations Championship showdown, the build-up to which has had more sub-plots than an Agatha Christie novel.

During the week South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus received a six-match suspension for comments made concerning officiating during his team's loss to France. Erasmus is therefore banned from attending Lansdowne Road, but he has declared he'll be holding an online watchalong from his hotel room.

The game itself is a massive challenge for Andy Farrell's Ireland, with Sheehan - normally used as a hooker - crucial to hopes of earning what would be a significant victory.

"There was talk about it for a while when Dan Sheehan was selected to play number 8 against Munster a couple of weeks ago, you felt there was a longer-term reason for this," Lenihan told RTÉ Radio 1's Morning Ireland.

"There's no doubt Dan Sheehan is one of the best rugby players in the world. He has a skill set that means if he had to play in the centre tomorrow, you'd think he could do that.

"But it is different in terms of his role as a forward now as opposed to scrummaging in the front row and throwing into the line-out. He's now part of that line-out. He has to act as a lifter. He could be a jumper, but I think the biggest challenge for him will be at the base of the scrum.

"At number 8, you're responsible for controlling the ball, for picking it at the right time. It especially becomes difficult as your scrum is on the retreat. So, as a consequence of that, I think Ireland, every scrum engagement they'll have, they'll look to go for channel one, get the ball in and out as quickly as possible.

"Sheehan, I won't say I feel sorry for him, but he carries huge responsibility into this game now.

"He's playing out of position. He's the captain of the team. He's one of the players that everybody looks up to. He's the Roy of the Rovers. They might as well ask him to take the place kicks as well!"

Lenihan doesn't have much sympathy for Erasmus who, he says, "has history" when it comes to stirring things up.

"It's not the first time we've seen this," Lenihan added. "I was in South Africa in 2021 when South Africa played the Lions. The Lions won the first test and Rassie went down a similar route after that.

"He has history here. To be fair, I think World Rugby are also complicit in this whole issue because they tweaked the laws in relation to hip tackles and knee tackles only on 1 November. Nick Timoney came out during the week saying look, it's very difficult to adjust.

"Rassie claimed there were 16 tackles that went unpunished when they played against France. Now, that's been disputed by others but the bottom line, regardless of what happens, you can't accuse match officials of match fixing. I mean, that's a step too far.

"The minute he came out with that statement, I always felt he was going to get a ban. The whole of South Africa is up in arms over it, I've no doubt. As a result, he's created a cause for his players. So, it just makes tomorrow's game even more interesting."

As for Ireland's chances of scalping the Boks, Lenihan concedes it's going to be a really big ask.

"It's going to be very challenging," he said. "Ireland haven't put in a top-class performance against one of the top three sides in the world for two years now.

"I think there were signs against Argentina and in the second half against Fiji that Ireland's attack has improved significantly, especially with the back three that they've selected. But as always against South Africa, it comes down to physicality.

"It comes down to winning the collisions and the scrum, of course, is the key talking point.

"As a consequence of that, I think the bench is going to be huge and when you look at the overall strength that South Africa have at the moment, and especially with Ireland, a number of players out injured, you have to tip the scale in terms of South Africa for this one."
