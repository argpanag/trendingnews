LoL Worlds 2026 Top 20 Players - Hotspawn

Worlds season is upon us, League of Legends fans. And with one of the most stacked team lineups in recent history, that means only one thing: it’s time to rank the Worlds 2026 Top 20 Players.

Ranking the top 20 players is always a tough task, especially considering how there is a discussion to be made about how dominant Eastern teams continue to be. That said, I’ve tried to include a couple of Western names in there because I genuinely think they deserve to be there.

My criteria considered the player’s season overall, their individual stats, how influential and impactful they have been compared to the rest of the team this season, and their overall potential, so a player who peaked at some time previously will still likely make the list even if he’s not playing in his best shape (although he would be ranked lower, of course).

It’s a personal ranking, completely subjective, so feel free to challenge me on any of the placements. Without further ado, check out my Top 20 players at Worlds 2026.

As much as I like Bin and I believe he’s a core member of the BLG roster, the whole drama around him earlier this season made him lose a lot of points in the standings. We all know how good he is individually, but he has repeatedly shown some of his tendencies that can hurt a team like BLG.

There is no question that if he delivers, BLG is a Worlds contender. That said, his impact upon returning wasn’t as strong as it was earlier in the season (partially due to the meta as well), which is why I’ll keep him at No. 20 for now.

Even though Team Liquid beat LYON in the LCS final and Josedeodo had the upper hand over Inspired, I still rate Inspired very highly going into Worlds. Despite going 8-8 across the 16 games he played, Inspired still finished with a +310 XP differential at 15 while being even in gold and CS.

The biggest strength of the player is his ability to lead his team and give his teammates a clear direction on how to play the game. As long as the team sticks to what he says, LYON is expected to go far in this tournament.

Caps is still the king of the mid lane in Europe. In the Summer Split, he recorded 10 solo kills, a +389 XP differential at 15 and a CS lead at that point in more than 70% of his games. Ever since Perkz joined, it feels like Caps’ mechanics and gameplay quality have gone up again, getting closer to the peak level we witnessed back when he first stormed onto the scene.

If there is a team Western fans should put hope on, it’s gotta be Caps and G2. After proving they can take down the LCK at First Stand and MSI, this is EU’s best chance at a strong run in recent years.

ON has been consistently one of the key players behind BLG’s success this season. While he’s rarely the flashiest or the guy hard carrying his team, he delivered alongside his teammate Viper when it mattered the most. While we all know who the best support in the world is, ON has become an all-around player who can manage different roles and tasks at a high level.

Caps is not the only G2 player on the top 20 list this year. After a year of adaptation in 2025, Worlds 2026 will be the culmination of SkewMond’s growth. He has been the best jungler in the LEC for the majority of the season and has already shown he can match the best Asian junglers on a good day.

His stats are even more impressive, with a gold differential of nearly 500 at 15 minutes and a 4.8 KDA across the entire summer split. G2 no longer has to rely on Caps exclusively and it’s given SkewMond the keys to carry the team to the top.

It would be weird to have a Worlds 2026 Top 20 Players list without Faker, yet rating him anywhere close to the top 10 is, in my opinion, not deserved this year. Yes, Faker is still the GOAT and the value he brings to the table goes well beyond his individual play, but Faker has struggled a lot more this year due to his weaker fundamentals.

He averaged a -272 gold and -6.0 CS differential at 15 in summer, with a 2.8 KDA and 58% kill participation. He was behind in lane more often than almost anyone else on this list. In a meta where leads can be built progressively in the early game, this is not a small handicap to have. We’re likely going to see T1 ramp up like they did over the past years, but for now, 15th seems the right place to put him in.

Speaking of LCK veterans, welcome ShowMaker back to Worlds! The 2020 world champion has had a stellar second part of the season and has proven he’s still strong enough to be among the kings of the mid lane in Korea.

While the team couldn’t finish anywhere higher than 4th in playoffs, ShowMaker has still been doing heavy lifting for his team. Most importantly, he proved he can take on several jobs without forcing the team to build the game around one playstyle, giving them that bit of extra flexibility.

Moving from the LCK to the LPL, we have Viper in 13th. The Korean ADC once again proved he’s one of the most versatile players in the world, capable of fitting into almost any team identity while still having that carry potential untouched.

Even in a season of ups and downs as BLG had, Viper was still at the top of nearly every single department. There is no question he will keep the form heading into Worlds, especially considering this is probably the roster with the highest peak potential he had in years.

After being considered the best jungler in the world in the mid-season, Kanavi’s second part of the season was rougher than expected. While he still delivered strong performances, the frequency of bad games went up, affecting HLE as a whole as well.

The innate aggressiveness of his playstyle has always been a double-edged sword: it was back when he played in the LPL and it still is today. If HLE wants a shot at the title, however, he must find better consistency in his gameplay.

If we are strictly looking at Gumayusi’s stats, his numbers wouldn’t tell you he’s a top-tier player. But if we consider the context and the team he’s playing with, you would understand that the guy perfectly fits HLE’s identity and he’s probably the only one who can consistently make that work.

Similar to his previous T1 days, he can play the map without having his teammates constantly play for him and you know he’ll show up later in the game to be HLE’s main damage source. This, however, doesn’t mean Gumayusi can’t play aggressively, so keep an eye on him since the Worlds 2026 meta will likely revolve around aggressive bot lane bullies.

Moving onto the top 10, we have Zeka, who has been instrumental to some of HLE’s success in the season. As one of the few teams that likes to play through its top side, Zeka was one of the best profiles to match Kanavi’s early-game aggression.

Compared to previous years, when he was often considered below the top 3 mid lane kings (Chovy, Bdd, and Faker), Zeka has made huge steps forward and is arguably now the second-best LCK mid laner.

After two years of struggles, Canyon is seemingly back on top form right before the start of Worlds 2026. His ramp-up coincided perfectly with Gen.G’s streak of success at the end of the regular season and playoffs. It seems like Canyon has had more agency and freedom to play aggressively, with the whole Gen.G squad being more proactive with their play. If Gen.G wants a shot at the title for real this time around, Canyon will have to maintain that form up until the very end.

Speaking of Gen.G, we have Kiin in 8th. Consistency is the perfect word to describe him: a player who can do everything at an extremely high level, bringing the team the flexibility needed and without the need to make top lane a pivotal role in the team’s game plans.

In terms of weak-side play, I still rate him as the best top laner in the world. Overall, however, I still think there is someone better than him… (and you can probably guess who it is)

Ruler had a pretty rough year for the most part but really stepped up in LCK playoffs. With an 832 DPM and dealing nearly one-third of Gen.G’s total damage, he put on a show in the LCK playoffs. There were never doubts about his peak potential, but his current form is a warning sign for everyone else.

Despite Ruler’s ramp-up, Peyz had a much more consistent and stellar showing if we consider the whole season. Joining T1 at the end of last year, the team quickly realized they had to learn how to funnel for him. Peyz, on the other hand, just clocked in one carry performance after the other, bringing T1 some wins that should’ve probably been losses.

It’s crazy to think that the guy is still only 20 years old and is arguably the best ADC in the world. This is his chance to beat his master and help T1 take that fourth consecutive title.

I genuinely think that Kiin and Zeus belong in the same tier, but the latter’s carry potential and clutch factor are the main reasons why I rate him slightly higher. The numbers in playoffs weren’t the greatest and you could argue that Kiin was the better player.

That said, when it comes to international tournaments, it feels like Zeus can always find that extra bit of performance. And in a meta where top lane seems likely to have more agency and carry-oriented picks, I believe he will once again be one of the leading forces for HLE.

Speaking of leading forces, Keria is without a doubt the leading force for T1. While Peyz is the hard carry later on, Keria is the one doing a lot of the heavy lifting in the early-mid game. Whether it’s the 2v2 bot lane or creating pressure on the map through roams, Keria is impeccable.

But the one thing that distinguishes Keria from all the other supports is his playmaking. At a random moment in the game, even if they are on the back foot, Keria is the type of player to find the winning play to either come back or close out the game. The frequency at which he does it is incredible and if it wasn’t for the nature of his role, he would probably be rated even higher on this list.

It’s time for the podium and the third place goes to the left-handed god Knight. The Chinese mid laner had no competition for most of the season and earned 14 PotM (player of the match) awards and nearly 40 PotGs. BLG can threaten opponents through every lane, but Knight is the constant pain point to manage at every single moment.

The only question mark is whether he can transition that dominance against the LCK mids. The only way for us to know is when the Swiss stage kicks off on October 23.

Speaking of PotGs, Tarzan was the only player to beat Knight this season (in split 3), proving how important the Korean jungler is to the team. Anyone’s Legend’s win against BLG in the LPL Finals became reality because Tarzan was able to shut down Xun in that series.

His consistency and ability to play anything at the highest level made him my number one pick in the jungle power rankings. If AL will be in the fight for the title this year, Tarzan will inevitably be one of the major contributors to that success.

Once again, Chovy is at the top of the world… who could’ve guessed it? His laning phase has gotten even better, to the point where it feels like he has control over everything that happens for the first 10 minutes of the game.

Paired with the better synergy and playstyle from the rest of the squad, Chovy has been able to extend his leads even further. There is no doubt he comes in as the tournament’s strongest mid laner, but the question is whether that is enough for him to win the title. After so many years of chasing the trophy, it’s probably time to break that curse.

Here’s my Top 20 Players at Worlds. Do you agree with the list? Did I miss anyone? Let us know in the comments!
