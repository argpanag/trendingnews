Kiwi star learns suspension verdict for World Cup final after illegal act in semi

TheTools · 08.11.2026 07:05 · source · permalink

The New Zealand captain was put on report in the win over Tonga.

New Zealand are breathing a sigh of relief ahead of the Rugby League World Cup final after James Fisher-Harris escaped with a warning for raising his knees on Saturday night. The Kiwis captain was placed on report after lifting his knee and hitting Solesi Foketi in the head during a hit-up in the 12th minute of the semi-final.

The match review committee handed down its verdict on Sunday morning, and although Fisher-Harris copped a charge for dangerous contact he only received a warning. If the incident occurred in the NRL it would have resulted in a fine, but there's no fines in the World Cup and players receive warnings for a first-time offence.

A second offence would result in a ban for Fisher-Harris. Journalist David Riccio said on Channel 7 on Saturday night: "I've seen throughout this World Cup it's very much leaning (towards) a more lenient approach than what we're seeing in the NRL season itself.

"I think it's a real focus on trying to keep the best players on the park during this World Cup tournament. Yes, he's got a little bit of something to answer to, but I don't think it is a match suspension."

Fisher-Harris has been a vital cog in the vaunted New Zealand forward pack. The skipper played 55 minutes in the 34-10 victory over Tonga on Saturday night, running for 152 metres and making 33 tackles.

James Fisher-Harris raised his knee into Solesi Foketi. Image: Channel 7

Naufahu Whyte in some doubt for World Cup final

The 30-year-old will be pivotal to the Kiwis' chances of winning the final next Sunday night against the winner of the second semi between Australia and Samoa. And he might need to step up even more with Roosters hero Naufahu Whyte in some doubt.

Whyte only played 13 minutes from the bench against Tonga due to an ankle injury. However Roosters coach Trent Robinson revealed on Channel 7 that Whyte reckons he'll be right for the final, and Riccio said it was simply a precaution that he didn't come back on.

It's also good news for fellow forward Xavier Willison, who passed a head injury assessment in the second half and returned to the field. Star fullback Kalyn Ponga also got through unscathed after returning just 23 days since breaking his forearm in the tournament opener against Australia.

"He's a freak of a player but you see the defensive mindset he had tonight, where he saved a couple tries there," said Fisher-Harris. "That's what I love about KP, he's a freak of nature but I think his leadership skills are very underrated."
