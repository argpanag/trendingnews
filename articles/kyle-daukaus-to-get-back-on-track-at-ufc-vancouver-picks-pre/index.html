Kyle Daukaus To Get Back on Track at UFC Vancouver: Picks, Predictions, Odds

TheTools · 15.10.2026 14:24 · source · permalink

Kyle Daukaus’ 2nd UFC run has been wildly successful so far, with an exception to a set back on the White House Lawn to Bo Nickal, Philly’s finest put together back to back first minute finishes against two respected vets in Michel Pereira and Gerald Meerschaert. He now faces an away game against the Canadian Marc-André Barriault in Vancouver on Saturday.

Kyle Daukaus has earned the nickname “The D’Arce Knight” for his absolutely terrifying front choke sequence, he has the kind of dangerous submission skills that makes his front headlock something to avoid at all costs if you are an opponent of his. With 17 MMA Wins for Daukaus and 12 by submission, 8 of them have come by front choke.

Daukaus isn’t exactly a one trick pony, despite his BJJ Black Belt his 2nd UFC stint has featured some legit work in the stand up and in the clinch, and with that crisp work on the feet has come some legit power. It was not long ago that Daukaus was the trendy underdog pick to beat Bo Nickal at the White House off of the back of what he looked like in his two return fights.

The main criticisms of someone like Kyle Daukaus is that despite his extremely high level BJJ, he often can struggle to employ that BJJ because his offensive wrestling leaves a lot to be desired, which forces him to be on his feet when he is at a disadvantage, and in Daukaus’ career his durability has been suspect, with 3 of his 5 losses happening by way of the knockout, so if he has to spend prolonged periods on the feet, his defensive woes will come back to haunt him in a fight against someone as pressure based as Marc-André Barriault.

Marc-André Barriault got his nickname “Powerbar” as a result of his relentless pressure and cardio base. One of those old school pressure do or die fighters, he lands 5.65 significant strikes per minute but also absorbs 5.94 per minutes, as he does find himself prone to being in those back and forth stand up wars that take years off of a fighters career, which has become a lot more difficult to deal now at the age of 36 for the Canadian.

MAB doesn’t work his offensive grappling, but his defensive grappling will be put to the test in this affair. Barriault’s defensive grappling is not a glaring weakness, but also not a massive strength, something he does do is punish poor grappling entries with dirty boxing and clinch work, which could prove important if he able to defend Daukaus’ grappling advances, he could find damage in close which could flip the fight upside down at any moment. If MAB wants to keep his path to victory, he has to keep Daukaus off of him so he can employ his own pressure striking style and force Daukaus to wilt under that pressure.

A couple years ago, I would have a very different tune on this fight. Marc-André Barriault’s pressure fighting style and competitive defensive grappling would seem like a nightmare for the Kyle Daukaus of old that would wilt under pressure and fall at the first sign of adversity. Fast forward to 2026, and MAB has taken multiple steps back and become just a tad slower, a tad less athletic, and most certainly less durable in his own right.

Daukaus is no longer that BJJ black belt that panics and folds once the grappling fails, he can hold his own on his feet and does have some stopping power in his strikes right now. I believe his new found confidence and ability on the feet has allowed his grappling to be more effective, as opposed to his earlier run where he couldn’t mix the martial arts. I have Kyle Daukaus finding a finish, and he could do it either way against the older Canadian on enemy turf.

Official Pick: Kyle Daukaus ITD (+100)
