Queen Mette-Marit of Norway reappears hand in hand with King Haakon on a surprise visit

TheTools · 17.11.2026 20:05 · source · permalink

Royal news

Mette Marit accompanied her husband on a surprise appearance

Hand in hand, coffees in tow, King Haakon and Queen Mette-Marit of Norway wandered through the heart of Trondheim on Tuesday morning, looking relaxed and in no hurry. It was a warm, unguarded scene, and the people nearby noticed. The appearance wasn't on the Royal Court's official calendar. 

The couple was photographed leaving the Britannia Hotel, where they’d spent the night, before setting off on foot through the city center. For royal watchers, the real story was the queen herself. According to the palace’s latest guidance, Mette-Marit isn’t expected to resume official engagements until summer 2027. 

A private trip with a very big date in mind

The palace confirmed that the king and queen were in Trondheim and planned to spend part of the day at Stiftsgården. Guri Varpe, the Royal Court’s head of communications, clarified that the visit was private, with no official engagements announced.

Stiftsgården has been the monarch’s official residence in Trondheim since 1906 and is one of the largest wooden buildings in Scandinavia. It regularly hosts royal stays, receptions and official events, and it’s set to play a starring role next year.

One of the reasons for the trip was to move ahead with preparations for the royal blessing ceremony scheduled for August 22, 2027, at Nidaros Cathedral. The tradition dates back to King Olav, who was blessed rather than crowned in 1958, and King Harald and Queen Sonja followed suit in 1991. Trondheim Mayor Kent Ranum said he’d had the chance to talk with the king and queen about the celebration, and that both are excited about it.

The cathedral was another stop on the day’s itinerary. Shortly after 2 p.m., Haakon and Mette-Marit arrived to see the space up close and meet some of the people who will help organize the ceremony. They even posed for a photo with someone who happened to be nearby. Bishop Olav Fykse Tveit explained that the king and queen had expressed a wish to visit the cathedral and meet the team behind the preparations. The August 2027 celebration will be especially meaningful for both Trondheim and the Norwegian monarchy.

All eyes on the queen

Beyond the 2027 planning, it was Mette-Marit’s presence that drew the most interest. The queen had a lung transplant in June after years of living with pulmonary fibrosis. She has stepped back from official duties to focus on her recovery, and every public sighting since has been followed closely. Until now, the palace had said she wouldn’t return to official engagements before next summer. That’s why it came as a surprise to see her strolling through Trondheim with the king and joining a day tied to one of the Crown’s most important upcoming celebrations.

The trip was private, though, and it doesn’t necessarily mean the queen will go back to her official schedule any earlier than planned. Her appearance at Haakon’s side was a rare chance to see her in public, but it didn’t announce any change to her plans.

A small protocol slip

The visit also came with a minor protocol anecdote. The night before, the royal standard flew over both the Royal Palace in Oslo and Stiftsgården at the same time. That’s unusual, since the standard normally isn’t raised in two places at once. The Royal Court explained that a mistake had been made and confirmed that the king and queen were in Trondheim.

In fact, the couple had already been spotted on Monday evening at Værnes Airport, where an 18-year-old recognized them and said she’d been surprised to run into the pair.

There’s still no news on when Mette-Marit will return to the official calendar. But her day in Trondheim delivered fresh images of the king and queen together, and a meaningful moment for a queen whose every step is being carefully watched.
