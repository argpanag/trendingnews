The Princess of Wales gives a masterclass in red carpet glamour on India tour

TheTools · 18.11.2026 03:29 · source · permalink

On day two of her Mumbai visit, Catherine set the gold standard of diplomatic dressing, honouring India’s tradition of craftsmanship

The Princess of Wales does red carpet glamour like nobody else, and she pulled out all the stops for the Earthshot Prize Awards on Tuesday night. 

Catherine wore a deep red and gold anarkali by the Indian designer Mahima Mahajan, while the Prince of Wales wore a bespoke bandghala by Manish Malhotra.

Earlier in the day, the Princess cut a businesslike figure on the second day of her visit to Mumbai, where she and William are attending a string of engagements relating to the Earthshot Prize.

Catherine wore two different suits on Tuesday: a cream design by British luxury label Edeline Lee, and a periwinkle blue one by Gabriela Hearst later in the day for the Children’s Earthshot Awards.

In fact, tailoring has been a consistent theme of her tourdrobe so far. On Monday, we saw her in a pistachio-hued blazer by Veronica Beard with Alexander McQueen trousers, and in the afternoon, a bespoke Roksanda midi dress.

Like the purpose of the trip itself, Catherine’s wardrobe has highlighted sustainability, innovation and the relationship between India and the United Kingdom. For independent labels which work hard to uphold rigorous ethical and sustainability standards, this is valuable support.

The eveningwear

Catherine’s Mahima Mahajan anarkali was made from handwoven silk georgette with traditional Banarasi gold detailing, in a rich vermilion hue achieved through the use of vegetable-based dyes. It featured hand-embroidery and glass bead embellishments, with a “conscious” choice of materials. 

The message imbued in the design is one that honours India’s longstanding tradition of craftsmanship. Indeed, much of the global fashion industry relies on India’s artisan workshops, and this look celebrates that tradition.

William’s bespoke bandghala featured hand-embroidered buttons. It was designed by Manish Malhotra, one of India’s most celebrated fashion and costume designers. The garment was made in India, naturally, a perfect homage to this year’s Earthshot Prize hosts.

The kind of careful planning, preparation and craft that went into these looks will have been significant. This is the gold standard of diplomatic dressing.

The blue suit

This is by Gabriela Hearst, a New York-based designer (and former creative director of Chloé) who is something of an agenda-setter when it comes to ethical and sustainable standards. The designer was raised on a ranch in Uruguay, and her sourcing of materials reflects this knowledge.

Catherine’s suit, which she last wore to Wimbledon in July, is a summer fabrication made up of 49 per cent virgin wool, 30 per cent silk and 21 per cent linen.

The Princess also wore Hearst’s designs ahead of the Earthshot Prize Awards in Boston in 2022.

The suit was paired with Van Cleef & Arpels’ Magic Alhambra earrings.

The white suit

Catherine’s first outing on Tuesday was to meet with the 15 finalists of this year’s Earthshot Prize at Mumbai’s Bandra Worli Sea Link Promenade.

She chose a cream Edeline Lee suit for the occasion, which she teamed with a Holland Cooper shirt bodysuit, an Asprey London necklace featuring a trio of woodland charms and a pair of £90 earrings by Chalk. The look was completed by her faithful Gianvito Rossi 105mm suede courts and a pair of Ralph Lauren sunglasses.

The Princess has a number of pieces by Lee, a Canadian-born, London-based designer whose designs have become a favourite with high profile women who wield “soft power” with their wardrobes.

This is thanks to her fabrics – including a signature bubble jacquard – which are non-creasing. Lee’s cuts are formalwear friendly without looking stuffy or dated. Every piece is made in London, indicating a very low carbon footprint, and timeless enough to wear for decades.
