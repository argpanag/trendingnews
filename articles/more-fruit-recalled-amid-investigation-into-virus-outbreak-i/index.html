More fruit recalled amid investigation into virus outbreak in Canada | insauga

A recall warning has been issued amid an investigation into a foodborne illness outbreak in Canada, officials say.

The Canadian Food Inspection Agency issued an alert Tuesday for certain raspberries and products that contain raspberries “due to possible norovirus contamination.”

The CFIA said the recall was triggered by an investigation into a foodborne illness outbreak.

Earlier this month, the agency announced a recall of two Alasko brand raspberry products due to the same issue.

The affected products in that case were distributed in Ontario, Quebec, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island.

The CFIA said at the time that recall was triggered by an investigation into an illness outbreak and that there had been reported illnesses associated with the products, but did not specify how many people may have gotten sick.

In the notice issued Tuesday, the agency said there haven’t been any reported illnesses associated with the latest impacted products, which were distributed in Ontario.

They were sold at the following Summerhill Market locations: (1) 446 Summerhill Avenue, Toronto, Ontario (2); 3609 Dundas Street West, York, Ontario; (3) 484 Eglinton Avenue West, Toronto, Ontario; (4) 1054 Mount Pleasant Road, Toronto, Ontario; (5) 32 Wellington Street West, Aurora, Ontario; (6) 1014 Bathurst Street, Toronto, Ontario.

Summerhill Market said in a social media post that the recalled Alasko raspberries are in those products.

Consumers are being advised to check to see if they have any of the recalled products.

If so, they should be thrown out or returned to where they were purchased.

The CFIA said a food safety investigation is underway and more recalls could be issued. It is also verifying that the impacted products have been removed from the marketplace.

“People with norovirus illness usually develop symptoms of gastroenteritis within 24 to 48 hours, but symptoms can start as early as 12 hours after exposure,” the CFIA said.

“The illness often begins suddenly. Even after having the illness, you can still become re-infected by norovirus. The main symptoms of norovirus illness are diarrhea, vomiting (children usually experience more vomiting than adults), nausea and stomach cramps.”

Other symptoms could include a low-grade fever, chills, headache, muscle aches, and fatigue.

“Most people feel better within one or two days, with symptoms resolving on their own, and experience no long-term health effects. As with any illness causing diarrhea or vomiting, people who are ill should drink plenty of liquids to replace lost body fluids and prevent dehydration,” the CFIA said.

“In severe cases, patients may need to be hospitalized and given fluids intravenously.”
