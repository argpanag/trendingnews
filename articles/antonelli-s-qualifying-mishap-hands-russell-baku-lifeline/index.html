Antonelli's qualifying mishap hands Russell Baku lifeline

TheTools · 25.09.2026 13:48 · source · permalink

Getty

George Russell has been handed a major world championship lifeline after he capitalised on Kimi Antonelli's qualifying crash to take an emphatic pole position for Saturday’s Azerbaijan Grand Prix.

Antonelli, who has reigned supreme so far this season – winning eight of the 14 rounds staged to secure an 81-point lead – displayed the first dent in his title armour when he misjudged the opening corner and hit the wall in Baku.

The Q1 accident meant Antonelli could play no further part to leave him 16th on the grid for Saturday’s race.

Russell had all but written off his championship hopes but Antonelli’s demise coupled with his impressive pole lap, his first since the Austrian Grand Prix in June, will provide him with a glimmer of hope that the fight remains on.

Remarkably, Russell was nearly nine tenths faster than anybody else. He will be joined on the front row by Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc with Oscar Piastri third for McLaren.

Russell said in his post-qualifying interview: "It has been a while since I have been on pole, so it definitely feels good.

"It is probably the biggest (pole) margin of my whole career, even in my Formula Two and Formula Three days, and that felt really sweet. The car has been feeling great this weekend. It was just the perfect lap at the end.

"We are in a great place to go for the win, but this is Baku and anything can happen."

Isack Hadjar lines up from fourth ahead of Lando Norris and Lewis Hamilton. Max Verstappen qualified only eighth.

Antonelli (20) had not looked like a man rattled by the expectation of closing out his first title and becoming the youngest Formula One world champion.

But Friday’s qualifying provided the first sign he could yet buckle under the pressure.

Antonelli was playing catch-up after being sidelined for half of Thursday’s practice sessions on the Caspian Sea with a hydraulic failure. And in the opening phase of qualifying he thudded the barrier as he entered the first turn, sustaining terminal damage to his Mercedes.

Antonelli attempted to limp back to the pits, with his front-left tyre dangling off his Mercedes, but he was instructed by race engineer Peter Bonnington to park his stricken machine.

"Let me try, mate," he pleaded over the radio. "Let me try."

"We just better park up, mate," said Bonnington. "We’re not going to be able to fix that."

Antonelli replied: "I’m sorry guys. My god, f***!"

The Italian was left to watch the remainder of qualifying as an observer and stew on his error.

However, all is not lost for Antonelli. Unlike a week ago in Madrid, overtaking is possible here. He will also be able to draw confidence from his lung-busting drive back from 19th to win in Monza earlier this month.

But Russell will see Saturday’s fixture as a major chance to take only his third win of the season and reel Antonelli back in.
