Oil prices fall after Iran supreme leader and top security official discuss conditions to end war

TheTools · 27.10.2026 05:09 · source · permalink

In this article

Crude oil prices fell for the second consecutive session Tuesday, driven by a renewed diplomatic push to de-escalate tensions in the Middle East.

Brent crude futures, the international benchmark, lost 1.7% to close at $113.88 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures fell 1.8% to settle at $99.90 a barrel.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei met with his top national security official Mohsen Rezaei to discuss the Islamic Republic's conditions for ending the war with the U.S., according to a Tuesday post in English to the supreme leader's social media account.

Khamenei said the current state of "neither war nor peace" needs to end, according to the post. He discussed "safeguarding national and regional security by maintaining control" over the Strait of Hormuz.

Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia's defense minister and Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian held direct talks in Tehran on Monday to de-escalate tensions. Senior military and intelligence officials from Pakistan and Turkey, who are allied with the Saudis, also participated.

Saudi Arabia has come under repeated attack from Iran-allied Houthi militants in recent weeks. The Houthis have bombed energy infrastructure as well as civilian targets such as airports.

The Houthi strikes threaten to disrupt Saudi oil exports, which have proven resilient during eight months of conflict in the region.

Iran Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said the talks in Tehran were a step toward regional security cooperation and further consultations are possible, according to the state media outlet Tasnim.

The diplomatic push comes as U.S. President Donald Trump said in an interview with Fox News on Sunday that he was approaching a decision on whether to strike a deal with Iran or intensify military operations, suggesting that additional attacks remained an option.

Brent oil prices are expected to remain near $110 a barrel through the end of the year, said UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo in a Tuesday note. The oil market hasn't normalized yet even though exports from the Middle East have increased compared to prior stages of the war, Staunovo said.

"Vessels remain exposed to attacks, insurance and freight costs have risen sharply, and increased ship-to-ship transfers required to cross the Strait of Hormuz are absorbing tanker capacity," the analyst said.

Global oil inventories have fallen by around 500 million barrels since February, Staunovo said. The release of strategic stockpiles by governments is largely over, he said. Commercial inventories will fall more sharply in the coming months as a consequence if security conditions don't improve, the analyst said.

"With global visible oil inventories at multi-year lows and likely to take time to rebuild once the conflict is resolved, oil prices may also remain higher for longer as inventories first need to be replenished," he said.
