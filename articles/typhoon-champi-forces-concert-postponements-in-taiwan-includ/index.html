Typhoon Champi forces concert postponements in Taiwan, including Jessica and PLAVE

TheTools · 23.10.2026 22:30 · source · permalink

Once a super typhoon, the 30th typhoon "Champi" approached Taiwan, paralyzing the entire island and disrupting schedules for K-pop artists preparing concerts in Taiwan.

According to OSEN reporting on the 23rd, the aftermath of the giant typhoon "Champi" approaching Taiwan postponed that day's scheduled concerts by PLAVE and Jessica and the "2026 Hualien K-wave Original Sound Festival" performance schedule.

Earlier, Taiwan's Central Disaster Response Center announced that as of 6:30 a.m. that day one person had died and 94 were injured due to typhoon Champi. About 1,300 residents in mountainous areas evacuated, and facilities damage continued, including a large crane involved in breakwater construction at Fugang Harbor in Taitung County being swept away by waves.

According to Taiwan's Civil Aeronautics Administration under the Ministry of Transportation and Communications, as of 10 a.m. that day a total of 977 flights were canceled, including 262 domestic flights and 715 flights on routes to and from international destinations and mainland China, and 96 passenger ships were canceled. The Taiwan Stock Exchange was closed for the day, and 20 local governments, including the capital Taipei, issued business and school closures.

Due to this large-scale emergency response, various performances and concert schedules planned for that day were disrupted. PLAVE had originally planned to perform the 2026 world tour "KEEP IT MANIC" in Taipei at Linkou Arena on April 23 and 24, but the school closure order caused the April 23 performance to be postponed to 6 p.m. on the 25th (local time). The performance scheduled for 4 p.m. on the 24th will proceed as planned, but if a school closure is also issued on the 24th due to storm damage, further schedule adjustments are planned.

Jessica's 2026 concert tour "Reflections" in Taipei, originally scheduled for 6 p.m. on the 24th at the National Taiwan University Gymnasium first-floor performance hall, was postponed to Feb. 20 next year. Although no special measures such as school closures had been issued for the 24th, the promoter decided to postpone due to traffic congestion and safety concerns caused by strong winds and heavy rain. The venue and time of the performance remain unchanged.

In particular, the Hualien East Gate Square was scheduled to host the "2026 Hualien K-wave Original Sound Festival" on the 24th and 25th, featuring K-pop artists including TNX, Xdinary Heroes, BTOB, Hyolyn, AtHeart, Lee Chaeyeon and Ryeowook, but this was also postponed. The "2026 Hualien K-wave Original Sound Festival" will be held at the same venue one day later, on the 25th and 26th at 7 p.m.

Typhoon Champi was located about 220 km east of Taiwan's coast that morning and was moving along the northeastern coast. It had briefly intensified to an ultra-strong level and weakened somewhat, but powerful gusts with maximum instantaneous wind speeds of up to 191 km/h (about 53 m/s) were observed near its center. Taiwan's meteorological authorities predict the typhoon will move northward past northeastern Taiwan toward the southwestern islands of Japan and then toward the Chinese coast. However, changes in its speed or path could make direct landfall on Taiwan's main island possible, so authorities are urging caution.
