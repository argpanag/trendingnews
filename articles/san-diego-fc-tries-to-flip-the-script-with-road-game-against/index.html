San Diego FC tries to ‘flip the script’ with road game against Lionel Messi and Inter Miami

TheTools · 19.09.2026 22:47 · source · permalink

There are two ways to look at San Diego FC’s match on Sunday against Lionel Messi and Inter Miami.

One is that getting on a plane for five hours and flying across the country to face the defending MLS Cup champion with arguably the greatest player in the sport’s history is not ideal when you’ve lost four straight by a combined score of 10-2 and won only two of 14 on the road this season.

“You can also look at it the other way,” SDFC defender Christopher McVey said. “If we go there and perform, that can also flip (things) a lot and get the confidence we need. I would look at it as more of an opportunity.”

And they’ll get one.

If ever there was a good time to brave the humidity of South Florida and the artistry of Messi, it might be this week. Inter Miami has other things on its mind than a team that lost its last MLS outing 5-nil at home.

There’s new coach Cristian “Kily” Gonzalez, Messi’s (presumably) hand-picked replacement for interim coach Guillermo Hoyas, who stepped in when Javier Mascherano unexpectedly resigned in April, four months after lifting the MLS Cup.

Gonzalez, Messi’s former teammate with Argentina, was announced last month but didn’t finalize his U.S. work permit until Tuesday.

He got off the plane, went straight to his first training session, then was on the sideline the next night for the Campeones Cup, a contrived annual event pitting the MLS and Liga MX champions. Inter Miami, wanting more silverware for the mantle, took it seriously and played the 39-year-old Messi and 39-year-old Luis Suarez (and many of their other regulars) the full 90 minutes on a steamy night in a 2-0 victory against Cruz Azul.

Messi celebrated his 100th Inter Miami goal and 49th career trophy by confronting MLS commissioner Don Garber on the field afterward, angrily pointing his finger at him not once but twice.

Why?

Messi is almost never made available to media, and MLS isn’t commenting, either. That started the speculation train rolling down the tracks, whether it was from Garber suspending him for a game after skipping the 2025 All-Star Game or from Messi’s displeasure with MLS officials.

“He’s still mad at me, man,” Garber said in a July interview with Front Office Sports. “We’re communicating a little bit more. … I’m not sure that he looks at me as being the guy that picks the officials, I don’t, but he’s gotta complain to somebody. I’ll take it.”

There are also the recent fines and one-game suspension of Hoyas over Inter Miami’s penchant for coming out of the locker room late before the opening kickoff or at halftime, viewed as a form of gamesmanship.

Another thing: Inter Miami (12-4-9) is in second place in the Eastern Conference but has its own run of poor form, with only one win in its last eight league matches.

“Every challenge is also an opportunity,” said SDFC coach Mikey Varas, whose team enters the day just outside the playoffs in the Western Conference. “It’s an opportunity to show who you are in these tough moments. … They have top players. They have the best player in the history of the game, but it’s (a match) that we’re all really excited for because there’s not a single person who has woken up in the past couple of days very happy with where we’re at and what’s happening.

“We just want to get to work to see if we can flip the script.”

SDFC (8-11-6) also had a midweek game, but Varas held back his regulars from the 1-0 loss to the Tijuana Xolos in the Baja Cup exhibition Wednesday, instead using reserves and a half-dozen players from Right to Dream’s international academy that happened to be in town training. That allowed him to hold a few extra training sessions with his starters to prepare tactically for Inter Miami’s potent offense.

SDFC also has the advantage of familiarity on its side. McVey and goalkeeper CJ dos Santos both played at Inter Miami with Messi before coming to SDFC. They know him, they know his tendencies, they’re not intimidated by him.

“He’s very good when he comes toward you with speed, facing you,” said McVey, a central defender. “Then it’s very hard to defend him and you’re going to need help. But if you can get to him early before he gets the ball and has back turned to you, then that’s your best opportunity to do something about it.

“Then again, there are a lot of other good players on that team, so you can’t just focus on one player. It’s going to be very important for us to keep the ball as much as possible, to have them with less of the ball.”

It is the first-ever meeting between the teams, and the tendency for young players is to be in awe of Messi (and SDFC has one of the youngest rosters in MLS).

“Initially when you first meet him, he has an aura about him,” dos Santos said. “Like, he’s almost glowing. He just has that air about him. But once you have a conversation with him, he’s like everybody else. You talk about family, you talk about life experiences, you talk about football. It was really fun getting to know him on a personal level.”

Dos Santos was the backup goalie for Cape Verde, which played Argentina in Miami in the knockout phase of the World Cup in July. Messi spotted his former teammate in the tunnel before kickoff and immediately came over to say hi.

“It just shows how he is an individual and as a human being,” dos Santos said. “He didn’t have to do that until the end of the game. I’m very grateful and very appreciative of that kind of gesture.”

San Diego FC (8-11-6) vs. Inter Miami (12-4-9)

When: 4 p.m. Sunday

TV: Apple TV
