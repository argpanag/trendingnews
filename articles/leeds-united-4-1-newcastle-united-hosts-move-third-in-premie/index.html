  3. Leeds up to third with fine win over Newcastlepublished at 22:58 BST 14 September
    22:58 BST 14 September

    FT: Leeds 4-1 Newcastle

    Charlotte Coates
    BBC Sport

    Leeds United climbed to third in the Premier League with a superb Elland Road performance as they inflicted a heavy 4-1 defeat on a helpless Newcastle United.

    A 14-minute burst in the first half proved too much for the Magpies, with an own goal from Lewis Miley following strikes by Jayden Bogle and Dominic Calvert-Lewin, before Noah Okafor netted in the second period.

    Leeds now sit third on eight points - just four adrift of champions and leaders Arsenal.

    Daniel Farke's side needed a slice of luck to take the lead when Ao Tanaka's 32nd-minute strike took a wicked double deflection off Sean Steur and Miley before looping into the bottom corner.

    But just two minutes and 42 seconds later, Calvert-Lewin thought he had headed in Leeds' second but the final touch came off the top of team-mate Bogle's head for 2-0.

    But, a minute into stoppage time, Calvert-Lewin claimed his goal with a composed finish across Lukas Hornicek - taking his career tally against Newcastle to eight.

    It went from bad to worse for Newcastle in the second half when substitute Okafor slotted home Leeds' fourth of the night.

    Substitute Bazoumana Toure managed to find a consolation goal for the visitors in the 90th minute, but it was too little too late.

  4. 'It's a lot of work at the training ground to be that intense'published at 22:57 BST 14 September
    22:57 BST 14 September

    FT: Leeds 4-1 Newcastle

    Leeds United

    Leeds boss Daniel Farke on the game: "For each manager, it's a nightmare to concede so late after a performance like that. We allowed them to score, doesn't take anything away from us. My boys were brilliant, perfect start. Even in a perfect performance there's always something to moan about, this was definitely one of the rare points I have to criticise.

    "Energy, quality, we knew we needed to find a good balance but we were aware of their [Newcastle's] pace and their counter attack. We found the balance today, we were the dominant side.

    "We have good fitness levels, you always need to find the right triggers. When you press, everyone has to be on the same page. It's a lot of work at the training ground to be that intense. It's not easy otherwise all the other opponents would do it. Such hard work, we had more chances to score tonight, overall happy with the performance."

    On the first goal being lucky: "Wouldn't say lucky. We brought ourself into a good shooting position, there was quality of the strike for my players. Sometimes there's deflections but it was an important combination of us in that area."

    On Dominic Calvert-Lewin: "He plays with confidence, his fitness is up. I expect him to lead the group. He's taken responsibility, never in doubt that he scores goals. It's not just the goals though, the way he plays is outstanding. He's one of the standout strikers in this league."

    "Tonight, we'll enjoy the win. Tomorrow, we prepare for our next game on Sunday."

Leeds analysis: Reason for optimism

Farke 'very happy' with 'top class' Leeds

Two wins and two draws have taken Leeds up to third in the Premier League after four games, with only Manchester City and Arsenal having more points.

After securing safety on their return to the top flight last season with a 14th-place finish, this is a start Leeds fans would have dreamed of.

In fact, only in 1995-96, 1998-99, 2001-02, and now 2026-27 have Leeds avoided defeat in each of their first four in the competition in the Premier League era.

And that is not the only positive, as Elland Road is becoming the fortress Farke will have hoped for, with their last defeat coming on 3 March.

Calvert-Lewin was at his physical best against the team he has more goals against than any other.

The Leeds number nine certainly shows up at home. Since the start of last season, only Erling Haaland (16) and Igor Thiago (13) have scored more Premier League home goals than Calvert-Lewin's 10.

It is hard to know what Leeds' true ambitions are this term, but they may be using Sunderland's seventh-place finish last season as an example of what is achievable.

Newcastle analysis: Jaissle has work to do

Newcastle couldn't match the physicality of Leeds - Jaissle

After Eddie Howe handed in his resignation following the departure of key players such as Anthony Gordon, Sandro Tonali and Bruno Guimaraes, many tipped new boss Matthias Jaissle's Newcastle to struggle this season.

After conceding a 99th-minute equaliser against Liverpool on the opening day, perceptions shifted and carried on into victory at Tottenham Hotspur and a comeback draw with Bournemouth.

But this thrashing by Leeds is a wake-up call.

At times, Newcastle have looked wide open at the back with goalkeeper Hornicek making the most saves in the league so far.

Not only did Leeds score four, but they had 16 attempts on Newcastle's goal and will feel they should have scored more.

Once Leeds' challenge gathered pace, it felt as though the Magpies had no answer for it - and the scoreboard reflected that.

After an encouraging start with five points out of a possible nine, the real work will start for Jaissle after this humbling loss.
