Winning formula in Game 5? 'Rays baseball at its finest'

TheTools · 20.10.2026 04:06 · source · permalink

CLEVELAND -- The Rays were Rays-ing throughout Sunday evening at Progressive Field.

What does that mean? Well, for one, it means Tampa Bay is heading home one win away from punching a ticket to the World Series, as a 3-1 win over Cleveland in Game 5 of the American League Championship Series gave the club a 3-2 lead in this best-of-seven round.

It also means the Rays remain quite dangerous, because they’re doing all of the things that have made this a successful 2026 season featuring 98 regular-season wins, an AL East championship and an AL Division Series sweep of the Yankees.

The timely hits were there. The defense was as crisp as could be. The pitching delivered.

“That was Rays baseball at its finest,” right-handed reliever Tyler Wells said.

So many elements go into what makes Tampa Bay such a good, well-rounded baseball team.

The ability to generate offense

The Rays have some sluggers, but they’ve hit only two home runs during the ALCS.

“Hopefully, they're coming,” manager Kevin Cash said.

However, even though they still didn’t during a homerless Game 5, it wasn’t an issue for Tampa Bay, which scored a run in each of the first three innings of a playoff game for the fourth time in franchise history. (The previous occurrences were the 2019 AL Wild Card Game at the Athletics, ‘20 ALWC Game 2 vs. the Blue Jays and ‘20 ALDS Game 2 vs. the Yankees.)

Yandy Díaz led off Sunday’s game with a double, later scoring on a bases-loaded walk by Jonny DeLuca. The Rays knocked left-hander Parker Messick from the game after two innings, as Jonathan Aranda’s one-out RBI single in the second made it a 2-0 lead. And Liam Hicks added a one-out RBI single in the third.

“There’s still pressure on the offense after, but I think that early momentum shift is huge, and especially in the first inning,” DeLuca said. “Once bats get hot, the saying of it being contagious is true. So I feel like we’ve got it going, and we’re going to keep it going.”

“We know how good Parker Messick is,” Cash added. “I think we took advantage of getting ourselves in some favorable counts and getting some big hits.”

Sterling defense

We rarely see Tampa Bay beat itself in the field. Clean defense has become the expectation.

The Rays showed it time after time once again in Game 5. Ben Williamson made a diving stop at second base to rob José Ramírez of a hit in the fourth. Taylor Walls, an AL Gold Glove Award finalist at shortstop, made a spectacular spinning play up the middle before nabbing Petey Halpin at first to end the fifth.

“Taylor Walls is one of the best shortstops on the planet,” Cleveland manager Stephen Vogt said. “That's why he's in there. He makes every play. If you hit it to him, he catches it, you're out. And they've played some really good defense. Ben Williamson had a couple nice plays at second base tonight as well.”

It’s important to note how much Aranda contributes at first as well. His scoops on some of the throws from Walls, Williamson and others are as important as the tosses themselves.

“He killed it over there,” right-handed starter Drew Rasmussen said.

The pitchers sure love to see it.

“It makes your job so much easier,” Wells said. “That’s that trust piece, right? To be able to know that those guys are going to go out there and they’re going to make plays for you.”

Dominant pitching

Rasmussen’s seven-inning, one-run gem came as no surprise. The 31-year-old ace is having a tremendous postseason, having delivered three strong outings, previously tossing eight scoreless frames in ALDS Game 1 vs. the Yankees and six-plus innings of two-run ball in Game 1 of this ALCS against the Guardians.

But more pieces of Tampa Bay’s pitching staff came up big again. Left-hander Cam Booser recorded the first two outs of the eighth. Wells came in and struck out Ramírez to strand a runner on first in that inning, letting out a ton of emotion while coming off the mound.

Then, All-Star closer Bryan Baker’s breakout campaign continued with his fifth save over six scoreless appearances this postseason. The 31-year-old right-hander is the third AL/NL pitcher to finish a game in each of his first six appearances of a postseason while giving up only one total hit, along with Jason Motte (2011 Cardinals) and Mark Wohlers (1996 Braves).

The high-leverage arms in the bullpen are delivering when called upon, and the Rays will hope that continues despite no off-days remaining in the ALCS, with Game 6 on Monday night and a potential Game 7 on Tuesday night.

“It’s probably a good thing to have so many options,” Baker said. “There are so many different matchups that you run into -- lefty, right, different parts of the order, parts of the order with power, parts of the order without. We’ve done a good job of giving Cash and the coaching staff a lot of options in different scenarios, and we’ve proven that a bunch of different guys can go out there and do it.”
