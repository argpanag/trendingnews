Lewis Moody: I do not believe rugby caused my MND

TheTools · 25.09.2026 22:04 · source · permalink

In emotional documentary, World Cup winner reveals relief after talk with specialist and insists he reflects on his career ‘only with joy’

Lewis Moody, the former England captain, says he is “at peace” after receiving reassurances from a medical expert that there was no direct link between his outstanding rugby career and his diagnosis with motor neurone disease.

Moody, who captained England during their 2011 World Cup campaign and won 71 caps for his country and also three Test caps for the British and Irish Lions, was diagnosed with the illness in October last year.

Moody, who was awarded an MBE for services to rugby after England’s 2003 World Cup victory, is the latest former rugby player to be diagnosed with the disease. Both Doddie Weir, the former Scotland and Lions player, and Rob Burrow, a rugby league player, died after suffering from the condition, as did Joost van der Westhuizen, the South African World Cup winner. Ed Slater, the former Leicester and Gloucester lock, was forced to retire from rugby in 2022 because of MND.

The 48-year-old, who won 10 trophies with Leicester Tigers before finishing his career at Bath, in an emotional and compelling new TNT Sports documentary, asks Martin Turner, one of the UK’s leading specialist neurologists dealing with MND, if his rugby career had any impact on his condition.

“One of the questions that someone asked me recently was, ‘You must be devastated that rugby’s put you in this position?’ Moody said. “I was like, ‘Is he meaning motor neurone disease?’ I was just baffled by it, but it made me ask the questions of the specialists, ‘Is that the reason I am here?’

“I literally asked Martin that direct question. And Martin was like, ‘Rugby isn’t the reason you have MND. There are a number of things that come together, coincidentally or not, that you are more predetermined or disposed to than anyone else. It might be genetic. It might be lifestyle.

“So, I suppose it was a relief in many ways because in my head I was like I was almost angry at the person for asking that question, but it made me question it more and make sure that I checked in with the specialist.

“There is an anecdotal conversation around there potentially being a link with extreme exertion and motor neurone disease that would be one aspect of why it happens.

“I’ve been living with it for a year, and when anyone talks about it to me, I literally feel like someone’s punching me in the face with information. For me, I try and stay away from too much of the detail around it.”

A 2022 study by the University of Glasgow found that rugby players in its sample were 15 times more likely to develop MND, yet much about the disease remains unknown. Initial studies have shown that another group also at an increased risk of developing MND is cross-country skiers, a sport which involves relatively little risk of head trauma.

Moody says he has no regrets about his rugby career but is now determined to use his energy to fight to raise funds and interest in finding a medical solution to the disease, following on the work done by Weir and Burrow.

“I’m at peace with my diagnosis. I was at peace almost immediately. That might sound strange, but the uncertainty is the most harrowing part of it,” Moody said.

“I look back and think about my career rarely, but when I do, only with joy. Considering I had 16 operations, dealt with ulcerative colitis during that time, all the other things that come with being dropped and form, and the criticism around performance or around leadership. I still f------ loved it. Absolutely loved it. I would do it all again in the heartbeat.

“There’s nothing I can do to change my circumstances, other than to use my energy to raise awareness so that the people who are fighting this disease have the resources they need so we can get to the outcome we all want. It isn’t far off.”

Moody’s son Dylan, 18, is a goalkeeper for Championship side Southampton and alongside Arsenal player Max Dowman, is a current member of England’s Under-19 squad. Moody’s youngest son Ethan, 16, is a promising rugby player who is in Bath’s age grade set-up.

“I’d absolutely love it,” Moody said, when asked if he’d welcome Dylan representing England at senior level in football. “He’s got his professional contract and he’s doing really well with England.

“He’s got a lot of work to do until he gets to that stage, but he’s got a great attitude and if he sticks at it and he really wants it, I’m sure he’ll get where he wants.

“I’d like to think I’m supportive. I remember talking to Dylan and I asked him what he wanted to do. He said: “I want to play for England.” I had that classic adult response and went: “Is that a bit lofty?” But I apologised because it’s not my place to diminish his excitement. If that’s his goal, I’m going to be there to support if he needs advice. I love the fact the kids are passionate about something they enjoy. Ethan is in the throes of the academy set-up with Bath. I’m excited.”
