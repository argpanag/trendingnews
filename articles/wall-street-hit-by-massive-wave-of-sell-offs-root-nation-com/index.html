Wall Street Hit by Massive Wave of Sell-Offs - Root-Nation.com

A sharp drop in technology stocks on Wall Street sent the global MSCI stock index down 1.5% on Friday. Investors began moving capital into safer assets, including US government bonds. The sell-off was driven by concerns that large-scale investments in AI development, which require substantial financial resources, may not generate sufficient returns for companies.

Shares of processor maker Intel plunged 28.6%, while Amazon securities fell 1.4%. The financial results of both companies showed that significant investments in AI technology development are accompanied by heavy cash spending. Intel’s warning that demand for personal computers was slowing provided an additional negative signal for the market. The company expects this trend could continue into next year.

Alphabet, Google’s parent company, also continued to lose market value. Its shares fell another 1.4% on Friday after plunging 12.6% the previous day. The decline followed reports that the company’s large-scale AI spending had pushed its cash flow into negative territory.

“We have seen such powerful growth across this entire segment,” said Sahak Manuelian, managing director of global equity sales and trading at Wedbush Securities, commenting on the performance of AI-related companies’ shares. According to him, an extremely sharp decline can inevitably follow an almost parabolic rise. That is what the market is seeing now, as some investors begin withdrawing money from the segment and looking for other areas in which to invest.

Earlier, Gene Goldman, chief investment officer at Cetera, called Friday’s stock trading a genuine “nightmare,” with AI spending the main culprit. Significant cash burn and high capital expenditures are making investors question whether future AI revenue will justify the money invested.

Goldman also pointed to additional factors that intensified negative market sentiment. Oil prices are rising amid increased attacks in the Middle East, while concerns about a hack of Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, further complicated the situation. Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies also fell against this backdrop.

At the close of Friday’s trading session, the Dow Jones Industrial Average had lost 572.25 points, or 1.09%, ending at 51,770.31. The S&P 500 fell 140.32 points, or 1.82%, to 7,580.07. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 747.73 points, or 2.86%, to 25,437. The global MSCI index, which tracks stock performance across countries worldwide, lost 17.15 points, or 1.51%, falling to 1,115.47. Earlier, the pan-European STOXX 600 ended the session down 0.36%.

The sell-off in Intel shares spread across the semiconductor manufacturing sector. The PHLX Semiconductor Index, which tracks the segment’s performance, ended the day down more than 7%. During trading, it fell to its lowest level since May, while the final decline was the largest since late June. The technology sector of the S&P 500 also posted its worst result since early June, losing 4.2%.

Amid the technology-stock sell-off, investors began favoring defensive sectors. Demand increased for shares of consumer and utilities companies. Energy companies posted the strongest performance, helped by higher oil prices and the financial results of Exxon Mobil and Chevron. Both oil corporations reported substantial profits driven by high refining margins. Those margins were supported by disruptions to oil supplies caused by Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries and the war with Iran.

In currency markets, the Japanese yen was heading toward its third consecutive day of gains against the US dollar on Friday. This followed the Bank of Japan’s decision to leave interest rates unchanged while revising its inflation forecast upward. The yen received additional support from a new sell-off in AI-related stocks, which prompted investors to seek safe-haven assets outside the dollar more actively.

The dollar index, which measures the currency’s value against a basket of major currencies including the yen and euro, rose 0.04% to 101.92. The euro, meanwhile, fell 0.13% to $1.1163. The dollar declined 0.1% against the Japanese yen, to 157.54 yen. Negative sentiment also prevailed in cryptocurrency markets. Bitcoin fell 2.49% to $76,877.30, while Ethereum lost 1.01% and traded at $2,236.76.

US government bonds, by contrast, rose in value. The large-scale technology-stock sell-off drove investors into this traditional safe-haven asset. The yield on benchmark 10-year US Treasury bonds fell 5.8 basis points to 4.931%. The yield on 30-year securities declined 1.97 basis points to 5.3093%.

The yield on 2-year Treasury bonds, which typically responds to expectations for the US Federal Reserve’s future monetary policy, fell 6.64 basis points to 4.59%. Meanwhile, the 10-year yield was approaching its largest monthly decline in nearly three years.

Oil prices reached their highest levels in six months at the close of trading. Traders were preparing for a possible escalation of the war with Iran after the US midterm elections scheduled for Tuesday. The conflict has already lasted eight months, but there are still no signs that it is ending. An attack on a tanker near the Strait of Hormuz on Friday provided an additional reminder of the risks to energy supplies.

US WTI crude closed at $104.12 per barrel, adding 2.08%, or $2.12. Brent futures rose 2.4%, or $2.76, to $117.63 per barrel.

Precious-metals prices, by contrast, fell sharply. Gold dropped to its lowest level in one year on Friday, while silver fell below $50 per ounce for the first time since November 2025. Rising energy costs and persistent inflation concerns strengthened expectations that US interest rates would remain high.

The spot price of gold fell 3.63% to $3,874.58 per ounce. Silver fell even further, declining 6.36% to $49.06 per ounce.
