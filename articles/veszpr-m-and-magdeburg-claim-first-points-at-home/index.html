Veszprém and Magdeburg claim first points at home

TheTools · 10.09.2026 08:50 · source · permalink

The 2026/27 season of the EHF Champions League officially got underway on Wednesday night, as seven games were played on the opening evening of the group phase.

Defending champions Barça took a while to warm up before cruising past SAH - Aarhus (39:28), while last season's EHF FINAL4 participants had mixed fortunes. SC Magdeburg took the points against Kolstad (37:22), while Aalborg saw victory slip away as Paris salvaged a 33:33 draw at the buzzer. The other French side in action, Nantes, had to wait until the very last second to edge Plock 26:25.

Paris were the only away team to take a point, as Celje, Porto and Plock all returned home empty-handed. While Kriens-Luzern came close in Kielce, they lacked the experience needed to create the first upset of the season.

GROUP A

One Veszprém HC (HUN) vs FC Porto (POR) 36:28 (15:14)

H2H: 8-1-0
Top scorers: Hugo Descat and Nedim Rémili (Veszprém) 7 goals ; Guilherme Borges Moraes Silva (Porto) 7 goals

With five saves in the first 13 minutes, Veszprém’s Emil Nielsen proved to be the main difference between the two teams in his first EHF Champions League match for the Hungarian side after lifting the trophy with Barça in June. The hosts took an early three-goal lead, and even a five-goal one at the end of the first quarter. But both Porto goalkeepers stepped it up a gear to match Nielsen’s antics, and as Guilherme Borges scored into the empty goal on the buzzer, Porto were down by just one at half-time. However the Portuguese side ruined all its good work in the first five minutes of the second half, conceding five unanswered goals. All the hosts then had to do was cruise to their first win of the season, even offering playing time to all their 16 players.

GROUP B

HBC Nantes (FRA) vs Orlen Wisla Plock (POL) 26:25 (11:11)

H2H: 4-1-2
Top scorers: Ian Tarrafeta (Nantes) 9 goals; Dawid Dawydzik (Płock) 7 goals

It took Nantes nine minutes to score their first goal, and by then Płock had already beaten Christoffer Bonde five times. But that did not mean the hosts were not going to fight back. As Bonde made crucial saves between the posts and Ian Tarrafeta made a decisive impact after entering the court, “Le H” were back in the game by the time the half-time break came into sight. The game remained tight until the very last second, as the hosts moved two goals ahead on multiple occasions but never put the game beyond their opponents' reach. In the end, it all came down to the final second, when Noam Leopold converted a penalty to help his side edge Płock by the smallest of margins.

GROUP C

Aalborg Håndbold (DEN) vs Paris Saint-Germain (FRA) 33:33 (16:18)

H2H: 1-1-6
Top scorers: Mads Hoxer and Juri Knorr (Aalborg) 7 goals; Ferran Solé (Paris) 10 goals

Both teams seemed to be on an even footing in the first quarter of the game, but Paris’ fast breaks clearly made the difference. As Mikkel Løvkvist made a couple of important early saves, Paris pushed every ball and took a three-goal advantage in the 18th minute, when Ferran Solé scored his third goal. It took a while for Aalborg to narrow the deficit, but as Thomas Arnoldsen scored on the buzzer, the hosts were down by only two at the break. The Danish side came out of the dressing room with the momentum in their hands. As they scored five goals in a row between the 36th and 41st minutes, Aalborg moved three goals ahead. Paris fought back, with Solé in impressive form, scoring 10 times in total. It was Solé who, on the buzzer, converted a penalty to secure a 33:33 draw and help Paris take their first point of the season.

HC Zagreb (CRO) vs RK Celje Pivovarna Laško (SLO) 31:29 (14:12)

H2H: 12-1-12
Top scorers: Ihar Bialauski (Zagreb) 9 goals; Aljuš Anžic (Celje) 11 goals

In one of the oldest derbies in European handball, the hosts made the better start, opening up a three-goal lead after just five minutes. But despite Lucian Korošec receiving a direct red card in the 18th minute, the Slovenian side stayed in the game. Aljuš Anžic was instrumental in that regard, scoring five times in the first half alone to keep Celje within two goals at the break. In a game that remained tight for a long time, the difference came between Zagreb’s posts. Urh Kastelic, making three consecutive saves, helped the Croatian side open up a four-goal lead, the biggest of the evening, with 14 minutes left to play. Despite Gleb Kalarash being shown a red card in the final 10 minutes, Zagreb had built a big enough cushion to prevent their opponents from completing a comeback and secured two important points.

GROUP E

Barça (ESP) vs SAH - Aarhus (DEN) 39:28 (18:13)

H2H: 1-0-0
Top scorers: Dika Mem (Barça) 7 goals; Emil Lærke (Aarhus) 8 goals

For their maiden game in the EHF Champions League, Aarhus gave Barça a good run for their money. Despite Sergey Hernández making five saves in the first 10 minutes, the Danish side stayed in touch on the scoreboard. But then it all unravelled for the visitors, who failed to score between the 16th and 24th minutes while conceding five unanswered goals in the meantime. Once in front, the Blaugrana played at their own pace and let their experience do the talking. Despite Emil Lærke scoring eight goals, the Danish side were never able to mount a comeback, as Dika Mem and Ludovic Fabregas powered Barça to victory.

GROUP F

Industria Kielce (POL) vs HC Kriens-Luzern (SUI) 30:29 (19:15)

H2H: 1-0-0
Top scorers: Aleks Vlah (Kielce) 8 goals; Marin Šipić (Kriens-Luzern) 8 goals

Making your EHF Champions League debut is no easy thing, and Kriens-Luzern certainly learnt that on Wednesday. Trailing from the opening minutes, the Swiss side were never quite able to get back on level terms. Kielce’s Dejan Milosavljev was in the spotlight in the first half, making seven saves and helping the hosts steadily increase their advantage to four goals at the break. But Kriens-Luzern never let their heads drop. As Marin Šipić answered almost every goal scored by Artsem Karalek, the visitors remained within touching distance throughout the second half. But staying close was not enough, and as Aleks Vlah scored three goals in a row in the final five minutes, Kielce secured their first victory of the season.

SC Magdeburg (GER) vs Kolstad Håndball (NOR) 37:22 (19:12)

H2H: 2-0-1
Top scorers: Ómar Ingi Magnússon (Magdeburg) 7 goals; Simon Jeppsson (Kolstad) 5 goals

The 2023 and 2025 EHF Champions League champions began their new campaign with a win, but it was one they had to work for early on. Despite taking an early three-goal lead, inspired by Daniel Pettersson, Magdeburg saw their opponents fight back shortly afterwards. It was not until the final 10 minutes of the first half that Magdeburg shook off Kolstad's resistance. By conceding only one goal while scoring six of their own, the German side opened up a comfortable seven-goal advantage by the break. The hosts did not stop there and steadily increased their lead throughout the second half. The advantage reached 10 goals in the 40th minute and 15 goals by the final whistle.
