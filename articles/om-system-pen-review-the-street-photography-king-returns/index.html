OM System Pen Review: The Street Photography King Returns

TheTools · 09.09.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

I don’t believe I’m writing this, but the OM System Pen is what I consider to be the single best camera for street photography that I’ve used in a very long time. And wow, did they not hold back. I’m writing this article on Labor Day here in America, and you should know that I’m doing it while holding back a tear in my eye. Maybe it’s the pain in my left wrist while typing, but that tear came when I typed the title of this review. We waited 10 years for this moment for a new serious Pen camera. The OM System Pen isn’t the direct successor to the Olympus Pen F according to what a handful of journalists and I were told in a meeting. But in so many ways, it’s better.

I really have to say this: the past few years in photography have felt amazingly reaffirming, because brands are starting to realize more and more that we need rangefinder style camera bodies. The OM System Pen is exactly that, with a list that I realize is insanely long.

  • A variant of the current 20MP sensor at the heart
  • Excellent image stabilization
  • Weather resistance to an IPX1 rating
  • A tilting/swivel LCD screen
  • The fastest autofocus I’ve seen in an OM System camera with 121 phase detection points
  • Human and Pet AI scene detection
  • A new battery
  • A great viewfinder even though it’s only 2340k
  • Touchscreen menus, finally
  • The color creator that embraces the reason why I fell in love with the Pen F in the first place
  • 8 frames a second in mechanical shutter
  • A plastic body that comes in black or silver

So, essentially, what hasn’t changed is pretty much the sensor resolution and the deep feelings in my heart.

For the first time in many, many years, I think I’m finally ready to buy a Micro Four Thirds camera again.

OM System Pen Review Conclusions: Is It Worth Buying?

Is the OM System Pen worth buying? Overwhelmingly so, yes. I’m so insanely happy that they didn’t hold back, especially now that they’re an independent brand. There’s so much that makes me want to slip the wrist strap of this camera onto my right hand and make me say, “I do.”

First, let me get my few complaints out of the way. I wish that the body had more metal, that the sensor had a shield for when you’re switching lenses, and that it had dual card slots for something like a Micro SD card in addition to the SDXC card slot. I also wish that this camera had a joystick, but in reality, it didn’t need one.

Otherwise, I don’t have anything to complain about. I love that there’s a new battery. Folks may complain about this, but those folks probably invested in OM System already, and I don’t think that they’d ever consider buying this camera.

I adore the color creator and only hope that OM System’s support from their fiefdom (because capitalism is not a community) gives us more color profile options.

Then there’s the IP durability rating, the insanely fast autofocus performance for street photography, the viewfinder, the ergonomics, etc. What’s most worth discussing here is the touchscreen menu. In fact, it’s so good that I now firmly believe that OM System has the single best menu system of all the brands out there.

There’s so much to love about this camera. The OM System Pen receives the Phoblographer’s Editor’s Choice award. And all this at a body only price of $999.

Experience: How is the OM System Pen For Photographers?

There are some things about the OM System Pen that are kind of odd ergonomically speaking. For example, the on/off switch is on the right side when I really think it should’ve been on the left in the same way that it was with the Pen F. Still, that’s not a complete dealbreaker for me.

During my test of the pre-production unit, the LCD screen stopped working for some odd reason. I hope that this won’t be an issue with the camera system in the production version.

Using the camera for stuff like street photography, on the other hand, is an absolutely beautiful experience where the design gets clearly out of the way of the shooting process. I often set the camera to scene detection with it selecting the focus point and the subject. It did so quickly, accurately, and exceptionally well. For street photography, it made the art form essentially a point-and-shoot affair, so all I needed to do was focus on ensuring that I could anticipate what was happening in the frame. Luckily, the autofocus works well for folks with deeper melanin in their skin when you’re in low light as well. These days, that’s one of my biggest and most important tests.

Because this camera is also so lightweight, it didn’t have much of an issue being in my hands all day either.

So here’s the really surprising thing. I took this camera with the 17mm f1.2 and the Voigtlander 17.5mm f0.95 and the autofocus was so good that I felt that I didn’t really need to do zone focusing at all. The slight difference in light absorbing also doesn’t matter as much too, because at ISO 6400, the images still look really solid. More importantly, I didn’t need to use the pre-set zone focusing feature that OM System cameras have either.

It’s so nice to feel like there’s a camera from the company with proper autofocus for humans again. Oh, and autofocus for pets is also pretty good too. The woman in the image above was really happy when I told her that her dog is adorable, and she kept talking about the little fluff too.

A reminder to folks who read Phoblographer often, I’m a high-functioning blind man with a condition called keratoconus. So often, I sometimes take images of a scene because I feel there’s something there but I can’t totally tell that there is until I look back at the image later. In this way, I embrace the philosophy of Diado Moriyama.

When I say that shooting with the OM System Pen gets out of the way of making images, I really do mean that. It lets me do street photography the way that it should be: where I’m focusing on what’s around me and trying to find fascinating little encounters that make you look closer at the scene later.

During my testing, it didn’t rain, but I’d love to see how this camera does when it’s pouring out.

OM System Pen Image Quality Findings

The Phoblographer has used Capture One to edit RAW files for a very long time now. The OM System Pen doesn’t have support in the software yet, and that’s honestly just fine. That’s because the OM System Pen is a great camera for just shooting JPEGs. In the past month, I’ve talked to more and more photographers who are all so sick of sitting in front of a computer all day editing their photos. Really, in 2026: the juice isn’t often worth the squeeze when social media platforms feed your images to AI training platforms, and you’re shooting for an algorithm or something. If I’m making images for my website, a client, or a print, then I’ll edit.

And just because I don’t want to edit, that doesn’t make me not a serious photographer. If anything, it makes me more serious about shooting and much more of a photographer than a photo editor.

Images made with the Olympus 17mm f1.2 and the Voigtlander 17.5mm f0.95 all look really stunning with the built-in Color Creator profiles. My favorite is rich film in the vivid setting.

Tech Specs

The following specs are taken from the OM System tech sheet provided to the Phoblographer.

  • 20.4 MP 4/3 Live Mos sensor
  • TruePic IX Image Processor
  • Supersonic Wave Filter (image sensor dust reduction system)
  • 3.0-inch vari-angle monitor (electrostatic capacitance touch panel), Approx.1.04M dots (3:2)
  • Eye-level OLED electronic viewfinder, approx. 2.36M dots
  • IPX1 certified weather sealing (applies when the body is used with an OM SYSTEM splash proof lens)
  • Cross-type phase detection AF (121 points), Contrast AF (121 points)
  • Body only: 344 g (12.1 oz)
  • Approx. 125.8mm(W) x 74.5mm(H) x 43.3mm(D)
