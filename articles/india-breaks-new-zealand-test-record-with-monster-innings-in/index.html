A destructive century from Rishabh Pant helped India tally the highest team total in a Test match played in New Zealand. (Getty Images: Hagen Hopkins)

In short: 

India has registered the highest team total in a Test played in New Zealand after declaring at 9-720 in Wellington.

Centuries from KL Rahul (136) and Rishabh Pant (128) allowed India to attain a 352-run first-innings lead over New Zealand.

The innings fell 39 runs short of India's all-time highest Test score of 7-759, scored against England in 2016.

India has broken the record for the highest Test score in a match played in New Zealand as it put itself in a commanding position to win the first Test in Wellington.

New Zealand reached 2-119 at the close of play on day four as it fought to save the first Test after India declared on a mammoth 9-720 with an imposing first-innings lead of 352. 

India's assault was led by Rishabh Pant (128), whose six sixes took his career tally to 108 in just 53 Tests, now second behind all-time best Ben Stokes (138). Pant surpassed former NZ captain Brendon McCullum (107) during the innings.

New Zealand's hopes of a result rest on batting for much of the remainder of the match and forcing a draw, despite losing Tom Latham for a duck and first-innings centurion Henry Nicholls for 31.

Opener Devon Conway, who scored his 22nd Test half-century on his way to an unbeaten 59, and Rachin Ravindra (27 not out) will resume on day five with the Black Caps still 233 runs from forcing India to bat again.

India's pace spearhead Jasprit Bumrah (1-33) again made an early breakthrough, sending Latham back on the second ball of the innings, the New Zealand skipper edging behind to take a pair.

Nicholls, whose 175 was the backbone of New Zealand's first-innings 368, got an edge and a bit of thigh pad on a Prasidh Krishna delivery, and Dhruv Jurel showed stunning reflexes to take the catch at short leg.

Jurel had earlier become the sixth batter to score a half-century in India's innings before being caught in the deep for 78, triggering the declaration an hour before tea.

India had resumed on 4-486 on another sunny morning at the Basin Reserve with the clear intent of pushing their 118-run lead out to the sort of tally that would take the possibility of a New Zealand victory out of the equation.

There are few better batters for such a task than Pant, and the swashbuckling wicketkeeper showed the full range of his shots as he approached his ninth Test hundred.

Pant was finally caught on the boundary for 128 off the bowling of Ravindra to bring up lunch, but the punishment for New Zealand continued into the second session.

Jacob Duffy finished with 1-201 off 33 overs. (Getty Images: Hagen Hopkins)

India's final score was well clear of its previous best innings tally in New Zealand of 520 all out in Hamilton in 2009 — a match they won by 10 wickets.

All-rounder Glenn Phillips (3-154) picked a couple of wickets as the Indian batters went for their shots ahead of the declaration to make his figures a bit more respectable.

Seamer Jacob Duffy, though, finished with unenviable figures of 1-201 from his 33 overs, only 28 runs shy of New Zealand's most expensive ever bowling display, which spinner Stephen Boock tallied against Pakistan in 1989.
