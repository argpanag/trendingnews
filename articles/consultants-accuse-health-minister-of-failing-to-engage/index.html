Consultants accuse health minister of failing to engage

TheTools · 16.10.2026 23:01 · source · permalink

The Irish Hospital Consultants Association (IHCA) has criticised the Minister for Health, for what it claims is a failure to engage on issues for the past 20 months.

It said it has only met with Minister Jennifer Carroll MacNeill once during her time at the Department of Health, in March 2025, despite repeated invitations to attend conferences, open headquarters and discuss urgent patient-focused reforms.

The IHCA is holding its annual conference in Kilkenny today, and relations between the sides appear to have strained, in particular over the minister's approach to the Public Only Consultants Contract (POCC), and her seeking a uniform rostering of consultants in hospitals on Saturdays, to help ease overcrowding and other improvements.

The association has accused the minister and the Government of a "closed door" approach, in what it says is in sharp contrast to the minister's policy towards other health unions and representative associations.

IHCA President Professor Gabrielle Colleran said that if engagement is being systematically discouraged, or prevented, it would be a matter of profound concern.

The organisation also said that HSE CEO Anne O’Connor, who was due to speak at today's conference, had to cancel at short notice.

The IHCA represents around 5,000 consultants.

Its conference will discuss financial constraints, workforce pressures, medical negligence and how clinician-led innovation can transform service delivery and improve patient outcomes.

A spokesperson for the Minister for Health said she has regular engagements with the trade union that represents doctors, the Irish Medical Organisation (IMO), as well as with all other trade unions representing healthcare workers.

She said she engages with consultants on a regular basis at conferences, meetings and when she visits health services around the country.

Minister MacNeill said she is unable to attend the IHCA conference on this occasion, owing to existing diary commitments.

She added that the Secretary General of the Department of Health met the IHCA on 17 September to discuss the implementation of the Public Only Consultant Contract, extended day and Saturday services, and the shared challenges the increased demand and demographic pressures present for the health services and the measures being taken to respond to them.

Further engagements are planned in the coming weeks, the Minister's spokesperson added.

The IHCA is not a registered trade union and is not affiliated to the Irish Congress of Trade Unions, but it operates as a representative body for consultants. It was established in 1988.
