Andre Agassi Shares Rare Family Photos With Steffi Graf and Jaz

TheTools · 09.10.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

Former tennis champions Andre Agassi and Steffi Graf have offered fans a glimpse into their family life through a series of photographs Agassi shared on Instagram in October 2026. The images featured Graf and their daughter, Jaz Elle, alongside moments from Agassi's appearances at the US Open and Laver Cup, drawing attention to the family's life away from the spotlight.

According to reports published by Yahoo and AOL on October 8, one photograph showed Agassi, Graf and Jaz talking with former French tennis professional Yannick Noah. The candid image attracted particular attention because Graf rarely appears publicly on her husband's social media accounts.

The photographs also documented Agassi's activities at the 2026 US Open, including a star-studded exhibition in which he teamed with Andy Roddick against Roger Federer and John McEnroe. Other images showed him signing US Open posters, talking with Carlos Alcaraz and Billie Jean King, spending time with ball kids, and appearing alongside New York Knicks forward Karl-Anthony Towns.

Agassi also included a photograph of several books, among them his own work, Billie Jean King's autobiography and Roger Federer's biography. His daughter appeared in another image wearing an orange shirt bearing the family name in bold black lettering.

Introducing the collection with a humorous caption, Agassi wrote:

"Guess the kids call this a photo dump?" Andre Agassi wrote.

The 2026 Laver Cup took place at The O2 Arena in London from September 25 to September 27. Agassi captained Team World, which lost 13-5 to Team Europe. His wife and daughter accompanied him during the event, according to the AOL report.

Agassi also discussed his relationship with Graf in an interview with HELLO! ahead of their 25th wedding anniversary in October 2026. He described mutual understanding and support as important elements of their marriage, particularly as he returned to the demands of professional tennis through his Laver Cup captaincy.

Asked how Graf viewed his involvement in the competition, the 56-year-old said:

"She likes seeing me happy, just like I like seeing her happy," Agassi told HELLO! "She knows this is a week in Laver Cup that I value, that I bond with, that I connect to. It's a lot of work, but she's never been scared of me in work [mode]."

Beyond their public appearances, Agassi and Graf have emphasized raising their children to develop independent identities despite their parents' international sporting success. Their son, Jaden Gil, has pursued baseball as a pitcher, while Jaz has developed interests in dance and equestrian activities.

Agassi explained that growing up around famous parents had given the children a chance to distinguish public recognition from everyday life. He credited the family's approach to living modestly and allowing their children to observe how fame affects people.

Reflecting on their upbringing, Agassi said:

"They got the best seat in the house to realise what life is and what life isn't," Agassi said. "They see us, they learn from what we do, not from what we say. And then they see how other people sometimes treat us. So it's very easy for them to understand ideas of grandeur versus the reality of life."

He further explained how the couple's behavior shaped their children's perspective on celebrity and personal choices.

"I attribute that to a tool of being a more accomplished, recognisable parent. But again, it's only a tool if you live a certain way. We live pretty grounded. They see how you're treated, and it makes a lot of sense to them on what they choose for themselves."

Agassi has previously discussed how his own childhood influenced his approach to fatherhood. In a 2019 interview with Virgin Media Television, he described the pressure his father placed on him to pursue tennis from an early age and explained that he and Graf wanted their children to choose their own paths.

Discussing the importance of raising his children, Agassi said:

"Fatherhood is everything," Agassi said. "My greatest responsibility is raising them. As they get older, the decisions get a little bit more precarious and the worries get less but more intense.

I have parented differently from my father — I wouldn't wish that parenting experience on anyone I cared about, because it came with its pains, despite its intentions. I've tried to take the best from it, and teach them the things I held on to that were good."

He also explained that the couple deliberately avoided directing their children toward the sport that defined both parents' careers.

"We wanted the kids to make their own choices and would never have said ‘no’ to what they were, but we didn't go out of our way to expose them to tennis," Agassi said. "Our prayer was not tennis."

Agassi has also kept a personal reminder of his relationship with his son: a handmade necklace Jaden created when he was five years old. The necklace carries the message "Daddy Rocks," according to AOL.
