Porsche Mission S previews 911-topping supercar - carsales.com.au

TheTools · 15.10.2026 22:08 · source · permalink

Porsche has pulled the covers off the Mission S, a mid-engined ‘vision’ model that previews the brand’s evolving design language and confirms it’s developing a new super sports car platform to sit above the 911.

Indeed, this is not a limited-run hypercar like its last magnum opus, the Porsche 918 Spyder, but a permanent part of the range.

Porsche hasn’t had a car sitting above the 911 since the 918 Spyder bowed out a decade ago. The Mission S is the clearest sign yet that gap is about to be filled – and this time it’s sticking around.

“The Mission S represents a mid-engined super sports car platform positioned above the 911 – not as a one-off hypercar, but as a permanent and versatile addition to our customer offering,” said Porsche chairman Dr Michael Leiters.

That’s a big shift from the 918 and Carrera GT, which were strictly limited runs. Think less unicorn, more ongoing model line.

The car was unveiled on a pier in Queens, New York, with Manhattan as the backdrop – the same spot where the 550 Spyder, the brand’s first super sports car, was famously photographed in 1953. Nice touch.

Those historical nods run right through the design. The round headlights and ‘torpedo’ guards are classic 911, while the shut lines merging into the light graphics tip the hat to the G-model. The flat nose comes from the 911 GT1, the roofline under the rear wing recalls the 959, and the ‘car-in-car’ rear treatment is also GT1-derived.

Add a cooling set-up inspired by the 917’s radiator fan, a fully integrated active rear wing, and wheels that reinterpret the 928’s famous ‘dial’ design with a lightweight focus. It’s a greatest-hits album, basically, remastered for the 2030s.

“This vision model illustrates a fundamental strategic direction that we will soon be translating into our future product portfolio,” said Porsche head of style Tobias Sühlmann.

The concept is the first product to emerge from Porsche’s ‘Sportwagenschmiede 35’ (roughly, “sports car forge”) strategy, outlined at a capital markets day in Weissach last week.

That plan leans on ‘Value over Volume’. In other words, fewer variants, a push into higher-margin segments and a commitment to keep investing in petrol, plug-in hybrid and electric powertrains.

Inside is where it gets interesting.

Porsche has deliberately ditched a fixed central touchscreen and passenger display. Instead, a retractable MagSafe-compatible holder lets your own smartphone handle navigation and info duties, backed by a streamlined My Porsche app.

The driver gets five round dials, with an analogue-needle tacho front and centre. But it gets better… much better.

Depending on drive mode, the tacho physically moves closer to the driver, and the dial tubes rearrange themselves side-by-side or one behind the other.

There’s also a key holder to the left of the steering wheel (as per tradition), push-pull dash controls, a reimagined Sport Chrono clock and a seatbelt buckle built into the centre panel.

The colours lean on Porsche’s racing heritage: Firn White Metallic, Light Red Metallic and Black Chrome outside, Special Black inside.

What Porsche hasn’t said is anything about what’s behind the seats.

The press material is silent on engine, power, weight and performance. Although tell tale signs suggest the company is leaning towards a combustion engine: stacked quad exhaust pipes poking out of its tail end suggest engine acoustics will be real, not synthesised.

Earlier reports suggested Porsche was weighing up Volkswagen Group hardware shared with the Audi Nuvolari and Lamborghini Temerario, but none of that has been confirmed.

The Mission S is a design study, so the car you see won’t be the car you buy.

But Porsche is clear the platform underneath is real, and the show car’s design cues will filter into future models.

“A Porsche must be recognisable as a Porsche at first glance,” concluded Leiters, adding that the new concept car is a “glimpse” of the design features and ideas that will be seen in the near future.

There’s no word on timing, pricing or Australian availability.

The last halo concept, the all-electric Mission X, was unlikely to be built in right-hand drive, so local fans will be hoping a permanent model line means a better shot at RHD this time.

One thing is certain though – whatever this concept evolves into will be exceedingly expensive.
