Gov. Braun accepts recommendation to put Evansville man to death

TheTools · 02.11.2026 17:15 · source · permalink

INDIANA (WFIE) - Indiana Governor Mike Braun has accepted the parole board’s recommendation to execute Jeffery Weisheit.

“I am accepting the Parole Board’s unanimous recommendation to deny clemency for Jeffrey Weisheit. The Indiana Department of Correction will continue preparations to carry out the court-ordered sentence of execution on November 5, 2026,” said Gov. Braun.

Earlier Monday, we shared that efforts to delay Jeffrey Weisheit’s execution were ongoing, despite the parole board’s recommendation to the governor that it happen as scheduled Thursday.

Attorneys were asking the governor to delay the execution for an investigation into the lethal drugs purchased by the state.

Those attorneys say the state bought the drugs, then set a hearing seeking to shut the pharmacy that made them down.

That hearing is just four days after Weisheit’s execution.

“What we have submitted in our application underscores that this is a protocol that is completely outmoded,” says Joe Perkovich, Weisheit’s attorney. “It’s something that effectively was dusted off from 1/4 century ago and is completely unfit for purpose because Indiana relies on prison guards to install IV lines for the delivery of a massive overdose That is something that is only capably in the hands of well-trained, experienced medical professionals.”

The execution is scheduled for November 5th, while the Indiana Board of Pharmacy’s emergency show-cause hearing to suspend Pharmaneek’s license is set for Monday, November 9, just four days later.

“So as far as we know, the Department of Corrections does not possess the drugs. What we do know is that this was purchased from an entity, a pharmacy that the attorney general’s office is actively shutting down and has a hearing to do just that four days later from the execution date,” Perkovich said.

Weisheit’s attorneys say they are concerned about the possibility of a botched execution.

“What we’ve seen in recent debacles, botched executions, failed executions, such as of Miss Christa Pike in Tennessee in September 30th. There you’ve got rampant indications of failed IV lines as well as indications of adulterated drugs. And the combination of the two put her in a coma for a week but failed to kill her. And Indiana is running the risk of doing the same sort of thing here,” Perkovich said.

Our reply from the governor’s office about the request for an investigation was answered with the statement about accepting the parole board’s recommendation.

Attorneys for Weisheit also shared a statement after the governor’s decision:

“The governor has yet to address the central questions emerging from the State’s records. Why has the state chosen to pay staggering sums to a drugs suppliers that the Attorney General’s Office is simultaneously seeking to shut down and sanction? Why should the public have any belief that the drugs are what they are said to be and not compromised, like the range of medicines the Attorney General’s Office has found to be in that condition in their efforts to shutdown this pharmacy? We urge the governor to follow the path taken by Gov. Lee in Tennessee and call for a thorough review of Indiana’s lethal injection protocol.”
