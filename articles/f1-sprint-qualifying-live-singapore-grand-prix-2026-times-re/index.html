  1. Report: Verstappen takes pole after another settings issuepublished at 15:16 BST
    15:16 BST

    Andrew Benson
    BBC F1 correspondent in Singapore

    Red Bull's Max Verstappen beat Mercedes driver George Russell to sprint pole at the Singapore Grand Prix.

    Verstappen, fresh from his and Red Bull's first victory of the season in Malaysia last weekend, was 0.12 seconds quicker than the Briton, with Ferrari's Charles Leclerc third.

    McLaren's Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris were fourth and fifth ahead of Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, who went early in final qualifying and fell down the order from initially being second behind Leclerc.

    Championship leader Kimi Antonelli, using the Mercedes upgrade that underperformed in Malaysia while Russell is on the previous specification, could manage only seventh.

    And there were further glitches with the engine settings provided by governing body the FIA five days after the formation laps in the wet in Malaysia saw cars reduced to crawling speed as a result of a software bug.

    The settings issue, which was combined with a glitch in the F1 timing system that delayed the start of second qualifying, did not affect the order.

  6. Singapore cockpit 'like a sauna'published at 14:57 BST
    14:57 BST

    Drivers still have the full 62-lap grand prix to come this weekend, and we know Singapore is the most physically demanding circuit on the calendar, so Max Verstappen was asked to gives us a bit more insight into the conditions inside the cockpit at Marina Bay.

    "It's just like a sauna and trying to do some exercise in it. It's always very hot," he said.

    "You just try to cool yourself as well as you can between the runs, because the whole cockpit is hot - so your feet, your upper body, your head.

    "It's never comfotable, but that's also what's so unique about this track - and we love it as well."

  7. Verstappen 'did not expect' sprint polepublished at 14:53 BST
    14:53 BST

    Polesitter Max Verstappen is being interviewed by former driver David Coulthard after securing the 11th sprint pole position of his career. "So, it is always close in Singapore, and also, your lap itself, you're pushing as hard as you think is possible to the walls and places," says the Red Bull driver.

    "But once we bolted on the soft tyre, it felt like the car was coming alive a little bit more - but I did not expect this. Very happy to be first."

    He added: "I think the last few races we have been stronger, but this track still highlights a few little weaknesses in the car, but we managed to drive with them as well as possible."
