Live updates: Duffy blames prior administrations and dated systems for airspace meltdown | CNN Politics

TheTools · 15.11.2026 13:30 · source · permalink

Here's the latest

• Airspace meltdown: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy deflected when asked by CNN whether he is accountable for Friday’s nationwide ground stop that massively disrupted weekend air travel, saying, “I take responsibility for what was given to me by prior administrations,” and repeating his call for more funding to modernize dated aviation systems.

• On Capitol Hill: Top Democrats on two House panels overseeing the aviation industry say federal officials will brief them tomorrow on Duffy’s request for tens of billions more dollars in funding. President Donald Trump backed Duffy’s call, saying that more money is needed “FAST!” Democrats have signaled willingness to fund the department but skepticism about how existing allocations have been spent.

• Signs of improvement: Cancellations and delays have lingered but fewer flights have been disrupted today as airlines begin to resume their normal schedules.

Updates

NFL referee will be pulling double duty today

One NFL official — backup referee Don Willard — will call a pair of football games Sunday as last week’s airspace meltdown has lingering effects on the sports calendar.

Willard will work as a side judge at the Miami Dolphins’ game versus the Colts in Indianapolis at 1 p.m. ET, then travel to Cincinnati to work the Bengals’ game against the Pittsburgh Steelers tonight at 8:20 p.m.

Indianapolis’ Lucas Oil Stadium is roughly 100 miles away from Cincinnati’s Paycor Stadium.

This highly unusual move by the NFL is due to the ongoing nationwide impact to air travel this weekend, which forced the league to adjust its officiating assignments for Week 10 games by reassigning officials to games within driving distance of their hometowns.

College football has also been impacted by the air travel woes, with an SEC clash between Ole Miss and Oklahoma pushed to Sunday after Ole Miss said it was unable to depart Friday night.

Duffy says he wants to "get away" from company that runs critical airspace network

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said he wants to “get away” from the contractor involved in upgrade work that likely triggered Friday’s nationwide ground stop, but that he can’t because the company owns the entire network.

Asked today if the Department of Transportation plans to cut ties with L3Harris, Duffy told NBC News, “I can’t. And the reason is that L3Harris, decades ago, got the contract to run and own the network that operates communication for the FAA.”

The transportation secretary said that’s why he’s asking for tens of billions of dollars from Congress to build a “brand new architecture of a network.”

Duffy said telecommunications companies like AT&T, Verizon and Lumen have the architecture ready for such a system.

Some background: The transportation secretary has publicly criticized the aerospace and defense company as the Trump administration faces scrutiny over last week’s airspace meltdown.

At a news conference yesterday, Duffy said he’s “stuck” with L3Harris and that the contractor may have made “mistakes” during upgrade work at an Arkansas facility Friday, though he’s yet to pinpoint if a faulty router or contractor error was at the root of the sweeping telecommunication outages.

L3Harris has said it is supporting the Federal Aviation Administration in its investigation.

Sunday flight cancellations top 1,500 as air travel recovers from nationwide ground stop

In the wake of Friday’s nationwide ground stop, airlines and airports across the country Sunday have cancelled over 1,500 flights within, into, or out of the United States as of 10 a.m. EST.

Continuing a chaotic weekend of air travel, at least 1,572 flights have been cancelled, according to FlightAware.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has primarily blamed the ground stop on upgrade work to replace old copper wiring at an air traffic location in El Dorado, Arkansas, and asked Congress for tens of billions more dollars to update the nation’s airspace systems.

Van Hollen criticizes Duffy for "pointing fingers" at Congress after ground stop

Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland criticized Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy for blaming Congress after Friday’s nationwide ground stop for not providing additional funding.

“Unfortunately Secretary Duffy’s first response just the other day was to point fingers and blame Congress,” Van Hollen told CNN’s Dana Bash on “State of the Union,” adding that “Congress has met the Trump administration’s request and it was Republicans who in their so-called Big Beautiful Bill that put in the $12.5 billion of which they’ve only spent one-third.”

Van Hollen told Bash that he would support additional, “necessary” funding but called on administration officials to come to Congress and “justify” the ask.

Duffy says he welcomes congressional oversight as he pushes for more funding

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the Trump administration should not be blamed for failing to secure additional money to improve the nation’s airspace systems, saying he and President Donald Trump have been pushing for upgrades and that previously allocated funding has only been for certain improvements.

“This is the first president we’ve ever had who cares this much about modernizing the airspace,” Duffy said of Trump today on CNN’s “State of the Union.” “He talks to me about it all the time. We have continual conversations.”

Following Friday’s nationwide ground stop, the transportation secretary has urged Congress to give his department an additional $50 billion.

Duffy says Trump was "fully engaged" with airspace disruption despite online silence

President Donald Trump was “fully engaged” with the nationwide ground stop on Friday and the subsequent travel disruptions, despite his online silence on the topic until this morning, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has said.

Speaking on CNN’s “State of the Union,” Duffy told Dana Bash Trump’s “engagement has been remarkable,” when asked about the president staying quiet on the topic on social media.

“The president is fully supportive, fully engaged, and was fully tracking what’s happening,” he added.

Trump has told him resources from other departments can be pulled into resolving the current aviation issues, Duffy said, “because the safety of the traveling public is number one for him. Like it is for me.”

"Obsolete" copper wire was being replaced in Arkansas, Duffy says

An old copper wire at an air traffic location in El Dorado, Arkansas was being switched to a more modern fiber optic system when an issue occurred Friday, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on CNN’s “State of the Union”.

Duffy said the copper wire was “obsolete” and he needed to have it replaced with fiber.

Some context: Defense contractor L3Harris has said it is supporting the Federal Aviation Administration in its investigation.

Duffy says router that may have caused airspace meltdown is being analyzed by FAA, FBI

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Sunday that the Federal Aviation Administration and the FBI are on site at one of contractor L3Harris’ labs analyzing the router that may have led to Friday’s airspace meltdown.

“What we’ve done is we pulled the router that was a problem, that was broadcasting all of this data into our network,” Duffy said on CNN’s “State of the Union.”

“We have to unwind. Was this a technical install error or was this an issue with the router itself? When I have that information, I’ll pass it along. But that investigation is just beginning right now,” Duffy said.

Duffy told ABC News that he does not believe anyone intentionally tampered with the router, saying that L3Harris has done similar work in the past.

“We know who did the install — a good American patriot, we believe, a veteran. So, we don’t think that someone tried to sabotage the network on the install at all. But we don’t we don’t actually have the root cause,” Duffy said on ABC’s “This Week.”

L3Harris said Saturday that it was cooperating with the FAA’s investigation and that it “takes the nationwide ground stop and its impact on travelers, airlines and air traffic operations seriously.”

Duffy said in a social media post that routers from facilities in Texas and Arkansas were sent to L3Harris headquarters in Florida, for examination.

“We have the knowledge and plan for the Flight Control Systems, we need Congress to give the money, FAST!” Trump said today in a social media post.

The president over the weekend has taken to social media to discuss several unrelated matters, including his efforts to renovate a Washington, DC golf course.

Duffy wants billions of dollars to upgrade the air traffic control system

The cause of Friday’s nationwide ground stop could have been routine maintenance on telecommunication lines, according to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, who is calling for increased funding to improve the country’s air traffic control system.

At a news conference on Friday, Duffy urged Congress to give “emergency funding” and approve $50 billion dollars to revamp the air traffic control system, increasing a previous ask for funding of more than $30 billion.

“What’s more important than having an air traffic control system that actually works?” Duffy asked, warning that more disruptions to air travel would happen unless the system is updated.

The current air traffic control system uses strips of paper with the names of flights on them to be handed off from one controller to another, and floppy computer discs and copper wire to handle communications for nearly a third of the system.

On Friday night, President Donald Trump held a phone call with GOP Sen. Eric Schmitt, where he pushed for Congress to pass legislation modernizing air traffic control, airport towers, terminals and other critical infrastructure as soon as possible, according to a source familiar with the conversation.

In 2025, Duffy and the Department of Transportation announced a plan to build a new air traffic control system by 2028 that would cost roughly $31.5 billion.

Since then, 69% of copper communications have been replaced by high-speed fiber, and control towers and radio sites have been modernized, Duffy outlined yesterday.

With new architecture, and with more funding, the Federal Aviation Administration could work with “the greatest telecom companies in the world,” rather than current contractor L3Harris, which Duffy said yesterday he was “stuck with.”

The ground stop is a “sign” for Congress to “lean in,” Duffy said. “Let’s make the investments and look forward to a brighter future in aviation, a safer future, a more efficient future in aviation.”

FAA and DOT will brief Congress after Duffy's calls for more agency funding

The top Democrats on two House panels overseeing the aviation industry announced today that employees from the Federal Aviation Administration and Transportation Department will brief their staff on Monday, as questions swirl around the failures that led to Friday’s nationwide ground stop.

Monday’s briefing, the lawmakers said in a news release, will revolve around the Trump administration’s request for an additional $30 billion in funding to upgrade the US air traffic control system.

The representatives — Rick Larsen, ranking member of the House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure, and André Carson, ranking member of the Subcommittee on Aviation — expressed skepticism about the ask, adding that they expect to hear from Federal Aviation Administrator Bryan Bedford by an early December hearing.

Larson and Carson pointed to the $12.5 billion passed by Congress in June 2025 to update air traffic control systems.

“Explain to Congress, again, why they need more money?” Larsen and Carson wrote.

That echoed calls for a clearer accounting from other Democratic lawmakers, including Rep. Greg Stanton, who also sits on the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee.

The department is behind in its modernization effort, Stanton told CNN last night. “That is not the fault of Congress. They probably chose the wrong contractor,” he said, adding, “So, we have a lot of questions to ask about why they are so behind and why they didn’t test it enough.”

What we know about the cause of Friday's nationwide ground stop

Welcome back to our coverage.

After flights nationwide were grounded for hours on Friday after a systems failure, thousands of flights were delayed or canceled, disrupting the travel plans of passengers heading into the weekend.

Here’s what we know about the cause of the ground stop:

Outages likely stemmed from an archaic copper wire switch

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told reporters yesterday that upgrade work on an old telecommunication system could be a cause for the ground stop.

In at least one remote Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) location, Duffy said contractor L3Harris was doing routine maintenance to switch telecommunication lines from an old copper wire to fiber, which he described as “straightforward” work that has been happening across the country for decades.

Duffy also said it’s unclear whether there was a contractor error or faulty equipment during the switch. L3Harris said in a statement to CNN that it is working with the FAA to investigate the incident.

FAA issued ground stop because majority of air traffic control centers were affected

FAA chief Bryan Bedford said yesterday that while as many as 17 of 22 air traffic control centers were affected, none of the facilities “actually went dark.”

He explained that the agency received reports from various facilities about frequencies “going in and out” that required “them to switch between primaries and backup.” And because the agency couldn’t immediately explain why so many facilities were impacted, it issued a nationwide ground stop.

‘Zero evidence’ of nefarious activity

The department does not believe the outages were caused by a cyber attack or any “nefarious” activity, Duffy and Bedford stressed at the news conference.

“A lot of questions came in yesterday. ‘Was this a cyber attack? Did some foreign nation attack this network?’ And the answer to that is we don’t believe so,” Duffy said.
