    FT: Newcastle 2-2 Arsenal

    Newcastle manager Matthias Jaissle, speaking to BBC Match of the Day: "Mixed feelings. Like the first home match here against Liverpool where a little bit more was possible.

  4. 'Something we can build on'published at 20:19 GMT 21 November
    20:19 GMT 21 November

    FT: Newcastle 2-2 Arsenal

    Newcastle United

    Newcastle manager Matthias Jaissle, speaking to Sky Sports: "After the first half, I would agree, a big, big point. When you feel the momentum in the second half, you almost feel it's two points too less.

    "But stay humble and see the big picture over 90 minutes, I think it's a fair result and something we can build on."

    On changes in second half: "We tried to encourage the players. Tactically, it wasn't too much different, honestly. It's just about believing in the way, being front-footed, being aggressive and believing in your team-mates.

    "After the high ball regains, we need to use the momentum and that's what the lads did second half. Really vertical and using the gaps and space, and done with success."

    On timing of Barnes goal: "That helped and then you see what's possible here with our fans. Unbelievable atmosphere, the energy - I'm getting goosebumps just talking about it - and that needs to be the standard.

    "This unity, players and the fans and then the playing style, that synergy. That should give us confidence that we can do much better in the future."

    On causing Arsenal problems: "When we play our style, believe in how we want to do it, then we can cause many oppositions problems. We need to do that consistently, match by match and then we can climb the table.

    "Today was already a difficult fixture and there are difficult ones ahead of us. The second half was something we can build on and we needed this."

    FT: Newcastle 2-2 Arsenal

    Arsenal

    Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta, speaking to BBC MOTD: "I have a very clear feeling that we lost two points today. We were exceptional in the first half - how efficient we were - but we needed to score three or four goals and we didn't.

    "We needed to raise the standard in the second half because we knew there would be a response. They raised and we were worse. We gave them the first goal in a way that is unacceptable. We didn't control it.

Arsenal go six clear after lively draw at Newcastle

Noni Madueke rescued a point for Premier League leaders Arsenal after they gave away the lead against Newcastle at St James' Park.

The Gunners deservedly went ahead after 33 minutes when Kai Havertz robbed the ball from left-back Lewis Hall and threaded it to Eberechi Eze, who finished high into the net.

But a rare error by Martin Odegaard allowed Newcastle to equalise four minutes after half-time through Harvey Barnes.

Malick Thiaw put Newcastle ahead from close range, before Arsenal keeper David Raya was forced off injured.

But the impressive Madueke dragged Arsenal level with 12 minutes to play, his low shot going through a crowd of players - although Newcastle keeper Lukas Hornicek got two hands to the ball and should have done better.

The draw ended a run of five consecutive Premier League victories for Arsenal as their title defence suffered a rare early bump.

Things started going wrong when, under pressure from Joe Willock following a Riccardo Calafiori throw-in, Odegaard miskicked the ball and it was picked up by Newcastle and played through for Barnes to finish low past Raya.

The Arsenal keeper had suffered a blow to the head diving at the feet of Willock shortly before, and his thinking could certainly be questioned when he kicked the ball behind for a corner under pressure on 62 minutes.

Big Dan Burn was left unmarked from the set-piece, and his downward header caused a goalmouth scramble before defensive partner Thiaw slammed home from a yard out.

Raya then suffered a shoulder injury and had to be replaced by Kepa Arrizabalaga in a very worrying development for Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta.

They failed to build on dominating in the first half and Newcastle, rejuvenated after the break, deserved at least a point.

Arsenal are six points clear of second placed Brighton - who play at Hull on Sunday - while Newcastle stay 12th with 13 points from 11 matches.

Arsenal analysis: Misfiring Gunners don't get it done

Mikel Arteta post-match interview - Newcastle 2-2 Arsenal

For only the second time in the past 23 Premier League games where they led at half-time, Arsenal failed to seal the win.

While Eze grabbing his first of the season is welcome, it remains a concern for manager Arteta that they do not score enough goals.

They dominated early doors, Eze striking the outside of the post after five minutes.

Madueke had five shots in the first half - the joint most of any player in the Premier League in 2026-27 - and constantly got the better of Hall down the right wing.

But no Arsenal player has more than three league goals so far this season and their blunt edge played a part in costing them three points here - although Manchester City's failure to beat Fulham in the early kick-off means this defeat is not as damaging as it could have been.

Newcastle analysis: Boos for ex-hero Bruno

Matthias Jaissle post-match interview - Newcastle 2-2 Arsenal

Newcastle fans gave Arsenal midfielder Bruno Guimaraes a hostile reception on his first return to St James' Park since leaving in a protracted summer transfer.

But for all the jeers, most fans would love to have the Brazilian's drive and creativity back in their midfield.

With Joelinton likely out for months with a thigh injury and Nico Gonzalez on the bench following illness, teenagers Sean Steur and Lewis Miley started in the middle and were outplayed.

Manager Matthias Jaissle made three half-time changes, including subbing off 18-year-old Steur for Jacob Ramsey.

Willock dropped deeper and his energy, while artless, was the spark Newcastle needed to turn the tide in the midfield battle.
