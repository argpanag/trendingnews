6, including repeat offenders, to be charged for participating in public assemblies without permit

TheTools · 27.09.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

A total of six persons, aged between 31 and 70, will be charged in court on Monday (Sept 28) for their involvement in various public assemblies and processions without a permit, including in a prohibited area.

Police said in a news release on Sunday that these incidents happened between November 2024 and February 2025.

They added that two of the six individuals had previously been investigated for the same offence and were subsequently issued with warnings for their involvement in public assemblies without a permit under the Public Order Act.

Under the Act, a police permit is required for organising or participating in a public assembly or procession in a public place, unless explicitly exempted from doing so.

Such activities at prohibited areas without a police permit will result in higher penalties.

According to the police, the two persons continued to be involved in multiple public assemblies without a permit and/or in a public area despite these past warnings.

Two other individuals had similarly been repeatedly involved in such cases despite being under investigation for offences under the Act, police said.

The six being charged are:

  • A 31-year-old, who faces three charges for participating in a public assembly without a permit. He had previously been issued with a stern warning for two prior offences under the Act, and for obstructing the course of justice in relation to his involvement.
  • A 56-year-old woman, who faces two charges for participating in a public assembly without a permit. She was twice issued with a stern warning for the same offence.
  • The four others, and the 31-year-old, will also each face one charge of participating in a public assembly in a prohibited area. They had done so outside the State Courts on February 3, 2025.

Of the five facing charges for public assembly without a permit in a prohibited area, two woman, aged 37 and 38, are said to have had numerous run-ins with the law. 

Received stern warnings

Between 2017 and 2024, the 37-year-old woman is said to have received stern warnings and a 12-month conditional warning over similar conduct.

Meanwhile, the 38-year-old woman was previously convicted for organising a public procession without a permit outside the Istana, and for various public assemblies between June 2017 and January 2021. 

She was fined $3,000 and issued with a 24-month conditional warning.

The police reminded members of the public that those found organising or participating in a public assembly without a police permit could face a fine of up to $10,000, six months' jail, or both.

"Members of the public should apply for a police permit prior to organising public assemblies," police stated, adding that those who wish to express their views but do not have a permit can consider the Speakers Corner, a long established space for Singaporeans to express their views.
