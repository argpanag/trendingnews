Daniel Kinahan to apply for bail in coming weeks

TheTools · 12.10.2026 10:58 · source · permalink

Daniel Kinahan, who is awaiting trial on an alleged organised crime offence, is to apply to the High Court to be released on bail in the coming weeks

The 49-year-old, who is currently detained at the maximum security Portlaoise prison, appeared at the Special Criminal Court charged with directing the activities of a criminal organisation between 18 October 2015 and 6 April 2017, both dates inclusive.

The alleged offence is contrary to section 71A of the Criminal Justice Act 2006, as amended by the Criminal Justice (Amendment) Act 2009, and as applied by section 74 of the Criminal Justice Act 2006.

His solicitor Darragh Mackin told the court this morning that they would be lodging the papers in the usual way.

Mr Mackin also said the timetable of the trial needs to factor in an expert examination of the digital evidence in the case, as the defence is seeking to have this evidence examined.

He is also seeking disclosure of all information relating to two other international investigations.

These relate to an investigation in Spain for an alleged organised crime offence and an investigation in Dubai in relation to money laundering.

Mr Mackin said that Mr Kinahan had been acquitted in both cases.

Prosecuting Counsel Dominic McGinn applied for an adjournment to allow the State prepare the book of evidence in the case. He told the three judges that this work is ongoing and that there was "a complex factual matrix" behind the charge that Mr Kinahan is facing.

He said Mr Kinahan was extradited from Dubai in August and that it would take a number of weeks to prepare the book of evidence outlining the case against him.

He also said that as an illustration of the complexity of the case, the statement of facts which formed the basis of the extradition order runs to 189 pages.

Mr Kinahan did not speak during the brief hearing and the presiding Judge Caroline Biggs remanded him in custody until 21 December.

Mr Kinahan will "vehemently defend" the case against him in the Special Criminal Court, Mr Mackin said.

He spoke outside the court after the brief hearing during which Mr Kinahan was remanded in continuing custody.

He said Mr Kinahan and his defence team were "anxious to make progress" and they were "comforted" that the book of evidence "will now be served before the end of the year".

Mr Mackin also said that they were looking forward to a trial in the early part of 2027.
