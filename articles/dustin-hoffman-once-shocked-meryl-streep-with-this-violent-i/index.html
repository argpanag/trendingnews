Dustin Hoffman once 'shocked' Meryl Streep with this violent improv

TheTools · 10.11.2026 16:55 · source · permalink

Dustin Hoffman dishes on everyone in Hollywood in new memoir "Look at Me," including Steve McQueen and his alleged drug habits.

In many ways, “Look at Me” is exactly the memoir you’d expect.

Throughout Dustin Hoffman’s roughly 300-page book (out now), the two-time Oscar winner dutifully recounts what it was like to make classic movies such as “Tootsie” and “All the President’s Men,” with a smattering of dishy anecdotes sprinkled throughout. (Yes, he got stoned with Paul McCartney, and yes, Maggie Smith was as intimidating as advertised.)

But the Hollywood legend gets immensely personal as well, as he reflects on aging and the near-death of his first son, Jake, as well as his complex relationship with “The Graduate” director Mike Nichols. Hoffman, 89, also opens up about the 2017 sexual misconduct allegations against him and the effect they took on this mental well-being.

Here is more of what we learned from reading Hoffman's “Look at Me," co-written with journalist Ariel Levy:

Robert Duvall once hopped in the shower with pal Dustin Hoffman

Hoffman recalls his younger days in New York in the 1950s and ‘60s, living with both Gene Hackman and Robert Duvall as struggling actors. Duvall was a particularly close friend. 

“I was with him the day JFK got shot,” Hoffman writes. “I remember walking around New York City in a trance, everyone just completely shook, really in an intense state of mourning. People were all talking to each other and it really felt like New York was a family. ‘This is a terrible thing to say,’ Duvall whispered, ‘but this is a perfect day to pick up girls.’” 

Hoffman continues by saying that Duvall “liked girls as much as I did.” 

On one occasion, “when I had a young lady from class over at the apartment and we happened to be taking a shower together in the morning, Duvall pulled back the shower curtain, naked, and got in with us,” Hoffman writes. “He extended his hand to my girlfriend and said, ‘Nice to meet you, I’m Bob Duvall.’”

Hoffman 'shocked' Meryl Streep during this 'Kramer vs. Kramer' scene

Hoffman calls Streep “one of the great actresses of her generation,” but writes that they occasionally clashed over their different styles of working. While Hoffman liked to be spontaneous and try things out on the fly, Streep came to the set of “Kramer vs. Kramer” fiercely prepared with deliberate choices in mind. 

In one scene of the Oscar-winning divorce drama, Joanna (Streep) and Ted (Hoffman) meet at a restaurant to discuss custody of their young son. 

“As we were shooting, I did something that felt completely natural: I got up to storm out and knocked a glass off the table without warning,” Hoffman writes. “It shattered against the wall. Meryl was shocked. The reaction that you see in the movie is genuine: she sort of jumps in her seat. It’s perfect. But she didn’t have an opportunity to think it through in advance, and I don’t think Meryl liked that. To be fair, I’m not sure I would have liked it myself.”

In another scene, Hoffman recalls interrupting Streep during a long breakup speech, to show how self-centered his character was. 

“I don’t think Meryl liked that either,” he says. “She probably thought I was trying to steal her scene, which was nonsense. … It’s self-defeating to worry about being upstaged.” 

Steven Spielberg wasn't 'enthusiastic' about gay take on 'Hook'

In a later chapter, Hoffman writes with fondness about his friendship with Robin Williams, his costar in Steven Spielberg’s grown-up Peter Pan adventure “Hook.” Hoffman played the eyelinered villain Captain Hook opposite Bob Hoskins’ devoted Mr. Smee. 

“My Hook was pompous and British,” Hoffman writes. “Bob Hoskins and I decided that he should also be gay. We thought Hoskins’s character, Mr. Smee, and Hook were so obviously in a long-term committed relationship; there was just no other way to play it. We told Spielberg our idea, and he wasn’t particularly enthusiastic about it, but we went with it anyway. I love our scenes together. They’re a very domestic couple, Hook and Smee, especially for pirates.” 

Why Hoffman nearly quit his Oscar-winning 'Rain Man'

After “Kramer vs. Kramer,” Hoffman won his second Oscar for best actor for playing the autistic Raymond Babbitt in 1988’s “Rain Man” opposite Tom Cruise. But during production, Hoffman often felt insecure about his performance, until he gradually started to gain confidence through improvising with Cruise.

“I almost quit ‘Rain Man’ because I couldn’t find the character,” Hoffman writes. “I felt ridiculous, totally fake. I remember watching the dailies with Barry Levinson, the director, and thinking, ‘This is awful, I’ve got to get out of this.’ Barry was very calm, very reassuring. ‘Give it a couple weeks,’ he told me. ‘If you still can’t get him, quit then. Just see if you can find one moment that feels authentic.’”  

Hoffman reveals where Steve McQueen allegedly stashed his cocaine

While filming 1973’s “Papillon,” Hoffman recalls that costar Steve McQueen was one of the highest paid actors at the time, and therefore, he made extravagant demands.

“In addition to the money, he got to choose a ‘perk,’” Hoffman writes. “He wanted his motorcycle and his pickup truck shipped to Jamaica, where we’d be filming most of the picture.” Despite pushback from the studio, “no other vehicles would suffice. This may have had something to do with the hubcaps of his pickup truck being filled to the brim with cocaine.”

While they were filming in Jamaica, McQueen “smoked pot all day long, from the minute he woke up, and he tempered that cloudy high with cocaine, for a little zip,” Hoffman adds. “I guess he was ‘self-medicating,’ as they say today. He never seemed stoned or wired, though. That was just the Steve McQueen experience.”
