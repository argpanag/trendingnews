High tides at the end of October could exceed alert level III by up to 30cm.

TheTools · 23.10.2026 09:21 · source · permalink

According to the Provincial Meteorological and Hydrological Station, the high tide during the full moon of the ninth lunar month is forecast to peak on October 27-28, with water levels at stations throughout the province exceeding alarm level III by 3-30cm. According to the Provincial Meteorological and Hydrological Station, the high tide during the full moon of the ninth lunar month is forecast to peak on October 27-28, with water levels at stations throughout the province exceeding alarm level III by 3-30cm. Low-lying areas and areas with poor drainage are at risk of flooding, affecting daily life and production.

As of 7:00 AM on October 23rd, the peak tide levels on rivers and canals in the province are gradually rising. At My Thuan, the peak tide reached 176cm, 4cm lower than alarm level III; Cho Lach reached 173cm, 3cm higher than alarm level I; and Tra Vinh reached 180cm, approximately at alarm level II.

Forecasts indicate that water levels will continue to rise from October 24-26, rising slowly on October 27, showing little change on October 28, and then gradually receding until November 2. The highest tide peak at My Thuan is likely to reach 210cm, exceeding alarm level III by 30cm; Cho Lach will reach 205cm, exceeding by 15cm; and Tra Vinh will reach 207cm, exceeding by 17cm. The remaining stations are forecast to exceed alarm level III by 3-7cm.

Flooding is likely to occur from October 23-30, with depths ranging from 5-50cm depending on location. The deepest flooding is predicted to occur between October 26-28, reaching 30-50cm. Particular attention should be paid to areas along the Tien, Ham Luong, Co Chien, Mang Thit, and Hau rivers; islets and islands; areas outside the dike system; and roads that are frequently flooded.

High tides can affect traffic, residential areas, orchards, livestock farms, and aquaculture farms. The risk of natural disaster is at level 2.

Local authorities need to inspect and reinforce dikes, and proactively prepare plans to prevent flooding and landslides when high tides combine with upstream water levels, northeasterly winds, and rain. People should elevate their belongings and monitor water levels to protect their property and production; note that peak tides usually occur in the early morning and late evening on October 27-28.
