Sea of 16,386 donated handmade poppies honour County Durham fallen soldiers at market

TheTools · 02.11.2026 07:33 · source · permalink

A sea of thousands of handmade poppies are honouring fallen soldiers in a moving County Durham tribute.

Knitters and crocheters from across the UK created the poppies in memory of Durham Light Infantry (DLI) soldiers who lost their lives in the First and Second World Wars.

Durham Market Poppy display. From left, Lee Edwards for Durham Market, organiser Louisa McLean, Caroline Kerr from Durham Market and Joan Hetherington who made some of the Poppies (Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)

Durham Markets launched the project in April, seeking 16,386 poppies - one for every DLI soldier killed in the two World Wars.

But support poured in from individuals and craft groups across the country, with the original target almost doubled to more than 30,000 poppies donated.

Creating Durham Market Hall poppy display (Image: Durham Markets)

From yesterday (Friday, October 30), exactly 16,386 poppies will be displayed at Durham Market Hall's entrances as a public tribute to those killed in the wars.

Considerable work has also gone into preparing the thousands of poppies for their new home.

Durham resident Joan Hetherington visited the Market Hall almost daily for eight weeks, tying poppies to netting for the display.

Durham Market Hall poppy display (Image: Durham Markets)

Members of 234 (City of Durham) Squadron ATC also lent a hand, attaching about 700 poppies during a two-hour session.

Arthur Lockyear MBE, Chair of the Durham City Remembrance Organising Group, said: "I would like to offer my heartfelt congratulations to our great friends at Durham Markets Company and the many wonderful volunteers who have worked so tirelessly to create this truly stunning poppy display.

"An incredible 16,386 poppies have been installed, each one representing a soldier from the Durham Light Infantry who lost their life during the First and Second World Wars. This is an incredibly moving tribute to the courage, sacrifice and service of those who gave so much for our freedom".

Chair of the Durham City Remembrance Organising Group, at Durham Market Poppy display (Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)

He added: "I would also like to extend a heartfelt invitation to residents of County Durham to come together on Sunday, November 8 for Remembrance Day in Durham City.

"Let us line the streets of our city in our thousands, standing shoulder to shoulder in a powerful expression of gratitude, respect and remembrance for His Majesty's Armed Forces – past, present and future.

"Remembrance is an opportunity to honour those who made the ultimate sacrifice, recognise those who continue to serve, and demonstrate that their courage and commitment will never be forgotten.

"I hope the people of Durham will turn out in tremendous numbers to ensure our city gives them the tribute they deserve".

Durham Market Poppy display pictured organiser Louisa McLean (Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)

Thousands more poppies will also be used in further tributes after the Market Hall display is complete.

Most will now be used by The Light and The Story in their own installations honouring fallen DLI soldiers, taking the tribute beyond the Market Hall and further across Durham.

The Market Hall tribute will be on view during normal opening hours, Monday to Saturday from 9am until 4.30pm.
