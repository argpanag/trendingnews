Andy Burnham's £290 charge for cars over 10 years old as vehicle tax rises

TheTools · 27.11.2026 08:57 · source · permalink

Owners of vehicles more than 10 years old could face significant tax increases next year. The majority of newer models are subject to a flat-rate £200 annual vehicle tax charge.

However, this changes considerably for cars manufactured before 2017, where tax bills can vary dramatically depending on emissions levels. This is something prospective buyers may fail to consider when looking at purchasing an older vehicle.

Some motorists could find themselves facing hefty tax charges running into hundreds of pounds, while others will pay considerably less, reports Birmingham Live.

It all hinges on emissions levels. Vehicle tax rates are set to rise once again from next spring in line with inflation under Andy Burnham's Government.

Bills will range from £20 up to a substantial £825 for cars produced between 2001 and 2017. Tax bands are graded from A to M on an ascending scale, with most vehicles typically falling between bands E and H.

The Band G charge will stand at £290 from April. Band E will be set at £210, while Band H will rise to £340.

Ultimately, the amount you pay will depend on your vehicle type and its pollution levels. Drivers have already been feeling the pinch in recent months due to soaring fuel prices as a consequence of the Iran war.

The RAC said: "The Government will increase VED rates for cars, vans and motorcycles in line with the Retail Prices Index (RPI) from April 2027.

"This will affect the majority of VED bands, while the standard rate for car drivers is expected to rise by £5 which means the tax rate for petrol, diesel and hybrid cars registered on or after April 1, 2017 will likely be £205."
