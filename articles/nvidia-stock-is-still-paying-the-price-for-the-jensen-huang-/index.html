Nvidia's (NVDA) stock remains damaged goods despite CEO Jensen Huang being back at headquarters in trademark loafers.

"It's been a traumatic situation for investors. They can't figure out which way is up. So we are going to see volatility," Yorkville Ives partner Dan Ives said on Sozzi Unleashed.

Shares of Nvidia rose more than 2% in premarket trading on Friday, following a 3.9% decline on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving and a 5.86% decline on Tuesday.

The stock popped more than 31% on Monday as investors digested the stunning news of Huang's return as CEO, just 12 days after he resigned amid a personal scandal and federal chip-export probes.

The dramatic reversal of fortunes for Huang followed intense lobbying from President Trump and Vice President JD Vance, who intervened directly with directors and said Huang had been cleared of national security concerns.

(NVDA )

Huang said at an all-hands meeting on Tuesday that he was in therapy, but struck a defiant tone on his management style and lack of succession planning.

Despite the initial rally upon his return, Nvidia still faces Justice Department and SEC investigations into alleged chip smuggling through blacklisted Singapore-based firm Megaspeed, which prompted the board to appoint a new chief compliance officer.

Huang is also scheduled to testify before Congress next week, in a closed-door House interview on Dec. 1 and a public Senate Banking Committee hearing on Dec. 3.

Volatility will likely be the name of the game in the near-term for Nvidia investors, Ives says.

"We'll see volatility into next week. Obviously the China hawks are going to be out [when Jensen appears]. The knives are going to be out because of the concerns about where this is all going," Ives added.
