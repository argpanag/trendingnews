Bobby Gould, Hard-Working Washington Capitals Winger Who Reached the Stanley Cup Final With Boston, Dies at 69 - That Eric Alper

TheTools · 11.10.2026 14:38 · source · permalink
  • Music

Persistence carried a seventh-round draft pick from small-town Petrolia, Ontario, through years of minor league bus rides and into a decade of steady, honest work in the National Hockey League. Bobby Gould, the gritty right winger who became a fixture with the Washington Capitals and helped the Boston Bruins reach the Stanley Cup Final, died on October 9, 2026, in Sarnia, Ontario. He was 69.

Robert Alexander Gould was born on September 2, 1957, in Petrolia, where he played for the hometown Petrolia Jets before heading south to the University of New Hampshire. Over four seasons with the Wildcats from 1975 to 1979, he grew into one of the top forwards in the ECAC, finishing his senior year with 31 goals and 59 points and earning second-team All-ECAC honours.

In 1977, the Atlanta Flames selected him in the seventh round of the NHL Amateur Draft, 118th overall, while the WHA’s Calgary Cowboys chose him in the eighth round, 70th overall. Gould never played in the WHA but eventually made it to Calgary all the same. After turning pro with the CHL’s Tulsa Oilers and posting 60 points with the Birmingham Bulls, he made his NHL debut with Atlanta during the 1979 to 1980 season and moved with the franchise when it relocated to Calgary. In the spring of 1981, he scored three goals in 11 playoff games for the Flames.

On November 25, 1981, Calgary traded Gould and Randy Holt to the Washington Capitals for Pat Ribble and a second-round pick. Given a full-time NHL role at last, he made the most of it, scoring 18 goals and 31 points in his first 60 games with Washington. He went on to top 20 goals three times, scoring five goals in four playoff games in 1983 and setting career highs with 23 goals and 50 points in the 1986 to 1987 season.

On March 20, 1987, Gould found himself in an unexpected spotlight when he dropped the gloves with Pittsburgh star Mario Lemieux. Despite giving up five inches and 25 pounds, he landed an uppercut that broke Lemieux’s jaw and sent the Penguins centre to George Washington University Hospital for the night. “The first thing that came to mind when he said, ‘Let’s go,’ was that I could get him off the ice for five minutes,” Gould said. “I never thought about hurting him.”

On September 28, 1989, Washington traded Gould to the Boston Bruins for defenceman Alain Côté. In what would be his final NHL season, he played 77 games and helped the Bruins advance all the way to the 1990 Stanley Cup Final, eliminating his former Capitals teammates along the way. He spent one more season with the AHL’s Maine Mariners before retiring in 1991.

Across 697 NHL games, Gould recorded 145 goals, 159 assists and 304 points, adding 28 points in 78 playoff games.

Bobby Gould leaves behind a career built on determination, durability and quiet reliability, the kind of player coaches trusted and teammates counted on night after night. From the rinks of Petrolia to the Stanley Cup Final, he proved that a late-round pick with a strong work ethic could carve out a lasting place in the game, and the communities of southwestern Ontario will always remember him as one of their own.
