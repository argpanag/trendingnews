Calls for RTÉ to clarify recruitment of ex-Newstalk hosts Ger Gilroy and Kieran Cuddihy

TheTools · 06.11.2026 10:39 · source · permalink

RTÉ’s sports department was “not happy” with the appointment of broadcaster Ger Gilroy to host a new podcast due to comments he previously made about the station, an Oireachtas committee has heard.

During a sports committee hearing, the process around the former Newstalk presenter’s appointment took centre stage, including whether he had been “brought shopping” for a tracksuit top.

Amid tense exchanges with Fine Gael Senator and former RTÉ sports presenter Evanne Ní Chuilinn, RTÉ deputy director general Adrian Lynch told the committee that “the RTÉ sports department were not happy with the appointment of Ger Gilroy because of comments he has made about RTÉ in the past”.

The broadcaster defended the process around his appointment, rejecting suggestions from committee members that he had been “tapped up” or “headhunted” for the role.

RTÉ’s director of audio Patricia Monahan told the committee there was an expression-of-interest process for the role, which drew 20 internal expressions. Alongside this, she said external candidates were identified and put through the same assessment process as internal candidates, with Gilroy being approached in June after this process to establish whether he was interested in the role.

Fianna Fáil Senator Alison Comyn said Monahan had “tied us in knots” with the explanation around the hiring process. Monahan said there was a clear, fair and transparent process in place, but Fine Gael TD Brian Brennan said of the sequence outlined: “If that’s not a tap-up, I don’t care what you want to say.”

“It’s almost like you’re dancing around not wanting to say we headhunted somebody,” committee chair Alan Kelly said.

RTÉ executives repeatedly declined to say how much Gilroy is being paid, indicating it would not become public as he is not among the station’s top 10 highest earners.

Monahan said Gilroy “100 per cent” came through a formal process. The committee was told the budget for RTÉ’s podcast unit is €800,000 annually, inclusive of salaries, but that fit-out costs for a new studio were not included in this.

There was criticism of coverage of women’s sport and an absence of female voices on Gilroy’s Howler podcast. Monahan said this had been communicated to Gilroy as a piece of “editorial feedback”.

Kelly said what was being described regarding podcasting at the station was “farcical”.

There were several exchanges over clothing, with Comyn saying she had been given “serious assurances” that Gilroy had a wardrobe allowance of €5,000. Monahan responded that he did not have an allowance and was not sponsored by anybody, but said that potentially some money had been allocated in a programme budget.

Later, Brennan asked whether Gilroy was “brought shopping” for clothes, with Monahan saying he was not “to her knowledge”. She added that she thought a single item of clothing was bought for him for a photo shoot. Lynch then interjected to suggest it was “potentially” a tracksuit top.

The committee heard that Gilroy’s appointment was only disclosed to the head of sport in the days before it was announced, and that the head of sport and the head of radio were not involved in the appointment process.

It came after Monahan was asked to clarify how Gilroy was hired.

The National Union of Journalists (NUJ) also called for clarification about how current Liveline presenter Kieran Cuddihy was recruited.

It was later clarified in the Oireachtas media committee on Wednesday that Cuddihy applied via an expressions-of-interest process.

Last month Monahan, who herself joined the national broadcaster from Newstalk, claimed in a newspaper interview that RTÉ had run “an expressions-of-interest process, and obviously Gerard expressed an interest, and we decided he was the one who came through the process and was appointed”.

It subsequently emerged, however, in an interview with Gilroy in The Irish Times that rather than Gilroy expressing an interest in joining the station, RTÉ’s head of podcasts, Hugh Ormond, had “pitched [him] a daily sports podcast”.

In a letter to RTÉ’s chief people officer, Annette Malone, the head of the NUJ, Séamus Dooley, said it was “a matter of utmost seriousness” regarding “the nature of the appointment”.

Dooley said such an approach was contrary to “procedures and agreements on recruitment” and he said it indicated that “the culture of secrecy and private deals still prevails”.
