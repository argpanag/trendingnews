Storyline Of The Day – Time To Step Up
Didn’t take long for the depth to be tested, did it?

Entering the season, the strength of this team was on the wings. When Alex Laferriere and Trevor Moore are the fifth and sixth players at that position on the depth chart on July 1, that’s got the makings of a very deep group.

Then Kevin Fiala had a setback which has cost him the first three games of the season. Then Mats Zuccarello went out, on a week-to-week timeline. Now, the Kings have lost three straight games to begin the season and are without two of the wingers who were a big part of why that position group felt very exciting. With Alex Turcotte also out, the Kings are essentially down a full forward line, which puts the onus on others to step up around them.

“Every team goes through it and I think that’s what depth is for,” Peter Laviolette said this morning. “It gives opportunity for others to step up and for me, it’s having the depth to fill these holes while they’re out. Hopefully not anything long term, but every team has to deal with that.

All of a sudden, Moore and Laferriere move up the depth chart and a line that looked like a damn good third line becomes your second line, with expectations and a need for production rising accordingly. That depth on the wings is being called up early, but not in the way it was hoped. I suppose if there’s one area where you have the pieces, it’s in that position, but with players out of action, now other players will need to step up, both into larger roles and within their current ones.

I think some of the good news is that Moore and Laferriere played their best hockey of the season together last year. With Quinton Byfield at center, that line was dynamic. Both have shown second-line production at times in their careers. Now they’ll need to show it and urgently.

“When you’re missing important players like that, we need not just me and Laf, but we need everyone to step up,” Moore said. “We’ve got to contribute offensively and take that step……I’m excited to play with Laf [again]. I thought we had some good chemistry last year, so it should be fun.”

Over the last three seasons collectively, Moore’s on-ice goals for, among forwards he’s played at least 200 minutes with, is highest with Laferriere on a per/60 basis. In 22 games together during March and April of last season, Laferriere had 17 points while Moore had 16. Solid production from players in that position.

The addition to that line is Haula, who I think has been one of the team’s best players through the first three games of the season. Just seems to impact the game a number of different ways. He’s one of two players on the roster – along with Laferriere – who has a point in all three Kings games played this season. His goal in San Jose and his assist against Florida were both hard-working points. Haula isn’t known exclusively for the offensive side of the game but he’s been around a 35-40 point guy over the last handful of seasons. Good middle-six production.

Moore certainly knew Haula, from playing against each other over the years, but as he’s seen him more as a teammate, his game has stood out differently than maybe he expected.

“He’s been one of our best players the first three games here, he’s a guy who has done it for a long time, he’s a really good center,” Moore said of Haula. “A lot of speed and he plays a really responsible game for sure, which I maybe didn’t appreciate as much when playing against him. He’s a very responsible player and he just plays hard. You see him in San Jose, bulldoze the d-man and gets to the net. For a guy who is maybe not the biggest in the league, he plays hard.”

Behind those players is a third line that looks familiar from post-deadline last year, with Scott Laughton at center between Jared Wright and Joel Armia. A fourth line, assuming 12 forwards, has Corey Perry alongside Samuel Helenius and Andre Lee. There wasn’t much offense at all from the bottom six down the stretch last season. The Kings need elevated play from both lines in order to help compensate, otherwise it’ll place a ton of pressure on the top line each night. It’s a concern, certainly, but one that will play out starting tonight.

3 To Watch For –
– On the housekeeping front, forwards Mats Zuccarello and Alex Turcotte were both placed on injured reserve today.

With that comes a minimum of seven days out of action. I think that rules them out only for tonight’s game in Vegas and Tuesday’s game versus Edmonton. Not to say that both players will certainly play the following game versus Boston, but that they would be eligible to. Turcotte feels shorter than Zuccarello, but for the next two games, don’t count on either player.

With the IR designation opens up roster spots if the Kings want or need them. With both players not counting towards the 23-man limit, the Kings have two open spots in theory if they want to use them. Depending on how tonight goes, wonder if maybe we see one utilized on a forward in advance of Tuesday’s game against Edmonton. I’d have my eye on Kenny Connors if that were to materialize.

– On forward Jared Wright, season debut for him tonight in Vegas and he does so with a two-year contract extension signed this morning.

“He’s got a big smile on his face so that’s a big deal, you sign an NHL contract that’s a one-way contract, that’s well deserved and well earned,” Laviolette said this morning. “He was here and played well at the end of last year. He had a great training camp and it was just a matter of time before he was back. He had a great start, I think he had two goals down there, so good for him, and now he’s back. He was happy about it and everybody found out this morning, so that was a big deal inside the room.”

I don’t really see any downside to the extension. It’s a league minimum contract for each of the next two seasons, coming in at a $925,000 cap hit. I’ve said from the start that I think Wright is already an NHL-caliber player, even if that’s in a fourth-line role today. If he continues to develop and improves in the areas he detailed yesterday, specifically with the puck in the offensive zone, there’s a chance this could be a real bargain. In 59 career AHL games, Wright has scored 19 goals and zero of those came on the power play. He scored 17 of 19 at even strength with six game winners. If he could find somewhere in that 15-goal range in the NHL, along with his skating and forechecking and what I’d expect to be a place on the penalty kill over the long haul, he’ll play in the NHL for a long time, in the Top-9 in my opinion. If not, he plays a game that can still translate towards a role lower in the lineup.

Easy extension to sign for the Kings, to lock in a guy like that at a low number.

– Big test tonight for a Kings penalty-killing unit that was substantially better on Tuesday.

Los Angeles went 6-for-6 on the kill in that game, after not finding their groove in Colorado or San Jose. Now, they’ll face a Vegas power play that ranked sixth in the NHL last season and scored five goals in four games played against the Kings.

“I think we did a good job through the neutral zone, I feel like we forced them to dump a lot of pucks, we got sticks on a lot of pucks, which made it hard for them to enter with possession,” forward Erik Haula said. “I feel like that’s huge. Then, we could apply pressure and we worked pucks out pretty well.”

Haula added that the help from the defensemen and wings on faceoffs was big as well, which allowed the Kings to start with full-ice clears early.

In-zone, the Kings felt they better worked as a four-player unit then they did in the first two games of the season. In Colorado specifically, the slot was available far too easily, which made it easy on the Avalanche power play Being better defending entries, as Haula said, limits the amount of zone time and then the Kings were better against the in-zone setup when it did exist.

“We just worked as a unit, we stayed in shot lanes and made it harder to get pucks into the inner-part of the o-zone,” defenseman Joel Edmundson added. “We’re getting better. First game wasn’t how we wanted it to go, but watching lots of video, working on it in practice and getting used to playing with some new guys. That’s definitely more like it, for sure.”

Worked on Tuesday. See if the Kings can keep that part of the game rolling. When you are undermanned, special teams becomes that much more important. The kill will be a big part of that.

The Kings continue to look for their first win of the 2026-27 regular season and they’ll visit the defending Western Conference champions in pursuit of it. Not exactly an easy challenge, but it’s the one in front of them. Puck drops at 7 PM from T-Mobile Arena!
