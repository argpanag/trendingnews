Sunshine Protection Act in danger

TheTools · 26.09.2026 00:49 · source · permalink

RENO, Nev. (KOLO) -Will there ever be a day when we don’t have to set our clocks two times a year?

One solution could be the “Sunshine Protection Act”. The bill is endorsed by President Trump.

But Senate Majority Leader John Thun says he doesn’t think the 60 votes are there to pass the “Sunshine Protection Act”.

The bill would place our country on permanent Day Light Saving Time.

It received bi-partisan support in the house. The senate is only in session for one more week.

During last legislative session Nevada Lawmakers looked at a bill that would place the state on permanent standard time.

If passed it would have residents set their clock back on November second of 2025 and leave them there for the rest of the year and beyond.

Selena La Rue Hatch from District 25 sponsored the bill.

She pointed to among other things public safety.

”From a public safety aspect car accidents go down when we are not changing the clocks,” said La Rue Hatch back in February of 2025. “So, I think from a public safety aspect this us a positive change.”

The bill never made it out of the Nevada Legislature,

But La Rue Hatch says if re-elected, she will introduce the bill again.

She says there’s a reason she wants to stay on Standard Time.

“The reason I brought the bill was because I hear from constituents all the time about how frustrating it is for them to change the clock,” she says. “Especially if they have kids or they have animals it is so disruptive. As a teacher I see that disruption in our schools. But I also heard from doctors who said it led to increase in heart attacks, and strokes and car accidents. You know what, if this is a way be we can improve our quality of living our quality of health outcomes, and not spend one single penny why aren’t we doing it.”

She says the move would not cost the state a dime. But could improve the health of Nevadans.

She says she wants Standard Time, because that’s the only time states like Nevada are allowed to keep the clocks from changing twice a year.

It would literally take an act of Congress for a different rule—hence: the ‘Sunshine Protection Act”.

She says permanent Day Light Saving Time means kids will be headed to the school in the dark and that’s a safety issue.

She also adds, Standard Time is more in-line with our circadian rhythm.

Several states do not change their clock and stay on Standard Time all year long.

They include Arizona, Hawaii, American Samoa, Puerto Rico, and the Virgin Islands.

It is interesting to note the ski industry has come out opposed to the “Sunshine Protection Act”.

Representatives say the loss of an hour of sunshine in the winter would have a big impact on their resorts.
