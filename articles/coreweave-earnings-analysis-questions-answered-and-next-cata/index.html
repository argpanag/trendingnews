CoreWeave Inc (CRWV): Trading at $60.79 (+24.75%) as of Nov 11, 12:14 PM EST (Market Cap: ~$26.88B) || Prior close $48.73 || 1M: -25.82% || 52W range $40.58–$138.25

Verdict: Funding fears eased

Questions answered

  • Is demand real? Revenue was $3.66B, up 168% year over year, against about $3.55B expected. Backlog reached about $132.5B, up 138%, from $104.2B at the end of June.

  • Will guidance hold? FY2026 revenue guidance was raised to $13.0–$13.3B, above the $12.86B consensus.

  • How will it pay for the buildout? Management cited roughly $9B of customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months. It also said it does not plan more parent-level unsecured debt through 2027. Debt fears had driven a roughly 40% one-month slide.

  • Is regulation a threat? The CEO said post-midterm regulatory developments should not materially affect the expansion pipeline.

  • Is it improving at the bottom line? Q3 EPS was -$1.27 against -$1.55 expected (+18% surprise), per the earnings feed. The adjusted loss was $0.95 against $1.16 expected. The two figures use different bases (GAAP versus adjusted).

The streak is mixed. Q4 FY2025 and Q1 FY2026 were big misses, with the stock falling 18.8% and 17.3%. Q2 and Q3 were beats, and Q2 was followed by a 22.2% rise. This stock moves sharply on earnings in both directions.

Questions still open

  • Interest cost: Q4 interest expense is guided to $1.05–1.15B, up from $846M in Q3. That is roughly a $4.4B annualized burden. One analysis puts it at about 1.4x implied Q4 adjusted operating income, so growth is not yet covering financing.

  • Who is prepaying? Prepayments from cash-rich hyperscalers are real liquidity. Prepayments from AI labs that themselves need to raise capital may only move money around the same ecosystem.

  • Build versus contract: About 4.6GW is contracted, but only about 1.7GW is active (~37%, down from 41%). The year-end target is above 1.85GW, which implies modest Q4 activation.

  • Remaining funding need: Street estimates run from $22.9B (assuming 20% prepayments) to $36.7B (no prepayments). The most conservative estimate is $76B. Bond yields near 15% make that range costly.
