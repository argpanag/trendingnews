Colin Farrell shares hearbreaking 'fear' for son with rare genetic disorder

TheTools · 16.10.2026 09:49 · source · permalink

Colin Farrell has opened up about his hopes and worries for his son James, providing an intimate look into their family's story and the legacy he's committed to creating for him.

The Castleknock-born star, 50, is preparing to launch a new foundation in honour of his eldest son, who has been living with a rare genetic condition for the past 20 years.

Speaking to People, Colin openly discussed the incredible progress James has made throughout his life, while emphasising the need for enhanced support for adults with developmental disabilities.

James, 20, has Angelman syndrome, a rare neurogenetic disorder that affects movement, speech and cognitive development.

Explaining what drove him to establish the Colin Farrell Foundation, the actor shared that his son turning 20 had prompted him to reflect on what lies ahead, reports RSVP Live.

"I suppose the reason why we're establishing the foundation now is that James is of an age – he's 20 – and he's had an extraordinary 20 years," he said.

"He works incredibly hard to achieve the most, quote unquote, menial of things, the most typical of things – benchmarks that we all experience as typical human beings who don't have any cognitive issues or developmental delays."

Paying tribute to his son's progress, Colin said: "James has fought very hard to achieve certain benchmarks, and he's done incredibly well. He's a really well-adjusted, really happy young man. He's extraordinary, but now we're looking towards the rest of his life. What's the next chapter of James's life look like?"

Yet it's the prospect of James one day navigating life without his parents by his side that weighs most heavily on the Hollywood star.

"My fear would be, God forbid, if and when something happens to James's mother or to me, that James would be 30 or 40, and then he would have to go somewhere, into some kind of institute or some kind of residential care, and there'd be nobody there to call over, take him out and have lunch with him and all that."

Colin remains resolute that his foundation will transform the options available to families supporting adults with intellectual disabilities, providing advocacy, education and programmes designed to nurture greater independence.

He's equally passionate about establishing a summer camp as part of the initiative, offering families further opportunities to access the right support for their loved ones.

"But there are very, very few places, and so I want to change that," he said. "I want to be part of that, with the great team we already have behind this foundation.

"Building a summer camp will be part of the foundation as well. That would be a big one for me to leave behind, and it's all because of James. You know, it's all in James's honour."

The actor revealed that the prospect of missing so many years of his son's future weighed heavily on him.

"It's a terrifying thought that I'll miss the last, you know, 40 years or 50 years of his life, just because I won't be there to shepherd and protect him."

At its core, Colin's greatest hope is for James to experience a life filled with stability, meaningful relationships and the everyday joys that matter most.

Speaking candidly about his aspirations, he explained: "But what would the perfect life look like? You know, just a life of connection, where James, in the last 10, 20, 30 or 40 years of his life, will be somewhere where he feels like he belongs, where he feels safe, and where he can garden and watch movies and swim in the pool and go out to the beach and just have a full, meaningful and connected life."

He finished on a heartfelt note: "You know, that'll be the dream."
