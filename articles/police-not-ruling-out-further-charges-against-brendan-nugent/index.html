Latest from Wangaratta following attack on MP

Further serious charges may be laid against a Victorian man over his alleged attack on Indi MP Helen Haines on Monday.

Victoria Police have already laid three charges against 43-year-old Brendan Nugent after he allegedly struck independent MP Helen Haines with his car.

Ms Haines, 65, remains in hospital in a critical but stable condition.

Victoria Police and the Australian Federal Police (AFP) told a press conference on Wednesday morning they have not ruled out laying further charges, including attempted murder.

"While I say this investigation is far from complete, we are not ruling out further serious charges," Victoria Police Chief Commissioner Mike Bush said.

"I want to further add that we have deployed a number of staff into Wangaratta to give assurance to members of the public.

"We do believe this was a deliberate but isolated act."

Chief Commissioner Bush said there were no "intelligence holdings" against Mr Nugent and police had no information to suggest he was an "immediate threat".

Detectives on Tuesday night charged Nugent with dangerous driving causing serious injury and intentionally causing serious injury.

He was also charged with the federal offence of causing harm to a Commonwealth public official - which carries a maximum penalty of 10 years' imprisonment.

He is due to appear in the Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on Wednesday.

In a statement, Victoria Police said officers would allege the incident was targeted.

The ABC has confirmed Haines has lost one leg and doctors are fighting to save the other after she was pinned between two cars outside her electorate office in Victoria's north-east.

Haines, 65, suffered critical lower-body injuries in the Wangaratta attack and was flown to The Alfred hospital in Melbourne.

Helen Haines was critically injured when she was struck by a four-wheel-drive on Monday.

ABC News / Ian Cutmore

Parliamentary colleagues and members of the community have expressed their shock and support for Ms Haines, who has been the member for Indi since 2019.

Family of accused address 'devastating' incident

On Tuesday officers searched Mr Nugent's Tarrawingee home, roughly 15 kilometres east of the rural city of Wangaratta.

Family members of Mr Nugent also released a statement, saying "all of our love and all of our thoughts are with Helen and her family and friends".

In the statement, Mr Nugent's father Rod Nugent and Rod's partner Christine Cunningham said Ms Haines had been their friend for more than 20 years and Monday's events were "devastating and unfathomable".

"Helen Haines is a deeply respected, valued and much loved member of our local community," the statement said.

"We would like to sincerely thank members of the local community and police who came to Helen's aid yesterday after the shocking and horrific crime.

"We will be assisting Victoria Police in any way we can."

- ABC
