Micah Nori praises ‘joy’ LaMelo Ball brings to Minnesota before Timberwolves-Blazers clash

TheTools · 17.11.2026 00:43 · source · permalink
  • NBA on ClutchPoints
  • Minnesota Timberwolves

Upon his return to Minnesota after five years with the organization, Blazers coach Micah Nori raves about the LaMelo Ball fit in Minneapolis.

MINNEAPOLIS — After five seasons under head coach Chris Finch, Micah Nori headed west for his first head coaching gig with the Portland Trail Blazers. Tonight, he returns to a place he called home for five years and still has plenty of love for.

“I have five years of knowledge of Finchy and this ball club,” said Nori, who has his Blazers at a 9-3 record, fourth in a loaded Western Conference.

When asked if he plans to use his knowledge of Minnesota Timberwolves' schemes to his advantage, the first-year Blazers head coach said, “It's not fair to our guys to try to get all of that into them in a matter of one shootaround. You got to think that this is one of 82… We talk about playing how we're going to play and not worry about opposition as much. So that is a fine line. … I think you'll see more out of ATOs, like we might draw something up to try to take advantage of something of how I know they're going to defend something or whatever, and then I’m sure Finchy will do the same thing, because quite honestly, we play very similar styles of basketball.”

While Nori returns to an organization he has a stark amount of familiarity with, this season’s iteration of Minnesota’s roster has seen  significant alteration compared to what Nori left behind. Part of that change includes the acquisition of all-star point guard LaMelo Ball, who has brought the ‘joy’ to an organization that needed it, according to Nori’s evaluation.

“I think he's helped Finchy,” said Nori. “The fact that when you get a personality like LaMelo Ball, you can see even when I've talked to (Finch) on the phone, how much he's enjoying this … quite honestly, in the joy that he's brought to that practice facility and that team. I think that it’s been a lot of fun for him.”

In addition to noting the vibes that the newly acquired Ball has surely helped curate, the Blazers' new head coach had quite the complimentary comparison for the Timberwolves' new floor general. 

“As far as getting ready for him, that's a different story — it's not fun for us,” he said. ”It might be a bad comparison, but he's very similar a little bit to Luka (Doncic). And I mean by this, it's from a sense that you know what you're gonna get. If you send him left, you're gonna get stepped back. If you send him right, he can get to the rim, he can make any pass, and on demand. So, he is a problem offensively, and the one thing that he does do is he makes offense very simple for a lot of people, because he can go where he wants to go. He's very good, obviously — an elite passer.  He can shoot the ball, so you gotta make sure that you're up.”

While Nori was loved in all areas of Minnesota’s building, there will be no letup on the court against their former coach from the players’ perspective, says guard Bones Hyland.

“Yeah, it's gonna be good to see Micah again,” said Hyland, who spent a season and a half with Nori in the Timberwolves’ organization.

“Obviously, he meant a lot. You know, on the coaching staff… back-to-back Western Conference appearances. … That's big time. He got a head coaching job over with Portland, so that's, you know, that's — I feel like that's every coach's dream to want to be a head coach in the NBA. So, obviously, that's a big accomplishment for him, but, you know, we want to take Micah’s head off, so. You know, ain't no bad blood, you know what I'm saying. But we want to take his head off.”

Nori reciprocated that message, noting Hyland’s pregame comments and said: “It’s vice versa.”
