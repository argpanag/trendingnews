Reform UK 'has no interest in Wales', says Senedd member who has defected to Plaid Cymru

Sarah Cooper-Lesadd, the member of the Senedd who has today defected from Reform UK to Plaid Cyrmu, has said she left in part because of Reform’s hostile approach to vulnerable groups. (See 4.59am.) She also said Nigel Farage’s party had no interest in Wales.

Here is another extract from her statement explaining her defection.

The party centrally has no interest in Wales. Their Senedd group is a staging post in the effort to get Nigel Farage into Downing Street, and that was glaringly obvious at its conference this week.

To everyone who voted Reform in May: I have not stopped listening to you. You told me you felt ignored and let down. Those concerns are real, and they will be answered by delivery, not division.

Wales is a small country with enormous potential. Devolution is how that potential is realised, with decisions made closer to the people they affect.

I believe in a Wales that is confident, compassionate and ambitious. A Wales that uses the powers it has to improve people’s lives and seeks the powers it still needs.

Plaid is serious about governing, serious about delivery and serious about Wales. It is a party that believes in our communities and believes Wales can do more when decisions are made closer to the people they affect.

I know what I am asking of Plaid members who spent May campaigning against the party I stood for, and I will work to earn their trust.

I came into politics to give people a voice, improve lives and get things done, including for every child growing up in care in Wales today. Plaid Cymru is the best place for me to do that, and I am ready to get to work.

Reform UK hit by fresh blow in Wales as Senedd member defects to Plaid Cymru

It is not a good day for Reform UK in the Welsh Senedd. Just as the party’s leader announced he was standing down, a Reform UK MS (member of the Senedd) announced she is defecting to Plaid Cymru.

Sarah Cooper-Lesadd, who represents Pen-y-Bont Bro Morgannwg (Bridgend and Vale of Glamorgan), said in a statement:

Today I have left Reform UK and joined Plaid Cymru and its Senedd group.

I grew up in the care system. I stood for the Senedd to be a voice for care-experienced people, who are still badly under-represented in our politics. I want to improve people’s lives, stand up for people who are overlooked and get things done. My approach is evidence-led and rooted in what works for our communities.

Reform has no interest in the work I came here to do. They are not serious about governing Wales. They are far more interested in division, opposition and headlines than in finding solutions. There is a real appetite in the group to want the government to fail, and no interest in what that failure would mean for the people we represent.

They oppose measures without any coherent explanation. I wanted to vote for the supplementary budget, which every party should have supported, and I argued against tabling the Nation of Sanctuary debate [a debate organised by Reform attacking the Welsh government policy on support for asylum seekers], and I was criticised for sitting it out. Having just spoken about my own childhood trauma, I could not in good conscience sit through it.

I cannot support Reform’s attacks on people seeking sanctuary or other vulnerable groups. I particularly reject the way minorities have too often been used as political targets. I do not believe anyone should be scapegoated or dehumanised to win votes, and I will not be part of a politics that does that.

Reform UK's leader in Wales, Dan Thomas, says he is stepping down after referral to police - but has done nothing wrong

Dan Thomas, the Reform UK leader in Wales, says he is stepping down having been reported to the police. He says he has done nothing wrong, but has not given further details in the statement he has released.

He says:

It has been the honour of my life to have been the leader of Reform Wales and see this party go from zero to 34 elected members and become the largest opposition group in Welsh history. With deep regret, I am now standing down from this position due to personal reasons.

Over the weekend, I was reported to the police and was released without charge. I stress that I have done nothing wrong, but I do not want the coming headlines to be a distraction from the fantastic work our Senedd team is doing in holding this government to account.

I will continue to serve my constituents in Casnewydd Islwyn and will fully back whoever the group selects as its next leader.

Haigh says AI can never replace the role of teachers.

There’s no way that AI can ever, could ever, overtake the need for quality teachers and teaching assistants in schools.

I’m sure nearly everyone in this room can point back to a teacher or someone in their school that had a profound impact on their life and their life choices. So we need to make sure that those interactions and that relationship is 100% protected.

As we go forward in all the reform around AI, working people need to be at the heart of the I debate. And we’re committed again to working with trade unions and employers in this.

Where our AI makes work more productive, then the benefits should be felt by workers, not just shareholders or tech bros.

Haigh says the government views young people’s apprenticeships as “incredibly important”.

Haigh says the Tories and Reform UK has shown what they stand for in their opposition to the Equal Pay Act.

Q: Will you pause cuts to UKRI [UK Research and Innovation]?

Haigh says the government is spending £68bn on R&D.

Q: [From a firefighter] Do you agree firefighters need more resources to fight wildfires?

Haigh says her grandfather was a firefighter. She thanks them for all they did this summer. The government is holding an extreme weather soon. It will be in partnership with the FBU, the fire brigades union.

Q: How will you ensure shop workers get guaranteed hours?

Haigh says she once worked on a zero-hours contract herself. She says the government has just finished a consultation on guaranteed hours.

Haigh says the Labour manifesto promised the biggest wave of insourcing for a generation.

From April next year, all government bodies will have to apply a public interest test when deciding if services can be insourced.

Haigh is now taking questions.

Q: [From Kelvin, a Unison member, chair of Unison’s national social care committee] Do you agree with Unison on the need to take profit out of the care sector.

Haigh says the care sector has been undervalued for far too long. That is what underpins the PM’s personal commitment to tackling social care.

A strong national care service will be underpined by its workforce, she says.

She says the Louise Casey review is specifically looking at the role of private equity in this sector.

AI has enormous potential to improve lives. To transform our public services, make our businesses stronger and deliver new scientific breakthroughs. Reform UK launched network for women in party, claiming it will put them 'at heart' of its agenda

Reform UK has launched a network for women in the party. At a launch event, Sarah Pochin MP, the group’s parliamentary chair, said:

Women for Reform is giving a platform to the great talent and ability within our ranks. Now is the time to get things done. This new group within Reform UK will become a powerful vehicle for shaping our future policy, with women and women’s safety at the very heart of our agenda.

Women for Reform is giving a platform to the great talent and ability within our ranks. Now is the time to get things done. This new group within Reform UK will become a powerful vehicle for shaping our future policy, with women and women’s safety at the very heart of our agenda.

Pochin and her colleagues have their work cut out, because Reform UK is a very male-orientated party. The latest poll from YouGov has Reform UK neck and neck with Labour, with both parties on 23%.

But, while Reform UK polls 28% with men, with women it is at just 19%. No other party has a gender gap that large.

And in the Commons just 25% of Reform UK MPs are women. The equivalent figures for other main parties are: Greens – 80%; Labour – 47%; Lib Dems – 45%; and the Conservatives 24%. Immediately after the 2024 election, Reform were at 0%. The numbers went up because Pochin won a byelection, and Suella Braverman defected.

Irish PM claims Sinn Féin may have set back cause of Irish unity by alliance with SNP and Plaid Cymru

Micheál Martin, the Irish PM (taoiseach) has suggested Sinn Féin may set back the cause of Irish unity by aligning itself with Scottish and Welsh pro-independence parties.

As the Press Association reports, Martin, who is leader of Fianna Fáil, questioned whether Sinn Féin was on the right strategic track after the party signed a memorandum of understanding with the SNP and Plaid Cymru to put pressure on the UK government to call unity referendums in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Wales.

Speaking to the media today, Martin was asked about Michelle O’Neill, the Sinn Féin leader in Northern Ireland, saying at yesterday’s event that he was not doing enough to advance the cause of Irish unification. He replied:

Fundamentally, I would disagree.

I’m not into campaigning, but I would make the point that strategically we have never embroiled Northern Ireland in British politics.

We’ve always identified the north as exceptional, as being an issue in terms of the partition of Ireland.

And I think strategically I’m not clear that Sinn Féin is on the right track at all, and may even set back unity by that move that they’ve made.

Martin said Fianna Fáil was a party that believes in Irish unity. But he said his current focus is on progressing new mechanisms to deal with the legacy of the Troubles and on examining reforms to the institutions established under the Good Friday agreement.

No 10 insists that government planning 'enhanced residency' test for donors before Reform UK got its £72m

Downing Street is pointing out that the government was talking about an “enhanced residency” test for Britons newly arrived in the UK who donate to political parties back in August. At the lobby briefing earlier, the PM’s spokesperson faced claims that ministers are only talking about this now in response to the recent news about Ben Delo and Christopher Harborne giving Reform UK £72m. Part of the reason why this allegation sounded plausible is that the spokesperson was unable to clearly show that this was something on the government’s agenda before the £72m story broke. (See 1.41pm.)

It now emerges that, on a Saturday at the end of August, the Ministry for Housing, Communities and Local Government sent out a press release that mentioned a proposal for “enhanced UK residency requirements for donors seeking to give more than £100,000”. It was not published online. But on Tuesday 1 September MHCLG sent out a briefing note to journalists saying:

We’re planning a new test to stop wealthy people living overseas giving huge donations to political parties despite not living in the country. This will make sure donors have a genuine connection to the UK, rather than simply appearing to live here.
