Trains cancelled after bedbug infestation

TheTools · 02.10.2026 20:29 · source · permalink

Bedbugs have infested trains across the South of England, leading to services being cancelled, The Telegraph can disclose.

Trains run by Southeastern have been removed from schedules after staff discovered the bugs crawling inside carriages.

The outbreak affects West Marina train care depot near Hastings, East Sussex, which serves routes between London and Kent. Sightings have also been reported on Southeastern’s High Speed 1 line services, raising fears the bugs are spreading.

Drivers have found bedbugs in their cabs and crawling on their bags. According to internal messages seen by The Telegraph, drivers have refused to board affected trains, and accused their bosses of failing to take the issue seriously.

At least three services were cancelled on Friday, with more having been cancelled since the first sighting on Sept 22.

One train on Tuesday, nicknamed Bedbug Express by insiders, did several journeys between London and Kent stations with two carriages locked and out of use, after a driver saw the insects at one end of the train.

Pest controllers were called to the West Marina depot on Friday morning, with a van parked outside the staff entrance on Cliftonville Road.

An operative driving the van would say only that the company had a regular contract with Southeastern for pest control at some of its depots.

Service control logs shared with The Telegraph by concerned railway staff revealed the full details of how the “Bedbug Express” was allowed to run.

A Southeastern incident log from earlier this week said a driver had refused even to get into the cab of a train containing bedbugs to move it across the depot unless “provided with a white full-body protection suit ... in case any bugs transfer to themself” [sic].

No such suits were available, forcing the cancellation of two trains, including one blocked in by the bedbug train.

Southeastern maintenance staff tried to take the Bedbug Express out of service, but internal logs noted that service controllers had not been advised of the problem. So the train ran for all of Tuesday, but with the affected coach and the one next to it locked and out of use.

Three years ago, a sharp rise in the number of bedbug sightings on trains, partly blamed on a resurgence in travel after the Covid-19 pandemic, caused panic among commuters.

Described as “nits for fully grown adults”, the small brown insects hide in warm nooks and crevices of beds and other furniture, before creeping out at night to bite humans, causing spots and rashes.

Removing them is typically expensive and time-consuming, and can involve disposing of infested large furnishings.

The news comes after the headquarters of The Guardian newspaper near King’s Cross had a bedbug infestation, necessitating pest controllers to be called in to fumigate and cleanse the building.

Locals living opposite West Marina depot – which is separated from their homes only by a length of chainlink fence – were stoic when told of the infestation.

Lorraine, a schoolteacher who asked not to give her full name, expressed concern about a pile of discarded furniture outside a depot building marked “train crew messroom”, saying: “That wasn’t there the other day.”

“It doesn’t surprise me,” she whispered from behind her front door after The Telegraph explained the bedbug problem. “They do all the cleaning here, they wash the trains on that side.”

She continued: “From what I know, they’d have to be carried in like fleas. But that’s always a risk with deliveries.” She explained that sometimes delivery drivers sat on the wall opposite, at the foot of the depot fence, before bringing parcels to her door.

A Rail, Maritime and Transport union spokesman said it was “working closely with the company to deal with what is a very difficult situation and we share their concerns for passenger safety”.

A Southeastern spokesman said four trains had been reported as having bedbugs on them, of which two were confirmed then cleaned before being returned to service.

Oly Turner, the train company’s head of drivers, said: “We have a great relationship with our drivers, who have helped hugely in cooperating with us over these isolated incidents.

“When we receive reports such as these, we check trains thoroughly before they are put back into service when given the all clear. Cases have been contained to the drivers cabs in only two of our trains and no bed bugs have been identified in areas for passengers.

“We continually carry out robust cleaning across our trains and stations, ensuring our customers and employees can travel safely and with confidence.”

Southeastern was contacted for comment.
