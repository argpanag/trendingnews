Abstract

Identification of partial loss-of-function (hypomorphic), reduced penetrance pathogenic missense variants (RPPVs) and assessment of the clinical relevance of these variants is needed for improved hereditary cancer risk estimation. Here we show that 70 candidate hypomorphic missense variants in the DNA-binding domain of BRCA2 reduce homologous recombination DNA repair activity and endogenous cell survival, decrease RAD51 foci formation, and increase sensitivity to Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors. The 34 “strong” hypomorphs with greatest effects on function are associated with moderate risks of female breast cancer and later ages of diagnosis than pathogenic variants (PVs), and 14 of these variants can be classified as “likely pathogenic” by integration of functional data into a ClinGen BRCA1/2 variant curation model. In addition, 32% of tumors with hypomorphic variants and corresponding loss of heterozygosity for BRCA2 display homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) compared to 83% of tumors with PVs and 7% with benign variants. These RPPVs represent a class of BRCA2 variants that have implications for the clinical management of carriers.

Introduction

Germline variants in breast cancer predisposition genes such as BRCA2 are divided into low risk (odds ratio (OR) < 2.0, not clinically relevant), moderate risk (OR 2-4), and high risk (OR > 4) categories1. Classical high-risk loss-of-function pathogenic variants (PVs) in BRCA2 predispose individuals to elevated risks of breast, ovarian, prostate, and pancreatic cancer1,2,3. Female carriers of BRCA2 PVs have a 69% lifetime risk of developing breast cancer, 25% risk of developing a second primary breast cancer4,5,6, and 17% risk of ovarian cancer. However, a small number of BRCA2 PVs, termed reduced penetrance pathogenic variants (RPPVs), that confer a reduced risk of cancer relative to classical BRCA2 PVs and predominantly result in partially aberrant mRNA splicing, have been identified7,8,9,10. Several of these have been observed in FANCD1 (biallelic BRCA2 variants) Fanconi Anemia (FA) patients11,12,13. While partially functional (hypomorphic) missense variants may also be RPPVs, two multiplexed assay of variant effect (MAVE) studies that evaluated nearly 7000 SNVs in the BRCA2 DNA Binding Domain (DBD) did not discriminate clearly between high penetrance variants and RPPVs14,15. Thus, more precise functional and clinical studies are needed to determine whether other clinically relevant RPPVs exist.

National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) management guidelines for females with BRCA2 PVs include options for risk-reducing mastectomy and salpingo-oophorectomy16,17,18. Furthermore, tumors with BRCA2 PVs may show genomic instability and homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) and respond to targeted treatment with selected Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors19,20. However, as RPPVs may be associated with moderate risks of cancer, there is a clinical need to systematically discriminate RPPVs from highly penetrant PVs, and develop specific management and treatment guidelines for carriers.

Herein, we report on the identification and comprehensive functional characterization of 70 candidate hypomorphic missense variants in the BRCA2 DBD using multiple orthogonal functional assays. These 70 candidate hypomorphic variants were selected for analysis because of high BayesDel in silico prediction scores for pathogenicity and intermediate homology-directed repair DNA repair assay scores. The hypomorphic variants conferred significantly reduced risks of breast cancer and lower rates of HRD-associated somatic tumor features compared to classical PVs. In addition, a subset of these variants were clinically defined as likely pathogenic using BRCA2 specifications from the ClinGen BRCA1/2 variant curation expert panel (VCEP) based on the ACMG/AMP variant classification framework21,22. The results establish the existence of large numbers of hypomorphic missense variants in BRCA2 that are RPPVs and have important implications for clinical management of variant carriers.

Results

Homology-directed repair (HDR) analysis of BRCA2 DBD missense variants

The HDR functional assay can be used to measure the functional impact of BRCA2 missense variants on BRCA2 homologous recombination DNA repair activity. Thresholds for abnormal and normal functional effects have previously been established using non-splicing pathogenic/likely pathogenic (P/LP) and known benign/likely benign (B/LB) missense standards, whereby variants with HDR scores >2.50 had >99% probability of being functional/normal and HDR scores <1.49 had >99% probability of being non-functional/abnormal22,23,24,25,26,27. Overall, the assay has 100% sensitivity (95% confidence interval (CI), 79–100%) and 100% specificity (95%CI, 93–100%) for pathogenic missense variants25. From 535 missense variants that were selected for study based on a combination of presence in ClinVar and prediction of pathogenicity by in silico prediction models, 465, including 21 known P/LP and 45 known B/LB standards, have previously been reported24. The remaining 70 variants had partial loss-of-function, denoted as hypomorphic, with HDR scores between 1.49 and 2.50. Herein, HDR scores for 58 of the 70 hypomorphic variants are reported along with 12 previously reported variants and the P/LP and B/LB standards (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Data 1). Among the 70 hypomorphs, 34 (49%) showed strong functional impact with HDR scores of 1.49 to 2 (hereafter referred to as “strong hypomorphs”). The remaining 36 (51%) had weaker HDR effects with scores ranging from 2 to 2.5 (referred to as “weak hypomorphs”) (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Data 1). Immunoblotting for BRCA2 showed that most of the 70 candidate functionally hypomorphic variants had similar levels of protein expression as wild-type BRCA2, suggesting that the hypomorphic effects in the HDR assay were not caused by expression or protein stability issues (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Domain architecture of hypomorphic variants

The location of the hypomorphic variants within four subdomains of the DBD 3D protein structure was evaluated in order to elucidate how these partially functional variants impaired BRCA2 activity. Hypomorphic variants were enriched in the helical and OB1 domains (47 of 70 (67%)), whereas 17 (24%) were in the OB2 domain, including 10 in the tower domain, and six in the OB3 domain (Supplementary Fig. 2a–e and Supplementary Data 1, 2). Of 34 strong hypomorphs, 25 (74%) were in the helical and OB1 domains. These findings are consistent with the presence of 15 of 21 (71%) known pathogenic missense variants in the helical and OB1 domains (Supplementary Data 2). Thus, both functionally abnormal and hypomorphic missense variants are enriched in subdomains that bind DSS1, which restrains intrinsic dsDNA binding of BRCA2-DBD to ensure BRCA2/RAD51 targeting to ssDNA28. In addition, 59 of 70 hypomorphs (84%) were either in secondary structures or in subdomain/intermolecular interfaces, consistent with known pathogenic missense variants (95%), suggesting that hypomorphic variants are located predominantly in residues that are critical for maintaining three-dimensional folding of BRCA2 (Supplementary Data 2). Cross-species protein sequence alignment by Align-GVGD27 showed that 52 of the 58 (90%) residues containing the 70 hypomorphs, in addition to 16 of 17 (94%) residues containing the 21 known pathogenic missense standards, were highly conserved across 27 primates and a larger set of 35 vertebrates29, and well conserved across species from pufferfish to Homo sapiens in contrast to the poorly conserved residues containing benign missense standards (Supplementary Data 1, 3–5). Thus, both hypomorphic and known pathogenic missense variants are located mainly in highly evolutionarily conserved residues.

Endogenous impact of hypomorphic variants

To validate the partial functional effects of the 70 hypomorphs at the endogenous level, the influence of CRISPR/Cas9-based knock-in of the individual hypomorphs on cell survival of haploid human HAP1 cells, which require BRCA2 for viability30, was measured. Fifty eight residues containing the 70 hypomorphic variants in coding exons 15 to 25 of BRCA2 were targeted with variant-specific site directed mutagenesis libraries consisting of all possible single nucleotide variants encoding the hypomorphic variants and corresponding nonsense and silent variants. Genomic DNA samples from day 5 (D5) and D8 were extracted and subjected to deep paired-end sequencing to assess relative effects of each variant on cell survival. The average sequencing depth for each variant was 5,413 for D5 replicates and 4,666 for D8 replicates (Supplementary Data 6, 7). Variant frequencies were normalized, and the log2-transformed fold change (LFC) values of D8 to D5 ratios were calculated as functional scores. Using this approach, functional scores of 67 hypomorphs along with 162 nonsense and 146 silent variants with no SpliceAI predicted acceptor or donor site splicing effects (SpliceAI score<0.20) were measured. Three variants were excluded because of overlap with protospacer protection edit sites. Using a two-component mixture model of all variant data, including the nonsense and silent variant standards, thresholds for 95% sensitivity for functionally abnormal variants (log2-fold change = −0.58) and 95% specificity for silent, functionally normal variants (log2-fold change=0.22) were determined (Supplementary Data 7). Scores between the two thresholds were considered intermediate. Of the 67 assessed hypomorphs, 47 (70%) were intermediate, five (8%) were functionally abnormal, and 15 (22%) were functionally normal (Fig. 2a, Supplementary Fig. 3a, Supplementary Data 1, 7). Notably, the five hypomorphs with abnormal functional scores also displayed HDR scores <2.0, whereas 14 of the 15 functionally normal hypomorphs showed HDR scores >2.0. Overall, the endogenous cell viability assay results were consistent with the HDR-based partial functional effects of these variants (R = 0.61) (Fig. 2b).

Comparison with BRCA2 MAVE assays

Two large-scale BRCA2 MAVE assays of BRCA2 DBD single nucleotide variants have recently evaluated the impact of BRCA2 missense variants on BRCA2 activity. MAVE variants were evaluated using a Bayesian two-component mixture model calibrated to all known pathogenic and benign standards that accounted for variance and batch effect (VarCall). Variants were assigned to seven functional categories (Pathogenic strong (P_strong), P_moderate, P_supporting, VUS, Benign (B)_supporting, B_moderate, B_strong) based on posterior probabilities of pathogenicity with thresholds defined by likelihood ratio estimates according to a ClinGen/ACMG/AMP variant classification framework. Of the 67 hypomorphic variants evaluated in the HAP1 MAVE14, 35 (52%) were assigned to the P_strong/moderate/supporting functional categories, five were VUS, and 27 (40%) were B_strong/moderate/supporting (Supplementary Data 1). Similarly, 37 (56%) of the 66 hypomorphs evaluated in a murine embryonic stem cell (mESC) MAVE15 were P_strong/moderate, 9 were VUS and 20 (30%) were B_strong/moderate (Supplementary Data 1). Overall, the MAVE assays identified the strong hypomorphic variants as P_strong and the weak hypomorphic variants as predominantly VUS or B_strong/moderate (Supplementary Data 1). As such, the MAVE assay did not distinguish variants with strong hypomorphic effects from variants with classical full pathogenic effects.

Comparisons with other functional assays and predictors

A subset of the hypomorphic variants have also been evaluated for effects on BRCA2 activity in several other small-scale functional assays. In a DLD1 BRCA2-deficient cell-based drug sensitivity assay (MANO-B), three of the four strong hypomorphs evaluated were categorized as classes 4–5 (functionally abnormal), and the four weak hypomorphs evaluated were categorized as class 1-2 (functionally normal)31 (Supplementary Data 1). Furthermore, in a saturation prime editing (SPE) assay32 seven strong hypomorphs with HDR scores <2.0 were non-functional, whereas six weak hypomorphs with HDR scores >2.0 had uncertain findings (Supplementary Data 1). In addition, in an mESC complementation assay, five of six weak hypomorphs with HDR scores >2.0 exhibited normal function33. These comparisons show that these assays distinguish between the strong and weak hypomorphs. However, as with the MAVE studies, these assays cannot discriminate between strong hypomorphs and established PVs. The ability of in silico prediction algorithms to predict hypomorphic effects was also assessed. The Align-GVGD model predicted 31 (44%) hypomorph variants as class C65 (likely non-functional), and 13 (19%) as class C0 (likely functional). The AlphaMissense model predicted 57 of 70 (81%) hypomorphs as likely pathogenic, 10 as indeterminate, and only three as likely benign. BayesDel, the preferred in silico predictor used by the ClinGen BRCA1/2 variant curation expert panel (VCEP), predicted 28 (40%) as pathogenic, 25 (36%) as VUS, and 17 (24%) as benign (Supplementary Data 1). Thus, AlphaMissense was the best predictor of the functional impact of hypomorphic variants, but this model also did not discriminate between hypomorphic and functionally abnormal or pathogenic variants.

PARP inhibitor sensitivity of cells expressing hypomorphic variants

Breast and ovarian tumors associated with BRCA2 PVs frequently respond to targeted PARP inhibitor therapy19,20. To test whether the hypomorphic variants conferred sensitivity to PARP inhibitors, HAP1 cells subjected to endogenous targeting using the same mutagenesis libraries as described above were treated with olaparib between D5 to D8 (approximately 4-6 population doublings). DNA from cells isolated at both time points was subjected to deep sequencing, and functional scores were calculated as before. DESeq2 analysis was performed to evaluate whether the PARP inhibitor elicited significant effects on cell survival for each hypomorphic variant independent of the effect of the variant on intrinsic cell viability. Overall, 63 (94%) of the 67 targeted hypomorphic variants and 31 of 32 targeted strong hypomorphs (97%) individually showed significant response to olaparib treatment (FDR < 0.05) under these experimental conditions (Fig. 2c, Supplementary Fig. 3b, Supplementary Fig. 4, Supplementary Data 1 and 8), with the combined effects of intrinsic cell viability and olaparib exposure yielding functional scores for hypomorphic variants that were similar to nonsense PVs. Thus, hypomorphic variants may confer sensitivity to PARP inhibition.

Exatecan sensitivity of cells expressing hypomorphic variants

Next, HAP1 cell lines stably integrated with ten strong hypomorphic variants, 5 weak hypomorphic variants, along with a benign missense variant and two silent variants as controls, were generated. Cell lines containing PVs were not established due to lack of viability. BRCA2 protein expression was similar for all lines (Supplementary Fig. 3c). HDR reporter assay analysis of these stable lines showed partially reduced HDR activity for the hypomorphic variants relative to silent variants (P = 0.04) (Fig. 2d, Supplementary Fig. 3d). Clones were subsequently treated with varying concentrations of exatecan (TOP1 inhibitor delivered by the recently FDA-approved antibody-drug conjugate trastuzumab deruxtecan). Full dose-response curves across a range of drug concentrations were generated, and 312.5 pM was identified as the most informative dose (Supplementary Fig. 3e). Clones were grouped by survival as Good protection (25–80% survival compared to control), Intermediate protection (5-25% survival compared to control), and Little or no protection (limited) (≤5% survival compared to control). Weak hypomorphic variants and benign controls were enriched (6/9) in the good/intermediate protection categories, whereas the strong hypomorphic variants were enriched (7/9) in the Limited protection category (Fig. 2e and Supplementary Data 9). The results suggest that strong hypomorphic variants confer sensitivity to Exatecan, similarly to PARPi, and independent of the expression of mediators of drug response and proliferation.

RAD51 focus formation in stable hypomorphic HAP1 cell lines

Since BRCA2 is required for irradiation-induced RAD51 focus (IRIF) formation34, the influence of the endogenously targeted hypomorphic variants on RAD51 foci formation in a subset of the stably integrated HAP1 cells was also assessed. Specifically, RAD51 foci were enumerated following immunohistochemical staining with anti-RAD51 antibodies after exposure of cells to 8 Gy of gamma-irradiation. The hypomorphic lines and BRCA2 siRNA knockdown cells showed significantly lower RAD51 IRIF formation than the silent variant lines (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 2f and Supplementary Fig. 3f), which further validated the partial BRCA2 activity of the hypomorphic variants.

Cancer risks associated with hypomorphic variants

Frequencies of hypomorphic variants in a consecutive cohort of women undergoing hereditary cancer testing at Myriad Genetics were evaluated using a multivariate logistic regression method that controlled for age, race and ethnicity, and personal/family cancer history (Supplementary Methods). In addition, frequencies of hypomorphic variants among female breast cancer patients referred for hereditary cancer testing at Ambry Genetics were compared to frequencies from gnomAD v.4.1 (excluding the UK Biobank)35 and AllofUs36 reference populations using multivariate logistic regression models adjusted for race and ethnicity. Of the 70 hypomorphic variants, 37 were observed in 283 (0.024%) patients in the Myriad Genetics cohort, with 25 of the 37 variants observed at least twice. Similarly, 24 were observed in 89 (0.024%) patients in the Ambry Genetics cohort, with 15 of the 24 variants observed at least twice (Supplementary Table 1, 2). Strong hypomorphs were consistently associated with moderate risks (OR = 2 to 4) for breast cancer (Myriad Genetics: OR = 2.41, 95%CI = 1.51–3.85; Ambry Genetics cases vs gnomAD v4.1 females: OR = 2.09, 95% CI = 1.16–4.08) (Fig. 3, Tables 1, 2). Similarly, hypomorphic variants with consensus functional results from the HDR assay, HAP1 targeted viability assay, and the large MAVE study (termed “consensus P hypomorphic variants” based on integration of functional data under the PS3/BS3 ClinGen/ACMG/AMP classification specifications)22 yielded moderate risks for breast cancer (Myriad Genetics OR = 2.57, 95%CI 1.52–4.34) (Table 1). Associations between strong hypomorphs and breast cancer for Ambry Genetics vs AllofUs were uninformative due to small numbers of women with these hypomorphic variants in the AllofUs study. Neither weak hypomorphs (HDR score 2.0-2.5) nor consensus B/VUS variants from the three functional assays yielded clinically relevant (OR > 2) risks of breast cancer (Fig. 3, Tables 1, 2). Protein-truncating PVs conferred high risks (OR > 4) of breast cancer that were significantly different from the strong hypomorphs and consensus P hypomorphic variants in the Ambry Genetics vs gnomAD v4.1 and Myriad Genetics studies (Fig. 3, Tables 1, 2).

While the use of clinically tested datasets raises the possibility of some inflation of the risk estimates, overall these findings suggest that strong hypomorphs represent a clinically distinct category of moderate risk, reduced penetrance missense variants.

The age of breast cancer onset among carriers of strong hypomorphic variants was also compared to those with DBD PVs in the two clinical studies. The mean age of breast cancer diagnosis was 57 years for carriers of strong hypomorphs compared to 51 years for carriers of DBD PVs from the Ambry Genetics dataset (p = 3 × 10−4) (Supplementary Table 3). Similarly, carriers of strong hypomorphs in the Myriad Genetics study had a significantly older age of breast cancer onset than carriers of DBD truncating variants with similar personal/family cancer history and race and ethnicity (p = 0.018) (Supplementary Table 3).

ClinGen/ACMG/AMP clinical classification of hypomorphic variants

To define the clinical utility of the hypomorphic variants, the point score-based Bayesian interpretation of the ACMG/AMP guidelines from the ClinGen BRCA1/2 VCEP classification framework was utilized37,38 (42). The consensus results for the hypomorphic variants from three orthogonal assays (HDR assay, the endogenous cell viability assay, and the HAP1 MAVE assay) were used to assign an integrated functional category and PS3/BS3 code point score for each variant14. Among the hypomorphic variants, 25 were consensus P strong/moderate, and 22 were consensus B strong/moderate/supporting (Supplementary Data 1, 10). A PS3/BS3 code was not assigned to any variant with discordant functional results. Each hypomorphic variant was assigned points for each VCEP code leading to a summary clinical classification (Supplementary Data 1, 10). Among the hypomorphic variants, 14 (21%) variants, including 10 of 34 strong hypomorphs, were classified as likely pathogenic (LP). In contrast, 18 (27%) variants, all of which were weak hypomorphs, were likely benign (LB). The remaining 35 of 67 (52%) remained as VUS, consistent with reduced functional effects and reduced penetrance (Fig. 4 and Supplementary Data 1, 10).

Comparison with ClinVar classifications

Among the 70 hypomorphic variants, 55 (79%) have been reported in ClinVar by clinical testing laboratories (Supplementary Data 1). These variants account for approximately 3% of all 1817 DBD missense variants39. Of these 55 variants, 42 (76%) have been consistently reported as VUS, whereas the p.G2609D and p.Q2655R strong hypomorphs have been classified as P/LP. However, the p.Q2655R variant has been reported in a Fanconi Anemia (FA) patient carrying compound heterozygous variants in BRCA211, suggesting hypomorphic activity. A further 11 hypomorphs showed conflicting classifications. When considering the ClinGen/ACMG/AMP classifications from the current study (Supplementary Data 10), one of 14 hypomorphic variants designated as LP in the current study was classified as LP in ClinVar, two had conflicting ClinVar results, eight were VUS and three had not been observed. Furthermore, the 18 hypomorphic variants classified as LB in the current study were either discordant, VUS or not observed in ClinVar (Supplementary Data 1). Of 10 hypomorphic variants with discordant classifications in ClinVar (P/LP versus VUS, or B/LB versus VUS), six (60%) were reclassified as LB or LP, and four remained as VUS in the current study (Supplementary Data 1). In addition, among the 42 non-discordant VUS in ClinVar, 18 (43%) were reclassified as LB or LP, with 24 remaining VUS (Supplementary Data 1).

Tumor features associated with hypomorphic variants

Tumor characteristics of 70 BRCA2 hypomorphic variants were compared with those of known pathogenic and benign BRCA2 variants from 8424 pan-cancer clinical tumor samples that were assessed by FoundationOne CDx comprehensive genomic profiling (Foundation Medicine, Inc.) during routine clinical care. Loss of heterozygosity (LOH) was algorithmically assessed in a subset of samples, with “homozygous” variant status indicative of loss of the reference allele. Hypomorphic variants were predicted to have LOH in 36% of tumors, an intermediate frequency between ClinVar-annotated PVs (68%, p < 1 × 10−4) and benign variants (20%, p = 0.00279; Fig. 5a). However, the strong hypomorphic variants were predicted to have LOH in 56% (18/32) of samples, which was compatible with LOH rates for ClinVar PVs (p = 0.185). Furthermore, hypomorphic variants were clonal (present in most tumor cells) in all (5/5) samples with predictions available, a truncal clonality pattern more consistent with ClinVar BRCA2 PVs (88%) than benign variants (57%) (Fig. 5b).

The clinical relevance of hypomorphic variants was further assessed with HRDsig (homologous recombination deficiency signature), a machine learning algorithm that leverages more than 100 copy number-derived features to detect genomic scars associated with HRD in pan-cancer samples40. Predicted heterozygous BRCA2 variants (without LOH) were not associated with HRDsig positivity, irrespective of pathogenicity classifications (Supplementary Fig. 5). Tumors with homozygous ClinVar PVs and P_strong missense variants displayed median HRDsig values (0.9 for both) above the validated threshold of 0.7. In contrast, tumors with homozygous strong hypomorphs (n = 17) and weak hypomorphs (n = 8) were HRDsig positive in 32% (8/25) of samples and displayed median HRDsig scores marginally lower than the HRDsig threshold (0.5 and 0.6, respectively), that were significantly lower than ClinVar PVs (p = 1.18 × 10−6) but significantly higher than ClinVar benign variants (p = 2.35 × 10−6) (Fig. 5c). Interestingly, tumors harboring homozygous P_strong variants and hypomorphic variants were largely found in HRD-associated cancer types, including tubo-ovarian, breast, prostate, and pancreaticobiliary cancers (67% (174/260) and 69% (18/26), respectively) (Fig. 5d). The distributions for homozygous hypomorphic variants were more similar to ClinVar PVs (78%) (p = 0.344) than ClinVar benign variants found in HRD-associated cancers (36%) (p = 0.00134), suggesting that BRCA2 hypomorphic variants drive tumor formation similarly to BRCA2 PVs. Finally, analysis of individually recurrent variants (n > 1 observations, range 2–73 samples) showed that homozygous P_strong alleles were generally associated with HRDsig positivity (67%, 133/198), though there was considerable variability in both homozygosity rates (range 0-100%) and HRDsig positivity rates (range 0–100%) (Fig. 5e). While hypomorphic variants were less frequently recurrent, p.D2723E and p.F2562L were detected as homozygous in multiple samples and had individual samples that were HRDsig positive but intermediate median HRDsig scores of 0.62 and 0.44, respectively (Supplementary Table 4). Thus, across a broad spectrum of somatic genomic features, these observations provide clear clinical relevance for P_strong variants and further demonstrate that many tumors harboring hypomorphic variants with loss of the wild-type allele exhibit HRD-associated phenotypes comparable to BRCA2 PVs, while a subset display an intermediate phenotype.

Discussion

Many highly penetrant PVs in BRCA1 and BRCA2 have been reported. While most are protein truncating variants, several highly penetrant missense variants have also been identified22,24,25,26,41. However, more recently the p.R1699Q variant in BRCA1 was identified as the first clearly defined missense RPPV in BRCA1 or BRCA2 with lifetime risks of breast cancer from 20% to 30%, rather than the 55% to 70% associated with truncating variants42. Furthermore, a small number of non-splice site missense variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 have been identified as possible RPPVs in consensus studies by clinical testing laboratories and in studies of Fanconi Anemia patients. These findings suggested that additional missense variants may exist that partially disrupt activity or stability of BRCA1 or BRCA2 but do not influence RNA splicing. Guidelines for cancer risk counseling and clinical management of individuals with high penetrance PVs in BRCA1/2 are well defined based on evidence collected over many years16,17,18. However, management strategies and clinically meaningful risk thresholds have not yet been established for RPPVs. Thus, further investigation of the existence of hypomorphic variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 associated with reduced penetrance for breast and other cancers are needed. Only with the availability of these data can appropriate clinical management guidelines for women with these variants be developed.

Herein, we report on 70 functionally hypomorphic missense variants in BRCA2 that constitute candidate clinically relevant RPPVs. All of these variants exhibited intermediate or partial activity in a well-validated HDR assay that was previously calibrated to breast cancer risk24,26. These variants were divided into 34 strong (HDR scores 1.5-2.0) and 36 weak hypomorphs (HDR scores 2.0–2.5). As the HDR assay was based on overexpression of mutagenized full-length BRCA2 cDNAs in V-C8 brca2-deficient cells, we further validated the endogenous effects of these variants on cell viability of haploid cells using a CRISPR/cas9 based knock-in approach. Of the 67 assessed hypomorphs, 47 (70%) were intermediate, 5 (8%) were functionally abnormal, and 15 (22%) were functionally normal, based on calibration with protein-truncating PVs and benign synonymous changes. The 52 intermediate and deleterious variants included 31 of the 34 strong hypomorphs. HDR activity and RAD51 foci formation in a series of stable HAP1 cell lines showed strong concordance with these findings and 8 of 10 stable HAP1 cell lines containing strong hypomorphs were hypersensitive to Exatecan. In addition, 49 of the 52 intermediate and abnormal variants conferred sensitivity to the olaparib PARP inhibitor in the endogenously modified HAP1 cells (FDR < 0.05) (Supplementary Data 1). Next, the results from a large MAVE study of almost 7000 BRCA2 DBD SNVs14 were compared to those from the HDR and the targeted endogenous viability assays. The large MAVE study identified 35 of the 70 hypomorphs as P strong, moderate, or supporting. When the results from all assays were combined, a total of 25 variants yielded consistent P strong/moderate functional results and were identified as consensus P hypomorphic variants. Although conservative, this approach of pooling all assays together provided a high degree of confidence that these 25 variants result in hypomorphic, partially reduced BRCA2 activity. It is likely that additional variants within the original 70 also have hypomorphic effects, including the 49 with intermediate and deleterious effects in the endogenous cell viability assay and sensitivity to olaparib. However, further studies will be needed to confirm these candidates.

Importantly, association studies showed that the strong hypomorphs and consensus P hypomorphic variants conferred more moderate risks of breast cancer than established PVs, and were associated with an older mean age of breast cancer onset than DBD protein-truncating variants (Tables 1, 2 and Supplementary Table 3). In contrast, weak hypomorphic variants with HDR scores>2 and in the consensus B/VUS category were not associated with increased risks of breast cancer, suggesting that this subset of hypomorphic variants with a smaller reduction in BRCA2 activity are not clinically relevant in terms of risk of breast cancer. Overall, the 95% confidence intervals for the breast cancer risks in both clinical studies did not overlap with either benign missense variants or pathogenic protein-truncating variants (Fig. 3). Thus, the consensus P hypomorphic variants appear to represent a new category of clinically relevant RPPVs in BRCA2. While observation of a variant in trans with a BRCA2 pathogenic nonsense or frameshift mutation in Fanconi Anemia (FA-D1) patients11,12,13 is also considered evidence of RPPV status, only the p.F2562L and p.R2625S consensus P hypomorphic variants have been detected in a Fanconi Anemia (FA-D1) patient11,12,13.

Integration of the conservative consensus functional assay findings into a ClinGen/ACMG/AMP variant classification model for BRCA2 classified 14 of the consensus P hypomorphic variants as LP. This number represents a substantial increase over the two currently established LP hypomorphic missense variants in ClinVar. This result suggests that the current ClinGen/ACMG classification models can identify RPPVs, but these models cannot distinguish RPPVs from high-penetrance variants. A recent study proposed a classification framework for RPPVs in high-penetrance genes, stratified by evidence of effect size43. However, given the rarity of individual hypomorphic variants in large scale case-control datasets, the implementation of this framework may not further facilitate the classification of variants as RPPVs without consensus and well-validated functional data.

Collectively, the RPPVs may confer estimated lifetime risks of breast cancer >20% (when combining the age adjusted ORs with SEER prevalence data)8, which will qualify RPPV carriers for enhanced screening through breast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) added to mammography, often starting at an age earlier than recommended in the general population. However, RPPV carriers may require less intensive screening and management compared to those with high penetrance PVs. Risk-reducing mastectomy may be recommended for BRCA2 carriers with >30-40% lifetime risk of breast cancer17, but this approach may not be recommended for women with RPPVs, consistent with management of ATM and CHEK2 pathogenic variant carriers, who have lower risks and later average age of breast cancer diagnosis. Given the high risks of ovarian cancer associated with classical BRCA2 PVs, it is possible that BRCA2 RPPVs will confer reduced but still clinically relevant risks of ovarian cancer. If this is confirmed then the current threshold for consideration of risk reducing salpingo-oophorectomy (RRSO) for BRCA2 PV carriers of 5% lifetime risk16 may still be exceeded and RRSO may be recommended for carriers of BRCA2 RPPVs. Thus, further studies are needed to assess the age-related and lifetime risks for ovarian cancer as well as prostate and pancreatic cancers among individuals with BRCA2 RPPVs.

Importantly, tumors harboring BRCA2 strong hypomorphic variants and with corresponding LOH showed an intermediate phenotype based on somatic features associated with HRD. This included significantly lower median HRDsig scores (below the positivity threshold) compared to those containing PVs or P_strong missense variants, but higher median HRDsig scores than tumors with benign BRCA2 variants or wildtype BRCA2 (Fig. 5c, e), suggesting an intermediate somatic HRD phenotype. While a subset (32%) of BRCA2 strong hypomorphic variant tumors with LOH exhibited HRDsig positivity the remainder displayed attenuated or absence of HRD-associated copy number features. This raises the possibility that development of the genomic scar detected by HRDsig is directly linked to levels of BRCA2 activity. Although PARP inhibitors are established targeted therapies for tumors with germline BRCA2 PVs, >90% of which have HRDsig positivity in HRD-associated cancer types44, it is not known whether tumors with BRCA2 RPPVs with and without HRDsig positivity will display sensitivity to these agents, even though the RPPVs confer PARPi sensitivity in HAP1 MAVE studies and to Exatecan in cell lines. Further studies are needed to validate these drug response findings, which have significant implications for management of carriers with cancer.

The helical and OB1 domains which form an interface with DSS1 were enriched for hypomorphic variants (67%) suggesting that these hypomorphs may play important roles in DSS1-mediated stability of the BRCA2 protein and promotion of BRCA2-RAD51 targeting to ssDNA. Interestingly, 10 (14%) hypomorphs, including five strong hypomorphs were located in the tower domain. This report is the first evidence of cancer associated missense variants in the tower domain and suggests that this domain is required for BRCA2 activity but may be tolerant of significant structural changes. Although only a small number of variants showed reduced BRCA2 protein expression compared to wildtype BRCA2 in the overexpression-based HDR analysis, and the stable cell lines showed expression levels similar to wildtype BRCA2, additional biochemical and structural studies will be needed to confirm the absence of effects on protein expression and stability and to elucidate the impact of hypomorphic variants on BRCA2 folding and on the interaction with DSS1 and ssDNA.

There are some limitations to this study. The 70 hypomorphic variants may not fully represent the behavior of all possible hypomorphs in the BRCA2 DBD. Furthermore, the studies reported here focus on the effects of BRCA2 variants on Homologous recombination repair and cell viability. Given the recent identification of a BRCA2 missense separation of function variant that influences replication fork stability and protection but not homologous recombination repair, further studies will be needed to assess the influence of BRCA2 hypomorphic variants on replication forks. Likewise, additional studies will be needed to assess the tissue specific functional effects of the hypomorphic variants since the current studies were performed predominantly in HAP1 cells. In addition, the association analyses involving the Ambry Genetics study compared variant frequencies in high-risk women meeting NCCN guidelines for hereditary cancer testing with frequencies from unaffected participants from two population-based studies. While this approach had the potential to result in overestimated risks of breast cancer, the results were very similar to the findings from the Myriad Genetics study, which yielded estimates of the fold-increase in risk for variant carriers versus non-carriers of the same age, race and ethnicity, and family history. Although results from comparisons of differentially ascertained populations should be interpreted with caution, the independent analyses of variants from both testing laboratories confirmed reduced penetrance for both the strong hypomorphs and the consensus P hypomorphic variants. Furthermore, it was necessary to pool the hypomorphic variants for estimation of cancer risks and assessment of phenotypic characteristics due to the rarity of the variants in the large clinical cohorts. Thus, while the strong hypomorphic variants as a group were associated with reduced penetrance, further studies with larger numbers of individuals are needed to assess the penetrance of the individual variants. Furthermore, 14 strong hypomorphic variants were individually clinically classified as LP/P variants using ClinGen/ACMG BRCA1/2 VCEP specifications. Additional studies using other sources of evidence will be needed to confirm whether the remaining strong hypomorphic variants are also RPPVs.

In summary, 70 hypomorphic missense variants in the BRCA2 DBD were shown to have partial effects on the homologous recombination DNA repair activity of BRCA2. By combining multiple functional assays together 25 consensus pathogenic hypomorphic variants were identified. These hypomorphic variants conferred moderate risks of breast and ovarian cancer and were designated as RPPVs. Tumors with hypomorphic RPPVs had a 37% probability of losing the second BRCA2 allele and a further 50% probability of developing as an HRDsig positive tumor, suggesting that many tumors found to contain BRCA2 RPPVs may not develop as a result of the RPPV and may not exhibit PARPi sensitivity. Importantly, individuals carrying these RPPVs likely meet the risk threshold for high-risk breast cancer screening through MRI for cancer prevention. The identification of a new risk category for inherited variants in this study represents a paradigm shift in this field that will require development of new risk management strategies for carriers of these variants. Overall, these findings provide for more precise personalized risk assessment with potential to avoid and/or delay risk reducing surgery, which is expected to result in better patient care.

Methods

Ethics

This study complies with all relevant ethical regulations and was approved by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board (IRB 21-008216).

Variant selection and annotation

BRCA2 DBD (amino acids 2481–3186) missense variants (n = 535) were selected for HDR functional analysis based on observations in ClinVar, predicted effects by BRCA-ML45 or BayesDel46 in silico models, and other functional studies31,32,33. These included 21 established pathogenic/likely pathogenic (P/LP) and 45 benign/likely benign (B/LB) DBD missense standards. From the 535 variants, 70 candidate partial loss of function, hypomorphic variants with no known or SpliceAI predicted effects on RNA splicing, including 58 variants not previously reported24,were selected for further study. Variants were annotated according to the Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS) nomenclature (RefSeq transcript ID: NM_000059.3) and with predicted scores from in silico models27,46,47 and SpliceAI48 (Supplementary Data 1).

Cell lines and reagents

HAP1 cells (Horizon discovery) were maintained in IMDM (Gibco) and V-C8 BRCA2-deficient cells (a kind gift from Dr. M.Z. Zdzienicka) were cultured in DMEM-F12 (Gibco), supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin at 37 °C in 5% (v/v) CO2. To select haploid cells, 1×107 HAP1 cells (Horizon Discovery) were resuspended in 5 µg/ml Hoechst 34580 (BD, 565877) and sorted at 4 °C. HAP1 cells were transfected using Turbofectin 8.0 according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Origene). Cell lines were not re-authenticated. All oligos and primers were synthesized by Integrated DNA Technologies and listed in Supplementary Data 6. BRCA2 siRNA (4390824) and negative control siRNA (4390843) (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were used in immunofluorescence studies. Anti-BRCA2 (OP95) (Calbiochem) monoclonal, and RAD51 (N1C2) (GeneTex) polyclonal antibodies were used for western blotting and immunofluorescence analysis, respectively.

Homology-directed repair (HDR) assay

BRCA2 variants were incorporated into a 3X FLAG-tagged full-length BRCA2 cDNA expression vector by site-directed mutagenesis24,25,26. Variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing and BRCA2 expression was verified by Western blot (OP95, WB-1:1000)9. iSce1 expression plasmid was co-transfected with BRCA2 variant constructs into 1 × 106 DR-GFP reporter V-C8 brca2-deficient cells. Homologous recombination-dependent repair of iSce1-induced DNA double-strand breaks was quantified by flow cytometry as the proportion of green fluorescent protein (GFP + ) cells after 72 h. HDR activity was normalized and rescaled to a 1 to 5 range defined by the established p.Asp2723His PV and wild-type BRCA2. All variants were evaluated in triplicate experiments from at least two independent clones. The 99% probability of functionally abnormal or normal categories relative to known pathogenic and benign missense standards was estimated by a Bayesian regression model23.

Three-dimensional structural modeling

BRCA2 DBD hypomorphs and missense PVs were mapped on the BRCA2 protein structure using Pymol software and the PDB source file (PDB ID: 1MJE). Three-dimensional structural modeling was based on the crystal structure of the BRCA2-DSS1-ssDNA complex49. The interface residues were defined as those with at least one non-hydrogen atom within 5 Å of the other domains or DSS1. Secondary structures of hypomorphic and pathogenic residues were determined using the DSSP algorithm (version 2.0.4).

Multi-species amino acid sequence conservation

BRCA2 amino acid sequences were curated from Align-GVGD27 and UCSC Comparative Genomics datasets29. Sequence alignments were conducted across ten diverse species between pufferfish (Tetraodon nigroviridis) and homo sapiens (Supplementary Data 3), across 27 primates (Supplementary Data 4), and 35 vertebrates (Supplementary Data 5). Sequence conservation analyses were performed on amino acid residues containing BRCA2 DBD hypomorphs, known pathogenic and benign missense variants.

Stable BRCA2 DBD variant cell lines

HAP1 cells (2 × 105 cells per well) were co-transfected with 4 μg of Cas9/sgRNA construct and 1μg of pUC19 targeting vector encoding individual BRCA2 variants using TurboFectin 8.0 (OriGene). Cells were selected with puromycin (Gibco) for 72 h, seeded as single cells by serial dilution, and expanded for 7 days. Genomic DNA from seeded clones was genotyped for BRCA2 variants by Sanger sequencing. Protein from seeded clones was immunoblotted for BRCA2 expression using anti-BRCA2 (OP95, WB-1:1000) monoclonal antibody. HAP1 stable lines were subjected to the DR-GFP HDR reporter assay. 2 × 105 stable line cells in 6-well plate were transfected with 3.3 μg of iSce1 and 1μg of DR-GFP plasmid and the number of GFP+ cells was quantified by flow cytometry after 48 h.

MTS Assays

HAP1 clones were seeded at 500 cells per well in 96-well plates and quadruplicate wells were treated with diluent (0.1% DMSO) or exatecan mesylate (Cayman Chemical, Ann Arbor, MI) from a final concentration of 625 pM downward in 2-fold dilutions. After a 5-day incubation, viable cell mass was assessed using an MTS assay. Mean absorbance values from replicate drug-treated wells were corrected by subtracting background A490 readings from wells containing only medium and then normalized to diluent-treated wells. Values shown are the mean ± SEM of three independent biological replicates.

Immunofluorescence

HAP1 cell lines stably expressing hypomorphs or silent variants were cultured on coverslips. Silent variant stable lines were used as negative controls following treatment with BRCA2 siRNA or negative control siRNA for 72 h. For RAD51 IRIF staining, the hypomorph and negative controls were exposed to 8 Gy of gamma-irradiation and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Cells were pre-extracted, fixed, permeabilized, and blocked (Supplementary methods). The primary RAD51 (N1C2, IF-1:100) antibody in PBS containing 3% BSA was incubated with cells overnight at 4 °C. Secondary antibody was diluted in PBS with 3% BSA and incubated with cells at room temperature for 1 hr. Cells were washed and incubated with DAPI for 1 min. and were mounted with anti-fade solution and visualized using an ImageXpress Micro Confocal (Molecular Devices). The counting scheme for RAD51 foci was defined with approximate minimum width of 0.4 µm and maximum width of 1.2 µm for punctate foci within nuclei from the images. DAPI staining was used to define the boundaries of nuclei prior to counting the RAD51 foci.

Generation of hypomorph-specific mutagenesis libraries and Cas9/sgRNA plasmids

BRCA2 DBD exons 15–25 containing 70 hypomorphs were selected for endogenous targeting. Exons 18 and 25 were split into N-terminal and C-terminal targeted regions due to large exon size, resulting in 13 target regions. sgRNA-annealed oligonucleotides were ligated into pSpCas9(BB)−2A-Puro (PX459 v.2.0) (Addgene, 62988) following BbsI (New England Biolabs, R0539L) digestion to create a Cas9–sgRNA co-expression construct for each individual SGE. For each SGE, 600 − 1000 bp homologous arms upstream and downstream of the target region were amplified from wild-type HAP1 gDNA and cloned into a BamHI-HF-digested pUC19 vector using a NEBuilder HiFi DNA assembly Cloning kit. Cloned plasmid backbones were subjected to mutagenesis by inverse PCR, using mutagenized codon “NNN” primers for all possible nucleotide changes at selected hypomorph-containing residues50. A protospacer protection edit (PPE) and a 3-nucleotide mutation were introduced into backbone plasmids to prevent re-cutting by Cas9/sgRNA and facilitate specific reamplification of the targeted DNA, respectively14.

CRISPR/Cas9 endogenous targeting viability assay

Multiple sgRNAs for each target region were evaluated and the optimal sgRNAs were selected (Supplementary Data 6). In each experiment, 5 × 106 haploid-sorted HAP1 cells were co-transfected with 4 µg of target-specific hypomorph library and 16 µg of Cas9/sgRNA targeting construct. After selection in puromycin (1 µg/ml) for 3 days cells were harvested at day5 (D5) (24 h after puromycin selection) and day8 (D8) post-transfection. Genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted using the Monarch Genomic DNA Purification Kit (New England Biolabs, T3010L) and target regions were amplified by PCR to add barcodes for multiplexing (Supplementary Data 6). PCR reactions were performed in 50 μl reactions using Q5 High-Fidelity 2X Master Mix (New England Biolabs, M0492L), cleaned and concentrated using Ampure XP beads, and sequenced for 150 cycles on an Illumina MiSeq or NextSeq. Base calls were performed by the instrument control software (Supplementary methods).

Functional read count processing

The log2 ratio between the frequency of D8 and D5 read counts was used to measure the depletion/enrichment effect for each variant in the endogenous cell viability and drug response assays. Variants with under-represented read counts (<10) at D5 were excluded from further analysis. For each variant, the average score was calculated from all non-missing values among replicates. Log2 ratios of variants were linearly scaled within each exon and across exons in replicate experiments relative to median silent and median nonsense SNV values to yield a functional score for each variant for each assay (Supplementary Data 7).

CRISPR/Cas9 drug response assay

An olaparib PARP inhibitor sensitivity assay was performed to evaluate the influence of the DBD variants on drug response. HAP1 cells were transfected and selected with puromycin as in the cell viability assay. Targeted cells were harvested at D5 and split, with half retained for a cell viability assay, and half treated with 5 μM olaparib for 72 h. After 3-days of olaparib treatment, genomic DNA was extracted from harvested cells, PCR amplified and sequenced along with D5 baseline genomic DNA as before14. Sequencing data were processed and functional read counts determined (Supplementary methods, Supplementary Data 8).

Study populations

The study population from Ambry Genetics included female breast cancer “cases” qualifying under NCCN guidelines for hereditary cancer genetic testing by Ambry Genetics between 2012 and 2024. Eligible patients had available clinical phenotype information, were 18 or older, and were negative for PVs in cancer-associated genes other than BRCA2. Public reference populations were females from gnomAD (v.4.1 excluding UK biobank)35 or unaffected females from the AllofUs cohort. Variants with allele frequency (AF) > 0.001 were excluded from the analyses. The analysis of this clinical-testing cohort was deemed exempt from review by the Western Institutional Review Board. The study population from Myriad Genetics was drawn from a consecutive cohort of female patients who underwent hereditary cancer testing with a multigene panel between September 2013 and January 2025. Eligible patients had complete clinical phenotype information, were 18 or older, and were negative for PVs or VUS in cancer-associated genes other than BRCA2. Women from states that prohibit the research use of genetic data were excluded from analysis. The study was conducted according to a protocol approved by the Advarra Institutional Review Board (Pro00036775) with a waiver of informed consent.

Tumor analysis

For tumor genomic analyses, the Foundation Medicine cohort consisted of patients who received FoundationOne CDx comprehensive genomic profiling of tumors during clinical care between January 2017 and October 2025 (n = 8424), with one sample per patient considered51. Cancer type was assigned based on submitted requisition forms, review of pathology reports, histopathological examination, and evaluation of genomic findings. Samples with elevated tumor mutational burden (≥10 mut/Mb) and with additional pathogenic mutations in BRCA2 or other genes associated with homologous recombination deficiency signature (HRDsig) positivity (BRCA1, PALB2, BARD1, RAD51C, and RAD51D) were excluded. Clonality and zygosity of variants were derived based on the variant allele fraction, genome-wide copy number model, and local copy number state52. HRDsig was developed and validated to classify HRD status in solid tumor tissue biopsies using a copy number scar-based machine learning classifier trained on hundreds of copy number features40. This study was approved by the Western IRB (Protocol No. 20152817) with waivers of informed consent and HIPAA authorization.

Statistical analysis

Associations between BRCA2 DBD variants and the risks of breast cancer were performed for Ambry Genetics cases and gnomAD v.4.1 (females excluding UK Biobank)35 by weighted logistic regression adjusted for the relative frequencies of races and ethnicities, and for Ambry Genetics cases and AllofUs36 using weighted logistic regression adjusted for age of cancer onset and relative frequencies of races and ethnicities. Analysis of the Myriad Genetics study involved a multivariable logistic regression method adjusted for age, personal/family cancer history, and race and ethnicity to estimate breast cancer risks as ORs with 95% CIs53,54,55. These adjusted ORs may be interpreted as the fold-increase in risk for a carrier compared to a non-carrier and may be consistent with those obtained from a population-based study53,54,55. Phenotypic comparisons for age of breast cancer onset were conducted by unpaired Student’s t-test for the Ambry Genetics study and by logistic-regression models adjusting for race and ethnicity and personal/family history of breast cancer in the Myriad Genetics study. A Fisher’s Exact test was conducted for the amino-acid sequence conservation analysis, and an unpaired Student’s t-test was used for the HAP1 stable line HDR reporter assay. All analyses were performed with R software (v4.3.2) and all tests were two-sided. Functional data in bar graphs or scatter plots were presented as means from replicate experiments.

ACMG/AMP framework for classification of BRCA2 DBD hypomorphic variants

The ACMG/AMP rule-based framework combines evidence from population, computational and functional and other data. The combination of evidence yields variant classifications of benign (B), likely benign (LB), pathogenic (P), likely pathogenic (LP), and variant of uncertain significance (VUS). In this study, ACMG/AMP scoring rules established by the ClinGen BRCA1/2 Variant Curation Expert Panel (VCEP)41 were used for clinical classification of BRCA2 DBD hypomorphs. Consensus functional results from three functional assays were integrated into the ClinGen/ACMG/AMP BRCA1/2 VCEP classification model under the PS3/BS3 rule (Supplementary Data 1). Detailed ACMG/AMP criteria used in this study can be found in Supplementary methods.
