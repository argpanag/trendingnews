Helen Mirren’s Graduated Bixie Offers A Fresh Take On The Classic Pixie Cut

TheTools · 23.10.2026 11:16 · source · permalink

With its soft layers and asymmetric shape, the graduated bixie is the latest short haircut to know about

Known for her timeless elegance, Dame Helen Mirren has once again made a case for the graduated bixie. Having traded her signature bob for the cut earlier this summer, the actor was recently spotted in New York City showcasing a fresh style while promoting her latest film, A Talent for Murder, at Today and Late Night with Seth Meyers.

Despite undergoing a dramatic hair transformation for the film, which sees her silver tresses replaced with a darker jaw-skimming bob and a side-swept fringe, Mirren has since opted for a new look in the form of a fresh, new take on the pixie.

Enter: the graduated bixie. Like the graduated bob, this style offers the face-framing benefits of a bob with the modern length of a pixie. Coming as a fresh option for those looking to add shape and movement to mature hair, it delivers both versatility and style.

Though the technique originated in the Sixties, it has continued to evolve into a modern classic. 'The cut remains as relevant as ever,' Mark Hayes, International Creative Director at House Of Sassoon, previously told Elle UK, 'You have to cut carefully ensuring the shorter back and the longer, face-framing front work together to enhance bone structure, add volume, and create a dynamic shape,'

It's easy to see why Mirren has leant to the style, and she's not the only one. Previously seen on Hallee Berry, Michelle Yeoh, and Pamela Anderson, to name a few, the graduated bixie lends itself to a more effortless approach to styling while its asymmetric layers offer plenty of scope to experiment with texture and shape.

Proving that shorter styles can be as versatile as their longer counterparts. The cut can be styled sleek and sculpted for a polished finish or tousled for a softer, more relaxed look. The benefit of having longer layers around the face also allows for more styling possibilities, allowing the cut to be swept to the side or styled into a fringe.
