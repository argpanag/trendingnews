Who owns Flydubai?

Flydubai is wholly owned by the Dubai government, which established the airline in 2008 through the Dubai Aviation Corporation.

Operating from Dubai International Airport, the airline serves 131 destinations including to the Middle East, Europe, Africa and Asia.

Flydubai is often considered as the low-cost sister carrier to the United Arab Emirates’ (UAE) Emirates airline. It is a separate entity, although both ultimately share the same owner - the Dubai government, via its sovereign wealth fund, the Investment Corporation of Dubai.

Currently, the airline’s fleet consists of variations of the Boeing 737 aircraft.

The airline has maintained a solid safety record since its establishment. Its only fatal incident was in March 2016, when a flight from Dubai to Rostov-on-Don, Russia, crashed during a landing attempt amid high ground-level wind and poor visibility. All 62 passengers and crew members on board were killed.

Captain and co-pilot taken to hospital with injuries, Saudi aviation operator says

How Dubai became one of Israel's most popular foreign destinations

Dubai - from where the diverted Israel-bound flight was returning - has become one of Israel’s busiest and most frequently served international destinations.

This is thanks to the normalization of relations between Israel and the United Arab Emirates (UAE) in 2020, through the brokered Abraham Accords, which allowed commercial aviation services between the two countries to resume.

Since then, the aviation corridor between Ben Gurion Airport (TLV) and Dubai International Airport (DXB) has seen significant growth, with both Israel- and UAE-based airlines expanding operations to meet the demand for tourism and business travel.

According to flight tracking website FlightRadar24, there are typically 70 flights running between Dubai (DXB) and Tel Aviv (TLV) per week. Flydubai is the main carrier operating the route, in addition to Emirates and Israeli airlines Israir and El Al.

Data from Israel’s Ben Gurion Airport in December showed that 1.1 million Israeli passengers traveled to Dubai in 2025.

Flight emergency brings Israelis to a country that doesn’t recognize their state

The emergency landing of an Israel-bound flight in Saudi Arabia marked a rare mass arrival of Israelis in the kingdom, which has no diplomatic relations with Israel.

Just a few years ago, Israeli airlines and Israel-bound flights were forbidden from even entering Saudi airspace, forcing Israeli holidaymakers to take an hours long detour while traveling east to avoid the vast kingdom.

That changed in 2022, when Riyadh unilaterally allowed Israeli flights to use its airspace as part of a push by the Biden administration to normalize ties between the two countries. It was a milestone for Israeli aviation, significantly shortening routes to Asia, and what allowed Wednesday’s Dubai-Tel Aviv flight to fly over Saudi Arabia.

This wasn’t the first time scores of Israelis unexpectedly landed in the kingdom. In 2023, a Tel Aviv-bound Air Seychelles flight made an emergency landing in the Saudi Red Sea city of Jeddah because of an electrical problem, forcing passengers to stay at an airport hotel. And last year, another Flydubai plane landed in the Saudi capital Riyadh after a passenger suffered a stroke.

Such incidents have been seen as rare signs of Saudi-Israeli cooperation, even as formal normalization remains out of reach.

The Gaza war and its devastating humanitarian toll have hardened Saudi Arabia’s position on recognizing Israel. Riyadh says it will not establish diplomatic relations without the creation of an independent Palestinian state.
