Every Match From a Set Down, Then Jodar Dismantles Medvedev Without One

TheTools · 17.10.2026 17:09 · source · permalink

Share this article

Daniil Medvedev threatened to walk off the court in the middle of his Shanghai Masters semi-final on Saturday, turning a straight-forward first-set dispute into theatre before Rafael Jodar cut through the noise and sealed a 6-3, 6-4 victory to reach his first Masters 1000 final.

The moment that stopped play

The flashpoint came with the first set at 4-2 in Jodar’s favour, 40-40 on the Spaniard’s serve. Jodar’s cross-court forehand hit the line, after which Medvedev’s return sailed long and was called out. The Russian was not disputing his own shot going wide. He wanted the chair umpire, Adel Nour, to show him a replay of Jodar’s forehand, which he believed had landed out. Nour explained that protocol only permitted a review of the final shot of the rally.

Medvedev disagreed with that interpretation, arguing that because his own ball was out, Jodar’s forehand was the last shot that mattered. Rather than move on, he told Nour: “I’m not moving; I can retire, I don’t care. The ATP gives me a fine anyway. I will retire; show me the ball.”

Nour checked and was ultimately allowed to show Medvedev the replay. The moment Jodar’s forehand appeared on the big screen, it showed the ball landing in, exactly as the automated line system had ruled. Medvedev had no case, and everybody on court knew it. The standoff had lasted several minutes, achieved nothing, and left the world number five looking frayed well before the set was done.

Medvedev apologised for the incident, but yet again the 30-year-old found himself at the centre of attention for the wrong reasons. It is a familiar pattern. His confrontation with a linesperson in Beijing earlier this month led to a disqualification. Controversy in China is becoming a recurring feature of his autumn calendar.

Jodar’s quiet authority

Medvedev saved four set points, but Jodar regrouped and took the opening set when the Russian returned long. The Spanish 20-year-old then took a medical timeout before the second set for treatment on a leg and hip issue, yet returned to the court and promptly raced to 2-0.

He broke to go 4-3 up when Medvedev double-faulted, and Medvedev tumbled to the ground trying to save a match point. Medvedev clung on for one more game, but Jodar dropped no more points, sending down a decisive forehand to clinch victory. The entire match lasted one hour and 44 minutes.

The numbers tell their own story. Jodar finished with 21 winners to Medvedev’s 15, converted four of seven break points and won 69 points to Medvedev’s 61. For a player who had entered this tournament ranked 13th, having started the season ranked 168th, the margin of control was striking.

A generational passing of the baton

Jodar, a decade younger than the man he defeated, denied Medvedev a chance to return to the Shanghai final he won in 2019. At 20 years old, Jodar becomes the youngest finalist in Shanghai Masters history. His route to this point has been built on resilience: every one of his four matches at this Masters 1000 event required him to come from a set down, until Saturday when he never needed to.

For Medvedev, the defeat continues a painful trend at the semi-final stage of Masters events. He had entered the Asian swing in strong form, winning in Hangzhou and going 11-1 before Shanghai, and the tournament remained his best realistic route to reinforcing his position in the ATP Finals qualification race. That route is now closed.

Shanghai was Jodar’s fifth ATP Tour semi-final of the season and his second at Masters 1000 level. In the final on Sunday, he faces Alejandro Davidovich Fokina, who eliminated top seed Alexander Zverev 5-7, 6-4, 6-3 in the other semi-final. The Shanghai Masters title will be going to Spain regardless of the result.
