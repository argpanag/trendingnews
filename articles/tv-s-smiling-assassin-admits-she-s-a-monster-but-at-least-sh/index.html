TV’s Smiling Assassin admits she’s a monster but at least she’s a ‘morally complex’ one, she says

Each week, Benjamin Law asks public figures to discuss the subjects we’re told to keep private by getting them to roll a die. The numbers they land on are the topics they’re given. This week, he talks to Mara Lejins. The lawyer, 31, is best known as the Smiling Assassin, one of the trivia experts, on the TV quiz show The Chase Australia.

DEATH

Do you like committing murders in your TV role? [Laughs] Actually, it’s the hardest part of the job. It’s quite sad when you eliminate contestants. Luckily, I’m so insanely competitive that this becomes secondary to wanting to win.

Ah, so you murder their hope as well. Yeah. One contestant was a foster mum caring for a large number of children who wanted to pay for them to go on an interstate holiday. She was salt of the earth, doing one of the most important jobs in society. But you can’t just throw a game …

So you’re not just a murderer – you’re a monster, too. [Laughs] A morally complex murderer.

If you were allowed to get away with murder, who would you kill? That’s an insane question! I don’t want to kill anyone! I don’t think I could carry that mental torment with me.

Have you ever had a near-death experience? I actually have. My dad will freak out reading this because it’s the most traumatising day of his life.

And not yours?! It didn’t bother me! So here’s the back story: my grandfather came from Latvia and worked on the Snowy Hydro, so my dad grew up in the Snowy Mountains. Skiing’s been a really big part of my family’s life. When I was at uni, my dad would often pick me up and we’d spend a day skiing together. We were skiing off-piste one day; there was really heavy snow. All of a sudden, I skied into this valley, did a front flip, opened my eyes … and found myself hanging upside down in a creek hole.

Oh, my god. And I’m below the snow line. One of my arms is pinned, all this snow is falling in and I’m getting suffocated. Luckily, Dad was skiing behind me, not in front of me, and was able to help. But there were several minutes where I didn’t know what was happening and I couldn’t breathe. And I remember thinking, “I’ve had a really nice life.” It makes me less scared, in a way.

Of what? Dying. I wasn’t like, “There’s so much I haven’t done!” I was like, “That’s OK.”

MONEY


You originally appeared as a contestant on The Chase spin-off Beat the Chasers in 2021. That was a crazy week. I’d applied for federal prosecutor’s job. In the morning I got a phone call saying I’d got it; in the afternoon I won 60 grand. I was so happy; my whole life changed.

What did you spend that 60 grand on? Well, I’d read online that if you have a windfall, connect happiness to it. So I bought the laptop that I’m currently using – and a championship ring.

Yeah, you did. Every time I look at that ring I think, “I’m a winner”. But the bulk of my money went to my [tuition] fees at Columbia [Lejins took a leave of absence from her job to complete her master’s of laws in New York]. It meant I could have a great year in the US without having to work.

So I give you a hundred bucks. You have to spend it within the hour on yourself. What are you buying? I’m going to the Colombian cafe near my house and I’m getting four arepas and cassava chips. It’s now my favourite food in the entire world. You know when you discover things later in life and you’re like, “How have I lived my whole life without this?”

RELIGION

Did you grow up religious? Not at all. I went to an Anglican school from kindergarten but a lot of people sending their kids there weren’t religious. The school obviously didn’t want to alienate its customer base!

If not a religion, what do you believe in? In junior school, we had the Golden Rule Award every week at assembly. Obviously, the Golden Rule has a Christian basis: “Do unto others as you would have them do unto you”. Any time I have a complex moral decision to make I remember this. Filming the show, I ask, “How would I want to be treated if I was a contestant? What level of respect would I want?”

Have you ever violated the rule? When I was about eight, I’d play Scrabble with my grandma and her partner. They were quite blind and I discovered that when I put my hand back into the Scrabble tile bag [to pick up more tiles] I could have a look to get the ones I wanted. I was already really good at Scrabble, but now I was smashing them. When my parents discovered that I was cheating, they were horrified.

So you’ve destroyed a foster mother’s dreams and you’ve taken advantage of the blind. Wow. [Laughs] I know, I’m so relatable.

When we see you as the Smiling Assassin – smashing trivia answer after trivia answer – it seems like we’re watching someone who is completely without doubt. Any tips for us about how to achieve that state? People have said to me – for a lot of my life – that I come off as very confident. It’s funny because I’m not a particularly confident person. I’m just a very outgoing, positive, happy person and, somehow, that gives an aura. I think most people undersell themselves. But honestly, I don’t think it’s wrong to have doubt. Doubt is a powerful part of being a human. How horrible would the world be if everyone was so sure of themselves?

Who do you idolise? Dolly Parton.

To which of the seven deadly sins are you most susceptible? Pride.

Where do you find community? My family – and the trivia community of Australia.

Oh, that’s really wholesome. Yeah, I didn’t even even know it existed until I was on the show and I got invited to join this trivia team. They’re the sweetest, kindest people and they just love trivia.

What’s a good trivia question about religion for our readers? OK: What colour shoes are favoured by popes – and why?
