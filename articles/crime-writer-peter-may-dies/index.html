Crime writer Peter May dies

TheTools · 29.09.2026 15:39 · source · permalink

Scottish crime writer Peter May has died at his holiday home in the south of France, his publisher riverrun has announced.

May, whose books have sold around 15 million copies worldwide, died on Friday night. His final novel, Island of the Dead, is due to be published in January 2027.

May began his career as a journalist in Scotland and was named Young Journalist of the Year at 21. He published his first novel, The Reporter, in 1976, aged 26, before moving into television after the book was adapted for the BBC. He subsequently worked as a scriptwriter and producer on Take the High Road and the Gaelic-language drama Machair.

His later fiction included the China Thrillers and the French-set Enzo Files, but it was The Blackhouse, published in 2011, that brought him international success and led to the Lewis Trilogy. His work became closely associated with the Isle of Lewis and the Hebrides.

May published 26 books with Quercus and was particularly successful in France, where he received a number of literary awards, according to his publisher.

Although he had announced his retirement, May completed one final novel, Island of the Dead, in July this year. He had been preparing for a farewell tour of Scotland and Lewis to coincide with its publication in January 2027.

In a statement, riverrun said: “Peter May was not only one of the best writers of crime fiction we had, but he inspired enormous affection in his readers and in those lucky enough to work with him.”

The publisher added: “His kindness, generosity and largeness of spirit, loyalty, engagement with life and the world made him a much-loved, as well as much-admired, figure. He is irreplaceable.”
