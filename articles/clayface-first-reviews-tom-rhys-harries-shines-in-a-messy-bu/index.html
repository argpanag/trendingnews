Clayface First Reviews: Tom Rhys Harries Shines in a Messy but Distinctive DC Horror Story

TheTools · 22.10.2026 15:02 · source · permalink

News

Critics say the body horror and standalone approach give the third DC Universe movie a welcome change of texture, even if it doesn't always deliver.

By Christopher Campbell | Published October 21, 2026

TAGGED AS: DC Comics, movies

The comic book adaptation Clayface releases in theaters this weekend, and the first reviews are now online. Tom Rhys Harries stars as the titular character in the film, which is the third theatrical installment of the DC Universe, following Superman and Supergirl. While aiming for a Halloween-timed body horror entry for the franchise, the movie may succeed as a relatively standalone throwback, but its script and effects could disappoint the majority of its audience.

Starring: Tom Rhys Harries, Naomi Ackie, David Dencik, Max Minghella

Directed By: James Watkins

Here’s what critics are saying about Clayface.

Is it a must-see part of the DC Universe?

In nearly every department, Clayface exceeds expectations — all without getting ideas above its spiritual B-movie station. – Guy Lodge, Variety

Clayface emerges as one of the year’s big surprises. – Frank Scheck, The Hollywood Reporter

The film’s complete lack of identity crisis is going to go a very long way with fans. – Amelia Emberwing, Pickety Witch

Clayface does nothing to expand this corner of the DCU…if you lower your expectations enough, then you might just enjoy it.  – Josh Wilding, ComicBookMovie.com

What’s there is decent enough. – Courtney Howard, Fresh Fiction

It’s at least stronger than the doldrums of the villain-centered films that Sony Pictures has put out over the years. – Joshua Mbonu, Geek Vibes Nation

It’s certainly different, and for comic book movie fans absolutely bored to death by the same old stuff, it’s something of a respite. Yet, divorced from all that, Watkins’ film confounds much more than it compels. – Kate Erbland, IndieWire

Clayface could have been worse, but what’s frustrating is the knowledge that the movie could have been better, too. – Spencer Perry, ComicBook.com

Does it do justice to the title character?

[It’s] a template, like Superman before it, for adapting the comics (and, in this case, Clayface from Batman: The Animated Series) with a sincere dedication to what made audiences fall in love with the characters in their original mediums. – Swara Salih, But Why Tho?

The first (and basically last) scene in which his Clayface-y powers are completely utilized, set in a mob-run nightclub, is a total blast. – Cody Dericks, Awards Watch

How does it compare to other DC Studios titles?

Clayface is considerably more interesting all round, in large part because it doesn’t yet feel bound to any superhero property. – Guy Lodge, Variety

Clayface has a more distinct identity than other DC entries across both universes thus far. – Robert Kojder, Flickering Myth

Neither as woefully awful an adaptation as Supergirl nor as insulting to some comic book readers as Lanterns. – Josh Wilding, ComicBookMovie.com

So much of Clayface lacks the visual language of the best of this universe, giving up on atmosphere and never looking like it takes place anywhere but on a backlot. – Brian Tallerico, RogerEbert.com

Will it remind us of any other movies?

The film…is as indebted to cartoonish ‘90s horror outings like Darkman and Thinner as it is to anything in its own direct franchise history. – Guy Lodge, Variety

The circular staircase and dark, rain-slicked back alleys feel like distant homages to Hitchcock and classic noirs. – Courtney Howard, Fresh Fiction

Clayface delivers the sort of psychological depth and emotional resonance that enable it to bear comparisons to the works of such body horror maestros as David Cronenberg and Sam Raimi. – Frank Scheck, The Hollywood Reporter

Clayface mirrors David Cronenberg’s body-horror classic The Fly, right down to a love affair doomed by melting body parts. – Brian Tallerico, RogerEbert.com

With an emphasis on shock and body horror over action, it’s much closer to The Substance than Superman. – Cody Dericks, Awards Watch

It’s Morbius, but slightly better. – Josh Wilding, ComicBookMovie.com

Does it work as a standalone film?

Tonally and stylistically, this nasty, gnarly body-horror movie is its own ugly animal…in the context of a single, genre-melding romp, they’re fresh and ickily enjoyable. – Guy Lodge, Variety

As the latest entry in a genre that’s been dying for some variety, it’s a relief that the film is so atypically small-scale. – Cody Dericks, Awards Watch

There are plenty of references to other DC Comics characters (heroes and villains), but none are mentioned by name in this DC movie. – Karina Adelgaard, Heaven of Horror

It would be easy to overlook some narrative flaws if the film dropped hints about how it fits into the tapestry of everything else around it, but containment means it feels more disposable. – Spencer Perry, ComicBook.com

Who is the movie really for?

[It’s] unconcerned with pleasing the broadest possible crowd. – Guy Lodge, Variety

I can’t sit here and tell you that Clayface is going to have broad appeal. What I can say is that its refusal to chase that appeal is its greatest triumph. – Amelia Emberwing, Pickety Witch

How are the horror elements?

Abetted by consistently inspired prosthetics work, there’s enough stomach-churning, actually face-melting corporeal silly-putty play here…that will fuel nightmares well into the coming months.  – Guy Lodge, Variety

The gore is intense at times, causing many squeamish moments where audiences will want to cover their faces. Clayface is bloody, it’s gruesome, it’s nothing like you’d expect. – Chalice Williams, Black Girl Nerds

This incarnation blessedly doesn’t skimp on the grotesque gore, bloody brutality and bleak undertones. – Courtney Howard, Fresh Fiction

It wants to make you uncomfortable, and it does! There are scenes in here that make even someone like me balk. That is, perhaps, the greatest compliment that I can offer. – Amelia Emberwing, Pickety Witch

The film saves its most gory, darkly innovative moment of violence for the very end of this set piece, letting Matt do something that I’ve truly never seen any character do in any film before. – Cody Dericks, Awards Watch

It’s never particularly scary, more unnerving…but that doesn’t negate the intense bloodshed or visual gnarliness typically kept away from today’s comic book audiences. – Matt Donato, Daily Dead

As a “baby’s first body horror flick,” Clayface should at the very least suffice. – Joshua Mbonu, Geek Vibes Nation

The dialogue is so atrocious and wooden that it feels almost deliberately bad at times, while the story hits one familiar note after another. – Josh Wilding, ComicBookMovie.com
