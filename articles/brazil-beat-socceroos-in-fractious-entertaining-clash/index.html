Brazil beat Socceroos in fractious, entertaining clash

TheTools · 29.09.2026 08:37 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Socceroos

Topic:Socceroos

Brazil battled back from 2-1 down to win 4-2 in Brisbane. (Getty Images: Matt Roberts)

The Socceroos have fallen to a 4-2 defeat against Brazil in front of 52,423 fans at Lang Park in Brisbane.

Fresh from a superb performance in Townsville on Friday night, the Socceroos will once again take plenty from a contest that ebbed and flowed throughout 90 thoroughly engaging minutes.

With Tony Popovic naming an unchanged team for just the second time in his tenure, it seems likely that this XI is the one that will start the Asian Cup in January.

On the basis of what they showed against the number five team in the world, they should serve Australia very well indeed.

Australia fell behind early to a Brazil team that came out the blocks firing, scoring through a superb Vanderson strike in the opening three minutes.

They could have fallen even further behind when Pat Beach pulled off two sensational saves to twice deny Raphinha just a couple of minutes later.

The Socceroos had barely had control of the ball at that stage, the promised response from a badly burned Brazil team in full evidence.

However, the hosts showed superb composure and self-confidence to hit back, gradually regaining control in the midfield and scoring two goals in the space of four minutes to take the lead mid way through the first half.

Alessandro Circati turned home a corner at the back post, a goal initially ruled out for offside before VAR intervened.

Connor Metcalfe's stunner gave the Socceroos the lead. (Getty Images: Albert Perez)

Then, Connor Metcalf prompted an explosion of joy from the home fans in the stands by drilling home a magnificent strike just minutes later, taking a touch and hammering a left-footed shot into the top corner from the edge of the area.

The pressure was clearly getting to Brazil, with the normally Imperturbable Carlo Ancelotti screaming into the face of the assistant referee after Tete Yengi was fouled.

The visitors drew level on the stroke of half time, first of two penalties awarded to the visitors converted by skipper Raphinha after another lengthy VAR review when the referee inexplicably failed to give a penalty after Harry Souttar made a rough challenge.

Tempers threatened to boil over in the second half when Endrick and Souttar clashed, Aziz Behich coming in a sparking a small melee in front of the Socceroos bench.

That finally sparked Brazil, who turned the style back on as Bruno Guimarães finished a sumptuous move from the left to give the visitors the lead.

Vinícius Júnior was then fouled in the penalty area and converted to seal the victory in front of the biggest football crowd ever at Lang Park.

"Honestly, they [the Socceroos] press really well," Ancelotti said.

"They are really well organised, they surprised us in this sense.

"They put much more pressure in the front and we had difficulty to build from the back.

"They are a very good team, in my opinion."
