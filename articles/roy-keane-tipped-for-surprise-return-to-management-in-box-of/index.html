Roy Keane tipped for surprise return to management in 'box office' appointment

TheTools · 28.09.2026 12:53 · source · permalink
  • Amy Hamerslagh, Apprentice Sports Writer
  • Published: 13:53, 28 Sep 2026
  • Updated: 13:53, 28 Sep 2026

Roy Keane has the potential to be an inspiring appointment as the next Celtic manager, according to his former chairman Niall Quinn.

The Manchester United legend was present for the Old Firm derby prior to the international break to support his old club and friend Martin O'Neill.

The current Celtic boss has come under pressure after a disappointing run with the Hoops which has seen them lose three straight games.

There are question marks over his future after he hinted he could walk away if he feels he can't fix the issues plaguing his side.

The Scottish side lost to Rangers and also suffered a loss in the opening game of their Europa League campaign with a home loss to Ferencvaros.

O'Neill's contract expires at the end of the season and he has the option of a further year, which currently looks unlikely Celtic will extend.

Given his Celtic history and connection to O'Neill, Keane has been linked with the job and the former Ipswich Town boss is not ruling out a managerial return.

It's been 15 years since the 55-year-old was sacked by the Tractor Boys after he lost seven of his final nine league matches.

However, Niall Quinn, who gave Keane his first job in management at Sunderland in 2006, believes he would be a "box office" appointment for Celtic.

Speaking on the Graeme Souness Tackles podcast, Quinn said: "If Roy wanted to put his name in the hat, it would be huge. He hasn’t managed for a while, but you’d be a testament to say that when it’s in you, it’s in you forever.

"I think he’s more or less said that himself, and I certainly wouldn’t object to thinking that would be a hell of a thing to do. I had him myself at Sunderland, and he really got the city going – because he’s box office, the man. There’s no doubt about that."

Quinn questioned whether the Ireland legend would want to return to managing after he has established himself as a respected pundit.

He added: "I just think he looks very happy doing what he does these days, I’ve never seen him smile so much. 

"Going back in at the deep end, and having referees and things go wrong, and matches at the weekend and players… I don’t know if the fire will still be raging in him."

However, Keane has been noted recently telling Micah Richards on Sky Sports that he would be open to a return to the dugout.

He said: "Strangely enough, at weekends when I'm watching games, there are teams I watch and you go, 'I think I could go in there and do a job.'

"But it’s mostly at weekends. It’s not through the week. It’s not Monday morning when I’m having my scrambled egg at the local cafe, just reading a paper.

"All these managers driving up and down the motorways and stresses. But yeah, I do. I think there’s always something there. 

"The pilot light is still on for something. If someone gave me a phone call and told you, you know what? Who knows? It’s going to be unlikely."

The former United captain admitted that when he analyses matches, he looks at it from a 'manager's point of view'.

He also revealed there had been opportunities in recent years that he has turned down to continue with his current broadcasting work. He said: "It's well and good sitting here going, 'Yeah, yeah.'"

"But the reality is then, and I've had opportunities the last few years, but then there's contracts and staff issues and you go, 'Really?' And sometimes you go, 'Stick to what you're doing.'

He has previously turned down the Celtic job in 2014 before Ronny Deila was appointed, with the Irishman claiming that Dermot Desmond told him the job was his if he wanted it.
