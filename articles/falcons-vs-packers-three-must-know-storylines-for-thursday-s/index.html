Falcons vs. Packers: Three must-know storylines for Thursday's Week 3 prime-time game

TheTools · 25.09.2026 01:15 · source · permalink
  • WHERE: Lambeau Field (Green Bay, Wis.)
  • WHEN: Thursday, 8:15 p.m. ET
  • WAYS TO WATCH: Prime Video, NFL+

Stream live local and prime-time games on mobile with NFL+ .

Thursday night’s battle features two teams yearning for a spark to break out of early-season doldrums. The Packers are playing at home for the first time in 2026 after narrowly avoiding an 0-2 start with a comeback win over the Jets in overtime. Meanwhile, the 0-2 Falcons are still searching for some positive vibes after being trounced in their home opener by the Panthers, 34-3.

Green Bay has a chance to make history on Thursday. With a win, the Packers will break the NFL record for consecutive victories in home openers (currently tied at 13 with the 1976-1988 Miami Dolphins). The Falcons have dropped four straight at Lambeau Field, with their last win in Titletown coming back when the team's current president of football, Matt Ryan, was a rookie quarterback (2008).

Yes, we’re still early in the season, but as far as Week 3 games go, the stakes couldn’t be much higher for either club.

Three must-know storylines

1) Can the Packers clean it up? It’s been an uncharacteristically sloppy start for a team with postseason aspirations after making the playoffs in six of the past seven seasons.

Matt LaFleur’s crew leads the league in penalties and penalty yards. It ranks last in rushing yards. Also, Jordan Love has the lowest completion rate of any quarterback with at least 30 pass attempts (52.1%). The only QB over the last 10 seasons with a lower rate in his first two games of a season (min. 70 attempts) was Trevor Lawrence in the first two games of his career (50%, 2021), per NFL Research.

Yikes.

Obviously, the Packers have been playing shorthanded without edge terror Micah Parsons (PUP list) and bell-cow back Josh Jacobs (Commissioner’s Exempt List), but that doesn’t excuse the penalty issues. Green Bay's not getting any better news on the injury front, either, after losing starting right tackle Zach Bako-Bewele (torn patellar tendon) for the season and wideout Jayden Reed to a back ailment in the win over the Jets.

Maybe the Packers are catching the right opponent at the right time. The Falcons have their own health woes, with CB1 A.J. Terrell (groin) joining an ever-growing list of starting defensive players on the sideline. There’s no way to fix everything with a short week to prepare, but playing a cleaner game -- with Love completing 60-plus percent of his throws -- would be a sign of progress for Green Bay.

2) Is Michael Penix Jr. the hero the Falcons need? Penix is set to make his 2026 debut on Thursday, marking his first game back since tearing his left ACL last November. It’s a very welcome development for a team that averaged a league-low eight points and failed to reach the red zone with journeyman Cooper Rush starting the first two contests.

Penix was cleared for full participation in practice about a month ago, so he's had time to gear up for his return to game action. It’s a huge season for the eighth overall pick of the 2024 NFL Draft, with Atlanta's new brass facing a decision on whether to exercise the fifth-year option in his rookie deal next spring. He compiled a record of just 4-8 as a starter in his first two seasons, completing less than 60 percent of his passes and throwing for fewer than 200 yards per game.

Thursday will give Penix a chance to begin rewriting the narrative around his career, and the Packers are only one game removed from giving up 39 points to a Vikings offense that no one is confusing for a juggernaut. Penix might want to avoid falling into his habit of holding on to the ball for too long, though, going against the team with the third-highest pressure rate in the league (45.6%). Atlanta certainly has stockpiled an enviable collection of weapons at the skill positions, with RB Bijan Robinson, WR Drake London and TE Kyle Pitts all capable of performing at an All-Pro level, which leads me to my next point.

3) Will the Falcons utilize their pass catchers? Atlanta’s wide receivers and tight ends have combined for 17 catches, 173 receiving yards and zero touchdowns in 2026, all the fewest among the league’s WR/TE units through Week 2.

The aforementioned Pitts has only one catch for 15 yards, and he’s been targeted just four times. That’s a problem Atlanta offensive coordinator Tommy Rees acknowledged this week, telling reporters he has to do a better job of showcasing Pitts, who signed a contract extension this offseason that made him one of the highest-paid tight ends in the league. Perhaps the quarterback change will help in that regard, but Pitts has just two touchdown catches in the 12 games Penix has started.

Penix has the arm strength to give the offense the downfield passing game it lacked in the first couple weeks. Now it’s up to the coaching staff to exploit a Packers defense that ranks 28th in red-zone TD percentage allowed (85.7%).
