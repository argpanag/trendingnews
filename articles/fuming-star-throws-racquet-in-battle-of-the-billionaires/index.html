Shelton sending down thunderbolts

We’re still on-serve in this first set, but Ben Shelton just sent down the fastest serve recorded at the tournament so far – a 239.7km/h thunderbolt.

It wasn't an ace, but as close to it. Alcaraz kind of just stuck the racquet out in the hope it would get back over the net, but it wasn't to be.

The time between the ball coming off Shelton’s racquet to hitting Alcaraz’ was just under nine tenths of a second. Crazy.

Third seed’s win overshadowed by bizarre comments

Jessica Pegula is through to the semi-finals with a 3-6, 6-4, 6-3 win over Emma Navarro.

It is fair to say Mary Carillo did not enjoy commentating it though.

“After this match I’m going to burn my clothes,” the American tennis Hall of Famer bizarrely said after watching an error.

“Look, I have nothing but respect for both of them. They’re very bright, and they’re very hard workers, all of that. This has been a tough match.”

Carillo then walked her comments back, sort of.

“It could be the lateness of the hour that’s getting to me and knowing I have to call the next match too and come back and call the women’s match. You know what, never mind, I’m fine.”

Tiafoe dreaming bigger in latest semi-final berth

Frances Tiafoe has higher hopes for himself in his latest US Open run, after a nerve-testing five-set comeback to reach the semi-final for a third time and underscore his mettle at the year’s final major.

The 11th seed has provided treasured memories for the home fans in New York, from his dragon-slaying defeat of Rafa Nadal en route to the 2022 semi-final, to his blockbuster win over compatriot Ben Shelton two years ago.

But the Cincinnati runner-up this year is ready to write a new chapter this time around, after a roller-coaster comeback performance against Alex Michelsen over five sets.

"I'm just a different person in general. Those two runs (to the semi-finals) were great, something I will cherish forever, but I'm not that person who I was when I made one in 2022 and when I made one in '24. I'm not that person. I'm excited to see what this me does," said Tiafoe, who plays either Shelton or defending champion Carlos Alcaraz next.

"I have higher hopes for myself. So yeah, it's great. The tournament is still going. There's a lot more tennis to be played, a lot more sets to be played, and a lot more physical things to be done. We're going to enjoy this, but we got to keep going."

Frances Tiafoe of the United States reacts. Getty Images
