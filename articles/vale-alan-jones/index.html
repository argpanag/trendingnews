Vale Alan Jones

TheTools · 05.11.2026 05:19 · source · permalink

Australian motorsport has farewelled one of its most influential figures, with former Formula 1 World Champion Alan Jones passing away on 5 November at the age of 80, following a short illness.

Jones, who celebrated his 80th birthday earlier this week, secured his place in sporting history in 1980 when he became the second Australian to win the Formula 1 Drivers’ World Championship, following the achievements of Sir Jack Brabham.

Motorsport Australia President Andrew Fraser described Jones as a titanic figure in the Australian motorsport landscape.

“Alan Jones was one of the most significant figures in Australian motorsport history, whose achievements on the world stage brought immense pride to our nation,” Fraser said.

“Alan’s place in the history of Australian motorsport is assured, having become the second Australian to win the Formula 1 World Championship in 1980.

“With 12 Grand Prix victories and a career that spanned the highest levels of international and domestic competition, his was a record built on determination and a fierce and fearless competitive resolve.

“Alan was a singular character, formidable on and off the race-track.

“His contribution to our sport will be remembered for generations, and we extend our deepest sympathies to his family and loved ones.”

Born in Melbourne on 2 November 1946, Jones inherited his passion for racing from his father, Stan, before heading to Europe to pursue his ambitions in international motorsport.

After years of determination and perseverance, he made his Formula 1 debut with Hesketh in 1975.

His breakthrough arrived two years later with Shadow, claiming his maiden Grand Prix victory at the Austrian Grand Prix before joining Williams in 1978.

It was with Williams that Jones enjoyed the defining period of his career, helping establish the team as a major force in Formula 1.

Four victories in 1979 laid the foundations for an extraordinary 1980 campaign, during which five Grand Prix wins secured the championship and delivered Williams its first Drivers’ World Championship.

Jones added two more victories in 1981 before initially stepping away from Formula 1 at the end of that season. He later made brief returns with Arrows and Haas, with his final Grand Prix appearance coming in Adelaide in 1986.

Motorsport Australia Chief Executive Officer Josh Blanksby recognised Jones’ life has touched countless motorsport fans – many of which will have fond memories of watching him race either in person or on television.

“Alan’s passing is a great loss for Australian motorsport and for the many people whose lives he touched throughout his remarkable career,” Blanksby said.

“Generations of fans will remember the excitement, passion and pride he brought to the sport, both on and off the track.

“Our deepest condolences go to Amanda, his family and all those who had the privilege of knowing and watching him.”

His contribution to motorsport extended well beyond Formula 1, with Jones maintaining a strong presence in Australian competition throughout the following decades.

He competed at the Bathurst 1000 on multiple occasions, claimed the 1982 Australian Sports Sedan and GT Championship with a remarkable clean sweep of all nine rounds in a Porsche 935, and won the 1993 Bathurst 12 Hour in a Mazda RX-7.

He also finished runner-up in the 1995 Bathurst 1000 alongside Allan Grice.

Following his retirement from competitive racing in 2002, Jones remained closely connected to the sport through television commentary and various roles within international motorsport.

His achievements were recognised with an MBE in 1980, induction into the Sport Australia Hall of Fame in 1985 and the Australian Motor Sport Hall of Fame in 2016. He was subsequently elevated to Legend status in 2019.

Alan is survived by his wife, Amanda, and his children, Christian, Amber, Zara, Jack, Camilla and Emma.
