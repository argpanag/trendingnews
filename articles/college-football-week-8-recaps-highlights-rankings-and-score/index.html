Signing off for the night 👋

We’re signing off for the night, but here’s a recap of what was supposed to be a pretty laid back Saturday.

For the first time since 2007 an AP Top-2 team has lost against an unranked opponent for three straight weeks.

We saw five (yes, you read that right) unranked over ranked wins in Week 8, topping Week 6’s four and becoming the most upset-packed weekend thus far.

  • No. 2 BYU fell 39-31 in double overtime to UCF
  • No. 8 Texas Tech lost 37-24 to Cincinnati
  • No. 11 Tennessee lost to South Carolina 24-27, No. 15 Iowa fell to Minnesota 13-10 — both on a walk-off field goal
  • No. 16 Southern Cal was upset by Wisconsin, 24-7

Week 8 saw three undefeated teams fall, leaving No. 1 Miami, No. 9 UCLA and No. 10 Utah left with flawless records. 

It’s the first time in the Division 1-A and Division 1-AA era that started in 1978 (now known as FBS and FCS) that three or fewer undefeated FBS teams remain through Oct. 24.  

The night is young, though.

No. 10 Utah is hosting No. 14 Houston in an after-hours special, giving the Cougars a shot at taking down Week 8’s fourth undefeated team. The Cougars lead 3-0 going into the half.

No. 19 Texas keeps playoff hopes alive, defeats No. 7 Ole Miss

As expected, this Top 25 matchup came down to the absolute wire, but the Longhorns came out victorious, avoiding their third-straight loss after falling to Oklahoma and Florida the past two weeks. 

Texas survived Ole Miss 34-27, crossing its fingers in the final minutes. 

On a recovered fumble as the clock wound down, Ole Miss made a desperate attempt to stay away from adding a second loss to its record.

Trinidad Chambliss had six incompletions in the Rebels’ final drive, but managed to pick up first downs while controlling the clock when Ole Miss needed it most. 

The Rebels were dealt a flag for an illegal shift, pushing them back to the Longhorns’ 29 yard line with two seconds left on the clock, forcing a Chambliss Hail Mary which was intercepted in the endzone as the clock hit zero. 

No. 5 Georgia and No. 12 Kentucky are now the only SEC teams with a single conference loss.

Wisconsin upsets No. 16 Southern Cal

FIVE Top 25 teams have now fallen to unranked opponents in Week 8 — the 16th ranked Trojans being the latest victim. 

USC’s defense was no match for Wisconsin’s relentless run game during the Badgers’ 24-7 upset in Madison. 

The Trojans’ lone touchdown is the least amount of points a Lincoln Riley team has scored during his career as a head coach.

Sebastian Cheeks had four solo tackles and 2.5 sacks, spearheading the Badgers’ consistent pressure on USC’s signal caller and backfield.

Utah aims to salvage nightmare Saturday in Big 12

College GameDay's matchup is finally kicking off in Salt Lake City as No. 10 Utah hosts No. 14 Houston in a Big 12 showdown out West. 

But to be frank, the Big 12 has made a big mess today, and the conference's chances at sending multiple teams to the 12-team dance took a massive blow today. 

The conference entered Week 8's Saturday with three of the FBS's five remaining undefeated teams — No. 2 BYU, No. 8 Texas Tech and No. 10 Utah. The Big 12 had done everything right through Week 7, pursuing the hope of sending multiple teams to the CFP for the first time. 

However, after the Red Raiders fell to Cincinnati and the Cougars to UCF, the multi-bid dream just got a whole lot harder to imagine. BYU and Texas Tech each only have one more ranked opponent on their schedules — No. 10 Utah for BYU and No. 25 Arizona for TTU. 

Worse case scenario (for the conference), No. 10 Utah loses to No. 14 Houston tonight. With no undefeated teams left, the Utes and BYU's Week 10 matchup could very well act as a CFP eliminator for the losing side. 

BYU holds a marquee win over former-No. 2 Notre Dame, but if the Cougars fall again, they will have to somewhat rely on the Irish to beat or at least test No. 1 Miami in Week 10 to salvage that resume win from Week 7. 

As for the Red Raiders, their best and only ranked win is over No. 14 Houston in Week 3, and last year's CFP No. 4 seed has little to no room for error in it's final five matchups. 

There's obviously a lot of ball left to play, and the way this season is going, we could see a record amount of two and three-loss playoff teams come December.

But just as the SEC has cannibalized itself the past three weeks, the Big 12 ate itself up today and the result could haunt them heading into Halloweekend.

UCF stuns No. 2 BYU in double OT

The AP's Top 2 teams have a target on their back this season, and it seems to be a bit too large. For the third consecutive week, one of the nation's top two ranked squads has fallen — to an unranked opponent. 

No. 1 Texas fell to Oklahoma in Week 6, No. 1 Georgia fell to Auburn in Week 7 and now No. 2 BYU has been slain by the UCF Knights, 39-31 in double overtime. 

The win marks the Knights first over an AP Top 5 team in program history.

We'll start near the end of regulation. 

With 15 seconds left on the clock, a personal foul on BYU gave UCF a fresh set of downs on the Cougars 28-yard line. Head coach Scott Frost kept his offense on the field, and Knights' quarterback Keyone Jenkins oddly spiked the ball in a miscommunication.

Frost had seen enough following another quick incompletion, and UCF set up for a 46-yard attempt — kicker Will Stone’s career long. But the kick was met by Cougar hands who blocked the potential game-winner. 

It was only third down, so technically BYU had another down after recovering the blocked kick, but Stone’s helmet came off in the play, forcing a 10 second run off that forced overtime. 

BYU struck quick in the first overtime as Bear Bachmeier found Legend Glasker for a 28-yard touchdown on just the third play. But UCF matched the Cougars on its first play with a strike to Josh Derry.

No. 2 BYU in OT, TWO top-15 teams fall to walk-off field goals 

Okay, wow.

You should be able to settle your TVs on BYU vs. UCF now as the Knights take the nation's No. 2 team to overtime. 

But in case you couldn't flip the channel around fast enough or your multiview is too small, let's recap the other two exhilarating endings in around 10 minutes of real time. 

We almost saw UCF walk off the Cougars, but while that late-game chaos unfolded, No. 11 Tennessee and No. 15 Iowa actually did fall to the dreaded walk-off kick. 

Minnesota 13, No. 15 Iowa 10

Ball control — that’s the name of the game. And Iowa just couldn’t hold onto the rock. 

Four different Hawkeyes fumbled the football, losing just one of them to the Minnesota defense in their 10-13 loss to the Golden Gophers. 

In addition to their pressure on Iowa’s ball carriers, Minnesota’s secondary was a force to be reckoned with.

Matt Kingsbury nabbed an interception off a tipped pass from the Gophers’ quarterback, Hank Brown.

Iowa could’ve had an extra shot on offense after a near-interception from Jacob Wallace, but the call was reversed and ruled incomplete, giving the ball back to Minnesota. 

On the final drive of the game, the Gophers’ kicker Luke Drzewiecki drilled a 46-yard walk-off field goal to seal the deal.

South Carolina 27, No. 11 Tennessee 24 

Another crazy ending happened in Columbia, South Carolina, with the Gamecocks walking it off with a 48-yard field goal from Malik Orsan.

LaNorris Sellers wasn't going to settle for overtime, let alone a loss, driving the South Carolina offense into field goal territory on a desperate last-minute effort.

No. 1 Miami cruises, No. 6 Indiana survives to start day

Though No. 8 Texas Tech fell in an upset, the other ranked teams all picked up wins. Some easier than others.

  • Playing as No. 1 for the first time since 2002, Miami (Fla.) routed Pitt 52-23. Heisman hopefuls Darian Mensah (387 passing yards, 4 TDs) and Malachi Toney (16 catches, 194 yards, 3 touchdowns) dominated.
  • No. 6 Indiana won at Michigan for the first time since 1967, getting by with a 13-6 OT win. The Wolverines out-gained IU 224 to 212, but Hoosiers QB Josh Hoover's 4-yard score in OT was the only TD on the day.
  • A big first half carried No. 18 LSU to a 38-17 win against Auburn. LSU led 31-10 at the half with QB Sam Leavitt totaling four touchdowns and nearing 400 passing yards.
  • A huge second half for No. 23 SMU meant a 59-20 win against Cal. The Mustangs outscored the Golden Bears 42-0 after halftime with QB Kevin Jennings breaking the program's career record for touchdowns. He had three TD passes on the day, giving him 74 in his career.
  • Northwestern QB Aidan Chiles had five touchdowns (three passing, two rushing) for the No. 24 Wildcats in an easy 42-14 win against Rutgers.
  • In non-top 25 land, FAU and Rice combined for 100 points in a 51-49 FAU win. Rice QB Gael Ochoa rushed for 259 yards and scored five total touchdowns and FAU QB Caden Veltkamp passed for 339 yards. FAU won despite allowing 608 yards.

UPSET: Cincinnati beats No. 8 Texas Tech

And then there were four. Cincinnati has wiped out Texas Tech from the undefeated list. 

Cincinnati rolled the No. 8 Red Raiders, winning 37-24 behind a big day from QB JC French IV and RB Cole Tabb. French recovered from a pick-six to finish with 172 passing yards, 46 rushing yards and four touchdowns. It's the Bearcats' first win against a top-10 team since beating No. 9 Notre Dame 24-13 on Oct. 2, 2021. It's the program's first top-10 win at home since defeating No. 7 Rutgers 30-11 on Nov. 18, 2006.

Tabb picked up 173 yards and iced the game with a 58-yard run in the fourth quarter — the first rushing touchdown allowed by Texas Tech all season.

Checking in as early games hit halftime

We'll see if today's games end up anywhere close to as wild and upset-heavy as last week, but at least one top-10 team is already on major upset alert.

Cincinnati is rolling at home against undefeated No. 8 Texas Tech, leading 24-6 at the half. QB JC French IV has three touchdown passes, while Red Raiders QB Will Hammond has struggled (82 yards, 1 INT).

New No. 1 Miami has had a much easier time. The Hurricanes are up 31-7 at the half against Pitt thanks to Malachi Toney's 12 catches for 159 yards and two touchdowns. Darian Mensah has 274 yards and three touchdowns on 22 of 26 passing. Also in the ACC, No. 23 SMU is trailing Cal 20-17.

Over in the Big Ten, No. 6 Indiana has relied on its defense to take a 6-0 halftime lead at Michigan. Josh Hoover has only 60 passing yards for the Hoosiers, whose 22-game win streak ended last week against Ohio State. The Hoosiers' defense allowed only 93 yards in the first half to the Wolverines. Also in the league, No. 24 Northwestern is making it look easy against Rutgers, up 28-6. The Wildcats posted 311 yards before the break, averaging 8.7 yards per carry.

In the SEC, Sam Leavitt's strong first half has No. 18 LSU up 31-10 on Auburn. Leavitt has four total touchdowns, with WR Jackson Haris his favorite target (139 yards, 2 TD catches).
