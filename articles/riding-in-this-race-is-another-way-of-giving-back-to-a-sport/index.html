‘Riding in this race is another way of giving back to a sport that has given me so much’ says broadcaster and Racing to School Ambassador Leonna Mayor as she prepares for Autism in Racing’s fundraising race later this month. – Racing to School

TheTools · 16.10.2026 17:20 · source · permalink

Former jockey-turned-broadcaster and Racing to School Ambassador Leonna Mayor talks to us about prepping for an Autism in Racing fundraising race at Nottingham on 28th October. She joins eight other riders for the 1m2f race, raising funds to support autism-friendly racedays, mentoring opportunities and community activities.

A cause close to home

I hadn’t really considered riding in a fundraising race until Bobby Beevers from Autism in Racing asked me to take part. We’ve worked together on and off over the years, and he knew my younger sister has autism. It felt like the right time to do something for a cause that has personally affected my family for the last decade.

My sister is now 17, and I see the daily challenges she faces with interactions and crowded spaces. Autism-friendly racedays are ideal for her and so many others who have similar experiences. Autism in Racing works with racecourses to offer support and access to quiet and sensory spaces, helping people with autism and their families enjoy a day out together.

Back in the stirrups – real and mechanical

My preparation has included riding out on Gun Carriage and Battlefield Boy at Dylan Cunha’s yard in Newmarket. For the race, Dylan is kindly letting me ride one of his stable stars, Asgard’s Captain, a six-year-old gelding whose successes include last year’s Great Metropolitan Handicap at Epsom and this summer’s Shergar Cup Challenge at Ascot. I’m an Ambassador with the charity Racing to School and the team has kindly loaned me a mechanical horse, to help with my training.

 

Leonna fitness training at home on one of Racing to School’s equicisers

Although I still ride, I haven’t ridden professionally as a jockey since 2013, so returning to a racing saddle and short stirrups has certainly been a shock to the system! I’d forgotten how much cardio fitness you need, as well as strength in your legs and core to keep your balance. I’m doing as much as I can around raising my young son Teddy and working three jobs – for ITV Racing, Sky and William Hill – and with my own horse and dog thrown in for good measure!

In the saddle with competition horse, Pascal

I’m really excited to be back in a race – right down to returning to the weighing room with the people I used to race ride against. Riding in this race is another way of giving back to a sport that has given me so much. Alongside raising funds, it’s a chance to raise awareness of autism and highlight the work Autism in Racing does to help more people access and enjoy the sport.

Leonna and trainer Jamie Osborne pictured after a winning ride as a professional jockey

I’ve already passed my £3,000 fundraising target, but I’ll continue asking for support as training continues and the day gets closer.

Inspiring the next generation through Racing to School

I want to help raise awareness of everything racing offers to anyone who wants to be involved. My role as a Racing to School Ambassador gives me the chance to support young people discovering the sport.

The charity celebrates its 25th anniversary this year, providing free, curriculum-linked learning days at racecourses, training yards and stud farms across Britain. I want to help young people discover what racing offers while they are open to new ideas and beginning to think about different career paths.

Leonna joins in with Bedford Drive Primary pupils from Birkenhead for a Racing to School day at Aintree Racecourse

I recently joined Racing to School at Derby College to meet equine students and talk to them about the many career opportunities within the sport. I enjoy spending time with young people, answering their questions and giving them a feel for a range of jobs they may not have considered. There is plenty of fun to be had on a Racing to School day,  with Equicisers, VR headsets and jockey kit giving the groups a hands-on introduction to racing.

The charity will engage 18,500 young people in 2026, and inevitably some of these may need further support to access the sport again through initiatives such as Autism in Racing and the existing racecourse sensory rooms. It’s great that racing is offering so much support in this way, even though people don’t always realise what’s on offer. Above all, we need to bring more of the next generation into racing, and if I can help, it’s something I want to do.

You can support Leonna in her race through her GoFundMe page.
