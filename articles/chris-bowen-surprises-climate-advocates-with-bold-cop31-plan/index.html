Chris Bowen surprises climate advocates with bold COP31 plan

TheTools · 16.11.2026 22:56 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Climate Change

Topic:Climate Change

Chris Bowen has emphasised a draft COP31 document "won't be the final draft". (Reuters: Umit Bektas)

In short:

Australian Climate Change Minister Chris Bowen has released the draft of an ambitious climate agreement at the COP31 summit.

The document was based on submissions from 160 countries and detailed 144 provisions.

They include calling for an immediate transition from fossil fuels and for developed nations to target net zero by 2030.

An ambitious draft climate agreement released at the COP31 climate summit in Türkiye has called for countries to accelerate the phase-out of fossil fuels.

The document argues for a tripling of climate adaptation projects to at least $US120 billion ($178 billion) by 2035 and for 35 per cent of the world's energy use to go electric.

"Overall, this is some of the strongest language we've seen in a draft COP decision," Greenpeace Australia Pacific COP31 lead Simon Bradshaw said.

"The question now is how much of it will survive the tough days of negotiations ahead."

About 200 countries are discussing environmental targets at the COP31 conference in Türkiye. (Reuters: Murad Sezer)

The comprehensive document arrives at the start of the second week of the annual climate talks, where almost 200 nations will discuss environmental targets, including electrification and emissions reductions and how to implement them.

Australian Climate Change Minister and COP31 president of negotiations Chris Bowen released the 18-page draft in Antalya on Monday, local time, but warned it would not be the final version of the document.

"It is very much to enable consideration over the next week," Mr Bowen said at the press conference.

"There are elements in this draft that not all parties will agree on.

"What we'll be concentrating on in coming days are those issues where people can find common ground. Because the world wants us to act on climate change."

The 18-page document was based on submissions from 160 countries and more than 500 pages of feedback, Mr Bowen said, and detailed 144 provisions.

It expresses "deep concern that global warming is expected to exceed 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels" and reaffirms the need to accelerate action to minimise the overshoot, noting climate change was already harming human health through disease and heat stress.

It also calls for developed countries to take the lead on climate action, and to achieve net zero emissions by 2030 and net-negative emissions by 2040.

'We cannot afford false solutions'

In a move that appears to have surprised climate advocates, the draft cover decision also calls for "accelerated action to phase out fossil fuels, including through the immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems".

Andreas Sieber, the head of political strategy at 350.org, said the credibility of both Türkiye and Australia was now on the line.

"The draft puts COP31's defining battles squarely on the table: the energy transition and adaptation finance," Mr Sieber said.

But he said the wording stopped short of establishing a clear road map to end fossil fuels that moved beyond the "diplomatic waiting room".

"After the biggest fossil fuel crisis in history, we cannot afford false solutions," Mr Sieber said.

"You cannot claim to lead the world's climate negotiations while refusing to lead on the transition away from fossil fuels."

Australia's fossil fuel record under scrutiny

The Australian government has come under fire for its record on fossil fuel expansions during the climate talks, as it continues to be one of the world's leading coal exporters.

At least 37 coal, oil and gas projects have been approved since 2022.

Mr Bowen has previously defended the country's record on exports, telling The Guardian before the climate talks that Australia was selling fossil fuels to countries that were "in the middle of their transition".

"And they need, in the meantime, to use fossil fuels, and they've got to get them from somewhere," Mr Bowen said.

Activists and members of the Greenpeace COP31 delegation took to the streets of Antalya. (Supplied: Greenpeace/Marie Jacquemin)

But the public pressure is escalating.

More than 60 civil society groups released an open letter on Monday, before the draft was announced, to Mr Bowen calling on Australia to use its role as the leader of negotiations to "lift ambition" at the climate talks.

Representatives from the 64 groups, including 350.org, Oxfam and Amnesty International, said "while the world heaves under the strain of escalating climate impacts", they had seen "little sign of the leadership needed to meet the moment".

"As the world's third-largest fossil fuel exporter, Australia's silence can be read as lack of will. We ask you to set out a clear path for the road maps in week two. This is critical for keeping the 1.5C limit alive," the open letter read.

Electrification pledge

Other proposals in the draft cover decision include increasing electricity's share of global energy use to 35 per cent by 2035, known as the 35-by-35 pledge, boosting climate adaptation funding, and expanding carbon capture and cutting methane emissions.

The document also noted the strain geopolitical tensions were causing across global energy markets and the role electrification could play to address it.

"Such tensions have contributed to heightened uncertainty and volatility in global energy markets and fossil fuel supply chains, with implications for energy security, access and affordability," the draft states.

It noted that increasing the role of electricity in global energy systems would have "significant benefits to emission reductions, energy security, energy efficiency, economic development and energy access".

The wording was welcomed by climate think tank E3G program lead Natasha Green.

"We welcome the positive language on clean electrification and the transition away from fossil fuels in the draft cover decision," she said.

"And look forward to the presidency showing more clearly how finance can be a critical enabler of 35-by-35."

Greenpeace activists unfurl underwater banners. (Supplied: Greenpeace/Marie Jacquemin)

Representatives were expected to deliver strong feedback on some proposals, including an accelerated fossil fuel phase-out, Mr Bowen said, but the draft needed to be ambitious.

"We need to show that the world is keeping up climate action," he said.

"We need to send a message to the boardrooms and living rooms: boardrooms to keep investing, living rooms to give hope that countries of the world are going to keep on tackling the climate crisis, which is still here and getting worse."
