Crypto Bill Stalls, Smart Money Calls: Time to Buy the Dip?

TheTools · 19.09.2026 04:28 · source · permalink

The latest political friction in Washington has reignited market debate over whether the digital asset sector has stalled or opened an entry point. When the U.S. Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act in a narrow 49-to-50 procedural vote, short-term traders rushed to sell anything tied to digital assets. Bitcoin quickly slipped below $76,000 to test key support near $75,000, pulling digital asset equities downward across the board.

But the legislative setback does not erase the industry's underlying progress. Clearer federal rules would provide an undeniable long-term tailwind, yet modern financial rails rarely wait on congressional calendars. When stock prices detach from the progress of the businesses behind them, investors have a reason to look more closely.

When Politics Causes Panics, Price Floors Appear

The legislative roadblock centered on a procedural cloture vote, which required 60 votes to end debate and advance the CLARITY Act toward final passage. It received 49 votes, leaving the legislation stalled for now.

The market reaction was immediate. Crypto exchanges, companies holding digital assets, and payment-related stocks all moved lower.

Market pullbacks driven purely by headline politics often create temporary mispricings. Failing to pass a market structure bill does not mean a regulatory ban, nor does it create new restrictions. Instead, it leaves existing frameworks in place. Regulators and business leaders continue operating under established precedents, including Securities and Exchange Commission rules that facilitate tokenized asset contracts.

While speculative traders exited their positions, Bitcoin (BTC) held firm around $75,000. This suggests genuine structural demand beneath the market. Rather than a sign of fundamental breakdown, this price action resembles a standard policy-driven shakeout, transferring equity from leveraged retail accounts into the hands of long-term investors.

Code Trumps Congress: Layer 1 Networks Launch Anyway

While lawmakers debated in Washington, companies continued rolling out new blockchain products. On the day of the Senate vote, Circle Internet Group, Inc. (NYSE: CRCL) rolled out the public mainnet for Arc, an institutional Layer 1 blockchain engineered specifically for corporate finance, sub-second settlement, and automated commerce.

This launch demonstrates how blockchain networks are expanding regardless of congressional votes. Circle oversees USD Coin (USDC), which maintains an active circulation of more than $74 billion.

By integrating this liquidity directly into the Arc transaction layer, Circle is transforming stablecoins from passive trading collateral into active corporate settlement instruments. The company also partnered with Visa (NYSE: V) to fund a $10 million investment round in Velocity, a corporate treasury platform focused on cross-border payments.

Trading platforms are advancing their distribution channels with equal urgency. Coinbase Global, Inc. (NASDAQ: COIN) expanded its footprint by joining the Cashtag Partner Program on X. This integration embeds live trading functionality directly in social media feeds, lowering customer acquisition expenses and giving users direct access to trading tools without requiring them to navigate third-party apps.

These technical milestones show enterprise builders aren't waiting on Capitol Hill. Digital asset rails are actively solving real-world settlement challenges today.

Book Value Bargains: Finding Value in the Sell-off

The market pullback has opened rare opportunities across companies tied to the digital asset space.

Strategy Inc. (NASDAQ: MSTR) saw its share price fall below $125, leaving the stock trading at a price-to-book ratio of about 0.89.

The company carries a book value of about $141 per share, backed by a corporate treasury holding approximately 846,000 Bitcoins.

When an operating business trades at an outright discount to its underlying asset value, public markets are pricing in an extreme policy discount.

Wall Street maintains a Moderate Buy consensus on Strategy, with an average price target near $239. That projection implies an upside potential of 85% as asset values realign with historical norms.

Coinbase presents another notable valuation gap. Shares trade near $163, despite the company generating about $7.18 billion in annual revenue and roughly $1.71 billion in trailing operating cash flow. Analyst consensus currently stands at a Hold, with an average price target near $220, suggesting about 31% upside.

Circle trades near $79 per share, with annual revenue of about $2.75 billion and net profit margins hovering near 15.5%. Analysts track an average target price near $100, indicating roughly 25% upside from current price levels.

Short sellers have expanded their bets across these equities, driving short interest metrics into elevated territory for both Coinbase and Strategy. When bearish sentiment becomes heavily crowded during a successful support test, even modest positive news can spark a swift and volatile short squeeze.

Smart Money Accumulates While Washington Argues

Filing records show that large institutional investors are taking the opposing side of retail selling. Institutional pension funds have steadily increased their exposure during this dip. The California State Teachers' Retirement System maintains ownership across all three companies, while the Arizona State Retirement System recently acquired more than 42,000 shares of Circle. The Virginia Retirement Systems also accumulated over 38,000 shares.

Those holdings show that public pension funds continue to maintain exposure to digital asset companies despite the recent political and market uncertainty.

Prudent investors should weigh risks alongside these opportunities:

  • Volatility remains significant, as Coinbase carries a beta of about 3.4, while Strategy carries a beta near 3.6.

  • High beta levels mean equity fluctuations can outpace spot crypto swings during corrections.

  • Operating results can vary quarterly, as shown by Coinbase, which recorded a revenue contraction of roughly 18.5% year-over-year (YOY) in its latest quarter.

  • Macroeconomic developments, including future interest rate decisions, could influence broader tech and financial equity multiples.
