Purdue names former fighter pilot, University of Arkansas chief as president

TheTools · 05.11.2026 21:24 · source · permalink

WEST LAFAYETTE, IN — Purdue University trustees announced their decision to hire a retired military fighter pilot and currrent president of the University of Arkansas to become Purdue's 14th president.

Jay Silveria will replace Mung Chiang, Purdue's 13th president, who became president of Northwestern University as of July 1, when former President Mitch Daniels took the reins temporarily.

The trustees voted unanimously in a special meeting Thursday afternoon on campus to hire Silveria, whose contract will begin Feb. 1 through June 2032 with a base annual salary of $1 million and bonuses for measurable goals, as with Daniels and Chiang. Those goals include such items as fee affordability, student success, reputation and research excellence. The board can increase his compensation at its discretion.

Daniels will remain in Hovde Hall until Silveria begins on Feb. 1.

Who is Jay Silveria?

The 63-year-old Houston native retired from a 35-year career in the U.S. Air Force in 2020. Until 2025, Silveria served as the executive director of the Washington, D.C., branch of Texas A&M University's Bush School of Government and Public Service.

Silveria left his post in the nation's capital to be president of the University of Arkansas in January 2025. In March, Silveria made statewide headlines after he chose to fire a tenured professor over her social media posts that criticized Israel's attacks on Palestine, despite her successful appeal to the U.S. Faculty Committee on Appointment, Promotion and Tenure.

In a statement addressing the termination, Silveria said he was concerned that the professor's employment could affect the university's funding, citing the Arkansas law Act 721 of 2025, requiring public schools and universities to monitor and report complaints of antisemitism and encouraging the legislature to consider reducing funding if instances of antisemitism were not adequately addressed.

Despite appealing to the University of Arkansas System Board of Trustees in May, the board upheld her termination.

In 2017, Silveria made headlines for a speech he gave to the U.S. Air Force Academy's preparatory school after racial slurs were written on the doors of black students. A veteran fighter pilot, Silveria had become head of the Air Force Academy in Colorado Springs not long before.

"That kind of behavior has no place at the prep school, it has no place at USAFA, and it has no place in the United States Air Force," Silveria said in a video shared online. "You should be outraged not only as an airman, but as a human being."

Silveria earned his bachelor's degree in science from the U.S. Air Force Academy in 1985, according to his bio from the University of Arkansas, completing his undergraduate pilot training in 1986. Silveria earned a master’s degree in social science from Syracuse University and master’s degrees from Air University at Maxwell Air Force Base, Alabama, and the National War College at Fort Lesley J. McNair in Washington, D.C.

Silveria was also a senior executive fellow at Harvard University and attended the Harvard Seminar for New Presidents at the Harvard Graduate School of Education.

At the beginning of his term as president of the University of Arkansas, Silveria said the university system had long been a "beacon of opportunity and progress for our state."

According to university records, Chiang's tenure as Purdue's president was the shortest in Purdue history second only to Abram Shortridge, who left after 18 months in 1874.
