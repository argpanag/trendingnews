Fighter jet buzzes central Pa. during plane intercept near Camp David

TheTools · 20.09.2026 10:57 · source · permalink
  • A NORAD F-16 fighter jet intercepted a civilian aircraft that violated a temporary flight restriction near Camp David.
  • The fighter jet deployed flares to get the pilot's attention before escorting the aircraft out of the restricted area.
  • The low-flying jet caused a stir on social media across central Pennsylvania on Saturday morning.

A low-flying fighter jet over central Pennsylvania on Saturday morning intercepted a civilian aircraft near Camp David, according to the North American Air Defense Command (NORAD).

NORAD command said an F-16 fighter jet intercepted a civilian aircraft near Camp David in Thurmont, Maryland, around 7:50 a.m. on Sept. 19.

During the interception of the small aircraft, the F-16 deployed flares to gain the pilot's attention, according to NORAD.

"The flares were used to draw attention from or communicate with the pilot. Flares are employed with the highest regard for safety, burn out quickly and completely, and pose no danger to people on the ground," NORAD's statement read.

The aircraft was safely escorted out of the area, according to NORAD.

Social media across central Pennsylvania, and particularly Adams County, was abuzz following the low-level flight of the fighter jet, which drew significant public attention.

Temporary Flight Restrictions (TFR) were in place in the airspace surrounding Camp David in Thurmont while President Donald Trump spent the weekend at the presidential retreat.

According to the FAA, the TFR was instituted at 7:59 p.m. on Friday, Sept. 18, and remains in place until at least 10 a.m. on Sunday, Sept. 20.

No general aviation aircraft are permitted within 10 miles of Camp David during the flight restriction, while all aircraft between 10 miles and 30 miles of Camp David are required to be in contact with air traffic control and squawking a discrete code at all times during their flight.

The 10-to-30-mile radius, as shown in the TFR, extends from Thurmont and covers an area that includes Gettysburg, Hanover, Spring Grove, Chambersburg, and Shippensburg in Pennsylvania, along with Westminster, Frederick, Hagerstown, and Germantown in Maryland.

According to NORAD, pilots of any civilian aircraft should respond to an interception by speaking on frequency 121.5 or 243.0, and should immediately turn around to reverse course "until receiving additional instructions on one of those frequencies."
