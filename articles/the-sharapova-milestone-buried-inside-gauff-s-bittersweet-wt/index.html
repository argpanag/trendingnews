The Sharapova milestone buried inside Gauff's bittersweet WTA Finals exit

TheTools · 14.11.2026 05:05 · source · permalink

Share this article

Coco Gauff earned her 40th career win over a top-10 player by beating Jessica Pegula on the opening day of the 2026 WTA Finals in Indian Wells. She became the youngest woman to reach that mark since Maria Sharapova in 2009.

But her season ended in disappointment on Thursday. Despite winning two of her three group matches, Gauff missed out on the semi-finals.

Sharapova was 22, had three Slams and was already established

Sharapova had already won three Grand Slams and spent time ranked No. 1 when she collected that 40th win. For her, the number was confirmation of a completed champion. Gauff turned 21 in March. She has one Grand Slam title, the 2023 US Open, and one WTA Finals crown from 2024, and she is still accumulating.

Seven of those 40 victories came in 2026 alone, in a season that included the Cincinnati title, finals in Miami and Rome, and semi-final appearances at both Wimbledon and the US Open. Roughly half of her entire top-10 haul arrived before her 20th birthday, a pace that suggests the final count will reach well beyond where Sharapova eventually stopped.

“No Grand Slam, I could have done better,” Gauff told the WTA as she reflected on her 2026 season. Yet she finished with a 49-15 record, made her fourth straight WTA Finals appearance and reached 40 career wins over top-10 players. Those numbers show how much she has achieved, even in a year that fell short of her own high standards.

Two wins from three, and still on the plane home

Gauff beat Pegula on day one, then recovered from 1-5 down in the second set against Marta Kostyuk to win 6-4, 7-5, taking the last six games in a row. Two wins from three in a field of eight qualifiers is not a failure by any measure. The problem was Linda Noskova.

The Wimbledon champion won her other two group matches with a set percentage that left Gauff third when the three-way tiebreak was applied. Gauff finished 2-1 in Group 1 at the 2026 WTA Finals but was eliminated on sets percentage, with Noskova topping the group and Jessica Pegula taking the second semi-final place. It was the second consecutive WTA Finals in which Gauff has been eliminated despite winning multiple group matches, a persistent frustration at an event she won as recently as 2024.

She is historically consistent against the best players on the planet. Converting that into Slam titles remains the work still ahead. The Australian Open in January is her next chance to close the gap.
