Nasdaq slides 2% Friday to post worst week since June, pressured by AI sell-off and oil prices: Live updates

TheTools · 15.10.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Stocks slid on Friday as traders notched a mostly losing week amid a shift away from tech and artificial intelligence-related names as well as a rise in oil prices.

The S&P 500 lost 1.09% and ended at 7,659.44, while the Nasdaq Composite shed 2.14% to close at 26,140.03. Both indexes ended the session below their 50-day moving averages. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 175.28 points, or 0.34%, to 52,002.41. The sell-off sent the Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) above 22 for the first time in four months.

Chipmakers were lower again. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) lost 3%, led by declines in Micron Technology, Advanced Micro Devices and Sandisk. Semiconductors have struggled this week amid jitters about competition from China. The SMH slid 10% week to date for its biggest weekly decline since April 2025.

Those declines, coupled with drops in other stocks linked to AI, dragged the S&P 500 to a losing week. The benchmark index fell about 2% this week, while the Nasdaq shed more than 4% for its worst week since June. The Dow, on the other hand, advanced 0.7% on the week.

Investors have also grown concerned that tech companies are borrowing too much for the AI buildout. Shares of neoclouds like CoreWeave, which has seen its total debt rise to more than $51 billion, were down nearly 12%, bringing the stock's week-to-date losses to 25%.

"The question is: Is this a systemic issue?" said Michael Rosen, Angeles' chief investment officer. "Obviously, increased debt issuance is something to observe and continue to watch and be concerned about if it continues to get to levels of excessiveness. But we're not remotely close to that right now."

Other areas of the market such as energy saw gains Friday, with stocks like Chevron helping to keep the Dow's losses in check. Oil prices moved higher amid rising tensions between the U.S. and Iran as well as between Russian and Ukraine. West Texas Intermediate crude futures increased 3.51% to settle at $101.41 per barrel, while Brent crude futures advanced 3.72% to $109.86.

Simultaneously, investors have been grappling with hotter-than-expected inflation data, which has been exacerbated by elevated energy prices in the wake of the U.S.-Iran war. Wholesale inflation this week saw its biggest gain since 2022, while consumer inflation remained well above the Federal Reserve's target of 2%.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh spoke at an IMF event in Bangkok overnight. And while he didn't comment on whether the Fed would raise rates before year-end, traders took that as a sign that the central bank will hold policy steady later this month. The CME Group's FedWatch tool points to an 82% chance the Fed keeps its overnight rate unchanged at its Oct. 27-28 meeting.

The three leading U.S. indexes ended Friday's session with losses.

The S&P 500 dropped 1.09% to finish at 7,659.44, while the Nasdaq Composite lost 2.14% to 26,140.03. The Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 175.28 points, or 0.34%, to 52,002.41.

— Sean Conlon

As the market sell-off intensified Friday afternoon, the Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) rose to levels it last closed at four months ago.

The VIX rose to 21.32, as of 3:10 p.m., rising more than 4.8 points on the session. It's on pace for its highest close since June 10, when it closed at 22.22.

Typically, when the VIX is above 20 that indicates greater fear in the market. The index last closed above that level on July 29, when markets fell after the Federal Reserve kept interest rates steady at its meeting that month. The next day, stocks rose, and the day marked a bottom in a market where many AI-related stocks had been selling off in the previous month.

— Davis Giangiulio

Wells Fargo Investment Institute isn't buying longer-term bonds yet, despite the move lower in Treasury yields.

The firm doesn't believe investors are still adequately compensated for the risks associated with extending duration, which is a measure of interest rate sensitivity.

"Financial conditions are tightening, long-term borrowing costs face upward pressure, and investors are increasingly demanding additional compensation to hold longer-maturity bonds," said Darrell Cronk, president of Wells Fargo Investment Institute and chief investment officer of Wealth & Investment Management.

"Currently, we do not believe we have reached the peak in interest rates for this cycle, so caution is warranted."

Shares of renewable energy firm Nordex jumped nearly 14% in early deals after the company raised its earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) margin guidance for full-year 2026 to 11.5% to 12.5% from 8% to 11%.

In a pre-results release, the Hamburg-based wind turbine maker also said it expected third-quarter earnings of 278 million euros ($312.3 million), up from 136 million euros the previous year and ahead of consensus expectations.

Nordex said performance was driven by higher margins in its services segment, strong project execution and previously flagged risks to its projects not being as severe as expected.

Citi analysts said the update should have a "positive knock-on effect on 2027 consensus estimates and beyond."

"Nordex's stock is, in our view, priced for outer-year earnings risk tied to the German outlook. While we think bears may stick with this view into Germany's 2027 wind auctions, this update means that the earnings starting point is higher, making the outer-year decline implied by the current valuation even steeper," they said in a Friday note.

Government borrowing costs around the world were slightly lower on Friday, as the recent global sell-off continues to cool.

The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield fell 10 basis points on Thursday, taking its weekly decline to around 15 basis points, according to LSEG data. It was around 1 basis point lower on Friday. Yields move inversely to prices.

German, French and U.K. yields were also marginally in the red. The 10-year yields on their bonds have all fallen between 7 and 8 basis points this week.

Investor nerves over governments' mounting debt piles and a lack of fiscal consolidation have driven many yields to the highest level in decades, but the sell-off has eased amid lower oil prices and following a strong U.S. Treasury auction.
