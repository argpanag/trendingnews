    20:10 BST 17 October

    FT: Newcastle 0-1 Aston Villa

    Newcastle United

    Newcastle boss Matthias Jaissle, speaking to Sky Sports after their defeat: "It was in fact a game where we were dominating, overseeing a team giving a response, we made many situations where you saw we were about to win that game until we conceded the goal.

    "We need to be honest - we were shaking a bit until half-time but we showed again a good response. Second 45 minutes, really aggressive from our lads. We won the duels, unfortunately we were not smart enough in the final decision-making in the opposition box so that is something we need to work on. We need to threaten the opposition much more when we have the ball in the final third. We had the occasions but in the end, the lucky punch was missing."

    On what he told his players at half-time: "Belief, purely belief because the first 30 minutes was really good, until we conceded the goal actually. It's a bit typical for a young squad really, emotion-wise a bit shaky with ups and downs during a match and we need to learn to find the reset straight away and consistently believe in the things we were doing before. I cannot say anything against the attitude but the goal is missing."

    On Sven Botman's and Joelinton's first-half challenges and yellow cards: "Honestly, it was really fast, both actions. So I was in the beginning sure there was no foul from Joelinton but it went so fast. The decision-making was clear from the referee and I think they checked even the foul from Sven but I didn't see it right now."

    On improving defensively: "I think in both areas, right? Because today we didn't score. Defensive-wise; I think the structure and discipline was good but not all the time and in the Premier League against a strong opponent one occasion is enough to concede a goal and that was too easy - losing a man in the box, we need to put pressure on the cross. Again, we need to be more efficient offensive-wise."

    On whether Dan Burn is close to returning to his role at centre-back after being played up front late in the match: "I think you're smart enough to know this was a tactical approach for the additional time. We went all in and in that occasion, I'm aware he is a centre-back so don't doubt that.

    "He is getting the physical back which is really important for him. He was quite long out but he is getting better and better physical wise. Hopefully soon we can see him again in his natural position."

Fulham celebrate first Premier League win of the season

Fulham boss Alvaro Arbeloa says securing the club's first Premier League victory of the season before a crunch period of games for them was "massive".

The Cottagers had been winless in their previous six league matches but secured all three points against Hull City thanks to Alex Iwobi's 33rd-minute strike.

The result offered respite for head coach Arbeloa, who succeeded Marco Silva in the Craven Cottage hot seat in July but had not seen his side's promising displays translate into wins.

The result lifted Fulham's points tally to five and moved them off the foot of the table and out of the relegation zone, while Hull sit ninth.

"It's a massive game for us," Arbeloa told BBC Match Of The Day.

He pointed out key upcoming games for Fulham - at Coventry next Saturday, then an EFL Cup tie against Crystal Palace on the following Wednesday - and described that as "a massive week".

"It won't be easy, but we know how we need to play and compete. We have to be competitive in this league," Arbeloa added. "We have our way and hope we can win many games."

Arbeloa's side took the lead just after the half-hour mark in the 100th meeting between the sides in all competitions.

Hull twice failed to clear their lines, allowing Sander Berge to slide the ball into the path of Iwobi on the edge of the box.

Iwobi curled a low shot that took a deflection off Lucas Herrington and squirmed under Hull goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, who perhaps should have done better.

Hull struggled to create much of note, with Mohamed Belloumi dragging a shot wide of the post in the second half with arguably their best effort.

The only negative for Fulham was the dismissal of Ryan Sessegnon in added time after his studs caught the back of Robinio Vaz's calf from behind.

Arbeloa said his side had been "working so hard" and "deserved" to break their duck.

"We were having the same feeling as other matches, when we were controlling the game for long spells and having good chances but didn't score," he said.

"Our rivals punish our mistakes or take their chances, but we were brilliant for 90 minutes. We controlled the game, had many chances and didn't concede any clear chances to Hull."

Fulham analysis: Possession pays off

Arbeloa praises 'brilliant' Fulham after first league victory

Will this prove to be a turning point for Arbeloa?

There was hardly mutiny on the banks of the Thames, despite Fulham's meagre return of just two points from their first six games before this contest.

Yet defeat to a newly promoted side would have certainly caused a degree of twitchiness among fans and the club's hierarchy.

Arbeloa had urged his side to stick to their methods and they patiently weaved neat passages of intricate passing play.

Iwobi was at the heart of it all, the experienced head pulling the strings in midfield, while Oscar Bobb, Shea Charles and Josh King provided youthful vitality.

Once again Fulham had such a monopoly on possession at Craven Cottage (76.2%) that they may as well have put all their houses and hotels on it.

This time, however, they did pass Go and collected their reward - three points.

They made 717 passes in this game, their highest total in a Premier League match this season.

In truth, the margin of victory should have been greater - Garcia headed against the bar, while Tzolakis dealt with clear cut-chances for Iwobi and King.

This result should provide the springboard for the Cottagers and drive belief in Arbeloa's philosophy which, according to Iwobi, his players are fully on board with.

"He's a great man, a great manager. He's always talking to us," the Nigeria international told BBC Final Score.

"Because he was playing recently he understands what we're going through. The players are trying to give 100% to help him out."

Hull analysis: Honeymoon over for shot-shy Tigers?

Jakirovic reacts to Hull City's defeat to Fulham in the Premier League

This was an afternoon that is unlikely to linger long in the minds of the Hull fans venturing back on the East Coast Main Line or via the M1.

The Tigers failed to register a single shot on target during the entire contest.

Not one.

It was the first time they failed to do so in a Premier League game since they played Crystal Palace in May 2017.

They spent most of the game attempting to win the ball back, but Fulham's backline proved able to handle things whenever the Tigers went direct.

Chances were few and far between as a result.

Coach Sergej Jakirovic's face was etched with thunder when Belloumi lifted a late opportunity from a free-kick high into the stands.

At least his side managed to hang on, always within touching distance, in a game when really the home side should have been out of sight.

That might bode well for Hull in the long run, even if Fulham goalkeeper Bernd Leno collected the easiest clean-sheet bonus of his career.

The worry for Jakirovic would be that this insipid performance seeps into their next few games.

Hull will not want the honeymoon period of their top-flight return to be over just yet.
