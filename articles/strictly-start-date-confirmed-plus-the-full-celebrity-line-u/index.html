Strictly start date confirmed, plus the full celebrity line-up

TheTools · 08.09.2026 21:41 · source · permalink

A new trio of hosts. A fresh, even shinier logo. And 15 more celebrities. What could go wrong?

This year’s Strictly Come Dancing is returning on Saturday 19 September, and there are changes afoot. In addition to brand-new hosts Emma Willis, Josh Widdicombe and Johannes Radebe, the BBC has revealed that the dancers will be paired in a different way, and the launch show will feature “more dancing than ever before”. 

Alongside a minute-long trailer, it has been announced that each celebrity contestant will learn who their pro partner is in front of the studio audience via three specially choreographed group dance routines.

The 2026 series will be under serious scrutiny, not just due to the scandals that have hit the show in recent years, but because of a (mildly) controversial new hosting line-up. Everything you need to know about the 24th series of Strictly Come Dancing is below.

When will Strictly start?

It’s official. Strictly will return on Saturday 19 September on BBC One at 7.25pm.

Which celebrities are on the show this year?

Best known for playing omelette-obsessed Gwen in Gavin & Stacey, Melanie Walters has been acting since the Eighties, with appearances in shows including Coronation Street, Beyond Paradise, Being Human, Hollyoaks, Doc Martin and, more recently, Death Valley.

“Performing in Pantomime for the last 15 years, my Strictly ‘fix’ has always been watching in a dressing room rather than my front room,” says Walters. “This year I am completely thrilled and happy to swap my fairy wand for sequinned dance shoes. I just hope I don’t follow Cinderella and leave one on the ballroom stairs. I cannot wait to take part in this absolutely iconic show, it’s so exciting. See you in September!”

One of the UK’s biggest sports content creators, John Nellis has a combined audience of 20 million across his social media channels. His football-themed videos have attracted high-profile guests including Lionel Messi, Kylian Mbappé, Erling Haaland and Cristiano Ronaldo. Originally from County Down, he worked as an air traffic controller before launching his social media career.

“I’m still in disbelief that I’ve been selected for Strictly and, if I’m honest, I’m a little nervous as I’ve never danced before!” said Nellis. “It’s such an incredible opportunity, and I’m going to throw myself into every moment. I can’t wait to meet everyone, learn a new skill and, hopefully, be much better at family parties by the end of it!”

The 25-year-old Londoner is the second gold-medallist to join the line-up, along with Sarah Storey (see below). Tabby Stoecker is a Team GB skeleton athlete who triumphed in the mixed team event in the 2026 Milano-Cortina Games. Growing up in Highgate, she spent her teenage years training in flying trapeze before being identified from 10,000 applicants to try skeleton at a Team GB recruitment event during her A-levels.

“I am over the moon to be joining the cast of Strictly 2026,” said Stoecker. “I’ve had the wildest year of my life so far, so to be rounding it off with a childhood dream of dancing in the Strictly ballroom is surreal and I can’t wait to get stuck in!”

Glaswegian actor Lawrence Robb joined Emmerdale in 2020, playing Mackenzie “Mack” Boyd, a member of the Dingle family. He’s also appeared in Legally Blonde: The Musical and Mojo in the West End. “I don’t know who’s more excited about this, me or my Mother,” the 34-year-old said. “I can’t quite believe that I am going to be getting my dancing shoes on this September on one of my favourite shows of all time!”

Channel 5 viewers will recognise Graeme Hall as the channel’s resident dog-tamer. He’s hosted Dogs Behaving (Very) Badly for almost a decade, in which he helps owners of unruly pets regain control of their homes. But will he be as strict about his own training?

“I have always loved dancing, so to have the opportunity to learn from the best in the business is a dream come true,” he said. “I have watched the show for years, so to be a part of it feels like a very exciting challenge, albeit more than a bit nerve-wracking! I can’t wait to get started.”

Football star and pundit Shaun Wright-Phillips has 36 caps for England in a career that saw him play for Manchester City, Chelsea and Queens Park Rangers among other teams. The son of Ian Wright (himself a footballing legend), Wright-Phillips is now a pundit who can be heard on BBC, Sky Sports and ITV.

“Dancing is in my blood – but mainly at family events!” says Wright-Phillips. “Strictly is definitely outside my comfort zone, but I’m super excited to get started and really looking forward to the challenge!”

Best-known, since 2022, for playing Lady Baela Targaryen in HBO’s Game of Thrones prequel, House of the Dragon, Bethany Antonia is the ninth celebrity announced for the 2026 series. Appearing on screen since she was 14, her breakout role was in the 2020 teen thriller Get Even, and she’s also appeared in Nolly, Stath Lets Flats and Doctor Who.

Antonia says: “I can’t quite believe that I will be heading to the Strictly ballroom this September! I have grown up watching Strictly, and I am so excited to take the challenge on and (hopefully!) learn a new skill. See you on the dancefloor!”

Daughter of Ray Winstone, Jaime Winstone began acting in 2004, and has notched up several screen roles, including Made in Dagenham, Tomb Raider, Dead Set, Torville & Dean and Mad Dogs. She’s arguably best known for playing Barbara Windsor in the BBC biopic Babs, and for then portraying a younger version of Windsor’s iconic soap character Peggy Mitchell in an EastEnders flashback episode to celebrate the soap’s 40th.

“I am so excited to announce that I will be joining the Strictly Come Dancing cast this year,” says Winstone. “I am no stranger to a dance floor, but the ballroom is a whole new world for me. I love a challenge though, so can’t wait to enter my showgirl era and get super sparkly!! Let’s get physical – see you on the dance floor!”

With 19 gold medals, 48-year-old Sarah Storey is Great Britain’s most successful ever Paralympian. Born in Manchester without a functional left hand, at age 14 she began her career as a swimmer and took part in four Paralympic Games, winning 10 golds, before switching to cycling in 2008. She only retired this year, yet the training sessions will continue – but now they’ll be in a dance studio.

“Embarking on a new challenge so soon after stepping down as an athlete is hugely exciting,” said Storey. “I have always loved watching Strictly, and my daughter Louisa is a very talented dancer, so to be able to learn from the best and hopefully understand even more about her world is incredibly motivating! I can’t wait to get started!”

The 41-year-old is a TV presenter and co-helms the Hits Radio breakfast show. The son of politician and House of Lords crossbencher Richard Best, Will has hosted TV shows such as Dance Dance Dance, Love Shaft, Suck My Pop and, most recently, the revival of Big Brother alongside Strictly alumnus AJ Odudu. He also recently won Celebrity Mastermind with the specialist subject of Danny Dyer.

He said: “What an absolute dream. Albeit a pretty terrifying one. I have no idea if I can actually dance, but bring on the sequins, bring on the quicksteps, bring on the dance floor! I’m going to try to enjoy every minute of it.”

Since winning Love Island in 2025, Cach Mercer has amassed a TikTok audience of almost 50 million, following him for his posts on Afrobeat, hip-hop and street dance. Mercer also works as a model, a DJ and as an ambassador for a mental health charity.

He said: “I have grown up watching the show with my family and am so excited to now be part of the cast. Dancing is a huge passion of mine and I can’t wait to learn Ballroom and do the Foxtrot for the first time!”

Hair stylist to the stars Chris Appleton has clients including Jennifer Lopez, Sofía Vergara, Ariana Grande and Katy Perry – and has amassed four million followers on Instagram alone. His career began in a hair salon in Leicester before he moved to London and landed his first big-name client, Rita Ora. Since then, his work has featured in magazines including Vogue, Vanity Fair and Harper’s Bazaar, and he has styled Kim Kardashian for seven consecutive Met Galas.

He said: “I may know my way around a salon floor, but the dance floor is a whole different story – and I can’t wait to get started.”

One of many Australian soap stars who’s also carved out a successful pop career, 41-year-old Delta Goodrem is best known to UK viewers as Nina Tucker in Neighbours and the voice behind 2000s hits such as Lost Without You and Born to Try. Down Under, she’s had nine number-one singles and has won 12 Aria Music Awards. She was also a coach on The Voice Australia for seven years and represented Australia in this year’s Eurovision Song Contest, finishing fourth.

But can she dance the foxtrot? Goodrem says: “There is one stage I’ve never stepped on to and that is the ballroom floor! I’m absolutely thrilled to be joining Strictly and can’t wait to get started!”

No surprise at all to see “broadcaster and author” Dani Dyer (she was on Love Island in 2018) in this year’s line-up, as she was originally included in last year’s, before injury forced her out before the competition began. It remains to be seen if she will once again be paired with Nikita Kuzmin, who instead made the final with another Love Island escapee, Amber Davies, last year. If you’re not au fait with televised dating shows, Dyer is also the daughter of Danny Dyer and wife of West Ham United captain Jarrod Bowen. As befits the partner of a footballer, Dyer says she is “over the moon” to be back on the show. She’ll be a competitor: she has already won Celebrity SAS: Who Dares Wins this year.

The EastEnders actress was the first celebrity to be announced for the 2026 series. Turner moved into Albert Square in 2004 at the age of 16 as the gobby teenager Stacey Slater and left on Christmas Day 2010. A stab at non-EastEnders acting saw her star in, among other things, the BBC’s army drama Our Girl, before being lured back to E20 in 2014. Stacey departed once again last year. Turner has said she is “excited and terrified” to be on the show but that she thought it was “about time” she plucked up the courage to take part (which suggests multiple offers in previous years). Good news, Lacey – EastEnders actresses fare well, with three of them lifting the Glitterball Trophy previously.

Who are the new hosts?

The biggest change this year is in the presenting line-up, with Strictly stalwarts Tess Daly and Claudia Winkleman having left the show in 2025. They have been replaced, in a shock move, by three presenters: TV host Emma Willis, stand-up comedian Josh Widdicombe and Strictly professional Johannes Radebe. Their chemistry in screen tests was, we’re told, “off the charts”, but it remains to be seen how Strictly’s throuple actually works. Early rumours suggested Radebe would be a “roving reporter”, though the BBC has named him a “host”, the same as Willis and Widdicombe.

Who are the professional dancers?

As the BBC refreshes the series for 2026, there has been something of a cull of the pros. Gone are Karen Hauer (the show’s longest-serving female pro), Nadiya Bychkova, Luba Mushtuk, Gorka Márquez and Michelle Tsiakkas (particularly cruel given that she only joined in 2022 and only ever had one celebrity partner – “My dream is shattered,” she has said).

This means the 15 remaining professionals are Dianne Buswell, Julian Caillon, Vito Coppola, Amy Dowden, Carlos Gu, Katya Jones, Neil Jones, Nikita Kuzmin, Lauren Oakley, Jowita Przystal, Johannes Radebe, Aljaz Skorjanec, Alexis Warr, Kai Widdrington and Nancy Xu. They will be joined by five new pros: Aleksandra Isaeva, Cristian Priori, Lethabo Monametsi, Maddie Ingoldsby and Mark Karmalita.

What else is new for this series?

Aside from a slightly redesigned – and we’ll say it, not quite as good – logo for the show, we’re unaware of any other major changes – the judging panel, for instance, remains as Shirley Ballas, Anton Du Beke, Motsi Mabuse and Craig Revel Horwood. However, there are changes afoot for sister show It Takes Two. Comedian and former Strictly contestant Ellie Taylor is to become the new host, with episodes every weeknight recorded at Strictly’s home, Elstree Studios. She’ll be joined by costume designer Vicky Gill to provide insight into who’s wearing what. Says Taylor: “I can’t wait to immerse myself in all the gossip and sequins flying out of the ballroom in order to bring my fellow Strictly fans everything they need to know.”

The BBC has announced no new themed weeks yet, but has confirmed the return of movies, icons, Hallowe’en and musicals, as well as a return for the instant dance challenge, which debuted to a positive reaction last year. And, of course, there’s the annual trip to Blackpool.
