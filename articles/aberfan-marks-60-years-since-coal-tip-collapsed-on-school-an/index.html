Aberfan marks 60 years since coal tip collapsed on school and homes, killing 116 children and 28 adults

TheTools · 21.10.2026 09:19 · source · permalink

Summary

  • People in Aberfan, south Wales, are remembering the disaster 60 years ago when a coal waste tip collapsed on a school and homes

  • A prayer and moment of silence was held at 09:15 to mark the moment, and a male voice choir formed in the aftermath of the disaster will sing

  • On 21 October 1966, 116 children and 28 adults were killed when the colliery spoil tip collapsed and engulfed Pantglas Junior School and surrounding houses

  • On what was the last day of school before half term, children were settling down to lessons when tragedy struck

  • Emergency services, miners and people who rushed to the village began an around-the-clock battle to dig out those trapped by the debris

  • King Charles, Queen Camilla and the Prince of Wales will later join survivors, bereaved parents and other members of the community

  • The tragedy was said to be the late Queen Elizabeth's biggest regret that she didn't visit until eight days after the disaster - she later returned to the village in Merthyr Tydfil county a further three times

  • Warning: This live page will contain upsetting details that some may find distressing

People in Aberfan, south Wales, are remembering the disaster 60 years ago when a coal waste tip collapsed on a school and homes

A prayer and moment of silence was held at 09:15 to mark the moment, and a male voice choir formed in the aftermath of the disaster will sing

On 21 October 1966, 116 children and 28 adults were killed when the colliery spoil tip collapsed and engulfed Pantglas Junior School and surrounding houses

On what was the last day of school before half term, children were settling down to lessons when tragedy struck

Emergency services, miners and people who rushed to the village began an around-the-clock battle to dig out those trapped by the debris

King Charles, Queen Camilla and the Prince of Wales will later join survivors, bereaved parents and other members of the community

The tragedy was said to be the late Queen Elizabeth's biggest regret that she didn't visit until eight days after the disaster - she later returned to the village in Merthyr Tydfil county a further three times

Live Reporting

Edited by Chris Wood, Miriam Barker and Ben Frampton

  1. 'One little girl... she had beautiful black curly hair'published at 10:08 BST
    10:08 BST

    Mair Morgan was a 25-year-old teacher who had worked at Pantglas Junior School for a year when the disaster happened.

    She recalls it as a misty morning with drizzly rain, as she rang the bell to start school at 08:50.

    After hearing a "terrible noise", she got her pupils outside before the impact of the slurry, and remembers seeing the caretaker rushing to help other children.

    Bethania Chapel was eventually opened as a place of rest for the victims of the disaster, but before that, the teachers were asked to identify the children.

    "I found that very hard," said Mair.

    "I remember one little girl, I can see her face now. She had beautiful black curly hair."

  2. Survivors and families of victims gather for private servicepublished at 10:00 BST
    10:00 BST

    Huw Thomas
    BBC News, reporting from Aberfan

    At the cemetery where victims are buried, events were private, away from the media and other visitors to Aberfan.

    Survivors were joined by family members of those who died to mark the moment the disaster happened exactly 60 years ago.

    A programme of events, shared with me, explained how wreaths were laid by representatives from the emergency services that responded that day.

    There were also dignitaries at the cemetery, including Wales' first minister, members of parliament and local government.

    Chaplain to the mayor of Merthyr Tydfil Rev David Powell led the prayers while the Bishop of Llandaff closed proceedings with a benediction.

    The Ynysowen Male Voice Choir, formed to help men in Aberfan to cope in the years after the disaster, also performed for those who gathered in the misty rain.

    Prince William will unveil an Aberfan Memorial Tree at the school later today.

  8. The moment time stood still in Aberfanpublished at 09:02 BST
    09:02 BST

    One of the most poignant artefacts associated with Aberfan is a clock which stopped at the moment coal spoil struck the village - approximately 09:13.

    It was picked up by Mike Flynn, one of the rescuers, and has been used since to help illustrate the tragedy.

    The clock had been stored in a box in his son's house, but in 2022 he donated it to the national museum at St Fagans to ensure it is preserved for the nation.

    "Certainly the Aberfan community and the wider Welsh community really own the clock because of what it stands for and the significance of it," said Mike Flynn Junior.

  9. How will the anniversary be marked today?published at 08:49 BST
    08:49 BST

    King Charles III, Queen Camilla and Prince William are set to join survivors, bereaved parents and members of the community to pay tribute to those who died on this day in 1966.

    Wales falls silent every year at around 09:13 to mark the approximate time the Aberfan disaster claimed the lives of 144 people, including 116 children.

    On the 60th anniversary, the royals will visit the Aberfan Memorial Garden which stands on the site of Pantglas Junior School.

    They will spend time with pupils who survived, siblings of those who died, and members of a support group for mothers who lost children.

    The Village's Ynysowen Male Voice Choir - formed in the aftermath of the disaster - will also perform.

    08:42 BST

    Jordan Davies
    BBC News, reporting from Aberfan

    It’s damp and dark in Aberfan and the air is heavy with the memory of what happened here 60 years ago today.

    In the community centre, the heart of anniversary events, people are working hard to make sure today goes as smoothly as possible - as the world’s media lines up outside.

    There’s a huge security operation, with roads cordoned off and police officers with search dogs.

    At the top of Aberfan, you can see the gleaming white flagstones in the memorial for those who died - a poignant and permanent reminder of this tragedy.

  11. What happened in Aberfan on 21 October 1966?published at 08:34 BST
    08:34 BST

    It was the last day before half term, and pupils had just sat down to lessons at Pantglas Junior School in Aberfan.

    Just after 09:00, a colliery spoil tip above the small mining community collapsed.

    About 150,000 tonnes of slurry slid down the mountain and towards the village, engulfing the school and nearby houses.

    The disaster claimed the lives of 144 people, including 116 children.

    At the time, Aberfan, in Merthyr Tydfil county, was a village of a few thousand people, just like countless others dotted across the south Wales coalfield.

    But its name is now ingrained on the national consciousness, and every year on 21 October, the nation pauses to remember those that were lost.
