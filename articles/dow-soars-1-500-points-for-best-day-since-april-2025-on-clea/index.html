Dow soars 1,500 points for best day since April 2025 on clear-cut Democratic win, falling oil: Live updates

TheTools · 03.11.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

Stocks ripped higher on Wednesday as a decisive victory by Democrats in the U.S. midterm elections — and President Donald Trump's acceptance of the results — removed political uncertainty risk hanging over Wall Street.

Democrats won majorities in both the House of Representatives and Senate, and scored victories in key gubernatorial races like Texas. The Democrats will now be a check on the policies of Trump, who's enacted business-friendly tax cuts, while also pursuing tariffs and a war in Iran that's boosted inflation.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 1,569.40 points, or 3.03%, to close at 53,356.12. The 30-stock index moved back above 53,000 for the first time since Sept. 8 and posted its best day since April 9, 2025, when Trump announced a pause on his so-called reciprocal tariffs. The S&P 500 gained 1.58% to end at 7,688.62, while the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.44% to 25,736.51.

Stocks extended their gains after U.S. crude oil fell more than 8% as Vice President JD Vance blamed the Iran war for the election losses the Republican party suffered Tuesday. Brent futures traded more than 8% lower as well.

"There's just going to be even more pressure to end the war," said Peter Boockvar, chief investment officer at One Point BFG Wealth Partners. "The American people have obviously expressed their displeasure with inflation and the price of gasoline, amongst other things, and maybe this just pushes for an end to it. We'll have to see."

Trump told The New York Times he was "absolutely willing" to accept the results and told The Wall Street Journal that he and Republicans would work with Democrats.

The rebound "was in part due to the president's initial tone and reaction to the results," said Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets. "Still more uncertainty looms, but one thing is certain, there will be gridlock and historically this is good for the market."

Shares linked to the economy that could benefit from a more stable environment led the gains. Walmart added 3%, while Bank of America rose about 4% as investors looked for stocks outside of technology.

Heading into the election investors worried a Democratic sweep could weigh on some areas of the market, especially stocks related to the artificial intelligence build-out. Investors feared a big Democratic win could mean more restrictions on data centers nationwide. The Texas governor win by Gina Hinojosa, who's pledged an immediate moratorium in the key data center hub, added to the fears Wednesday.

Shares of CoreWeave were lower Wednesday, along with Elon Musk's SpaceX, which is headquartered in Texas. Nvidia also dropped in the session by 3%.

Another reason for the bullish sentiment Wednesday morning was that history shows the market does well under a divided government, where fewer policies and regulations are able to interfere with the business outlook.

Andrew Slimmon of Morgan Stanley Investment Management said the markets "like gridlock — they don't like a party with too much power."

The three leading U.S. indexes surged on Wednesday.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 1,569.40 points, or 3.03%, to end the day at 53,356.12, while the S&P 500 added 1.58% to 7,688.62. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.44% to 25,736.51.

Bitcoin has rebounded after briefly falling to the $75,000 level in the aftermath of the U.S. midterm elections, but the recovery has yet to attract meaningful support from new buyers, according to Bitfinex. It was last trading at about $80,000.

Analysts at the crypto exchange say gains since Oct. 30 have largely been fueled by short sellers closing their positions, while investors who recently entered the market continue to sell as prices return to their purchase levels. At the same time, outflows from bitcoin ETFs have pushed cumulative net flows for the year into slightly into negative territory, further weakening demand.

"Sustaining this bounce requires actual spot demand," the report says. "Aggregate bitcoin futures open interest in BTC-notional terms rose to 701,548 BTC on Nov. 3, its highest since Sept. 22. Perpetual funding rates have held close to 11 percent annualized over the past two weeks despite the price correction, while spot buyers have shown little aggression. Together, these readings suggest the bid under $77,000 is primarily underpinned by perpetual futures positions, rather than spot demand."

— Tanaya Macheel

Helen of Troy shares jumped about 26% in intraday trading after Empower Brands publicly disclosed its $37-a-share cash takeover proposal. Empower, jointly owned by Spectrum Brands and Oaktree Capital Management, said the proposal implies an enterprise value of approximately $1.6 billion.

Empower initially offered $34 a share on August 19, which Helen of Troy rejected on September 3. It increased the proposal to $37 on October 12. Helen of Troy's board unanimously rejected the revised proposal on October 20, determining that it significantly undervalued the company, according to the company's investor presentation. 

The rejection was publicly confirmed today.

Helen of Troy, whose brands include OXO, Hydro Flask and Osprey, says its standalone strategy offers greater long-term value. It also said Empower declined a nondisclosure agreement that would have allowed access to additional information. Empower characterized the proposed agreement as overly restrictive.

"The Company and Board actively engaged with and were responsive to Empower Brands and its advisors, including offering a customary non-disclosure agreement that would allow them to receive information," the company said in a statement. "Empower Brands refused the offer, highlighting the opportunistic nature of the approach and lack of diligence in determining the value of Helen of Troy."

No deal has been agreed.

Helen of Troy maintains that the proposal undervalues its business, but says its board remains open to opportunities that benefit shareholders.

"The Helen of Troy Board, after comprehensive review alongside its financial and legal advisors, has unanimously determined that Empower Brands' proposal substantially undervalues Helen of Troy and its prospects for continued growth and value creation, and is not in the best interest of Helen of Troy or its shareholders," the Company said in a statement.

— Deena Zaidi

Democrats strong showing Tuesday night could create a challenging regulatory environment for the financial services and crypto industries.

Investors should expect Elizabeth Warren to chair the Senate Banking Committee, where her opposition to crypto is likely to stall supportive crypto legislation, including the Clarity Act, Compass Point analyst Ed Groshans said in a note Wednesday. Instead, Cryptoasset adoption will largely depend on SEC and CFTC regulatory actions.

"Warren is opposed to cryptoassets," he said." She has sought amendments to limit tokenization … does not support the regulatory approval of perpetual futures. We do not expect the SBC to act on any legislative efforts, such as the Clarity Act, to broaden cryptoasset adoption with Warren as Chair.

Groshans also said the banking sector should anticipate increased political and headline risk, particularly around Basel III revisions. He sees the greatest opportunities for financial services legislation are capping consumer interest rates at 36% and addressing credit card interchange.

Spreads on CoreWeave's five-year credit default swaps moved 3% higher on Wednesday to 1,139.7 after the election created more hurdles for data center plays.

Credit default swaps are contracts where buyers pay for protection in case the borrower can't repay its debt. The spread widening in CoreWeave's CDS means investors view the debt as riskier. The yield on its 9.75% bond due in 2031 also ticked 3 basis points higher to 15.919%. One basis point is equal to 0.01%, and yields and prices move in opposite directions.

Democrats, seen as friendlier to data center restrictions, had resounding wins across the country, including in Texas -- where the newly elected governor has said she wants an immediate moratorium on data centers in the state. Ballot measures on their development were also shot down in various states.

CoreWeave has been trading above 1,000 in recent weeks as investors became more concerned about the debt being taken on to fund artificial intelligence infrastructure. That level is generally seen as signaling distress.

Investors are on edge as polls across the U.S. begin closing in an election that could bring gridlock to government. But if history is any indication, they may have little to worry about.

A CNBC analysis of S&P 500 returns after elections from 1980 through 2024 shows the benchmark rose an average of 0.2% one day after the election and 1.1% in the month following. One year out, the benchmark averages a 13.8% return and rose in 91% of cases. That's including general elections.

Looking at just post-midterm returns, the record is even stronger. The S&P 500 has risen after each of the last 11 midterm elections between 1982 and 2022. One year out, the index returns 13.7% on average.
