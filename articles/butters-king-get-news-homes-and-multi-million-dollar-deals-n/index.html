Millions spent as Butters, King move and Blakey prepares to follow suit

Zak Butters inked a mega deal worth at least $17 million over nine years at the Western Bulldogs, Ben King committed to Hawthorn for $9 million in the next seven years and Nick Blakey told the Kangaroos he wants to take-up their eight-year, $12 million offer on Friday.

The three blockbuster deals were the centre of attention as the AFL’s trade period reached its halfway point in an uncharacteristic flurry of activity.

Trades will not be officially lodged at the weekend, but the focus will be on Blakey’s bid to get to North Melbourne, where his father is a hero of the 1996 and 1999 premierships.

Sam McClure and Jake Niall report the Roos have prepared for all possible scenarios in Blakey’s short-term future, after their board approved the offer made to him; a deal that would make him one of the highest-paid players in the competition.

Blakey was one of five Sydney players suspended at the end of the 2026 season following the fallout from the Pullman Hotel incident; a scandal that is still being investigated by Victoria Police.

In that context, the Roos consulted their AFLW players and kept them informed about the club’s pursuit of Blakey.

Meanwhile, the complicated side deals that helped offset Port Adelaide’s pain at losing Butters and soften the blow for Gold Coast from King’s defection were a subject of intense scrutiny on today’s edition of the Real Footy podcast, which caps off a busy week at the trade table.

Dogs, Hawks cap busy day with pick swap

In the final transaction of the working week, Hawthorn and the Western Bulldogs – the two big winners of Friday’s trading – have agreed to a pick swap.

The Hawks – who officially recruited Ben King from Gold Coast today – get a future third in 2027 tied to Gold Coast.

Just hours after landing Zak Butters from Port Adelaide, the Bulldogs receive Hawthorn’s future third in 2028.

A future third in 2028 will, in all likelihood, be an earlier selection than a future third in 2027. Why? The 2027 pick is pushed back by Tasmania’s raft of concession selections.

The 2027 draft is also further diluted by the 12 17-year-olds Tasmania can select as part of their draft concessions.

Butters, King headline Friday deals

The deadline day of the trade period is not until Wednesday, but there was plenty of action today. Here’s a snapshot of the developments:

  • The Western Bulldogs secured Port Adelaide’s Zak Butters as a free agent on a deal worth at least $17 million over nine years. Port opted not to match the offer.
  • The Dogs and Power were also major players in one of the most complex trade deals ever executed. Five clubs, 20 draft picks and two players were tied up in the multi-layered transaction.
  • Ben King became a Hawk, leaving Gold Coast, who chose not to match his free agency deal but stand to gain youngster Aidan Schubert in a separate transaction.
  • Sydney’s Nick Blakey declared his desire to join North Melbourne, where his father was a dual premiership player.
  • St Kilda picked up Brisbane’s Sam Marshall and pick 41 in this year’s draft. The Lions scored the Saints’ first pick in next year’s draft.
  • Sydney’s Harry Cunningham, aged 32, will take a rookie contract at Adelaide.

Dogs fall out of Coleman race

The likeliest scenario entering the weekend for contracted Lion Keidean Coleman is for him to be at Brisbane next year.

The Western Bulldogs are believed to have expressed interest in him during the season, but they no longer have the necessary draft capital.

No other clubs are poised at this stage to make a move in this trade period for Coleman, who will be an unrestricted free agent at the end of next season.

The Lions have made it clear publicly and privately that they want to retain him.

Saints land Lions’ premiership midfielder

Sam Marshall is now a Saint.

The former Brisbane Lion has been traded to the Saints along with a second-round pick No.39 (now No.41) in this year’s draft, while the Lions have received the Saints’ first-round selection in 2027.

A Queensland native, Marshall played for the Sandringham Dragons while boarding at Melbourne Grammar School.

He was a first-round pick in the 2024 national draft (no.25), playing 11 senior games in his debut season, including the 2025 premiership. He managed just the two senior games this year.

“While still early in his career, Sam has already shown exciting potential through the midfield in a strong Brisbane side,” Saints football boss Lenny Hayes said.

“He’s a classy young player with a strong work rate and plenty of upside. We look forward to welcoming Sam to the Saints and seeing what he can bring to our side next season.”

Flynn yet to be ‘in’ with Magpies

A deal between Carlton and Collingwood for Blues ruckman Flynn Riley could go deep into next week.

The Pies are basing Riley’s value on the deals which sent back-up rucks Max Heath and Liam Reidy to new clubs last year, whereas the Blues believe he is worth more. Riley is the Blues’ No.3 ruck, but they rated him highly enough to offer a two-year deal, but the Pies have offered more.

Heath crossed from St Kilda to Melbourne last year for a fourth-round pick, while Reidy headed from Fremantle to Carlton for a swap of third- and fourth-round selections.

The Pies do not have a pick between 29 and 66 this year, while the Blues’ next pick after 25 comes at 60. The Blues and Pies both have a full suite of picks in 2027 and 2028. With both clubs having other trades to finalise, this one might take a while.

AFL approves Butters’ deal

Butters gets his homecoming

The Bulldogs have snared the star recruit of the trade period, boldly declaring, “Zak Butters is home”.

The club has just spruiked it’s success in finalising the deal to secure Butters, who barracked for them before he was drafted by Port Adelaide.

Butters, 26, played 156 games and kicked 73 goals for Port Adelaide after being selected with pick 12 in the 2019 draft. By releasing their statement about Butters, the Dogs implied Port Adelaide had chosen not to match their restricted free agency contract offer to him, worth somewhere between $17 million and $17.5 million over the next nine seasons.

He was named in the All Australian team three times at Port Adelaide and won the club’s best and fairest award the same number of times.

Butters-adjacent deal approved

The AFL has approved the monster five-club pick swap deal that sits alongside the Zak Butters free agency move to the Western Bulldogs.

As of mid-afternoon, Port Adelaide had not formally chosen to not match Butters’ nine-year, contract offer from the Dogs, worth between $17 million and $17.5 million.

But it is now a fait accompli that they won’t match it, given the pick deal, in which the Power and Melbourne traded early draft picks and the Dogs give up their first choice this year – among a host of other future picks exchanged.
