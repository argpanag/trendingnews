LNG Canada to move forward with Phase 2 expansion project in Kitimat, B.C. | CBC News

TheTools · 30.09.2026 18:56 · source · permalink

Expansion of LNG Canada project would double exports from Kitimat to overseas markets

LNG Canada says its multibillion-dollar Phase 2 expansion in Kitimat, B.C., is going forward, in a move that could double exports of liquefied natural gas from the northern coastal facility to overseas markets.

In a statement Tuesday, LNG Canada CEO Chris Cooper said the project will create thousands of jobs and further strengthen Canada's role as a trusted energy partner.

"LNG Canada Phase 2 is another nation-building investment that demonstrates Canada can build big things when governments, First Nations partners, local communities, skilled trades, contractors and investors work together with shared purpose," Cooper said.

LNG Canada is a joint venture company made up of Shell, Petronas, PetroChina, Mitsubishi Corporation and Korea Gas Corp.

Its Kitimat plant shipped its first cargo load of LNG destined for Asian markets last year.

Speaking in Vancouver Tuesday, Prime Minister Mark Carney said the private sector investment of about $33-billion in LNG Canada's Phase 2 will make it the second-largest facility of its kind in the world.

"It will connect low-cost, low-carbon Canadian energy to global markets."

Carney said the expansion and increased LNG output will amount to billions for the national economy, and create up to 4,000 new jobs in Kitimat alone during peak construction.

Skeena-Bulkley Valley Conservative MP Ellis Ross, a longtime LNG advocate and former chief councillor of the Haisla Nation, meanwhile, said the real credit for the project should go to local Indigenous leaders who have been working on building an LNG industry for decades.

"It’s really a nothingburger, it’s a bunch of politicians taking credit for something they had nothing to do with," he said of Tuesday's announcement.

"You got to remember the NDP right up until 2017 opposed LNG. They said it was just a pipe dream. They said if LNG got developed, Victoria would be underwater. The Liberal government said there was no business case for LNG."

But while the LNG industry has strong political support from the federal government, as well as the campaigning B.C. NDP and Conservative Party of B.C., critics worry its rapid expansion will further contribute to Canada's rising greenhouse gas emissions at a time of climate crisis.

What is LNG?

LNG is made from natural gas, a fossil fuel often extracted in northeast B.C. and Alberta through a practice called hydraulic fracturing or fracking. 

It involves drilling down into the ground and injecting a mixture of water, sand and chemicals into the ground to force the release of the oil and gas.

The gas is then shipped via pipeline to terminals, which cool it to about –160 C, at which point it can be shipped overseas for use as a fuel.

The expansion project is expected to roughly double the production of liquefied natural gas from 14 million to 28 million tonnes annually, LNG Canada said.

It also said it is working with Coastal GasLink to expand the capacity of the existing 670-kilometre pipeline through the construction of five new compressor stations across northern B.C., including territory that was blocked by Wet'suwet'en hereditary chiefs and their supporters opposed to the line's construction, which led to nation-wide protests and arrests.

LNG is often referred to as "clean" because burning it for fuel causes fewer emissions than coal or oil — but that claim is disputed by some analysts because of the energy and destruction it takes to produce.

One of the known impacts of fracking is an increase in earthquakes, which have been felt with increasing frequency in B.C.'s Peace River region in recent years.

Critics also point out ramping up LNG production would sharply increase the amount of emissions from natural gas production in British Columbia.

Environmentalists argue that reducing emissions carries more urgency in the wake of a federal report warning Canadians to prepare for a future that's 5 C hotter — which will lead to the disappearance of glaciers in western Canada and longer, more intense summer droughts, among other impacts.

Political priority

Still, the project has been identified as one of national importance under Carney, and has support from both the B.C. NDP and B.C. Conservative Party — who have each pledged to ramp up LNG production should they form government following the provincial election on Oct. 24.
