Global markets seek direction as oil rises ahead of US inflation release

TheTools · 14.10.2026 11:53 · source · permalink

Reporting the Changing World

Anadolu Ajansı © 2026

Global markets are searching for direction with tech stock volatility, surging oil prices, and mixed signals from American bank earnings, while all eyes turned to the US inflation data to be released on Wednesday.

The US inflation, which is expected to accelerate due to rising energy costs, will give insight into the Federal Reserve’s potential monetary policy pathway moving forward.

The Fed is 78% likely to maintain its rates this month, money market estimates show.

Ongoing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East continue to dampen risk appetite.

December-delivery Brent crude is up 0.6% at $106.3 a barrel on Wednesday following a Yemeni Houthi militias’ attack on Saudi Arabia.

Meanwhile, New York Fed President John Williams said he still thinks the bank should hike rates once more this year but no further rate hikes will be necessary, expecting inflation to decline significantly next year.

The US 10-Year Treasury bond yield dropped by one basis point to 5.21% following Williams’ statements.

The US dollar declined 0.1% in value to 102.2, while gold dropped 0.1% to $4,158 per ounce.

The daily crude oil production by the OPEC group rose 920,000 barrels month-on-month in September, totaling nearly 20.1 million barrels, leading to a downward revision of global oil demand growth for this year and an upward revision for next year.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) said rising borrowing costs in a period of high debt burdens exert mounting pressure on public finances, urging fiscal consolidation measures to be implemented.

The New York Stock Exchange traded negatively due to a broader selloff in chipmaker stocks.

Micron Technology shares dropped 3.6%, SK Hynix fell 3.2%, AMD declined 1.9%, and Western Digital’s shares were down 1.6%.

Separately, major US banks reported their financial results.

JPMorgan Chase’s profit and revenue fell short of expectations, while its shares dropped by nearly 1%.

Citigroup shares contracted 5.6% despite profit and revenue exceeding estimates, while Goldman Sachs shares fell 0.2%.

Wells Fargo posted better-than-expected profits but its revenue did not meet expectations, losing the bank 3.1% in stock value.

At the same time, US existing-home sales grew 1.5% in September, beating expectations of a 0.8% decline.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.35% and the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.15%, while the S&P 500 traded flat on Tuesday. American indexes started Wednesday on a positive trend.

European stock markets also saw selloffs amid tech stock declines.

Dutch chipmaker ASML’s shares dropped 4.1%, German tech firm Infineon’s shares declined 2.6%, Aixtron shares fell 3.4%, and Suss Microtec shares contracted 3.5%.

Germany’s BMW posted a 6.3% drop in global sales in the third quarter to 551,072 units as sales in China declined, but the carmaker’s shares closed Tuesday up 2.4%, led by the European electric vehicle (EV) demand.

Germany’s consumer price index (CPI) rose 0.6% on a monthly basis and 3.3% year-on-year, within estimates.

The UK’s FTSE 100 fell 0.06%, while Germany’s DAX 40 and France’s CAC 40 declined 0.07%. Italy’s FTSE MIB 30 traded flat.

European indexes started Wednesday on a mixed trend.

Near the close, however, Asian equity markets traded mostly higher.

China’s producer price index (PPI) rose 5% on an annual basis, marking its fastest rise since June 2022, and rising above the 3.8% estimate. Rising energy prices contributed to the rise, while signaling that the country’s economy is gradually moving away from deflationary pressures.

China’s CPI rose 0.9% on an annual basis this month, accelerating moderately compared to August but coming in below estimates, revealing that domestic demand remained sluggish.

At the same time, China’s exports and imports surged above expectations by 36.3% and 31%, respectively, gaining momentum compared to August.

Chinese economic policymakers may have to implement further policy support to reach short- and long-term growth targets, analysts say.

The People’s Bank of China cut the one-year lending rates of three policy banks by 25 basis points on Sept. 29.

Near the close of trading on Wednesday, South Korea’s Kospi rose 1.2%, Japan’s Nikkei 225 gained 0.9%, and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng was up 0.2%, while China’s Shanghai Composite traded flat.
