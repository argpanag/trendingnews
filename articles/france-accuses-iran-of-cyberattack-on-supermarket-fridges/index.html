France accuses Iran of cyberattack on supermarket fridges

TheTools · 10.11.2026 00:06 · source · permalink

France has opened an investigation and accused Iran of being behind recent cyberattacks targeting refrigeration units in supermarkets.

French authorities said on Monday that they are probing a cyber unit affiliated with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) over the attacks, which caused fires and large amounts of food waste.

Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said that the Iranian ambassador had been summoned and that sanctions would be brought against Iran at a “European level”.

“I can confirm that this attack was carried out by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps of the Islamic Republic of Iran,” Barrot said of the cyberattacks on French supermarkets that occurred October 11 and 12.

Barrot told reporters that the attack was “unprecedented in its level of aggression”.

“This is the first large-scale cyberattack carried out for sabotage purposes on French soil,” he said. “It represents a major attack on the nation’s interests and on the security of the French people. That is why it will not go unanswered.

“We will also take any other measures that are proportionate and necessary, whether public or private, to respond to this attack and deter anyone who might seek to repeat it,” Barrot added.

Investigators say the attackers had used Farsi and IP addresses linked to devices located in Iran to carry out the attack, during which they gained internet access to the refrigeration systems and remotely cranked up temperatures to 50 degrees Celsius (122 degrees Fahrenheit) overnight.

Millions of euros worth of food was spoiled, according to retailers. In some cases, the raised temperatures sparked fires or melted equipment.

French supermarket groups E-Leclerc, Intermarche, Carrefour, Monoprix and Cooperative U were all hit.

At least 300 of Intermarche’s stores and its Netto brand were affected. Cooperative U said more than 100 of its supermarkets were targeted, while Carrefour said 80 supermarkets were affected, and Monoprix reported that about 5 percent of its roughly 490 stores were hit.

Supermarkets in several other European countries reported similar refrigeration problems on November 2. Overnight temperature spikes were recorded in Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Luxembourg and Sweden. Dozens of stores and a food distribution hub were hit.
