Merckx, Indurain, Froome, Seixas and Vollering meet Pope Leo XIV in a historic moment for cycling

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:19 · source · permalink

Road 23/11/26 22:42 Migue A.

The Vatican gathered on Monday, November 23, some of the greatest legends in the history of cycling alongside several current champions. Eddy Merckx, Miguel Induráin, Bernard Hinault, Chris Froome, Demi Vollering, and Paul Seixas participated in a private audience with Pope Leo XIV, who took the opportunity to advocate for the values of this sport and its importance beyond competition. The meeting also served to deliver 15 bicycles to young people in vulnerable situations.

Merckx, Induráin, Seixas, and Vollering star in a historic meeting at the Vatican

As we reported a few days ago, Pope Leo XIV was scheduled to receive a broad representation of international cycling in a meeting organized by the International Cycling Union (UCI) and the Dicastery for Culture and Education of the Vatican.

The audience took place this morning in the Clementine Hall of the Apostolic Palace and, according to Vatican News, included around 120 participants, among cyclists, representatives of federations, UCI officials, and organizers of major competitions such as the Tour de France, the Giro d'Italia, and the Vuelta a España.

The meeting presented an unusual image, with several generations of champions sharing the spotlight. Among the attendees were Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, Miguel Induráin, Greg LeMond, Francesco Moser, Vincenzo Nibali, Mark Cavendish, and Chris Froome.

Also participating were some of the great figures of women's cycling, such as Demi Vollering, Annemiek van Vleuten, Jeannie Longo, Elisa Longo Borghini, and Mariana Pajón, as well as Paul Seixas, one of the representatives of the new generation, and the Spaniard Ricardo Ten, a reference in paralympic cycling.

Merckx and Moser notably highlighted one of the historic details of the day. Both had previously met with a pontiff in May 1974, when Paul VI received the participants of the Giro d'Italia before its departure from the Vatican. More than half a century later, they returned to meet with Leo XIV.

A Scott electric bike and several jerseys for the Pope

As is often the case in such meetings, gifts also took center stage.

The president of the UCI, David Lappartient, presented the Pope with an urban-use electric bike. A white Scott with a low-access frame, accompanied by a personalized rainbow jersey with the name "Leone XIV" and signed by cyclists present at the audience.

They were not the only gifts. Vincenzo Nibali presented the pontiff with a pink jersey from the Giro d'Italia, while Demi Vollering gifted him a yellow jersey from the Tour de France Femmes.

The delivery of these garments recalls previous visits by great champions to the Vatican. In 2018, it was Peter Sagan who gifted Pope Francis a personalized Specialized Venge, while Egan Bernal did the same in 2021 with a Pinarello Dogma F12 after his victory in the Giro d'Italia.

The current pontiff's relationship with cycling also had another recent chapter. On June 1, 2025, Leo XIV received and blessed the peloton of the Giro d'Italia during the passage of the last stage through Vatican territory.

Leo XIV emphasizes that cycling is much more than winning races

During his speech, the Pope wanted to address both the champions and the leaders of international cycling, highlighting the values that this sport represents.

In his official speech, published in full by the Vatican, Leo XIV pointed out that the victories and defeats of cyclists reflect experiences common to all people and that sport can become a means of personal growth.

He also highlighted the possibility of finding tranquility during bike rides, away from the traffic and noise of cities, and related some of the great climbs in cycling to the sanctuaries and pilgrimage sites located in the mountains.

One of the most striking messages came when he recalled the historical relationship between the bicycle and the Church, especially through the Virgin of Ghisallo, proclaimed patroness of cyclists by Pius XII in 1949.

"The bicycle can even be an instrument of evangelization", the pontiff stated.

Leo XIV also remembered cyclists who have died in traffic accidents, both athletes and people who use bicycles for their daily commutes to work or school.

Nibali, Moser, and Ricardo Ten share their experiences of the audience

After the meeting, some participants shared their impressions with Vatican media.

Vincenzo Nibali acknowledged the personal and historical importance of the day:

"It was a beautiful audience, something that will remain in the history books of cycling. Seeing that the Vatican, the Pope himself, is so close to the sport is of enormous value."

The Italian also recalled that during his professional career, he passed by numerous sanctuaries located in mountain passes and that, even during his training, he chose some climbs precisely because of the monasteries he found at their summits.

Francesco Moser explained that riding a bike alone allows for reflection and shared an amusing anecdote. During his conversation with Leo XIV, they discovered that they both knew the same religious figure from Lima, with whom Moser had gone cycling.

For his part, the Spaniard Ricardo Ten emphasized the effort behind sporting successes:

"All athletes know that behind a great success, there is a lot of effort, perseverance, and the harder it is to achieve that goal or target, the greater the reward."

Greg LeMond also highlighted the power of cycling to unite people from different countries and cultures, while Mariana Pajón encouraged young people to persevere in their goals despite difficulties.

Fifteen bicycles to help young people in need

Beyond the meetings between champions and the gifts to the pontiff, the day concluded with a solidarity initiative organized by Athletica Vaticana and the UCI.

Outside the Casa Santa Marta, a ceremony was held where 15 bicycles were delivered to young people served by Caritas of Rome, as part of the solidarity program that both organizations jointly develop.

Two of the beneficiaries who participated in the delivery were Muhammed Jobarteh, 19 years old and from Gambia, and Abdurahman Conteh Abbas, 24 years old and from Sierra Leone.

In both cases, the bicycles will have a very concrete utility. Muhammed will be able to travel to his study center and workplace, while Abdurahman will have a transportation alternative that will allow him to reduce expenses and access new job opportunities.

An initiative that served to close the meeting by translating the messages about the values of cycling into a practical application, where the bicycle ceases to be a sports tool to become a means of mobility, independence, and social integration.
