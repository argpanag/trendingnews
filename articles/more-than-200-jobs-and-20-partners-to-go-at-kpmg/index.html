More than 200 jobs and 20 partners to go at KPMG

TheTools · 17.11.2026 15:25 · source · permalink

By Malavika Santhebennur

18 November 2026 • 2 minute read

The Australian Financial Review has reported that KPMG Australia will cut 230 jobs and around 20 partners in the wake of allegations of audit misconduct, mishandling of confidential client documents and information, and mistreatment of the whistleblower who made the allegations.

The roles will mostly be cut from the consulting unit and the internal business services division, alongside the deal advisory and infrastructure team.

Led by Brad Miller, the consulting division has seen a dip in revenue by at least a third, according to the media report.

The number of job cuts is less than the 400 initially expected, as many staff and partners have left the firm for other employers.

KPMG Australia said that audit and assurance, mid-market and private, and tax and legal are not included in these role reductions as client demand remains strong in these businesses.

This latest round of lay-offs has brought the total number of redundancies to around 630 out of the 9,000 staff at the accounting giant.

KPMG Australia will inform those impacted staff by the end of Thursday and is also looking at redeployment and overseas secondments where possible.

The latest round of job cuts has followed several rounds of trimming staff at the beleaguered accounting firm, with reports that it was rumoured to be undertaking large-scale redundancy proceedings in August in the wake of the ongoing audit scandal.

In September, it cut its executive ranks to $13 million to allow KPMG to fund band increases for around 150 junior partners. At that time, its partner headcount had reduced from 700 to 650.

The remuneration changes, which were part of KPMG’s internal review known as Project Vector, were due to weaker demand for consulting.

Since the allegations surfaced, KPMG Australia has lost several of its audit contracts from clients like Lendlease, ANZ, Macquarie Bank, and IAG. It was recently reported that Crown Resorts is also dumping the firm as its auditor after first appointing KPMG in August 2020, and seeking a replacement.

In addition, the Department of Finance has agreed with KPMG to further extend the pause on KPMG bidding for Australian government work until 28 February 2027, during which period the firm will provide Finance with an implementation plan and progress reports on implementation.

KPMG Australia was recently condemned for signing a defence deal worth $5.2 million during the ongoing procurement ban that commenced in mid-June and amid the department’s independent review.
