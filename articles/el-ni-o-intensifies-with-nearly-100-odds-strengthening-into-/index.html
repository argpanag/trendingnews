El Niño intensifies with nearly 100% odds strengthening into 'Super' status as Atlantic hurricane season peaks

TheTools · 14.09.2026 10:24 · source · permalink

El Niño has dramatically suppressed the Atlantic hurricane season, while supercharging tropical activity across the Pacific.

El Niño intensifies alongside high odds of a historic event

El Niño continues to strengthen, with odds now exceeding 90% that it will become a very strong event and a 75% chance it could become a historic event from October through December, according to the latest release from the Climate Prediction Center. FOX Weather meteorologists Stephen Morgan and Marissa Torres break down what it all means.

THE TAKEAWAY

  • This El Niño has a 75% chance of becoming the strongest El Niño since record keeping began (1950).
  • El Niño has dramatically suppressed the Atlantic hurricane season, while supercharging tropical activity across the Pacific.
  • According to NOAA's September forecast, there is a 97% chance of a Super El Niño this fall and winter that will last through early spring.

El Niño conditions have strengthened significantly in the Central Pacific, now with a 75% chance of reaching historic strength between October and December 2026, according to NOAA.

El Niño is typically associated with reduced tropical cyclone activity in the Atlantic Basin and increased storm activity in the Eastern and Central Pacific basins, along with a host of other weather impacts around the world outside of hurricane season.

WHAT IS A SUPER EL NINO?

Potential for historic El Niño.

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

El Niño is one of three phases of a climate cycle called the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO). It describes natural temperature changes in the central Pacific Ocean that alter atmospheric circulation and can greatly influence global weather.

El Niño describes warmer than average water across the equatorial Pacific, while La Niña describes colder than average water conditions, and ENSO neutral refers to a state where temperatures are close to average.

WHAT’S THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A TROPICAL DEPRESSION, TROPICAL STORM AND HURRICANE?

El Niño impact on hurricane season.

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

During El Niño, above-average water temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific Ocean alter the position and strength of the subtropical jet stream. It's typically more robust and extends well into the Caribbean and Atlantic Ocean. Think of the subtropical jet stream as a belt of very fast winds high in the atmosphere.

WHAT IS THE JET STREAM?

It's these strong winds that hinder tropical development in the Atlantic during an El Niño. Calm conditions are generally more conducive to hurricane formation.

((CSU/CIRA & NOAA) / NOAA)

Meanwhile, in the Pacific, the script is completely flipped.

EL NIÑO SURGES TOWARD 'MONSTER' TERRITORY, SIGNALING AN ACTIVE WINTER FOR EAST AND WEST COASTS

"Because El Niño concentrates its deepest pool of warm water and lowest wind shear across the central and eastern Pacific, it transforms that basin into a hyper-fueled engine for major hurricanes and monster typhoons," the FOX Forecast Center said.

These climate patterns have certainly held true so far this season, with the Pacific producing 16 named storms, including six hurricanes, three of which have reached major hurricane status. Meanwhile, the Atlantic has produced just five named storms and no hurricanes as the climatological peak of the season arrives.

If it forms, the Atlantic's first hurricane would develop later than any first hurricane on record since the beginning of the satellite era (1960).

Colorado State cuts 2026 Atlantic hurricane forecast amid strengthening El Niño

Colorado State University (CSU) has again lowered its Atlantic hurricane season forecast Wednesday, citing the high potential for a very strong El Niño during the peak of the season. When water temperatures in the El Niño zone reach at least 2 degrees Celsius above average for at least three consecutive months, it is considered a Super El Niño. These events are not as common as a typical El Niño and have a massive influence not only on the Atlantic hurricane season, but also other weather patterns around the globe.

El Niño conditions have continued to strengthen through the summer, with water temperatures in the El Niño zone already 1.8 degrees Celsius above average, according to NOAA's latest observations.

According to NOAA's September forecast released Thursday, there is a 97% chance of a Super El Niño this fall and winter that will last through early spring.

Water temperatures spiking in the El Niño monitoring zone.

(FOX Weather)

This corresponds to NOAA's updated 2026 Atlantic hurricane season forecast, where scientists cited a very strong El Niño as the main driver for an expected below-average Atlantic hurricane season this year.

In that same forecast, the agency cautioned that a record-breaking El Niño event could produce atmospheric impacts never before seen.

Typical El Niño winter impacts.

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

"A strong event could have different impacts than those traditionally observed as opposed to similar impacts with greater intensity," NOAA said.

Outside its impact on hurricane season, El Niño also has a strong effect on winter weather across the Lower 48.
