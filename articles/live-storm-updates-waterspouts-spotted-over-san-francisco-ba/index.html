Live storm updates: Waterspouts spotted over San Francisco Bay amid severe weather

TheTools · 17.11.2026 07:40 · source · permalink

A series of storms will bring heavy rain, flash flooding, gusty winds and possible debris flows to the San Francisco Bay Area over the next week.

According to ABC7 Eyewitness News meteorologist Drew Tuma, this is a strong Level 2 storm on the ABC7 Exclusive Storm Impact Scale and could be bumped up to a Level 3 as it nears the region.

In a forecast briefing released before 4 a.m. Monday, the weather service projected that the next system is expected to arrive Tuesday night and continue into early Friday morning, with the greatest impacts forecast for Wednesday and Thursday. Rain could linger across the region through the weekend.

Moderate to heavy rainfall could cause minor flooding, while areas affected by recent wildfires face a higher risk of flash flooding, mud and debris flows. Some roads could become impassable because of flooding or damage, forecasters warned.

A powerful winter storm is expected to dump up to 4 feet of snow in parts of the Sierra Nevada this week, with the National Weather Service warning that mountain travel could become nearly impossible Wednesday and Thursday.

Palisades Tahoe announced Monday that they're opening early on November 21, as opposed to the original plan of November 25.

For Bay Area residents planning a trip to Lake Tahoe, Tuesday during daylight hours appears to offer the best travel window before conditions deteriorate Tuesday night.

Dramatic unsettled weather around the Bay Area led the National Weather Service to issue warnings about tornadoes and waterspouts Monday afternoon.

As images and video circulated on social media showing a pair of waterspouts forming south of the Bay Bridge near Candlestick Point and Brisbane, the Weather Service issued what's called a "special marine warning" at a little after noon.

Special marine warnings are essentially severe storm warnings affecting coastal waterways that are expected to produce winds of 34 knots or more. They are also issued for waterspouts.

No damage was reported from the waterspouts Monday, but several photos and videos of the dramatic weather phenomena were posted online.

According to NOAA's storm events database, there have been no recorded waterspouts over the San Francisco Bay in the last 28 years.

This comes as a Tornado Warning was issued for parts of Santa Cruz County.

At 1 p.m., the Weather Service issued two tornado warnings for Santa Cruz County, one for the Boulder Creek area and one for Davenport.

No reports of any tornadoes forming in those areas had been reported as of about 2 p.m.

Weather Service meteorologist Rachel Kennedy said people should keep an eye out for similar alerts and warnings throughout the remainder of the week as a series of early-season storms rolls through Northern California.

"It looks like a very convectively active week," Kennedy said.

She recommended that people turn on the emergency alert function on their cell phones and regularly check the Weather Service's social media accounts.

Massive waves continued to pound the Bay Area coastline Monday, causing flooding and sand buildup on roads near the Pacifica Pier as crews worked to clear affected areas.

Roads around the pier that were flooded are now covered in sand, creating travel challenges for residents. Throughout Monday morning, waves splashed over barricades and onto sidewalks, causing ponding on nearby roads.

The Pacifica Pier has taken a beating from high surf over the weekend and Monday. Crews have already been working to shore up the pier, which has experienced cracking. A café on the pier was previously demolished.

Residents said the impacts from the weekend storm have made it more difficult to get around the area.

"A lot of these streets are one-way. So for us to get to our homes, we can't, like, drive. We weren't able to drive through Beach Boulevard. And so it's more of just like a kind of an inconvenience right now because my home isn't on Beach Boulevard. So like, walking my dog, for example, I have to walk all the way around or like, I can't drive through here," said Carla Paredes, a Pacifica resident.

Nearby at Rockaway Beach, intense waves also battered the shoreline, narrowly missing cars in the parking lot near Nick's Rockaway restaurant.

Crews continued working near the pier Monday as dangerous surf conditions persisted along the coast.
