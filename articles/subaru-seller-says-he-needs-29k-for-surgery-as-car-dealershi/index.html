Subaru seller says he needs $29k for surgery as car dealership Sunday Drive enters liquidation - Chris Lynch Media

TheTools · 27.11.2026 06:38 · source · permalink

A man who sold his Subaru through Sunday Drive’s Christchurch dealership said he needed the money for surgery and was still waiting for his $29,000, more than a month after the dealer told him it had received the buyer’s funds.

Chris Lynch Media has agreed to refer to the customer as Tee. He learned on Friday that Sunday Drive had gone into voluntary liquidation when Chris Lynch Media read the company’s statement to him. He said he was shaking as he tried to take in the news. He had owned his white 2001 Subaru Impreza WRX STI for just over three years and had counted on the sale to give him some money to fall back on.

Chris Lynch Media has seen the signed consignment agreement, emails and other correspondence between Tee and Sunday Drive.

Asked what he wanted to know from Sunday Drive, he said, “Are you going to pay me my money or what is it? Because I need that money for my surgery.”

Tee signed a consignment agreement on 13 October that listed the Subaru at $31,000. Two days later, Sunday Drive’s Auckland-based founder Taylor Campbell confirmed Tee would receive $29,000 from the sale.

On 19 October, Mark Phillips, who worked at Sunday Drive’s Christchurch dealership, put that assurance in writing in an email to Tee.

“Your $29,000 net payout is fixed and unconditional. It cannot be withheld or reduced due to buyer finance or any dealership dealings with the purchaser.”

Phillips also wrote, “The purchaser’s funds have been received and are securely held prior to the vehicle being shipped.”

The email said Tee could expect payment within seven to 10 business days of the buyer taking delivery. The delivery date remains unconfirmed.

Tee said that when he later contacted Phillips about the payment, Phillips told him he had resigned. Tee said he then resigned from his own job as a CNC machinist at a steel company to pursue the money.

Sunday Drive’s PR agency sent Chris Lynch Media a statement announcing the liquidation after the outlet was asked about Tee’s case. Campbell said the company had spent months talking to investors and working towards a sale. Overdue payments had mounted and the business could no longer keep trading while negotiations continued.

“We have let people down, and I’m very sorry for the stress we’ve caused.”

Campbell said he had put his life savings into the business and borrowed personally to keep it going. He acknowledged customers and suppliers were still owed money.

The statement named Baker Tilly Staples Rodway’s Tony Maginness as liquidator. It did not say what had happened to the proceeds from Tee’s Subaru or whether he would receive the $29,000.

When Chris Lynch Media put further questions to Sunday Drive’s PR agency, it replied, “Please refer any enquiries to the liquidator.”

The liquidator has been approached for comment.
