Meghan wears £28,000 outfit at Claridge's with Malala Yousafzai 

TheTools · 08.10.2026 12:19 · source · permalink

Meghan has stepped back into the limelight in London for the first time in years – wearing a £28,000 outfit to take afternoon tea with Malala Yousafzai at Claridge’s.

The Duchess of Sussex, 45, and the Nobel Peace laureate, 29, were photographed leaving the luxury hotel together in Mayfair before sharing a warm hug goodbye outside the foyer yesterday afternoon.

Meghan was wearing a camel Tove Studio coat worth £1,695, a £630 cashmere sweater in wheat by LA-based label CO and Ralph Lauren's £1,700 white satin Masina trousers in 'lux cream'.

She carried a black Chanel handbag worth around £7,000 and wore £795 Saint Laurent ivory and tan ‘Chris’ loafers. Around her neck was Logan Hollowell's Lovers Duet Diamond and Emerald Chain necklace, valued at £16,200. Her entire outfit came to just more than £28,000.

In a striking contrast to her Chanel arm candy, the Duchess also carried a canvas tote bag featuring a striking design depicting women wearing hijabs. 

The pair enjoyed a classic British afternoon tea yesterday with fellow diners saying they ate cucumber sandwiches and scones with jam and clotted cream, with the scones apparently being a favourite of Meghan's.

Meghan and Malala first joined forces in October 2020 for a virtual discussion on the International Day of the Girl, when they spoke about girls' education.

Their London meet-up is believed to have been Meghan's first public appearance in the capital since Queen Elizabeth II's funeral in September 2022.

Months earlier Meghan and Prince Harry were met by some boos as they arrived at St Paul's Cathedral for a service of thanksgiving to mark the Queen's Platinum Jubilee.

While the Duchess has transited through Heathrow on several occasions since then, she is understood to have avoided central London during the ongoing concerns about her security.

Malala, originally from Pakistan but now living in Birmingham, was shot by the Taliban in 2012 for speaking up about young female schooling and went on to win the Nobel Peace Prize in 2014 – the youngest ever recipient at 17 – before going to Oxford University.

Meghan's focus over the past week has been on the latest As Ever launches but this latest outing suggests she may be preparing to return to philanthropic work, perhaps in the field of girls' education, now that she is back in the UK.

Meghan and Malala dined in Claridge's world-famous Foyer and Reading Room, where afternoon tea costs £95 per person. Champagne is available for £30 more but Malala, a Muslim who does not drink alcohol, and Meghan appeared to stick to tea and mineral water.

Set in the hotel's famed Art Deco interiors and accompanied by live piano music, it is one of Mayfair's most exclusive and celebrated dining rooms.

Fellow diners discreetly took photographs of the pair as they chatted animatedly over their meal, according to a hotel insider. The women were polite but barely interacted with other attendees as they were surrounded by protection officers. No one knows yet what they were talking about.

The source said they were accompanied by security throughout the visit. Police vehicles were spotted outside Claridge's during their meet-up, identifiable by their distinctive number plate lights.

The meeting comes amid Harry and Meghan's ongoing battle to get taxpayer-funded police protection in Britain.

Meghan and Malala's discussion in 2020 came during the Covid pandemic, with Harry also joining the virtual talk on barriers to education for young women. 

Meghan said on the call: 'When young girls have access to education, everyone wins, and everyone succeeds. It just opens the door for societal success at the highest level.

'What I had realised very early on was that when women have a seat at the table, conversations in terms of policy change, conversations in terms of legislation and the dynamics of the community are all shifted. 

'And when you have to see how you get a woman to embrace her voice, you have to start with where she is a young girl.' 

Meanwhile, Harry said he had taken his own access to education for granted. 'We do take it for granted, and it is a privilege, but every single person, every single child, every single young person needs an education,' he said.
