Red-hot Hawks blow finals series apart in boilover over Dockers

TheTools · 03.09.2026 17:47 · source · permalink

Hawthorn has soared into a home preliminary final after dismantling Fremantle at Optus Stadium

MINOR premier Fremantle's flag hopes have been dealt a major blow as Hawthorn produced a smash-and-grab masterclass with a 32-point win in Thursday's qualifying final.

Sam Mitchell's Hawks will progress to a home preliminary final in a fortnight while Fremantle will take the harder path, hosting the winner of Carlton-Geelong in the first semi-final next weekend.

DOCKERS v HAWKS Full match coverage and stats

The qualifying final marked Hawthorn's first since 2018, when it went out in straight sets, with the 10.12 (72) to 5.10 (40) victory putting it in the perfect position for a first premiership since the last of its treble in 2015, with Mitchell in that side.

Hawthorn finished with fewer inside 50s and fewer disposals but applied unrelenting pressure all night. It was an even spread for the Hawks, producing a mature display across the board, restricting Fremantle to only five goals for the game.

The triumph ended the Dockers' unbeaten 12-0 record at Optus Stadium this season at the most inopportune time for the hosts.

The Hawks went to work in the first half, kicking six goals to one, opening up an unassailable 33-point half-time lead to silence the crowd of 59,446 fans, Fremantle's best ever at home.

The defeat also extended Justin Longmuir's wait for a finals win, dating back to 2022, having lost a last-gasp home elimination final to Gold Coast last year, posing further questions about their readiness for a premiership assault.

The Dockers and Hawks clash in the first qualifying final of the 2026 Toyota AFL Premiership Season

In slippery conditions, Freo struggled with Hawthorn's heat, over-using the ball. The Hawks forced turnovers and looked dangerous on every entry led by Nick Watson, Dylan Moore and Jack Gunston (all two goals each).

Fremantle has rallied from second-half deficits throughout this season, but it couldn't find a way back against a resilient Hawks who had all the answers when the questions were posed.

Karl Worner's desperate tackle ignites Fremantle, but Dylan Moore quickly flips any momentum with a cracking goal

Hawks co-captain James Sicily (26 disposals, 10 marks, 12 intercepts) was a brick wall in defence, while Will Day – playing his first final after missing the previous two campaigns due to injury – was tough in the contest with 14 contested possessions from 24 disposals.

Watson was a constant menace, with Longmuir deploying the recalled Brandon Walker on him from the get-go. Walker had his moments, before a third-term ankle injury ended his night, but Watson was afforded too much space, proving critical to the win.

A dejected Brandon Walker is forced out of the game in the third term after rolling his ankle

For Fremantle, Jordan Clark, Andy Brayshaw and Neil Erasmus never stopped trying but it wasn't enough as the Dockers slumped to their third loss in four games.

In the first half, Freo had more of the footy but struggled to transition, while the Hawks' finals-like pressure forced turnovers, underlined by 10 tackles inside 50 before half-time.

The Dockers' famed triple threat were mere bystanders as Freo recorded its lowest first-half score in a final ever, despite only trailing on inside 50s 29-25.

It was a first half of few highlights for the hosts, although 150-gamer Clark's spectacular clunk, which posterised enigmatic Hawks small forward Jack Ginnivan, offered a bright spot.

Jordan Clark launches over a Hawk to pull down a spectacular grab and give the Dockers a much-needed spark

Earlier, the Dockers kicked the opening goal through All-Australian Luke Jackson but the first term was all Hawthorn. The Hawks booted the next four playing smart finals footy.

The sell-out home crowd was hushed but still hopeful, only for goals from the canny Gunston followed by the industrious Moore to extend the lead to 33 points at half-time.

Luke Jackson drifts forward for a strong mark before finishing truly to spark the home crowd

Amiss' goal within a minute of the restart sparked the crowd, but Hawthorn replied through Ginnivan. Freo kept coming with goals from Brayshaw and the retiring Sam Switkowski but Hawthorn replied within minutes each time to maintain its buffer at the final break.

Harry Morrison's goal early in the last, after more Watson magic on the wing, all but settled matters.

No Wharfie Time rally
The Dockers trailed by 33 points at half-time, with only one goal on the board, yet given the side's home record this season and propensity for barnstorming second halves, the Freo faithful had hope. Coming into the game, Freo boasted the best second-half differential (446) in the competition, ahead of Sydney (370) and Brisbane (251). The optimism was fuelled after Jye Amiss' goal 58 seconds into the second half but the rally never came, nor did "Wharfie Time" which has grown in reputation in its own conspicuous absence.

Hawks trump triple threat
Freo's forward triple threat had combined for 153 goals coming into Thursday's game, spread between Amiss (58 goals), Josh Treacy (54) and Patrick Voss (41). But they were all kept quiet by Tom Barrass, Josh Battle and Sicily respectively who all clearly won their individual battles. Battle was a standout, denying All-Australian centre-half forward Treacy a single mark for the game. Treacy only got his first kick with five minutes left, resulting in a consolation goal when the game was done and dusted. 

James Sicily produces a commanding performance, racking up 26 disposals and 10 marks to repeatedly repel Fremantle and lead his side into a prelim

ARC OK with Wizard opener
The back page of Thursday's local paper was emblazoned with "No Wizards Allowed", so creative Hawks forward Nick Watson was always going to be a talking point through the night. That came early when Watson soccered Hawthorn's opener, crumbing a pack with devastating effect. But replays suggested Heath Chapman's finger grazed the ball after Watson made contact. The game was delayed as the ARC took a look, yet ticked it off, allowing the goal to stand. In the end, it was academic with the Wizard and Hawks way too good.
