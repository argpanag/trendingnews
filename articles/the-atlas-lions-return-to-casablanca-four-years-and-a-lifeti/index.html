The Atlas Lions Return to Casablanca, Four Years and a Lifetime Later

TheTools · 12.11.2026 14:38 · source · permalink

Casablanca – Morocco will return to Casablanca’s Mohammed V Stadium on Friday for their 2027 Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON) qualifier against Niger, marking the national team’s first appearance at the venue since June 2022.

Much has happened in those four years. Morocco have reached two consecutive World Cup knockout stages, changed national team coaches twice, and introduced a new generation of players. Mohammed V Stadium, meanwhile, has undergone extensive renovations and hosted matches during the 2025 AFCON.

Friday’s encounter offers Casablanca supporters an opportunity to welcome back a national team whose achievements and expectations have changed considerably since its last visit.

The last time Morocco played at Mohammed V Stadium

March 29, 2022, remains one of the stadium’s most memorable nights in recent years.

Morocco defeated DR Congo 4-1 in the second leg of their World Cup qualifying playoff to book a place at Qatar 2022.

Azzedine Ounahi scored twice, while Tarik Tissoudali and Achraf Hakimi added the remaining goals in front of the Casablanca crowd.

Vahid Halilhodzic was still Morocco’s head coach, with the Atlas Lions preparing for what would become a historic World Cup campaign.

The national team’s final appearance at Mohammed V Stadium that year came on June 13, when they defeated Liberia 2-0 in an AFCON qualifier.

Although the match took place in Casablanca, Liberia were officially the home side because they lacked an approved stadium.

Fayçal Fajr and Youssef En-Nesyri scored Morocco’s goals that evening.

Months later, Walid Regragui replaced Halilhodzic and led Morocco to the semi-finals of the 2022 FIFA World Cup, making the Atlas Lions the first African team to reach that stage.

Four years later, Morocco reached the quarter-finals of the 2026 World Cup before losing 2-0 to France.

A renovated Mohammed V Stadium awaits

Casablanca’s historic stadium has also experienced significant changes since Morocco last played there.

Opened in 1955 and affectionately known as “Donor”, Mohammed V Stadium has long been associated with the city’s football culture, particularly the rivalry between Raja and Wydad.

After closing in October 2023, the complex underwent major renovation work between March 2024 and March 2025.

According to figures from the National Agency for Public Equipment (ANEP), the project involved replacing 45,000 seats, installing a new hybrid pitch, and upgrading the dressing rooms, lighting, surveillance systems, and media facilities.

The stadium also got new access arrangements, hospitality areas, and facilities for players, journalists, and supporters. 

The stadium reopened on April 12, 2025, with the Casablanca derby between Raja and Wydad.

It subsequently served as one of the venues for AFCON 2025, hosting group-stage fixtures, a round-of-16 match, and the third-place playoff.

Friday’s fixture will therefore mark the national team’s return to a stadium that has already welcomed domestic and continental football since its renovation.

A different generation returns to Casablanca 

Morocco’s national team coach Mohamed Ouahbi, appointed in March 2026 after Regragui’s departure, now leads a squad combining experienced internationals with younger players.

Ouahbi previously guided Morocco to the 2025 FIFA U-20 World Cup title in Chile, where the Atlas Cubs defeated Argentina 2-0 in the final.

Speaking about the upcoming match during his latest press conference, Ouahbi recalled watching Morocco’s 2022 victory over DR Congo on television.

“Playing in Casablanca will be a first for me,” he said.

The coach also explained that bringing the national team to different cities would allow more supporters to watch their players in person.

“We also want to give all regions of Morocco the opportunity to watch the national team up close,” he said, expressing confidence that the Atlas Lions would receive strong support wherever they played.

Ouahbi added that his priority was to repay that support through the team’s performances on the pitch.

His latest senior squad includes established names such as Hakimi, En-Nesyri, and Brahim Diaz, alongside younger options including Yassir Zabiri and newly called-up Ayoub Oufkir.

Several players who featured in Morocco’s 2022 matches at Mohammed V Stadium remain involved, but the team’s technical leadership and attacking options have changed.

Morocco enter Friday’s encounter at the top of Group A in the 2027 AFCON qualifiers, with six points from victories over Gabon and Lesotho.

The Atlas Lions will face Niger again on November 17 at Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat. Niger will officially be the home team in that match, despite playing on Moroccan soil because the country does not currently have an approved stadium.

For Casablanca supporters, Friday’s fixture brings the national team back to a familiar ground after more than four years. The last time Morocco played there, the celebrations centered on reaching the World Cup. Now, the team returns with two more World Cup campaigns behind it and a different set of expectations.
