Apple Ships iOS 27.2 With Multilingual Siri AI, Adds Apple Pencil Support to iPhone Duo — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 26.10.2026 23:35 · source · permalink

Apple began distributing iOS 27.2 on October 27, a broad software release that expands its rebuilt Siri AI assistant to five additional languages and gives the company's first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, support for the Apple Pencil.

The update reaches users in Japan and other markets, with the same Siri AI feature set also arriving on watchOS 27.2 and macOS 27.2. Apple simultaneously pushed 27.2 releases for iPad, Apple TV, Vision Pro and HomePod, according to Engadget.

Siri AI widens its language base

The headline addition is the more conversational Siri AI, which Apple says can grasp a user's personal context, carry out actions inside apps, read what is on screen and draw on broad general knowledge. Support extends to French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish, with more languages to follow. The company has said the feature is unavailable in the European Union and mainland China.

Hardware eligibility is limited. Siri AI runs on every iPhone 16 model and later, plus the iPhone 15 Pro and iPhone 15 Pro Max. The remaining iOS 27.2 changes are available across all iPhones.

A dedicated Siri app lets users scroll back through earlier exchanges or start new ones, and conversations sync privately across Apple devices through iCloud. The assistant can now pull details from finer-grained sources, including photos sent in Messages, attendees listed on Calendar invitations and voicemails. Users who want to keep certain software out of the assistant's reach can block individual apps through the Excluded Apps setting, and a new granular text selection tool allows partial copying of Siri's answers.

On the camera side, a Siri mode folds in visual intelligence, so the assistant can answer questions about an object in front of the lens, split a bill or look up the nutritional content of a dish. A writing tool, marketed in Japan as "Siri de Sakubun," drafts and edits text wherever a keyboard appears; in Messages and Mail it adjusts tone to match the recipient by referencing earlier correspondence.

Duo gains Pencil and a ring light

The iPhone Duo, which reached users only days before the release, picks up two features of its own. It now works with the Apple Pencil (USB-C), the only stylus model the device supports. The pencil functions on both the outer and inner displays for annotating PDFs and images, sketching, and capturing handwritten notes, including scribble input in any text field.

Most observers had expected Pencil compatibility to take considerably longer after Apple unveiled the foldable last month, 9to5Mac reported. The second exclusive is Edge Light, a feature previously confined to macOS that rings the display with illumination to brighten a user's face during video calls. It works with FaceTime and third-party apps such as Zoom, and pairs with the Duo's laptop-style folding mode for hands-free calling.

Other additions in the release touch Apple's stock apps. Shared Albums in Photos now supports editing captions and comments, and Messages offers suggestions for contextual actions based on the text of a conversation. Photos and Image Playground can also read Google's SynthID watermark, which flags images created or modified with generative AI.

Apple cautioned that some features may not be available depending on region or iPhone model.

Note: Apple said certain features may be unavailable in some regions or on some iPhone models.

The rollout puts Apple's AI strategy in front of a far larger audience at a moment when rivals are racing to localize assistants for non-English markets. Extending Siri AI to Japanese, Korean, French, Portuguese and Spanish widens the addressable base considerably, though the exclusion of the EU and mainland China leaves two of the world's largest smartphone markets outside the tent.

For the iPhone Duo, the quick arrival of Pencil support matters beyond convenience. A foldable's inner screen is its main selling point, and stylus input is one of the few capabilities that distinguishes a large folding display from a conventional handset. Delivering it within days of launch gives Apple a stronger argument to buyers weighing the device against rival foldables.

Developers, meanwhile, face a fresh round of testing. App actions routed through Siri AI and the new in-app context sources change how software surfaces information, and the Excluded Apps control gives users a way to cut off access entirely.
