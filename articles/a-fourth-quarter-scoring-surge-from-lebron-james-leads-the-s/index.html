A fourth-quarter scoring surge from LeBron James leads the Sixers to a three-point win against the Milwaukee Bucks

TheTools · 21.11.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

James had eight points through three quarters, but scored 13 points in the fourth to improve the Sixers to an Eastern Conference-best 14-3 record.

    Perhaps LeBron James was pacing himself on Saturday afternoon or hoping that he wouldn’t have to dig deep on a day in which the 76ers were playing the basement-dwelling Milwaukee Bucks.

    But James came up big in the clutch as the Sixers hung on to beat a pesky Bucks squad, 132-129, at Xfinity Mobile Arena.

    James had eight points through three quarters, but scored 13 in the fourth to lift the Sixers to an Eastern Conference-leading 14-3 record, while the 1-16 Bucks lost their 13th straight game.

    James gave the Sixers some separation on the scoreboard by attacking the rim, with and without the ball. One of the defining buckets of that stretch came on a feed from Joel Embiid that led to a James dunk with 4 minutes, 24 seconds remaining. In addition to his 21 points, James finished with six rebounds and six assists.

    His fourth quarter helped the Sixers overcome the Bucks’ Tyler Herro, who finished with a career-high 50 points. The Sixers struggled to guard Herro for much of the afternoon. Herro’s ability to score as a movement shooter and off the dribble gave the Sixers fits while down two point-of-attack defenders in Jaylen Brown and Dean Wade (both out with hamstring injuries).

    Herro appeared to hit a three from the right wing that would have tied the game as time expired, but upon review, officials said the shot came after the buzzer. Officials appeared to miss Embiid stepping out of bounds before he was awarded two free throws with 1.5 seconds remaining. Milwaukee did not have a challenge left.

    Technically, the Sixers improved to 12-0 in clutch games, defined by the NBA as games within five points and under five minutes to go, but this was one they wished never got to that point.

    Barlow returns, Walker more at the four

    Dominick Barlow (12 points, four rebounds) made his season debut after missing the first 16 games with a lower back injury. He entered the rotation immediately with Wade and Adem Bona both out for Saturday.

    He appeared to be moving well and got his first bucket on a tip-in early in the second quarter. Then on the next possession he got a lob on a fast break from James, who had instant chemistry with Barlow. At first, Barlow and Jabari Walker shared the floor when Barlow took the court, with Walker shifting more into his natural four role. Barlow is also more suited to the four, but on this team, with the Sixers needing help in the frontcourt behind Embiid, Barlow was playing more at the five, as coach Nick Nurse said he would.

    Barlow again entered the game at the start of the fourth and hit a corner three and got a pair of layups off passes from James before passing up a potential open look at a three later in the quarter.

    Walker, meanwhile, played more at the four than he has at almost any point this season. Walker posted nine points, seven rebounds, and four assists.

    Simons gives lift off bench

    For nearly 36 minutes, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope and Anfernee Simons were the only Sixers to hit a three.

    Before VJ Edgecombe hit one from the right corner with 47.1 seconds remaining in the third, Simons and Caldwell-Pope were a combined 7-for-12, while the rest of the team was 0-for-11.

    Simons finished with 18 points, including four threes off the bench.

    Slow start shooting for Maxey, Embiid

    It was a tough shooting start for both Tyrese Maxey and Embiid, who were a combined 2-for-9 through one quarter and 6-for-17 at halftime. Embiid got going late in the second quarter after the Sixers were down seven, with a dunk in transition that bloodied the face of Herro and later had another three-point play.

    One of the few weak spots amid the Sixers’ strong start to the season has been their starts (28th in first-quarter net rating).

    Outside of his first-half shooting struggles, Embiid did have nine rebounds and five assists. Maxey overcame his 2-for-7 half by going 7-for-7 at the line to put the Sixers up 60-59 at the half. However, the first half belonged to the Sixers despite shooting just 3-for-16 (19%) from three-point range.
