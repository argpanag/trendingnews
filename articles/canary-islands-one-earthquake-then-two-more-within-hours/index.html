Canary Islands: one earthquake, then two more within hours

TheTools · 22.11.2026 04:33 · source · permalink
  • On Sunday, the IGN recorded three minor earthquakes off the Canary Islands in less than six hours.
  • The strongest earthquake, measuring 2.5 on the Richter scale, occurred around 90 kilometres off Lanzarote and was above the 30-day average.
  • The authorities regard low-level seismic activity on the volcanic islands as normal and monitor it continuously.

On Sunday, the Spanish National Geographic Institute (IGN) recorded several earthquakes off the Canary Islands, the strongest of which had a magnitude of 2.5. It occurred at 4.04 am, shaking the seabed around 90 kilometres off Lanzarote, with the epicentre at a depth of 30 kilometres. The European-Mediterranean Seismological Centre (EMSC) is also analysing the IGN’s data.

This was followed by two weaker tremors off the coast of Tenerife. At 5.45 am, the IGN recorded a tremor measuring 2.0 on the Richter scale at a depth of 42 kilometres, approximately 40 kilometres off the island, and at 9.39 am another, measuring 2.1, struck around 20 kilometres off Tenerife, with its epicentre at a depth of 22 kilometres. Less than six hours elapsed between the first and the last of the three tremors.

In any case, the week was slightly more active than average, as the IGN recorded 17 earthquakes in the Canary Islands region over the past seven days – slightly more than usual. Calculated over 30 days, the figures total 55 earthquakes, an average of 1.8 per day.

Canary Islands: Minor earthquakes are considered normal activity

Over the same 30-day period, the average magnitude of the earthquakes in the Canary Islands was 2.0. The earthquake off Lanzarote, with a magnitude of 2.5, therefore stood out slightly, whilst the two tremors off Tenerife remained close to the average. As the islands are of volcanic origin, low levels of seismic activity are considered normal there.

Experts from the IGN and the Canary Islands Volcanological Institute (INVOLCAN) are keeping a close eye on seismic activity. Seismologists and geologists do not automatically interpret a cluster of small earthquakes as indicating a heightened risk, but they are nevertheless monitoring the situation closely.

Earlier, at 7.05 am on Saturday, the IGN had recorded an earthquake near El Hierro with a magnitude of 2.2; the epicentre was 17 kilometres deep. On Tuesday at 10.02 am, the earth trembled around 40 kilometres off Gran Canaria with a magnitude of 2.5 at a depth of 5 kilometres. On 16 November 2026 at 3.02 am, the stations recorded an earthquake near La Palma with a magnitude of 2.0; its epicentre was 1 kilometre deep.
