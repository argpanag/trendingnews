ECPI okay for non-reversionary pensions

TheTools · 18.11.2026 03:26 · source · permalink

News

SMSFs still have the ability to claim exempt current pension income for a non-reversionary pension of a member who has passed away.

A senior SMSF stakeholder has confirmed the ability to claim exempt current pension income (ECPI) continues to exist for a non-reversionary pension after the death of a member, even though technically the income stream has ceased.

“A lot of people get this wrong from an ECPI point of view. People will say: ’Oh well, the pension ceased today [when the member died] so [there is no pension interest] tomorrow, therefore the fund doesn’t get ECPI,’” Accurium senior SMSF educator Anthony Cullen told attendees of a technical webinar held last week.

“[They also say:] ‘I didn’t draw enough pension payments out up until today [when the member passed away so] I don’t get ECPI either.’

“That is not the case. The laws were changed back in 2013 that basically has allowed us to operate in this environment [where] even though the pension technically ceases, the fund still has an ability to claim ECPI against that interest, provided that the benefits are cashed as soon as practicable.”

Cullen pointed out Accurium is still seeing situations where a misunderstanding of the rules leads to accountants commuting a pension because a member has died, rolling that money back into accumulation phase and not claiming ECPI.

“That is another myth that we need to make sure we don’t get caught [out with],” he said.

According to Cullen, it is best practice for administrative purposes to continue keeping the assets supporting that income stream isolated from the other holdings of the SMSF.

“Even though the pension might cease today because the member passed away today and their spouse might start a death benefit pension in three months’ time, the tax-free and taxable components are locked in,” he noted.

He indicated doing so will also be beneficial for the operation of the administration software the fund is using.

‘[Leaving the non-reversionary income stream as a pension interest] will allow the system to allocate any earnings in the proportion of the tax-free [and] taxable component. It will make it easier to claim ECPI and apply for [any actuarial] certificates if you need to,” he said.

“[This is] opposed to putting it back into accumulation phase and potentially mixing it or blending it with other non-reversionary pensions or an accumulation interest that was always in existence as well.”
