Leeds dominate Chelsea for first Stamford Bridge win since 1999

Noah Okafor scored twice to deliver Leeds' first win at Chelsea since 1999 and propel the visitors up to sixth in the Premier League.

Chelsea, looking to bounce back from defeat at Sunderland, struck first through Emmanuel Emegha on his full debut.

The Netherlands forward, who joined in the summer, scored his first goal for the club in the 28th minute, lashing home a left-footed strike.

But Okafor levelled three minutes later, taking advantage of the Blues' scrambling backline to slide into the far corner.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin spurned several chances to register in the first half - hitting the crossbar and having two headers saved - but made no mistake from the penalty spot early in the second half.

Referee Michael Oliver awarded the spot-kick when Morgan Rogers was deemed to have used his arm to block Jayden Bogle's attempt at goal, and Calvert-Lewin confidently found the bottom corner.

Leeds were in full control in the 58th minute when Okafor hooked a bobbling ball into the roof of the net.

Xabi Alonso swiftly made a double change, bringing on Danny Welbeck and Pedro Neto, but Chelsea's woes in front of goal continued as Leeds held firm.

Brenden Aaronson put the icing on the cake for Leeds deep into stoppage time, breaking clear from the halfway line and slotting into the bottom corner.

Chelsea slip to eighth in the league with Leeds climbing to sixth, just a point shy of fourth-placed Bournemouth.

Chelsea analysis: Pressure building on Alonso

Xabi Alonso post-match interview - Chelsea v Leeds United

Chelsea's attack was firing at the start of the season, with 15 goals in their first six games, but the Blues have struggled to maintain that threat.

The absence of joint-top scorer Joao Pedro is clearly being felt and Alonso has spent the past few weeks trying to find a solution.

Welbeck, Neto and Morgan Rogers have all been deployed through the middle - with little success as Chelsea had failed to score in three of their past four across all competitions - but Emegha offered hope their fortunes could be about to turn.

Injuries have restricted the 23-year-old's game time this term, but he marked his first league start with a fine strike to break the deadlock.

But he was unable to impose himself further on the game as Chelsea found it difficult to work beyond Leeds' high press during a competitive first half.

After losing just one of their opening six games across all competitions under Alonso, Chelsea have now lost five of their past eight.

Boos could be heard from some Chelsea fans in the latter stages, suggesting patience is already starting to wear thin with the Spanish boss who only took charge in the summer.

Leeds analysis: Whites dreaming of Europe

Daniel Farke post-match interview - Chelsea v Leeds United

The last time Leeds won at Stamford Bridge in December 1999, they moved above Manchester United to top the Premier League standings.

Although there is no talk of title contention - at this stage - Leeds are quietly building momentum and on the fringes of a European place.

Their strong start to the season has been built largely on the foundations of a strong defensive line - only Arsenal and Brighton have conceded fewer than their tally of nine.

Leeds experienced just how prolific Chelsea could be earlier this season when losing 6-3 at Stamford Bridge in the Carabao Cup, but had clearly learned a lesson and kept the Blues at bay.

Calvert-Lewin led the line as an archetypal number nine should, bullying Chelsea's defenders and bringing his team-mates into play.

The England striker got his reward from the penalty spot after earlier chances went begging, but Okafor was the star of the show.

The Switzerland forward showcased different qualities for each of his strikes - wriggling beyond defenders for his first and emphatically smashing home his second.
