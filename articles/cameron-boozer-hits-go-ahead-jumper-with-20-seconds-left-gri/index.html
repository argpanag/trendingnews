Cameron Boozer hits go-ahead jumper with 20 seconds left, Grizzlies rally to beat Warriors 111-108

SAN FRANCISCO (AP) — Cameron Boozer made a go-ahead jumper with 20 seconds left and finished with 25 points and 13 rebounds, and the Memphis Grizzlies came from 12 points down over the final 3:47 to beat the Golden State Warriors 111-108 on Friday night.

After De'Anthony Melton's 3-pointer with 3:47 left put the Warriors ahead 106-94, Golden State missed nine shots and went without a field goal the rest of the game.

Stephen Curry scored 24 points, rookie Yaxel Lendeborg added 18 points, 13 rebounds and six assists in his home debut, but the Warriors blew it as Memphis capitalized with a 17-0 run.

Ty Jerome followed up his career-high 45-point performance in a 123-103 season-opening win Wednesday at home against Utah with 28 points.

Jerome's 3-pointer with 1:24 left pulled Memphis to 106-105. Curry had a 3 blocked by Zach Edey, then Jaylen Wells tied it at 106 on a free throw.

Curry and Brandin Podziemski each missed 3s before Boozer delivered. Podziemski added 18 points, hitting a go-ahead 3-pointer with 2:09 left in the third quarter and also a late layup as Golden State took a 90-84 lead into the fourth.

The testy matchup featured a pair of double-technicals — first on Grizzlies big man Isaiah Stewart and Golden State's Draymond Green 3:51 before halftime.

Lendeborg picked up his second technical foul in two career games after a double-technical with Jerome at the 9:45 mark of the third quarter.

Coach Steve Kerr said he would like to see Lendeborg fun the floor better. The forward has drawn his share of attention already after he said he “lost all respect” for Luka Doncic and called out the Lakers star for “complaining” in a season-opening 127-105 loss at Los Angeles.

The game featured two top rookies — No. 3 pick Boozer and Lendeborg, selected 11th overall.

And the Warriors started two-way center Graham Ike to help combat the Grizzlies' size. That gave the team two rookie starters in a home opener for the first time since the NBA began recording starters in 1970-71.

Podziemski briefly went to the locker room after being hit in the head by Edey after scoring on a layup at the 8:36 mark of the opening quarter.

Grizzlies: At Sacramento on Sunday night.

Warriors: At Phoenix on Saturday night.

MILWAUKEE (AP) — Jacob Misiorowski stepped on the mound for a World Series debut like no other.

“I had so much adrenaline built up,” he said.

He threw a pitch the likes of which had never been seen in the Fall Classic.

The Miz broke the World Series velocity record with his very first delivery, a 103.5 mph fastball to the Tampa Bay Rays’ Yandy Díaz that was taken high out of the strike zone for a ball. He allowed one run over five innings to get his first postseason win as a starter, helping the Milwaukee Brewers to a 3-2 victory in Friday night’s opener.

“I don’t even know how to tell you how I felt,” the 24-year-old right-hander said a half-hour after the end of a crazy ninth inning, prolonged by a game-extending video review. “I still don’t know how I felt. I feel like I’m still trying to take everything in and understand what happened.”

Misiorowski gave up four hits and one walk, striking out a season-low three.

Aroldis Chapman of the Chicago Cubs had the previous mark for fastest Series pitch at 103.2 mph in the ninth inning of Game 2 in 2016, an offering that resulted in a groundout to second by Cleveland’s Coco Crisp. The previous high by a Series starting pitcher since tracking began in 2008 was 101.1 mph by the New York Mets’ Noah Syndergaard to Kansas City’s Ben Zobrist that was taken for a ball in the first inning of Game 3 in 2015.

Miz reached 100 mph with 38 pitches, topping the previous Series high of 24 by Chapman in Game 5 of 2016. The most by a starting pitcher in the Series had been six by Tampa Bay’s Tyler Glasnow in the 2020 opener against the Los Angeles Dodgers.

Tampa Bay scored in the fourth when Jonathan Aranda walked, took third on Junior Caminero’s double over center fielder Garrett Mitchell and came around on Liam Hicks’ grounder to first baseman Jake Bauers.

Nearing the end of his first full major league season, Misiorowski has reached 100 mph with 134 of 260 postseason pitches. But his spin rate was down, a sign of a season in which he has pitched 188 innings — up from 141 1/3 innings last year. And he got just seven swings and misses, five on his fastball.

Tampa Bay batters fouled off 20 of his 78 pitches and tipped another.

During the regular season, Misiorowski reached 100 mph with 1,115 pitches, including a high of 105.5 mph to the Chicago Cubs’ Pete Crow-Armstrong on June 27.

“What we’re used to seeing in the regular season was Miz just mow through people and have two strikeouts an inning. That’s unrealistic,” Brewers manager Pat Murphy said. “This is the best team in the American League.”

Miz's first pitch was so fast the scoreboard at American Family Field couldn't keep up, flashing 103.4 mph — one-tenth of a mile less than it registered on MLB’s Statcast system.

“He’s such a special pitcher,” Rays manager Kevin Cash said. “I felt like for him to be out of the game at five innings, there’s a small win in that.”

Misiorowski threw fastballs on 10 of his first 12 pitches, then mixed in more offspeed stuff. In all he tossed 48 fastballs, 17 cutters, seven sliders and six curveballs.

Miz finished with 30 pitches at 101 mph or higher, 11 of 102 mph or more and three at 103-plus mph.

Warming up in the bullpen, he couldn't take in the introductions before Milwaukee's first World Series game in 44 years. He thought about how far he had come, how he was at Double-A Biloxi two years ago.

“It was crazy," he said. “It’s everything I dreamed of.”
