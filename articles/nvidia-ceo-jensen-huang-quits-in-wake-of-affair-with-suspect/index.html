Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang quits in wake of affair with suspected Chinese spy, as firm's stocks plunge

TheTools · 12.11.2026 18:08 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Jensen Huang has resigned as CEO of tech giant Nvidia, the company's board says, with chief financial officer Colette Kress appointed interim boss

  • The company's shares have recovered some ground after initially plummeting more than 13% when US markets opened shortly after news of Huang's resignation

  • It comes after the New York Times reported that he had a second affair with an executive at his own company

  • On Sunday, Huang admitted an affair with a woman, who was reportedly from another company and suspected to be a Chinese spy

  2. Singapore probed Alice Huang and banned her from the countrypublished at 17:45 GMT
    17:45 GMT

    Tessa Wong
    Asia Digital Reporter, Singapore

    Days after the Nvidia scandal first broke, the Singapore government has given a statement regarding the suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang and Megaspeed, the Singaporean company she was associated with.

    The statement says that Huang was never investigated for espionage.

    However, she was investigated last year as part of a probe into several companies including Megaspeed, over potential false declarations in relation to the purchase of computer servers.

    Authorities said they took her passport in June 2025 to ensure she could not leave Singapore during the investigations.

    In February this year, authorities concluded there was insufficient evidence that Huang had committed offences regarding the potential false declarations.

    However, they found she had "provided information which she knew to be inaccurate... to downplay her role in Megaspeed".

    Authorities gave her a stern warning and banned her from Singapore. They returned her passport on 26 February, and she left the same day.

    Earlier, Megaspeed published a statement saying the company "takes its compliance obligations under US export control laws extremely seriously".

    The BBC has approached the company for further comment on the statement from the Singaporean government.

  4. Nvidia's next CEO will have more oversight as company chair appointedpublished at 17:00 GMT
    17:00 GMT

    Dearbail Jordan
    Senior business and economics reporter

    Tucked inside the announcement of Jensen Huang’s departure and his temporary replacement, is the news that Nvidia has appointed its first-ever chair.

    This is a big deal. Part of a company chair’s responsibility is to have oversight of the chief executive. Nvidia has gone without one for more than 30 years.

    Huang often lauded the company’s “flat” management structure. “If you want to empower people, you want to make the organisation as flat as possible so that information travels quickly,” he once said.

    Huang had dozens of people reporting to him, which may well have sped up the flow of information and decision-making but also resulted in an extraordinary concentration of power in one man’s hands.

    Now, Nvidia’s new boss, whoever that ends up being in the long term, will have someone overseeing them - in this case, Harvey Jones, a tech industry veteran who was the former boss of Synopsys, a chip design software maker.

  5. Nvidia not the only company to suffer amid Huang's resignationpublished at 16:44 GMT
    16:44 GMT

    Anuk Weerawardana
    Business reporter

    Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO didn't just affect Nvidia's share price today but also that of companies that previously benefitted from its hype.

    Neocloud companies like CoreWeave and Nebius - essentially companies that rent out data centres to AI developers - dropped roughly 5% and 16% in share value respectively after the US market opened today.

    Both companies had previously received large investments from Nvidia and now rent out its hardware to manage AI workloads.

    "Nvidia became such a crucial cog in the machine that it has become an investor, financier and ultimately strategic backer of a lot of companies involved in the supply chain," says Ben Barringer, head of technology research at investment platform Quilter Cheviot.

    Nvidia is one of the "key mechanisms" that keep the investment cycle moving so any bump in the road could mean ripples across the wider AI ecosystem.

    Yet, Barringer tells me there is still a high probability that the chipmaker will continue to support companies that ultimately buy and use its chips in the long-term.

  10. Nvidia scandal puts pressure on the Trump administrationpublished at 15:24 GMT
    15:24 GMT

    Bernd Debusmann Jr
    White House reporter in Washington DC

    The fallout from Jensen Huang's personal life and subsequent resignation puts the Trump administration in an extremely difficult place politically.

    President Trump has repeatedly stood by Huang, and earlier this week suggested that Huang might be able to remain in his position.

    But beyond the alleged affairs and Huang's resignation, the wider issues that have come to light this week have the potential to reach far into the Oval Office and the uppermost levels of American political power.

    Even before Huang's resignation, Democrats on Capitol Hill were accusing the administration of being "too deferential" to Nvidia, and of endangering US national security to sell advanced Nvidia H200 chips to China at Huang's urging.

    At the centre of this debate is Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, who reportedly clashed with other administration officials - including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent - over the export controls.

    Democrats may expect him to come testify before Congress about the matter, which could become a potentially embarrassing moment for the White House.

    It's still not clear when exactly Trump was informed of the issue and what he was told, but he is likely to face mounting questions about why Huang was allowed to remain in such close proximity to the administration once he knew.

  16. Huang, who steered Nvidia to enormous success, ultimately undone by his own love lifepublished at 14:20 GMT
    14:20 GMT

    Zoe Kleinman
    Technology and AI editor

    If I’d told you this time last week that Jensen Huang was about to leave Nvidia, you would not have believed me.

    The man who co-founded this enormously successful AI chip company, working tirelessly to build it from the ground up, steering it through some disastrous times with endless energy and ruthless determination, shaping into the giant it has become, ultimately undone by his own love life.

    It’s an extraordinary tale and a strangely old fashioned one for a tech firm with its sights set on the future.

    Colette Kress, who takes over for now, will be a familiar face internally and that must feel reassuring.

    She’s worked at the firm for 13 years and for those who follow Nvidia’s considerable finances, she is often present on earnings calls. She is a billionaire in her own right.

    But she’s not an engineer and will undoubtedly have a very different leadership style to the demanding boss that was Jensen Huang. She will also have to convince investors that its business as usual for Nvidia, and the innovation for which it is renowned will continue at pace.

    A wobbly Nvidia would be bad news for the entire AI industry because its ever-advancing chips drive the most powerful AI models.

    As for Jensen himself - we have to assume the investigation into his relationship with the woman accused of being a Chinese spy will continue, and the results of that may well determine what he does next. He's probably not short of offers at the moment - I expect Nvidia's rivals can't believe their luck.
