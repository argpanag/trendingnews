Ireland star 'would definitely have won All-Irelands' says his former GAA coach

TheTools · 03.10.2026 04:48 · source · permalink

Dara O’Shea, his former Gaelic football coach confidently declares, could easily have a couple of All-Ireland winners’ medals to his name by now.

And St Jude’s GAA club would have a county title if he opted to stay at home, instead of chasing his dream of a career in professional football.

That’s how well regarded Ireland’s stand-in captain is around his old Templeogue outfit.

“Oh God, every day,” says Gerry Carty, currently the senior hurling coach at St Jude’s, when asked if he wishes O’Shea picked Gaelic games over association football.

“I was involved with our senior footballers for seven years, we lost two county finals to Kilmacud Crokes, in 2018 and 2021.

“Would he have been playing in 2018? Probably not. He definitely would have been playing in 2021 and we would have won that county title with Dara on the pitch.

“He would have helped us win that title.

“Gareth Roche was the manager and I was helping manager - there was a big management group there - and I have no doubt that if we had Dara on the pitch that night we would have won the county title.

“We lost by a point in a game where we were five points up with 10 minutes to go.

“No doubt from a club perspective and a county perspective, Dara would definitely have All-Ireland medals and we would probably have a county medal.

“If Dara had stayed in Ireland he would be playing for Dublin now, he would have a couple of All-Ireland medals in his pocket under Dessie Farrell.

“I assume he would have gone for football ahead of the hurling route. He was a very good hurler, but he was naturally a footballer.

“But Dara could play rugby, he did in school. He could play any sport.”

It’s no surprise here that O’Shea is the man chosen to lead Heimir Hallgrímsson’s young squad through the most difficult international window of their careers.

There is one more game to go - a ‘home’ fixture against Israel tomorrow night, over 2,000km southeast of Lansdowne Road - before he hands the armband back to Nathan Collins.

“He always had a little bit of aggression in him. You can see that on the soccer pitch,” says Carty.

“He has evolved into a great communicator as well. I really admire and respect the way he communicates on the pitch.”

The long-time St Jude’s clubman remembers when he first recognised the Ipswich Town defender’s passion for sport.

O’Shea arrived at St Pius X Boys’ school in Terenure in first class and was given the seat next to Gerry’s son John.

“My son had just started in St Jude’s and I was helping in the academy,” says Carty.

“Both Dara’s parents were working and my wife was working from home, so she’d just pick them up.

“We lived behind a GAA pitch, so we used to throw them over the back wall, and we’d throw hurls, rugby balls and footballs over with them and they’d be out there all day.

“Then we’d go up to St Jude’s and he was superb there. Dara played every sport. He played for Terenure Rangers in Bushy Park. He was a great kid.”

His soccer commitments grew when a St Kevin’s coach spotted O’Shea and convinced him to join the illustrious schoolboy side.

“That became a challenge for us,” Carty recalls, “because we played our Gaelic matches on Saturdays and the DDSL played their elite games on the same day.

“But Dara would always show up. He used to go out to the St Kevin’s match, play for them, then he’d leave there and we’d be playing our match in the afternoon and he’d play for us.

“He’d play Gaelic football or hurling. He played both.

“I remember the year they played in the hurling Féile, they were Under-14s. Dara played two matches in midfield, half a match in goal, then left at half-time.

“We were playing in Sandymount and St Kevin’s had an All-Ireland final at Wayside Celtic, so Dara went up at half-time in our third match and played with St Kevin’s.

“We played on and came back to St Jude’s around seven o’clock that night, and who was sitting in the car park with his All-Ireland medal around his neck?

“Dara was waiting for us to come back. They’d won their final, they won 4-0 that day, I believe. The big thing for us was getting the kids back for chips, pizza and cokes.

“That was Dara in a nutshell. He was just a sports addict.”

O’Shea moved to West Brom in 2015, but that wasn’t the end of his GAA playing days.

“He went to Man United and Celtic and all these places, but he picked West Brom,” Carty recalls.

“Tony Pulis was manager at the time and they won over his dad. They were impressed by the way they treated Dara.

“A year later Dara still wasn’t a professional player, he was still a youth in their academy, and he came back and played for St Jude’s in a final and scored four or five goals.”

Carty likens his former player to a Gaelic football legend of the same name (but different spelling).

“He was an out-and-out midfielder in Gaelic football, like the original Darragh Ó Sé,” he says.

“You have to consider, in a GAA house like mine, having a little kid coming into the house called Dara O’Shea was quite funny.

“The Kerry Darragh Ó Sé presented medals at one stage. He was up one day in our academy talking to the kids.

“Dara would be an out-and-out midfielder in my eyes, because of his athletic ability.

“I’d be full sure he’d be a current big-name player with the Dublin senior footballers, because he’d be coming to his absolute peak now.

“But he had to become a professional athlete, because he was so determined to do it. It’s all he ever thought and talked about.

“He’s just a great young lad and everyone at Jude’s is so proud of him.”
