Clement rejects City exhaustion claims ahead of Hornets clash

TheTools · 01.11.2026 16:46 · source · permalink

Norwich City boss Philippe Clement has dismissed suggestions that his high-intensity training regime has left his players burnt out and exhausted ahead of their clash with Watford.

Claims made in the national media ahead of last weekend’s victory at Charlton suggested the City squad were “running on empty” and had been given insufficient time for “rest or recuperation”.

Those issues were cited as contributing to the Canaries’ poor run of results.

Danish striker Mathias Kvistgaarden had already pushed back against those suggestions, insisting the intensity of the training regime was nothing unusual and that the squad remained united and focused on turning their form around.

Clement shares that view and suggested the claims are an easy stick to beat the club with given their poor start to the campaign

“Did you see that against Charlton?

“Yeah, of course. It's the world we live in now. In the moment, you don't take the points that you want to take or that you have to take; people will come up with stories about what the reasons are behind it. But for sure, that's not one of the reasons,” said Clement.

“Yes, they've been working hard, but that's also the only way to get results. But they're not exhausted, for sure not.

“It's also funny because before Charlton they had two days off also in a row. So, people like to make up stories sometimes when you don't get the points.”

External criticism can be difficult to shut out, particularly for young players, but Clement says his squad know the truth and have not been distracted by the claims.

“No, but it's external noise, and they know the truth. They know what's been going on here.

Clement insists his squad are united and focused on building on the Charlton win (Image: Paul Chesterton/Focus Images Ltd)

“They know how united they are with each other, but also with the staff, with all the staff,” admitted Clement.

“So, there's a really good chemistry here, good synergy between everybody, and everybody is desperate to get better results than in these first starting games of the season and to get better results in the next months to come. So, everybody's focused on that and not on external noise.”

The Canaries now face a demanding run of three Championship games in eight days, with trips to Derby County and Bristol City following today’s home meeting with Watford.

That schedule means Clement will need to manage his squad carefully, and he outlined it could affect late decisions over the availability of some players for the Hornets’ visit.

“There are still a few players with some smaller things that we need to judge tomorrow to see if we can put them in the game or not. I will not say too much about names because I don't want to make the job of Watford easier.

“So, we need to take a look at that this afternoon and tomorrow, because it's not only a Saturday game. We have Tuesday again and then Saturday again. So, it's about making smart choices with the squad.”

Pelle Mattsson was withdrawn at half-time during last weekend’s win at Charlton with hamstring tightness (Image: Ben Peters/Focus Images Ltd)

Watford arrive at Carrow Road without an away win this season and are enduring a similarly difficult start to the campaign.

Hornets’ boss, Alessio Dionisi, has recently tinkered with his system and switched to a back five. Clement expects the visitors to stick with that approach but admits he has prepared his players for a back four as well.

“They started this season with four at the back. They had difficulty keeping balance in the team. They brought in a few players with a lot of quality at the last minute, too, that I know, like Krepin Diatta.

“He was on the market because his contract at Monaco ended in the summer. But his wage demand was really, really high at that moment. In the end, he went to Watford.

“I don't know if it's with the same demands that he had in the summer, but he's a really good player. They also took Myron Boadu, who I know from my time at Monaco. So, they have several really quality players,” he said.

“Stephy Mavididi went there too. They already had good wingers, so they have a lot of offensive quality.

“But they struggled to keep balance in the team. Then, in the last two games, they went to a back five. They won against Charlton.

“And then they had the Wolves game, which they lost 1-0, but they conceded only three shots on target. So, yeah, we will see if they come tomorrow with five or four. We're prepared for both.

Watford manager Alessio Dionisi has recently switched formation, moving to a back five (Image: PA)

“But we expect they're going to stick with five because they had success with that. It's going to be hard to break open that wall, and you need to watch out for all the quality they have offensively in transition.”

The Watford clash marks Clement’s 50th game in charge of the Canaries in all competitions since his arrival last November.

The Belgian was unaware of the milestone but is keen to reach many more in the future.

“That goes fast; 50 already, and that's without friendly games. I'm not counting, clearly.

“And it's not about me; it's about this club and where we want to help them, to guide them with a lot of other people in the club, to guide this club where it needs to be.

“So, I hope I can put a lot of extras and maybe even put a zero behind it in the future, because I really love being here.”

The 52-year-old has built a special connection with City fans during his time at the club and is grateful for their support throughout the recent difficult period.

He hopes those inside Carrow Road can replicate the backing shown by the travelling supporters at Charlton last week and help drive his side towards another three points.

Clement has urged Norwich City supporters to get behind the team once again, just as they did at Charlton (Image: Ben Peters/Focus Images Ltd)

“Yeah, I'm really happy about that. But the most important thing is that they support the players. That's the most important thing.

“That's also what my first feeling was after this Charlton game, which was so powerful. We started the game badly, not good on the ball, and not winning the duels.

"Then going behind, even getting a chance against after 20 seconds, but there was not one boo. And the fans really got behind the players to push them and to support them.

“And that was a really big signal. So I hope to have a Carrow Road like that, but that we start the game much better than we did against Charlton, of course.”
