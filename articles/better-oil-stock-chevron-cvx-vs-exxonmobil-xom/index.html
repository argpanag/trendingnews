Chevron (NYSE: CVX) and ExxonMobil (NYSE: XOM) are two of the world's largest integrated energy companies. They both own upstream exploration and extraction businesses, midstream pipelines, and downstream refineries.

Over the past 12 months, Chevron's stock has rallied 45% while ExxonMobil's stock has risen 58%. Both stocks benefited from rising oil prices, but which stock is the better buy right now?

The differences between Chevron and ExxonMobil

Chevron is smaller than ExxonMobil, but both companies generate most of their revenue from their upstream businesses. However, Chevron generates more of its profits (about 80%) from its upstream business than ExxonMobil (about 70%).

That's because ExxonMobil operates a larger downstream business -- which acts as a secondary profit engine -- than Chevron. ExxonMobil's downstream business produces a lot of petrochemicals, while Chevron's downstream business has minimal exposure to that market.

When crude oil prices rise, upstream businesses typically grow their revenues faster than their expenses. However, downstream businesses often struggle as their input costs rise. When crude oil prices decline, upstream businesses struggle, but downstream businesses fare better -- as long as the crack spread (between crude oil costs and retail fuel prices) remains stable.

Therefore, Chevron's streamlined, upstream-heavy business model makes it a more attractive investment than ExxonMobil when oil prices spike. But when oil prices pull back, ExxonMobil's scale and downstream diversification could make it a more compelling investment.

However, the price of Brent crude oil -- currently at $110 per barrel -- remains far above ExxonMobil's breakeven price (for covering capex and dividends) of $35 per barrel and Chevron's breakeven price of $50 per barrel. Both companies plan to increase their oil and gas production by about 3% annually through the end of the decade.

Chevron pays a forward yield of 3.2%, while ExxonMobil pays a lower forward yield of 2.3%. Chevron has raised its dividend annually for 39 consecutive years, while ExxonMobil has maintained that streak for 43 years. If they reach 50 years, they'll become Dividend Kings.

Both companies have plenty of room to raise their dividends. Over the past 12 months, Chevron and ExxonMobil had payout ratios of 67% and 53%, respectively.
