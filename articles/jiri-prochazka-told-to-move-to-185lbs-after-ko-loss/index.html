Jiri Prochazka Told To Move To 185lbs After KO Loss

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:09 · source · permalink

Jiri Prochazka responds to his second consecutive knockout loss at UFC Qatar as Terrance McKinney suggests a move to middleweight.

Jiri Prochazka was coming of a knockout loss to Carlos Ulberg heading into UFC Qatar. The former light heavyweight champion was looking to bounce back and get a rematch for the title. However, his plans were thwarted last night by Navajo Stirling. Ulberg’s training partner knocked out Prochazka in the second round.

Consequently, the Czech light heavyweight has been told to move down weight classes following his second consecutive knockout loss. Lightweight contender Terrance McKinney suggested that the former champion is better suited to the 185lb division while also commending Navajo Stirling for his victory.

“Bruh Jiri need to go to 185 also Navajo is him sheesh,” wrote McKinney. Before the fight, Prochazka actually addressed changing divisions. “But I believe like light heavyweight division is my division. And sometimes I believe I'm able to make a middleweight also, but it will be very hard," he said during media day.

Judging from his octagon interview, it does not appear as if Jiri Prochazka is considering changing divisions. In fact, he addressed his recent loss on social media following the main event in Qatar.

Jiri Prochazka Vows To Make Changes After UFC Qatar Loss

Prochazka posted a video from his hotel shortly after the fight. He revealed that he needed a few stitches following the knockout. However, he insisted that he felt sharp, prepared and strong going into the contest. He also gave Stirling credit for finding the opening.

“Back in the hotel, a few stitches. One more time, congrats to my opponent, really nice catch. I felt sharp, felt prepared, felt strong, but maybe too much tactic, and not too much pressure,” Prochazka said. The former champion admitted that he needed to review the fight and identify what went wrong. He also acknowledged that there was plenty of work ahead of him. Nevertheless, he made it clear that he had no intention of giving up.

“No more like this. No more like this. No more like this. I will do everything. Believe me. Believe me. Thank you for your support. Let’s get back to work,” he added. Prochazka also accompanied the video with a simple message: “Accept, smile and continue.”

The defeat marked the first time in his 40-fight professional career that Prochazka suffered back-to-back losses. Both came by knockout against fighters from Ulberg's City Kickboxing team. Despite McKinney's suggestion, the former champion has yet to announce any plans to leave the light heavyweight division. All that said, it cannot be denied that Navajo Stirling did what he promised he would.
