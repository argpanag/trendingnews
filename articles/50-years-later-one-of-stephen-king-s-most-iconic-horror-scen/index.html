50 Years Later, One Of Stephen King's Most Iconic Horror Scenes Ever Is Still Impossible To Spoil

TheTools · 03.11.2026 21:43 · source · permalink

There are certain horror movie scenes or images that are so striking that they're well-known outside of the context of the film they came from. How many people haven't seen Psycho but can still hear Bernard Herrmann's screeching score as the shower curtain opens and the knife comes down? Or what about people who skipped The Blair Witch Project but have seen the ending shot where Mike stands in a corner? These moments are iconic, but there's a reason why they're so famous: they're great scenes, and knowing about them beforehand does nothing to undermine how chilling they are when you actually see the film. Perhaps the greatest of all horror scenes like this is the blood-soaked ending of Carrie.

Carrie, Brian De Palma's 1976 film version of Stephen King's first novel, came out 50 years ago today, kicking off a half-century of King movie adaptations. Because of the film's place in horror history, not to mention its critical success (stars Sissy Spacek and Piper Laurie both got acting nominations at the Academy Awards, a rarity for horror films), Carrie has become a pop culture reference point. It is a seminal genre film, and as a result, its legacy looms large. Even if you're not a fan of scary movies, it is all but impossible to not be aware of Carrie's climax. In a crowded field that includes countless high school movies like Back to the Future, Footloose, and Mean Girls, Carrie might just have the most memorable prom scene despite — or because of — how grotesque it is. Cultural osmosis means it's impossible to go into watching Carrie for the first time fully blind about the ending, yet no amount of pre-existing knowledge can "spoil" the impact of actually watching Carrie's betrayal and subsequent rampage play out.
