Trump’s comments about immigration are needlessly provocative to his base

President Donald Trump already had problems with his base.

And he seems to have unnecessarily exacerbated them by floating a path to legal status for some undocumented immigrants.

Trump’s comments in a “60 Minutes” interview airing Sunday set off many criticisms — some of them veiled, others not so much — from Republicans and MAGA activists.

Trump summarized his proposal as: “Get the killers out, get the rapists out, get all the rough ones out, get the drug dealers out. But let the people that are here — and in some cases here for 10 and 15 years — work a deal for them.”

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin later seemed to double down on the contours, while claiming it didn’t amount to “amnesty.” Trump also denied Monday that he was talking about amnesty.

But many have responded by saying, “No amnesty.” Others went further. They pointed directly to Trump’s “60 Minutes” comments, called the White House’s denial that he was talking about amnesty “insulting,” and said he risks a base revolt if he moves forward.

It seems unlikely Trump will actually pursue a path to legal status as part of a deal with Democrats. He has floated similar ideas previously, after all, and it’s never come to anything.

But merely floating it appears to have caused him real problems at an inopportune time.

President Donald Trump said he would stand by Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth if he’s impeached, telling CNN’s Kaitlan Collins that Republican Rep. Thomas Massie, who introduced articles of impeachment earlier Monday, is “a stone cold loser.”

President Donald Trump said he would stand by Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth if he’s impeached, telling CNN’s Kaitlan Collins that Republican Rep. Thomas Massie, who introduced articles of impeachment earlier Monday, is “a stone cold loser.”

Massie’s introduction of the articles of impeachment means a vote will have to happen by Wednesday.

“So, I say this — Pete Hegseth won two wars, oh, let’s impeach him? What about impeaching the gentleman from Biden, his person that disappeared for two weeks and nobody had any idea where he was?” Trump said, alluding to former Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin’s quiet hospitalization in late 2023 and early 2024.

“What about impeaching that went, that ran Iraq and Afghanistan, and all of these other wars, where thousands of lives have been lost,” Trump added.

Massie: GOP “earned” midterms losses after failing to stand up to Trump

Trump rules out amnesty and says his deportation policy “hasn’t changed”

President Donald Trump said Monday that his deportation policy “hasn’t changed,” after he suggested in an interview he was open to letting some undocumented immigrants stay in the country.

“Get the killers out, get the rapists out, get all the rough ones out, get the drug dealers out. But let the people that are here — and in some cases here for 10 and 15 years — work a deal for them,” Trump said.

Trump has a history of floating policy ideas and then quickly backing away from them.

Asked whether he told Miller he wants changes, Trump said: “We have a very strong deportation policy, there’s no amnesty, no anything.”

Trump says additional military action in Iran "won't be necessary" for now

President Donald Trump said after a meeting with Qatar’s prime minister that he doesn’t believe additional action in Iran is necessary, suggesting instead he’s prepared to allow the status quo in the region continue until Tehran agrees to a new deal.

“Should there be any change with respect to developments in the Middle East, the United States of America is fully ‘locked and loaded,” Trump said during a surprise appearance in the White House briefing room Monday afternoon. “We don’t think there’ll be any reason to use it.”

The president said Qatar, along with other Gulf Arab nations, “would prefer the status quo.”

“We’re seeing momentum toward the end of this conflict. I think it’s going to be ending fairly soon,” Trump said.

He said the conflict was “effectively over” and that he would be “keeping it the status quo” for now.

How a Pete Hegseth impeachment could play out

The House is now on a collision course with an impeachment vote for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

So how might that play out? And what are the political pluses and minuses for both sides?

On the Democratic side, the gambit makes sense as an opportunity to respond to voters’ concerns and try to change course in the Iran war. It also puts Republicans who have been openly critical of Hegseth on the spot.

But the gambit is also risky, as all impeachments are, in part because it’s being led by a Republican, outgoing Rep. Thomas Massie, whose priorities don’t totally overlap with Democrats’.

On the GOP side, there is no question that impeachment is a headache that could divide the party at an inopportune time.

But it could also help Republicans focus on a scapegoat rather than the president who is actually most responsible for the war — and for the other things Massie wants to impeach Hegseth over.

GOP Rep. Massie explains Hegseth impeachment articles: "He's violated the Constitution"

GOP Rep. Thomas Massie explained his reasoning for bringing eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth today at a press conference just minutes after formally introducing them on the House floor.

“We’re not indicting him for being bad at his job. In fact, he was very effective at killing schoolchildren in Iran, he was very effective at extrajudicial killings,” the outgoing Kentucky lawmaker said, referring to a US strike that hit a school at the beginning of the Iran war.

The articles focus mostly on Hegseth’s actions in the Iran war, but Massie also pointed to strikes against alleged drug boats that have killed at least 243 people.

Massie gave an emphatic “no” when asked whether any other House Republicans had already given him a commitment they would vote in support of his efforts, but added a vote would end up being “on everybody’s conscience.”

Asked why he did not move to impeach Trump instead, Massie replied that the president should see it as “a metaphor,” although he later insisted to CNN’s Manu Raju that the Hegseth move was not a symbolic one even though it faces slim odds in the Senate.

“One of his horses shot out from under him, and then maybe he will see things differently,” Massie explained on bringing articles against Hegseth versus Trump. “I think politically, it’s an impossible hurdle, but also I think this is the better target. You know, sometimes you apply the ordinance where the best target is, and the best target is Pete Hegseth.”

McBride says “anti-trans politics was a flop" in midterms

Delaware Rep. Sarah McBride, the first transgender member of Congress, argued Monday that “anti-trans politics was a flop this election” after Republicans spent hundreds of millions of dollars on attack ads about transgender policies in the midterms.

“Republicans were once again employing a tired strategy as old as politics itself — to divide and misdirect the public’s attention, to tell them that their neighbor, their trans neighbor, their immigrant neighbor, was to blame for their challenges in life, rather than the wealthiest 1% that were rigging an economy,” the Democratic representative said on a press call hosted by the Human Rights Campaign.

McBride also said that the new Democratic majorities in Congress were “not built by throwing vulnerable people under the bus,” but “by candidates who have defended their voters and their constituents, all of them, regardless of their sexual orientation.”

Leaders from HRC — the nation’s largest LGBTQ advocacy organization — echoed McBride, sharing election results and polling data that they said showed criticism of transgender policies “failed to motivate voters at the ballot box.”

In the losing effort to defend their majorities in Congress, Republicans made the issue a huge point of emphasis. Data from AdImpact shows that GOP advertisers spent more than $300 million this cycle on ads about transgender policies, more than 10 times as much as Democrats.

GOP Rep. Lauren Boebert will lose reelection to Democrat Eileen Laubacher in Colorado, CNN projects

Retired Rear Adm. Eileen Laubacher, a Democrat, will flip Colorado’s 4th District, CNN’s Decision Desk projects, ending conservative firebrand Rep. Lauren Boebert’s tenure in Congress.

Boebert’s loss comes after she elected to run in a more conservative district last cycle in an effort to preserve her political future. But Laubacher, who served more than three decades in the Navy, capitalized on the national headwinds against the GOP and focused her campaign on affordability issues.

The win denies Boebert, an ally of President Donald Trump and one of the most outspoken conservatives in the House Republican conference, a fourth term. Boebert was first elected in 2020, after she upset a fifth-term Republican incumbent in a primary.

In that time, she has emerged as one of the most recognizable, and at times polarizing, faces of the GOP’s MAGA wing, drawing attention from both Democrats and Republicans. Former President Joe Biden, whom she heckled during his 2022 State of the Union address, once billed her as “one of the leaders of this extreme MAGA movement.” The 39-year-old has also been a source of frustration for Republican speakers Kevin McCarthy and Mike Johnson.

Trump endorsed Boebert days before the election, praising her in a Truth Social post as a “MAGA Warrior, who has been with us from the very beginning.” But the pair have also been at odds at times. She drew the president’s ire earlier this year when she campaigned with one of his most vocal Republican critics in the House, Kentucky’s Thomas Massie, in his primary. The appearance prompted Trump to call her “dumb” and “weak-minded” and he threatened to withdraw his endorsement.

Boebert also crossed Trump and House Republican leaders last year when she joined a Democratic push to force the release of the Jeffrey Epstein case files, resisting a White House effort to sway her vote. At the time, GOP leaders opposed the release of the files, citing the potential disclosure of the names of Epstein’s victims.

This wasn’t the first time Boebert faced a tough reelection fight. She won reelection in 2024 by over 11 points, but nearly lost her race in a different district in 2022. She beat Democrat Adam Frisch by only 546 votes.

Johnson refuses to tell Trump to change course: "The voters made the wrong choice"

House Speaker Mike Johnson pushed back on the idea that Republicans should “course correct” despite the resounding rebuke of the direction of the country by voters in the midterm elections.

“I’m not going to tell the president how to do his job,” Johnson said when asked by CNN if the party should course correct given Republicans’ stunning wipeout in the midterms.

“I’m sorry that the voters made the wrong choice. I really do believe that this next two years is going to be a bit of a circus. It’s going to be chaos, and I hate to say that, but I think that’s where the Democrats are going to lead the House, and that’s a great, a great tragedy,” Johnson said, adding if Republicans had remained in the majority, they could have “stayed on course.”

Johnson also condemned the effort by Rep. Thomas Massie to bring articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

“Impeaching a Cabinet secretary right now, especially at the Department of War in the middle of hostilities abroad, would be a terrible, terrible look,” he said.

Johnson added he did not think Cabinet secretaries should be impeached for “political gain or for cheap tricks.”

“And I think that’s what this is, and I think everybody knows it,” he said.
