Grab Malaysia seeks to make service fee model permanent as drivers can earn over 5% more

TheTools · 27.11.2026 20:53 · source · permalink
    bookmark

PETALING JAYA: Grab Malaysia is working with the Transport Ministry (MOT) and its agency, the Land Public Transport Agency (APAD), for the permanent adoption of the Grab Service Fee (GSF) after the trial showed participating drivers earning over five per cent more.

Executive director Rashid Shukor said Grab Malaysia has seen consistently strong positive results based on the data since the concept was introduced in August 2024.

"We would love for it to be a permanent fixture, and we’ve seen better customer experience due to fewer cancellations.

"Right now, the concept is on an opt-in basis. So we are working closely with MOT and APAD to get this (GSF) approved because we want it to benefit everyone,” he told a media roundtable session titled "Behind the Percentage: Balancing Earnings and Reliable Rides for Malaysians" here today.

By replacing fixed commissions with the variable GSF, the earnings structure covers longer pickup efforts while keeping passenger fares unchanged.

According to Rashid, more than 170,000 Grab Malaysia driver-partners have seen an increase in their earnings after opting into the platform’s updated earnings structure, which pays partners for pickup

distance and time from the moment a booking is accepted.

"This underscores its focus on building stable, sustainable income opportunities for driver-partners while keeping rides reliable and affordable for Malaysians,” he said.

To a question on whether Grab Malaysia plans to introduce autonomous vehicle service, Rashid said the e-hailing  provider is exploring the idea here but it is just at an early stage.

"It’s extremely early stage and we just want to learn from what’s happening in Singapore as well,” he said.

It was reported that Grab has begun operating its autonomous vehicle service, branded as Autonomously Intelligent Ride, in Punggol, Singapore.

Public operations began on April 1, 2026, following earlier trials, and Grab has announced an expansion from fixed-route shuttles to on-demand direct rides. - Bernama
