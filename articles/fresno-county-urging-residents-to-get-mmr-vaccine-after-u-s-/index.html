Fresno County urging residents to get MMR vaccine after U.S. loses measles elimination status

TheTools · 18.11.2026 02:27 · source · permalink

FRESNO, Calif. (KFSN) -- The U.S. is seeing a rapid resurgence of measles, one of the world's most contagious diseases.

Measles is a viral infection that the CDC says has killed six Americans this year alone.

"If you see your child or an adult with a very high fever, the red eyes, runny nose, and a cough, call your medical provider right away," said Leticia Berber, a health educator with the Fresno County Department of Public Health.

Tuesday, the Pan American Health Organization announced the U.S. lost the measles elimination status amid a 30-year high in reported cases.

In 2000, the nation achieved elimination status when the virus stopped spreading continuously.

But in the last year, CDC data showed an alarming spread.

Cases skyrocketed from under 2,300 last year, to over 5,400 so far in 2026.

And deaths doubled from three to six.

"These figures are a serious warning for our region. Measles is resurging globally," said Pan American Health Organization Director Dr. Jarbos Barbosa.

Experts say the areas hit hardest have the lowest vaccination rates.

Earlier this year, ABC30 and ABC News unveiled a map pinpointing the parts of the country at highest risk.

On it, lighter blue counties face the lowest risk, while darker blue ones face the highest.

It shows much of the Central Valley, including Fresno County, at higher risk -- with only between 60-69% of residents vaccinated.

One Fresno County mother says her son was among those not vaccinated when he contracted the virus.

"As a parent, you're going through your head, like, 'Where was I?'" said Stone. "I honestly don't even know how he contracted that," said Candace Stone.

Since 2023, four Fresno County residents have tested positive for measles.

All of them recovered.

Officials say they closely monitor each confirmed case and even potential infections.

As cases continue to climb across the U.S., they're urging people to get vaccinated.

"The best defense that we have against the measles is the MMR vaccine, which is the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine," said Berber. "You want to prevent from spreading it to other individuals."
