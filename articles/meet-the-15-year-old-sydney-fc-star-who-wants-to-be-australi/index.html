This 15-YEAR-OLD Might Be Australia's Next Ellyse Perry: Meet Willa Pearson

Willa Pearson has already rewritten the record books in the A-League Women, earned a professional contract with Sydney FC and captained Australia at youth level. 

All that by the age of 15. Let that sink in.

But the teenage sensation has an ambition that stretches far beyond football, and all the way to the 2032 Olympic Games in Brisbane.

Pearson wants to represent Australia in not one, but two sports, on home soil.

“My end goal is to play for Australia in both football and cricket at the 2032 Brisbane Olympics,” Pearson revealed to Football360.

“One day, hopefully. Get that on camera.”

It is an audacious ambition from one of Australian football’s brightest prospects, who is determined to follow in the footsteps of dual international Ellyse Perry.

Perry made 18 appearances for the Matildas and appeared at the 2011 FIFA Women’s World Cup before turning her sole focus to cricket, which has yielded seven ICC Women’s T20 World Cup titles and two ODI World Cup trophies with Australia.

And while cricket’s place on the Brisbane 2032 Olympic program is yet to be confirmed, Pearson insisted: “I’m not giving up cricket or soccer anytime soon.

“I can’t even imagine my life without either of them, even if I have to take a step down and play locally. They both have a really special place in my heart.”

In fact, Pearson believes her commitment to two sports has helped accelerate her remarkable rise.

“I play my best cricket when I’m playing soccer and my best soccer when I’m playing cricket,” she explained. “It’s almost having that mental switch to relax from the other one.

“Ellyse Perry is an amazing role model, just the way she plays both sports and the temperament she plays them with.

“Playing for the Matildas at such a young age is definitely something I aspire to.”

Three years ago, Pearson was sitting among more than 75,000 spectators at Stadium Australia, watching the Matildas take on England in the 2023 Women’s World Cup semi-final.

She was a Year 7 student surrounded by school friends, captivated by the atmosphere and the sight of iconic figure Sam Kerr scoring one of the most extraordinary goals in Australian football history.

“I remember the insane noise of the stadium when she got up to warm up,” Pearson recalled.

“Every time the ball came near her, it was so loud. Then that goal – everyone just went bonkers. There were bottles going everywhere, hot chips.

“That really was a moment for me. Women’s sport is a real thing here in this country, especially football. This is the real deal.

“It really inspired me. There’s a pathway here and hopefully one day I can get onto that pitch and play with these people.”

Fast-forward to February 2026 and Pearson was celebrating a remarkable milestone.

Having already become the youngest player in A-Leagues history when she debuted for Sydney FC at 14 years and 312 days, the defender surpassed Kerr’s long-standing record to become the competition’s youngest goalscorer.

Pearson was just 15 years and 51 days old when she found the net in Sydney’s 4-0 victory over Brisbane Roar, breaking a record Kerr had held since 2008.

Incredibly, Pearson – who captained Australia at junior level and was part of the Junior Matildas squad for the 2026 FIFA Under-17 Women’s World Cup in Morocco – had no idea what she had achieved.

Pearson said: “I remember doing a post-match interview with the commentators and they were like, ‘You’ve just broken a record.’ I was like, ‘Wow, no way.’

“I was in total disbelief.”

The achievement attracted widespread attention, but Pearson was largely oblivious to the reaction.

“I don’t have social media, so I didn’t really pick up on that, which I think is a good thing. It keeps me very grounded,” she said.

“I don’t think I really need it. I’m pretty busy as it is and maybe it’s just a bit of a time-waster. I definitely don’t plan on getting it anytime soon.”

Pearson’s extraordinary rise has come as little surprise, especially to those close to her. 

Earlier this year, her coach at the time Ante Juric told Football360 he had identified Pearson’s potential when she was just 12, describing her as a rare talent who could emerge once every two or three decades.

The three-time A-League Women Championship-winning coach even revealed he had planned to make Pearson Sydney FC captain against Brisbane before his departure from the club.

“So she would’ve been the youngest ever captain as well as everything else,” Juric revealed.

The former Socceroo also compared Pearson with Matildas stars Ellie Carpenter and Alanna Kennedy at the same age, praising her maturity, composure and natural defensive instincts.

For Pearson, that endorsement carried enormous significance.

“To have someone who has so much knowledge of the game and has coached so many amazing players speak highly of me is very humbling,” she said.

Juric was also the coach who handed Pearson her first professional contract – a moment she has not forgotten.

“He was amazing for me. I loved him and the way he treated the girls,” Pearson said. “He was the first coach to give me a professional contract, so that’s a really special thing that I have with him.

“I’m very grateful for the belief he had in me.”

Pearson has since signed a new Sydney FC deal through to 2028, having progressed from the club’s development squad to their first team.

“I remember being in the academy, training on the synthetic fields, and watching the older girls before training, thinking, ‘Wow, one day I want to do that.’

“To be able to do that now, train on the grass field at Sky Park and wear this badge fills me with so much pride.”

For all her remarkable achievements before her 16th birthday, Pearson’s daily life remains remarkably familiar.

There’s school, catching public transport to training while relying on family to help manage a demanding schedule involving professional football and cricket.

Getting behind the wheel is also more than two years away.

“My mum, dad, brother and sister have sacrificed so much for me,” she said. “I’m eternally grateful for everything they’ve done, whether that’s helping me mentally when things get stressful or driving me around.

“I don’t have my licence yet, and I’m sure they can’t wait until I do.”

There are also the less glamorous responsibilities that come with being one of Sydney FC’s youngest players.

“I leave school a little bit early, jump on the bus and public transport, get to training, do the bottles and sometimes pump up the balls,” she said.

“When I’m walking down to Sky Park, lots of the girls pull over and ask if I want a lift. It saves me a couple of hundred metres.”
