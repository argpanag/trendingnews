Gardaí sweep RTÉ headquarters in Dublin after bomb threat

TheTools · 06.11.2026 09:41 · source · permalink

RTÉ and other media organisations around the country were evacuated this morning following a bomb threat.

The alarm was raised after an email was sent to the national broadcaster, as well as other media outlets across Ireland and Britain.

Staff were evacuated from the buildings on the Donnybrook campus in Dublin and gardaí were called to the scene, which was declared safe around lunchtime.

RTÉ moved to pre-recorded programmes as a precautionary measure, however RTÉ's Today with David McCullagh programme on Radio 1 remained on air from the mobile broadcasting 'roadcaster'.

Garda sniffer dogs carried out searches of the buildings.

The broadcaster said the safety of its staff and personnel was paramount.

RTÉ Director General Kevin Bakhurst said: "An email was sent into RTÉ and other organisations about 8.33am. I received it 8.40, and we had quick conversations.

"I spoke to the deputy director general, one of my colleagues rang the gardaí.

"On the basis of the information we had, we decided that we wanted to put the safety of the staff first and evacuate the buildings."

Mr Bakhurst said the threat had caused "a lot of disruption" but added: "Luckily we have contingency plans in place - which we reviewed quite recently."

He said: "It's a disgraceful attack on the media, on free media, and unfortunately, probably where it comes from, they don't really appreciate the importance of free media."

Mediahaus and Independent News Media also evacuated their offices on Talbot Street in Dublin city centre for a time as a precautionary measure.

A spokesperson for Mediahaus Ireland said: "Like a number of other media outlets, the Irish Independent received threatening correspondence this morning.

"Our main offices in Dublin city centre were evacuated for a short time while security checks took place.

"We wish to thank An Garda Síochána for their assistance."

Virgin Media also received the threat but did not evacuate and continued to broadcast.

A spokesperson said: "Virgin Media Television received a security threat email that was widely circulated to multiple European media platforms.

"We followed our well established security protocols and continue to liaise with An Garda Síochána.

"We remained on air throughout and no evacuation took place."

In a statement, gardaí said they were "aware of a threat made against media organisations here and in Europe".

"An Garda Síochána has been liaising with the Irish media organisations, and the threats will be investigated."

Authorities working to 'establish who is responsible' - Govt

The Government said authorities are working to "establish who is responsible" for the "very serious" threats against media organisations.

In a statement, it said: "The threats made this morning against media organisations here are very serious.

"Hoax threats such as these cause fear, distress and disruption to people going about their daily lives and activities; they achieve no positive purpose whatsoever. It is not known yet who is responsible for these threats, whether it is individuals or a centralised or co-ordinated effort.

"However, these threats come on the heels of the ongoing disruption to aviation around Europe caused by similar threats, and it seems likely that this is a co-ordinated campaign of disruption."

RTÉ Radio 1 on air from the roadcaster with David McCullagh

The Government said An Garda Síochána and the Defence Forces are liaising with their European security counterparts and that every effort will be made to identify those responsible and to hold them to account.

The Government noted that gardaí had been aware of the risk of disruptive threats during Ireland's ongoing Presidency of the Council of European Union.

The threat purportedly comes from those behind a series of hoax bomb threats which affected at least 27 flights across Europe yesterday, as well as further alerts earlier in the week.

Some Aer Lingus and Ryanair flights were among those impacted.

Ireland not immune to hybrid threats - Defence Forces chief

The Chief of Staff of the Irish Defence Forces has said that it is too early to say whether the threats, and a cyberattack on refrigeration systems across Europe, are interrelated.

Lieutenant General Rossa Mulcahy said the world has changed dramatically and Ireland is not immune to hybrid threats.

"Everyone is familiar with the war in Ukraine and that kind of changed the landscape. We have now, on mainland Europe, a conventional war and we also have a significant increase in hybrid threats and hybrid attacks across many states.

"We've seen that ourselves in the HSE attack, the attack on our refrigerators and also in our maritime domain, the threats and research around our critical underwater infrastructure."

He was speaking following an address at a conference on security and defence at Dublin Castle.

The 'Defending Democracy: Hybrid Threats, Critical Infrastructure and Societal Resilience' event was hosted by Oireachtas committees as part of Ireland's EU Presidency.

Asked if these types of attacks are growing, he said: "I can't say that they're all interrelated. I think that's too early to say. Certainly, on the hoax bomb threats, that again we've had multiple countries, multiple different airlines, and multiple different incidents. So whether it's coordinated or not, it's too early to say.

Lt Gen Mulcahy said that all agencies had to work together.

"The threats we're seeing, the air hoaxes, the aircraft hoaxes. That's not a defence matter. It's not a garda matter. It's transport as well, and it's foreign affairs.

"It's that whole of Government, whole of society approach to dealing with these kind of actions, and they're not necessarily hybrid threats that we're seeing now, but they may well be when the analysis plays out."

The State needs to be ready to respond and "stand up those capabilities that are there across the Government and other agencies to support society and provide for the defence of the State and the resilience of our population," Lt Gen Mulcahy added.
