Selma Blair in custody after attacking flight attendants on plane to JFK Airport: Sources

TheTools · 13.10.2026 00:27 · source · permalink

The "Cruel Intentions" star attacked two flight attendants, one of whom had a bite mark and another had suffered a cut, law enforcement sources told NBC New York.

Actress Selma Blair was taken into custody after allegedly attacking two flight attendants on a flight heading to JFK Airport. She also faces charges after law enforcement sources said she also assaulted a Port Authority police officer as she was being taken to the hospital, as well as a nurse at the hospital. NBC New York’s Ida Siegal reports.

Actress Selma Blair was taken into custody and faces multiple charges after assaulting flight attendants aboard a plane en route to John F. Kennedy Airport in New York City, according to law enforcement sources.

The Port Authority Police Department responded to a call around 11 p.m. Sunday about a passenger who had been physically abusive aboard a Delta Airlines flight from Los Angeles International Airport, a spokesperson for the department said. The passenger was identified as the 54-year-old Blair.

The "Cruel Intentions" star attacked two flight attendants, one of whom had a bite mark and another had suffered a cut, law enforcement sources told NBC New York. One flight attendant was taken to the hospital for treatment, Port Authority said.

The plane went on "lockdown," sources said, adding that Blair was restrained in some way, but had not been duct taped. Listen to audio from air traffic control detailing the injuries in the player below.

Port Authority Police and the FBI responded as the plane landed at the Queens airport. Blair was taken into custody and issued a violation summons by the FBI for the on-board assault, according to a senior law enforcement official.

The violation she received can carry up to a year in prison, the senior law enforcement official said. A summons is issued in lieu of an arrest and, in essence, amounts to a federal misdemeanor, an official told NBC New York. The recipient of the summons is expected to show up for a court hearing at a later date where a penalty is negotiated, typically resulting in a fine or counseling.

As she was being taken to Jamaica Hospital for evaluation, Blair assaulted a Port Authority officer by kicking her repeatedly in the head, law enforcement sources said. She also allegedly assaulted a nurse at the hospital.

A law enforcement source in Queens said Blair will face two assault charges for the attacks on the officer and the nurse. She was arraigned Monday night in Queens criminal court, where her lawyers posted her $20,000 cash bail.

Blair had a support dog with her at the time of the incident, according to sources. Her lawyer met her at the hospital and took possession of the dog.

A representative for Blair said the actress "experienced a medical emergency that led to the alleged conduct." She "received immediate medical attention" after landing and was recovering at the hospital, the rep said, without providing further details regarding the medical episode. Blair revealed in 2018 that she had been diagnosed with multiple sclerosis.

The FBI was investigating the incident, and confirmed to NBC News it had been contacted about a "potential assault aboard a flight that arrived into JFK." The bureau told NBC News "as per long-standing DOJ policy, we do not comment on ongoing investigations."

The Federal Aviation Administration also said it would investigate a "passenger disturbance" on the flight.

In a response to a request for comment from NBC News on Monday, Delta did not identify Blair as the passenger, but said it has "zero tolerance for unruly or unlawful behavior, as the safety of our customers and crew always comes first."

Blair, whose major film credits also include 2001's "Legally Blonde" and 2004's "Hellboy," is due back in court on Jan. 19.

Sunday night's incident was not the first aboard a plane for Blair. She had a verbal outburst on a flight in 2016 from Cancun to Los Angeles in which she accused someone of wanting to kill her, TMZ reported at the time. She was taken off the flight on a stretcher and transported to a hospital when the flight landed in Los Angeles.

She later admitted to Vanity Fair that she had blacked out after mixing alcohol and medication, and apologized for her behavior.

Jay Blackman, Sofia Sutter and Doha Madani of NBC News contributed to this report.
