Uruguay and Neltume Ports Settle US$600M ICSID Arbitration | No Compensation Paid - News and Statistics - IndexBox

TheTools · 09.09.2026 20:45 · source · permalink

Uruguay has resolved a US$600 million international arbitration dispute with Neltume Ports, a case tied to container terminal operator Montecon and activities at the Port of Montevideo. The deal follows Neltume Ports' permanent withdrawal of its claims against the Uruguayan state before the World Bank's International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID).

As part of the settlement, Uruguay will not make any payment, nor will it acknowledge any liability or take on extra commitments. Neltume Ports, which holds the majority stake in Montecon, has dropped all claims, actions, and demands against Uruguay, and both Neltume and Montecon, together with their affiliated entities, have pledged not to pursue any future legal action over the issues that gave rise to the arbitration.

The conflict started in April 2024, when Chile-based Neltume Ports, acting for itself and for Montecon, filed for arbitration against Uruguay under the bilateral investment pact between Uruguay and Chile. The claim sought roughly US$600 million from the state. The dispute stemmed from modifications to the container terminal setup at the Port of Montevideo, particularly a 2021 accord between Uruguay and Katoen Natie regarding Terminal Cuenca del Plata (TCP), the port's dedicated container facility. That accord extended TCP's concession through 2081, with Katoen Natie pledging a substantial investment program. Montecon, which mainly operates at Montevideo's public docks, contested the deal's effects on its operations and on competitive conditions for container handling.

Uruguay's Presidency has confirmed that the agreement entails no compensation for Neltume or Montecon and imposes no obligations or conditions on the state. Jorge Diaz, the Prosecretary of the Presidency, called the result a victory for Uruguay on the global stage.

This settlement does not conclude all international cases involving Montecon. A separate arbitration brought by ATCO, another shareholder in Montecon, is still pending. The Uruguayan government anticipates that this case will be settled in a comparable fashion, though no definitive agreement has been reached so far.
