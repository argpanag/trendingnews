Protests call for Centrelink to 'stop profiteering off poverty' as payment cancellations resume

TheTools · 27.10.2026 19:57 · source · permalink

Australians took to the streets of Melbourne CBD and Townsville today, calling for Centrelink to "stop profiting from property". The protests were in regard to the return of Centrelink's 'punishment regime', and have call on the government to abolish the Targeted Compliance Framework (TCF).

As of yesterday, the TCF has allowed Centrelink to recommence cancelling payments related to compulsory activities for job seekers. Last week, advocacy groups had called the change "absurd", calling it a decision that is "designed to punish" income support recipients, Australian Unemployed Workers' Union member Jessica Harrison said. 

"Suspended or cancelled payments mean missed rent, going without food, increased financial stress and risk of homelessness," she said.

Between 2024 and 2025, cancellations to Centrelink payments due to compulsory activities had been paused in stages due to unlawful administrative practices from the DEWR coming to light. 

Now, as of October 26, recipients who are required under compulsory activities to apply for and accept jobs will once again have their payments threatened with suspension.

These suspensions or cancellations could occur if the individual fails to reconnect with employment services within four weeks, or for those who refuse or fail to start suitable employment. 

Social services groups banded together to express their opposition to the proposed suspensions and cancellations, voicing fears of how it would increase the number of unemployed Australians and lead to deeper poverty rates. 

The Australian Unemployed Workers Union, Antipoverty Centre, Welfare Rights Centre, ACOSS, Economic Justice Australia and NATSILS have collectively asked the government to abolish the TCF immediately and to provide full backpay and compensation to every person affected by unlawful decisions.

On Tuesday, the rallies taking place in Melbourne and Townsville will also call on the government to: 

  • Abolish the TCF and its arbitrary publishments, including Work for Dole, compulsory volunteering and arbitrary job search requirements

  • Increase social security payments to a fair rate tied to the Henderson Poverty Line, which will allow anyone on benefits to pay for both food and rent.

  • Replace privatised job agencies with a non-profit agency that informs where the jobs are, without a profit incentive or the right to punishment.
