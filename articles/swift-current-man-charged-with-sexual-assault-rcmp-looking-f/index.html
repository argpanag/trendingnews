Swift Current man charged with sexual assault; RCMP looking for more victims | CBC News

TheTools · 03.10.2026 17:07 · source · permalink

Police say the accused used Snapchat to contact young girls

Swift Current RCMP are asking anyone who may be a victim to come forward after a 23-year-old man was charged with sexually assaulting teenage girls. 

Aniken Edwards is charged with two counts of sexual assault and one count of sexual touching, RCMP said in a Friday news release.

He remains in custody and is scheduled to appear in provincial court on Monday.

Police said they received two reports of sexual assaults against teens in September and Edwards was arrested after an investigation. 

“We limit what we release about sexual assaults to be mindful of the well-being and the privacy of the victims. In this case, investigators are now looking into whether there may be additional victims,” the release said. 

Investigators believe the accused befriends people who are younger than him, it said. 

“It’s believed the accused often hangs out at places frequented by teenagers. He is also known to use Snapchat to communicate with teenage girls."

Anyone who thinks they or someone they know may be a victim, or who has information about the investigation, can call RCMP or anonymously contact Saskatchewan Crime Stoppers.

"Sexual assault is any intentional, unwanted touching of a sexual nature. It is also a criminal offence for an adult to sexually touch a person under the age of 16, or to invite or counsel a person under the age of 16 to touch them — regardless of consent," the release said.

"If you think you are a victim, know that you won’t navigate this process alone. Saskatchewan RCMP takes every report of sexual assault seriously. Investigators are specially trained in trauma-informed and survivor-centered approaches and are dedicated to supporting survivors, and ensuring they have the supports they need."

If you’re in immediate danger or fear for your safety or that of others around you, please call 911. For support in your area, you can look for crisis lines and local services via the Ending Sexual Violence Association of Canada database. ​​
