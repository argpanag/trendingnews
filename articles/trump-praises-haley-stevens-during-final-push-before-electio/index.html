Curtis Hertel Jr., the chair of the Michigan Democratic party, appeared to say on social media that Haley Stevens had been expelled from Michigan’s Democratic Party over public her break with Abdul El-Sayed.

“Congresswoman Stevens is not a current member of the Michigan Democratic Party, nor will she be in the future,” Hertel said in a post.

In a separate statement, Hertel threw his full support behind El-Sayed and tore into Stevens for her decision to support a Trump-backed candidate like Rogers.

“Haley Stevens’ decision to campaign with Rogers—a man who has repeatedly sold out Michigan’s working class, wants to pass a national abortion ban, and has pledged to be a loyal vote for Trump’s agenda—shows that she has abandoned everything she claims to care about,” Hertel wrote.

He continue: “We reject the attempts by Stevens and bad-faith actors to divide our party and undermine our momentum, and will not allow these eleventh-hour distractions deter us.”

One man's campaign finance operation offers a glimpse in Republican dark money network

In the summer of 2024, a non-profit called the Green Dragon Coalition wired the last $20,000 of a $445,000 grant to a Super Pac. Its tax return for that year would describe the purpose of the transfer as “educational”.

The same day, according to Federal Election Commission (FEC) records, the Super Pac spent $8,000 attacking Celeste Maloy, the Republican congresswoman for Utah’s second district, four days before the primary that would decide whether she kept her seat. It had already spent more than $400,000 promoting her challenger, Colby Jenkins, who was running to her right with the endorsement of the rightwing Utah senator Mike Lee.

One man was involved at every stage, and his name is on the paperwork for the charity that raised the money, the non-profit that passed it on, the Super Pac that the non-profit donated to and the campaign this money eventually supported.

His name is Thomas Datwyler, and his businesses are mostly headquartered in a rented address in Hudson, Wisconsin, a town of 15,000 near the border with Minnesota. Datwyler operates at an industrial scale, acting as treasurer of more than 350 federal political committees in the current election cycle, including 115 congressional campaigns and 100 Super Pacs, and as an officer of more than two dozen non-profits.

The extent of Datwyler’s activities over several political cycles raises questions about the attitude of Republican campaigns and candidates to campaign finance in the run-up to the November midterm elections, and the evolution of so-called “dark money” electoral funding.

Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer has declared his firm stance in support of Abdul El-Sayed in response to Stevens’ endorsement of Republican candidate Mike Rogers.

Schumer also harshly castigated Stevens for the move, referring to it as a “despicable betrayal” in a post to social media.

“Senate Democrats are united in our support for Abdul El Sayed because we know he will fight for the economic interests of working families in Michigan,” Schumer said in a statement released today. “Unlike Mike Rogers, who is a tool of Donald Trump and billionaire donors. We Democrats equally united in our total revulsion at the racist, Islamophobic, and over-the-top false attacks on Abdul by Republicans.”

“These attacks are evidence of the total bankruptcy of the Republican Party today, who resort to ugly and un-American fearmongering because they have nothing to offer on the problems that concern people today.”

Schumer had initially endorsed Stevens for the Democratic senate seat in Michigan, though he began to support El-Sayed following Stevens’ loss in the primary election.

Staff for Haley Stevens quit following her Mike Rogers endorsement

Some of the people who worked for Stevens’ senate campaign have quit following her Rogers endorsement. Her long-time chief of staff, Justin German, and her communications director, Logan Basch, were among those who left, according to CNN correspondent Manu Raju.

German later released a full statement on behalf of the campaign team endorsing El-Sayed:

“As Haley Stevens’ Chief of Staff for eight years, I speak on behalf of our team in wholeheartedly supporting Abdul El-Sayed for Senate. This election is too important to sow division or stand on the sidelines. We are shocked and heartbroken by Congresswoman Stevens’ decision to support Mike Rogers.

Those of us who are Michigan voters are supporting Democrats up and down the ballot, and have been working all season to elect strong Democratic majorities to the House and Senate – we will continue to do so.

As of this morning, many members of our team have resigned, including myself. We encourage everyone to exercise their civic duty, vote for Abdul and Democrats up and down the ballot, and stand up for the America we all believe in.”

Despite El-Sayed’s request to not speak about Stevens, reporters immediately questioned him about her recent actions anyway, in which he responded: “At the end of the day, I actually don’t think about Haley Stevens,” adding, “I’m just less interested in the politics of it all than I am in the policy of it all.”

“If you are somebody who believes that we should stand up to hate, and you have watched this last campaign, I think we probably would not want to support Mike Rogers and Donald Trump and JD Vance,” he continued.

“If you’re somebody who believes that we need more health care, not less, then you probably don’t want to vote for the guy who voted to raise prescription drug prices… and takes money from big pharma and big insurance,” he continued.

“If you’re somebody who believes that you should have a right to a union, you probably don’t want to support the guy who busts unions. So I’m just saying that the principles that we believe in as Democrats are really quite clear,” he added. “I stand for all of them.”

In his first public comments since Stevens’ endorsement of Rogers, Democratic candidate Abdul El-Sayed told reporters at a press conference outside of a rally he held today, the very same rally that Stevens was disinvited from attending, that he was not interested in speaking about “somebody who’s not here right now.”

“I have a sense that you all are here today because of the decisions of somebody who’s not here right now. I’m just going to tell you, this is a state of 10 million people who deserve a lot better from their government,” he said. “I’m a lot more focused on the 9,999,998 that are not the ones you guys are talking about.”

“I’m not in the business of driving clicks for you. I’m in the business of trying to solve problems for the people of my state.”

Haley Stevens’ endorsement of Mike Rogers was met with immediate pushback from Democrats, who condemned Stevens for her “betrayal”.

Neera Tanden, the former senior advisor to president Joe Biden, wrote on X: “Standing with Mike Rogers is a betrayal of everything Haley Stevens has fought for her entire career. It’s also a betrayal of all of her supporters that believed in her. I hope Abdul El-Sayed has a strong victory on Tuesday and that all Democrats and like-minded independents support vote for him.”

Chris Murphy, Democratic Senator from Connecticut, hospitalized following a stroke

Chris Murphy, a Democratic Senator from Connecticut, has been hospitalized following a stroke, according to his staff.

“Yesterday afternoon, Senator Murphy started experiencing numbness in his arm while at his son’s hockey game,” a statement from Murphy’s office said. “He was taken to a local hospital, where it was discovered he had experienced a stroke. He was transferred to Johns Hopkins for further monitoring and is responding well to treatment. The doctors are encouraged by his progress, he is awake and alert, and we are optimistic he will make a full recovery.”

Judge throws out Trump administration lawsuit over Alaska Senate race

A federal judge in Alaska on Sunday rejected a Trump administration lawsuit to block certification of the state’s Senate race if a candidate with the same name as the Republican incumbent garners enough votes to shift the outcome, Reuters reported.

The case, filed in October, centered around two identically named candidates in the closely watched Alaska Senate race: Dan S. Sullivan, the incumbent Republican senator seeking reelection, and Dan J. Sullivan, his challenger.

The story of the two Dan Sullivans – up until now a strange sideshow in the Alaska Senate contest – has become a flashpoint in the final days of the race, following revelations that staff for Mary Peltola, the Democratic nominee, may have recruited the second Sullivan to join the race.

Peltola has denied personal knowledge of such a scheme, and it’s not clear that the tactic, widely used in politics, would be illegal. But Donald Trump has since called for her disqualification from the ballot and criminal prosecution, and the Republican governor of Alaska has announced a probe into the matter.

Haley Stevens officially endorses Mike Rogers in Michigan Senate race

Haley Stevens, the erstwhile primary challenger of Michigan Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed, has officially broken with her party and endorsed El-Sayed’s Republican opponent in the pivotal race.

Stevens, who made a surprise appearance this afternoon alongside Rogers at a Detroit church, posted a video to social media making the endorsement.

“You may have seen our Democratic nominee for US Senate, Abdul, said I was no longer welcome in his campaign and I should stay away,” Stevens said, appearing to speak directly into a phone camera while seated inside a vehicle. “And he’s right. And that’s why I am endorsing Mike Rogers for the United States Senate today. I don’t belong in Abdul’s campaign.”

Just days ago, Stevens and El-Sayed, bitter foes during the Michigan Senate primary, had planned to participate in a joint event at a different Detroit church from where she ultimately appeared with Rogers. Top Democrats had brokered her appearance as a way to mend ties and demonstrate party solidarity in the final days of a closely-run Senate contest.

But those plans disintegrated after Stevens demanded El-Sayed condemn in explicit terms a third-party ad related to one of the issues that most fiercely divided them during their primary: US support for Israel.

El-Sayed, a progressive, has been an outspoken critic of Israel and its war in Gaza and supports cutting off military aid to the country. Stevens, on the other hand, is moderate and fiercely pro-Israel.

In response to the demand that he condemn the ad, El-Sayed accused Stevens of reopening a settled primary election, and his campaign disinvited her from his remaining campaign events. (The ad in question linked US support for Israel to rising living costs in the US, a claim Stevens labeled offensive.)

This afternoon, Stevens appeared to have retaliated with her surprise appearance alongside Rogers, dropping her endorsement video soon after.

In the video, Stevens said that she was still “a very proud Democrat” but that she did not feel El-Sayed’s campaign represents the ideals she believes Michigan should stand for in the Senate.

“Who we are as Michiganders is not hate, and is not what we are getting out from our Democratic nominee right now,” Stevens said.
