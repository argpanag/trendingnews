Trump says US 'not putting on the brakes' in AI race against China

TheTools · 26.09.2026 17:03 · source · permalink

President Donald Trump said Saturday he talked “a lot” about artificial intelligence with Chinese President Xi Jinping this week before adding that the U.S. will continue to aggressively pursue AI dominance.

OpenAI agents self-posted dozens of users’ photos to third-party services without permission

OpenAI agents self-posted dozens of users’ photos to third-party services without their permission, the artificial intelligence company reported on Friday in a new blog where they list AI security incidents.

“We have discovered 53 cases where images that people had uploaded were posted to image-hosting sites as links that weren’t publicly listed,” OpenAI said in a post on X. “The images came from accounts that allowed their data to be used to improve our models, and after we disassociated the images from the accounts and ran them through a privacy filter.”

The company said the breaches happened before the safeguards they implemented following the Hugging Face incident in which experimental OpenAI models hacked AI developer platform Hugging Face without the knowledge of employees.

CEO Sam Altman said on Friday: "There is an extensive and ongoing review related to our agents’ use of internet access during training and evaluation."

He said the company has been publishing summaries of incidents and will continue.

"We have not been as fast as we would have liked but we are trying to balance our desire for transparency with gaining a clear understanding from petabytes of agent activity logs, and working with impacted organizations," he added.

"We are prioritizing as best as we can based on severity, and adding resources," Altman continued. "Hugging Face is still the most severe event we’ve seen. We will be as transparent as we can be subject to things like vulnerabilities in other companies that our agents have found, which will be their call to disclose or not."

China 'respects' Trump's decision to refer to artificial intelligence as 'super intelligence'

China's Foreign Ministry put out a statement Saturday saying it "respects" President Donald Trump opting to rebrand artificial intelligence as "super intelligence."

However, Chinese officials have not adopted the term themselves in government communications, despite Trump claiming that China's president, Xi Jinping, took a liking to it.

Throughout the week, Trump has repeatedly said he feels "super intelligence" is a more accurate term because the adjective "artificial" makes AI sound "fake."

Trump says AI dominance is national priority, claims China trails US by 18 months

While speaking to reporters outside the White House on Saturday, President Donald Trump said that despite his cordial three-day summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping, he still intends to outpace America's most significant rival in the race to develop advanced artificial intelligence.

"The United States of America is not going to be putting on brakes," Trump said before departing to Knoxville, Tennessee. "They want to stop our progress because we're leading China by a lot, and we're going to keep it that way."

"When you're leading, you don't open it up to each other," Trump added.

Trump claimed the U.S. is ahead of China by about a year and a half in AI development. Experts have said the U.S. is likely only ahead by about six months.

Trump also reiterated his new talking point first debuted Tuesday during his speech at the United Nations General Assembly: artificial intelligence should be called "super intelligence."

"I don't call it AI anymore, I call it SI because it's a much better name. It's a much more accurate name," Trump said. "Artificial means it's fake and it's not fake. It's very strong intelligence."

Hegseth celebrates court win against Anthropic in Pentagon contract fight

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth celebrated a Friday court ruling that allowed the Pentagon to continue blacklisting Anthropic from potentially lucrative U.S. military contracts.

Hegseth marked the decision by reposting a statement he issued in February criticizing Anthropic, adding only an American flag emoji and a pointing-down emoji.

After Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei refused to allow the Department of War to use Claude for "mass domestic surveillance" or "fully autonomous weapons," the Trump administration responded by removing the company's products from its supply chain altogether.

Hegseth and administration officials said Anthropic trying to dictate how the U.S. military used its tools constituted a threat to national security.

Anthropic sued, arguing there was no evidence of Claude being a national security risk and that it was not trying to undermine military objectives. The company also said the Pentagon could have sought a narrower remedy and agreed not to use Claude for surveillance or autonomous weapons.

In a 2-1 ruling, the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals sided with the Pentagon, holding that it could bar Anthropic from government contracts because the company restricted the military's use of its AI tools for certain "lawful" purposes.

Kamala Harris warns AI algorithms are ‘trying to dumb people down’ in flower analogy

Former Vice President Kamala Harris used yellow and pink flowers to explain how social media algorithms can repeatedly feed users similar content, arguing that the technology can influence what people see online.

Harris made the remarks during an appearance on "Off Air with Big Boy" after host Big Boy asked about AI, TikTok, Instagram and the role personalized algorithms could play in shaping how Americans consume information, including ahead of the midterm elections.

"There is a part of this that is about understanding that, you know, if you keep seeing the same thing over and over again, know that you are being targeted," Harris said. "Because you looked at it the first time, they're just going to keep sending it to you, which means they're trying to dumb people down with the assumption that people don't have any interest beyond what they've already seen."

Harris, who previously played a leading role in the Biden administration's work on artificial intelligence, continued with a colorful example.

"The algorithm's suggesting I have no more interest — because I'm looking at this yellow flower, I'm only interested in yellow flowers, as though my brain can't handle the fact that there may be a pink flower that I might be interested in looking at," Harris said. "Part of it is understanding how the algorithm is manipulating us and figuring out how we're going to psych it out a bit."

AI company founder says worries about the technology are ‘overblown’

Despite dire warnings about the need to rein in artificial intelligence by tech leaders like OpenAI CEO Sam Altman at the United Nations this week, Deeplearning.AI founder Andrew Ng told FOX Business that he thinks those fears are “overblown.”

“There is no plausible path that AI will lead to human extinction or whatever’s the hype line of the day,” he assured. “Instead, a handful of businesses have drummed up or sown fears of AI for various incentives.”

He said he’s noticed recently that when AI does something wrong, businesses will say “It’s not my fault, my rogue AI agent did it, almost as a disclaimer of responsibility.” 

“Over the last few years, there have been strong incentives for some businesses to sow AI fears,” he said, adding that AI companies are more fearful of competition than AI itself.

Ng said that AI companies really want government to pass laws constraining AI “to stifle the little guy.”

OpenAI agents attempted to hack government website without employee knowledge: report

OpenAI agents attempted to hack and grabbed open-source information from at least three government websites over the summer without the knowledge of employees, according to a report.

OpenAI confirmed to Commerce Department and SEC interactions to the Times and said it was investigating the Education Department incident.

Walmart CEO promises not to use AI to charge customers more

In a Friday letter Walmart’s CEO promised the company would never target customers using artificial intelligence to charge them more. 

“We price the product, not the person,” John Furner wrote.

“AI has added incredible new ways for us to serve our customers and members, and it rightfully also raises questions about the information it has access to,” he continued.

Furner said that Walmart wants to give customers the same amount of personal attention they’ve always received in-store when they’re shopping with AI assistant “Sparky” online.

"When you engage with Sparky, that’s an invitation to serve you better, not to use your personal information to set a personalized price,” Furner promised. “We’ve never used the relationships our associates have with customers to charge more, and we won’t do that with AI.”

New Mexico jury finds Facebook liable of deceiving users in privacy protection case

Pope warns a 'paradise of machines' could undermine the daily lives of humans

Pope Leo XIV warned Friday that a "paradise of machines" could pose a risk to humanity during the opening to his four-day trip in France.

In a speech at the Élysée Palace, the pope said it was an urgent concern of his that "we do not lose our humanity amid the paradise of machines that are invading and conditioning our daily lives," according to the Guardian.

The phrase "paradise of machines" is a reference to Georges Bernanos’s 1947 essay "France Against the Robots," in which he argued that industrialization and technological advancement was contributing to the dulling of the human spirit and mass loneliness.

Pope Leo has made artificial intelligence one of the defining issues of his papacy, creating a commission within the Vatican in May to address the growing ethical concerns with AI.

"Technology should not be considered, in itself, as a force antagonistic to humanity," Pope Leo wrote in his May 2026 encyclical Magnifica Humanitas. "At the same time, each phase of progress has also revealed the ambiguity of tools that can cause harm when not oriented toward the good."

Appeals court rules Pentagon can continue blacklisting Anthropic from government contracts

A federal appeals court ruled Friday that the Trump administration is allowed to exclude Anthropic from being considered for all U.S. military contracts.

In a 2-1 ruling, the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals found that Pentagon acted lawfully when it removed Anthropic's Claude AI from its supply chain.

The court argued that the company's ability to restrict how its models performed on certain "lawful" military tasks constituted a national security risk under federal law.

Back in February, the Pentagon claimed Anthropic had asked whether its product was used in the January military operation to capture Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro, Fox News Digital reported at the time.

Sources told Fox News Digital that Anthropic posed this question in a way that suggested it may not approve of its potential involvement in the Maduro operation.

The relationship between the Department of War and Anthropic deteriorated further when CEO Dario Amodei put out a blog post on Feb. 26, saying he would not allow Claude to be used for "mass domestic surveillance" or "fully autonomous weapons."

"Anthropic understands that the Department of War, not private companies, makes military decisions," Amodei wrote. "However, in a narrow set of cases, we believe AI can undermine, rather than defend, democratic values."

Shortly after Amodei's statement was released, War Secretary Pete Hegseth announced that the federal government would cease using all of Anthropic's technology.

"Our position has never wavered and will never waver: the Department of War must have full, unrestricted access to Anthropic’s models for every LAWFUL purpose in defense of the Republic," Hegseth wrote in a post on X at the time. "Cloaked in the sanctimonious rhetoric of 'effective altruism,' they have attempted to strong-arm the United States military into submission."

New York City set to regulate AI companies, with one proposed bill mandating a 'kill switch'

The New York City Council is readying legislation to rein in the artificial intelligence industry as AI companies continue to grow their presence in the city.

One bill will ban businesses from selling or deploying AI systems in the city unless an independent inspector certified they met city standards for data quality, bias, privacy and security, Fortune reported Friday morning.

That bill will also require AI labs to engineer a "kill switch," i.e. a human override to shut down a model or agent in the event it goes rogue.

Failing to comply with these regulations will lead to $25,000 fines per violation, City Council Speaker Julie Menin told Fortune in an interview. Since the penalties would be assessed for each AI agent involved, the total fines could be substantial.

Another piece of legislation will reward whistleblowers with a portion of the fines collected from AI firms who break the law, Fortune reported.

The new regulations are expected to be introduced on Friday ahead of an Oct. 5 Committee of the Whole hearing with all 51 council members in attendance.

Mark Zuckerberg casts doubt on AI 'doom' rhetoric, says firms don't need to collaborate on safety

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg said Wednesday that companies developing advanced artificial intelligence models do not need to work together to prevent the catastrophic outcomes that many industry leaders have warned about.

In an interview with former Wall Street Journal columnist Joanna Stern, Zuckerberg also cast doubt on the mores alarmist members of the AI sector, some of whom have said human extinction is possibility if AI improves without guardrails in place.

"I think that there's a lot of rhetoric that is filled with doom, and my view on this is that there are a number of labs that are working on building advanced AI, and I don't think that we need some kind of industry-wide coordination to not necessarily mess this up," he said on Stern's show "New Things."

"I think that just each lab needs to take the time, and when it sees that there are issues, you just take the time that you need internally to make sure that you're proceeding safely," Zuckerberg added.

Meta launched its personal AI assistant, Muse, on Sept. 8 in the United States. It's available on major app stores and has been integrated into Meta's Ray Ban audio glasses.

Elon Musk predicts nearly all computing will move to space as Google tests AI plan

Elon Musk is making a sky-high prediction for artificial intelligence. The SpaceX CEO says computing will eventually move almost entirely off Earth.

“The amount of compute in space will obviously round up to 100% of all compute,” Musk wrote on X in response to a video about Google’s Project Suncatcher.

The project explores whether solar-powered satellites carrying Google’s AI chips could work together as an orbital data center. In the video Musk responded to, researchers describe satellites flying in tight formation and linking their chips to handle larger computing tasks.

The idea is still in the testing stage. Google has said it plans to launch two prototype satellites with partner Planet by early 2027 to test the hardware in orbit. The X post sharing the video claimed Google would launch chips on a SpaceX Falcon 9 next week, but Google’s published plan does not confirm that timeline. Google says the satellites could tap into near-constant sunlight to power AI chips. Its first planned test involves just two prototypes, leaving a long way to go before Musk’s vision of moving nearly all computing into orbit could become reality.
