Campsite Ranger Hunts Colorado's Hardest-to-Book Sites | ActionHub

TheTools · 09.10.2026 01:54 · source · permalink

News

Chris Dorsey created Campsite Ranger to allow folks to select the date range and area they’d like to take a trip within. (Courtesy photo)

Anyone who has tried to lock down a prime Colorado campsite knows the drill: Reservations open six months out, the best spots vanish in hours, and even a lucky booking can be wiped out by wildfire or smoke. An Arvada father has built a website to take that scramble off your plate.

Chris Dorsey, 38, created Campsite Ranger, a platform that tracks cancellations at 49 campgrounds and permitted areas across the state and alerts users when a spot opens. Users pick a date range and an area, set a watch, and the tool continuously checks recreation.gov for openings, then pings them so they can book.

“A minute later, an hour later, a day later, somebody’s probably going to cancel, and there will be an opening,” Dorsey told the Arvada Press.

Cancellation alerts are something other sites already do. Dorsey built Campsite Ranger to go further. It follows timed-entry permits, including the ones sold by Rocky Mountain National Park, and sets calendar reminders for upcoming permit releases so campers who can plan ahead do not miss them. It also pulls together conditions that are usually scattered across different pages — weather, air quality, and safety alerts — and keeps an eye on campground closures, which happened often this year amid the wildfires, then points users toward a nearby spot that remains open.

“The whole point of my site is to be proactive,” Dorsey told the Colorado Sun.

Watching one area for one date is free, and Dorsey said it always will be. Paid options cover multiple campgrounds and multiple date ranges — a one-time trip pass, or a lifetime membership for $25 a year, a price that may rise as sign-ups grow. The site is about a month old, with a small user base Dorsey of mostly friends and colleagues at the moment.

Dorsey said stated goal is to get more people outside, especially parents of young kids.
