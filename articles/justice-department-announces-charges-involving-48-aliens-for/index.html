Justice Department Announces Charges Involving 48 Aliens for Election-Related Crimes

TheTools · 26.10.2026 13:14 · source · permalink

Today, the Justice Department announced charges involving 48 aliens in Pennsylvania, California, Florida, New York, and Nevada in connection with illegal voting, fraudulent voter registration, and related election crimes. The cases include voting by aliens in federal elections and false claims of U.S. citizenship to register to vote or to vote.

Case details are as follows:

Eastern District of Pennsylvania

Four aliens, including an illegal alien, have been charged with illegally voting.

  • Alfredo Santiesteban, 73, an illegal alien from Cuba, has been charged by information with one count of voting by an alien. Santiesteban allegedly voted in the 2024 general election.
  • Polish national Artur Kopacz, 50, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by information with two counts of voting by an alien. Kopacz allegedly voted in the 2022 and 2024 general elections.
  • Cuban national Osmal Loy, 70, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by information with one count of voting by an alien. Loy allegedly voted in the 2024 general election. 
  • Israeli national Yehuda Peer, 30, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by information with one count of voting by an alien. Peer allegedly voted in the 2024 general election.

Middle District of Pennsylvania

Five aliens have been charged with illegally voting.

  • Kenyan national Nina Nyawara, 21, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien. Nyawara allegedly voted in an election held for the purpose of electing a candidate for President, Vice President, U.S. Senator, and Member of the House of Representatives in 2024.
  • Russian national Inna Henry, 52, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien. Henry allegedly voted in an election held for the purpose of electing a candidate for U.S. Senator and Member of the House of Representatives in 2022.
  • Kenyan national Joyce Donovan, 38, a lawful permanent resident, with voting by an alien. Donovan allegedly voted in an election held for the purpose of electing a candidate for President, Vice President, U.S. Senator and Member of the House of Representatives in 2024.
  • Dutch national Joyce Pitts, 80, a lawful permanent resident, with voting by an alien. Pitts allegedly voted in an election held for the purpose of electing a candidate for President, Vice President, U.S. Senator and Member of the House of Representatives in 2022.
  • Greek national Nourten Kuruju, 64, a lawful permanent resident, with voting by an alien. Kuruju allegedly voted in an election held for the purpose of electing a candidate for President, Vice President, U.S. Senator and Member of the House of Representatives in 2022.

Central District of California

The following 13 defendants in this jurisdiction face felony charges, including false statements in a naturalization application.

  • Belizean national Omar Alexander Muñoz, 72, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in September 2019, claiming he was a U.S. citizen, then voted in U.S. elections in 2020, twice in 2022, and in 2024. He also lied in his application to become a naturalized U.S. citizen when he falsely stated under oath that he had never claimed to be a U.S. citizen.
  • Indian national Amandeep Kaur, 30, a lawful permanent resident, who is seeking to become a U.S. citizen, allegedly registered to vote in December 2022 and voted in the November 2024 U.S. election.
  • Salvadoran national Ana Domínguez, 60, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in July 2006 and lied in her naturalization paperwork in March 2026.
  • Guatemalan national Axel Giovani Prera García, 46, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2002 and 2005, and lied in his naturalization paperwork in June 2025.
  • Maxican national Flavio Alejandro Zúñiga, 43, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2003 and lied in his naturalization paperwork in February 2026.
  • Cambodian national Butleng Taing, 43, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in July 2021, voted in the 2022 and 2024 U.S. elections, and lied in his naturalization paperwork in August 2025.
  • Honduran national Carlos Antonio Galeas, 54, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2012 and 2016, then lied in his naturalization paperwork in September 2018.
  • Mexican national Ernest Gutiérrez Ruvalcaba, 57, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in February 2024 and voted one month later.
  • Mexican national Jesús Narez-Madrigal, 52, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2014, voted in the 2024 U.S. general election, and lied in his naturalization paperwork in June 2024.
  • Mexican national José Antonio Bañuelos-Díaz, 42, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2007 and lied in his naturalization paperwork in March 2025.
  • Pakistani national Muhammad Khan, 50, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly submitted a provisional ballot in 2020 and lied in his naturalization paperwork five years later.
  • Canadian national Rouzbeh Seyamaknejad, 63, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in December 2018, lied in his naturalization application in January 2024, and voted in the November 2024 U.S. election.
  • Mexican national Sulema de León, 35, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2013, lied in her naturalization application in 2019, and voted in the November 2020 U.S. general election.

Eastern District of California

The following six defendants have been charged with voting by an alien in a federal election, a misdemeanor that carries a statutory maximum sentence of one year in federal prison.

  • Mexican national Maricela Nazarín González, 43, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2020, indicating that she was a U.S. citizen, and voted in elections in 2022, twice in 2024, and 2025.
  • Guatemalan national Rony de Jesús Sicaja, 38, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2017, and voted in the June 2022 primary election and the 2024 general election in the United States.
  • Mexican national Camino Giles Jiménez, 51, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly voted in three U.S. elections despite knowing he is a noncitizen, the most recent election being the June 2026 primary.
  • Mexican national Juan Antonio Rivas García, 46, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2018 and 2020, both times falsely asserting under oath that he was a U.S. citizen. Rivas voted in the 2022 and 2024 U.S. elections.
  • Filipino national Giezell Viduya Adawag, 41, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote the day of the November 2024 general election, falsely certified that she was a U.S. citizen, and voted in the election that day.
  • Indian national Kamlesh Navnitlal Shah, 67, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote on the day of the November 2024 general election and voted that day. He is charged by information.

Northern District of California

The following five defendants have been charged with attempted procurement of naturalization contrary to law, false statement under oath in any matter relating to naturalization or citizenship, and voting by an alien.

  • Mexican national Verónica Bullene, 43, a.k.a. “Verónica Hernández Martínez”, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly made false statements in her naturalization application that she had never claimed to be a U.S. citizen when, in fact, she did so under penalty of perjury to register to vote in 2022 and 2023 then voted in 2024.
  • Georgian national David Shapiro, 41, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2024 and later voted in a U.S. election, and made false statements in his naturalization application that he had never claimed to be a U.S. citizen.
  • Myanmar national Moe Thida Lwin, 57, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in 2024 and then proceeded to illegally vote in a U.S. citizen. She made false statements in her naturalization paperwork as well.
  • Tongan national Manakotau Mataele Finau, 61, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote in the 2024 election, voted, and then lied about it in naturalization paperwork.
  • Ecuadoran national Maribel del Carmen Garcés Barriga, 62, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly made false statements in her naturalization paperwork that she  had never claimed U.S. citizenship and had never voted in a U.S. election or had registered to vote. In fact, Garcés fraudulently registered to vote in 2024 and proceeded to vote in a U.S. election.

Southern District of California

The following four defendants have been charged with false claim to citizenship to vote, unlawful attempted procurement of naturalization, and false statement under oath in matter relating to naturalization or citizenship.

  • Filipino national José Probitsado Amaro, 51, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote by falsely claiming to be a U.S. citizen. He then cast a ballot in the November 2024 general election and made false statements during his naturalization interview, denying that he had ever claimed U.S. citizenship and misrepresenting his voting history.
  • Canadian national Jennifer Jung, 31, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote by falsely claiming to be a U.S. citizen. She then cast a ballot in the November 2024 general election and made false statements during her naturalization interview, denying that she had ever claimed U.S. citizenship and misrepresenting her voting history.
  • Guatemalan national Erick Ronaldo Soto Mendizábal, 64, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly registered to vote by falsely claiming to be a U.S. citizen. He then cast a ballot in the November 2024 general election and made false statements in his naturalization application, denying that he had ever claimed U.S. citizenship and misrepresenting his voting history.
  • Filipino national Pamele Arbués Line, 56, a lawful permanent resident, allegedly voted in the November 2024 general election, and made false statements in her naturalization interview, denying that she had ever claimed U.S. citizenship and misrepresenting her voting history.

Middle District of Florida

Six aliens have been charged with election related crimes. 

  • Cuban national Jamie Orlando Sosa Sardina, 60, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by indictment with making a false statement claiming U.S. citizenship to vote and illegal voting by an alien. Sardina allegedly registered to vote in 2022 and voted in the 2022 and 2024 federal general elections.
  • Ecuadorian national Martha Haydee Desantis, 81, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by indictment with making a false statement claiming U.S. citizenship to vote and illegal voting by an alien. Desantis allegedly re-registered to vote in 2024 and voted in the 2022 and 2024 federal general elections.
  • Canadian national Lise Marie Johnson, 69, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by indictment with making a false statement claiming U.S. citizenship to vote and illegal voting by an alien. Johnson allegedly registered to vote in 2021 and voted in the 2022 federal general election.
  • Cuban national Luis Manuel Perez, 72, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by information with illegal voting by an alien. Perez allegedly voted in the 2022 and 2024 federal general elections.
  • Canadian national Diane Wendy Curley, 55, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by information with illegal voting by an alien. Curley allegedly voted in the 2022 and 2024 federal general elections.
  • Brazilian national Pedro Igor A. Franco, 37, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged by information with illegal voting by an alien. Franco voted in the 2024 federal general election.

Northern District of New York

German national Marva Cook, 66, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien. Cook allegedly voted in an election held in part for the purpose of electing a candidate for the U.S. Senate and the U.S. House of Representatives.

Eastern District of New York

Three aliens have been charged with election related crimes. 

  • Irish national Eoin Cranney, 35, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien in a federal election. Cranney allegedly submitted a fraudulent voter registration application and voted in the 2024 federal general election.  In a voluntary interview with federal agents, Cranney admitted, in substance and in part, that he was not a U.S. citizen, that he had registered to vote in 2024, and that he had voted in the 2024 federal general election knowing that he was not a U.S. citizen.
  • Guyanese national Ronald Sheldon Baird, 47, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien in a federal election. Baird allegedly submitted a fraudulent voter registration application and voted in the 2024 federal general election.  In a voluntary interview with federal agents, Baird admitted, in substance and in part, that he was not a U.S. citizen and that he had voted in the 2024 federal general election knowing that he was not a U.S. citizen.
  • Jamaican national Karlene Daniels Samuels, 55, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien in a federal election. Samuels allegedly submitted a fraudulent voter registration application and voted in the 2024 federal general election.  In a voluntary interview with federal agents, Baird admitted, in substance and in part, that she was not a U.S. citizen and that she had voted in the 2024 federal general election knowing that she was not a U.S. citizen.

District of Nevada

Filipino national Jullie Ann Sumalinog Moore, 44, a lawful permanent resident, has been charged with voting by an alien. Moore allegedly voted in an election held in part for the purpose of electing a candidate for the office of President, Vice President, Member of the Senate, and Member of the House of Representatives. 

 

A complaint, indictment, and information merely contain accusations. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in a court of law.
