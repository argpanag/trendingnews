Should JD Vance run for president? I say go for it, habibi! | Arwa Mahdawi

TheTools · 17.11.2026 15:21 · source · permalink

Now the vice-president’s luck is turning, he seems reluctant to commit himself to the next presidential race – and I, for one, hate to see it

How do you think history will remember JD Vance? As the first vice-president to spark a vigorous debate about their eyeliner routine? As one of the last public figures to see Pope Francis alive? As the only politician in US history to have been the subject of a rumour about sexual relations with a couch (even though it was unfounded)?

I have no idea. But it seems increasingly unlikely he will be remembered as the 48th president of the US. After Maga’s midterm thrashing, Vance’s political future doesn’t look as bright as it used to. So much so that there has been a flurry of recent headlines debating whether or not Vance, once considered Donald Trump’s heir apparent, may slink away from a presidential run altogether.

At CNN, for example, “people close to [Vance]” report the VP is giving “serious consideration to not running for president in 2028”. One of the people with the misfortune of being close to Vance noted that he is 42, a mere baby, and can afford to sit out the next election. “He’s young enough that he can have a comeback should this environment make success impossible,” the unnamed source said. What “environment” would that be, eh? The one in which Vance and his boss managed to muck up the country so badly that even the world’s stupidest podcast bros have turned against them?

The US news website Axios dug up “two sources with knowledge of [Vance’s] plans”, who shared the very secret, very exciting revelation that Vance does not know what his plans are. “People familiar with Vance’s thinking say he’s genuinely undecided about running,” Axios added. If you can’t bear all the suspense, bad news: we may have to wait until late spring for a definitive answer. According to Axios, Vance is “wary that launching a campaign too early could shift attention away from Trump and risk alienating him”.

That’s a valid concern, considering Vance doesn’t seem to be in the president’s good books. After the midterms, JD wrote a long, and quite sensible, social media post arguing the war in Iran was why Republicans had lost so badly. (Vance has always had just enough brain cells to see the war was a terrible idea and has repeatedly tried to distance himself from it, even while defending it in public.) Trump, who still hasn’t endorsed a successor, shrugged off that critique in a post-election 60 Minutes interview, saying he had no problem with it, and even made a similar statement to the Wall Street Journal. However, he also unceremoniously booted Vance from Iran negotiations.

Other Republicans were more direct with their criticism of Vance. “I don’t think the vice-president did himself any favours by sending out that email or tweet or whatever it was after the election,” senator John Kennedy said in an interview with CNN.

Vance is not an idiot. But he is also not the political genius one imagines he likes to think he is. The reason he went from writing about hillbillies to being one of the youngest vice-presidents in US history at super speed is less about his political instincts and more about the fact he had a little help from some billionaire friends. Chief among these was Peter Thiel, who hired Vance at his investment company in 2016, poured millions into Vance’s Senate campaign and brokered Vance’s first meeting with Trump. Elon Musk and Tucker Carlson also helped ingratiate Vance with Trump.

He has been exceptionally lucky. Now, however, his luck is turning. The Trump administration is lurching from failure to failure and Vance is inextricably tied to this dumpster fire. Even Fox News seems sick of him. “You guys have been in charge for the last two years,” Laura Ingraham said in an interview, when Vance deflected blame for Friday’s nationwide flight stoppage. “Why hasn’t it gotten fixed?” Oooh, I’ll answer that one, Laura! It’s because they’re incompetent.

While this may not be the very best month of the vice-president’s life, I’d urge him not to shelve his immediate presidential ambitions. Please run in 2028, JD, habibi! This is a fairly selfish ask, I admit, as I’m eager to see him squirm. Unless Trump miraculously manages to fix cost-of-living problems, I’m not sure any Republican could win in 2028. As vice-president, Vance is going to have an extra tough time. Watching him try to distance himself from Trump will involve mental gymnastics so elaborate, they’ll make Simone Biles look like an amateur. Still, I suspect the man will land on his feet. If Vance’s brilliant political career goes bung, he has plenty of practice at reinventing himself.

Arwa Mahdawi is a Guardian columnist
