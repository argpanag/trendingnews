Amorim discusses 'fun' Frosinone, final third struggles and confirms Pavlovic's return

TheTools · 21.11.2026 13:49 · source · permalink

On the eve of AC Milan’s game against Frosinone, Ruben Amorim spoke to the media at the usual pre-match press conference. 

The international break is officially over and Milan will be back in action tomorrow. After wins against Inter and Genoa, the goal is to make it three consecutive wins, although an in-form Frosinone side will be looking to make life difficult for them.

Amorim’s plan appears to be to field an attacking-minded starting eleven, picking up where he left off against Genoa. It’s certainly a bold plan as Frosinone will surely look to cause issues on the counter, as also highlighted by Oliver in his prediction.

Amorim’s pre-match presser

Speaking to the media at Milanello, Amorim shared his thoughts on the game, the status of the players after the international break, and the tactical solutions moving forward.

On Fondazione Milan…

“We as a club have a big responsibility. Fondazione Milan is really important because it reflects our values and helps the community. Our doors are always open for everyone. It’s also good for the players to see and live different realities.”

On those involved in international duty…

“I think everyone is ready. Of course, we had to control the load during the week, because some of them travelled a lot. I’m really pleased with our players getting game time, because that’s the best way to maintain, so we are ready for the game.”

On Pavlovic’s status and Frosinone’s style..

“Starting with Pavlovic, he’s doing better. He will be in the squad. I don’t know if he will start or not, so I will see. He wants to play and I want him to play. We miss that fire that he has. Let’s see, but for sure he will be in the team.

“Frosinone are fun to watch, they are the team that runs the most, and we are second so I think we can fight that. They are really good with 1v1s on the flanks. If you watch their games it’s hard to understand their movement. There is some logic in their chaos, but it’s hard to spot.

“They play a different style, more direct, which allows them to have the ball more inside the box. So it’s going to be a really hard game. As I said against Venezia, there are some teams that we need to be really focused against because their styles can cause issues.”

Camarda is getting little game time, despite the Ramos struggles. Is a strong deputy striker missing?

“Two different questions. Of course we can say that one more option would be a good thing, but I think the struggles of Ramos are the struggles of the team. We are not reaching the final third, we are one of the worst teams in that sense. So we don’t have a lot of shots.

“I don’t want loads of shots outside the box, because it’s hard to score with those. We need to get into the box and we are not helping Ramos. What I saw against Genoa is that without Ramos, we wouldn’t have scored those goals. But again, we need to do better in the final third, my goal is to push the team forward. That’s my job.

“It’s true that he’s working hard and doing a lot of good. I know that it’s not his sole job to press, we spent a lot of money on a player to score goals, but we are not doing enough to help him. We need to push the team forward.”

Are you happy with the league standings so far? And against Genoa, you basically played with four attacking players. Can this be a solution for the future?

“Starting with the last question, it depends on the opponents and the moment of the team. I don’t believe that playing with more attackers means you will reach the final third more. Sometimes you get that effect from having more midfielders, for example. We are always going to change in some matches depending on the opponent.

“On the first question, it’s difficult to say. I think that we have grown as a team quite fast, but I’m not pleased with the overall situation. We need to be so much better and as such, I’m not pleased with where we are in the standings. But of course, I need to say that I’m happy with how the players have worked.”

During the break, we saw Camarda scoring for the U21s. Could a loan be the right solution?

“I was a young player myself once too. A young player wants everything really badly, which is a good thing. That said, he needs time to adapt, it’s really hard to play for a club like Milan. You just said there is pressure on Ramos, imagine what it’s like for Camarda.

“I don’t know what will happen in January, but he needs to be focused on these games. Everything can change in just a few weeks.

“I’m really happy with how he has been since he returned from international duty, I heard that Baldini spoke with him at length (as Baldini said himself), and he returned in a happier mood. I understand the question about January, but January is so far away right now.”

The feeling is that the team is struggling to keep control at the end of the games…

“Sometimes even when we are winning, we have dropped the levels in every action of the game. I think we are controlling everything better, you saw it in the second half against Genoa without spending a lot of energy, but I think it’s all connected to the attack.

“If we don’t create danger when we are leading, we will struggle a bit to kill the games. Of course, we need to address that and we are working on it.”

On Musah as a left-back and if more versatile players will be signed…

“That was a specific situation. I don’t know and I’m not going to tell you guys what we are thinking about January. I wanted a right-footed player against Genoa. You saw the runs of Rabiot, so I wanted someone right-footed to pass the ball on immediately.

“Musah is not going to play as a left-back tomorrow, I can tell you that, but it’s an option. I like to have players who can play in several positions.”

On Pulisic’s positive form…

“I think it helps any player to feel the confidence from the club. I think it’s clear that was done with the new contract. Then I also told you when he wasn’t playing that he’s really important for us. I can tell you today that Pulisic perhaps is the one player we cannot replace.

“The movement in between the lines he brings, we simply don’t have another Pulisic. His absence is always felt a lot, so from that you can understand how important he is for us. He doesn’t talk a lot, but he lets the pitch do the talking.

“I would put him in the same category as Rabiot. Players who are on another level. I don’t like speaking about individuals, but I think everyone can see this.”

Can the January transfer window serve to raise the team’s ambition?

“That is the biggest mistake of the big clubs. Looking at the table and deciding whether to invest or not. I think the key is to have a long-term plan. Stick with your plan. We have one and everything is very clear.

“Trying to become a champion by spending money is the biggest mistake. The key is following your plan. I look at the table to evaluate the process as a whole, not to decide whether to spend a lot on signings.”

Are you keeping track of your players so they don’t stay up late at night playing GTA?

“I will let my friend Gennaro Gattuso respond. Jokes aside, I don’t care.”

Is a winner a dreamer who never gives up? Are you a dreamer? 

“I wouldn’t call it a dream but the goal is to win every game. If you look at my journey, every club I chose, I clearly am a dreamer [laughs]. I really want to be part of this club for the rest of my career, but I also know that I need to prove myself.

“I’m a dreamer, yes, but also very focused on the goal.”
