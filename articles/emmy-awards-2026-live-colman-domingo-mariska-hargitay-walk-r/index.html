  3. Will Colbert be recognised for his last season of The Late Show?published at 00:33 BST
    00:33 BST

    Nardine Saad
    at the 78th Primetime Emmy Awards

    The Late Show went off the air in May, ending the late-night chat show's 33-year run on CBS, as well as Stephen Colbert's 11-year stint as its host.

    The comedian is up against ABC's Jimmy Kimmel Live - whose host has repeatedly sparred with the Trump administration - HBO’s perennial Emmy winner Last Week Tonight with John Oliver, Comedy Central’s late-night vehicle The Daily Show and NBC’s long-running variety series Saturday Night Live.

    If the category sounds stacked with heavyweights, that's because it is. This year the Television Academy combined its talk series and scripted variety series contenders, bringing several repeat nominees and winners into the fold.

    Since it became eligible for the Emmys in 2016, The Late Show With Stephen Colbert has been nominated nearly every year since. It won for outstanding talk series for the first time last year after Colbert's fellow late-night hosts rallied behind him on the heels of CBS announcing the show would end.

    It remains to be seen if history will repeat itself tonight, but it's worth pointing out that the final season has already picked up six wins at the 2026 Creative Arts Emmy Award, which were held last weekend.

  12. Welsh actor Gerran Howell: Brits shocked by US healthcare system shown in The Pittpublished at 23:46 BST
    23:46 BST

    David Willis and Regan Morris

    Welsh actor Gerran Howell, who does a convincing American accent as Dr Dennis Whitaker on the buzzy medical drama The Pitt, says members of the medical community have been the show's biggest fans.

    “It's taken off," Howell told the BBC at a pre-Emmys party. "It's become a real word-of-mouth thing. It's amazing."

    And, he added, he's grateful it has recently become available to audiences back home in the UK.

    Howell says the The Pitt's reputation for showing the realities of an American emergency department in action have fascinated British audiences and healthcare workers.

    "I think the medical communities are very different," he says. "I'd like to think of it as maybe a bit of a cautionary tale for our system, however different it is from the American system."

    He adds that he knows many Americans are envious of Britain’s free national healthcare.

    “A lot British people are very shocked seeing the American healthcare system," he says. "But you know, we've got our faults too.”

  16. The steady climb of streamers at awards shows, explainedpublished at 23:26 BST
    23:26 BST

    Steven McIntosh
    Entertainment reporter

    Given the huge changes in the entertainment landscape over the last two decades, it’s no surprise that the Emmy nominations are once again dominated by streaming services.

    You’re far more likely to see a Netflix or Apple show cropping up these days than TV shows that are broadcast the old-fashioned way - once a week, in a scheduled time slot.

    That’s not to say there aren’t shows from traditional broadcasters being recognised at this year's awards show. School-based comedy Abbott Elementary is nominated across multiple categories and has won a couple of big prizes in the past.

    But traditional shows often find it harder to build awards momentum. Streamers have the luxury of choosing how many episodes they make per season and can whip up enough anticipation to make their release feel like a real event.

    Shows such as Abbott Elementary, in contrast, are often obligated to churn out 20 to 25 episodes per season in order to fill the broadcast schedules. That means they arguably spread themselves more thinly, and don’t ignite the same must-watch passion in fans.

Welsh actor Matthew Rhys, who stars in Widow's Bay and The Beast in Me, is nominated for his work in both shows at tonight's Emmys.

But, when asked if he would rather win an Emmy or a BAFTA Cymru - the Welsh branch of the British Academy of Film and Television Arts - he tells the BBC without hesitation it would be the latter.

“Home audiences are always the one you want to please the most, always,” Rhys says.
