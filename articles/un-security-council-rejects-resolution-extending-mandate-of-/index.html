UN Security Council rejects resolution extending mandate of expert panel on Iran sanctions

The UN Security Council on Thursday failed to adopt a draft resolution that would have extended the mandate of the Panel of Experts supporting the 1737 Iran Sanctions Committee.

The resolution, drafted by the United States, received 11 votes in favor, two against and two abstentions. Russia and China, both permanent members of the UN Security Council, voted against the draft resolution, blocking the measure.

In August 2025, France, Germany and the United Kingdom triggered the "snapback" mechanism to reimpose UN sanctions on Iran that had been suspended under the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) agreement and Security Council Resolution 2231.

Before the vote, representatives of China and Russia both argued that Resolution 2231, which endorsed the JCPOA on Iran's nuclear issue, expired on Oct 18, 2025, and that the Iran nuclear issue has since been removed from the Security Council's agenda.

The United Nations (UN) General Assembly on Thursday adopted a resolution allowing Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas' participation in this year's general debate via video, following the United States' denial of a visa to him.

The resolution, adopted with 152 votes in favor, 3 against and 4 abstentions, decides that Palestine may submit a pre-recorded statement by Abbas, which will be played in the General Assembly Hall during the general debate at the 81st session after introduction by its representative who is physically present.

The resolution also decides that Palestine may deliver statements via video conference or submit a pre-recorded statement at meetings of the high-level international conference for the peaceful settlement of the question of Palestine and the implementation of the two-state solution.

The resolution allows Palestine to submit a pre-recorded statement of its president or any other high-level representative at any high-level meeting or UN conference, as well as at international conferences and meetings convened under the auspices of the General Assembly or other UN organs, if representatives of Palestine are prevented from participating in UN meetings.

The United States on Wednesday extended visa restrictions on Palestinian officials, including Abbas, preventing him from attending the UN General Assembly high-level week in person for the second consecutive year.

"The leadership of the State of Palestine rejects and condemns the action of denying the Palestinian delegation headed by His Excellency President Mahmoud Abbas, to exercise their lawful right to participate in the works of the United Nations and the high-level week of the UNGA week," said Riyad Mansour, permanent observer of the State of Palestine to the United Nations.

This year's general debate will be held on September 22-26 and September 28.
