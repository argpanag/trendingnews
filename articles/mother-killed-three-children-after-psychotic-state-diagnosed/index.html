Mother killed three children after psychotic state diagnosed as ‘stress’

TheTools · 24.10.2026 10:34 · source · permalink

Yasmeen Ali, 44, sentenced to indefinite hospital order after NHS admitted failings in her care

A mother killed her three children in a “psychotic state” days after the NHS discharged her with “acute stress”.

Yasmeen Ali, 44, fatally stabbed her children – Fares Bash, 7, Joury Bash, 3, and 9-month-old Mohammed Bash – and tried to take her own life at the family’s home in Sea Mills, Bristol, in February 2024.

She was charged with murder but admitted three charges of manslaughter by reason of diminished responsibility during a hearing at Bristol Crown Court on Friday.

On Friday, she was sentenced to an indefinite hospital order, after Mr Justice Linden heard psychiatric evidence that her offending was “almost entirely attributable to mental disorder”.

The judge also told the court that the NHS have admitted failings in her care, after she was discharged with an “acute stress reaction”.

He said he had read an independent report about Ali’s care by Avon and Wiltshire Mental Health Partnership NHS Trust and a letter from its chief nursing officer apologising “for failings” in care.  

The court heard Ali, who was born in Sudan, came to the UK in 2018 to join her husband, Alfil Bash. However, their relationship became strained during her third pregnancy.    

The judge described how the couple argued about money, with Ali telling family her husband was spending on gambling and becoming “bad tempered when he had lost”.

Police were called to the family home in December 2023, where officers noted Ali hiding behind them and visibly shaking. Joury demonstrated how Mr Bash had put his hands around Ali’s throat, the court heard.

Mr Bash was arrested and remanded into custody, but the charges were dropped after the prosecution offered no evidence in the case against him on June 10, 2024.

Mental health assessment cancelled

Concerns were raised over Ali’s mental health on Feb 6, 2024, after she was “frightened” about her husband returning or being evicted from the home, which was in his name. 

An assessment under the Mental Health Act 1983 – to establish whether Ali required inpatient treatment – was requested, but was not progressed “owing to the lack of any available hospital bed”.

The assessment was cancelled on Feb 9 and was discharged from mental health services on Feb 12, after clinicians believed she had suffered an “acute stress reaction” on Feb 6, the judge said.

A neighbour called 999 shortly after midnight on Feb 18 with concerns over Ali’s welfare and her children.

Police attended the property and could hear “screaming and chanting”, with Ali discovered partially clothed with self-inflicted stab wounds to her throat and stomach.

Her three children, who had suffered “terrible injuries”, were discovered deceased on her bed, the judge said.

“They are clearly consistent with you being in a psychotic state when you inflicted them,” he added.

‘A total shock’

At Southmead Hospital, Ali told a nurse she had received messages to kill her children at midnight, “but no-one died and I asked Allah for forgiveness”, the court heard.

Mr Justice Linden said Ali, who was initially unaware she had harmed her children, was diagnosed with a schizoaffective disorder.

“Your actions came as a total shock to those who knew you, and saw you on a regular basis. You were clearly a loving mother to your children and cared deeply for them,” he told Ali.

Representing Ali, Andrew Langdon KC said his client “was unable to form any rational judgment or exercise any control”, and “didn’t realise that her children were dead”.

A spokesman for Avon and Wiltshire Mental Health Partnership NHS Trust said it was unable to comment on an individual basis until the publication of a review by Bristol Safeguarding Board, expected to be in a few weeks.
