El Niño ramps up; warming could be big hit to PNW snowpack | Rogue Valley Times

TheTools · 15.11.2026 14:30 · source · permalink

Published 6:30 am Sunday, November 15, 2026

By Don Jenkins

The current El Niño far exceeds the “very strong” threshold and likely will have the staying power to be the strongest on record, the National Weather Service reports.

The latest readings show the sea-surface temperatures along the equator in the central Pacific were 2.5 degrees Celsius higher than normal, according to the weather service’s Climate Prediction Center. If sustained, the temperature would be the highest on record for late fall and early winter.

Temperatures 2 degrees above normal trigger a very strong El Niño. Forecasters said Nov. 12 that they expect El Niño to warm winter temperatures and dampen snowpacks in the Northwest.

During past very strong El Niños, Oregon’s winter temperatures averaged 1 degree Fahrenheit above normal, but that was enough to raise snow levels by 500 to 1,000 feet, Oregon State Climatologist Larry O’Neill said. “That could be really significant,” he said.

Forecasters are less confident in making predictions about precipitation in the Northwest, though expect California to be in the path of soaking storms.

The climate center has been reporting for several months that this El Niño could be the strongest ever, according to records dating back to 1950.

El Niños are graded on a three-month rolling average. The October-November-December record is 2.3 degrees Celsius above normal set in 1997.

O’Neill said he expects this El Niño to break that record. The climate center puts the chances at 60%.

Sea-surface temperatures have not peaked, according to forecasting models. The El Niño probably will continue to strengthen until early next year, the climate center said.

El Niño may already be contributing to warm early November temperatures, but won’t have its greatest impact until December, January and February, O’Neill said. Arctic blasts, unrelated to El Nino, could still bring cold spells to the region, he said.

The World Meteorological Organization predicted last month that this El Niño will smash records.

The organization predicted sea-surface temperatures will be 3.7 degrees Celsius above normal in the October-December period. The record for any three-month period is 2.6 degrees set between November 2015 and January 2016.

October was warm in the Northwest, though O’Neill attributed that to rising global temperatures and the trend toward warmer months, not El Niño.

Idaho had its third warmest October on record, while Oregon and Washington had their 10th and 17th warmest Octobers, respectively.

California had its 15th warmest October. The U.S. had its fourth warmest October. Records date back to 1895.
