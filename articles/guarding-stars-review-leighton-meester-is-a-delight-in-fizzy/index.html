Guarding Stars review – Leighton Meester is a delight in fizzy Netflix holiday romance

TheTools · 18.11.2026 22:07 · source · permalink

As a female bodyguard protecting an A-list star, the Gossip Girl actor shines in Christmas movie a cut above the competition

Though it is my personal opinion that Christmas should not begin until after Thanksgiving, I am pleased to say that we already have at least one treat in Netflix’s holiday movie assembly line: Guarding Stars, starring Leighton Meester as a frosty bodyguard tasked with protecting Jared Padalecki’s movie star at, of all places, his family’s Montana ranch, falls on the fizzy side of seasonal fluff.

Good holiday movies are an exercise in knowing silliness, and so it is a relief when the first 10 minutes of this movie, directed by Elizabeth Allen Rosenbaum, strikes the rare December balance between hearth-like warmth (the twinkle in Meester’s chocolate eyes when her Hannah Brooks lands a verbal punch) and complete lunacy (she lands that punch in an argument with her ex about … cuddling … literally seconds after thwarting an assassination attempt in Times Square). As a homespun band of hot would-be cowboys later drawl at a Christmas parade in “Montana” (Canada, per usual): “Christmas is back, y’all.”

So is Meester: the actor still best known for playing the Upper East Side’s ultimate ice queen in Gossip Girl is one of the few who can pull off “career woman with a heart of stone” with enough zest to overpower cliche. One believes, even accepts, when Meester’s Hannah, dressed in FBI office drag and glasses almost campy in their unsexiness, refuses both hugs and the suggestion of paid rest following the death of her mother, instead opting for the assignment in Montana because she feels “constantly overlooked and underestimated, nobody sees me coming”.

Guarding Stars, adapted from Katherine Center’s novel by Erin Cardillo and Richard Keith, is so named for the unenviable – at least, to the no-nonsense Hannah – task of protecting celebrities as opposed to, say, defusing IEDs in Afghanistan. Is it ever plausible that Meester, forever shaded with peak millennial resting bitch face, toted an assault rifle in the Middle East (as doctored pics helpfully suggest)? Absolutely not, but Meester is an amiable and capable salesman of a woman perpetually unimpressed, particularly when she jiujitsus a skeptical Jack Stapleton (Padalecki) to the ground.

As an action movie star akin, as best as I can tell, to John Krasinski-as-Jack Ryan, Padalecki is perfectly suitable – grizzled, wearing aviator sunglasses, voice 20% huskier than usual and just the faintest whiff of the narcissism of a real People’s Sexiest Man Alive. There’s an immediate spark to the two leads – one a fake action hero, the other too vigilant for her own good, both lonely. But it is mostly a testament to Meester’s sparkling intelligence – you know she knows what’s up – that one happily glosses over 1. Why Jack needs a bodyguard (a stalker, I guess?) and 2. Why Jack immediately insists that his bodyguard also plays his fake girlfriend for Christmas (mom has cancer).

No matter! Hannah promptly gets a makeover straight out of the Sundance catalog (somehow, not one of the movie’s many clear brand sponsors) and bunkers down at the ranch with Jack’s ailing mother (Andie MacDowell, bringing the movie equivalent of simmering homemade stew), gruff father (Shaun Johnston), wary brother (Noah LaLonde) and rodeo queen sister-in-law (Siobhan Williams). Also, a handful of kids and some horses, all creatures that lone-wolf Hannah would ordinarily neutralize in less than two minutes. Meester/Hannah instead make slow, steady inroads on the verboten realm of “the personal” with enough sincerity and self-awareness to puncture one’s perhaps military-grade sap defenses. (That the obligatory grief/trauma reveal hits harder than expected – which is to say, it hits at all – is thanks largely to the savvy use of camcorder footage, an instant nostalgia trapdoor for this film’s core audience of elder millennials.)

It’s not that Guarding Stars is tinkering much with the recipe; it checks the same boxes and clears the same hurdles as any other holiday romcom – mollifying snowball fight, awkward “fake” kiss, unconvinced family, late-stage family blow-up and romantic revelations – but with the swiftness and action sequence flourishes of a professional athlete. Or, perhaps more accurately, an ex-army bodyguard adept at the tuck and roll. Female hyper-competence, it turns out, pairs well with holiday cheer, and when it’s Meester once again taking no prisoners, you have my attention.
