'Act of total evil' left Waterford community devastated

TheTools · 18.10.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

The joint funeral mass of Kim and Phillip English, and their sons Callum and Jayden, has heard that an "act of total evil" has left a family and community "devastated and heartbroken".

Hundreds of mourners have gathered at the Sacred Heart Church, where Parish Priest Fr Gerry Chestnutt said that last Tuesday morning, "forces of darkness came in the dark and devastated a family, taking four innocent lives".

Bishop for Waterford and Lismore Alphonsus Cullinan is presiding over the Mass while Fr Chestnutt delivered the homily.

The chaplains from the schools attend by 12-year-old Callum and nine-year-old Jayden are also con-celebrating the funeral mass.

President Catherine Connolly and Taoiseach Micheál Martin are represented by their aides-de-camp.

The Government is represented by Chief Whip Mary Butler and Minister for State John Cummins. Sinn Féin President Mary Lou McDonald is also in attendance.

In his homily, Fr Chestnutt said that following the arson attack on the family home in the Birchwood Estate: "The feelings of all are clear: heartbreak, shock, devastation and, yes, anger.

"What comforting words can we find this day? For the families, you have received in so many ways an outpouring of love and support which, you can be assured, will continue from this day on.

"What comforting words can we hear this afternoon to offer hope and consolation? The gospels provide words that speak of reality but also of hope and promise. The words of Saint Matthew's gospel speak of the reality of tragic death and grief".

Fr Chestnutt said that all those gathered in the church today were especially keeping two other children who were critically injured in their thoughts and prayers.

The parish priest paid tribute to each family member individually and said

Fr Chestnutt said: "Philip was funny, lovable, sensitive and caring. Family always was his priority. Any photograph that you have seen of him sums him up, always smiling.

"Kim had a different personality to Philip: vocal and feisty, but the most loving, kind and caring person.

"As parents, they adored their children. Their life as a family was a journey of love. And there were many journeys as three of their children faced illness. Through all of that they were completely united and dedicated.

"Callum, that mischievous smile. The photograph put up by Johnville Football Club is him. The smile, the winner's medal placed between his teeth. A lovable rogue. You tested the patience of family, friends, principal and teachers in Scoil Lorcáin. But Callum, you were loved by all.

"Everyone in Johnville, in fact everybody in this community is heartbroken. On behalf of all of us, Callum, you are a winner. A dark deed took life from you but your light will shine forever.

"Jayden, a force of love, life and energy came into the world when you were born. Boundless energy, you rarely stayed still. You were full of fun and mischief. Very often you were heard before you were seen.

"Life was never dull at home, in the Birchwood Estate or at Saint Joseph's School when you were around but you were adored by all," Fr Chestnutt added.

Fr Chestnutt also described the Philip, Kim, Callum and Jayden as a "beautiful family" who had done "no wrong to anybody".

He said: "On my own behalf and on behalf of everybody in this community, to you, Philip, Kim, Callum and Jayden, thank you for being yourselves, a beautiful family who did no wrong to anybody.

"We are better people for knowing you, for you were the salt of the earth and lights in this community".

The funeral cortège will stop for a moment outside the Birchwood Estate later this afternoon before continuing to Kilbarry cemetery, where Kim, Philip, Callum and Jayden will be laid to rest.

On Saturday, two people appeared in court charged in connection with the arson attack on the English family home.
