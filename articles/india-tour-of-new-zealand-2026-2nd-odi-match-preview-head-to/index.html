India tour of New Zealand 2026 2nd ODI: Match preview, head-to-head and streaming details

TheTools · 06.11.2026 12:30 · source · permalink

Historically, India have prevailed in ODIs, securing 64 victories out of 124 matches.

Coming off a commanding five-wicket win (DLS) in the first match, India travels to Hnry Stadium in Wellington seeking to strengthen their 1-0 advantage against New Zealand in the five-game ODI series.

India arrive in Wellington filled with confidence, bolstered by a powerful pace attack and strong performance from the top order. Led by Shubman Gill, the visitors got a significant lift in Auckland as Virat Kohli showcased his skill with an impressive 74-ball half-century, while Rohit Sharma‘s explosive opening role continues to pose a serious threat despite an early challenge in the last match. India’s primary strength, nonetheless, has been their disciplined depth in bowling. Young pacers Anshul Kamboj and Gurnoor Brar delivered match-altering spells alongside left-arm bowler Arshdeep Singh, while Axar Patel maintained strict control during the middle overs. As they head to Wellington, India’s main objective will be to manage the challenging windy overs, establish a strong batting base, and intensify their bold short-pitch bowling approach to pressure the Blackcaps from the outset.

Mitchell Santner’s Blackcaps have a challenging road ahead to restore confidence after a disappointing 185-run performance in the first match. The hosts anticipate notable enhancements from their top order, relying on experienced batsmen Tom Latham, Will Young, and Henry Nicholls to deliver steadiness against the new ball. Dynamic middle-order players Daryl Mitchell, Tim Seifert, and Glenn Phillips displayed moments of aggression in Auckland, but New Zealand critically requires one of their key batters to turn a start into a significant century to establish a challenging target. In bowling, fast bowlers Kyle Jamieson and Jacob Duffy head a pace attack suited to Wellington’s swing-friendly conditions, with Blair Tickner and Santner providing effective spin support. Should New Zealand be able to take early wickets and endure India’s bowling attack, they have a solid opportunity to equalise the series.

Head-to-Head:

Historically, India have prevailed in ODIs, securing 64 victories out of 124 matches. New Zealand have attained 52 wins, with one game ending in a tie and seven games deemed no result.
