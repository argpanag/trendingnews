Melania Trump Celebrates White House Design, Preservation at Ceremony

First lady Melania Trump hosted leaders in historic preservation at the White House on Wednesday, using a ceremony tied to America's 250th anniversary to highlight her work preserving and redesigning some of the nation's most recognizable presidential spaces.

Speaking from the White House Rose Garden during the White House Historical Association's 2026 Presidential Sites Summit, the first lady described the Executive Mansion as a living record of the nation and argued that architecture and design can help preserve American history for future generations.

"The White House stands as an architectural autobiography of America, embodying the enduring values of ambition, democracy, and civic life through its design, craftsmanship, and the objects within," she said.

The gathering was part of the Presidential Sites Summit, which is bringing together leaders and staff from presidential libraries, museums, historic homes, birthplaces, and other sites from around the country.

The three-day summit, running Sept. 8-10, is focused on lessons from the America 250 celebration and how institutions can carry that momentum into the future.

The summit comes two months after the United States marked the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence on July 4th.

The White House's broader Freedom 250 initiative began in 2025 and is continuing through 2026.

Melania Trump used Wednesday's event to recount several preservation and design projects she has championed at the White House, including the tennis pavilion, renovations to the queen's bedroom and bathroom, restoration of the Rose Garden, and conservation of historic wall covering in the president's dining room.

She described discovering an American War of Independence-themed wall covering designed by French artist Jean-Julien Deltil and produced by Jean Zuber & Cie in the 19th century, which had been concealed in the private family quarters.

"My mission to restore and reveal it was driven by a commitment to preserving a vital piece of our American narrative," she said. "The successful unveiling of this work was not merely an act of restoration; it was an investment in the future of our nation's story."

The first lady also pointed to the White House tennis pavilion as an example of her philosophy that new construction should respect the historic character surrounding it.

Completed in 2020, the classically inspired structure incorporates features including Doric columns, limestone, and a copper roof.

The pavilion subsequently received both a 2025 Palladio Award and a John Russell Pope Award for its architecture.

Traditional Building identified architect Steven W. Spandle's pavilion as the 2025 Palladio Award winner for commercial new design and construction under 30,000 square feet.

"Great design transcends aesthetic appeal; it shapes our lived experiences," she said. "It fosters collaboration, conveys dignity, inspires ambition, and creates a sense of belonging to something larger."

Her preservation efforts have also included work on the East Room floor, the State Entrance and Hall marble floors, the president's elevator, the White House bowling alley, and the children's garden, according to her office, with other design work extending to the presidential retreat at Camp David.

The first lady concluded by emphasizing stewardship as the country moves beyond its semiquincentennial celebration.

"As stewards of the places shaped by those before us, we are tasked with making decisions that honor future generations," she said. "It is our duty to ensure that the choices we make today are worthy of the legacy we pass forward."
