Australia's flag carrier has ended three Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner routes this year as it adjusts its network to meet market demand. This has seen Qantas' Dreamliners, which have become a backbone of the carrier's long-haul network, be redeployed on alternate routes. Three city pairs now no longer have 787-9s from major East Coast airports.

Qantas has cut its Dreamliner flights from Brisbane and Sydney to destinations in the US and New Zealand. Below is an analysis of these routes, with data from Cirium, an online aviation analytics company.

Qantas Terminates Boeing 787-9 Flights From Brisbane To Auckland

With Auckland being Qantas' largest international market, it was a surprise move to see the airline withdraw its 787-9 flights from Brisbane Airport (BNE) to Auckland Airport (AKL). The City of Sails (Auckland) is New Zealand's largest city and the busiest airport. AKL is also the carrier's operational hub and crew base in New Zealand; it is also the current gateway for its nonstop New York-JFK services, which operate from Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport (SYD) to New York via Auckland.

In May of this year, Qantas adjusted its 787-9 service from Brisbane, instead placing its older Airbus A330-200 aircraft on the route. Brisbane to Auckland is a popular route, with several airlines already serving the city, including Air New Zealand, China Airlines, Jetstar, Qantas, and Solomon Airlines, maintaining year-round services.

Qatar Airways is also set to throw its hat in the ring from next June, adding a fifth freedom service from Brisbane to Auckland.

Sydney To Dallas/Fort Worth Upscaled To The Airbus A380

From September, Qantas made the decision to redeploy its Airbus A380 Superjumbo between these two key oneworld alliance airline hubs. Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) sees daily services onboard the double-decker, feeding passengers and cargo through one of the world's busiest airports.

Qantas also maintains year-round services to Dallas/Fort Worth from Melbourne (MEL); however, these flights are now operated with the airline's 787-9 Dreamliner instead.

The red-tailed airline has a fleet of 10 A380s with 485 seats across four cabins. This is made up of 14 seats in first, 70 in business, 60 in premium economy, and 341 in economy.

Flights From Sydney To Honolulu Change To The Airbus A330

Despite Qantas cutting back services to Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (HNL) in 2025 (losing its nonstop Melbourne to Honolulu link), the airline otherwise maintained regular flights from Sydney to the Aloha state with the 787-9 until Octoberthis year.

Since October, the airline has redeployed the A330-200 and A330-300 to serve Honolulu, with the 787-9 now operating other trunkline routes for Qantas.

With these changes, the question arises of what Qantas' most popular 787-9 routes are within its network. The top activity by aircraft type for the airline is that of domestic flights from Melbourne to Perth Airport (PER), which record seven weekly departures, and then continue to London Heathrow Airport (LHR), which is currently routed via Singapore Changi Airport (SIN). With seven weekly flights, Brisbane and Melbourne to Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) is tied with the above route for 787 flights.

With six weekly flights, Sydney to Santiago, Chile, is the airline's next busiest route by weekly departures, followed by Sydney to Vancouver with five, and Melbourne to Dallas and Sydney to Auckland (onwards to JFK) with four weekly departures for December 2026.

Other notable mentions are Sydney to Melbourne (three departures a week), Sydney to San Francisco, and Sydney to Singapore, which continues to Paris.
