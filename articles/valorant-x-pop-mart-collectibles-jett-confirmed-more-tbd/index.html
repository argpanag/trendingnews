VALORANT x Pop Mart collectibles: Jett confirmed, more TBD

TheTools · 16.09.2026 14:15 · source · permalink

Pop Mart confirms a VALORANT figure collection with Jett revealed first. Key open questions: release timing, regions, pricing, and blind-box format.

Some games don’t just live on your screen. They live in people’s group chats, cosplay plans, desk setups, and the small rituals that make fandom feel personal. That is why “collectible” culture keeps colliding with esports titles like VALORANT, where character loyalty can be as real as team loyalty.

Riot Games is tapping that collector impulse again through a new collaboration with Pop Mart for a VALORANT figure collection, with Pop Mart teasing the partnership on social and prompting fans to “lock in.”

Why VALORANT fits Pop Mart’s collector culture

VALORANT’s agents are already designed like icons: instantly recognizable silhouettes, signature ability effects, and strong “main” identities that players stick with for years. In fandom terms, that makes the roster feel closer to a cast than a set of loadouts, which is exactly the kind of attachment that turns into merch demand.

Pop Mart’s strength is turning characters and aesthetics into objects people want to display, trade, and hunt for. When that meets a game where fans argue lovingly about which agent is most “them,” the product does not need much explaining. It just needs to exist.

What Riot Games and Pop Mart have confirmed so far

Riot Games and Pop Mart have confirmed the collaboration is coming, but they have not shared a release date, pricing, product formats, or regional availability.

What has been shown so far is one clear signal: Pop Mart unveiled Jett as an agent receiving an official figure. That matters because Jett is not just a popular pick, she is one of the game’s most instantly legible characters, even to people who do not play regularly.

Pop Mart also has recent experience working with Riot Games. Earlier in 2025, they released an Arcane blind-box series with nine standard designs and two rare secret figures. A similar bundle was also released for Teamfight Tactics / Golden Spatula (the Chinese version of TFT), including availability tied to Worlds 2025 in China.

One timing detail also stands out. With Pop Mart’s tease landing in mid-September, the collection could align with the VALORANT Champions Tour event in Shanghai beginning September 24, especially given Pop Mart’s popularity in China.

Why the blind box question matters more than it sounds

To non-collectors, “blind box” can sound like a gimmick. To collectors, it is the whole game.

If the VALORANT collection follows Pop Mart’s familiar blind-box format, then the biggest emotional driver is not only “Do I like this agent?” It becomes “Can I pull the rare one?” and “Will I trade for the one I actually main?” That shift changes the buying behavior from a single purchase to a repeatable chase.

It also changes what “character selection” means. A lineup rumored to include fan-favorite agents like Viper, Jett, Phoenix, and others is not only about popularity. It is also about how Pop Mart might structure rarity, and which agents become the “secret” figures that collectors obsess over.

What this means for marketers watching games and fandom commerce

Fandom merch works best when it respects how fans already behave, not when it tries to manufacture behavior from scratch. The VALORANT x Pop Mart collaboration is a clear bet on the idea that players want physical symbols of their digital identity.

1) Characters are brands, and “maining” is a purchase signal
VALORANT agent preference is a form of identity. Turning agents into collectibles gives fans a way to make that identity visible on shelves, desks, and in content backgrounds.

2) Timing merch around live events can concentrate demand
If the release does align with the Shanghai Champions Tour window, it turns a regular merch drop into something closer to a moment, with natural spikes in attention from competitive storylines and social chatter.

3) Format choices (like blind boxes) shape the entire demand curve
A blind-box approach can create repeat purchases and trading culture, while a direct-purchase model captures more straightforward “buy my fave” behavior. Marketers should read format as strategy, not packaging.

4) China is not just a region, it is a collector ecosystem
Pop Mart’s popularity in China is a distribution and culture advantage. When the collector scene is already strong, fandom products can travel faster through communities that treat collecting as a hobby.

5) Merch is content fuel, not only revenue
Figures show up in unboxings, desk tours, cosplay prep, and stream backgrounds. Even without any formal creator plan mentioned, the product category naturally invites shareable moments.

The bigger signal here is how gaming IP keeps expanding into lifestyle objects that fans use to decorate their real lives. For marketers, the lesson is simple: when an audience has a strong sense of identity inside a world, they will pay for ways to carry that identity outside of it.
