Mountain collapses into river in Nepal landslide

TheTools · 30.09.2026 00:15 · source · permalink

Days of heavy rainfall trigger wave of landslides and avalanches across the Himalayas

A mountain has collapsed into a river in Nepal during a wave of landslides and avalanches across the country.

Video footage shows trees and buildings sinking into Kali Gandaki River in Lete village, in the northern Mustang district, on Sunday.

The latest landslide happened as Nepal continues to rebuild a month after a glacial flood killed more than 1,450 people and left more than 6,000 missing. Efforts to recover bodies along the border with Tibet are ongoing.

Since Thursday, heavy rain across the mountain districts has triggered floods and landslides, killing at least 21 people, according to Nepal’s national disaster agency.

At least 56 people were killed because of heavy rainfall that triggered similar natural disasters in the northern Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, which shares a border with Nepal.

In a separate incident, 16 climbers are believed to have died after an avalanche hit a camp in the Himalayas.

Nepali guides and support staff were at a base camp on Mount Himlung, a 23,380ft peak north of central Nepal, when their tents were buried under snow on Sunday morning.

An eight-member rescue team from the Nepal National Mountain Guide Association rushed to the mountain from Kathmandu by helicopter. They rescued 10 people and recovered four bodies, but 12 climbers are still missing and are presumed dead.

On Monday, the rescue mission was abandoned because of poor weather.

The deceased have been identified as Chhebi Bhote, Pasang Bhote, Birendra Gurung, and Ghan Shyam Rai, who were all Nepali nationals.

“Today, the entire rescue team descended from the mountain. We had rushed up in the hope of finding survivors, but we had no luck,” a spokesman for the rescuers said.

“The avalanche was enormous – no one had believed that Himlung Base Camp could experience something of that scale. We recovered four bodies, making it clear that no one survived. Still, 12 cook staff remain missing at Himlung Base Camp.”

Earlier this month, more than two dozen climbers from two expedition companies, Himalayan Holidays Trekking and Imagine Nepal Trek and Expedition, had been sent to prepare for the expedition, manage the route and fix ropes on the peak before the autumn climbing season.

Bibhuti Chand Thakur, of Himalayan Holidays Trekking, said at least eight members of his expedition team had been reported missing.

Hrishikesh Bhaskar Yashod, Uttar Pradesh’s relief commissioner, said that India’s most populous state had recorded 2.6 inches of rain between Friday and Sunday morning, compared with the usual quarter-inch of rainfall for the period.

India’s central water commission has also said that 30 river-monitoring stations across seven Indian states are in a “severe situation”.

Dozens of Israelis are also stranded in the village of Samagaun in the Manaslu region, west of central Nepal, after harsh weather triggered rockslides, blocked roads and damaged bridges.

The hikers are calling to be evacuated as they fear conditions could deteriorate further.
