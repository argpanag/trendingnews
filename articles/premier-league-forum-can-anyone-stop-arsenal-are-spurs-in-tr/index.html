Premier League Forum: Can anyone stop Arsenal? Are Spurs in trouble again? Can Hull and Ipswich stay up?

TheTools · 13.09.2026 18:43 · source · permalink

Bruno Guimaraes scored a debut goal off the bench Stu Forster/Getty Images

This felt like a significant result for Arsenal. In the second half against a spirited Sunderland side, they conceded a penalty when Ezri Konsa appeared to grapple with Dan Ballard. It was a decision that incensed Mikel Arteta, who described it as “unacceptable” in his post-match press conference and left the champions facing the prospect of defeat at the Stadium of Light.

David Raya had other ideas. Enzo Le Fee struck a perfectly good penalty, low to Raya’s left, but the Spanish goalkeeper had anticipated it and plunged down to tip the ball onto the base of the post. It was a stunning save. Even more remarkable: 121 seconds later, Arsenal were ahead, with Bruno Guimaraes scoring a debut goal from outside the area, having come off the bench at half-time.

Sunderland threw everything at Arsenal, but the champions stood firm. Can anyone stop them this season?

Their north London rivals Tottenham Hotspur, however, endured another tough afternoon, failing to score for a fourth straight game as they were held to a 0-0 draw by Everton. At the final whistle, boos echoed around the Tottenham Stadium.

Liverpool and Chelsea, who also came into the season full of optimism, were also held to frustrating home draws. Andoni Iraola’s side still do not look like they are clicking and could not find a way past Fulham at Anfield, while Chelsea continued to attack with verve and defend catastrophically in their 2-2 draw with Hull City.

Aston Villa manager Unai Emery was furious following the defeat by Nottingham Forest, believing James McAtee should have been sent off for the challenge that left Ian Maatsen injured. In Crystal Palace’s defeat by Ipswich Town, Axel Disasi was sent off for a reckless challenge on Jack Clarke.

Amid the goals and the controversy, the two least-fancied promoted teams continue to impress. At the end of the day, Ipswich were sitting in eighth and Hull in third and might have a better chance of staying up than many expected.

This is the latest Premier League Forum, in which writers from The Athletic give their views on the weekend talking points so far, and you can join the discussion in the comments.

Let us know your views and what else you noticed from the weekend’s action so far.

Can anyone stop Arsenal?

Ahmed Walid: No.

While every other contender is still sorting their weaknesses and adapting to a new manager, Arsenal already have a strong base and have added to it by signing Christos Tzolis, Guimaraes and Konsa.

With a fully fit Bukayo Saka, Martin Odegaard and Kai Havertz — who only started one Premier League game together last season — stopping this team is harder than it seems.

This is a side with no clear weakness, proficient in all phases of play, and has the individual quality to win any match even if they aren’t at their best.

Dan Sheldon: The strength of Arsenal’s squad is undeniable, and their win over Sunderland is evidence of that.

It wasn’t Arsenal’s best performance, but they scored twice, kept a clean sheet and maintained their unbeaten start to the season.

But if anyone is going to be able to catch them, which, over the course of a 38-game campaign seems unlikely, then it will likely be Manchester City.

In Erling Haaland, Enzo Maresca’s Manchester City has the firepower to blow teams away, but they are getting used to life without Pep Guardiola, whose exit left a huge void, and that, along with their squad, is why Arsenal will be difficult to stop.

Greg O’Keeffe: Arsenal have come through a couple of difficult Premier League games, at home against Chelsea and now the Stadium of Light, and still their defence looks fearsome.

Chelsea and Sunderland — not to mention Napoli in the Champions League — all posed different and exacting challenges, but Mikel Arteta’s men are imperious.

Their spine is built of top players operating at their best, with Raya’s penalty save in the second half proving pivotal.

Guimaraes is fitting in nicely, and getting that long-awaited title over the line seems to have dimmed not an ounce of Arteta’s intensity (he fumed afterwards about the decision to award Sunderland the spot kick).

Manchester City will push them all the way, and Maresca’s extra firepower might just make the difference, but it’s only the men from the Etihad who look capable of making the Gunners sweat as they mount their convincing case for a title defence.

Are Spurs in trouble again?

Dan Sheldon: All that money spent in the summer, yet not a single goal scored in six hours of Premier League football is nothing short of an embarrassment.

If there was any momentum heading into this season, buoyed by the promise of a respected coach in Roberto De Zerbi, and significant investment in the transfer window, then it left the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on Saturday.

And it left to a chorus of boos as the club’s supporters let their feelings be known.

Although the table currently says otherwise, Spurs should still have more than enough quality to avoid a repeat of 2025-26, but the onus is on De Zerbi to solve their myriad problems sooner rather than later.

Greg O’Keeffe: Everton didn’t spend a penny on strikers, they even ended the transfer window in a goalscorer deficit.

It’s well known that Tottenham, at least on paper, had a far more ambitious summer. They are committed to forking out £60million ($81.2m) on Omar Marmoush, and spent more on attackers such as Savio. They also boast the experience and prowess of Dominic Solanke.

Somehow on Saturday evening, though, Marmoush did not manage a single shot on target in his 77 minutes on the field, and his team tested Jordan Pickford directly just twice as their goalless pain continued.

It was David Moyes’ side who had the greater expected goals too (1.17 to the hosts’ 0.64).

Last week Marmoush said his new team should “not be too hectic” or “pressured” to score goals, and eventually they would arrive. He also had glowing praise for the manager, Roberto De Zerbi.

But after another hapless blank, the pressure on Tottenham to start scoring will only become greater.

De Zerbi didn’t waste a second at the final whistle, running onto the pitch to give Mathys Tel some coaching. He will need to expedite those lessons if he is to convince the booing fans they are not, somehow after all that lavish recruitment, in for another difficult campaign.

Ahmed Walid: Yes, and they need to sort their attack and high press quickly.

Tottenham struggled to break down Everton’s defence, which follows weak attacking performances against Brentford, Nottingham Forest and Newcastle United — even if they had bright spells in the latter.

Meanwhile, the high press was dysfunctional once more, as it was during the defeats by Brentford and Newcastle United.

Time is needed for this newly assembled team to gel, but without goals — and consequentially wins — pressure is added on a squad that is clearly devoid of any confidence.

Despite the millions of pounds spent, it’s ironic that Spurs’ best player in their four Premier League games has been Rodrigo Bentancur.

Ipswich and Hull were favourites for relegation. Can they stay up?

Greg O’Keeffe: Selhurst Park is a notoriously difficult place for away teams, and yet there went promoted Ipswich — boldly sealing victory at the death and finishing the day in eighth place.

Hull pulled off an even more surprising result, grabbing a well-deserved point from an encounter at Stamford Bridge which they navigated with confidence and ambition.

Sergej Jakirovic’s defensively tough side are now third. Tigers fans may want to print off the league table right now, because while it may not get much better for them this season in terms of league position, it serves as further tangible proof that their many doubters before a ball was kicked this season were premature.

Zian Flemming always felt like a smart signing for Gary O’Neil’s side. His first goal for the club since his deadline-day move bodes well, as does the patient and potent manner in which they sought the win when other promoted teams may have settled for a point.

It’s early days but you’d be a brave man to bet against at least one of these two, if not both, leveraging their bright starts into survival.

Dan Sheldon: The Premier League is seemingly bucking what was becoming a worrying trend.

In the 2023-24 and 2024-25 seasons, the three promoted teams were all relegated at the end of the campaign, which was the first time in the league’s history that this had happened in consecutive years.

Sunderland and Leeds United bucked that trend in 2025-26, and Ipswich and Hull have shown early signs that they will, too.

It has been a tough start for Coventry City, rooted to the bottom of the table, but their fellow promoted teams have shown enough quality in the opening matches that survival is more than possible.

Ahmed Walid: Maybe — it’s too early to tell.

Hull and Ipswich have put in positive performances, with clear ideas of what they want to do on and off the ball.

The problem, though, is that the rest of the league is stacked with individual talent.

Even if Fulham and Crystal Palace have endured tough starts to the season, they have the player quality and experience to edge Hull, Ipswich and Coventry.

It’s always good to remember that Hull were third in the Premier League table in late October 2008, and only managed to avoid relegation on the final day of that season.

What next?

Manchester has become one of the most talked-about cities in the world in recent months and today the city hosts its first derby of the season. Maresca’s side travel to Old Trafford with a 100 per cent record as the transition to the post-Pep era appears to be going smoothly.

Michael Carrick has had a more challenging start to the season, after that surprising defeat by Hull in their first game, but there have been positive signs in recent performances that United are finding their stride.

In the earlier kick-off, Frank Lampard is looking for his first Premier League point as his Coventry side host free-scoring Brighton & Hove Albion.

On Monday, Matthias Jaissle’s Newcastle travel to Elland Road to face Daniel Farke’s Leeds, with both sides unbeaten in their first three games.
