Wendell Carter's versatility allows Orlando Magic to maximize their size advantage

TheTools · 16.11.2026 22:25 · source · permalink

No team can fully control the cards they are dealt.

They enter the season with plans and believing they have depth when they are fully healthy. And then injury wheel delivers problems.

The Orlando Magic have a team filled with size and length. But any roster is going to be forced to throw out some things when one position group gets hit with injuries.

Jonathan Isaac's season still has not started as he deals with continued issues from his left knee sprain last year. Noah Penda is recovering from a partially torn hamstring suffered during the preseason. Paolo Banchero suffered a high ankle sprain in the second game of the season.

Even Tristan da Silva missed a pair of games with a sore calf.

That forces the team to get creative. Every team will have to do that at some point and test out creative lineups.

It has forced the Magic to lean into their size and trust the versatility of one of their key players.

Wendell Carter has gotten off to a stellar start this season. One of his key advantages is that he can hold his own on th eperimeter and be a bull in the paint. The Magic's defense hinges on its center being able to be a presence.

That size and versatility is something the Magic have had to lean into this season.

The Magic have a lot of playable centers with Goga Bitadze and Nikola Vucevic backing Wendell Carter up. Their battle for the primary backup minutes is still ongoing.

But so far, Sean Sweeney has opted a different path. He has used all three early in the season. He is going big.

"I think we have turned up our defense these last couple of games," Wendell Carter said after shootaround Monday. "I think offensively, we are sticking to our process. Our process is coming with the results we've been expecting for a while. I think we just continue to do what we've been doing, we will be successful."

Carter has been rejuvenated this season, averaging 13.5 points per game and 9.3 rebounds per game while shooting 60.4 percent from the field and 40.0 percent from three. His defensive versatility opens the door for the team to try these two-big lineups.

The question is whether this is something the Magic can continue to turn to or just a necessity with the injuries that have hit the team.

Two-Big Lineups

Generally, the belief around the league is that it is getting smaller. The league has been aiming for more versatility. The power forward position has transformed completely in the last decade.

Wendell Carter entered the league as a power forward and undersized center -- he played 63 percent of his minutes at power forward according to Basketball-Reference in 2022 -- to a full-fledged center and defensive stalwart.

He is now playing limited minutes alongside either Nikola Vucevic and Goga Bitadze. This season, he has played 30 minutes alongside Vucevic. Last year, Wendell Carter played only 22 minutes alongside Goga Bitadze and Moe Wagner combined last year.

The Magic are indeed stitching lineups together. And this two-big arrangement is still going through a workshop.

The Carter-Vucevic two-man lineup has an abysmal 87.3 offensive rating and 106.6 defensive rating. The Magic often play zone when that duo is in the game. The group is also not rebounding particularly well.

The lineups with Bitadze and Vucevic have played 21 minutes with a 131.7 offensive rating and 118.6 defensive rating. The lineups with that duo have a 42.3 percent offensive rebound rate and a 65.0 percent defensive rebound rate.

The Magic are continuing to explore different ways to use their players.

"We have size," Goga Bitadze said after practice on Sunday. "Vooch is a huge guy and me as well. Defense as well. Sometimes it's hard because we haven't done that a whole lot. It's been working well for us.

"I guess the advantage is just having size on both ends of the floor. Defensively, being good at the rim. Vooch is such a high-level player. He knows how to play when you put him at any position. Whatever lineup is out there, we have to make it work."

None of this works without those players' IQ figuring out a way to make it work.

Vucevic can look a bit awkward defending the perimeter. But Bitadze has been a good backstop. Orlando has been playing zone defense for parts of these possessions to help keep their size near the paint.

Coach Sean Sweeney is still figuring out the way to deploy them all. They are still figuring out how to make it work.

Carter's versatility

While the Orlando Magic have yet to find a big lineup that works well for Wendell Carter, they know his versatility is what makes him unique and holds their defense together.

Carter is good because he can hold his own in the post but also switch onto the perimeter. That is what makes these bigger lineups so possible. Carter has proven himself capable on the interior and on the perimeter in small stretches.

It gives the Magic a lot more versatility defensively as they steadily climb the defensive ranks again.

"I think it's using my size is something I can use against anybody, whoever I'm playing against," Carter said after shootaround Monday. "Be able to use my length. But also being smart and not fouling. That's something I have been struggling with to start the season. I think using my size is the most important."

It is obviously not perfect yet. But Carter can help everywhere.

Opponents are shooting just 55.9 percent at the rim with Carter as the defender, according to data from Second Spectrum. That ranks sixth in the league among centers who average at least 30 minutes per game.

Orlando has a 110.0 defensive rating with Carter on the floor, a number bested only by Bitadze's 105.0 rating. The Magic have a team-worst 115.1 defensive rating when Carter is off the floor.

Wendell Carter is essentially to the Magic's defense what Franz Wagner is to the offense.

Finding the best ways to deploy him remains one of the biggest tasks for Sweeney in the early part of the season.

Orlando has spent the early part of its season stitching lineups together, just trying to find the players who work well together.

They have had to go bigger. That has helped them explore how well some of their bigs can play. Maybe that will be something to log for later in the season.
