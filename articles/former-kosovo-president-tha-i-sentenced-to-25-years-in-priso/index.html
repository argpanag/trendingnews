Former Kosovo president Thaçi sentenced to 25 years in prison

Former Kosovo Liberation Army commander and later Kosovo president Hashim Thaçi has been sentenced to 25 years in prison as he is held criminally responsible for war crimes during the violent break from Serbia in the 1990s by the international court in The Hague.

Thaçi had denied all charges.

The proceedings, deeply unpopular in Kosovo and neighbouring Albania, recall the period of brutal conflict between 1998 and 1999 where both Serbian and Kosovan armed organisations committed war crimes in the context of the KLA’s armed uprising and Belgrade’s military response.

Thaçi and co-defendants 'criminally responsible' for war crimes of detention, cruel treatment, torture, murder, ruling says

We are now getting a bit more on the findings against Thaçi and his three co-defendants.

Here is from the Kosovo Specialist Chambers & Specialist Prosecutor’s Office:

“The Trial Panel found that all four Accused were criminally responsible for the war crimes of arbitrary detention against 385 individuals, cruel treatment against 49 individuals, torture against 303 individuals, and murder against 96 individuals.

The Trial Panel found the accused not guilty of crimes against humanity because the Specialist Prosecutor failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that there was a widespread or systematic attack directed against a civilian population. The accused were also found not guilty in connection with a number of specific incidents of war crimes.

The judges found that during the Kosovo conflict Mr. Thaçi, Mr. Veseli, Mr. Selimi and Mr. Krasniqi contributed significantly to the common criminal purpose of targeting perceived opponents of the Kosovo Liberation Army’s political and military goals, including Kosovo Albanians associated with other political or military forces, like the LDK and FARK; individuals alleged to be associated with the FRY/Serbian authorities; and certain members of ethnic minorities, including a number of Roma and Serbs.

The targeting of such opponents took the form of killings; arrests and detention without due process; physical and psychological mistreatment; as well as other forms of abuse, intimidation and harassment.

With limited exceptions, the Trial Panel found no evidence that any of the victims had engaged in any criminal activity, taken an active part in hostilities or presented any other legitimate security concern for the Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA).”

And here is a bit more detail on Thaçi himself:

“In the case of Hashim Thaçi, the Trial Panel found that as a member of the General Staff of the KLA and in his capacity as the head of the Political Directorate, he was a key member in formulating and implementing the common purpose that entailed setting up detention facilities; identifying individuals perceived as opposing the KLA; arresting and detaining them; and, if necessary, killing them.

This included the targeting of alleged “collaborators”, the setting up of a structure, competent bodies and regulations to do so, and the deployment of “special warfare” against Opponents, including through the publication of communiques and political declarations and other public statements expressing tolerance and encouragement for the commission of crimes against opponents.

The Trial Panel further found that Hashim Thaçi also personally participated in crimes, including the arrest, arbitrary detention and interrogation of 13 parliamentarians and the arrest, detention, transfer and murder of Behajdin Allaqi.

He further disseminated false information about detainees and victims of the crimes committed by KLA members, gave false assurances of compliance with international humanitarian law on behalf of the KLA, and contributed to a policy of impunity in respect of perpetrators of crimes against opponents.”

Von der Leyen's offer to Canada 'cannot be overestimated in political and trade significance' - snap analysis

Senior correspondent

Ursula von der Leyen’s dramatic offer to open the gate to Canada to become the EU’s first ever associate member (9:54) cannot be overestimated in political and trade significance.

It, at once, aligns what Canadian prime minister Mark Carney called the “middle powers” in his Davos speech in January but creates an opening for a speedy enhanced trade deal particularly on critical raw materials and rare earths.

It also sets Canada apart from the UK which could, once upon a time, have some sort of associate membership with the EU were it not for the drive for a clean break Brexit.

Two points of realpolitik make it possible that the long winding road that typifies third country’s relationships with the EU could turn into a high speed motorway.

Canada has something the EU desperately needs: rich supplies of CRM and rare earths in a bid to diversify from China which has a stranglehold on both.

And Europe has something Canada urgently wants: new market for products it can no longer sell into the US in the face of a trade war with Donald Trump.

Looking at the bigger picture a new enhanced trade deal between the EU and Canada could leave the UK somewhat scrambling in a two speed race.

Keir Starmer talked about forging a CRM and rare earths deal with Canada but it never materialised.

If political energy in Brussels is now focused on Canada that could be bad news for Andy Burnham.

Switching to French now, von der Leyen says “we also have to win back the hearts of Europeans.”

She also says the EU needs to look beyond its borders in the western Balkans.

She says the process “will continue on merit.”

But she says the adherence to EU values is “non-negotiable,” as she says she was “horrified by the pictured from Ratko Mladić’s funeral in Belgrade last week.”

Social media 'depriving children of their childhood,' von der Leyen says, as she confirms strict rules on social media

Von der Leyen then turns to the question of online safety, particularly for children.

She warns that “young Europeans now spend four to six hours a day on screens,” as they “are pulled ever deeper into feeds designed to keep them scrolling.”

“What we are witnessing is a great capture of our children. A capture of their attention, their focus, their minds. This is depriving children of their childhood.”

“What we are witnessing is a great capture of our children. A capture of their attention, their focus, their minds. This is depriving children of their childhood.”

She references the death of a 14-year-old Belgian girl, Oléa, who killed herself as a victim of online bullying.

She quotes her mom who said: “I am convinced that had it not been for these omnipresent networks, my daughter would still be with us.”

Von der Leyen says “enough is enough” and confirms plans to bring forward “the Kids’ Act” with strict restrictions on social media usage by children.

“We will take a gradual and differentiated approach. Each age has its own sensitivities, and therefore each will have its own tailored protection level.

No social media under the age of 13. No personal account under the age of 15.

That means from 13 to under 15, only mini accounts set up and supervised by parents or guardians, with limited features and time restriction to one hour a day and between 15 and 18, safe design will be an obligation for the platforms.”

'European democracy is being threatened' by disinformation, foreign interference, von der Leyen says

Von der Leyen then turns to the issue of democracy in Europe.

She says “our European democracy is being threatened” with “disinformation and foreign interference seeking to divide us, seeking to erode trust in our democratic fabric and free media.”

She pointedly says “the threats to our democracies do not only come from the outside,” as Europe sees “the rise of anti-democratic, anti-European forces.”

But she says “nothing is inevitable,” as she celebrates the change in Hungary, with the new pro-EU government led by Péter Magyar after 16 years of Viktor Orbán.

“12 April will stay in our memory for a very long time,” she says, referencing to the election day in Hungary.

“Hungarian people took their future in their hands. They chose democracy. They chose Europe. They brought Hungary back to where it belongs, at the heart of the European Union.

Years of backsliding have started to be reversed. The fight against corruption and state capture is speeding up. There is real progress on fundamental rights, on academic freedom, and now billions of euros of investment in Hungary have been unlocked. There is, of course, much more work ahead.

But … this shows the hard work pays off.”

Von der Leyen says EU will draft tougher rules for emergency migration scenarios

Von der Leyen then turns to migration, and not surprisingly touches upon the migration crisis in Ceuta.

She says “vulnerable people are being exploited by callous traffickers and smugglers, by our adversaries.”

She addresses the Spanish people, saying “Ceuta border is a European border, because Ceuta is Spain and Ceuta is Europe – and Europe will be there for you.”

She talks about the EU’s pact on migration and asylum, which “needs to be fully implemented.”

But she says the EU needs new rules for emergency situations, “especially in scenarios when mass movements are engineered and weaponised.”

“This is why we will propose a new European Emergency Response instrument. It will only be triggered under a strictly defined set of criterias. And in those cases, it will allow for a time limited and strictly defined flexibility of standard procedures.”

“This is why we will propose a new European Emergency Response instrument. It will only be triggered under a strictly defined set of criterias. And in those cases, it will allow for a time limited and strictly defined flexibility of standard procedures.”

She says the message is clear: “we will always comply with our values and fundamental rights, but we will never compromise on protecting our borders because it’s us Europeans who decide who comes to our union and under what circumstances.”

She also talks about the need to strengthen the EU’s border agency, Frontex, and proposes a new “Mediterranean Youth Skills and Jobs Initiative” to work with partners in third countries.

EU's von der Leyen calls Israel's E1 settlement project 'unacceptable'

She says “the deep suffering in Gaza and unchecked settler violence in the West Bank have deeply affected Europeans.”

“It is painful for many Europeans that member states have been unable to build the necessary majorities for a unified response. Some member states have moved ahead. Others are considering to do so. When Europe is divided, it cannot change the course of events,” she acknowledges.

She says the EU is “leading the way in supporting Palestinians,” and will continue to do so.

She says “Europe supports the right to self-defence for Israel, and the Palestinian people have an equal right to live in safety and dignity.”

“It is Europe’s role to keep the flame of the two state solution alive.”

EU 'will give our strongest ever support to Ukraine over the toughest winter of the war,' von der Leyen says

Switching to German, von der Leyen moves on to Ukraine.

She says “the coming winter will be tough for Ukraine,” but says nothing can break “the Ukrainian spirit of resistance.”

“Our task remains to support Ukraine as best we can,” she says.

She says the EU “will give our strongest ever support to Ukraine over the toughest winter of the war.”

She says the EU is now using its funding to help Ukraine buy air defence systems, and wants to work on developing “an affordable modular missile defence system,” made in Europe.

But she says “more is needed.”

The EU will work on “setting up a new programme for joint projects with Ukrainian companies,” and work to “tighten our sanctions on Russia.”

“What is needed is not necessarily new sanctions, but above all the enforcement of existing ones. Our sanctions … are putting enormous pressure on the Russian economy. Russia is doing everything it can to get around them. So we must close the loopholes.”

She goes back to English, talking about Russia’s indiscriminate attacks on civilian populations in Ukraine.

“This is a deliberate strategy of terror,” she says.

She talks about a story of a Ukrainian girl named Sasha and her resilience in returning to gymnastics after losing her leg in a Russian strike.

“Just seven months after the attack, she was back in the gym with her new prosthetic leg. She trained harder than ever. She started winning gold after gold. This summer, she travelled the world with her Dance of Freedom tour. Her aim is to tell the world about Ukrainian resilience and the fight for freedom," she says.

Sasha is in the audience and gets a standing ovation.

Von der Leyen says “there is no room for any kind of doubt: Europe will defend each and every single square metre of its territory.”

'Threats are mounting on our soil' with threat from Russia growing, von der Leyen warns, as she calls for 'European Security Council'

Von der Leyen then moves to security, as she references the recent incidents in Lithuania, Denmark, and Poland just this week.

“Russia’s war of aggression on Ukraine rages on and threats are mounting on our soil,” she warns.

“We need to be clear about what this was an attack using military grade material carried out by Russian operatives on European soil and attack not only on one member state, but against all of our union.”

She adds that “hybrid threats facing Europe are less and less hybrid and less and less threats.”

“Cyber sabotage, arson. Drone incursion. They are ramping up every day.”

Von der Leyen says the EU’s preparedness needs to be raised, with existing systems “made for a different world.”

She says the EU needs “its own counter-hybrid playbook,” and how it can respond to incidents just below the threshold of armed aggression.

She calls for the EU’s equivalent of Nato’s Article 4 (note: Article 4, not best known Article 5), “an emergency security protocol.”

“When triggered by one member state, all member states should convene to coordinate a European response to deter further escalation and to mitigate the impact,” she says, calling for the establishment of a “European Security Council,” which could also involve Canada, Ukraine, the UK and Norway.
