Billy Evans is finally ready to talk about his portrayal in You Can See Everything.

"You've got to learn really painful lessons sometimes in life, and with Elizabeth going through everything she did in the press, the lesson that was ingrained in us is you can't lose your voice," Evans tells CBS News' in his first broadcast network interview.

That was his answer to the question of why he and his partner, disgraced Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, had agreed to participate in the documentary with Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim. The film, which has debuted to resounding box-office and critical success, has sparked much debate, particularly for his portrayal.

The network posted several teaser clips of Evans' interview with Matt Gutman on social media on Friday, ahead of the broadcast of the full interview scheduled to air on Sunday.

Guttman asked Evans whether he was aware that he would be shown fully naked in the much-discussed shower scene early in the film.

"I would say that our recollections are pretty different on that," Evans told Gutman. "Now, did I know they were filming me in the shower? Yes, but we would film 16 hours a day. Like, all sorts of things were filmed with assurances made. I was just as surprised as the rest of the country to have my whole body on camera for the world to see, and it's been an awkward experience having your kids' teachers see that. It's really uncomfortable. I'm not proud of it. I didn't want it. It's been weird seeing the commentary about, oh, he's just showing the world. I'm a confident guy. I'm not that confident."

When he learned about the scene, he said he asked the filmmakers to alter it. "'Can you pixelate this? Like, is there anything you can do?,'" he says he asked them, "and they refused to do so," he said.

He insisted that counter to rumors, he didn't want the scene to be shown. "I very much did not. You know, to the point in which I'm like asking them and pleading to them not to show this before the film became public in theaters, and apparently it was something that Nathan and A24 really wanted to show, but it's not something I did, and it's embarrassing," he said.

"I'd also say the color grading was way off," he added.

Gutman asked Evans about how he felt about being depicted as a villain in the film. "I would have preferred for that not to be the case, but it's entertainment," he said.

"I think my goal was to have people see Elizabeth, and when I watch that movie, I see the person that I love and exactly who she is."

Gutman pressed Evans on the question of whether Fielder became "infatuated" with Holmes over the course of filming.

Evans disputed that interpretation of the film, saying "I think a lot of a lot of people are infatuated with Elizabeth Holmes," he told Gutman. "I think you're taking things maybe too literally."

When Gutman pushed back saying that was the impression many people got from watching the film, Evans countered, "I think Nathan cares a lot about Elizabeth, and I didn't get the impression for a minute that it was romantic."

Gutman referenced the scene at the end where Holmes and Fielder are playing in the waves, but Evans said, "I was five to the right of them, also in the water."

The full interview airs on CBS on Sunday at 9 a.m. ET.
