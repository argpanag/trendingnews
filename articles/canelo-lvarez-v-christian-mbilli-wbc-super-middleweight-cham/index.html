Round 6

Álvarez is boxing beautifully off the back foot now. An excellent round for the Mexican and the easiest of the fight to score.

Guardian’s unofficial score: Álvarez 10-9 Mbilli (Álvarez 57-57 Mbilli)

Round 5

Mbilli takes the initiative, putting together combinations and keeping Álvarez occupied with a steady stream of punches. Both men find openings to the midsection, but neither has really shifted into top gear. It’s a tactical, closely contested fight so far, with the Frenchman’s greater activity giving him the edge on our card.

Guardian’s unofficial score: Álvarez 9-10 Mbilli (Álvarez 47-48 Mbilli)

Round 4

Another competitive round with Mbilli increasing his output and doing some good work to the body while Álvarez grows a little too comfortable on the defensive. Canelo is slipping punches and looking to land the occasional big counter, but he’s not throwing enough. Mbilli finishes strongly, his speed and volume beginning to pose serious questions for the Mexican. Álvarez shades it on our card, but the champion is making him work for everything.

Guardian’s unofficial score: Álvarez 10-9 Mbilli (Álvarez 38-38 Mbilli)

Round 3

Mbilli is finding his rhythm now, ripping off combinations and using his superior workrate to back Álvarez against the ropes. Canelo is showing his age at times, but his lead hand looks as sharp as it has in years and his footwork remains crisp as he repeatedly pirouettes out of danger. It’s an intriguing contrast in styles: Mbilli has the advantage in volume, while Álvarez is landing the cleaner, more accurate punches and doing his best to dictate the pace. Another close round.

Guardian’s unofficial score: Álvarez 9-10 Mbilli (Álvarez 28-29 Mbilli)

Round 2

Mbilli is throwing and landing more and appears to have found his footing after a slow start. Álvarez is mostly picking his spots and pot-shotting. This one could have gone either way but it says here the champion nicked it.

Guardian’s unofficial score: Álvarez 9-10 Mbilli (Álvarez 19-19 Mbilli)

Round 1

Álvarez holds his ground in the center of the ring early before circling Mbilli, looking the sharper and more composed fighter. The Mexican puts together a couple of crisp combinations, including a thudding left hook, while Mbilli is slow to get going. The Frenchman finally begins letting his hands go in the final 30 seconds, but he’ll need to increase his volume to trouble Canelo. CompuBox has Álvarez landing 10 of 24 punches to Mbilli’s seven of 43. A clear opening round for the challenger.

Guardian’s unofficial score: Álvarez 10-9 Mbilli (Álvarez 10-9 Mbilli)

It’s a muted, pyrotechnic-free ringwalk by his theatrical standards as he enters surrounded by his team and the recording artist Tito Double P.

Munguía retains WBA 168lb title by UD 12

Munguía has retained his WBA super-middleweight title by a 12-round unanimous decision. The three judges at ringside handed down scores of 116-112 and 117-111 (twice).

The 30-year-old Mexican entered the ring as roughly an 18-1 favorite with the bookmakers. Zon nearly made a mockery of those odds, coming within a whisker of stopping the Mexican champion in an extraordinary sixth round, but Munguía was dominant throughout the second half of the contest to wrap up a close but uncontroversial win.

We may have just witnessed the round of the year in Riyadh. Nikita Zon had Jaime Munguía on the brink of defeat in a white-knuckle sixth, rocking the Mexican champion with a barrage of punches that left him barely able to stay upright. But Munguía somehow weathered the storm and came roaring back, hurting a punched-out Zon in the closing stages as the two traded hellfire in a remarkable display of courage and violence.

There was controversy, too, when referee Tony Weeks stepped between the fighters as Munguía appeared close to being stopped, giving the champion precious seconds to recover. The intervention could prove decisive.

The numbers tell part of the story: Zon landed 28 of 65 punches to Munguía’s 18 of 54, with 43 of their combined 46 connections coming on power shots. An unforgettable three minutes.

Jaime Munguía is (finally) in the ring for the final preliminary bout of the night, defending his World Boxing Association super-middleweight title against unbeaten Russian challenger Nikita Zon.

The 30-year-old Mexican (46-2, 35 KOs) claimed the belt with a unanimous-decision victory over Armando Resendiz in May and now makes his first defense against Zon (12-0-1, 10 KOs), who is fighting outside his native Russia for the first time.

Munguía, who lost to Canelo Álvarez by unanimous decision in May 2024, will be looking to make a statement ahead of his compatriot’s appearance in the main event. The winner could move into position for a 168lb unification fight with whoever emerges victorious between Álvarez and Christian Mbilli later tonight.

While we wait (and wait, and wait) for the final undercard bout, a few choice quotes from Thursday’s final press conference in Riyadh, where Álvarez and Mbilli struck contrasting notes ahead of tonight’s WBC super-middleweight title fight.

For Álvarez, who is for the first time since 2022 after losing his undisputed championship to Terence Crawford last year, the message was one of unfinished business.

“After 20 years as a professional, I’m glad to be here,” the 36-year-old Mexican said. “I’m the challenger now, but always in my mind, I’m the champion.”

He added: “I don’t believe in shortcuts. I’m going straight for the champion because I already have the experience. I’ve been a champion for many years and I want to be a champion again.”

Mbilli, the unbeaten Cameroon-born Frenchman making his first defense of the full WBC title, was no less confident.

“I’m ready for this fight. I’m ready to shock the world,” the 31-year-old said. “I have a lot of respect for Canelo and I want to congratulate him, but on Saturday it’s going to be a different story. I’m coming for the win.”

Asked about his preparations, Mbilli offered a simple formula: “My training camp was perfect. My mind is ready for this fight. I don’t need to do anything special to win; I only need to be myself.”

What’s happened so far

The undercard is nearing its conclusion as we build towards tonight’s main event between Álvarez and Mbilli, with one preliminary bout remaining before the headliners make their ringwalks. Jaime Munguía is up next to defend his WBA super-middleweight title against Nikita Zon in a scheduled 12-round contest.

In the most recent bout, Rafael Espinoza retained his WBO featherweight title with a third-round TKO of Ricardo Blandon, who was unable to continue after suffering a cut caused by a punch. The unbeaten Mexican improved to 29-0 with 25 knockouts. It should be noted the stoppage felt awfully premature.

Earlier, Japan’s Reito Tsutsumi overwhelmed Raymundo Rojas with a series of heavy left hands to force a second-round stoppage at 2min 27sec, improving his professional record to 6-0.

On the non-televised card, Brayan Leon outpointed Hector Aguirre over eight rounds, while Emiliano Rojo stopped Flavius Biea in the fourth. Bek Nurmaganbet needed less than three minutes to dispatch Marcos Vazquez, dropping him twice before securing a first-round knockout with just three seconds to go.

Before that, Sultan Almohammed earned a unanimous decision over Erick Villegas, while Carl Jammes Martin edged David Picasso by split decision in a closely contested featherweight contest.

Preamble

Hello and welcome Riyadh’s Kingdom Arena for tonight’s fight between Saúl “Canelo” Álvarez and Christian Mbilli from Riyadh, where one of boxing’s biggest names is attempting to reclaim his place atop the super-middleweight division in circumstances that are anything but ordinary.

For the first time since 2022, Álvarez enters the ring as a challenger. The 36-year-old Mexican superstar, who lost his undisputed championship to Terence Crawford 13 months ago, is seeking to regain the World Boxing Council title against the unbeaten, Cameroon-born Frenchman Mbilli, who has promised to “shock the world” in the biggest fight of his career.

But the backdrop is almost as compelling as the matchup itself. The contest is going ahead despite a wave of drone and missile attacks on Saudi Arabia that have forced the cancellation or postponement of other major events and raised questions about whether the fight could safely take place. After weeks of uncertainty, the fighters are here, the stage is set and the show, remarkably, goes on.
