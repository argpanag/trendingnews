  1. Helen Haines: An anti-corruption campaigner respected across the political spectrumpublished at 13:28 BST
    13:28 BST

    Angus Thompson
    Live reporter

    Although not necessarily a household name across Australia, in the political sphere Helen Haines is both well-known and well regarded.

    So much is obvious from the outpouring of messages from politicians and party leaders across the breadth of the political spectrum. In an age of increasingly divisive politics, she is seen by many as a unifying force.

    The independent MP has only been a federal parliamentarian for a relatively short time - having been elected in 2019. However, in that time she has become an influential advocate for integrity in politics.

    Haines proposed legislation for a national anti-corruption watchdog in 2021, and has been credited for driving the creation of the National Anti-Corruption Commission under Labor in the previous parliamentary term.

    She currently sits on the parliamentary committee responsible for its oversight.

    Haines has often banded together with other key independents - including the so-called "teals" - who have formed a progressive, lower house bloc on issues that include climate action and gender equality.

    Allegra Spender, another independent MP who has collaborated with Haines, has called Haines "the best of us".

  3. Shock and sadness from Australia's political leaderspublished at 12:26 BST
    12:26 BST

    As we've reported, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese - who leads Australia's Labor Party - describes the incident as an "attack", and has said: "There is no place for violence in a democracy."

    Opposition leader Angus Taylor - head of the Liberal Party - says he is "deeply saddened and distressed" by the news, and says that Australians will be "angry and appalled by the attack on Helen".

    Senator Matt Canavan, leader of The Nationals, says his heart goes out to Helen Haines and her family "upon the news of this shocking attack". He goes on: "I pray for her full recovery from this traumatic time."

    Greens leader Senator David Shoebridge says he is "horrified" to hear of the "apparent attack", adding: "Helen is an extraordinary human being and brings people together like few others."

    And Senator Pauline Hanson, One Nation leader, says violence against elected representatives is "never acceptable". She adds: "Members of parliament and their staff must be able to serve their communities without fearing for their safety."

    11:54 BST

    It's now been more than six hours since the Australian MP Helen Haines was hit by a car in what Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has called an "attack". Here's what we do and don't know about the incident.

    What we know

    • Haines's family says the incident happened outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, in the state of Victoria
    • She was hit by a white Toyota Landcruiser at 15:35 local time (05:35 BST), police say
    • A 43-year-old Tarrawingee man was arrested at the scene by a passerby - police say a knife was found in the car
    • They say they are investigating whether "this was an intentional act", but that they do not believe there is an "ongoing threat to the community"
    • Albanese says "this wasn’t an accident" and "there was a deliberate act, it would appear" - he also says counter terror police are involved in the investigation

    What we don't know

    • Haines sustained "critical injuries" according to police, but we don't know any specifics about her condition
    • As stated above, police say they are still investigating whether this was a deliberate act
    • The suspect has not yet been named by officials

    This is a developing story so stay with us as we bring you the latest updates.

    11:29 BST

    Helen Haines's family says the incident happened outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, a city of around 30,000 a few hours north-east of Melbourne in the state of Victoria.

  10. Albanese seemed close to tears in TV interviewpublished at 10:12 BST
    10:12 BST

    Simon Atkinson
    Australia reporter

    Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese became emotional as he talked about Helen Haines in a live television interview on ABC's 730.

    The PM appeared close to tears when he was asked whether he and his colleagues talk among themselves about the threats and risks they face.

    "Yes," he replied, adding: "My concern at this point in time is for one, one of my colleagues."

    He described Helen Haines as having "integrity".

    "She's someone who is very passionate about her community. She's someone who's not shy about coming forward, about what she thinks that community needs."

    Albanese added: "I don't want to see a political system in which you can't have conversations with people at the supermarket, in the street, at street meetings, public forums.

    "That's the important part of our democracy.

    "I think there has been a rise in temperature. And there has been too much shouting, not enough discourse. Not enough polite conversation and disagreement.

    "And in a democracy, we should be able to have discussion, political debate, put different views, but do it in a way that is respectful."

  13. Albanese says it appears to be a 'deliberate act'published at 09:52 BST
    09:52 BSTBreaking

    Simon Atkinson
    Australia reporter

    "This wasn’t an accident. There was a deliberate act, it would appear," Anthony Albanese tells ABC's 730 Programme.

    "We certainly hope for good news about Helen’s condition over the next 24 hours."

    He says that counter terrorism police would be going through the history of the suspect to try to establish a motivation.

    Albanese adds he has been asking for some time for people to dial down the temperature in politics.

    "If you look at anyone's social media feeds, and look at the comments, people will say things that they would never say face to face, but the temperature has undoubtedly been increased, not just here in Australia, of course. We've seen incidents internationally as well," he says.

    "There is no place for violence in a democracy."

  14. Knife seized from car; MP has 'critical injuries' - policepublished at 09:48 BST
    09:48 BSTBreaking

    Victoria Police have released the following statement:

    "Police have arrested a man after a woman was struck by a vehicle in Wangaratta, Victoria this afternoon.

    "It is understood a white Toyota Landcruiser struck the 65-year-old woman outside an office on Murphy Street at about 3.35pm local time.

    "When he exited the Toyota the driver, a 43-year-old Tarrawingee man, was arrested at the scene by a passer-by. He will be interviewed in relation to the incident.

    "The woman was taken to hospital with critical injuries.

    "A knife was located by police and seized from inside the Toyota.

    "At this stage, police are investigating as to whether this was an intentional act.

    "It is not known yet if the parties are known to each other. Major Collison Investigation Unit detectives are en route. The investigation remains ongoing."
