Discussion

Showers have begun reaching Kauai as of 3 PM with the quickly approaching cold front just off to the northwest. The rest of the islands remain dry and mostly sunny, but conditions will quickly change as the front continues southeast. This front should reach Oahu early this evening, Maui by late evening, then begin breaking up as it starts reaching the Big Island overnight. A few showers may linger into Thanksgiving Day in north facing slopes, but the magnitude of dry air arriving should limit this potential to some degree.
Winds will turn north and increase, but remain below wind advisory/warning thresholds in locations outside of the Maui and Big Island summits. A 75-100 kt mid level jet streak will dig over the state later this evening then pull away Thursday evening. Winds will rapidly increase on the the Haleakala, Mauna Kea, and Mauna Loa summits this evening, then peaking late tonight and Thursday. The strongest winds are expected with the Big Island summits where a High Wind Warning is in effect. Gusts may exceed 100 mph there. With Haleakala's lower peak elevation, gusts won't be as high, but still Wind Advisory worthy around 60 mph.
After the front passes through the islands a cool and dry air mass will descend across the state through Saturday. Clouds and rain chances will be limited to windward locations, if any develop at all. For lower elevations, expect much cooler weather from Thanksgiving Day into Saturday morning with daytime temperatures in the mid to upper 70s and morning low temperatures in the upper 50s to mid 60s.
A surface high will build in from the northwest behind the front and winds will gradually veer northeasterly on Friday, then moderate easterly trades will return for the weekend. A weak trough breaks through the ridge Sunday and Monday. Latest model runs indicate a piece may break off and become a cutoff low west of the state early next week. Marine

A cold front will reach Kauai and sweep down the island chain tonight into Thanksgiving Day, bringing strong northwesterly winds to the coastal waters beginning tonight. Additionally, an extra large north-northwest swell will also push seas well above 10 feet for most waters. Therefore, the Small Craft Advisory that was previously in effect has been expended to include all Hawaiian coastal waters due to advisory-level winds, seas greater than 10 feet, or a combination of the two. Winds will gradually weaken and veer to the northeast Friday into the weekend as high pressure building to the northwest regains influence over the Hawaiian Islands. Seas will also gradually subside this weekend as the extra-large, north-northwest swell fades.
