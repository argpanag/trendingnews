Supreme Court to hear Trump attempt to withhold DOGE documents

TheTools · 13.10.2026 18:17 · source · permalink

WASHINGTON — The Supreme Court on Tuesday took up the Trump administration’s bid to prevent a watchdog group from seeking information about the Department of Government Efficiency, which played a key role in firing thousands of federal workers and canceling government funding.

The court agreed to hear a Justice Department appeal that sought to prevent Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington from obtaining the records.

The underlying litigation focuses on whether DOGE is an agency that is subject to the Freedom of Information Act. A ruling is due by the end of June.

The Trump administration has argued that DOGE, spearheaded by former administration adviser Elon Musk, was not subject to what are known as FOIA requests.

Nikhel Sus, a lawyer at CREW, said in a statement that lower courts had uniformly ruled against DOGE.

“We will continue to pursue transparency for the American people, who deserve to know the details of the Trump administration’s efforts to unilaterally remake the federal government,” he said.

The White House did not immediately respond to a request seeking comment.

DOGE, which played a key role in dismantling the U.S. Agency for International Development, was a controversial feature of Trump’s early months back in office, triggering various lawsuits. Its operations have since dwindled.

The case had already reached the Supreme Court once at an earlier stage of the litigation. Then, in a provisional decision, the court blocked parts of a lower court ruling that required thousands of pages of information to be handed over.

It also meant plaintiffs’ lawyers could not depose Amy Gleason, the DOGE administrator.

Litigation has since continued. As a result of the Supreme Court’s earlier intervention, the plaintiffs narrowed their requests for information, but the government still objected. After it lost in lower courts, Solicitor General D. John Sauer again asked the justices to weigh in.

The information requests remain on hold while the case plays out.

The plaintiffs say the information they still want is about DOGE’s guidance to its workers about canceling contracts and firing government workers. None of those requests concerns any communications with the White House that may be off-limits under FOIA, they say.

A key question lurking in the case is whether DOGE is technically a government agency and therefore subject to FOIA, which allows members of the public to seek internal documents.

The Trump administration says that, despite its name, DOGE is merely a presidential advisory body that is not subject to public records requests under FOIA.

Further complicating matters, when DOGE was set up, it effectively took the place of a previous government entity called the U.S. Digital Service. The Trump administration now refers to the body as the U.S. DOGE Service, or USDS.

Soon after Trump returned to office in January 2025, CREW filed a FOIA request seeking information about DOGE. The group later sued.

In March 2025, U.S. District Court Judge Christopher Cooper in Washington ruled DOGE is “likely” covered by FOIA, saying that “the public would be irreparably harmed by an indefinite delay in unearthing the records CREW seeks.”

The documents in question all relate to the question of whether DOGE is a government agency.
