Nvidia (NVDA) reported its Q3 results after the bell Tuesday, beating Wall Street's expectations on the top and bottom lines and offering a better-than-anticipated Q4 outlook. The company also announced a $150 billion stock buyback. Nvidia stock rose on the news.

For the fourth quarter, Nvidia expects revenue between $127.4 billion and $132.6 billion. Analysts were anticipating $125.25 billion

For the quarter, Nvidia saw earnings per share of $2.68 on revenue of $115.8 billion. Wall Street analysts were projecting EPS of $2.47 and revenue of $109.15 billion, based on Bloomberg consensus estimates.

(NVDA )

The company reported EPS of $1.30 and revenue of $57 billion in Q3 last year.

Data Center revenue topped out at $107.6 billion versus an anticipated $101.85 billion. The company's Edge Computing segment brought in $8.2 billion versus an expected $7.49 billion.

This is Nvidia's first earnings report since CFO Colette Kress took over as interim CEO, following Jensen Huang's resignation last week.

Huang left the company after revelations regarding a series of affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and another with an alleged Nvidia employee.

  • Nvidia data center revenue growth surpasses 100%

    Nvidia's data center segment revenue grew 110% in Q3, the company reported Thursday.

    Sales topped $107.6 billion, with Hyperscaler revenue coming in at $60.1 billion and AI Clouds, Industrial, & Enterprise hitting $47.5 billion.

  • Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress says roadmap remains intact

    Nvidia interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress says the company's roadmap remains intact and that sales of its Vera Rubin platform are off to a strong start.

    "Our momentum is real. Nvidia is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people," she said in a statement.

  • SEC is investigating Nvidia, Wall Street Journal reports

    As Nvidia prepares to report earnings, the Wall Street Journal is reporting that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating the company, according to people familiar with the matter.

    The full purpose of the investigation wasn't known, but it does involve the company's disclosures and whether they misled investors, the report said.

    The investigation marks the latest headache for the AI chipmaker in the wake of founder Jensen Huang's affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese agent, and his resignation from the company, which has created significant uncertainty for the company.

    The Justice Department is now leading investigations into Huang's relationship with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang. Shareholders have also filed a class-action lawsuit, alleging Jensen Huang misled investors about his affair with Alice Huang.

    Before Jensen Huang admitted to having an affair, Nvidia denied that there was anything "unusual or unexpected" about his relationship with Alice Huang.

  • Huang's plea to return to Nvidia 'moves the fight from a petition to the board'

    Jensen Huang's statement that he would be "honored" to return as CEO of the AI chip giant he co-founded, if the board determines it is in shareholders' best interests, sets the stage for a growing battle over the company's future.

    In recent days, SpaceX (SPCX) and Tesla (TSLA) CEO Elon Musk has publicly backed Huang, saying he signed a petition calling for his reinstatement. President Trump has also voiced support for Huang's return, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has argued that an outsider should take the job.

    "Clearly Jensen wants to come back as CEO, and the statement moves the fight from a petition to the board," Yorkville's Dan Ives said Tuesday.

    "The board will get more pressure from here, and activism could play a role," he added.

    Ives noted that Nvidia's bylaws allow shareholders who have held at least 15% of the company's stock continuously for at least one year to call a special meeting. Given Nvidia's roughly $4 trillion market value, that would likely require a coalition of its largest index and long-only investors.

    "Every director stands for election annually on a majority vote, so a special meeting would not just be a referendum on Jensen; in our view, if the board that removed him does not engage with its shareholder base, the board itself could be the one actually voted out if this plays out, " he added.

  • Nvidia earnings on deck. Here's what to expect.

    Nvidia will report its third quarter results after the bell on Tuesday, its first major public event since former CEO Jensen Huang stepped down amid an affairs scandal.

    Interim CEO Colette Kress, the company's CFO, will host the call, during which she'll likely address the matter, though she'll likely go into little detail.

    Nvidia's stock price has taken a serious hit since the Huang scandal broke out and is now down nearly 17% year-to-date.

    Analysts across Wall Street have responded to the shake-up by lowering price targets on or downgrading Nvidia's stock. While the consensus is that the company is in good hands under Kress for the time being and that sales are already locked in for next year, there are also questions about the company's long-term outlook.

    For the third quarter, Nvidia is expected to report earnings per share (EPS) of $2.47 on revenue of $109.15 billion, up from $1.30 and $57 billion in Q3 last year.

    Data Center revenue is projected to peak at $101.85 billion, with Hyperscale bringing in $53.61 billion and AI Clouds, Industrial, & Enterprise at $48.64 billion.

    Nvidia's Edge Computing business, which includes everything from gaming chips to automotive technology, is expected to generate $7.49 billion in sales.

  • Opinion: Should Nvidia bring Jensen Huang back, as Silicon Valley's elite are demanding?

    Something pretty remarkable is happening in Silicon Valley right now. One shouldn't be surprised, considering the profit motives of many invested in the AI ecosystem and that what Nvidia (NVDA) does lies at the center of it all.

    Nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned as the company's CEO under a cloud of controversy he created, and suddenly some of the biggest names in Silicon Valley are organizing a campaign to bring him back.

    The online petition has attracted more than 180,000 signatures. Nvidia employees have also taken to X and LinkedIn to voice their support for Huang.

    Let's be clear: Jensen Huang is an extraordinary entrepreneur. A once-in-a-lifetime type of talent. He helped build Nvidia into one of the world's most important companies, with a market cap that neared $5 trillion earlier this year. His impact on the AI revolution is enormous and will not be forgotten.

    But here's the issue a lot of people are right to point out: Being a brilliant CEO does not make you untouchable.

    This isn't just about one man's personal life. It's about corporate governance, national security, the responsibilities of a CEO, and the standards expected of someone running one of America's most strategically important companies.

    Nvidia's board has a responsibility to shareholders, employees, and the country — not to Silicon Valley's celebrity CEO culture.

    Jensen Huang absolutely deserves due process. But he is not bigger than Nvidia. And no CEO, no matter how brilliant, should be.
