Mourinho: ‘The 12 points give us some breathing space, but we don’t want to be in the Champions League play-offs’

TheTools · 23.11.2026 12:11 · source · permalink

‘PSV are a good side, who deserve more points than they have, and it won’t be easy,’ added the manager.

José Mourinho spoke at the pre-match press conference ahead of Real Madrid’s clash with PSV at the Bernabéu, on Matchday 5 of the Champions League (Tuesday, 9.00 pm; Orange TV and Movistar Champions League): “PSV are a good side, who are worth more than the points they’ve picked up so far. They’re coming here to play for the points and aren’t coming here thinking they’re going to lose. They have good players and a very experienced manager. Some of their players have many years’ experience in European competitions too. It’s by no means an easy match, and three days between matches is fine; two days is tough. That’s the way it has to be, but it’s tough. They also played on Saturday, just like us, although it was an easier match, in which they were leading 4–0 by the 60th minute. As I always do, I bring in a couple of fresh, energetic players when there’s little time between matches. The 12 points give us some breathing space in the table, but it’s for a play-off spot that we don’t want to be in.”

“I shouldn’t give you any details about PSV. I have to try and keep my thoughts to myself, but they clearly have their own identity. They have a very clear identity on the ball, and it’s no surprise that they have a manager who’s been at the club for a few years. That’s a long time for a team, and they have very good, experienced players like Kostić and Perišić. It’s going to be a tough match for us and for them.”

A player in the squad with the makings of a manager

“We’re always having conversations about football, mainly about our own game and our own affairs. I like the players to feel comfortable and to trust what we ask of them, and we’re always talking. It’s still too early to think about which players might one day become managers in general terms, because we’ve got a lot of young players and very few who are nearing the end of their careers. Bernardo Silva will be president of Benfica, but as for a manager, I find it hard to think of who that might be.”

Vini Jr. is young, and people can forget that he arrived very young and reached an extremely high level far too soon. He’s won everything, but when people were out to get him, he always said he’s a really good lad and very empathetic towards everyone. That’s a fundamental foundation. A goal from Vini Jr. and the tie was over. Young managers at Europe’s top clubs

“The fundamental difference is that in our generation, to appear the part you had to be the part, whereas now there are many who appear the part before they are, and some who appear the part without ever actually becoming it. This is a fundamental difference, but more than football having changed, it’s that the world has changed, and now we have the tools and the means to analyse things: the size of the coaching staff, the level of analysis, tactical cameras, live streaming, the facilities available to manage training loads, and the way we train and recover. The world has changed and you have to adapt. As Charles Darwin once said.”

The fixture list

“If we want to make money, we have to work hard and take risks. That’s life, and if there’s a player who wants to go on strike because we play too many matches, well, we’ll have to ask him if he wants to earn 50 per cent less. Everything on a financial level carries a lot of weight, and we have to adapt; we have to endure and adjust to a new and difficult situation. Today I’m a club manager and tomorrow I might be in charge of the national team. We club managers wonder why there are so many international fixtures, trips, friendlies and the Nations Cup, which is of no interest whatsoever – but that’s just a club manager’s view. For those in charge of the national team, these are crucial matches, and for the federations too. They’re also important for the federations’ financial management. It’s a conflict of interest from a sporting perspective, but in the end it’s money that decides everything – so let’s just get on with the game. Points picked up in the dying minutes

“It depends on the situation. Winning matches in the dying minutes has many good aspects and also some bad ones. It has its risks. The other day, in the last 15 minutes, the risk was there. When you take risks, you change your set-pieces and completely alter the team’s approach by going all out. That also opens the door to setbacks, where you might not pick up a single point. The perfect, ideal situation is a match like those against Valencia, Málaga, Sevilla or Santander, where within five minutes you already know how the match is going to end. I said after the match that Celta deserve a lot of credit for the tough match they gave us. The travel arrangements, not being able to train, being together for the first time the day before the match, not doing any tactical work… I’m also fortunate to have some good liars who tell me they’re raring to go, when in fact they’re not. They’re fantastic liars whom I like a great deal, but they’re liars.”
