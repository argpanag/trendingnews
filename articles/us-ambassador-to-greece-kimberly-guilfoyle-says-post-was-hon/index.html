  6. Guilfoyle controversy raised wider questions inside Greece about US influencepublished at 15:57 BST
    15:57 BST

    Nikos Papanikolaou
    BBC News

    Kimberly Guilfoyle had already faced political criticism in Greece following a Wall Street Journal report in September that prompted several opposition parties to demand explanations from the government.

    The newspaper reported that, during a dinner in Athens in March, Guilfoyle allegedly suggested the US could bring down governments - including Greece's.

    "We can do that to any country we want. We can do that here," she reportedly said, referring to the prospect of the Romanian government's collapse.

    Greece's Energy Minister Stavros Papastavrou reportedly challenged her remarks, arguing that the US could not replace a democratically elected Greek government.

    Guilfoyle's lawyer has disputed the Wall Street Journal's characterisation of the exchange.

    The report prompted criticism from opposition parties, and many of them demanded explanations from Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis's government.

    Opposition parties also raised concerns about Guilfoyle's reported involvement in promoting American liquefied natural gas (LNG) exports through Greece and the wider Balkan region.

    The Greek government subsequently denied that any threat had been made.

    Government spokesman Pavlos Marinakis said there had never been an issue of the Greek government being threatened, while Foreign Minister Giorgos Gerapetritis rejected calls to summon Guilfoyle to the foreign ministry over the incident.

    The controversy raised wider questions among opposition parties about American diplomatic influence in Greece and the government's relationship with Washington.

  8. Despite short briefing, reporters were not left empty handedpublished at 15:23 BST
    15:23 BST

    Bernd Debusmann Jr
    at the White House briefing room

    The big news of the day has become the administration's request that Kimberly Guilfoyle resign from her position as ambassador to Greece.

    That will come as no surprise to those who have been following the mounting controversies about Guilfoyle, who had allegedly asked a Trump donor to pay her hefty credit card bill in exchange for access to his administration.

    Audio recordings obtained by the Wall Street Journal reportedly include Guilfoyle boasting of her connections to prominent Greek business tycoons who she said bankrolled luxury travel and flights on private jets.

    According to the newspaper, she also boasted to an official in Athens that the US could topple the Greek government.

    Her lawyers denied any wrongdoing by Guilfoyle and said she was owed the money for speaking engagements and consulting work for the donor.

    The controversies had become increasingly difficult for the Trump administration to defend, and when Trump said yesterday that "we're not happy" - it was clear to most in Washington that her position was no longer tenable.

    Katie Zacharia's first time at the White House press briefing began as many in the room expected - with thanks for President Trump, a man for whom she has long wanted to work for.
