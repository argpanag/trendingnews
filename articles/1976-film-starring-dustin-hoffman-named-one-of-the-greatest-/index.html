  • Dustin Hoffman's role in the 1976 thriller Marathon Man challenged him to portray fear, grief, and desperation, leading to a memorable performance alongside Laurence Olivier.
  • The film adaptation of William Goldman's suspense novel was ranked No. 50 on the American Film Institute's list of the 100 most thrilling American films, with TCM calling it one of the '70s most memorable contributions to the suspense genre.
  • Despite initial misconceptions, Laurence Olivier's famous remark to Dustin Hoffman on set was not a criticism of his acting method, as Hoffman clarified in a 2006 interview, and the film earned critical acclaim and commercial success.

Dustin Hoffman built one of Hollywood's most acclaimed acting careers, but few roles challenged him like his performance in the 1976 thriller Marathon Man.

The film adapted William Goldman's 1974 suspense novel of the same name.

With the movie bringing the book's tense atmosphere to the big screen, the American Film Institute ranked Marathon Man No. 50 on its list of the 100 most thrilling American films.

TCM called the movie "one of the '70s most memorable contributions to the suspense genre."

"Marathon Man is notable on many levels," TCM said. "The film brought together two of the leading exponents of screen acting for their respective generations — Dustin Hoffman and Laurence Olivier — to combine their talents for a taut and solid thriller."

Hoffman played Thomas "Babe" Levy, a Columbia University graduate student and runner preparing for the Olympic marathon.

Dustin Hoffman in Marathon Man. United Archives / Getty Images

Dustin Hoffman's Marathon Man Role Led to Laurence Olivier's Famous Acting Remark

The role challenged Hoffman to portray Babe's fear, grief and growing desperation. His approach to the character later became part of Hollywood lore.

A story circulated for years that Hoffman had stayed awake for three days to get into character. When Hoffman arrived on set, his exhausted appearance caught co-star Laurence Olivier's attention.

The British actor asked him why he looked that way, and Hoffman explained his situation. Olivier then replied, "My dear boy, why don't you try acting? It's a lot easier."

Olivier's remark was misinterpreted as a criticism of Hoffman's acting method. Hoffman eventually cleared up the story in a 2006 interview.

"Well… It's a very good example of the press," the actor said during his appearance on Inside the Actors Studio. "Because I'm the one who told the story! I told it to Time magazine and they totally, you know, distorted it.

"I was in the middle of a marriage break-up. It was very painful," he continued. "And I was supposed to have been awake for three or four days and I'm thinking 'Well, let me see if I can do this, I won't sleep.' It was an excuse to go [partying at] Studio 54."

The actor said Olivier understood what he was going through and believed the remark was not a harsh criticism of his acting method.

Hoffman earned a Golden Globe nomination for Marathon Man and a joint BAFTA Best Actor nomination recognizing that film and All the President's Men. Beyond his performance, the film also became known for several memorable scenes and lines.

The movie's infamous dental torture scene, featuring Dr. Christian Szell (Olivier) and Babe, introduced its most recognizable line. "Is it safe?" Szell repeatedly asks Babe the question while drilling into his healthy teeth without anesthesia. The chilling tension made the phrase and the scene difficult for audiences to forget.

Marathon Man earned $21.7 million, per Box Office Mojo. It currently has an 82% score on Rotten Tomatoes.
