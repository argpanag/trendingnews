Price community honors the service and sacrifice of veterans - ETV News

TheTools · 11.11.2026 22:19 · source · permalink

PRICE — Community members gathered at the Price City Peace Gardens on Wednesday, Nov. 11, for the annual Veterans Day Ceremony, coming together to honor the men and women who have served in the United States Armed Forces and recognize the sacrifices made to protect the nation’s freedoms.

The ceremony drew a strong crowd, with local veterans, families and residents attending to show their appreciation for those who have served. Hosted by Price City, the event featured patriotic music, remarks from local leaders, a military honor ceremony and an opportunity for the community to reflect on the meaning of Veterans Day.

Megan Marshall, Price City’s event coordinator, opened the program by welcoming attendees and expressing gratitude to the veterans and their families.

“To our veterans, thank you for your service, your sacrifices, and the freedoms that you have helped protect,” Marshall said. “We also recognize your families and friends for their love, support, and sacrifices right along with you.”

Marshall also recognized the organizations and businesses that helped make the gathering possible. USU Eastern provided breakfast, Intermountain Electronics supplied coffee and hot chocolate, and IGotPoop contributed giveaways including complimentary hats to Veterans. Along with gifts for all the Veterans in attendance donated by a generous community member.

The program began with the Pledge of Allegiance, followed by the national anthem, performed by Benjamin Jones.

Price City Mayor Terry Willis was the first featured speaker. Willis, the city’s first female mayor and a United States Navy veteran, joined the Navy in 1974 and served at duty stations on both coasts and in Iceland before continuing her commitment to service through local government.

Willis welcomed the crowd and reflected on the relatively small percentage of Americans who have served in the military, reminding those in attendance that veterans represent a unique group of individuals who have answered the call to serve.

“Some of us volunteered and some of us were told to serve,” Willis said, recognizing the wide range of roles veterans have filled throughout their military careers, from combat positions to mechanics, pilots, intelligence personnel and countless other responsibilities.

She also acknowledged that military service leaves lasting memories, both good and difficult, that veterans carry long after their time in uniform.

“But we all know the legacy that we gave so others could sleep at night,” Willis said.

Following Willis, Commander Kelly Madsen of American Legion Post 3 addressed the gathering. A United States Navy veteran who served from 1985 to 1989, Madsen continues to support local veterans through his leadership with the American Legion.

Madsen’s remarks focused on the history of America’s military branches and the many individuals who have contributed to defending the country throughout its history.

He highlighted the six branches of the United States Armed Forces, beginning with the Army, Navy and Marine Corps, all established in 1775, the Coast Guard in 1790, the Air Force in 1947 and the Space Force in 2019.

Madsen also made a point to recognize the United States Merchant Marines, whose contributions have often received less attention despite their important role in supporting military operations.

During World War II, Merchant Marine crews transported troops, ammunition, fuel, food, vehicles and other essential supplies across dangerous waters. Their ships faced enemy submarines, mines and aircraft, and many Merchant Marines never made it home.

“Different missions, different uniforms, or in many cases different roles. But all connected by one thing, service to America,” Madsen said.

He reminded the crowd that behind every military uniform is a person with family, friends and a life outside of service. When someone takes the oath to serve, the commitment extends beyond an individual job and becomes a responsibility to something greater than themselves.

Madsen also emphasized that the sacrifices of military families deserve recognition, as they endure deployments, separation and uncertainty alongside their loved ones.

“Freedom has never been free,” Madsen said, noting that the ability to gather, speak freely, worship and live without fear has been protected by generations of Americans willing to stand in harm’s way.

He encouraged those in attendance to remember not only veterans who served in combat, but also those who served during peacetime, those stationed overseas and those who carried physical and emotional wounds home with them.

He also paid tribute to those who never returned home, reminding the community of the ultimate sacrifice made by service members throughout the nation’s history.

“Thank you for your service. Thank you for your sacrifice. Thank you for your courage, and thank you for standing ready to defend America’s freedom and make the country what it is today,” Madsen said.

The ceremony concluded with a traditional three-volley salute and the sounding of Taps, providing a moment of reflection and remembrance for those who have served and those who gave their lives in defense of the country.

Before closing the gathering, organizers encouraged veterans to collect their complimentary hats and gifts as a small token of appreciation from the community.

With a strong turnout and support from local organizations and businesses, Price City’s annual Veterans Day Ceremony once again provided an opportunity for residents to come together and recognize the men and women whose service has helped shape the nation.

As the speakers emphasized throughout the morning, honoring veterans means recognizing more than their time in uniform. It means remembering their families, acknowledging their sacrifices and appreciating the freedoms their service has helped preserve.

Price City and the Carbon County community extend their heartfelt gratitude to all who have served and continue to serve. Their dedication and sacrifice will never be forgotten.
