Viral Bruno Mars Ticket Listing Shows $168 Gap Between Ticketmaster Buyer Price and Seller Payout

TheTools · 07.10.2026 16:45 · source · permalink

Home »

A Bruno Mars fan’s Ticketmaster resale listing is drawing attention after she said a seat she listed for $299 appeared to buyers for $422.45 — while her own projected payout would be just $254.15 after Ticketmaster deducted a seller fee.

In a TikTok video posted by @brickbuster69, the seller showed a Ticketmaster “Edit Listing” screen for Section 235, Row 19, Seat 4. The screen showed the ticket listed at $299, a $44.85 service fee and a projected payout of $254.15.

The $44.85 deduction amounts to a 15% seller fee.

The seller then said she found the same section, row and seat on Ticketmaster at a much higher buyer-facing price. The Mary Sue reported Wednesday that the price displayed for the seat was $422.45.

That creates a $123.45 difference between the seller’s chosen $299 listing price and the amount shown to a prospective buyer. Compared with the seller’s projected $254.15 payout, the total gap is $168.30.

“That is so gross,” the seller said in the video.

The numbers offer a particularly clear example of how differently the economics of a single resale ticket can look depending on which side of the transaction a fan occupies. But the $168.30 spread should not automatically be characterized as money Ticketmaster itself keeps.

Ticketmaster says sellers on its resale marketplace determine the base price of their tickets and receive those proceeds minus applicable fees. It also says resale service fees may be shared among parties involved in putting on an event and can cover operational costs as well as profits.

Ticketmaster currently tells buyers that they will generally see the total ticket price, including mandatory fees, upfront before taxes. That means at least part of the discrepancy between a reseller’s base listing and what a buyer sees can reflect buyer-side charges that are now incorporated into the displayed price instead of appearing only later in the purchase process.

What is not apparent from the seller’s video is exactly how the additional $123.45 above her $299 listing price was divided, or whether any portion reflected a markup separate from service fees.

Ticketmaster Resale Fees Already Under FTC Scrutiny

The episode comes as Ticketmaster and parent company Live Nation are defending a federal lawsuit in which the Federal Trade Commission and seven states have challenged several of the company’s ticketing and resale practices.

Among other allegations, the FTC’s complaint claims Ticketmaster earns money on resale transactions through fees charged on both sides of the marketplace and, in some cases, has the ability to increase a resale ticket’s price independently of those fees.

The government alleges Live Nation and Ticketmaster collected $986 million in resale fees between 2019 and 2024. It separately alleges the companies collected more than $187 million from markups Ticketmaster added to resale tickets during that period.

Those claims remain allegations. They do not establish that Ticketmaster added a separate markup to the Bruno Mars ticket shown in the TikTok video.

Just last week, a federal judge rejected Live Nation and Ticketmaster’s attempt to dismiss the FTC case, allowing claims involving ticket limits, resale activity and allegedly deceptive fee displays to move forward.

The court’s ruling was not a finding that Ticketmaster violated the law. It determined that the government had alleged sufficient facts for the case to continue.

Bruno Mars Tour Has Already Drawn Pricing Complaints

The resale controversy follows months of attention around pricing for Mars’ massive “The Romantic Tour.”

When tickets initially went on sale in January, TicketNews reported fan complaints about primary-market prices reaching as high as $2,400. Live Nation subsequently said the tour generated record-setting demand and moved more than 2 million tickets in a single day.

The latest video involves a different part of the market: a fan attempting to resell a ticket she already holds. It does not establish who originally set the ticket’s face value, how much the seller initially paid or precisely how the buyer-side charges on the $422.45 listing were allocated.

It does, however, put the two sides of a Ticketmaster resale transaction into unusually concrete terms: a fan chooses a $299 resale price, stands to receive $254.15 if the ticket sells, while another fan looking at the same seat sees a price of $422.45.
