Where do the most Halloween night crashes happen in Connecticut? Explore our interactive map

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:48 · source · permalink

Data from the Connecticut Transportation Institute revealed many distinct clusters in the state.

With Halloween weekend approaching, ghosts and goblins may be on your mind. However, some of the biggest dangers could be lurking on Connecticut's roads.

NBC Connecticut analyzed crash data from Halloween night and the early morning hours of Nov. 1 between 2020 and 2025 to find out where crashes are happening, when they're most common, and what drivers and pedestrians should know before going out and about.

The data revealed 881 crashes statewide, with more than half occurring between 4 p.m. and 8 p.m., when trick-or-treaters are typically out and daylight is fading.

Of the 881 crashes categorized by severity in the map, 636 resulted in property damage only, 242 involved injuries, and three were fatal.

The map also identifies 30 crashes where a drug or alcohol test was given, 710 where it was not, and 141 where it was unknown.

In New Haven, nine crashes were recorded around the New Haven Green, while another 15 occurred near Southern Connecticut State University.

"Where you have the grid system built within New Haven, the intersections there tend to see more crashes as parties take place," said Dr. Eric Jackson, executive director of UConn's Connecticut Transportation Institute. "As you know, bars and clubs are letting out. You'll see those impaired driving crashes kind of spike as well."

In Hartford, there were 40 crashes during Halloween night and the overnight hours, including crashes on city streets and Interstate 84.

Other clusters appeared along Interstate 95 between Bridgeport and Stratford and near the interchange connecting I-95, I-91, and the U.S. Route 1 Interchange.

But experts caution that a higher number of crashes doesn't necessarily mean an area is more dangerous. More vehicles and pedestrians can create more opportunities for collisions.

"As people go into the town to trick-or-treat or to engage in festive activities, you'll see more crashes occur simply because there's more vehicles in that area than there typically is," Jackson said.

About 5% of the drivers involved in Halloween crashes in the data were suspected of being under the influence.

"A very small percentage of those drivers involved in crashes actually tested positive for drugs or alcohol in their system," Jackson said.

When are Halloween crashes most common?

Jackson also provided NBC Connecticut with Halloween night crash data from the years 2015 to 2025. In that data, the most crashes were reported in New Haven, Hartford, and Bridgeport.

The crash data from 2015 to 2025 also found that vehicle-versus-vehicle crashes were most common between 4 p.m. and 5 p.m., with 288 reported during that hour over the 10-year period.

Crashes involving pedestrians and bicyclists peaked between 7 p.m. and 8 p.m., when eight crashes were recorded during the same period.

New Haven had the highest number of Halloween crashes over those 10 years, with 121, followed by Hartford with 84 and Bridgeport with 83.

Despite the number of children typically out trick-or-treating, children did not make up the majority of pedestrians and bicyclists involved in crashes in NBC Connecticut's earlier analysis. The median age was 27.

"I think that's a positive for Connecticut, that we're not seeing this dramatic trend or in a one-day period or two-day period that we have a lot of children that are being struck by cars," Jackson said.

Connecticut State Police said Halloween presents additional challenges because of the increased number of pedestrians on the roads, particularly as it gets darker.

"It is a huge day, right? Because of the increased probability of there being more pedestrians on the roadway," Lt. Josue Dorelus said.

Jackson said looking at crash patterns like these matters because the information can help determine where additional safety efforts are needed.

"All of our decisions need to be data-driven," he said.
