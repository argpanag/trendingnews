Spain train crash: at least 30 dead after collision in Toledo province

TheTools · 28.10.2026 11:01 · source · permalink

High-speed service reportedly runs into back of another train, knocking two carriages off track

  • Spain train crash – latest updates

Spain train crash – latest updates

At least 30 people have been killed after two high-speed trains collided in Toledo province in central Spain, on the line between Madrid and Seville.

Footage broadcast by the regional channel CMM TV ​showed a derailed train with at least one carriage remaining on an elevated section of track. Twisted metal, broken panels and wreckage could be seen strewn across an embankment beneath the line.

Castilla-La Mancha regional government said the death toll had risen to 30 from 10 after earlier warnings that it could go up.

Miguel, ‌a passenger travelling with several colleagues in the rear car of one of the trains, described the moment of the accident to Cope radio. “We heard a massive roar, a truly enormous thud that went on for 10 seconds, as if the train were ‌about to derail,” he said. “Through the right-hand window, we could see a wrecked train – I imagine it must have been ​the other one.”

Esther Padilla, a spokesperson for the Castilla- La Mancha region, told the state broadcaster TVE that the collision was between two high-speed trains carrying hundreds of passengers and heading in the same direction from the capital, Madrid, to Seville in the south-west. One ran into the rear of the other, which had stopped, knocking two of its carriages off the track, she said, adding that the cause of the crash was not yet known.

Government sources told El País the crash could be worse than the Adamuz rail disaster in January in which 46 people were killed.

The collision on Wednesday involved a train from Spain’s state-owned Renfe company and the private rail company Iryo. It appeared the Renfe train had rammed into the Iryo service, Spanish media reported. Trains from those two companies were also involved in a Adamuz crash.

Manuel Galán, the mayor ​of ​Urda near the city of Toledo, said he had ​been ‌asked by ​emergency ​services to prepare a sports hall to accommodate the injured.

Spain’s main political parties, which are gearing up for next month’s general election, suspended their planned activities for the day.

The prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, expressed his shock at news of the crash. “I remain in constant contact with the transport minister, and with the president of Castilla-La Mancha,” he said in a post on X. “Emergency services are working on the ground to assist those affected and ensure their safety, in coordination with all the involved administrations. My love, solidarity and support to all the victims and their families.”

The opposition People’s party leader, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, said on X: “Very concerned about the information we are getting about a new rail accident. The priority now is to attend to the victims and support their families. My full support to the emergency teams working on the ground.”

Reuters contributed to this report
