WICKET! Ahmed run out Asalanka 2 (England 199-6)

Three hours into this innings and we’ve still got 14 overs to go! Get a move on, lads. Rehan Ahmed edges off Madushanka … but the low bounce means the ball doesn’t carry to Mendis. And then a run out! What was Ahmed doing? He hits the ball straight to cover, decides on a single that Jacks isn’t having, and Charith Asalanka takes down the stumps with his throw. England are imploding!

36th over: England 199-6 (Overton 0, Jacks 7)

35th over: England 196-5 (Ahmed 0, Jacks 6) Colombage follows up his wicket with three dots. It’s been a strong debut.

WICKET! Buttler lbw Colombage 31 (England 196-5)

Oh, that’s got to be out. Colombage hits Buttler in front but the umpire says no. The only explanation is that there’s some bat on it … Sri Lanka go upstairs and there’s nothing on UltraEdge. That’s knocking over middle and leg and Colombage has his second! The leggie’s done it with a similar delivery to the one that got Root: forcing the batter back and getting just enough turn.

34th over: England 193-4 (Buttler 31, Jacks 3) Chameera bowls a slower ball and it looks to have flummoxed Buttler, who swings and lets go of one hand … but he’s got such power that he’s knocked it over mid-on for four.

33rd over: England 185-4 (Buttler 24, Jacks 2) Colombage is back as Jacks tries to settle – there’s a slip in for the leg-spinner. And he’s on the spot, testing out the forward defence. And then one spins big and stays low, very nearly taking Jacks’ off-stump. Just two off the over and Sri Lanka are starting to build a bit of pressure.

32nd over: England 183-4 (Buttler 23, Jacks 1) It’s Will Jacks, not Rehan Ahmed, at six.

WICKET! Brook c Madushanka b Chameera 67 (England 182-4)

Ooops. Brook comes down the track to swing at Chameera … and he’s got his placement wrong, finding the man at deep cover. A wicket out of nowhere, really.

31st over: England 182-3 (Brook 67, Buttler 23) Brook continues to play spin well off the back foot, his hands rapid when he wants to rock back for a single.

30th over: England 175-3 (Buttler 22, Brook 61) Brook skips down the pitch against Chameera … and slashes the Durham air. He runs hard for two moments later.

29th over: England 172-3 (Buttler 22, Brook 58) Buttler looks in danger for a moment with a reverse-sweep, the ball looping up, but he nicks a couple. Wellalage is knocked around for ones and twos. Time for drinks.

28th over: England 164-3 (Buttler 17, Brook 55) Buttler skips down the crease to clip Asitha Fernando for three and is looking pretty settled.

27th over: England 160-3 (Buttler 14, Brook 54) Oh man, what’s happened here? Has Buttler been run out at the non-striker’s end … no, replays show he’s comfortably home after running through on a Sri Lanka misfield. Buttler closes Wellalage’s latest over with a thump down the ground for four.

26th over: England 154-3 (Buttler 9, Brook 53) Plenty of empty seats at Chester-le-Street, with tickets still available on Durham’s website.

Harry Brook reaches 50!

25th over: England 147-3 (Brook 51, Buttler 4) Brook knocks one off Colombage and that’s his half-century. He’s probably the best all-format batter in international cricket right now.

24th over: England 139-3 (Brook 45, Buttler 2) Another decent over from Madushanka, Sri Lanka’s most economical bowler today.

23rd over: England 136-3 (Brook 44, Buttler 1) Root’s ODI batting average this year drops to … 180. Just have a look at these numbers.

WICKET! Root b Colombage 44 (England 135-3)

The leggie strikes! His first wicket is one of the greats, and he’s bowled him, too. Colombage forces Root back into the crease, and gets a hint of turn to knock back off-stump. Mark Wood’s on commentary for it; one career closes and another begins.

22nd over: England 133-2 (Root 43, Brook 43) A run-out chance … and Root would have been a goner with a direct hit at the striker’s end after Brook wanted one off the returning Madushanka.

21st over: England 129-2 (Root 42, Brook 42) Brook has had a look at Colombage and is putting his foot down, driving for another boundary. Root is quietly knocking about ones at the other end.

20th over: England 119-2 (Root 41, Brook 32) Root very nearly chops on, pushing at a wide one against Chameera … but the inside-edge runs away for four.

In relation to new OBO favourite Colombage, John Starbuck writes in:

In my mostly inglorious cricket career I was a leggie too. The main advantage is that most batters rarely faced one, but the general opinion was that leggies got plenty of wickets, but gave away a massive number of runs. Can’t have one without the other, but optimism is essential.

19th over: England 113-2 (Root 36, Brook 33) Colombage is back, giving the ball air … and Brook drives through cover for four. He looks on for a big one today.

18th over: England 105-2 (Root 34, Brook 27) England reach three figures and Brook pulls Chameera for four, the bouncer failing to rush the right-hander.

17th over: England 97-2 (Root 32, Brook 21) Brook dabs through point and sprints away for two as Wellalage continues. I wonder if he’s got KP on his mind.

16th over: England 94-2 (Root 31, Brook 19) Chameera is back as Sri Lanka decide, strangely, to take Colombage off after two good overs.

15th over: England 90-2 (Root 29, Brook 17) Brook wallops a sweep off Wellalage for four. My mind has just gone back to Colombo in January, when the England captain and Root both reached three figures to secure a series victory.

14th over: England 82-2 (Brook 10, Root 28) Always a joy watching a leggie, the hardest thing to bowl in this stupidly hard game. Colombage bowls another tight over.

13th over: England 80-2 (Root 28, Brook 8) It’s not a long-hop by Wellalage, but it’s short enough for Brook, who is so quick to get back and cut for four. Root then plays the cutest of reverse-dabs for a couple. Time for drinks.

12th over: England 72-2 (Root 25, Brook 3) A huge moment in the life of Sachindu Colombage. The debutant is on to bowl. He’s got a funky start to his action, arms everywhere … and he’s appealing for leg-before against Root! The ball goes straight on from the leg-spinner and Sri Lanka decide to go upstairs … I think it’s missing leg-stump … and DRS agrees. It’s a tidy start from Colombage: not much turn there but he’s accurate enough.

11th over: England 68-2 (Brook 1, Root 23) Out comes the captain, who punches for one to keep the strike.

WICKET! Duckett lbw Wellalage 32 (England 67-2)

Left-arm spinner Dunith Wellalage is on as Root and Duckett take their stand beyond 50. But Wellalage traps Duckett leg-before! It’s flat and quick, with Duckett failing to connect with his sweep. The batter reviews immediately, hoping he’s been struck outside the line of off-stump … but DRS shows three reds. Spin gives Sri Lanka the breakthrough – a smart piece of bowling by Wellalage.

10th over: England 64-1 (Root 21, Duckett 31) Fernando is back after changing ends … and Root plays an immaculate cover drive for four. Nasser Hussain asks the question on comms: why don’t Sri Lanka bring the keeper up to the stumps? Root then marches out of his crease to guide the ball through the covers again. Duckett clips for another boundary and the powerplay belongs to England. Sri Lanka need to sort out their lines.

9th over: England 51-1 (Root 12, Duckett 27) Time for Dushmantha Chameera, replacing Fernando. Sri Lanka are bowling a bit too straight to Root, who clips again for four. Duckett copies his partner, finding the midwicket boundary to move to 27.

8th over: England 42-1 (Root 7, Duckett 23) Root opens up his blade to play his trademark guide to third for a single. Duckett’s timing is getting better, too, the ball starting to ping off the bat.

7th over: England 36-1 (Root 6, Duckett 18) Better from Duckett, who middles the cut this time, off Fernando and away for four. A whip through midwicket follows and Duckett is finding his flow. Root, meanwhile, has become the eighth man to hit 23,000 (!) international runs. Duckett very nearly gifts square leg a catch before punching through the off-side for three.

Adil Rashid ruled out of the ODI series

6th over: England 25-1 (Root 6, Duckett 7) Madushanka goes wide and Duckett can’t nail his cut. An in-form version of the left-hander would probably have found the boundary. And some news from England: Adil Rashid has been ruled out of the series with an injury to his bowling hand. Time for Rehan Ahmed to step up. Root closes the over with a lovely clip through midwicket.

5th over: England 20-1 (Root 2, Duckett 6) Asitha Fernando had a strong Test series in England a couple years ago, taking 17 wickets in three matches at 24.64. He goes a bit too straight against Duckett, who clips away for one, and Root plays a similar shot for two.

4th over: England 17-1 (Root 0, Duckett 5) A strong follow-up over from Madushanka, who is getting some tasty shape. There’s a bit of bother for Kamindu Mendis, at slip, who takes a blow to the right knee at the end of the over.

3rd over: England 16-1 (Root 0, Duckett 4) In comes Joe Root.

WICKET! Banton b Fernando 10 (England 16-1)

Poor work at deep fine leg by Madushanka, who goes for a one-handed stop by the rope but lets it slip away for four. Banton very nearly offers a return grab to Fernando … before a corker to hit the top of off! Brilliant from Asitha Fernando, who nips it away from the right-hander, beating the prod forward to take the off bail.

2nd over: England 12-0 (Banton 6, Duckett 4) Dilshan Madushanka is on from the other end. The left-armer hoops the ball into Banton, who whips away for his first boundary. A wide down the leg-side follows but Madushanka eventually finds his spot, threatening the stumps with his inswinger.

1st over: England 5-0 (Banton 1, Duckett 4) A bit of shape away from the right-hander for Fernando as Banton gets going with a single. Ben Duckett had a poor Test series against Pakistan but did hit 141 in England’s last ODI, against India in July. He clips a full toss away for four.

Right, time to get going. Tom Banton is opening in an ODI for the very first time. Asitha Fernando has the new ball.

There’s an international debut for the legspinner Sachindu Colombage. Some ask to come in and bowl at that England middle order. I’m fascinated to see how Rehan Ahmed, carded at six, goes; he’s in because Adil Rashid has an issue with his bowling hand.

The teams

England: Ben Duckett, Tom Banton, Joe Root, Harry Brook (c), Jos Buttler (wk), Rehan Ahmed, Will Jacks, Jamie Overton, Jofra Archer, Gus Atkinson, Josh Tongue

Sri Lanka: Pathum Nissanka, Kamindu Mendis, Kusal Mendis (c, wk), Pavan Rathnayake, Charith Asalanka, Janith Liyanage, Dunith Wellalage, Dushmantha Chameera, Sachindu Colombage, Asitha Fernando, Dilshan Madushanka

England win the toss and choose to bat first

Wood is part of Sky’s commentary team for this game, and he’s joined by his former captain Eoin Morgan. They take in the toss … which is won by Harry Brook. England will have a hit.

Mark Wood retires from international cricket

The big news this morning is that Mark Wood has called it a day at the highest level. Go to his Cricinfo profile and the numbers won’t jump out … but that doesn’t tell his story. You’re better off heading to YouTube to watch that spell at Headingley or the one in St Lucia, bowling at absurd pace. The body, inevitably, suffered. He took 18 wickets when England won the 2019 World Cup and nine at 12 when they won the T20 equivalent in 2022. He won the Ashes at home and, while things didn’t work out for the collective, he thrived in Australia, too. Some career.

Preamble

Hello, hello, hello and welcome to the last series of the English summer, stretching its way right until the close of September. It’s back to 50-over cricket, the forgotten child, with Sri Lanka hoping that the longer form gives them more of a chance; the T20 series was a miserable affair for the visitors, with defeats by 119 runs, six wickets (47 balls to spare) and eight wickets (62 balls).

After a few lean years in the ODI game, England have won their last two series, away to Sri Lanka at the start of the year and then at home against India. Brendon McCullum has lost the Test job but his white-ball group have had a pretty impressive 2026. It would be quite the splash if Kusal Mendis’ side halt the streak here. We get going in Durham at 12.30pm BST.
