Isiah Dudley and Shai Bolton were instrumental in the final quarter as Fremantle kicked the last six goals of the match to book a grand final spot. (Getty Images: Cameron Spencer)

Fremantle has pulled off a sensational last-quarter comeback to surge into the AFL grand final with a tense 12-point win over Sydney.

Trailing by 27 points late in the third quarter, the Dockers kicked the last six goals of their preliminary final to win 12.11 (83) to 10.11 (71) at the SCG.

Fremantle advances to the second grand final in club history, where they will meet either Hawthorn or Brisbane.

Justin Longmuir's men were staring down the barrel of an early exit before Hayden Young converted a difficult set shot after the three-quarter time siren, and were full of running in a gripping finish.

Young (28 disposals), Andrew Brayshaw (29), Caleb Serong (28) and Shai Bolton (two goals) were instrumental in the victory.

But it wasn't assured until the dying stages, when Isaiah Dudley ran into an open goal with less than two minutes on the clock.

"Words can't describe how proud I am of everyone for stepping up," Fremantle captain Alex Pearce told Channel 7.

"There were just so many moments where guys stood up under the fiercest of pressure.

"We played our best footy in the last quarter when everything was against us."

Luke Jackson finished with two goals in an entertaining ruck battle with Brodie Grundy.

Dudley's second major was the Dockers' fifth of a dominant final quarter, in which Patrick Voss's huge fend-off and checkside goal on the run was a highlight.

"[Our forwards] were beaten early, and it was hurting us a little bit," Pearce said.

"Our ball use into the forward line was diabolical at times. We were just kicking it to the red jumper.

"But wow, our forwards stood up [in the final quarter]. They stood up like big men."

Sydney's brave effort came without injured captain Callum Mills and the five stars banned in the fallout from the Pullman Hotel scandal.

Errol Gulden (36 disposals) was brilliant in setting up their lead, with Logan McDonald (three goals) dangerous in attack.

"It's gut-wrenching, devastating, but I'm overwhelmed with pride in what we've been able to do over the last couple weeks," Swans stand-in captain Dane Rampe said.

"We didn't get that result tonight, but I felt over the last couple of weeks we took a couple of steps in the right direction towards [getting] back respect and trust of not only our fans and our club, but the AFL community in general."

Dean Cox looks emotional at the post-game press conference

C

By Chris De Silva

By Chris De Silva

A lot in this post-game chat from Dean Cox, who looks as though he might have shed some tears in the locker room with his team before getting to the podium.

"I'm extremely proud. To put on a performance like that, I'm just extremely proud of everyone uniting and galvanising to get our season to the last game, and we fell short. 

"To Freo's credit, they ramped up towards the end of the third. We were in a really good position for three and a bit quarters, but this time of the year, you can't give a team any sort of momentum. 

"They outnumbered and we blasted out of our back half and they got repeat entries and took their moments, that's what good teams too. 

"I thought our pressure on the ball carrier and the system that we played stood up. 

"I've been really proud of the ability to play the players we have all throughout the year. When they weren't playing throughout the year, they acquitted of themselves really well. 

"It is raw, but that is footy, footy is a hard game. I learnt a hell of a lot. I've got a chance to really reflect on the season we've had. 

"We want to be playing these games of footy every year, that's what we strive for. I love prelim finals. 

"We've got to learn, and I do as well, and we'll spend the off-season doing that. They've been really supportive and wanted to action everything we wanted to put in place. They've just been really hungry to improve. 

"It's hard to say it's a missed opportunity when you play a really good footy team. 

"Prelims are big games and there is a lot at stake because there is a grand final next week. 

"The older players, they certainly understand that they don't just come around every year. They're hard to get to, so that'll settle in the next few days."
