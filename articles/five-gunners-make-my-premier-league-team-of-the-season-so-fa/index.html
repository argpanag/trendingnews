Five Gunners Make My Premier League Team of the Season So Far. Who Makes Yours? - Just Arsenal News

TheTools · 16.11.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

I normally write my half-season reviews near Christmas. Yet I’ve noticed that several outlets have put together their team of the season so far during this international break.

So here’s my XI…

Goalkeeper and Defence

David Raya | GK | Arsenal

Was unrivalled last season in terms of keepers making saves so good they win points for their team. If anything, the Spaniard’s concentration this campaign has gone from strength to strength.

Including the Community Shield, the 31-year-old has 10 clean sheets so far. It’s not like the Gunner is always busy for 90 minutes, but that makes his focus even more impressive. It’s hard for a keeper to be a spectator for most of a game and then have to make a stunning save the one moment they’re called upon.

Riccardo Calafiori | LB | Arsenal

For years now, full-back has been such an attacking role in football. Yet few seem to play it like the Italian does for the champions.

The 24-year-old has the footballing intelligence not just to know when to join an Arsenal attack, but to find himself in the middle of it. That’s led to two goals and two assists so far this season. He’s one of the few Gunners who isn’t afraid to shoot from distance.

The only doubt about the defender since he moved to England has been his ability to stay fit, but Piero Hincapié allows Mikel Arteta to rotate the two every few days.

Gabriel | CB | Arsenal

Opponents this season have worked out how to defend Arsenal’s set pieces, meaning the goals have dried up for the Brazilian.

Yet the 28-year-old continues to show that there’s a skill to defending, a quality the champions don’t get enough credit for.

His centre-back partner deserves equal credit for the Gunners getting to November without really missing Saliba, but the Frenchman’s usual partner in crime has stepped up to lead the back four. Raya is protected from having to deal with too many shots on target.

Five 1–0 wins isn’t down to luck. It’s an art form.

Ezri Konsa | CB | Arsenal

Considering how inflated the UK transfer market was this summer, around £52 million for an England international is already looking like one of the bargains of the season.

The 29-year-old’s job was to use his Premiership experience to provide decent cover while Saliba rested his back. The biggest compliment to Konsa is that fans and pundits have barely mentioned the Frenchman since he’s been injured. That wouldn’t have been the case in previous years.

It couldn’t have gone better for a boyhood Gooner who has given Mikel Arteta no reason to rush Saliba back. I knew Konsa was good, but not this good. He seems to have walked straight into our dressing room and become a massive leader.

Jayden Bogle | RB | Leeds United

There haven’t been too many outstanding performers at right-back this season, but the way Daniel Farke is using Bogle has been one of the tactical tweaks of the campaign so far.

Leeds are a hard nut to crack defensively, but their right-back is one of the few players given creative licence by his manager. The 26-year-old stays out wide but makes diagonal runs into the box when Leeds attack.

For a defender, it’s insane how many touches and chances he gets in the opposition box. Only Dominic Calvert-Lewin has had more shots on target for Leeds this campaign!

Midfield and Attack

Maxim De Cuyper | LM | Brighton

Calafiori deserves to be in this team, but De Cuyper has been one of the players of the season so far. So how do I get both left-backs into this XI?

Thankfully, Fabian Hürzeler has solved that issue by putting his defender into midfield at times, such has been his attacking threat.

The Belgian has four goals and four assists this campaign, from open play as well as set pieces. That’s more goal involvements than any other Premiership defender, but very soon he might not be called one anymore.

Pascal Groß | CM | Brighton

The most romantic story of the season so far might be the form of the Seagulls midfielder.

The German returned to Brighton with the assumption that he’d offer experience on and off the pitch. Yet at 35, you could argue he’s having the best campaign of his career. At an age when his minutes need to be managed, he’s making it impossible for his manager to rest him.

It’s rare in today’s football to have a central midfielder who’s an all-rounder. Groß protects Brighton’s back four, wins tackles, scores, sets up goals, takes set pieces and is a leader.

Declan Rice | CM | Arsenal

Put your hand up if you were worried at the World Cup that the midfielder had given so much, physically and mentally, for so long that his body had broken down.

At the very least, the assumption was that he’d be eased into the season, something most Gooners agreed with.

Yet Mikel Arteta sees the player every day in training and clearly knows what the rest of us see every week. Rice’s energy levels are insane.

The 27-year-old has spoken about talking to his manager about how to add more goals to his game, but that might come down to having the right balance around him. His display at Anfield was the best individual performance of the campaign so far.

Rayan | RM | Bournemouth

There hasn’t been an outstanding right midfielder this season, and I could equally have picked any of the Cherries’ midfielders, as none of their attackers stays in one position. I selected the Brazilian because that’s the area of the pitch he starts in, although at times he’s also been a makeshift striker.

He suits Marco Rose’s approach perfectly, always harassing defenders to win back possession and shooting on sight.

He hasn’t quite translated his goalscoring form in Europe to the Premiership, but after his display at Old Trafford, there’s a feeling the 20-year-old is only going to get better.

Erling Haaland | ST | Manchester City

With 14 goals in 14 games this season, the Norwegian is still easily the best in the division at putting the ball in the back of the net.

Man City are on a loop with the 26-year-old. When they’re not winning and he’s not scoring, everyone points out how few touches their forward gets. Yet when you have a player who needs so few opportunities to score, the juice is worth the squeeze.

There’s something old-school about their striker. Invisible for most of a match, then clinical when one of his few chances comes. He’s the only man in England who can do that.

Nicolas Jackson | ST | Aston Villa

After often being mocked at Chelsea for his finishing and frustrating management with his discipline, the striker seems to have found his mojo again at Villa Park.

Has he benefited from knowing he’s first choice at Villa? Or is it because he’s working with Unai Emery again?

Or it could be both.

With nine goals and one assist in his first 14 games for his new club, this is the version of Jackson they thought they were getting at the Bridge. It remains to be seen if the 25-year-old can stay this clinical, but in terms of most improved player, he makes my XI.

Dan Smith

Five Gunners make Dan’s XI. Has he got the balance right, or would you make room for someone else? Give us your team of the season so far.
