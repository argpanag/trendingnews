Vanoli on edge, times are tough: a must-win match

Fiorentina have already reached a point in the season where they can no longer afford to wait. Their league position remains worrying, results are still not coming, and the gap between summer expectations and performances on the pitch has become increasingly clear.

The Viola need a turnaround, ideally an immediate one, to prevent an already complicated season from becoming an even tougher campaign like last season (rescued by Vanoli himself after the Pioli-Praè disaster). The match against Sassuolo must give the Viola a fresh direction, restore confidence and, above all, deliver three points that have been missing for far too long. Not just for the standings (Fiorentina would make a significant leap with a win at the Mapei), but also to lift the mood and build enthusiasm ahead of Sunday's clash with Juventus.

Vanoli on edge in press conference, defends Fagioli

Paolo Vanoli, in particular, is feeling the intensity of the situation. The coach arrived in Florence aiming to get a struggling team back on track after Grosso's spell in charge, but the time available and the results so far are making the task difficult. As reported by Il Corriere Fiorentino, the sense is that the coach is also starting to feel the pressure, and his demeanor at his latest press conference was proof of that.

Particularly when discussing Fagioli and Oulai's improving performances, the coach snapped:

Enough, let's stop talking about it. I've told you who I compare him to. He responded brilliantly, but I have to say Fagioli has been criticized too much. That's what bothers me about you. He's been the linchpin of this team; he had one bad half. Everyone needs to keep their feet on the ground. He's an important player and an international. Fagioli can do everything on the pitch, playing as a central midfielder or a deep-lying playmaker.

Vanoli knows that getting his Fiorentina back on track takes time, but the league table waits for no team. Against a Sassuolo side led by former Fiorentina man Alberto Aquilani and featuring the ever-dangerous Berardi, they will need a performance full of character, finally backed up by a win—their first since the trip to the Penzo to face Venezia (September 11, 2026). Because right now, for the Viola, winning is not just important: it has become essential if they are to change the course of their centenary season.
