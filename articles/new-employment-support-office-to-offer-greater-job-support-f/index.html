New employment support office to offer greater job support for persons with disabilities

TheTools · 04.10.2026 10:00 · source · permalink

A new Employment Support Office (ESO) will be launched to better support employment for persons with disabilities, according to the Taskforce on Assurance for Families with Persons with Disabilities.

It is part of the taskforce's recommended measures, after looking at how Singapore can better support persons with disabilities (PwDs) throughout different life stages, they said in a press conference on Friday (Oct 1).

Throughout their engagements with over 200 individuals, including PwDs, caregivers, employers and community partners, the taskforce found that many families expressed concerns over the uncertainties in their future. 

In particular, families shared their worries about the "post-18 cliff" — when PwDs graduate from schools — and whether PwDs would be able to secure and retain employment as well as live independently.

According to the Comprehensive Labour Force Survey by the Ministry of Manpower (MOM), only 34.7 per cent of PwDs aged 15 to 64 are employed. Of this group, only 24.4% of employed residents with disabilities worked for the same employer for at least one year, as of 2025.

MOM also shared that most PwDs were most commonly and consistently employed in the Community, Social & Personal Services, Food & Beverage Services and Wholesale Trade sectors.

The taskforce on Assurance for Families with Persons with Disabilities is chaired by Senior Minister of State for Social and Family Development Goh Pei Ming.

Other members include Minister of State for Health and Digital Development and Information Rahayu Mahzam, Senior Parliamentary Secretary for Social and Family Development and Law Eric Chua, Senior Parliamentary Secretary for Manpower and Finance Shawn Huang as well as Senior Parliamentary Secretary for Education and National Development Syed Harun Alhabsyi.

3 key shifts

The taskforce identified three key shifts to provide greater assurance for PwDs and their families: Planning for the future, finding and growing in a job and living well in the community.

One of the measures recommended by the taskforce is putting in place Future Care Planning support from 2027, so that PwDs could map out their plans and next steps with their families and care support planners to make informed decisions.

To help PwDs live well in the community, Rahayu shared how the government will be equipping polyclinics with the capabilities to support and better address the needs of PwDs from 2027 onwards. 

Frontline healthcare professional will be trained on how to better communicate with PwDs and a Health Communication Passport will be introduced in selected polyclinics in 2027 for persons with intellectual disabilities and autism.

One-stop job support

SG Enable will establish the ESO, which will serve as a one-stop touchpoint for all employment-related guidance and support for PwDs throughout their employment journey. 

It will facilitate access to various services including skills upgrading, career advisory and employment support services.

The ESO is slated to be fully operational by the second half of 2027, said the Ministry of Social and Family Development (MSF).

It builds on SG Enable’s existing Job Placement and Job Support (JPJS) services to support PwDs beyond job placement and navigate their longer-term career and skills development, said a SG Enable spokesperson.

The ESO will focus on providing longer job support, especially for those with higher work support needs. It will also provide continued help when workplace challenges arise.

Employers can also turn to the ESO for guidance on how to define and redesign roles when hiring PwDs.

This includes developing sector-specific playbooks, co-developed with Trade Associations and Chambers, with a step-by-step process on how to hire and integrate PwDs. The ESO will also provide employers with hands-on guidance.

The ESO is a move to reach the 40 per cent employment rate target for PwDs as part of the Enabling Masterplan 2030, an SG Enable spokesperson told AsiaOne.

New grants

MSF will also streamline and enhance existing funding support for employers with the new Enabling Employment Grant, which will be launched in the first half of 2027.

This single grant will combine and replace the three current schemes: Open Door Programme Job Redesign Grant, Open Door Programme Training Grant and Employer Development Grant.

In 2025, the three above-mentioned grants supported about 100 employers, collectively disbursing around $220,000 in total, an MSF spokesperson told AsiaOne.

The new Enabling Employment Grant will support employers with the hiring of in-house job coaches, customised in-house training for employees with disabilities, more inclusive training courses for employers and a wider range of workplace accommodations.

Being employed and being able to do a single task is just one aspect of employment.

"How the employer works with the employee, together with the co-workers, then create a conducive environment for them to gain skills [over] time and have a meaningful career," said Huang.

MSF and SG Enable will also establish a new Supported-Enterprise Enabling Grant later this year to support enterprises that employ a significant proportion of PwDs with moderate support needs.

Funding from this grant can be used to support measures such as hiring of training officer and allied professionals.

Work readiness, upskilling and wage support

The Ministry of Education (MOE) will build work readiness in PwDs' schooling years by providing more opportunities for applied learning and soft skills development.

More work exposure opportunities will be introduced for students in special education (SPED) schools that offer the national curriculum as well as for students with disabilities in Institute of Technical Education (ITE) and polytechnics.

From 2027, MOE will also offer more targeted career and planning guidance starting from SPED schooling years, as well as introduce transition support programmes to support the growing number of SPED graduates entering ITE and polytechnics.

Upskilling will become more accessible as SG Enable extends the 95% course fee grant to eligible courses delivered by training providers accredited by the Skills and Workforce Development Agency, with the first batch of expanded accredited courses expected by the first half of 2027.

The Ministry of Manpower will increase wage support for employers hiring PwDs by raising the income ceiling and increasing the quantum and duration of the temporary wage offset.

This will be done through the enhancement of the existing Enabling Employment Credit, which will be implemented from 2027.

"Ultimately, an inclusive Singapore is built upon what each of us does on a day-to-day basis in our community, in our workplaces, and in the neighbourhoods that we live in," said Chua.
