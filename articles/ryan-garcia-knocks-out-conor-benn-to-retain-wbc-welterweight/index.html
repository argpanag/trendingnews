So much for Garcia feeling at peace. The champion spots Teófimo López at ringside and immediately calls him into the ring.

“Teófimo, get that ass up here! Where you at?” Garcia shouts. “Unified time!”

López appears perfectly willing to oblige and the two begin jawing at one another from a distance. “You want to get crazy, we going to get crazy,” Garcia says. “I’ll beat your ass right now.”

Then Garcia turns his attention to Devin Haney and the possibility of fighting for the undisputed welterweight championship.

“We got to clear this shit out,” he says. “Hopefully we got to beat Devin Haney’s ass again for undisputed. Fuck the Haneys!”

Never a dull moment.

“It’s surprising that I feel at peace,” Garcia says. “In my back room, we were playing worship music the whole time, praising my Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. I got that joy and I got that peace and it feels great.”

As for the right hand that finished Benn, Garcia says injuries had prevented him from showing it consistently in the past.

“I’ve always had a right hand, but I always had injuries. So this is kind of the first two fights I had no injuries on my right hand.”

Garcia also takes a moment to address the crowd after defending his title on Mexican Independence Day weekend.

“I know some people don’t see me as Mexican because I don’t speak Spanish,” Garcia says. “But just know my love never goes away from you guys. I always love you and I’m going to always fight strong.”

Garcia, who was born and raised in California, walked to the ring draped in Mexican and American flags after entering to Vicente Fernández’s El Rey. And he’s getting another enormous reception from the crowd here at T-Mobile Arena.

Preamble

Hello and welcome to Las Vegas, where Ryan Garcia and Conor Benn will meet tonight for the WBC welterweight championship in a main event that offers a revealing snapshot of boxing in 2026.

Four years ago Benn’s career was thrown into limbo after two positive drug tests scuppered his blockbuster fight with Chris Eubank Jr. Two years ago Garcia was serving a one-year suspension after testing positive for ostarine around his career-best victory over Devin Haney, a result subsequently overturned to a no-contest. Tonight they will each collect an eight-figure payday beneath the lights of T-Mobile Arena, with reports putting the combined purse at around $30m. Few sports are better at turning yesterday’s scandal into tomorrow’s main event.

There is, beneath all the uncomfortable baggage, an intriguing fight. Garcia, 28, is making the first defense of the title he won by dropping and outpointing Mario Barrios in February, completing a remarkable turnaround after his return from suspension had ended in a flat defeat by Rolando Romero. Benn, 29, enters his first world title fight with the chance to follow his father Nigel, who held the WBC super-middleweight championship more than three decades ago.

And he arrives as a considerable underdog. Benn’s natural instinct is to come forward and make fights uncomfortable, promising this week to bring “tenacity, ferocity, spite” from the opening bell. The problem is that Garcia, for all his well-documented flaws, possesses exceptional hand speed and a vicious left hook tailor-made to punish exactly that sort of aggression. Benn’s most plausible route to an upset may therefore require him to walk directly toward Garcia’s best weapon.

Then there is the weight. Benn had not made 147lb in more than four years and described getting there as going through the “pits of hell”. Yet he came in at 146lb on Friday, his lightest in seven years, while Garcia weighed 145.5lb.

“No one’s given me a chance,” Benn said this week. “The pressure’s on Ryan completely.”

The talking, tweeting, typing and streaming are nearly over. Garcia has the belt. Benn has the famous name, the family history and very little to lose. The fighters should be making their entrances roughly an hour from now.
