S&P 500, Nasdaq edge higher ahead of midterm results; Apple falls

TheTools · 03.11.2026 16:05 · source · permalink

The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq rose on Tuesday on a further drop in crude oil prices ahead of US midterm election results, while Apple joined the list of Big Tech companies whose earnings failed to impress investors.

Apple AAPL.O fell 2% after the iPhone maker warned the cost of memory chips would continue to rise, pressuring its holiday-quarter gross margin.

Chip stocks were among top gainers, with Nvidia NVDA.O climbing 2.7% after falling for the last four sessions.

The broader Philadelphia chips index .SOX jumped 2.7%, while AMD AMD.O gained 4% ahead of its quarterly earnings due after the bell.

A drop in oil prices provided more support, as Brent crude LCOc1 fell over 1%, extending declines after a sharp drop in the last session as investors monitored developments on a peace deal between the US and Iran.

US President Donald Trump said he made a deal with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and that Washington would no longer be providing military aid to Israel after the end of this year.

At 9:57 a.m. ET, the Dow Jones Industrial Average .DJI fell 256.52 points, or 0.49%, to 51,727.46, the S&P 500 .SPX gained 21.45 points, or 0.29%, to 7,563.40 and the Nasdaq Composite .IXIC gained 155.39 points, or 0.62%, to 25,319.75.

On the downside, software stocks were among top decliners, with Gartner IT.N dropping 17.2% and US-listed shares of Thomson Reuters TRI.O and Shopify SHOP.O sliding about 10% each after all three reported quarterly earnings.

Gains in technology stocks offset wider weakness, as seven of the 11 major S&P 500 sectors were trading lower. Consumer staples .SPLRCS and materials .SPLRCM were the laggards.

AI-powered analytics software provider Palantir Technologies PLTR.O forecast revenue above estimates for the last three months of the year after topping third-quarter expectations. Its shares, however, fell 4.9%.

The Nasdaq-100 .NDX closed more than 10% below its all-time closing high on Monday, confirming that the tech-heavy index has entered a correction as investors grow wary of Big Tech's soaring AI spending and little evidence of adequate returns.

Midterms results awaited

American voters will deliver a verdict on the first two years of President Donald Trump's second term in Tuesday's midterm elections, with polls and nonpartisan analysts favoring Democrats to wrest the House from Republicans, while control of the Senate remains uncertain.

"A divided government seems to be very good for markets because no new legislation can get put through," said Kim Forrest, chief investment officer at Bokeh Capital Partners.

Morgan Stanley analysts said in a note earlier this week that the elections should help remove a major market overhang. They believe the US market correction that began in June is in its "final innings".

Another risk factor could be the US renewing attacks on Iran after the midterms, something that Trump has hinted at in recent weeks. J.P.Morgan analysts said in a note earlier this week that they view any dip in the markets due to geopolitical tensions as a buying opportunity.

However, when asked by reporters on Monday if he was considering striking Iran after the midterms, Trump ​said, "No."

Declining issues outnumbered advancers by a 1.19-to-1 ratio on the NYSE, while advancing issues outnumbered decliners by a 1.43-to-1 ratio on the Nasdaq.

The S&P 500 posted 3 new 52-week highs and no new lows, while the Nasdaq Composite recorded 19 new highs and 104 new lows.
