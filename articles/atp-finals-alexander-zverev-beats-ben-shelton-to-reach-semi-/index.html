ATP Finals: Alexander Zverev beats Ben Shelton to reach semi-finals

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:13 · source · permalink

Alexander Zverev has won a tour-leading 67 matches this season

  • Published
    17 November 2026

World number one Alexander Zverev produced another serving masterclass against Ben Shelton to qualify for the ATP Finals knockout stage with a match to spare.

The German, 29, lost just two of the 31 points played on his serve in the second set as he completed an impressive 6-2 7-6 (7-4) win over fourth-ranked Shelton.

The two-time champion only made 10 unforced errors and landed 25 winners in a ruthless display to reach the semi-finals at the season-ending tournament for a fifth time.

The result confirmed Rafael Jodar's elimination, following the Spaniard's earlier 4-6 6-2 6-2 loss to French rising star Arthur Fils in the John McEnroe Group.

Zverev demonstrated the superb level which has taken him to the number one ranking and his first two Grand Slam titles in 2026.

He always appeared in control as he extended his unbeaten run against Shelton to seven matches - including victory in September's US Open final.

Having lost his first match as world number one at the Paris Masters in November, Zverev has backed up his status in Turin with two sweeping victories.

Just as in his victory over Jodar in his opening match, Zverev showed remarkable consistency on serve to starve the American of opportunities.

Shelton, who has risen to a career-high fourth in the world after winning four titles and reaching his first major final in 2026, managed to create just two break points in the match.

They came in the first set, when he already trailed by a break after Zverev struck in the opening game.

Shelton avoided falling a double break behind by twice saving a break point with an ace, but was quickly punished for his failure to take either of his chances in the following game as Zverev raced away with the set.

Zverev, who also won this year's French Open, maintained his grip on the match by winning each of the first 23 points on his serve in the second set.

Shelton managed to gain a foothold, and denied Zverev the chance to serve for the match by recovering from 15-40 down in the ninth game.

But, while the American reclaimed the first mini-break of the tie-break with a massive return, Zverev remained composed and fired in a wonderful on-the-run forehand winner to complete victory in stunning style.

With it, Zverev secured back-to-back wins against top-10 players for the first time since the 2024 ATP Finals before he faces Fils in the final group match.

Rising star Fils earns first ATP Finals win

Frenchman Fils claimed his first career win at the ATP Finals by battling to a comeback victory over Jodar which kept him in contention for a semi-final place.

In beating Jodar, 20, the 22-year-old Fils became the first Frenchman to record an ATP Finals victory since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2011.

This was the youngest match by combined age at the season-ending tournament - which features the eight top-performing men - since a 21-year-old Novak Djokovic defeated a 20-year-old Juan Martin del Potro in 2008.

It was Fils, who has risen to a career-high ranking of seventh in the world during a season in which he triumphed at the ATP Masters 1000 in Cincinnati, who came out on top in a meeting between two of the men's game's most exciting rising stars.

Arthur Fils is the first man born in 2004 or later to win a match at the ATP Finals

Initially second-best as he struggled for rhythm - particularly on serve - in the opening set, Fils reset impressively to dominate sets two and three against an opponent who appeared to be fading physically.

A finalist at the Next Gen ATP Finals three years ago, Fils will fight for a place in the semi-finals against the already-qualified Zverev on Thursday, hoping to edge Shelton to a place in the knockout stage.

"It's going to be tough but I know that I can battle. I'm not afraid," said Fils of his showdown with Zverev, who plays Shelton later on Tuesday.

"It will be a nice match against the world number one. I don't have anything to lose, I'm just going to enjoy myself."

World number two Carlos Alcaraz plays Daniil Medvedev, while Flavio Cobolli meets Frances Tiafoe in the second round of Ivan Lendl Group matches on Wednesday.

In the men's doubles, Britain's Henry Patten and Finnish team-mate Harri Heliovaara fell to defeat in their second match.

The world number ones and defending champions won the first set but were beaten 6-7 (5-7) 6-3 10-4 by fourth seeds Marcelo Arevalo and Mate Pavic.
