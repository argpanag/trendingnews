Meet Dacia's remixed Renault Twingo

TheTools · 08.09.2026 10:00 · source · permalink

► New Dacia Spring electric city car revealed
► Uses tech and platform from new Twingo
► Will be priced from under £18,000 in the UK

Dacia has unveiled its new Spring electric city car, completely overhauling its smallest EV with a little (or a lot of) help from Renault. ‘We only kept one thing from the previous Spring,’ says Dacia’s design director, David Durand. ‘The name.’

This new electric city car represents the start of a new era for Dacia, with the brand aiming to unveil three more EVs after this one by 2030. Makes sense to start at the beginning of the range, right? Read on for your full debrief.

It definitely LOOKS completely different…

Should we be calling this the Springo? Anti-Twingo? Either way, Dacia’s latest new small electric car naturally shares a lot with Renault’s cutesy new Twingo EV (and the forthcoming Nissan Wave), with Durand telling CAR the point of the Spring’s new look is to ‘be the total opposite.’

‘How do we make it the opposite? It’s the proportions, the trunk at the rear, the choices we made for the interior. It’s about how we can invent something more serious and with different positioning but be as exciting as our big brother Renault,’ he says.

Hence why this new Spring has been deliberately designed with a much more ‘robust’ and chunky general look, with blocky bumpers and tough panelling all around. The front-end plays with Dacia’s ‘link’ branding, with horizontal DRLs that, to our eye, make it look a little like a Vauxhall. Whoops.

That robust and sober new look unfortunately extends to the Spring’s colour palette, with this ‘Agave’ colour being offered alongside a black, silver and white – no primary colour pops here. Durand admits that some of this is due to having to stick to ‘existing or already developed colours’ for getting production going, but he assures us that ‘we will come very quickly with a new colour to extend the palette, and we’ll choose one that is a little more flashy.’

The Spring’s overall stance is much more balanced than its predecessor, being both 15cm longer and 18cm wider than the old one. This’ll surely help with handling, which will be a positive step for Dacia given we don’t necessarily rate the old Spring but admire the Twingo’s handling very much.

What’s the interior like?

Naturally, a good amount of the car’s overall structure is shared with Renault – but Dacia has made some efforts to make it feel different.

As well as a Dacia steering wheel and screen running its own super-simple operating system, the brand’s YouClip attachments enable a bunch of accessories to be attached at certain points. It’s functional inside and spacious to sit in, with just the right amount of technology required and nothing else.

Dacia has elected not to include the Twingo’s sliding rear seats, though, with a fixed bench in the second row allowing just enough room for adults to sit in for short journeys.

Boot space, meanwhile, is a pretty decent 337 litres – enabled by a split boot floor with a bonus load area below. Dacia also claims the Spring is the only electric city car to be available with a (optional) frunk that boosts space and provides somewhere neat for your charging cable.

Can I have some performance specs, please?

Yes. The Dacia Spring runs on the RGEV Small platform (this was once named AmpR Small, before Renault Group’s electric-focused Ampere spin-off was brought back into the fold at the start of 2026) and uses the same 27.5kWh LFP battery pack. A single 80bhp electric motor powers the front wheels, propelling the Spring to 62mph in a leisurely 12.1 seconds and on to a top speed of 81mph.

Dacia claims the Spring offers a maximum driving range of 155 miles and comes as standard with a 6.6kW AC charger for a 15-80 per cent charge in nine hours. An optional ‘charge pack’ boosts AC charging to 11kW and enables 50kW DC charging, speeding things up to a 28-minute charge. And, just like the last Spring, this one comes with vehicle-to-load functionality, allowing you to power devices directly from the car.

How much will the new Spring cost?

Dacia in the UK says the new Spring will be priced from under £18,000 with two trims – Expression and Journey – being your only choice at the point of order. Journey trim adds larger 16-inch wheels, keyless entry, a rear camera and a larger infotainment system that includes in-built nav.

UK prices will be clarified closer to the car’s launch later in 2026, when Dacia finalises how much of the government grant can be applied.
