International matches on cards as oval upgrade unveiled - News | InDaily, Inside South Australia

TheTools · 16.11.2026 01:43 · source · permalink

An SA sporting facility’s $10 million seating and lights upgrade is match ready for a new summer innings.

A $10 million upgrade to Karen Rolton Oval in the western parklands is completed with improved lighting, and more seating for spectators watching sport including women’s cricket.

The revamp was jointly funded by the state government and the South Australian Cricket Association, with each contributing $5 million towards turning the oval into a “world class sporting precinct”, with hopes for international cricket matches to be hosted at the venue.

It includes the installation of new 1500 lux lights to meet the new Australian standard for domestic cricket, while the former 750 lux lights have been relocated to Oval 2, providing an additional lit oval for both cricket and community sport.

The upgrade was expected to make the site that is home to elite women’s cricket matches more accessible for spectators by removing the steep and inaccessible eastern embankment and replacing it with new terraces and seating.

SACA chairman and president Will Rayner said the “world-class” upgrades could attract international cricket to Karen Rolton Oval.

It follows the rescheduling of a planned international women’s T20 match between Australia and New Zealand that was set to be hosted at Karen Rolton Oval in March next year.

“We’re working with all the powers that be, be it Cricket Australia or the International Cricket Council, to get as much quality cricket here into South Australia,” Rayner said.

The upgrade was completed in time for Tuesday night’s Women’s Big Bash League game between the Adelaide Strikers and Sydney Sixers at Karen Rolton Oval.

“This investment in Karen Rolton Oval is a major step forward for women’s cricket in South Australia — not only for the professional game, but for community and junior cricket,” Rayner said.

“The upgraded lights will enhance night matches and lift the experience for players, fans and broadcasters alike, while ensuring this venue serves the next generation of cricketers.”

Outside of women’s cricket, Karen Rolton Oval has also hosted the men’s Sheffield Shield final in 2025 that saw the SA Redbacks break a 29-year drought by defeating Queensland.

The oval will also play host to multiple One Day Cup matches this season, and support local football matches and community events.

Rhiannon Pearce said the upgrade was an “incredible milestone” and would be central to growing women and girls’ participation in cricket across the state.

“The new terracing and lighting creates a better experience for spectators and has come just in time for what will be a great summer of women’s cricket here in Adelaide,” Pearce said.

“With women’s cricket continuing to grow at an extraordinary pace, it’s fundamental we have appropriate facilities to cater for this increase in participation.”

Women’s participation rates in cricket have steadily increased with about 91,000 Australian women participating in cricket last year, according to Australian Sports Commission.

The completed upgrade follows the announcement of a $25 million redevelopment to West Adelaide Football Club after facilities were damaged by storms and flooding earlier this year.
