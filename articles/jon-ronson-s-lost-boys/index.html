Jon Ronson's lost boys

TheTools · 02.09.2026 16:08 · source · permalink

The gonzo journalist’s quest to explain the crisis of masculinity offers plenty of diversion, but don’t expect coherence

By Rachel Cunliffe

At what point, I wonder as I turn the final page of The Castle, did the gonzo journalist Jon Ronson decide what his latest book was  actually going to be about? Was it when he found himself in the nightclub-style basement of a mysterious neo-gothic fortress in rural Connecticut full of princess gowns and tiaras with his foot being bitten by a large dog called Cerberus? Was it watching countless YouTube videos of children dressed as babies having food fights, or Canadian adults dressed as Disney characters like Elsa acting out deranged scenarios? Was it realising he was missing his wedding anniversary by dashing off last minute to Nantucket to investigate a boating accident from 15 years ago that might or might not have something to do with the rest of what we shall loosely term for these purposes “the story”?

Ostensibly, the answer is the flurry of late-night texts Ronson received in June 2023 from his son after he and his friends were lured to a “party” at the book’s eponymous castle. The text stream ends: “We left. Was completely fucked. All good now.” Whether down to basic parental instinct or Ronson’s nose for a good story, soon he is off on a quest to meet the mysterious castle owner and find out why exactly he was trying to enlist young strangers from the internet to be part of a speculative reality TV game show called “101 Princesses”.
