Failed asylum seekers will be deported without notice

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:53 · source · permalink

Migrants who have exhausted appeals process to be stripped of five-day grace period before they are expelled from UK

Rejected asylum seekers are set to be deported without notice.

Shabana Mahmood, the Home Secretary, is to change the law to scrap the five-day grace period before migrants are removed from the UK after all their appeals have been rejected.

Failed asylum seekers were previously able to use that time to lodge further claims in an attempt to delay their deportation, but will now be expelled within hours.

Currently, most enforced deportations can only legally take place after the minimum five-day notice period has expired.

The changes are to be proposed in an amendment to the Government’s Immigration and Asylum Bill, which returns to the Commons on Tuesday.

The reforms mirror the system adopted by Denmark, which Ms Mahmood visited this year and has been seen as a model for the UK’s asylum reforms.

When Denmark introduced a similar reform, the number of migrants awaiting removal was cut from more than 2,185 cases at the end of 2020 to under 1,000 at the end of 2025.

The change comes on top of measures which force migrants to raise any grounds for appeal at the start of the asylum process to prevent them from continuing to raise different claims to thwart their removal.

Any claims raised after the start must be based on a material change of circumstance, such as a country of origin becoming unsafe because of an unexpected regime change.

Immigration enforcement teams will also be given a six-month period to remove failed asylum seekers before the Home Office will be required to issue another Removals Notice.

Currently, Home Office teams have 21 days to deport a failed asylum seeker if the flight is grounded because someone is disruptive or because of bad weather.

Anna Turley, the border security and asylum minister, said: “This Government is ramping up removals of those with no right to be here, with over 80,000 illegal migrants and foreign nationals returned since the general election.

“Now we are ensuring those migrants who have exhausted their right to appeal can be removed promptly, rather than being given an opportunity to frustrate their legitimate removal from this country.”

Tories and Reform attack plans

Other reforms in the bill also enable late claims to be fast-tracked under the Independent Immigration Appeals Authority, leading to swifter removals of those with no right to remain in the UK.

The Tories and Reform UK attacked the plans, saying only leaving the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) would pave the way for immediate deportations and taking back full control of Britain’s borders.

Chris Philp, the shadow home secretary, said: “Failed claimants should be on the first plane home.

“As long as Labour keep Britain in the ECHR, removals will keep being dragged through the courts, leaving taxpayers to keep paying for the accommodation while they wait.

“The Conservatives will take the UK out of the ECHR, so that illegal immigrants can be deported as soon as possible.”

Zia Yusuf, Reform UK’s home affairs spokesman, said: “Britain is being invaded and this has been facilitated by Tory and Labour governments.

“The only way to reverse this invasion is to leave the ECHR and then embark on the biggest mass deportation programme Britain has ever seen.

“Reform UK will detain and deport all illegal migrants the Tories and Labour allowed in.”
