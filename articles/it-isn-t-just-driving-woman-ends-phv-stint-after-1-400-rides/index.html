'It isn't just driving': Woman ends PHV stint after 1,400 rides, says experience changed how she sees drivers

TheTools · 09.09.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

After more than 1,400 rides, a private-hire vehicle (PHV) driver has officially ended her stint.

The driver, Michelle Lee, shared her experience in a Facebook post on Tuesday (Sept 8), reflecting on the unexpected lessons she picked up from spending hours behind the wheel and speaking to strangers.

The Tesla, which she had used for her PHV work, is now "officially no longer a PHV" after she removed the vehicle's decals.

Lee said she initially started driving simply because she wanted to try something "completely different".

But what began as an experiment eventually gave her a new appreciation for the work that PHV drivers do every day.

One thing that stood out for her was how quickly some passengers made assumptions about her.

She recalled passengers getting into her Tesla and asking how much she paid to rent the car.

"Why are you driving PHV?" was a question she said she has heard often.

Lee acknowledged that most passengers were likely asking out of genuine curiosity, but said the encounters made her reflect on how people sometimes associate certain jobs with a person's financial situation or social status.

"Whether your driver owns the car, rents it, is struggling financially or simply chooses to drive, that person deserves the same respect," she wrote.

Physically, mentally exhausted

She said she typically drove for only three to four hours a day, but even that left her physically and mentally exhausted.

She described having to constantly monitor the road, surrounding vehicles, motorcycles, pedestrians, navigation, pick-up locations and passengers —all at the same time.

Her experience made her think about drivers who spend eight, 10 or even 12 hours on the road to make a living.

"After experiencing a small fraction of that, I don't think I can ever casually say, 'Aiyah, just driving only.'"

"It isn't 'just driving'," she wrote, describing the job as requiring hours of concentration.

She also encountered difficult passengers, such as those who refused to wear seatbelts, people who asked her to stop illegally because they were running late, and those who expected her to wait for them.

But Lee said she did not want these experiences to define her time as a PHV driver.

Instead, what she remembers most are the conversations, saying she realised that "everyone is carrying something".

Passengers opened up to her about their jobs, families, relationships, financial struggles, illnesses, dreams and regrets.

Some conversations lasted only 15 or 20 minutes, but remained with her even after the rides ended.

There were plenty of kind passengers too, she said.

Moving on to a new chapter

Eventually, Lee decided it was time to stop driving.

With her PHV decals finally removed, Lee said she realised they represented a chapter of her life that she had never planned for but was grateful for the experience.

"I got a front-row seat to humanity."

She now plans to spend more time on money coaching through her project, Better Money EQ, and creating content around having a healthier relationship with money.

AsiaOne reached out to Lee for more information.
