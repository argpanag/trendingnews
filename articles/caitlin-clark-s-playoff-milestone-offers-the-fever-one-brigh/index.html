Caitlin Clark’s playoff milestone offers the Fever one bright spot before must-win Game 2 | Sporting News

TheTools · 29.09.2026 11:37 · source · permalink

Rodney Knuppel

John Jones-Imagn Images

The Indiana Fever had little reason to celebrate after Sunday’s 102-85 loss to the Las Vegas Aces. A’ja Wilson scored 38 points, the Aces shot 59.4 percent from the field, and Indiana left Las Vegas one defeat from elimination.

Caitlin Clark still gave the Fever something to carry into Game 2. She finished with 15 points and 10 assists, with the assists ranking second in a postseason game in franchise history, according to an Indiana team announcement.

It was a milestone hidden inside a difficult night. Clark shot 6-of-17 from the floor and committed six turnovers, but she also created scoring chances while Las Vegas repeatedly made Indiana work for clean looks.

Ten assists tell only part of the story

An assist total can make an offense look healthier than it felt. Clark found teammates often enough to reach double figures, yet the Fever never found a sustained answer for the Aces’ defense or their own mistakes.

Indiana committed 16 turnovers. Las Vegas turned those giveaways into 28 points, a number coach Stephanie White singled out after the game. Those possessions prevented the Fever from building momentum and gave the Aces opportunities before Indiana’s defense could get set.

Aliyah Boston scored a team-high 18 points, while Kelsey Mitchell went 4-of-16 for 12. Clark and Mitchell have powered the Fever’s backcourt all season, but Indiana needs both players to find a better rhythm when the series moves home.

The milestone matters because it shows Clark can still create for others against an elite opponent. The concern is how hard the Aces made every possession around those assists.

A record worth more if Indiana keeps playing

Game 2 is Tuesday at Gainbridge Fieldhouse. The best-of-three series ends if Las Vegas wins again, leaving the Fever with no time to treat Sunday as a learning experience.

Clark does not need to chase another record. Indiana needs her passing to lead to easier shots, fewer rushed possessions and a more balanced attack. Boston’s interior scoring gives the Fever one option; Mitchell’s shooting could give them another if she finds space.

The Aces have already shown what happens when Indiana’s offense stalls. Clark’s 10 assists are impressive. Turning that playmaking into a win is the challenge waiting at home.
