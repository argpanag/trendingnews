Eagles News: Patriots writer says A.J. Brown trade “keeps looking worse by the day”

TheTools · 16.11.2026 14:57 · source · permalink
  • Philadelphia Eagles News

Philadelphia Eagles news and links for 11/16/26.

Welcome to The Linc, the daily Philadelphia Eagles links roundup at Bleeding Green Nation!

A.J. Brown looked really good for the Patriots against the Eagles in training camp. But he’s been pretty bad since getting banged up in Week 1. He hasn’t been able to get healthy and Pats fans seem to be wondering if he might be cooked at this point. Brown’s 26 targets this season have resulted in just 11 receptions for 128 yards (11.6 average). As a reminder, the Eagles will receive the Patriots’ 2027 fifth-round pick (which will become increasingly favorable for Philly as New England continues to lose) and their 2028 first-round pick from the Brown trade.

Let’s get to the Philadelphia Eagles news and links …

10 things we learned from Patriots vs. Lions - Pats Pulpit

The A.J. Brown trade keeps looking worse by the day. A.J. Brown’s inability to do anything positive this year has been almost laughable. He got hurt in the first game, missed a good chunk of time on injured reserve, came back, was a non-factor, and then got hurt again. He returned on Sunday, was unable to get open and had a drop as well. He finally made a nice catch-and-run to move the stick on a third down, and then promptly went to the locker room with a hamstring injury. It’s been a rough year for Brown, and the Patriots front office: they effectively bid against themselves to trade for him and still gave up a first-round pick in 2028 to do so. Unless he somehow manages a complete 180, the trade is going to go down as a massive failure, and potentially hamstring the Patriots for years to come.

Howie Roseman seen chatting up one of the top quarterback draft prospects - BGN

The Philadelphia Eagles are on their bye this weekend and Howie Roseman is spending part of the team’s time off by attending Saturday’s college football game between Duke and the University of Miami. This isn’t necessarily a formal scouting trip for Roseman; his son, Jake, attends Duke and contributes to the Blue Devils’ football program. Eagles rookie progress reports, midway through the season - PhillyVoice

There have been a lot of excuses made for Makai Lemon’s low impact rookie season, some of which are valid, in my opinion, but some of which are not. Certainly, he is playing in a non-functional offense getting targets from Tanner McKee and Joe Milton. That’s valid. However, I’ve seen a number of Eagles fans cite a stat from PFF that says that Lemon has a high “separation percentage.” If you’ve seen this stat and have been encouraged by it, I hate to yuck your yum but there may not be a more useless metric in the NFL statistical zeitgeist. Here were PFF’s “separation percentage” leaders in 2025. It’s a “who’s who” of low impact receivers. I mean, JAHAN DOTSON (!) was in the top 10, lol. […] Lemon is not Jalen Reagor. But he is also not Amon-Ra St. Brown. If his drop issues are just a short-term rookie issue, then he can grow into a useful, gritty chain mover / frisky blocker from the slot in an efficient offense. However, I just don’t see a lot of explosiveness, and I do think his ceiling is capped by the combination of that and his lack of size.

Examining Howie Roseman’s Eagles bye week: From trading Jordan Davis to watching Miami-Duke - The Athletic

Moro Ojomo hasn’t shown it with sacks this season — and the sacks need to come — but the advanced pass-rushing data is encouraging. He leads the Eagles with 16 hurries and is tied for a team-high with 21 pressures. His pressure rate of 9.6 percent is down from 11.6 percent last season and 12.2 percent in 2024, but it’s still a strong rate for an interior rusher. His pressure rate and quarterback hit rate of 2.3 percent are both better than Carter and Davis. He also has the type of football character and work ethic worthy of an investment, and would fit on even fronts if the Eagles changed defensive schemes. The Eagles lost a respected locker-room figure with Davis’ departure, but I’d feel comfortable entrusting Ojomo with a bigger presence. He’s already called “Coach Mo” among the defensive linemen. The Eagles will have to pay a high price to keep him. It’s the type of move Roseman should feel comfortable making. It might even take off some of the sting of losing Milton Williams two years ago.

NFL playoff chance tiers: Where all 32 teams fit in 2026 postseason picture - ESPN

Tier 8: Just rest up for 2027. Philadelphia Eagles (2-7). Chances to make the playoffs: 0.3%. Fret not, Eagles fans, because if there’s one thing Philadelphia does better than any other team in the league, it’s taking advantage of the opportunity a losing season provides. Recognizing that they had virtually no shot at the playoffs with quarterback Jalen Hurts out for the season, the Eagles made some amazing pre-deadline trades, somehow getting a second-round pick (plus a Day 3 pick swap!) for center Cam Jurgens and first- and second-round picks for Jordan Davis. So sure, 2026 is effectively over for the Eagles, but they set themselves up so well for 2027 and beyond.

Steelers stock report after 38-18 loss to the Bengals - Behind The Steel Curtain

Checking in on the Eagles’ Week 11 opponent.

Offensive Firepower. Aaron Rodgers started this game 0/7 passing. Admittedly, it wasn’t all his fault, which is why we will talk about the entire offense here. The Steelers’ passing game is incredibly limited, and not really built to play against teams like Cincinnati that can light up the scoreboard. Their receivers struggle to separate, and that won’t get better over the course of their last eight games. The Bengals were more than prepared for the Steelers’ run game, which they didn’t even try to establish until they were already down multiple scores. From the quarterback to the playmakers to the coaching, something is going to need to change drastically to take this team seriously against any quality competition.

Giants vs. Jaguars odds: Opening lines for Week 11 matchup - Big Blue View

The New York Giants will open Week 11 as 6.5-point underdogs at home against the Jacksonville Jaguars, per FanDuel. This game has been penciled in as a loss for the Giants for weeks now, and it shouldn’t be much of a surprise to anyone paying attention that the 5-3-1 Giants are (nearly) touchdown underdogs to the 7-2 Jaguars.
