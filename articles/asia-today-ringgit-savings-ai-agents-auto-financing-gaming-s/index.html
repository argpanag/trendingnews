Asia Today: Ringgit Savings, AI Agents, Auto Financing & Gaming Stocks

TheTools · 26.10.2026 16:45 · source · permalink

Asia’s business and technology sectors continue to see major developments across financial services, artificial intelligence, automotive financing, vehicle manufacturing and entertainment.

From OCBC’s launch of Malaysian ringgit savings accounts for Singapore customers to Nvidia and VAST Data’s collaboration on secure AI agent infrastructure, companies are expanding services and investing in emerging technologies.

Meanwhile, Singapore-based automotive marketplace Carro has secured a $352 million loan facility, while a Japanese startup is exploring AI to help automakers match China’s vehicle development pace. Bandai Namco’s stock performance also reflects investor debate over AI’s impact on established entertainment franchises.

These developments highlight the region’s evolving business landscape, growing AI adoption and changing investment priorities.

Singapore-based bank OCBC is introducing Malaysian ringgit savings options for customers in Singapore, giving them greater flexibility when managing money for travel and spending across the border. The move is aimed at customers who regularly visit Malaysia and want to hold ringgit without relying entirely on currency exchanges during their trips.

Nvidia and VAST Data are working together to help businesses deploy AI agents in controlled and secure environments. The initiative combines AI infrastructure with security tools designed to manage how autonomous systems access data and perform tasks. The partnership aims to address growing concerns about data protection and the risks associated with increasingly capable AI agents.

Singapore-based automotive marketplace Carro has secured a $352 million auto loan facility to support its vehicle financing operations. The funding is expected to strengthen its ability to finance automotive transactions as the company continues to expand its digital marketplace and related financial services. The deal reflects the importance of financing in supporting vehicle sales across Southeast Asia.

A Japanese startup is promoting artificial intelligence tools to help automakers accelerate vehicle development and compete with Chinese manufacturers. China's automotive industry has gained ground through faster product development and the integration of digital engineering into manufacturing. Japanese companies are increasingly exploring AI to reduce development timelines, improve efficiency and respond to changing competition in the global automotive market.

Japanese entertainment company Bandai Namco, known for its Gundam franchise and video games, is attracting investor attention as markets assess AI's potential impact on the entertainment industry. The debate centres on whether established intellectual property and creative franchises can retain their value as AI changes how digital content is produced. The company's stock performance reflects broader investor questions about the future of established entertainment businesses.
