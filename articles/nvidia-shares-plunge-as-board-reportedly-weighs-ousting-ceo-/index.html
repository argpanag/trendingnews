Nvidia shares plunge as board reportedly weighs ousting CEO Jensen Huang - follow live

The board of tech giant Nvidia is considering whether to oust its CEO Jensen Huang, the BBC's US partner CBS News and other US media report

Stocks in the firm plummeted again after the New York Times reported that Huang had a second affair with an executive at his own company

On Sunday, Huang admitted an affair with a woman, reportedly from another company - she was also allegedly a Chinese spy

Nvidia is already investigating the affair Huang has admitted. The FBI is also looking into it. What does Nvidia's code of conduct say?

  1. Singapore firm linked to suspected spy says it will work 'transparently' with US governmentpublished at 09:10 GMT
    09:10 GMT

    Tessa Wong
    Asia Digital Reporter, Singapore

    Megaspeed, the Singaporean company associated with suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang, has issued a statement days after Nvidia launched an investigation into Jensen Huang’s affair earlier this week.

    Its chief executive, Tan Yong Pong, also known as James Tan, said the company "takes its compliance obligations under US export control laws extremely seriously" and is "committed to working constructively and transparently with the US government".

    He said the company had co-operated with US Commerce Department inspections.

    The company says it has engaged an unnamed "leading US law firm" to conduct a census of its graphics processing units and verify its compliance practices.

    The statement does not address allegations that Alice Huang is a Chinese spy. The BBC has previously sent queries to Megaspeed.

    Megaspeed is a Singapore-based company that runs data centres across South East Asia and is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in the region.

    It is suspected of allowing Chinese companies backdoor access to these chips, which the firms are not allowed to obtain under US export curbs.

    Megaspeed was at one point run by Alice Huang, before she transferred ownership to Tan.

  2. Pressure from Congress mounts on Jensen Huangpublished at 09:00 GMT
    09:00 GMT

    Shaimaa Khalil
    North America correspondent

    Three Republican committee chairmen - Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford - have requested a closed-door briefing from Jensen Huang on 18 November.

    Wednesday was his deadline to accept or propose another date.

    They want answers about his interactions with Alice Huang, an alleged Chinese intelligence asset, and Nvidia’s safeguards against foreign espionage. China denies the spying allegations.

    Moolenaar has warned that a subpoena compelling Jensen Huang to appear would likely follow if he did not agree to the briefing or offer an alternative date.

    There are also calls for him to go. Republican Senator Kevin Cramer told CNBC: “As a matter of his own conscience, he should resign.”

    At the heart of lawmakers’ concerns is whether sensitive US technology or information was put at risk.

    When it announced its investigation on Sunday, Nvidia’s board said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised - but that was before today’s developments.

  3. Brokerages downgrading Nvidia in rare movepublished at 08:36 GMT
    08:36 GMT

    Archana Shukla
    Asia business reporter

    Top investment firms have cut their ratings on Nvidia as governance concerns grow over the potential ousting of chief executive Jensen Huang.

    Financial firms Baird and Bernstein Research have both downgraded their outlook for Nvidia’s stock, while Morgan Stanley has removed it from its Top Pick list.

    Bernstein’s lead analyst, Stacy Rasgon, has halved his price target for the stock. “Our gut tells us that we probably haven't yet fully probed the depths of what might be found here,” he wrote.

    It’s a rare development. For more than four years, Wall Street has been almost united in backing the chip giant.

    Analysts also warn that potential curbs on chip sales to China and data centres in South East Asia could hurt near-term sales.

    A downgrade tells investors to be cautious because of looming risks. Most brokerages still rate Nvidia a buy.

    Since the scandal broke, Nvidia has lost nearly half a trillion dollars in market value.

  4. This is playing out like a Shakespearean tragedy, says analystpublished at 08:10 GMT
    08:10 GMT

    Jensen Huang's potential departure from Nvidia is playing out like a Greek or Shakespearean tragedy, Bob O'Donnell, the chief analyst of Technalysis Research, tells the BBC News channel.

    Any departure would raise questions about who could take over - and how they would change the company in the coming years, O'Donnell says.

    "It's hard to believe," he says. "[Huang] is bigger than life... a big visionary."

    Nvidia has "a consequential crisis of governance" and it will need to decide whether Huang staying is worth more than distrust among stakeholders, adds R Ray Wang from Constellation Research.

    The scandal isn't so much a crisis for the AI industry as it is for Nvidia, he says, which has to consider whether Huang's possible replacement would have his ability to bring the same level of partnerships.

    "That's really what the issue is... because a lot of these partnerships and investments have been made because of the trust with Jensen."

  5. US should 'stop fabricating' espionage allegations: Chinese foreign ministrypublished at 07:42 GMT
    07:42 GMT

    At the daily press conference held in Beijing by China's foreign affairs ministry, the BBC's Laura Bicker asked about the impact of the Nvidia case.

    "China stands ready to work with the US to fully implement the important common understandings reached between the two heads of state and promote the building of a constructive and strategically stable China-US relationship based on respect, fairness and reciprocity."

  7. What is 'key-person risk'?published at 06:32 GMT
    06:32 GMT

    Shaimaa Khalil
    North America correspondent in Los Angeles

    Jensen Huang helped shape the AI boom. His decisions have consequences for businesses, people’s investments around the world and government policy.

    That’s what investors mean by “key-person risk”: when so much depends on one individual.

    There’s no publicly identified successor. There may be a confidential plan, but there isn’t an obvious next person everyone expects to take over.

    So if he goes, investors will want answers quickly: who replaces him, how much changes, and can Nvidia keep delivering on its plans?

  9. Nvidia shares plunge as alleged affair rattles investorspublished at 05:29 GMT
    05:29 GMT

    Osmond Chia
    Business reporter

    Shares in Nvidia have plunged since news that the firm's chief executive Jensen Huang was suspected of having an affair with another woman.

    The firm's shares fell by 8% in after hours trading on Wednesday, continuing a sell-off that has seen more than $500bn (£376bn) in its stock market value wiped out since Friday.

    Nvidia shares have been highly coveted due to the firm's dominance in making the chips that have powered the global AI boom.

    Worth around $4.2tn, Nvidia is the second-highest valued firm by market value after electronics giant Apple.

  10. Huang is a 'very charismatic' CEO, business professor sayspublished at 05:04 GMT
    05:04 GMT

    Lily Jamali
    North America Technology Correspondent

    It was clear on Sunday that Nvidia's board was not inclined to push him out. Now, they may have no choice to place him on leave, at the very least.

    Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has helmed the firm for more than three decades.

    "The problem with founder-managed companies tends to be that the boards almost behave as though they are boards for private companies or family firms," said Prof Shivaram Rajgopal of Columbia Business School on Tuesday.

    "There's always some psychological debt to the person who appoints you. In this case, of course, it's the very charismatic CEO," he continued.

    Four of the ten members of the board, excluding Huang, have been in place almost continuously since the 1990s.

    "Maybe this is a chance to show that they are independent," Rajgopal concluded.

  18. How might the scandal impact the stock market?published at 02:23 GMT

  19. A timeline of the Jensen Huang scandalpublished at 02:08 GMT
    02:08 GMT

    Nvidia has endured a tumultuous week, as AI boss Jensen Huang became embroiled in scandal. Here is how it unfolded:

    8 November: Huang admits to an extramarital affair in an email apology to Nvidia employees.

    “I owe my family and all of you an apology,” Huang tells his company. “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.”

    His admission follows news reports that the US is investigating a suspected Chinese spy who had maintained a close relationship with the Nvidia boss. The woman, Alice Huang, had been previously photographed together with the Nvidia chief on several occasions.

    Nvidia's board of directors launched an independent external investigation. They had earlier said Huang's interactions with the woman are "not unusual or unexpected".

    9 November: US President Donald Trump says the FBI is investigating the scandal, adding that Huang told him he had been unaware the woman he was with was a spy.

    Beijing publicly rejects the spying allegations as a smear.

    11 November: The story takes another turn after a report that Huang also had an extramarital relationship with an employee who reports directly to him.

    Nvidia's board reportedly prepares to meet to decide on Huang's future at the firm.

    The company's shares have been falling all week, erasing more than $500bn (£376bn) off Nvidia's market value.

  20. Why does this matter?published at 01:44 GMT
    01:44 GMT

    Shaimaa Khalil
    North America correspondent in Los Angeles

    Ordinary people wouldn’t necessarily know how powerful or involved Jensen Huang, the jet-setting tech boss who rubs shoulders with world leaders, is – though some might recognise the guy in the leather jacket.

    But many people have money invested in Nvidia through their pensions and investment funds often without realising it. So a sharp fall in its shares can affect their savings too.

    Nvidia’s shares fell about 8% in after-hours trading following the reports that Huang could be removed.

    Then there is the wider AI industry. Companies spending huge sums building AI systems depend on Nvidia’s chips. They’ll want reassurance about supplies, new products and commitments that had already been made.

    Nvidia’s engineers, its technology and its expertise would still be there. But replacing someone with Huang’s influence inside the company and across the industry would be a considerable test.
