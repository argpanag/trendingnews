Futures rally after Wall Street's nightmare week

TheTools · 23.11.2026 11:10 · source · permalink

In this article

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.

Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk. 

The Monday mood is positive after a nightmare week on Wall Street.

The bounce back across the major markets has gained steam throughout the premarket session, despite a rough handover from Asia. 

A decent rebound for U.S. stocks looks set to help major markets crawl away from correction territory. 

Futures tied to the Dow Jones Industrial Average are up over 1.5%, while the S&P 500 futures and Nasdaq-100 futures look set to outperform.  

It follows a widely divergent handover from Asia and Europe. Technology stocks sank in overnight trading, with SK Hynix among the biggest losers, weighing on South Korea's Kospi. 

But in Europe, tech rallied in early trade, pushing the Europe Stoxx 600 into the green. 

On Friday, the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average closed in correction territory, off more than 10% from their records. The Nasdaq Composite lost its gain for the year and the more concentrated Nasdaq-100 entered bear market territory on an intraday basis.

Anthropic is higher in premarket trading on Monday, following an 8.5% drop on Friday. 

CrowdStrike shares are down over 7% in early trading, after it announced a $43.9 billion deal to buy Zscaler. Shares in the cloud security company are up over 15% in premarket trading. 

Meanwhile, chip stocks are also in the green, rebounding from last week's sell-off. 

Nvidia remains in the eye of the storm. Shares are higher in premarket trading, after falling around 14% on Friday. 

Director of National Intelligence, Jay Clayton, says there is an investigation into Alice Huang and her former company, Megaspeed. This comes amid reports that an associate of hers was a key decision-maker at the company. 

Americans should brace themselves for more outage-related disruptions. 

Jay Clayton said on CNN's "State of the Union" that outages may continue as companies and the government upgrade their systems to adapt to the AI age.

CNBC's Alex Harring reports that the tech outages are fueling a new doomsday economy.

This festive season could be marked by a new tradition — agentic holiday shopping. 

New Salesforce research suggests shoppers are more comfortable using AI to research products and get recommendations than handing an agent the ability to pay: roughly three-quarters say they trust AI recommendations, while only 9% of personal agent users say they've shared financial information to make purchases.

Employees at Eric Trump-backed robot startup lose health benefits as company's cash crunch deepens

Employees at Foundation Future Industries were told that their health benefits through administrator Rippling have been terminated, according to a memo obtained by CNBC, adding to mounting signs of financial distress at the humanoid robot startup advised and backed by Eric Trump.

Foundation told staff in a Friday evening email that they'd be offered coverage through COBRA, the federal law that generally lets workers temporarily keep their health insurance after losing benefits by paying the full premium out of pocket.
