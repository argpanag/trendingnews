Cleary learns fate over ‘super stupid’ moment... but faces fresh headache

TheTools · 14.09.2026 04:37 · source · permalink

Penrith’s superstar halfback Nathan Cleary has learned his fate after being sin-binned and subsequently charged for a dangerous tackle in Sunday’s win over the Roosters.

In the final seconds of the opening half of Penrith’s clash against the Sydney Roosters, Cleary ran through the kicking legs of Hugo Savala and immediately knew he was in danger of being sent from the field.

“There’s force in it. (It’s) very dangerous and high risk of injury,” referee Gerard Sutton was heard saying on the field.

The four-time premiership-winner and 2026 Wally Lewis Medallist was hit with a grade one dangerous contact charge on Monday morning.

He is only facing a fine of $1,000 as a result and will be available for selection in the preliminary finals.

Speaking on Fox League, Matty Johns believed Cleary could be facing an extended stint on the sidelines.

“He could be facing a two or three weeks suspension,” Johns said on Finals Footy with Matty Johns.

Former NRL Dally M winner Todd Carney added, “You look at the vision again. He’s not going for the body.

“He’s obviously shadowing the kicker and he ends up hitting his leg.

“The rules say it’s a dangerous play, but it’s a big one.”

Cleary himself spoke post-game, calling his decision to attempt a charge down on Savala a “stupid” one.

“My mind was going a million miles an hour if I’m honest (after the sin bin),” Cleary said.

“There was a lot of guilt and I felt like I let the boys down. It was a super stupid decision.

“I thought I saw an opportunity to make a big play on half time but it was the opposite.”

Cleary won a charge downgrade in the pre-season when represented by his father and Penrith coach Ivan at the judiciary.

When asked by Fox League’s Bryan Fletcher if the club could use that tact again, the Panthers star laughed it off.

“He did alright last time so hopefully I don’t have to go but we’ll see,” Cleary replied.

Cleary was also seen limping late in the contest and deferred a conversion attempt to Dylan Edwards.

The superstar halfback is set to undergo scans on his foot on Monday afternoon according to Australian Associated Press after telling the Panthers physio “my foot is f***ed”.

“I copped a little bump on the foot and it was a pretty important kick so I just wanted Dyl to take it. I wasn’t sure how the foot was going to hold up so it was just precautionary to be honest,” the Panthers halfback said.

Players have previously been suspended in the past for similar incidents.

In Round 3, Trey Mooney was slapped with one-match ban (Grade Two) after running through the legs of Tanah Boyd.

However, the NRL made big changes to the judiciary code in 2022 which meant in finals matches players would be eligible to accept a fine for most third offences instead of facing a ban as is customary in the regular season.

Zac Hosking avoided a ban for a similar incident in the finals last year.

Hosking collected the legs of Reece Walsh on a field goal attempt, but it was only deemed grade one dangerous contact.

As was the case with Walsh, Savala also was quick to get up and not injured in the play.

“He’s fine. Hugo Savala got straight up…it’ll be a grade one,” Luke Keary said on Fox League.

Cleary did, however, appear to accelerate at Savala and collected his legs with more force.
