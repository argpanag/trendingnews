Pablo’s long wait for his first goal continues, but West Ham are top of the Championship

TheTools · 09.09.2026 17:43 · source · permalink

Jarrod Bowen celebrates after scoring West Ham's winning goal at Bolton on Tuesday James Gill - Danehouse/Getty Images

“If Pablo scores, we’re on the pitch” was the cry from the away end.

It is not often West Ham United fans set out to disobey trespassing laws, but there was a stated exception for their £20million striker at the Toughsheet Community Stadium. The chant occurred on the cusp of half-time during West Ham’s hard-fought 3-2 victory against Bolton Wanderers on Tuesday night, courtesy of two goals from club captain Jarrod Bowen and one from Konstantinos Mavropanos.

The linesman had just flagged Pablo for offside, after his outstretched left leg had failed to get on the end of Bowen’s cross. The passage of play, which did not feature in West Ham’s video highlights, prompted head coach Nuno Espirito Santo to put his hands on his head. Pablo belatedly rose from the turf and looked towards the heavens. Much to the stewards’ relief, no pitch encroachment took place. The forward remains scoreless in 21 appearances since joining from Portuguese side Gil Vicente in January.

Offensively, it was another frustrating encounter for Pablo, who was withdrawn in the 66th minute. But, despite his barren run, he was involved in two of West Ham’s goals. His sole shot on target was saved by Bolton goalkeeper Gavin Bazunu, with Bowen converting the rebound. Pablo was fouled in the build up to Mavropanos’s equaliser, with summer signing Arne Engels providing the assist from a free kick.

“I hope they (the goals) come soon, because look what Pablo does for the team — he’s amazing,” Nuno said in his post-match press conference. “We believe that the goal is going to come, but he never says no to the hard work and the movements, he links the game, he defends; fantastic player.

“But I honestly believe there’s room for improvement from all of us. We try to sustain the levels, which is very hard in this league, but also realising that for players that there are these ups and downs. Today was Jarrod’s night. I hope the next game (against Wrexham) it’s going to be Manor (Solomon), Joel, Pablo, or Taty (Castellanos). What they need is our confidence and all of them have it.”

On a night when West Ham went top of the league, the head coach was right to eulogise Pablo’s all-round contribution. His pressing and unselfishness off the ball helps their playing style. In starts against Portsmouth (in the Carabao Cup), Burnley, Wolverhampton Wanderers and Bolton this season, Nuno’s side have scored first in three of those games. His defensive contribution is noticeable when he is not present. The team let a two-goal lead slip against Burnley after Pablo’s injury-enforced substitution at half-time, and he did not feature against Charlton Athletic or Watford, with West Ham failing to win both games.

Pablo’s ongoing issue is that he lacks the instinct of a natural goalscorer. His £20m fee far exceeds his offensive output. Across the forward’s four appearances this season, he has only registered two shots on target. Piroe and Taty Castellanos, who missed the victory over Bolton with a hamstring injury, have a combined total of eight shots on target.

Pablo’s last goal came against Arouca on December 28, 2025. There was no major fanfare following his arrival at the London Stadium four days later, but a sense of hope among the fanbase that he would not mirror other misfiring strikers such as Titi Camara, Jonathan Calleri, Simone Zaza, Pablo Barrera and others.

Pablo is a polarising figure whose work ethic is appreciated, but lack of potency continues to cause frustration. In a division where most clubs vying for promotion have attackers capable of reaching double figures, Pablo is not the dependable goalscoring figure Nuno needs. Instead, it will be Piroe, Castellanos and Bowen who the head coach will lean towards.

Against Bolton, it was Bowen who showcased his attacking verve. By his standards, the captain had a slow start to the season, failing to score in the first three league games.

But the England international has now registered three goals in his last three games. He could have completed a hat-trick in the 88th minute, were it not for a block from Bolton midfielder Josh Sheehan. Shortly after making it 3-2, Bowen jumped over the advertising board, with his arms aloft as team-mates and photographers chased after him.

It is West Ham who are now top of the table and being pursued, ahead of Friday night’s home game against promotion contenders Wrexham.

Eleven days ago, after the 1-1 draw against Watford, supporters in the away enclosure at Vicarage Road sang “You’re getting sacked in the morning” towards Nuno. Since then, the team have won three successive games.

“I think we’ve started to take the pressure off our shoulders,” Bowen told West Ham’s official website after the game. “There was a lot of expectation and pressure on us, and of course the first few weeks were a bit of a reality check for the whole group. It wasn’t going to be easy.

“We saw that we’re going to have to earn every single point, and I don’t think we’ve been at the levels we really expect of ourselves, but we’re winning games. We know we’ve got a lot to improve on, but I think we’ve turned a corner.”
