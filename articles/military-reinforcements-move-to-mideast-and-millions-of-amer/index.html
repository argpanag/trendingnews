Today's top stories

Heavier fighting in Iran could resume in the coming weeks as military reinforcements arrive and some U.S. personnel leave the region. This shift comes as a person briefed on the U.S. and Israel's war planning told NPR that the countries were preparing plans for a possible attack on Iran after the U.S. midterms. Iran said it is prepared.

  • 🎧 There are clear signs of preparations for a possible attack, NPR's Greg Myre tells Up First. The U.S. is deploying a third aircraft carrier to the Middle East, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth recently visited Israel to meet with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. President Trump will likely consider military action after Nov. 3 to avoid a spike in oil prices before the midterm elections. In response, Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said that Iran is ready for both diplomacy and war. While the U.S. and Israel possess the power to inflict heavy damage, Iran shows no signs of making significant concessions, Myre says. Instead, it demands formal control over the Strait of Hormuz and the withdrawal of U.S. troops from the region.

Although Election Day is more than a week away, almost 21 million Americans have already voted, either by mail or early in-person voting, according to the University of Florida's Election Lab.

Trump must sit for a deposition in his $10 billion defamation case against the BBC, a federal magistrate judge ruled. Trump is required to comply with court rules or face sanctions, the judge said yesterday. The case centers on a Panorama broadcast from late October 2024, which allegedly edited the president's Jan. 6 speech to make it appear he incited a violent siege of Congress. Trump has described the broadcast as false, misleading and defamatory under Florida law.

  • 🎧 It's unclear if Trump will sit for a deposition after he previously canceled for "matters of national importance" and suggested January as an alternative, NPR's David Folkenflik says. The case initially focused on the economic harm the broadcast posed to Trump's brand and financial prospects. Although the BBC apologized for the splicing, it now seeks to understand Trump's mindset during his testimony, as he may have intended to encourage violence. BBC attorneys say more than 200 defendants from Jan. 6 said Trump's speech motivated them. The BBC is actively pursuing details about how Trump has profited since regaining the White House, most likely to assess the extent of the damage he faces, Folkenflik says.

Deep dive

Trump promised to lower grocery prices in his second term. While some individual items have gotten cheaper since he returned to office, overall grocery prices have risen, driven by factors such as increased labor costs, tariffs and higher energy prices due to the conflict with Iran. Specific issues facing a single crop, animal species or geographic region can cause the price of one item to rise well above inflation, while lowering the price of another. NPR analyzed five everyday food items — ground beef, eggs, coffee, butter and chocolate chip cookies — to better understand the math behind grocery store prices.

  • 🍔 Ground beef prices have skyrocketed, reaching $6.79 per pound last month, which is an 85% increase over the past decade, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. This surge is largely due to weather and financial pressures on producers.
  • 🥚 Prices for eggs fell to $2.27 per dozen last month. Outbreaks like the avian flu can cause significant price fluctuations. In March 2025, the average dozen reached a record high of $6.23.
  • 🧈 In September, a pound of butter averaged $3.96 — down 17.3% from last year. U.S. butter production has surged as dairy farms expanded their herds and boosted milk supply for popular items like Greek yogurt. This has resulted in an oversupply of butter.
  • 🍪 Chocolate chip cookie prices hit a record $5.35 per pound this January, and last month dropped by 20 cents. The price spike is partly due to factors affecting the sweet treat's ingredients, such as higher flour costs driven by the war in Ukraine.
  • ☕ The U.S. imports 99% of the coffee it consumes. Severe weather and climate change have affected harvests, and the price of coffee is still 44% higher than it was two years earlier, despite Trump removing tariffs on the product.
