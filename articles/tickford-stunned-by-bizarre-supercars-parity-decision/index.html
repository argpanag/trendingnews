Tickford stunned by ‘bizarre’ Supercars parity decision

TheTools · 30.10.2026 02:16 · source · permalink

The category has mandated a tweak to the bootlid gurney on the Holden Commodore ZBs in a bid to improve high-speed stability ahead of the penultimate round at Sandown next week.

Holden’s ZB and the Gen2 Ford Mustang have been racing in Super2 since 2023 in the same specification in which they’d competed in the Supercars Championship from 2020-22.

Supercars said the change has been made following an analysis of data, runway test results and a track outing with an Eggleston Motorsport Holden at Sandown last week.

The parity review took place following repeated triggers of the 2022-era system in place for Super2, which takes lap times from the six fastest cars from each make in races.

The resulting change appears to further blur the line between sporting and technical parity that has already been under question following recent tweaks to the Gen3 cars in the main game.

“We’re pretty disappointed in the process,” Tickford manager Matt Roberts told Speedcafe.

“We had parity up until now. The ZB won the last two championships against the Mustang in the main game and the first two [Super2] championships in ’23 and ’24.

“All of a sudden the Ford has won one and quite possibly another [Super2 title] and now it’s a problem.”

Roberts said he was blindsided by a phone call from Supercars on Thursday notifying him of the impending change and had been unaware Wednesday’s track test took place.

“It wasn’t a phone call of discussion, it was a directive phone call – this is happening and there’s no room for any argument,” he said.

“It’s bizarre. We would have been [at the test] with a car to have there as a benchmark or reference. It’s pretty poor how it’s been handled. We’re not happy at all.”

Leading Holden Super2 teams have been agitating for a change since late last year.

Tickford’s four-car team dominated the 2025 season and has split the bulk of the spoils this year with the Blanchard Racing Team, which runs Tickford-built cars.

Eggleston snapped Holden’s Super2 round victory drought when it installed Aaron Love for the Perth round in August, while Brad Jones Racing’s Brad Vaughan won a race last time out at Bathurst.

Roberts feels the Ford teams are being punished for the effort they have put into their Super2 operations.

“We’re one of the few teams that put a lot of time and effort and resources into our Super2 program,” he said.

“We feel like it’s being taken away from us. Is it poor team performance? I’m not sure. But we’re fairly disappointed with the news.

“The guys will work hard now, that’s what we do, we’ll knuckle down and focus on executing this weekend as we normally would.

“It makes us even more hungry now to go out this weekend and beat them.”

BRT Ford driver Lochie Dalton leads the standings on 1059 points ahead of Sandown, which Vaughan (BJR Holden) second on 945.

Tickford quartet Nash Morris (894), Campbell Logan (885), Reuben Goodall (813) and Ben Gomersall (759) round out the top six.
