The startup behind Twitter's resurrection was just forced to change its name

TheTools · 05.09.2026 00:51 · source · permalink

The new site launched on Twitter.now on August 28, but a court order has forced it to rebrand.

On September 3, the judge overseeing the trademark dispute ruled that X still retains the trademark for Twitter, but not the word "tweet" or the bird logo. Operation Bluebird quickly rebranded, changing the name and domain to Tweet.App, as reported by Ars Technica. The original story, published on August 27, follows.

Less than a year after Elon Musk acquired Twitter in 2022, he officially changed its name to X. Now, a startup called Operation Bluebird is arguing that Musk gave up the trademark to the Twitter name with that rebrand, and it's launching a "new" Twitter based on that theory. 

In a LinkedIn post this week, Stephen Coates, a trademark lawyer behind Operation Bluebird, announced the launch of the new site at Twitter.now. But it doesn't appear to be the smoothest launch. As of Thursday morning, the website was down and inaccessible for some users. Operation Bluebird didn't immediately respond to a request for comment from Yahoo Tech.

"Twitter.now is not an attempt to recreate the old platform," Coates said in his post announcing the launch. "We are building a different service, with trust signals that provide context for what users see and tools that allow people — not an opaque algorithm — to decide how much credibility and noise reach their feeds."

According to The Register, the new Twitter asks you to pay $20 to gain early access to the platform as a founder. You can also pay $40 or more to gain access in the "Fighter tier," which gives you a special badge on your profile.

Both Coates and Operation Bluebird made a point to note that the new Twitter isn't associated with Musk's X Corp. or Twitter, Inc. An FAQ page listed on Twitter.new, a separate domain launched late last year for people to claim handles, says, "Twitter.new is an independent company. It is not affiliated with X Corp, Elon Musk, or the former Twitter Inc."

It's unclear if Operation Bluebird's gambit will be successful. The company filed to claim the Twitter trademark last year, but Musk's X Corp. quickly filed a lawsuit to challenge the claim. 

"Twitter never left and continues to be exclusively owned by X Corp.," the company said in its filing. As Ars Technica notes, the judge in the case tentatively ruled in April that X may have given up its trademarks on the word "tweet," the bird logo and, possibly, the Twitter name itself. However, a written order has yet to be released.

For now, it appears Twitter is back, at least in name. It's unclear how long that may last, though, especially if it continues to have trouble keeping the servers up.

Updated on September 4 to include information about the trademark ruling concerning the Twitter name.
