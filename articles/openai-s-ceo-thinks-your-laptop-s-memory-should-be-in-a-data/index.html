Sam Altman, OpenAI's chief executive, argued on the Cheeky Pint podcast episode published October 29, 2026, that the world's scarce memory chips belong in data centers instead of in personal machines. Speaking with Stripe co-founder John Collison, he said: "It's quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world's limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer."

DRAM, or dynamic random access memory, is the working memory every computer and server needs. Altman said personal devices use memory about 5% of the time, and that "you want maximum utilization in the cloud if you're trying to deal with this very supply-constrained environment."

He stated an opinion as one of the largest buyers of data center memory, without a product plan, contract, or timeline attached.

Micron Technology (NASDAQ:MU) fell 5.30% on October 30 to close at $884.74, a market value of about $1.00 trillion, during a broad pullback in large technology stocks. Shares traded at $889.00 after hours.

What Altman's Memory Argument Covers

His case is about allocation while supply is tight. Memory sitting idle in a personal device is capacity a shared server could use, which runs counter to the industry push to sell AI-ready PCs that run models locally.
