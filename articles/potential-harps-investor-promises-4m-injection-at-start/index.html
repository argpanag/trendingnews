Potential Harps investor promises €4m injection at start

TheTools · 12.10.2026 12:05 · source · permalink

Potential new Finn Harps owner Freddy Gonzalez has promised an initial €4 million investment into the club if his takeover proposal is passed this month.

Gonzalez, a 48-year-old former Philippines international footballer, has long dreamed of owning a football club and, after a year of searching, has honed in on the Donegal side in recent months.

He is the co-founder of Terry SA, a multi-million euro retail company in his native country and has family ties to the International Container Terminal Services, one of the Philippines' most respected business dynasties, which has revenue in the billions each year.

Harps, currently fan-owned and set to finish bottom of the First Division, will hold a Special General Meeting next Monday in Donegal, to address the approach by Gonzalez. It is being supported by their board.

Members must approve the takeover by a 75% majority and a further meeting will be held to seal the deal.

The club had been seeking investment due to a bleak outlook for the future. Tom Markham of Markham Capital and Sports Advisory Limited was approached by Harps to identify potential investors who may be interested in the club.

Their current annual playing budget is around €250,000, a small fraction of the leading Premier Division clubs.

Gonzalez has promised works on their current home of Finn Park, help to complete their new stadium in Stranloar in the future and invest in their first-team to go full-time next campaign.

After being presented with the Harps opportunity, he said it was the perfect fit for his vision of owning a club.

He has spent the last few months in Donegal in a bid to get the move over the line.

Speaking on Sunday Sport on RTÉ Radio 1, he said: "After reviewing everything it just fit my criteria in terms of being a community-oriented club, having a wonderful academy and being a pillar of the county.

"And so when I came in May I was able to see everything. I was able to see all the infrastructure and so straight away I was this is where I could realise my vision.

"It has been a complicated five or six months, especially during the due diligence phase.

"About a week or so ago we agreed already on the heads of terms. The notices have gone out and it's just up to the members now to vote and hopefully it gets done and then we can start straight away."

Quizzed on what his initial investment will look like, he said: "Based on what we've already committed to spending in terms of the squad value next season, renovations here in Finn Park, fixing the pitch, doing everything is already over €4m.

"And that's just for the beginning.

"The €4m spend is the renovations of Finn Park. It's the squad for next season. It's the staffing.

"It's the recovery and rehab facilities that we're already committed to. We've rented a number of buildings already in town in Ballybofey. We'll be building an office.

"We'll be building a club shop on Main Street. There's a lot of things that we've been working on behind the scenes that people don't know about. In the next three to four months, people will be surprised that all of a sudden there is all this Finn Harps infrastructure coming out."

Sligo Rovers were set to be the only full-time team in the division in 2027, after their relegation last Friday.

In previous years, the team dropping from the Premier has been hugely advantaged by being the sole full-time side in the division.

Cork City are 20 points clear with a single match left this season, their third promotion this decade as they move between the leagues.

But Harps have targeted breaking the trend in the coming years, even if it isn’t next season, by turning pro.

Gonzalez spoke further about transitions on and off the pitch: "I commend the board and the volunteers for doing the work they've done with what they've had.

"But the club couldn't continue going on like this because there's no real structure.

"So we need the right people with the right expertise in the right positions to be able to communicate and work with FAI, with the League of Ireland, with UEFA, with sponsors, with different brands and companies.

"And you need professionals to do that. On the playing side, in order to attract better players, we need to be full-time. And so that's straight away starting next season, all the players on the first team are full-time.

"We will still have academy players in the squad, but the majority, including the academy players, will have full-time contracts. It’s about strengthening the squad where it needs to be.

"It's also about getting Premier-ready players while we're in the First Division. We don't want to qualify for the Premier and not be ready to be there, because all that's going to happen is you're just going to yo-yo back. And then you're going to be rushing to try to get Premier players while you're in the Premier.

"The strategy here is starting at the end of the season and we've already been evaluating different players from across Ireland, Northern Ireland, UK, around the world, through all our different connections.

"And it's about being Premier-ready before we get to the Premier Division. And so that's how we're going to maintain being in the Premier, by building already from next season towards 2028.

"Our target for next season is the top four. And the target for 2028 is to win the First Division so that by 2029, we're in the Premier."

It’s also possible the new owner could help fund the Donegal Community Stadium.

Harps have told members that it is not feasible that the stadium and centre of excellence, of which they’ve secured millions of euro of funding from the state, can be transferred to the new potential owner.

They will remain under the co-operative society.

In a notice to its members, Harps said: "The proposed acquisition does not include Freddy taking any interest in the New Stadium or the Centre of Excellence at this time but does include a commitment by Freddy and the Club to work together in respect of grants and a possible structure of how these facilities will be owned and run.

"Alternatively, Freddy is committed to using his own funds towards delivering the New Stadium and Centre of Excellence."

Gonzalez said the prospect of 'free money’ is welcome but he is keen to move on both projects, with the Donegal Community Stadium stalled for close to two decades.

He explained: "We will be reviewing different grants and as they come and because you know the thing with grants is that who doesn't love free money? But we have to review every little detail of every grant to make sure that it's good for the club. It's not necessarily great to take free money but with all these restrictions behind it.

"So the club needs to be able to be flexible and to be able to do things quickly and without any bureaucracy. So if we need let's say a stand to be fixed, we can do it on our own without having to wait for grant funding. That's the way we're looking at this."

As members plot which way to vote, the former Philippines international footballer has promised that he will leave a plan for the future for the club, after his tenure.

"The way I have this set up is that if anything were to happen to me, there is a line of succession.

"But however, there will be a trust in place that will make sure that if there is any shortfall in the years after I'm gone, this will be delivered to the club and the board of advisors that I will have in place will be making sure that you hire the right CEO, you hire the right sports director, you hire the right staff, that it remains professionally run throughout this whole time and for the future."

Harps chairman Ian Harkin has revealed the cost of the sale will be literally wiping out debts accrued in recent seasons. The last Annual General Meeting recorded them at over €700,000.

The chairman explained: "We need to get the vote over the line and then we deal with selling the club.

"We have negative net assets. We've got creditors within our club.

"Freddy is going to clear those creditors as the first step. And that's pretty much the acquisition cost of buying the club. We've then got commitments with regards to funding the first team.

"We did speak with some other people as well and Freddy stands out among other people. Anybody that you meet that has met Freddy will see his passion and his desire for what he wants to do. There is a leap of faith with that as well, but that's the same in any walk of life, in any business and whatever you're doing.

"But Freddy comes from a football family. He was a professional footballer himself. There's not many people out there like him. He's quite unique."

He is also aware of the difficulties of the grants awarded and the change of model for the club.

"We've been awarded a number of grants and we're going to be working with Freddy after the agreement has been signed and how to best structure those because they're extremely complicated from having so many different stakeholders from the council to trustees to shareholders to the Department of Sport, Department of Justice.

"There's a lot of different parties involved in our club."

History has shown outside investment can bring more negatives than positives for a club in the League of Ireland but the Harps chief is adamant this could transform the Donegal side.

He believes the mere presence of the businessman in the county since the summer shows his loyalty to the project.

"I can only go on what I've met from having worked with Freddie over this last number of months. He's on the ground.

"He's away from his family. He's got two daughters, one in New York, one in York and his wife is back home in the Philippines and that's a massive commitment on its own.

"He is committed and put his money in. Even without the deal going through yet, there is a lot of commitment being shown. I have every confidence it will work."
