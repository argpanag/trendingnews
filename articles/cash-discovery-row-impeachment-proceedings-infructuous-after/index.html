Cash discovery row | Impeachment proceedings 'infructuous' after my resignation: Justice Yashwant Varma to Parliament

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:09 · source · permalink

Justice Yashwant Varma (L) said there was no evidence to show that the cash was placed by him or at his instance at the store room.

Credit: PTI file photos

New Delhi: Justice Yashwant Varma has told Parliament that the impeachment proceedings against him have become "infructuous" and would be "without constitutional foundation" following his resignation, even as he insisted that he is "innocent" of the allegations that included that he possessed "ill-gotten funds", sources said.

His response through a letter on September 10 came following the Lok Sabha Secretariat sharing the report of the Judges Inquiry Committee that found him in the wrong, claiming that the charges of discovery of "unexplained" cash at his residence, interfering with evidence and providing evasive explanation on the sequence of events have been "proved".

The report was tabled in Parliament on August 12. Justice Varma, who was transferred to Allahabad High Court from Delhi High Court after the recovery of a large amount of cash in burnt condition after a fire incident at his official residence here, had resigned on April 9 in the midst of the inquiry. 

In the two-page letter, which also annexed the resignation letter to the President and another letter to the inquiry panel sent on April 9, he said he has neither drawn salary nor received any facility, allowance, benefit or perquisite attached to the office of a high court judge after his resignation.

Citing a 1978 Supreme Court judgement that maintained that a resignation by a judge "takes effect 'ex proprio vigore', that is, by its own force with immediate effect or on and from the date that may be specified" he, said, "thus, upon tendering my resignation in writing with immediate effect, I ceased to hold the office of a Judge on 9 April, 2026 itself, and my entire relationship with the office was severed."

"Upon my resignation, proceedings directed towards my removal from office did not survive and cannot be continued. The constitutional power under Articles 124(4), 124(5), and 217 of the Constitution is a power to remove a sitting Judge from office. The said power is not envisaged to be exercised against a former Judge who has ceased to hold office. This position has also been consistently recognised in parliamentary practice," he said.

The inquiry report had claimed that Justice Varma had "failed" to provide a "satisfactory explanation" regarding the "presence, source or ownership" of "substantial unexplained Rs 500 denomination currency notes" found in the storeroom in his official residential premises on Tughlaq Crescent in Delhi. 

In his last letter to the inquiry panel on April 9 withdrawing from the proceedings following his resignation, Justice Varma had denied the charges saying it "defies ordinary logic and common sense" that he chose to store cash in a place which is accessible to several people. 

He said there was no evidence to show that the cash was placed by him or at his instance at the store room. He also said the argument that his residence was a secured premise was not correct as the CRPF officer who appeared as a witness had maintained that the back gate to the residence was unmanned and the security was limited to the main entrance.

Justice Varma also denied the findings in the report that he gave an “evasive explanation” was not correct and insisted that a “plain reading” of his response on 22 March, 2025 showed no denial of recovery of cash.

He also questioned the dropping a number of witnesses from the proceedings.
