The 49ers have Nick Bosa and DeForest Buckner back. Now they need their next Dee Ford

TheTools · 06.11.2026 02:02 · source · permalink

Rookie pass rusher Romello Height thinks he can provide a burst of speed off the edge that the team has been missing since Ford’s departure.

SANTA CLARA — A clip from the 49ers’ famous 2019 season played Thursday in the team’s defensive-line meeting room. 

Kris Kocurek, the fiery defensive-line coach who’s been around since that overpowering march to a Super Bowl appearance, snapped his head toward rookie edge rusher Romello Height as the front four — Nick Bosa, DeForest Buckner, Arik Armstead, and Dee Ford — engulfed another hapless quarterback.

“You see this, Romello?” Kocurek asked. “This is what I need you to do: Burn the edge.”

Kocurek was pointing at Ford, the 243-pound end known for his jaw-dropping burst off the snap and around offensive tackles. Back issues ended Ford’s career in 2021, but the 49ers mounted an otherworldly pass rush while he was healthy over the first several weeks of 2019. That season’s defense smothered most quarterbacks the team faced and even held Aaron Rodgers, who was still in his prime with the Green Bay Packers, to the worst statistical performance of his career.

This was natural tape for Kocurek to show his group in 2026, especially because the 49ers just reacquired Buckner via trade and returned him and Bosa — who’s been out with a calf strain — to practice on the same day this week.

The 49ers are asking both veterans to reprise their old roles. They’re asking Height, a 242-pound speed rusher who began blossoming around Week 4 against the Denver Broncos, to replicate Ford’s impact from the outside. 

“Watching that play reminded me of myself,” Height said. “The way Dee Ford got off the ball. His feet were so rapid. His get-off reminded me of myself so much. His bend. Me and him have the same game, in a way.”

Height played at four colleges over six seasons, and one was Auburn — Ford’s alma mater. So although Height has never met his predecessor, he was familiar with his tape before the 49ers drafted him in this year’s second round.

“I would see Dee Ford coming off the edge,” Height said. “He was electric — fast and twitchy.”

The 49ers’ pass rush fell off after Ford was injured. It has never truly reapproached its peak 2019 form. Even the 49ers’ 2022 defense, which ranked as the NFL’s top unit by advanced metrics, never mustered comparable pressure, even though Bosa was the league’s Defensive Player of the Year.

Much of that traced back to the loss of Buckner, whom the 49ers traded to the Indianapolis Colts following the 2019 season. In the years since, they’ve strained to replace his durable interior pass-rushing presence. Javon Kinlaw, a 2020 first-round draftee, couldn’t do it. Neither could Javon Hargrave, a big-ticket free agent signing in the 2023 offseason. And although the 49ers received solid production from Maliek Collins, acquired via trade in 2024, the rest of the defense fell apart around him. 

GM John Lynch resolved to fix the issue this offseason, trading for former Dallas Cowboys defensive tackle Osa Odighizuwa — who last season nearly racked up more pressures (51) than all 49ers’ interior defensive linemen combined (56). The 49ers also drafted Gracen Halton in the fourth round.

But Lynch wasn’t done. His pro personnel department, led by Assistant GM R.J. Gillen, scoured other teams for veteran talent and zeroed in on Buckner early in the season. So did Bosa, who jokingly asked coach Kyle Shanahan about a potential reunion with his former teammate around that time. 

During a Week 4 game against the Washington Commanders, Buckner sprinted about 40 yards to chase down speedy quarterback Marcus Mariota. Gillen and Lynch marveled at that tape inside 49ers headquarters.

“DeFo’s play has been outstanding this year — I think he’s playing as good of football as he’s played in his career,” Lynch said Thursday on KNBR. “You don’t just bring guys back because you’re feeling nostalgic. You bring guys back because your scouting department is doing their job.”

Said Bosa: “I mentioned it to Kyle jokingly, but the fact it actually happened — it’s a full-circle moment.”

With Buckner and Odighizuwa both aboard, the 49ers are one of only five teams that have two defensive tackles with 20 or more pressures this season.

And that draws a circle back to Height’s role.

“We’ve got the push up the middle now,” Height said, “so the speed rush is super important. It’s critical off the edge, because the quarterback is going to step up a lot.”

Discipline will be the staple that can tie the new-look pass rush together when it takes the field for the first time Sunday against the Las Vegas Raiders. 

To that end, Buckner is already delivering. The 32-year-old veteran spent time this week working offsite, putting in extra training after dark with young lineman Mykel Williams.

Buckner has arrived at the 49ers’ facility to begin working out at 5:30 a.m. daily. Height, who had been showing up at 7 a.m., is following suit. 

“My new routine is 5:30,” Height said. “He motivated me. Things like that are just motivation to the young guys. He’s done it at the highest level, so all I can do is pick his brain.”

Buckner, drafted in 2016 (the year before Lynch and Shanahan took over), had already served as a model for the 49ers’ desired culture during his first stint with the team.

“When I came in as a rookie [in 2018], he was everything we were looking for as a 49er,” star linebacker Fred Warner said Wednesday. “I learned so much from him early on. It’s rare that you get a second opportunity to come home.”

Bosa, who was drafted in 2019 and played only one season with Buckner, echoed Warner’s sentiment.

“This would make a good documentary,” Bosa said. “The last game I played with him was the Super Bowl. And it feels like my rookie year was more than one year of my career, just because of how impactful it was on me.”

Seven years later, with some of his original teammates now grizzled veterans, Buckner is making a strong impression on a new crop of 49ers youngsters. And the defensive line, an eclectic combination of veteran stars and productive young talents, thinks it has the ingredients to replicate its 2019 magic. 

“Seems like we’re pretty stacked,” Bosa said.

It’s Height, playing Ford’s role, who might bind it all together. 

“Humbly, I feel that there’s going to be a lot of chaos in the backfield,” Height said. “[Having both Bosa and Buckner] is going to open up a lot of lanes for me and open a lot of doors. Because Bosa is potentially going to get the slide [protection] every time. It’ll free me up to get a lot of one-on-ones.

“I can’t wait.”
