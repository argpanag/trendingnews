Alcaraz and a memorable comeback: "It's one of my most special matches"

TheTools · 07.11.2026 02:42 · source · permalink

The Murcian was euphoric after defeating Fritz in Paris, talking about why he doubted his tennis at first and stating that he will remember this comeback "for a long time".

Today, Carlos Alcaraz made us remember why he is a special tennis player. After his return to the circuit from injury, we needed, perhaps, one of those matches where the Murcian creates excitement, showcasing that palette of skills he puts at the service of magic in crucial moments. His victim was Taylor Fritz, from whom he stole the ticket to the ATP Finals 2026 to hand it over, in passing, to his compatriot Rafa Jódar; the setting, an ATP Rolex Paris Masters 2026 where he advances for the first time to the semifinals, coming closer than ever to opening his account in Masters 1000 this year in his last opportunity.

To get to this point, however, the one from El Palmar must have faced the abyss. He did so head-on, just a small push away from falling, during several match points he brilliantly saved, only to transform into that unstoppable wave of tennis that overwhelms the strongest players. Taylor felt it firsthand as he lacked the killer instinct needed to finish off a genius like Carlos, with a surprisingly prominent factor: the crowd.

Alcaraz points to the Parisian crowd as one of the reasons for his comeback

Closing a wound opened after that match against Hugo Gaston in which the local crowd got into his head, Alcaraz himself acknowledged at court side that the cheers and atmosphere of the French crowd, who stood up thanks to his magic and chanted his name during the comeback, were absolutely essential for completing a dream victory.

"I tried not to fall apart in those complicated moments of the second set, I tried to stay mentally strong and save those problems, those break points, those match points that Taylor had. At 5-3 with his serve, I would say, I don't remember exactly, but in that game we produced some incredible tennis, both of us, with impressive points. We made the crowd rise, giving a new energy to this match... and I absorbed that energy, I leaned on the people. I gave them what I think they wanted to see today. I'm very happy to have been able to play an additional hour and a half of tennis, for them, to entertain them. It was incredible. The feeling today is unparalleled. Happy to have given myself a new opportunity to step on the court tomorrow, to have saved those opportunities, and to have kept fighting".

The complicated moments Carlos referred to came after a dismantling start to the match, in the first hour and a half where Fritz outplayed him from the baseline, digging into a hole of errors that showcased the discomfort of the Murcian. Why?: "At 5-4 in the first set, I felt nervous. He started to play more solidly, better from the baseline, and I was struggling a bit to find my game from the back, which made things harder. I had my chances, but I didn’t take them, making decisions I regret, to be honest. In the second set, the feelings were similar. After 5-3, I began to shake off those nerves, to enjoy on the court, as I hit a couple of great shots and several fantastic points," said Alcaraz, who focused on a very important aspect that allowed his comeback today: enjoying tennis again.

"I started to smile, to generate energy from the stands. I tried to use it to entertain them. At that point, I began to appreciate every second and every point; that was the key today. Did I ever see myself losing? Of course, I won't lie to you. He had several match points and was returning very well and playing great from the back. I saved several match points with some passing shots that, truthfully, were impressive. It was the moment to do something like that. If I had lost, fine, but I didn't want to leave the court feeling I could have done something more, or that I should have chosen the aggressive option, which is my playing style.
