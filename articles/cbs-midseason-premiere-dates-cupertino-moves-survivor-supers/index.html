CBS Midseason Premiere Dates: 'Cupertino' Moves, Survivor' Supersized

TheTools · 16.11.2026 15:00 · source · permalink

Breaking News

CBS‘ Survivor continues to age backwards. As most long-running reality series are managing the decline by reducing the number of cycles in a season or the length of episodes, Survivor has been growing. The reality juggernaut, which continues to deliver two seasons a year, expanded from one-hour to hour-and-a-half episodes starting with Season 45 in fall 2023.

The show’s episodes will further extend in the upcoming Season 52 to two hours, airing from 8 p.m. to 10 p.m. on Wednesdays beginning Feb. 24. Survivor will be followed by the return of Undercover Boss after a five-year hiatus with a reboot hosted by Drew Carey.

One of CBS’ three new scripted series which premiered this fall, NCIS: New York, is getting a boost with a special airing following the AFC Championship game on Sunday, Jan. 31.

The other two are moving to new nights in midseason. Starting Jan. 17, Silicon Valley legal drama Cupertino is relocating from Thursday to Sunday 10 p.m. where it will be tucked behind CBS’ top two dramas, Marshals and Tracker. The network had been warming the slot for Cupertino this fall by airing encores there since the series’ launch which have been getting almost as many eyeballs as the first-run episodes on Thursday. (Because of frequent NFL game-related delays, CBS slots drama repeats in the Sunday 10 p.m. hour in the fall.) And earlier this month, CBS ran an original Cupertino episode at 8 p.m. on Sunday followed by repeats at 9 p.m. and 10 p.m.

Cupertino, from The Good Wife creators Robert and Michelle King, has been an OK linear and strong streaming performer, more than doubling its delivery in 7-day delayed multi-platform viewing. The show’s departure will break up the Kings 9 p.m.-11 p.m. block on Thursday. The duo’s Elsbeth at 9 p.m. will now be followed by Matlock, which is returning to Thursday for a new season — and a new story arc — on Jan. 21, swapping slots with Elsbeth which followed the Kathy Bates drama the last two seasons.

Returning to its regular Thursday berth is veteran comedy series Ghosts whose Season 6 also will premiere Jan. 21 behind Georgie & Mandy’s First Marriage, reuniting the two comedies that ran together in the 8 p.m. hour the last two seasons.

Because of the size of Ghosts‘ full-season order of 22 episodes (including a two-part Halloween special) and the series’ midseason launch, CBS is double-pumping episodes on Monday and Thursday. On Jan. 25, the network is reopening its Monday 8 PM comedy block anchored by Ghosts, followed by Eternally Yours, the freshman vampire comedy from Ghosts showrunners Joe Port and Joe Wiseman, which drew modest viewership airing behind Georgie & Mandy’s First Marriage this fall.

The supernatural pairing of Ghosts and Eternally Yours may prove more compatible, setting up potential crossovers.

To make room for the reinstated Monday comedy block, FBI and offshoot CIA will be sliding an hour each to 9 p.m. and 10 p.m., respectively, with the latter succeeding the low rated Harlan Coben’s Final Twist in the 10 p.m. hour following the end of its Season 2 run.

Two returning CBS drama series, Fire Country and NCIS: Origins, are bowing out early after getting reduced orders for 13 episodes and 10 episodes, respectively this season.

In the Friday 9 p.m. slot, Fire Country will be succeeded by its red-hot spinoff Sheriff Country, which just hit a new series high on Nov. 6, beginning Jan. 8. The move will create a cop drama 9 p.m. – 11 p.m. block with Boston Blue. Replacing Sheriff Country in the 8 p.m. hour will be new light crime procedural Einstein from Monk creator Andy Breckman starring Matthew Gray Gubler, which is intended for a broad, 8 p.m. audience.

On Tuesday, NCIS: Sydney will slide into NCIS: Origins‘ berth as part of the NCIS-branded night with its fourth season beginning Jan. 26. And serving as a bridge between the two installments of Survivor will be The Price Is Right At Night and Stacks of Cash, a new game show hosted by Mike O’Malley.

This fall season to date, CBS has averaged 8.1 million viewers with seven of the top 10 series, including three of the top four new series, in Nielsen Live+7 Day multi-platform viewership.
