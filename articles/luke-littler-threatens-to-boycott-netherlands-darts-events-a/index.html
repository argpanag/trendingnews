Luke Littler threatens to boycott Netherlands darts events after booing

World No 1 jeered in Amsterdam defeat to Jonny Clayton

Ross Smith beats Price to win World Series of Darts Finals

Luke Littler has threatened to boycott tournaments in the Netherlands after being booed and whistled during the World Series of Darts Finals in Amsterdam. Littler lost to Jonny Clayton in the quarter-finals, with Ross Smith going on to win the event.

The back-to-back world champion faced a hostile environment during his win against the home favourite Danny Noppert on Friday and it escalated on Saturday night as the referee had to intervene in the last-16 win against Nathan Aspinall.

Littler has taken a stance against volatile crowds before, refusing to travel to European Tour events in Germany as a result of his treatment from fans there. He is “100%” prepared to do the same in the Netherlands.

“With the Germans, I haven’t been for a long time, we will have to see in Dortmund whether it changes or stays the same,” Littler told Online Darts. “If the Dutch stay like this, then I’m not going to do any Euro tours over there.”

“There’s just no point in me travelling, wasting my time whilst I’m getting booed. I just want to play my darts, obviously sign autographs,” the world No 1 added. “The past two nights, I’ve done nothing, I’ve just walked off stage without thanking anybody. There’s no point thanking everyone when they are booing me.”

Despite the bearpit atmosphere, Littler came out on top in a last‑leg decider with Aspinall but was defeated 10-4 by Clayton in their quarter-final on Sunday to end the 19-year-old’s hopes of becoming the first player to win all 10 major televised tournaments in a calendar year, having triumphed in the first five.

After a competitive start, Clayton levelled at 3-3 with a 136 checkout and he soon took charge of the contest, completing his victory with a checkout of 40 to set up a semi-final tie with fellow Welshman Gerwyn Price.

Price went on to beat Clayton 11-7 and followed up with a 10-7 semi-final victory over James Wade to set up a final against Smith, who had claimed a 11-8 win over Gian van Veen in the other final-four tie.

Initially little could separate Price and Smith during the final but with the score locked at 5-5, Smith suddenly took control, winning the next six straight legs and finishing with an average of 103.89.

Smith’s 11-5 win over Price in the final secured a maiden World Series of Darts Finals crown for the Englishman. Victory also sealed a second major PDC title for “Smudger”, and his first since the European Championship in 2022.

His path to the final saw him beat Josh Rock 10-7 in the quarter-finals to set up that meeting with Van Veen, who had recovered from trailing 8-6 to claim a 10-8 win over Dirk van Duijvenbode in the last eight.
