Navajo Stirling wants no excuses from Jiri Prochazka after UFC Qatar clash

TheTools · 21.11.2026 23:01 · source · permalink

Undefeated UFC light heavyweight contender Navajo Stirling wants to hear no excuses from Jiri Prochazka following their fight this weekend.

Stirling, the No. 7-ranked fighter in the 205-pound division, is set to headline UFC Qatar against former light heavyweight champion Prochazka on Sunday (Philippine time) at the ABHA Arena in Doha.

In an exclusive interview with GMA News Online, the Wellington, New Zealand native called out what he sees as Prochazka’s tendencies to make excuses following losses.

“I think he makes a lot of excuses for himself and he copes a lot. At the end of the day, he got knocked out,” Stirling said, referring to Prochazka’s knockout loss to current champion Carlos Ulberg.

“The fight's still going on, you walk in with your chin open, and you get knocked out,” he added.

Stirling also questioned Prochazka’s claims that he has become a “new version” of himself, saying he does not want the former champion to diminish the significance of a potential victory.

“He's saying that he's a ‘new version’ of himself. When I beat him, are we going to get another video of him saying, ‘Oh, I tried all these new things and it didn't work, he didn't beat the best version of me’? I don't want him to take away from my win.”

The 29-year-old, who trains at the renowned City Kickboxing gym in Auckland, pointed to Prochazka’s past explanations following his defeats to Alex Pereira and Ulberg.

“For him, first it was an early stoppage against Pereira, then it was ‘dark magic,’ and then it was ‘no mercy.’ I'm sick of hearing that. What is it going to be this time? You've got to burn the excuse book. That's the mental flaw I see in him, and I'm looking to shut him out,” Stirling said.

Prochazka questioned the stoppage following his first meeting with Pereira in 2023, while also suggesting after their 2024 rematch that the Brazilian drew strength from “pre-fight rituals” and “shamans.”

In his most recent outing, Prochazka appeared to be in a favorable position against Ulberg after the latter suffered a torn ACL midway through the opening round. However, the Czech fighter stood in front of his opponent with his hands down, leaving himself open to a fight-ending left hook with only over a minute remaining in the round.

After the bout, Prochazka said he had lost because he showed Ulberg mercy when he should have shown none.

Stirling, meanwhile, said he would have no problem giving Prochazka credit if the former champion proved to be the better fighter on fight night.

“If he beat me, I went in as the best Navajo Stirling; I would 100% give him his props and say he beat the best version of me and is levels above me,” Stirling said.

“I don't think that'll happen. Whatever version shows up, I won't be a ‘shoulda, coulda, woulda’ type of fighter making excuses like I've seen him do. That's the difference in mentality coming into this fight.”

—JKC, GMA News
