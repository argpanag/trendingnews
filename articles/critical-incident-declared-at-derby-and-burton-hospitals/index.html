Critical incident declared at Derby and Burton hospitals

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:01 · source · permalink

A critical incident has been declared

  • Published
    8 hours ago

Derby hospital bosses say additional measures have been put in place to keep services running after declaring a critical incident.

University Hospitals of Derby and Burton (UHDB) NHS Foundation Trust says seasonal respiratory illness, including flu and Covid-19, is adding to pressures which have led it to declare the alert on Monday.

Royal Derby Hospital is facing the greatest operation pressures, said the trust, with its accident and emergency unit's waits averaging about nine hours.

Jo Wright, interim executive chief operating officer, urged people to continue to attend in an emergency or for planned appointments.

Declaring a critical incident means an NHS trust or health body is struggling to meet the demands being placed on its services.

A similar situation is currently putting strain on hospitals in nearby Nottingham.

The trust has said it is caring for "a significant number of patients with flu and Covid" which can affect where and how patients can be treated.

It said it had about 67 flu cases and 24 patients with confirmed Covid-19 at Royal Derby Hospital currently.

Wright said: "Our hospitals are very busy, but we have plans in place to help us manage periods like this safely.

"We are putting additional clinical support where it's needed most, helping patients move through their care as quickly and safely as possible, and working with our partners to help people who are ready to leave hospital do so.

"We are still here for everyone who needs our care - so if it's an emergency, please don't hesitate to come to our emergency departments, and please continue to attend any planned appointments unless we contact you to tell you otherwise.

"We are really grateful to our colleagues, who are working incredibly hard to make sure our patients continue to get the care they need."
