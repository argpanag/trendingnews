Netflix’s Narnia Reboot Is Changing One Beloved Book Character For the Better

TheTools · 02.10.2026 00:00 · source · permalink

Just a few months before its release, Netflix has been revealing new information about its upcoming Narnia: The Magician's Nephew adaptation, including one small change that will make one character much more important. Narnia: The Magician's Nephew, written and directed by Greta Gerwig (Lady Bird, Barbie), will kick-start a new franchise for Netflix, adapting C.S. Lewis' fantasy novels. The Magician's Nephew will explore the origins of Narnia by following two young children from London who embark on an epic adventure.

Just like the Pevensie children from Walden Media and Disney's adaptation of The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, Digory Kirke (David McKenna) and Polly Plummer (Beatrice Campbell) will accidentally cross the barriers between worlds in Narnia: The Magician's Nephew. They'll venture into the Wood Between the Worlds, release the future White Witch (Emma Mackey) from her slumber, and witness the creation of Narnia by Aslan (Meryl Streep), all in hopes of finding a cure for Digory's mother's illness.

Digory Kirke's Mother Is Named "Mabel" in C.S. Lewis' The Magician's Nephew

Before they find themselves in the mysterious study of Digory's uncle, Andrew Ketterley (Daniel Craig), where they find the magical rings that transport them to the Wood Between the Worlds, Digory and Polly are just two regular children in London. Digory lives with his eccentric uncle as his father is away in India and his mother is gravely ill with some unnamed terminal illness, heavily implied to be cancer. Polly and Digory bond over their difficult family circumstances and become firm friends.

While we don't see very much of her in The Magician's Nephew, first published by C.S. Lewis in 1955 as a prequel to his previous five Chronicles of Narnia novels, Digory's mother has a key role in the story. Due to her illness, Mabel Kirke has moved herself and her son into her sister Letty's house, shared by Letty's enigmatic husband, the titular "magician." While she spends much of the book bedridden, Mabel represents everything Digory loves and fights to save.

During their adventure, Digory faces many temptations that could cure his mother, most notably fighting back against the corruption of Jadis, who would one day become the White Witch of Narnia. Mabel's impending death drives Digory's desperation and vulnerability. Digory almost gives in to temptation, but renounces Jadis' offer and plants the tree that would ward her away from Narnia's south for centuries. In return, Aslan allows him to take a magical apple from Narnia back to Earth to cure his mother.

Despite being barely featured in The Magician's Nephew, Mabel Kirke is perhaps one of the most important figures, as she drives the entire story forward and acts as the emotional core for Digory. She has an even deeper meaning, however, that many people are unaware of, but which Netflix's Narnia: The Magician's Nephew adaptation will finally bring into the spotlight, simply by changing Mabel's name in the upcoming movie to Flora—the name of C.S. Lewis' own mother.

Carey Mulligan Will Play "Flora Kirke" in Narnia: The Magician's Nephew

Academy Award nominee Carey Mulligan joined the cast of Narnia: The Magician's Nephew in May 2025 as Digory's mother, the sister-in-law to Daniel Craig's magical uncle, Andrew. Instead of playing Mabel Kirke, however, Carey Mulligan, best known for her roles in Maestro, Doctor Who, Promising Young Woman, and more, will be portraying a renamed character, Flora Kirke. This will give the character a deeper meaning, as Mulligan's Flora will now be named after C.S. Lewis' own mother, Florence "Flora" Augusta Lewis.

Flora Lewis tragically passed away from cancer in 1908, when C.S. Lewis was only nine years old. The trauma of losing his mother at such a young age inspired the character of Mabel Kirke in 1955's The Magician's Nephew—the young main character's mother who is plagued with a terminal illness, driving the hero to embark on an epic adventure in hopes of finding a cure. In Digory's case, he is successful in curing his mother, whereas C.S. Lewis was powerless to stop his own mother's decline.

Writing The Magician's Nephew allowed C.S. Lewis to symbolically rewrite his own childhood grief, granting the allegory of his mother a miraculous ending in which she is healed. After eating the apple Digory brings home from Narnia, she spends the rest of her days laughing and playing with Digory and Polly before moving out of London and back to the country when her husband returns from India. Writing this story must have been a therapeutic experience for C.S. Lewis.

Now, changing Mabel's name to Flora in Narnia: The Magician's Nephew acts as another beautiful tribute to C.S. Lewis and the inspiration behind the story itself. Just as the real Flora was key in inspiring C.S. Lewis, she will be pivotal in inspiring Digory and Polly to continue their adventure in Narnia: The Magician's Nephew, which will eventually lead to all the incredible events of Lewis' other novels. Notably, the apple that cures Digory's mother also one day provides a way back to Narnia.

After Digory's mother is cured of her terminal illness by the apple from Narnia, Digory and his reformed uncle, no longer dabbling in magic, plant the apple core in the garden, which grows into a grand tree. Years later, the tree blows down, and Digory uses the wood to build a wardrobe—the same one the Pevensie children would one day use to venture into Narnia. This makes Flora's plight in Narnia: The Magician's Nephew the backbone of the entire budding franchise.

Greta Gerwig is expected to return for one other Narnia project for Netflix, while more filmmakers will join to adapt C.S. Lewis' other novels for the new franchise. The next story is expected to be another adaptation of The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, first published in 1950. Adaptations of 1951's Prince Caspian: The Return to Narnia, 1952's The Voyage of the Dawn Treader, 1953's The Silver Chair, 1954's The Horse and His Boy, and 1956's The Last Battle may follow.

The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe features Digory Kirke as the elderly man who takes in the evacuated Pevensie children, so the fact that he will be developed first in Narnia: The Magician's Nephew is very exciting. Beyond just Digory, the fact that his mother will have greater meaning and more of a connection to the book's history is thrilling, and proves just how much care Netflix and Greta Gerwig are taking with this new adaptation.
