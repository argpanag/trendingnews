Highlights

No Bull had 20 points and 20 assists since 1969. The Australian did it, and tied an Aussie record.

  • Josh Giddey had 23 points, 14 rebounds and a career-high 22 assists against Denver
  • His 33rd career triple-double equals Ben Simmons for the most by an Australian
  • Giddey says coach Tiago Splitter told him what he needed to hear

Josh Giddey has done what no Chicago Bulls player has done in almost 60 years, not even Michael Jordan, as he equalled Ben Simmons with 33 triple-doubles in one of the greatest performances in NBA history on Monday, November 23, 2026 (AEDT).

The 24-year-old Australian is the first Bulls player with a 20-point, 20-assist game since Clem Haskins had 23 points and 21 assists against Boston on December 6, 1969. He is also the first Bull with more than 20 assists in a game since Ennis Whatley in 1985, and only Guy Rodgers, with 24 in 1966, has had more for the franchise.

Only two players in NBA history have had +23 points, +14 rebounds and +22 assists in NBA history – Nikola Jokić on March 7, 2025 (31-21-22) – and Giddey.

His 22 assists in the most by any Australian in NBA history.

Giddey had 23 points, 14 rebounds and a career-high 22 assists, but the Chicago Bulls lost 151-145 in overtime to the Denver Nuggets at the United Center, falling to 4-12.

"It gives you confidence as a player, but I mean, the assists, it's the other guys that do the work," Giddey said.

"I think I just got to get them the ball and they make shots and make plays. So, my job as a point guard is easy with the group we have, and the offence we run.

"There's a lot of actions, a lot of, you know, free-flow movement where a lot of guys are getting different looks, whether it's, you know, Buddy (Hield) shooting threes or it's Sticks on pick-and-pops, and it's guys cutting to the rim.

"We've got a lot of different weapons offensively, and it's my job to be able to put them in positions to be successful, and they take care of the rest."

It came two days after Giddey scored two points in 23 minutes and was benched for the fourth quarter of Chicago's loss to Washington. Against Denver, he played 41 minutes and shot 10-from-17, including 2-from-5 from three, with five turnovers.

He scored 11 of his points in the fourth quarter, including Chicago's last five of regulation. Giddey said he spoke with Bulls head coach Tiago Splitter about accountability.

"That's one of the things about him," Giddey said.

"I think he's a really good communicator and he probably, as a former player, understands it as well, and he can relate to us as players a lot more, and he was really good about it.

"It's not the first time, but he doesn't sugarcoat it. He tells you what you need to hear, not what you want to hear.

"Obviously you want to be out there trying to help your team win games, but I wasn't surprised.

"I wasn't sitting there confused as to why I wasn't out there.

"He hit it on the head the next day, and we move forward and moved on to tonight.

"It's a thing as a player you appreciate, a lot more after the fact. In the moment, you probably don't want to hear those things, but looking back now, it's obviously what I need to hear, and he's really great at communicating things like that."

Giddey's previous career high was 19 assists, against Cleveland in March.

It was his second triple-double of the season and the 33rd of his career, level with Ben Simmons for the most by an Australian. Giddey is the youngest player in NBA history to record a triple-double, at 19 years, 84 days, on January 2, 2022, for the Oklahoma City Thunder against the Dallas Mavericks.

He is averaging 10.8 points, 6.4 rebounds, and 9.3 assists in 16 games this season after an inconsistent start.

His 22 assists fed a Chicago Bulls side that tied the franchise record with 25 threes, from 55 attempts. Buddy Hield scored 33 with seven threes, Jalen Smith had a career-high 26 and Buzelis 21. Chicago were without their two leading scorers, Norman Powell (hamstring) and Caleb Wilson (broken hand).

The Bulls host the Cleveland Cavaliers and fellow Australian Tyrese Proctor on Tuesday, November 24 at 12pm (AEDT).
