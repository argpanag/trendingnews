  8. 'Impossible to say we're on right path' - McGinnpublished at 22:37 GMT 16 November
    22:37 GMT 16 November

    FT: Switzerland 4-0 Scotland

    Asked if he is "confident that the team is on the right path", Scotland midfielder John McGinn tells BBC Scotland: "Well obviously the trajectory of the results means it's impossible for me to say yes.

    "But we have been here before. Unfortunately, we've been in moments where you need to stick together, show your qualities, players need to come into the group and add more and you need to turn results around, which we have shown before.

    "We've qualified for three out of four major tournaments and if we are going to qualify for a fourth, we need to be much better.

    "We know that, we know what it takes, but you don't have time in football as a bunch of players to do that, so come March we need to be a lot better and improve.

    "The manager can't kick the ball for us, it's up to us to make the right decisions in those moments, execute it, show a bit of quality and be a lot better on the ball, be a lot better off the ball, and that doesn't matter what tactics you have or what formation you play, you need to be a lot better in yourself and not a lot of us can say that tonight."

  16. 'Score doesn't reflect game' - Pocognolipublished at 22:17 GMT 16 November
    22:17 GMT 16 November

    FT: Switzerland 4-0 Scotland

    Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli tells BBC Scotland: "The score is heavy with the 4-0, but I don't think it's a reflection of the game, if I'm really honest and objective.

    "I think we had some very good moments in the game, we started the game like we wanted, aggressive away from home.

    "We could decide to be low on the pitch, but we did what we wanted to plan.

    "I think we played a good first half, we created chances, we were dominant in some moments in the game, and we faced Switzerland with different shapes and adapted well from the first minute.

    "I think we conceded the goal from maybe one of the first chances they had.

    "We also created chances, maybe even closer to the goal, but we missed it, it means we were not clinical in that moment in the box.

    "In the second half, yeah, we started poorly from the first minute, a poor corner we gave away.

    "Then you feel that, OK, we will get punished.

    "But I think when we made the substitutions, the substitutions made an impact, and we should have scored two goals in that period.

    "We got punished for the same reason like in the first half.

    "At the end, it's a bad feeling, the score, but I think that the players try to do their best and create moments that we want to see."

First-half Ferguson own goal sets second-string Swiss side on way

Quick-fire double from Boteli & Sanches at start of second half

Amdouni's late deflected strike seals comfortable win

Scots finish third in group, leaving them in pot three for Euro 2028 qualifying

Scotland's uninspiring Nations League campaign ended with a humbling defeat against much-changed yet still superior Switzerland to bring further questions over the work of head coach Sebastien Pocognoli.

The Scots required victory in St Gallen - as well as a favour from North Macedonia in taking points off Slovenia - to finish second and secure a pot two seeding in next month's Euro 2028 qualifying campaign.

Five minutes into the second half at the Sitterstadion and their part in that deal was dead and buried.

Quick-fire goals from Winsley Boteli and Alvyn Sanches added to Lewis Ferguson's unfortunate - but clumsy - own goal, which undermined Scotland's decent start.

On a historic night, where captain Andy Robertson became the most-capped men's international on his 103rd appearance, the Scots turned in a slipshod showing.

They were back to their old tricks, shooting themselves in the foot through Bologna midfielder Ferguson's mistake, before being sliced and diced by the World Cup quarter-finalists' second-string side.

Boteli's replacement, Zeki Amdouni, added insult to injury late on with a deflected strike, resulting in a damning and potentially damaging result for Pocognoli, who has won two and lost three of his opening six games.

Two days away from the one-year anniversary of their famous victory against Denmark, where the never-say-die attitude was apparent and belief palpable, such qualities appear a stretch away from this easily beatable Scotland side.

Scotland analysis: Pocognoli has lots to ponder

And so Pocognoli's first act is up already.

There was a 10-15 minute period in the first half where the Belgian's promised methods - a solid, united, high press - was at the best the Tartan Army have seen.

A cohesive Scotland team pushed their classy hosts, twice coming close from the heads of Aaron Hickey and John McGinn.

The latter's attempt was particularly tame, while the former went off injured, again. The early signs suggest another hamstring problem. The Brentford right-back just can't catch a break.

Perhaps that's how Pocognoli is feeling, too. Questions surrounding him, his system and his methods will surely only drag on over the next four months before his side play again.

This campaign was understood to be one of learnings, of trial and error. There has been plenty of the latter, but little of the former. Perhaps if Scotland were failing through invention and imagination, there'd be a greater acceptance.

But with one shot on target - that barely memorable - and a handful of wasted opportunities, it was all too lacklustre.

Pocognoli's first four games came in quick succession and, while there wasn't too much grace in this two-game camp, he now has lots of time. And lots to ponder.

Lewis Ferguson won't want to see this one back...
