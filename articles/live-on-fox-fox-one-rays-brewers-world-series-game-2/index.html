LIVE on FOX/FOX One: Rays-Brewers World Series Game 2

TheTools · 25.10.2026 01:02 · source · permalink

MILWAUKEE -- Game 1 of the World Series was so tense and tight that it required two final outs. The Brewers survived an overturned out to hold serve at home, but the visiting Rays made it clear that this Fall Classic, which continues with Game 2 on Saturday night at American Family Field, will be a fight to the finish.​

“Hopefully every game’s like that, to be honest, but we end up on the other side of it,” Rays shortstop Taylor Walls said. “That was a blast. That atmosphere was great. Just the back-and-forth, really feeling like you have to lock in and focus on every pitch, it’s fun competing that way.”

Credit to the Brewers for shaking off the rust of the weeklong wait that came with their quick NLCS clinch. The bright lights and pregame pomp and pageantry of Game 1 didn’t faze the first Brewers team to reach the World Series stage in 44 years. They scored early and fended off the Rays late in a 3-2 win.

In all best-of-seven postseason series, teams taking a 1-0 lead have gone on to win the series 128 of 199 times (64%). In series with the current 2-3-2 format, teams that have taken a 1-0 lead at home have gone on to win the series 70 of 104 times (67%), including the Brewers’ 2026 NLCS sweep over the Dodgers.

But in this Series of similarly styled squads, pulling away won’t be easy for either side. And the Rays have an ace up their sleeve in Game 2 in the form of Drew Rasmussen, an All-Star arm who has been absolutely dialed-in this October.

Now, he gets to go up against the Brewers team that drafted him in the sixth round in 2018 and gave him his first Major League opportunities before trading him to Tampa Bay in 2021.

“Had the Brewers been playing anyone else in the World Series, man, I’d be rooting for them,” Rasmussen said. “It’s a really unique, really cool matchup. I’m excited for this one.”

A ninth-inning tightrope act aside, the Brewers had the better bullpen performance in Game 1, and it made all the difference. With Rasmussen going next-level in these playoffs, it’s possible the Rays have the upper-hand in the starting department in this one, though Milwaukee rookie Logan Henderson is not to be overlooked. The Brewers have won 16 of the 20 games he’s started this year, including the postseason.

And if we learned anything from Game 1, it’s that the weeklong layoff did not affect the Brewers the way it has some other quick pennant clinchers of Fall Classics past.

“Once the game starts, you kind of just feel relieved to just get back to playing baseball, and everything feels normal and comfortable,” Milwaukee captain Christian Yelich said. “And the guys settled in right away and did a great job. So we’ve still got a ways to go, but it’s nice to get that first one.”

If the Brewers get the second one, they’ll become the 58th team to take a 2-0 lead. Of the previous clubs to do it, 80.7% went on to win it all, including 45 of 55 in best-of-seven World Series.

When is the game and how can I watch it?

Game 2 is underway and airing nationally on FOX and FOX Deportes with streaming on FOX One. 

All series are available in the US on MLB.TV with authentication to a participating Pay TV provider. Games also are available live internationally, although not in Canada. Sportsnet is MLB's exclusive English language broadcaster in Canada for every postseason game, while TVA Sports will be covering the entire AL postseason and the World Series in French and Broadcaster RDS will cover the entire NL postseason in French.

Who are the starting pitchers?

Rays: RHP Drew Rasmussen
Key Stat: Rays are 14-1 in his 15 starts since the All-Star break
There was a case to be made for Rasmussen starting Game 1 on full rest, but the Rays prioritized getting him an additional day to recover before sending him to the mound against the team that drafted him and eventually traded him to Tampa Bay. It’s been a successful part of the formula for the dominant right-hander, who has gone 11-0 with a 1.73 ERA, 99 strikeouts and only 18 walks in 93 2/3 innings since the All-Star Game.

The Rays won two of Rasmussen’s first three postseason starts, as he shut down the Yankees in Game 1 of the ALDS then silenced the Guardians in Game 5 of the ALCS. The extra rest has also allowed Rasmussen to work deeper into games, as he has pitched into the seventh inning in each of his three outings this month.

Brewers: RHP Logan Henderson
Key Stat: Brewers are 21-4 in Henderson’s 25 career starts, regular season and postseason, including 2-0 in these playoffs

The Texan is no stranger to the World Series, having attended one game in each of the Astros’ four trips to the Fall Classic between 2017 and 2022, the last of those coming the year after the Brewers made Henderson their fourth-round pick. Injuries have slowed his path to this point, including last year when he was just returning from a forearm injury and was left off the Brewers’ roster for the NLDS and the NLCS.

But he went 11-3 with a 2.47 ERA during the 2026 regular season and has been at the center of the Brewers’ pitching plans in the postseason, starting each Game 2 behind the presumptive NL Cy Young Award winner Jacob Misiorowski. A wild pitch and a pair of errant pickoffs marred Henderson’s NLCS Game 2 start against the Dodgers on Oct. 12; when he allowed four runs (two earned), it marked the first time in 25 career starts, regular season and postseason, that he allowed more than three runs. 

“There’s definitely value in having thrown a couple of games now,” Henderson said. “There’s a lot of feelings you’re feeling the day of. Tensions are going be higher. Adrenaline is going to be higher. But I learned some things about myself within the game that would help moving forward.”

How will the bullpens line up after the starter?

Rays: The Rays should be in pretty good shape after a couple days off, plus a five-inning start by Nick Martinez in Game 1 and an off-day looming on Sunday. They only had to use Steven Matz (for five outs and 31 pitches), Kevin Kelly, Garrett Cleavinger and Cole Sulser on Friday night. That means they have a fully rested and ready Bryan Baker if he’s needed in the ninth inning. The same goes for top lefty Cam Booser, setup man Tyler Wells, left-hander Ian Seymour and any starter who might be held in reserve (like Freddy Peralta or Griffin Jax). Plus, no reliever aside from Matz threw more than 12 pitches.

Brewers: Aaron Ashby, Abner Uribe and Trevor Megill have combined for 28 1/3 of the Brewers’ 44 2/3 bullpen innings so far in the postseason, and they carried the final four innings again in Game 1. Ashby’s 1 2/3 innings doesn’t preclude him from pitching again in Game 2, and the Brewers still have two other quality left-handers in DL Hall and JoJo Romero should they want to save Ashby for something late. He only threw 20 pitches, the same number as Megill.

Any injuries of note? 

Rays: Starter Shane McClanahan was ruled out for the postseason, although he will be ready to go next spring. Otherwise, nothing new. 

Brewers: The biggest question mark for the Brewers on the eve of the World Series was Brandon Woodruff, and that remains the case as he did not appear in Game 1 after being added to the World Series roster. Will he stay healthy if he pitches? Will he perform in his first outing since July 4? He spent Friday night seeking advice from Ashby, since it was Woodruff’s first game in the bullpen since 2018.

Who is hot and who is not?

Rays: Aranda had a tremendous World Series debut, going 3-for-3 with a walk. Simpson also made an impact at the plate with a pair of hits and the back half of a double-steal with pinch-runner Jorge Mateo. Caminero scorched a ball at 112.7 mph, worked a walk and generally appeared to be poised in his at-bats. Mesa continued to struggle at the plate, and he’s hitting just .147 in the postseason. It’s been a similarly tough month for Hicks, who has posted a .491 OPS in the playoffs, and DeLuca, who’s batting .176. Ben Williamson is also just 1-for-11 so far this postseason.

Brewers: Chourio tallied two hits and another RBI, giving him eight RBIs in this postseason and 19 postseason RBIs in his career, extending his all-time record for a player shy of his 23rd birthday. Chourio (2-for-4) and Contreras (2-for-3) became the first Brewers with multiple hits in a World Series game since Paul Molitor in Game 7 in 1982. Contreras leads the Brewers with a 1.184 OPS in the team’s first nine postseason games.

Anything else fans might want to know? 

• With Jacob Misiorowski and Logan Henderson, the Brewers are the third team to start pitchers under the age of 25 in each of the first two games of a World Series, joining the 1972 Cincinnati Reds (Gary Nolan, Ross Grimsley) and the 1966 Baltimore Orioles (Dave McNally, Jim Palmer).

• Game 1 was the first time there were no home runs in the first game of the World Series since 2000 (Mets vs. Yankees). Those are the only two homerless WS Game 1s since the start of 1987. 

• The Rays are now 0-3 in Game 1 of the World Series. However, it’s worth noting that they are 2-0 in Game 2; they beat the Phillies, 4-2, in 2008 and the Dodgers, 6-4, in 2020.

• Simpson was in the No. 3 spot in Tampa Bay’s lineup in Game 1, marking the fourth time a player with zero career home runs (including the regular season and postseason) started a World Series game batting third, according to the Elias Sports Bureau. Simpson joined Ted Cather (1914 World Series Game 2 for the Boston Braves) and Chick Shorten (1916 World Series Games 3 and 5 for the Boston Red Sox).
