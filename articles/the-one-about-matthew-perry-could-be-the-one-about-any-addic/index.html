‘The One About Matthew Perry’ could be the one about any addict you know - The Boston Globe

TheTools · 29.10.2026 02:27 · source · permalink

A dashing but insecure kid becomes a class clown, a hyperactive seeker of laughs and good times. He tries drinking as a teen and discovers that alcohol gets him to a calmer, happier place. He has a go-to prayer: “God, you can do anything you want with me, but please make me famous.” He gets his wish, but that “anything you want with me” part exacts a terrible price.

The new three-part Netflix series “The One About Matthew Perry” celebrates the life of the late “Friends” star, who died in 2023 of a ketamine overdose. It relives the glory days of “Friends” and the white-hot fame it brought to its six stars, and the prosecution and conviction of the people who illegally sold and administered Perry’s ketamine, a drug which, ironically, he had initially used legally to treat his drug and alcohol addictions.

But the series, directed by Mary Robertson and made with the participation of Perry’s sister Mia Perry Bowick, works largely as a portrait of the rapacious, ever-vigilant monster that is addiction itself, and the one-step-forward, two-steps-back dance of death that so many addicts enact until it’s too late to do anything about it. Perry, who prayed for fame, was worn down by it quickly. In and out of rehab and sober living facilities, he generally had trouble making it to one year of sobriety, even as he became a public voice for recovery who never stopped offering support to other addicts.

“I should be dead,” he wrote in his best-selling 2022 memoir, “Friends, Lovers, and the Big Terrible Thing,” and he was right. In 2018 a ruptured colon and a subsequent case of sepsis left him on death’s door, and numerous overdoses and car accidents brought him close. He kept falling down, kept getting back up, until the day he didn’t. Watching “The One About Matthew Perry,” you’re amazed he lasted as long as he did.

The spotlight of fame didn’t help Perry, but in a sense, his story could be that of any addict. That’s because addiction doesn’t care how famous, or handsome, or charming, or successful you might be. It’s always willing to jump into that inner void that needs filling. It’s hanging out, laughing, getting stronger as you flail for the guardrails. Perry had good friends, the TV kind and the real kind, and they were ultimately helpless. The one about Matthew Perry, it seems, could really be about someone you know and love right now.
