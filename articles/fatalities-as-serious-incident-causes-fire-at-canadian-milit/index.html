Fatalities as 'serious incident' causes fire at Canadian military base

TheTools · 18.10.2026 08:07 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Fires

Topic:Fires

A fire at a Canadian military base has killed several people and wounded others. (AP: John Ulan/The Canadian Press)

In short:

Canadian Defense Minister David McGuinty says people have been killed and injured in a serious incident that resulted in a fire on a military base in Alberta.

The blaze started at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright at around 4pm on Saturday, local time.

What's next?

Authorities are still working to determine what happened and expect to provide further information soon.

People have been killed and injured in an incident at a military base in Alberta that caused a major fire, according to Canada's defence minister, while the media is reporting that large numbers were being transported to hospitals.

Fire crews scrambled late into the night as fierce winds whipped flames beyond Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Wainwright and into the surrounding rural area.

It was not immediately clear how many people were killed or injured, but a doctor at a hospital in the city of Edmonton, 180km north-west, told Canada's national broadcaster that patients were being brought to hospitals across the city as healthcare facilities around the base became "completely overwhelmed" by the number of people needing treatment.

Doctors called the disaster a "one in every 15- to 25-year event". (AP: John Ulan/The Canadian Press)

"This number of patients would overwhelm the city of Edmonton," Warren Thirsk, an emergency department physician at the Royal Alexandra Hospital, told CBC News. 

"It is more patients than any of us want to count right now. It's a disaster. It's a one in every 15- to 25-year disaster."

The town of Wainwright, near the base, has about 7,000 residents.

Defense Minister David McGuinty said the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) were involved in investigating the incident and described the situation as "very, very active and delicate." 

He said authorities were still working to determine what happened and expected to provide further information soon.

The RCMP said officers were called shortly after 4pm on Saturday following reports of a fire in a training area at the base.

Firefighters faced a volatile situation. 

Large areas of the province were hit during the day by a windstorm with gusts of up to 100 kilometres an hour.

Wainwright is one of Canada’s busiest military training bases, where soldiers prepare for combat operations. (Reuters: Camden Scott/Canadian Forces)

The wind toppled some trees and sent signs flying and garbage cans rolling down streets.

By 11pm, the Municipal District of Wainwright said on social media the fire was being held, and that property and critical infrastructure were protected. 

There was no evacuation order for residents.

Mr McGuinty, speaking to reporters in Ottawa, confirmed the casualties but had no other details to share.

"I cannot recall such an incident in my short time for sure as a minister, but I cannot recall an incident like this in recent memory," Mr McGuinty said.

"We will have more to say in the coming hours and days."

He said armed forces members were at the scene and that RCMP were also involved.

"This is an active and delicate situation and soon to be an active and delicate investigation,"

Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe said his province was sending assistance to Alberta but did not specify what resources were being deployed.

"Our thoughts are with all the brave women and men affected," Prime Minister Mark Carney said earlier in a social media post.

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith described the fire as "significant and tragic" and said it was affecting many military personnel.

CFB Wainwright is one of Canada's busiest military training bases, where soldiers prepare for combat operations. 

It is home to the Canadian Manoeuvre Training Centre, which conducts realistic battlefield exercises, and the 3rd Canadian Division Training Centre, which provides military training.

Canadian Conservative opposition leader Pierre Poilievre said he had spoken with Mr Carney and the defence minister to offer support to those affected by the fire.

"Pray for Wainwright, everyone," Mr Poilievre said. 

He urged people in the area to follow emergency instructions and keep roads clear for first responders.

The fire also disrupted advance voting ahead of Monday's referendum on whether Alberta should begin a process that could lead to independence from Canada.

Elections Alberta said two polling locations, in Lloydminster and Vermilion, closed early on Saturday and the ballots were transported securely.
