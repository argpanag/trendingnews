Packers will need help down the stretch | NEW Media Inc.

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:47 · source · permalink

Los Angeles Rams defensive tackle Aaron Donald (99) eyes Green Bay Packers quarterback Jordan Love (10) during the Packers 20-3 win over the Rams on Nov. 5, 2023, at Lambeau Field. Love completed 20 of 26 passes for 228 yards and a touchdown, while Donald finished with four tackles and a sack. (Greg Mellis | Green & Gold Express)

The Green Bay Packers are at the halfway point of their season, and it feels a lot like the last four seasons under the stewardship of Matt LaFleur and Jordan Love.

They are 6-4 with some big wins against New England and Dallas in the early portion of the season. They also have some crippling losses and fatal flaws that look destined to end their season.

Love looks every bit the part of a franchise quarterback, and he has some dynamic receivers capable of making plays down the field. Apart from that? The Packers have some things they need to figure out. Here is a report card for the first half of the season.

Pass Offense: B

When Love has time to throw, he has the capability to be as good as any quarterback in the NFL. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden are both likely to surpass 1,000 yards this season. They’ve been as good as group in the NFL.

The disappointment in the passing game once the ball gets in the air has to be Tucker Kraft.

Kraft signed a big extension before the season began and has yet to live up to it. His most recent performance saw him catch one pass for 19 yards. The Packers are paying Kraft like he’s one of the best tight ends in football. He has not played like that.

The biggest problem the Packers have, and what could be their fatal flaw, is their offensive line.

The Packers’ first game of the season saw them get bombarded by Minnesota’s pass rush. Their most recent game was the same with Love taking eight sacks and a multitude of pressures.

If and when the Packers season ends, it will likely be a result of Love taking a beating from a defense that is capable of getting pressure on the quarterback.

Rush Offense: F

Prior to the last two weeks, Green Bay’s run game was on pace to break a record for the least effective ground game in the history of the NFL. The Packers do not have any answers at running back, and their offensive line has been just as bad in opening holes for their running backs as they have protecting the quarterback.

Josh Jacobs missed six games on the commissioner’s exemption list, and at least one more due to a finger injury that required surgery.

The Packers remain at the bottom of the league in rush yards-per-game, and there do not appear to be a lot of answers on the forefront. Perhaps Kaleb Johnson, who has given them a spark in back-to-back weeks could be an answer for them.

For now, however, there is not much to be optimistic about.

Pass Defense: B

This group was supposed to be something that was a major concern coming into the season as Micah Parsons was set to miss the first part of the season recovering from his torn ACL. Now, Parsons’ return is imminent, and the Packers defense has been pretty good for most of the season. Jonathan Gannon has been able to get the most out of his players with Evan Williams and Xavier McKinney both having career years. Javon Bullard has continued to ascend in the slot.

Lukas Van Ness leads the team with 5.5 sacks, and looks like a player who was worth of a first round selection. The interior of the defensive line has been a bit of a disappointment with Devonte Wyatt still not making a lot of plays as a pass rusher.

The cornerback spot appears to have found a player in rookie Brandon Cisse, and Keisean Nixon has played better this season.

The question now is what happens when Parsons returns. Does Gannon take the same approach Jeff Hafley did a season ago, or does he stay on the path he’s traveled so far this season?

Rush Defense: B

Green Bay’s run defense has been a pleasant surprise so far this season. The Packers have only had one bad game, and it came against Bijan Robinson in a Thursday night game after the Packers defense had to play in overtime against the New York Jets.

They gave up more than 100 yards to just two runners this season. Robinson, and Detroit Lions’ star Jahmyr Gibbs. Gibbs required 29 carries to get there, too, so the Packers have done a good job in that regard.

The defensive line could be a cause for concern as the season wears on as they are dangerously thin there. They could get some reinforcements back with Jonathan Ford and Warren Brinson both possibly returning from injured reserve before December.

If they stay healthy, they should be able to get after opposing run games like they have all season long.

Special Teams: D

Hold the plans to make a statue for Cam Achord, the Packers new special teams coordinator.

Trey Smack has missed a league-high nine kicks if you include extra points, and that included a 53-yard field goal in the last game against Minnesota. Smack is like riding a roller coaster, and you never know what you’re going to see in a given week.

Can the Packers afford that as they compete for a playoff push? They have done it in not-so-distant past with Anders Carlson being allowed to kick through his struggles before ultimately ending the season.

The return game was solid before Skyy Moore missed time with injuries, and now he may miss more time thanks to re-injuring his ankle in the recent loss against Minnesota.

Coaching: B

There are going to be a lot of detractors for LaFleur as this season continues. A loud portion of the fanbase was ready to move on from him after last year’s debacle in Chicago ended their season.

The reality is that LaFleur has done a good job getting his team out of the 1-2 hole they dug themselves at the beginning of the season. With the way last year ended, it would have been easy for LaFleur’s team to quit. They didn’t. They fought their way into contention with big wins over Chicago, Dallas and New England.

LaFleur’s teams still make too many mistakes and mental errors that cost them in the biggest spots. That’s not all on him, but it is something that ultimately reflects on the head coach. He’s been here for eight seasons now, and those mistakes appear to be a trend.

So, what’s up with the rest of the season?

The second half of the season does not get any easier. They’ll start with a Wednesday night game against the Los Angeles Rams, who have found their rhythm after a slower start to the season than what was anticipated.

They’ll have home dates at the end of the season with Houston and Detroit, which might be games where the Packers are simply clawing to get into the playoffs. The Buffalo Bills and Chicago Bears will both provide litmus tests for a Green Bay team that has failed the majority of those through the last four years.

For the Packers to avoid another seventh seed and likely first round exit, they’ll likely need to run the table and get some help.

Is that possible? Sure. Is it likely? That’s another story. The most likely scenario for this team is getting into the playoffs as the final seed in the NFC, if they do not miss the dance altogether.
