Survivor Recap: It Takes Two

TheTools · 12.11.2026 02:30 · source · permalink

Survivor

Survivor

I know I do a lot of bitching about the New Era of Survivor (honestly, seven tenths of these recaps are just dick jokes and vitriol), but if we’re going to take one thing with us into the Open Era, I think it might be voting out a pair. It’s an innovation I don’t hate, but that mostly has to do with how this chaos-prone group of contestants decided to play the game. Yes, this episode convinced me. Let’s put one in the win column for Jeffrey Lee Probst.

The beginning of the episode expertly sets up everything about to happen, starting with everyone hating on Mike, a tray of sliced turkey club wraps at a shareholders’ meeting that no one ever bothers to take the cover off of. He sits down with Maggie and tells her the reason he flipped the vote to Brady last episode was that he was last in on the plan to get rid of Kristin, like he’s last in on every vote. She tells him exactly what he needs to hear, “If I was the last person to get information, maybe you should ask yourself why people don’t trust you.” Okay. Great. Now he knows. But what is he going to do about it?

The other consequential thing is Carter’s Salty and Sweet Alliance. He’s sick of all the plans going totally haywire, and he wants a group that is going to stick to a plan no matter what. Dude, don’t you realize you’re in the Open Era where Jeff’s motto is “No Fear!” Wait, that’s the wrong bumper sticker. Don’t you know his motto is, “[Calvin pissing on a Chevy logo.]” Nope, also the wrong sticker. Don’t you know his motto is “permanent uncertainty”? How are you going to stick to plans when everything is uncertain? Anyway, he gets together Kilby, Lewis, Cristian, Maggie, and Thien An and calls it the Salt Alliance because his favorite movie is the 2010 Angelina Jolie action-mystery film, Salt. No, it’s because they’re supposed to be the salt of the earth or something. To solidify this alliance with Cristian, he shows off his hidden immunity idol that he inherited from Rob, who quit on day six because he only got on the show so that his wife would be cast on The Real Housewives of Rhode Island.

As soon as the salt is on the pretzel, they get all twisted up. Everyone is going to be competing in pairs in the next immunity challenge. Yeah, how is that sticking to the plan? What I like about this is that they allow the players to decide what the pairs are going to be and don’t leave it up to chance. They are all unsure whether the pair will be voted out or will be fighting against each other, so they take a very interesting approach to forming the groups. Mike chooses Maggie, thinking that she’s well connected, so if they’re sending the group home, he might get spared because people don’t want to oust her. Carter and Cristian pair up because no one knows they’re working together. Devin and Jelly are bound together by a curse as ancient as the whale immunity idol that they dug up from the seabed. But other than them, most of the other pairs are unlikely and spread across alliances. Kilby and Kristin get together, as do Eric and Thien An and Lewis and Sharonda.

They get to the challenge, and Jeff shares some news. Our Brody Brady decided that he wanted to go home to tend to his thumb (which he partially cut off opening coconuts) and will no longer be on the jury. Are we sure that’s why? I don’t know; this whole stunt smells like the potent cologne of sour grapes. It’s like earlier this season when Jonathan had to leave because he had an owie. I would assume our Brody Brady is not used to losing and was like, “Brody. If this Brody can’t be the $1 million Brody, then this Brody is going home to Brody hospital to get his Brody thumb Brodied back onto his Brody body Brody. I mean, fuck this game, Brody. Don’t they know that I’m Brady the Brody?” And then they put him in the back of a van and drove him to the airport, where he just unceasingly said, “Brody” in different inflections for the entire ride.

Jeff tells everyone that they will be competing in pairs, winning immunity in pairs, and that a pair of players will be going home; however, everyone will be voting as individuals. He also says that immunity idols or other advantages, like Shots in the Dark, if played for one player, work on the whole pair, ensuring both players’ safety.

The challenge happens in three phases, with a pair of two being eliminated at each leg. The first challenge is to stand there and endure Eric’s incessant chattering about The Sopranos, spaghetti (which is the reward), and his fiancé the longest. At one point, Carter even says, “Can we focus up down there?” Kristin and Kilby are eliminated first, along with Maggie and Mike. There is also an advantage for the second part of the challenge for the people who listen to Eric the longest. (JK. They have to balance a ball on a plank.) Lewis and Sharonda make the only smart decision and throw the rest of the challenge, figuring that the energy they save by stepping down is better than whatever they might get from the advantage. This ends up being wise because, just as Carter and Cristian are about to win the whole damn thing by stacking a bunch of puzzle pieces, their stack falls to the ground, opening the door for Lewis and Sharonda to win. See, maybe if they didn’t stay listening to Eric for as long as they did, they might have come through when they needed it.

As they’re getting their necklaces from Jeff, Lewis tells Sharonda that he saw fire in her on day one, and when she was eliminated first in last week’s challenge, he knew that fire was back. Is this an unlikely alliance that is going to grow legs? I sure hope so, particularly because Sharonda has quite the week, as we’ll get to in a minute.

Lewis and Sharonda also have to send someone on a journey, and they choose Cristian. When the boat lands in the middle of the ocean, Cristian reads that he lost his vote, and if he gets a clue on the island, he might win it back but also win an extra vote for tonight. At first it doesn’t really make sense, but then he cuts a rope and a trough full of buoys empties out into the ocean. He soon realizes that some of them have letters on them and collects them all up to figure out what they say: “Dig Under Treemail.” He then leaves all the buoys and goes back to camp. Cristian comes back, tells everyone the truth, goes and digs up his vote and extra vote, with Mike watching over him like the corporate overlord he is.

Now the castaways have to select a pair who is going home, and this is where some deft selection comes into play. Everyone is tired of Mike for never saying anything of note and Eric for chattering more than a set of dentures on a roller coaster. However, no one wants Maggie or Thien An to go home, so both of those pairs are out, as are Lewis and Sharonda, who are immune, leaving only Carter and Cristian, Devin and Jelly, or Kilby and Kristen. Carter is whipping votes against Devin and Jelly for obvious reasons: they’re a close duo who everyone knows is working together. As Jelly accurately points out at tribal, it’s not like they win challenges or have lots of influence; it’s just that they’re clearly besties. Also, if Carter can get rid of both of them, then the Salt fan club has the majority and can coast the rest of the game. Devin and Jelly are going for Carter and Cristian, saying they’re both players no one has a problem with, and that is dangerous. When Carter hears this, he sits down with them and says that the vote is Kristin and they should just go for her. Jelly loves this idea because it means she can hold on to her extra vote and her idol for another week.

This is where our girl Sharonda comes in. She’s sitting down with Thien An and says, “We never talk game, but who are you voting for?” T&A Music Factory tells her Devin and Jelly. Sharonda has been working with both of them; she knows about Jelly’s idol, and she is skeptical of this plan. She doesn’t rat it out, but she tells Devin, “You need to come up with a game plan.” She asks Jelly who she is voting for, and Jelly tells her that everyone told her to vote for Kristin. Sharonda tells her, “That’s just a smoke screen.” This is what we would call on Real Housewives a “sniper from the side.” Sharonda is not trying to get into the mess, but she is trying to use a bit of influence to make sure exactly what she wants to happen happens. As we’re leaving camp for Tribal Council, the edit sets it up as Carter and Cristian versus Devin and Jelly, and whoever plays their idol is going to win.

At Tribal, it’s another live one with lots of whispers and gossiping and everyone not being sure what the plan is and who they can rely on. I usually hate a live tribal, but I’m kind of loving the vibes this season. Is it casting? Did they make this happen by getting us a bunch of nutcases who don’t know what they’re doing? Or is this Jeff’s “No Fear Chevy Peeing Constant Uncertainty” actually doing what it’s supposed to be doing? At one point, as everyone is chattering, he asks Maggie if she even hears the whispers anymore because they’re so constant. She’s basically like, “Jeff, STFU. I can’t hear the 14 different conversations happening around me.” The only thing of consequence that happens is that Christian goes to Eric and gives him his extra vote, saying he loses it if he uses his SITD and wants Eric to play it for Devin and Jelly.

The players are ready to vote, and Kilby, as he has at every Tribal, runs up to the voting booth with his arms outstretched like he’s just been cursed by the witch in Weapons. When Eric votes, he says that he knows this vote expires tonight, but they should have enough of a majority that he doesn’t need to use it. Dude! What is wrong with you? Use it! It’s like if you have a bunch of brown bananas on the counter that you know are going bad, do you just throw them away or do you make banana bread? You get out the walnuts, the chocolate chips, the Pam spray, and you Betty that M-er F-er Crocker. Why let a vote go to waste?

Before Jeff can tally the votes, a lot of advantages have to be played. Kristin tries her SITD, and it comes up “Not Safe.” Cristian was being paranoid all afternoon, and I was readying my whole monologue about how SITD is stupid, and he shouldn’t even think about it or waste his time going for it, and then it comes up SAFE, and he and Carter are protected. Monologue cancelled, I guess. Then Jelly plays her idol to everyone’s surprise, and it is on like Brody Kong. (That is the video game Brady played on the plane ride home while he sulked.)

Every vote comes up Devin and Jelly until the very end, with Jelly even cackling, “You tried it,” as Jeff keeps saying the votes don’t count. There is one vote for Carter and Cristian that also doesn’t count, and one vote left. It’s Kilby and Kristin, and that’s what Kilby gets for being possessed by a witch. We lose two excellent characters and one great player (no, Kilby, it’s not you) in one fell swoop. I feel kind of bad for them, but all in all it is an excellent episode, and that is much more important than keeping either of them around. As we watch the votes during the credits, a few things become clear: Sharonda, still sniping from the side, voted for Devin and Jelly just so she’d be in the majority in case Jelly was too stupid to play her idol. She got what she wanted and is still undercover. Also, Devin and Jelly were smart enough to split their votes in case one of the other pairs had some kind of advantage, which is exactly what happened, making sure that they were the ones choosing who went home no matter the outcome. A fantastic outcome for fans and a great night for the helicopter alliance, which is currently flying high over the rest of the competition.
