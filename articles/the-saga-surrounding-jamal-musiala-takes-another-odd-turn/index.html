The saga surrounding Jamal Musiala takes another odd turn

TheTools · 21.11.2026 17:00 · source · permalink
  • Bayern Munich: Bundesliga

FC Bayern picked a heck of a week to mount a public challenge to one of its core players.

Talk about timing.

Just a day after Bayern Munich legend Karl-Heinz Rummenigge set off a series of bombs when talking about the club’s stance on Jamal Musiala, the club hosted its Annual General Meeting, where club members get an opportunity to sound off.

In this case, a club member who claimed to be a neurologist was critical of the club’s recent comments about Jamal Musiala and the possibility of a rebate. The club member had this to say:

”What I want to address is Jamal Musiala’s situation and the way it has been discussed publicly over the past few days and weeks. I am a doctor myself, specificallya neurologist. I mention this because, in my professional life, I frequently have conversations in which I have to communicate a diagnosis to people. I have enormous respect for Jamal Musiala for speaking about his situation publicly. I know how incredibly difficult that can be for people going through something like this, and in a situation like this, what you need is support.

“Support of the kind expressed by people like Max Eberl and Vincent Kompany, who have stood by him and had his back. What I don’t believe is helpful in a situation like this is publicly calling him out and questioning whether he will ever reach his previous level again, as Mr. Dreesen and Mr. Hoeneß have done, and, above all, as Mr. Rummenigge took to an extreme yesterday. In my view, that has very little to do with ‘Mia san Mia’ and the FC Bayern family.And, dear Jamal, if you happen to see or hear this in any way: I believe in you. Many people believe in you. Show them what you can do. Come back stronger! Thank you very much.”

As expected, public sentiment is rallying around Musiala, but all of the support from fans might not be enough to sway Bayern Munich brass off its stance in wanting some cash back. Support from fans might not be enough to sway the Bayern Munich brass off of its stance in wanting some cash back.
