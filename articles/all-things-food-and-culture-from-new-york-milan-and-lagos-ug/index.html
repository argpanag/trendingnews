All things food and culture from New York, Milan, and Lagos: Ugo Ugochukwu on his Hometown

TheTools · 27.11.2026 18:04 · source · permalink

2026 champion Ugo Ugochukwu talks about growing up in New York, and spending time in Milan and Lagos

2026 champion Ugo Ugochukwu's plans for next year are already taking shape, and while he is looking to the future, we recently sat down with him to talk about the past.

The Campos Racing driver talked about growing up in New York, while also having roots in Italy and Nigeria. From food to music, he takes the best part from each country.

NEW YORK

“I obviously grew up in New York City, so it’s quite a chaotic place, I'm sure most people would see it like that.

“I think for me, growing up there was a bit more normal. You just kind of get used to it, you get used to all the noise and that stuff doesn't really affect you.

“But moving out, I have obviously lived in Italy for at least half of my life now as well, and that's a very different environment, much quieter.

“I grew up on the Lower East Side, so I'd say it's a bit quieter than Midtown. It's not as busy, but not as busy for New York, it's still lots of people.

“But I used to do normal stuff growing up. I went to school and I have strong memories of being in the park with friends and mates and just hanging around, being a kid, nothing crazy.

“We’d play football, go to tennis, and on weekends, movies, I enjoy going to the movies quite a bit.

“New York has quite a lot of culture, everyone knows I like my music, and a lot of great musicians have come from there.”

MILAN AND LAGOS

“Of course Milan is a little bit different. Like I said, a bit quieter. I think for me, obviously, I started racing in Europe quite young. But it was not such a huge transition for me.

“Half of my family is Italian, so I've been coming to Italy since I was very young, and obviously, I speak the language and I've got a few family friends.

“So for me, obviously, it was pretty easy to switch back and forth. I still do between New York and Italy, and I stay there during the main season. But then, of course, I go back to New York during the off season and get to go back home a little bit for a few months.

“I've been back to Nigeria more often before than in the last few years because I haven't been so many times. But it's always cool to go back there and to see my family in Lagos.”

DIFFERENT FOOD CHOICES

“I'm going to be real, I'm eating Italian food in New York. So that's exactly the same, whether I'm in New York or in Italy. But I think New York is quite unique because you get all types of cuisine.

“I do have to say my favourite cuisine in New York has to be Chinese takeaway. So it’s not a posh, fancy restaurant. But Chinese takeaway is definitely my favourite thing to order. Probably a little bit like a cheat meal.

“But Italian food, I'd probably say the best is pasta. I really like, more specifically, Poli Ragu pasta. I'd probably say that's my favourite one. Nigerian food I'd say just jollof rice, chicken, and plantain. Keep it simple.”
