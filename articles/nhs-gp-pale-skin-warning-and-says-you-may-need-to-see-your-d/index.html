NHS GP pale skin warning and says 'you may need to see your doctor'

TheTools · 28.10.2026 10:06 · source · permalink

An NHS GP has issued a warning about 'signs in the face' which many believe indicate a severe iron deficiency - and whether medical attention is required. Dr Punam Krishan, who has featured on BBC Morning Live and competed on Strictly Come Dancing, addressed the matter following widespread online discussion about the topic.

She explained that guidance circulating on social media was implying that numerous common symptoms associated with fatigue could signal something far more concerning. Dr Krishan highlighted the debate surrounding 'ferritin face', stating: "Let's talk about a term that's doing the rounds on social media at the minute. Because if you've seen ferritin face and it has resonated, I want you to hear me out before you go off and buy iron tablets for yourself.

"Lots of women have been sharing pictures of themselves looking pale, dark circles under their eyes, thinning hair, and saying it's down to low iron. Now, in my surgery, women regularly tell me how tired they are, but immediately they explain it all away because, okay, it's probably because I'm busy. I'm a mum. My kids aren't sleep, and it's probably just my hormones."

Dr Krishan cautioned that while people shouldn't dismiss the possibility, they also shouldn't hastily resort to supplements. She added: "When you've got so much going on, it's easy to assume that tiredness is just part of life. So, if you see a picture that looks like you or feels like you, it's easy to just go, I'll go grab some supplements and carry on regardless."

Addressing the matter of iron deficiency, she remarked: "Low iron can make you look pale and it can be associated with hair shedding. And for those who don't know, ferritin is a protein. It's a protein that stores iron in the body. So, it tells us how much iron you've got in reserve.

"Your body uses that iron to make haemoglobin, which carries oxygen around your body and your red blood cells. And if that's too low, we call this anaemia, which can also make you feel very tired and weak and breathless."

She said that people may need to take the initiative and request their doctor to perform tests: "The bit that a lot of people don't often realise is that your iron reserves can actually run low before your haemoglobin drops. So, you can have symptoms at that stage as well. So, if you're being told, well, you're not anaemic, that doesn't always mean that your iron is fine".

"You also need to get your iron levels checked." However, there are additional factors to consider - as anaemia can stem from insufficient B12 or low folate levels, while an underactive thyroid may also trigger fatigue, thinning hair and dry, pale skin.

She explained: "So ferritin face isn't a medical diagnosis as such. Your appearance can give us some clues but it doesn't give us the answers. So if you are feeling tired all the time, you're breathless, you're jaded, your heart's racing, you notice your hair is shedding, people are saying, 'Oh, you look dead pale to me.'".

"Please go and talk to your GP and we can work out which blood test might help. And if your iron is actually low, it's also important to understand why your iron is low because heavy periods, diet, trouble absorbing iron or bleeding elsewhere can also play a part. And we need to address the cause, not just replace the iron.

"And that's why I'm sharing this because taking high doses of iron without knowing your numbers isn't advisable. And importantly, it can delay us finding the real cause."
