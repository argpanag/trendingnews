RJ Mitte, actor best known for Breaking Bad role, dies aged 34

TheTools · 12.10.2026 21:10 · source · permalink

Mitte, who played Walter White Jr on acclaimed show, had cerebral palsy and advocated for disability representation

The Breaking Bad actor RJ Mitte died on Monday aged 34, his representatives confirmed.

Mitte died in Brownsville, Texas. No cause of death was given.

“It is with profound sadness that we share the tragic passing of beloved actor, philanthropist, and passionate disability rights advocate RJ Mitte,” Mitte’s representatives said in a statement. “Drawing from his own experience living with cerebral palsy, Mitte brought authenticity to the role and built a platform on disability awareness and inclusion.”

Mitte played Walter White Jr, the son of Bryan Cranston’s high school chemistry teacher turned drug kingpin, on 56 episodes of the acclaimed AMC show, often cited as one of the best television programs of all time.

Mitte had cerebral palsy, a condition he shared with his Breaking Bad character.

In 2015, he spoke to the Guardian about being a disabled actor who plays disabled characters.

“You accept the risks and you accept these will be the parts you play. But it’s all about how you play those parts – you have to show the inner strength that people have.”

Mitte’s costars paid tribute to him after news of his death broke.

Aaron Paul, who played Jesse Pinkman, said in a statement: “I am at a loss. I am heartbroken over this news. RJ, I love you my friend. You will be missed.”

Dean Norris, who played Walt Jr’s uncle Hank Schrader, wrote on social media: “Though RJ didn’t play my TV son, I always looked out for him as a young kid on set. He was a sweetheart of a young man and will be greatly missed. This is so heartbreaking, I can’t believe it. Thinking of him and his family.”

In addition to Breaking Bad, Mitte had roles on several series, including Switched at Birth and The Guardians of Justice. Just days before his death, he was promoting his new movie, Talking to Strangers, billed as a “romantic tragedy”.

Earlier this month, Mitte appeared on the Joe Vulpis Podcast, speaking in depth about his time on Breaking Bad. He noted his character was based on a real-life friend of the show’s creator, Vince Gilligan.

“It really felt real playing this character and getting into the groove with this character,” Mitte said.

Offscreen, Mitte was an outspoken advocate for disability representation in entertainment, and served in various philanthropic roles, including as an ambassador for United Cerebral Palsy and as president of the Mitte Foundation.

Born in 1992, Mitte was raised in Mississippi by adoptive parents. In a 2016 interview with the Guardian, he said he found out he was adopted when he was eight years old.

“My step-brother told me. He’d overheard my mum and my stepdad at the time talking about it, and he threw it in my face. But I didn’t really care. It didn’t seem relevant, because I never once in my life felt unloved or like I was a burden.”
