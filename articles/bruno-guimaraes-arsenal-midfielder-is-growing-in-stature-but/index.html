Bruno Guimaraes: Arsenal midfielder is growing in stature but Mikel Arteta still wants more from Brazilian ahead of Newcastle return

It's all eyes on Bruno Guimaraes this Saturday. The Newcastle hero who captained the team to cup success returns in Arsenal colours. And he returns as a growing cog in the Gunners' system.

Despite being Newcastle's key man, the Brazilian endured a slow start to life at Arsenal. An early-season injury did not help, nor did the early-season form of Myles Lewis-Skelly in the Brazilian's absence.

But now he is back in the North East as a first-choice option, having impressed in Arsenal's recent league wins over Liverpool and Hull, while also scoring crucial goals against Sunderland and Leeds.

But is Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta satisfied? Not yet.

"He's still not there," said Arteta after the 1-0 win over Hull last time out. "He's much better, he's hitting serious levels now, but we can still take the player to another level."

The comments were a surprise, given Guimaraes had just received a standing ovation from the Arsenal crowd as he was substituted following an impressive display.

In a largely frustrating game for the Gunners before Martin Odegaard's second-half winner, the Brazilian was at the heart of the action. He recorded more touches and passes than any other Arsenal player, while he was equally effective in the defensive side of the game.

When Arteta made attacking substitutions in search of a winner, he took Declan Rice off before Guimaraes. That is how well he was playing - but it is still not enough for the Arsenal manager.

"I told him in the dressing room - he's very good, he had a massive impact, he owned a lot of moments in the game," said Arteta. "But there is still much more to come."

Pushed on those comments this week, he said: "It's about impact, the amount of things he can do to influence the game and own the game."

To work out what Arteta is getting at, it is worth going back to the analysis the Spaniard made of his £75m signing when he first joined.

"A player which obviously has tremendous timing and intuition to win the ball back and progress play very, very fast," said Arteta back in August.

"He's a player that is very comfortable putting people through, moving between the lines, moving in behind."

Winning the ball back and playing it forward very fast are two areas where Guimaraes has excelled so far this season.

From a defensive point of view, only Chelsea's Romeo Lavia has a better ground-duel success rate this season among Premier League midfielders.

His ball-winning ability was no better seen than his roar of delight in front of the Arsenal fans in the first half of the win at Anfield, after he stopped a dangerous Liverpool attack into the penalty area.

But, as Arteta says, Guimaraes also gets the ball forward - and quickly. The numbers back that up too. He ranks very highly among Premier League midfielders for line-breaking passes.

But perhaps this is where Arteta is demanding more from the Brazilian. Rice is one of the top two or three players at line-breaking passes and Guimaraes - who plays with the England midfielder in a double pivot - has slightly lower numbers.

If Rice is the standard in Arteta's eyes, then Guimaraes has work to do to reach that level.

Another area where Guimaraes could improve is scoring goals. He netted nine Premier League goals last season and it was another key skill that Arteta identified back in August.

"He's got a smell and a really good timing to get in the box," he said. "And he has the capacity to finish the actions."

Now, Guimaraes has already shown his goalscoring prowess against Sunderland and Leeds, but they were strikes from the edge of the area. Useful, yes - but they are not classic Guimaraes goals.

Just seven of the Brazilian's Premier League goals for Newcastle came from outside the penalty area, with the remaining 24 coming from inside the penalty box. Guimaraes is yet to show the close-range threat that Arteta rates so much.

He seemingly has a lot to do to reach the levels Rice is hitting - but that is not an easy feat.

In a recent interview with GQ Magazine, Rice revealed how it can take up to a year to get used to Arteta's demands in midfield.

Rice remembered seeing Christian Norgaard - another midfielder who arrived at Arsenal with plenty of Premier League experience - struggle to keep up. "You're not going to grasp this for like six, seven months," Rice recalled about what he said to Norgaard.

Arteta recognises that too. "He's getting better at it," he said this week. "And he will get better at it when the relationships and understanding of what we do increases day by day."

"With our manager, he doesn't care who you are," Rice added. "You're getting detail. You're going into the game knowing everything."

Guimaraes is feeling that harsh side of Arteta right now. Even though he is playing and performing, there is plenty of work still to do.
