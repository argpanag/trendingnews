Behind the grade: Residence Pictures' Vicki Matich on Pierre - Televisual

TheTools · 16.10.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

Residence Pictures’ Colourist Vicki Matich explains the creative process behind the grade on new Channel 4 drama, Pierre.

The approach to grading the new Channel 4 series Pierre (06×48’) produced by The Lighthouse was centred around creating a visual identity that felt dark, sleek and more mature, while still retaining some sense of warmth beneath the darkness., I wanted the grade to reflect the increasingly complex world that London duty solicitor Pierre finds himself navigating, as he investigates institutional corruption and deals with the pressures and complications of his personal life.

From the outset, the nocturnal quality of the series was something we wanted to embrace. The first episode begins and ends at night, which immediately established a visual tone for the show. Rather than treating the night photography as something to neutralise or brighten, we leaned into it and allowed the darkness to become an important part of the show’s identity. The intention was for the images to feel moody, seductive and cinematic to reflect the noir with a twist.

A key part of this process was finding the right balance within the darker palette. We explored warm, muted greens and yellows, which gave the night sequences a richness and depth, particularly for scenes set in darker urban locations such as the vandalised dry cleaners, while avoiding some of the more familiar blue or desaturated treatments often associated with night-time drama. There was an underlying warmth detectable within the grade that helped keep the characters and their world feeling human, as the characters’ stories become increasingly tense and morally complicated.

Texture was also important in creating this atmosphere. I introduced film grain to give the images a more tactile, organic quality, alongside subtle warping, which added another layer of depth and character to the picture. The aim was to create an image that felt cinematic yet reflective of the gritty setting, with enough texture to draw audiences into Pierre’s world.

The grade also needed to work across the different environments and lighting conditions within the series. To help establish a consistent foundation, I developed a bespoke LUT which gave us a starting point for the grade while still leaving enough room to respond to the individual requirements of each scene. Having that foundation was particularly useful in maintaining a cohesive visual identity across the series, while allowing the look to develop naturally as the story progresses.

One of the most interesting aspects of the process was finding ways for the grade to respond to the narrative. Pierre moves through moments of investigation, personal conflict and bursts of action, and the visuals needed to have enough flexibility to shift with those changes in tone and reflect how Pierre becomes physically and psychologically drawn into the investigation. The LUT therefore wasn’t intended to lock the series into one fixed look. Instead, it provided a visual framework that could be pushed or pulled depending on what was happening within the story.

As the revelations unfold, the grade becomes part of that progression. Changes in tone and intensity are supported through subtle shifts in colour, contrast and atmosphere, while still maintaining the distinctive visual language established from the opening episode. It was important that these changes remained nuanced rather than becoming overt visual signposts; the audience should feel the shift in the world and the characters without necessarily being aware of the grading decisions behind it.

The cinematography provided a strong foundation for this approach, giving us an image that could support a more textured and atmospheric treatment in the grade. The most rewarding grading work is often when you are able to build on what is already present in the photography, rather than imposing something completely separate – Pierre allowed us to carefully shape those existing qualities into a cohesive visual identity.

Ultimately, the aim was for the grade to feel intrinsic to the world of Pierre. The darkness, warmth, texture and muted palette all work together to reflect a story that sits in morally complex territory, while retaining enough cinematic richness and intimacy to keep us connected to its characters. It was about finding that balance between a look that felt distinctive and mature, and one that remained completely in service of the story.
