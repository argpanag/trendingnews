How will Reds line up against City?

The recent international break has not been kind to Iraola, with key men Alexander Isak [thigh], Cody Gakpo [ankle] and Jeremy Jacquet [hamstring] having all returned early to the AXA Training Centre with injury.

The France defender will be available, though, to face City, Iraola has since confirmed in his pre-match press conference, although there was less good news about his attacking duo.

"Alex Isak and Cody Gakpo will not play the game. They are not ready. They both don't look like long-term," said the Reds boss, meaning an untimely change of personnel will be required in the forward line.

The pair's absence is a massive blow for Iraola, especially given the understanding Isak and Gakpo have developed so far, while the Swede's thigh injury also leaves a huge hole to fill in the No 9 role after his impressive start to the campaign that has seen him score four times in the Premier League already.

All of which could mean Lewis Koumas, 21, deputising for Isak, with Hugo Ekitike still months away from recovering from his ruptured Achilles.

Will players be able to cope with 'demanding' schedule?

Liverpool now face a tricky run of seven games in three different competitions over the course of the next three weeks that will test both the players' fitness levels, as well as Iraola's own ability to rotate and manage their workloads.

That fixture list includes a daunting trip to take on in-form Brentford, followed by home games with high-flying Brighton & Hove Albion and champions Arsenal in the league, either side of Chelsea visiting Anfield in the League Cup.

Iraola has so far only had to prepare for one league game after a Champions League encounter and it is revealing it was the underwhelming goalless home draw against winless Fulham that came just three days on from an energy-sapping win over Atletico Madrid.

How the Basque navigates this period - and remember he never had to contend with such a packed schedule while at Bournemouth - could be key to how the his side fare during this run, something the Reds boss is all too well aware of.

"We still have to get ready because the schedule that we have now, it's very difficult," he said. "We are playing against top-four teams and then in between Champions League and then in between Chelsea in the Carabao Cup and everything in two, three days.

"So we have to get ready because this next month, after this break, it's going be very demanding for us."

What to do with Wirtz?

The elephant in Liverpool's room continues to be £116m signing Florian Wirtz and even more so after the German's latest anonymous display at Bournemouth just prior to the international break.

"That was one of the poorest I've ever seen him in a Liverpool shirt," said Jamie Carragher of Wirtz's performance at the Vitality on the Jamie Carragher Podcast.

"I think going forward Iraola's got a decision to make here. Wirtz wouldn't be in my Liverpool team. Manchester City at home, for me, he can't be in the team.

"I thought this Bournemouth game was a big game for Wirtz. Because if he doesn't produce [against Bournemouth], he's then going to have a three-week break. By the time we come back, we're into October. Before you know it, it'll be Christmas in six to eight weeks, or it'll feel like we're getting to Christmas. He has to do something!"

With injuries to Gakpo and Isak, though, Iraola will now need his two other £100m attackers - Wirtz and Bradley Barcola - to shine in order to get a result against City, with the Reds boss no doubt hoping Jurgen Klopp's star dust may have rubbed off on his compatriot during the recent international break.

Sky Sports News reporter Mark McAdam, however, is adamant Iraola will get the best out of the playmaker.

"Liverpool beat off Bayern Munich and Man City to sign Wirtz, he was probably one of the most coveted No 10s in world football at the time and Liverpool have attracted him," McAdam said on the latest Sky Sports Premier League podcast.

"So he does not become a bad No 10 overnight, I just think there are some basics there that will need to be sorted - the physicality of the Premier League, the ability to make decisions far quicker than he has ever had to do and what is his best role within that Liverpool side?

"I'm convinced Iraola will have everything within what they do to find the best in him and that is what he has done historically, he has always found the right role for players."

Can the right balance be found in midfield?

Iraola favours a two-man midfield to both screen the back four, as well as break up opposition attacks while starting their own counters, but this very much remains a work in progress.

Interestingly, it is Ryan Gravenberch - such a key figure under previous head coach Arne Slot - who has been the one to miss out, with the Reds boss favouring a partnership of Alexis Mac Allister, revitalised after a slump last season, and Dominik Szoboszlai as his twin No 6s.

And having conceded twice in each of their opening two Premier League games at Newcastle United and home to Nottingham Forest, Liverpool have since kept three straight clean sheets for the first time since the start of their 2024/25 title-winning campaign as the Basque looks to find the right balance in front of the defence.

"He [Iraola] is still trying to strive for that ideal balance in that Liverpool midfield at times," SSN reporter Vinny O'Connor said on the Sky Sports Premier League podcast.

"But with those three clean sheets in the Premier League in the last three games, we are seeing a team that defends the box a lot better and when they get the ball, they get it forward a lot quicker and creating chances as well, which bodes well for the future."

One clear indicator Liverpool's players are buying into the new manager's way of playing is they top the Premier League for most sprints [636] after the opening five games, although conversely they are also bottom when it comes to distance covered [551.36km - but that is often just a reflection of how much possession a team has enjoyed].

Can Iraola get statement Anfield win to kick-start Reds reign?

On the one hand, Iraola heads into Sunday's game having made an unbeaten start to life as Liverpool head coach, making the 44-year-old the first manager not to lose any of his first five league games in charge of Liverpool since Joe Fagan all the way back in September 1983.

However, those opening five top-flight contests have been favourable to the Basque and as we noted earlier, things now start getting harder - starting with the visit of the league leaders this Sunday - meaning we will be able to make a better judgement about his Reds start come November.

One anomaly Iraola will be desperate to correct against City this weekend, however, is the lack of a home league win so far under his tenure - in fact, you have to go all the way back to April 26 against Crystal Palace, five games ago, for their last victory at Anfield.

The new man has masterminded impressive home wins against Atletico Madrid and Tottenham Hotspur in the Champions League and League Cup respectively so far this campaign, but recording a statement Premier League win would be transformative for him.
