  • Clinton expressed regret that more was not done to stop bin Laden, stating that he always believed the al-Qaida leader was the biggest threat.

Former President Bill Clinton said the CIA called off a pre-9/11 plan to capture or kill Osama bin Laden because the agency feared an unsuccessful operation could kill innocent civilians.

"I had authorized the CIA to try to take him out," Clinton said Sunday during an interview with John Catsimatidis on WABC's "Cats Roundtable."

Clinton said he would have preferred bin Laden be captured alive but believed stopping the al-Qaida leader was the overriding priority.

"I was scared, and I was mad because we tried to get him before, and the CIA at the last minute killed the plan that I think might well have succeeded," Clinton said.

The former president said CIA officials feared civilian casualties if the operation failed.

"They were afraid that if they missed, they'd kill a lot of innocent civilians," Clinton said. "They didn't want to run the risk."

Clinton said he did not second-guess the decision at the time but now wishes more had been done to stop bin Laden.

"I wish we had done even more to get him," Clinton said. "I always thought he was our biggest threat."

The 9/11 Commission later documented several efforts during Clinton's presidency to capture or kill bin Laden.

The commission found that Clinton administration officials said the president did want bin Laden dead.

An elaborate 1998 CIA plan to use Afghan tribal fighters to capture bin Laden was abandoned after agency leadership and officials in the field concluded the risk of failure was too high.

Clinton also recalled immediately suspecting bin Laden when he watched the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks unfold on TV while he was in Australia.

"I was watching television in Australia when the second plane hit," Clinton said. "I immediately said that bin Laden did this."

Clinton said his daughter, Chelsea Clinton, was in Lower Manhattan at the time and that he and former first lady Hillary Clinton initially could not reach her.

Bin Laden was killed in May 2011 when U.S. Navy SEALs raided his compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, during the Obama administration.

Clinton also warned that another major terrorist attack on New York remains possible, although he said the U.S. is better prepared than it was before 9/11.

"I think we have to stay on guard about that," Clinton said. "We have to make sure we're getting all the intelligence we can on the issue.

"You only have to miss once," he added. "It's just something that has to be a continuing part of our national security plan."
