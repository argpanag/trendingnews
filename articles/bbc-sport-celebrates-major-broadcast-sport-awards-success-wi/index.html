BBC Sport celebrates major broadcast sport awards success with top honours

TheTools · 06.11.2026 18:01 · source · permalink

Wayne Rooney, Gabby Logan and the viral Harry Kane World Cup interview among BBC Sport's headline winners

BBC Sport enjoyed a successful night at the Broadcast Sport Awards 2026, taking home some of the industry's most prestigious honours, led by victories for Wayne Rooney as Pundit of the Year, Gabby Logan as Sports Presenter of the Year, and TV Moment of the Year for the unforgettable Harry Kane post-match interview that became one of the defining viral moments of the FIFA World Cup 2026.

The awards recognise excellence across sports broadcasting and cap an outstanding year for BBC Sport, which delivered world-class coverage across television, radio, digital and social platforms, bringing audiences closer than ever to the biggest sporting moments in the world.

Wayne Rooney - Pundit of the Year

Wayne Rooney's transformation into one of the most compelling and authentic voices in sports broadcasting was recognised with the Pundit of the Year award.

After first joining BBC Sport's coverage of the FA Cup, Rooney has been on a journey to become a firm audience favourite through his appearances on Match of the Day, earning praise for his insight, honesty and unique perspective as one of football's all-time greats.

In 2026, he firmly established himself as one of the standout voices of BBC Sport's FIFA World Cup coverage, becoming a central part of the team's analysis across television, digital and social platforms throughout the tournament.

The award marks a significant milestone in Rooney's broadcasting journey and reflects the impact he has made with audiences in a remarkably short period of time.

Gabby Logan - Sports Presenter of the Year

Gabby Logan was honoured as Sports Presenter of the Year following another exceptional year at the heart of BBC Sport's biggest events.

From leading coverage of the FIFA World Cup, Six Nations and London Marathon to anchoring European Athletics Championships broadcasts and presenting Match of the Day, Logan's authority, versatility and warmth have once again made her one of the most trusted and respected broadcasters in British sport.

World Cup viral sensation wins TV Moment of the Year

BBC Sport's coverage of England's dramatic 3-2 FIFA World Cup Round of 16 victory over Mexico won TV Moment of the Year for the now iconic post-match interview with Harry Kane.

Conducted by Kelly Somers moments after England's thrilling victory at the Estadio Azteca, the interview instantly captured the nation's attention when an emotional and exhausted Kane appeared to temporarily lose his voice while reflecting on one of England's most dramatic World Cup victories.

The moment quickly became one of the defining viral clips of the tournament, generating millions of views across BBC Sport's platforms. It was further amplified by the reaction from Wayne Rooney, Micah Richards and Joe Hart in the BBC Sport studio, with their laughing response becoming a viral hit in its own right.

The match itself proved to be a historic moment for BBC Sport. England's unforgettable victory sparked the biggest day ever recorded for BBC iPlayer, the BBC Sport website and app, and BBC Sport's social video views.

Despite a one-hour delayed kick-off and a 2am BST start, the match attracted a peak audience of 9.1 million viewers across BBC One and BBC iPlayer, with an average audience of 7.8 million. Rewriting the record books, it also delivered the largest television audience ever recorded for a live UK broadcast between 2am and 4am BST, underlining the extraordinary national interest in England's World Cup journey.

Commentators of the Year - Ed Leigh and Tim Warwood

BBC Sport's winter sports coverage was also recognised on the night as Ed Leigh and Tim Warwood were named Sports Commentators of the Year.

The pair were honoured for their outstanding work across the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics bringing expertise, energy and passion to BBC Sport's coverage and helping audiences connect with some of the Games' most dramatic and memorable moments.

Sir Chris Hoy documentary - Sport Documentary of the Year

Acclaimed documentary Sir Chris Hoy: Cancer, Courage and Me was named Sport Documentary of the Year.

Produced by BBC Sport and BBC Breakfast, the powerful film was recognised for its honest, moving and deeply personal storytelling, as Sir Chris shared his experience following his cancer diagnosis.

The film follows the six-time Olympic gold medalist as he speaks openly about his 2024 terminal cancer diagnosis, his family life, and how he uses his athletic mindset to reframe his purpose and support others.

The documentary resonated with audiences across the UK and demonstrated the unique ability of storytelling to spark national conversation and human connection.

Alex Kay-Jelski, Director of BBC Sport, said: "These awards are a brilliant recognition of the talent, creativity and commitment of colleagues right across BBC Sport. From record-breaking audiences and digital engagement to world-class live events, documentaries and analysis, these awards reflect the ambition we have and our commitment to delivering the very best sports coverage for audiences across the UK."

Shortlisted recognition

BBC Sport's success was also reflected across a number of highly competitive categories, earning shortlist nominations for:

  • Sport Broadcast/Streamer of the Year – BBC Sport
  • Sports Production of the Year – London Marathon
  • Sports Production of the Year (Quadrennial Event) – FIFA World Cup 2026 and Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympic Games
  • Young Talent of the Year (On-Screen) – Amber Sandhu
  • YouTube Channel of the Year – BBC Football (BBC Sport and Little Dot Sport)
  • Sport Podcast of the Year – Football Daily

The awards underline another exceptional year for BBC Sport, which continued to deliver record-breaking audiences, innovative digital content, world-class storytelling and unforgettable moments from the biggest sporting stages on earth.
