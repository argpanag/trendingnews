U2 salutes Dolly Parton with NFL halftime performance of Torn | CBC News

TheTools · 28.11.2026 01:20 · source · permalink

Late music icon co-wrote emotional tune, recorded vocals weeks before her death

U2 offered a moving salute to Dolly Parton on Wednesday night, performing their new song that features the late music icon at the halftime of an NFL game near Los Angeles.

The superstar Irish band delivered a frisson-inducing performance of Torn in which lead singer Bono harmonizes with Parton's haunting vocals.

Images of Parton were projected on massive fabric panels hanging above the band, who performed on a spare, circular stage at mid-field during the halftime of the game between the L.A. Rams and the Green Bay Packers in Inglewood, Calif.

Torn was the only track from the band's new album Carnaval De Luz that made Wednesday night's brief setlist, alongside stalwart hits like Sunday Bloody Sunday, Where the Streets Have No Name and Pride (in the Name of Love).

Last vocal recording 

Parton co-wrote the tender song exploring the strain within a family divided, with U2 and Irish singer-songwriter Johnny McDaid. 

She recorded its vocals in Nashville in June, mere weeks ahead of her death from cancer — making Torn her final recorded vocal performance, according to her official website.

Just ahead of the song's release earlier this month, Parton's team shared on social media a gracious and humble note she'd written to Bono after recording the tune. 

Ahead of the song's release, Parton's representatives posted a brief note the late music icon had sent to U2 lead singer Bono in June, after she'd recorded her vocals. (Dolly Parton/Instagram)

"Hope you like what I did. I did quite a few things. Take from it what you want and what you need, but I love the song, and I think it could be something really special," she wrote.

Bono says Parton had pushed through illness to complete the collaboration.

"When she got ill, she got out of her bed to sing this song. And you can hear that,” he told Rolling Stone in late September, adding that since her death he's struggled to listen to their song.

Torn is the closing track on Carnaval De Luz and also features Canadian record producer and musician Daniel Lanois, a longtime U2 producer and collaborator, playing pedal steel.
