NATO fighter jet cleared to shoot down Russian missile over Poland as Warsaw pushes for US troops

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:08 · source · permalink

Poland's envoy briefed the White House National Security Council and renewed Warsaw's call for permanent U.S. troops

Mark Rutte warns Russia after missiles, suspected drone breach Polish airspace

Fox News anchor Jon Scott reports on NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte warning the alliance will defend every inch of Polish territory after a suspected airspace breach by Russia on ‘Fox Report.’

Poland announced Tuesday it had renewed its push for a permanent U.S. military presence in the country after two Russian airspace incursions in four days, including one in which a Swedish fighter jet under NATO command was cleared to shoot down a Russian cruise missile but held its fire.

The latest incursion came overnight Nov. 24, when a Russian drone briefly entered Polish airspace during attacks on Ukraine, four days after three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles had crossed into Poland, prompting NATO to scramble fighter jets and bolster air defenses along its eastern flank.

Poland's chargé d'affaires in Washington, Bogdan Klich, said Tuesday that he briefed the White House National Security Council on the violations and renewed Warsaw's call to permanently station American troops in the NATO member country.

"Yesterday, I briefed the U.S. National Security Council on the violation of Polish airspace by Russian Kh-101 missiles and once again discussed the issue of the permanent deployment of U.S. troops in Poland," Klich wrote on X.

The new diplomatic push follows the incidents involving Russian assets and heightened concerns that Moscow's war in Ukraine will spill into NATO territory.

The Nov. 20 incursion involved a Swedish JAS 39 Gripen pilot operating under NATO command who was authorized to engage a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, according to Lt. Gen. Ireneusz Nowak, commander of Poland’s Armed Forces Operational Command, as reported by The Kyiv Independent.

NATO's Combined Air Operations Centre in Uedem, Germany, issued conditional authorization to fire, but the pilot could not positively identify the object visually or through the aircraft's onboard electro-optical systems because of severe weather, Nowak said. No weapons were fired.

Polish authorities monitored 11 potentially threatening aerial objects during the attack, three of which entered the country's airspace, according to Nowak.

The Swedish Armed Forces said its jets had tracked several unidentified objects over Poland during the mission, which lasted several hours under difficult weather conditions.

Polish F-16 fighter jets, a Swedish Saab 340 early warning aircraft, a Dutch aerial refueling aircraft and ground-based air defense systems were also supporting the operation.

Polish prosecutors confirmed Sunday that debris recovered at three locations belonged to Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, revising an initial military assessment that one of the objects might have been a drone, according to reports.

Preliminary findings indicated the missiles were manufactured in October or November 2026 at a factory in Dubna in Russia's Moscow region, prosecutors said.

The second incursion happened overnight Nov. 24, when a Russian drone entered Polish airspace from Ukraine during another wave of Russian strikes on Ukraine.

Polish forces tracked the unmanned aircraft and received authorization to shoot it down but did not fire after it changed course and headed east, citing potential risks to civilians and property on the ground.

Deputy Defense Minister Paweł Zalewski said Polish authorities believed the violation was deliberate.

"It's hard to imagine this being a coincidence, so we assume it was an intentional act," Zalewski told RMF FM, according to Reuters.

After an emergency meeting of the North Atlantic Council on Monday, Nov. 23, NATO's supreme allied commander in Europe went on to authorize the forward deployment of an Airborne Warning and Control System, or AWACS, aircraft to Poland.

"All Allies stand in full solidarity with Poland. NATO remains united, ready, and committed to defending against any threat," NATO spokesperson Allison Hart said on X and as Warsaw summoned Russia's ambassador, according to Reuters.

In September, Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said U.S. and Polish officials were working toward an agreement that could accommodate up to 10,000 American troops.

Roughly 7,000 U.S. troops were stationed in Poland on a rotational basis at the time while a permanent installation would expand Washington's military commitment along NATO's eastern flank.

"We want the kind of U.S. presence that would reassure us but not be threatening to Russia," Sikorski said during a Council on Foreign Relations event in New York, Bloomberg reported.
