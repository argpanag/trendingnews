Marcos opens ASEAN Summit, urges unity amid global challenges

Leaders of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) strike the traditional handshake pose during the formal opening of the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits at the Philippine International Convention Center in Pasay City on Sunday (Nov. 15, 2026). The gesture reflects the regional bloc’s unity, solidarity, and shared commitment to cooperation. (RTVM screenshot)

MANILA – A call for regional solidarity, unity and collective action set the tone as President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. formally opened the 49th Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit and Related Summits on Sunday.

 

During the opening ceremony at the Philippine International Convention Center (PICC) in Pasay City, Marcos underscored the importance of regional cooperation in navigating mounting geopolitical tensions, economic uncertainties, technological advances, climate change, and transnational threats.

 

“We differ in size, history, culture, and circumstance, but we come as equals, carrying the hopes of our peoples and the shared responsibility to transform them into a common purpose. We come from different shores, yet we have chosen to travel forward together,” he said.

 

Marcos said ASEAN unity is crucial as the region confronts increasingly complex challenges.

 

He recalled that during the ASEAN Summit in Cebu earlier this year, developments in the Middle East threatened regional stability and disrupted transportation, energy and food supplies.

 

Despite these challenges, ASEAN member-states reached a consensus to work together and forge a decisive regional response, he said.

 

“Refusing to be distracted, we reconfigured the summit, focused on fundamentals, and began the work that faced us,” Marcos said.

 

“ASEAN responded quickly, rethinking, recalibrating, and redesigning, while keeping our longer-term work at center; this collective work served us well and enabled us to focus on the task before us,” he added.

 

Marcos stressed that no single member state has all the resources, capabilities, or solutions needed to address the challenges and opportunities confronting the region.

 

“But where national capacities are tested, regional strength reinforces,” he said.

 

3 strategic priorities

 

Marcos said the Philippines’ 2026 ASEAN chairship is anchored on three strategic priorities: peace and security anchors, prosperity corridors, and people empowerment.

 

These priorities, he said, align with the implementation of the ASEAN Community Vision 2045, which sets the region’s long-term direction toward a peaceful, prosperous, and resilient community.

 

Marcos emphasized that peace provides the stability needed for economic growth, while prosperity enables people to enjoy better lives.

 

“At the heart of our community are our peoples and the very reason that ASEAN exists,” he said.

 

Under its chairship, the Philippines has advanced initiatives to strengthen maritime cooperation and combat trafficking, cybercrime, online scams and other transnational threats.

 

The country has also pushed for closer economic integration through the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement, progress on the ASEAN Power Grid, stronger trade and supply chain linkages, and support for micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs).

 

The Philippines has likewise sought stronger engagement with dialogue partners and other countries to develop coordinated responses to regional and global challenges.

 

“Evidence that ASEAN's influence rests not only on our collective work, but on the enduring truths and the principles that we uphold,” Marcos said.

 

“And so, over the coming days, alongside our external partners, the choices we will make shape how ASEAN meets the challenges of today, seizes the opportunities of tomorrow and prepares the region for the coming generations,” he added.

 

Beyond regional cooperation

 

With the theme “Navigating Our Future, Together,” the Philippines’ chairship underscores the importance of collective leadership in keeping ASEAN responsive to the changing regional and global landscape.

 

The summit brings together leaders of ASEAN member-states and their dialogue partners to advance regional priorities and strengthen partnerships.

 

Beyond high-level discussions and diplomatic engagements, the Philippines is also using its chairship to showcase its cultural heritage, tourism destinations, local industries and Filipino hospitality.

 

ASEAN leaders rode electric tricycles to the plenary hall for the summit's formal opening.

 

At the International Media Center at the World Trade Center Metro Manila in Pasay City, an Experiential Pavilion welcomes local and foreign media delegates with products, coffee, food, and cultural offerings from Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao.

 

These initiatives underscore the administration’s efforts to make the ASEAN gathering not only a diplomatic milestone but also a platform to promote the Philippines’ tourism, culture, and economic potential. (PNA)
