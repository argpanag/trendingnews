Cost blowout ‘a monument to incompetence’

TheTools · 20.10.2026 19:06 · source · permalink

! -- meta property="og:image" content="https://wellington.scoop.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Wellington.Scoop_.jpeg" -->

by Ben McNulty
Most decisions we make around the Council table aren’t traumatic. The Town Hall is an exception. Today’s lessons learned review is beyond confronting.

The project was poorly scoped. Neither the scale nor complexity of works required was properly understood before construction commenced.

The original design was highly deficient yet the contract was awarded despite an incomplete design, and then contractors sat around being paid to do essentially nothing while the design was fixed.

Councillors were kept in the dark for months after finding out costs would blowout in 2023, and there was no advocacy to the government to provide WCC legislative flexibility.

The advice was frank. The contract never should have been signed in 2018.

The assurance out of today is that there have been lessons learned and integrated into how the council operates moving forward.

Our Triennium Plan contains multiple clauses that would stop a Town Hall repeat.

The Chief Executive has made clear councillors will not be left in the dark on project blowouts and we’ve begun to make changes to get councillors onto the project boards of our major construction projects.

Those responsible for the bad advice are long gone. Nothing criminal or fraudulent occurred in the initial stages of the project.

I want to thank our officers who fronted up today for the review. They provided free, frank and direct advice and didn’t shrink from calling out problems.

I appreciate however that all of that is cold comfort. Every single Wellingtonian will put in close to $1,400 for the Town Hall rebuild – closer to $5,000 when interest and depreciation are accounted for.

The Wellington Town Hall will forever be a monument to incompetence. This one project represents over 20% of our total borrowings.

It can never be allowed to happen again.

Is this review being made public? I assume an OIA could be made if not?

Is there a public report or am I setting off on yet another LGOIMA hunt. Probably with everyone else.

Finally there is some acknowledgement of the degree of mismanagement observed by the ratepayers of this local authority.
Many of us have alluded to such over many projects proposed and undertaken over the past years.
Acknowledging mismanagement on the Town Hall project raises a subsequent question of who was/were responsible and what has been done to ensure such mismanagement will not happen again.
Given the period when this has been going on, it could be reasonable to assume that this approach would be almost ingrained within the staff. Now it is in the open we will watch to see how future projects are managed.

This isn’t news to those of us ratepayers who’ve been paying attention.
In the interests of transparency, I do hope there is a public report.
