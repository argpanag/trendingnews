6 people killed after 3-alarm fire destroys Sunset Park home, injures residents and firefighters

TheTools · 05.11.2026 09:48 · source · permalink

SUNSET PARK, Brooklyn (WABC) -- Six people died, and several others were injured after a three-alarm fire tore through a home in Sunset Park, Brooklyn, early Thursday morning.

The fire broke out just before 1:40 a.m. at a home on 58th Street. Flames shot from the windows as firefighters battled the blaze, which officials said appears to have started on the second floor before spreading throughout the two-story building. The fire did spread to a neighboring home.

Four adults were pronounced dead at the scene. They will have to be identified by the Medical Examiner. Two others, an Asian man and a woman, died while being transported to the hospital.

Four residents and three firefighters suffered non-life-threatening injuries.

Among the injured, a man went to Staten Island University Hospital with burns to his feet, and a woman went to Maimonides for treatment of smoke inhalation.

"This was a devastating fire. It will take a long time for this neighborhood to recover from their immense grief," Mayor Zohran Mamdani said.

Firefighters remained at the scene Thursday morning after bringing the fire under control. They had arrived within 3 minutes and 18 seconds. Crews were seen pouring water onto the home as thick smoke filled the air. Ladders extended above the building while firefighters worked to extinguish the flames and searched for any fire hidden behind walls and siding.

The damage to the home was extensive. The front of the building was heavily charred, with much of its exterior destroyed.

There was a fear among firefighters that the fire could spread down the entire row of homes on the street because they are "row-frame. They are entirely made of wood," officials said.

Firefighters rescued two people who were trapped inside behind window bars.

Two lithium-ion batteries were found at the scene, and investigators are working to determine whether they played any role in the fire. The cause remains under investigation.

The fire displaced eight residents. The American Red Cross is assisting those left homeless by the blaze with temporary housing in hotels.

Mayor Mamdani appeared on a television news broadcast before his press conference Thursday morning and praised the efforts of the FDNY.

"We are currently tracking this fire that claimed the lives of six New Yorkers. It is devastating, and it is a reflection of a number of things. One, I want to always acknowledge the courage of our firefighters. who go in every single day, who go in every single day, no matter what the level of danger is, looking to save people's lives," Mamdani said. "And two, the importance of fire safety in this time. We are investigating what the cause of this fire is, but I can tell you that e-bike fires as a whole have increased to more than 400 fires this year. They are something that can cause people to lose their lives as well as their livelihoods. We are really looking at every avenue we have to keep them safe."

Before this fire, seven people had died in lithium-ion battery fires so far this year. Four of them, a mother and three children, died in a fire last week in the Bronx.
