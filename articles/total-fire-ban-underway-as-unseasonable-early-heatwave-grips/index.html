Total fire ban underway as unseasonable early heatwave grips Sydney

TheTools · 04.09.2026 07:18 · source · permalink
  • Australia
  • NSW
  • Weather

Updated September 4, 2026 - 11.30pm. First published at 7.18am

A total fire ban is under way in the greater Sydney region as abnormally warm spring weather grips the city, even as snow is predicted in other parts of NSW.

Saturday’sban was made ahead of hot, windy conditions forecast to drive the area’s bushfire threat to extreme levels.

A bushfire started to burn at Calga on the NSW central coast on Saturday afternoon. 9News

A bushfire started to burn at Calga in the NSW central coast region on Saturday afternoon.

The blaze, which is currently three hectares, is burning in a south-easterly direction towards the Pacific Motorway.

Firefighters, assisted by water bombing aircraft, are working to slow the fire’s spread.

The fire is currently burning at a watch and act level and those in the area of Calga should prepare now.

Residents should keep up to date on the NSW Rural Fire Service Fires Near Me website.

Less than a week into an unseasonably warm spring, millions have faced the hottest start to the season in more than 50 years, with some parts of Sydney bracing for temperatures as hot as 33 degrees on Saturday.

The last time the city reached above 24 degrees in the first few days of spring was in 1969.

Weatherzone’s Ben Domensino said if Sydney did reach 30 degrees on Saturday, it would be for the first time in six months, and only the fifth time on record that a 30-degree day has happened this early in spring.

“In records dating back to 1859, Sydney’s Observatory Hill weather station has only reached 30 degrees three times in August and once during the first five days of September,” he wrote.

The NSW Rural Fire Service (RFS) issued the warning ahead of the dangerously warm weather, urging residents living near bushland to prepare their homes and review their emergency survival plans.

Father’s Day is set for sunny skies, though temperatures will drop significantly to a top of 23 degrees, however a moderate fire danger will remain in place.

Under a total fire ban, all open flames are strictly prohibited, and all fire permits are suspended.

The fire ban comes as hot and windy weather is expected to drive bushfire risks to extreme levels. AAP

Gas and electric barbecues may still be used, as long as strict safety guidelines are adhered to.

RFS officials warned property owners whose homes are not fully prepared should consider leaving for a safer location well before any fire breaks out.

“Take action now to protect your life and property,” the NSW RFS said in a statement.

“These are dangerous fire conditions.

“If you and your property are not prepared to the highest level, go to a safer location well before the fire impacts.”

Coastal wind warnings are also in place for the Hunter Coast, Illawarra Coast, Batemans Coast and Eden Coast.

Travellers are also advised to reconsider non-essential trips through high-risk bushland areas throughout Saturday.

In addition to community warnings, the RFS indicated that Harvest Safety Alerts may be issued to agricultural operators, and workers are urged to monitor local conditions closely.

In a stark contrast, the state’s south is expected to cop snowfall that same day.

Thredbo’s Top Station, which is Australia’s highest weather station, could receive snowfall on Saturday afternoon, and that dusting of frost could also extend to lower parts of the mountain and even Thredbo village overnight.

Further south, Melbourne is not expected to exceed 20 degrees on Saturday, with a string of cooler mid-teen maximums following the passage of a cold front.

As the first week of spring comes to an end, forecasters say an El Niño system will drive temperatures above average across Victoria, Tasmania, South Australia, NSW, the ACT and Queensland.
