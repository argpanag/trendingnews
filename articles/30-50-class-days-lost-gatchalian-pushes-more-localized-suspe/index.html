30-50 CLASS DAYS LOST; GATCHALIAN PUSHES MORE LOCALIZED SUSPENSIONS

AN average of 30 to 50 class days out of the 180-day school calendar were lost because of class suspensions over the past two years, according to Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian, who called for more localized and targeted suspensions instead of broad shutdowns of face-to-face classes.

Gatchalian cited a study by the Second Congressional Commission on Education, or EDCOM II, in saying that an average of 30 to 50 class days were lost to suspensions during the period.

His office also said class suspensions disrupted face-to-face learning in various parts of the country on at least 20 weekdays over the past 30 days.

“This is concerning. While we know that class suspensions seek to ensure the safety of learners and teachers, widespread disruption to classes can aggravate learning loss,” Gatchalian said.

He said classes should be allowed to continue in schools or areas that are unaffected and considered safe.

“Bagama’t tinitiyak natin ang kapakanan ng ating mga mag-aaral tuwing may kalamidad, kailangan din nating tiyaking magpapatuloy ang edukasyon sa mga ligtas na lugar,” he added.

Gatchalian is also pushing Senate Bill No. 53, or the 21st Century School Boards Act, which seeks to strengthen the role of local school boards in basic education.

Under the proposal, local school boards would be responsible for timely, organized and localized measures to keep basic education going during disasters, calamities and emergencies.
