JPMorganChase, Mastercard, Visa report cyberattack on third-party service providers

TheTools · 21.11.2026 01:19 · source · permalink

Payment services were unaffected, the companies said.

JPMorganChase said Thursday morning that "third parties who provide services" to the banking giant "were the target of a cyberattack."

"Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses," the statement continued.

"There is no disruption to our services," according to the statement.

JPMorganChase, the largest U.S. bank, did not disclose when the cyberattack occurred or name the third-party companies that were targeted.

"Where appropriate, we will contact affected customers directly and offer support, including complimentary credit monitoring," the company said in its statement.

JPMorganChase said on Monday that it experienced technical issues that triggered online delays across several major U.S. banks and blamed the outage on a "software bug." The company said Thursday that it found "no connection to service disruptions some customers experienced earlier this week" regarding the third-party provider data breach.

Mastercard also reported on Thursday that one of its third-party providers was hacked, while Visa said earlier this week that it was aware that one of its third-party providers experienced a data breach.

"They are associated with an incident involving a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific," Visa said in its statement. "Visa’s systems were not involved and were not impacted. No payments data such as card numbers were affected."

"We were recently informed of an incident at a third-party provider that resulted in the exposure of [a] limited amount of email addresses – non-payments information – from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program," Mastercard said in its statement Thursday.

"There is no impact to the Mastercard payment network or the ability for people to use their cards," Mastercard further said. "At this time, the exposure has been contained, and our teams are working with the provider to support the impacted cardholders as needed."

There have been several recent tech outages across the nation that affected air travel, email and hotel reservations, and even slowed classified internal communications at the National Security Agency.

Top Trump administration officials continued this week to insist that there is no evidence that those outages were the result of cyberattacks.
