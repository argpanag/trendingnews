BBC Young Musician 2026 won by 18-year-old violinist Sofia Demetriades

TheTools · 02.11.2026 00:36 · source · permalink

Sofia took the prestigious title after impressing judges Joanna MacGregor, Ben Gernon and Errollyn Wallen

On Sunday 1 November, eighteen-year-old violinist Sofia Demetriades, from London, was named winner of BBC Young Musician 2026. The enthralling final, which took place at the Bristol Beacon on 15 October and is available to enjoy now on BBC iPlayer and BBC Sounds, saw Sofia captivate with a performance of Tchaikovsky's Violin Concerto, accompanied by the BBC National Orchestra of Wales conducted by Alpesh Chauhan.

Sofia studies with So-Ock Kim and has just started her undergraduate degree at the Royal Academy of Music in London, having previously attended the RAM Junior Department and having taken part in Beare’s Chamber Music. Her past accolades include winning first prize at both the Haslemere International String Competition and the Lipinski-Wieniawski International Violin Competition. She has performed as a soloist across the UK, Europe, and Canada. Sofia is supported as a student by the Beare’s Cultural Trust.

In the final, Sofia competed against recorder player Alma Nunez Debretzeni (17) from St Albans, who studies at Parmiter’s School in Hertfordshire and at the RAM Junior Department, and saxophonist Kezia Colton (18) from London, who studied at the Royal College of Music Junior Department in London before recently taking up a course at CRR Conservatoire à rayonnement régional de Paris - Ida Rubinstein.

Winner Sofia Demetriades says: “I am so honoured and thrilled to have been a part of BBC Young Musician. I loved playing in the quarter-final on my eighteenth birthday. I didn’t think I could top that, but to go on to be crowned winner is my absolute dream! Thank you so much to the judges, my teacher So-Ock Kim, J&A Beare Cultural Trust for the support and generous loan of the Vuillaume violin, the Royal Academy of Music and everyone who supported me on this journey. It was pure delight to share the stage with BBC NOW, Alpesh Chauhan and my fellow finalists - I really hope to play alongside them again soon.”

BBC Young Musician 2026 judge Joanna MacGregor says: “Sofia’s artistry is truly extraordinary: her innate sense of colour, musical style and architecture combines with an effortless technique, and a joy for whatever music she’s playing. In a competition full of gifted and dedicated young musicians, Sofia’s sensitivity and sophistication stood out in every round. She’s a brilliant communicator, whether playing delicately intricate Szymanowski or the gorgeous, big-boned romanticism of Tchaikovsky’s violin concerto, which she led in the Final with authority and thrilling virtuosity. I’m very excited to watch Sofia’s musicianship develop even further in the years ahead.”

Suzy Klein, Head of BBC Arts and Classical Music TV, says: “Our warmest congratulations to Sofia, and to all the inspiring musicians who took part in this year’s competition. For almost half a century, BBC Young Musician has been a byword for excellence in classical performance, showcasing extraordinary musical talent and offering a unique platform to discover the next generation of British classical music stars. It’s been ten years since an unforgettable final which brought Sheku Kanneh-Mason, Jess Gillam and Ben Goldscheider to public acclaim, and together the three of them performed beautifully at The Proms this summer. If their example is anything to go by, this year’s Young Musician finalists have a very bright future ahead of them and we look forward to seeing what they do next.”

The final was presented by broadcaster and writer Petroc Trelawny, and it was judged by pianist and composer Joanna MacGregor, conductor and broadcaster Ben Gernon, and celebrated composer and Master of the King’s Music, Errollyn Wallen.

Sofia Demetriades follows in the footsteps of pianist Ryan Wang, who won BBC Young Musician 2024, and celebrated classical music stars such as cellist Sheku Kanneh-Mason, violinist Nicola Benedetti, trumpeter Alison Balsom and pianist Benjamin Grosvenor.
