Golden Eagle stunner as wonder mare fails to fire: ‘Hard to watch’

TheTools · 31.10.2026 06:14 · source · permalink

Skyhook has edged out Decorum in a photo finish to win the Group 1 Golden Eagle (1500m) at Randwick on Saturday, but the story of the race was hot favourite Sheza Alibi’s disappointing run.

Sheza Alibi jumped at $1.50 but got caught well back in the field and couldn’t get a run. Jockey Luke Nolen tried multiple times to steer the wonder mare to the outside, but simply didn’t have the room.

With the Peter Moody-trained superstar out of contention, Skyhook and Decorum burst clear on the home straight in a thrilling finish.

A photo was needed to separate the pair with Skyhook ($10) getting the win, while Beiwacht completed the top three.

While Skyhook gets the plaudits, the run of Sheza Alibi was “hard to watch”, according to Channel 7 legend Bruce McAvaney.

“Sheza Alibi, trainwreck by the way, we’ll talk about that for a while,” Bruce McAvaney said on Seven.

“He wants to come out, he can’t get out, he’s got nowhere to go. He can’t go to the inside, there’s no galloping room.

“He resigns himself to pulling the mare back and effectively pulling her out of the race .

“It was very hard to watch.”

Trainer Peter Moody looked on in disbelief before confirming the mare will be spelled until Autumn.

“We had a barrier trial didn’t we, along way to come,” he told Seven with a wry smile.

“You can’t jump over them. Got a beautiful run in transit, every other race they have opened up and fanned out but they didn’t in this one.”

Earlier at Randwick, Stand My Ground had just scored a first-up win in the $500,000 Four Pillars Midway (1500m) when Marc Chevalier revealed his next project.

“We are already thinking about the Five Diamonds, 2027 – but we might have a few runs before,’‘ Chevalier said.

And it will pay not to disregard Chevalier’s latest ambitious plan for Stand My Ground as it was an outstanding training effort to have the mare ready to win the Four Pillars.

Chevalier revealed he had his eyes on the Four Pillars prize from the moment his filly ran unplaced behind the ill-fated Ohope Wins in the ATC Australian Oaks at Randwick last April.

“We have been preparing this mare for (the Four Pillars) the past six months,” Chevalier said.

“After the Oaks, we decided to give her a good break, bring her back in 15 weeks before the Four Pillars and we would get her ready off two (barrier) trials and she would be ready to win.

“Everything has been spot on and this is a very sweet feeling.’‘

Stand My Ground ($10), well ridden by Tyler Schiller, held off the late surge from Cuban Cigar ($7) to win by a half head with Magical Moments ($8) just over two lengths away third.

Dusty Bay, the $6.50 favourite, led for home but faded for 10th while topweight Alabama State ($7) raced outside the leader before finishing seventh.
