Man United star Bryan Mbeumo stays up late to play GTA6

TheTools · 19.11.2026 08:48 · source · permalink

The world has been waiting 13 years for GTA6 and footballers are not immune from the lure and addictive nature of the game. 

In fact, top flight players are notorious gamers given their amount of free time after training and Bryan Mbeumo was one of many to share his excitement at the midnight release. 

No doubt new Man United boss Mauricio Pochettino would prefer his players to get a solid night's sleep ahead of taking his first training session on Thursday but Mbeumo couldn't resist a late-night session. 

He shared the loading screen of the Rockstar game at midnight on his Instagram story with the word 'historic' as the clock ticked into Thursday morning. 

It remains to be seen whether Mbeumo stayed up into the early hours playing but an estimated three million UK adults were expected to call in sick or take annual leave to play. 

Sleep is vital for athletes to recover and perform at their best and one NBA star even predicted professionals across sports would see their output 'plummet' as a result of GTA's pull. 

Bryan Mbeumo shared this picture on his Instagram story at midnight when GTA went live

Mauricio Pochettino's first morning of training coincides with the release of GTA, played by Mbeumo and plenty of other athletes who looked forward to the late-night release

In a previous video clip online, Mbeumo was being interviewed while on his console and showed he'd pre-ordered the game, saying: 'I'm just waiting now, the game will make me stay at home every single day... after training.' 

Pochettino, who flew on a private jet to Manchester on Wednesday ahead of his first day in the job on Thursday, will no doubt have a keen eye on whether any of his stars are bleary-eyed in training. 

The new boss has an incredibly tight turnaround to prepare for the huge clash with rivals Liverpool on Sunday at Anfield, but perhaps the Reds will also be dealing with GTA-mania. 

Alexander Isak was asked about the game's release while on international duty with Sweden and admitted: 'Yeah, so I think it’ll be difficult to reach me from the 19th, when it comes out. I’ll be locked in. That’s the plan.'

A renowned sleep coach for top athletes, Nick Littlehales, has opined on the effects late-night gaming can have. 

'They get lost in it, it’s an engaging distraction, and it satisfies their competitive edge, but just like mindlessly scrolling YouTube or social media, you need to put your own filters and blockers in place, otherwise you’ll find yourself doing something ridiculously stupid and playing for hours and hours, hurting your performance,' he told Ozoon. 

'We know it’s unrealistic to expect a player to just stop after exactly two hours when they’re in the zone. You have to accept it and ask them: "Are you prepared to sacrifice your nocturnal sleep to be awake and stimulated? Is it worth it?" If they insist on it, we look at scheduling it. Maybe they game late on Tuesdays, Thursdays.

'You must have these open conversations instead of outright banning late-night gaming. If you just throw the book at them, they end up taking sleeping tablets, using snus, and taking unregulated supplements they don’t understand just so they can cope. When you help them manage their gaming, they usually start to self-regulate.'

Arsenal's Declan Rice could be another to be locked into the new GTA having previously spoken about how much gaming helps him switch off. 

He told GQ: 'I can never sleep after night games. It’s impossible. I’ll be that wired. I'll play Call of Duty, Grand Theft Auto, FIFA. Anything. Just play with a load of my friends. Adrenaline, everything. You don’t really settle down.

'I feel like when you’re playing at this level, your mind’s just constantly – it’s just going.'

Football clubs, businesses and even schools are braced for the effects of the new game, with one head teacher in Chelmsford, Essex, entering students into a raffle to win a £10 Amazon voucher in an attempt to stop them bunking off. 

UFC star Conor McGregor announced that he was giving the day off to 400 employees at his various businesses so they could play the game. 

And Real Madrid goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois shared an image of himself playing the game, flying a helicopter after it was launched.  

The astonishing level of anticipation for GTA6 has banked more than £1.14bn in pre-orders alone and is set to become by far the highest grossing game ever made. 

It debuted with a score of 99 on review aggregator website Metacritic – equalling 1998's The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game ever.

Some 2.8million UK adults are set to play the game on launch day alone, according to a Uswitch poll commissioned earlier this month.

Over in the US, a number of athletes were frank in their honesty about how many of them would be addicted to the game. 

Houston Rockets center Steven Adams previously said: 'You guys should anticipate November, the performance of the league, league-wide… GTA is dropping.

'So look at NFL, look at all the leagues and watch them plummet, mate. Just for the month of November, performance is going to go down. It should come back up, though.

'Most of the boys will get it done, hopefully they finish the game. Coaching as well, by the way. The coaching and scouts are going to be horrendous, mate. Just for November, but it should bounce back in December, hopefully. Should be good.'

'I told my mom she might not hear from me for a couple of days,' Los Angeles Rams receiver Tutu Atwell, a Miami native, said with a grin. 'We've been waiting for this one for a long, long time, You know it's going to be a big one.'

Plenty of other athletes share Atwell's excitement, but will prioritize their work on the field.

'I think a lot of athletes are responsible,' said Los Angeles Rams offensive lineman Steve Avila. 'But if you could check guys' sleep scores … I think you'd see that some guys' hours are going to go way down, for sure.'

New York Jets running back Breece Hall concurs: 'Yeah, definitely getting the film done early, and then spending the extra hour or two on the game,' Hall said. 'But you've got to make sure you handle the main thing first before you have some fun.'

There were some technological concerns in the hours leading up to the game's release, with the official PlayStation Network status website yesterday acknowledging some players were unable to access online features.

A statement on the website read: 'You might have difficulty launching games, apps, or network features. We're working to resolve the issue as soon as possible. Thank you for your patience.'

Declan Rice is another star to stay up late gaming as it helps him switch off after big games

Those issues appeared to be cleared up by midnight, with PlayStation declaring all of its services were 'up and running' in the hours after the game went live.

BT and EE called the upcoming launch an 'unprecedented gaming download event' because of the size of the game and the number of people expected to play.

The pre-download drove the second-highest broadband traffic peak ever on BT's networks, only beaten by Arsenal v Atletico Madrid in the Champions League in May, and Virgin Media O2 saw its highest-ever daily data volume last Tuesday.

While the exact number of people who have pre-ordered GTA VI is not known, Sony said it is the most pre-ordered game in the history of PlayStation Store.

Schools have urged pupils not to use the release of the game – which carries an age rating of 18 – as an excuse to skip lessons.

Sales of gaming consoles and accessories surged in the run-up to launch day.

A man walks past a Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA VI) digital billboard advert in Milan - the game's release has become a global event 

Department store chain John Lewis said it had seen a 62 per cent year-on-year increase in sales of gaming consoles and accessories, while retailer Currys reported a 149 per cent increase in PlayStation 5 consoles alone.

GTA VI returns to the neon-soaked streets of Vice City, Rockstar's fictional version of Miami, which has featured in previous instalments of the series.

The game follows two protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, who find themselves caught up in a criminal conspiracy in the fictional state of Leonida, inspired by Florida.

The pair become embroiled in a Bonnie and Clyde-style crime story after 'an easy score goes wrong', leaving them 'forced to rely on each other more than ever if they want to make it out alive'.
