Up Close With Amazon's New Kindles: Sleek Aluminum, Flat Bezels, and Page-Turn Buttons Are Back

TheTools · 01.10.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

Amazon is refreshing almost its entire ereader line, rolling out new versions of the base Kindle, Paperwhite, and Colorsoft. I spent time with the updated Kindle lineup at Amazon's New York offices, and while the new bodies are undeniably sleeker, very little has changed under the hood. The real highlights might actually be the new accessories—especially if you've been longing for physical page-turn buttons. Here's a closer look at what's new, what isn't, and my first impressions.

The Hardware: Thinner, Flatter, and Fancy Metal Options

The base six-inch Kindle has received a design overhaul, making it a millimeter thinner than the previous version. Even better, it completely loses the raised bezel of the last model and joins the Paperwhite in featuring a completely flat front face. Without that frame surrounding the screen, the Kindle feels much sleeker in the hand. The new design doesn’t add waterproofing, though; the base Kindle still isn’t rated IPX8 like the Paperwhite and Colorsoft.

The standard Kindle is available today for $149.99, but if you want to get slightly fancier, you can get a model with an aluminum body for $189.99. The design is otherwise identical, but with an aluminum back instead of plastic. The metal's coolness and sturdiness make it feel even nicer to hold. Better yet, the onboard storage doubles from 16GB to 32GB.

For $199.99, the new seven-inch Kindle Paperwhite also gets a slimming down, but changes are otherwise minimal. I had a hard time noticing any real difference compared with the previous Paperwhite.

The more significant upgrade is the $249.99 Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition, which gets a new aluminum housing and doubles the storage from 16GB to 32GB. It also adds pogo pins for use with a new case accessory.

The new Kindle Colorsoft receives the same chassis overhaul as the Paperwhite (for $289.99), with an aluminum Signature Edition option that packs 32GB of storage (for $319.99). Launch details for the new Paperwhite or Colorsoft models are sparse; Amazon says they will “become available for preorder on a rolling basis.”

Kids' versions are available for all three new Kindles: the $179.99 Kindle Kids, the $229.99 Paperwhite Kids, and the $299.99 Colorsoft Kids. These models include a colorful cover and a 12-month subscription to Amazon Kids+, which offers access to books, audiobooks, apps, and videos for children ages 3 to 12 (a subscription is usually $69.99 per year). Keep in mind that the non-Paperwhite version still isn't waterproof, with or without a cover. That said, all kid-friendly models come with a no-questions-asked two-year replacement warranty.

The Screens: Exactly the Same as Last Generation

You might have noticed that I haven’t mentioned the screens on any of these new Kindles. That’s because they’re exactly the same as the previous models. The E Ink displays haven’t been updated at all.

The Kindle and the Paperwhite both have monochrome screens that display 300 pixels per inch (ppi). The Kindle’s front light can adjust screen brightness based on your environment. The Paperwhite’s front light can adjust both screen brightness and color temperature.

The Colorsoft shows 150ppi color and 300ppi black-and-white, with the same color and contrast range as before. Its screen appears darker than that of the Kindle and the Kindle Paperwhite because of the color filter it uses, something I noticed on the Kindle Scribe Colorsoft.

In other words, what you read on the latest Kindles will look exactly as it would on a previous-gen model.

The Accessories: Bringing Back Physical Buttons

Amazon also announced two new Kindle accessories that bring back a feature found on much older Kindle models: page-turning buttons. The $34.99 Kindle Click is a Bluetooth remote with two large gray buttons that turn the pages on your Kindle forward and backward. The Click also features two smaller buttons for adjusting brightness without touching the device. It’s round, flat, and comfortable in the hand. It’s only available in white, so it doesn’t exactly match the various colorful Kindle options like Ube, Matcha, and Fig. 

The Page-Turn Cover is a magnetic cover for the Kindle Paperwhite and Colorsoft that adds physical page-turning buttons to the right of the screen. It seems like a nice enough accessory if you don’t like swiping while reading, but at $79.99, it’s pretty expensive.

I look forward to testing all these new Kindles and accessories as soon as I can get them in PC Labs, so stay tuned for my full reviews.
