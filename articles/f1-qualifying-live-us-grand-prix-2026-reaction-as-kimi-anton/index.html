  1. Report: Antonelli takes US pole on weekend he can win titlepublished at 23:53 BST 24 October
    23:53 BST 24 October

    Andrew Benson
    BBC F1 correspondent in Austin

    Kimi Antonelli took pole position for the United States Grand Prix on the weekend on which he can potentially clinch his first world title.

    The Mercedes driver beat Red Bull's Max Verstappen by 0.179 seconds, with the Dutchman's team-mate Isack Hadjar third ahead of Ferrari's Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton.

    Antonelli whooped with delight as he took his first pole position since the Belgian Grand Prix in July and said: "It feels good to be back."

    The 20-year-old Italian will become world champion on Sunday if he wins the race in Austin with Hamilton lower than fourth and Russell lower than eighth.

    Hamilton is already below that position having qualified fifth, while Russell will start sixth ahead of the McLarens of Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris.

  3. 'Those guys just took another step' - Hamiltonpublished at 23:48 BST 24 October
    23:48 BST 24 October

    Lewis Hamilton, who qualified fifth for the US Grand Prix, speaking to Sky Sports: "Q1 and Q2 felt really strong - we were right there in the fight. The car was just as good, we didn't make any changes and it was a really good lap in Q3, but those guys just somehow took another step.

    "I don't know what it is. I think we're a few tenths off on the straight, but great lap by Kimi [Antonelli]. The power deficit to the Red Bull and the Mercdes is still significant, and our car is clearly as good as those maybe through all the corners, but we lose that down the straights.

    On if he can gain places tomorrow: "Starting position isn't eveything [in Austin], but it's a very, very hard track to... you have to have really good pace to overtake. But it's a long race tomorrow; more degradation than anticipated yesterday on the long runs, hopefully our car will be decent tomorrow.

    "And we'll do the best job we can to apply pressure. We'll do absolutely everything tomorrow."

    23:44 BST 24 October

    George Russell has stopped to chat to Sky Sports after qualifying in sixth place for Sunday's 56-lap race. "Qualifying was looking OK to be honest until we got to Q3 - I did two bad laps," says Russell.

  5. 'The last time I started from fourth here I won' - Leclercpublished at 23:40 BST 24 October
    23:40 BST 24 October

    Charles Leclerc speaking to Sky Sports about what happened to the Ferrari's pace in Q3: "I don't think the others have changed anything from Q2 to Q3, where we've lost time is on ourselves. That is mostly the tyres. The out-lap was extremely difficult, there were a few more clouds in Q3, we lost one or two degrees in track temperature. For us it was night and day. Already from the out-lap I was having lots of difficulties. It's a shame it happens in Q3 because I strongly believe we had the pace to be fighting at the front but unfortunately not today again. The last time I started from fourth here I won. I hope we can do the same tomorrow."

    23:29 BST 24 October

    Red Bull's Max Verstappen, who will start the US Grand Prix on the front row in P2: "Q3 was not good. Q1 and Q2 was nice, but then, for whatever reason, on both sets I just didn't have a good balance.
