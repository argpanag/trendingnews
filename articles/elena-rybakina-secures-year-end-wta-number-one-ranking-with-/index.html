Elena Rybakina secures year-end WTA number one ranking with Tokyo win

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:49 · source · permalink

Rybakina's first Grand Slam title came at Wimbledon in 2022

  • Published
    29 October 2026

Elena Rybakina secured the year-end women's singles number one ranking by beating Yulia Putintseva in the last 16 of the Pan Pacific Open in Tokyo.

The 6-2 6-3 victory for Rybakina means she cannot be overtaken in the closing weeks of a stellar year in which she has won the Australian Open and US Open titles.

Rybakina triumphed at the Ningbo Open last week to win her fourth title of 2026 and, after a first-round bye in Tokyo, served six aces and produced 25 winners in beating Putintseva.

She will play former US Open runner-up Leylah Fernandez, who she beat 6-2 6-2 in the Ningbo Open final, in the Tokyo quarter-finals.

About the year-end ranking, Rybakina said: "It means a lot. This is something we worked for with the team.

"So many great players and we are all really close to each other, and I'm happy that now with last week and with today's win I made it possible to secure number one at the end of the year.

"It's an amazing achievement and hopefully we can continue this way next year."

The 27-year-old, who like Putintseva represents Kazakhstan, beat Sabalenka in the finals of the Slams in Melbourne and New York this year and usurped the Belarusian to become world number one in September.

A quarter-final victory over China's Zheng Qinwen at the US Open made sure she would move to the top of the singles rankings for the first time.

Rybakina's next tournament will be the defence of her singles title at the season-ending WTA Finals, which take place from 8-15 November in Indian Wells.

Rybakina's journey to world number one

Rybakina has won three Grand Slams in her career

Rybakina's push for the world number one ranking started at the end of 2025.

After losing to Sabalenka in the quarter-finals of the Wuhan Open last October, Rybakina took the Ningbo Open title one week later before beating Sabalenka to win the season-ending WTA Finals.

She carried that momentum into 2026, getting the better of Sabalenka again but this time in a major final. She beat her fellow big server in three competitive sets to win the Australian Open - her second Slam title almost four years after her first at Wimbledon.

Since then, Sabalenka has come out on top in their Indian Wells final and Miami Open semi-final, but Rybakina took the Stuttgart title on clay as Sabalenka's form fluctuated.

After disappointing early exits at the French Open and Wimbledon, Rybakina reached the final in Toronto, losing in straight sets to Iga Swiatek.

Rybakina's quest to overtake long-time rankings leader Sabalenka suffered another setback when she was forced to retire injured from her Cincinnati Open quarter-final against Swiatek.

Rybakina later revealed that the left ankle problem, which hindered her movement and prevented her pushing off properly when serving, had been "bothering me for a long period of time" but was something she felt she had "under control".

Speaking before the US Open, Rybakina admitted her preparation "wasn't great" as she was unable to train for much of the week leading into the tournament.

However, that did not stop her from becoming world number one and winning the event.
