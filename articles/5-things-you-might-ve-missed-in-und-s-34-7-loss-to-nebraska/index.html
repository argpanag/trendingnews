5 things you might've missed in UND's 34-7 loss to Nebraska

TheTools · 20.09.2026 04:33 · source · permalink

LINCOLN, Neb. — The UND football program lost for the first time this season with a 34-7 setback at Nebraska in front of more than 86,000 fans at Memorial Stadium.

Here are five things you might've missed during the game.

ADVERTISEMENT

1. Tylutki plays some safety

Early in the game, UND played Grant Tylutki at safety alongside Tyler Erkman. Tylutki had previously been used exclusively at nickel.

Tylutki, a 5-foot-9 sophomore from Cold Spring, Minn., finished fourth on the Fighting Hawks with seven tackles and shared a tackle for loss.

"Just felt like sitting him behind (Jonnie) Wiltshire (at nickel) was not very good coaching when he's standing next to me during games," UND coach Eric Schmidt said. "That doesn't help our football team. He got a little dinged up, so we'll see where he's at."

2. Broderick handles short yardage

In the third quarter, with UND facing a third-and-1 and a fourth-and-1, the Fighting Hawks turned to a new short-yardage specialist in Minot's Griffin Broderick.

Broderick played during UND's 3-0 start to the season but mostly when the game was in hand late in the second half.

On third-and-1 against the Huskers, Broderick was stuffed for no gain. The Hawks, though, went back to the strong, young back and Broderick carried the pile for 2 yards on fourth-and-1.

That first down led to UND's lone scoring drive, which was capped by Caleb Goodloe's 7-yard touchdown catch on fourth-and-1.

3. Tough start for run defense

UND gave up 112 rushing yards in the first quarter as the Huskers built a lightning-quick 14-0 lead.

On those two scoring drives, the Huskers stayed on schedule and didn't have a third down longer than third-and-4.

Schmidt was asked in the postgame press conference what UND did defensively to be more stout against the run after the first two drives.

"Our guys figured out they were running outside zone ... point blank," Schmidt said. "Everybody in the stadium knew what was going on, and we still couldn't stop it. It was bad on the play caller, me, not doing a good enough job being able to get our guys lined up."

UND's best chance to flip momentum came on the Huskers' second drive when Nebraska went for it on fourth-and-2 from its own 45. But Nebraska picked up the first down with a 5-yard handoff to wide receiver Jacory Barney.

4. Hawks can't hit on trick play

Trailing 31-7, UND's first drive of the fourth quarter reached the red zone behind the running of quarterback Jerry Kaminski, who ran three times on the drive for 37 yards.

After a defensive pass interference call, UND had first-and-10 from the 15. On fourth-and-5 from the 10, the Hawks went to a trick play using wide receiver Korey Tai, who was a high school quarterback.

Tai looked to throw rolling to his right off a reverse but was under too much pressure in the backfield to make a strong throw. Tai's pass was incomplete as UND turned the ball over on downs.

5. UND avoids adding injuries

UND escaped Nebraska mostly healthy, Schmidt said.

The Hawks appear to have avoided a major setback after starting tackle and captain Trace Thaden needed medical attention on the field for a lower-body injury.

Thaden eventually limped off on his own power and returned to the field on UND's next series.

"Ankle got rolled up on, and he was in some pain," Schmidt said. "It wasn't one of those deals where the look in his eye was like, hey, I'm done here. It was something that probably he's done before. I think we came out relatively healthy."
