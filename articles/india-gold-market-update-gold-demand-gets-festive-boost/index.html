India gold market update: Gold demand gets festive boost

TheTools · 25.11.2026 22:15 · source · permalink

17 November, 2026

Kavita Chacko

Kavita Chacko

Highlights

  • Gold prices came under pressure in October and into November, weighed down by the expectation of further Fed rate hikes, a stronger US dollar and investor selling
  • Demand was healthy during the key festive period, with buying broad-based across regions and in both jewellery and bars and coins
  • Indian gold ETFs recorded a fifth consecutive month of net inflows in October, with the trend extending into November
  • Gold imports rebounded in October from September’s lows, supported by festive restocking and softer prices, but remained below historical seasonal levels
  • Meanwhile, trading in gold futures fell sharply in October, with volumes at their lowest since January 2025.

Looking ahead

  • Lower prices and the ongoing wedding season should support jewellery demand, though any material rise in prices could temper buying. Investment demand is expected to remain steady.

Gold prices under pressure

Following a relatively stable first half of October, gold prices fell sharply, ending the month 6.3% lower at US$3,913/oz,1 marking a second consecutive monthly decline. The decline was driven by expectations of further Fed rate hikes, a stronger US dollar, and an investor shift towards bonds. Selling in gold futures also added to the pressure. Domestic gold prices followed the international trend, falling 1.8% in October. The weakness continued into November, with prices falling a further 1% to US$3,870/oz by 12 November and domestic prices by 5.3%. The sharper domestic decline month-to-date partly reflected the INR strengthening by 1.7% since end-October. Year-to-date gold prices are down 11% in USD terms and 4% in INR terms.

Domestic gold price discounts narrowed from an average of US$66/oz in September to US$5/oz by 12 November as seasonal demand improved. Prices nevertheless remained at a modest discount to import parity, indicating adequate supply, with industry feedback pointing to old-gold supply and unofficial inflows as contributing factors.

Healthy festive buying

Market feedback indicates that gold demand was strong during the key festive period of Dhanteras and Diwali,2 with retailers across India reporting higher footfall and sales. The decline in gold prices since mid-October supported demand. Buying was broad-based across regions, with both jewellery and bars and coins seeing healthy uptake. This was despite prices remaining elevated even after the pullback: average prices during this year’s festive period were nearly 10% higher than during last year’s festive period which fell in October, while prices on a y/y basis were up 17%. Overall, retailer estimates indicate that demand in value terms increased 15%-25% y/y, while volumes were largely flat to slightly higher.

Jewellery reportedly fared better than a year ago with more customers supporting overall sales, although the average weight of individual purchases remained lower, with consumers opting for lighter pieces. Promotional offers, newer designs and lightweight jewellery helped attract buyers. Old-gold exchange for new jewellery remained broadly stable and accounted for a sizeable portion of the overall sales (40-60% at many retailers), although some reported a marginal decline from recent levels as prices softened.

Bar and coin demand was also healthy during the festive period, largely concentrated in smaller denominations of 0.5g–5g, reflecting token purchases.

Resilience in gold ETFs inflows

Indian gold ETFs recorded positive net inflows for the fifth consecutive month in October. At INR35bn (US$364mn), inflows were 22% lower m/m but remained above the average monthly inflows of INR24bn (US$250mn) recorded over the preceding six months.

Assets under management (AUM) declined 3% m/m to INR1,799bn (US$18.8bn) as of end-October, largely reflecting the decline in gold prices during the month. However, holdings increased by 2.2t to 126t. Cumulatively, 31.3t has been added to holdings during the first ten months of the year, an increase of 22% y/y. The momentum has been carried into November, with net inflows during 1-11 November estimated at INR24.5bn (US$262mn), corresponding to an addition of 1.75t in holdings. Dhanteras, a key gold buying occasion, may have also supported these flows, with investors extending their buying beyond traditional physical gold to financial gold products.

Continued inflows amid softer gold prices suggest that investors have been using price dips as buying opportunities. Notably, the resilience in flows was despite domestic mutual funds temporarily suspending fresh subscriptions to gold fund-of-funds schemes in mid-October. This points to sustained investor interest in allocating financial gold to their portfolios. This was further reflected in new account openings: a total of 64,163 folios (accounts) were added during October, taking the total number of folios to 12.74mn.

Futures trading cools

Trading activity in India’s gold futures market slowed considerably in October. Average daily turnover fell to INR176.5bn (US$1.85bn), its lowest level since August 2025, while average daily volumes declined 30% m/m to 12.7t, the lowest since January 2025. The weakness in trading can be linked to the decline in gold prices, which likely reduced interest in hedging and trading. This was in line with the broader global trend, as gold trading activity weakened across markets in October. India’s futures trading accounted for 0.5% of global gold trading volumes and 1.3% of global exchange-traded volumes during the month.

Gold imports rebound, but remain subdued

Gold imports rebounded sharply in October from September’s subdued levels, rising to their highest in six months. The recovery was likely driven by festive restocking amid softer gold prices. In value terms, imports stood at US$5.51bn, more than tripling m/m, but remained 63% lower y/y. Gross import volumes are estimated at around 70t, compared with 23t in September and 128t a year ago.

Despite the October recovery, the seasonal build-up in imports remained subdued. Combined imports during September and October this year are estimated at around 93t, well below the 2021–25 average of approximately 180t for the same period. This suggests that part of the domestic demand was met through alternative supply channels, including unofficial imports and recycled gold from old-gold exchange programmes. Market feedback and persistent price discounts point to an increase in unofficial supplies following the hike in import duty in May.

Overall, official gold imports during January-October 2026 stood at US$47.36bn, down 6% y/y, while gross volumes are estimated at around 470t, a decline of approximately 14% y/y.
