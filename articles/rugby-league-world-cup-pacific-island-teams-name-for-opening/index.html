Rugby League World Cup: Pacific Island teams name for opening round

TheTools · 17.10.2026 12:51 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Pacific

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._f0.YIZs2-J2FpwgcBcJJb29s1vXmFAWHLh_p1oUyv-l-RA/pacific/1917323%2Frugby-league-world-cup-pacific-island-teams-name-for-opening-round

All eight Pacific Island teams competing at the 2026 Rugby League World Cup have named their line-ups for the first round of matches this upcoming weekend.

Toa Samoa will be the first island team in action Friday, meeting France in Sydney, while Fetu Samoa kick off the women's competition against Australia later on Friday night.

Matches on Saturday will see the Papua New Guinea Kumuls take on Lebanon and the PNG Orchids face France, with both games hosted in Port Moresby.

Tonga will battle England in Perth late Saturday night, with Fiji and the Cook Islands clashing on Sunday, while Fiji Bulikula meet the Kiwi Ferns on Sunday afternoon in Newcastle.

Izhar Khan / AFP

Samoa v France

Kick off: Friday, 16 October, 7:45pm NZDT

Venue: CommBank Stadium, Sydney

Samoa coach Ben Gardiner said they are ready, with a balanced team to battle France.

"It's the strongest team we've been able to put together since the last World Cup," Gardiner told NRL.com.

"We've got a real balance of experience and youth and some guys that are in great form so it's a great opportunity for us, our fans and our people, Gardiner said.

"Our aim over the last four years was to build experience into our team, grow out key positions and bring guys along so that when we hit Rugby League World Cup 26, we were ready with our best team available."

France captain Benjamin Garcia, who played three years in Australia with the Broncos and Panthers Under-20s teams, told NRL.com they will need to play their own style of play against Toa Samoa.

"We know they have some of the best players in the world - they are big, strong and fast," Garcia told NRL.com.

"We can't play a game where we just go through our sets. We have to try something different. We want to give a good impression to the fans and show them the way France wants to play.

Former Fiji Bati Jowasa Drodrolagi is one of the reserves in the French team for the match.

Papua New Guinea v Lebanon

Kick off: Saturday, 17 October, 3:25pm NZDT

Venue: Santos National Football Stadium, Port Moresby

The evergreen Rhyse Martin is leading the Kumuls into his third World Cup.

Centre Nene MacDonald takes part in his fourth World Cup.

NRL.com said Cowboys speedsters Zac Laybutt and Rob Derby will look to light it up out wide, while Eels forwards Luca Moretti makes his Kumuls debut having represented Italy in 2022.

Kumuls coach Jason Demetriou says the team will play for Martin, as the 33 year old will start the next chapter of his professional life by taking up a post as the PNG Chiefs' football operations manager next year.

"He's playing - he'll lead the team as he's done for many years. I've spoken to Rhyse the last several weeks about how excited he is," Demetriou told NRL.com.

The Francis brothers Tony and Tupou, who have Tongan heritage, provide the Pasifika connect in the Lebanon side.

They are being coached by former Wallaby and Argentina coach Michael Cheika, who was also with the Sydney Roosters when they won the NRL Grand final two weekends ago.

England v Tonga

Kick off: Saturday, 17 October, 8:05pm NZDT

Venue: HBF Park, Perth

Playing at his fourth RLWC, Jason Taumalolo leads a powerful Tonga outfit.

Taumalolo led a number of Tongan players switch allegiance to Tonga and his example four world cups ago continues to influence fellow Tongans to opt out of Austraia or New Zealand, including NRL final winners , Roosters' Rob Toiae and Salesi Foketi.

Head coach Kristian Woolf has high praises for Foketi.

"Salesi has played for a Tonga B side but he hasn't played for Tonga before," Woolf told AAP.

"We need some young guys who are on the up and bring that enthusiasm and those three have that hunger and athleticism that comes with young players.

"They have had outstanding seasons and they were great in the grand final."

Roosters premiership winners Hugo Savala and Victor Radley join Herbie Farnworth, Dom Young, AJ Brimson, Morgan Knowles and Kai Pearce-Paul in England's squad for the opener.

Dream Team winger Zach Eckersley and his Wigan teammate Sam Walters make their Test debut along with Savala and Bulldog Max King.

Fiji v Cook Islands

Kick off: Sunday, October 18, 4:05pm NZDT

Venue: McDonald Jones Stadium, Newcastle

The Bati are missing some of their biggest names.

Viliame Kikau, Tui Kamikamica and Maika Sivo are three of their missing stars.

Captain Api Koroisau will be a key man, with Brandon Wakeham and versatile Dolphins utility Kurt Donoghoe combining in the halves.

Young Bayleigh Bentley-Hape will start at fullback with Sunia Turuva moved to the wing.

Coach Mick Potter said they will take their chances against both Australia and New Zealand in pool play but will focus on Cook Islands first up on Sunday.

"We are not going to the world cup to make up the numbers. We just need to stay connected as a team and take the pressure."

Neither Marata Niukore or Valentine Holmes will play against Fiji.

Sharks centre KL Iro provides some real spark in the backline and will skipper the side with Esan Marsters named to start at halfback.

Kick off: Friday, 16 October,10:05pm NZDT

Venue: CommBank Stadium, Sydney

Fetu Samoa were sent off by the people in Apia last week.

The send off was organised by government and gave people the opportunity to meet their stars and wish them well for the campaign.

Fetu Samoa is making a comeback to the world cup since 2008.

They will be led by Annetta Nuuasusala, who has been a regular in the NRL Women's competition, and this year played for the Warriors.

Australia defeated them 60-0 in October 2025.

New Zealand v Fiji

Kick off: Sunday, 18 October, 3:50pm

McDonald Jones Stadium, Newcaslte

Fiji Bulikula head coach Jone Wesele believes the team can qualify for the top two in their pool.

He has confidence in his team, which is maing its debut at the event. The squad includes a number of NRL players like winning Roosters Taina Naividi.

"There will always be pressure. It's something that we can't control. Those are external forces. But I believe if I try to be someone else, it'll be a problem, he said.

"Regardless of the pressure, unity is our ingredient in this journey. It takes the whole group, the whole team, to make it happen, and I believe that."
