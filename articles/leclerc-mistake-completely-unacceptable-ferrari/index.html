Leclerc mistake 'completely unacceptable' - Ferrari

TheTools · 22.11.2026 09:22 · source · permalink

Josh Suttill, Ben Anderson, Edd Straw

Charles Leclerc admitted he "felt quite embarrassed for the team" after a clash with Ferrari Formula 1 team-mate Lewis Hamilton in the Las Vegas Grand Prix that team principal Fred Vasseur labelled "unacceptable".

Leclerc and Hamilton collided at Turn 7 on lap 38 of 50, with the stewards handing Leclerc a 10-second time penalty for causing the collision, which dropped Hamilton out of the lead pack.

After the race, Leclerc apologised but left enough room in his answers to indicate he did not fully believe his penalty was justified.

"I've taken a penalty, so I guess the blame is on me from the stewards' side and I guess from the majority, so as a result I guess it was deserved," Leclerc said after the race.

Leclerc told Sky Sports F1: "I just felt quite embarrassed for the team in general. We'll review."

Speaking to written media afterwards, Leclerc defended going for the move, saying: "I don't think that this was as on the limit as any other fight.

"I felt there was a place to get there without taking too much risk.

"I don't think, maybe Lewis...I probably underestimated the vision that Lewis had at that point.

"With the kerb on the inside, it was very tricky.

"Again, I don't want to discuss the penalty; at the end of the day, I'm the one taking it in 10 seconds."

It follows a meeting between the Ferrari drivers and Vasseur in Brazil that attempted to clear the air after a run-in in Mexico that arguably handed victory to Max Verstappen there.

That meeting "went well", according to Vasseur and the drivers behaved themselves initially when racing each other in Brazil, before their clash in Las Vegas just one round later.

"It's a tricky situation to be in and obviously speaking about these things here in front of you, it's difficult," Leclerc said.

"It's not acceptable; this is all I can say in a way. I feel like it's always tricky whenever we find ourselves on track."

Clash 'unacceptable' - Vasseur

Vasseur told Sky Sport Italia "clearly it's unacceptable" and said his message was similar to the one he issued after Mexico, where he'd called for the drivers to show more respect to the 2000-plus Ferrari employees.

"Charles did clearly a mistake. It's penalising the team a lot, and it's completely unacceptable. Today; honestly, we are...I'm more than upset because that message was clear after Mexico," Vasseur said.

"We had a chat all together in Brazil, today we passed the message to think about the team when they were racing together, and the outcome is this one, but it’s mega frustrating for all of us."

Vasseur said it demonstrated "a lack of respect for the team, for the sponsors", and he said Ferrari told Leclerc during the race to "think about the team" before the clash.

"All the other teams, they were able to overtake each other 20 times during the race. [Kimi] Antonelli-[George] Russell, [Oscar] Piastri-[Lando] Norris, even Isack [Hadjar] overtook Max [Verstappen].

"Honestly, you can't imagine the frustration for me."

Vasseur said he would discuss the matter with the drivers the day after the race.

"Charles will digest the situation. I think it's not the right place to discuss with them two minutes after the race," Vasseur said.

"I saw Charles a bit down and I told him what I have to say. I plan to discuss with him tomorrow."

So is it time for different rules and tougher guidelines?

"Honestly, it's not a matter of rules. It's not what you can do differently, except that you have to respect your team-mate," Vasseur said when The Race asked him that.

"In this kind of race, you have to overtake the car in front of you. You can't stay behind; it's completely unmanageable.

"You just have to do it with respect when it's your team-mate, to take less risks, perhaps to come side by side and to come back behind. But you can't put it on the paper. I think the race of today, nobody will write a scenario like this. Nobody can imagine a race like this.

"They just have to act in a professional way.

"Today it wasn't professional."

Vasseur felt it was "probably the escalation of tension" between the two drivers that had caused this latest clash.

Hamilton's careful response

After their Mexico run-in, Hamilton launched into a scathing criticism of Leclerc's racecraft. But after their Vegas clash, he was much more reserved.

"I'm not going to get into the scenario," Hamilton said when asked about the clash.

And asked whether Ferrari needed proper racing rules, Hamilton said: "That's for Ferrari to decide, obviously. I've done all I can and I've said everything and more.

"It's been difficult. All I can do is just show up and give it everything I can. And hopefully, in the background, they eventually figure it out."

He said it was a "shame" for Ferrari given the lost points and the strong recovery he'd made from picking up a 10-second time penalty of his own for cutting the track while fighting Norris to climb back into contention.
