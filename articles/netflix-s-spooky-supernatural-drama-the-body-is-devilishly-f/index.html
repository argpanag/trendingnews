Netflix's spooky supernatural drama The Body is 'devilishly fun' – Review

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:15 · source · permalink

Streaming on: Netflix
Episodes viewed: 8 of 8

Given the current state of… well, everything, it’s easy to look back on the noughties with fondness. But rose-tinted glasses don't account for the darkness of that era, when Nokia mobile phones, velour tracksuits and Kelly Clarkson bangers went hand-in-hand with fat-shaming and media-wide misogyny, not to mention how everything bad was labelled “gay”. Teenagers often bore the brunt of this, something The Body recognises in its religious subversion of classic teen films from the time.

Sold as Mean Girls and Bring It On meets The Crucible, Quinn Shephard's new series pulses with a lust and forbidden desire rarely felt in the more chaste teen dramas of today. An early scene where the dance troupe throb and convulse to a choir version of Kylie Minogue's ‘Confide In Me’ borders on euphoric, practically divine in its embrace of young womanhood. One character even dresses up as ‘Slutty God’ for Halloween, in what feels like a natural evolution of Sarah Michelle Gellar’s Cruel Intentions schoolgirl snorting coke from her cross necklace. That film’s sapphic influence is also embedded in The Body, far more intensely here, however, with a frequently painful depiction of how suppressed queer feelings often played out in school circa 2004.

Kristina Bogic and Sara Boustany admirably navigate this high-wire act, balancing connection and deception through their roles as popular girls Anya and Elise. However, it’s the aforementioned ‘Slutty God’, Lisbeth, who stands out most thanks to a near-predatory turn from Geena Meszaros: think Morfydd Clark’s terrific and terrifying work in Saint Maud via Mean Girls’ Regina George in Catholic drag. These performances elevate some characters who otherwise lack depth, and a similar thing could be said of the themes at play, which point at connections between religion, queerness and adolescence without interrogating them further. Combine that with the story’s somewhat sluggish pace — hardly a match for the energy these twisted girls bring — and The Body fails to surpass the sum of its parts. But it’s devilishly fun throughout.
