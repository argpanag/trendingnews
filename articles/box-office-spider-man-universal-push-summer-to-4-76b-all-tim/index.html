Box Office: 'Spider-Man', Universal Push Summer To $4.76B All-Time Record

TheTools · 07.09.2026 17:56 · source · permalink

MONDAY AM: The summer box office wound up hitting an all-time record with $4.76 billion, unseating 2013’s previous high of $4.756B.

Rentrak estimates that all pics this weekend will gross between $117.8M to $118.2M to get us there. Universal won the summer for the period of May 1-Sept. 7 with $1.23 billion, 48% of that coming from Christopher Nolan’s highest grossing movie of all-time, The Odyssey. Disney was second with $1.03 billion, and Sony crossed $1 billion, 93% coming from what’s bound to be the highest grossing movie ever at the domestic box office, Spider-Man: Brand New Day.

SUNDAY AM: Forget about any of the new films at the Labor Day weekend box office. They didn’t really dazzle. All that matters is whether or not we ring in a record summer, beating 2013’s $4.756 billion.

According to Rentrak, the 3-day domestic marketplace of $88M gets us to $4.732 billion by end of Sunday, already the second-best summer ever. From the chart below, Rentrak is forecasting a photo finish. If the 4-day marketplace in North America rings up between $114.1M-$115.9M, then 2026 has claimed a new all-time record for summer at between $4.758B and $4.759B.

Note that August hit an all-time record stateside with $1.276 billion, per Rentrak, unseating August 2016 which grossed $1.02B in a month led by Warner Bros/DC’s David Ayer-directed Suicide Squad (which finaled at $325M domestic). It was the third time that August cracked $1 billion at the domestic B.O., the previous time being 2014 ($1.016B).

SATURDAY AM: Rentrak reports that we’re $112.2 million away from having a record summer at the domestic box office, the previous high being 2013 with $4.756 billion. Per sources this morning, it looks like we’re going to get there with the Friday-Monday Labor Day frame ringing up over $118M.

For the period of May 1-Sept 3, Rentrak had the domestic marketplace at $4.643 billion and if we hit the forecast that studios are seeing for the weekend, we’ll be at $4.76B. Rentrak had a footnote on its summer note yesterday saying that there’s a chance that summer 2026 could finish $8 million–$23 million below the 2013 record of $4.756B. Remember, this summer was longer than 2013’s record: 130 days versus 123 days.

It’s not often that Labor Day becomes this nail-biting at the box office. Studios often use the frame as a dumping ground, rarely attempting to lure moviegoers out. The only time really they appealed to the masses was 2021 when Disney booked Destin Daniel Cretton’s Marvel title Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings in an effort to get moviegoers back into cinemas post Covid, that title still owning the best opening for the holiday at $94.6M.

With an eye at $22M (and maybe $23M) over 4-days, Sony/Marvel Studio’s Spider-Man: Brand New Day is one of the best holdovers for the holiday frame after The Sixth Sense ($29.2M), Crazy Rich Asians ($28.5M), and Marvel’s Guardians of the Galaxy ($22.9M). As we’ve already written, the movie is chasing history, and ultimately in the next few weeks, or even days, will step on 2015’s Star Wars: Force Awakens ($936.6M) to become the highest grossing movie of all-time at the domestic B.O.

Also, let’s not ignore the holdover power of Universal’s The Odyssey which by the end of this weekend after its eighth sesh of $16.4M over 4-days, will stand at $588.1M, Universal’s second-highest grossing movie at the domestic box office after Jurassic World ($653.4M) and ahead of Super Mario Bros Movie ($574.1M).

The rest of the newcomers are quite light, though Paramount’s Mark Wahlberg-Yahya Abdul-Mateen II Civil rights action thriller By Any Means is coming in higher than the $6M spotted with $8.5M over 4-days and a B+ CinemaScore. Lionsgate Grindstone’s Fall 2: Deadpoint is looking at $2.7M over 4-days in 12th place with a B- while A24’s Onslaught isn’t igniting hipsters with $2.3M over 4-days and a C CinemaScore.

Busting into the top 10 is Sony Pictures/Crunchyroll’s re-release of the 1988 Akira which made $1.215 million on Friday from 787 locations for what’s looking $3.5M over 4-days, $2.8M over 3-days.

For all the pom-poms about the comeback of theatrical, smaller films have a very hard time, bad reviews or good reviews. To have something akin to A24’s specialty win, The Invite, ($25.6M, 96% critical Rotten Tomatoes score, 89% audience), a distributor needs to invest and push. However, the reality of the situation for many who aren’t opting for an awards season or even a genre audience pull is that they’re often using theatrical as a means of marketing to get a film into the next home window. It’s the primary financial model for many distributors, even the big studios.

That said, one of the exceptions is Roadside Attractions and Saban’s Buddy which is having an exceptional hold and speaking to its under 25 audience (at a huge 44% per PostTrak). Buddy could wind up as the distributor’s second or third highest grossing movie ever behind I Can Only Imagine ($83.4M domestic) and ahead of either Judy ($24.3M) or Peanut Butter Falcon ($20.4M). This is all with a P&A spend under $5M for the Sundance acquisition. The 3-day on Buddy is $5.9M (+9%) while the 4-day is looking like $7.4M in 7th for a running total by EOD Monday of $15.98M at 1,771 theaters. The 18-34 crowd here alone is driving 73% of business. Pic is drawing mostly men at 52% and it has a 55% definite recommend. Best definite recommends of 66% and 67% are respectively coming from the 25-34 and 45-54 age brackets.

FRIDAY PM: With a four-day weekend estimated to be around $21 million, Sony/Marvel Studios’ Spider-Man: Brand New Day will be less than $16M away by EOD of Monday of overtaking Star Wars: Force Awakens ($936.6M) as the highest-grossing movie of all time at the domestic box office. The pic’s running cume by the end of Labor Day is looking like $921M.

With grosses like this, you don’t get there without audiences seeing a movie at least two to three times. Today, we told you that the Destin Daniel Cretton-directed movie is the fastest to cross $900M at 36 days and the second ever to fly past that point after Force Awakens 10 years ago. Spider-Man: Brand New Day is booked at 3,520 theaters and is looking at $3.7M today, and a sixth 3-day of $16.7M, -26%. Spidey still has PLF screens.

A bit of box office trivia: Daniel Cretton stills own the record for the best opening ever over Labor Day with Disney/Marvel Studios’ Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings at $94.6M in 2021.

Second place will go to Universal’s eighth weekend of The Odyssey at 2,312 theaters with a Friday of $2.8M, 3-day of $11.5M, -20%, a 4-day of $15.2M for a running cume of $586.98M. With a global cume currently at $1.59 billion, the question is whether it topples Jurassic World‘s global haul of $1.67 billion to become the highest-grossing movie ever for Universal; Nolan’s all-time highest is roughly $80M away from hitting that record. On a worldwide basis it seems feasible, while beating Jurassic World‘s domestic record of $653.4M (Uni’s best ever stateside) might be a stretch.
