The UEFA Nations League action rolls on into Friday night as Italy kick off ‘another new era’ with Roberto Mancini back in post. The former Manchester City boss famously led Italy to the Euro 2020 title, but they’ve since failed to qualify for the next two World Cups.

His first opponents, Belgium, also have a new man in charge after Rudi Garcia’s summer exit, with former Dutch star Mark Van Bommel now in place.

Italy v Belgium

WHEN: Friday, 24th of September, 7:45pm
WHERE: Stadio Olimpico, Rome.

Italy to win & under 3.5 goals

The days of Italy handing out defensive masterclasses seem long gone, but that might be Mancini’s best route to re-establishing them as a team capable of qualifying for major tournaments.

His side did not concede more than one goal in any game during their Euro 2020 success, and back-to-back 1-0 summer friendly wins over Luxembourg and Greece under interim boss Silvio Baldini show they possess an ability to nick tight wins.

In front of a home crowd in Rome, I can see a sharp-suited Mancini getting the job done, up against a Belgium team who won only two games in 90 minutes at the World Cup and look to be reaching the very end of their Golden Generation.

Moise Kean to score

If Mancini can get his backline sharpened up, Italy have players capable of scoring goals at the other end, despite his decision to omit Mateo Retegui from the squad.

After scoring five goals in World Cup qualifying, he failed to fire in the playoffs, and then suffered a broken leg in April. He’s since returned to action with Saudi side Al Qadsiah and is scoring goals.

However, Mancini looks ready to place his faith in Moise Kean, who scored in both playoff games and is the top scorer in the current squad with 13 international goals.

Federico Pio Esposito 1+ shot on target

Retegui’s absence will give chances to other players, and rising star Federico Pio Esposito netted the winner in those two games away in Luxembourg and Greece back in June.

He was third for shots on target in World Cup qualifying, behind Retegui and Kean, and this could be a key chance for him to impress Mancini after scoring two Serie A goals so far this season at Inter Milan.

Kevin De Bruyne 1+ shot on target

There’s no shortage of Golden Oldies in Belgium’s squad, including Romelu Lukaku and Kevin De Bruyne, who boast a whopping 256 caps between them.

The pair have shared four World Cups and three European championships together, but have never reached a major final, and they bowed out in the quarters this summer.

However, the duo have agreed to stay on for at least another year, and it’s worth remembering that no Belgium player fired off more shots than KDB at the World Cup (20), while he also averaged one shot on target per match.
