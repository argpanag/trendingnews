Rail disruption across Cornwall and Devon after urgent track repairs at Exeter

TheTools · 13.11.2026 14:13 · source · permalink
  • News Home
  • More from News

Friday, 13 November 2026 at 08:02

By Kate Lockett

Train services across Cornwall and Devon are being disrupted this morning (Friday, November 13) following urgent repairs to the track at Exeter St Davids.

All lines are currently blocked at the station, affecting services operated by CrossCountry, Great Western Railway (GWR) and South Western Railway (SWR).

Passengers are being warned to expect cancellations, delays of up to 60 minutes and changes to services, with major disruption expected until 9am.

The disruption is affecting CrossCountry services between Plymouth and destinations including Derby, Leeds, York, Newcastle, Glasgow, Edinburgh and Aberdeen, as well as services between Paignton and Manchester Piccadilly.

GWR services are also affected on routes between Penzance and Exeter St Davids and London Paddington, as well as between Paignton and Exmouth and London Paddington.

Other affected routes include Exeter Central to Barnstaple and Okehampton, and Exeter St Davids to Cardiff Central.

SWR services between Exeter St Davids and London Waterloo are also affected, with trains starting and finishing their journeys at Exeter Central instead.

Passengers are being advised to check their entire journey before travelling, as alternative routes may also be affected.

GWR passengers can use their tickets at no extra cost on Dartline Coaches route 369 between Exeter Central, Exeter St Davids, Newton St Cyres and Crediton.

Rail replacement transport has also been requested between Exeter St Davids and Newton Abbot, although arrangements are still being confirmed.

CrossCountry passengers can use their tickets on GWR services between Plymouth and Bristol Temple Meads at no extra cost.

SWR passengers can continue to travel between Exeter Central and London Waterloo, although they are advised to check for further delays and alterations.

Passengers whose trains are cancelled may be able to travel on alternative services, with ticket acceptance arrangements in place across participating operators.

Anyone who decides not to travel, or is unable to complete their journey because of the disruption, may be entitled to a full refund.

Passengers are being urged to check the National Rail Journey Planner for the latest updates before setting off.

Those affected by delays may also be entitled to compensation and should keep their tickets and details of their journey to support any claim.
