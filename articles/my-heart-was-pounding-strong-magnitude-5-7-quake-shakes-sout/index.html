'My heart was pounding': Strong magnitude 5.7 quake shakes South Island

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:59 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

The quake hit near Arthur's Pass at 11:30pm. Photo / GeoNet

A 5.7 magnitude quake has shaken the South Island.

The “strong” quake hit at 11.30pm, 15km southeast of Arthur’s Pass.

GeoNet reported the quake had a depth of 5km and caused “strong shaking” in the Arthur’s Pass area.

The jolt was reportedly felt widely across the Mainland as well as in the North Island.

One social media user said they felt the quake in Halswell: “Looked around as I thought someone had knocked my chair,” they commented.

Another social media user said the jolt woke them up in Charing Cross in Selwyn.

A resident told the Herald they felt the earthquake in Kaiapoi: “Mild shake, went on for quite a few seconds”.

“Felt in Hokitika while sitting in the couch playing my phone. Scary,” another person said.

“Ive never NEVER felt a rolling earthquake like that one before! That was insane - Bromley here. Whole room was rolling,” another resident said on social media.

“My heart was pounding and I got up, definitely brought back horrible memories,” a Christchurch listener said in a text to Newstalk ZB.

A Woolston resident said the jolt “rocked the bed for at least two minutes”.

Multiple social media users reported feeling the quake both across Canterbury and the West Coast areas.

GeoNet shows the quake was felt as far south as Invercargill and reportedly even as far north as Auckland.

It was initially reported to be 5.1 in magnitude but shortly after it was revised to be 5.7.

A weak 3.1 aftershock followed in the same area less than 15 minutes later.
