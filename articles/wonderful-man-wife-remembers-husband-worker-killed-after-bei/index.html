‘Wonderful man’: Wife remembers husband, worker killed after being hit on I-275

CINCINNATI (WXIX) - A construction worker is dead following an early Friday crash on Interstate 275, according to the Springdale Police Department.

The crash happened a little before 4 a.m. near Route 747 on I-275, police said.

Joshwa Christopher Holland, 27, of Adams County, was working on the interstate when the Hamilton County Sheriff’s Office said he went into the single open lane of traffic.

A 2015 Ford Fusion hit Holland, according to the sheriff’s office.

Joshwa was taken to Bethesda North Hospital, where he died from his injuries, Springdale police said.

The sheriff’s office said speed or impairment are not factors and no charges are pending.

Joshwa worked for Jurgensen Construction, the sheriff’s office said.

Wife remembers caring, passionate man

His wife, Shayla Holland, described him as a “wonderful man who was always willing to help those in need.”

“We were truly soulmates. I mean, you could ask anyone who really knew us,” she said.

Shayla and Joshwa met in school. They had been friends since kindergarten, dated in high school, before rekindling in June 2024 and getting married the next year.

But what drew Shayla to Joshwa was how he loved and cared for others.

“It didn’t matter who you were or where you stood with anything. Josh didn’t care. He didn’t discriminate. He loved everybody for who they were, and he was always willing to help,” she said.

Shayla said Joshwa was a member of Laborers’ Local 1410 and worked for the John R. Jurgensen company.

He was passionate about safety in work zones, regularly posting reminders to drive slow and pay attention in construction zones.

“He would wear the brightest colors that he could, and he had all the reflective gear that he could even think of putting on. It broke his heart every time he would see something on Facebook about a worker being killed in construction zones. I never thought after all of those times the posts I had seen that my husband shared that would be my reality one day,” Shayla said.

Joshwa leaves behind a loving family, including a stepson who Shayla says he loved like his own.

“Right now I’m just in the moment, and I feel like I’m in a dream that I can’t wake up from, but I just have to keep telling myself that I have God on my side and clinging close to him,” Shayla said.

Shayla said she doesn’t hold this tragedy against the driver, but she urges people to drive slowly and pay attention in work zones so another family doesn’t have to go through something like this.
