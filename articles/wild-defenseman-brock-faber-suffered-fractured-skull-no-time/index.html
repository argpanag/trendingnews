Wild defenseman Brock Faber suffered fractured skull, no timetable for return

TheTools · 30.09.2026 00:11 · source · permalink

Wild defenseman Brock Faber took a Miro Heiskanen shot to the helmet in Friday's preseason finale. Matt Krohn / Imagn Images

ST. PAUL, Minn. — Brock Faber, the Minnesota Wild’s top-pair right-shot defenseman, sustained a skull fracture Friday night when struck on the left side of his head by a deflected shot and underwent surgery Sunday, president of hockey operations and general manager Bill Guerin said Tuesday. There is no timetable yet for Faber’s return.

Guerin said the surgery was successful and that Faber is doing well and resting comfortably at home.

“He just had a serious surgery and an injury, so we’re just gonna update you guys when the time’s right,” Guerin said. “But he’s doing well. He’s doing really well, which is good news. And we’ll just follow the doctors’ leads.”

Faber was struck near the left ear hole of his helmet with 9:36 remaining in the second period of a 2-1 loss to the Dallas Stars in the Wild’s preseason finale. He fell immediately on the ice as assistant athletic trainer Brian Grogesky hurried to help him. Faber eventually got up and skated under his own power to the bench.

He played the next shift and three more shifts total in the period before being pulled in the third period and taken to the hospital.

Asked why the training staff didn’t pull him from the bench immediately in the exhibition game, Guerin said, “Yeah … there wasn’t. You know, it just didn’t … he seemed OK. As things progressed, we had to pull him.”

Faber, who has averaged 25:03 per game the past three seasons (sixth-highest in the NHL), recorded a career-high 15 goals and 51 points last season. The Wild’s season opens Thursday in Nashville.

“Brock’s a great player,” Guerin said. “He’s a big player for us. So it’s always gonna affect your team when guys like that are out. But the biggest thing here is that he’s doing well, and sometimes you just have to put the hockey aside and worry about the human being, and that’s kind of what we’re doing here now.

“Brock’s a son. He’s a brother. He’s got a girlfriend. There are people that care about him, so we got to worry about the human being right now, not so much the lineup.”

Faber, 24, has missed only six games in his career, and two were rests before last season’s playoffs. Guerin wouldn’t get into whether he’ll need to look for a replacement with Faber on injured reserve indefinitely.

As of now, the Wild will go with Jared Spurgeon and Quinn Hughes on the top pair, Olli Maatta and Jonas Brodin on the second pair and Daemon Hunt and Zach Bogosian on the third pair. David Spacek has also made the team out of camp as the seventh defenseman.
