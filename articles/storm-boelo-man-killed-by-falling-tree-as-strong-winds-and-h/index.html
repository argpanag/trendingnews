  1. More rain and strong winds on the waypublished at 19:32 GMT
    19:32 GMT

    Annie Flury
    BBC Weather

    We're ending our live coverage of Storm Boelo, but the unsettled weather remains - and there's more to come. Here's a forecast for the days ahead:

    While Storm Boelo will move away overnight, Monday morning will bring yet more rain to many parts of south-west England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

    This will move quickly north-eastwards reaching central, eastern and northern England, as well as most of Scotland, by around lunchtime.

    As the rain clears, slightly drier conditions will spread across the UK from the south-west, and a few bright or sunny spells are possible, especially in Wales and south-west England.

    Southerly winds will bring warmer air, especially in the south where temperatures may peak at 18C or 19C.

    However, another deep area of low pressure is already approaching from the Atlantic. As this moves to the north-west of the country during Monday and Tuesday it will bring another dose of rain and gales.

    A Met Office yellow wind warning has been issued for much of Scotland, as well as parts of northern England and Northern Ireland, on Tuesday.

  3. ScotRail says 'vast majority' of services to run on Monday - but warns to check before you travelpublished at 19:01 GMT
    19:01 GMT

    Rachel Grant
    BBC Scotland

    ScotRail faced a day of disruption across its routes, but says the "vast majority" of services were expected to run as normal on Monday.

    A significant amount of work has already been carried out to get the railway back up and running - fallen trees and debris cleared from tracks, overhead lines repaired, and floods and power cuts tackled.

    Network Rail says its teams would continue throughout the night to get the railway ready for Monday morning.

    However, it urged passengers to check their journeys before setting off if they were planning to travel on a number of routes, including:

    • Inverness to Kyle of Lochalsh
    • Inverness to Thurso or Wick
    • Glasgow Queen Street to Fort William or Oban
    • Ayr to Stranraer

    ScotRail's Mark Ilderton says: “We're working closely with Network Rail to get these reopened as quickly as possible, but our priority remains the safety of our customers and staff, and trains will only resume when it is safe to do so."

    The ferocious winds haven’t abated as the day has gone on and all nations of the UK have experienced some very high wind gusts along with heavy rain.

  8. Dumbarton High Street counts the cost from severe floodingpublished at 18:08 GMT

    Shiona McCallum
    Reporting from Dumbarton

    Walking along the high street in Dumbarton, on the north bank of the River Clyde, many shops and cafés are closed today because of the storm. At some, workers have already begun the clean-up.

  17. Dozens of cancellations and bumpy flights for Scotland travellerspublished at 15:40 GMT
    15:40 GMT

    Rachel Grant
    BBC Scotland

    The airports at Glasgow, Edinburgh, Inverness and Aberdeen have all had flights cancelled during the storms this weekend.

    Journeys to and from London Heathrow, Dublin, and the Northern and Western Isles are particularly affected, and some flights to other English airports, Belfast and Amsterdam also cancelled.

    But for those passengers still able to travel, their journeys have not all been completely smooth, with gusts requiring repeated attempts to land, and some bumpy rides.

    Edinburgh Airport, which has had 81 cancelled flights, has reported 11 flights diverted to other airports since the start of the weather warnings on Saturday, with one flight from Faro even being diverted to Oslo.

    A spokesperson for the airport tells us that in very bad weather the problem can be getting passengers off the plane - while pilots can land in gusts of up to 50mph, the passenger steps to the doors can only be used in gusts of up to 45mph.

    Fortunately, this has not been a problem during Storm Boelo, with all passengers able to disembark as usual so far.

    • Irish Ferries also cancelled several crossings earlier, but its 13:45 sailing from Dublin to Holyhead has departed

    Airports

    • The arrivals and departures boards at Glasgow and Aberdeen airports are showing a string of cancellations, as well as some cancelled flights to and from Edinburgh airport
    • Some flights have also been cancelled from Leeds Bradford

Dozens of cancellations and bumpy flights for Scotland travellerspublished at 15:40 GMT

Rachel Grant
BBC Scotland

Live tracking from Flightradar24 shows flight cancellations at Edinburgh Airport

The airports at Glasgow, Edinburgh, Inverness and Aberdeen have all had flights cancelled during the storms this weekend.

Journeys to and from London Heathrow, Dublin, and the Northern and Western Isles are particularly affected, and some flights to other English airports, Belfast and Amsterdam also cancelled.

But for those passengers still able to travel, their journeys have not all been completely smooth, with gusts requiring repeated attempts to land, and some bumpy rides.

Edinburgh Airport, which has had 81 cancelled flights, has reported 11 flights diverted to other airports since the start of the weather warnings on Saturday, with one flight from Faro even being diverted to Oslo.

A spokesperson for the airport tells us that in very bad weather the problem can be getting passengers off the plane - while pilots can land in gusts of up to 50mph, the passenger steps to the doors can only be used in gusts of up to 45mph.

Fortunately, this has not been a problem during Storm Boelo, with all passengers able to disembark as usual so far.
