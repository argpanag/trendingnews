Popular Aussie clothing chain collapses into administration

TheTools · 18.11.2026 23:39 · source · permalink
  • Breaking
  • Business
  • Retail

November 18, 2026 - 11.39pm

Retailer General Pants has entered voluntary administration, putting close to 500 jobs on the line.

Owners Alquemie Group appointed Alvarez and Marsal as administrators, and they took control overnight.

Retailer General Pants has entered voluntary administration, putting close to 500 jobs on the line. Angela Wylie

“The recent Australian retail trading environment, and rapid structural changes in the sector, including the growing dominance of offshore online-only retailers, has seen General Pants progressively reduce its physical store footprint,” Alquemie Group said in a statement.

“To address this, Alquemie Group actively focused on a range of options for General Pants including a sale process or significant capital injection to help it better compete against large scale competitors.

“None were ultimately viable.”

In a statement to nine.com.au, administrators said all 35 General Pants stores and its online store remain open and trading as usual.

Customers can continue to shop in-store and online, with gift cards still being accepted.

Its website was live on Thursday morning, with shoppers offered 30 per cent off almost everything for Black Friday and an extra 30 per cent off selected sale items.

The well-known fashion chain operates stores across Australia and New Zealand, according to its website, and employs just under 500 staff, many of whom work casually. No staff have been affected as yet.

Founded in 1972 in Sydney, it carries some of the biggest names in fashion and streetwear including Adidas, Levi’s, Birkenstock, Dickies and Ray-Ban, alongside Aussie favourites like Abrand Jeans, Rolla’s and Lioness.

In a statement, administrators said all 35 General Pants stores and its online store remain open and trading as usual.  Instagram/@general_pants

General Pants already axed hundreds of jobs over the past two years and reduced its bricks and mortar stores by about half.

Joint administrator Jason Tracy from Alvarez and Marsal said the immediate focus is keeping the stores trading and supporting staff and customers while they “pursue a sale and/or recapitalisation of the business.”

“With Black Friday next week, we are focused on maximising trading performance and expect strong demand over the period,” he said in a statement.

General Pants’ website was live on Thursday morning, with shoppers offered 30 per cent off almost everything for Black Friday and an extra 30 per cent off selected sale items. General Pants

The chain has been battling financial trouble for years and has relied on shareholder funding after recording heavy losses.

The Australian Financial Review reports that Alquemie accrued almost $59 million in losses between the 2023 and 2025 financial years, while receiving $22 million in funding from shareholders.

Loans from related parties rose to $43.8 million over the same period.

The first meeting of creditors will be held by November 30, within eight business days of the appointment of administrators.

NSW Secretary at Shop, Distributive and Allied Employees Association retail union Bernie Smith labelled the news “sad and distressing” for staff, especially ahead of Christmas.

“As we are with DJs, the SDA’s priority will be to ensure all General Pants workers receive their full financial and legal entitlements,” he said.

“We are in contact with the company and will be sure we are represented at discussions with the administrators.

“As with DJs, we appeal to shoppers to treat shop floor staff with decency and sympathy at this most stressful time.”

The brand’s collapse comes fresh on the heels of department store juggernaut David Jones falling into administration, which has already impacted more than 1500 staff.

Meanwhile, Aussie labels Veronika Maine and Cue went into administration in September.
