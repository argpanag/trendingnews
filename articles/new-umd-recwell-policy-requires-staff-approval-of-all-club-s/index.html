New UMD RecWell policy requires staff approval of all club sports’ external communications

A new University of Maryland Recreation and Wellness policy will require club sports teams to receive written approval from professional staff before sharing any external communication, according to an email sent to teams on Wednesday.

Phoebe Murphy, RecWell’s club sports and camps coordinator, notified team leaders of the policy change Wednesday afternoon in an email obtained by The Diamondback. The announcement comes days after the university indefinitely suspended the club tennis team after members emailed “racist imagery” to other collegiate teams in the region prior to its fall tournament in College Park, The Diamondback reported Monday.

The graphic distributed by the club tennis team depicted Ku Klux Klan hoods, including eye holes, in the lower left and top right portions of the Maryland flag, according to a Tuesday morning social media post from the Howard University Tennis Club, one of the teams that received the graphic.

In a follow-up email sent to teams Wednesday evening, Murphy said the types of information that now require approval include emails, social media posts, team programs, documents, flyers or other attachments. Club sports teams must submit any external communication they wish to share with individuals or organizations not affiliated with the university for approval by professional staff at least 48 hours before its intended distribution.

Each team’s president and treasurer are also required to sign a form acknowledging the policy change by 11:59 p.m. Friday, according to Murphy’s email. There are no exceptions to the policy, she added, and violations could potentially lead to “all club sports activities and events” being cancelled.

Teams can receive written approval from either Murphy or Kurt Klier, the RecWell club sports and camps assistant director, before sharing the submitted materials, according to Murphy’s Wednesday email.

The new policy is effective immediately, RecWell confirmed in a Wednesday evening statement to The Diamondback.

External communication refers to any messages sent to people or groups that aren’t affiliated with the university that go beyond “an operational basis,” such as routine scheduling or confirming games. These kinds of “normal” club business emails with other universities do not necessitate formal approval, according to the email.

Any internal or external emails that include attachments or embedded images that contain anything other than plain text — such as images, maps or logos — must be approved before sending, according to the follow-up email. Any attachments sent through other internal or external communication channels that contain anything other than plain text must also be approved.

Invitations to future tournaments or recruiting emails sent to non-university-affiliated groups will require prior approval as well, according to the policy.

The emails did not indicate whether the policy would be permanent, and RecWell did not comment on the policy’s duration to The Diamondback.

In a social media post Wednesday evening, Maryland Club Tennis apologized for sharing the “abhorrent” image.

The team met with the visiting teams to apologize in person and sent additional apology emails, according to the post, but felt a public statement was still warranted because the image’s harm extended beyond just those who received it.

“As student leaders, we recognize the hateful history and significance of the symbol and deeply regret the hurt it has caused,” the post read. “Moving forward, we are committed to engaging in education and training about the history and impact of racism and hate symbols.”

University President Darryll Pines condemned the incident and apologized for the graphic’s “racist imagery” in a campuswide email Tuesday.

The matter was referred to the University of Maryland Police Department for investigation, according to the email, and it will also be reviewed by this university’s civil rights and sexual misconduct office. This university also reached out to Black leaders across campus to offer support and “address harm where it most occurred,” the email read.

Pines reiterated the university administration’s apologies to Howard University’s club tennis team and others that received the graphic in a Wednesday morning interview with The Diamondback.

“The university administration were horrified by these developments and shocked that they have occurred from students from our campus,” he said Wednesday. “It’s … a big moment to use a negative incident like this as a teaching and way to educate and reiterate that hate has no place in our community.”
