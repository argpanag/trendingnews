Céline Dion in Nanterre: the final October evening of the Paris concert series

Céline Dion will perform on Saturday, October 17, 2026, at Paris La Défense Arena in Nanterre, on the western outskirts of Paris. The venue is now called Plenitude Arena, so both names may appear in schedules and directions. This is the last announced concert of the autumn segment of "Céline Dion Paris 2026", a series that began on September 12. For audiences traveling from other cities and countries, this detail is particularly important: the performance is not one stop on a long European tour, but part of a limited run of evenings on the same stage.

The Paris program brings together her French- and English-language repertoire, from intimate ballads to more rhythmic songs. Familiar studio recordings take on arrangements designed for a large stage, accompanied by a live band.

For the October 17 performance, it is worth checking ticket availability and validity conditions well in advance, especially if you are planning travel and accommodation around the concert.

Why the return to Paris matters for her career

The singer from Québec built her career in both French and English. She introduced herself to European audiences by winning the Eurovision Song Contest in 1988, representing Switzerland with "Ne partez pas sans moi". Her hallmarks include expressive ballads, long vocal phrases and powerful song endings.

The French-language part of her catalog is particularly associated with Jean-Jacques Goldman and the 1995 album "D'eux", featuring "Pour que tu m'aimes encore" and "J'irai où tu iras". Her English-language hits include "The Power of Love", "Because You Loved Me", "It's All Coming Back to Me Now", "All by Myself" and "My Heart Will Go On" from the film "Titanic". Their recognizable introductions can easily inspire the audience to sing along.

After the 2019 album "Courage" and the music for the 2023 film "Love Again", her solo performance of "Hymne à l'amour" at the opening of the Paris Olympic Games in 2024 signaled her return to concert audiences. The current series brings her back in a complete live concert program.

What audiences have already heard during this concert series

Unlike concert previews where the repertoire can only be anticipated, this series offers concrete information from performances that have already taken place. At the opening concert on September 12, 29 songs were performed during a show that lasted approximately two and a half hours, according to a report by Le Parisien. The opening song was "On ne change pas". During the evening, audiences heard "Because You Loved Me", "Dans un autre monde", "Courage", "It's All Coming Back to Me Now" and "The Power of Love", while the documented setlist also included "My Heart Will Go On" and "Pour que tu m'aimes encore".

At the premiere, "I'm Alive" was presented in a more dance-oriented arrangement, incorporating musical motifs from "I Feel Love" and "Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This)". The transition from ballads to dance-pop demonstrates how much broader her repertoire is than the familiar image of a singer of slow love songs.

It is important to distinguish songs already performed from a setlist guaranteed in advance. The titles mentioned were documented during previous evenings, but it has not been confirmed that their order or number will be identical on October 17. Similarly, the duration of the opening concert does not guarantee the exact finishing time of subsequent performances.

New song "Dansons" connects the current show with her French-language repertoire

In April 2026, Céline Dion released "Dansons", a new French-language song written by Jean-Jacques Goldman. This is an important detail for understanding the current program: the Paris series does not rely exclusively on nostalgia and past hits. The song marks a new point of creative collaboration following their work on the album "D'eux" and the song "Encore un soir". The opening concert included "Dansons", giving audiences an opportunity to hear how the new release fits alongside her recognizable songs.

Four sections, live musicians and stage design

The Paris concert is divided into four stage sections. The creative concept was developed by Willo Perron and Perron Studios, while the visual content for the LED screens was designed by Thibaut Grevet. Monumental structures, projections and lighting transform the appearance of the stage throughout the program.

Scott Price, her longtime musical director, plays an important role in the musical production by leading the instrumentalists. Thanks to the live band, slower songs can take on a different dynamic from their studio recordings, while the more energetic sections gain additional rhythmic power. The opening evening also featured an operatic aria from "La Wally", illustrating the breadth of the program: the concert does not follow a strict genre division but combines pop, French chanson, dance elements and vocal performances rooted in classical technique.

Concert costumes, developed with fashion houses under the guidance of artistic adviser Annie Horth, are another element of the stage identity. From seats farther away, their details are easier to follow on the large screens.

Who will find this performance particularly appealing

Longtime fans familiar with her French-language repertoire will probably pay particular attention to the songs created with Goldman, while international audiences will easily recognize her major English-language hits. For lovers of vocal pop, the way Céline Dion shapes a melody is especially important: songs such as "All by Myself" and "The Power of Love" require a different kind of concentration from rhythmic numbers. Audience members who know only "My Heart Will Go On" have an opportunity here to discover a much more layered selection.

According to a statement from her team, approximately 35,000 people attended the opening of the series. An audience of this size encourages collective singing of familiar choruses, while slower songs call for more attentive listening.

If seeing the performer herself clearly is important to you, compare the location of seating sections with the stage configuration before choosing a ticket. In an arena this large, sitting close to the performance area is a very different experience from sitting high in the stands.

Paris La Défense Arena and Plenitude Arena are two names for the same venue

The arena's address is 99 Jardins de l'Arche, 92000 Nanterre, in the Paris La Défense area. The former Paris La Défense Arena is now called Plenitude Arena: the name has changed, but the location has not. The venue is next to the La Défense business district, rather than in the historic center of Paris.

The venue was designed for major sporting events and concerts, with adaptable arrangements of stands and audience areas. For concert configurations, a maximum capacity of up to 45,000 people is stated, although the actual number of places depends on the stage setup and organization of each event. This flexibility makes large-scale productions, LED screens and different viewing perspectives possible. However, visitors should not expect the same sense of proximity to the performer as in a theater or a smaller concert hall.

How to reach the arena by public transport or car

From Paris, the most practical option is to take public transport toward the La Défense transport hub, from where the arena can be reached on foot. Surrounding roads may be congested on the day of the concert.

  • RER A: La Défense or Nanterre Préfecture stations.
  • RER E: La Défense or Nanterre-La-Folie stations.
  • Metro line 1: La Défense terminus.
  • Tram T2 and suburban train lines L and U: La Défense station.
  • Car: parking facilities are available nearby; the arena lists Indigo Valmy as approximately a five-minute walk away and Indigo CNIT La Défense as around fifteen minutes away.

After the concert, many visitors will head back to the stations at the same time. Check the last departures and any possible engineering works on the rail lines. For travelers from other cities, accommodation near transport routes serving La Défense is a practical choice.

Arriving by car is possible, but the organizer recommends public transport because of traffic on the day of the event. If a car is necessary, it is useful to reserve a parking space in advance. For arrivals by taxi or chauffeur-driven transport, designated drop-off and pick-up zones are available; it is advisable to arrange these before setting off rather than searching for a vehicle immediately outside the exits after the concert.

Door opening time and start of the performance

Some announcements for October 17 list 19:30, but the arena explicitly states that doors open at 18:00 and Céline Dion's performance begins at 20:00 local time for this series. It also states that there is no opening act. This distinction matters to visitors: 19:30 should not automatically be interpreted as the moment the singer takes the stage, but the later starting time should not be used as a reason to arrive at the last minute either. Before traveling, check the current instructions accompanying your own ticket and the venue's information once again.

The entrance for the appropriate section is indicated on the ticket. Because of security checks and audience guidance, it is useful to have the ticket ready before reaching the barriers. If the performance follows the duration of the opening evening, departure will be later in the evening, but this is not a confirmed finishing time for October 17. Therefore, allow extra time for the return journey by public transport.

Tickets, security rules and accessibility

Strict rules on ticket resale and transfer apply to this series. The arena warns that tickets obtained outside accepted channels may be invalid, and resale takes place within the original purchase system. Before paying, check how a ticket can be validly transferred: a forwarded photograph of a code is not a guarantee of entry.

It is advisable to save your digital ticket to your phone or mobile wallet in advance. The QR code should not be shared publicly, as it is intended for a single entry verification. The arena also requires security screening and restricts the items visitors may bring inside. Small bags up to A4 size are permitted, while larger bags, backpacks and luggage are not allowed. Prohibited items include cameras, laptop computers, glass and metal bottles, and alcoholic beverages brought from outside. A small, empty, soft plastic bottle is permitted and can be filled at designated points.

Visitors with reduced mobility have a dedicated access route through entrance 38 and appropriate seating areas. Required seating arrangements and accompanying assistance should be organized before arrival. Catering outlets are available inside the venue, while merchandise stands are located on the outdoor plaza and in the interior concourses, which may be useful for those who want to arrive early and avoid the largest crowds.

What visitors can plan around the concert evening

La Défense is known for its modern architecture and the pedestrian area around the Grande Arche. Visitors coming to Paris for the concert can spend the day sightseeing before heading toward the arena with plenty of time to spare. For an overnight stay after the performance, Nanterre and the area around the La Défense transport hub are practical locations.

This performance concludes the Paris concert series for 2026, while new dates at the same arena have been announced for May 2027. If your trip has not yet been arranged, check ticket availability and conditions before making non-refundable transport or hotel reservations.
