An Emirati royal passed out on a London street after a cocaine binge while looking after a young child.

Mahrah Al Nahyan, 32, took the Class A drug days before being found lying near an unconscious man in west London while a barefoot toddler played nearby.

Al Nahyan, who is part of Abu Dhabi's royal family through marriage, admitted one charge of child neglect at Southwark Crown Court on Tuesday.

The court heard Al Nahyan, who receives an £8,000 monthly allowance, was discovered unconscious by members of the public on June 13.

The child nearby was cared for by members of the public until police arrived, The Times reported.

When Al Nahyan came to, the court heard she said: "This is not a slaughterhouse, why do you care so much? Why are you so worried?"

An unresponsive man was also discovered nearby on the pavement

Rio Pahlavanpour, prosecuting, said the members of the public "noticed the child was unattended, without shoes on the street with a scooter and members of the public intervened as [the child] seemed to be in close proximity to moving traffic".

Al Nahyan was found to have drug paraphernalia on her person when searched but no illegal substances.

She has since been admitted to the Priory clinic in Woking, Surrey, for drug addiction treatment.

Stephen Vullo KC, defending, said Al Nahyan had not taken drugs on the day she was found, but was very tired having taken cocaine in the previous days.

He added she had shown "significant remorse and resolve to overcome her addiction".

The court heard the 32-year-old had shown a clear 'resolve to overcome her addiction' - The Times/Ben Montgomery

"The immediate future is she intends to complete all of the steps of a three-month programme, which will come to an end at the end of this month," Mr Vullo said.

"Her plan after that is to rent a property in the Guildford area and become a day patient there, where she will attend Narcotics Anonymous."

He added: "She made a grave mistake that day and she knows it."

Mr Justice Johnson KC, the judge, said: "But for the intervention of strangers there was a serious risk they would be seriously injured or worse. They could have run away, become lost. They could have been taken or harmed by a stranger and could have become distressed seeing you and your friend unconscious in the street."

The judge sentenced Al Nahyan to a two-year community order with a rehabilitation requirement and fined her a total of £6,114.
