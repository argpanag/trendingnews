Stephon Castle has recently been one of the NBA's best three-point shooters

TheTools · 22.11.2026 20:44 · source · permalink

San Antonio has been strong in a whole bunch of areas to start the season, but shooting hasn't been one of them. Quite the opposite, actually. The Spurs have struggled from the perimeter, currently sitting 24h in the league three-point percentage. But Stephon Castle has been a bright spot lately after a sluggish start through his first 12 games.

Castle has shot 53.8% from deep in his last five performances, and no matter how you slice it, that's one of the best marks in the league. It ranks seventh out of 211 players shooting at least three attempts per game, fifth out of 154 players with four attempts per game, and fourth out of 111 players putting up 5 per game.

The uptick in efficiency has raised his season percentage to 37.4% when it was sitting at 31.5% just before the Golden State Warriors game on Nov. 13. He's unlikely to continue shooting at more than a 50% clip, but he doesn't need to. Steph continues to show his proof of concept with his shotmaking.

Castle's shooting turned around last spring too

Last season, the former UConn champion went from an inconsistent shooter to a deadly threat from outside over the last two months of the regular season, averaging 42.9% from deep in March and April. He wasn't able to keep it up during the playoffs, but his summer work tells us he knows the shooting must improve. Spurs fans saw Castle working on his threes in video after video during the offseason.

Most were probably hopeful that Castle would hit the ground running with his shooting but he got off to a slow start. That's okay in an 82-game season, especially when the team plays the first 20% of the campaign at a 68-win pace. More than being okay, it's pretty terrifying to see how much better the Spurs can play despite being the only team in the NBA ranked top five in offensive and defensive rating.

The Spurs are past the small-sample stage

It's popular to downplay the success or struggles of a team at this point of the year by saying "it's early" but at 17 games played, the NBA season is more than 20% completed. There may be a long way to go, but sample sizes can no longer be characterized as small. A picture is forming and San Antonio is a dominant team once again, housing players who have shown the ability to improve during the year.

Stephon Castle's late-season improvement in the latter months of the 2025-26 campaign weren't singular. Dylan Harper got better. Victor Wembanyama got better. The entire team clicked in February, stringing together an undefeated month before finishing the year with only a handful of losses. They were one of the league's hottest ball clubs heading into the playoffs.

San Antonio still has nearly five months to go before reaching the postseason. But that just means bad news for everyone who has to deal with them. At least when the playoffs roll around, the Spurs are contained to a few teams in the West until the Finals. Right now, every squad in the NBA has a date with the Silver and Black, except for Utah because they already took their whackings.

Do you think the Pelicans want to play Wemby and the Spurs again? I don't. But they'll have to. It's the cross they and the rest of the league bear for... doing nothing wrong, necessarily. They just happen to exist in a time when San Antonio has ridiculously talented young stars with few weaknesses. It's not fair, but fairs are for eating cotton candy, playing games, and winning prizes.

You get none of that with this Castle draining five threes and Wemby dropping 50 points. And you're not meant to.
