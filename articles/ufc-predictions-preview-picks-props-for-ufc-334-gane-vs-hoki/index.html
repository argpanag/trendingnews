UFC Predictions: Preview, Picks & Props for UFC 334 - Gane vs. Hokit

The UFC heads to Madison Square Garden in New York City for a numbered fight card with two title fights. We'll take a look at each bout across three platforms, including the lady GOAT of MMA attempting to solidify her legacy and an Elder who deserves the utmost respect. Our betting lines this week come from the RotoWire MMA betting page and are accurate as of the post date of this article. 

UFC Predictions to Consider on DraftKings

Evan Elder ($8,000)

Samuel Sanches is a fast combination puncher with dynamite in his hands, but he swings so wide that Elder should be able to hit reactive takedowns, and the Brazilian's upright stance will provide a nice target for the UFC veteran's powerful leg kicks. Elder is athletic enough to prevent getting trapped against the fence, and I question how controlled Sanches will look if he can't end this fight quickly.

Bia Mesquita ($9,100)

Mesquita and Macy Chiasson are both grapplers, and while that can sometimes lead to a stand-up fight, Chiasson's game simply isn't built to kickbox for three rounds, as she is easily pressured and tends to initiate clinch situations out of habit. I expect Mesquita to be in close quarters pretty quickly after the bell sounds, and Chiasson will likely waste time playing jiujitsu when she is taken to the ground. 

Charles Radtke ($7,900)

Stephen Thompson is getting a surprising amount of respect from Vegas in his final fight, particularly since fighters who can relentlessly pressure and grapple effectively are almost solely responsible for his current skid, which has seen him drop five of his last six bouts. Radtke also has power in his hands, which has me wondering how "Wonderboy" gets his offense going in this one. 

Heili Alateng  ($7,000)

Alateng only has one speed in the Octagon, applying suffocating pressure before unloading with big strikes. Juan Diaz can throw a crisp 1-2 combination, but his primary strength is grappling, and we have never seen "The Mongolian Murderer" controlled for an extended period on the ground. Diaz has also never had to deal with the kind of pressure Heili will bring, and he stands too flat-footed to avoid taking damage. It needs to be noted that Heili is taking this fight on about a week's notice after Adrian Yanez needed to withdraw from the bout, but this still represents a nice test for Diaz, and the Chinese fighter should be in good relative shape after picking up a unanimous decision victory against John Castaneda in September. 

Kevin Holland ($7,800)

It's always difficult to read how engaged Holland is for a given fight, but he specifically told the media how much this fight matters to him earlier this week. As long as he is checked in, I like Holland's chances as the more complete martial artist, who also carries a whopping 10-inch reach advantage. It's also worth noting that Uros Medic hasn't been outside of the first round in three years, and Holland has been stopped by strikes just twice in 44 professional fights.

Amanda Nunes ($7,400)

I suspect Nunes will be incredibly popular as an underdog here; in her prime, I can imagine her being as big as a (-170) favorite over Kayla Harrison. However, it has been over three years since "The Lioness" stepped into the cage, and she will have a very strong and skilled Judoka in front of her. Nunes was outmuscled by the likes of Cat Zingano early in her career, but she will have so many skill advantages here that there is only one pick to make at this price. 

Terrance McKinney ($8,900)

McKinney may be a one-round wonder, but I would be surprised if it took nearly that long to beat Jim Miller, who is on the wrong side of 40 and stands squarely in front of his opponents. While Miller will have a chance if he can get this fight to the floor, McKinney is so explosive and powerful in the opening frame that I doubt the UFC legend will know what hits him before he can respond. A write-up like this would normally go in the betting section, but the 60-second bonus will never be more predictable than in this kind of matchup.

UFC predictions to Consider on PrizePicks

Donte Johnson OVER 20.5 Significant Strikes, Jared Gordon OVER 31.5 Significant Strikes, and Luis Gurule UNDER 25.5 Significant Strikes 

While it's not inconceivable that Johnson attempts to wrestle former kickboxer Cesar Almeida, we have seen how tired he gets late in fights, and "Lock Jaw" counts just one submission among his nine wins. Instead, I think Johnson will attempt to stay on his feet and throw hammers, while Almeida uses his defense and pace to draw this fight out.

Gordon is a much more likely candidate to use his wrestling, but Melquizael Costa is a tricky customer on the ground, so I don't expect him to stay in bottom position for very long. Though I do expect grappling to take place here, enough time should be spent in space to exceed this total.

Bilal Hasan is so fast, powerful, and tricky with how he marshals his offense that I would be surprised if it took him long to finish the slow-footed Gurule. For as long as he is in the fight, "Grim" will need to find a way to slow the onslaught, so expect him to tie up and look to take the fight to the floor whenever possible.

UFC Bets to Consider

Drew Dober Wins via KO/TKO (+215)

Dober's power and speed will play exceptionally well against an opponent in Chris Duncan who absorbs 4.6 significant strikes per minute in the Octagon. Duncan will have a chance to lean on his wrestling, but he has never been particularly adept at holding fighters down, and Dober should have the agility to scramble back to his feet when grounded.

Ciryl Gane Wins via KO/TKO (+135)

Whatever else you want to say about Josh Hokit, it needs to be understood that he is just three fights removed from a win over Denzel Freeman. While this doesn't speak to his skill level, it is a stark reminder that he is progressing up the ladder at breakneck speed, and I don't think he is anywhere near ready to face an opponent like Gane. It goes without saying that Gane will be the better striker, but even if "The Incredible Hok" can get the champion to the ground, too many questions remain regarding his ability to fight over an extended period. We saw Gane handle takedown attempts well in the first round against Tom Aspinall, and any failure for Hokit may be catastrophic.

Yousri Belgaroui Wins via KO/TKO (+470)

Belgaroui is another fighter facing his stiffest test to date, but this line is far too juicy for someone who has finished his last four wins by knockout. We have seen Caio Borralho shut down against pressuring strikers with good footwork, and Belgaroui has shown the ability to get back to his feet when taken down. We have also seen the Brazilian get tired in the past, so even if Belgaroui needs to weather a storm, I do not doubt that his power will carry into the later rounds.
