Ulster Club SFC: David McCague 'understands' St Eunan's anger at Ulster exit

TheTools · 25.11.2026 12:23 · source · permalink

McCague's side will meet Crossmaglen in the Ulster Club decider after their controversial semi-final win

  • Published
    59 minutes ago

Scotstown manager David McCague says he "understands" why St Eunan's have lodged a formal objection after the decision to award his side's late extra-time equaliser in their Ulster Club SFC semi-final win at Brewster Park on Saturday.

Trailing by one with just seconds remaining in extra time, holders Scotstown were awarded a free which was taken by Kieran Hughes. Replays suggest the ball tailed wide and while the umpires did not indicate a point or a wide, referee Kieran Eannetta awarded the score which sent the game to penalties.

St Eunan's protests were waved away and the defending champions went on to book their place in the 6 December final against Crossmaglen with a 4-2 shootout victory.

The Letterkenny club have since expressed a "deep sense of frustration and injustice" and have lodged a formal objection, which will be heard by the Ulster CCC later this week.

While acknowledging the "ambiguity" around the call to award the point, McCague felt it was unfair to be critical of the referee and umpires.

"I'm not so sure I would share it, but I can understand their frustration given the ambiguity of the call that was made to award the point," he told BBC Sport NI's Mark Sidebottom.

"Having said that, I think we have to respect the officials and respect their decision to take on that volunteer role in such a public forum as well and the fall out from decisions are huge.

"There were many stages during the game I would have argued my toss about other decisions that there was a level of ambiguity around, but I have to live with them and accept them."

'I can't definitively tell you if it was over the bar or not' - Beggan

McCague and Scotstown captain Rory Beggan both agreed that it was hard to "definitively" say either way whether it was a score or a wide, even after watching various replays.

"We got the slice of luck that the ambiguity around the decision went our way, but it's funny how images have been sourced that have shown a different picture and I've been sent a few of those that show it was more clear-cut than what we witnessed on the RTE cameras [during Saturday's broadcast of the game], so perspective is a very important factor in this and the various perspectives people have of the ball and where did it cross and I've not seen a definitive or conclusive angle to prove either side of that," added McCague.

Goalkeeper Beggan said he cannot "definitively tell you if it was over the bar or not".

"It looked over the bar to me from where I was standing so I can understand why the referee made his decision, but if the shoe was on the other foot and you see it from their point of view, you'd feel aggrieved, but to be honest I haven't paid too much attention to it," he added.

When asked whether the introduction of Hawk-Eye, the GAA's camera-based score detection system which is only currently in place at Croke Park and in Semple Stadium, across provincial grounds could help avoid controversies like the call on Saturday, McCague was unsure.

"It's worth exploring but there is the prohibitive cost of it. We have it in two stadiums in the country if I'm not mistaken and the level of cameras needed to impact and enforce it correctly is huge.

"Again, the kick on Saturday was way above the two posts so you'd need the full sweep of the Hawk-Eye technology to guarantee it and take away the human impact of the decision, so I don't think it's realistic at all of our provincial grounds at the moment."
