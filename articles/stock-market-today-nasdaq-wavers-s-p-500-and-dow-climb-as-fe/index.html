  • Bond traders still see more Fed hikes ahead

    The Fed won't be sitting on its hands for long, says the market.

    Traders see about an 85% chance that the Fed raises its benchmark rate by a quarter point at its next meeting on Dec. 9, according to Bloomberg data. The data comes from futures contracts that let traders bet on where the Fed sets rates. Those odds are about where they stood Tuesday, before the decision.

    Further out, traders expect the Fed's rate to climb from about 3.9% now to nearly 4.7% by September 2027. That's about three more quarter-point hikes, slightly more than they expected Tuesday.

    The Dec. 9 meeting is next. A hike would match what traders already expect. Another hold would mean they've gotten ahead of the Fed.

  • Warsh announces changes to FOMC meetings schedule starting next fall

    Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh announced a change to the FOMC meeting starting next fall.

    Warsh said eight meetings per year is the right number for the Federal Open Market Committee. But in 2027, one meeting will focus exclusively on broad monetary policy strategy with neither a vote on the Fed's funds rate nor a policy statement.

    The rest will be regular policy meetings. Warsh also expects that in 2028, policymakers will hold 2 meetings dedicated to strategy and 6 for setting policy.

    "Getting policy right involves assessing near-term developments at timely, regular, and information-rich intervals," said Warsh.

    "But it's equally important we reserve time and space as an intellectually diverse group for strategic deliberations for thinking anew about big questions, looking out over longer time horizons," he added.

  • Robinhood warns its AI agents could make unauthorized trades, sparking legal disputes

    Robinhood (HOOD) warned Wednesday that its new artificial intelligence agents could execute unintended trades and expose the online trading platform to new regulatory and legal risks.

    Robinhood opened its platform to AI agents brought by customers in May, and began rolling out an in-app version last month. The company warned in its third-quarter regulatory filing that the expansion creates "greater reputational risk" as the technology reaches more investors.

    In an interview last month, Robinhood said customers are responsible for trades executed by their agents. But the company now acknowledges that courts or regulators might not uphold its customer agreements that assign responsibility to its customers. 

    The new warnings were added in the risk factor section of the company's 10-Q filing. Robinhood shares fell nearly 8% in Wednesday premarket trading after it reported weaker-than-expected third quarter profits Tuesday.

  • The stock market's best 6 months of the year start Monday

    Yahoo Finance's Jared Blikre writes:

    The stock market's favorite season starts Monday. For decades, November through April has beaten the rest of the year by a mile.

    That's the Halloween indicator, also called the "best six months" or "sell in May." Buy stocks at the end of October, hold them through April, then step aside for the summer.

    Yale Hirsch found the pattern in 1986. "You can divide the year into two sections and have practically all the gains in one six-month section and very little in the other," his son Jeffrey Hirsch, editor in chief of the Stock Trader's Almanac, wrote in the 2026 edition.

    Since 1950, the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) has gained an average of about 7% from November through April, versus less than 1% from May through October, according to the Stock Trader's Almanac. Put $10,000 into only the good half each year, and it grew to about $1.3 million. Put it into only the bad half, and it shrank to about $4,300.

  • AI worries expose dollar's hot-money problem

    Reuters reports:

    Investors have long treated the US dollar as a refuge when markets fall, but foreign money chasing returns from the AI boom could weaken that safe-haven role if the trade unwinds.

    Foreign capital has poured in to US mega-cap equities, hyperscaler corporate bonds and power-grid infrastructure. That has made the dollar an increasingly crowded ‌bet on American exceptionalism, backed by faster growth, higher yields and persistent foreign demand for US assets.

    But if the AI narrative stumbles, the dollar's safe-haven status could collide with its growing ‌role as a risk-sensitive asset owned increasingly by price-sensitive investors. A sustained selloff in stocks could lead investors to sell US assets, potentially drowning out the safe-haven demand for the currency.

    If the AI trade unwinds, "it's not as clear the dollar benefits in the ​same way it would have done in the past," said Dominic Bunning, head of G10 FX strategy at Nomura.

Robinhood warns its AI agents could make unauthorized trades, sparking legal disputes

Robinhood (HOOD) warned Wednesday that its new artificial intelligence agents could execute unintended trades and expose the online trading platform to new regulatory and legal risks.

Robinhood opened its platform to AI agents brought by customers in May, and began rolling out an in-app version last month. The company warned in its third-quarter regulatory filing that the expansion creates "greater reputational risk" as the technology reaches more investors.

In an interview last month, Robinhood said customers are responsible for trades executed by their agents. But the company now acknowledges that courts or regulators might not uphold its customer agreements that assign responsibility to its customers. 

The new warnings were added in the risk factor section of the company's 10-Q filing. Robinhood shares fell nearly 8% in Wednesday premarket trading after it reported weaker-than-expected third quarter profits Tuesday.

US-listed shares of SK Hynix (SKHY) rose 8% in premarket trading on Wednesday, after the memory maker's record profit and revenue quelled recent investor worries over the AI trade.

South Korean SK Hynix shares (000660.KS) jumped 10% while peer Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) gained 5%. US-based Micron (MU) also rose 2%.

The South Korea-based company reported a 328% jump in quarterly revenue to roughly $78.7 billion, while operating profit surged 632% to about $62.7 billion, delivering an 80% operating margin. Revenue and operating profit increased by 32% and 38%, respectively, from the previous quarter.

Growth was driven by surging AI data center demand, higher memory prices, and early shipments of HBM4, which SK Hynix expects to replace HBM3E as its mainstream product.

The stock market's best 6 months of the year start Monday

Yahoo Finance's Jared Blikre writes:

The stock market's favorite season starts Monday. For decades, November through April has beaten the rest of the year by a mile.

That's the Halloween indicator, also called the "best six months" or "sell in May." Buy stocks at the end of October, hold them through April, then step aside for the summer.

Yale Hirsch found the pattern in 1986. "You can divide the year into two sections and have practically all the gains in one six-month section and very little in the other," his son Jeffrey Hirsch, editor in chief of the Stock Trader's Almanac, wrote in the 2026 edition.

Since 1950, the Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) has gained an average of about 7% from November through April, versus less than 1% from May through October, according to the Stock Trader's Almanac. Put $10,000 into only the good half each year, and it grew to about $1.3 million. Put it into only the bad half, and it shrank to about $4,300.

AI worries expose dollar's hot-money problem

Reuters reports:

Investors have long treated the US dollar as a refuge when markets fall, but foreign money chasing returns from the AI boom could weaken that safe-haven role if the trade unwinds.

Foreign capital has poured in to US mega-cap equities, hyperscaler corporate bonds and power-grid infrastructure. That has made the dollar an increasingly crowded ‌bet on American exceptionalism, backed by faster growth, higher yields and persistent foreign demand for US assets.

But if the AI narrative stumbles, the dollar's safe-haven status could collide with its growing ‌role as a risk-sensitive asset owned increasingly by price-sensitive investors. A sustained selloff in stocks could lead investors to sell US assets, potentially drowning out the safe-haven demand for the currency.

If the AI trade unwinds, "it's not as clear the dollar benefits in the ​same way it would have done in the past," said Dominic Bunning, head of G10 FX strategy at Nomura.
