Exact date clocks change in Ireland as darker evenings and 5pm sunsets days away

TheTools · 17.10.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

As we leave summer behind and with autumn now in full swing, the signs of the changing season are unmistakable.

The days are growing shorter, the evenings darker and the temperatures are steadily dropping. Leaves are falling from the trees, and the chill in the air signals that winter is just around the corner.

While the colder days and earlier sunsets might be a drawback as we hurtle towards winter, the one positive is that people in Ireland can look forward to an extra hour of sleep later this month as we mark the official end of the extended daylight period of the year.

Clocks go back across all EU member states on the last Sunday in October and forward on the last Sunday in March. One helpful way of remembering which way the clocks will change is - "spring forward, fall back".

This year, the clocks will officially go back one hour at 2am on Sunday, October 25, marking the end of daylight saving time for 2026.

The clocks were last adjusted on Sunday, March 29, when they went forward.

Most modern devices, including smartphones and smartwatches, will update automatically at 2am on Sunday, October 25, resetting to 1am. Traditional clocks, however, will need to be changed manually.

While the extra hour in bed next weekend is a bonus, with darkness setting in around 5pm later this month, longer evenings spent indoors and commutes in the dark will soon be the norm.

According to timeanddate.com, sunset today (October 17) in Dublin is at 6.24pm, dropping gradually to 6.09pm by next Saturday, October 24 - and dropping further to 5.07pm on Sunday, October 25 - the day the clocks go back.

Health experts are urging households to take small steps to make the transition smoother. Sudden shifts in time can affect the body's internal clock, impacting sleep quality, mood and energy levels.

Sleep specialists recommend adjusting bedtime gradually in the lead up to the change. A simple 15 minute shift can help the body adapt naturally.

Experts at Sleepeezee explained: "One of the best ways to ease into the clock change is to adjust your sleep schedule beforehand. Try going to bed 15 minutes earlier each night to allow your body to adjust gradually to the lost hour of sleep. This small change will help minimise the shock to your internal body clock and make the transition smoother.

"Consistency is key when it comes to maintaining a good sleep schedule. Going to bed and waking up at the same time each day, even on weekends, will help reinforce your body's natural circadian rhythm. Your body will then naturally know when it's time to wind down, making it easier to fall asleep, even when the clocks shift."

Looking ahead, clocks will go forward next year on Sunday, March 28, 2027, and back on Sunday, October 31, 2027 - continuing the familiar seasonal cycle.
