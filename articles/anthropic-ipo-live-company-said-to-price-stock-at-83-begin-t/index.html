Anthropic IPO live: Company said to price stock at $83, begin trading Thursday

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:36 · source · permalink
  • ANT

Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) will price its IPO at $83 per share, ahead of its Thursday trading debut, according to Bloomberg. That's above earlier estimates of $80. The outlet previously reported that shares were oversubscribed by as much as 6x.

Anthropic, which Yahoo Finance named its Company of the Year for 2026, reported in its most recent amended S-1 that its third-quarter revenue climbed to $17 billion, a 12x year over year increase.

For the nine months that ended Sept. 30, Anthropic's sales eclipsed $33 billion. It brought in $2.5 billion in all of 2025 and just $41 million in 2023.

Anthropic's sales continue to improve. Its number of customers spending more than $100,000 has increased from roughly 1,500 in December 2025 to more than 11,900 as of Sept. 30. Its computing efficiency has also jumped, meaning it's seeing more revenue per dollar spent on computing infrastructure.

"There's no real historical analogy for a company that has been this successful this fast," NYU Stern School of Business professor Arun Sundararajan told Yahoo Finance.

Anthropic's public debut also comes amid a wave of upheaval in the AI industry, ranging from market jitters to questions about the technology's impact on jobs.

It also faces plenty of competition from rival OpenAI (OPAI.PVT), as well as heavy hitters like Google (GOOG, GOOGL) and Meta (META).

Now the market will determine how that risk factors into the company's future prospects.

  • Anthropic's IPO day will test the AI trade. But what happens next matters more.

    The response in the days and weeks after Anthropic's (ANTH.PVT) looming initial public offering will be more important to the broader AI ecosystem than the IPO itself, according to former longtime Cisco (CSCO) CEO and JC2 Ventures founder John Chambers.

    "I think more important than what [the stock] comes out at is, after the IPO is out, do we see steady gains in terms of this stock and therefore encouraging others to go public and follow forward on it?" Chambers said on Sozzi Unleashed.

    Given the rising concerns about the health of the AI ecosystem, it's a coin flip as to how Anthropic's debut on public markets will go — and what the days after will look like.

  • Here's the cash windfall the Anthropic IPO may bring to banks like Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan and Morgan Stanley

    It stands to be a bountiful holiday quarter for some of the world's biggest banks, courtesy of the gift-giving team at Anthropic (ANTH.PVT).

    Using the SpaceX IPO fee rate, allocations, and a range of potential proceeds, EvercoreISI analyst Glenn Schorr estimates Anthropic's offering could generate a roughly $562 million equity capital markets (ECM) fee pool. That would equal about 1% of fourth-quarter consensus EPS across the banks he covers.

    In Schorr's base case, co-lead underwriters Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley would each haul in $112 million in fees. Bank of America, Citigroup, and JPMorgan would each rake in $84 million.

    A total of 22 banks appear on Anthropic's IPO tombstone, an advertisement recognizing the investment banks and other financial firms helping take the company public.

    "A successful listing could also encourage more AI companies to test the public markets, accelerate broader IPO momentum, and reinforce the view that the AI trade still has room to run," Schorr added. Read more.

  • Why CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz is looking forward to Anthropic and OpenAI going public

    CrowdStrike (CRWD) founder and CEO George Kurtz said he can't wait for Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) and OpenAI (OPAI.PVT) to become public companies, as doing so will force them to act more honorably.

    "I think when you're a public company, you have a different standard. And I know that as a public company for many years," Kurtz said at Yahoo Finance Invest. "So I'm actually looking forward to them being public, because I think the level of transparency has to go up. They're going to have to be more diligent in what happens. You just can't have these agents escape and say 'whoops.' And you're going to have to work very closely with the security companies, because you can't have the church and state, you can't use your own technology to prevent against what happens. Because when you have a failure in your own technology, you can't just say 'whoops.' So you're going to need checks and balances."

    Kurtz added, "I think the public market is going to force that level of clarity, transparency, to make sure that A) we understand what's happening, and B) they're operating at a higher level of safety than they are today."

  • What Anthropic's IPO could mean for Amazon and Google

    Anthropic's IPO will make plenty of people a whole lot richer on paper virtually overnight, including big-name investors like Amazon (AMZN) and Google (GOOG, GOOGL), two of the company's largest backers.

    The tech giants have invested billions of dollars in Anthropic, and, according to the AI lab's amended S-1 investor prospectus, the pair will hold more than 5% of Anthropic's outstanding stock when it goes public.

    But those hefty investments also carry substantial risks if the company falls short of Wall Street's enormous expectations.

    "There's risk to their investment in that … who knows in five years whether Anthropic will be the most profitable company in the world — or worth nothing," Dorothy Lund, co-director of Columbia Law School's Ira M. Millstein Center for Global Markets and Corporate Ownership, told Yahoo Finance.

    "There are a lot of questions."

    According to Amazon's latest quarterly filing, the company has invested $18 billion in Anthropic so far, and currently has a financing agreement with the startup worth up to an additional $15 billion.

    As of Sept. 30, those investments were worth $92.5 billion in Anthropic nonvoting preferred stock. Amazon also said it holds $146.2 billion in notes, which would convert to stock when Anthropic goes public.

    Google doesn't break out the exact value of individual investments, but in April it revealed plans to invest upwards of $40 billion in the company if it meets certain performance obligations. That's in addition to prior investments worth roughly $3 billion.

    Anthropic and Google aren't just investors in Anthropic; they are also major compute suppliers.

    In May, The Information reported that Anthropic entered into a $200 billion agreement to use Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) over a five-year period. Anthropic has also agreed to spend more than $100 billion via Amazon's AWS over the next 10 years.

    That means in addition to investment gains, the cloud giants will also earn revenue on sales to Anthropic. Such deals have frequently raised concerns about circular investing, creating an unsustainable trend in the AI market. Read more.

  • Anthropic says a key AI safety alarm has been triggered, days before its IPO

    Anthropic told would-be investors that AI speeding up its own research might "imminently" trigger one of the company's key safety alarms. On Tuesday, in the same filing that set a price range for its record-setting IPO, it said that has now happened.

    "We made a determination that we have met this threshold," the company wrote in its amended prospectus. It based the call on "what we expect from future models and our overall rate of progress, rather than on what we have observed from current models."

    The safety threshold sits in Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy — the company rulebook for adding tougher safety measures as its AI gets more powerful. This one is triggered when AI is doing enough of Anthropic's research to roughly double how fast its models improve. The policy says that pace would pack two years' worth of AI progress, at its 2018-2024 rate, into a single year. The filing says AI "has already been accelerating our research and development." Crossing the line means more safeguards, not a shutdown.

    The fear is AI will improve itself faster than people can check it. In 2023, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei joined other AI leaders in warning that the "risk of extinction from AI" should be a global priority alongside pandemics and nuclear war.

    Anthropic says humans stay in charge. It doesn't expect to safely use Claude "to fully autonomously design and build successive generations of frontier models without meaningful human supervision or understanding," the filing says, and it is weighing policy changes "to keep humans in control."   • Anthropic's new IPO filing puts its computing bill near $900 billion

    Anthropic's first operating profit lasted one quarter. Its computing bill, nearly $900 billion, is just getting started.

    The maker of Claude (ANTH.PVT) put a total on that bill in its amended IPO filing, which lists about $895 billion of commitments, nearly all of it for computing and data centers. About $70 billion comes due within a year. The full bill is almost seven times the roughly $132 billion of cash and short-term investments Anthropic held at the end of September.

    The filing also added third quarter results. Revenue jumped to about $17 billion from $11.6 billion in the second quarter. But Anthropic reported a $567 million operating loss, one quarter after its first operating profit of $974 million.

    Anthropic books most of its computing 12 to 18 months ahead. When demand ran past that capacity last quarter, it bought $5 billion of short-term computing that "currently costs significantly more" than its long-term deals, the filing says. A $2 billion non-cash charge tied to a donation-matching program added to the loss.

    Three days before the filing, Seaport Research Partners analyst Jay Goldberg warned on Yahoo Finance that the profits wouldn't last.

    "They will be profitable probably in the next quarter or two. But then they're going to start going back into losses," he said. Read more.

  • 12 new terms Anthropic says you must understand before investing in the company

    Anthropic listed a total of 39 terms in the early pages of its prospectus. Some, like "AI," are straightforward, but many others are not. Yahoo Finance selected the ones that may be foreign to you — save this list for easy use come Anthropic IPO day.

    Agent loop: "Refers to the iterative cycle in which an AI agent receives a task, takes an action, such as planning a project, running code, or calling a tool, observes the result, and decides its next step, with this cycle repeating until the task is complete. The agent loop is the core execution pattern underlying agentic AI behavior."

    Applied AI: "Refers to a discipline and organizational function focused on embedding AI capabilities directly into production business environments to address specific customer requirements. Applied AI teams, which include forward-deployed engineers, work with enterprises to design and deploy custom solutions, specialized workflows, and domain-specific implementations tailored to a customer's own systems and processes."

    Compute multipliers: "Refer to algorithmic and methodological improvements in training and inference that increase the effective output of a given amount of compute."

    Constitutional AI: "Refers to an approach to training AI models using a set of written principles, which we refer to as Claude's constitution, currently organized around four priorities: being broadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with our guidelines, and genuinely helpful. Under Constitutional AI, our models evaluate, critique, and revise their own responses against these principles, enabling us to define and control model behavior through auditable principles, rather than relying solely on fixed rule-based instructions or human reviewers."

    Context window: "Refers to the maximum amount of text, code, or other content, measured in tokens, that an AI model can process in a given request, encompassing instructions, prior conversation history, user input, and the model's output."

    Interleaved thinking: "Refers to a capability whereby an AI model interposes discrete reasoning steps between successive tool calls within a single response, rather than confining its reasoning to a single planning phase at the outset."

  • Anthropic's IPO has a line around the block. History says that's not the signal to watch.

    There's a line outside Anthropic's IPO, and the bankers want everyone to see it. Any nightclub owner knows the crowd outside says more about the door than the party.

    Anthropic's (ANTH.PVT) offering has drawn multiple times the orders for the shares available, Bloomberg reported Wednesday, led by anchor-size orders. With a private placement, Anthropic and its co-founders are raising as much as about $90 billion at a targeted $1.4 trillion valuation, which would make it the biggest listing ever.

    Impressive? Sure. Rare? Not even close.

    Saudi Aramco (2222.SR) drew nearly five times the shares on offer in 2019, or $119 billion of bids for a $26 billion sale. SpaceX (SPCX) pulled in more than $250 billion of demand for its June debut, about four times the deal. Facebook, now Meta (META), and Rivian (RIVN) had packed books too. Both were down roughly 40% to 70% a year after their first-day open, by Yahoo Finance calculations.

    Most IPOs are heavily oversubscribed, which lets underwriters pick who gets shares, according to University of Florida professor Jay Ritter. Investors know it, so they pad their orders on hot deals, expecting them to be cut back in size. Plenty of that demand is often fast money looking for a quick flip.

    That's why the first month of trading can mislead. "For the first 30 days you've got a pop with the IPO, but then after the lockup, that's when the market's actually going to give us the true valuation," Robert Schein, chief investment officer at Blanke Schein Wealth Management, told Yahoo Finance.   • Who are Dario and Daniela Amodei, Anthropic's CEO and president

    Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) will begin publicly trading Thursday, in what could be one of the biggest market debuts in history. And leading the lab are the brother-and-sister team of CEO Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei.

    The two previously worked at rival OpenAI (OPAI.PVT) before leaving to found Anthropic in 2021 along with five other former OpenAI colleagues.

    Dario

    Dario, 43, has quickly become a household name beyond Silicon Valley, gaining attention for his outspoken views on AI safety and his clashes with the Trump administration over the use of Anthropic's AI models.

    The chief executive of one of the most consequential AI companies around, Dario holds degrees, including a PhD in biophysics and computational neuroscience from Princeton University.

    He was a postdoctoral fellow at Stanford University's School of Medicine, applying machine learning to biomedical data, according to a biography on Anthropic's website. 

    Prior to founding Anthropic, Dario worked at Chinese tech giant Baidu (BIDU) before moving to Google's (GOOG, GOOGL) AI lab, Google Brain. Google eventually folded Google Brain into its DeepMind group, forming Google DeepMind.

    Following his time at Google, Dario moved to OpenAI, where he served as VP of research and helped develop the company's GPT-2 and GPT-3 AI models. 

    Daniela

    Daniela, 39, graduated with a degree in English from the University of California, Santa Cruz. She spent an early portion of her career as a fellow with Conservation Through Public Health, working on the campaign for former Pennsylvania congressman Matt Cartwright, and spending time on Capitol Hill, according to her LinkedIn profile.

    She then transitioned to the tech industry, taking a position at Stripe (STRI.PVT) and rising to risk manager before moving to OpenAI, where she served as an engineering manager and vice president of people. Before leaving to found Anthropic, she was the VP of safety and policy.

    Like Dario, Daniela says safety is central to Anthropic. Read more.

  • Why token usage could become a key metric for AI investors

    With Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) heading toward the public markets on Thursday, industry watchers say AI token usage could become an increasingly important metric for investors, much like subscriber counts were for video streaming services. But they also warn that token usage remains an opaque measure to assess.

    Most of Anthropic's revenue comes from customers paying based on how much they use its AI models, a pricing structure the frontier lab describes in its IPO filing as "metered in tokens and priced according to the model selected." A token is a small chunk of text, roughly a short word or part of a word, that an AI model reads or generates.

    In an amended filing this month, Anthropic reported $33.2 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026, up from $16.3 billion in the first half of the year. During the first six months, consumption-based revenue, which is tied to customer usage and measured in tokens, accounted for roughly 80% of sales, while subscription-based revenue made up the remaining 20%.

    Industry bulls point out that even as token prices have fallen across the industry, lower model rates can make AI cheaper to use, thereby driving higher consumption. AI frontier lab: OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar recently noted higher usage after cutting prices on Luna, its cheapest model series.

    "We saw use go up 10x," Friar said last month. "So that tells you there's a lot of price elasticity of demand right now." Read more.

12 new terms Anthropic says you must understand before investing in the company

Anthropic listed a total of 39 terms in the early pages of its prospectus. Some, like "AI," are straightforward, but many others are not. Yahoo Finance selected the ones that may be foreign to you — save this list for easy use come Anthropic IPO day.

Agent loop: "Refers to the iterative cycle in which an AI agent receives a task, takes an action, such as planning a project, running code, or calling a tool, observes the result, and decides its next step, with this cycle repeating until the task is complete. Applied AI: "Refers to a discipline and organizational function focused on embedding AI capabilities directly into production business environments to address specific customer requirements. That's why the first month of trading can mislead. Why token usage could become a key metric for AI investors

With Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) heading toward the public markets on Thursday, industry watchers say AI token usage could become an increasingly important metric for investors, much like subscriber counts were for video streaming services. Most of Anthropic's revenue comes from customers paying based on how much they use its AI models, a pricing structure the frontier lab describes in its IPO filing as "metered in tokens and priced according to the model selected." A token is a small chunk of text, roughly a short word or part of a word, that an AI model reads or generates.
