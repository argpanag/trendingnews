His mother has acknowledged responsibility and apologized amid suspicions that the parent-run company of Ai Zeng (13), a member of girl group A2O LITE launched by SM Entertainment founder Lee Soo-man, is delaying employees' wages.

Ai Jing's mother posted a statement on Weibo, a social networking service (SNS), on the 14th and said, "I sincerely apologize for causing concern for me recently."

"In the past, I ran an education-related business, but the company fell into serious management difficulties due to the rapid deterioration of business conditions, which led to debt disputes," the mother said. "No matter what circumstances, it is my fault to face and take responsibility for not paying the amount to be paid on time. We deeply apologize to all those who were affected by this," he apologized.

"We are in contact with the parties concerned one by one to discuss solutions, and we are continuously communicating," he said. "We will not avoid responsibility until all problems are resolved and will take responsibility until the end."

However, he said, "The responsibility for this incident is a matter that my parents have to take and resolve appropriately," and added, "The child has nothing to do with the business and related disputes I ran in the past."

Lee Soo-man held a joint debut showcase of boy groups A2O SOUL (A2O Soul) and A2O LITE at KINTEX in Goyang, Gyeonggi Province on the 12th. The member who received particular attention on this day is Ai Zeng, the youngest member of A2O LITE. The 13-year-old Ai Zing immediately drew attention with her dancing skills reminiscent of the early days of singer BoA's debut.

However, soon after its debut, suspicions spread around the Chinese online community that a company run by Ai Jing parents was delaying wages.

According to local media such as Daehabo, all employees of a Beijing-based education company whose mother is represented by Ai Jing and whose father is an auditor claimed that the company has been in arrears since 2024. They agreed to pay back in installments after labor arbitration, but the company was overdue again after implementing it for only three months.

On the corporate information platform, it was also found that there were two cases of court enforcement with the company and Ai Jing's parents as the executioners. The compulsory execution amount is about 3.45 million yuan (about 690 million won). However, it was not disclosed whether the amount was unpaid wages.
