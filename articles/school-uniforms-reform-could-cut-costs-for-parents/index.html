School uniforms reform could cut costs for parents

TheTools · 23.11.2026 00:03 · source · permalink

Hildegarde Naughton’s proposals also seek to ensure that uniform items are made widely available from multiple retailers, including supermarkets. Picture: iStock 

Reforms to Ireland’s school uniform policy, which could see crests on jumpers limited and more flexibility on footwear, are to be considered by the Government.

Education minister Hildegarde Naughton is to seek Cabinet approval to begin a nationwide consultation process about school uniform policy, as part of a bid to cut costs for parents.

The review is expected to consider limiting the number of crested or school-specific items.

It will also consider whether crests, where used, should be either ironed on or sewn on, rather than being directly embroidered onto garments.

Other measures being considered include:

  • Barring schools from requiring specifically branded jackets and coats;
  • Providing students with more flexibility on footwear;
  • Cutting down on unnecessary changes to school uniforms.

Ms Naughton’s proposals will also seek to ensure that uniform items are made widely available from multiple retailers, including supermarkets and other shops.

It will also call for school uniforms to be made comfortable, practical, and suitable for year-round use.

There will also be measures considered to help support clothing banks, uniform swap shops, and other sustainability measures.

The consultation process is slated to begin in January, with parents, students, schools, boards of management, and school uniform suppliers to be asked to submit feedback.

The changes are being brought forward as a means to reduce costs for families, improve comfort for students, and promote a more sustainable approach.

However, the reforms would also recognise the important role a uniform plays in a school’s culture and identity, a Government source said.

The Department of Education has already launched engagement with retailers and representative bodies, including Ibec, the Small Firms Association, and Retail Ireland.

This is in an attempt to ensure the views of suppliers and manufacturers are considered during the consultation process.

Dara Calleary's pathways to work initiative

Meanwhile, social protection minister Dara Calleary is expected to bring the Government’s strategy on pathways to work to Cabinet today.

The initiative is set to focus on youth unemployment as well as on the long-term unemployed.

It comes just weeks after Tánaiste Simon Harris criticised people in long-term unemployment, describing “the dole as a safety net, not a way of life”.

Mr Harris said: “At a time of full employment, it is simply not acceptable for any person to remain on the live register year after year after year.”
