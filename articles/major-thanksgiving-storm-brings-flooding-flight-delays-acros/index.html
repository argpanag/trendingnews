Major Thanksgiving storm brings flooding, flight delays across US

TheTools · 27.11.2026 07:32 · source · permalink

A massive Thanksgiving storm hammering the Midwest and north-east with heavy rain and winds, along with high tides out west, is causing widespread disruptions across the US.

More than 80 million Americans expected to be travelling this week. On Thursday 460 flights in the US were cancelled and another 2,508 flights into, out of, or within the US were delayed, tracking site FlightAware said.

The FAA has previously warned that delays would be possible "throughout the week" as a result of the rain and wind that are forecast.

Meanwhile, almost one million households were without power on Thursday night, with Pennsylvania, Maryland and Virginia the hardest hit states.

On Thursday, a powerful storm hit the eastern US, bringing damaging winds of up to 70mph, torrential rain, and major coastal flooding.

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill declared a state of emergency as several communities along the Jersey Shore saw widespread flooding.

New York Governor Kathy Hochul also declared a state of emergency for New York City, Long Island and Westchester County.

In New York City, organisers of the annual Macy's Thanksgiving parade have postponed the event to Saturday.

Bridges closed in Maryland due to severe winds and waves battered beaches in Delaware as water seeped onto the boardwalk.

In Maryland, almost two million customers have been asked by the water utility to use water for essential use only due to "intermittent power disruptions".

On the wettest Thanksgiving on record in Washington DC, tidal waters surged and soaked runways at Reagan Washington National Airport, according to the National Water Prediction Service.

More than 980,000 households were reporting power outages by Thursday evening, with Pennsylvania, Virginia, Maryland and New Jersey hit particularly hard.

Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro said that utility providers were working tirelessly to restore power. Meanwhile, energy companies in Virginia and New Jersey warned it may take some time, even days, for power to return, as crews had to wait until it was safe to access damaged areas.

On Thursday afternoon, President Donald Trump said he would consider releasing emergency funds for the east coast as it faces down the holiday storm.

Across the US, severe weather is also descending on the West Coast. The NWS has warned of a series of Pacific storms that are expected to bring rain, mountain snow and strong winds.

Many are bracing for potential flooding as king tides peak along the coast.

Water levels along California's vast coastline could reach over 8ft (2.4 metres) in some areas on Thursday, according to the NWS, breaking records set just last month.

Both San Diego and Los Angeles reported a high tide of over 8ft earlier in the week, and beach-goers were advised to avoid coastal areas.

Train services along the Amtrak Pacific Surfliner, which runs along the California coastline, cancelled several trains due to "washout conditions from high tides", the company shared on social media.

Storms are also forecast for the Pacific Northwest, including the Canadian province of British Columbia.

In Miami, some travel delays persisted into Thursday after a FedEx plane overshot the runway the night before, which prompted the closure of all but one runway at the airport for a brief period.

A statement FedEx provided to US media confirmed that one of its flights had "experienced an issue upon landing".

It added: "We are grateful that our crew is safe and there are no reported injuries. We are coordinating closely with investigating authorities, and any additional questions should be referred to the FAA."

An airport employee who spoke anonymously to CBS News, the BBC's US partner, raised the possibility that rainy conditions had been a factor.

"They tried as hard as they could to stop, but I don't know if it was the weather because the rain and the wind were really bad, and they just couldn't stop in time and went right off the runway and veered off into the grass," they said.

Wednesday's incident in Miami came 11 weeks after a cargo plane crashed on landing at the same airport, killing five people and injuring five others in vehicles on the ground with which it collided.

The American Automobile Association (AAA) predicted that 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles (80km) from home for the Thanksgiving holiday over a seven-day period.

Rebecka Pieder contributed reporting.
