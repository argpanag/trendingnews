Dramatic moment dog saved from mineshaft

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:14 · source · permalink

A lucky beloved pet dog has been rescued after becoming trapped in a mineshaft in western Victoria.

Magic the doberman was chasing rabbits through bushland in Nerrina, near Ballarat, on Monday when she fell into a mineshaft, plunging 6m below the ground.

CFA firefighters have rescued a beloved dog after she fell into a mineshaft in western Victoria. Picture: Invermay Fire Brigade

A specialist fire brigade high-angle rescue team hoisted the terrified dog from the dark mineshaft.

The entire operation took two hours, with the rescue team unable to reach the mineshaft from their vehicles, instead having to navigate the bushland on foot.

The firefighters were forced to enter the 6m mineshaft to rescue the beloved pet. Picture: Invermay Fire Brigade

"Locating-difficult. Accessing-difficult, impossible by vehicle meaning a long walk in for #frv67 #frv68 Tech Rescue lugging rescue gear," a spokesman said.

Despite the difficult terrain and challenging conditions, Magic was freed from the mineshaft, with the owner "elated" by the rescue.

"Dog super happy and almost climbed the last metre or so herself," the spokesman said.

The rescuers urged people to be mindful of dangerous disused mineshafts. Picture: Invermay Fire Brigade

Speaking to the ABC, rescuer and firefighter Daryl Rowe said this was not the first time the owner had had a dog trapped in a mine.

"Interestingly, the same owner, we rescued another one of his dogs about 10 years ago (from a mineshaft)," he said.

"There are thousands of mineshafts up there."

Magic the doberman was safely back on solid ground after two hours. Picture: Invermay Fire Brigade

He said there were so many mineshafts in the area that people and animals had often been rescued after falling in.

He said people should be mindful of disused mines by watching for dips and depressions in the ground.

He urged people to stick to the tracks and pathways when exploring the bush.

"It's not feasible to cover all the mineshafts up there; locating them would be another issue, and the cost would be prohibitive," he said.

"You don't want to stop people from walking through the bush."
