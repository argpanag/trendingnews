Important Safety Tips for Kids This Halloween

Halloween is a night of costumes, candy and excitement, but it can also be one of the most dangerous nights of the year for children. Young trick-or-treaters are more than twice as likely to be struck and killed by a vehicle on Halloween than on other days of the year, making traffic safety especially important.

Experts say a few simple precautions can help prevent accidents and keep the holiday fun for everyone.

Here are safety tips from the National Safety Council (NSC) and the American Red Cross.

Costume Safety

  • Choose costumes, wigs and accessories made from flame-resistant materials. Make sure costumes fit properly to prevent trips and falls. Light-colored clothing can also improve visibility.

  • If children will be out after dark, attach reflective tape to their costumes and treat bags or give them glow sticks to help drivers see them.

  • Choose non-toxic Halloween makeup instead of masks, which can obstruct vision. Test makeup on a small patch of skin first to check for irritation. The Environmental Working Group offers a list of safer makeup choices.

  • Remove all makeup before bedtime to prevent skin and eye irritation.

Safe Trick-or-Treating

Keep these precautions in mind before heading out on Halloween night:

  • Supervise young children. A responsible adult should accompany young trick-or-treaters. Hold their hands and keep them close when crossing streets or walking near parked vehicles.

  • Practice street safety. Remind children never to dart between parked cars and to use sidewalks and crosswalks whenever possible.

  • Watch for drivers. Teach children to make eye contact with drivers before crossing, but never assume a driver has seen them or will stop.

  • Be aware of blind zones. Closely supervise children under 12, especially in areas with heavy traffic or large vehicles that have significant blind spots.

  • Plan ahead for older children. If older children are trick-or-treating without an adult, agree on a safe route and a specific time to return home. Remind them never to enter a stranger's home or vehicle.

  • Leave e-bikes and scooters at home. These vehicles pose additional risks on Halloween, when streets are crowded with pedestrians and drivers may have difficulty seeing children. Helmets are essential when riding, but they cannot prevent every serious injury.

  • Put away phones. Parents and children should keep their heads up and remain alert. Responding to a text is never worth the risk of an accident.

  • Check Halloween treats. Tell children to wait until they get home before eating candy so parents can inspect treats and check for potential food allergens.

Beware of Vehicle Blind Zones

One of the most overlooked Halloween hazards is the danger posed by vehicle blind zones, particularly around larger SUVs and pickup trucks.

Young children can be completely hidden from a driver's view when standing directly in front of, beside or behind a vehicle, even when the driver checks the mirrors.

According to Kids and Car Safety, the front blind zone of a large truck or SUV can extend up to 15 feet, while the rear blind zone can reach 50 feet. Generally, the larger the vehicle, the greater the potential danger.

Drivers should carefully check around their vehicles before moving, especially in neighborhoods crowded with trick-or-treaters.

"Talk to children about the dangers around vehicles but never rely on them to protect themselves," said Amber Rollins, executive director of Kids and Car Safety. "Children live in the moment. They're thinking about candy, not danger. In just one second, a family's life can change forever. It's our job as adults to stay alert, stay aware, and keep them safe."
