Alcaraz faces significant US Open challenge as Demon sets up clash with giant killer — LIVE

TheTools · 04.09.2026 20:31 · source · permalink

Alex de Minaur has set up a clash against a dangerous Dutchman responsible for one of the biggest upsets in New York in recent years after finally getting his US Open underway with a dominant first round victory.

Meanwhile, Spanish sensation Carlos Alcaraz has corrected course after an early scare and now leads Jaime Faria heading into the fourth set in his first tournament back after several months off the tour due to a wrist injury.

With Jannik Sinner absent due to injury and Novak Djokovic knocked out in the first round, Alcaraz is the prime attraction in the men’s draw. The defending champion struggled to find his range early but has rebounded to lead 4-6 6-0 6-3.

De Minaur, meanwhile, had been hindered by rain-induced scheduling issues, which meant some rivals had already booked third round appearances before the Australian star stepped on to court.

But he wasted little time after doing so when posting a 6-2 6-2 6-4 triumph over Italian Andrea Guerrieri on Day 4.

He is the sole Australian winner to date on an otherwise bleak for Aussie hopefuls, with Maya Joint the latest Aussie to fall, though she pushed No.9 seed and four-time major semi-finalist Elina Svitolina to the brink when beaten 6-2 6-7 (6) 6-4.

In a match transferred from a major stadium at Flushing Meadows to an outside court, De Minaur was cruising when rain hit again midway through the second set, which forced the No.6 seed to endure another delay.

But when play resumed, the dual-US Open quarterfinalist progressed with little difficulty to set up a clash against Dutchman Botic van de Zandschulp on Thursday in the Big Apple.

“These tournaments, it’s all about adapting and that’s the name of the game,” the world No.7 said, as reported by AAP.

“There’s too much to play for to let something like this affect you, so I’m glad with the way I handled it. I’m glad I got in and out, played a relatively clean match and now I can focus on the next one.

“It definitely helps to be able to get on the court, get off the court and in straight sets. Every little bit helps. Now I’m moving onwards, I’ll be ready for tomorrow. It should be a battle, a physical one.”

De Minaur has won all four clashes to date against the Dutchman, with the most recent a tight three set affair in a quarterfinal of a tournament the Australian won in Rotterdam in February. But he will be wary of the threat posed by his rival with reason.

Just two years ago van de Zandschulp, whose flatter style is well-suited to the hard courts at Flushing Meadows, knocked out dual-champion Carlos Alcaraz in straight sets in a second round stunner, which gives de Minaur reason to be on alert.

There was less joy for the other Australians involved in early action on Day 4, with Taylah Preston and Kimberly Birrell both ousted in second round matches, while Daria Kasatkina was unable to rally when resuming in her first round clash.

Preston, who recently broke into the Top 100, did not produce her best against powerful American veteran Taylor Townsend when beaten 6-1 6-1, while Birrell tested the 18th seeded Ekaterina Alexandrova before falling 6-1 2-6 6-3.

Kasatkina resumed at deuce when trailing former world No.2 Paula Badosa 6-1 5-4 and scarcely got warm before her US Open was over, with the Spaniard claiming the final game to progress in a match held over from Day 3 due to rain.

Veteran James Duckworth arrived in New York in good form but was beaten by China’s Wibing Yu 7-5 6-3 6-1 in just under two hours.

In other clashes, world No.1 Aryna Sabalenka just made light work of Polina Iatcenko on Arthur Ashe Stadium when joining stars including Jessica Pegula, Karolina Muchova and Marta Kostyuk in the third round.

There was a significant upset in the men’s draw with Rafael Jodar, whose surge from obscurity to a spot just outside the Top 10 over the past 12 months has been astonishing, trounced by lucky loser Yunchaokete Bu 6-2- 6-1 6-1 in a stunning result.

The Spaniard, who was initially drawn to play Thanasi Kokkinakis before the Australian withdrew with an ankle problem, was considered a title threat after a summer that saw him reach a final in Washington D.C and a semi-final in the Canada Masters.

Brandon Nakashima, the 16th seed who has also endured a great summer, was beaten by fellow American Alex Michelsen in straight sets as well, but the latter is a significant talent who is more than capable of shining on hard courts.

American star Ben Shelton, the Canada Open champion, backed up from a testing first round win to progress with a four set triumph over former Wimbledon semi-finalist Hubert Hurkacz, while 2021 champion Daniil Medvedev was another winner.
