At least 68 killed in Spanish high-speed train crash, as prime minister says 'whole country is grieving'

TheTools · 28.10.2026 18:09 · source · permalink

Summary

  • At least 68 people have been killed and dozens more injured after a Spanish high-speed train collided with another train south of the capital Madrid

  • The updated death toll makes this crash the country's worst rail disaster since 2013

  • Spain's transport minister says an "inexplicable" decision by the control centre led to the collision after a computer failure initially caused trains to halt

  • The rail operator Iryo says its train was "struck from behind" by a Renfe train. More than 500 passengers were travelling on the two trains

  • Speaking at the crash site, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez says Spain is "grieving" - most major parties have suspended general election campaigning

  • Sánchez also vows to find out "why this tragedy occurred" - here's what we know so far about the cause

  • Witnesses have described "terrible" scenes, while Roberto Gómez, a Spanish journalist travelling on one of the trains, says it was "extremely chaotic and very distressing"

  1. Spanish authorities using DNA to locate victims' familiespublished at 17:55 GMT
    17:55 GMT

    Spain's Civil Guard says its "top priority" is to identify relatives of the train crash victims using DNA samples.

    Four offices for relatives have been set up in Madrid and other parts of Spain, according to the force.

    So far, Spanish authorities have confirmed 68 people died in the crash, with dozens more seriously injured.

  2. Panic and shock at centre for the injured in rural Spainpublished at 17:38 GMT
    17:38 GMT

    Isabel Núñez is a local councillor in Urda, close to the crash site, who has been helping receive injured people at the town's sports hall.

    She tells BBC Mundo that people with minor injuries, such as cuts or bruises, have been picked up by family members or transported back to where they live by bus.

    Some people were "very dazed" and "were completely in shock" when they arrived, Isabel says, adding "many didn't even know which carriage they had been travelling in".

    She goes on: "Urda’s two local police officers were among the first to arrive at the scene of the accident.

    "This morning we have seen everything. A man arrived on a motorbike, completely disoriented, looking for his wife, who had been on the train. He was desperate.

    "We also helped a man with a head wound who had left his carriage to stretch his legs and was saved because of that.

    "His carriage was the one that caught fire and where most of the victims were. He was relieved and grateful to be alive, but deeply saddened by the whole situation."

  4. In Madrid hundreds are queueing to donate bloodpublished at 17:09 GMT
    17:09 GMT

    Elizabeth Conway
    BBC Sport Spanish football reporter in Madrid

    I've just stepped outside in Madrid to find a queue of more than 100 people waiting to donate blood in the city centre.

    The donation van is usually parked on Gran Vía, Madrid’s main shopping street, where there are normally only a handful of people waiting. I’ve never seen a queue anything like this before.

    In coffee shops today, the train crash was all anyone seemed to be talking about.

    There’s a real sense of shock and heartbreak across the city, with people sharing news and information about what’s happened on social media.

    Due to the volume of donations, Madrid president Isabel Díaz Ayuso is asking others to "wait until tomorrow and the following days, when it will still be just as necessary".

  6. Questions for government after second deadly rail crash this yearpublished at 16:39 GMT
    16:39 GMT

    Guy Hedgecoe
    Reporting from Urda

    This tragedy comes just nine months after 46 people died in a rail collision on the same Madrid-Seville line.

    That accident unleashed an intense debate about the safety of Spain's high-speed rail system, the largest in Europe and until recently the envy of many neighbouring countries.

    It also piled pressure on the left-wing government of Pedro Sánchez, as many Spaniards wondered if his administration had under-invested in the rail system.

    This latest crash will intensify the doubts about the rail system's safety and criticism of the government's management of it.

    In particular, Transport Minister Óscar Puente, is likely to come under fire.

    The leader of the main opposition People's Party (PP), Alberto Núñez Feijóo, who hopes to unseat Sánchez in next month's general election, has said today that Spaniards "have a right to a complete and independent review of the entire rail network".

  9. Recap: Spain suffers one of worst rail disasters in modern European historypublished at 16:08 GMT
    16:08 GMT

    At least 68 people have been killed after a Spanish high-speed train collided with another train south of the capital Madrid, with dozens more seriously injured.

    Speaking from the crash site, Spain's Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez says "the whole of Spain is grieving", adding: "Lives have been shattered, families have been torn apart, the pain is immense."

    Passenger Eduarda was travelling with her two cats and told Hoy por Hoy, a Spanish radio programme, that after the collision she heard screams and saw a carriage on fire, with people under the train who had died.

    Transport Minister Óscar Puente says a computer failure led to the initial halt of six moving trains, including the two trains that subsequently collided.

    "In the event of a system failure, the control centre takes over," Puente says, adding it was the control centre which decided to allow the trains to move again, without any speed restrictions.

    That decision, Puente said, was made "under inexplicable circumstances", and resulted in the collision.

    An investigation is under way, dozens are being treated in hospital and three days of mourning have been announced by the prime minister.

  11. Sánchez announces three days of mourningpublished at 15:35 GMT
    15:35 GMT

    Sánchez also says any "failures in the security system" will be addressed.

    Referencing a deadly rail crash which killed 46 in Spain on the same line earlier this year, he says: "At the moment it looks like the causes behind both crashes have nothing in common, but it's not up to me but to the investigation to establish that.

    "We will need to do everything in our power to restore public confidence in our high-speed railway network. It is unacceptable for a country like Spain to experience two high-speed rail accidents in the same year."

  12. Sánchez: If there has been any negligence, it will come to lightpublished at 15:34 GMT
    15:34 GMT

    More now from Spanish PM Pedro Sánchez, who has been speaking near the crash site.

    He says: "Today Spain is grieving, the whole of Spain is grieving, and we express all our solidarity and empathy with the relatives of the victims and the injured who are still in hospital, and whom we wish a speedy recovery.

    "Behind each victims is... a destroyed family, pain, and anguish... which the people of Spain share."

    He says the first priority of the government is to "offer support to the injured and victims and relatives of this terrible tragedy".

    The second objective, he goes on, is "to clarify what happened, why this tragedy occurred".

    He adds: "The ministers of transport and interior are trying to determine what was the origin of this tragedy, of this terrible accident... and we will get to the bottom of this, and if there has been any negligence, it will come to light."

  15. 'It was a terrible few seconds'published at 14:57 GMT
    14:57 GMT

    Eduarda was travelling with her two cats and told Hoy por Hoy, a Spanish radio programme, the crash propelled her forward, breaking the table in front of her seat, with passengers around her suffering from cuts and bruises.

  18. 'The impact was terrible... the carriage is completely destroyed,' passenger sayspublished at 14:21 GMT
    14:21 GMT

    Paul Kirby
    Europe digital editor

    Spanish journalist Roberto Gómez has described being on the high-speed train when it crashed into the train in front on the same track.

    Gómez said he had been asleep when the train stopped and an announcement was relayed to passengers that there was congestion on the tracks. The train began to move again, he explained, and then the crash happened.

    "The impact was terrible," he told Onda Cero radio. "You can imagine, the carriage is completely destroyed. All the suitcases are all over the wrecked carriage."

    Gómez suffered only minor injuries but described another passenger falling on top of him and sustaining a deep gash on his head.

    "We were able to help people who had suffered severe blows to their chest and ribs," he said.

    The situation was extremely chaotic and very distressing, and several minutes later he and others eventually managed to climb out of the window, he added.

  19. Crash puts most campaigning on hold in Spanish election campaignpublished at 14:10 GMT
    14:10 GMT

    Paul Kirby
    Europe digital editor

    For most Spanish political leaders the devastating scenes emerging on the high-speed rail line south of Toledo mean that the campaign ahead of the 29 November general election is for the moment on hold.

    Alberto Núñez Feijóo, the conservative People's Party leader who's seen as the front-runner in the election race, has said "the priority now is to attend to the victims" and the PP has suspended all its campaign activity.

    Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's Socialist PSOE has also cancelled its events today, and he's given all his "affection, solidarity and support" to anyone caught up in the tragedy.

    But there's no such suspension of politics for Spain's far-right Vox party, which is seen as a possible coalition partner for Feijóo's conservatives after next month's vote.

    Santiago Abascal has gone on TV to condemn the "deterioration of infrastructure and institutions in Spain which frankly is totally unbearable" and he has blamed it on the current government, complaining of the "neglect, ruin, betrayal suffered by the Spanish people".

    There has been no confirmation yet of what led to the accident, but Abascal also refused to halt campaigning ahead of a regional vote in January in the aftermath of the Adamuz rail disaster, which claimed 46 lives on the same high-speed line.
