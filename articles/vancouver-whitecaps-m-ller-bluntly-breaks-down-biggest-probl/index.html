Vancouver Whitecaps' Müller bluntly breaks down biggest problem with MLS | Daily Hive | Offside

TheTools · 22.11.2026 18:21 · source · permalink

Thomas Müller is one of the biggest stars in all of sports to play in Vancouver.

The former Bundesliga star is one of the greatest German players of all time and, even as he is late in his career, he has raised the level of the Vancouver Whitecaps, helping them become one of the top teams in MLS.

But with that success comes the question of whether MLS will ever be considered one of the best leagues in the world.

MLS has long been considered a step below the top-tier leagues in Europe — perhaps several steps below. The signing of top players like Müller and Lionel Messi has helped to legitimize the league, but they have also dominated in MLS competition in the twilight of their careers, which speaks to the lower caliber of play.

‘There is no big pressure’

When Müller sat down with Jay Janower of Global News for an extended interview, he was asked to compare MLS to leagues in Europe, and Müller’s assessment was bluntly honest.

“It’s difficult to compare because of the system,” said Müller. “There is no relegation; there is no big pressure. There is, ‘Oh yeah, we do a rebuild.’ Nobody’s happy to lose, but it’s totally different when you fight for getting relegated.”

The lack of relegation in MLS has long been a criticism of the league from outside of North America. In the U.S. and Canada, this is simply how professional sports works: there’s no relegation in baseball, football, or hockey — how unthinkable would it be for the Vancouver Canucks to be bumped down to the AHL for finishing last in the league last season?

In other professional soccer leagues, the threat of relegation — and the motivation of promotion — is a fundamental part of the sport.

According to Müller, that threat of relegation keeps teams sharp and the competition meaningful.

“Maybe you lose budget for the next year, the whole club,” said Müller. “You lose people working at the club and the budget changes. It’s totally different for fans. The pressure. The players have maybe no contracts for next year when you go down.

“So the playing style changes when you play a bottom-of-the-league team in Europe; they fight for their lives.”

‘The playing style is… careless defensively’

There’s actually motivation for teams in MLS to finish lower in the standings, as it gives them a higher pick in the MLS SuperDraft, even if it’s not the primary pipeline for talent acquisition for MLS teams.

The lack of relegation and the motivation for rebuilding changes how teams play.

“Sometimes I have the feeling, because of the system, the playing style is a little bit more, let’s say, optimistic, or careless defensively,” said Müller. “Just a little bit more, the French would say, laissez-faire. That’s maybe good for seeing some nice offensive plays when the games are opening up.”

In other words, without the threat of relegation, there’s less motivation to play a tight defensive game at the back end. This might lead to more exciting games, which has been a selling point for MLS as it tries to expand its share of the sports market in North America, but the quality of play suffers.

“When I compare it, I see sometimes really, really good individual actions in players. What I miss sometimes is maybe the consistency,” said Müller. “The good and the bad, the gap is bigger, but the good is…” and Müller reached his hand up above his head to indicate the quality.

“We can compare it with golf,” he continued. “Even a 10 handicapper has sometimes shots that a professional couldn’t do better. Maybe the free kick top corner. But the bad shots of a professional would be never that bad.

“Sometimes it’s more the attention of the players to be focused over 90 minutes; that’s what I see sometimes is lacking.”

While Müller may have criticisms of MLS, that hasn’t prevented him from re-signing with the Whitecaps for another season, as he has been very clear about enjoying his time in Vancouver.
