Finn Harps accounts show scale of financial challenges ahead of takeover vote - Donegal Daily

TheTools · 13.10.2026 20:19 · source · permalink

Finn Harps recorded a significantly reduced annual loss in 2025, although the Donegal club faced substantial financial challenges according to its latest accounts.

The accounts of the Finn Harps Co-Operative Society Limited for the year ending December 31, 2025 show a deficit of €20,127 compared with a loss of €100,552 in the previous 12 months.

The accounts were released to members ahead of the first of two special general meetings where they will consider and vote on a proposed takeover of the club by Filipino entrepreneur Freddy Gonzalez.

On Monday night next, October 19, Harps members will meet and if they pass a special resolution on the table a second meeting will be held on November 3 to confirm the resolution and rubber-stamp the transaction.

Harps’ numbers outline the perilous position faced by the club ahead of their final game of the season at home to Bray Wanderers on Friday night. Harps will finish bottom of the First Division.

In the 2025 calendar year, Harps’ negative reserves stood at €725,538.

Income fell from €929,458 to €882,431, while administrative expenses were reduced from €1.08m to €965,153.

The auditor drew attention to the financial position, noting that the negative reserves and a net current liability position of €170,127 gave rise to a material uncertainty relating to the club’s ability to continue as a going concern.

A note to the accounts said: “The board of management consider that the economic environment is difficult and that the outlook presents significant challenges. Whilst the board of management have instituted measures to preserve cash these circumstances create uncertainties over future results and cash flows. It also creates uncertainties in the Society's ability to continue as a going concern, realise its assets and discharge its liabilities in the normal course of business.”

The accounts also note the engagement with the Markham Capital and Sports Advisory Services team, which began in October 2025, and ultimately led to the door being open to Gonzalez’s interest in the club. 

The accounts state that creditors would be settled in full as part of the acquisition process, while the co-operative intends to continue its community work, fundraising, capital grant projects and associated grant applications.

The club’s cash and cash equivalents stood at €27,810 at the end of 2025, down from €31,645 a year earlier.

Despite the continuing difficulties, the reduction in the annual deficit represents an improvement on the previous financial year.

Harps spent just under €1m in 2025 (€965,153) while they brought in €882,431 plus €66,717 of operating income, which including a wage subsidy scheme and a Department of Transport grant.

Sponsorship income dropped to €64,500 in 2025 from €99,120 in 2024, merchandise sales took in €65,509 as against €73,737 in 2025.

Gate receipts fell from €129,500 to €120,650, although season tickets showed an increase, jumping from €43,919 to €51,861. The half-time draw and lotto took in €36,854 with the 500 Club bringing €66,269.

Harps’ cut their spending on wages and salaries, with the spend in 2025 recorded as €384,659 compared to €406,471 in the previous 12 months.

Harps showed an outlay of €186,276 in player wages, €92,022 to first team management and coaching staff and €106,361 to administrative staff. Separately, the accounts show that in 2025 the average monthly number of employees, during the year was 18 players, three management and coaching staff and three administrative staff. 

Harps’ spend on training facilities and expenses was cut from €30,403 to €17,943 while merchandise stock cost fell dramatically from €67,394 to €13,164 and meals, travel and accommodation dropped to €14,524 from €32,056.
