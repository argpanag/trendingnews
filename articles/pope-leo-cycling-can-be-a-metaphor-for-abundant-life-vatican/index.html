Pope Leo: Cycling can be a metaphor for abundant life - Vatican News

TheTools · 23.11.2026 11:28 · source · permalink
  • Pope Leo XIV
  • sport
  • evangelization

By Christopher Wells

Recalling his own experience of following cycling events, Pope Leo XIV on Monday told cyclists and those involved in cycling that following the exploits of athletes “goes beyond the competitive event itself,” with victories and defeats expressing “the values and challenges of which human experience is made up.”

Speaking to participants in a meeting promoted by the Union Cycliste Internationale, the Pope said, “Cycling can therefore be understood as a metaphor for that ‘life in abundance’ that is characteristic of every authentic sporting experience.”

Cycling and spirituality

Pope Leo reflected on the “spiritual dimension” of sport “that is not separate from physical exertion, but rather ennobles it.”

He highlighted especially the “humble manner of travelling by bicycle” as “a worthwhile proposal for fostering our interior life,” as it can “cultivate attentiveness to creation, away from the noise and traffic of the city.”

He noted, citing the works of Madeleine Delbrêl, the association of spirituality with cycling: “in our journey of faith, ‘rather like a bicycle, we cannot remain upright without moving.”

An instrument of evangelization

The Holy Father also recalled Pope Pius XII's decision to make Our Lady of Ghisallo the Patroness of cyclists in 1949, drawing a connection to the classic cycling routes that often conclude on the summits of mountains, where one often finds shrines or chapels.

“Devotion to Our Lady of Ghisallo has brought together various pilgrimages of cyclists here in the Vatican,” Pope Leo continued, “bearing witness to the fact that the bicycle can even be an instrument of evangelisation.”

He pointed to the Blessed Don Giovanni Fornasini, who travelled on bike from village to village to encourage his people and bring them the Sacraments during the Second World War, and who was martyred precisely because of his apostolic zeal.

Precisely in view of this history, Pope Leo encouraged cycling leaders, race organizers, and cycling experts “to ensure that this sport continues to promote experiences of authentic humanity.”

Finally, the Holy Father recalled cyclists who have been injured and killed in accidents, inviting the faithful to remember them in prayer.
