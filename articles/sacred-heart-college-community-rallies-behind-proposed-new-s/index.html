Sacred Heart College community rallies behind proposed new sports centre

TheTools · 24.10.2026 18:02 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

A capital-raising campaign for a proposed new $32 million multifunctional sports facility at Sacred Heart’s Glendowie campus has been launched, with several sporting alumni name-dropped in connection with the project.

The planned 4000sq m Marist Brothers Sports Centre will include a sports hall the size of three basketball courts, strength and conditioning facilities, teaching spaces, changing rooms, offices and a multipurpose community and event space.

Two of the college’s sports prefects, James Vettori (son of former Black Cap Daniel Vettori) and Luca George, announced the proposed new build to their 1500 schoolmates.

In doing so, they acknowledged that some of the college’s famous former students, including All Blacks captain Sean Fitzpatrick, All White Tim Payne, world champion sprint cyclist Ethan Mitchell, Warriors and Kiwis star Logan Swann, and dual rugby code exponent Craig “Postie” Innes, would be giving their time to the capital-raising campaign.

The Marist Brothers Sports Centre will be the hub of the school’s sports precinct, sitting alongside the Aquatic Centre and serving as a gateway to the 22ha of playing fields.

To date, $17m has been raised, with the balance targeted to come from grants, sponsorship and the Sacred Heart College community, with the aim of opening the facility in late 2028 to coincide with the college’s 125th jubilee.

The campaign’s tagline is “Where it All Begins” – a nod to Sacred Heart’s proud sports tradition dating back to 1903, and an acknowledgment that Year 7 is where it all starts for the boys, where they first pull on the college’s colours and enjoy playing sport.

Sacred Heart has appointed a health advisory board from within its community to guide the sports faculty for the benefit of students.

Founding board members include Professor Sir Collin Tukuitonga from the University of Auckland’s Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, mental health advocate Sir John Kirwan, and Dave Monro, New Zealand Heart Foundation’s chief adviser on food and nutrition.

Headmaster Patrick Walsh noted that Sacred Heart had a proven track record of outstanding sporting results.

“We wish to complement our dedicated staff and talented students with facilities to match.

“At the same time, the health and wellbeing curriculum will ensure that all students benefit from this extraordinary facility.”

The sports centre has been designed by Jasmax Architects, which has undertaken an extensive consultation and design process with key stakeholders at Sacred Heart.
