Rolex Paris Masters Predictions: Tennis best bets and picks for Monday, November 2, plus tournament futures

TheTools · 02.11.2026 05:13 · source · permalink
  • Tennis
  • ATP
  • WTA

Zachary Cohen runs through his tennis predictions and tennis best bets for all of the action at the Rolex Paris Masters on Monday, November 2.

The final ATP Masters 1000 event of the 2026 tennis season is here, as the top players in the world are competing at the Paris La Defense Arena for the Rolex Paris Masters. With that in mind, I’ll be handicapping all of the action over the next week or so. That begins with my Day 1 tennis predictions and best bets for Monday, November 2. Keep reading for some of my favorite selections for this loaded slate, but don’t stop there. Gill Alexander will be posting his best bets on the VSiN picks page daily, and I’ll also be adding some additional plays there.

Rolex Paris Masters Futures

Carlos Alcaraz To Win Tournament (+187 – 2 units)

I had a bigger play on Alcaraz to win Vienna last week, as the Spaniard’s draw was remarkably easy. In Paris, Alcaraz will face the winner of Alex Michelsen and Jiri Lehecka in the second round, then he could see some of the following names before even reaching the semifinals: Ugo Humbert, Arthur Fils, Taylor Fritz. It’ll take a lot more for Alcaraz to lift the trophy this week, but I still think he’ll get it done. In Vienna, Alcaraz had a TennisViz Performance Rating of 9.00. That was the first time he posted a PR of 9.00 or higher since his run to the Indian Wells semifinals in March. It sure seems like he’s starting to find his peak level again, and that level is something only Jannik Sinner can match. Well, Sinner remains out as he continues to rehab his knee, and Alcaraz should take advantage of that. The only thing that can keep the Spaniard from winning this 1000 is the Spaniard. If he is dialed in with his game plans, and doesn’t get bored of them, he’ll win this event.

Daniil Medvedev To Win Quarter 3 (+240)

Medvedev had won seven consecutive matches before losing to Alcaraz in the semifinals in Vienna, and that was a match in which he handled himself extremely well in a 6-4, 7-6 (2) defeat. All in all, Medvedev is 18-3 since the US Open, his hold rate is up to 84.7% on the season, and his ace rate is up at 13.2% on hard courts this year. With Medvedev earning more free points on serve this season, he’s starting to look more like the Medvedev from 2023. Well, that player is extremely dangerous indoors. With his elite returning, flat groundstrokes from the baseline, and overall brilliance as a tactician, I think he’ll make a run here. Medvedev won this tournament back in 2020, and his draw here is somewhat manageable.

Rolex Paris Masters Day 1 Best Bets – November 2

Hubert Hurkacz vs. Lorenzo Musetti Over 24.5 Games (-110)

If Hurkacz beats Musetti, it’s likely the Pole will do so in a lengthy match with several tiebreakers. Hurkacz just isn’t a strong returner, so consistently getting into a decent server’s games will be tough indoors. However, even a Musetti victory should come in a long match. If Hurkacz is on as a server, he’ll consistently target the Musetti backhand and give himself chances to win sets. Ultimately, I see both players getting on the board. However, I wouldn’t rule out a 7-6, 7-5 or 7-6, 7-6 victory for someone. That’s why I’m going Over on games instead of taking the +120 for Over 2.5 sets. It’s not worth it to lose with the right read for an extra 30 cents.

Daniel Merida Aguilar ML (+142) vs. Alexander Blockx

Merida Aguilar lost to a red-hot Medvedev in Vienna, but there’s no shame in that. Let’s see how he looks in a meeting with Blockx, who might be a little fatigued at this point in the season. The 21-year-old hasn’t advanced in a tournament since the US Open, so Merida Aguilar is getting him at the right time. I think the Spaniard’s baseline game will hold up extremely well here. We just need him to hit his spots as a a server. If he does, we should see some tight sets in this one, so it’ll be worth holding a +142 ticket.

Ugo Humbert ML (-154) vs. Botic van de Zandschulp

Humbert came up short in his attempt to knock off Novak Djokovic last tournament, but he did take a set and put up a strong fight. Beating van de Zandschulp should be a bit easier, and that’s especially true given Humbert’s love for competing indoors. Having the home crowd also won’t hurt, so I’ll happily back the better player at a reasonable -154 price. Humbert is 5-0 against van de Zandschulp in his career, and he had a 6-3, 6-4 win over him on indoor hard courts earlier in the year.
