Cricket NSW says North Sydney Oval will always be off limit for the Bears

Cricket NSW has all but ruled out the Perth Bears returning to North Sydney Oval after knocking back the incoming NRL club’s request to host their comeback game at the venue.

The Bears were informed on Monday morning that Cricket NSW won’t give up the ground, as it would result in grade cricket club Norths giving up 50 per cent of its home matches, as well as finals if they qualified. Women’s cricket internationals and Big Bash games are also scheduled during that period.

North Sydney Oval is also acting as a back-up ground to the Sydney Cricket Ground, meaning there would be no fallback option for Sheffield Shield matches if the SCG became unavailable.

Cricket NSW chief executive Lee Germon said that even with more lead time to negotiate the logistical challenges, the Bears were unlikely to return to the venue.

“I think it’s very unlikely because of the impact,” Germon said. “We know that there’s at least a minimum of five weeks required for the ground to be out to enable it to occur. That doesn’t matter how much in advance you have the information or not. So I would say it’s very unlikely.

“It’s a key cricket ground. We’re also faced with this every year – we would love to hold a Shield final at the SCG, but we can’t because of AFL commitments. It’s the world in which we live, that cricket has the rights to North Sydney Oval in what is a cricket season, and cricket is the summer sport.

“So it would be very unlikely that we’d be able to accommodate any request of this nature moving forward.”

It means the Bears, who will represent Western Australia while wearing the colours and emblem of former foundation club North Sydney, will open their pre-season with a clash against Parramatta at Eric Tweedale Stadium in Merrylands. It will be an understated setting for a clash of such historic import, given the ground - situated within Granville Park in Merrylands – has a capacity of just 5000, with only 750 grandstand seats.

The cost of taking out and dropping in a cricket wicket to accommodate the football is about $175,000 and the Bears were initially prepared to cover half the costs. However, Germon said the decision was about protecting cricket’s interests, rather than about money.

Speaking at the release of the NRL draw on Friday, NRL executive chairman Peter V’landys said he was ready to intervene in a bid to end the stalemate to ensure the Bears returned to North Sydney Oval.

“It’s their spiritual home, and why would you [rob] the fan of that?” V’landys said at the time. “It’s beyond comprehension. So there’s got to be a resolution reached to it.”

However, the short time frames meant Cricket NSW had to make a definitive call, and that came on Monday morning.

Asked how he thought the news would go down with V’landys, Germon said: “I don’t know. I don’t know him, but I don’t really care. This is a decision that we’ve made, and it’s a decision in the best interests of our sport …

“Let me just say that this was not a commercial decision; this was a sport and a cricket decision. I just wanted to reiterate that because it wouldn’t have mattered in the end what money was offered to compensate us; we just cannot do this from a cricket and a sport perspective.

“Absolutely, I understand they wanted to host the game there. I understand the ground’s importance to the Bears, just as it is to our sport as well. But also, the world has changed, and we live in the world of venue hire agreements that none of us are probably totally happy with. That’s just the reality that we have to deal with as sports administrators, but I understand the importance of the Bears and obviously wish them luck with their trial game and the season ahead.”
