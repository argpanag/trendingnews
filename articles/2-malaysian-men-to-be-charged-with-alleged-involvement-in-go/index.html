2 Malaysian men to be charged with alleged involvement in Government official impersonation scam

TheTools · 20.10.2026 22:00 · source · permalink

Two Malaysian men, aged 25 and 26, will be charged on Wednesday (Oct 21) for their alleged involvement in a Government official impersonation scam that resulted in a woman losing $3,500 in cash, about $20,000 in foreign currency and jewellery.

In a statement on Tuesday (Oct 20), the police said the 40-year-old victim received a call on Oct 13 from an individual claiming to represent electronics retailer Harvey Norman regarding a delivery.

When she said she had not made any purchases, the caller told her that her identity might have been misused and that a police report would be filed on her behalf.

The following day, another person claiming to represent Harvey Norman contacted her and guided her through the cancellation of the purported orders. 

She was then instructed to contact someone claiming to be a staff member of the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) via WhatsApp.

The purported MAS staff member told the woman to share her screen and log into her bank accounts under the pretext of checking them.

She was also instructed to disclose her internet banking credentials and one-time passwords, approve transactions made through her accounts, and photograph her jewellery and cash before handing them over for further checks.

The woman subsequently met an unknown man outside her residence and handed him $3,500 in cash, about $20,000 in foreign currency and jewellery.

She realised she had been scammed when she visited MAS and was told that none of the people she had been communicating was an MAS staff member. 

Through follow-up investigations, police officers identified the man who met the victim and arrested him on Oct 19, along with a male accomplice. 

The accomplice was found in possession of 17 pieces of gold jewellery believed to be scam proceeds.

A follow-up raid at a budget hotel led to the recovery of cash amounting to $55,000 and 24 pieces of gold jewellery, which were also believed to be scam proceeds.

Preliminary investigations found that the two men had allegedly been tasked by unknown individuals believed to be members of a transnational scam syndicate to collect cash and valuables from victims and pass them to other individuals.

The two men will be charged with abetment by conspiracy to assist another person to retain benefits from criminal conduct.

If convicted, they face up to 10 years' imprisonment, a fine of up to $500,000, or both.

Rising trend of Malaysians assisting scam syndicates

The police said they have observed an increasing trend of Malaysian nationals travelling to Singapore to help scam syndicates collect cash, gold and other valuables from victims.

The police reminded members of the public not to transfer cash, jewellery or other valuables to unknown persons, leave valuables out for collection, or share their device screens and banking credentials with others.

"Singapore Government officials will never ask you to transfer money over the phone, request your banking login details, instruct you to install mobile apps from unofficial app stores, or transfer your call to the police," said the police. 

Anyone with information about such crimes can call the police hotline at 1800-255-0000 or submit information online at www.police.gov.sg/i-witness.
