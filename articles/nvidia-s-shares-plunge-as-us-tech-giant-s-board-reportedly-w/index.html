Nvidia's shares plunge as US tech giant's board reportedly weighs ousting CEO Jensen Huang - follow live

TheTools · 12.11.2026 01:27 · source · permalink

Summary

  • The board of tech giant Nvidia is considering whether to oust its CEO Jensen Huang, the BBC's US partner CBS News and other US media report

  • Stocks in the firm have plummeted after the New York Times reported that Huang had a second affair with an executive at the company

  • The company has already launched an investigation into an affair between Huang and an alleged Chinese spy, Alice Huang. The FBI is also investigating it

  • Earlier this week, the CEO admitted that he had been "unfaithful" to his wife and apologised to her and their children

  • It remains unclear what, if any, intelligence Alice Huang – also known as Huang Le – allegedly gathered and passed to Beijing. China has rejected the spying allegations

  • Nvidia is the world's second most valuable company after Apple, and is at the heart of the AI race between the US and China

The board of tech giant Nvidia is considering whether to oust its CEO Jensen Huang, the BBC's US partner CBS News and other US media report

Stocks in the firm have plummeted after the New York Times reported that Huang had a second affair with an executive at the company

The company has already launched an investigation into an affair between Huang and an alleged Chinese spy, Alice Huang. The FBI is also investigating it

Earlier this week, the CEO admitted that he had been "unfaithful" to his wife and apologised to her and their children

It remains unclear what, if any, intelligence Alice Huang – also known as Huang Le – allegedly gathered and passed to Beijing. China has rejected the spying allegations

Nvidia is the world's second most valuable company after Apple, and is at the heart of the AI race between the US and China

Live Reporting

Edited by Chris Graham and Brajesh Upadhyay

  1. Successors for Jensen Huang are thin on the groundpublished at 01:12 GMT
    01:12 GMT

    Kali Hays
    US technology reporter

    Huang has been seen as so crucial to Nvidia's ongoing success that the company – which is the second-most valuable in the world – had not been planning for his replacement.

    There is no name that has surfaced this week as an obvious successor, even as Huang had an affair with a woman who was possibly acting as a spy.

    Should Nvidia's board choose to oust Huang, or place him on leave pending the just-launched investigation into his extramarital affairs, there are seemingly few people within the company who could immediately take on the role of CEO.

    One possibility is Nvidia co-founder Chris Malachowsky. He has been at the company as long as Huang, and for years led its product engineering work. He is the only executive that is at the same level of seniority as Huang.

    Huang's children also work at Nvidia in executive positions, but they are relatively young and inexperienced.

    Any permanent replacement for Huang would likely be an outside hire.

  2. Nvidia may find it difficult to ignore Huang's alleged second affairpublished at 00:54 GMT
    00:54 GMT

    Kali Hays
    US technology reporter

    Nvidia's board is meeting today to consider whether to remove Jensen Huang, as the chipmaker confronts reports of two extramarital affairs involving its longtime chief executive, according to reports from the Wall Street Journal and the Financial Times.

    Huang's affair with a presumed spy for China, as bad as that sounds, was widely seen in technology circles as something the billionaire co-founder of Nvidia could move past.

    But a second affair, if confirmed, and with an employee, is something that any corporate board would have a hard time letting go.

    As one technology industry insider put it to the BBC on Wednesday: "He's cooked."

    In the first case, Huang had insisted in private that he had no idea the woman was possibly feeding information to Beijing, telling President Donald Trump as much on Monday.

    The CEO is considered a crucial part of Nvidia's massive success, and Nvidia has become a vital part of the artificial intelligence boom in the US.

    The company is one of the biggest manufacturers of a computer chip required to operate AI tools and language models.

  3. Who is Jensen Huang?published at 00:45 GMT
    00:45 GMT

    Osmond Chia
    Business reporter

    The Nvidia boss leads one of the most important companies in the AI race, designing the chips that power the technology.

    Jensen Huang had co-founded Nvidia as a young engineer in 1993 and led the company into a now multi-trillion dollar semiconductor giant as the AI industry boomed.

    The entrepreneur was an early AI believer, pivoting his video-game chip business to develop the semiconductors critical for AI development.

    Huang has been the public face of Nvidia and has achieved near rock-star status among admirers.

    In his birthplace of Taiwan, fans call him "Daddy Huang", while others have likened him to the "Taylor Swift of tech".

    He has an estimated net worth of $145bn (£109bn), according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index.

  4. Why is Nvidia so important?published at 00:41 GMT
    00:41 GMT

    US tech giant Nvidia is the world's most valuable publicly-traded company - worth some $4.5tn (£3.4tn) - and a leading provider of advanced computer chips.

    These computer chips are critical for all kinds of technology - from video games and autonomous vehicles to massive data centres and cloud servers.

    But most importantly for the AI arms race, these chips are used to power artificial intelligence, including large language models and generative AI systems.

    The US's biggest competitor in the race to build the most powerful AI systems is China, and Washington has restricted exports of the most advanced Nvidia chips to Beijing since 2022.

    The US has justified export restrictions on national security grounds, arguing that such exports could enhance China's military capabilities, although some restrictions have been eased under President Trump.

  5. Nvidia's share price plunges in after-hours tradingpublished at 00:28 GMT
    00:28 GMT

    Michael Race
    Business and economics reporter

    Shares in Nvidia, the world's second most valuable company behind Apple, have plunged almost 7.5% in after hours trading.

    The chip-making giant ended the day on Wall Street in positive terrority after a difficult few days following the news of an affair between CEO Jensen Huang and a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy.

    But that has changed since the market closed, with reports of a second affair emerging.

    Nvidia is widely considered one of the most important companies behind the artificial intelligence (AI) boom, given it is the sole manufacturer of a computer chip used in AI tools.

    Its chips have become a matter of national security for US officials who are keen to win the AI race against China.

  6. Nvidia board meets over future of CEO Jensen Huangpublished at 00:13 GMT
    00:13 GMT

    Kali Hays
    US technology reporter

    US tech giant Nvidia is reportedly looking today at whether its CEO will remain with the company.

    The company's board is meeting as its stocks plummeted following US media reports about a second alleged affair involving CEO Jensen Huang.

    While the company just yesterday hired a major law firm to conduct an investigation into Huang's affair with a woman who is allegedly a spy for China, today the New York Times reported that Huang also had an affair with an Nvidia employee.

    That pushed the board to consider Huang's ongoing position as CEO of the company, according to reports from the Wall Street Journal and the Financial Times.

    Nvidia's board had previously appeared supportive of Huang, saying it had no reason to believe the company's secrets or national security were compromised related to Huang's first affair.

    As of late Wednesday evening, Nvidia's board had not released any statement. Calls to multiple company representatives and Nvidia board members have not received a response.
