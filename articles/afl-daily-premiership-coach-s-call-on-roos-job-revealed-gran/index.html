GRAND FINAL UMPIRES CONFIRMED

The AFL has confirmed the umpiring panel for Saturday’s grand final between the Dockers and Lions.

Hayden Meyer headlines the selection, with Brett Rosebury, Brendan Hosking and Nick Foot named as the four field umpires.

Nathan Williamson has been named the emergency field umpire.

But Meyer’s inclusion will be of most interest, after he made the controversial holding-the-ball call late in last Saturday’s preliminary final between the Lions and Hawks.

It’ll be the third big dance he’ll officiate in, while Rosebury will run out for the 10th time in his career, Hosking his second and Foot his second.

Between them, the quartet have umpired a total of 107 AFL finals and 13 grand finals.

HORSE MAKES CALL ON ROOS JOB

Sydney premiership coach John Longmire has reportedly ruled himself out of running for the vacant North Melbourne role.

That’s according to SEN’s Tom Morris, who reports the veteran’s decision comes after “multiple phone chats” with Kangaroos president Sonja Hood. He never met with North.

Longmire is said to have made his decision on Monday, after mulling over additional job information passed on by club chief executive Jen Watt.

Longmire played 200 games for North Melbourne, including the club’s 1999 premiership.

The 2012 Swans flag-winner’s call comes after fellow premiership coach and good mate Adam Simpson also ruled himself out.

Morris reports that Roos football boss Todd Viney “suggested to the Simpson camp that although the process was open, their research told them that second-time coaches are less likely to be successful”.

Jaymie Graham, Corey Enright and Xavier Clarke are among the first-timer names that shape as contenders for the North job.

CEO Watt is chairing the panel that will pick the club’s next coach, which includes Viney, Andrew Swallow, Darren Crocker, Nick Dowling and Steele Sidebottom.

‘ALWAYS A CHANCE’: UNCERTAINTY OVER FAGAN’S FUTURE REVEALED

A layer of uncertainty over the future of Brisbane mastermind Chris Fagan has been revealed, as he and his side prepare for a shot at a historic third-straight premiership.

The Herald Sun’s Chief Football Reporter Jay Clark revealed on Monday afternoon that “there is a chance” the much-loved Lions coach’s time at the helm “could be complete” after this weekend’s big dance.

The report made reference to Fagan’s previous stresses within the highly-scrutinised role, and that the 65-year-old will hold conversations with his family and closest friends post-season about what is next.

“I guess there’s always a chance,” three-time Brisbane player Jonathan Brown told Fox Footy’s On The Couch on Monday night.

“I have heard that similar speculation for the last week or so, but I haven’t heard any authority on it.

“So you could understand if he wanted to walk off into the sunset, but especially if he won on Saturday, I think he’d be too tempted to go for four next year. But I’m not sure, you’re a lot closer, Leigh.”

Brown’s long-time coach and AFL Hall of Fame Legend Leigh Matthews added: “Well, I mean, he’s in his 10th year, and I know for the last few years, at the end of each year, I mean, he doesn’t worry about his contract.

“That’s irrelevant to him. It’s at what point does he get to the end of the year ... because coaching is a lifestyle and ‘Bucks’ (Nathan Buckley) knows that.

“It’s a lifestyle that you’re going to have to be prepared to live, and at some point of his time he might decide ‘I don’t want to do that anymore.‘

“But this has been the situation, I would have thought, the last few years, so it’s nothing new much really.”

Fagan is currently contracted until the end of 2027, with Clark’s report stating he certainly won’t coach beyond then at Brisbane.

His reign at Springfield began in 2017, and after taking Brisbane through the back end of a rebuild during his first two seasons as head coach, has qualified for finals in eight consecutive campaigns.

STOOD DOWN SWANS STARS SET TO RETURN TO CLUB FOR FIRST TIME

Sydney’s quintet of players who were stood down in August for breaching club standards are reportedly set to return to the club for the first time since their punishments were handed down.

AFL Media reported on Monday that Isaac Heeney, Chad Warner, Nick Blakey, James Jordon and Riley Bice are expected to return to their facilities in some capacity this week, after the Swans were eliminated from the premiership race last Friday night against Fremantle.

It remains unclear whether the five key players will have formal exit meetings like the rest of their teammates, though the possibility of an apology from them to the wider playing cohort remains possible.

The outlet’s report also stated that Jordon is understood to have recently met up with a group of Swans players, among other teammates who have kept in close contact with those punished.

Separately to the club’s punishments, a police investigation into an alleged sexual assault at the Pullman Hotel in East Melbourne remains open, which saw Victoria Police travel to New South Wales and speak to four people as part of their investigation.

Police state they have identified all parties who were in the room at the time of the alleged incident, and are not looking for anyone further. The Age revealed early this month that Bice has been cleared of any criminal conduct in the unfolding investigation, and is said to be treated as one of the potential witnesses.
