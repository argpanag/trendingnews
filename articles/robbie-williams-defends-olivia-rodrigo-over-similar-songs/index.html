Robbie Williams defends Olivia Rodrigo over ‘similar’ songs

TheTools · 10.11.2026 08:06 · source · permalink

Former Take That star says plagiarism accusations are often ‘absolute bollocks’ after fans notice echoes of his 2012 hit Candy

Robbie Williams has defended Olivia Rodrigo after fans pointed out similarities between their songs.

Rodrigo recently released her first Christmas song, Take Me Home (4 Christmas), which was inspired by The Waitresses’ 1981 song Christmas Wrapping.

Fans have claimed Rodrigo’s song is also similar to Williams’ 2012 single Candy, which went to number one in the UK.

The speculation prompted Williams to defend Rodrigo, saying he thought plagiarism accusations in the music industry were often “absolute bollocks”.

Candy is the third song from the former Take That singer’s ninth album, Take The Crown. It was co-written with Gary Barlow, another member of Take That.

Comparisons have been drawn on social media between the tempo and melody of each song’s verses before and after their choruses, with both using eclectic guitars and adopting an upbeat, pop rhythm.

Social media users have also noted melodic similarities between both songs’ bridge, although Williams’ takes him into a final chorus while Rodrigo’s closes out the song.

Williams wrote on Instagram: “There’s something about the music industry that’s been bothering me for a while. People coming after people because they’ve spotted a similarity between two songs.

“Some of it is absolutely justified. Most of it, I think, is complete bollocks.

“I’ve had plenty of people come after me over the years because something I’ve released apparently sounds like something somebody else released.

“And sometimes I listen and think, fair enough, I can hear that. Other times I think, are you having a f---ing laugh?”

Discussing Rodrigo, he added: “It sounds to me as though she may have accidentally stumbled across a musical echo of mine in one of her new songs.

“And do you know what? I couldn’t care less. I don’t know whether there’s technically a publishing issue, a copyright issue, or no issue whatsoever.

“And I don’t want to know. I’m not going after her. Because I think at some point, some f---ing sanity has to prevail.

“Now, I appreciate I’m in a position in my career where I can afford to take that view. Not everybody is. And genuine theft is genuine theft. Artists deserve to be protected.

“But there’s a difference between protecting your work and treating every vaguely familiar sequence of notes as a winning lottery ticket.”

Williams said he felt it was inevitable that artists would sometimes produce music that had a similar sound or melody to other songs.

He wrote: “We all stand on the shoulders of people who came before us. Sometimes consciously. Sometimes without even knowing it. That’s how music works.

“If we keep punishing people for accidentally arriving somewhere somebody else has already been, eventually we’re going to frighten the creativity out of everyone.

“Anyway, Olivia, if you’re reading this, I think you’re f---ing brilliant.”

Neil McCormick

Christopher Butler of The Waitresses revealed Rodrigo had contacted him after being inspired by his song Christmas Wrapping.

Take Me Home (4 Christmas) has a similar beat and Rodrigo uses a comparable vocal style to the 1981 Christmas hit.

Rodrigo’s track was released on Nov 5 accompanied by a music video that features Cynthia Nixon, the Sex And The City actress, as well as Ben Marshall, the US comedian seen on Saturday Night Live.
