SIC to review MotoGP ticket prices, track rental charges to boost revenue

TheTools · 28.10.2026 08:17 · source · permalink

CEO Azhan Shafriman Hanif says the circuit will also develop additional revenue streams amid rising operating costs.

Its CEO, Azhan Shafriman Hanif, said the circuit would also develop additional income sources amid rising operating costs.

“We will review the prices and ensure that any increase does not burden fans too much. Our costs have increased. The last price review was back in 2022 after the Covid-19 pandemic,” Bernama quoted him as saying.

MotoGP has been a fixture on SIC’s calendar since 1999, except for cancellations in 2020 and 2021 following the Covid-19 pandemic.

In July, the youth and sports ministry announced a five-year extension for Malaysia to host MotoGP from 2027 to 2031, involving estimated total financing of RM457 million.

Under a progressive cost-sharing arrangement, SIC will bear 10% of annual hosting and organising costs in 2027, with its share rising to 50% by 2031 to reduce reliance on federal funding.

Azhan said the government previously bore the full rights fee while much of the event’s economic benefit flowed to hotels, food and beverage businesses, logistics providers and local suppliers.

He said SIC’s direct event income came from ticket sales, hospitality, retail sales and other commercial activities within the venue.

Azhan said rising track rental costs were squeezing margins, with current charges at about RM70,000 to RM80,000 a day, inclusive of marshals and circuit facilities.

He said SIC would consider Malaysian event organisers’ needs and benchmark its rates against local and international circuits.

“What we need to know is the comparison with tracks around us and also international tracks. How much are they charging? How much are we charging? It goes back to the value and whether the value we provide is commensurate with what they pay,” he said.

Azhan said improvements to the spectator experience would also need to precede any increase in ticket prices.

“We planned to increase the price a few years back, but we think that the product needs improvement before we increase the ticket price. We can learn from our F1 experience.

“There were many things F1 asked us to provide, including giant screens, sound systems and access to water and shade. These are the things we will look at before increasing MotoGP ticket prices,” he said.

Azhan said an equal split between domestic and foreign spectators over the next five years would be ideal.

“We have to work hand in hand with Tourism Malaysia and MotoGP to do more promotion and attract more foreign tourists to Malaysia,” he said.
