What to Watch For

Alvarez is the obvious focal point for Houston, but Jeremy Peña has quietly built a strong case as one of the more complete players in the lineup — .278 average, 17 home runs, 46 RBI, and 9 stolen bases. Isaac Paredes adds another layer of danger with 18 home runs and 75 RBI, giving the Astros a middle of the order that can punish any mistake. On the mound, Hunter Brown (3.31 ERA, 5 wins) and Hayden Wesneski (3.18 ERA, 4 wins) represent Houston’s most reliable rotation options heading into a critical stretch.

For Arizona, Gabriel Moreno’s .298 average gives the lineup a steady presence behind the plate, while Nolan Arenado brings 21 home runs and 65 RBI to a lineup that needs its veterans to produce. Michael Soroka (3.40 ERA, 8 wins) is currently listed on the 15-day injured list with an arm issue, which puts added pressure on Brandon Pfaadt (3.49 ERA, 7 wins) to anchor the rotation. Paul Sewald leads the bullpen with 24 saves, and Dennis Santana’s 1.69 ERA makes him one of the more reliable late-inning options Arizona can deploy in close games — exactly the kind of performance the bullpen needs more of down the stretch.
