Canadian military won't release records discussing RCAF commander’s F-35 photo

TheTools · 24.09.2026 03:52 · source · permalink

RCAF commander Lt.-Gen. Jamie Speiser-Blanchet was photographed signing the fuselage bulkhead to be installed on a F-35 fighter jet.

Documents discussing a photo of the air force’s top officer signing the fuselage of Canada’s first F-35 are cloaked in secrecy with the Canadian military refusing to release the records.

Canada’s purchase of American-built F-35 fighter jets has become highly controversial in the midst of deteriorating relations between the two nations.

Royal Canadian Air Force generals have advocated for buying the planes and further integrating and co-operating more with the U.S. military even as President Donald Trump threatens to economically cripple Canada and eventually annex this country.

While Canada has committed to purchasing 16 F-35s, Prime Minister Mark Carney initiated a review of whether to purchase a remaining 72 jets the RCAF wants.

In February, RCAF commander Lt.-Gen. Jamie Speiser-Blanchet travelled to the F-35 assembly facility in Fort Worth, Texas, for a ceremony to mark a production milestone for Canada’s first F-35. During the event, Speiser-Blanchet was photographed signing the fuselage bulkhead to be installed on the jet.

Usually such a ceremony would be worthy of publicity by both F-35 manufacturer Lockheed Martin and the Canadian Forces.

But the Canadian military refused to release an original photo of the signing event and is now refusing to release records, requested under the Access to Information Act, regarding its internal discussions on the decision to suppress the image. The Ottawa Citizen filed that request on April 24. The newspaper has filed a complaint to the Information Commissioner of Canada about the military decision to withhold the records.

Shannon Clark Larkin, the executive director of the Department of National Defence’s access-to-information branch, did not respond to a request for comment.

A DND source said the military was not keen on revealing records showing their behind-the-scenes advocacy for the F-35. The Ottawa Citizen is not naming the source as they were not authorized to speak publicly about the matter.

The Ottawa Citizen also submitted an access-to-information request for the original photo of Speiser-Blanchet signing the F-35 fuselage. But DND refused to release that as well, instead providing a grainy photocopy of the image earlier this year.

There is no word on when Carney will make a decision on whether to purchase the remaining F-35s. But his review has angered the Trump administration and the U.S. has warned Canada it could face dire consequences if it doesn’t buy more F-35s. The deal to purchase the fleet of F-35s would cost taxpayers around $27 billion. Another $5.5 billion will be needed to achieve full operation capability for the planes, according to a report from the Auditor General.

Carney is also considering whether to act on a proposal to purchase Swedish Gripen fighter jets. Gripen manufacturer Saab has proposed building the jets in Canada, a move it says would create a large number of high-tech jobs and provide significant industrial benefits.

The Liberal government has been frustrated by Canadian military officers and defence officials who appear to be publicly advocating for the F-35s.

Stephen Fuhr, secretary of state for defence procurement, acknowledged the issue in an interview with the Ottawa Citizen in July. “I don’t think some of these things are necessarily helpful,” Fuhr said. “It’s in a review. We shouldn’t be talking about it. And the government will make a decision when they feel it’s time to make a decision.”

The latest incident involved RCAF Lt. Gen. Iain Huddleston, who in the summer was on LinkedIn responding to a suggestion from an official with a lobbying group for F-35 manufacturer Lockheed Martin. The official suggested not proceeding with a plan to build the Gripens and instead purchasing the F-35s and futuristic unmanned fighter jets.

The comment was directed at Carney, Fuhr, DND deputy minister Christiane Fox and others. Huddleston, deputy commander at NORAD, indicated that he found that comment “insightful.”

The Ottawa Citizen requested comment from Huddleston on July 7. Huddleston has yet to comment.

Huddleston’s action followed a CBC report on July 4 that Crista-lynn Ferguson, director general of fighter capability at DND, distributed custom-made business cards at a recent defence show in Ottawa highlighting the F-35.

In addition, military police are still conducting an investigation into who at DND or in the RCAF leaked internal records to the news media promoting the value of the F-35 over the Gripen.

An interview was requested with Ferguson, but DND stated she was not available. DND spokesperson Suzanne Lalande stated that Ferguson had the business cards made on her own initiative and did not follow approved federal government standards. She said Ferguson had “voluntarily ceased” using the card. The cards did not “communicate a position on the F-35 review or any related government decision-making process,” according to Lalande.
