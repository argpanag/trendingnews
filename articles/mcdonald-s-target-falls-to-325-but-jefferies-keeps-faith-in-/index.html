McDonald's Corp (NYSE:MCD, XETRA:MDO) retained Jefferies' backing despite a price target cut to $325 from $350, with management still needing to convince investors that a sales recovery is achievable.

Shares traded down 1% to $253.67 on September 15, as the broker maintained its 'Buy' rating.

Behind that confidence is the analysts' view that fixable summer marketing and menu missteps drove much of the sales slowdown, alongside pressure on lower-income consumers and intense promotions.

They expect marketing and menu calendar adjustments to rebuild momentum, supported by returning digital deals, Spicy Chicken McNuggets, and a SpongeBob x One Piece promotion.

The bank's forecasts put third-quarter US same-store sales growth at 0.5% versus 0.2% consensus, followed by 1.6% versus 1.3% in 2026 and 2.5% versus 2.4% in 2027.

Beyond those immediate fixes, analysts highlight the Best Burger rollout, Big Arch expansion and premium beverages as sales drivers requiring relatively modest additional investment under the "NEXT" strategy.

Alongside those initiatives, the franchise structure supports projected operating margins in the mid-to-high 40% range in financial year 2026, with administrative cost leverage potentially helping in 2027.

Delivering those gains will also require franchisee cooperation, however, and the analysts caution that securing agreement on pricing, promotions and value offerings could take longer than adjusting marketing.

That puts the September 23 investor day in focus, when management must demonstrate a credible path to faster sales growth and stronger margins to rebuild confidence, Jefferies says.
