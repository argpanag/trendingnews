Trapped in her own home: Woman makes five 111 calls before police remove boarder

TheTools · 17.11.2026 21:40 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

A woman who locked herself in her bedroom all night out of fear of her allegedly aggressive boarder was “shocked” by the lack of urgency shown by police in response to her multiple 111 calls.

The Auckland renter, who wishes to stay anonymous, said her “threatening” boarder refused to leave her home despite being handed a trespass notice.

Police told the Herald increased demand for high-priority jobs meant they could not attend the callout on Friday October 30.

“I said, ‘so you’re telling me you’re going to leave a violent, threatening person in my home who has been aggressive and there’s nothing I can do about it’,” the woman said.

Five 111 calls were made before police turned up the next morning – more than 12 hours after the episode began.

The boarder has since been trespassed and was served a three-day Police Safety Order, preventing him from entering the property.

The woman claimed the first red flag was when the man told her he could only afford one week’s rent and one week’s bond after moving back to New Zealand.

“He said he would pay the other two weeks’ bond the following week after he was able to get money from overseas accounts.

“I was silly and trusted him.”

An agreement was amended and signed. However, the man stayed just two nights and said he wanted to move out, before allegedly doing a U-turn two days later, saying he wanted to stay.

The woman reminded him he was yet to pay the remainder of his bond, before she got “really bad vibes” and gave him notice she wanted to end the tenancy.

When they couldn’t agree on when he would move out, he allegedly refused to give back his key until his bond had been returned and became threatening and aggressive towards her.

“He puffed himself up and kept making advances down the hallway towards me.”

The woman described the moment she barricaded herself in her bedroom, broke down in tears and called friends and 111 out of fear for her safety.

“The police on the phone said they were going to send police around to do a trespass... I didn’t know the process; I didn’t know what to do so they said they’d come out and do it.”

Four hours and three calls later, she claimed police told her they could no longer attend as it was a “civil matter”.

She claimed officers told her to serve the trespass notice herself, which she did with the help of friends the following morning.

“When we served the notice, he was acting all crazy saying he ‘had the right to be there’, and he ‘wasn’t going to accept it’.

“He was trying to push his way out his room calling me a psycho b***h.”

Under the Residential Tenancies Act, the landlord of a boarding house may terminate their boarder’s tenancy if they threaten people or property, or cause serious disruption to other tenants.

A Tenants’ Union spokesperson told the Herald the woman was “likely within her rights” to terminate the tenancy immediately because of his aggression.

Police Waitematā North Area Response Manager Senior Sergeant Carl Fowlie confirmed to the Herald police received three calls on Friday October 30 and two calls about 8.10am the next day before responding.

“Police attended the address a short time later and spoke with both parties.

“The boarder was served a trespass notice and a three-day Police Safety Order. He left the property with his belongings while police were in attendance.”

The woman said she believed police “needed to do a better job” at responding.

“I was awake for over 30 hours with no sleep because I was scared to sleep.

“I just imagine a woman living alone with no male friends having to do this... she’d just put up with the person being in her home.”

Fowlie said the community’s safety was the “highest priority” for police.

“Police triage reports to ensure staff are where they are most needed to manage risk and prevent harm.

“When we are unable to attend immediately, advice will be given and/or relevant follow-up inquiries will be made.”

Thinking about getting a boarder?

The Tenants’ Union spokesperson said opening your home to a boarder is a big decision.

“It’s important you do your research and if necessary, seek advice on what your obligations under the [Residential Tenancies] Act are, and what protection you have.

“I do hope this story doesn’t discourage people from taking on boarders. Our country is in the midst of a housing crisis, and the vast majority of renters and boarders in New Zealand seek nothing but a place to call home while preserving their wellbeing and dignity.”
