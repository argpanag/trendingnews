Ukrainian president Zelenskyy and wife to visit North Bay

TheTools · 11.09.2026 05:49 · source · permalink

A statement from the Prime Minister's office says the president of Ukraine, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, and his wife, the First Lady of Ukraine, Olena Zelenska, are coming to North Bay this week.

The couple will be visiting Canada from Sept. 9 to 11.

No details on the local visit have been released, but the City does have an active Ukrainian community.

In February this year, the group gathered to mark the fourth anniversary of Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

“Today is about remembering those slaughtered by Russians and raising some donations. All proceeds will be directed to support the Ukrainian Armed Forces in their continued fight for freedom and sovereignty,” said Nadiia Stukalova, spokesperson for the Ukrainian community in North Bay,

This week's visit "underscores Canada’s continued leadership in supporting Ukraine and reflects our unwavering commitment to a just and lasting peace," says the government statement.

"The President and the First Lady will attend events in Calgary, Toronto, and North Bay. President Zelenskyy will also address Cabinet members at the Cabinet Planning Forum in Banff. The visit’s programme will focus on shared priorities between Canada and Ukraine, including deepening defence, economic, and energy cooperation. It will also address the human cost of Russia’s war of aggression against Ukraine and build on the strong people-to-people ties that connect our countries."

Prime Minister Carney is not expected to accompany Zelenskyy to North Bay.

Earlier this year, there was a unanimous vote in the House of Commons that would make every September Ukrainian Canadian Heritage Month in Canada.

MP Pauline Rochefort said that Ukrainian families who have settled in her community of East Ferris are “like family.”

In an email to BayToday this morning, Rochefort said "The visit of President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to North Bay highlights the strong ties that have developed between the city and Ukrainians who have made North Bay their home. In recent years, our residents and organizations have welcomed newcomers from Ukraine and supported efforts to assist those affected by the war. The occasion also reflects Canada’s ongoing support for Ukraine and the enduring connections between Canadians and Ukrainians."

A source tells BayToday that Zelenskyy may not leave the airport. Local Ukrainian leaders tell us that they have no idea why he is here.

However, a possible reason is that the International Test Pilots School of Canada, which recently relocated to North Bay, started training 80 Ukrainian pilots at Jack Garland Airport.
