  5. How common are US school shootings?published at 17:04 BST
    17:04 BST

    By Jake Horton

    Shooting incidents at US schools have become more common over the last 15 years, according to the K-12 School Shootings database.

    It records every time a gun is fired or pointed in a school. In 2010, it recorded 15 incidents but in recent years there have been hundreds.

    The number of incidents peaked in 2023 at 352 - and so far this year there have been 149.

    These figures include gun incidents at schools in which people were not killed or injured, but the Everytown for Gun Safety group has cross-checked the database with media reports and says this year incidents have resulted in 33 deaths and 63 injuries as of 15 October.

    School shooting trends are broadly in line with figures on mass shootings across the US which have increased over the last decade but fallen in recent years, according to the Gun Violence Archive.

  7. 'A loss that feels unimaginable', and 'absolutely broken': How local politicians have respondedpublished at 16:33 BST
    16:33 BST

    Four people have died, including three children, after a school shooting at Silver Rail Elementary School in the US state of Oregon yesterday. Here is a reminder of how some politicians have responded:

    Bend Mayor Melanie Kebler said it was an "awful day" for the community which had a "profound impact on so many people, especially the children, families, educators and school staff whose lives have been forever changed".

    "I'm struggling to make sense of a loss that feels unimaginable," she told a press conference yesterday evening, and added, addressing those impacted: "We grieve alongside you and we will get through this together."

    Governor Tina Kotek told the same press conference that her heart is "absolutely broken" and the Oregon state is ready to support those in the community "in whatever you need".

    She also thanked the "quick thinking and courage" of school staff, parents and first responders at the scene, and extended her thoughts to the community member who "heroically intervened to stop further loss of life":

    "Your actions carry a heavy emotional burden and I just want you to know that my thoughts are with you as well."

    Oregon Senator Jeff Merkley asked people to "hold the students, teachers, families, and entire Silver Rail Elementary School community in your hearts" as details emerge about the victims.

    In a post on X, he said he was grateful to first responders and emergency personnel who responded to the "horrific shooting".

  10. What are the gun laws in Oregon?published at 16:06 BST
    16:06 BST

    Oregon has passed a broad range of gun safety laws in recent years, placing it 12th out of 50 states in terms of their strictness, according to gun control advocacy group Everytown for Gun Safety.

    The Oregon State Police conducts background checks on all people attempting to purchase firearms. Around 96% of background checks are approved within minutes, it says.

    Reasons for a person being denied include having been convicted of a felony or found to be mentally ill and subject to an order prohibiting them possessing a firearm.

    A person under the age of 18 cannot buy a firearm in Oregon. The state does allow juveniles to possess one with the permission of their parents but only for certain uses like hunting.

A bystander saw the shooting begin to unfold, pursued the attacker into the school and shot him dead, police said.

Three minutes later, officers arrived at the scene. They confirmed that the attacker had been killed, and then searched the school.

The victims included three children aged 5, 8 and 11 and one teacher, Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said. A seven-year-old child and a 54-year-old teacher were taken to a local hospital with "critical injuries", he said.

Schools throughout the city of Bend have been closed on Friday, with officials saying the community needs a moment to heal.
