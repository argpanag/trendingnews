India eye winning finish against Maldives to seal semi-final berth | The Shillong Times

Share post:

Moosa demands greater discipline from blue tigers

Dhaka, Nov 10: Defending champions India will look to secure their place in the semi-finals of the South Asian Football Federation (SAFF) Championship when they take on Maldives in their final Group B fixture here on Wednesday, with a draw enough to ensure their progression to the last four.
However, the nine-time champions are expected to target a convincing victory rather than settle for a point, having opened their campaign with a hard-fought 3-2 win over Pakistan on November 5.
Suhail Bhatt was the star of India’s opening match, scoring twice in the first half to put his side in a commanding position. A draw against Maldives would not only take India into the semi-finals but also ensure they finish at the top of Group B.
India are competing in the tournament with a second-string squad, as the senior national team is currently preparing for two away international friendlies against New Zealand later this week. The side in Dhaka is being coached by Under-23 head coach Naushad Moosa.
India also have some room for error, as even a narrow 0-1 defeat against Maldives could be enough to secure qualification, given their superior goal difference. India currently have a goal difference of plus one, while Maldives are at minus three.
However, a heavy defeat could complicate their qualification prospects, something the defending champions will be keen to avoid.
Pakistan have already strengthened their position in the group after registering a comprehensive 3-0 victory over Maldives on Sunday. The result has left them with three points and a goal difference of plus two from their two matches, putting them in a strong position to reach the semi-finals.
The tournament’s tie-breaking regulations could also play a crucial role in determining the final standings. If two or more teams finish level on points, their head-to-head records will be considered first, followed by goal difference in matches involving the teams concerned. The higher number of goals scored in those encounters will then be taken into account.
Despite beginning their campaign with a victory, Moosa believes his young side still have considerable room for improvement, particularly in terms of coordination, defensive organisation and decision-making under pressure.
“We want to improve our coordination, especially in transitions, defensive organisation and decision-making in key moments. Most importantly, we want to build further chemistry and competition within the group,” Moosa said ahead of the crucial encounter.
India’s opening match against Pakistan exposed some defensive shortcomings, particularly towards the closing stages. Having established a commanding 3-0 lead until the 90th minute, the Indian side allowed their opponents to score twice in stoppage time, turning what appeared to be a comfortable victory into a nervy finish.
The late lapse has prompted the coaching staff to focus on better game management and maintaining concentration throughout the contest.
Reflecting on the final moments of the Pakistan match, Moosa admitted that his team had failed to maintain the required discipline.
“The last few minutes were not what we expected. Conceding two goals in added time is something we need to look at and learn from.
“At this level, concentration, game management and maintaining our discipline are extremely important until the final whistle,” he added.
With qualification within reach, India will now be keen to address those concerns and produce a more complete performance against Maldives.
A positive result would not only take the defending champions into the knockout stage but also provide the young squad with further confidence ahead of the semi-finals.
For Moosa, Wednesday’s contest will be another important opportunity to assess the depth and potential of the emerging players, while ensuring that India remain firmly on course to defend their title. (PTI)
