Fernando Sá on Porto win: “Everything changed in defence after half-time”

Fernando Sá, head coach of FC Porto’s basketball team, pointed to the defensive turnaround after half-time as the decisive factor in their return to winning ways, with an 81-76 league victory over SC Braga. In his assessment of the game, he also highlighted the league’s increasingly demanding standards, the need to maintain intensity when switching from the Champions League to domestic competition, and the fans’ support. Summing up Porto’s comeback, he said: “Everything changed defensively in the second half, and that was the key to victory.”

The result got FC Porto back to winning ways, but did not erase the difficulties they faced during the game. Fernando Sá focused his analysis on the team’s response to opponents with strong outside shooting, stressing a message that ran throughout his assessment: performance depends on a level of concentration that cannot vary according to the competition or the opposition.

The first challenge was clear: contain a highly effective outside-shooting team without compromising their defensive shape. The coach acknowledged that Porto failed to do so in the second quarter, before finding greater consistency after half-time.

“We knew it was going to be an extremely difficult game. We were facing the team with the best outside shooting percentage in the league, with several players posting excellent percentages, so we knew we needed to be very disciplined in our one-on-one defence to avoid leaving gaps. In the second quarter, we couldn’t do that, which forced us to provide help and ended up opening up the court,” Fernando Sá explained. “They had made one three-pointer and then hit five in the second quarter. In the second half, everything changed defensively, and that helps us. They had 50 points at half-time and scored 26 in the third and fourth quarters, and I think that was the key to victory.”

The contrast between the 50 points conceded before half-time and the 26 allowed over the next two quarters supports the coach’s assessment. Rather than offensive efficiency, Sá saw the ability to close down space as the reason for the positive outcome.

That defensive demand ties into a broader warning about the league. For the coach, the quality of the opposition means they cannot afford to let their guard down, particularly when switching from the Champions League to domestic action.

“The league keeps improving. Teams are well built, have good coaches and work very well, and we’ve said that many times,” he stressed. “The moment we think we’re going to have an easier game, we’ll lose. The biggest difference I notice right now is the switch from the Champions League to the domestic league. The players need to maintain their intensity, focus and ambition, because that’s the only way we’ll achieve what we want.”

The warning leaves no room for treating domestic fixtures as an easier challenge. In Sá’s view, maintaining the same competitive commitment is essential to achieving their goals, not a detail that can be made up for during the game.

There was also time to acknowledge the support from the stands. The coach was pleased with the turnout on a Sunday afternoon when there was also football later in the day.

“We have a group of supporters that keeps growing from game to game,” he said. “Even on a Sunday afternoon, knowing there’s football later, seeing the arena as full as it was makes me very happy. Thank you all.”

Alongside the demands he placed on the team came thanks to those who support them. Sá praised both the turnout for the game and the growing fan base, adding a note of satisfaction to his assessment of the victory.
