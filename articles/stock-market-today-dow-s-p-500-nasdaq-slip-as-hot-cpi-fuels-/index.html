US stocks slipped Tuesday as investors digested a hotter-than-expected inflation report, cementing bets that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates next month.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) fell 0.3%, while the S&P 500 (^GSPC) decreased roughly 0.4% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) tumbled 0.7%. The moves in major indexes come after a sharp rally in US stocks on Monday.

The Consumer Price Index, released on Tuesday morning, showed prices rose 0.4% in October from a month earlier, higher than the 0.3% economists were expecting but cooler than September's 0.6% gain. October's CPI report did not include annual inflation figures because last year's government shutdown limited the data available.

Traders now overwhelmingly expect the Fed to hike interest rates by 25 basis points, with a subset of Fed watchers expecting a 50 basis point jumbo hike. Recent comments by Fed governor Chris Waller reinforced those bets, as he noted he would be open to a larger hike.

(^DJI )

Nvidia (NVDA) investors continue to reel from the board's investigation into CEO Jensen Huang following reports of his affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Nvidia stock dropped more than 5% on Tuesday after falling over 4% the day before.

In other tech news, Anthropic began its roadshow and released an updated filing ahead of its highly anticipated IPO. The AI lab is forging ahead with its public debut despite recent volatility in tech stocks, and is seeking a valuation of $1.4 trillion.

Oil prices are falling, with Brent crude (BZ=F), the international benchmark, and WTI crude (CL=F), the US benchmark, declining by around 1%.

While most of the third quarter earnings season is in the rearview mirror, Nvidia-backed neocloud CoreWeave (CRWV) and quantum computing stocks Quantum (QBTS) and Rigetti Computing (RGTI) report results after the bell today.

  • Treasury yields jump as traders rush to price higher Fed rates

    Shorter-term Treasury yields jumped sharply after the CPI inflation data was released, keeping the pressure on the Fed to raise rates at its next meeting.

    The 5-year yield (^FVX) jumped 10 basis points to 4.75%, while the 10-year Treasury yield rose 7 basis points to 4.93%. The 30-year yield (^TYX) increased 3 basis points to 5.29%.

    Traders now see an 84% chance that the Fed will raise interest rates by 25 basis points in December. Bets that the Fed will hike by 50 basis points jumped from 0% yesterday to 15% today.

    The move comes after Fed governor Chris Waller said he would be open to a jumbo 50 basis point rate hike in December if inflation came in hot.

    "Inflation is too high … this means that even if, as I expect, the monthly rate of inflation improves in October, I am leaning toward supporting an increase in our policy rate of 25 basis points at the FOMC's December meeting," Waller said in a speech in London.

    He added that if the October inflation data comes out higher than expected, then stronger action may be needed. "In that case, I believe a 50-basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting," Waller said. "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."

  • Vanguard and Fidelity have a warning for retirement savers

    Yahoo Finance's Ivana Pino writes:

    Fidelity and Vanguard recently released long-term forecasts for stock market returns, and both companies delivered a sobering outlook for retirees: Long-term investment returns are either modest or expected to decline.
