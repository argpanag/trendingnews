Private Rooney inquest hears vehicle was surrounded

TheTools · 18.11.2026 13:27 · source · permalink

An inquest into the death of Private Seán Rooney, who was killed in Lebanon four years ago, has heard how the military jeep he was driving was surrounded by angry locals, some armed with AK rifles, after their vehicle became separated from a convoy heading to the airport in Beirut.

The second day of the inquest into the soldier's death heard evidence of the incident from the three other passengers in the vehicle being driven by Pte Rooney.

It included a highly charged moment when Corporal (then Trooper) Nathan Bryan revealed that he had volunteered shortly before the fatal shooting to drive the vehicle.

The witness broke into tears and the deceased’s mother, Natasha McCloskey, could be heard sobbing audibly as Cpl Bryan recounted how he had told army personnel after the shooting that he wanted to "get something off my chest" by outlining his offer but that Pte Rooney had insisted on driving.

Pte Rooney, 24, from Newtowncunningham, Co Donegal, died in the attack, which took place near the southern Lebanese village of Al-Aqbiya while on UN peacekeeping duties on 14 December 2022.

The deceased, who was attached to the 27th Infantry Battalion at Aiken Barracks in Dundalk, Co Louth, suffered fatal gunshot wounds after their UN-marked vehicle was fired on by armed militia linked to Hezbollah.

The fatal incident occurred as two army Toyota Land Cruisers, each carrying four occupants, were bringing two soldiers from their base in Camp Shamrock in southern Lebanon to Beirut Airport to fly back to Ireland after they had been granted compassionate leave due to family bereavements.

The inquest heard evidence that the briefings for such trips would normally be held in a company office in the camp with a second shorter "frago" briefing just before leaving.

However, several witnesses told the inquest at Dublin District Coroner's Court there was no detailed briefing on the day of the fatal incident.

They also described how the convoy became separated after they had stopped at EP28 – a border outpost near Tyre which marked the frontier of the UNIFIL area of operation at the Litani River – to remove UN flags from their vehicles when one of the jeeps’ engines cut out.

The inquiry also heard that soldiers in the two vehicles had to use mobile phones to communicate with each other after they became separated because they could not contact each other with their radio sets.

The evidence of the witnesses, some of which was held in private on grounds of security issues, was based on statements they provided to gardaí in early 2023.

The inquest heard that the military jeep driven by Pte Rooney tried to catch up with the lead vehicle but they realised after about 10 minutes that they had missed a turn for the route to Beirut about 4km from EP28.

However, Cpl Bryan, who was in the rear of the vehicle, described how they had experienced four separate "DFOMs" – denials of freedom of movement – after they had driven into a village where their vehicle was greeted with a hostile reception by locals who became increasingly aggressive.

Cpl Bryan said the incident began when their vehicle was initially blocked about 200m after they had turned around on the outskirts of the village to return to a meeting point agreed with the lead vehicle.

The inquest heard a green van with some men standing in the middle of the road had prevented them from returning south towards EP28, while an attempt was made to open Pte Rooney's door in the vehicle.

While Pte Rooney was reversing the vehicle to turn around after they were not allowed to pass, Cpl Bryan said they were rammed by the green van.

He said they were stopped by another larger group and vehicles as they headed back towards the village.

After Pte Rooney tried to reverse the jeep again, they were surrounded by a large group of locals who were trying to get them out of the vehicle.

Cpl Bryan said that Pte Rooney's door was opened briefly at this point before he managed to lock it again.

He recalled how one male, in his 30s, produced a semi-automatic pistol and pointed it at their window while making a death threat symbol by drawing his finger across his throat.

The inquest heard someone began firing at the front of the vehicle, aiming at the bonnet and tyres, while efforts were also being made to open the boot door.

Cpl Bryan said Captain David Kelly in the lead vehicle had communicated that they would come to their assistance but they advised them that it was "too unsafe" and proceeded to "say our goodbyes to him and each other in the car."

He said the back window was smashed and the boot was forced open during the call.

Cpl Bryan said shots were fired through the rear of the vehicle as Pte Rooney was trying to push vehicles in front of him out of the way.

He recalled digging his head between his legs and pulling the other rear seat passenger, Trooper Shane Kearney, down.

Cpl Bryan said it was the last thing he recalled before waking up with the vehicle overturned on its side and Trooper Kearney appearing unconscious, while he could not see his other two colleagues in the front seats.

He described how he managed to crawl out of the vehicle and shout for help to a crowd of around 15 people.

He heard one voice tell him in English: "Don’t worry. You are OK now."

Cpl Bryan claimed he passed out again before waking briefly in an ambulance and then in hospital where he learnt that Pvt Rooney had "taken a bullet in the brain and did not make it".

In reply to questions from counsel for Pte Rooney’s family, Seán Guerin, SC, he said that they were looking for the turn for the airport after the convoy was separated but "the most important thing" was to get back to the lead vehicle.

Mr Guerin observed that the deceased might not have suffered fatal gunshot wounds but for his bravery in trying to get them out of "a situation of enormous danger".

The inquest heard the Irish soldiers never drew their weapons at any stage during the incident.

Trooper Shane Kearney, who suffered a serious head injury in the collision, said they had tried to catch-up with the lead vehicle after EP28 but believed it must have turned off somewhere as they were driving for 10-15 minutes without seeing it.

He told the inquest that another vehicle passed the outpost before they left and the lead vehicle may have presumed it was them.

During the fatal incident, Trooper Kearney remembered seeing a young male aged outside the vehicle with a pistol that did not have a magazine, who returned a few minutes later with the weapon loaded.

He recalled how they were initially saying there was no need to panic as they were in a bullet-proof vehicle before Corporal Josh Phelan indicated he would need to communicate they were "in a bit of bother".

The witness said he and Trooper Bryan were trying to keep Pte Rooney and Corp Phelan calm as they were "up the walls" after the rear window was smashed in.

Trooper Kearney said they realised they were in "a bad situation" before his next memory was "waking up in a hospital in Dublin".

In reply to questions from coroner Myra Cullinane, Trooper Kearney said he had not received any training about journeys leaving the UNIFIL area.

Asked if they had been provided with any guidance or rules before leaving, he replied: "Not that I remember."

However, he said a basic rule was that Defence Forces members could not go anywhere in just one vehicle, while the lead vehicle would always "keep eyes" on the one behind it.

He said the two vehicles were travelling at about just 20 metres apart up to EP28.

Trooper Kearney said they were trained "to an extent" about what to do in DFOM situations and would first try to de-escalate a situation before seeking further assistance if that was not possible.

Trooper Kearney, who had been dressed in civilian clothes at the time as he was going back to Ireland for his uncle’s funeral, confirmed that he did have access to body armour which was stored in the boot of the vehicle.

The witness told Mr Guerin that they had not been given any specific information about a threat level before going on the journey and a recent incident in the area.

Trooper Kearney said he was not familiar with the convoy route and he believed the only member of the convoy who had ever taken the route before other than as a passenger was Capt Kelly.

In evidence, Cpl Phelan said he, Trooper Bryan and Pte Rooney had volunteered for the convoy and they were briefed verbally by Capt Kelly about the journey at the vehicles.

"We had no hard copy of the map and the plan was to follow Capt Kelly," he observed.

The inquest before a jury of six women and four men resumes tomorrow morning.
