Very high sulfur dioxide gas emission observed from Mayon Volcano crater, says PHIVOLCS

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:26 · source · permalink

A very high volcanic sulfur dioxide (SO2) gas emission rate was observed on Thursday from the crater of Mayon Volcano, the Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS) reported.

The very high emission rate of 8,165 tonnes/day based on averaged measurements by stationary UV spectrometry, state volcanologists said.

"This is the 2nd highest emission from Mayon recorded since volcanic gas concentrations began decreasing on 16 July 2026. Since the volcano’s status was lowered to Alert Level 2 on 5 August 2026, SO2 gas emission has averaged only 4,196 tonnes/day," they added.

The abrupt rise has been accompanied by a slight increase in the occurrence of low frequency volcanic earthquakes or LFVQs that are attributed to volcanic degassing.

The agency said elevated volcanic SO2 emissions may be a warning of a "potential shift to renewed eruptive activity at the summit crater that could generate volcanic hazards all around the volcano, the impacts of which can be worsened by the prevailing poor weather conditions."

Possibility of eruptive unrest

Mayon Volcano remains at Alert Level 2 (moderate level of unrest) but that there are currently new possibilities of eruptive unrest recurring, said PHIVOLCS.

"A new phase of eruption could generate life-threatening volcanic hazards that include pyroclastic density currents (PDCs) or uson, rockfall, ballistic fragments that can be thrown out as projectiles from the crater and extensive ashfall that could severely impact communities downwind of the volcano," the agency also said.

The public is reminded that it is prohibited to enter the 6-kilometer radius Permanent Danger Zone (PDZ) and communities and local government units within the pre-determined zones of PDC hazards must prepare and be ready to respond in case activity escalates and the alert level is stepped up to Alert Level 3. — BAP, GMA News
