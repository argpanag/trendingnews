It has been more than 10 days since the release of Nitesh Tiwari’s mythological epic, Ramayana: Part 1. While the film is sparking conversations about its monumental scale and portrayal of specific instances from the epic, discussions are also focusing on its budget and box-office performance.

According to industry tracker Sacnilk, Ramayana: Part 1 crossed ₹1000 crore (gross) at the global box office. It became the 11th Indian film to do so, after Tiwari’s Dangal, Dhurandhar: The Revenge, Baahubali 2, Pushpa 2: The Rule, and Dhurandhar, which are among the top five highest grossing films of all time.

As of today (November 16, 2026), Ramayana: Part 1 has raked in ₹1,384.50 crore (worldwide gross) in 12 days, with its India net collection being ₹769.55 crore, as per Sacnilk.

With Ranbir Kapoor, Sai Pallavi, and Yash in the lead, Ramayana: Part 1 details events from the epic, showing Ravana’s (Yash) rise as the powerful king of Asuras. It also introduces Rama as a sensitive warrior who marries Sita (Sai Pallavi) and gets exiled to the forest for fourteen years. The film’s climactic sequence, involving a fight between the demigod Jatayu (voiced by Anupam Kher) and Ravana, has been praised for its craft and imagination.

Ramayana: Part 1 is reportedly made on a budget of ₹2000 crore. The film’s whopping budget has been a point of discussion since its first look came out last year, and producer Namit Malhotra claimed in an interview that the two-part epic costs ₹4,000 crore. When the film’s trailer came out, it was met with criticisms particularly for its VFX and many viewers questioned claims about the mammoth budget. After the release, however, all apprehensions regarding the VFX have got cleared.

Yash, whose banner Monster Mind Creations has co-produced the mythological epic, opened up on the budget during a pre-release promotional event in Bengaluru on October 15, 2026. He supported Namit’s statement and said, “In India, there is a problem. People don’t like it when you talk confidently. That’s what happened with Namit’s statement. But in the West, it’s the total opposite. If you tell them humbly that we have made a good movie, they will dismiss it as a small project. That’s exactly why Namit has confidently said that this movie is mounted on ₹4000 crore. We need to prove the strength of the Indian film industry to the global market,” Yash explained.

The film’s box-office performance will give some respite to Yash after his last outing as an actor-producer, Toxic proved to be a major failure at the box-office.

Meanwhile, filmmaker Ram Gopal Varma took to X on November 15 to praise the film, calling it the “biggest hit ever in cinematic history.” He delved into the debate around the movie’s budget. He wrote, “I got to know that the actual cost of Part 1 is a little less than 800 and that includes mutually beneficial deals, global tax benefits, subsidy elements, which will eventually bring the cost to less than that. And not only me, but any reasonably experienced filmmaker, by seeing the film, can see the cost can’t be more than that.”

It remains to be seen how the film progresses at the box office as anticipation builds for the second part, which will cover the rest of the story, bringing in Hanuman, played by Sunny Deol, and a host of other characters, as Rama takes the next steps to rescue Sita from Ravana’s Lanka.
