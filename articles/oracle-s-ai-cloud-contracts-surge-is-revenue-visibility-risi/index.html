Oracle ORCL is strengthening revenue visibility as surging AI cloud contracts expand its long-term revenue pipeline. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, the company booked more than $30 billion of additional AI cloud contracts, lifting remaining performance obligations (RPO) to a record $664 billion, up $209 billion year over year. Management expects around half of its RPO to convert into sales over the next 36 months, creating a substantial multi-year revenue pipeline.

The growing backlog is already translating into stronger results. Cloud Infrastructure revenues jumped 121% year over year to $7.4 billion in the fiscal first quarter. Oracle delivered 850 megawatts of additional data-center capacity and had delivered more than 300,000 GPUs since the end of the fiscal fourth quarter. AI infrastructure utilization remained high at 97.9%, and GPUs up for renewal were renewed or resold at an average 20% premium. Most of the new RPO was generated through prepayment or bring-your-own-hardware arrangements, helping reduce Oracle's incremental funding requirements.

However, Oracle's AI opportunity remains capital intensive. Capital expenditures surged to $28.5 billion in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, while free cash flow was negative $5 billion. The company expects full-year CapEx of approximately $90-$95 billion as it expands data-center capacity to meet demand.

Oracle's expanding AI cloud contracts, record RPO and accelerating Cloud Infrastructure revenues are strengthening revenue visibility. The upgraded fiscal 2027 guidance for total revenues of at least $90 billion, representing 34% year-over-year growth, further supports this outlook, although high capital requirements remain a key consideration for investors.

How Microsoft & Amazon Challenge Oracle's AI Cloud Pipeline

Microsoft MSFT is extending its AI-cloud reach as Azure demand exceeds capacity. In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, Microsoft reported $678 billion in commercial RPO, up 84%, with 25% growth excluding OpenAI, while Azure growth reached 43%. That contracted pipeline, alongside AI infrastructure expansion, positions Microsoft to challenge Oracle for long-duration AI workloads and revenue visibility.

Meanwhile, Amazon AMZN is scaling AWS around AI demand. In the second quarter of 2026, the company reported a $496 billion AWS backlog, up triple digits year over year, while AWS revenues increased 36.7%. Amazon also said its AI revenue run rate exceeded $25 billion, with most AI capacity contracted for at least five years. These commitments strengthen Amazon's long-term contracted revenue pipeline and intensify competition with Oracle in AI cloud infrastructure.
