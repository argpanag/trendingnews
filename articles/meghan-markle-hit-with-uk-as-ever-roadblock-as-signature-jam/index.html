Meghan Markle is reportedly preparing to introduce As Ever to British shoppers for the first time, but one of the most recognizable products from the lifestyle brand will be missing from the initial lineup. The Duchess of Sussex is looking to expand the business beyond the United States, with teas, scented candles, and other lifestyle accessories expected to reach the UK market. However, food import and export regulations have reportedly blocked the planned launch, keeping the signature fruit spreads and edible flower sprinkles off the list of products available to British customers. The development comes as Meghan continues building As Ever following its American debut, with the reported expansion marking another significant step for the growing brand.

Meghan Markle Plans First UK Launch For As Ever

MEGA

According to The Sun, Meghan is preparing to bring As Ever to Britain after initially making the lifestyle products available only to American customers. The reported expansion would mark the first international market for the brand, which officially launched its first collection in April 2025. British shoppers are expected to have access to a selection of As Ever products, including tins of tea, scented candles, and limited-edition bookmarks. The tea is reportedly priced at approximately £11 ($15) per tin, while the hand-poured scented candles will cost around £50 ($66). Meanwhile, the bookmarks, featuring phrases written in Meghan's handwriting, are expected to retail for about £15 ($20).

One industry source told the publication, "Any consumer business would want to grow into as many markets as possible."

However, the company has not publicly confirmed a launch date for the British collection.

While the UK expansion could introduce As Ever to a new audience, some of the brand's best-known products reportedly won't make the initial cut. According to the report, Meghan's fruit spreads and edible flower sprinkles will not be available when the British range launches because of food import and export regulations. The exclusion is notable because the fruit spreads helped establish the brand's identity long before its official retail debut.

Meghan initially generated publicity for the venture by sending jars of strawberry jam to friends and celebrities, several of whom shared the gifts on social media. The early promotional campaign helped build interest in the lifestyle business, originally introduced as American Riviera Orchard. As Ever has since expanded to include strawberry spread, raspberry spread, and orange marmalade, along with honey, baking mixes, and other products. However, British customers will reportedly have to wait longer to purchase the fruit spreads locally. The report does not specify which particular import requirements are preventing their inclusion or when those restrictions might be resolved.

Meghan Markle Previously Faced Trademark Objections In Britain

MEGA

The reported import restrictions aren't the only regulatory challenge As Ever has encountered in the UK. In June 2025, the UK Intellectual Property Office issued a partial provisional refusal concerning parts of the company's trademark application. According to The Sun, officials found the proposed list of goods and services too broad and lacking "precision and clarity." However, the company subsequently secured protection for many of the listed products in December.

The earlier trademark objection is separate from the food import rules now reportedly affecting the British launch. The latest setback therefore does not mean that As Ever has been prohibited from operating in the UK. Instead, the reported restrictions concern which products can be included in the initial collection.

Meghan Markle Faces A 'Very Different Test' In British Market

Spread Pictures / MEGA

Even without the signature spreads, the move into Britain could present new challenges for Meghan. In September, PR and brand communications consultant Sam Gauchier suggested that As Ever would face a different competitive environment in the UK.

"I also think the UK is a very different test," Gauchier told Express US, in remarks reported by the International Business Times.

The consultant argued that British consumers already know the lifestyle products Meghan sells, including tea, jam, and other home goods.

"In the UK, it's like… Tuesday," he remarked.

Gauchier suggested that Meghan's celebrity status might attract initial interest, but long-term success would depend on whether customers returned to purchase the products again. He also noted that established British brands already offer similar products across different price ranges. The comments were made before the latest reports about the planned product lineup and were not a direct response to the jam restrictions.

Meghan Markle Continues Expanding As Ever Despite Obstacles

¡dehoy! Agency / MEGA

The reported British launch comes during a busy period for Meghan's lifestyle brand. As Ever recently introduced a holiday collection featuring chocolates, gift baskets, and seasonal accessories. The festive range also drew online criticism, with some royal watchers accusing Meghan of copying King Charles' holiday offerings. Meanwhile, Meghan announced a charitable partnership with Malala Yousafzai to support girls' education through proceeds from selected As Ever products. The collaboration sparked accusations that Meghan was using the Nobel Peace Prize winner to promote her business.

Despite the controversy, As Ever continues to introduce new products and explore opportunities beyond the American market. For British customers, however, the reported expansion includes one notable omission: the fruit spreads that put the brand in the spotlight. It remains unclear whether those products will eventually join the UK lineup.
