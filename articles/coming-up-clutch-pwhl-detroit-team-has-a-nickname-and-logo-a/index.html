Coming up Clutch: PWHL Detroit team has a nickname and logo at last

TheTools · 11.11.2026 10:30 · source · permalink

At last, the Detroit PWHL team has a nickname: The Clutch.

The announcement was made Tuesday, Nov. 10, after several months of anticipation from fans and team personnel. When Hilary Knight was in Detroit on Aug. 26, she couldn't wait to find out, saying it's "one of the most exciting things is when you finally get the name, you get the logo."

The Clutch open the 2026-27 season on Sunday, Dec. 6, against the Las Vegas Vanguard (another expansion team which announced its name Tuesday) on the road at T-Mobile Arena, and have their first home game at Little Caesars Arena, against the New York Sirens on Dec. 12 (noon, TV20). They'll take the ice for the first time at the end of this month, with scrimmages against the Ottawa Charge and the Hamilton Havoc (also announced Tuesday) set for Nov. 23-24 in Ancaster, Ontario, near Hamilton.

Although the team now has a name and logo, its inaugural-season sweaters will feature "DETROIT" diagonally down the crest, as announced in early October.

Detroit, Hamilton and Las Vegas weren't the only PWHL expansion teams to get official names and logos on Tuesday: San Jose was also christened, as the Sea Lions.

Along with the Clutch name, Detroit released its first logo, referencing a wheel and automotive clutch −a nod to the Motor City.

“I was blown away. The double meaning of the logo and brand is cool and going to be perfect for our team,” forward Daryl Watts said in a statement. “I already love the jersey; the metallic silver pops off and catches your eye. Overall, the brand represents the area really well and speaks to Motor City. I hope fans really like it.”

The name of the team has been a hot topic since the arrival of the franchise was announced during a gala press conference on May 6 at Little Caesars Arena, which is where the squad will play its home games.

At that time, Amy Scheer, PWHL executive vice president of business operations, explained everything that goes into selecting a nickname.

"It's equally fun and excruciating at the same time," she said. "When we go to a city, we want to have a nod to the history of the city. We want it to feel like the name belongs here. A lot of thought goes into it. We make sure we learn the history of the city, and not just the history in terms of facts, but also capturing sort of the mood of the city, the soul of the city, the passion of the city.

"So a lot of thought and consideration goes into it. Then when you think about the intellectual property has to work across both countries [U.S. and Canada], with different IP laws. It's a very thorough, in-depth and fun process. We throw a lot of names out that are horrible and we throw a lot of names out that we laugh at. Then you find the few that really work and hope they pass the IP process."

Now, the team has its name and logo. All that's left to do is hit the ice.

“We were inspired to create an identity that naturally represented both Detroit’s deep-rooted automotive legacy and hockey culture together, and that spoke to the strength, grit, and maker mentality of Detroit as a city and community,” said Amy Scheer, PWHL executive vice president of business operations, in a statement. “It was fan determination that brought a PWHL team to Detroit, showing up season after season for women’s pro hockey, and we worked to capture that spirit with the Detroit Clutch, an identity that has been a long time coming for Hockeytown.”
