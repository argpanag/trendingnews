    Bayern Munich v Arsenal (20:00 BST)

    Tom McCoy
    BBC Sport journalist

    The champions of Germany and England meet in an eagerly anticipated fixture which, but for Bayern's Munich semi-final defeat by Paris St-Germain, would have been last season's final.

    The hosts' expansive, high-risk approach contrasts with Arsenal's more measured and disciplined style.

    The Bavarians, unbeaten at home in the group stage or league phase of European competition for 13 years, have scored 23 goals in their four home games this season.

    However, they have kept just three clean sheets in 10 matches this term and conceded three times in last week's draw with Viking, a result boss Vincent Kompany blamed on individual mistakes.

    Bayern will need to be almost error-free against an Arsenal side with a similarly outstanding record at this stage.

    The Gunners have won 14 consecutive Champions League games in the opening phase of the competition, including a 3-1 victory when the sides met at the Emirates last November.

    Harry Kane drew a blank that night, but the former Tottenham star has an excellent record against his old north London rivals and is looking to score in a 10th consecutive Champions League game.

  14. Why Eze is struggling to find his form at Arsenalpublished at 19:49 BST
    19:49 BST

    Bayern Munich v Arsenal (20:00 BST)

    Alex Howell
    BBC Sport Arsenal reporter

    The sound of fans being wowed by his football is something that has followed Eberechi Eze wherever he has played.

    The Arsenal forward is known for his off-the-cuff skill, maverick nature and entertaining style, but lately, those familiar elements of Eze have been missing.

    Sunday's 1-0 win over Nottingham Forest was Eze's first Premier League start of the season. But replaced at half-time, having completed just nine passes and 22 touches of the ball, many have started to question whether he can reproduce the performances he was once known for.

    There's an argument that Eze does not get a run in one position or a run of games to build momentum, but the stats show he gets a substantial amount of game time.

    The Gunners played 63 matches last season and Eze started 36 of those (57%). Since his debut for Arsenal in August 2025 he has started in 56% of games (40 out of 72).

    His 11 goals in 61 appearances have been scored across just eight games.

    And he has delivered some huge moments in an Arsenal shirt, scoring five against rivals Tottenham Hotspur across two Premier League matches, with a hat-trick in the home fixture - while he also scored winners against Newcastle and former club Crystal Palace as the Gunners won the title.

    Aston Villa v Viking (20:00 BST)

    Tom McCoy
    BBC Sport journalist

    After an understandably slow start following a major summer overhaul, Aston Villa are beginning to find form.

    Sunday's 1-0 victory at Newcastle United was their fourth win in five games, a run that includes back-to-back Champions League successes.

    Bodo/Glimt have shown in recent years that Norwegian sides cannot be underestimated, and Viking, who pipped them to the Eliteserien title last year, are flourishing under joint head coaches Bjarte Lunde Aarsheim and Morten Jensen.

  17. 'I've waited 34 years for this' - Viking's 'special' storypublished at 19:46 BST
    19:46 BST

    Aston Villa v Viking (20:00 BST)

    When Viking FK ended a 34-year Norwegian title drought last year, it was days like these in the Champions League they were dreaming of.

    A visit to Aston Villa on Wednesday comes on the back of a remarkable 3-3 draw with Bayern Munich last time out, where Viking led 2-0 at half-time but then needed a last-minute goal to rescue a point.

    After that game, Viking captain Zlatko Tripic - who joined the club in the second tier in 2018 - told Norwegian channel TV2: "I could have played another 90 minutes. I'm so incredibly proud. I've waited 34 years for this."

    Tripic is one part of the Stavanger club's revival story - along with a rarity in modern football, joint head coaches.

    Former assistants Morten Jensen and Bjarte Lunde Aarsheim both stepped up together in 2021 and led them to last season's title - while they are top again this season.

    The pair spoke to the BBC's Euro Leagues show last month before their Champions League debut.

    Aarsheim, who spent the bulk of his playing career at Viking, said: "It's a special story. We were born Viking fans, we've been playing in Viking.

    "We were working together as assistant coaches and the dream job in managing Viking came up.

    "We said let's do it together. It will be better and the chances to get the job will be better."

    In a recent interview with the Guardian,, external he said: "For us, Viking is Barcelona. The players have to understand that we see Viking as more than a club. In our heads, it's the best team in the world."
