Heavy snow causes chaos and disruption across Norway

TheTools · 12.11.2026 11:28 · source · permalink

Snow has arrived across large parts of Norway, bringing a bit of chaos to the cities: from public transport disruptions to damaged cars on the roads and many people unable to reach their destinations.

On Tuesday, meteorologists warned that winter snow was on its way to Norway, with up to 15 centimetres expected in Eastern Norway and up to 30 centimetres in the north.

The snowfall was initially forecast across Oslo, Akershus, Østfold, Vestfold, and lower parts of Buskerud. However, on Thursday morning, the warning was extended to cover several additional areas, with a yellow danger alert now in place for Innlandet and Hamar.

A separate snow warning was also issued on Wednesday for parts of Western Norway.

The usual chaos

As is often the case, the first heavy snowfall of the season has caused problems across the country. Public transport services have been cancelled, and there have been many road accidents, including several vehicles sliding off slippery roads.

"It's always surprising how little snow it takes to cause problems. There's always chaos with the first snowfall," said meteorologist Hanne Beate Skattør at the Norwegian Meteorological Institute as reported by newspaper Aftenposten.

"We are receiving a number of reports of skids, traffic accidents and slippery driving conditions in connection with the recent snowfall," operations manager Per Magne Eftang, from the South-East Police District, wrote in the police log just before 8am, according to another article from the daily.

"The snowfall causes changed driving conditions, which in turn change road grip and braking distances. Plan to spend a little longer than usual getting to work and appointments. Drive according to the conditions and keep a good distance from the vehicle in front," he added.

If there was any lingering doubt, you should already have winter tyres fitted if you are driving.

Some of the most notable incidents include dozens of buses cancelled in Vestfold and a school bus sliding off the road in Larvik. "The bus has skidded off the road and into the ditch with all four wheels. There are 24 children on the bus who appear unharmed,"   the police log said, according to public broadcaster NRK.

The capital

With the first snowflakes falling in Oslo at 5.30am, public transport delays and traffic disruptions also hit the capital.

According to newspaper VG, over 1,000 bus departures were delayed and 65 percent of bus routes in the area were cancelled.

Oslo police also received several reports of fallen trees damaging parked cars. On the E6 motorway at Skulleruddumpa, two lorries became stuck. "One is facing out of Oslo and the other is facing into Oslo. This is causing some traffic problems at the scene," police reported.

The snowy weather also affected Oslo Airport, causing several delays, although staff from airport operator Avinor said they were working hard to clear the runways. 

"There has been a lot of snow at Oslo Airport, so it is in full winter operation. We are ploughing continuously. It is quite normal under such conditions that there will also be some delays in air traffic. All the planes also have to go through de-icing before they can take off," communications adviser Helena W. Jensen at Avinor told NRK.
