24 dead after boat carrying mourners to funeral capsizes in Nigeria

At least 24 have died after a boat carrying mourners to a funeral capsized in southern Nigeria.

Up to 300 people are believed to have been on the vessel when it sank on Friday in Bayelsa State in the country's South South region.

The boat was travelling between communities for a burial service at the time, and search and rescue operations remained underway on Saturday, according to Manzo Ezeki, spokesman for Nigeria's National Emergency Management Agency. He did not clarify how many passengers are still missing.

Police spokesman Musa Mohammed said on Friday that 24 people were rescued and required medical care. The boat, travelling between the villages of Ayama and Ukubie, appeared to have suffered a mechanical failure and capsized at about 1000 GMT, Mohammed said.

Passengers reportedly asked the operator to direct the vessel toward land but he allegedly refused, according to Mohammed. The operator then fled the area and the manager of the boat was taken into custody.

Earlier reports had indicated that two adults and two children died in the accident, while 19 survivors were treated at a local clinic and five others were transferred to a hospital in Yenagoa, the state capital.

Marine police, local divers and other emergency responders continue to search for additional victims.

Sophie Igoniwari, a witness, described chaotic boarding and overcrowding before the boat left Ayama. Igoniwari said that several victims were brought ashore after the boat capsized shortly after departure.

Boat accidents are common on Nigeria's inland waterways, especially in riverine communities where overcrowding is widespread and safety regulations are often ignored. Faulty boats without life jackets sometimes operate in the country due to a lack of enforcement of safety requirements.

The Bayelsa state government responded to the tragedy by urging boat operators and passengers to adhere to safety regulations.

“Overloading, speeding, recklessness and traveling without life jackets must stop. No journey is worth a life,” Bayelsa Commissioner for Information Ebiuwou Koku-Obiyai said in a statement on Saturday.
