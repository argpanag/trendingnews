US bans Chinese cars on overseas military bases over spying fears

TheTools · 27.10.2026 14:10 · source · permalink

Security guards ordered to turn away BYD vehicles because of intelligence-gathering capabilities

The US is to ban Chinese-made “connected cars” from entering a military installation in Japan owing to security concerns.

Security guards on the gates of Kadena air base, in Okinawa, are under orders to refuse access to all cars manufactured by BYD Auto of China from Monday, South Korea’s JoongAng Daily quoted US military officials as saying.

It added that the ban would probably be extended to other US installations in Japan as well as those in South Korea.

The ban covers privately owned vehicles, including those of base personnel, contractors’ cars, taxis, delivery vehicles and commercial vehicles.

In June, the US defence department designated 188 Chinese companies as “Chinese military enterprises operating directly or indirectly in the United States” and placed them on a watch list. BYD is on that list, along with Alibaba and Baidu.

The US is concerned that BYD electric cars employ software-centric vehicle technology that can detect and map its surroundings, gathering data through as many as 10 high-performance on-board cameras and dozens of sensors.

A vehicle that is equipped with real-time data transmission capabilities and remote-control functions could share that intelligence with the company and the Chinese government.

Questions were previously raised by the opposition People Power Party (PPP) in Seoul about the number of “connected cars” being granted access to South Korean military facilities.

At least 190 Chinese vehicles with intelligence-gathering capabilities have entered South Korean bases, including the ministry of defence compound in Seoul and the headquarters of the joint chiefs of staff, but also air force bases housing F-35 fighters and the hub facility for the navy’s submarine command.

The ministry has yet to introduce upgraded security measures but announced in September that steps were being drawn up to enhance protection from illicit intelligence-gathering.

Kang Dae-sik, a spokesman for the PPP, accused the government and the ministry of defence of taking too long to counter the threat.

“Our government’s awareness and response are severely inadequate, given the risk of real-time leaks of military facility information to China,” he said.

Kadena is the US air force’s largest overseas base and a critical component in the defence of Japan. It is an important part of Washington’s strategy in the Asia-Pacific region, including the containment of China, North Korea and Russia.

The installation has a complement of around 20,000 personnel and serves combat jets, air reconnaissance units and mid-air refuelling aircraft, as well as helicopters and tilt-rotor Osprey transport aircraft. The US military recently positioned Typhon launch systems for Tomahawk cruise missiles at the facility.

BYD has been approached for comment.
