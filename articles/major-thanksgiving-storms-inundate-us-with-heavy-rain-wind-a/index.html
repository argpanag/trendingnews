Major Thanksgiving storms inundate US with heavy rain, wind and flooding

TheTools · 26.11.2026 21:25 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Weather

Topic:Weather

In short:

Thanksgiving storms have brought flooding, power outages and heavy snow across the United States.

Nearly 200,000 homes and businesses had no power in Virginia and another 177,000 in Maryland.

What's next?

The storm is expected to hamper air travel at eastern US airports from Thanksgiving Day into Friday morning, local time.

Thanksgiving storms have forced the postponement of Macy's 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York and brought flooding, power outages and heavy snow to other parts of the United States.

The strengthening storm along the eastern seaboard raised worries about how the annual parade's massive balloons would fare in the windswept canyons between Manhattan's skyscrapers.

Macy's said in a statement that it was postponing the event from Thanksgiving Day (Thursday, local time) until Saturday in an abundance of caution after consulting with city officials.

Workers rushed to deflate the parade's balloons following the rescheduled New York event. (AP: Yuki Iwamura)

Nearly 200,000 homes and businesses had no power in Virginia and another 177,000 in Maryland, according to poweroutage.us.

The storm was expected to hamper air travel at eastern US airports on Thanksgiving Day into Friday morning, with reduced visibility, greater turbulence, strong winds and aircraft icing, the National Weather Service said.

A person holds onto their upturned umbrella outside Radio City Music Hall as winds rattle New York. (Reuters: Adam Gray)

Heavy rain and high wind hit both coasts

The weather service posted wind advisories from northern Georgia to most of the mid-Atlantic to southern New England.

All of New Jersey was under a state of emergency, as were parts of New York.

Across the east coast, wind gusts of up to 112 km/h toppled trees and power lines.

The storm was flooding coastal villages and cities from North Carolina through New Jersey as the high tide rolled in on Thursday morning.

Avalon, New Jersey, saw some of the worst flooding in its history, said Scott J Wahl, the business administrator for the Borough of Avalon.

"Not quite Hurricane Sandy," Mr Wahl said, for his village on the far south of New Jersey's Atlantic coast.

Two men kayak down a street in Norfolk, Virginia. (AP: Stephen M Katz/The Virginian-Pilot)

Lance Miller, 54, said floods in his neighbourhood of Ocean City, a small coastal town in New Jersey, were about a foot deep and that driving to the nearest grocery store would probably "destroy your car".

"I wouldn't do that, I'm a car guy," Mr Miller said.

"You're gonna have a good foot of water that you'd have to drive through."

Mr Miller, who had his Thanksgiving Day meal preparations set, said he would be staying indoors and having dinner with his wife and daughter.

But he said that some of his friends in the area were "not so lucky" and had flooding inside their homes.

"So they're dealing with clean-up, you know," he said.

More than 600,000 homes and businesses were without electricity on Thursday afternoon, with the majority of those outages in East Coast states, according to poweroutage.us.

Highways were also shut down.

The nearly 29-kilometre Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel over the Chesapeake Bay in Virginia was closed for several hours on Thursday morning because of high winds.

NC 12, the only highway connecting Hatteras Island on the North Carolina Outer Banks to the mainland, was closed after seawater breached the dunes again and covered the two-lane highway.

Crews did not know when it might reopen.

In Norfolk, Virginia, tornado warnings blared on phones around the metro area as families prepared to celebrate Thanksgiving.

Winds topping 80 km/h were reported all around the metropolitan area of more than 1.8 million people.

Central states see heavy snow while California faces coastal floods

Winter storm warnings and watches were posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois, with up to 33 centimetres of snow expected around St Louis by Thursday afternoon.

Snow fell as far south as North Alabama, where giant flakes dropped from the sky on Thursday morning.

King tides have caused coastal flooding in low-lying communities along portions of the east coast and California on the west. (Reuters: Mike Blake)

King tides continued surging on the West Coast, with coastal flood warnings issued from Southern California to the San Francisco area to the Oregon and Washington coasts.

A video showed a massive wave tearing down a portion of the pier in the city of Pacifica, California, on Wednesday and slamming it into the ocean.
