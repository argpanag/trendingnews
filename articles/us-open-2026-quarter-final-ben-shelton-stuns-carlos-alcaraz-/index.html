That’s all for tonight. Er, this morning. Thanks for following along with us and be sure to read Tumaini Carayol’s full report off a match for the record books.

Shelton also spoke warmly about the communication with his father and coach, Bryan, during a match that stretched deep into the New York night. “For us, it was just enjoyable to be a part of that match,” he says. “Obviously, the level of tennis, how long the match was going, the fight that it was, how even the match was back and forth throughout. I think that’s something that we can appreciate. Win or lose, have a smile on our face.”

And after nearly four and a half hours on court, is he worried about recovering in time for Friday’s semi-final with Frances Tiafoe? Shelton’s answer at 3.46am on Wednesday morning was to the point. “Two days? Yeah.”

Shelton admits he was “pissed” after losing that 70-minute opening set, particularly after breaking Alcaraz to force the tiebreak. But that late break also gave him belief. “I knew that my serve was only going to get better, and if I’d already dialed in the return to the point that I could break him, I thought my chances were good to get that second set.”

That proved prescient. Shelton broke Alcaraz three times in a runaway second set, and from there felt able to “play and swing more freely”. The key, he says, was staying patient on return: making Alcaraz play, punishing missed spots and not getting discouraged by the inevitable aces and service winners. “I think for me to be able to hang in there in the rallies, have a game plan from the ground and be able to execute that game plan, and not be losing all the baseline points, also helps with the return.”

Shelton: 'That was a war'

How does Ben Shelton feel after Tuesday night’s/Wednesday morning’s quarter-final win? “I’m tired,” the American said to a gaggle of reporters in the bowels of Arthur Ashe Stadium, in lieu of a formal press conference given the late hour.

“That was a war, epic match. I mean, so physical. Carlos, one of the greatest champions in our sport already at 23 years old. It’s always fun being on the court against him. It’s ridiculous, the amount of stuff he comes up with sometimes, but the most enjoyable match that I’ve had in my career, for sure.”

What an ending. After 4hr 28min, Shelton finally gets over the line in a fifth-set tiebreak to reach the US Open semi-finals, surviving Alcaraz’s furious fourth-set comeback and a deciding set in which neither man could find the break.

The serve that carried Shelton for so much of the night held firm when it mattered most. He won 70% of his first-serve points and 57% behind his second serve, with 54 of his 156 serves going unreturned. Remarkably, after Alcaraz broke him three times in that fourth-set rout, Shelton did not surrender serve in the decider.

And the contrast in errors tells plenty of the story. Alcaraz finished with 38 winners to Shelton’s 34, but committed 53 unforced errors to the American’s 35. Shelton also dominated the short exchanges, winning 55% of rallies lasting four shots or fewer, while Alcaraz controlled the longer ones. In the end, at nearly four and a half hours and deep into the New York night, the margins could scarcely have been finer.

Shelton wins 6-7 (5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6 (7)!

Alcaraz does his part and this historic quarter-final will be decided by a fifth-set tiebreaker. First man to 10 points will face Frances Tiafoe in a US Open semi-final on Friday.

Shelton races to 30-love and “Let’s go, Ben!” chants ring through the lower bowl. Shelton gets it to 40-love, igniting the chants again (and louder), before spraying a backhand past the baseline. But the American is able to hold from there, leaving it to Alcaraz to force a deciding first-to-10-points tiebreaker.

Alcaraz holds at love for 5-all, a game that includes a punishing 19-shot exchange that sees Shelton finally blink first. It’s 3.11am. What is life.

An emphatic servicde game for Shelton, who holds at love in barely a minute as the match spills into its fifth hour. Alcaraz will serve to stay in the tournament after the change of ends.

Fifth set: *Shelton 6-7, 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 4-4 Alcaraz (*denotes next server)

A couple of unforced errors have surfaced in Alcaraz’s game at the worst time as he goes down 15-30 on his serve. The crowd swells. And now Shelton has just painted the line with a scorching cross-court backhand winner for 15-40 and two break-point chances! This could be the match! He squanders the first after coming to net early in the rally and hitting a backhand long. And Alcaraz saves the second with another brilliant display, coming from nowhere to hit a forehand volley winner than Shelton can’t return into the court. Deuce, and could that be Shelton’s best chance at closing it out wasted?

Not so fast. Alcaraz has just double-faulted and Shelton will have a third break chance. But he can’t convert that one either, sending a running backhand into the net. Now Shelton sends a forehand long and Alcaraz is one point from the crucial hold. (They’re all crucial now.) And he wins it with a 122mph serve out wide that Shelton can’t handle. We’re on serve in the fifth!

Fifth set: Shelton 6-7, 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 4-3 Alcaraz* (*denotes next server)

Shelton in trouble here. He’s quickly gone down love-30 on his serve and Alcaraz tastes blood. But the American wins two fighting points for 30-all. Shelton then hits an unreturnable serve for game point, but follows with an unforced error off the forehand for deuce. From there Shelton nails down the hold, capping it with a 144mph ace to stay on serve in the decider.

This will be the latest finish in US Open history

Fifth set: *Shelton 6-7, 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 3-3 Alcaraz (*denotes next server)

Alcaraz closes out a drama-free hold with a 113mph ace down the middle.

And at the stroke of 2.51am it became official: this match will be the latest finish in the history of the US Open. As mentioned earlier, the previous record was 2.50am, set by Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner in their 2022 quarter-final. Alcaraz won 6-3, 6-7 (7), 6-7 (0), 7-5, 6-3 after 5hr 15min.

Shelton then hits a forehand volley winner, igniting his side of the crowd, but Alcaraz answeres with a backhand tracers off the ground for 30-all. Another blistering forehand winner by Alcaraz give him a break point at 30-40, but Shelton comes to net early in the rally to erase it for deuce. More “U-S-A! U-S-A!” chants. Two quick points for Alcaraz and he’s escaped with the hold.

Alcaraz holds comfortably, closing it out with a 124mph ace. It’s 2.34am, the lower bowel of Ashe is nearly full, there’s plenty of folks still upstairs and, yes, they’re still selling Honey Deuces (though it seems most stands have run out of the souvenir cups).

Now it’s Shelton who is pressured on his serve, making back-to-back unforced errors from 15-all to go double break point down. He saves the first with another wild exchange of volleys at the net, but blinks on the second with a backhand error to end an eight-shot rally. “Olé, olé, olé” chants ring down from the loge seats in Ashe as the crowd tries to will Alcaraz on.

For anybody wondering, the latest finish in US Open history is 2.50am, set by Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner in their 2022 quarter-final. Alcaraz won 6-3, 6-7 (7), 6-7 (0), 7-5, 6-3 after 5hr 15min.

Here’s a glimpse of the US Open grounds at 1.40am, with Shelton and Alcaraz still going inside Arthur Ashe Stadium. Fountain Plaza, normally one of the busiest thoroughfares at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, is almost completely deserted, the concessions shuttered and only a handful of people still milling around.

Third set: Shelton 6-7, 6-1, 6-3 Alcaraz

At 15-all, Shelton pounds a 124mph serve before coming to net and finishing with a muscular forehand winner. He’s two points from the set. Alcaraz answers with a sharply angled backhand winner from the baseline for 30-all. Then, another truly extraordinary point ends after a trade of apparent volley winners, when Alcaraz hits a blistering forehand winner moving into the backcourt for break point at 30-40. A massive point, this. And Shelton gets the better of Alcaraz over a 19-shot rally to save it, bringing the crowd to its feet once again! Deuce. Shelton then outlasts Alcaraz in an 11-shot exchange for set point and the Spaniard looks worse for wear, having been run ragged in these extended rallies. And Shelton forces Alcaraz into another forced error to take the third set in 48 minutes!

Unlike the second-set blowout, this was a much sterner test. The American protected his serve superbly, saving all three break points he faced in the set and losing only seven points on serve altogether. Through three sets he has saved six of seven break points and held in 13 of 14 service games.

The serve remains terrifying – still topping out at 149mph – but Shelton has been winning this match with more than raw velocity. He’s getting 66% of his first serves in and has won 68% of those points, while an extraordinary 39% of his second serves have gone unreturned.
