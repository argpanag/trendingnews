Synopsis

A band of deeper moisture and resultant clouds and showers stretching from the Big Island eastward will continue sagging south tonight. Drier and more stable conditions are expected as the trades ease through midweek. A cold front will move through the state Wednesday night into early Thanksgiving Day, bringing gusty northerly winds and increased chances for showers. Trades will return by next weekend.

Discussion

A band of moisture associated with a WSW to ENE oriented mid level trough that brought showers to all the islands earlier this morning has sagged south to the Big Island this afternoon. Considerable cloudiness and frequent showers are occurring on that island's windward areas, with a few lingering on Maui as well. Farther north, drier air has ended the shower activity with only some fair weather cu in windward and mountain areas.
A subtropical ridge is building in from the northwest and will be centered overhead by Monday night and Tuesday. This will allow winds to become light and variable Tuesday night through Wednesday. A drier than normal airmass and inversion heights between 4,000-5,000 feet will also lead to very limited shower activity.
The ridge will break down Wednesday as an amplifying trough digs southeast toward Hawaii. This sharp trough will swing through Wednesday night and spark cyclogenesis just northeast of the islands early Thursday morning. A surface cold front moving in from the northwest will sweep into Kauai by Wednesday evening, passing through Oahu and Maui County overnight, then finally reaching the Big Island by the morning of Thanksgiving Day. A short period of deeper moisture behind the front will lead to fairly widespread showers Wednesday night and early Thursday, with heaviest totals on northward facing slopes. Rainfall amounts will gradually fade along the frontal cloud band as it weakens passing through each island, meaning Kauai and Oahu may see higher rainfall amounts than Maui or the Big Island. As the strong mid level jet stream swings through it will also lead to a period of high winds on the summits of the Big Island and Maui.
High pressure will build back in to the north of the islands next weekend and should persist into the following week. The typical trade pattern will return, although with dry air lingering, it appears the dry season may be upon us.

Aviation

Moderate to breezy trade winds are expected through the evening before slightly weakening by tomorrow. Showers are expected to primarily impact windward Big Island, but are possible across all windward and mountain areas. Expect MVFR conditions within showers and VFR elsewhere.
AIRMET Sierra is in effect for mountain obscurations over windward areas of Maui and the Big Island. Expect conditions to gradually improve for Maui through tonight, while windward Big Island will likely continue seeing low clouds and showers until tomorrow.
AIRMET Tango is in effect low level moderate turbulence downwind of island terrain. This will likely stay in effect through today, but conditions should start to improve by tomorrow as winds weaken to moderate speeds.

Marine

Trade winds will gradually drop off through Tuesday as weakening surface ridge north of the islands moves southward. A Small Craft Advisory (SCA) remains in effect over the typically windy waters surrounding Big Island and Maui, but it should be dropped late tonight as trades decline. The ridge will be pushed over the islands late Tuesday, disrupting trade winds as a front approaches. The front will reach Kauai Wednesday night, then bring strong north winds as it sweeps down the rest of the island chain Thanksgiving Day. A SCA will be needed due to a combination of the strong trades and rough elevated seas that will be dominated by an extra large north-northwest swell. Winds will shift out of the northeast and decline Friday and Saturday.
A small north-northwest will decline tonight and Monday, followed by much larger swells this week. The nearshore PacIOOS Waimea Bay buoy continues to register the north-northwest swell near 4 feet 12 seconds this afternoon. Data from NOAA buoy 51001 indicate that this swell will gradually decline tonight, and the swell should fade late Monday. A large northwest swell will push surf along north- and west- facing shores well above High Surf Advisory thresholds Tuesday into Wednesday. An extra large northwest swell is expected on Thanksgiving Day. While surf will well exceed High Surf Warning thresholds, northerly winds brought by the front will produce very rough conditions. This swell will shift more northerly and decline on Friday and Saturday.
East shore surf will fall below seasonal average tonight and will be very small Monday through Wednesday as trades diminish and eventually become disrupted. Fresh to strong northerly winds and wrapping north swell will likely affect eastern shores Thursday and Friday. A small, long-period south swell will fade tonight, with another small bump possible late in the week.

Fire weather

Light and variable winds will develop from Tuesday into Wednesday as high pressure builds over the state. A cold front will swiftly move from northwest to southeast down the island chain from late Wednesday through Thursday morning. Breezy and cool northerly winds will mark the frontal passage for each island and drying trends will follow for the foreseeable future. Subsidence inversion temperature heights will range from 5,000 to 6,000 feet near Maui and the Big Island for the rest of the afternoon.
