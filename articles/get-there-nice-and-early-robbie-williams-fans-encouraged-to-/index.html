'Get there nice and early': Robbie Williams fans encouraged to plan ahead for weeknight gig

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:48 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Auckland is once again ready to be entertained by British pop legend Robbie Williams.

The Angels hitmaker touches down at Eden Park tomorrow night for one of his biggest shows since his 2003 Western Springs spectacle.

But gig-goers are being reminded it is also a weeknight, so they should allow plenty of time to sort out their plans.

Last here in 2023 for two massive concerts at Mission Estate Winery in Napier, the Herald’s Doug Laing labelled the performance “a phenomenon to mirror Napier’s biggest event”, and praised the singer’s showmanship and crowd interaction.

Judging by recent Australian shows, punters can expect Williams’ hits like Better Man and Rock DJ, as well as impressive staging and vocal chops.

Tickets are still available from the official ticketing partner, AXS, for as low as $79 plus booking fees.

If you’re heading to the show tomorrow, here’s everything you need to know – from how to get there to what to expect if you’re taking public transport.

When does the show start?

Gates open at Eden Park at 5pm on Tuesday.

Opening acts, Kiwi band Drax Project and UK indie-pop outfit The Lottery Winners are expected to take the stage from 6.30pm.

An official start time for Williams has not been announced, but judging by his Australian shows, the singer has been getting to the stage about 8.30pm and performing for about two hours.

How to get there (and back) on a weeknight

Trains, buses and special event buses are included in your ticket from 3pm.

As with previous concerts, special North Shore event buses will run from 4pm directly to Eden Park every 10 minutes, stopping at Albany, Constellation, Sunnynook, Smales Farm and Akoranga stations.

Auckland Council transport and infrastructure delivery deputy chair councillor Shane Henderson said fans should feel they can ditch the car and make the most of bus and train services.

“We have more bus and train services heading to and from Kingsland than ever before, so it’s never been easier to forget the car and hop onboard,” he said.

“Simply show your ticket to the driver when boarding and remember to keep your event ticket for the journey home.”

Henderson urged people to get to the concert “nice and early” to avoid coinciding with evening peak commuters.

“If you’re coming from the North Shore, a special event bus service will run along the Northern Busway every 10 minutes from 4pm-7pm before the show, and for one hour afterwards.”

After the concert, these buses will depart from the corner of Sandringham Rd and New North Rd and return to the North Shore.

“Staff will be on hand to help direct people, and fans should listen for announcements and follow directions for a smooth exit.”

Mobility parking on Cricket Ave is accessible via Walters Rd for ticket-holders with mobility parking permits.

For those travelling by train, get off at Kingsland Station for a short walk to Eden Park.

Following the show, trains will use both platforms at Kingsland Station to depart towards the city. To head west, use both platforms at Morningside Station.

If you are getting dropped off or bringing your own car, rideshare services and drop-off areas are located at the Dominion Rd end of Bellwood Ave and Morningside Drive.

Roads around Eden Park will be closed during the event, and parking restrictions will be in place, so expect delays.

What’s the weather forecast?

MetService meteorologist Lewis Ferris said while there’s not much bad weather expected, a few afternoon showers may be possible. But it’s unlikely they will impact the festivities.

“The showers are more likely to affect the western coast, so central Auckland and Eden Park may escape them,” he said.

“It’s still worth bringing a jacket, especially for the journey there or earlier in the day.”

Winds should remain light, with a high of about 22C.

What to bring, and what not to bring

Small bags are allowed, but only if they are A4 size or less.

Signs, banners or flags of this size are also acceptable, as long as they don’t have poles or political messaging on them.

Umbrellas are not allowed, but people are encouraged to bring ponchos or rain jackets in case of inclement weather.

Eden Park’s website has a seating plan where you can check whether your seat is covered and plan accordingly.

Empty one-litre water bottles can be brought in, but lids will be removed before entering the stadium floor.

Feeling peckish? You can also bring in non-commercial food you’ve prepared yourself, but there will be plenty of food and drink options available for cashless purchase at the venue.

On the not accepted list are portable chairs or seats, alcohol purchased outside the stadium, drugs, aerosol cans, breakable containers, knives, fireworks and chilly bins.

If you’re planning to bring young children to the concert or experience sound sensitivity yourself, the Kennards Hire Sound Sensitivity Stations at Eden Park will be giving out free earplugs on the night – one portable station, and one in the South Stand on Level 3 next to the Access Community Health Sensory Room.

Children under 14 must be supervised by an adult at all times.
