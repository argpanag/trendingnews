Every word from Arteta's pre-Brighton presser

TheTools · 19.09.2026 17:08 · source · permalink

Mikel Arteta has spoken to the press ahead of our final match before the extended international break against Brighton & Hove Albion.

We're looking to continue our perfect start to the campaign, and the boss has been speaking about our winning start, taking on the Seagulls and much more.

Read every word below, with a video of the full press conference to follow.

on how Ben White is:

Ben is all good, we have another training session this afternoon, so very positive about everybody. 

on if he’s positive about Timber, Mosquera and Hincapie:

Yes, we have another session; they are all willing to be part of it. We have another session to try to make sure they are fit. 

on if he’s now able to influence referees by calling out mistakes:

No, I think I’m here to try to give my opinion, to defend my club, to give the very best to our players. In order to do so, obviously you guys can ask some questions, and we need to respond in the most direct way, I think, and fair way. I will make mistakes sometimes but I will try to be as fair as possible. 

on wanting officials to get decisions right:

We discuss many topics in this room and they’re important, all of them. We have a space to talk, to give our opinions, to have discussions, to have personal conversations. It’s everybody’s duty to try to improve the game and that’s what we’re here for. 

on if we’ve heard from Pro Ref:

These are conversations that will remain private, as usual, and they have to be kept that way. 

on if he’d like an apology regarding the penalty at Sunderland:

It’s not about the apology; it’s just agreeing whether the decision should have been different or not. Maybe [Pro Ref] have a different opinion. Let's try to improve and move forward together. 

on Fabian Hurzeler providing an apology after our last game at Brighton:

That’s all done, that’s all in the past. I have huge respect for Fabian, what he’s done and what Brighton have done as a club, it’s just remarkable. Tomorrow is going to be a beautiful game to watch, I’m sure. 

on the potential to go eight wins in a row in all competitions:

We've got a really tough opponent tomorrow, so we're going to have to really earn the right to win the game, but certainly that will be the mindset of the players and the team. 

on if he’s going to sign a new contract:

I think we are all very much aligned; whenever the time is right, we'll get that done. There is nothing to be concerned about. 

on signing a contract two years ago during the international break:

I don't know if the timing will be the same or different, I think we both have the same ambition and the same will, so whenever we have time we'll get that resolved.

on if he’s happy with the new longer break:

Let's see, it's something that we'll have to experience. As you've seen, different national teams, different managers have taken different approaches in terms of the numbers that they have put in the squad, so it's going to be really important to have good communication with them, so they understand where our players are, what we expect, what they expect as well, and work together. That's always the best way.

on the mentality of the team when they consistently have to impose themselves away from home:

That's one of the challenges of the schedule, and the team has responded so well. Playing with dominance, it doesn't really matter if we play at home or away, we want to be the same team and we want to cause the same kind of problems to the opposition. We have managed to win the three games, and we've got the last one against a really tough opponent, as I said, against Brighton tomorrow, and we'll try to do the same.

on if the onus is on our leadership group to support younger players:

You cannot coach that. I think you have it or you don't, because it's a split-second, a very emotional reaction when you are in the middle of the game, and I absolutely loved it, because we've all been there, and just encouraging the confidence that it gives to a 16-year-old to go again and deliver in the next action, it's huge. I think he has mentioned it already, and it's good to see that bond, that connection within the team to try to help each other.

on if Guimaraes is predominantly a left or right midfielder:

He can play on the left, on the right, in the middle, he can rotate from there. He's such a dynamic, versatile player that he can deliver great moments in any position, and it will be more down to what we need on the day, the relationship we have with the other midfielders, and for sure what the opponent does as well.

on how Raya has come from the National League to become one of the best goalkeepers in the world:

That's a great story, I think for any kid as well, to look at trajectories and pathways, and how you can reach the highest level in football, and always maintaining that will, that motivation, and that enthusiasm that it can happen. Football now, especially internationally, is so connected that you never know, you might be playing a game, somebody scouts you, and you can end up in two or three years' time playing at the highest level. David, I think it's his mentality. Obviously he has tremendous quality, but he has overcome a lot of difficulties, a lot of challenges, to reach this point. When he reached this point, it wasn't easy in the beginning; he really had to dig in and prove himself, and with all the coaches, with Inaki [Cana] especially, doing amazing work with him daily, and all his teammates, he has earned the right to be one of the best now. 

on if he’s been Mikel’s best signing:

I don’t know - he’s been a tremendous signing, there’s no question about that. Whether he’s been the best or not, it’s just an opinion, but it doesn’t matter, we are so happy to have him. He’s helped take the team and the club to a different level, and now we need to maintain that level. 

on if this type of international break could be better for momentum:

Let’s see how it pans out. I think every national team is different - some play in Europe and others travel the world for two and a half weeks, so we have to wait and see how it goes. 

on if this team could be another Invincibles:

We just have enough on the plate today to prepare for the game against a really good Brighton tomorrow. We know how tough that’s going to be to earn the right to win it, that’s it. 

on if Gyokeres is struggling for confidence:

No, obviously, especially in the Premier League he hasn’t played that many minutes, but last week in Napoli he played and had a good game. That’s about moments as well, we need to give him all the support and confidence that he needs because we know the player we have, the capacity he has to score goals and what he can give to the team. 

on if the team are still adapting to him or if he’s adapting to them:

I think the team are doing certain different things this season as well, so it’s a bit of both. But that’s not just for Viktor; it’s for everybody - it’s the constant evolution of the player and team. Our job, and especially my job, is to try to fit that in and find those connections and level of confidence for the player to give his best for the team.

on if a change in environment is good for him after his past exploits with Sweden:

He scored a few goals [last time]. That’s what a striker needs, they need minutes and goals to boost their energy. We have a game tomorrow, and hopefully he can deliver then.

on investing in young players from around the world:

It’s us as a club, we know we want to constantly maintain this level and have the pathway already in-house - which is obviously the best way to bring players from the academy or youth teams to us. We need to be one of the best in the world at recruiting in the country and abroad to maintain that level.

on when young players are tempted by moves away to other big clubs:

It is challenging because there are a lot of parties there and the way the system is at the top, it’s not easy. There are certain moments in those years when there are gaps where those big decisions need to be made, and they have the choice to make that decision as well. Something is not only what we want to do, it’s what the player, agent or family wants to do at times as well. That’s not easy, but we’ll for sure try to keep the best talent in-house.

on if he’s been surprised by how well we’ve started the season:

We know how complicated it is to win a football match at this level, so to win seven, it tells the story. After the World Cup especially, there were a lot of unknowns about how the players and the team were going to react. We didn't have any time with them to work together, but as I said, they won. I've seen their attitude, and what I was seeing, I was really impressed with. Then you have to put it down to performances and the element of luck as well to win football matches, and we are here and then we want to make another one for sure tomorrow.

on if Dowman is asking to start more matches:

That's the best way to knock on the door - to do it on the pitch, not in the office. I think with that you're just left out of arguments when a player delivers constantly on the pitch every single day in training. That's the best way, that's the best signal for the manager, that's the best signal for his teammates as well that they will demand that player play and what Max did the other day amongst all the players as well that haven't played that many minutes, was really impressive.

on what Eze brings to the left wing:

He's always played there in his career; that's where all his biggest moments in his career and the highlights have come, and I think it's a place that he feels very, very, very comfortable. He can occupy different spaces through them but that's the space that generally he has created big moments for previous clubs as well to win matches.

on how to decide if Eze or Tzolis should start:

In form, in the opponent, in the relationship that we have within that unit, there are a lot of factors.

on the impression Konsa has made so far and his link-up with Gabriel:

I heard some great things about Ezri before he joined, and when you have him daily, you just realise the magnitude of the player that we signed - not only the player but the person, the way he presents himself, the way he's able to connect with people immediately. We talk about Big Gabi, when you see those two in the corridors talking, the way they interact with each other, it seems like they've been here for a long, long, long time together. That is great because that's a partnership that, in my opinion, is extremely important on the field, and then his attributes, the quality that he delivers to the team are something that really makes us better.

on how Dowman is developing as a person by being around the first team:

In a really great way as well. When you see how those players behave daily, you know the level of attention they put into any detail, the way they look after themselves, the standard that they set. If you become part of that environment, that's only going to raise your level. It's going to educate you in a way that becomes normal and then you will hit another level because then you will be able to add to that. That's where Max is at the moment.
