School uniform policy reform aims to cut parents' costs

TheTools · 23.11.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

Getty

A consultation process on school uniform reforms aimed at cutting costs for families has been announced by the Minister for Education and Youth.

Tomorrow, Hildegarde Naughton will ask the Cabinet to approve the consultation, which will begin in January.

She has called it the most significant review of school uniform policy in years, focused on affordability, comfort and sustainability while ensuring that families, students, schools, patrons and suppliers all have an opportunity to help shape the final approach.

The proposed reforms are aimed at reducing costs for families, improving comfort and practicality for students, and promoting more sustainable approaches to school uniforms, while continuing to recognise the role that school uniforms play in school identity and culture.

The minister also intends to seek feedback directly from children and young people as part of the consultation process.

The Department of Education and Youth has already commenced engagement with key business and retail representative bodies, including Ibec, the Small Firms Association and Retail Ireland, to ensure the views of suppliers and manufacturers are fully considered as part of the consultation process.

The proposals have been informed by feedback from parents, students, the Inspectorate and wider evidence regarding back-to-school costs.

Measures under consideration include limiting the number of crested or school-specific items across school uniforms and PE uniforms; requiring crests to be sew-on or iron-on rather than embroidered onto garments; preventing schools from requiring specific branded jackets and coats; and ensuring uniform items are widely available from multiple retailers, including supermarkets and high street stores.

They will also include the provision of greater flexibility regarding footwear requirements, and supporting clothing banks, uniform swap shops and other sustainable initiatives.

The department is also considering the reduction of unnecessary changes to school uniforms that increase costs for families, as well as how to ensure uniforms are comfortable, practical and suitable for year-round use.
