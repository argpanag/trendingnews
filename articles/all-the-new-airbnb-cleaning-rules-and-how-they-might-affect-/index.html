All the new Airbnb cleaning rules – and how they might affect your next holiday

Airbnb’s new rules on which chores hosts can reasonably ask their guests to do, such as washing dishes or stripping bedsheets, are now in force.

The short-term letting platform has updated its policy on which house rules and requests hosts can enforce when a guest comes to stay.

These new rules have now taken effect, applying to all reservations booked on or after Friday, 20 November.

The new policies stirred controversy when they were announced in October, as hosts and guests clashed on social media.

A number of hosts argued that running the dishwasher and taking out the rubbish are standard, easy tasks, while guests said that they were paying customers, not houseguests, and should not have to complete chores.

Some people felt so strongly about the new rules that a Change.org petition was created in retaliation, calling for hosts to continue to be allowed to ask guests to wash up as “self-catering is not a hotel service”.

The petition has received over 31,000 signatures as of 20 November, as the rules came into force.

In response to the backlash, Airbnb said that its goal was to “make sure guests know what to expect before they book” and said it could “have done a better job explaining why and how we’re making these changes”.

It has now updated its guidance on its hosting page to make it clearer what hosts can and cannot enforce.

What are the new policies?

Airbnb said it has made rules easier to find on the listing page, so guests can evaluate whether the home is a good fit before booking.

It has done this by adding a new “House rules” section to listings that makes it clearer for guests to see and for hosts to fill in. They will also appear in the guest’s Trips tab and in reservation reminders.

Under this tab, guests will see a list of “House Rules” and “Additional Rules”. In “House Rules”, hosts can choose from seven standard house rules on a drop-down list to fill in when uploading their Airbnb listing.

These house rules cover whether guests must lock up at check-out, the maximum number of guests, quiet hours when noise should be kept to a minimum, and whether pets, events, photography, or smoking are allowed.

“House rules help guests understand what hosts expect, but we’ve heard from our community that they can be confusing, onerous and hard to manage,” an Airbnb spokesperson said.

“So we’ve redesigned them to make them easier to discover and clearer to set, alongside a new policy that keeps requests reasonable, with no surprises.”

Airbnb says hosts can also write out any added conditions to the stay under the “Additional Rules” section on their listings, but they must be “reasonable” and meet certain platform requirements.

The few examples Airbnb gave are:

  • Washing up: Hosts ask guests to put washing up in the sink, but can’t ask them to wash up kitchen items and put them away.
  • Laundry: Guests can be asked to gather used towels, but they shouldn’t be asked to remove sheets from the bed or start a load of laundry.
  • Rubbish: Hosts can require guests to separate rubbish and recycling into designated indoor bins, but they shouldn’t be asked to take rubbish off-site.
  • Utilities: Guests can be required to turn off air conditioning when they leave, but they shouldn’t be asked to avoid using a listed amenity.

Certain policy exceptions will apply based on the Airbnb location, such as allowing hosts to reasonably ask guests to limit shower time due to drought-related water restrictions or any local laws.

Guests are expected to follow house and additional rules and hosts can report guests to the platform if there is an issue. Airbnb said it will impose consequences on guests for repeated or severe violations.

Likewise, guests can also report hosts for enforcing rules and requests that do not comply with the new policies.

Airbnb said it will be reviewing all listings from 20 November to make sure hosts have complied with its new policies, and will let them know by email or the app if any requests they are making of guests violate them.
