Synopsis

A strong high pressure system northeast of the state has sent an old frontal boundary/trough southwestward to just north of the islands. Scattered to numerous showers are occurring on windward Kauai down to Maui. Showers will increase in coverage as this feature continues to sink south tonight before washing out late Tuesday. Trade wind showers are likely for much of the week for windward and mountain areas due to persistent high pressure to the north and waves of deeper moisture passing through.

Discussion

Scattered to numerous showers have advanced into windward and mountain areas of Kauai, Oahu, Molokai, and Maui this afternoon. The low level trough/old frontal boundary is clearly evident on satellite imagery in the form of a narrow band of agitated cloud cover. This band will continue to settle south tonight into Tuesday, spreading increased shower chances down the island chain. Drier and more stable air has been in place across the Big Island today with only some low topped fair weather cu in the windward areas. Seabreeze convergence on the leeward side has produced more cloud cover, but shower activity has been kept to a minimum. As the trough arrives tonight, shower activity in Big Island windward areas will increase significantly with light to moderate rainfall amounts through Tuesday.
A mid level trough well west of the state today will progress east Tuesday and begin arriving Tuesday night while it weakens. This feature is expected to largely dissipate by Wednesday and shoudn't have too much of an impact on our sensible weather. Trade showers will continue though in the typical areas.
Reinforcing surface high pressure will arrive north of the state late week. An uptick in higher elevation wind appears likely Thursday night into Friday, which may eventually prompt Wind Advisories. Otherwise, daily shower coverage will be dependent on pockets of deeper moisture passing through in the trade flow.

Aviation

Breezy to locally strong trades this afternoon should ease to moderate to locally breezy speeds by Tuesday. Showers will be on the increase this evening as an old frontal boundary moves through the state. Frequent clouds and showers should be expected tonight especially over windward and mountain areas. MVFR ceilings and visibilities are expected at area terminals such as PHTO and PHLI tonight through Tuesday. Weather conditions should generally improve during the day Tuesday, but showery weather will likely still produce periods of MVFR conditions throughout the day.
AIRMET Tango remains in effect below 7000 feet for low level moderate turbulence over and downwind of island mountains. AIRMET Sierra is expected to return this evening as showers increase across windward and mountain areas.

Marine

Fresh to strong trade winds will gradually ease through Tuesday as robust high pressure far northeast of Hawaii slowly weakens. A Small Craft Advisory (SCA) remains in effect for all Hawaiian waters due to a combination of the elevated trade winds and large combined seas. Some leeward zones will drop out of the SCA late tonight or early Tuesday as winds and seas gradually lower, with the SCA scaling back to the eastern half of the state and then to the typically windy waters around Big Island by Tuesday night or Wednesday. Moderate to locally fresh trade winds will prevail Wednesday, then strengthen again Thursday and Friday as a stronger high passes north of the state. Slight weakening of the trades is then possible late in the weekend into early next week as a forecast low pressure system passes by far to the north and weakens the high.
A moderate, medium period north swell that peaked this morning will continue generating advisory level surf for north facing shores of Big Island and Maui through tonight and below advisory level surf for the rest of the islands north facing shores. This swell will gradually fade tonight through Wednesday, with harbor surge conditions gradually improving tomorrow. A Marine Weather Statement continues to highlight harbor surges at Kahului and Hilo due to the direction (350 to 360 degrees) of the swell. Small background northwest and north swells will keep small to tiny surf in place Wednesday through Friday, then a small north- northwest swell will be possible during the weekend.
Rough surf along east-facing shores will remain above seasonal average today due to elevated trade wind swell and some wrapping north swell. East shore surf is expected to be around seasonal average Tuesday through Thursday, followed by a slight increase Friday through the weekend. Mainly small background level surf will persist along south-facing shores during the next several days, with a small long-period swell possible on Friday.

Fire weather

Lighter wind speeds and higher humidity levels along with increasing shower coverage will limit any critical fire weather threats through Thursday. Wind speeds increase Friday mainly over higher elevation areas where humidity levels will be high. Temperature inversion heights near Maui and the Big Island range from 6,500 to 7,500 feet elevation this afternoon.
