Where will TCS Stock be in the Next 5 Years?

Investors who bought Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) stock about a year ago are not happy.

In fact, it's not only TCS; almost the entire IT space has given negative returns in the last year. For example, TCS is down a whopping 33% in the last year, while the Nifty is down slightly above 8%.

In this editorial, we look at where the TCS stock could potentially be in the next five years.

Please note, the environment for IT companies is extremely dynamic, and no one can predict the future with certainty.

The Q1 revenue stood at Rs 722.75 bn, growing 2.2% sequentially and 13.9% YoY. This was the fourth consecutive quarter of growth. BFSI, Technology, software, and services, regional markets, and products and platforms led growth.

The operating margins stood at 24%, down 130 bps sequentially, primarily due to wage hikes.

In Q1FY27, TCS saw strong order book closure and large deal momentum continued.

The company delivered a TCV of US$ 9.5 bn, including net new AI-led business transformation deals such as, US$ 800 m mega deal with SKF; 6th mega deal win in the last 5 quarters multi-million dollar strategic partnership agreement with ServiceNow, and multi-million dollar deal with Europe-based Fortune Global 50 firm.

At the end of Q1 FY27, A1 saw US$ 2.6 bn in annualized revenue, which is up 13.6% QoQ. While it is small in the context of the company's overall revenues, the company is seeing good growth.

TCS continues to advance its Infrastructure to Intelligence strategy through unique partnerships with frontier AI companies and new launches.

The company announced a global premier partnership with Anthropic, which gives it early access to Claude family of models, 50,000 licenses, a joint GTM campaign, co-creation of industry solutions, and TCS iON as a training and certification partner.

The company also became the first GSI partner for Mistral AI. Together, they will build sovereign and custom AI models for enterprises and establish a dedicated COE for developing industry solutions across BFSI, healthcare, manufacturing, and public sector.

TCS also launched TCS SovereignSecure CloudTM for Europe, directly addressing rising demand from governments and regulated enterprises for compliance, sovereign, and AI-ready cloud infrastructure. This strengthens TCS's leadership presence in Europe as clients prioritize data residency, operational control, and geopolitical risk mitigation.

The company also launched a dedicated Global Value and Innovation Center business unit to help enterprises build, operate, transform, or divest global capability centers.

Here are some key risks investors should watch.

TCS remains exposed to slower discretionary technology spending, longer client decision cycles and macroeconomic uncertainty. Its annual report specifically flags geopolitical and economic volatility as risks to demand.

While AI is making life easier for most, not so for IT companies. In fact, generative AI and automation can allow clients to achieve more with fewer human hours. This could reduce traditional application-development and maintenance volumes and put pressure on billing rates for companies like TCS.

North America accounted for about 48.3% of TCS revenue in Q1 FY27, making US technology spending, regulation and economic conditions particularly important.

Wars, changing trade policies, immigration restrictions, and geopolitical tensions can affect client budgets, delivery costs and cross-border operations. Top of Form

Over the last year, TCS's valuation has come crashing down, in line with most peers in the sector. Data from Screener shows TCS trading at a PE of 14.4 times, versus the median 5-year PE of 29.7 times. The stock's return ratios are healthy, at 51.8% ROE and 63% ROCE.

For a company like TCS, the challenge is shifting its capabilities quickly enough to keep pace with advancing AI trends.

While the company itself is investing heavily in AI and digital platforms, there is no certainty that these investments will translate into proportional revenue growth or margin expansion.

The biggest worry for IT companies is that new technologies could reduce the need for large-scale outsourcing or shift value toward product-based or platform-driven models, where traditional IT services firms may face structural disadvantages.

Technologies evolve so quickly and disruptively. So, no one can predict where an IT stock could be in the next 5 years. It's easier to predict for a brick-and-mortar company, but doing so for an IT company is risky.
