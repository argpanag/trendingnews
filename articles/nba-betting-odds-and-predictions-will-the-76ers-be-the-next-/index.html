💪 The strongest teams according to the NBA betting odds

With those teams in mind, let’s look at the top five and their key players, big offseason moves, strengths, weaknesses, and more.

⚡ Oklahoma City Thunder

The Oklahoma City Thunder enter this season off a 64-18 record, finishing first in the West but losing 4-3 in the Western Conference Finals. 

The Thunder made few roster moves, but they drafted Aday Mara and Bennett Stirtz in the first round, re-signed Isaiah Hartenstein, and saw a few players, most notably Lu Dortz, leave via a trade to the Atlanta Hawks. 

With that, there’s a lot of status quo with the Thunder. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is a perennial MVP candidate, and if Jalen Williams can stay healthy, that’ll only make them better. 

In terms of weaknesses, it’s really hard to pinpoint anything; this is a deep team. If SGA gets hurt, that could lower their ceiling, but overall, this will be a strong team, and it all starts on opening night against the team that ended their season last year: the Spurs. 

🏀 Back the Thunder to win the NBA Championship at +260 with BetMGM 🏀

🌵 San Antonio Spurs

The Spurs had a fantastic 2025-26, reaching the NBA Finals, but they fell to the Knicks 4-1. They enter this season with their top NBA Draft pick, Jayden Quintance (No. 20 overall), and also brought in Tobias Harris on a two-year deal. 

Their biggest move was giving superstar Victor Wembanyama a five-year, $252 million deal. 

Alongside Wembanyama is a strong collection of guards, like Dylan Harper, who will only get better and more dynamic. Stephon Castle will also look to take another step forward. Finally, De’Aaron Fox averaged 18.6 points per game last season. 

If I had to nitpick a weakness, it's 3-point shooting. They were 14th in 3-point shooting last season, at 35.9%, and all three guards mentioned shot below that percentage. 

Still, as long as they have Wembanyama, they’ll be fantastic. 

🏀 Back the Spurs to win the NBA Championship at +270 with DraftKings 🏀

🍖 Philadelphia 76ers

No team in the NBA made more big moves than the 76ers, signing James and trading for Brown. They also brought in Dean Wade and Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, and drafted Labaron Philon with the No. 22 overall pick. 

In acquiring Brown, they traded away Paul George; Andre Drummond signed with the Knicks; Kelly Oubre Jr. signed with the Pacers; and Quentin Grimes signed with the Los Angeles Lakers. 

Beyond James and Brown, the 76ers also have Joel Embiid, VJ Edgecombe, and Tyrese Maxey. With all of this star power and depth, the 76ers are a legitimate title contender. 

There are things to worry about, though, including Embiid’s injury history and James being 42. 

🏀 Back the 76ers to win the NBA Championship at +800 with Caesars 🏀

🗽 New York Knicks

The Knicks finished 53-29 and won the NBA Finals, taking down the Spurs in 5 games. Their top two signings were Drummond and Jordan Clarkson. They did see big man Mitchell Robinson sign with the Celtics. 

In bringing in Drummond, he could potentially be an upgrade from Robinson, but Robinson was unique with his ability to protect the rim. They also have Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, Karl-Anthony Towns, and Josh Hart. 

As for their weakness, it’s the realization that there’s been a different NBA champion in each of the last eight years. That said, the Knicks will still be a top team in the East. 

🏀 Back the Knicks to win the NBA Championship at +1000 with FanDuel 🏀

☘️ Boston Celtics

The Celtics are coming off a 56-26 record, finishing 2nd in the East, but losing in the first round to the 76ers, 4-3. 

They drafted Chris Cenac Jr. No. 27 overall and also signed Tobinson, Mike Conley Jr., Neemias Queta, and Jordan Walsh. 

They traded away Brown and got George in return, but also saw Nikola Vucevic sign with the Magic. 

From here, there’s no question that Jayson Tatum is the No. 1 option on this team, with George as strong support and Derrick White at point guard. Not only that, but Queta adds some excellent depth behind Robinson, and Payton Pritchard averaged 17 points per game last year. 

Robinson should be great, but he has a history of injuries. If Queta steps up, I do have some worries about the center position, but overall, the Celtics will look to replace Brown and see what that looks like.

🏇 Dark horses in the NBA betting odds

With the top five teams out of the way, here are three legitimate dark-horse contenders that could come out of nowhere, continuing our NBA betting preview.

🍁 Toronto Raptors

Anytime a team brings in Kawhi Leonard, they’re going to immediately enter the conversation of being a title contender. 

In addition to Leonard, they drafted Allen Graves at No. 19 overall, signed Kyle Anderson, but also saw Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick leave in the trade for Leonard, and Sandro Mamukelashvili signed with the Lakers. 

Leonard now joins Scottie Barnes, which could be a fantastic duo.

I would like to see them maybe make a deal mid-season if things are going well to upgrade the center position from Jakob Poeltl, though. He averaged 10.7 points, 7 rebounds, and fewer than 1 block per game last year.

In addition to Haliburton coming back, they’ll also have a full season of Ivica Zubac. He played just five games for the team last year. 

Of course, there are a lot of unknowns, but there’s legitimate star power coming back, and Zubac will be a great big man.

🪄 Orlando Magic

The Magic are a team that’s felt like they're on the cusp, but they’ve been bounced early from the playoffs for years. Last year, they went 45-37, finishing No. 8 in the East, but lost 4-3 in the first round to the Detroit Pistons. 

The team brought in Jonathan Isaac and Vucevic, and also has Jalen Suggs, Desmond Bane, Franz Wagner, Paolo Banchero, and Wendell Carter Jr. 

The Magic now have a new head coach, Sean Sweeney, who has worked with superstars in the past like Wembanyama, Luka Doncic, and Antetokounmpo. 

So, with a new head coach who’s used to getting the most out of stars in previous roles, the Magic’s core needs to improve on the perimeter. 

If they can improve there and Sweeney fills in nicely, the Magic could be a sleeper team to watch.

🙋 FAQs ahead of the 2026/27 NBA season

1. How quickly can a team’s betting odds change?

Betting odds for a team to win a game or even the NBA Finals can change in an instant. A star player could get hurt, a trade could happen, or a coach could be fired. Numerous factors can massively alter a team's odds on a per-game basis or in the NBA Finals. 

2. How many games are there in a season, and how many games might a team play in a typical week?

All NBA teams will play 82 games during the regular season. So, with all 30 teams, there are 1,230 total games. 

In terms of a given week, most teams will play around three games. That said, you’ll see some teams play between two and five games per week. Five games in a week are uncommon, but four can happen, especially with back-to-back games. 

3. How often has a rank outsider defied the odds and won the NBA Championship?

Since the 2002-03 season, there have been eight teams to win the NBA Finals with preseason odds of +1000 or longer: 

  • 2022-23: Denver Nuggets (+1800)
  • 2002-03: San Antonio Spurs (+1100)
  • 2003-04: Detroit Pistons (+1500)
  • 2007-08: Boston Celtics (+1000)
  • 2010-11: Dallas Mavericks (+2000)
  • 2013-14: San Antonio Spurs (+1200)
  • 2014-15: Golden State Warriors (+2800)
  • 2018-19: Toronto Raptors (+1850)
