Anthropic IPO live updates: AI lab tops $2 trillion market cap after record offering

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

This is CNBC live coverage of Anthropic's IPO and Nasdaq debut.

Anthropic opened at $111.50 per share in the artificial intelligence lab's Nasdaq debut on Thursday after the company completed the biggest initial public offering on record.

The stock will trade under the ticker "ANT" after the company raised $81.3 billion in the IPO. The Claude creator sold 980 million shares at $83 a piece on Wednesday, lifting the size and price of its offering.

The 5-year-old company captivated the corporate world with its Claude AI models and products, which have fueled its meteoric rise this year. Anthropic said in its prospectus that it generated $33.24 billion in revenue for the nine months ended Sept. 30, up a staggering 1,200% from the same period last year.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei co-founded the company in 2021 after he and a small group of researchers defected from OpenAI, now its chief rival. Anthropic has faced sharp criticism over its positions on safety, regulation and national security, including by President Donald Trump and other senior government officials. 

CNBC's reporters are covering Anthropic's IPO live on air and online from our bureaus in San Francisco and Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, and from the Nasdaq in New York City.

Thursday was Anthropic's day, and offered Wall Street a much-needed break from Nvidia news.

Nvidia, a major Anthropic investor and partner, has been roiled by scandal in recent weeks. Jensen Huang, who co-founded the chipmaker 33 years ago, resigned as CEO following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one relationship with an alleged Chinese spy. 

The chipmaker agreed to purchase roughly $10 billion worth of Anthropic's Class N common stock as part of a private placement that took place immediately following the completion of the offering. Nvidia previously agreed to invest up to $10 billion in the company in November of last year.

"We cannot wait any longer. We must pass my legislation to get some guardrails around this tech," said Warner, referring to legislation unveiled Thursday that would require disclosures from publicly traded companies on AI-related financing.

"Wall Street shouldn't be cashing in on a company that could turn out to be a ticking time bomb or a Trojan horse for hackers and foreign adversaries. Going public cements a broken business model and rewards its reckless pace," Jayapal wrote.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who is running to be the next Senate majority leader, said on Wednesday, "The last 24 hours and the next 24 hours show us how urgent it is to address the risks posed by AI."

That IPO "will be great for the billionaires, but we need to act urgently to address the threats to the rest of us," Van Hollen said in a social media video.

Anthropic has an unusual governance structure.

The company is a public benefit corporation, which means it's required to balance the financial interest of stockholders, the best interests of those affected by its conduct and its "company purpose." Anthropic said in its prospectus that its purpose is to facilitate the "responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity."

Anthropic's seven co-founders also wield a lot of power over the company. Prior to the completion of its offering, Anthropic issued a single share of Class F common stock to a limited liability company called "Founder LLC." Anthropic's co-founders are the sole members of Founder LLC, and the entity holds 50.1% of the voting power of the company's outstanding shares.

Each co-founder sold 3.38 million shares in the IPO, according to the prospectus, for total proceeds of $1.96 billion.

Additionally, Anthropic introduced an independent governance body called the "Long-Term Benefit Trust" in 2023, which has the authority to elect and remove the majority of the company's board of directors.

Amodei told JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon in a fireside chat on Friday that he thinks talent and culture are the "most important things" that drive a company's success, which is why he and his co-founders have tried to place so much emphasis on those areas at Anthropic. 

Anthropic requires every employee to pass a culture interview, which is taken very seriously, Amodei said. He added that he spends roughly half of his time focusing on talent and culture at the company. 

"We've been very disciplined about turning down people who are deeply technically talented, but who don't fit our culture," Amodei said. "We don't want people who are jerks. "It means that the leaders of the company can trust the employees," Amodei said. "The employees of the company can trust the leaders of the company to be candid. Everyone feels that we're being honest with them, and that has this enormous value."

Retail investors with access to the Anthropic IPO who try to sell the stock for a quick profit may face a penalty from their brokerage.

Anthropic made shares available to retail investors through brokerages including Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, E-Trade and some of their respective affiliates, the AI startup said in an IPO prospectus.

Many of them have "anti-flipping" policies meant to discourage investors who purchase IPO shares from dumping them fast.

These policies apply only to retail investors who place an order with their brokerage firm to buy the Anthropic IPO at its offering price — something many investors don't have access to.

For example, Fidelity clients who sell IPO shares within the first 15 calendar days from the start of trading in the secondary market would be barred from participating in future stock IPOs through the brokerage. Investors get a six-month ban after the first offense and could be permanently barred after the third offense.

Anthropic has had two major disputes with the Trump administration this year, but tensions have begun to thaw between the two camps in recent months. 

Amodei joined Trump for a private dinner at the White House in September, which marked the first one-on-one meeting between the two leaders. Days later, Amodei attended a luncheon at the White House, where he signed a voluntary AI safety accord.

Three months earlier, the company had to temporarily disable access to two of its most advanced models, Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, in order to comply with an export control directive from the government that cited "national security authorities." Anthropic restored the models after roughly two weeks of tense negotiations.

In March, Anthropic was banned by the Pentagon after discussions about how the military could use its AI models spiraled out of control. The company sued the Trump administration to try to reverse the blacklisting.

Retail investors who don't want to buy Anthropic stock directly will get access to the AI startup courtesy of many popular mutual funds and exchange-traded funds.

Index funds won't add Anthropic stock immediately. Some will add them within days or weeks, depending on the inclusion rules of specific indexes.

For example, the Morningstar U.S. Total Market Index will add the stock after five trading days. The Nasdaq-100 will add it after Dec. 18. The S&P 500 won't add it for at least a year.

Many actively managed funds held pre-IPO Anthropic shares — and investors in those funds automatically get a sliver of Anthropic's IPO when it starts trading. A handful of the most heavily concentrated funds held pre-IPO shares worth more than 10% of their overall portfolios.

Most investors' exposure to Anthropic in mutual funds and ETFs will likely be relatively low — well below 1% of the overall portfolio for most funds, experts said.
