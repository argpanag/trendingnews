A Day in Nottingham: It’s Great to Be Back | The Office of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex

Prince Harry and Meghan, The Duke and Duchess of Sussex, spent the day in Nottingham, reconnecting with the community they have long supported. The day marked a special homecoming for the Duke and Duchess, who first visited Nottingham together in December 2017.

The visit began at CRS in St Ann’s, a long-standing creative hub that gives young people a safe space to explore musical performance and production. The Duke was introduced to CRS during a private trip to St Ann’s in 2013 and has championed the organisation’s work ever since – helping build a thirteen-year partnership with Epic Partners and Latimer to help young people build positive alternatives to involvement in violence and crime.

The Duke was introduced to CRS during a private trip to St Ann’s in 2013 and has championed the organisation’s work ever since – helping build a thirteen-year partnership with Epic Partners and Latimer to help young people build positive alternatives to involvement in violence and crime. 

While at CRS, The Duke and Duchess observed a youth drama workshop led by Nottingham-born actor Andre Squire before diving deeper into the studio’s broader mission to help local youth facing disadvantage or isolation discover their voice, learn from caring mentors, gain practical skills, and build their confidence. 

The Duke also convened with core partners to discuss the Violence Impacting Young People (VIYP) initiative and plans for the redevelopment of CRS. VIYP brings together early intervention, trusted youth provision, and creative and sporting opportunities to reduce violence impacting young people in St Ann’s and Sneinton. Nearby, The Duchess joined young women in a bracelet-making and empowerment workshop focused on uplifting one another and creative expression.

The day continued over lunch at Himmah, a vibrant community space supporting local households experiencing food insecurity. The visit reunited two communities long-connected to The Duke and Duchess – Himmah and the Hubb Community Kitchen (HCK). 

Following the Grenfell Tower fire in 2017, The Duchess began working with women affected by the tragedy who gathered to prepare meals for their families, friends, and neighbours.  In 2018 The Duchess went on to support these women in creating the charitable cookbook Together: Our Community Cookbook featuring recipes contributed by the women and a foreword written by The Duchess. 

The cookbook first raised money to keep HCK running, and in 2021, The Duchess facilitated a donation of additional proceeds raised through the cookbook to Himmah, helping purchase kitchen equipment and covering costs for its foodbank. The Duke made a further donation several years later that financed more than 650 food parcels and helped purchase equipment for Himmah’s community center hub.

Four of the original HCK volunteers that The Duchess stayed in touch with over many years joined Himmah’s chefs in preparing the special lunch, bringing the community spirit of Together into the Himmah kitchen. It was a meaningful opportunity for The Duchess to have a special reunion with these women whose recipes helped make the cookbook possible, and celebrating their shared and lasting commitment to food and community support.

After lunch, The Duke and Duchess visited Himmah’s foodbank – the largest in the region – to meet the team, learn how its warehouse operates, and help pack food parcels for local households. Across both locations, the visit highlighted the importance of food and trusted community spaces in fostering a sense of connection.

The day concluded at Epic Partners, a local charity dedicated to supporting young people at risk of violence through inclusive sports, youth, and mentoring programs. The Duke and Duchess joined Harley, a young Epic community member who has developed a passion for baking, to decorate a cake for Co-Chief Operations Officer Michaela Castillo-Williams’ 30th birthday – a personal celebration within a community built on encouragement and care. Michaela’s own journey began through the Full Effect Leadership programme, progressing from volunteering to employment and now leadership within Epic. Her story offered another powerful example of the lasting possibilities created when young people receive support and the opportunity to grow.

The Duke and Duchess initially came across Epic during their 2017 visit to Nottingham Academy, where they met young people supported by the programme. The Duchess later returned privately to spend time with young women in Epic’s nurture group, many of whom she is still in touch with. The Duke has also since been involved in helping Epic develop its programme for young people affected by Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs), ensuring they have access to one-to-one mentoring, classroom assistance and behaviour support, and Music Mentors, a partnership bringing CRS musicians and mentors into Epic’s youth provision.

Throughout the day, the strength of Nottingham’s community organisations was evident: in the young people finding their confidence, volunteers supporting their neighbours and the circle of support, where those who were once helped return to help others. For The Duke and Duchess, returning to Nottingham was an opportunity to reconnect with a community close to their hearts and reflect on what comes next. Across the work here, a common thread emerged: opportunity grows through relationships built over time, the commitment of people who support one another, and places where everyone feels a sense of belonging.
