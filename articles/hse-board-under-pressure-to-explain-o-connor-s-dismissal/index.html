HSE board under pressure to explain O'Connor's dismissal

TheTools · 26.11.2026 03:44 · source · permalink

Pressure is mounting on the board of the HSE to elaborate on the events leading to Anne O'Connor’s dismissal as CEO after both the Taoiseach and Tánaiste called for more information to be provided.

Micheál Martin and Simon Harris yesterday moved to distance themselves from the board’s decision to sack Ms O’Connor as CEO after she confirmed she is to initiate court action against the HSE for the "unlawful termination" of her employment.

Ms O’Connor, who was in the role for less than nine months, may decide to start court proceedings against her former employer later today.

The HSE may also seek to bring its dispute with Ms O’Connor to mediation, where a private out-of-court settlement could be agreed.

Either scenario leaves the HSE open to significant costs, which would ultimately be borne by the Exchequer and increase political pressure on the Minister for Health Jennifer Carroll MacNeill.

Around lunchtime yesterday, the Taoiseach called on the HSE board to provide a "comprehensive account" of what happened, acknowledging that a further statement from Ms O’Connor significantly contradicted the HSE board’s version of events.

He also stopped short of expressing confidence in the HSE board, despite Minister Carroll MacNeill insisting that the board still maintained her support.

As of this morning, the board has yet to respond to the Taoiseach’s request.

Tánaiste Simon Harris also said it would be "helpful if the HSE board would provide an account, elaborating and expanding on the statement issued by them [on Tuesday]".

In the Dáil and in media appearances, the Coalition had previously adhered to the HSE board’s original statement in explaining Ms O’Connor’s dismissal, pointing to how the board’s decision was unanimous and came after an assessment of Ms O’Connor’s performance in the role.

Senior Government figures yesterday said the Chair of the HSE board Ciarán Devane is due to finish up in his role next year having served as chairperson since 2018.

The post has been advertised on the publicjobs.ie website.

Members of the Oireachtas Health Committee are hopeful that Mr Devane and the HSE board will appear for questioning next Wednesday, although details have yet to be confirmed.

The Fine Gael leader will likely comment on developments publicly for the first time this morning, as political strife stretches into a fourth day.

The Oireachtas Business Committee will also meet later today to finalise plans for the Minister for Health to address the Dáil on the unfolding controversy next Wednesday.

The Committee will hear a proposal for almost two and a half hours of statements next Wednesday afternoon from Minister Carroll MacNeill, as well as Opposition politicians.

Last night, Fine Gael said its parliamentary party had rallied around Minister Carroll MacNeill, with the Minister for Agriculture, Martin Heydon, saying the entire party was "fully united behind her".

Minister Heydon lauded her as a "reforming Minister" who "had done exceptional work to strengthen accountability and improve the delivery of health services in her role in the Department of Health".

However, opposition politicians claim explanations of her role and involvement in the events prior to Ms O’Connor’s dismissal lack credibility. Sinn Féin has derided them as a "cock-and-bull story".

The Oireachtas Health Committee yesterday heard evidence that the Chair of the HSE board contacted the Secretary General of the Department of Health in October, seeking input into Ms O’Connor’s performance reviews.

Opposition parties will likely argue today that this casts severe doubt on the Coalition’s contention that the HSE board’s decision to dismiss Ms O’Connor was completely independent of Government.

The Dáil’s powerful Public Accounts Committee will also contemplate its next move today and whether to hold a hearing into the controversy.
