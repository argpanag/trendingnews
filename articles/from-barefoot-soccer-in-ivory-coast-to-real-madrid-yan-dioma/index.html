From barefoot soccer in Ivory Coast to Real Madrid: Yan Diomande’s remarkable journey to stardom

TheTools · 31.10.2026 19:34 · source · permalink

Trip to the cradle of Yan Diomande. José Julián López played a key role at the AFI Academy, where Yan Diomande started out.

In the crowded Sicogi neighborhood of Yopougon, a district in northwestern Abidjan, survival is the top priority for most residents. Poverty and overcrowding define the modest community where Yan Diomande grew up. For him, the streets offered an escape. Hours spent playing soccer with friends seemed to fly by as they kicked makeshift balls fashioned from rags, old clothes, or worn-out socks across dirt lots, concrete surfaces, and bustling streets. Whenever someone in the group managed to get their hands on a plastic ball or an old leather one, it instantly became a treasured possession shared by everyone..

Even a basic pair of cleats—or soccer shoes with flat soles—was an unaffordable luxury for children growing up in this impoverished corner of Ivory Coast, where eating three meals a day remains an unrealistic dream for many families. Playing barefoot or in sandals was the norm.

But even as a little boy, Diomande stood out. Raised by his mother alongside his sister, Roxane, after his father moved to France in search of work, Yan displayed extraordinary abilities that set him apart from other children. He seemed born to play soccer. His dribbling was mesmerizing, and his shooting power was remarkable. That’s why his friends nicknamed him Roberto Carlos, after the legendary Brazilian left-back.

During one of countless pickup games in the neighborhood, Diomande caught the attention of Dez Bamba, a soccer-loving businessman and talent scout. Bamba immediately recognized something special and invited the youngster to visit AFI Sud Comoé Academy, a soccer development program he had founded and entrusted to Spanish coach José Julián López.

In 2014, the 10-year-old prospect arrived at the academy in Assouba, about 62 miles east of Abidjan. He joined a group of 40 promising youngsters, many of whom were attending school for the first time and finally had access to three, sometimes even four, meals a day.

“We quickly realized he was a very special kid with the ball,” López recalls. “He was a dribbler, fearless when taking on defenders, aggressive going forward, and always looking to attack. We immediately moved him up from the under-12 group to the under-14 team. Our first priority was teaching him patience and helping him understand the game. He could pick up the ball near his own goal, dribble past five or six opponents who were older than him, and keep going until he scored or someone finally took it away.

“He was always very intelligent and eager to learn, both in the classroom and on the field. I remember the way he looked at me when I explained different concepts. He loved listening and understanding why things worked the way they did.”

López, an expert in African soccer and Diomande’s first coach, now works as a coordinator at a soccer academy in Valdemoro, Spain.

The young prodigy’s development was remarkably fast, according to the coach who first recognized his potential.

“He was an exceptional talent and paid tremendous attention to everything. He quickly grasped the fundamentals and developed a much better understanding of the game. He had an enormous desire to improve, and that extended to the classroom, too. Our project was about providing the boys with a well-rounded education because 80% of the children who arrived couldn’t read or write. Yan excelled both academically and on the field. He always wanted to get better and put everything he had into it.”

His natural ability was undeniable, but the academy also helped him take his game to another level.

“Dribbling and shooting came naturally to him. We gave him his first pair of cleats, and he took off. His teammates could barely get the ball to reach the goal when they shot, but Yan was different. He even took the corner kicks because he had an incredible ability to strike the ball. Technically, he was magnificent. He could spend hours juggling without letting the ball touch the ground.

“We worked extensively with him on the tactical side, but his natural gift for soccer had been there since birth. His talent was extraordinary. He was a complete player, and he also had a great head on his shoulders. He was incredibly fortunate that someone like Bamba came into his life.”

For many children at AFI Academy, the facility was nothing short of paradise after lives marked by extreme poverty. López still gets emotional remembering what some of the youngsters told him.

“I get goosebumps whenever I think about what many of those boys used to tell me. When I asked them what they valued most about being at the academy, they said it was being able to eat every day. And everything was free.

“They developed as both players and human beings. We must have done something right with our scouting and development because 11 of those boys are now professional soccer players in Europe, while more than 20 play in Ivory Coast’s top division. But Yan’s story is extraordinary. He has gone from playing barefoot to becoming a global superstar.”

The young Diomande’s rise was relentless. He cleared every obstacle placed in front of him, no matter how difficult the challenge. López particularly remembers watching him compete against players as much as five years older.

“There isn’t a traditional league system there, so we organized friendly matches, sometimes against youth teams whose players looked like they could have been the fathers of our boys. Yan was never intimidated. He showed his class every time he stepped onto the field. He loved challenges.

“He usually played right wing, where he was a nightmare for defenders. He would tear them apart. Playing him on the left wasn’t particularly common back then, although that became more frequent years later.”

As Diomande grew older, his physical development, technical ability, and ambition made him stand out even more.

Fran Castaño, director of African Talent Elite Academy, has spent 13 years working with soccer players across the continent. He first watched Diomande in person at a qualifying tournament for the Africa Under-17 Cup of Nations.

“He caught my attention immediately. He got past defenders with remarkable ease, could provide an attacking outlet on either wing, had tremendous talent, and knew how to score goals. He scored several times during that tournament. His combination of skill and power was particularly impressive. He looked far superior to everyone else, even though he was only 15 and competing against older players.”

He offered options on both sides, he was talented and could score; at just 15 he looked far superior to the rest"

Everyone who watched him seemed convinced he was destined for European soccer. Getting there, however, was another matter.

“We used to travel to Spain twice a year to compete in tournaments. Some of the boys stood out, especially Yan, but nobody was willing to sign a long-term partnership agreement with a project involving players who were still so young,” López explains.

Castaño agrees that Diomande was an obvious talent worth investing in, but international regulations created a major obstacle.

“He was clearly a player worth taking a chance on, but he couldn’t move to Europe because FIFA regulations make it extremely difficult for players under 18 to transfer internationally. At 16, you can sign a contract, but you can’t play. That’s why he moved to Florida.”

‘The country is turning white’: Ivory Coast embraces its new Real Madrid superstar

Yan Diomande’s fairytale has reached its most spectacular chapter, triggering an extraordinary wave of excitement and national pride across Ivory Coast, the West African nation known for its elephant heritage and status as the world’s largest cocoa producer.

His move to Real Madrid has made him the first Ivorian to join the Spanish powerhouse, and his name is already being mentioned alongside national legends such as Didier Drogba and Yaya Touré, despite his young age.

Miguel Ángel Hoyo, Ivory Coast’s national basketball coach, spoke to AS from Abidjan about the enormous social and media impact of the transfer.

“It’s absolute madness here. People are barely talking about anything else. What everyone keeps emphasizing is that Diomande represents proof that talent, effort, and hard work can take you to the very top, even if you come from extreme poverty.

“People are incredibly excited and happy. In this part of the world, soccer is an escape from the everyday struggles of life. The news of his move to Madrid gave everyone an emotional lift. It has given people hope for the future.”

Hoyo, a native of the Spanish city of Ceuta who has spent more than 18 months as Ivory Coast’s basketball coach, also described the changing atmosphere across the country.

“Spanish soccer has always been hugely popular here. You regularly see people of all ages walking around in white Real Madrid jerseys or Barcelona’s blue-and-red colors. But now the balance is shifting, and the country is turning white. There is absolute euphoria surrounding this kid.”

Kader Guilavogui, a journalist with Sport Ivoire, one of the country’s leading sports news websites, says Diomande’s emergence has sparked a wave of national pride.

“We’re all incredibly proud of him, and there is excitement everywhere. You can tell he’s gained confidence after a difficult start adjusting to his new surroundings. He’s now showing much of what he’s capable of. He’s rising to the challenge and becoming an example for young people throughout Ivory Coast.”

The country is increasingly turning into one giant Real Madrid fan club in West Africa.

“Real Madrid already had a significant following here. After the controversial refereeing during the 2009 Champions League semifinal between Chelsea and Barcelona, many people sympathized with our national hero, Didier Drogba, and Barcelona became less popular with a lot of fans,” Guilavogui explains.

“Many supporters have switched their allegiance to Madrid since Diomande arrived. The presence of Mourinho, who is also an extremely popular figure here, has helped.”

The pride felt throughout Ivory Coast is shared by Diomande himself, according to people close to the young star.

Although he was thrilled when he arrived in Madrid, he has never forgotten where he came from. His humble beginnings continue to motivate him to work harder every day and never give up.

In addition to lifting his immediate family and closest friends out of poverty, Diomande regularly sends money back to the community where he grew up.

It is the same neighborhood where, as a little boy, he dreamed of becoming Africa’s version of Cristiano Ronaldo, encouraged every step of the way by his beloved sister Roxane, whose untimely death remains a heartbreaking loss.

From makeshift balls and bare feet on the streets of Sicogi to the bright lights of the Santiago Bernabéu, Diomande’s journey is about far more than soccer. It is a story of extraordinary talent, opportunity, perseverance, and a young man who has never forgotten the people and the place that made him who he is.
