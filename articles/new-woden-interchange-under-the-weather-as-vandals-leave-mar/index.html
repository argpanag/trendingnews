New Woden Interchange under the weather as vandals leave mark | Region Canberra

TheTools · 31.10.2026 00:34 · source · permalink

Bunting marks a section of the Woden Bus Interchange where glass panels were damaged and removed. Photos: Ian Bushnell.

Smashed glass panels at the new Woden Bus Interchange have been removed but won’t be replaced until at least January, six months after the first incident in June.

This and other acts of vandalism have prompted calls for more transport and police patrols to deter attacks on the facility.

The panels on the inner northbound platform (B) were damaged in separate incidents in June and August, only months after the facility opened in April.

An ACT Government spokesperson attributed the initial attack on 5 June to vandalism, adding that the causes of two further incidents on 13 and 28 August are still undetermined.

All glass panels were removed by 11 September to ensure passenger safety while replacement panels were procured and installed.

The spokesperson said the cost of the damage and reinstalling the new panels was unknown. It would depend on the outcome of the procurement process.

The Interchange has also been the target of other acts of vandalism, including relatively minor instances of graffiti and damage to some wayfinding bollards.

There have also been reports of toilet damage that forced them to close for a time.

The spokesperson said damage to public infrastructure was disappointing and came at a cost to the community.

But these were isolated incidents, and the Interchange continued to operate as intended, remaining a safe and reliable transport hub for thousands of passengers each week.

“Instances of vandalism and graffiti are addressed as they arise through routine maintenance and cleaning programs to ensure facilities and public amenities remain safe, functional and welcoming,” the spokesperson said.

It will take until January to replace the glass panels, which offer some protection from the wind.

Public Transport Association of Canberra chair Amy Jelacic said it was very sad to see such a beautiful new facility treated so poorly by some members of the community.

“I have been disappointed to personally see the inside walls of one of the toilets at the bus interchange covered in graffiti, and I know other community members have seen other instances of vandalism like this,” she said.

But she said nearby development would help keep vandalism at bay.

“Passive surveillance is a powerful tool for deterring vandalism, and Woden Town Centre is due for more development in coming months and years in that area,” she said.

“Generally, the more activity and residents in the area, the better the social outcomes. That means that things will, ideally, get better in the future.”

But at present the Interchange was in a physical environment ripe for antisocial conduct. Dr Jelacic backed the deployment of more Transport Enforcement Officers and police patrols to deter offenders.

Woden Interchange is “ripe for antisocial conduct”.

Independent MLA Fiona Carrick, who initially raised questions about the shattered panels, has also received complaints about the lack of weather protection at the Interchange, particularly on the inner, rapid-bus platforms, which are susceptible to cold southerly winds.

Ms Carrick has approached the government about providing more protection, but a government spokesperson said there are currently no plans for additional measures beyond reinstalling the glass panels.

“The glass panels formed part of the interchange’s original weather protection design, and their replacement will restore this amenity,” the spokesperson said.

Ms Carrick is also urging government to install a clock at the Interchange, given there is no real-time service information available since the old MyWay system was switched off two years ago.

“This lack of information is exacerbated at the Woden Interchange, which has printed timetables but no clock,” she said.

The government spokesperson said the installation of a clock was not being contemplated, but Transport Canberra was investigating options to provide real-time bus service information at bus stations and interchanges.

In the meantime, passengers could access up-to-date service information through digital channels, including the MyWay+ app, online journey planning tools, and the Transport Canberra information line on 13 17 10.

It remains a sore point for bus passengers that real-time Passenger Information Displays still haven’t been installed.

Dr Jelacic said a clock was a nice idea, but all Interchanges should have Passenger Information Displays that show the time as well as service information.

Ms Carrick said Transport Canberra should also do more to promote the Interchange’s two new high-quality bike storage cages, which she feared were not being used as much as they should be.
