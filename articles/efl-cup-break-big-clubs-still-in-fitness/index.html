EFL Cup Break: Big Clubs Still In & Fitness

TheTools · 26.10.2026 13:05 · source · permalink

The Premier League clears three midweek nights from 27–29 October so the EFL Cup fourth round can take the stage. For the big clubs still alive, this is not a soft free week. It is a fitness negotiation: who sits, who starts, and who cannot be risked at all while the league table is still tight at the top.

Table of Contents

Why the Premier League steps aside

Eight ties fill the calendar while league fixtures pause. Thirteen Premier League clubs are still in the competition, which is why the midweek feels like a shared cup break rather than a one-off distraction. Manchester City and Arsenal sit joint-top on 21 points after eight league games, Brighton are third on 16, and Liverpool fourth on 15. Chelsea (13), Newcastle and Aston Villa (11), Everton (10), and Bournemouth (9) all carry league agendas into the same window.

That density matters. When so many top-flight squads are still involved, the break is really about load management across the same three nights: cup progress without emptying the tank for the next Premier League weekend.

The big clubs still chasing the cup

The headline names still standing are Arsenal, Manchester City, Liverpool, and Chelsea, with Brighton right behind them in the league and also still in the draw. Around them sit a second tier of Premier League sides for whom a deep cup run would rewrite the season’s story: Newcastle United, Aston Villa, Everton, Bournemouth, Fulham, Crystal Palace, Brentford, and Sunderland.

Three lower-league clubs remain as well: Bradford City, Fleetwood Town, and Peterborough United. For the giants, those names are the reminder that rotation has a cost. For everyone else still in, the break is a rare midweek with something tangible to win.

Fitness sheets that already shape the break

The availability lists for the biggest clubs show why this pause is about bodies as much as brackets.

Arsenal head into the round without Bukayo Saka, William Saliba, and Christos Tzolis, all listed as missing. That is a different kind of cup problem: even with a joint-top league position, the spine and the main wide threat are compromised, so the break becomes a test of squad depth rather than a chance to rest a full first XI.

Manchester City can rotate heavily. Erling Haaland, Phil Foden, Rúben Dias, Joško Gvardiol, and several other regulars are among those projected on the bench rather than missing, while Antoine Semenyo is listed as missing and Matheus Nunes as doubtful. City’s break looks like controlled rotation from a position of league strength (21 points, seven wins from eight), not crisis management.

Liverpool sit in the middle of those two pictures. Dominik Szoboszlai, Conor Bradley, Jeremie Frimpong, Giovanni Leoni, and Hugo Ekitiké are missing, with Florian Wirtz doubtful, while Virgil van Dijk, Alisson, Ryan Gravenberch, and Alexander Isak appear among the players who can be held back as substitutes. The Anfield cup night is therefore a genuine fitness puzzle: enough quality to progress, enough absences that the league XI cannot be thrown out casually.

Chelsea carry a longer missing list than their league position suggests. Cole Palmer, Reece James, Emiliano Martínez, Jamie Gittens, and Emmanuel Emegha are missing, with João Pedro doubtful. When the creative hub and the captain are both unavailable, a cup midweek stops being optional squad polishing and becomes damage limitation around whoever is fit enough to start.

Brighton are also managing absences while sitting third: Kaoru Mitoma and Evan Ferguson are among those missing, with Ferdi Kadıoğlu and Mats Wieffer doubtful. For a side scoring freely in the league, the break is about whether their depth can protect both the cup tie and the next Premier League assignment.

Rotation as the real story of the pause

Look past the missing names and the projected cups elevens tell the same story in a different way. City’s probable side leans on Gerónimo Rulli, academy and fringe minutes, and creators such as Rayan Cherki rather than a full Haaland-led league XI. Liverpool’s projected group mixes senior pieces like Alexis Mac Allister and Cody Gakpo with younger attackers. Arsenal’s probable selection already assumes life without Saka and Saliba.

That is the Premier League break in practice. Clubs still in the EFL Cup are not using the midweek as recovery alone. They are choosing who gets protected for the league, who gets minutes to stay sharp, and which absences force a more conservative approach. The sides with the deepest benches can treat the round as rotation. The sides with thinner fitness sheets have to treat it as another full-intensity night.

Everton and Newcastle, for example, arrive with fewer headline absences but also less league cushion. Vitaliy Mykolenko is doubtful for Everton; Tino Livramento is missing for Newcastle. Their break is less about resting superstars and more about staying competitive on thin margins in both competitions.

What the big clubs want from these three nights

For City and Arsenal, the ideal break is simple: advance without adding to the injury list. For Liverpool and Chelsea, it is progress without asking a patched midfield or attack to play 120 minutes if it can be avoided. For Brighton and the rest of the Premier League pack still involved, it is proof that their squad can absorb a midweek cup tie and still look coherent when the league returns.

The table will barely move while these games are on. The fitness room will. That is why this Premier League pause matters to the big clubs still in the cup: not because the league stops caring for three nights, but because the next league weekend will be played with whatever decisions and knocks this round leaves behind.
