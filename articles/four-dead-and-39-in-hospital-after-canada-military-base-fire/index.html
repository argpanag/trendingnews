Four dead and 39 in hospital after Canada military base fire

TheTools · 19.10.2026 23:27 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Four soldiers killed after a major fire at a Canadian military base have been named, as officials say another 39 people were taken to hospital

  • At least 19 of those in hospital are in a critical condition after a grass fire spread on Saturday, officials say

  • Carney says the "violent, fast-moving" fire led to "one of the worst tragedies" for the military in peacetime

  • "It was like a scene out of a war movie," says a hospital resident who witnessed a military vehicle and ambulances bringing injured people in

  • Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, where the fire broke out about 16:00 local time (22:00 GMT) on Saturday, is one of the country's busiest military training bases

  2. How did a fire kill four soldiers and injure 39 people? What we heard from the news briefingpublished at 23:28 BST 18 October
    23:28 BST 18 October

    A deadly fire occurred at Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Wainwright in Alberta.

    After a statement from Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and a news briefing by officials, we now know more about what happened.

    Here’s a recap of the latest update from the authorities today.

    Casualties and rescue effort

    • The four people who died in the incident were all serving with 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry - here are their names
    • Thirty-nine patients are being treated in hospital, and at least 19 are in a critical condition
    • About 30 emergency units, including 22 ground ambulances and eight aircraft, responded to CFB Wainwright when the incident broke out

    How did the fire happen?

    • Soldiers were carrying out grenade and attack training before the fire
    • A grass fire started in the training area, and "uncommonly strong winds" caused it to spread rapidly and change direction
    • While there are reports of explosions, "we do not believe this [incident] to be a result of any oil and gas infrastructure nor any outside interference", officials say
    • The cause of the incident remains under investigation

  3. Rutland: Those injured were on the section of the attack rangepublished at 22:56 BST 18 October
    22:56 BST 18 October

    The people who were hurt were on a section attack range, not on a grenade range, Rutland says in response to a media question.

    Asked whether they practise during high winds, Rutland says they do.

    Part of the battalion has been moved out of the training area. The rest had a maintenance day on Sunday but will resume training soon, as they’re still due to go to Latvia, he says.

    It’s unclear when that training will resume, Rutland says.

  4. Fire 'not expected' to move near the range - Rutlandpublished at 22:51 BST 18 October
    22:51 BST 18 October

    Rutland is asked for more details about how the fire spread.

    He says soldiers were split between a section attack range and a grenade range on the military base when the fire was about a kilometre away.

    He adds that the fire was "not expected" to move near the range, but later struck the administration area of the attack range.

    Rutland is also asked if he can reassure people this won't happen again.

    He says this was a "very uncommon" occurrence, and an investigation is under way to find out if it was preventable. That needs to play out so they can get answers.

  7. ‘Not normal’ for a fire to move so quickly, Rutland sayspublished at 22:44 BST 18 October
    22:44 BST 18 October

    Rutland is asked about the mood at the army base.

    He says most of the soldiers were from Edmonton and the incident has caused "a lot of stress and strain".

    He is then asked about the protocol for operations at the base in high winds.

    He says it is the military, "so we have protocol for everything".

    He adds that a system is in place and the weather was well within standard procedure for most of the day.

    Rutland says the fire was kilometres away when the wind shifted and it is "not normal" for a fire to move a kilometre in 30 to 60 seconds.

  8. Soldiers were throwing grenades and carrying out attack training - officialpublished at 22:38 BST 18 October
    22:38 BST 18 October

    When asked about the training, Maj-Gen Rutland says the infantry was preparing for two roles.

    One was for a global response task force, which is ready to move within hours to any hot spot around the world.

    The other was preparation for a deployment to Latvia in April and May.

    "This was high-intensity combat training with live munitions," he says.

    "They were throwing hand grenades on a hand grenade range and conducting what we call a section attack, 10 soldiers attacking a simulated enemy," he adds.

    22:33 BST 18 October

    Rutland also says that, while there are reports of explosions, "we do not believe this [incident] to be a result of any oil and gas infrastructure nor any outside interference".

    He says the top priority is the care of soldiers and their families at this time, adding that the Canadian Armed Forces is providing support.

    Rutland has also named the four army members who were killed. We will identify them and bring you their names shortly.

  10. Grass fire ignited in training area - officialpublished at 22:29 BST 18 October
    22:29 BST 18 October

    Maj-Gen Wade Rutland, commander of 1st Canadian Division, says the army responded to a "serious fire incident" during a training exercise conducted by the Third Battalion Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry at about 16:00 local time on Saturday.

    During the training, a grass fire ignited in the training area, he says, adding that the cause is under active investigation.

    "What I would say is there was multiple training activities happening throughout the training area at the time," he says.

    "Uncommonly strong winds caused the fire to spread rapidly and shift direction towards the range administrative area."

  17. 'It was like a scene out of a war movie,' says hospital residentpublished at 21:19 BST 18 October
    21:19 BST 18 October

    Brandon Simmons, who lives in long-term care at the Wainwright Health Centre, witnessed the aftermath of the fire at the military base last night.

    He tells Reuters news agency that the site went into code orange and that nurses had dropped everything and immediately started grabbing stretchers, gurneys and any medical equipment they could before running to the front entrance.

    At the same time, he says, a military vehicle arrived at the centre and started unloading injured people, while nurses tried to stabilise everyone and “calm them down”.

    Brandon says he remembers seeing people with burns, while one person had their clothes "blown off" and was wearing only underwear.

    "I heard people screaming inside of those vehicles that will never end up being out of my mind," he recalls, saying it was "like a scene from a war movie".

    Brandon says ambulances kept coming "throughout the night", with between 12 and 20 parked along the road at one time.

  18. 'Code orange' emergency designation deactivatedpublished at 21:00 BST 18 October
    21:00 BST 18 October

    All “code orange” activations in Alberta have now been halted, Acute Care Alberta says in a statement.

    A code orange is an emergency designation used in Canada during a disaster or mass-casualty incident involving multiple severely injured patients who need medical care.

    The statement also reports the scale of the emergency response.

    It says Emergency Health Services Alberta deployed 30 units directly to Wainwright, including 22 ground ambulances, six fixed-wing units and two rotary-wing units.

    The agency says the active emergency response was sustained across the system for more than 10 hours.

    As of today, patients have been transferred from Wainwright Health Centre to four facilities: the University of Alberta Hospital, Royal Alexandra Hospital, Foothills Medical Centre and Red Deer Regional Hospital Centre, the agency adds.

  20. Loss of life 'heartbreaking', says Alberta premierpublished at 20:20 BST 18 October
    20:20 BST 18 October

    Alberta Premier Danielle Smith says the province is in mourning alongside those affected by the “tragic incident” at the Wainwright military base.

    “The loss of life and injuries are heartbreaking,” she writes on social media, adding that her thoughts are with those “facing uncertainty and grief during this incredibly difficult time”.

    Smith says she will travel to Edmonton later today to meet provincial ministers and federal officials. An Emergency Management Cabinet Coordination Committee has also been convened.
