Almost all of Anthropic’s employees are now millionaires | CNN Business

TheTools · 19.11.2026 23:27 · source · permalink

Anthropic’s stock market debut turned the vast majority of the young startup’s staff into overnight millionaires — some many times over.

The company’s headcount stood at 5,300 as of September this year, according to regulatory filings. After Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, about 600 employees entered the rarefied “centi-millionaire” club, with equity holdings valued north of $100 million, according to data from Hill.com, which tracks private-company shares.

As many as 2,800 staffers, known internally as “Ants,” have holdings valued above $10 million. And about 5,100 — 96% of the staff — are sitting on equity north of $1 million.

“What’s most surprising here is how quickly this this happened,” Andrew Benson, Hill’s founder and CEO, told CNN on Thursday, noting Anthropic has seen very little attrition. “It’s been pretty clear for some time that Anthropic is a rocket ship, and so it definitely discouraged people from leaving because they knew what the opportunity cost of that would be.”

It’s not unusual for Silicon Valley startups, which are typically light on cash, to pay their staff with a combination of salary and stock. The promise of a future acquisition or initial public offering tends to keep workers motivated to work hard and stick it out until payday.

But few startups have grown as big or as quickly as Anthropic. The company, founded in 2021, had fewer than 1,000 employees at the end of 2024.

As with most tech startups, Benson noted, “being early is more important, in terms of a financial outcome, than being senior … You could be very low on the totem pole, and if you joined in 2023 or 2024, you’re likely to have a significantly better outcome than a senior person who joined earlier this year.”

SpaceX, by contrast, spent more than two decades as a private company before its blockbuster IPO in June. That debut minted around 4,400 millionaires, out of a staff of 22,000.

Anthropic’s IPO is also unique for allowing non-founder insiders to cash out on Day One of trading. In most IPOs, staff are subject to a “lockup” period — typically three to six months — during which they can’t sell their shares. But Anthropic’s listing included a complex, staggered lockup schedule that allowed non-founder insiders to cash out up to 10% of their shares as soon as the stock began trading. (It’s not known how many insiders may have taken advantage of the opportunity Thursday.)

And unlike SpaceX, which has employees spread across Texas, California and Florida, Anthropic is uniquely concentrated in San Francisco, a city whose entire economy has been reshaped by sudden surges of tech wealth.

A rash of newly minted millionaires could soon amplify pressure on San Francisco’s housing market, where home prices are already rising at their fastest pace in years.

The typical home there sold for $1.7 million in October, up nearly 19% from a year earlier, according to Redfin. By contrast, the national median home price was roughly flat over the same period, at $416,700, according to the National Association of Realtors.

CNN’s Samantha Delouya contributed reporting.
