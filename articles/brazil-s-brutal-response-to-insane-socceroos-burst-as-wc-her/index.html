Brazil’s brutal response to insane Socceroos burst as WC hero’s wonder strike stuns

TheTools · 29.09.2026 20:37 · source · permalink

Stung by the criticism aimed at them after a disappointing display in Townsville, Brazil hit back at Brisbane’s Suncorp Stadium on Tuesday night with a 4-2 win over the Socceroos.

In front of 52,423 fans, the biggest crowd ever for a football game in Queensland, the Selecao scored two unanswered second-half goals to break the resistance of the Australians, who had hoped to build on their impressive performance in last week’s 1-1 draw between the two nations.

The Brazilians needed two penalties to help their cause but there was no doubting their superior class was evident on a pitch conducive to free-flowing football.

The Socceroos were left to rue a second-half fracas that sparked the tourists to life, having responded spectacularly to a dramatic opening.

The hosts looked set for a rough evening when Brazil struck through Vanderson after a slick build-up in less than three minutes - the fastest the Socceroos have conceded under Popovic.

But remarkably, the Australians wouldn’t just equalise, but take the lead in a crazy sequence of play.

First, Alessandro Circati dextrously flicked in a perfect cross from Connor Metcalfe, with an initial call of offside overruled by the VAR.

Then, it was Metcalfe himself who sealed a 2-1 scoreline with a stunning left-footed goal from beyond the box, a strike reminiscent of his heroics in the World Cup against Turkiye.

However, the Socceroos headed into half time 2-2 after Harry Souttar brought down Danilo Santos in the box, with the VAR awarding Brazil a penalty duly put away by Brazil’s Barcelona star Raphinha.

And a flare-up midway through the second half after Brazilian substitute Endrick clashed with Harry Souttar near the benches was just the tonic the visitors needed to find their best.

The five-time world champions made their intentions clear from the opening whistle, taking the lead inside three minutes and before the Socceroos had completed a pass.

A slick movement involving Arsenal star Bruno Guimaraes, Gabriel Bontempo and Rayan was finished off in style by right-back Vanderson, whose sizzling angled strike was too good for Socceroos goalkeeper Patrick Beach.

The shell-shocked Australians then needed a double save from Beach a minute later to avoid going further behind, with the France-based former Melbourne City gloveman twice denying Raphinha.

After their nervous start, the hosts were able to belatedly settle into the contest.

For the second match in succession against Selecao, the Australians weren’t afraid to take risks on the ball, with dangerman Nestory Irankunda again at the forefront of most of Australia’s attacking raids.

However, it was defender Circati who grabbed the Socceroos’ equaliser in the 28th minute, but not before VAR intervention.

Circati was initially flagged offside after flicking the ball into the net at the far post from a Connor Metcalfe corner that was headed into the centre-back’s direction.

The assistant referee’s flag seemed to spoil Australian celebrations.

However, on closer inspection, Metcalfe’s corner was shown to be headed into Circati’s path by Brazilian defender Gabriel, with the offside ruling being correctly overturned to allow the goal to stand.

The packed crowd got an ever bigger thrill five minutes later when the Socceroos went ahead through a superb left-footed strike from attacking midfielder Connor Metcalfe.

Fed by Circati, Metcalfe unleashed from just outside Brazil’s penalty area with a shot that flew into the top corner of the net.

The see-sawing nature of the first-half was to continue in the 45th minute when the South Americans equalised from the penalty spot.

This time VAR intervention was to work against the Socceroos after Japanese referee Yusuke Araki waved play on after what seemed an obvious foul by Souttar on Danilo.

After reviewing the footage, Araki changed his mind and pointed to the spot, with Raphinha converting the penalty to ensure scores were level at 2-2 at half-time.

The South Americans upped the ante in the second-half, going ahead in the 69th minute when Guimaraes scored from close range after being fed by substitute Endrick.

Vinicius Junior then sealed Brazil’s win in the 80th minute with his team’s second penalty of the contest after he had been fouled by Irankunda.

“Today, we played much, much better,” Guimaraes told Paramount+ post-match.

“A very difficult test for us. They played very well.

“It was a nice game to watch and a nice game to play. Very happy for the team performance.”

Socceroos young gun Lucas Herrington, playing in front of his home crowd, said it was “so special” to pull on the gold in front of a record Queensland crowd.

“It means everything,” he told Paramount+.

“I’ve grown up in Brisbane, so I know how big of a football community there is out here.

“It’s so great to see. There’s not heaps of Socceroos games in Queensland, so it’s been so nice to come home and see the reception we’ve got.”

In a match that became more spiteful as it went on, tempers boiled over with less than 25 minutes of time remaining when a disagreement between Souttar and Endrick spilled over into sideline melee just to the right of Australia’s bench.

Endrick took a theatrical dive after Socceroos assistant coach Paul Okon attempted to calm the situation.

Referee Araki eventually gained control of the situation, with both Endrick and Souttar being shown yellow cards for the initial confrontation that led to a host of players from both teams running several metres to get involved in the incident.
