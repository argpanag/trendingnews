An artist’s rendering of Voyager 1 barrelling through space. | Photo Credit: AP

Today, the Voyager 1 spacecraft will be one light-day away from the earth. This means a signal travelling at the speed of light will take one full day to go from the earth to Voyager 1, and signals from the spacecraft will similarly take one day to reach receivers on the earth.

This is a tremendous distance — almost 26 billion kilometres. Since February 17, 1998, Voyager 1 has been the farthest any space mission has been from us. And it keeps going at 61,000 km/hr. But even at such breathtaking speed, Voyager 1 is still barely out of the solar system. Each star’s gravitational and magnetic fields extend out for billions of kilometres around them, bending the destinies of multiple worlds to their stellar will.
