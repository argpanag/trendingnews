Ontario’s new school supplies portal for teachers may have pricing 'mistakes,' says education minister | CBC News

TheTools · 04.09.2026 08:40 · source · permalink

Teacher, opposition raised the alarm after some items cost more on the province’s portal than in stores

Kimiko Shibata, a part-time Grade 3 teacher, noticed some items cost more on the Ontario government’s Staples portal for teachers than they did on the retailer’s public website.

A new provincial portal for Ontario teachers that launched Tuesday is the cheapest place for teachers to buy essential supplies, says Ontario’s Education Minister Paul Calandra — though there may also be some pricing "mistakes."

This comment comes after one teacher noticed some items cost more on the Ontario government’s Staples portal for teachers than they did on the retailer’s public website.  

When Kimiko Shibata, a part-time Grade 3 teacher, went to access the portal Tuesday morning, she noticed Crayola markers cost about $5 — but they only cost $2 in regular Staples stores. She said pencil crayons also cost more; about $1 for the public but close to $5 through the teachers’ portal.

"It's almost begging teachers to spend our own money rather than buy through the portal," she told CBC Toronto on Tuesday. "It's a little bit ridiculous."

Crayola markers, for example, cost about $5 but only cost $2 in regular Staples stores, she said. She said pencil crayons also cost more; about $1 for the public, but close to $5 on the teachers portal. 

Shibata posted about the pricing on social media platform X Tuesday morning. When Shibata checked the portal on Wednesday, the prices of the markers had been reduced on the province’s portal. She posted that to social media, too. 

When it was first announced in March, the province said the portal will give teachers a way to buy school supplies without using their own money. Each elementary homeroom teacher may spend up to $750 through the Ontario portal. Part-time teachers, like Shibata, get less.

When asked about the price discrepancy on Tuesday, Calandra said the newly-launched portal may have some errors. 

"Are you going to be able to go on that portal and find mistakes? 100 per cent," he said.

The province worked with teachers to create a list of 200 essential school supplies that will be available on the portal for a cheaper price, Calandra said.

The minister added he's "absolutely, positively confident" no one will be able to find a cheaper price for the items on that list. He said teachers who see higher prices should tell their principal.

Opposition questions choice of portal's provider

Shibata said, though she’s a member of the NDP, this issue isn't about her politics and she just wants teachers to be able to access the prices they were promised.

"This is supposed to be a bulk purchasing thing where we're going to get these great deals," said Shibata.

Staples Canada has set up a dedicated line to respond to any price differences, according to a statement from a company spokesperson to CBC News. The statement said Staples is committed to ensuring that products on the province’s list are priced the same or less on the portal than on the company’s regular website.

Official Opposition Leader Marit Stiles said she wonders why Staples was chosen as the provider in the first place.

"That is an American-owned company," she said at a news conference Tuesday. "[The education minister] is spending $60 million taxpayer dollars on this deal with Staples and they can't even deliver a portal."

She said the money spent building the portal could have been put directly into classrooms in the province. 

"The idea that teachers, education workers are being forced to go to some Staples portal to order these products and it's not even working … is very alarming," Stiles said.
