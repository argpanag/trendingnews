JTBC Seeks About 500 Billion Won in Sale as Hoban, Taekwang Eye Bid

TheTools · 10.11.2026 07:47 · source · permalink

Capital Increase on Par With That of JoongAng Ilbo Enterprise Value Tops 700 Billion Won Including Net Debt Scarcity of General Programming Channels Commands Premium

According to investment banking sources on Nov. 10, JTBC's creditors are seeking a sale price of around 500 billion won. The amount would be used to repay JTBC's debt in one go through a third-party share placement. Industry observers say it will be difficult to cut the price significantly because public bonds held largely by retail investors are involved, leaving creditors obliged to meet a certain minimum recovery rate.

The sale covers JTBC along with its affiliated channels JTBC2 and JTBC4, which are classified as entertainment and lifestyle channels, respectively. JTBC Golf and JTBC Sports are operated by separate entities and are understood to be excluded from the sale.

Potential bidders cited in the market include Hoban Group, which has long shown interest in media companies, and Taekwang Industrial, which explored an acquisition of the JoongAng Ilbo. A number of other suitors that previously joined the JoongAng Ilbo bidding, including OK Financial Group, are also said to be interested.

The industry puts the EV — the value of a 100% stake plus net debt — at more than 700 billion won. The EV is calculated by adding the debt-to-equity swap portion to the rights offering proceeds. The EV could rise further depending on the size of the debt the company must actually repay. "In restructuring deals such as workouts, the size of the company's debt is effectively the company's value," an investment banking official said.

The size of the rights offering ultimately depends on how far the company's debt is adjusted. JTBC's total debt, including public bonds sold to retail investors and obligations tied to International Olympic Committee (IOC) broadcasting rights, is estimated at about 960 billion won.

One structure under discussion would cut the debt to the 700 billion won range during rehabilitation, repay part of it with the 500 billion won in acquisition proceeds and convert about 200 billion won into equity. With a capital reduction on existing shares carried out alongside a new share issue, the stakes of existing major shareholders such as JoongAng Holdings are expected to shrink to negligible levels. Because creditors who swap debt for equity also become shareholders, the acquirer would not hold a 100% stake even after paying 500 billion won.

If JTBC's EV is set at 700 billion won, the EV/EBITDA multiple would reach about 23 times based on last year's consolidated EBITDA of 30.6 billion won. The premium is seen as reflecting the scarcity value of a general programming broadcaster coming up for sale. Revenue over the same period fell from a year earlier to 373.8 billion won, with an operating loss of 28.7 billion won.

JTBC ran into a liquidity crisis as shrinking TV advertising, rising production costs and the burden of Olympic broadcasting rights fees converged. In June this year, it failed to repay 20.6 billion won in securitized borrowings, and at the end of August the Seoul Bankruptcy Court approved the start of rehabilitation proceedings. It has since pursued a sale of management control by seeking a new owner before the rehabilitation plan is approved. Ernst & Young Han Young is managing the sale. JTBC posted the sale notice on Nov. 9 and plans to select a preferred bidder in January.
