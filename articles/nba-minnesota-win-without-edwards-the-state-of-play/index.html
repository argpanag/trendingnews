NBA: Minnesota win without Edwards, the state of play

TheTools · 13.11.2026 13:44 · source · permalink

Without Anthony Edwards since the fifth game, the Timberwolves have put together five wins and routed Dallas 133-94. Point guard, bench, defence, schedule: what their 9-3 record is really worth.

The Minnesota Timberwolves lost their leading scorer after four games and have not slipped a place. Anthony Edwards was averaging 33.3 points when his right knee took him to the operating table on Thursday 29 October. Since then, his team have won six times in eight outings, including the last five, and have just beaten the Dallas Mavericks by 39 points (133-94, on Thursday 12 November).

The explanation comes down to one sentence: Minnesota did not have to go looking for a replacement, because they had already built a three-headed backcourt, piece by piece, in less than two years. What remains is to know what this 9-3 is worth. Here is the file, department by department, as a scout would fill it in.

Point guard: LaMelo Ball, 43 threes and 48 turnovers

LaMelo Ball was the last to arrive. On 10 July, a four-team trade brought him in from Charlotte. The price: two big men, Julius Randle heading to Brooklyn and Naz Reid sent to the Hornets, plus draft picks.

For now, the numbers back the gamble: 18.6 points and 6.3 assists per game, 43 made three-pointers at 40.6%. Without Edwards, his average has risen from 14.8 to 20.5 points. Over the five straight wins, it climbs to 24.4, with a high of 34 against Milwaukee on Monday 2 November. The other side of the ledger comes down to one number: 48 turnovers, four per game.

Assessment: a top-level point guard when his shot is right, costly when he forces it. To be managed, not questioned.

Edwards' spot: Dosunmu, from 12 to 21 points

The injured player's slot has gone to Ayo Dosunmu, acquired from Chicago on 4 February 2026 and then extended this summer. A substitute in the first four games, he was scoring 12 points per game. A starter since then, he is putting up 21.1 on 53.3% shooting. He has finished as his team's leading scorer in four of the last six games.

Minnesota have not found Edwards' 33 points in one man; they have spread them around. The team scored 122 points with him; they score 118 without him, with just as many assists (26 compared with 26.4).

Assessment: a successful replacement. A full-time starter, even when the boss returns.

The bench: Hyland, the minimum contract worth 24 points

Bones Hyland is the longest-serving of the three. Minnesota signed him on 27 February 2025 on a two-way contract, twenty days after he was waived by Atlanta. The club then extended him in two successive summers, each time for one year at the minimum salary.

With Edwards, he played 8 minutes per game and had scored 9 points in all. Since then, he has been playing nearly 16 minutes, averaging 13.6 points and has made 19 threes from 45. Against Dallas, he delivered 24 points and 8 assists in 23 minutes, five from six from deep, the leading scorer from a bench that produced 54 points.

Assessment: a sixth man found in-house. Inconsistent (4 points in New Orleans), but he can change a game off the bench.

The wings and the paint: 105.9 points allowed, the base has not moved

The defence has never depended on Edwards. Minnesota allow 105.9 points per game, the third-best mark in the league, and hold opponents to 42.9% shooting. Five of them have been kept to 100 points or fewer. On the only night without Rudy Gobert, 34, troubled by his back, San Antonio scored 121.

Around the French centre, two forwards hold the structure together. Jaden McDaniels (16.9 points) has a cumulative differential of +157 when he is on the floor, for a team total margin of +161.

Jonathan Kuminga, born in Goma, in the Democratic Republic of Congo, signed on 4 September for two years. A starter all twelve times, he brings 12.4 points, 5.7 rebounds and 40.4% shooting from three-point range. In Houston, on Wednesday 4 November, his two threes in the final minutes sealed the win.

Assessment: the most reliable department in the file. Dependence on Gobert to be noted.

The schedule: seven wins out of nine against losing records

This is the line that tempers everything else. Seven of the nine teams beaten now have more defeats than wins; between them, the nine have 38 wins and 66 losses. Milwaukee and Utah, beaten in Minneapolis, are 1-11.

Against the four opponents with winning records, the tally is two apiece: wins in Houston (117-107) and against Dallas, defeats against Golden State (114-119) and in San Antonio (111-121). The third defeat, in Cleveland, was decided in overtime (125-127), despite Edwards' 47 points.

The rest argues for Minnesota. Eight of the nine wins have come by ten points or more, five by more than twenty, and the average margin of 13.4 points is the best in the league. At home, the record is 5-1; on the road, 4-2.

Assessment: a record flattered by the opposition, not by luck. The margins, though, are real.

Overall verdict: a collective confirmed, one test still to pass

Minnesota sit third in the West, level with Houston, behind the Lakers (11-2) and San Antonio (10-2). The file still carries two reservations: 16 turnovers per game and 67.4% free-throw shooting. It also contains a deadline, Edwards' return, with his absence estimated at two months. The shots will then have to be redistributed in a backcourt that has acquired a taste for the ball.

In the meantime, the Wolves stay at home for six straight games. The first pits them against the Chicago Bulls (1-9), Dosunmu's former team, on Saturday 14 November, that is Sunday 15 November at 02:00 in Brazzaville. Portland (8-3), twice, then New York (6-5) will follow. That is where this 9-3 will get its true grade.
