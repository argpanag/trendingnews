Jail, caning for man who concealed 210 Kpods in detergent powder at Woodlands Checkpoint

TheTools · 02.10.2026 10:24 · source · permalink

A Singaporean, who was found with two vaporisers and 210 Kpods at Woodlands Checkpoint on Feb 6, was sentenced to three years' jail and five strokes of the cane on Friday (Oct 2).

Png Swee Sheng, 39, was first charged under the Misuse of Drugs Act (MDA) on Feb 10. 

He faced one count of importing a controlled drug, and another count of consumption of drug outside Singapore.

Etomidate, which was then temporarily listed as a Class C controlled drug under the Act, has since been delisted and placed under a new category in the Tobacco and Vaporisers Control Act called "specified psychoactive substance". 

Court documents seen by AsiaOne stated that Png was driving into Singapore via Woodlands Checkpoint at about 5.45am when he was stopped by Immigration and Checkpoints Authority officers for a search of his vehicle.

He was found with 210 pieces of assorted vape pods and two disposable vaporisers placed inside two detergent packages, and was referred to the Health Sciences Authority (HSA).

A total of 209 Kpods were then submitted for laboratory testing, while one was handed over to the Central Narcotics Bureau for investigations.

Of these, 208 were found to contain etomidate. 

Investigations revealed that Png had received a message via Telegram from an acquaintance known as "Pod", who offered him a job to deliver Kpods into Singapore.

He was purportedly offered between $500 and $800 for the delivery, and agreed to do so as he was in need of money, the court heard. 

The pods were picked up from R&F Mall in Johor Bahru at about 4am on the day he was arrested.

Following his arrest, Png provided two bottles of his urine samples to CNB as stipulated under the MDA's regulations.

They tested positive for etomidate.

He later admitted to consuming a Kpod while at R&F Mall, prior to entering Singapore.

Png pleaded guilty to the charges and was sentenced to three years' jail and five strokes of the cane on Oct 2.

Importers, sellers and distributors of etomidate e-vaporisers now face stiffer penalties under the Tobacco and Vaporisers Control Act.

If convicted, importers can be jailed for three to 20 years and receive five to 15 strokes of the cane, while sellers and distributors can be jailed for two to 10 years and receive two to five strokes of the cane.
