Closer David Bednar accepts record-setting qualifying offer from Yankees: sources

TheTools · 17.11.2026 21:07 · source · permalink

David Bednar accepted the qualifying offer from the Yankees ahead of Tuesday’s 4 p.m. deadline, multiple sources confirmed to the Daily News.

The one-year, $23.15 million deal gives Bednar a record average annual salary for a reliever. Still, it is somewhat surprising that he didn’t reject the qualifying offer and pursue a larger total sum on a multi-year contract in a market short on established closers, even if doing so would have attached international bonus pool money and draft pick compensation to his free agency.

Regardless, the Yankees can rest easy knowing that they have their closer locked in for 2027.

“Hopefully, we find a way to keep him,” Brian Cashman said of Bednar at the GM Meetings last week.

Bednar, 32, owns a 2.85 ERA since 2021 despite a disastrous 2024 campaign in Pittsburgh. The righty has thrived with the Yankees since being acquired from the Pirates ahead of the 2025 trade deadline, recording a 2.39 ERA and 45 saves over 94 innings. That includes 35 saves in 39 opportunities and a 2.47 ERA this past season.

Bednar also ranked in the 88th percentile or better in xERA, Chase%, Whiff%, Barrel% and groundball rate in 2026, and he has a 1.00 ERA over nine postseason innings for the Yankees. He’s shown that the bright lights of the Bronx do not bother him, which played a part in the Yankees extending the qualifying offer.

“We dropped a big qualifying number on him,” Cashman said. “That factored a lot. He’s a cool customer.”

Cashman also noted that Bednar has become a leader in the Bombers’ bullpen.

With Bednar accepting a $23.15 million salary for 2027, the Yankees are committing big money to a free agent reliever for the first time since Zack Britton ($39 million) and Adam Ottavino ($27 million) signed three-year contracts in January 2019. While Aroldis Chapman also signed a hefty extension with the team in November 2019, the Yankees’ biggest multi-year contracts for free agent relievers have gone to Justin Wilson (2 years, $5.5 million) and Tommy Kahnle (2 years, $11.5 million) since then.

They also haven’t paid a reliever a single-season salary higher than the $9 million Bednar made in 2026 since Chapman, Britton and Ottavino’s contracts soured.

But with the Yankees’ bullpen in need of a makeover this offseason — they didn’t have any obvious incumbents to replace Bednar in the closer’s role — Cashman said that he’s “not afraid” to spend lavishly on multiple relievers.

“I’ve done it before,” he added.

Cashman also called the reconstruction of his bullpen an offseason “priority.” That’s because a lot of spots are vacant.

While the Yankees have already agreed on a cheap deal to bring Tim Hill back for depth, the team has four other free agent relievers in Paul Blackburn, Ryan Yarbrough, John Schreiber and Michael Fulmer. That makes Brent Headrick the only other contracted reliever without health concerns who spent the entire 2026 season on the Yankees’ roster. Other relievers on the 40-man roster include Fernando Cruz, Kervin Castro, Angel Chivilli, Yerry De los Santos, Bradley Hanner and Luis Gil. Cruz is coming off a partially torn UCL and could be a non-tender candidate. The rest are far from solidified big leaguers.

In addition to Bednar, the Yankees would love to re-sign Blackburn after a career year, and they’ve checked in on Yarbrough, another depth piece.

As for external free agent options, Adrian Morejon is another big-ticket arm who got the qualifying offer but rejected it. He garnered interest from the Yankees at the trade deadline, but it remains to be seen if they are truly “not afraid” to swim in the lefty’s pricey waters after Bednar’s acceptance of the qualifying offer.

Alex Vesia and JoJo Romero are intriguing options as well.

Bednar was one of two players to accept the qualifying offer on Tuesday. The other was Twins catcher Ryan Jeffers, a player the Yankees had interest in ahead of the trade deadline this past summer. He’s not longer an option with Cashman looking for a right-handed hitting backstop to pair with Austin Wells.

Bednar is the second Yankee to accept the qualifying offer in as many offseasons. Last year, Trent Grisham took the one-year pact when it was valued at $22.05 million. The Yankees are hoping their investment in Bednar yields better results, as Grisham is back on the open market after an injury-riddled year that saw him regress offensively and defensively.
