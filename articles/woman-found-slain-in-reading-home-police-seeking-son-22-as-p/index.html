Woman found slain in Reading home; police seeking son, 22, as person of interest in case - The Boston Globe

TheTools · 08.10.2026 15:22 · source · permalink

READING — Police are seeking a 22-year-old man for questioning in connection with the fatal shooting of his mother in the family home Wednesday, officials said.

Middlesex District Attorney Marian T. Ryan said during a Thursday morning briefing that at around 12:38 a.m. Thursday, Reading police were called by Matthew Hanafin, a resident at 9 Central Ave., reporting he had come home after a 12-hour shift ending at 11 p.m. to find the body of his wife Andria Hanafin, 49, in the kitchen of the home

She was pronounced dead shortly after police responded to the home, Ryan said.

“It’s alleged to be domestic violence at this point, although not the traditional domestic violence situation that we think of,” Ryan said, adding that there was “an alleged dispute,” which led up to the murder.

She had no details on the dispute.

“It is a moment of reflection about the presence of guns in a home,” she said. “It is shocking. It is tragic.”

The murder weapon has not been located, Ryan said, adding that Ryan Hanafin had a license to carry that police suspended Thursday morning.

“We have two children now that have lost their mother. We have another child who’s at least a person of interest at this point,” Ryan said. “So this is at first and foremost a terrible tragedy.”

According to Ryan, the 22-year-old Hanafin had a valid license to carry dating to 2024. Reading Police Chief David J. Clark said police had uncovered no reason not to issue that license at the time,

.“I am primarily here to ask for your help in helping us to locate a person of interest and a vehicle,” Ryan told reporters.

The family has three children, the youngest child, 13-years-old, was home at the time of the 911 call, she said, and another daughter was away at college during the incident. A younger son said he had last seen his mother at 5 p.m. Wednesday evening when he left the house with some friends, and did not return until around 9 p.m., Ryan said.

When he returned he went directly to his room and did not see his mother, she added.

The family’s oldest son Ryan Hanafin was missing from the home, officials said.

Matthew Hanafin told police he had not spoken with his son since that morning, Ryan said.

Ryan Hanafin’s car, a 2010 Black Dodge charger, was also missing Ryan said, and had last been spotted on I-93 in Tewksbury around 7:25 p.m. Wednesday, prior to the report of the homicide.

Police said they don’t know where he was headed or his current whereabouts.

He was most recently employed six months ago and had at one point attended a local university, Ryan said. She didn’t name the employer or the school.

Reading Police Chief David J. Clark said residents in the area can expect a “large, long police presence at the scene.”

He said police “are looking to find out what happened at that home last night. We certainly don’t want a tragedy to be compounded by anything else.”

Ryan confirmed that multiple domestic animals were also inside the residence but did not have specifics. The family had no history of police visits or documented mental health issues, she added.

Travis Andersen of the Globe Staff contributed.
