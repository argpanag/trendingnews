Trump begins Georgia rally by claiming Mike Collins in better-looking than Jon Ossoff

Donald Trump is now speaking to a relatively small crowd in a hangar at Dalton municipal airport in Georgia in support of Republican candidates for the US Senate, Mike Collins, and governor, Rick Jackson.

He quickly moved on to repeating his familiar attack on Democratic senator Jon Ossoff, claiming that he looks like Pee-wee Herman, the 1980s character played by the actor and comedian Paul Reubens.

The president then claimed that Mike Collins, who trails Ossoff badly in public polls, was “a good looking man”.

“And he’s all man,” the president continued. “He’s all man. I’m not sure about Ossoff.”

Earlier this week, Trump also praised what he called the physical attributes of another Republican in a tight Senate race, Jon Husted, praising his chin, hands and “ass”.

Bipartisan group of senators call for investigation into disrupted Starlink access

Also today, a bipartisan group of senator called for an investigation into the reported disruption of US-maintained Starlink satellites for Ukraine.

Led by Republican senator Thom Tillis and Jeanne Shaheen, a Democrat, the lawmakers called on the Pentagon’s inspector general to examine these incidents.

In a letter, the senators wrote that to use Starshield, a modified version of Starlink’s satellite network built for governments and defense agencies, “Ukraine needs access to unlimited speeds, which was its service arrangement prior to this reported restriction”.

They added that they were “particularly alarmed by incidents in which US diplomatic, military, and security personnel were unable to reach designated bunkers during Russian air raid”, amid the reported disruptions.

“The possibility that American personnel were endangered by preventable disruptions, inadequate coordination, or decisions made by their own government demands immediate scrutiny and accountability,” the senators noted.

Reporting from Dalton, Georgia

Dan Hackworth finds north-west Georgia more hospitable to his conservatism than the Oregon community he left behind when he retired.

“Everywhere we go here the people are super friendly, and we don’t get a lot of the negative stuff yelled at you like we did up in the north-west,” he said.

Hackworth, a Trump voter on his way to the president’s appearance at the local airport, found himself in a parking lot a few miles away marveling at a gathering of anti-Trump protesters waving flags.

“I like Donald Trump,” Hackworth said. “I think he’s done fantastic for the country. I think the immigration problem that we’ve had for many, many years, has been a detriment to this country. And him trying to shut down the border and control the influx of not just illegal aliens but terrorists and communists, and people who want to do this country nothing but evil has been fantastic.”

He contemplated the apparent loss of some support for the president over the last year among conservatives.

“We’re in a real conservative area where I live, and I don’t get out with a lot of other people. I’m not involved in community stuff in this area. So I don’t really notice that where I live. But nationwide, I think he has. I think a lot of people are a little bit disturbed with the war in Iran, but I do like the idea that he is standing up for Israel. If we weren’t standing up for Israel, things would be a whole lot worse over there, I think Israel would be fighting and getting wiped off – trying to be wiped off – the face of the Earth.”

Hackworth described the Islamic republic as “absolutely pure and adulterated evil” and that higher gas prices were an acceptable tradeoff for preventing their acquisition of a nuclear weapon. “They will do anything to punish western values and if they got a bomb, they would absolutely use it.”

Asked what advice he would give the president, Hackworth talked about healthcare.

“I’d like him to find a way to make medical costs more efficient,” Hackworth said. “If a hospital wants a MRI machine, they have to pay whatever it is – millions of dollars. If the government would make a contract just like they do with the military [to say] we want X number of medical machines, and we ‘re going to give them to these different hospitals. We’re going to share the medical information. That type of stuff would make our healthcare costs go down, cause the hospitals wouldn’t have to pay so much money to get these machines, It’s like the military world.

“But you just described a command economy. Isn’t that communism?” I asked.

“A guy can kind of see that,” he replied. “But we do it. The states do it. Every state does it. What do they do, they need pickups, they go to GM and the state buys however many pickups every year and then they go here and buy so many things and give them out. Well, it’s basically the same thing. But our healthcare costs are absolutely insane, and that really needs to be worked out.”

Given the recent reporting it’s worth noting that five days on the job would make Katie Zacharia the shortest-serving press secretary in White House history.

Katie Zacharia fired as White House press secretary – report

Katie Zacharia, the new White House press secretary, has been fired from her role after just five days, according to the New York Times, citing three unnamed people familiar with the matter.

The Guardian has reached out to the White House and Zacharia for comment.

This comes after Donald Trump told reporters on Friday that he asked Zacharia to “reconsider” the job, following days of intense scrutiny around her legal credentials and a widely panned press briefing. The president said that Zacharia had been “treated very badly by the media”.

On Thursday, the new press secretary told the Guardian it was “unknown” how much she would actually interact with the media, and detailed that it was just six days between getting a call summoning her to Washington and stepping behind the press room podium for the first time.

Donald Trump was taking questions from reporters outside the White House when he made those comments about Katie Zacharia.

A reporter asked the president if he have liked to have known that Zacharia didn’t pass the bar. Trump replied:

Katie has been treated very badly by the media. Very, very badly. Very unfair. And I just spoke to her about it. I said – Katie, is this for you?

And she’s a nice person … But I said, is this for you, Katie, because honestly you don’t need this, you don’t have to be treated [this way].

Asked if he was going to keep her in the role, Trump said: “I’m going to let her make that decision.”

Pentagon reportedly holding off on releasing report on Iran school attack until after midterms

Top Pentagon officials have expressed their intention to hold off on releasing a report about a strike on an Iranian school that killed up to 168 people – most of them children – until after the US midterms, Bloomberg (paywall) reports today.

Defense secretary Pete Hegseth reportedly “communicated these plans”, according to Bloomberg’s sources.

A UN fact-finding mission has determined there are reasonable grounds to believe the US was behind the strike on the school in Minab on 28 February, as well as a strike on a sports facility in Lamerd on the same date, and that these constituted war ⁠crimes.

The first cited strike involved a US Tomahawk missile that collapsed the roof of the Shajareh Tayyebeh primary school in Minab, southern Iran, during morning classes on the first day of the US-Israeli war on Iran, killing 120 children aged seven to 12, as well as staff and parents.

The second cited strike, on the same date, targeted a “clearly identifiable sports complex and residential area” in the southern city of Lamerd with a weapon that scattered tungsten pellets.

The Guardian reported in September that the Pentagon’s own secretive investigation into the incident was understood to have concluded several months prior. There have been reports that senior US commanders had been warned that targeting data used during the war – including for the Minab strike – was dangerously outdated.

New filings show Democrats in key battlegrounds outpacing Republican fundraising

Democrats in key Senate battlegrounds have outpaced Republican fundraising, according to the most recent Federal Electoral Commission (FEC) quarterly filings.

While outside groups and Super Pacs have spent more advertising money to boost Republicans in many of these closely watched competitions, Democrats have had a stronger showing in the number of dollars their campaigns have received directly – leaving many with a strong cash-on-hand advantages going into the final stretch of the midterm elections.

According to the latest filings, James Talarico – the Democratic nominee in Texas who has surged in the polls – raised more than $60m in the last quarter (which ended on 30 September). Meanwhile, his Republican opponent, attorney general Ken Paxton, brought in just over $10m.

A similar disparity applies in the competitive races we’re watching this cycle. For example, in Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed raised more than $30m, compared with Mike Rogers’ $10m. Meanwhile in Maine, Troy Jackson’s campaign raised four times as much as Susan Collins: almost $20.5m compared with $5.2m.
