Funeral of young man found dead in Macroom hears he was 'a beautiful soul'

TheTools · 27.10.2026 15:54 · source · permalink

A large crowd gathered in Macroom this afternoon to pay their respects to the family of Jack O'Donovan, who has been remembered as a young man with 'boundless energy and a heart as big as they come' at his funeral mass today.

The 28-year-old from Dan Corkery Place in Macroom was sadly pronounced dead after his body was discovered at a local golf course late last week. Gardaí launched an investigation into the circumstances surrounding his death, and it's understood that foul play is not suspected at this time.

The young man is survived by his parents, Finbarr and Jackie, his three siblings, grandparents and a wide circle of extended family and friends. Hundreds of people have paid tribute to Jack and the O'Donovan family in the days leading up to his funeral.

At today's ceremony, Jack was remembered for his kind heart, his talent for sport, and his roguish nature, which never failed to put a smile on the faces of family and friends.

Very Rev. Canon Donal Roberts said: “I welcome you all here today. I know there’s a great sadness that has come to our parish here in Macroom since last Friday.

"I know how popular Jacko was, how much he was loved by his family, how they were so proud of Jack, and I know how he was loved by so many people here; he touched so many lives.

“Deep down, he was very soft; there wasn’t a bad bone in Jacko, and it’s because of that that there’s been so much sadness and so much pain at his loss at such a young age.”

Rev Roberts highlighted how many had travelled long distances to pay their respects to young Jack and read out messages of condolence left by those who knew him over the last few days.

One former teacher described him as a ‘really lovely boy who excelled at every sport he ever tried,’ a ‘beautiful soul’ and a ‘hero to all the other children.’

While there has been great sadness in Macroom following Jack’s passing, Rev Roberts also noted the many fond stories told about his life in recent days.

The young man was described as a ‘lovable rogue’ even as a little boy. The priest recounted the story of how, during a visit to an open farm in Killarney, Jack’s uncle was surprised at how quiet he had been. However, a little squeak from the back seat gave away the fact that Jack had managed to smuggle a guinea pig into the car, forcing them to turn around to return the little animal.

Jack’s heartbroken mother, Jackie, addressed the mourners at the outset of today’s ceremony at St Colman’s Church.

She said: “I’ll try not to waffle on now, but when I started writing, I couldn't stop. We are gathered here today under the circumstances that no parent, family member, or friend wants to face.

"As we mourn the unimaginable loss of our Jacko, we are here to do something far more powerful: celebrate a life lived at full throttle, boundless energy, a heart as big as they come.”

Jackie said that keeping up with a young Jack was ‘like an extreme sport.' She fondly recounted a family story where Jack's Uncle Eddie took the boy down to the airport to watch the planes.

While most children are happy to watch through the glass, Jackie described how her son managed to vanish, sneak onto the tarmac and wave at his uncle from his perch atop the luggage cart.

She continued: “As he grew older, that unstoppable energy found his true calling in sport. Jack was an amazing athlete on the football and soccer pitches.

"He was a natural force; he had incredible drive and movement, which made you proud to watch him play. He was a true competitor on the field, but a friend to everyone off it.”

Gifts brought to the altar by Jack’s loved ones symbolised the young man’s deep love of sport. Among the gifts were jerseys from Macroom FC and Arsenal, a football, Jack’s player of the year trophy, and his county runner-up medal in athletics and several other awards he earned throughout his sporting career.

Jackie took a moment to address the large crowd of young people who were in attendance at today’s service, and she urged them, ‘from the bottom of our broken hearts,’ to stay safe and to watch out for one another so that no other family has to go through the same pain they’ve experienced.

The family also gave thanks to Gardaí, Jack’s teammates at Macroom FC, and the large crowds who came to pay their respects at the reposal and at today’s mass. Jackie gave special mention to the guard of honour formed by members of Macroom FC, which she said meant a lot to the family.

“Jacko leaves behind a place that can never be filled, but he leaves us with memories that will make us smile through the tears for the rest of our lives … Rest in peace, Jacko, you will always be our favourite adventurer,” she concluded.

Following today’s ceremony, Jack was laid to rest at St. Colman's Cemetery in Macroom.
