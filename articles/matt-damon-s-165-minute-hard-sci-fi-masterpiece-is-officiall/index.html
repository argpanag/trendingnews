It's been a big year for Matt Damon so far. He reunited with Ben Affleck for two Netflix projects, The Rip and Animals, and played a starring role in Christopher Nolan's billion-dollar blockbuster, The Odyssey. However, long before Nolan and Damon collaborated on the adaptation of Homer's epic Greek poem, the two worked together on a sci-fi masterpiece that's officially leaving Prime Video.

Led by Matthew McConaughey, Anne Hathaway, and Jessica Chastain, Nolan's hard sci-fi blockbuster ended up grossing $782 million at the box office, and was nominated for five Academy Awards (it won one for Best Visual Effects). Damon plays a small but pivotal role in the movie as a scientist stranded on a distant, icy planet.

If you haven't guessed by now, the film is, of course, Interstellar. Released in 2014, Damon appears as Dr. Mann in the modern sci-fi classic, briefly acting as the foil to McConaughey and Hathaway's mission to find habitable planets for humanity to escape to. Though he's only in the movie for about 25 minutes, Damon holds nothing back, playing an unhinged and desperate scientist to perfection.

Nobody Knew Matt Damon Was in 'Interstellar'

While you'd think that Damon would receive some sort of billing in the marketing material for Interstellar leading up to its release, quite the opposite happened. He wasn't in any of the trailers, his name wasn't on the posters, and he was completely omitted from advance cast lists and plot summaries. No one knew he was even in the movie until they saw it, but that was by Nolan's brilliant design.

Before Interstellar, Damon had an established reputation as a likable, on-screen figure. Most of the characters he played were heroes. Flawed, yes, but heroes nonetheless. When he popped up as a surprise in Interstellar, Nolan wanted his presence to convey a sense of calm to the audience, like everything would be okay because that's how we'd been conditioned to feel about him. By turning the tables and having Damon play the villain, Nolan flipped the script, making Interstellar an even greater movie.

'Interstellar' Is Officially Taking Off From Amazon

While it is currently available to stream on Prime Video in the United States, all that will change in just a few short days. Interstellar is set to be removed from Amazon on Nov. 1, meaning the last to watch it will be Halloween. It should be noted that it's also available to stream on Paramount+, so it's not disappearing entirely, but seeing as how Prime has a much bigger audience, it's definitely a blow to those who like it easily accessible.
