Florida insurance rates are falling | Letters to the editor

TheTools · 23.10.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

Breaking News

Fort Lauderdale staff greenlights 8-tower Live Local project at Galleria mall

A Sun Sentinel investigation suggested Florida property insurers were losing money while their affiliated companies made millions. Sounds bad. Then you get to the dates. The financial data at the heart of the story goes back as far as 2017 — nearly a decade ago, before COVID, and before the current insurance commissioner took office.

The story makes something basic about business sound sinister. Imagine owning a restaurant and a separate company that handles payroll and management. The restaurant pays that company $1 million. That’s an expense for one company and it’s revenue for another. But nobody necessarily pocketed $1 million.

The management company still has employees and bills to pay. Insurance companies operate similarly, paying affiliates for underwriting, claims processing, technology and other services.

Regulators absolutely should scrutinize those charges. But an affiliate making money while an insurer loses money doesn’t automatically establish wrongdoing.

And confidential doesn’t mean hidden from regulators. Florida’s Office of Insurance Regulation has broad authority to examine insurers and their affiliates. The disputed report itself was an unfinished draft that Insurance Commissioner Mike Yaworsky said contained errors and data-validation problems.

Newer numbers tell a different story. Global reinsurance broker Guy Carpenter found Florida-focused homeowners’ insurers had lower direct expense ratios than comparable non-Florida carriers from 2018 through 2023 — 15.5% versus 18.9% in the most recent year.

Meanwhile, 20 new property and casualty insurers have entered Florida, bringing more than $850 million in capital that has a direct effect on reducing rates.

Suspicious and unfounded insurance claim activity has dropped by 40% since the governor and Legislature got control of the outrageous lawsuits that were once driving up insurance rates.

Florida homeowners are finally seeing relief in their insurance bills. My own business insurance dropped 15%. Readers deserve current data and context.

— Jason Kinsley, Tallahassee

Enough polling, already

Day in and day out, we hear that another new poll shows that 72% of registered voters approve or agree or disagree on whatever subject the pollsters are asking about.

Enough, already! That 72% repeatedly never shows up to vote, as our voter turnouts are anemic. The 72% are talking heads who simply want to vent.

The message: Get off your butts and vote! I, for one, do not want to hear anybody’s opinion on anything if they don’t exercise their right and their civic responsibility to vote.

— Douglas M. McIntosh, Esq., Plantation

The right choice for Plantation

I was born in Plantation and I care deeply about my hometown. Voters deserve more than campaign graphics with titles, board memberships and accounting credentials.

The mayoral candidate Tim Fadgen’s résumé lists years of professional and civic involvement but titles do not prove effective leadership. Residents deserve specific accomplishments, measurable results and a plan for addressing infrastructure, flooding, public safety, parks and city services.

Plantation is a city, not an accounting firm. Leading requires a broad understanding of community needs and mayoral responsibilities. I support Mayor Nick Sortal’s re-election because I believe Plantation’s future should be judged by leadership, accountability and a vision for the entire community — not simply a list of credentials.

Voters should scrutinize candidates’ records and demand concrete answers rather than assume that an impressive-looking résumé automatically makes someone the right choice for mayor.

— Irene Namer, Sunrise

Too much inspection information

I used to enjoy reading the Sun Sentinel over my coffee and breakfast, but daily headlines about restaurant cleanliness, or lack thereof, often ruin my appetite on the spot. I suppose disgusting articles generate online clicks, but graphic descriptions are unnecessary. They make me gag.

Why the obsession with critiquing restaurants? Any South Floridian knows that keeping any area pest-free is almost impossible, even in our homes, but especially in food service.

These gratuitous articles serve no purpose other than to shock people. Too much information!

— Paul Gereffi, Fort Lauderdale
