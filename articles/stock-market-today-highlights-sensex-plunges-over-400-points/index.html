Stock Market Today Highlights: Sensex plunges over 400 points, Nifty50 tumbles below 22,700 - The Times of India

TheTools · 22.10.2026 23:06 · source · permalink

UltraTech Cement was the top gainer among the stocks listed, rising 1.24% to Rs 10,945. Bajaj Finance followed, gaining 1.12% to Rs 935.50, while Bharti Airtel advanced 0.95% to Rs 1,910.30. Larsen & Toubro (L&T) climbed 0.77% to Rs 3,658.10, and ICICI Bank rose 0.56% to Rs 1,392.70. Kotak Mahindra Bank edged up 0.34% to Rs 438, while State Bank of India (SBI) gained 0.19% to Rs 965.75. Sun Pharma was marginally higher, up 0.01% at Rs 1,745.20.

On the losing side, Eternal was among the biggest decliners, falling 2.21% to Rs 304.85. Tech Mahindra dropped 2.11% to Rs 1,796.30, while Bharat Electronics (BEL) declined 1.91% to Rs 364.50. Power Grid Corporation fell 1.89% to Rs 247.15, and HDFC Bank slipped 1.83% to Rs 693.50. Trent shed 1.81% to Rs 2,767.90, followed by Infosys, which fell 1.73% to Rs 1,147.25. Titan declined 1.60% to Rs 4,310, while Reliance Industries lost 1.57% to Rs 1,188.30. ITC fell 1.47% to Rs 257.90, Hindustan Unilever dropped 1.39% to Rs 1,819.40, and Asian Paints declined 1.33% to Rs 2,293.20.

The recent market trend has been the outperformance of large-caps relative to mid-and small-caps. The broader market resilience in recent months has been more a function of liquidity than earnings.

Stock market today: Rupee offsets gains as Dalal Street struggles

Asian equities struggled to sustain gains in early trade on Thursday as uncertainty surrounding the Gulf and concerns over artificial intelligence offset optimism around technology earnings. MSCI’s broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan fell 0.2%, putting it on track for a fourth consecutive weekly decline. Japan’s Nikkei gave up earlier gains to trade 0.4% lower, while South Korean shares fell more than 1%. In the broader regional market snapshot provided, Hong Kong’s Hang Seng declined 2.09%, South Korea’s Kospi fell 1.92% and Shenzhen lost 1.41%. Shanghai was down 0.69%, while Australia’s ASX 200 slipped 0.47%. The mixed-to-negative regional cues come as investors assess geopolitical risks and their potential implications for oil prices and global market sentiment.

Oil prices rose on Thursday as fresh Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia prompted the United States to order some diplomats and family members to leave its missions in the kingdom. Brent crude futures gained $1.29, or 1.2%, to $110.11 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude rose 97 cents, or 1%, to $98.12. The latest escalation follows attacks on targets in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday, including a strike at a civilian airport in Dammam that killed one person and injured eight, and a drone attack on an oil field that killed two people. Meanwhile, Iran’s foreign minister said the country was prepared to defend itself, while President Masoud Pezeshkian questioned the possibility of dialogue amid US pressure. The developments have added to uncertainty over regional security and energy supplies.

Rupee strengthened 53 paise to close at 94.38 against the US dollar on Wednesday, supported by foreign currency inflows linked to Reliance Industries’ Jio Platforms IPO and intervention by the Reserve Bank of India.

The currency opened at 94.78, touched an intraday high of 94.35 and a low of 94.82 before settling at its strongest level in around six weeks.

However, weak domestic equities and elevated crude oil prices continued to pose risks to the currency’s gains. Anuj Choudhary, research analyst at Mirae Asset Sharekhan, said the rupee could maintain a positive bias amid broad dollar weakness and softer US Treasury yields. The USD-INR spot rate is expected to trade in a range of Rs 94.20 to Rs 94.80, he said.

Benchmark indices closed lower on Wednesday for a second consecutive session as selling in power, banking and financial stocks offset early gains. BSE Sensex declined 143.21 points, or 0.19%, to settle at 73,709.69, despite rising as much as 829.29 points to an intraday high of 74,682.19 before noon. Profit booking dragged the index more than 1,450 points from its high to a low of 73,229.45, before some late buying helped it recover. NSE Nifty50 fell 77.60 points, or 0.34%, to close at 22,811.35, after moving between 23,120.90 and 22,702.85. Midcap and smallcap indices also declined around 1.5% to 2.4%, reflecting broader risk aversion, according to Ajit Mishra, SVP–Research at Religare Broking.
