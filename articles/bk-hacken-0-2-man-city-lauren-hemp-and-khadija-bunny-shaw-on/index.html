BK Hacken 0-2 Man City: Lauren Hemp and Khadija 'Bunny' Shaw on target in Women's Champions League win

TheTools · 18.11.2026 21:11 · source · permalink

Match report as Lauren Hemp and Khadija 'Bunny' Shaw won the game for Man City at BK Hacken; the pair came on at half-time after a goalless first half; Mary Fowler saw a late penalty saved by Jennifer Falk; Man City remain unbeaten in the Women's Champions League this season

Senior Football Journalist

Wednesday 18 November 2026 19:59, UK

Lauren Hemp scored one and assisted another as Manchester City beat BK Hacken 2-0 in their penultimate Women's Champions League league phase game on Wednesday.

After a goalless first half in which the WSL champions struggled to break down a stubborn Hacken, Andree Jeglertz played his aces at half-time, bringing on Hemp and Khadija 'Bunny' Shaw.

The England international immediately brought a spark and directness Man City had been lacking in the first 45 minutes. Aoba Fujino was their best attacking threat to that point, but was one of the players - along with Beth Mead, who was largely ineffective - who made way at the break.

It was a fortuitous opener for Hemp, although made by her own tenacity. She wriggled through two defenders into the box, with Emma Ostlund's clearance hitting her on the shin before beating Jennifer Falk.

She hit the post too not long after as Man City looked for the comfort of a second, and soon found it as Hemp and Shaw linked up perfectly.

It was almost telepathic as the winger picked up the ball deep on the left, lifting up her head for Shaw, who immediately began her run. The Jamaica international muscled through three defenders and once she had the ball at her feet, it was inevitable that she would beat the goalkeeper into the far corner.

There was late drama too as Man City were awarded a contentious penalty. Shaw went down after taking an elbow to the face from Aivi Luik as the two jumped for a high ball. The striker looked dazed as she went off the pitch, with the referee sent to the monitor by the VAR officials.

She eventually pointed to the spot with Mary Fowler stepping up. However, Falk guessed the right way as the Man City forward went for the bottom left corner.

But it did little to alter the overall result as Man City won their second Women's Champions League game, and it sends them into third on nine points. However, there are seven games yet to play on matchday five, with Jeglertz's side facing Austria Wien at home in their final league phase game on December 16.

Hemp and Shaw prove their worth

Analysis from Sky Sports' Charlotte Marsh:

As if we needed a reminder, but it was clearer than ever just how influential Hemp and Shaw are for Man City after their half-time introduction.

Hemp was electric from the whistle. In the opening five minutes, she had already put in at least two crosses. Shaw made a key clearance at the other end too, proving that she can contribute across the pitch.

While Hemp's goal was created by talent and executed by luck, it was the second, as she pinged a ball forward for Shaw to finish, that truly showed why they are one of the most dangerous pairings in women's football. It was textbook from them both, but one that Hacken clearly had not studied.

Jeglertz would have been hoping to give his stars a rest, but they delivered when he needed them - ultimately what they are paid to do.

It was a shame Fujino had to be sacrificed. She was one of Man City's best players in the first half, but Beth Mead missed an opportunity to stake her claim for a regular starting spot. She offered little on her first Man City start since early October and will surely be disappointed.

But Hemp and Shaw reign supreme. It is a joy to watch them both play.

Jeglertz: We got more space in the second half

Man City boss Andree Jeglertz to the club's media: "A good game. We knew it would be a tough game to score against a very organised defending team. They have a great goalkeeper and we struggled in the first half. In the second half, they got a little bit tired, we got a little more space and we managed to get the goals.

"We try to get the best out of each player and put them in positions where they can really show what qualities they have.

"We have a great team together, we rotate players, and they all have a great impact on what we're doing. I think that will be very important in the future."
