Bronx community mourns slain 15-year-old student Javits Frimpong

TheTools · 30.10.2026 19:29 · source · permalink

CLAREMONT, The Bronx (WABC) -- A Bronx community is mourning the loss of a 15-year-old boy who was fatally shot while on his way to school Thursday morning, according to police.

Religious leaders, family members and community members gathered in the Bronx on Friday to honor 15-year-old Javits Frimpong. The vigil was held at the location where he collapsed after the shooting.

Those attending prayed for the Bronx and called on young people to stop the violence that continues to claim lives in the community. The event was organized by religious leaders and drew people whose own families have experienced similar tragedies.

Among those in attendance was the mother of Junior Guzman, who was killed in 2018 at age 15 in what was described as a case of mistaken identity. Fourteen Trinitarios gang members slashed him in the neck with machetes. He made his way to the steps of St. Barnabas Hospital, where he died.

Asked what advice she would offer Frimpong's family, Leandra Feliz said, "I'm only here to show support to the family because here we are again, another child of 15-years-old lost and I just want to be here to let the family know I'm here."

During the vigil, Frimpong's uncle received words of support from Feliz as the family continued to cope with the loss.

"Only god can give you strength to live one day at a time and learn to live with the pain through the loss of family."

A church leader also spoke about the toll youth violence has taken on the community.

"I don't want to be here doing this. I want to be invited to a birthday party. I want to be invited to a sweet 16 to pray. I want to be invited to open my church to a celebration, to a graduation, not to a funeral," the leader said.

Frimpong's father said he knows who was responsible for the shooting. He said his son had been bullied at school and that those involved had recently been suspended.

In a statement, the New York City Department of Education said, "Our hearts are with this young person's family and school community during this unimaginably difficult time. We are making counseling and other supports are available to students, families, and staff who need them, and supporting NYPD as they investigate."

Frimpong's father said he brought his son from Ghana to live with him about a year ago in hopes of a better life. The teen's mother is trying to travel to New York to see her son's body.

The shooting comes as city officials report that major crime in the Bronx declined by nearly 12% through Sept. 30 compared with the same period last year. Community members said those statistics do not reflect what they are experiencing.

"Our government and our government agencies are telling us, well, crime is going down. Crime is going down. But it's not what we're seeing. Younger and younger, our kids are dying," one speaker said.

Police have made no arrests and are searching for three teenage boys.

Mayor Mamdani posted to X on Saturday, saying that his mother will be granted a visa immediately so she can come to New York to say goodbye to her son.
