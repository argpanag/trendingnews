This Mod Adds Hardware Ray Tracing to Red Dead Redemption 2

TheTools · 22.11.2026 14:51 · source · permalink

Feature

Right now, it's still an experimental release, but what's here is promising.

While GTA 6 is in the headlines, it's another Rockstar game that has been the target of modding efforts on PC recently, with the new Reality Redemption 2 mod adding advanced lighting features to the classic cowboy game. Red Dead Redemption 2 looks stunning on PC even without the mod, with a lighting system that has aged remarkably well, but this new mod by developer BadassBaboon aims to take things to the next level with ray-traced sun shadows, ambient occlusion and global illumination.

The mod, which works only on the DirectX 12 version of the game at present, seems to run best on AMD graphics hardware, as we encountered some issues with the mod on Nvidia RTX cards and therefore did the bulk of our testing using a Radeon RX 9070 XT system.

The first major feature is ray-traced sun shadows, which operate alongside the game's existing shadowmaps and only support static geometry; dynamic objects are shadowed using the game's existing cascaded shadow map tech. Unfortunately, these come with a stiff performance penalty - around 19 percent at 4K in one scene and 29 percent in another - and only confer a slight visual improvement. Therefore, we'd recommend against having them enabled at present unless you have performance to spare.

The ray-traced ambient occlusion is easier to recommend, as it does a good job improving darkness beneath overhangs and around corners. Like the ray-traced sun shadows, the RTAO here works alongside the standard screen-space ambient occlusion (SSAO). However, some adjustment is require to avoid excessive darkening and unnatural halos, with results set at 0.5 to 0.7 producing a much more convincing result. Again, the performance cost is substantial, measured at around 44 percent at native 4K.

The most successful addition is ray-traced global illumination (RTGI), which manages indirect lighting that bounces colour across surfaces. This can be absolutely transformative for interior spaces, though unfortunately it can sometimes make outdoor scenes look overly bright or simply flat, particularly during dawn and dusk.

The setting here comes with up to three bounces by default, but changing this to two instead doesn't produce significantly different results in our testing. Again, the frame-rate cost is significant, practically halving performance at native 4K, even while the effect uses a quarter of the resolution.

While there's a sense that there's still more to come from this mod, if you're enjoying Red Dead Redemption 2 on PC, it's well worth an experimental install to see if you like the changes that it causes.

However, future versions of the mod may be easier to recommend to the general populace, if the effects can be revised to prevent visual degradation and to lessen their currently massive performance impact - which we counted at roughly 65 percent with all three effects active. However, running the game using FSR instead of native 4K can recover a lot of the lost performance - though picking and choosing individual effects is the best shout compared to enabling everything. Stability is another area with potential for improvement, as our initial testing on the RTX 4060 provoked repeated graphics errors and out-of-memory crashes, despite VRAM allocations not being exceeded.

There's certainly still a good case to be made for hardware ray tracing in Red Dead Redemption 2, given how transformative these upgrades were in GTA 5 Enhanced. Ray-traced reflections and a Vulkan version are among additions the developer has discussed, and we'd hope to see continuing improvements going forward. As it stands, this is more of a curiosity than a go-to mod install, but we've seen many times over the past year that development can be swift, and BadassBaboon's efforts ought to be celebrated even at this early stage.
