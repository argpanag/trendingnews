Grand Theft Auto 6 will feature significantly narrower roads than the previous installment.

TheTools · 20.10.2026 23:02 · source · permalink

During journalist Jason Schreier's recent visit to Rockstar Games' main Scottish studio, some interesting technical details about the development of Grand Theft Auto 6 emerged. Art director Aaron Garbut confirmed that the roads in the new game are significantly narrower compared to the previous installment in the series. In Grand Theft Auto 5, the streets of the fictional Los Angeles were deliberately designed wide, allowing players to maneuver freely at high speeds, avoid occasional civilian vehicles, and avoid the reality of metropolitan traffic. In the new project, the developers focused on narrow roads and heavy traffic from the very beginning to accurately convey the traffic density of the southern metropolis and avoid any unnecessary resemblance to arcade racing.

The decision to significantly narrow the roads had serious consequences for the entire level design structure and the open-world development process. Narrower streets required significantly more buildings, detailed architectural elements, and dense road infrastructure to maintain the realistic proportions of the virtual city. According to development management, creating such a dense environment has significantly increased the workload for the studio's artists, some of whom have been working on the virtual map since 2015. As a result, car chases and regular city driving will become much more complex, dynamic, and dangerous, as navigating pedestrians and dense traffic at high speeds will now prove a formidable task.

In addition to the road network, the combat mechanics and equipment selection interface have undergone significant overhauls. Lead developer Rob Nelson stated that the team initially aimed to bring the inventory closer to Western realism. Red Dead Redemption 2, where the character can't carry an infinite arsenal and is forced to pre-select a limited set of weapons. The developers spent several years experimenting with completely abandoning the traditional selector, benchmarking every possible panel, grid, and radial list. The developers were seriously concerned that empty slots on the screen would constantly remind the player of their loadout limitations, but after numerous unsuccessful interface iterations, the studio decided to return to the tried-and-true classic weapon selection wheel.
