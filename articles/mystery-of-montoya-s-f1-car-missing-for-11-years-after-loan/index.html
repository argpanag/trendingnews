Mystery of Montoya’s F1 car missing for 11 years after loan

TheTools · 07.10.2026 14:28 · source · permalink

Former garage director denies he took £750,000 Williams racer after being sued in £1m legal fight

A British Formula One team are locked in a £1m High Court fight over one of their Grand Prix cars, which they said vanished after they lent it out to the owner of a garage.

The £750,000 racing car was manufactured by Williams and used by Ralf Schumacher and Juan Pablo Montoya, the team’s 2001 World Championship drivers.

Afterwards, it became part of the manufacturer’s collection of “heritage cars”.

The team say the car disappeared after it was lent to Shropshire-based Autotech Performance in 2015 to become a showpiece at the company’s garage in Telford.

When Williams asked for the car to be returned in 2021, they said they received no response and that Autotech had since gone into liquidation and been dissolved.

The racing company is now suing Peter Thorpe, the former Autotech director and shareholder, for return of the car, or a payment of more than £1m.

Lawyers for Williams claim that Mr Thorpe has had the car removed from the garage, having wrongly claimed to his company’s liquidators that he was the owner of it personally.

Mr Thorpe, 58, is fighting the claim, insisting in his defence that he never told anyone that he owned the car, “because he knew this to be untrue”.

He claims that he does not know where the car is and has a poor memory after suffering a stroke. Mr Thorpe adds that he cannot remember speaking to liquidators and that the car was still on site the last time he went to the garage in the summer of 2021 before Autotech was dissolved.

Setting out the claim in case papers, Richard Clegg, the barrister for Williams, said: “The car is part of the claimant’s unique collection of its own heritage cars, the use or sale of which it strictly controls.

“In 2015, the claimant agreed to loan the car to Autotech on the terms of a document entitled ‘Historic Car Loan Agreement’.”

Mr Clegg said Autotech had been allowed to keep the car until October 2021 when Williams wrote to the company, seeking to terminate the loan, but received no reply.

He said Mr Thorpe had “wrongfully claimed” to a valuer involved in Autotech’s liquidation that he was the rightful owner of the car, having bought it himself.

“On a date unknown after the commencement of Autotech’s voluntary liquidation, or thereabouts, the car was removed from the premises,” he continued.

“It is to be inferred that it was the defendant who removed the car from the premises, or who arranged for its removal therefrom, and the defendant who thereupon took possession and control of it.”

He said Williams was seeking an order for return of the car, plus hire charges, or alternatively more than £1m if it was not given back.

‘Cognitive impairment’

Duncan MacPherson, for Mr Thorpe, said the businessman did not know where the car was and denies claiming to own it, since he cannot remember speaking to the valuer.

“He does not know who, in fact, owned the car then or now. The defendant suffered a stroke on Oct 7 2018 that caused him cognitive impairment,” he said.

“The defendant admits that the car was at the premises on Aug 8 2021, but does not admit whether it remained there until Dec 2021, because this lies outside his knowledge.”

Mr MacPherson said Mr Thorpe “believes that he has not told anyone that he owned the car, because he knew this to be untrue” and that the car could have been removed at some point after he last attended the garage and before the liquidation.

He said Williams was not entitled to any order against Mr Thorpe, while also disputing the alleged £750,000 value, claiming that the vehicle was a show car without an engine.

Williams’ claim against Mr Thorpe was filed in the High Court in London, but the case has not gone before a judge for a full trial.

A Williams spokesman said: “As the proceedings are active, we will make no further comment but have full faith in the legal system to resolve this matter.”
