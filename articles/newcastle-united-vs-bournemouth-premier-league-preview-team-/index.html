    Bournemouth manager Marco Rose, speaking to TNT Sports, on conceding late again: "Two time already in the last two games but it is what it is. But I'm really happy with the confidence the lads brought on the pitch today. We were so good in the first half and could've scored a third goal. More or less, from nothing, we conceded.     "It's good that we are in situations to be able to winning games. That's most important. We have to talk about the negatives, get better and try to finish the games. And if we can't finish the games, we need to defend our goal like it's the last game of our lives".

    On the positives: "The way we pressed in the first half was really impressive. They had no real solution to our pressing and how we got into the opponent's half. But in the end, we need to talk about the things we have to improve. The season is very young and now we get less time to train."

    On playing in Europe: "We have to stick together. I know how demanding it is for a club not used to having so many competitions. It is going to get really tough for us. We need positive results. We have to work for it. We should start on Tuesday in the Carabao Cup [against Lincoln City] and then we continue with the league and then we go to San Sebastian."

  3. 'We don't know what De Zerbi's Spurs team are about'published at 14:48 BST
    14:48 BST

    Nottingham Forest v Tottenham (15:00 BST)

    Chris Sutton
    BBC football expert

    Nottingham Forest are waiting for their first win under Oliver Glasner but I felt they were unlucky to lose against Leeds and also to only draw at Liverpool.

    I have been very disappointed with Roberto de Zerbi's Tottenham so far, however, and not just because of their poor results.

    When he came in at Brighton, De Zerbi built an amazing team in a short space of time and they were great to watch even if they made a few mistakes along the way.

    In contrast, we still don't know what his Spurs team are about, even though they have spent an absolute fortune on players.

    I think that's on De Zerbi because, although new players take time to gel, his side have put in two really insipid performances so far.

    He has put that down to the team not being fully fit and blamed their pre-season schedule, but they should still be doing much better than they are - with no points and no goals so far.

    Other clubs, with new managers as well as lots of new players, look much more organised so it is not an excuse.

    Still, Spurs have surely got to improve soon, which is why I have got them down to pick up a first point here.

    Sutton's prediction: 1-1

    Man City v Coventry (15:00 BST)

    Chris Sutton
    BBC football expert

    This is a tale of two Citys - one of whom has made the best of starts to the season, while the other has had the worst.

    Manchester City wiped the floor with Crystal Palace last week but it was their comeback to beat Bournemouth at the Etihad which felt bigger, because it was the beginning of a new era under Enzo Maresca.

    In contrast, Coventry City have had a terrible time. Not their 3-0 opening defeat at Arsenal, because they can forget about that, but they must feel so deflated after losing 1-0 at home to another promoted side in Hull City last time out.

    There were some doubts about Manchester City after the way they lost the Community Shield against Arsenal, but they look a different proposition already.

    Maresca has made more signings in the past few days, including Enzo Fernandez from his old club Chelsea for £125m. I've heard people question whether Fernandez is worth the big money that City paid, but Maresca knows him well and I see him as a great addition to their midfield.

    I said before the Bournemouth game I didn't think they could win the title without Rodri, but they have rebuilt their midfield now with Fernandez and Ayyoub Bouaddi joining Elliot Anderson as new signings, and they have got real strength in there now.

    I like Erling Haaland's new haircut too. It suits him far more than a ponytail. He looks a bit rougher and tougher and I don't think Coventry will keep him out.

FULL-TIMEpublished at 14:26 BST
14:26 BST

Newcastle 2-2 Bournemouth

Probably the right result. Newcastle's unbeaten start to the season continues after Bournemouth concede late for the third straight game.

Jacob Ramsey's first goal in the Premier League this season rescued a point for Newcastle

Newcastle fought back from 2-0 down to earn a hard-fought Premier League point at home to Bournemouth thanks to Jacob Ramsey's late equaliser.

The Cherries had taken an early lead when Alex Scott's deflected shot looped into the box and Marcus Tavernier stole a couple of yards on Amar Dedic at the back post and poked the ball under Lukas Hornicek.

Newcastle fans booed the officials after the goal - aggrieved by what they thought was a foul in an earlier phase of play after some grappling between Adrien Truffert and Anthony Elanga.

Bournemouth added a second when Justin Kluivert's fizzing long-range effort hit the post, bounced off the back of Hornicek and dropped to Alex Scott.

The England midfielder's effort from a tight angle spooned off Malick Thiaw beyond Hornicek and was chalked up as an own goal.

Newcastle pulled one back moments later when Harvey Barnes cut inside from the left and curled a stunning right-footed effort into the top corner.

A breathless and entertaining first half was followed by a second period which became more of an arm wrestle.

Newcastle gave a 63rd-minute debut to Belgium forward Matias Fernandez-Pardo, signed from Lille for £51m before the transfer window shut, in a bid to provide a bit more impetus.

Bournemouth looked to have shown showed the necessary grit to see out the remainder of the game only for Ramsey to strike with two time minutes of normal time remaining.

A Newcastle attacking move culminated in Thiaw, with his back to goal, knocking the ball down in the box and Ramsey seizing on it to score with a low cushioned finish into the corner of the net.

Analysis: Jaissle shows pragmatic side

This was the first meeting between the clubs since 1992 in which Eddie Howe has not been in either dugout - a total of 24 fixtures.

On that occasion 34 years ago the late Kevin Keegan was Magpies boss while Tony Pulis was in charge of the Cherries.

The Geordie nation will have relished some of the Keeganian attacking swagger shown by Newcastle's players so far under new boss Matthias Jaissle.

But aspects of their defensive play in this game, especially in the first half, were a tad reminiscent of the leaky Newcastle 'entertainers' team from the Nineties.

Bournemouth cut a rather disorganised home side apart with ease on occasions, with black-and-white striped shirts left chasing shadows as passes were threaded between the lines.

Newcastle were especially hesitant in possession at the back as Sven Botman carried the ball out of defence with the appearance of a man who had forgotten where he had parked his car.

Jaissle to his credit managed to get Newcastle a bit more organised after the break and they regained some control in midfield to stifle Bournemouth's creativity.

A bit more pragmatism allowed them to remain in the contest and provided the platform for Ramsey to equalise.

Jaissle looks like a main still finding his feet at St James' Park but a willingness to adapt when things are not going his way, and grind out a result, should serve him well.
