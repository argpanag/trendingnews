Man Utd being relegated would be the biggest shock in history, but it won't happen, says Paul Merson

TheTools · 27.11.2026 18:01 · source · permalink

Manchester United take on Brentford on Saturday with Mauricio Pochettino looking for his first win to take them out of the relegation zone; Arsenal then take on Man City, live on Sky Sports this Sunday, the reigning champions are aiming to go nine points clear of Enzo Maresca's side

Football Expert & Columnist @PaulMerse

Friday 27 November 2026 09:20, UK

In his latest Sky Sports column, Paul Merson previews a big weekend for Manchester United, before Arsenal take on Manchester City in a huge game live this Sunday.

Mauricio Pochettino is looking for his first win as United boss as he bids to take them out of the relegation zone. However, Brentford visit Old Trafford on the back of their recent win over Everton, which took them into the top six.

Then on Sunday, Arsenal host title rivals Man City. Enzo Maresca's side are already six points behind the reigning champions and have won just two out of their last seven matches in all competitions.

Read below for the Magic Man's thoughts ahead of a huge weekend in the Premier League...

'Brentford game is massive for Man Utd'

Manchester United are in trouble. 100 per cent. Of course they are. But I don't see them getting relegated, absolutely not.

I would be shocked and amazed, it would be the biggest shock in the history of anything. People will go: Mike Tyson got beat by Buster Douglas. But that was one punch. This is over 38 games.

Brentford is a massive football match for them. If they lost that game, I don't see them beating Newcastle if Matthias Jaissle's side get anywhere near the level they played against Arsenal in the second half.

Then by the time you're playing Coventry, you're more than a win away from getting out of trouble. As soon as that starts happening, that's when it starts getting worrying - when you need two weeks to get out of the bottom three, or four or five points away.

The next three weeks are massive for United. If they beat Brentford this week, that will settle them down.

But I look at the teams down there right now. Crystal Palace won't go to Old Trafford and win. Sunderland and Nottingham Forest won't. Fulham and Coventry won't. Hull won't. So that means to me that Man Utd are better than them.

That's why I don't see them getting relegated. When you look at it like that, there are a lot of worse teams than them.

Man Utd have bought too many 'four or nines' - that's what I call them. They're either 4/10 or 9/10. They're not in between. You can't do that at Man Utd. You have to be between a seven or an eight on a consistent basis. You can't be four one week and nine the next, it doesn't work.

The pace of the game is controlled by the other team, it's never controlled by Manchester United.

They have got no defenders. They've gone it horribly wrong, trying to build from front to back. But it's more than that, it's spine of the team.

They need a centre forward. They don't have a dictating midfield player who comes short and controls the game. The only way United can do that is if they sacrifice Fernandes going forward and playing him deeper, like a quarterback - and then hope you've got enough going forwards elsewhere.

I don't see anyone else in that United team who is capable of coming deep and playing the ball forward. Playing that pass on a consistent basis, making the right decisions.

'Man City have lost their fear factor'

Man City have got to go out and play for the fans, they're the ones who are paying their money. They're getting paid £300,000-a-week, the fans are the ones paying to watch them.

Man City are getting a little bit found out. I have always said, even when they were playing at their best, they give you chances. And you have to make those chances count.

It used to be: go to the Etihad, get your stomach's tickled, get out with a 3-0 defeat that you're more than happy with that, and go back on the coach - because they could win games by six or seven.

Then all of a sudden, they started winning games but they were also open at the same time. And now, teams don't fear them.

Teams know they will get chances if they have a go at them - and enough chances to win football matches, not just one or two chances.

If you are brave enough - like Fulham were by getting players in the opposition box - you can beat Man City.

This is the problem for them: they're too open and there's not that fear factor anymore. And I also don't think Erling Haaland gets the service he deserves.

You would like to think that City's players aren't being affected by the charges hanging over them, because no one knows what is going to happen. So you have to play until you know what's going to happen.

It's like life: no one's going to tell you what's going to happen tomorrow, so live today.

'If Arsenal beat Man City, the league could be over soon'

If Arsenal win this game, the league is finished. That would be nine points clear and this league could be over very, very quickly.

Arsenal were dominant in the first half against Newcastle and if it was a boxing match, then Newcastle would have thrown the towel in.

They came out the second half a bit lackadaisical and Newcastle were outstanding by playing on the front foot. But then Arsenal switched and decided they weren't going to lose.

And then they scored one of the best goals of the season. If the finish from Noni Madueke was good, it would be the goal of the season. But when you see the finish, it's not great and the goalkeeper should save it. If he whips it into the far corner, it's goal of the season.

I think we saw enough with Brighton last weekend against Hull. That's one of those games where you go: if you can't win it, don't lose it.

You feel sorry for Brighton, because Konstantinos Tzolakis was unreal. He should be playing for the New York Rangers this weekend. He was like an ice hockey goalkeeper.

But if you're experienced team and you've been in that situation before, a draw is just a bad day at the office but we've played well, the goalkeeper's had a blinder and you don't lose a football match away from home. But they lost.
