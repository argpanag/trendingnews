‘Criminal’ Teaser: Charlie Hunnam, Adria Arjona, Luke Evans & Emilia Clarke Lead Prime Video’s Star-Studded Crime Saga

TheTools · 05.11.2026 15:00 · source · permalink

Ed Brubaker and Sean Phillips’ acclaimed crime comics come to television in an eight-episode series divided into two interconnected stories.

Prime Video has assembled an impressive cast for “Criminal,” its long-awaited adaptation of Ed Brubaker and Sean Phillips’ acclaimed, multi-Eisner Award-winning graphic novel series. Led by Charlie Hunnam, Adria Arjona, Luke Evans, and Emilia Clarke, the sprawling crime drama follows generations of thieves, outlaws, and families bound together by old betrayals and unfinished business. The first teaser offers a look at the series, which premieres in January.

Rather than center on a single criminal enterprise, “Criminal” explores an interconnected underworld where the consequences of one generation’s mistakes tend to fall on the next. The eight-episode season is divided into two four-episode stories, adapting “Coward” and “Lawless,” among the most celebrated chapters of Brubaker and Phillips’ original comics.

The story begins with Leo Patterson (Hunnam), a careful, calculating thief trying to escape the life he inherited, and his childhood friend Ricky Lawless (Gus Halper), who is determined to follow in the footsteps of his notorious father. Their complicated history takes another turn when Ricky’s older brother Tracy (Evans) returns from the Army, forcing the family to confront a past it has spent years trying to bury.

Arjona plays Greta, a sharp-tongued car thief with ties to Leo’s past, while Clarke portrays Mallory, Ricky’s girlfriend, who fears how far he might go to survive. The extensive supporting cast includes Richard Jenkins, John Hawkes, Kadeem Hardison, Logan Browning, Michael Mando, Garrett Hedlund, and Patrick J. Adams.

First published in 2006, “Criminal” established Brubaker and Phillips as one of contemporary comics’ defining creative partnerships. Drawing on hardboiled crime fiction and classic noir, the series is less concerned with elaborate criminal schemes than with the damaged people carrying them out, and the loyalties, resentments, and bad decisions that keep pulling them back into trouble. That approach should lend itself particularly well to television’s longer-form storytelling.

Brubaker co-created the adaptation with crime novelist Jordan Harper, while filmmakers Anna Boden and Ryan Fleck (“Mississippi Grind,” “Captain Marvel”) direct the first four episodes. Dee Rees (“Mudbound”) is also an executive producer, alongside Phillips and others.
