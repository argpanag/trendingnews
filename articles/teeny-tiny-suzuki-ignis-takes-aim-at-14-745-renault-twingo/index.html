Teeny tiny Suzuki Ignis takes aim at £14,745 Renault Twingo

Suzuki’s new electric city car promises easy driving and clever use of space, but can it compete with rivals on price?

Suzuki is the latest car maker to go big on small electric cars, with the return of a familiar name to its line-up. The all-new Suzuki Ignis has been revealed ahead of sales starting in Europe in the summer of 2027, joining a growing number of small electric cars designed to tempt people out of their petrol-powered models.

There’s no word on pricing yet, but Suzuki will have its work cut out to beat the new Renault Twingo, which has gone on sale in the UK from just £14,745.

Suzuki has drawn on its vast experience of making tiny cars for the Japanese market, known as kei cars, in developing the new Ignis. And there’s more than a hint of the much-loved Honda e about the newcomer, especially with its friendly face, black roof and wheels pushed out towards the corners of the car.

At just 3,570mm long and 1,565mm wide, the Ignis is even smaller than the Honda e, while its height of 1,635mm should help make the most of the space inside. There’s room for four people and a tiny turning circle of just nine metres, which should come in handy on tight city streets and in supermarket car parks.

There’s a relatively small 26kWh battery tucked away underneath the car, using lithium iron phosphate technology that Suzuki says has been chosen for its safety and long life. That’s coupled with a new electric motor producing 90bhp and driving two wheels, although Suzuki hasn’t yet revealed how far the Ignis will go on a full charge or how quickly the battery can be topped up.

The Ignis has been developed from the ground up as an electric car, with Suzuki making the most of the space freed up by doing away with a petrol engine. Inside, the dashboard curves around into the doors, with a wide display screen and a handy storage tray with a design inspired by Japanese ceramics.

Although the Ignis has been designed with city driving in mind, Suzuki says it has spent plenty of time testing the car on winding roads across Europe, tweaking the way it delivers its power and how the suspension works to suit local roads.

Suzuki president Toshihiro Suzuki said: “Leveraging packaging expertise refined through the development of Japan’s mini vehicles, the all-new Ignis achieves both a lightweight, compact body that offers excellent energy efficiency and easy manoeuvrability, and a spacious cabin.”

The new Ignis will join the larger e Vitara in Suzuki’s electric car line-up, although the company’s timing means it’ll arrive in an increasingly competitive part of the electric car market.

With the likes of the Renault Twingo and its bigger brother, the Renault 5, already setting the benchmark for affordable small electric cars, much of the focus between now and next summer will be on just how affordable Suzuki can make its smallest electric car.
