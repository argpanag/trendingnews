Why LOUD Are The Most Incredible Story Of VALORANT Champions Shanghai - Hotspawn

TheTools · 14.10.2026 15:27 · source · permalink

Zahk

Share:

Zahk

Share:

2026 has belonged to VALORANT’s youngest talent. Nongshim RedForce set the mark for youngest international champions at Masters Santiago, only for Leviatán to break it months later at Masters London with four players under 20. Now LOUD are carrying that wave into VALORANT Champions Shanghai.

A roster rebuilt midseason, playing its first international together, has reached the upper final and locked in a top three finish. Team Vitality stand between them and a grand final on October 16 but their journey here has already been an incredible achievement.

LOUD had to fight for their ticket, finishing second at VCT Americas Stage 2 after beating NRG 3-2 in the lower final and then losing a five-map final to 100 Thieves. In Shanghai they opened with a 2-0 sweep of EDward Gaming, taking Lotus 13-8 and Summit 13-5 in front of a home crowd.

Their only loss came in the winners’ match against Vitality, a 2-0 defeat made up of a 13-7 Ascent loss and a 19-17 Summit marathon. LOUD clawed back from 9-3 down to force overtime, and the map equalled the record for the longest ever at a VCT global event, a mark that had stood since DRX beat FPX in Istanbul in 2022. They did not spiral or breakdown to the pressure: LOUD beat Global Esports 2-0 in the Group B decider, winning Split 13-8 after trailing 8-5 and Sunset 13-9 after racing to a 7-0 lead.

The playoffs have been even better. Against Paper Rex, LOUD trailed 8-4 at half on Split and 9-3 on Sunset, yet won 13-9 and then 15-13 in overtime to sweep. Next came reigning champions NRG. LOUD took Haven 13-9, dropped Sunset 13-10, then led 11-1 at the half on Summit and closed it 13-3, sending the holders into a lower bracket they would not escape.

Lukxo finished that series with 63 kills, 33 deaths and 9 assists, a +30 differential. IGL erde added a 50/30/23 line and three straight clutches on Summit: two 1v2s followed by a 1v4, an incredible performance for an 18 year old at his first ever VCT international.

The individual brilliance has been everywhere. Tkzin announced himself with three aces in his Champions debut against EDward Gaming, a record, finishing that series on 47 kills, 24 deaths and 7 assists at 321 ACS. Lukxo has been the entry engine, leading first kills in Americas and in Shanghai, and he piled up 52 kills against Paper Rex. “I’ll never stop being aggressive,” he mentioned in an interview with Hotspawn, crediting his teammates’ utility for setting him up.

That support comes from DaviH and Darker. DaviH went 26/19/24 with 85 percent KAST against EDG, showing his value beyond the frag column. Darker had a rough series against Vitality, finishing 19/49/26, with only 10 kills on map 2 but bounced back with an MVP performance of 232 ACS on Sunset against Global Esports. Utility from those two sets up the other three, and the spacing is excellent, so trades come even when tkzin goes down first. NRG’s IGL Ethan Arnold noted after their series that LOUD’s team fights and trades were better than most.

LOUD have also stood apart as one of the few squads running Chamber and Sage while most of Americas leaned on double duelist, a comp that handles the aggressive picks teams keep bringing. Their map pool has also looked excellent: despite banging Split 9 times so far in VCT Americas Stage 2, they have looked incredible on it at Shanghai, an ace up their sleeve.

Romanilly told Hotspawn that a strong foundation on every map lets LOUD adapt without reinventing themselves each week, and that at Champions “you need to understand the momentum of the games.” He named inexperience as the main downside of such a young roster, but said their drive to fix problems more than makes up for it.

The players say much the same. Lukxo described the group as almost a family, eating together daily, and put LOUD’s comebacks down to confidence after starting maps on the bad side. This is a team that has lived through constant roster change, and it now plays with complete belief in each other.

Everything now comes down to Friday’s upper final. Vitality have not stood still, beating Nongshim RedForce 2-1 (including a 14-12 overtime win on the decider Haven) and sweeping G2 2-0. Lukxo said the main lesson from the group loss was adaptability, pointing to Vitality’s slow pace and the wider EMEA style, while Romanilly admitted he wanted the rematch to set the record straight

If LOUD adapt, a VALORANT Champions 2026 grand final awaits the young squad, with symbolic weight to boot. LOUD lifted the trophy at Champions 2022 in Istanbul, and no organisation has won it twice yet. This time it would come with an entirely different roster, a feat unprecedented so far. The stars have aligned for this squad so far, and the trophy has never looked so close.
