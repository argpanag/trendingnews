This year, two major directors made long-awaited returns to the genre they are most closely associated with: sci-fi. First out of the gate was Steven Spielberg, who released Disclosure Day — his first new sci-fi film since 2018. The movie grossed around $250 million worldwide against a reported budget of $130 million and received mostly positive reviews.

Ridley Scott, however, wasn't as fortunate. Scott's sci-fi comeback vehicle, The Dog Stars, grossed just $40 million worldwide against a reported budget of $100 million. The movie also received lukewarm reviews. Ahead of its release, Scott expressed interest in returning to the classic sci-fi franchise that announced his arrival on the scene several decades ago.

The series began in 1979 with an instant classic that's currently streaming on Netflix in the United States. Scott last directed a film in the franchise in 2017. He has since been involved in a sequel and a spin-off television series as an executive producer. We're talking, of course, about the iconic Alien series​​​​​​​, which has had quite an uneven journey over the decades. At its peak, it was viewed as one of the most influential film franchises in Hollywood history. At its low point, it inspired crossover cash grabs.​​​​​​​

Scott returned to the series with the divisive 2012 release Prometheus, which generated more than $400 million worldwide against a reported budget of $140 million. He followed it up with Alien: Covenant, which underperformed with around $240 million worldwide against a reported budget of $100 million. The franchise lay dormant for several years before being successfully revived by Fede Álvarez's Alien: Romulus and Noah Hawley's FX series Alien: Earth.​​​​​​​

Here's How Long You Have Left to Watch the Sci-Fi Classic on Netflix

But we're talking about Scott's original Alien, which emerged as a sensation nearly five decades ago. The movie follows the crew of a spaceship that discovers a mysterious alien vessel and its terrifying inhabitant. The movie grossed nearly $200 million worldwide against a reported budget of between $8 million and $15 million.

It's now considered a cornerstone of the sci-fi genre and has influenced numerous films, shows, video games, and other works over the years. The movie holds a "Certified Fresh" 93% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "A modern classic, Alien blends science fiction, horror and bleak poetry into a seamless whole."
