How vulnerable is Minister for Health Jennifer Carroll MacNeill?

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:38 · source · permalink

Minister for Health Jennifer Carroll MacNeill has come under increasing pressure this week following the HSE board’s sacking of chief Anne O’Connor on Monday.

The Dún Laoghaire TD is clearly experiencing heavy turbulence – but can she keep going, and what are the factors that will influence this?

Confidence of colleagues

First order of business is to make sure your own side is behind you. Nobody at Cabinet, least of all the Taoiseach or the Tánaiste, is agitating against her publicly.

Similarly, there’s no chorus of unrest from the Government backbenchers. Privately, however, ministerial colleagues acknowledge she is in the eye of a storm and needs to avoid mistakes.

With matters finely balanced, anything that damages her narrative of events could change things rapidly. Ministers will look on with the usual grim fascination, knowing they’re all vulnerable to damaging controversy erupting at any time.

However, in a volatile situation where her judgment and actions are under the microscope, that is not a free pass. And it is contingent on events.

Internal Fine Gael politics

The wits in Leinster House haven’t been able to stop themselves from observing that Fine Gael leader Simon Harris might not be totally devastated at political damage incurred by Carroll MacNeill, who is seen as his most likely rival in any leadership contest.

But it’s not straightforward for Harris either. His first order of business has to be Government stability, and obviously this is a crisis whose ramifications extend beyond the Department of Health.

As Fine Gael leader, he also has to project the ability to protect his Ministers – for as long as their careers are politically tenable. Carroll MacNeill is certainly besieged, but is not out of the fight. For as long as that is the case, Harris has to back her.

Opposition attacks

The Opposition has trenchantly sought to put Carroll MacNeill at the centre of a narrative that has her engineering O’Connor’s departure – something she denies.

This is, of course, very damaging even as a charge, no matter if it’s disputed. But the Opposition will equally know that what they believe (even if it is believed by a lot of people) is different from what they can prove.

Anything that substantiates their allegations would really move the dial.

Separately, it may be that an Opposition party pushes the button on a motion of no confidence in the Minister.

Ninety per cent of the time, these signal the beginning of the end of a political crisis as the Government circles the wagons, whips the votes and wins the day.

Aontú leader Peadar Tóibín is already making noises about this, but parties might be well advised to keep this in reserve for now – the facts on the ground don’t suggest they would be successful as it stands.

And being too eager could cause the political fever to peak and then break.

But in the right circumstances, although vanishingly rare, a no-confidence motion can be fatal.

Nobody knows this more than Harris: a looming motion over his performance as minister for health, which it looked like the government could lose, prompted Leo Varadkar to go to the country in 2020.

Other key players

Carroll MacNeill has aligned herself very closely to the HSE board, even as wider political support for that board evaporated in the past 24 hours. If the board, or its chair, resigns, then she will be under more pressure for backing a board that didn’t survive the controversy.

Harris on Thursday seemed to move to insulate her somewhat from that. Every Minister had a legitimate expectation that a board under their remit would follow appropriate processes, he said.

He added that when a board made a decision, a Minister had every right to presume it had followed the correct processes.

He also said he had full confidence in her, and remarked she had “excellent judgment” and his full support, including in how she had backed the board so far.

If and when the board breaks its silence – and it has confirmed it will appear before an Oireachtas committee next week – every syllable members speak will be pored over for explicit or implicit criticism of the Minister, and tested for consistency with her story.

If cracks appear in her version arising from what the board has to say, that could further damage her.

Equally, O’Connor has shown that even defenestrated, she has tremendous power to drive this story with telling interventions.

No single person in this cast of characters is as influential as O’Connor at the moment – quite the turnaround from the position she found herself in last Saturday.

Time is a factor

There has always been the chance that a non-political process could interrupt the stride of full-blown crisis in Leinster House.

This was most likely to be the courts, which has a bit of a chilling effect (most of the time) on political tempers. Now it seems that mediation is on the cards, if O’Connor is minded to take it up.

A badly damaged politician knows time is a factor. They can try to stay alive in the hope the facts on the ground change, that people forget, get distracted or that tempers cool. Micheál Martin did this after the Jim Gavin fiasco last year.

Carroll MacNeill can’t be accused of going into hiding, having spent hours at committees this week. But the Opposition still has plenty of questions for her. Will mediation or a court case give cover to allow her to draw breath?

Conversely, the longer the controversy runs without a break, the harder and harder it gets to keep your head above water.
