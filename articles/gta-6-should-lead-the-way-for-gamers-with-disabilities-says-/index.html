'GTA 6 should lead the way for gamers with disabilities,' says campaigner

TheTools · 27.11.2026 07:34 · source · permalink

A campaigner for video games to be made more accessible for players with disabilities is hoping that Grand Theft Auto 6 can set the standard.

GTA 6 launched to positive reviews - it has been 13 years since Rockstar Games released the last edition of the series.

Mathew Allcock, 33, from Lakenheath, Suffolk, is a video game content creator and is visually impaired.

He called on Rockstar, as one of the world's largest gaming developers, to set an example to other studios. "It's vital for studios of this size to at least show growth and development in terms of accessibility because otherwise it's just setting a bad example," he said.

Allcock, who describes himself as an "accessibility advocate", urged game developers to include things like "large text, subtitling, audio navigation, menu narration and remappable controls".

He said the first thing he would do when playing the new game on Thursday would be to go through the settings and options available for players.

"I want to help gamers with disabilities make an educated purchase, give them the correct information they need to know [as to] whether they'll be able to play the game," Allcock said.

Marijn Rongen from the website Can I Play That?, which provides accessibility information to gamers, agreed.

"We've seen it before with The Last of Us 2, which had a staggering amount of accessibility features, and promoted them as well," he said.

"Following that game, we've seen others, especially AAA productions, implement similar features. The high contrast mode is a great example of this."

Rongen said he believed "no prominent accessibility outlets and content creators have gotten the opportunity" to have an early hands-on experience with the game.

In a 2023 survey of gamers with disabilities carried out by the rights and support charity Scope, two thirds of respondents said they faced barriers or issues related to gaming.

The most-used assistive technologies were options to adjust sound settings or the sensitivity of controllers.

Allcock said if the game's developers had not included accessibility settings, it could look like "they're not bothered about their disabled players and it leads to other studios potentially further down the line going, 'Well, GTA didn't do it, so we don't need to'."

Rockstar Games has been contacted for comment.
