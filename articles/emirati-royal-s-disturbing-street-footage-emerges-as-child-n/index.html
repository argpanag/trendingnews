FBI Director Kash Patel has mocked a false report claiming he had arrived in Russia for meetings with the FSB, posting “From Russia, with Love” on X. US broadcaster MS NOW initially reported that Patel had landed in Russia, but the FBI released footage showing him attending a law enforcement event at Liberty University in Virginia on October 14. The broadcaster later acknowledged its mistake. The controversy follows earlier reports about Patel's proposed visit to Moscow and St Petersburg, which had raised national security concerns among US lawmakers. Meanwhile, Ukraine has faced separate disruptions to its US-made Starshield satellite communications system, affecting drone operations against Russian forces. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has also raised concerns over intelligence-sharing as tensions grow over Washington's approach to Moscow. There is no established link between Patel's proposed Russia visit and the satellite restrictions.

Donald Trump’s agreement to bring more Russian diesel onto global markets is facing doubts over whether it can deliver the sharp fuel-price relief he promised, while also encountering bipartisan resistance in Washington. The Washington Post reported that diesel futures barely moved on Monday, with much of the initial decline following Trump’s announcement already erased. The promised Russian volumes amount to roughly 100,000 barrels per day this month and 125,000 in November, compared with Russia’s roughly 800,000 barrels-per-day exports last year. US retail diesel is averaging around $6.27 a gallon, about 71% higher than a year ago. Meanwhile, senators from both parties argue that allowing Russian diesel imports conflicts with a 2022 law restricting Russian energy imports and are demanding the administration reverse course. Even if Trump overcomes that challenge, analysts cited in the script question whether the Russian supply is large enough to significantly ease the wider global diesel shortage.
