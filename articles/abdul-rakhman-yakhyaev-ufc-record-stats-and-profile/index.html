Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev: UFC Record, Stats and Profile

TheTools · 24.10.2026 18:01 · source · permalink

Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev has fought ten times as a professional and won ten times. Only two of those opponents ever heard the horn that starts round two. The rest were gone inside the first, most of them in the time it takes to find your seat.

There is no slow build with him and no feeling-out process. He walks forward, he hurts whoever is standing opposite, and the referee gets involved. The Light Heavyweight division has had nine months to come up with an answer and has produced nothing yet.

The Lowdown

Born in Chechnya, fights under the Turkish flag, trains out of Germany. Twenty-five years old, six foot two, campaigning at 205lb. Professional record of 10-0, with four knockouts, five submissions and one lonely decision sitting on the sheet like a typo.

Eight of his wins have come in the opening round. Four of them took less than sixty seconds. His average fight time in the Octagon sits at just over a minute, which is less time than most fighters spend on their walkout music.

Nickname is “The Hunter”. He picked it himself, and the reasoning is not complicated. He told the UFC he likes taking belts off champions. “I hunt them.”

A Chechen Kid Who Ended Up Fighting for Turkey

Yakhyaev was born on 3 January 2001 in Chechnya, a republic still putting itself back together after two wars with Russia. He grew up there, then took his career elsewhere. Somewhere along the line he chose to compete under the Turkish flag, and Türkiye is the nation that appears beside his name on every UFC graphic.

Germany came later, and for a specific reason. He is a Master of Sports in both Combat Sambo and MMA, and he added a NAGA European grappling title before anyone in the West could pronounce his surname.

When the UFC put him on the card in Baku last June, he treated the whole trip as a home game.

“I can say it’s good and nice fighting in Baku because it’s close to Turkey, it’s close to Chechnya, so I am at home here,” he said in the build-up to the event.

The Plumber With a Banking Degree

Before the fighting paid anything, Yakhyaev worked as a plumber. He also went to college and came out the other side with a degree in banking, which is not the standard back story for a man who ends fights in eight seconds.

Neither of those careers lasted. Ask him why he started training and the answer is blunt to the point of being funny. He is fighting for money, he says, and to be better than everybody.

He is single, no wife, no kids. When he is not in camp he plays UFC on Xbox and watches films. Jon Jones is his favourite fighter, which tells you exactly what he thinks a Light Heavyweight is supposed to look like.

The Years Nobody Filmed

The amateur days were the hard ones. He has said so himself, and there is no attempt to dress it up.

“The tough fights were the amateur ones, because back then I couldn’t prepare properly,” he told the UFC.

He turned professional in 2021 and started stacking wins on the Russian regional scene, first through ACA Young Eagles, where he stopped Elbek Ravshanov with punches and then triangled Nazhmudin Yusupov. The move to Turkey’s KhanFight promotion brought a 24-second demolition of Mouhamadou Lamine in October 2022, then a five-round decision over Nikolay Kovalenko in January 2023 that handed him the KhanFight Light Heavyweight title.

Then he vanished for eighteen months. No fights, no announcements, nothing on the record between January 2023 and July 2024.

Two Nights in ARES, Two Belts

He came back in the French-based ARES FC promotion and did not ease himself in. Paulin Begai was guillotined in 92 seconds in July 2024, which made Yakhyaev the ARES FC Light Heavyweight champion. Two months later he defended it against Jacky Jeanne and needed 2:23 to take his back and squeeze.

That was the run that got the UFC’s attention. An invitation to Dana White’s Contender Series followed in August 2025, and Yakhyaev used exactly thirty seconds of it, dropping Alik Lorenz with an overhand right and swarming him with punches and knees until the referee stepped in. White sat there with his eyebrows somewhere near his hairline. The contract was a formality.

Doha, Vegas, Baku

The UFC debut came at UFC Qatar on 22 November 2025 against Rafael Cerqueira, and Yakhyaev opened with a spinning wheel kick five seconds in. That was followed by an overhand left, a right hook and a high kick that put Cerqueira down. The rear-naked choke came 33 seconds into the fight, a record-setting finish at UFC Qatar for the division.

“That was the plan the entire time,” he told Michael Bisping in the cage.

Brendson Ribeiro lasted longer in April 2026 at the Meta APEX, going 2:52 before being submitted with another rear-naked choke. Yakhyaev was irritated about it, because Ribeiro managed to land eight punches on him, which was seven more than his previous two opponents had managed between them.

“First fight is 100 percent,” he said. “Second, I get a little punched. Plan was no getting punched, just knockout or submission.”

Then came Baku on 27 June 2026 and Julius Walker, a former Division II basketball player with real athleticism and no chance to use it. Yakhyaev met him in the centre, threw a right hand, and the fight was over in eight seconds. It tied for the second-fastest finish in Light Heavyweight history and sits seventh on the all-time UFC list.

Sherdog reported he collected a $100,000 performance bonus for it, then immediately promised to go faster.

“Next time fight will be more short. Be ready guys. I think I’m ready for Top 10, Top 5, doesn’t matter.”

Nuremberg, Florida and the People Sharpening Him

His base is MMA Nürnberg in Germany, and he supplements that with camps at Fight Sports Deerfield Beach in Florida alongside Gesias “JZ” Cavalcante, one of the original Blackzilians. The training room there is stocked. UFC veteran Marc-Andre Barriault is a regular partner, as are unbeaten prospect Thad Jean and fellow Contender Series graduate Rafael Tobias.

He does not take time off between camps, and he is fairly open about why.

“You know what? If I’m not training, don’t have a fight, I feel really bad, so I need it,” he told UFC.com before the Baku card. “You can say it’s like a drug for me. I need it, I have to train and fight.”

The MrBeast Situation

After the Cerqueira win, MMA social media decided Yakhyaev was the spitting image of YouTube’s Jimmy Donaldson, better known as MrBeast. Side-by-side photos went everywhere. Yakhyaev, who had never heard of the man, went and looked him up.

He was not impressed with the comparison, telling Red Corner MMA that he did not know who MrBeast was until after Qatar, and that having watched some of his content he still cannot see the resemblance. His suggested fix was that MrBeast should get rid of the moustache.

Krylov at UFC 333

Next up is the sternest examination of the lot. Yakhyaev meets veteran Nikita Krylov on 24 October 2026 at UFC 333 in Abu Dhabi, on a card headlined by Alexander Volkanovski against Movsar Evloev at the Etihad Arena.

Krylov has been fighting professionally since Yakhyaev was in primary school. He has been in there with the best of the division across two separate UFC stints, and he has never once looked intimidated by a hot prospect. He is a finisher himself, which makes it unlikely this one troubles the judges.

Yakhyaev has spent his entire UFC run being the biggest favourite on the card, blitzing opponents who were told to survive the first minute and could not. Krylov has survived a lot worse than a first minute.
