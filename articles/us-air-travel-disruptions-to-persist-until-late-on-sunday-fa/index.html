US air travel disruptions to persist until late on Sunday, FAA head says

TheTools · 15.11.2026 09:02 · source · permalink

World

The problem was traced to FAA contractor L3Harris' installation of at least one new router at a remote facility.

Two travellers sleep in the baggage claim area at Washington Dulles International Airport, in Chantilly, Virginia, Nov 13 2026, after the Federal Aviation Administration grounded flights nationwide. (Photo: AP/Cliff Owen)

WASHINGTON: US air travel is not expected to return to normal schedules until late on Sunday (Nov 15), the head of the FAA said, as the aviation system gradually recovers from a nationwide flight shutdown that officials blamed on routine maintenance gone awry.

As of 9.35pm ET, airlines on Saturday had cancelled more than 6,400 US flights and delayed more than 8,000, according to FlightAware, an aviation website. That followed about 9,200 flights cancelled and 7,500 delayed on Friday after the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop because of disruptions to air traffic communications. FlightAware said 875 flights scheduled for Sunday were already cancelled.

Federal Aviation Administration head Bryan Bedford said on Saturday that he expected airline operations to largely return to normal by late Sunday. He also apologised for the disruption, adding that the system remains stable.

The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop on Friday that halted nearly all departing flights for almost five hours. It was the longest nationwide halt to air travel since the attacks of Sep 11, 2001, and one of the single worst US travel days ever.

The issue prompted the White House to convene an emergency meeting in the Situation Room on Friday and federal agencies to scramble to determine if it was a cyberattack. But the problem was traced to FAA contractor L3Harris' installation of at least one new router at a remote facility, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on Saturday.

"We're working to determine whether we had incompetence, meaning was it user error or installer error from L3Harris, or did they install a faulty router? We haven't determined what the fault was," Duffy said at a press conference.

The Professional Aviation Safety Specialists, a union representing FAA safety employees, said the issue was isolated to two routers that were updated Friday. An official told Reuters it was believed the routers were in Arkansas and Texas.

An FAA official said the agency conducted a safety assessment before the work. The union said PASS-represented technicians were not involved in the upgrades that led to the ground stop.

L3Harris said it took the issue seriously and is supporting the FAA in its investigation. Its shares last traded down about 4% after hours on Friday.

OVERLOADED COMMUNICATIONS CENTRES

Duffy said the trouble began on Friday afternoon, when air traffic control centres experienced instability in radar and communications systems. The router issue led to data overloading other communications centres, officials said.

"We had over 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, and what that did was (it) gave us issues with our communication with airplanes, and it also caused some disruption with our radar," Duffy added.

The issue affected communications at 17 of the FAA's 22 en route centres, which control aircraft during the cruise portion of a flight. Bedford said the FAA ordered the ground stop in case a centre lost all communications. "No facilities went dark. Nothing was fully lost," Bedford added.

The FAA said it was doing additional testing with L3Harris to see if there are any additional risks.

Delta Air Lines, United Airlines and American Airlines all cancelled about 25 per cent of their flights on Saturday, while JetBlue cancelled nearly 45 per cent. Airlines have issued travel waivers allowing passengers to rebook flights for later dates without cost.

Though the FAA lifted the ground stop on Friday evening, the disruption for passengers was far from over, with tens of thousands of people stranded in airports. Airlines were trying to get flight crews and other personnel in position to resume flights.

The disruption stranded some college football teams on Friday before games scheduled for Saturday.

FUNDS SOUGHT TO FIX SYSTEM STRAIN

Friday's disruption is the latest and most dramatic sign of strain on a US air traffic control system beset by ageing technology, equipment and communications problems and a shortage of several thousand controllers.

Duffy called on Friday for US$50 billion in funding to upgrade air traffic control systems, up from his earlier request of US$30 billion - after Congress approved US$12.5 billion last year.

House Speaker Mike Johnson told Fox News on Saturday that he supports approving more funding for air traffic this year. Johnson's Republicans control Congress until next year, when Democrats will take over because of their gains in the November 3 midterm elections.

US Representatives Rick Larsen and Andre Carson, Democrats slated next year to chair the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee and aviation subcommittee, respectively, said in a joint statement Saturday that their panels will "hold the administration accountable for what happened to make sure this chaos does not happen again."

Since receiving US$12.5 billion last year, the Transportation Department has awarded contracts worth US$4.3 billion but only spent US$1 billion, Larsen and Carson said.
