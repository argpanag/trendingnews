HSE board expected to appear at cttee over CEO sacking

TheTools · 26.11.2026 03:44 · source · permalink

The board of the HSE is expected to appear before the Oireachtas health committee next week to answer questions about its controversial sacking of former chief executive Anne O'Connor.

The Tánaiste told the Dáil that the board will contact the committee later to accept its invitation to give evidence.

The board has come under intense pressure to set out the circumstances surrounding its unanimous decision to sack Ms O'Connor, who had been in the post since March.

Tánaiste Simon Harris and Taoiseach Micheál Martin said it was "essential" for the board to face the committee.

"That's why I can confirm to the House this afternoon that a delegation of the HSE board, led by the chair, will be in touch to accept the invitation of the Oireachtas health committee to attend the health committee next week," Mr Harris told the Dáil.

"I think that's appropriate and an important thing to do."

Minister for Education and Youth Hildegarde Naughton has said she has "absolute confidence" in Minister for Health Jennifer Carroll MacNeill.

But she stopped short of expressing confidence in the HSE board following its dismissal of chief executive Ms O'Connor.

Asked on RTÉ’s Prime Time whether she agreed with the Taoiseach’s position, Minister Naughton did not answer directly.

She said it was for the board, as Ms O’Connor’s employer, to explain its decision.

She said the board would appear before an Oireachtas committee next Wednesday.

Social Democrats TD Pádraig Rice, chairperson of the committee, told RTÉ News tonight that he had not received confirmation yet that the board will come before the committee, but that he expects they will do so.

Speaking earlier, on RTÉ's Six One, Deputy Rice said the committee have many questions for the HSE board.

"We need to know why exactly Anne O'Connor was sacked. We need to have clarity on the process followed and what involvement if any the minister had in the process."

He said that the committee may need to hear from Ms O'Connor on this at some point as well, describing her as a long standing public servant.

Lawyers for former HSE Chief Executive Ms O'Connor have told the High Court that they have received a letter from the HSE inviting her to attend mediation.

Senior counsel for Ms O'Connor, Lorna Lynch, said they would be directed by Ms O'Connor. The ex-parte application that was expected to be moved did not proceed.

Ms O'Connor had yesterday said she instructed lawyers to bring a court application to challenge what she said was her "unlawful termination" from the role.

Either scenario through either mediation or the courts leaves the HSE open to significant costs, which would ultimately be borne by the Exchequer and increase political pressure on Minister Carroll MacNeill.

It is understood that Ms O'Connor was attempting to get the HSE board to rescind their decision to remove her, but that action has now been postponed.

Mr Harris added: "The next point I'd make in terms of facts is there had been a public indication from the now former chief executive that she was considering entering legal action or potentially court action, as is anybody's right.

"I noticed this afternoon that a mediation process is now being considered. I know this has been a difficult week for people working in the health service."

Sinn Féin's David Cullinane said the controversy has all the hallmarks of a minister who orchestrated a sacking and is now hiding behind the HSE board.

Minister for Health Jennifer Carroll MacNeil's narrative review is unravelling by the day, he said, adding that the Taoiseach and Tánaiste have been forced into a public U-turn by not expressing confidence in the HSE board.

Responding the Tánaiste, who sat alongside Ms Carroll MacNeill, robustly defended her and said that she was "doing a bloody good job."

Earlier, Mr Harris said he was "withholding judgment" until the full facts are made clear by the board.

Mr Martin also said that the board needs to provide a comprehensive explanation and full facts of its decision.

There was widespread shock when the board announced on Monday that it was ending the five-year contract of the chief executive.

O'Connor statement 'raised significant issues', says Taoiseach

Ms O'Connor has since released two statements, in which she claimed she was not aware she was at risk of losing her job until a Teams calls at 7.30am on Saturday.

The position taken by the Taoiseach and the Tánaiste is at odds with that of health minister Jennifer Carroll MacNeill, who said she supports the board and its decision.

Mr Harris has praised Ms O'Connor's work record.

He described her as a "good and decent" person who has provided "significantly good service" for the state and the health service.

"Sometimes things don't work out for a whole variety of reasons between a chief executive and their board, and there's very clear processes in place for when those things don't work out," Mr Harris said.

"I think it is really important when new information comes into the public domain, as it did in recent days, as it did yesterday, it's really important that a full account is given in relation to that information.

"It is now a statement of fact that the termination of the CEO of the HSE and the process that was followed in relation to that termination is now contested from a process point of view.

"You have a view given forth by the HSE board. You have a very different public account given by the chief executive, or the former chief executive, of the HSE.

"In light of that, it is entirely appropriate, in fact I think it is essential, that the board or a delegation of the board would accept the invitation from the Oireachtas health committee because they are the only ones who can account for that process. That is my view.

"That is the view of the Taoiseach. That is the view of the minister for health. That is the view of the Government. That in light of that new information, it would be extremely helpful, and I would go so far as to say it's central that the HSE would provide more information."

Mr Martin also said Ms O'Connor's statement raised "serious issues".

Speaking before confirmation that the HSE will appear before the committee, Mr Martin added: "We do need those issues bottomed out and also the reason as to why the board took the decision that it took, I think the matter is of significant public interest.

"The board or its representative should come before an Oireachtas committee and should outline their perspective and their position in relation to all of this.

"I think the minister for health accepts and is in agreement with the position of the Tánaiste and I on that, and particularly in the light of certainly evolving or the emergency of Anne O'Connor's statement and the position the CEO outlined.

"I await the board, if you like, coming forward and giving us, both in terms of the process adopted and also in terms of the reasons as to why they decided to terminate the contract."

Asked if he has confidence in the HSE board, Mr Harris said it is made up of "competent, serious" people.

"But I have to withhold judgment in relation to the process that was followed until we have the facts, because what we have now is the HSE saying one thing, we have former CEO of the HSE saying another thing. I can't be the arbitrator in relation to those two sets of facts," he added.
