Trump’s unprecedented trilateral meeting with Xi and Putin

Leaders from almost two dozen world economies are descending on the Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen this week for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) forum — but the main focus will be on three of them: President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

The three leaders are expected to engage in trilateral talks on the sidelines of the forum, though a senior U.S. official has said the meeting is unlikely to produce any sort of agreement.

All three are seeking something different, said Han Lin, China managing director for the Asia Group. “Putin probably wants recognition that Russia remains indispensable. Xi wants acknowledgement that China belongs at the center of global diplomacy, and Trump gets a stage for personal deal making with other major nuclear powers,” Han said.

Trump is also expected to have a bilateral meeting with Xi — but talks will be made complicated by the Nvidia scandal, which has raised security concerns among lawmakers and Trump administration officials amid the U.S. and China’s race for global AI dominance. Trump, however, has downplayed CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy, telling reporters yesterday that the U.S. “was not put at risk” by the relationship.

What’s with all the tech glitches?

Last week, it was Microsoft and the FAA. Yesterday, it was Chase and United Airlines. In the span of three business days, a string of technology failures has upended the lives of millions of Americans.

At around 6:30 p.m. ET yesterday, Chase Bank said transactions were posting normally, hours after customers had reported trouble accessing their accounts and withdrawing funds. United’s system outage lasted only an hour but resulted in some delayed flights.

While the four recent tech glitches appeared to be unrelated, the back-to-back problems underscored the vulnerability of networks at the backbone of the modern economy. On social media, users pointed to “enshittification,” a term used to describe the worsening quality of digital products and services. And experts blamed companies’ infrastructure for failing to keep up with demand.

How reliance on fewer systems is leading to larger problems.

U.S. loses its measles-elimination status

An independent commission within the World Health Organization is expected to announce today that the U.S. has lost its measles-elimination status, reversing one of the country’s greatest public health achievements this century. Sources familiar with the Pan American Health Organization’s vote said the decision was made after determining that the virus has circulated nonstop for more than a year.

Upwards of 5,400 cases have been recorded this year — more than double last year’s count and the highest since 1991. And yesterday, CDC officials sounded the alarm on pregnant women with measles, which one doctor called “a brand new phenomenon.” Preliminary case figures suggest 112 cases of confirmed measles during pregnancy.

Contributing to the uptick in cases is declining measles vaccination rates across the country. Among kindergarteners, 75% of counties and jurisdictions fall below the 95% vaccination threshold needed to prevent outbreaks, according to an NBC News investigation.
