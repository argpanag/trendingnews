SINGAPORE – The PM2.5 reading in Singapore’s northern region returned to the “normal” range after briefly entering the “elevated” range on Oct 27.

At 7pm, the PM2.5 readings were in the “normal” range islandwide, between 21 and 34, while the 24-hour PSI stood between 62 and 75, putting it in the “good to moderate” band.

At 4pm, the PM2.5 reading in the north was elevated, at 66. This fell to 43 at 5pm.

Air quality in Singapore had been healthy overnight, with hourly PM2.5 readings remaining in the normal range and the 24-hour PSI in the “good to moderate” band across the island.

PM2.5 readings are gauged hourly and reflect the current air quality. Scores from zero to 55 represent normal values, and those between 56 and 150 indicate an elevated concentration of fine particles.

Air quality is considered good to moderate when the PSI – a 24-hour rolling average of six key air pollutants – is in the range of zero to 100, and unhealthy when it is in the range of 101 to 200.

When air quality breaches the “unhealthy” threshold, people – especially the elderly, children and those with health issues – should start scaling down outdoor activities.

Following weeks of bad air quality, residents in Singapore saw some respite over the weekend when the PM2.5 levels stood in the normal range for most parts of the country, although the 24-hour PSI readings remained unhealthy in some areas.

Forecast for Oct 28

In its daily haze advisory on the evening of Oct 27, the National Environment Agency (NEA) said the PSI for Oct 28 is forecast to be in the good-to-moderate range of 60 to 90.

Smoke haze was observed over Kalimantan and Sumatra on Oct 27, NEA said, adding that it was dry over Singapore.

NEA said that showers are expected on Oct 28 over Kalimantan, Sumatra, Singapore and the surrounding region, which could continue to help with the haze situation.

The winds are forecast to blow from the east-northeast on Oct 28, NEA added.

For Oct 29 and 30, winds are expected to blow from the east-southeast or south-southeast.

“While this could transport smoke haze from southern Sumatra, showers are forecast over the region which should help to lower the risk of haze,” said NEA.
