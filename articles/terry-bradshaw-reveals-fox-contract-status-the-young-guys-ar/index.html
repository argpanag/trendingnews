Terry Bradshaw reveals Fox contract status: 'The young guys are coming, but I want to be relevant'

TheTools · 11.10.2026 23:00 · source · permalink
"This year was the first year that I got nervous. I wanted to extend my contract."

Terry Bradshaw isn’t going anywhere.

The longtime NFL on Fox analyst and Hall of Fame quarterback, whose teasing over his own retirement is beginning to rival that of Charles Barkley, is promoting a new memoir and revealed to the Los Angeles Times this week that he is under contract with the network for multiple seasons.

Bradshaw apparently signed a contract extension somewhat recently, and it will take him through the 2028-29 season, when Fox will air its next Super Bowl. The Fox NFL Sunday panelist revealed that he “got nervous” heading into the season, perhaps thinking that Fox was looking at a succession plan.

“I love my job. This year was the first year that I got nervous,” he explained. “I wanted to extend my contract. So I signed through the Super Bowl. Two more years. I’ll be 80.”

Asked about stepping aside in order to let newer voices come into NFL media, Bradshaw didn’t appear to be in any hurry.

“Everybody in any business has to go. The young guys are coming. But I want to be relevant,” he said. “I want to be good. I want to continue on. I want America to [tune in] and watch me. I want them to [like] me.”

Bradshaw’s continued commentary about his future at Fox has come amid increased external pressure from fans who have grown weary of the Pittsburgh Steelers great’s frequent flubs and non sequiturs. The 78-year-old Bradshaw has indeed become less focused and sharp in his analysis.

However, he remains one of the rare pregame show voices who will go beyond typical niceties and actually express an opinion. Just this season, Bradshaw has called for the New England Patriots to bench MVP runner-up Drake Maye and excoriated NFL decision-makers for overpaying quarterbacks. The logic behind Bradshaw’s arguments may not always add up, but the job of a commentator is to intrigue and provoke the audience.

Looking forward, it is perhaps just as likely that the business of the NFL pregame show will decline as it is that another star analyst will replace Bradshaw. Viewership for these shows is not nearly what it once was, as audiences get their news and highlights elsewhere, and podcasts and YouTube shows present alternatives to set the table on Sunday mornings.

Bradshaw has insisted he plans to retire from Fox Sports, but understands the network may not allow him to go out on his terms the way it did with his pal Jimmy Johnson in 2025.

Super Bowl LXIII would seemingly present a logical opportunity for Bradshaw to do so.
