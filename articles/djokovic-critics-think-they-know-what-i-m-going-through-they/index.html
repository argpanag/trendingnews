Djokovic: Critics Think They Know What I'm Going Through. They Don't.

TheTools · 03.11.2026 06:45 · source · permalink

Share this article

Novak Djokovic has hit back at critics who blame his age for a difficult 2026 season, saying physical problems, not a lack of preparation, held him back. Speaking before his Paris Masters opener against Valentin Vacherot, the 39-year-old said he is finally feeling better and close to leaving a tough 12 months behind.

“I see a lot of people who love giving their expert opinions, convinced they know exactly what I’m going through, but they don’t,” Djokovic said at his pre-tournament press conference.

The seven-time Paris champion is playing the event for the first time since 2023, when he beat Grigor Dimitrov in the final to win his most recent title there.

“I Suffered Enormously Physically”

Djokovic explained that his health has been the main issue this year. “This year, my priority has become feeling good on court rather than playing well, to be honest,” he said. “Because in most of the tournaments I’ve played, and I haven’t played many, I suffered enormously physically.”

He insisted his struggles had nothing to do with how he prepared. “It had nothing to do with my preparation or with not training enough,” he said. “Nevertheless, as soon as I started feeling better on court, which has been the case recently, we are close to getting out of the situation I’ve been in for the past twelve months.”

The Serb has played a reduced schedule in 2026, which he said last week was also driven by his wish to spend more time with his family.

“Beijing Was My Best Week of the Year”

Despite the difficulties, Djokovic is positive about his recent form. He won a record seventh China Open title in Beijing in October, the 102nd title of his career, beating Alexander Zverev, Daniil Medvedev and Alex de Minaur along the way.

“Immediately, my level of play improves, I enjoy myself more on court and my feelings are excellent,” he said. “Beijing was my best week of the year, apart from Australia, and I think I also played pretty well in Basel, even though I lost a very close match.”

In Melbourne in January, he reached the final of the Australian Open, losing to Carlos Alcaraz. In Basel last week, he lost to Jan-Lennard Struff 6-7(4), 7-5, 7-6(5) in the second round, despite creating six break points.

Turin Hopes Still Alive

The Basel defeat hurt his chances of qualifying for the ATP Finals in Turin. With Jannik Sinner out for the season, the top nine in the Race will qualify, and Djokovic left Basel trailing Rafael Jódar, then in the last qualifying spot, by 319 points. A deep run in Paris is now his only chance.

“I’m simply trying to enjoy the last few weeks of the season and, above all, to feel better physically on court,” he said. “Then the results will follow. We’ll see.”

A Tricky Opener Against Vacherot

His first opponent is a familiar one. Vacherot beat an injured Djokovic 6-3, 6-4 in the semifinals of the Shanghai Masters in 2025, on his way to a stunning title as a qualifier ranked No. 204.

The Monegasque arrives in Paris in good form after reaching the final in Basel last week, where he beat Jódar, Holger Rune and Taylor Fritz. It promises to be a demanding test for Djokovic as he begins what may be his final push for Turin.
