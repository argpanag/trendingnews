Charles Manson follower tied to Sharon Tate murders gets recommended for parole after 55 years in prison

TheTools · 10.10.2026 00:38 · source · permalink

The Charles Manson follower convicted in the 1969 Tate-LaBianca murders has spent more than 55 years behind bars

California attorney lays out case against parole board's plan to free killers serving life without parole

Former Sacramento DA Anne Marie Schubert is spearheading a lawsuit challenging new California Board of Parole Hearings guidelines that she warns will open a pathway to freedom for serial killers, mass shooters and more of the state's worst violent criminals.

Patricia Krenwinkel, a former follower of cult leader Charles Manson convicted in the 1969 Tate-LaBianca murders, has been recommended for parole after spending more than 55 years behind bars.

Krenwinkel, 78, was recommended for parole Tuesday for the third time in four years, setting up another potential showdown with California Gov. Gavin Newsom, who has blocked her release twice over concerns that she remains a danger to the public.

The latest decision, confirmed by the California Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation's inmate database, comes less than a year after Newsom reversed her previous parole recommendation.

In his October 2025 decision, Newsom found that Krenwinkel "lacks the requisite insight she needs to be safely released," despite acknowledging her efforts to rehabilitate herself while spending decades in prison.

"When considered as a whole, the evidence shows that she currently poses an unreasonable danger to society if released from prison at this time," Newsom wrote.

State records show Krenwinkel was previously recommended for parole in May 2022 and May 2025, but Newsom reversed both decisions, keeping her behind bars.

The decision does not guarantee Krenwinkel's release, as the parole board must first complete its review before Newsom can decide whether to let her go free or block her release for a third time.
