UFC Qatar predictions

TheTools · 21.11.2026 00:00 · source · permalink
  • UFC

Top light heavyweight contenders Jiri Prochazka and Navajo Stirling clash in the UFC Qatar main event

UFC Qatar is the definition of survival of the fittest.

The UFC’s 2026 matchmaking philosophy has embraced having veteran names defend their spots in the rankings and Saturday’s event is no different, with the entire main card having meaningful stakes. In the headliner, Jiri Prochazka bounces back from a disappointing light heavyweight championship loss to Carlos Ulberg as he takes on Ulberg’s teammate, the undefeated Navajo Stirling.

Given Prochazka’s popularity and always entertaining fighting style, it’s unlikely that he’s in dire need of a victory, but a loss to Stirling would certainly set him back and make the road to another title shot considerably longer; a Stirling win also muddies the waters, as it would make him more than worthy of facing the champion, who just so happens to be his friend and training partner.

Then there’s Aljamain Sterling in the co-main event, potentially moving one step closer to a fight with Alexander Volkanovski if he can get past blue-chip prospect Kevin Vallejos. It’s been nothing but tough matchups for Sterling, a former bantamweight champion, since moving up to 145 pounds, and Vallejos might be the tallest hill he’s had to climb yet. A title shot likely awaits Sterling if he hands Vallejos is his first UFC defeat, but Vallejos could just as easily steal Sterling’s thunder.

In other main card action, Dan Hooker welcomes perennial featherweight title contender Brian Ortega to the lightweight division, Kyoji Horiguchi defends his top 5 flyweight spot against Asu Almabayev, and one-time middleweight title challenger Jared Cannonier could be fighting for his job against Ikram Aliskerov.

What: UFC Qatar

Where: Ali Bin Hamad al-Attiyah Arena in Doha, Qatar

When: Saturday, Nov. 21. The seven-fight preliminary card begins at 10 a.m. ET, followed by a five-fight main card at 1 p.m. ET with the entire event streaming live on Paramount+.

(Numbers in parentheses indicate standing in the MMA Fighting Global Rankings)

Jiri Prochazka (4) vs. Navajo Stirling (6)

Navajo Stirling leveled up in a big way with how he handled Jan Blachowicz at UFC Belgrade. Even with Blachowicz past his prime, he’s still leagues above the other opponents Stirling had fought up until that point, and Stirling took care of business as the young, hungry contender. There is still plenty of room for improvement, but that was an important hurdle for Stirling to clear.

I think what’s tripping me up about Stirling is that nothing about his skill set jumps off the page and yet he finds ways to win, and now he’s finding ways to finish. That’s a scary development arc for a fighter who just turned 29 earlier this month. Like his teammate Carlos Ulberg, Stirling is effective even if he’s not flashy.

That’s in stark contrast to MMA’s premier mad man, the inimitable Jiri Prochazka. I’m starting to have serious concerns about Prochazka’s durability at this stage of his career. Yes, I know, he’s always fought a high-wire style and more often than not come out on top, but there’s always a limit to these things at Prochazka might be at the edge of it. Is he truly more focused after the Ulberg debacle, or are we starting to see fatal flaws in the former champion’s game?

I can’t shake the image of Prochazka putting an early scare in Stirling, before wandering into a lethal combination from the undefeated New Zealander. As much as it pains me to write this, Stirling by second-round knockout.

Pick: Stirling

Aljamain Sterling (4) vs. Kevin Vallejos (10)

As competitive as Aljamain Sterling has been at 145 pounds, the reality of featherweight might catch up to him here.

Sterling isn’t small for the division, but there’s a reason he was a champion at bantamweight, his ideal weight class. Guys like Vallejos hit a little harder, are more difficult to hurt, and most pertinent to this matchup, a little more difficult to take down. Vallejos has shown excellent wrestling defense in his first four UFC fights, and even if Sterling is the best grappler he’s faced yet, that’s still reason for concern if you’re Sterling’s team.

In the standup, Sterling can definitely make life difficult for Vallejos with his athletic, funky striking approach, and that could open up opportunities to force Vallejos into a clinch and bring him to the mat. Vallejos should be in full defense and get-up mode if this happens, which is easier said than done given Sterling’s spectacular scrambling ability.

Whoever wins will do so on the cards and make a strong title shot case in the process. I’m leaning towards Vallejos to prevent Sterling from completely neutralizing him with the grappling, and to land the more damaging shots over three rounds.

Pick: Vallejos

Dan Hooker (15) vs. Brian Ortega

OK, obviously on paper this looks like the washed vs. the washed, but you know what? I’m feeling confident in picking Dan Hooker here.

Neither Hooker nor Brian Ortega have been remotely close to the best versions of themselves in recent years, with Hooker having lost three straight dating back to 2024 and Ortega mired in an uninspiring 1-4 slump. Hooker hasn’t made it out of the second round during his losing streak, while Ortega has struggled to decision losses in his past two outings. Realistically, this is a toss-up.

But Hooker’s losses to Salahdine Parnasse, Benoit Saint Denis, and Arman Tsarukyan had a lot to do with a glaring athleticism gap, a problem that only gets worse as Hooker gets older. That won’t be an issue with Ortega, who has lost several steps since his title contender days. Ortega’s move up to 155 pounds also feels more like an act of desperation than a necessity.

I don’t know. It’s possible Ortega capitalizes on a Hooker mistake and chokes him out, but I’m predicting Hooker batters Ortega on the feet and flurries to force the referee to step in for the save in Round 2.

Pick: Hooker

Kyoji Horiguchi (4) vs. Asu Almabayev (10)

With respect to Asu Almabayev, Kyoji Horiguchi almost never loses to grappling specialists. Check the tape. There’s Patchy Mix and that’s it. You better be able to throw down or “Gooch” is gonna get ya!

It doesn’t help that Almabayev is small, even for a flyweight, so even if he latches on to Horiguchi, it’s not as if he can just Hulk Smash him around the octagon. Horiguchi has always had great grappling defense and Almabayev is going to waste a lot of energy trying to put Horiguchi on his backside.

It won’t be nearly as difficult for Horiguchi, who will fry Almabayev with his striking and probably score a knockdown or two en route to a convincing decision win. I’m not giving up on Horiguchi fighting for a UFC title again someday and you shouldn’t either.

Pick: Horiguchi

Jared Cannonier (14) vs. Ikram Aliskerov (15)

This could get weird.

In theory, Ikram Aliskerov should be too much for Jared Cannonier to handle at this stage of the veteran’s career. Cannonier is still in incredible shape at 42, as evidenced not only by his physique but his ability to consistently go the distance, often in five-rounders. His effectiveness in these spots is another question, but he doesn’t go away easily, that’s for sure.

Aliskerov is at his best when he can mix in a steady diet of takedowns to stifle overaggressive strikers, which could prove difficult against Cannonier. “The Killa Gorilla” has rarely been troubled by grapplers, so Saturday’s main card opener should largely be contested on the feet.

That won’t stop Aliskerov from grabbing hold of Cannonier when he can and forcing him to the fence, turning this into a mucky and truly middleweight-y affair. Cannonier’s biggest flaw is he sometimes fails to pull the trigger when he needs to, and that could be another problem for him against Aliskerov.

Aliskerov frustrates Cannonier and bangs him up enough to earn a decision.

Pick: Aliskerov
