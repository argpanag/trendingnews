Parliament Today: Conservatives force a recorded vote on Buy Canadian and a CRA security contract

TheTools · 03.11.2026 11:32 · source · permalink

MPs will record their votes on a Conservative motion that sets the government's Buy Canadian pledge against a federal security contract held by a U.S.-owned firm.

A deferred recorded division on a Conservative supply motion is the only item of government business on the House of Commons agenda today, with no bills scheduled for debate and no new legislation set for introduction. The motion, moved by Pierre Paul-Hus (Charlesbourg—Haute-Saint-Charles) and seconded by Jeff Kibble (Cowichan—Malahat—Langford), contrasts the government’s Buy Canadian Policy with a Canada Revenue Agency security contract awarded to the Canadian subsidiary of an American multinational. An opposition supply motion is not automatically a question of confidence, but it puts every MP on the record on federal procurement.

“Canadian suppliers by default”

The motion was debated Monday on an allotted day and the division was deferred to today. Its recitals quote Prime Minister Mark Carney’s pledge, made in response to the Trump administration’s trade war, that “whenever the federal government spends, we will select Canadian suppliers by default,” and his statement when the Buy Canadian Policy took effect on December 16, 2025, that Canada would become its own “best customer.” It also cites Minister of Government Transformation, Public Works and Procurement Joël Lightbound’s declaration that the government was “putting Canadian workers and industries first,” and the Prime Minister’s April 2026 promise to build with “Canadian steel, Canadian aluminum, Canadian lumber, Canadian workers.”

Against those statements, the motion sets out that in June 2026 the Canada Revenue Agency awarded a security services contract worth nearly $12 million to Universal Protection Service of Canada Corporation, a subsidiary of the American firm Allied Universal. It adds that Allied Universal’s subsidiary G4S reportedly holds contracts worth US$83 million with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and that Allied Universal has reportedly received more than US$290 million in contracts from the Trump administration in 2026 alone.

The operative clause of the motion—what it asks the House to resolve—is not reproduced in the Order of Business excerpt available for this brief, and no Question Period exchanges or Hansard debate on it were available. What can be said is that the motion is designed to test the gap between the government’s stated procurement policy and a specific award by a federal agency, and that the recorded division will show where Liberal, Conservative, Bloc Québécois and other members stand on that question. With the government holding a majority, the result is not in doubt; the political value for the opposition lies in the vote itself.

The deferred division follows Monday’s allotted-day debate in the current supply period, which ends December 10, 2026.

Other parliamentary business

– Business of Supply: the House remains in the supply period ending December 10, 2026; a maximum of seven allotted days applies under Standing Order 81(10)(a), with two days remaining.
– Government bills: none called for debate today.
– New legislation: no government bills are listed for introduction at today’s Daily Routine.

Private members’ business

No private members’ bills or motions appear in the schedule available for this brief.

Question Period is at 2:15 p.m. ET.
