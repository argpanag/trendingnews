Blue Jays non-tender fan favorite Davis Schneider after rough '26

TheTools · 23.11.2026 00:05 · source · permalink

TORONTO -- The Blue Jays have non-tendered Davis Schneider, the fan favorite who struggled to find a permanent role with the club in 2026. 

Schneider’s historic debut in 2023 will forever stand as one of the greatest in Blue Jays history, when he went 9-for-13 with two home runs over three days at Fenway Park in Boston. There have been other highs since then, when Schneider’s excellent plate approach paired with his power in just the right way, but he appeared in just 69 games last season with a .145 average and .625 OPS.

All other arbitration-eligible players were tendered contracts by the Blue Jays Friday, including Ernie Clement, José Soriano, Josh Smith, Louis Varland, Nathan Lukes, Spencer Arrighetti, Addison Barger and Bowden Francis. The Blue Jays’ 40-man roster is now at 39 players. 

The 27-year-old Schneider, who was a remarkable development prospect as a 28th-round pick out of high school in 2017, split last season between the big leagues and Triple-A Buffalo. Few players in the organization could rival Schneider’s pitch recognition, which led to Schneider walking more than once per game in Triple-A (57 in 52 games), but we didn’t see as much of Schneider’s power to counter that. At several points throughout the season, Blue Jays manager John Schneider said that he wanted to see more big swings on pitches in the zone.

It’s possible that Schneider finds a larger opportunity elsewhere, but in Toronto, it felt like his pathways to big league playing time in 2027 may have been limited, especially if another outfielder is added. Schneider has plenty of baseball ahead of him, though, and if he can find the right balance between his plate approach and power again, he could soon find himself right back in the big leagues. He deserves credit, too, for turning himself into a solid corner outfielder, which gives him a broader defensive profile. 

Otherwise, the Blue Jays’ tender deadline decisions weren’t much of a decision at all. While Clement faded down the stretch, his profile still fits this team very well. Smith may have been the other Toronto player closest to a true “decision” here, but the Blue Jays liked what they saw down the stretch and believed he could tap into more power.

This has been a quiet start to the offseason for Toronto, but with business picking up around the league, the Blue Jays are expected to be involved at every level of the market. They are among the teams who have met with Tarik Skubal, who could set the new MLB record for the highest annual salary paid to a pitcher, and they remain an excellent fit for outfielder Seiya Suzuki, who has a fine case as the top position player available.
