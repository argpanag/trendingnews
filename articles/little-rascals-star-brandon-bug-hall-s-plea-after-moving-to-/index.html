Little Rascals star Brandon ‘Bug’ Hall’s plea after moving to new home - realestate.com.au

TheTools · 10.11.2026 08:06 · source · permalink

Former child star Brandon “Bug” Hall has turned to the public for financial help as his family struggles to pay bills before the arrival of their sixth baby.

Hall, who shot to fame playing Alfalfa Switzer in the 1994 comedy The Little Rascals, launched a GoFundMe appeal asking fans to bail out his household expenses, Page Six reports.

The 41-year-old revealed he had fallen behind on payments after moving to a rural property in Arkansas, admitting he had run out of financial options.

“It’s now six years since I self-cancelled, and I have avoided doing one of these in all that time,” Hall wrote.

“I always said this was my choice, and so I didn’t want to offload the glorious burden of it unless there was no other choice. Well here we are.”

Hall asked supporters for assistance to get “the last few things buttoned up” ahead of his wife giving birth to their sixth child.

“Times are hard for everyone, and I’m no more deserving than anyone else, but I ask in your charity that you help where you can, whether monetarily, by sharing this, or by prayer and sacrifice,” he wrote.

So far, Hall has raised $US10,130 ($A15,500), surpassing his original $US2,000 ($A3,075) goal.

The public plea follows a string of legal run-ins for the former actor. In April, he was arrested and charged in Ohio with failing to appear in court over an October 2024 traffic citation.

In 2020, police in Texas arrested him for huffing air duster cans outside a hotel.

Hall previously told the Daily Mail he had taken a strict “vow of poverty” and relocated his family off the grid to live as a radical Catholic extremist.

At the time, he claimed he had donated his Hollywood earnings and most of his personal belongings to charity.

“My goal is to maintain a life as free of any need for an income as possible,” Hall said during the interview.

“If there’s a financial need that comes up, I’ll go take some work or do an odd job, for cash to fulfill that need.”

Before the shift to rural Arkansas, Hall lived with his family in Los Angeles and Texas.

He said they homeschooled their children, intended to strongly discourage them from attending university, and labelled formal education “nonsense”.

Hall also stirred controversy by describing same-sex marriage as against natural law and condemning abortion, while maintaining he had no interest in conventional work.

“I didn’t want to go work some job that was basically meaningless, making widgets to entertain people or distract people,” he said.
