Leaders from both parties express optimism as voters cast their ballots across the US

After months of election analysis, poll results and pure speculation, Democratic and Republican leaders are expressing their final predictions on Election Day, and they’re all signaling optimism for the night. Top House Democrats are confident they can gain a comfortable majority, while Trump Cabinet members are delivering positive messages about the state of the country and what that means for voters.

President Donald Trump himself, however, has privately conceded that his party may be facing the worst-case scenario, sources close to the president said.

We will begin to get the first evidence of whose confidence was well-founded in less than two hours, when the first polls close in Kentucky and Indiana. See CNN’s full guide to when polls close across the country.

Meanwhile, voting issues are being reported in some areas, forcing polling sites to extend their hours or move locations entirely. In Westchester County, New York, vote-counting machines have been unable to scan as many as 250,000 ballots in 332 polling locations, according to the county executive. Emergency ballots will be picked up by bipartisan election officials and escorted by police to a counting location tonight, Republican Rep. Mike Lawler told reporters.

Get caught up on the Election Day news here:

  • Signs of high turnout: As voters across the country head to the polls today, tens of millions have already voted over the previous weeks. More than 58.5 million pre-election ballots have been cast, according to data collected by CNN, the Associated Press and Catalist. That’s already well over the pre-election total from 2022. In the key Senate battlegrounds of Ohio and Iowa, signs of high or even record turnout are emerging.
  • Voter voices: At polling locations across the country, CNN reporters are speaking with voters to hear what they’re most concerned about and how they feel about today’s election. Susan Collins would win reelection.

Among the people watching that race today is President Donald Trump, who wrote, “TODAY, the eyes of the Country are on New York again!” and repeated his endorsement of Republican Rep. Mike Lawler.
  • DOJ involvement: A new appeals court order this afternoon gave the Justice Department the OK to warn election officials they could be prosecuted for not complying with its demands to change voting procedures. In Alaska, the DOJ sent target letters to more than a dozen people it believes helped recruit a Senate candidate with the same name as the Republican incumbent.

Trump political team prepares for potential "forensic audit"

In recent weeks, President Donald Trump has talked about requiring a “forensic audit” of how the enormous MAGA Inc. and Republican National Committee war chest was spent, two sources told CNN. Members of Trump’s political operation are preparing to be called to the White House to present a detailed report to the president.

One source told CNN that Trump required a similar audit of his campaign team in 2024, with demands to see how and where the money had been spent. Trump has privately said that the amount of money that his super PAC put into these races gave Republicans “a fighting chance.”

Another source noted that Trump has had private concerns that advisers may be making exorbitant amounts of money from those coffers. Some of these concerns spawned from reports his top political advisers, Chris LaCivita and James Blair made hundreds of thousands of dollars for their work for Republika Srpska. LaCivita and Blair are not expected to be part of any audit, one of the sources said — the pair donated the funds they received to Trump’s presidential library fund.

How are things going with those DOJ monitors?

There are scattered reports of minor issues with some of the 1,000 election observers from the Justice Department who were sent to all 50 states. But overall, election officials said the monitors haven’t been disruptive to voting.

In Michigan, the secretary of state said DOJ monitors in Dearborn and Flint were asking poll workers about their training. Barb Byrum, the clerk in Ingham County, told CNN that monitors asked some voters for their phone numbers.

“It’s not acceptable,” said Byrum, a Democrat.

A DOJ spokesperson told CNN that these were “standard questions” and “the same questions asked by monitors in the past, including during the previous administration.”

Isaac Cramer, the top election official in Charleston, South Carolina, said he heard from local clerks that some of the DOJ monitors in his county were collecting poll workers’ names and asking whether there were any Spanish-language ballots.

“It’s different than it was in August, when they were just sitting with a clipboard and observing,” Cramer said. “They are still following the proper procedures. But it seems a bit different.”

A Justice Department spokesperson told CNN that the agency is “looking into it and will work with local election officials to address issues,” she said.

The New Mexico secretary of state’s office said in a statement that DOJ observers were asking “unusual questions and questioning voters.” A DOJ spokesperson told CNN “we believe the issue is resolved” after a call with New Mexico officials.

Officials in Los Angeles told CNN that things have been progressing normally. So did officials from Maricopa County, Arizona, which has seen its fair share of election chaos over the years.

“Smooth sailing so far in Maricopa County,” county official Taylor Kinnerup said.

Georgia Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger said there have been “no issues whatsoever” with election monitors. “Every cycle, they come. And they are just observers.”

CNN’s Yahya Abou-Ghazala and Jason Morris contributed to this report.

3 counties to watch in the Texas Senate race

Texas’ highly competitive Senate race will play out across a massive array of 254 counties, spanning from big cities such as Houston to sparsely populated rural areas.

Here are three counties to keep an eye on as results come in:

Tarrant County

Tarrant County, home to Fort Worth, was once known as the state’s biggest red county. But it’s emerged as a true battleground in recent statewide elections. Democrat Beto O’Rourke won the county by less than 1 percentage point in 2018 as he narrowly lost the Senate race. President Donald Trump carried the county by 5 points in 2024 as he romped statewide.

Montgomery County

A suburban and rural county outside Houston, Montgomery County is the most populous GOP stronghold in Texas. Recent GOP statewide candidates have carried the county by 40 or more points. Paxton needs to run up the score here. He held the second-to-last rally of his campaign in Conroe, the county seat.

Nueces County

South Texas has seen some dramatic swings between parties in recent elections, but Nueces County, home to Corpus Christi, has been more closely divided. It’s another county that voted for O’Rourke in 2018 but for Trump in 2024. The county, which also touches a competitive House race, was the site of a Trump rally a week before the election.

Outside a polling place in Bangor, Maine, opinions about the Senate race between Republican Sen. Susan Collins and Democratic challenger Troy Jackson varied Tuesday. As Democrats try to take back the house, all eyes are on four extremely competitive districts in Pennsylvania.

These districts will test several parts of President Trump’s 2024 coalition: working class voters, Latino voters, former Democrats and more.

The biggest battleground has become Pennsylvania’s 1st Congressional District, represented by Republican congressman Brian Fitzpatrick — a district Kamala Harris won in 2024.

At a polling place in Bucks County — the heart of the district — voters came out in very high numbers Tuesday, according to the judges of elections here. The voters were also extremely divided.

“Republicans don’t seem to have a check on Donald Trump. They’re letting him do whatever he wants,” said Democrat Jennifer O’Donell.

“I don’t like paying higher prices but if it’s going to keep me from getting blown up down the road, I more than welcome it,” said Republican Ron Turner in support of Republicans and the war with Iran.

“These guys got to work together, if they can’t work together, nothing’s gonna get done, as you’ve seen over the last two years,” said Ed Breen, who voted for Fitzpatrick.

Strategists from both parties expect about 10 to 10.5 million voters to turn out, which would mean about 3 million will come today. Texas has reported at least 7.7 million votes so far, according to CNN’s analysis of early vote data.

DOJ sends criminal target letters in Alaska probe to people aligned with Dems

The Justice Department sent target letters to more than a dozen people it believes helped recruit a Senate candidate with the same name as the Republican incumbent, in Alaska — an indication that the department is working toward bringing federal charges, according to two sources familiar with the investigation.

The recipients include the leadership of the Elias Law Group, the law firm that represents both the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee and Democratic candidate Mary Peltola’s campaign, the sources said.

In a statement posted online, its chairman Marc Elias said, “No one should be surprised that President Trump’s Department of Justice is targeting me and my law firm on Election Day.

“We will not be intimidated by a sham investigation,” Elias said. “We are going to keep working around the clock to support our clients and make sure President Trump and his party enjoy a well-deserved beating at the ballot box.”

The target letters, which were largely sent to individuals aligned with the Democratic Party, are a remarkable move so close to Election Day.

Typically, the letters are sent to warn individuals that they may imminently face charges.

These letters also warn that the people must preserve documents, records, and communications while the investigation continues, a law enforcement official told CNN.

A New York Times report alleged that Peltola’s campaign worked with senior aides at the committee — which directs the party’s campaign strategy in Senate races — to find another Dan Sullivan to stave off votes from the Republican incumbent.

The incumbent is Dan S. Sullivan. The man running against him is Daniel J. Sullivan.

The Justice Department has dramatically accelerated the criminal probe since the report, CNN previously reported.

Court whiplash: Limits on DOJ warnings to election workers lifted

A new appeals court order Tuesday afternoon gives the administration the okay to warn election officials they could be prosecuted for not complying with its demands to change voting procedures.

The 6-5 ruling from the US 9th Circuit Court of Appeals is the third time, in less than 24 hours, the court has changed course on limits placed on the Justice Department by a district court judge last week that halted some of the administration’s most aggressive conduct toward election workers.

The latest order reinstates a decision that a three-judge panel issued Monday evening, wiping away the judge’s temporary restraining order. That order had blocked the administration from demanding that election administrators deviate from their usual processes and halted any efforts by the administration to intimidate or threaten those administrators. The DOJ denies that the letters sent to election offices — warning that it would prosecute officials who didn’t go along with its proposals for preventing noncitizen voting — amounted to threats. The full 29-judge court then voted less than three hours later to reinstate the TRO.

Tuesday’s “en banc” court — which was made up of 11 Circuit judges — did not pen a majority opinion to go along with the reversal. But a concurrence signed on by two of the judges said that courts generally do not “have the authority to enter an injunction prohibiting the Department of Justice from expressing its views about criminal statutes, even if the court determines that those views are incorrect.”

The dissenters, however, said the original panel opinion played down the threat and “ignores the reality that these ‘request[s]’ are made on pain of prosecution.”

“As the en banc court majority reinstates the stay on the morning of Election Day, state and local election officials and workers must choose between complying with two conflicting opinions of our court: follow our decision in California and risk prosecution by the United States or accede to the Defendants’ demands and risk violating state law,” the dissent said. “We should not force this impossible decision on election officials and workers on Election Day or the days that follow.”

The ruling is a notable win for the administration, which has seen most of efforts to meddle with election procedures run into a legal brick wall.

While the adminstration can now resume some of its pressure campaign on election officials, a big piece of it remains on hold due to a ruling overnight from a judge in DC. That judge blocked the administration from sending lists to election officials of people it claims to be noncitizens, for the purposes of demanding changes to voting protocols.

Westchester emergency ballots will get police escort to counting site, Lawler says

Emergency ballots cast in Westchester County, New York, will be picked up by bipartisan election officials and escorted by police to a counting location tonight, Republican Rep. Mike Lawler told reporters Tuesday.

Lawler, who is fending off a challenge from Democrat Cait Conley, said he was frustrated about widespread county issues affecting voters on Election Day, but he emphasized the error appeared to not have been “intentional.”

Westchester County voting machines have been unable to scan ballots due to a “printer error” in 332 polling locations across more than 40 municipalities, county officials said Tuesday.

“All of the ballots that have been cast already that are in the emergency orange bag will be transported from the polling site after 9 p.m. by police escort to a central location at the Board of Elections to be counted at the Board of Elections,” Lawler told reporters. “Those ballots will not be opened at the polling site, they will be opened at the Board of Elections, where attorneys from both campaigns will be, where both parties will have representation, so that there be no question, that there be no conspiracy theory, and that every vote is counted.”

The Board of Elections will have roughly 10 scanners, each able to process about 2,000 ballots an hour, Lawler told CNN’s Wolf Blitzer.

“It’s going to take a few hours,” he said, “but we expect to have results certainly into the wee hours of tomorrow morning.”

Lawler and Conley are submitting paperwork to court this afternoon to get a judge’s approval for tonight’s counting procedure, including the police escort, Lawler said.

“What I want to assure everybody is that every vote will be counted,” he said.

CNN’s Isa Mudannayake contributed to this post.

National Republicans scramble to shore up Louisiana Senate seat

National Republicans have been waging an 11th-hour effort to defend a Senate seat in Louisiana, another sign of how they’re on defense in the midterms in even solidly red territory.

The latest evidence: The Senate Leadership Fund, the top GOP super PAC in Senate races, spent more than $140,000 on Monday to boost the party’s nominee in Louisiana, Julia Letlow, according to a new campaign finance filing. It was the group’s first significant spending in the general election and went toward phone calls supporting Letlow.

In another signal of the race’s competitiveness, President Donald Trump held a telerally for Letlow on Monday evening. House Speaker Mike Johnson, who is from Louisiana, also joined the call.

Letlow faces Democratic nominee Jamie Davis in a state that Trump won by more than 20 points in 2024.

Letlow’s success is especially important to the president. He backed her in the primary when she was challenging GOP incumbent Sen. Bill Cassidy, one of the few Republicans left in Congress who sided against Trump in his 2021 impeachment.

Trump says the country is watching New York on Election Day

President Donald Trump has a message for New York voters on Election Day: The whole country is watching.

“TODAY, the eyes of the Country are on New York again!” Trump wrote in a Truth Social post, as he repeated his endorsement of Republican Rep. Mike Lawler, who is defending his seat in one of the country’s most competitive House races.

Lawler’s district covers Rockland County and part of Westchester County, where polling sites have had issues with voting machines today.

Trump to host White House viewing party after allies wanted to watch results with him

President Donald Trump will watch the midterm results come in with a small group of allies and Cabinet members in the East Room tonight, while the White House and Trump’s political operation, MAGA, Inc, have war rooms set up on campus and nearby to monitor the results, sources said.

One source said the viewing party came together in recent days, as Republican officials and allies told the White House that they hoped to watch the results with the president.

The scene will be reminiscent of how Trump watched the 2022 results. And while the circumstances could hardly be more different for Trump than they were in 2022, the outcome could be similar.

Back then, Trump, having finished his first term and departed Washington, was planning a political comeback, one that he believed would be boosted by candidates he endorsed posting massive wins.

In the final stretch of the race, Trump put himself on the ballot as he campaigned with his chosen candidates. On election night, he held a watch party at Mar-a-Lago and invited the press to watch what he and his advisers at the time believed could be a Republican landslide.

But as the night went on, it became clear that those candidates that had embraced the MAGA agenda flamed out in some of the most high-profile races. The press was quickly ushered out as Trump fumed at his staff over their role in helping choose the candidates.

Tonight, Trump will once again gather with top aides and advisers to watch the midterm results, this time as president of the United States, having completed the political comeback he sought. Yet, the White House is bracing for similar, potentially disappointing results.

Sources close to Trump said he has, at times, seemed hopeful that the polling was wrong, and that his party will be more successful than they suggest. Trump has told aides that he thinks his involvement — including campaign spending, telerallies, and trips to fundraise and promote Republican candidates — gave Republicans a fighting chance.

However, he has also privately conceded that his party may be facing the worst case scenario, the sources said. Two White House officials said that they would be ready for any scenario, including Trump choosing to speak to reporters as the results were coming in.

Cabinet members stay optimistic as they prepare for results at the White House

Members of President Donald Trump’s Cabinet have been filing through the West Wing today as aides and advisers prepare to absorb tonight’s results.

They have adopted an optimistic air as the voting unfolds across the country.

“Today is a big day,” Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins said after a television interview on the White House lawn.

“I don’t know that there will ever be a time in history that is marked with more success than what President Trump has led these last couple of years,” Rollins said before heading into the West Wing.

A few hours later, Interior Secretary Doug Burgum said he was “super optimistic about the future of the country and where it’s going,” and predicted positive economic news in the coming years.

He said he would spend the evening at the White House, where Trump is hosting a results watch party in the East Room.

Cabinet officials have been campaigning heavily over the past month for Republicans, looking to tout the administration’s accomplishments.

Burgum joined Trump Monday at his speech from the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, where the president mostly discussed his renovation projects around Washington.

Many Republicans were left scratching their heads at the choice of venue for Trump’s final speech before Election Day. But Burgum insisted it made perfect sense.

“It’s a perfect spot to basically say to America that just like closing the border was a choice, just like cutting taxes was a choice, just like cutting regulations was a choice, Americans do not have to accept decline and squalor in our major cities,” he said.

“You can choose to have a beautiful and clean city,” he went on. “Any mayor and any governor could do what President Trump did here in Washington, DC. It’s a role model for the country.”
