NRL great erupts at ‘shocking decision’ as tempers flare in controversy-filled Dolphins win

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:24 · source · permalink

The Dolphins have held on to narrowly defeat the Gold Coast Titans 24-20 on Friday night, giving their fans a scare a week out from their maiden finals series.

The Phins went into the contest undermanned, Selwyn Cobbo sitting out the week while Kodi Nikorima and Kulikefu Finefeauaki were late scratchings as Kristian Woolf’s side prepares for their maiden finals series.

It was quite the opposite for their Queensland counterparts, the Titans leaking 28 points in the first 26 minutes against South Sydney last weekend, and many tipping another heavy loss on Friday night.

It seemed the script was already written when the Dolphins nearly scored in the opening two minutes if not for a Jack Bostock error on the way through, turning the heat on Josh Hannay’s side early in the affair.

However it would be young superstar Jayden Campbell that would draw first blood in his 100th NRL game, soaring over Hamiso Tabuai-Fidow to snare a Lachlan Ilias bomb, and dot down for opening points.

The five-eighth has been a shining light in another subpar Gold Coast season, spending the majority of 2026 as the most experienced player in the Titans’ spine despite only just cracking the triple-digit club.

The home side ensured it was no fluke just three minutes later, a fumble in a Tevita Naufahu play-the-ball handing the Titans strong field position to double their advantage.

This time it would prove to be left-edge sweep delivering, Ilias’ deep pass to AJ Brimson giving the centre enough time to burn Bostock and slide over for an early 12-0 lead.

It could prove to be Brimson’s final game in Gold Coast colours after the club gave him permission to negotiate with rival clubs last month.

Parachuting Jake Averillo into the halves with Nikorima out, the Dolphins just couldn’t find an ascendancy with the ball in hand, giving away another two points after Max Plath was caught offside on the Titans’ line.

However, it took just one tackle to flip the contest on its head, Keano Kini’s second-effort to push Naufahu into touch was deemed a professional foul and sent the fullback to the sin-bin, just enough for the Phins to find their attacking prowess.

A deep pass from Katoa gave the Hammer enough space to dance through the defensive line for the Dolphins, and Woolf’s side would make it two tries in two sets after a dubious AJ Brimson error.

The Dolphins were handed a fresh set after referee Wyatt Raymond deemed Brimson dropped the ball in a contest, only for replays to reveal Bostock had fumbled the footy before Brimson had touched the ball.

“That’s a shocking decision,” Fox League’s Steve Roach said.

A simple unders ball from Averillo sent Herbie Farnworth through some subpar defence, cutting the Titans’ once-dominant lead back to just two points, however the Dolphins just couldn’t find any more gears once Kini came back on the field.

Whatever Krisitian Woolf told his side at half-time clearly worked, the Phins heading out of the sheds like a team possessed, tearing through the Titans’ ruck to score back-to-back near identical tries.

The first would come less than five minutes into the second stanza, an inside ball from Katoa handing Francis Molo a rare linebreak on halfway, the front-rower drawing Kini at the back before sending the retiring Ray Stone over for just his 12th career four-pointer.

If it ain’t broke, don’t fix it.

This time an inside ball from Kurt Donoghoe to Tabuai-Fidow sent the Maroons’ flyer into the backfield, racing around Kini to dot down for a double, and hand his side a sudden 10 point lead.

It could have easily become a cricket score if not for Dolphins own undoing, Bostock dropping two attacking bombs close to the line, before Naufahu bombed an absolute certain try after bobbling the put down in the corner.

Connelly Lemuelu seemed to celebrate his return with a meat pie, only for the Bunker to find a double movement in the process, keeping the Titans’ slim hopes alive.

And just as he’s done all season, Campbell did what he could to keep his side in the fight.

This time it would be a sloppy pass from Katoa straight into the lap of the milestone man, the five-eighth dashing 80-metres to slash it back to a one-score affair.

After heading into the clash as massive underdogs, the Titans had more than a sniff, coming within a blade of grass of a match-winner after Kini grounded a grubber on the dead-ball line.

Tempers threatened to explode in the final minute with Moe Fotuaitka finishing his season in the sin-bin after a scuffle with Tom Gilbert.

Despite finishing with just six wins, it’s a positive year for the Titans, who seemed to have locked in some key young components for Josh Hannay to build on.

The Dolphins seem set to finish third with the Roosters naming an understrength side for their clash against South Sydney, meaning they’ll play either the Warriors or Penrith in the opening round of the finals.

KATOA STRUGGLES WITHOUT KODI

Chalk it up to thinking about next week, chalk it up to missing his partner in crime, Isaiya Katoa wasn’t himself against the Titans.

The young halfback threw multiple intercept passes, gave away a penalty for back chat, and looked lost at times without his main No. 6 Kodi Nikorima on the left-edge.

Maybe he’s thinking about the finals a week early, but it’s a scary sight not only for next weekend, but also the 2027 season.

TITANS LOSE THE BATTLE, WIN THE WAR

The Gold Coast Titans did no worse than anyone expected in 2026, but geez this club has some big pillars to crow about this off-season.

A spine of Keano Kini, Jayden Campbell, Zane Harrison and Oliver Pascoe will only get better as time goes, and it appears almost all of the cogs are locked in alongside new coach Josh Hannay.

It’s a time way to finish the year, but things are only just beginning on the Gold Coast.
