October heat breaks records

TheTools · 12.10.2026 23:05 · source · permalink

DECATUR, Ill. (WAND) – Central Illinois is experiencing a significant departure from normal temperatures this week, with many areas already reaching 90 degrees—well above what we typically see in mid-October.

Springfield’s daytime high temperature peaked at 91 degrees today, breaking the previous record of 89 degrees back in 1962. This also makes it Springfield’s latest 90-degree day on record.

For perspective, our average high temperature for this time of year hovers in the upper 60s. Central Illinois usually experiences our last 90-degree day in mid to late September, making this week’s heat quite unusual for this time of year. Even overnight lows are running unseasonably warm in the low 70s. The last time temperatures reached the 90s in October was back in 2007. For Decatur specifically, the latest 90-degree day on record dates back to October 15, 1938– making today’s heat particularly noteworthy from a climate perspective.

Tomorrow will bring another day of unseasonable warmth with highs expected in the low 90s under continued southwest flow and abundant sunshine. However, this heat will be short-lived. Rain will return late tomorrow night, marking the end of the warm pattern and the beginning of a significant cooldown.

As rain continues through midweek, temperatures will gradually decrease. By the weekend, central Illinois will shift dramatically to fall-like conditions, with daytime highs dropping into the 50s and 60s. Overnight lows will cool enough to support patchy frost– a stark contrast to today’s summer-like heat.
