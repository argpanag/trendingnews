Storm Austen: Orange warnings issued as Ireland braces for torrential rain and wind

TheTools · 22.10.2026 23:07 · source · permalink

Emergency co-ordinators are planning daily meetings through the weekend as a double dose of storms approaches Ireland.

Storm Austen, due to hit Ireland early on Thursday evening and continue through Friday morning, is expected to be quickly followed by Storm Boelo on Saturday.

The latter is forecast to coincide with high tides, increasing the risk of flooding from high waves and storm surges, particularly along the west and northwest coasts.

“With two weather events now expected, local response teams will be very busy throughout the bank holiday weekend,” said Keith Leonard, head of the National Directorate for Fire and Emergency Management.

“The National Severe Weather and Flood Coordination Group will meet daily throughout the next few days to ensure the most effective response to any developments arising from the challenging weather conditions.”

The Irish Coast Guard, Water Safety Ireland and the RNLI have issued a joint appeal to the public to take extra care around coastlines and inland waterways over the next few days.

They have asked swimmers to avoid the water during the storms and to take extra precautions as the week goes on and daylight hours get progressively shorter.

ESB Networks has warned of possible electricity outages overnight as the first big storm of the season approaches Ireland.

Met Éireann has issued a number of status-orange weather warnings for intense rain and wind from around 7pm Thursday evening, continuing into Friday morning.

The progress of Storm Boelo is being closely monitored and more detailed warnings are due to issue tomorrow.

ESB Networks urged householders and businesses to “take necessary precautions” for Storm Austen.

“Prepare for possible power outages by having essential supplies on hand,” it warned.

It advised having a torch, battery operated radio, extra batteries and a fully charged phone on hand.

During or after the storm, it also warned people never to touch or approach any electricity wires that may have blown down.

Bus Éireann is also advising passengers of possible disruption to services.

“Met Éireann is forecasting difficult driving conditions as strong winds and heavy rain cross the country from this evening, and into the weekend as well,” said chief customer officer Allen Parker.

“There are also warnings about the possibility of flooding, debris and fallen trees on the roads.

“All Bus Éireann services are scheduled to operate as normal, however if any are impacted by the stormy weather, we will post details online as quickly as possible to keep our customers informed.”

Storm Austen is due to hit Ireland early on Thursday evening.

A status-yellow warning for Leinster and Munster for rain and wind will come into effect at 7pm on Thursday and be in place until 10am on Friday.

Counties Waterford, Cork and Kerry will have an orange warning for rain in place from 10pm on Thursday until 5am on Friday.

This warning will be extended to Dublin, Wexford and Wicklow from 2am on Friday until 8am.

People have been warned of flooding, disruption to public transport and difficult travel conditions. Outdoor events may be affected and people have been advised to be alert to debris and fallen trees or branches during the storm.

Weather conditions for the Dublin city marathon on Sunday are going to be blustery, Met Éireann meteorologist Gerry Murphy warned.

He said it is going to be wet “everywhere” in the country on Thursday as rain spreads northwards.

Local authority employees and other union members engaged in industrial action will work as required to deal with the impact of the storm.

Fórsa and Siptu, the two largest unions involved in work-to-rules as part of the public sector pay dispute, confirmed there were specific clauses for automatic derogations from industrial action in cases where there was a serious threat posed to life or wellbeing. Where derogations need to be requested, they are almost always granted immediately.

Dublin City Council said its flood response team was “preparing for the potential impacts of heavy rainfall across the city and has put measures in place to respond quickly should localised flooding occur”.

Storm Austen is the first named storm of the 2026/2027 season.
