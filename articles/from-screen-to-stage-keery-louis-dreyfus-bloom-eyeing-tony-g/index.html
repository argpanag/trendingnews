From Screen to Stage: Keery, Louis-Dreyfus, Bloom Eyeing Tony Gold | Daily Drama

TheTools · 01.11.2026 14:03 · source · permalink

The Great White Way is abuzz, and it’s not just the marquees lighting up Times Square. As Broadway’s 2024 season hits its stride, a trio of beloved screen stars – Joe Keery, Julia Louis-Dreyfus, and Rachel Bloom – are not just drawing crowds; they’re sparking serious chatter among industry insiders and awards prognosticators for potential Tony nominations.

It’s a familiar narrative: Hollywood stars crossing over to Broadway. But what makes this particular confluence so compelling is the nuanced, often unexpected, turns these actors are delivering, proving their mettle far beyond the comfort zone of a film set or TV soundstage. DailyDrama.com dives deep into why these performances are resonating and what their chances are for theatre’s highest honor.

Joe Keery’s Dramatic Revelation in ‘Appropriate’

For millions, Joe Keery is synonymous with Steve Harrington, the charismatic, surprisingly tender babysitter from Netflix’s global phenomenon, Stranger Things. His role on the hit sci-fi series cemented him as a fan favorite, but his Broadway debut in Branden Jacobs-Jenkins’ searing family drama, Appropriate, reveals a depth and intensity that has critics and audiences alike taking notice.

Keery joins a star-studded cast, including the formidable Sarah Paulson and the legendary Julia Louis-Dreyfus, tackling a complex portrayal of a young man grappling with his family’s dark legacy. Our sources close to the production note Keery’s commitment to the raw vulnerability of his character, a stark contrast to his more lighthearted screen roles. “He’s not just showing up; he’s truly inhabiting this role,” one Broadway veteran shared, praising his seamless integration into the ensemble. This isn’t just stunt casting; it’s a genuine theatrical performance that showcases Keery’s range and maturity as an actor, positioning him as a dark horse contender in the crowded Best Featured Actor in a Play category.

Julia Louis-Dreyfus’s Triumphant Broadway Debut

It feels almost impossible that Julia Louis-Dreyfus, a titan of television comedy with a staggering 11 Emmy Awards for her iconic work on Seinfeld and Veep, is only now making her Broadway debut. Yet, in Appropriate, Louis-Dreyfus proves that her comedic genius translates effortlessly to dramatic weight, offering a performance that is both razor-sharp and deeply affecting.

Playing the eldest sibling, Antoinette ‘Toni’ Lafayette, Louis-Dreyfus navigates a minefield of family dysfunction, racial tension, and personal grief with an understated power. Her ability to infuse even the most tense moments with a palpable, almost suffocating, sense of reality, while still hinting at the comedic timing that made her famous, is a masterclass. Industry buzz suggests she’s a strong contender for Best Actress in a Play, a category where her star power combined with critical acclaim could prove unstoppable. Her transition from the meticulously crafted world of TV sitcoms to the raw immediacy of live theatre has been nothing short of triumphant.

Rachel Bloom’s Unfiltered Solo Show

From the whimsical, often dark, musical comedy of Crazy Ex-Girlfriend to a one-woman Broadway show, Rachel Bloom has consistently defied categorization. Her current offering, Rachel Bloom: Death, Let Me Do My Show, is a deeply personal, laugh-out-loud, and surprisingly poignant exploration of grief, mental health, and the absurdity of existence, all delivered with Bloom’s signature blend of wit and vulnerability.

Solo shows are a unique beast on Broadway, demanding an incredible amount of stamina and raw authenticity. Bloom, however, thrives in this intimate setting, connecting with the audience on a profound level. Critics have lauded her ability to weave intricate musical numbers with stand-up comedy and deeply personal anecdotes, creating a theatrical experience that is both entertaining and genuinely moving. Given the success of similar solo performances at the Tonys in recent years – think John Mulaney or Alex Edelman – Bloom’s fearless and genre-bending show positions her as a compelling nominee for Best Special Theatrical Event or even Best Book of a Musical (if eligible for that category), showcasing her multifaceted talents as a performer and writer.

The Star Power of Broadway: A Winning Formula?

The influx of high-profile actors like Keery, Louis-Dreyfus, and Bloom is part of a larger trend on Broadway. Producers understand the box office draw of a recognizable name, but awards season buzz requires more than just celebrity; it demands genuine artistry. Historically, performers like Bryan Cranston (All the Way, Network), Denzel Washington (Fences), and Laurie Metcalf (A Doll’s House, Part 2, Hillary and Clinton) have seamlessly transitioned from screen to stage, earning critical accolades and Tony Awards along the way.

What differentiates this crop of potential nominees is their willingness to challenge audience expectations. Keery sheds his teen idol image, Louis-Dreyfus reveals dramatic prowess, and Bloom strips bare her soul. This commitment to the craft, rather than merely cashing in on fame, is what resonates with Tony voters, who value authentic theatrical contributions.

What’s Next for the Road to the Tonys?

With nominations typically announced in late April, the next few weeks will be crucial. Broadway critics and awards bodies will continue to weigh in, and performances will be scrutinized. The buzz around Keery, Louis-Dreyfus, and Bloom is more than just fleeting industry chatter; it’s a testament to their powerful, often surprising, contributions to the current Broadway season. DailyDrama.com will be closely watching as these screen favorites aim for their place in Broadway history. Could this be the year they add a coveted Tony to their already impressive trophy shelves?
