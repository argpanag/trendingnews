I Tried Apple’s New Foldable iPhone Duo. Here’s How It Compares.

TheTools · 13.09.2026 05:51 · source · permalink
  1. Electronics
  2. Smartphones

By Brenda Stolyar

Brenda Stolyar is a writer covering consumer technology. Her beats include smartphones, tablets, and wearables.

For the better part of a decade, foldable phones were a novelty. Cool to look at but strange to use, with many serious flaws, they were also extremely expensive, so dismissing them was a no-brainer.

Lately, companies such as Samsung and Google have refined their models, and foldables no longer have the issues they once did. 

But they’ve only ever run Android — until now. 

Apple just announced the iPhone Duo, its own foldable phone. I got some hands-on time with the device at Apple’s iPhone launch event in Cupertino, California, this week. 

Though I do recommend foldable phones, and we even have a guide to the best foldables, they’re not for everyone. Although these devices now offer more durability, and they support more practical real-world uses, they’re still quite a bit pricier than standard smartphones. 

The iPhone Duo is similarly expensive. At $2,000, it’s comparably priced to foldables from Samsung, Google, and Motorola. But it differs from those phones in a few key ways.

How Apple’s iPhone Duo compares to other foldables

Apple typically waits for other companies to experiment with new technologies before entering the market with its own product. The iPhone wasn’t the first smartphone, the iPad wasn’t the first tablet, the Vision Pro wasn’t the first mixed-reality headset — the list goes on.

In my hands-on time with the iPhone Duo, I compared it against the Android foldables I’ve tested, including my current top pick, the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8. It has a similar passport-shaped design, but with rounded corners that make it more comfortable to hold. It also has a 5.4-inch cover display and a 7.6-inch main display (so it’s a bit smaller than an iPad mini).

The two displays also share the same 1.4:1 aspect ratio, which, as on the Galaxy Z Fold8, is best suited for scrolling through social media, streaming content, and reading ebooks. When the iPhone Duo is open, you can switch between portrait and landscape mode, as well. But Apple added a few features that Samsung’s model lacks, and the difference is noticeable.

App handoff is seamless. Anything you view on the cover screen seamlessly transitions to the larger interior display when you open the phone. I tried it with apps such as Messages, Maps, Notes, and Netflix. Though the Galaxy Z Fold8 also hands off content from the outer screen to the inner one, it does so with a noticeable lag compared with the iPhone Duo. Switching between the two displays on the iPhone Duo is more fluid and instantaneous — there’s even a neat little animation that happens when the phone hands off apps from one screen to the other.

However, unlike the Galaxy Z Fold8, which allows you to run up to three apps simultaneously on the main display, the iPhone Duo supports only two at a time. You can place them side by side in both landscape and portrait mode, and you can pair certain apps so that they open together each time. I don’t typically use more than two apps at a time, even when I test foldables that support more, but the option to take advantage of the larger display with more apps would have been nice.

The under-display camera isn’t distracting. The iPhone Duo has a camera hidden under the inner screen that appears only when you’re using it, such as for a video call. The Galaxy Z Fold8’s hole-punch cutout, in contrast, can be distracting, especially when you’re using the main display to watch TV shows or movies. 

The interior screen’s matte coating could be a game changer. All of the other foldables I’ve tested have had a glossy glass interior screen, but the iPhone Duo’s inner display is matte. This not only reduces reflection and glare but also helps to reduce the visibility of the crease. 

All foldable phones have a crease down the middle of the inner screen, but as on the Galaxy Z Fold8, the crease on the iPhone Duo is impressively subtle. Though you can feel the slight dip in the display with your finger, it’s difficult to see unless you’re really looking for it. I did spot it when tilting the display at a certain angle and looking at the phone from the sides, but when I was staring directly at the screen, it was practically invisible.

But I’m particularly excited about the matte-coated screen because it closely resembles the E Ink displays I love on e-readers and digital notebooks. With this paper-like screen and compact display, the iPhone Duo will be great for reading ebooks and for taking notes, drawing, or marking up documents with a stylus (the foldable iPhone will be compatible with the USB-C Apple Pencil). The only other 2026 foldable that currently offers stylus support is the Motorola Razr Fold. I appreciate having the option, especially on a display that’s basically a tablet.

The iPhone Duo has some cool camera features. One function, called Duo Preview, allowed me to see myself in the cover display while someone took a photo of me, which gave me the chance to readjust my pose in real time. And Kid Cue, similar to Google Pixel phones’ Made You Look feature, shows adorable Snoopy animations on the cover display to draw a child’s attention while you’re snapping photos.

It feels durable and high-end. The frame is titanium, with Apple’s Ceramic Shield 2 coating on the front and Ceramic Shield on the back. With an IP68 rating, the iPhone Duo is more durable than the IP48-rated Galaxy Z Fold8. The IP68 rating indicates that the iPhone Duo is fully dustproof and water-resistant; Google’s Pixel 10 Pro Fold and 11 Pro Fold are the only other foldables with the same durability. 

This means you can be less careful with the iPhone Duo than you would with some other foldable phones, treating it like you would any other iPhone. In my hands-on experience, the hinge didn’t feel as smooth as that of the Galaxy Z Fold8, but it was easy to open and close, just not with one hand. Its rigidity does let you open the iPhone Duo at more angles, which is useful when you’re typing or propping the phone up to stream a video.

The bottom line

Other companies have had years to improve their foldables. This is Apple’s first, and it’s a great start. The iPhone Duo appears to have everything we look for when we’re recommending a foldable, namely a durable, premium design, an easy-to-use interface, smooth app handoffs, and, most importantly, actually practical uses. The company may have delivered all of those features on the first attempt, whereas Android-phone manufacturers took years and multiple attempts to get it right. 

But I’ll have to see how the iPhone Duo fares in the real world, especially when it comes to performance and battery life. Based on my initial impressions, the iPhone Duo seems promising — I just wish it didn’t cost $2,000. I’ll have final thoughts after deeper testing when the phone goes on sale in October.
