Man City charges: Premier League clubs do not expect City to be in Premier League next season if appeal fails

TheTools · 06.10.2026 00:45 · source · permalink

Man City have lodged their appeal against an independent commission finding them guilty of all charges related to serious breaches of Premier League financial rules; if it fails, City's rivals believe that a points deduction is not sufficient if they return to the top-flight after one year

Chief Correspondent

Monday 5 October 2026 20:44, UK

Sympathy is in short supply at other clubs after an independent commission laid bare the scale of Manchester City's rule breaking across nine seasons in a devastating decision published last week.

As City's legal team prepare for their appeal and sanctions hearing after being found guilty of more than 100 breaches of the Premier League's rules, their rivals in England and across Europe believe "the punishment should fit the crime".

With the looming prospect of City taking their bid to clear their name all the way to the High Court, rival clubs are becoming increasingly concerned about the damage that is being done to the image and integrity of the Premier League, the cost of the dispute and what they see as City's failure to take any responsibility or accountability for their actions.

City are adamant they have done nothing wrong, insisting they are innocent and that "a comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence exists" to support their position as they lodged their appeal.

But even a cursory reading of the decision which was published by the independent disciplinary commission puts into sharp focus the scale of the task facing City's lawyers when an appeal hearing takes place before Christmas.

The commission's findings are about as serious as it gets when it comes to financial rule-breaking and the fact that it was carried out over nine seasons and distorted the competition means City's rivals are not expecting Enzo Maresca's team to be playing in the Premier League next season.

Many believe that a points deduction is not a sufficient punishment if it would lead to City spending just one season outside the top flight.

It is only natural for clubs to think of their own self-interest and City's relegation would mean one fewer club going down this season as well as removing one of the favourites from the title race and opening up another European place. Self-interest is not the over-riding consideration though.

The consensus appears to be that rules have to be respected by all clubs, no matter their size or status - and it would not be sufficient to hand City a punishment that would last only one season for rules which were broken across multiple seasons.

City's rival Premier League clubs will not be involved in the sanction hearing, which could take place while the appeal process runs its course.

The same three-person panel which heard the evidence at the 42-day hearing in 2024 and delivered its core decision last Tuesday will decide on sanctions after the Premier League, as the prosecutors, put forward their recommendation and City's lawyers argue their case.

It will be down to the Premier League board to decide what recommended sanction their lawyers should present to the panel.

In 2023, the Premier League argued that Everton deserved a 12-point deduction for one breach of the Profitability and Sustainability Rules.

Everton, who were in a desperate financial position at the time, eventually ended up with a six-point deduction after they appealed the original 10-point sanction.

With both sides allowed to appeal the sanctions, and City appealing the judgment, Premier League clubs are prepared for this marathon dispute to keep on running until at least next year.

Clubs have been trying to draw a line under the legal disputes which have become a feature of the English game as they try to move towards what some executives call "the NFL model" where the league speaks with one voice.

That will not happen until the final chapter of the 115 charges case is finally written.

Carra calls for swift sanctions

Sky Sports' Jamie Carragher has urged any potential sanctions against Man City to be "done this season", and also believes that any possible punishment "should go on for years and years".

The independent commission concluded more than £830m of owner funding was used to inflate revenues and enable the club to comply with Premier League and UEFA financial rules through a series of "sham" arrangements.

Speaking to Sky Sports News at the Legends of Football Awards, Carragher said: "Now that we know that they're guilty, yes, there's an appeal, but now that they've got a big guilty verdict initially, now I don't think anybody wants to wait.

"It has to be done this season, because Man City need to be affected for next season.

"I know the word at the moment is sham; it'll almost make the Premier League of this season, the title to be won would be a sham, if Man City went on to win that league with what we know has gone on.

"So it has to be done this season, and I think the punishment, whatever it should be, I don't think it can be a one-off punishment for one season, because this has gone on for years and years, so I think the punishment should go on for years and years."
