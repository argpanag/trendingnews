2026 U.S. House Election Results

TheTools · 01.11.2026 03:00 · source · permalink

The handful of House members who flipped the toughest seats in 2024 all face challenging races again this year. These are districts that changed party control in the last election excluding those that redistricted prior to the 2024 and 2026 cycles.

Usually, voters support the same party for their congressional and presidential candidates. But there are exceptions to that rule, and those exceptions usually make for some of the most competitive races in the country. Here are the districts that voted for Trump in 2024 and a Democratic House member.

Voters in all 435 congressional districts choose their representatives in the U.S. House every two years. Going into the 2026 elections, 219 members caucus with Republicans – just one more than the minimum required for a majority when all seats are filled. Holding onto a majority that slim could be tough for Republicans in a midterm year when voters tend to vote against the party in the White House.

Democrats’ path to dismantling the GOP majority depends on flipping the districts where they believe Republicans are most vulnerable. Republicans’ path depends on defending the seats they already represent and ousting Democrats running in Republican-leaning territory. For Democrats to flip the chamber, they need to win three more seats than they did in 2024.

16% of incumbents will not return to their seats next year, opening up a lot of races. Under the right circumstances, open seats with no incumbent running could be considered fair game for either party. With new names on the ballot, there’s an extra degree of uncertainty that sometimes creates room for a flip in an otherwise unlikely spot – especially in a wave election.
