El Niño: What wetter winters and drier summers could mean for Hampshire

TheTools · 21.09.2026 16:52 · source · permalink

The sun is shining; it’s a beautiful September day – no wind, real warmth, sitting, absorbing the evening sun – yes, it's getting dark earlier – but the current run of weather makes you feel that all is well with the world. Yet lurking in the wings there is a thing called El Niño.

Apparently, all set to make the next few months even more unpredictable than our normal weather.

The press and TV have been full of coverage of the possible effects of this long-term weather phenomenon – readers may have seen a column I wrote on our changing climate. So, I thought I would do a little research and try to understand what it means - including how it could affect our lovely County.

El Niño is the periodic warming of the Pacific Ocean that gently changes our global weather patterns; it is expected to influence Western Europe, and Hampshire will apparently feel some of its effects. It won’t be overnight, but enough that we may notice gentle shifts in the seasons. It acts like a small push tilting our weather towards wetter, milder winters and warmer, drier summers.

For us that means the coming winter could bring even more steady rainfall. Hopefully not the dramatic downpours of a storm season, but the steady, lingering wet that makes even the current hard soil soft, fills ditches and keeps the chalk streams running high. Villages across the Test Valley may see footpaths muddier for longer and the kind of constant depressing grey drizzle that lingers. This will probably affect farmers with their winter planning, and we may all notice the ground staying saturated well into early spring.

By contrast, and we have clearly seen it this year, summer is likely to lean the other way: longer dry spells, higher temperatures and no doubt another hosepipe ban. Hopefully Southern Water is planning ahead. We are clearly aware of the effect of this in our gardens, but it is the wildlife — especially birds, hedgehogs, etc. — who may feel the strain of hotter, drier conditions.

The experiences of other countries vary widely. It can trigger difficult storms, but if we are prepared, it may just seem like another batch of British winter weather. Gentle shifts may happen, and they are manageable if we ALL prepare sensibly. Small actions taken across Hampshire can make a noticeable difference.

On reading the research, simple jobs like clearing gutters and checking downpipes before winter rain arrives prevent overflow and damp . Using water wisely in summer, from fixing dripping taps to watering gardens -assuming a ban is no longer in place - early in the morning, reduces the strain on local supplies. Thinking seriously about what we plant, planting drought-tolerant species such as lavender and rosemary makes gardens more resilient and less thirsty. Hampshire’s landscape — its chalk streams, rolling farmland and historic villages — depends on a delicate balance of weather and water.

El Niño nudges that balance. With a little preparation and the usual Hampshire community spirit, we should, like in previous years, take these changes in our stride. The message seems to be simple: El Niño is not a threat, just a reminder that small, thoughtful, timely acts help keep Hampshire resilient, whatever the weather decides to do next.

It is also as if we needed yet another reminder that our precious climate is changing!
