Brendan Cummins had a busier sporting weekend than most, lining out in goal for both his hurling and soccer clubs on successive days at the age of 51.

The Tipperary legend helped Ballybacon/Grange preserve their intermediate hurling status on Saturday before returning between the posts for local soccer club Suirside just 24 hours later.

As reported by Tipperary Live, Cummins played alongside his son Paul in Ballybacon/Grange’s 0-20 to 1-14 victory over Clonoulty/Rossmore in the county intermediate hurling relegation final.

Paul contributed eight points, seven of them from frees, as Ballybacon/Grange secured their place in the grade for 2027.

Remarkably, it was the third consecutive year the club had avoided relegation to Junior A by winning the relegation final.

For Brendan Cummins, the satisfaction afterwards was accompanied by plenty of relief.

It’s our three-in-a-row survival and we are glad to get out of it again. 

In fairness, our forwards had a ten-minute spell in the second half when we got three or four points in a row and the backs held out at the finish. Yeah, a lot of relief on an occasion like this.

Cummins credited the squad’s football commitments with helping them find the energy needed to get through another important survival battle.

There was little time for the veteran goalkeeper to put his feet up.

On Sunday, Cummins was back in goal for Suirside in the Second Division of the Tipperary Southern and District League.

That outing ended in a narrower defeat, with Two Mile Borris B winning 1-0.

The results were mixed, but getting through two games in two days at any age is no mean feat never mind at 51 with a life-time of serving Tipperary and his local clubs already under his belt.
