'A party' Why Man City fans are taking inflatable bananas to Anfield

TheTools · 11.10.2026 05:00 · source · permalink

The last two weeks have been among the most serious in Manchester City's history, found guilty by an independent commission of systemic cheating across nine years that tarnishes all of their achievements since their 2008 takeover.

City insist on their innocence and will appeal, but it casts a cloud over the whole game as they prepare to return to club football at Anfield on Sunday.

One response from the club's fans will be visibly on display at Liverpool this weekend, and emerged from another dark period in the English game. Following the guilty verdicts, the inflatable bananas are making a comeback.

There's an argument that they never went away, but given the events of the last few weeks the 1894 fan group has made sure that one of the most weird and wonderful associations with City supporters will be front and centre for the cameras on Sunday. And that will be enough to bring a smile to the face of at least one ex-player.

Imre Varadi quickly endeared himself with Blues after signing from West Brom in 1986, and scored a quarter of the team's goals in a disappointing season that saw them relegated from the top flight. They had hopes of bouncing straight back up and Varadi headed out onto the Maine Road pitch in November 1987 to warm up for a game when he was taken aback by something in the stands.

"I came jogging out one evening and someone was waving a banana. They started to call me Imre Banana and I'm thinking what?! The following game, there were 25-30 bananas and everyone was calling me Imre Banana," he told the Manchester Evening News this week.

"It grew and grew that season and just went crazy and then all the other football clubs started - Norwich had inflatable canaries, West Ham had an inflatable hammer. It just took off that season, it was incredible, and the craze is still going now. I don't know whether they couldn't say Varadi but it was just a craze that I'm proud of and is still going to this day. Even when the club was building the extra seating at the Etihad, there were bananas on billboards.

"It's the fun factor, and anything that brings the fun back into football is great. There was a lot of trouble in that era but we brought back the fun in football."

The bananas emerged from an interesting environment. There was a lot of soul-searching in English football given the hooliganism that plighted the 1980s and the Heysel disaster in 1985 that led to all English teams being banned from European competitions for five years. There were clear divisions in the country with Conservative leader Margaret Thatcher as prime minister for the entire decade, more tension and violence from the Troubles in Northern Ireland, as well as the Falklands War in 1982 and the ongoing Cold War splitting up the world.

And yet, in Manchester there was also the heyday of the Hacienda and plenty of Blues would spill out of there and head for Maine Road for an iconic double-header of a weekend. When City fan Frank Newton took an inflatable banana to a game to win a bet, somebody shouted up Imre Banana and the craze was born.

Nobody really understood it, but it was fun and that was enough for it to spread not just across the City fanbase but across a country of like-minded people who had been made to feel that football was some great societal ill and should spend time feeling sorry about every wrong that had happened. Instead, supporters took an inflatable of their choice to the game and started to have fun again.

City fans had a riot on their last away day when their 10 men were victorious in the derby at Old Trafford, but the guilty verdicts have wiped the smiles from their faces. They have had to challenge their own support for the club they love, and have had their different responses judged by those commenting on the cheating.

What's more, there have been discussions about how City fans and players will - or even should - be treated at visiting grounds given the stain on the club, and Anfield is a high-profile start given there have been several violent incidents at fixtures in recent years. The decision by City fans to take a load of inflatables on Sunday, then, is a defiant gesture that sets out their position.

A member of the 1894 Group told the Manchester Evening News: "It's to remind everyone it doesn't matter what league we end up in, we'll always be there. It's daft, it's a bit of fun. The City fans are going there for a party no matter how much the media ramps it up."

The bananas may be impossible to miss at Anfield and one man who will be doubly delighted to see them out in force is Varadi. Having kept seeing them at City games, he decided a few years ago to trademark the name Imre Banana (imrebanana.com) and set up a company to sell the inflatables; the club and kit sponsors Puma have even been on board since the beginning of last season, selling them in official shops.

"There's always humour with the City supporters, which is great," said Varadi. "I'm looking forward to seeing all the bananas in the stand at Anfield. It will be a bit like a carnival, I'm sure."
