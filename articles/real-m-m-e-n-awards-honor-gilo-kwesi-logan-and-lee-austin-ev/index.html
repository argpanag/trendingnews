Real M.M.E.N. awards honor Gilo Kwesi Logan and Lee Austin - Evanston RoundTable

TheTools · 15.09.2026 23:23 · source · permalink

Each September since 2008, the Joe “Butch” Martin (JBM) Fund has presented Real M.M.E.N. (Men Meeting Evanston’s Needs) awards to African American males who uphold Martin’s philosophy of sharing whatever he had with those in need and enhancing the lives of everyday people.

The Real M.M.E.N. Awards were held Sept. 12 at Evanston Township High School in The Terrace, where members of the Martin family presented the honors.

The memorial fund honors men who give their time, resources, skills, love and assistance without the expectation of recognition or compensation. Managed by the Evanston Community Foundation, the fund also awards an annual grant to a local nonprofit.

The 2026 Real M.M.E.N. awards went to Gilo Kwesi Logan, an author and internationally recognized equity consultant, and Lee Austin, whose work experience in the private and public sector includes servicing airline computing systems at Raytheon and 22 years of working at ETHS as the safety staff/events coordinator.

Logan received the award “for his unwavering work to create a more just and equitable community.” He is a fifth-generation Evanstonian who has taught middle school students and adult learners. He has a master’s degree in elementary education and a Ph.D. in adult education, both from National Louis University. In January, he published Protecting a Dream: The Life and Legacy of Chief Bill Logan and His Historic Moments with Dr. King, a book about his father, Evanston’s first Black police chief, Bill Logan. He is also the founder of the educational, social and cultural program S.O.U.L. Creations.

Austin received the award for his commitment to mentoring young people, including participants in the Fellowship of Afro American Men (FAAM) Basketball League and at ETHS. He attended Northwestern University before beginning a career in computer programming. He started a new vocation when he joined the ETHS Safety staff. Austin is a past recipient of the “African American Educator Award” at Second Baptist Church in Evanston, where he serves as a deacon. He has also been recognized with the “Those Who Excel” Award, given by the Illinois State Board of Education.

The JBM Fund also awards an annual grant to nonprofit organizations that provide direct services to community members in need, focusing on senior citizens and children. The fund awarded its 2026 service grant to the NAACP Evanston Youth Council.

The 2026 grant funds provided hotel accommodations for the NAACP Youth Council delegation to attend the 117th National Convention in Chicago, helping remove financial barriers and ensuring the experience was accessible for Youth Council members.

Martin read a thank-you letter from the NAACP Youth Council expressing gratitude and outlining the far-reaching impact of the grant funds. “Because of your support, our students were able to participate in nationally recognized leadership workshops, civic engagement sessions, youth programming and network opportunities that will influence them for years to come. Throughout the convention, our youth gained valuable knowledge about civil rights advocacy, public service, higher education, financial empowerment and leadership, while representing Evanston with pride.”
