Skill-game group seeks to intervene, urging extra 120 days as machines are removed

HARRISBURG, Pa. (WJAC) — Pennsylvania lawmakers and a group representing skill-game businesses have now filed separate requests asking the state Supreme Court to delay enforcement of its ruling that classified the machines as illegal slot machines.

State Sens. Gene Yaw and Anthony Williams, along with Rep. Jonathan Fritz, filed a petition Wednesday asking the court to extend its current stay through Feb. 10, 2027. They also want the court to keep the stay in place while it considers their request.

The lawmakers say they are working on compromise legislation to license, tax and regulate skill games. Their filing argues that widespread seizures before a new law is enacted would complicate inspections, licensing and the state’s ability to begin collecting revenue.

Separately, the Skill Games Business Consortium has asked to intervene in the underlying case. If permitted to join, the group says it would seek an additional 120-day extension, arguing lawmakers need more time to reach an agreement.

The consortium says businesses are already removing or disabling machines as the Oct. 13 deadline approaches. It warns that social clubs, VFWs, American Legions, small businesses and municipalities could face financial losses if enforcement begins before lawmakers act.

The Supreme Court ruled in June that skill-game devices are subject to the Gaming Act and state criminal law, but put enforcement on hold for 120 days. The court has not yet ruled on either new request.
