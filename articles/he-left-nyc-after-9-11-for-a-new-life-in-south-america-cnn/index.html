In our roundup of travel stories this week: The Englishman who walked around the world, why Norway is making toilets “great again,” plus a New Yorker driven to start life over in Argentina in the wake of 9/11.

Finding solace in Mendoza

David English planned to sign a “career-clinching contract” on the morning of September 11, 2001. An entrepreneur, English worked in Lower Manhattan, and the move was going to change his life.

Instead, he woke up to the terror attacks that changed the city and the lives of so many. English watched fighter jets flying over his New York apartment, and endured weeks of “false alarms and alerts” while breathing in the fumes drifting over from the site.

So a year later, he abandoned his American life – and the “horrible climate of fear” that took over the US after 9/11 – to start anew in Argentina.

And he never left, settling in Mendoza. Now he has a son – who was born in Argentina and wouldn’t have been born had it not been for the fateful decision English made in the wake of the attacks. Read about the solace he found in Mendoza and the life he built there.

Destination inspiration

Prefer to explore the world just one city at a time? Then we have some recommendations for you.

Busan is South Korea’s second-largest city and is a laid-back alternative to the bustle of the capital. In the words of one travel expert, “It offers something totally different — it’s by the coast, has beaches, a slower-paced way of life, and the people are much more vivacious.”

In Colorado, Aspen is famous the world over for its glitz, glamour and top-tier skiing. However, its 20th-century roots as a countercultural hub are still going strong.

“Freaks are people that don’t want to live in the traditional, cookie-cutter lifestyle of suburban America,” says DJ Watkins, a local author, curator and gallerist. “And Aspen has always attracted people like that.”
