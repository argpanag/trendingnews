Microsoft outage live updates: ‘Encouraging recovery progress’ in global outage that has impacted millions | CNN Business

TheTools · 13.11.2026 04:38 · source · permalink

Where things stand

• Making headway: Microsoft said it is seeing “encouraging recovery progress” in an hourslong outage that has affected millions around the world. The tech giant warns some users may still “experience intermittent issues.”

• What’s been impacted: The outage, which began around noon ET on Thursday, left workers unable to access Outlook email, Teams meeting tools and other staples of modern office life. AI companies said they were facing issues as well. Banks, governments, health services, railways and courts all reported disruptions across Microsoft products. The outage was not caused by a cybersecurity breach, the company has said.

• Global disruption: Outage reports “reached 74 countries and territories in about an hour,” according to Downdetector. While some workers scrambled to find workarounds, others expressed relief in a respite from the work grind.

Updates

Microsoft seeing "broader improvements" in North America

Microsoft just said it is seeing “encouraging recovery progress” at the same time as some social media users and CNN journalists report receiving emails in their inboxes again.

In a statement on X, the company reported “email delivery performance improving across a growing portion of affected Microsoft 356 infrastructure,” but warned some users may still “experience intermittent issues.”

While some social media users reported getting emails again, others said they were still unable to access them.

In an additional update issued later, Microsoft outlined the uneven pace of recovery in different regions. The company indicated that North America was recovering faster than Europe and Asia.

It reported “broader improvements in North America, sustained improvements across much of Europe, and encouraging recovery signals in Asia Pacific.”

UK police forces urge public to report crimes via telephone, not online

Several UK police forces told the public Friday to report crimes or issues by telephone rather than through their websites amid the Microsoft outage.

Police forces in Merseyside, Greater Manchester and Bedfordshire informed visitors to their websites that online submission forms were disrupted.

East Hertfordshire Police said in a statement posted to X that “some online form submissions on our website may be affected” because of the outage.

In another Friday post on X, Dorset Police said it was “unable to receive online form submissions.”

All police forces urged the public to call 101 if their issue is “time-critical but not an emergency.” (The UK emergency number is 999).

Microsoft says services are being "gradually restored"

Microsoft says it is making “additional progress toward recovery” and that traffic is “being gradually restored” as its engineers scramble to fix the worldwide outage that has left millions without the work tools they use every day.

In a statement, Microsoft said it is “seeing improvements in key scenarios, including mailbox access and delayed email processing, though some intermittent issues may continue.”

What to know as you're waking up in the US without email

Outlook is still down, as Microsoft’s rolling outage – now extending into a second day – continues to affect services for millions of people around the world.

The outage, which is also affecting Teams and Copilot, is disrupting government and business operations in the United States and elsewhere, with at least one company postponing a quarterly earnings call.

Microsoft executivesCharles Lamanna said in the early hours of Friday that there is currently no indication that the outage is the result “of malicious or cyber activity.”

Here’s what to know:

Email down: Outlook remains down and users are also having trouble accessing their Exchange Online mailboxes.

In an update at around 4:15 a.m. ET Friday, Microsoft said it would extend its “email timeout window” to 48 hours “to help protect delayed messages while service recovery continues.”

The email timeout window is the maximum amount of time that a mail server or email client such as Outlook will spend waiting to receive an email before returning an error.

AI companies affected: OpenAI said it was facing issues with Microsoft connectors, with some tools, including ChatGPT, not working “as expected.” Meanwhile, thousands of Claude AI users reported issues Friday morning, according to Downdetector. Claude creator Anthropic said shortly after 6 a.m. ET that a fix had been deployed and it was “monitoring for recovery.”

US lawmakers: The US House of Representatives said the outage impacted its users and its engineers were working with Microsoft, according to Reuters. Some congressional staffers expressed frustration on X. “Shall we start conducting all official business via DM?” said Sharon Eliza Nichols, communications director for Congresswoman Eleanor Holmes Norton.

US cities: City governments across the United States have reported email disruptions and have encouraged residents to call local offices and agencies if necessary.

  • Several police and sheriff departments said the outage was affecting their email and communication platforms, including Colorado’s Arapahoe Sheriff’s Office, which said the outage had delayed the processing of prisoners’ bail payments.
  • The outages are affecting email services in Miami Beach, Austin, and Jacksonville, though officials assured residents that emergency services were still active.
  • In California, officials from Pasadena, Fremont, Burbank, and Santa Barbara also issued statements warning of downed email use.
  • Baltimore County officials said on X that government staff could not send or receive emails and urged residents to contact relevant departments by phone.

Earnings call delayed: Workhorse Group, a Detroit-based electric vehicle manufacturer, pushed its third quarter earnings call to Monday, citing “business disruptions caused by the global Microsoft outages.”

CNN’s Gage Goulding contributed reporting.

What is affected by the outage?

Some office workers rely on Microsoft’s suite of applications for practically everything – emails, meetings, AI, creating spreadsheets, and writing and sharing documents. Any of those functions which involve email or Microsoft’s Exchange Online services are impacted, the company said.

Outlook: Email is the main thing impacted by the outage, leaving users likely unable to access or send any new emails.

Copilot: Users may also be unable to load chats with or receive responses from Microsoft’s AI tool. Copilot also cannot access the data it normally draws from, like emails, files and calendars.

Teams: Anything that has already been scheduled and requires Teams — Microsoft’s communications platform — to connect with a user’s calendar is likely impacted. But if users are simply just joining a call or exchanging messages, that is still working.

Word/Excel/Powerpoint: The basic functions of these apps are still working. However, other functions may be impacted, like if users are trying to print documents over the cloud or share documents with Sharepoint. Users may also not be able to view their most recently used files.

Other, more specialist Microsoft services – like those which pool a company’s data or allow IT to monitor a company’s devices – may also be affected.

It's a disappointing day for some workers using Gmail

While many workers using Microsoft’s systems have spent Friday rejoicing that they’re forced to take a break — unable to send or receive emails or conduct their usual Teams calls — those using Google services are lamenting in memes shared on social media.

Google systems including Gmail, Google Docs and Google Meet appear to be functioning just fine, or at least well enough to promise users an average working Friday.

One video on Instagram shows someone lying in bed with the word “Outlook” above their head. Another sits at a desk working on their laptop with the word “Gmail” above theirs.

Another video shows a meme of actor Pedro Pascal appearing to sob uncontrollably under text reading: “When you hear Outlook is down and start planning your afternoon wines… Then remember your workplace uses Google Workspace.”

Railways report communications delay due to Microsoft service disruption

Some railway networks are experiencing disruptions to their customer email services and routing schedules because of the Microsoft outage.

VIA Rail Canada said passengers “may not be able to receive email communications about their trip” due to technical issues with Microsoft services. The railway asked passengers to check their web-based train tracker or subscribe to SMS notifications for information on their journeys.

Germany’s Deutsche Bahn said Microsoft Outlook is experiencing “severe sluggishness,” causing significant delays in its abilities to send out and receive emails.

“As a result, the distribution of rerouting schedules is severely restricted. All relevant teams are involved in resolving the issue; current indications point to a server-side problem on Microsoft’s end,” it said in a statement.

In the UK, National Rail, which runs passenger rail services in England, Scotland and Wales, said the ongoing outage may be affecting customers’ ability to access train tickets by email.

It advised travelers to visit the website or app where tickets were purchased, or to contact the ticket retailer directly.

“If you are still unable to access your ticket, please contact a member of onboard staff or station staff for advice,” National Rail said on its website.

Rail Delivery Group, which manages National Rail, said there were no current disruptions to operations or train services as a result of the outage.

CNN’s Nina Avramova contributed to this report.

Microsoft extends email timeout window to 48 hours amid outage

Microsoft said that it would extend its email timeout window to 48 hours “to help protect delayed messages while service recovery continues,” according to an update posted at around 4:15 a.m. ET Friday.

The email timeout window is the maximum amount of time that a mail server or email client such as Outlook will spend waiting to receive an email before returning an error.

Microsoft said recovery efforts are ongoing and that it’s seeing “early signs of stabilization,” though service capacity has not returned to normal.

It's Friday and Microsoft is still down. (Some) workers are taking it easy

Memes and jokes abounded online as the global outage of Microsoft’s Outlook emails and Teams meeting platform crossed the 12-hour mark.

One joke that spread on Threads, X and LinkedIn was the rebrand of Microsoft365 to Microsoft364 – memorializing the one day of the year that the software company went dark.

Others reveled at not needing to respond to constant emails and notifications for at least one day.

Another account wrote: “Outlook is down. Your email does not find me well. It does not find me at all.”

Others on Reddit said they panicked and thought they were laid off when they could not access their work emails.

Here's where we're seeing the outage's impacts

At first, Microsoft said the connection issues were happening “in the Americas,” but over time it became clear the troubles were global.

Microsoft’s outage reports “reached 74 countries and territories in about an hour,” according to Downdetector, a website that tracks cyber outages.

Here’s where we’ve seen reports of outages:

  • Australian schools, courts and government services started their Friday morning with Microsoft Outlook and other apps out of service.
  • In Hong Kong, workers from banks including Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs told CNN their work was disrupted, as they were unable to access emails or Teams from the moment they signed on and all through their lunch break. “Sporadically some mails come in but there is probably millions of backlog to process,” one banker told CNN before heading off for lunch break.
  • The disruption has also hit Japan’s government services, Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary Masanao Ozaki said in a Friday presser. Government employees are responding to the outage by creating physical copies of documents, he added. Government ministries and agencies have been unable to send emails through most of the Friday workday.
  • Courts in the Philippines are also being slowed down by the Microsoft disruption, the Supreme Court said. “During this period, responses to emails and other transactions may be delayed,” it added in a post on X.
  • In the UK, a disruption to the National Health Service email system is expected to prevent some patients from getting their drug prescriptions.

CNN’s Brian Stelter, Hilary Whiteman and Junko Ogura contributed to this report.

Britons can expect delays in accessing prescribed medication today, says UK pharmacy group

A Microsoft outage causing disruptions to the email system of the UK’s National Health Service is expected to prevent some patients from getting their drug prescriptions Friday.

The outage “affects a pharmacy’s ability to communicate with GP practices and some patients,” a spokesperson for the National Pharmacy Association said in a Friday statement.

GPs are equivalent to family doctors in the US.

An NPA spokesperson told CNN in a separate phone call that, while many patients will get their medications, those with more complex prescriptions requiring a pharmacist to contact the prescribing doctor to check or query any details could experience delays.

The spokesperson added that Fridays are typically busy days at pharmacies.

“It’s just a massive pain. It will be slow today,” they said.

Microsoft reports "marginal success" in patching servers

More than 12 hours after Microsoft first warned of a global outage affecting some of its most popular products, the company said it had “observed marginal success” in fixing the problem.

“We’re continuing to monitor activity across regions to evaluate server status and determine actions that can be taken to improve capacity,” Microsoft said in a statement posted on its Service Health Status page early Friday.

“At this point, we have observed marginal success,” the company wrote.

Around 30 minutes later, Microsoft posted another statement saying the company has seen “positive results for service stability and increased capacity as a result of our latest traffic optimization actions.”

Reiterating what it said earlier Thursday, Microsoft stated it was not aware of any malicious activity causing the outage but will continue to treat the incident “with urgency and priority until full resolution.”

More than 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps and more than 90% of Fortune 500 companies use its Copilot AI chatbot, according to the company’s website.

The company reported “marginal success” in repairing the outage in its latest update, early Friday ET. A Microsoft executive confirmed the outage was not the result of a security breach.

As Europe woke on Friday, the outage remained widespread.

CNN’s Helen Regan, Hilary Whiteman, Karina Tsui, Kathleen Magramo and Ross Adkin contributed reporting.

Some airlines report comms disruptions due to Microsoft outage

Several airlines are reporting disruptions to their email support and communications due to the Microsoft outage, posting statements to customers on social media.

Indian budget carrier IndiGo said its “email support services are currently impacted, and we may not be able to receive your emails.” It directed customers to reach out via their website or mobile app.

Akasa Air, another low-cost Indian carrier, similarly said its “email support services are currently experiencing delays.”

And South African carrier CemAir said it was “experiencing some system disruptions due to the global Microsoft outage.”

None of the carriers reported flight disruptions.

Indian flag carrier Air India said it was “closely monitoring the ongoing Microsoft outage” and has rerouted emails. The airline said its flight operations “remain unaffected” and “there is currently no impact on any of our communication channels.”

Outage comes as Europe seeks to reduce reliance on US tech

The Microsoft outage comes at a time when Europe is pushing for greater tech sovereignty, aiming to run its own servers and reduce dependency on foreign providers.

Europe lacks the kind of mega tech companies that abound in the US, making it reliant on American firms for its digital services, but there is sentiment in Europe that it should become less reliant on the United States for its tech needs.

That sentiment has hardened during US President Donald Trump’s second term, when he has hit the continent with huge tariffs and threatened to take over the Danish territory of Greenland.

Earlier this year French Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said that government officials will ditch Zoom and other US-owned video conferencing tools and begin using French-owned software. The decision is intended partly to reduce “dependencies on non-European actors,” Lecornu said.

“The internet in Europe is essentially a US construct, a US system. And Europe doesn’t really have a competitor to any of that,” Neil Shearing, group chief economist at Capital Economics, told CNN earlier this year.

On Friday, that question resurfaced as Microsoft’s outage stretched beyond 12 hours, disrupting countries across the globe.

“That tech sovereignty conversation, while it’s been mostly political, I bet it’s real sharp today,” technology journalist and founder of The Rip Current podcast Jacob Ward told CNN.

CNN’s Anna Cooban contributed reporting.

US cities report disruptions to email services

City governments across the United States have reported widespread email disruptions since Microsoft’s global outage began and have encouraged residents to call local offices and agencies if necessary.

The outages are affecting inbound and outbound email services at local government offices across major cities, including Miami Beach, Austin, and Jacksonville, though officials assured residents that emergency services were still active.

Baltimore County officials said on X that government staff could not send or receive emails and urged residents to contact relevant departments by phone.

In California, officials from the cities of Pasadena, Fremont, Burbank, and Santa Barbara also issued statements warning of downed email use.

Several police and sheriff departments said the outage was affecting their email and communication platforms, including Colorado’s Arapahoe Sheriff’s Office, which said the outage had delayed bond payments for prisoners.

Scammers may take advantage of Microsoft outage, banks warn

Banks and financial institutions are warning customers to beware of scams and phishing attempts as cybercriminals may take advantage of the global Microsoft outage to target individuals and businesses.

“Stay alert and protect yourself,” Standard Chartered Singapore said on social media. “A technology outage can create opportunities for scammers.”

The Bank of Queensland said that scammers may ask for personal details or One-Time Passwords and warned customers to “hang up immediately” if they receive a suspicious call.

Customers have been advised to not click on links or open attachments from unknown sources, and never share PINs or passwords. Other banks have told their customers to be cautious of anyone claiming to be tech support personnel, and to report unauthorized transactions and suspicious messages.

Technology journalist Jacob Ward told CNN that the “the ripple effects” from the outage could “go on and on.”

Laura Sharman contributed reporting.

Outage forces US electric vehicle maker to postpone earnings call

The Microsoft outage has caused at least one American company to postpone its quarterly earnings call.

Workhorse Group, a Detroit-based electric vehicle manufacturer, pushed their Q3 earnings call to Monday, citing the technology outage.

Earnings calls are typically held virtually, either via a conference call or video webcast system, allowing company executives to discuss financial results with investors and analysts. Participants dial in by phone or join online.

Workhorse postponed its earnings call until Monday, November 16 at 4:30 p.m. ET.

Global outage not caused by cyber attack, Microsoft executive says

The global Microsoft outage was not caused by a cybersecurity breach, a Microsoft executive confirmed late Thursday.

“We will continue to share more information as soon as we have it, and for now I can confirm that there is no indication this is the result of malicious or cyber activity,” Charles Lamanna, executive vice president of Copilot, Agents, and Platform at Microsoft, said in a post on X.

Earlier, Australia’s Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said the company had told him that they “don’t believe they are dealing with a cyber attack.”

“They believe they are dealing in some ways similar to what we saw with respect to CrowdStrike where there is an update that has gone awry,” Burke added during a press conference in Sydney.

Burke was referring to the 2024 software glitch at cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike that crashed computers, canceled flights and disrupted hospitals all around the globe, causing billions of dollars in losses.

Australia’s spy agency and federal police were among the organizations hit by the Microsoft outage on Friday local time.

Outage shows global risks of companies relying on a handful of tech giants, experts say

The Microsoft outage highlights the “inherent dangers” of companies that depend on a handful of tech giants for critical global infrastructure, an expert told CNN.

“If one of those firms suffers a major outage, it has the potential to dramatically reduce business productivity globally during the outage time,” Computing Professor Toby Murray, of Australia’s University of Melbourne, said.

Murray added that the outage affecting Outlook and other major services like Microsoft Teams is “incredibly widespread, and its ramifications extend well beyond being able to send and receive work emails,” making it difficult for businesses to operate.

Mark Gregory, Associate Professor at Australia’s RMIT University School of Engineering, also said the outage highlights how dependent businesses and government agencies have become on technology providers.

it was “unthinkable” that a company like Microsoft could have a global outage after previous incidents.

Gregory was referring to the 2024 software glitch at cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike that crashed computers, canceled flights and disrupted hospitals all around the globe, causing billions of dollars in losses.

He highlighted the need for better contingency plans when critical services go offline.
