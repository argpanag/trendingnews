  1. Report: Antonelli wins as Norris loses out under virtual safety carpublished at 16:52 BST
    16:52 BST

    Andrew Benson
    BBC F1 correspondent in Madrid

    Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli won the Spanish Grand Prix after McLaren's Lando Norris lost out because of the bad timing of a virtual safety car.

    Norris was leading comfortably from pole position when the VSC was called on lap 15. The timing was terrible for the world champion - he had already passed the pit exit when the VSC was called.

    That meant Antonelli and Red Bull's Max Verstappen were able to pit under the VSC, saving time, while the caution period ended before Norris could make it around to the pits again.

    He did then come in but, to make matters worse, the stop was slow and he rejoined in fifth place.

    Norris was able to recover only to third around the new Madring track where overtaking is next to impossible.

    Antonelli's eighth win in 14 races this year puts an even tighter stranglehold on the championship for the Italian.

  5. Safety car 'should not define a whole race' - Norrispublished at 16:44 BST
    16:44 BST

    Third-placed Lando Norris speaking to Sky Sports on missing a pit stop during the safety car: "I need to speak to the team but it's not a guessing game. I was just unlucky, I was the only one on the grid that doesn't get a pit stop, so I lose 20 seconds on the whole grid. It's just out of my control, it's just out of their control. It is a rule that should not define a whole race.

    "A race should be won by the guy who can put in the best performance, who maybe takes a risk by going long in a stint. Maybe you get a safety car later on and things can help because you know that is one of the big factors. But for everyone to get a safety car, a VSC pit stop, it means nothing. It's unfair but it has happened in the past, others have won from it, I've probably won from it in the past.

    "I don't think many people have lost a race win over it so it is frustrating. I don't think it should be the way you lose. We have spoken about it in the past in drivers' briefings and said you should leave it [safety car] out for at least a lap, so it is equal for everyone. Today I was the only one who lost out and I lost out on about 20 seconds of race time and that is not how it should be."

  6. Antonelli 'could probably stay in the sport for 20 years' - Wolffpublished at 16:42 BST
    16:42 BST

    More from Mercedes' team boss Toto Wolff on Antonelli's attitude: "I think he's cool. Sometimes for me an inch too cool when he brushes the wall five laps to the end and comes on to the radio 'check the car, I think I brushed the wall' you're like 'Really? Again?' Maybe that's what helps keep him in the zone.

    "He gets in the car and just drives. I feel his youth helps him a lot there. He's 20. He could probably stay in the sport for 20 years. He's so above all our expectations and our own. He's just going with the flow. There's a genetic factor. You're born with cool as a cucumber or you're not. You see this in junior formulas and karting, but he's just another level. You see junior drivers crumbling in F2 but here he's just driving. We said to him that this was a race that we need to bring home where we need to score points, if it's P2 or P5 we don't care and he's able to do that.

    "He still has a team-mate that can show that he can go really fast. As a team we've made good steps forward. we don't want to take our foot off the throttle and be complacent and party too early. This is motor racing, it's a mechanical sport, things can fail, we don't look at the points."

  8. 'The race gods were with us' - Wolffpublished at 16:37 BST
    16:37 BST

    Mercedes' team boss Toto Wolff speaking to Sky Sports about championship leader and race winner Kimi Antonelli: "I think we've been mostly unlucky with safety cars and VSCs this year. Today the race gods were with us. We were able to literally to pull [Antonelli] in when the VSC came out. Unfortunate for McLaren because they were just past it. He drove well as well, very well.

    "Obviously there's two schools: you pit and give up the lead or you keep him out and hope for a red flag or an incident. I think they wanted to keep [Norris] out and so did Ferrari.

    "The process was great today. The strategy group had [the VSC] on their mind and then someone said that's not going to clear quickly with the Aston Martin in the wall. Then it was strategy to Bono and then Bono to Kimi. That was all in three seconds."
