ChatGPT brings Space to Plus users, adding mobile editing

TheTools · 20.11.2026 19:10 · source · permalink

FoxTPNL / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 4.0)

OpenAI's collaborative workspace moves beyond Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans, with phone editing and Slides now part of the package

OpenAI has opened ChatGPT Space to Plus subscribers, extending a collaboration tool that until recently belonged only to the company’s pricier tiers. The expansion arrived on November 20, 2026, and it brings mobile editing with it.

That second part matters more than it sounds. Until this update, anyone opening a Space page on a phone could look at it, read it, and pass it along. Changing anything required a trip back to a proper screen.

What Space actually does

Instead of an answer scrolling past in a conversation, users build interactive Pages that stick around and can be worked on together.

A Page can hold text, images, charts, and files. Teammates can edit and share those Pages, and ChatGPT sits inside the workspace as an assistant rather than a separate tab.

The full feature set also covers comments and interactive elements.

Space also replaces Library, the older section where ChatGPT kept users’ saved material.

Slides are currently rolling out as part of the Pages offering, so presentations now fall under the same roof. Sheets, the spreadsheet piece, has been signaled for a future release but is not here yet.

Not everything made it into this rollout. Self-updating pages remain unavailable for now.

From DevDay exclusive to wider release

Space made its debut at OpenAI’s DevDay event, held on September 29-30, 2026. At launch it was reserved for Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans.

Those tiers received full feature access from the start. Plus users, meanwhile, watched from the sidelines for roughly seven weeks before getting their turn.

The November 20 update was not limited to widening the guest list. OpenAI also simplified the interface, made performance improvements, and gave the Space home screen a revamp.

Free and Go users are still on Library. For them, Space remains something to read about rather than something to open.
