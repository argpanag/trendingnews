Michel Boeri on Monaco’s F1 Future and His Re-Election Bid: "I’m Not Going to Just Fade Away"

TheTools · 05.10.2026 07:15 · source · permalink

Seeking another term as president of the Automobile Club de Monaco, Michel Boeri discusses the Grand Prix’s future through 2035, new infrastructure around Port Hercule, relations with Formula 1, and the succession he believes must be prepared from within.

You have now confirmed that you will stand for another term as president of the Automobile Club de Monaco at the end of October...

I don’t think there was ever much suspense. Everyone knows I’m going to be the Jeanne Calment of the Automobile Club. So, at 122, I still have a few years ahead of me. In all seriousness, I believe the Automobile Club can only survive and move forward if it has new projects. We had a very important 2,500-square-meter extension project over the water, intended to allow Formula 1 to relocate some of its large vehicles in anticipation of another team joining the grid in one, two, or three years. I don’t know the exact timetable. In 2022, while we were in the middle of difficult discussions with Formula 1 about the future, a senior Monegasque authority insisted that we sign a three-year contract. I think it was a test of nerves: ours were undoubtedly stronger, theirs perhaps less so. The instruction came, and we signed. That automatically added €5 million to the cost for the following years of the contract. The Automobile Club is never brought to its knees. Having been forced to give way, we decided to get back to basics.

And you resumed negotiations, ultimately securing the Grand Prix’s extension through 2031, and then 2035?

With Stefano Domenicali [president of Formula 1], we went back over everything and offered him Garage 1 [at the pit lane entrance], which is an excellent facility. That allows him to bring in numerous sponsors at quite exceptional rates, and it enabled us to reopen discussions. We ultimately signed a contract that was treated as a continuation of the existing one, with a 5% increase instead of 33%. At the same time, we committed to handing over the international school in 2025 completely empty, so that the Paddock Club would have enough space, along with this 2,500-square-meter extension. There weren’t many other suitable places in Monaco where we could put them.

Specifically, in Port Hercule, opposite the Rascasse corner...

When Prince Rainier approved the construction of the Route de la Piscine, the quay was also doubled in size and, crucially, a platform of around 20,000 square meters was created, making a number of developments possible. The additional 2,500 square meters intended to accommodate the future Formula 1 truck were to be built on that reclaimed area. That was a year and a half ago.

In May, you told us that you were concerned that progress was stalling, potentially jeopardizing the contract. Where do things stand now?

The departments involved spent a year and a half navel-gazing. I’m not talking about us, I’m talking about them. I don’t know whether it was thoughtlessness, the pleasure of saying no, or extraordinary arrogance that drove the whole thing. It got to the point where they were even considering putting the 2,500 square meters on a barge. It’s laughable! Then common sense finally prevailed. I believe that, thanks to Prince Albert and a little push, the reclaimed area was approved, the necessary permissions were granted, and the problem is now resolved.

So the situation is now "administratively" resolved?

As I understand it, the Pastor Group will be carrying out the work, and the materials have already been purchased. In theory, it should simply involve driving piles into the silt and laying a concrete slab on top. Exactly what we need, done with the kind of precision that will please us all. So the project is settled. But when I put myself forward for another term, I had no idea that arrangements had already been made in the meantime. You have to admit that in Monaco, there are ordinary mortals like me, and then there are those in the know. There is no communication because the ordinary mortals are too stupid and those in the know are too clever.

Could the new port platform be ready by 2027?

My guess is that it will be handed over about six weeks before the Grand Prix. We would need it two weeks earlier than that. Once I officially know that Mr. Pastor is in charge of the work, I’d like to meet with him. I like him, unlike many people, and I don’t hide it. He is very aware of the Principality’s interests and, quite naturally, takes great pride in his company. That should help ensure the necessary effort is made.

Boats must leave the port...

As everyone knows, the Grand Prix is not just some whim of the Automobile Club. I’m not the port director, but it was either this or a kick in the teeth. It’s true that 15 boats were affected, the largest of them around 15 meters long. I put myself in the owners’ shoes. I have a boat myself, and I would be annoyed if it were moved. I can certainly understand why they’re unhappy. But Aleco Keusseoglou [deputy chairman of the Monaco Ports Operating Company, Société d’Exploitation des Ports de Monaco] is a man who knows his job well, and the people around him will certainly find alternative berths. Don’t tell me there isn’t a partial or complete solution somewhere in the Port of Monaco for 15 boats. The truth is, you don’t make money by doing nothing. I’m someone who likes consensus. I like to get along with everyone, but there are limits. I’m here for the national interest. I came into this role with nothing, and I’ll leave with nothing. Full stop. So everyone can make a small sacrifice, just as I have as a volunteer.

Why did you maintain your candidacy if that issue is now resolved? Do you have other projects up your sleeve?

You don’t think I’m just going to fade away! I don’t think I’ve always been treated very kindly, and I fully intend to keep pushing projects that I can’t tell you about yet, because I prefer to annoy people when the time is right. I don’t want to warn them in advance. That would spoil all the fun.

You always said you would only leave once you had secured what you believed was essential to the Grand Prix’s future. If it is no longer this platform...

The Grand Prix’s future is secured until 2035, but with this platform and the building that will be constructed on it, we might be able to rethink the grid area and perhaps even extend the contract further. All of that remains to be defined. It’s no secret, for example, that the pit lane will be difficult to sustain much longer. Space is limited, and the garages aren’t wide enough. And when you don’t have width on the ground, you build upward. I can already hear all the non possumus objections that will come from this area, but eventually people will see reason. I’m no gardener, but there’s more than one kind of tree, for example.

Your candidacy is another snub to those who thought you would be sidelined with the arrival of the Americans in Formula 1. So are you their best friend now?

Donald Trump canceled our last meeting. They’re not here out of charity. They’re here to make money. And we’re here to give them as little as possible and make as much as possible ourselves. It’s a straightforward, fair contest in an open market. Our American friends realized how responsive we could be. Unlike those who still call Monaco "the merry-go-round" and would do well to update their thinking, we reached an understanding in principle that the Grand Prix is a major draw for Formula 1. We weren’t going to be constantly hassled over whether a curb had two or three too many corners. And, very importantly, the circuit will not be changed, contrary to what I’ve heard many people suggest. Demolishing this or moving that would automatically trigger a fresh review of the circuit and require new FIA homologation.

Are you talking about people who are eyeing your position?

No, I’m not talking about my own position. I look at things as an administrator, not as a sportsman. I’m happy when our national champion wins, obviously. Beyond that, who finishes second or third is none of my concern.

Will you have rivals in the upcoming election?

There is one declared candidate: Thierry Boutsen. Yann-Antony Noghès also expressed an interest. Is that still the case? The election has two stages. The General Assembly elects the 15 members of the Board of Directors. Then, once elected, the Board meets and chooses its president. It’s not a direct election.

So there are electors, much like in Senate elections...

Exactly. Let me be clear: I don’t want this election politicized, and I don’t want us to start talking about salaries and perks, because we risk blowing up an institution that has stood for almost 60 years. Everything can evolve, but it has to happen calmly and thoughtfully. You don’t come in and turn everything upside down when you’ve never even set foot here. The Automobile Club is a restaurant serving 22,000 meals a year, an advertising agency, and an organization with 700 marshals to manage. It organizes three world championships and two historic events. It is essential to be able to talk things through and be heard by the Princely Government, for which I have the utmost respect, and of course by the Prince himself, who stands far above all these minor ups and downs.

Do you feel any apprehension ahead of the vote?

Never, and I’m not about to start at my age. Normally, my re-election isn’t the issue. What concerns me, if I’m re-elected, is who I’ll have around me, because one person alone can’t achieve anything. If I end up with duds on my team, we won’t get very far.

If you’re standing again, it must be because you have a team behind you, right?

There are 4,500 voting members, but maybe only 300 or 400 truly understand how the Automobile Club works, its complexities, and the time it demands. Don’t forget, the Automobile Club has around 70 employees and 1,200 volunteers. And volunteers will follow you if you’re hands-on and present. If you’re sitting in some fancy armchair surrounded by gold coins, it doesn’t have the same effect as being out there in the thick of it with them. That’s what my successors need to understand. You need a clear vision and, above all, strong cooperation between the permanent staff and the volunteers. This isn’t about personal gain or building a career. We’re here to serve the country. We serve Monaco’s image. We serve the Prince. That’s how we were brought up, and we’re not going to change because of personal demands or agendas. Broadly speaking, even using a very conservative estimate of €100 million a year, we’ve injected €5 billion into the Monegasque economy!

A Major Contribution to Monaco’s Economy...

Yes. I think we’ve done something for the country. But recognition, you know, is for people who believe in it. Still, I’m optimistic, and I think we have people perfectly capable of taking over successfully. My time will come, and my successor should join the current team and spend the next few years understanding what is really going on, rather than simply dreaming in the abstract about what can or cannot be done.

And earn Formula 1’s trust...

Obviously, our survival depends entirely on trust and on a de facto alliance with Formula 1. My days of clashing with Mr. Ecclestone, Mr. Balestre, and Mr. Mosley are behind me. It is no longer a national automobile club facing off against an international federation. We are now dealing with an international commercial enterprise with different branches. This is a world championship, and only one country pays less than we do! Others pay three or four times the fee we pay. Hotels are full, merchants are happy, and jewelry stores are thriving. I wasn’t put here to time cars. I was put here to make the factory run. That’s the only role I claim. And everyone around me, beyond whatever good advice they may offer, holds a key position that keeps this ocean liner moving forward.
