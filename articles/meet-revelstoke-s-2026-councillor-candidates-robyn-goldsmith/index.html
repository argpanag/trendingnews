Meet Revelstoke's 2026 councillor candidates: Robyn Goldsmith - Revelstoke Review

TheTools · 22.09.2026 12:00 · source · permalink

Published 5:00 am Tuesday, September 22, 2026

By Evert Lindquist

Robyn Goldsmith is running for election as Revelstoke city councillor this October.

Leading up to election day, Black Press Media is inviting all nominated mayor and councillor candidates to share a short submission about themselves and their platforms.

Submissions are being published in order of receipt from candidates.

Goldsmith says she’s running for council because she cares deeply about the community and wants to contribute her experience, energy and perspective to its future. She’s been in Revelstoke more than 11 years and, having grown up in Vancouver and lived elsewhere across B.C., she said Revelstoke stands out for the strength of community, local passion and vibrant natural beauty.

A lawyer by training, Goldsmith describes herself having a strong understanding of governance, accountability and decision-making. She worked for Tourism Revelstoke several years, leading the destination management planning process for town while bringing a resident-focused lens. This led her to collaborate with communities across the globe and learn from ideas implemented in other places, both within B.C. and abroad.

As executive director for Arts Revelstoke, she seeks grants, manages staff and budgets, and delivers cultural programming that has helped define the mountain town. Outside work, she’s an active community volunteer who’s also served on various boards and committees. Her experience in non-profit leadership makes her a strong believer in collaboration, responsible financial management and utilization of available resources.

At the council table, Goldsmith plans to use the City of Revelstoke’s existing plans and prior public consultation to guide decisions. She would prioritize capital planning that funds renewal of the systems the community already owns, with emphasis on a transparent process.

Goldsmith wants a city council that takes climate change seriously and considers extreme weather risk in decisions around land use, infrastructure and emergency preparedness. She intends to bring a solutions-oriented approach with innovative ideas as well, drawing from the experiences of other communities.

The City of Revelstoke’s 2026 general local election takes place Oct. 17.
