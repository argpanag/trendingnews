Few events garner more attention on Wall Street than Berkshire Hathaway's (NYSE:BRKA)(NYSE:BRKB) earnings reports. This is especially true now that Warren Buffett has retired and his successor, Greg Abel, is overseeing the company's day-to-day operations and its closely watched $359 billion investment portfolio.

While the September-ended quarter was more or less business as usual for the trillion-dollar conglomerate that Buffett built, with Berkshire recording $12.7 billion in operating earnings, another figure stole the show. Namely, a $9.6 billion purchase of the Oracle of Omaha's favorite stock by Abel.

The Oracle of Omaha's favorite stock is back on the menu. Image source: Getty Images.

Berkshire's bosses have spent $92 billion buying this stock since mid-2018

Through the first half of 2026, Abel's headline buy was Google parent Alphabet (NASDAQ:GOOGL)(NASDAQ:GOOG). While Buffett initiated the position in Alphabet before his retirement, it was Abel who grew it into a top-three holding at one point. Berkshire's new boss has put tech on the menu in a way that wasn't seen under Buffett.

But it wasn't a tech purchase that raised eyebrows in the third quarter. In fact, you won't even find evidence of this purchase in Berkshire Hathaway's quarterly Form 13F, which should be filed on Nov. 16.

In Berkshire's third-quarter operating results, on the final page before the executive certifications, the company details Abel's purchase activity in the stock Buffett holds nearest and dearest to his heart... Berkshire Hathaway.

Between July 1 and Sept. 30, Abel oversaw the purchase of 4,536 Class A shares (BRKA) and 12,384,959 Class B shares (BRKA), totaling approximately $9.6 billion. This more than doubles the $4.53 billion spent on share buybacks in the second quarter and brings cumulative share repurchases between Buffett and Abel since mid-2018 to approximately $92 billion.

As long as Berkshire Hathaway has at least $30 billion in combined cash, cash equivalents, and U.S. Treasury bills on its balance sheet -- it closed out September 2026 with $377.4 billion -- and Abel believes the company's shares are intrinsically cheap, buybacks can be made with no end date or ceiling.
