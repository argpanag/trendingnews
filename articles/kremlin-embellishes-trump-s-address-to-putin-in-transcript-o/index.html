Kremlin 'embellishes' Trump's address to Putin in transcript of trilateral summit

TheTools · 20.11.2026 11:11 · source · permalink

The Kremlin added that Trump called Putin "Vladimir Vladimirovich" and "Mr. President" (Photo: Sputnik/Vyacheslav Prokofyev/Pool via REUTERS)

The Kremlin altered how U.S. President Donald Trump addressed Vladimir Putin in its transcript of the public portion of talks in Shenzhen with China's leader, Russian outlet Agentstvo reported on Nov. 19.

While Trump referred to the Russian leader by his first name, "Vladimir," on one occasion and simply used a pronoun on another, the Kremlin altered the transcript of the trilateral meeting to make it appear that Trump had addressed Putin as "Vladimir Vladimirovich" and "Mr. President."

"The changes do not alter the substance of Trump’s statements, but the Russian translation makes them appear more formal and ceremonial toward Putin than in the original," Agentstvo noted.

The U.S. president said it was an "honor" to participate in the trilateral meeting with the leaders of Russia and China. He addressed Putin by his first name, "Vladimir," while referring to the Chinese leader as "Mr. President."

Trump then repeated his remarks, addressing each leader again. "It's an honor to be with you," he said to Xi Jinping before repeating the same words to Putin.

However, the Kremlin website translated the first sentence as: "I am very pleased to be here with you today, Vladimir Vladimirovich, and I am pleased to be here with you, Chairman Xi."

In the second statement, the Kremlin website attributed forms of address to Trump that he had not used: "It is a great honor for me to be here today with you, Mr. Chairman, and with you, Mr. President Putin."

Trump, Xi Jinping and Putin meet in Shenzhen

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit is taking place Nov. 18−19 in Shenzhen, China. Chinese President Xi Jinping, U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin held a trilateral meeting on the sidelines of the summit on Nov. 19. The talks lasted approximately 90 minutes.

The meeting began with a 10-minute session open to the media, during which Xi called for nuclear weapons to be "completely banned and eliminated."

Trump reiterated his longstanding hopes that the war in Ukraine could be brought to a swift end. He said the United States would no longer avoid engaging with "countries with which we have had disagreements" and complained that the International Criminal Court had "gotten out of control."

Putin also raised Russia’s war against Ukraine, saying he had discussed it with the Chinese leader the previous day and with Trump on Thursday.

"At the initiative of the president of the United States, we devoted considerable attention to this issue during our bilateral meeting," Putin claimed.

Although Russia launched and continues to wage the war against Ukraine, Putin described it as a "regional conflict," claiming that the United States, Russia and China have "many common interests in resolving regional conflicts," including the war in Ukraine.

Before the trilateral meeting, Trump held separate bilateral talks with Putin, whom he called his friend. According to the Kremlin, the two leaders discussed the war in Ukraine.

Trump also held a separate meeting with Xi, during which they discussed U.S.-China economic cooperation and tariffs.

Trump arrived at the summit on the evening of Nov. 18. Xi personally greeted the U.S. president at the airport, breaking China’s diplomatic protocol.
