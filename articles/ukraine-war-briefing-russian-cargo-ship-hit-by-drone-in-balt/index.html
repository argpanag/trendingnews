Ukraine war briefing: Russian cargo ship hit by drone in Baltic Sea as Finland PM says conflict ‘spilling over’

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:19 · source · permalink

Vessel erupts in flames after strike in Gulf of Finland; Nato vows to defend ‘every inch’ of Polish territory. What we know on day 1,733

  • A Russian cargo ship was hit by a Ukrainian drone and caught fire in the Baltic Sea on Saturday, as Finland’s prime minister said Kyiv had the right to hit back against its “aggressor”. The Finnish prime minister, Petteri Orpo, suggested the incident in the Gulf of Finland had to do with Russia’s war in Ukraine. “War is spilling over into our immediate vicinity,” Orpo said on X. “Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor.”

  • Hours later, a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating Finnish airspace, according to the Finnish defence minister, who drew a possible link between the two incidents. “The suspected airspace violation may be linked to rescue or monitoring operations involving a cargo ship that was damaged in the Gulf of Finland,” Antti Hakkanen said on X.

  • Petteri Blomvall, an officer at the Finnish Border Guard, said: “I can confirm that a Ukrainian drone strike hit the ship in the morning that caused the fire … The crew from the ship has been evacuated, the ship is anchored and the firefighting is ongoing.”

  • The Finnish Defence Forces identified the ship as the Sparta II, which is under Ukrainian and US sanctions, according to the site Opensanctions. The website said the ship was suspected of transporting weapons from Syria to Russia. The MarineTraffic maritime monitoring website said the Sparta II had departed St Petersburg around 11pm and was bound for the port of Baltiysk, in the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad.

  • Finland’s defence ministry said it had immediately informed Nato of the drone strike. It also said temporary restrictions were being enforced in the airspace and maritime area of the eastern part of the Gulf. Rattled by Russia’s invasion, Finland joined Nato in 2023, dropping decades of military non-alignment. The Gulf of Finland is a key maritime passage for Russia, leading to the former capital St Petersburg.

  • “I can guarantee ⁠that Nato will defend every inch of Polish territory,” Nato’s secretary general, Mark Rutte, said in Warsaw on Saturday, after two Russian missiles and a drone crashed in its territory on Friday. Poland accused the Kremlin of “intentional provocation” after the incident, as it occurred near where thousands of European troops allied with Ukraine were holding training exercises.

  • A meeting of Nato’s North Atlantic Council will be held in Brussels on Monday to discuss the violation of Polish airspace by Russian missiles. “In the near future, we ​will receive ​strong support from our NATO ​allies to bolster the protection of our eastern ​border,” Polish defence minister ‌Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz wrote ​on ​X on Saturday.

  • In Kyiv, residents say they are unbothered by Russian attacks on the city’s bridges, as Moscow tries to cut off the city centre in the west from the newer, largely residential east, Dan Sabbagh reports. Alongside its attacks on the capital’s electricity, heating and water systems, Russia has been attacking Kyiv’s bridges since early October, trying to cut off the city centre in the west from the newer, largely residential east, home to about a third of its population. Moscow struck the bridges twice again this week, first on Tuesday – overshadowed by Ukraine’s partial destruction of the Kerch road bridge to Crimea. Then again during a noisy Thursday night, hours after Vladimir Putin promised there would be a “far more painful” response to the demolition of his pet project.
