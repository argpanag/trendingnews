Polygon identifies GTA 6’s Johannes Zuch as a Jonathan Blow parody

TheTools · 21.11.2026 09:04 · source · permalink

Johannes Zuch, the fictional designer behind Grand Theft Auto 6’s playable first-person puzzle game Tenebrae Lux, reads as a Jonathan Blow parody in a new analysis from Polygon. The strongest clue is what happens when players sit down to play. Like Blow’s The Witness, Tenebrae Lux leaves players to deduce the rules instead of teaching them through a tutorial. Working out what the game is asking is part of solving it.

Polygon places the first encounter with Tenebrae Lux during a Chapter 1 mission. Its opening board puzzles ask players to move pieces in order to remove other pieces from the board. A later puzzle swaps the board for a loop of nearly identical rooms that players have to escape. Players who want to pick the game back up can return to it at Jason and Lucia’s home.

The comparison extends beyond the puzzles. Polygon also likens the minigame’s narration to the writing in Blow’s Order of the Sinking Star. There’s a credit for that side of the performance, too: Zuch is listed as the voice of the playable character. The fictional designer isn’t just a name attached to the game; he gets a role inside it.

Then there’s the credits gag. Zuch’s name appears in every opening credit for Tenebrae Lux, and GTA 6’s final credits list him as its sole creator. Giving him one credit would have been enough to establish who supposedly made the game. Putting his name on every opening credit makes that claim part of the joke. The punchline is the gap between the credits and the production: Polygon reports that a small team within Rockstar actually made Tenebrae Lux.
