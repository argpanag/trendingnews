Miami judge signs arrest warrant for Raúl Castro on state charges in Brothers to the Rescue shootdown

TheTools · 20.10.2026 09:55 · source · permalink

The warrant was signed by Chief Judge Ariana Fajardo Orshan of the Eleventh Judicial Circuit Court of Florida.

Former Cuban President Raúl Castro is facing more charges in Florida related to the 1996 downing of civilian planes flown by Miami-based exiles after the U.S. government indicted the former leader earlier this year. NBC6’s Steve Litz reports.

A South Florida judge has signed a state arrest warrant for former Cuban leader Raúl Castro for his involvement in 1996 shootdown of two humanitarian planes, NBC6 has learned.

The warrant was signed by Chief Judge Ariana Fajardo Orshan of the Eleventh Judicial Circuit Court of Florida.

Raúl Castro charged with murder in Brothers to the Rescue shootdown

Castro was already indicted by the United States on May 20 for his alleged role in the 1996 downing of two planes operated by the Miami-based exile group Brothers to the Rescue.

That indictment charged Castro and five others—Lorenzo Alberto Perez-Perez, Emilio Jose Palacio Blanco, Jose Fidel Gual Barzaga, Raul Simanca Cardenas, and Luis Raul Gonzalez-Pardo Rodriguez—with multiple charges: four counts of murder, two counts of destruction of aircraft, and conspiracy to kill U.S. nationals.

Castro, now 95, was Cuba's defense minister at the time of the shootdown. The five others charged were pilots allegedly involved in the operation.
