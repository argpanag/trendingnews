Florence Pugh Wants a Word With Evie Magazine

TheTools · 18.11.2026 19:53 · source · permalink

If you’re going to weirdly speculate about someone’s appearance on the internet, don’t be surprised if they show up in your comments. On Tuesday, Evie magazine (which was once described by its founder, Brittany Hugoboom, as the “conservative Cosmo”) posted a video on Instagram all about Florence Pugh’s face. In the video, Evie’s uncredited in-house content creator said that Pugh had “fallen victim to Hollywood’s war on round faces” while showing a photo of the actress at the Governors Awards on Sunday.

If you were being used by a right-leaning women’s publication as the example of the “subconscious push” to “make women look sharper, more sculpted, thinner” — not just on Instagram but in a full-fledged article, too! — wouldn’t you want to say something? Pugh did.

“Guys. I turned 30,” Pugh wrote in Evie’s comments section. “I’m a woman. Body’s and faces change. It’s not that deep, the fact that people can’t even tell between what’s aging and what’s surgery is disturbing.” The actress added that “sadly puppy fluff doesn’t last forever” alongside a heart emoji. (Too nice, in my opinion.)

On Wednesday, Pugh shared more of her thoughts in a lengthy Notes app statement on Instagram. “I find it so stressful waking up and have thousands of people talking about you and your body in ways that you can’t even comprehend,” she wrote, adding, “I have never been on Ozempic. I have NEVER done any face surgery.”

﻿Pugh offered — not that she needed to — that if her face has changed at all, it’s due to age and lifestyle changes. “Here’s the truth, I turned 30 and my New Year’s resolution was to change my relationship with alcohol, and to be able to do at least one pull up in the gym.” The actress said that she now has the opportunity “to be rested, to sleep, to wake up and work out and to eat regularly and healthily.” According to Pugh, she can now do six pull ups and lift 110 kg (almost 250 lbs).

“Let’s use this as a moment of self-reflection,” Pugh wrote. “If you were one of those people that have the instinct to pull apart bodies, I think let’s start with asking yourself why? And what get from it? And it’s okay to admit to yourself that it comes from something deeper. I’m not angry at you, I’m angry at the situation that’s being projected on to us.”

Pugh has previously spoken about how being a woman in the public eye means constant commentary on her appearance. After facing backlash for wearing a see-through Valentino dress in 2022, Pugh said the thing that “wound people up” was that she was “comfortable and happy.” “Keeping women down by commenting on their bodies has worked for a very long time,” the actress told Jodie Turner-Smith in a conversation for Elle UK. “I think we’re in this swing now where lots of people are saying, ‘I don’t give a shit.’ Unfortunately, we’ve become so terrified of the human body that we can’t even look at my two little cute nipples behind fabric in a way that isn’t sexual. We need to keep reminding everybody that there is more than one reason for women’s bodies [to exist].”

Evie might figure that out someday but probably not right now. Today, it is busy celebrating “International Men’s Day.”
