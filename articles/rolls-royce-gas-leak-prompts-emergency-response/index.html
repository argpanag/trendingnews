Rolls-Royce gas leak prompts emergency response

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:30 · source · permalink

Emergency services outside Rolls-Royce on Thursday

  • Published
    12 November 2026, 18:41 GMT

Emergency services have responded to a gas leak at a Rolls-Royce factory in Derby.

Derbyshire Fire and Rescue Service said it was called to the site in Raynesway at about 15:40 GMT on Thursday.

It said police, fire crews and ambulances responded to a "small chemical and gas leak", adding this was "not a nuclear incident", and that all employees were accounted for.

A 300m (985ft) cordon was put in place and locals were asked to remain indoors with windows and doors shut, however the guidance was lifted shortly before 22:00.

A BBC reporter at the scene was advised by police to stay in her car for her safety, and an alarm was heard coming from the building.

A number of roads were closed around the factory, including Royal Scot Way, Pride Park Way, from Derby Audi to the London Road roundabout, Harrow Street, and London Road, from the junction of Harrow Steet to Meadow Lane. They have all reopened, the fire service said.

In a statement, a fire service spokesperson added: "Emergency services and Rolls-Royce would like to thank local residents and businesses for their co-operation while the incident was resolved safely and as quickly as possible.

"The incident has now been handed over to Rolls-Royce."

The incident prompted an emergency response and several roads have been shut

The Raynesway Rolls-Royce site designs, manufactures, and supports nuclear reactors and nuclear steam-raising plants that power every submarine in the UK Royal Navy.

It is the subject of plans to create more factory space to meet the growth in demand from the Royal Navy and as a result of the recent AUKUS announcement.

In March 2023, it was confirmed that Rolls-Royce Submarines would provide all the nuclear reactor plants that will power new attack submarines as part of the tri-lateral agreement between Australia, the UK and US.

This increase in demand will see new manufacturing and office facilities being built on recently acquired land surrounding the existing Raynesway site.

It will also create 1,170 skilled roles within Rolls-Royce across a range of disciplines, including manufacturing and engineering.
