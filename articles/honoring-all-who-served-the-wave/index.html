Honoring All Who Served - The Wave

TheTools · 13.11.2026 08:02 · source · permalink

Members of the Daniel M. O’Connell American Legion Post #272 joined together in front of the Doughboy Memorial Statue on Veterans Day. Photo by John Schilling

It was perfect weather at the Doughboy Memorial Statue on Wednesday, Nov. 11, as members of the Daniel M. O’Connell American Legion Post #272 gathered at Beach 94th Street and Rockaway Beach Boulevard to observe Veterans Day and honor the countless men and women who have risked their lives in service of their country.

The annual ceremony began at 11 a.m. with some remarks from the post members who reflected on the larger significance of the holiday.

“On this day, Veterans Day, we are commemorating the service of all veterans of all wars,” said U.S. Army veteran and Legion Post Commander Brian Galarza in opening remarks. “We remember how men and women set aside their civilian pursuits to serve their nation’s cause, defending the freedom of mankind and preserving our precious American heritage.”

Each year, Legion Post #272, which has been based out of the Knights of Columbus on Beach 90th Street for the last decade, gathers at the statue for a Veterans Day ceremony, something the group has done since the holiday was first known as “Armistice Day” in recognition of the end of World War I in 1918.

Across the nation, this year’s theme for Veterans Day was “Honoring All Who Served,” something that rang especially true at this year’s ceremony as veterans from all over the peninsula and Broad Channel gathered together.

This year, however, marked the first ceremony without U.S. Navy veteran Michael “Mike” Honan, the past commander of Legion Post #272, who died on Jan. 3, 2026. Honan, a longtime Rockaway Park resident, was always on the forefront of the peninsula’s advocacy for veterans and their families, helping lead the peninsula’s Memorial Day Parade and Veterans Day ceremony each year.

Galarza, who led this year’s ceremony, acknowledged Honan’s absence, commending the beloved veteran not only for his service but also for his decades of dedication to helping veterans and their families.“Let us not forget what he did for us in this community,” Galarza said.

Following an opening prayer from Legion Post Chaplain Jim Trainor and words of reflection from American Legion Ladies Auxiliary President Jackie McGinty, Women Veterans representative Patricia Trainor, Legion Post First Officer Jim Hurley, Legion Post Second Officer Harry McMahon, and Legion Post Third Officer Charlie Ciliberti, all those in attendance pledged or saluted for the national anthem.

Before the ceremony came to a close, Galarza thanked everyone present for their attendance and continued support.

“We have more people here today [than] last year, and it’s because it’s about patriotism,” Galarza said. “We truly appreciate your presence here today…Happy Veterans Day!”
