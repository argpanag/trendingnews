Football: Japan in another Brazil test as Moriyasu's reign nears end

TheTools · 14.11.2026 10:02 · source · permalink

SINGAPORE - Japan will be out to get back at Brazil in an international friendly in Singapore on Saturday after their World Cup dream came to an early end in the round of 32 in Houston this summer.

Manager Hajime Moriyasu is set for the final two games of his eight-plus years in charge of the Samurai Blue after the Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia, originally scheduled for early next year, was pushed back to December 2027 amid security concerns in the country.

"This will be an opportunity to show the people in Singapore and Asia that Asian nations can compete at the world's top level," Moriyasu told a press conference.

"We'll be playing with the same objective (as Brazil) and go for the win. While we face a team who can teach us the quality required to be the best in the world, there'll definitely be a chance (to win)."

The Japan Football Association said Friday an international friendly planned on Dec. 30 in Osaka was canceled following the Asian Cup postponement.

Moriyasu, who signed a short-term contract after the World Cup until the end of the Asian Cup, will manage Japan for the last time against Paraguay on Tuesday. His successor Go Oiwa is expected to be in charge for the first time in a home friendly in March.

Japan won their previous friendly against Brazil in Tokyo in October last year when a second-half comeback sealed a historic 3-2 victory, their first-ever success over the record five-time World Cup winners at the senior level.

"Our win in 2025 might have kept them away from becoming complacent and contributed to their win over us at the World Cup," Moriyasu said. "But for us to get stronger, what's important is how we can beat Brazil when they come at us at full strength."

"They won't be letting their guard down tomorrow either, and we want to find out what we can do then, which spurs our future development."

Brazil, knocked out by Norway in the round of 16 at the World Cup, arrived in Singapore with a youthful squad. Italian manager Carlo Ancelotti voiced his hopes of beginning a new cycle, while backing Moriyasu's potential managerial career in Europe.

"(Japan) competed really well in the World Cup against us. It was a difficult game. It will be a difficult game tomorrow because they are really well-organized in defensive and offensive situations," Ancelotti said. "We need to have a competitive game to improve and to give experience to our young players."

Regarding Moriyasu, Ancelotti said, "He had the time to work with the national team. He built a strong, really well-organized team. I think he did a really good job and has all the qualities to train and manage anywhere in the world."

Forward Raphinha is back for Brazil after a right hamstring injury kept him out of their 2-1 comeback win over Japan in late June.

"For me, playing against Japan is like a World Cup match," said the Barcelona man, who leads Spanish La Liga with 15 goals this term.

"I see it as a very difficult, complicated game. But in my view, these are important games to see the level we are at, what we can improve, not only individually on the field but also collectively. It's a good test against a great team."
