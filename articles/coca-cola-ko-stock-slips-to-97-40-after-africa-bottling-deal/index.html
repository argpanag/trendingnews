Coca-Cola (KO) Stock Slips to $97.40 After Africa Bottling Deal — JournalArta

TheTools · 27.11.2026 20:37 · source · permalink

Coca-Cola shares edged lower in New York afternoon trading Friday after the beverage maker completed the $2.6 billion sale of a controlling stake in its African bottler.

Coca-Cola shares slipped in New York afternoon trading Friday after the company completed a $2.6 billion African bottling sale.

The stock traded at $97.40 at 3:34 p.m., down nearly 20 cents, or 0.19%, from its $97.59 prior close. It was a second straight decline.

Coca-Cola lagged the broader market as technology stocks rebounded. The S&P 500 rose 114.71 points, or 1.60%, to 7,297.49 in afternoon trading.

Over the past month, the shares have outperformed the benchmark. Coca-Cola gained 4.93% over that stretch, while the S&P 500 slid 5.48%.

The stock sits 2.09% below its one-year high of $99.48 set on Nov. 16, keeping it well outside correction territory. It remains 44.21% above its Jan. 7 low of $67.54.

Coca-Cola and Gutsche Family Investments completed the sale of a 75% controlling stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa to Coca-Cola HBC on Friday after receiving African regulatory approvals. The deal was announced in October 2025.

Coca-Cola sold 41.52% of its 66.52% stake, while Gutsche Family Investments sold its 33.48% holding. The transaction values the Johannesburg-based bottler at $3.4 billion.

"This transaction reflects the strength of the Coca-Cola system and our long-term commitment to building locally led, world-class bottling operations," Coca-Cola CEO Henrique Braun said.

Luis Felipe Avellar, president of Coca-Cola's Africa operating unit, backed the deal. He said the combination will create new opportunities for customers and communities.

The acquisition expands Coca-Cola HBC's reach to 43 European and African markets serving about 1.2 billion consumers. It becomes the second-largest Coca-Cola bottler globally by volume.

Coca-Cola retains a 25% stake in the African bottler. A separate option agreement allows Coca-Cola HBC to acquire that remaining interest within six years.
