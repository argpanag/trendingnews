Simple Minds Announce New Album ‘Sacrosanct’ And Share ‘Beginner Boys’

TheTools · 03.09.2026 07:50 · source · permalink

Simple Minds have announced their brand new studio album Sacrosanct, set for release on January 29, 2027, via BMG. The album follows 2022’s Top 5 record Direction Of The Heart and arrives after a busy period for the band, including a critically acclaimed career-spanning documentary, a joint memoir and a hugely successful global tour.

Last year, Simple Minds played to more than 1.5 million people, including some of their biggest audiences since 1985. It was during this period, energised by touring, that Jim Kerr and Charlie Burchill began crafting and recording the new album.

The band’s 2025 North American tour inspired Kerr and Burchill to draw on their art-rock and new wave roots after seeing fans in cities they had never previously played respond strongly to early songs such as “Sons And Fascination”.

That atmosphere runs through Sacrosanct, an eight-track album described as one of the band’s most vital records in decades, recapturing the magic and majesty at the heart of Simple Minds.

The first song to be released from the album is “Beginner Boys”, inspired by Kerr watching his twin grandsons grow up and thinking about what it means to raise a good man today.

Kerr said the song considers “how strength, tenderness, courage, and gentleness might still belong together, even when the wider culture around them can send mixed signals. It’s less a statement than a question I hope they’ll spend their lives answering for themselves.”

The album opens with “Elysium”, a track inspired by Magazine and named after the paradise of Greek myth. Its natural counterpoint is “King Of The Underworld”, inspired by the figure of Mephistopheles and the forces that rule over human appetites.

Elsewhere, “Jerusalem (State Of Mind)” is influenced by William Blake’s vision of Jerusalem as a longing for a better world, while “Our Secrets Are The Same” explores the lifelong bond between Kerr and Burchill, who met on a Glasgow street as eight-year-olds and have remained creative partners ever since.

The album’s most direct pop moment comes with “Songs”, a celebration of the power of music to lift people on good days and pull them out of darker ones.

The closing tracks, “Alive In This Moment” and “The Next Right Thing”, both deal with nostalgia from different perspectives. The former is about resisting the urge to reach back and instead remain present, while the latter reflects on the passing of time and the loss of those we love.

Speaking about the album title, Kerr said: “We called the album Sacrosanct because after a lifetime of doing this — nearly fifty years of writing songs together — it’s stopped feeling like a career and started feeling like something closer to faith. Calling the record Sacrosanct isn’t an exaggeration. It’s just honest.”

He added: “Making records and performing live is as much part of our lives now as when we started out. We feel grateful to those who support new music from Simple Minds and look forward to seeing you all on our upcoming world tour.”

The metalwork on the album artwork comes from a collaboration between Simple Minds and Glasgow-born sculptor Andy Scott, who has reimagined the band’s logo in steel. Scott, best known for The Kelpies in Falkirk, was awarded an OBE in this year’s King’s Birthday Honours for services to sculpture and fine art.

The release of Sacrosanct will coincide with Simple Minds’ biggest arena tour to date. The tour begins in Australia and New Zealand in February before heading to Europe and the UK in April and May.

The Simple Minds V Tour 2027 promises a panoramic live experience, celebrating five decades of music from a band who continue to evolve while maintaining the energy that has defined their career.

Before the 2027 tour begins, Simple Minds will headline the Sunday night of BBC Radio 2’s In The Park at City Park, Stirling, Scotland, on September 13.
