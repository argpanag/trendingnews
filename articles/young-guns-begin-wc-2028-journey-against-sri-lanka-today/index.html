Young guns begin WC 2028 journey against Sri Lanka today

TheTools · 08.10.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

ISLAMABAD: Pakistan’s T20 cricket team embarks on a new journey on Friday evening, turning the page on experience and placing its faith firmly in youth as it takes on a seasoned Sri Lankan side in the opening match of the three-game series at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium.

With the 2028 T20 World Cup in Australia and New Zealand firmly in their sights, the Pakistan think-tank has begun a deliberate transition, seeking to build and groom a young combination that can mature into a formidable unit over the next two years.

The series against Sri Lanka is therefore more than just another bilateral assignment. It represents the beginning of an important rebuilding phase for Pakistan, with the selectors prepared to provide opportunities to promising youngsters and assess their ability to handle the demands of international cricket.

The new-look side has already received valuable exposure during the Asian Games in Japan, where a number of young players performed impressively. The inclusion of several fresh faces, including Razaullah, has added further excitement to a squad that bears little resemblance to the Pakistan T20 outfits of recent years.

In a major departure from the past, Shaheen Shah Afridi, Babar Azam, former captain Salman Ali Agha and Haris Rauf are not part of the squad, leaving the stage open for the next generation to establish themselves.

Under new captain Sahibzada Farhan, Pakistan will be looking to give its youngsters the freedom and responsibility to develop into the core of the team that could eventually carry the country’s hopes at the 2028 World Cup.

Pakistan also enter the series with a strong record against Sri Lanka, having won 18 of the 30 T20 internationals played between the two sides. Pakistan also prevailed in their most recent meeting, defeating Sri Lanka by five runs in the Super Eight stage of the last T20 World Cup.

Sri Lanka, however, arrive with their own ambitions and under the guidance of former South Africa head coach Gary Kirsten, who has taken charge of the side. Sri Lankan captain Kusal Mendis acknowledged that his team would have to lift its performance after an inconsistent England series but said there were enough positives to build upon.

“Yes, it will be a challenging series for us as we are coming out of the England series where our performance was not up to the mark. Still, there were some positives from that series. There were some good performances which we want to carry forward,” Mendis said during the pre-match press conference. The Sri Lankan captain said playing Pakistan and adapting to conditions in Rawalpindi would not pose a major problem for his players. “We have faced Pakistan many times and played in these conditions on numerous occasions. These conditions are almost similar to the ones we find in Sri Lanka,” he said.

Mendis expected a closely fought contest and expressed confidence in Kirsten’s ability to guide the team. “Nothing to take away from Pakistan, but we are ready under our new coach Gary Kirsten, who knows the game and the conditions better,” he said.

For Pakistan, Farhan has thrown his weight behind the young squad and believes the team has been selected according to its future requirements. “As captain, I have full trust in my players. I am hopeful that they will deliver to the best of their abilities,” Farhan said.

He described the series as an important opportunity to identify the right combination for Pakistan’s future T20 commitments. “This series is important from Pakistan’s perspective as we want to see which combination will be best suited for our future international commitments,” he said.

The Rawalpindi pitch is expected to provide an interesting contest between bat and ball. Both captains believe the surface is likely to be batting-friendly and are contemplating the possibility of fielding three specialist fast bowlers.

“The tracks look full of runs. We are considering the inclusion of three pacers,” Mendis said. Farhan also indicated that Pakistan could adopt a similar strategy, with the pace attack expected to play a significant role.

The series could also provide international debuts to Razaullah and Imran Randhawa, two of Pakistan’s promising young prospects. Naseem Shah is expected to provide experience and support to the emerging pace battery.

For Pakistan, the real significance of Friday’s contest will extend well beyond the result. The management will be keen to identify players capable of becoming long-term members of the national T20 setup, while the youngsters will have a golden opportunity to announce themselves on the international stage. The weather forecast for Friday evening remains favourable, raising hopes of a near-capacity crowd at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium as Pakistan’s next generation steps into the spotlight.
