US Treasury chief says Fed should not hike rates over energy shock tied to Iran war

TheTools · 13.11.2026 14:18 · source · permalink

WASHINGTON, Nov 13 (Reuters) - US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Friday that the Federal Reserve should have an ‌open mind and not hike rates because of an "energy ‌supply shock" tied to the Iran war.

"I think the Fed should understand that ​this is an energy supply shock," while the underlying US economy is very strong, Bessent said in an interview on CNBC.

"So I think that to hike too much into an energy supply shock when ‌it's coming down, these ⁠are very noisy numbers," he said. "You know, the Fed's supposed to look into the future, not look ⁠in the rearview mirror."

A stronger-than-expected US jobs report last week bolstered the case for further Federal Reserve interest rate hikes, which had ​lost some ​momentum amid a post-election stock ​market selloff and growing fears ‌that weaker AI-driven demand could undercut the economy.

The US central bank left its policy rate on hold last month, with Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh saying policymakers would be watching for data to help decide whether more monetary policy tightening would be needed.

"I'm not ‌going to tell the Fed what ​to do. I am going to say ​they should have an ​open mind," Bessent said.

The Fed did three rate ‌cuts over the same period last ​year, he noted.

"And ​now to reverse just based on an energy supply shock, a reversal in a dramatic way isn't something that I ​would do if ‌I were on the Fed. But I'm not on ​the Fed."
