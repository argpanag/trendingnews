Inside Ejae and Samuel Kim’s Garden Wedding at Her Grandfather’s Former Home in Seoul

TheTools · 19.10.2026 19:59 · source · permalink

Musical artist Ejae and producer-turned-computer programmer Samuel Kim’s relationship first began as a creative collaboration. A mutual friend set the two up for a songwriting session at Sam’s home studio in May 2017. “We worked on a couple of songs, but mostly, we just connected,” Ejae recalls. “We both loved anime and the same kind of music.” The two kept things professional—until a night out to see Sam’s roommate’s band perform. “Later that night, [he] confessed his feelings,” the singer says. “We've been together ever since.”

The couple built their lives by each other’s side over the next eight years, settling together in Brooklyn, New York as they built their careers. On June 1, 2025, Sam took the next step in their relationship with a (sort of) surprise proposal. “I had caught on about a week before,” adds Ejae. “Still, it was fun and memorable, and I cried.” To throw her off the scent, Sam worked with Ejae’s best friend to set up a fake dinner. “Instead, Sam brought me to a park in Brooklyn where we spent so much time together, walking Honey, my late dog. He suggested a walk through to go down memory lane,” remembers Ejae. “When we turned a corner, all our friends had set up a picnic with photos of us and our dog all around.” After sharing a thoughtful speech, he popped the question and the singer said “yes.”

Shortly after the proposal, the hit film KPop Demon Hunters came out, which featured Ejae as the lead singer behind the fictional girl group HUNTR/X and the soundtrack’s hit songs. “Our lives changed completely,” says Ejae. The long press tour and awards season surrounding the film meant wedding planning would have to take a pause. “At first, we planned a November 2026 wedding in California, but ultimately after discussing location, we decided to hold it in Korea and moved the date up to October 2026. That left us about four months to plan everything,” says the bride. “It was hectic and a little stressful, but it was real teamwork, and everything has fallen into place perfectly.”

Bringing the wedding to Seoul allowed the couple to further infuse their heritage into their nuptials, along with stories of their lives from childhood to today. The celebration began with a Paebaek ceremony and welcome party at Congdu50, a restaurant with Korean fusion cuisine and sweeping views of the city. “Not everyone does Paebaek, but we wanted to have that traditional element as part of our wedding,” says Ejae.

On October 17, 2026, Ejae and Sam would exchange vows at Stage 28, a venue with meaningful history for the bride. “My grandparents lived in the house at the venue when I was a little girl,” she says. “I spent much of my childhood exploring the garden and walking with my grandfather in the hills behind the house.” The home and property were later purchased and turned into an event venue—making it the perfect choice for their family’s future nuptials. With its nostalgic significance to their family, Ejae’s brother had already tied the knot at Stage 28 as well. For Ejae and Sam’s wedding, the garden ceremony’s décor would evoke the shades of green and earthy tones from the bride’s childhood memories and the property’s landscape.

The venue’s large glasshouse would hold the reception. Ejae envisioned the space being filled with soft fabrics and flowing drapes to create an ethereal, surreal atmosphere. “The glasshouse represents my life today—a time when the dreams I worked toward for so long are becoming reality,” says the artist. “After years of dedication to music, the recognition I am experiencing makes this chapter feel almost like a dream.”

These whimsical aesthetics flowed seamlessly into the bride’s wardrobe choices for the big day. Ejae tapped Danielle Frankel to create a custom gown that captured this spirit as she walked down the aisle. “I had long admired Danielle's modern, but slightly twisted take on a classic wedding dress, and I knew I wanted to reach out to see if she would be interested in working with me on a custom dress,” she says. “From our first meeting, Danielle immediately understood my vision: ethereal, modern, chic, and timeless with a twist.” They worked together to create a tulle gown with a strapless neckline, a bubbled hem, and a nude chiffon layer that brought airiness to the look. “I kept my jewelry simple so the dress could make the statement,” adds the bride of her accessories. Heirloom jewelry passed down from her grandmother to her mother was the perfect sentimental touch to the ensemble.

As the party moved into the glasshouse, Ejae would swap her gown for a custom, body-skimming design by Zac Posen. “The dress is chiffon, like the ceremony gown, so the ethereal feeling carries over into the next chapter of the night,” she says. Working with Posen held special meaning for the bride. “Zac brought me to my first-ever CFDA Awards in 2025, and our creative partnership has only blossomed since,” Ejae shares. “Zac perfectly executed the vision I had for the reception dress—‘wow factor, with a sparkle.’” That shine would continue later in the night with a change into a shimmering Milla Nova mini for dancing.

Sam also put care into creating each look for his wedding day. “I really wanted to learn about bespoke tailoring, and after a lot of research, I chose Bhambi's Custom Tailors in New York, who could make a fully bespoke tuxedo in time,” he says. “It's a classic double-breasted tuxedo in Loro Piana fabric.” To help the newlyweds match at the reception, Posen also created a custom white tuxedo for the groom. “I see it as a new take on the reception suit and the best part is that it's extremely comfortable, fits perfectly, and is elegant,” says Sam.

The bride admits the week leading up to the wedding was a bit “crazy.” “My wedding team and I spent much of the week before the big day finalizing little details and ensuring everything would run smoothly,” she says. It all came together for a moment of connection and joy at the Paebaek ceremony. “Weaving traditional Korean wedding ceremonies into our wedding weekend was so special for us and our family,” says Ejae. “After our Paebaek ceremony, Sam and I relaxed and grounded ourselves in the moment together.”

“There was so much joy to be shared,” says Ejae of the wedding day. “The ceremony was beautiful, heartfelt, and fun.” The bridesmaids and groomsmen walked down the floral-adorned aisle to cheers from all their guests before the bride made her way to the groom. “I’ll never forget the feeling of walking down the aisle and seeing all of my closest friends and family members surrounding me with their love and support,” the bride says. “Sam and I had set intentions to try to stay in the moment and connect with each other and our guests. We definitely were both a bit nervous, but mostly excited to start our lives together as husband and wife.”

That feeling of excitement never stopped throughout the night. Says the bride, “Honestly, we are so proud that we had fun at our wedding!” Their wedding band, After Eight, kept up the energy with a set of songs curated by the couple for the entire reception. Dinner was full of speeches—in both English and Korean—as well as “great food,” says Ejae. Once everyone was on the dance floor, guests could refuel outdoors with bites from Korean street food tents. The party didn’t stop for the couple, as they headed off to Itaewon with a small group after the reception to dance the night away.

With celebrations now at a close, the newlyweds say an overwhelming feeling of gratitude remains: “We are so thankful for each other and for our family and friends, especially given how many of our guests traveled across the world to celebrate with us!”
