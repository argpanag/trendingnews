A federal judge on Tuesday referred Philadelphia District Attorney Larry Krasner and one of his top prosecutors to the Department of Justice for investigation, saying evidence uncovered in a murder case may constitute perjury, obstruction of justice, and conspiracy.

U.S. District Judge Paul Diamond, a George W. Bush appointee, said the evidence was "tantamount to a prima facie showing of conduct that may constitute false statements, false declarations made under oath, perjury, obstruction of justice, and conspiracy."

"I have thus reluctantly concluded that Philadelphia's highest law enforcement official and one of his top assistants may have violated the law," Diamond wrote in a 40-page memorandum.

"In these circumstances, with the issuance of this Memorandum, I refer the DAO, Mr. Krasner, and Stiegler to this District's United States Attorney's Office for investigation and other appropriate action."

Diamond also disqualified Krasner and Matthew Stiegler, chief of the Philadelphia District Attorney's Office's Conviction Integrity Unit, from participating in the case of Dennis Johnson, who is seeking to overturn his 2009 second-degree murder conviction. Diamond declined to disqualify the entire district attorney's office.

Krasner, a liberal prosecutor whose campaigns have received backing from billionaire George Soros, said Tuesday he "respectfully and vigorously disagree[s]" with Diamond's findings and plans to appeal "as soon as the law allows," according to The Philadelphia Inquirer. Stiegler did not respond to the newspaper's request for comment.

The controversy stems from a 2022 filing in which Krasner's office conceded Johnson was entitled to federal habeas relief because his trial attorney had been ineffective. A magistrate judge subsequently recommended granting relief.

But four district attorney's office supervisors reviewed the case this year and concluded the concession contained "material misstatements," prompting the office to move in June to withdraw it.

Diamond held three evidentiary hearings into the office's handling of the case and said he credited five prosecutors whose testimony was corroborated by contemporaneous documents.

He said he "largely discredit[ed]" Stiegler, whose testimony he found "often contradictory, evasive, or outright dishonest."

According to Diamond's findings, Stiegler proposed searching former Assistant District Attorney Jaclyn Mason's other cases for mistakes so she could be portrayed as a "rogue" prosecutor responsible for errors in the concession filing.

Krasner later called that a "really good idea" and urged four supervisors to pursue it, Diamond wrote. The supervisors rejected the proposal.

Diamond concluded Stiegler was urging the office to give the court "a false account to shield himself" and said material omissions and falsehoods in Stiegler's testimony "may well amount to perjury."

The judge said Krasner's conduct was "more troubling," finding that he urged supervisors to present what Diamond called a "false narrative to the Court" and repeatedly insisted someone remain involved in the case to "protect the office."

Diamond also cited testimony that Krasner warned there would be "consequences" if prosecutors alerted the court to the conflict, which two supervisors understood as a threat to their jobs.

Diamond said the alleged threat "could amount to obstruction of justice," while Krasner and Stiegler's alleged effort to persuade prosecutors to present a false account "might amount to criminal conspiracy."

The referral does not constitute criminal charges. The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania would determine whether to investigate or pursue charges. A spokesperson did not respond to the Inquirer's request for comment Tuesday.

The case comes amid broader scrutiny of Krasner's handling of post-conviction cases. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruled in May that the state attorney general must be notified when Krasner's office seeks to overturn certain convictions, citing what the court described as repeated failures of candor by the office.
