Social Security COLA Forecasts Predict Biggest Boost Since COVID

TheTools · 08.10.2026 07:12 · source · permalink

Reporter

Social Security recipients are on track for their biggest annual cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) since the inflation surge that followed the COVID-19 pandemic, with leading forecasts pointing to a 3.5 to 3.6 percent increase in benefits next year.

AARP currently projects a 3.6 percent COLA for 2027, while the Senior Citizens League (TSCL) predicts a slightly lower 3.5 percent. The official increase will be announced next week on October 14, after the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) releases its final inflation figures needed for the calculation.

Either forecast would be an increase from the 2.8 percent COLA beneficiaries received in 2026 and would be the largest adjustment since the 8.7 percent increase that took effect in 2023.

That unusually large 2023 increase followed the sharp inflation experienced after the pandemic. Consumer prices increased 7 percent during 2021, while average prices rose another 8 percent from 2021 to 2022, according to the BLS.

The annual COLA helps benefits keep up with rising costs. Without an annual adjustment, inflation would gradually reduce what beneficiaries can afford with their monthly payments. That matters particularly for the more than 70 million retirees and disabled Americans who claim Social Security benefits to help cover essentials such as housing, food, utilities and health care.

How Is the Social Security COLA Calculated?

The COLA is calculated using the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, or CPI-W. Social Security compares the average CPI-W during July, August and September with the average during the same three months in the previous year. The percentage increase, rounded to the nearest one-tenth of a percentage point, then becomes the next COLA.

Two of the three figures needed for the 2027 calculation have already been released. The CPI-W rose 3.4 percent year over year in July and 3.5 percent in August, according to TSCL. September's figure will determine the final adjustment.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics is scheduled to publish September's Consumer Price Index data at 8:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, October 14.

Previous COLAs

A 3.5 or 3.6 percent adjustment would be higher than each of the past three increases, although considerably below the increases beneficiaries received during the post-pandemic inflation spike.

This year's higher forecast reflects stronger than usual inflation throughout 2026. The Federal Reserve has attributed much of that renewed pressure to higher energy prices linked to the Middle East conflict, earlier tariff increases that have raised the cost of imported goods, and persistent price growth in services.

Social Security's historical figures show that the 5.9 percent and 8.7 percent increases for 2022 and 2023 were followed by substantially smaller adjustments as inflation eased following the pandemic. While this year's increase will be smaller, it would still be the largest since 2023.

How Much Could Social Security Benefits Increase?

The exact dollar increase depends on how much a claimant already receives, based on their earnings record and the age at which they retired.

The average retired worker received $2,087.52 per month in August, according to the latest Social Security Administration data. A 3.5 percent COLA would add about $73 to monthly checks, bringing the payment to about $2,161. A 3.6 percent increase would add roughly $75, bringing it to about $2,163. Per year, the increase would add about $876 to $900 to the average check.

Disabled workers received an average of $1,635.90 per month in August, and under current forecasts, that would rise by roughly $57 to $59 a month, to around $1,693 to $1,695.The average benefit for a nondisabled widow or widower was $1,934.37, which would increase by around $68 to $70 per month, to approximately $2,002 to $2,004 under the two forecasts.

For Supplemental Security Income (SSI) benefit recipients, paid to disabled and blind people with limited income and resources, the average benefit of $738.73 would rise by $25 to $26 per month.

What Happens Next?

The remaining piece of the COLA puzzle will be announced October 14, when the government publishes September inflation data. Social Security will then determine the final 2027 COLA using the complete July-through-September CPI-W average and announce it the same day.

The increase will apply to Social Security benefits for December 2026, which are paid to beneficiaries beginning in January 2027. SSI recipients generally receive their January increase at the end of December because January 1 is a federal holiday.

Recipients will receive personalized notices showing their new monthly benefit amounts. Social Security generally makes COLA notices available through my Social Security account and mails notices to beneficiaries in December before the increase begins.
