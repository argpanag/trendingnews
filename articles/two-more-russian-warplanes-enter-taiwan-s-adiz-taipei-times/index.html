Two more Russian warplanes enter Taiwan’s ADIZ - Taipei Times

TheTools · 18.11.2026 16:00 · source · permalink

Two Russian Tu-95 strategic bombers were detected entering Taiwan's air defense identification zone (ADIZ) from the south this morning, the second time this week such activity has been reported, the Ministry of National Defense said today.

The flights come as Russian President Vladimir Putin is in Shenzhen, China, for the APEC summit. He is scheduled to hold a trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping (習近平) and US President Donald Trump today.

Earlier today, Taiwan's envoy to the summit, Acer founder Stan Shih (施振榮), briefly shook hands with Putin.

Photo: AFP / Japanese Ministry of Defense

The two Tu-95s, known by NATO as "Bears," flew through airspace east of Taiwan before leaving the zone northeast of Keelung, the ministry said in a statement at 2:40pm.

The ministry published a photograph of one of the aircraft it said was taken from a Taiwanese F-16 fighter.

Taiwan's armed forces used joint intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance means to monitor the full course of the activity and "responded appropriately," the ministry said

Meanwhile, one Chinese military aircraft and two official ships were detected operating around Taiwan as of 6am today, the ministry said in its daily update of the Chinese People’s Liberation Army’s (PLA) activities.

One PLA aircraft crossed the median line of the Taiwan Strait and entered Taiwan’s southwestern ADIZ, the ministry said.

Taiwan’s armed forces “monitored the situation and employed [patrol] aircraft, navy vessels and coastal missile systems in response to the detected activities,” it said.

The ministry on Tuesday said that two Russian military aircraft had flown in a southerly direction down its east coast.

Japan also ‌reported the same activity that day and published a map showing the aircraft flying down Taiwan's east coast.

An aging Cold War-era design, some Tu-95s can carry nuclear weapons, but others are used for long-range surveillance and reconnaissance.

The Russian Ministry of Defense did not respond to requests for comment.
