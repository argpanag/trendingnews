President still intends to use a portion of her salary ‘for the common good’

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:05 · source · permalink

President Catherine Connolly still intends to use a portion of her salary “for the common good” as promised during the election campaign, but after just over a year in office, she is still considering the details of the donation.

Under legislation, the president’s pay is 10 per cent more than the remuneration of the Chief Justice, and it currently stands at around €370,000.

Former president Mary McAleese took a pay cut during the last financial crisis.

Her successor, Michael D Higgins, was elected in the aftermath of the crash, and he voluntarily waived his Oireachtas and ministerial pensions throughout his time in office.

Higgins also waived a portion of his salary for most of his time at Áras an Uachtaráin. His salary returned to its full level in late 2022.

Higgins gifted around €2 million back to the State over his 14 years as president between the waiving of pensions and the pay cut.

At the launch of her presidential election campaign in July 2025, Connolly was asked about her intentions for the salary.

Connolly said she would “look at the salary” and is “open to ideas on that”, adding: “I’m certainly going to use it for the common good.”

She said she would discuss it with her team and set out how she would use the salary “in due course”.

A statement from Áras an Uachtaráin on Wednesday said: “It remains President Connolly’s intention to make a donation in line with the commitment made during the presidential election campaign and she is continuing to consider the means by which she will do this.”

During her first year in office, Connolly had 345 public engagements, visited all 32 counties on the island and received 6,800 visitors at Áras an Uachtaráin.

She has visited Northern Ireland four times and has been to Britain twice, as well as attending summits in Spain and Slovenia.
