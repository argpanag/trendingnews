Gianni Infantino Involved in Bombshell New FIFA Scandal as Damning Report Emerges

TheTools · 12.10.2026 20:51 · source · permalink

Another investigation into controversial football chief Gianni Infantino has unearthed damning new details surrounding a scandal involving the FIFA president in the build-up to the World Cup.

Needless to say, the Swiss millionaire has found himself at the centre of controversy throughout 2026. He got the year off to a controversial start by presenting Donald Trump with the inaugural FIFA Peace Prize. Separately, plans to sell stakes in the World Cup sparked further backlash, raising questions about the commercial direction of football's biggest tournament.

That decision landed him in particularly hot water, with several prominent nations withdrawing their written support for his re-election ahead of March, including England, Scotland and Ireland, while Germany and France could yet follow suit.

However, the latest investigation concerns a separate controversy altogether, bringing fresh allegations about FIFA's dealings in the build-up to the World Cup into the spotlight. With his leadership already under scrutiny on several fronts, the new revelations threaten to pile further pressure on the FIFA president and leave his increasingly embattled position looking more precarious than ever.

Gianni Infantino Paid Double Salary, New Investigation Finds

An investigation carried out by the Times' Martyn Ziegler, who was the first to reveal Infantino's plans to make a fortune from World Cup stakes, has revealed that FIFA paid millions of dollars to the president on top of his standard salary and bonus, but did not publicly declare the payments in an alleged violation of its own statutes.

Sources say that some of the undisclosed money paid to Infantino was to cover his luxury lifestyle in Miami in the run-up to the World Cup, including renting a stunning waterfront mansion for tens of thousands of dollars a month.

Infantino has been receiving two separate salaries - one from FIFA in Switzerland which is declared in its annual report, and a separate one paid by the World Cup organising company in the USA, which was not declared.

Amid the Premier League's guilty verdict against Manchester City, which included having found that the club were paying Roberto Mancini a double contract, football's financial integrity has been hit with a similar farcical situation at the very top of the beautiful game's food chain.

Infantino's Luxurious Lifestyle is Being Funded by FIFA

In 2024, Infantino was stated by FIFA's annual report as earning 4.274m CHF (£3.88m). However, a financial return filed with the United States’ Internal Revenue Service by the company set up to organise the World Cup, FWC2026 US Inc, for that year shows Infantino was paid a further $1.76m (£1.3m) that was not publicly disclosed by the organistion in its annual report.

Sources said Infantino was paid similar sums - or potentially more - for 2025 and 2026. Fifa’s secretary general, Mattias Grafstrom, received no extra payment from FWC2026, nor did CONCACAF president Victor Montagliani, even though he was chairman of the FWC2026 board.

Part of his FWC2026 $1.76m payment for 2024 included $626,229 of “other reportable compensation” as well as base salary and bonus. Sources in international football have questioned whether this money was to pay for his use of the waterfront mansion in one of Miami Beach’s most desirable areas - FIFA has not confirmed if this was the case.

The property is in the ultra-exclusive Sunset Islands, a guard-gated community of four man-made islands. Infantino had the use of a mansion bought by the billionaire former Google chief executive Eric Schmidt for $27m (£20.41m) in 2023, which has its own pool and private dock, though it is believed he moved out late in 2025.

Former FIFA Chairman Hits Out at Infantino

Miguel Maduro, the former chairman of FIFA's governance committee, said the failure to make the extra payments public was “a clear violation of Fifa’s statutes” and needed independent investigation.

Article 43, paragraph 13 of FIFA's statutes reads: “The individual compensation of the FIFA president, the members of the council and the FIFA secretary general shall be made public.” Maduro told The Times:

“It is a clear contravention of the rules if he is paid other amounts by companies related to Fifa and it is not made public. The FIFA statutes impose an obligation of full transparency. It is not appropriate for the compensation sub-committee to authorise other forms of remuneration."

He concluded: “I agree the president of FIFA needs to be very well paid but that needs to be transparent.” Meanwhile, the newspaper has asked FIFA for an explanation of the extra undisclosed payments to Infantino but, in its response, it did not explain why they were not declared in the annual report.
