TSX climbs as lower oil prices curb inflation worries

TheTools · 09.11.2026 10:07 · source · permalink

Canada’s main ‌stock index opened higher ⁠on ​Monday, as a ​slide ‌in oil prices tempered ‌inflation worries, ​while ‌investors ​awaited key U.S. ⁠inflation ⁠data ​due later in the week.

The S&P/TSX Composite ⁠Index was up 0.7 per cent at 36,038.46 ⁠points ​at ⁠09:30 a.m. ‌ET.

Oil dropped sharply on Monday ​after US President Donald Trump downplayed the ‌likelihood of renewed strikes on Iran, though attacks on ships near the Strait of Hormuz kept supply concerns in focus.

Trump said on Sunday that he ⁠was highly ​unlikely to order fresh attacks on Iran as long as talks to end the conflict progressed, even as both countries reported that shipping had been hit in or near the Strait of Hormuz.

Brent crude ​futures slid by US$4.09, or 4.2 per cent, to US$94.23 a ‌barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate lost US$3.57, or 3.9 per cent, to US$87.26.

Wall ​Street’s main ‌indexes opened higher on Monday, as ⁠investors ​attempted to shake off last week’s AI ​anxieties ‌while awaiting more clarity on the controversy surrounding ‌Nvidia ​CEO Jensen ‌Huang.

The ​Dow Jones Industrial ⁠Average rose ⁠540.52 points, or ​1.05 per cent, to 51,826.25. The S&P 500 rose 59.90 ⁠points, or 0.81 per cent, while ​the Nasdaq Composite ⁠rose 200.72 points, or ‌0.82 per cent, to 24,780.15.

The gains kick off a pivotal week expected to offer some insight into whether the selloff post the midterms last week went too far or was warranted. Analysts warned ​that several concerns were converging under the surface.

Shares of ‌Nvidia (NVDA-Q), the second most valuable company in the world, fell 7.3 per cent in early trading after an internal investigation into whether CEO Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort raised questions about his future at the AI chipmaker.

“This was just the cherry on top for investors looking to pull ‌out of ​AI-linked stocks. It’s a ‌very bad headline that’s affecting tech stocks,” said Nadia El Bilassy, senior market analyst at ​Equiti Group.

The focus on Nvidia may subside later ⁠this week “when we have more important things to discuss, like the inflation ⁠data,” she added.

The Labor Department is scheduled to release its Consumer Price Index report for October on ​Tuesday.

Fears of a December rate hike also persisted following October’s stronger-than-expected jobs data.

Shares of some chipmakers rose in early trading, with Intel and Marvell Technology up 1.1 per cent and 1.6 per cent, respectively, while Qualcomm fell 0.3 per cent.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index ended Friday with its biggest weekly loss since March 2025.

“The AI correction in equities could have much further to run,” wrote Thomas Mathews, head of markets for Asia Pacific at Capital Economics.

“One further risk would be any ⁠concrete sign that the hyperscalers were paring back capital ​expenditures,” he added.

Among the hyperscalers, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Amazon were up between ⁠2 per cent and 3 per cent.

Investment giant KKR was up 0.4 per cent after its quarterly profit hit a record high. Constellation Energy, an energy ‌firm with exposure to the AI boom, rose 1.7 per cent after its adjusted third-quarter profit surpassed expectations.

Of ​the 335 companies in the S&P 500 that had reported earnings as of Thursday, 82.4 per cent beat estimates, according to Tajinder Dhillon, head of earnings and equity research at LSEG Data and Analytics.

Berkshire Hathaway’s class B shares were 0.4 per cent ​lower after the conglomerate reported lower third-quarter profit.
