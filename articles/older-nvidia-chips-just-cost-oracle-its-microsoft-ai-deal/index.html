Oracle's stock has lost over half its value since June, and negative free cash flow makes each lost renewal sting more.

Oracle (NYSE:ORCL) shares fell to $109.21 on November 6, 2026, a new 52-week low. The drop came after people familiar with the matter said Microsoft (NASDAQ:MSFT) is winding down its deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle.

Those people said Microsoft declined to renew the contract and has spent recent months switching the capacity off. They said Oracle's hardware ran on older Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA) chips that no longer met Microsoft's specifications. The chip generation was not specified, and Microsoft declined to comment.

The low was about 5.2% below the November 5 close of $115.16. Oracle then turned and closed at $116.01, up 0.74%, for a market value of $361.69 billion.

Microsoft fell 0.99% to $530.61, and no cause was reported. For a company worth $3.94 trillion, a rental contract from the early Bing chatbot days is a small line item.

The two accounts conflict because a top customer reportedly decided Oracle's chips were too old for its needs, while Oracle says even its oldest GPUs get rented again at a higher price.

What Microsoft Is Actually Ending

The deal was announced in 2023 and gave Microsoft server access to run the then-new AI version of Bing. Its full size was never determined, and its value was never disclosed.

At the time, renting computing power from a rival looked like an early sign of an AI chip shortage. Microsoft is now letting the contract run out and has given no reason of its own.
