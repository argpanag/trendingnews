Fresh Cyclone Warning Raises Livelihood Concerns at Vizag Fishing Harbour

TheTools · 16.11.2026 01:48 · source · permalink

Fresh weather warning creates uncertainty at fishing harbour

A fresh weather warning over the Bay of Bengal has raised concerns among the fishing community at Visakhapatnam Fishing Harbour, with fishermen facing uncertainty over when they can safely resume their operations.

The warning comes after successive spells of adverse weather along the Andhra Pradesh coast. For fishing families, another interruption could mean additional days without income, particularly for those who depend on daily catches and harbour-based trade.

IMD tracks developing weather system

According to the India Meteorological Department's coastal weather bulletin issued on November 16, a deep depression was located approximately 870 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam at 11:30 PM IST on November 15.

The IMD indicated that the system was likely to intensify into a cyclonic storm over the central Bay of Bengal on November 16 and a severe cyclonic storm over the west-central Bay of Bengal on November 17. It was subsequently expected to move northwestwards towards the Andhra Pradesh coast.

The bulletin also forecast squally weather and moderate to rough sea conditions along the North Andhra coast, with a distant cautionary signal in force at Visakhapatnam Port.

Fishing restrictions affect daily earnings

Whenever adverse weather warnings are issued, fishing activity can be disrupted by restrictions on venturing into the sea. Such interruptions affect not only boat operators and crew members but also workers who depend on the arrival of fresh catches.

Repeated suspensions can create financial difficulties for families that rely on daily earnings from fishing. Boat owners also face expenses related to vessel maintenance, equipment protection and other operating costs.

Fisherwomen face additional livelihood challenges

The impact extends beyond fishermen to women involved in purchasing fish at harbour auctions, processing catches, drying fish and selling them in local markets.

Fresh fish must be sold quickly, while dried fish businesses depend on suitable weather conditions. Rain and persistent cloud cover can damage fish laid out for drying, resulting in losses for traders.

For women who invest their own money or borrow to purchase fish, repeated disruptions can deepen financial difficulties and make it harder to meet household expenses.

Coastal community awaits weather developments

With the Bay of Bengal system under close observation, fishing communities are watching official weather updates and awaiting further guidance from the authorities.

The latest situation highlights the vulnerability of coastal livelihoods to repeated weather disruptions. Timely warnings, safe harbour arrangements and effective support for affected fishing families remain important as Visakhapatnam faces another period of uncertain weather.
