Large out-of-control hillside fire near Queenstown to burn overnight

TheTools · 06.10.2026 07:25 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Smoke rises from the Walter Peak fire, which had spread across more than 146 hectares by 3pm. Photo / Fire and Emergency NZ

Six helicopters continue to battle a huge vegetation fire beside Lake Wakatipu as smoke drifts toward Queenstown.

About 25 firefighters and support staff are fighting the Walter Peak fire, which has grown since it was mapped at 146ha with a perimeter of almost 6km at 3pm.

Photos show flames along the lower slopes and a large plume of smoke obscuring much of the mountainside.

Fire and Emergency NZ said there were three areas still burning and the fire was yet to be contained.

“Firefighters from Queenstown, Frankton, Glenorchy and Mossburn are working on the western flank to consolidate the perimeter. They and the air attack will continue working until sunset, when operations will be suspended overnight.”

Crews have successfully protected the Walter Peak homestead and other nearby buildings.

People have been warned they may continue to see flames and smoke overnight and into tomorrow.

“As the fire is still travelling quickly downwind it is feeding on patches of vegetation, which is causing smoke to intensify as it moves through those areas.

“Smoke will continue to drift across the lake towards Jacks Point and Hanleys Farm, so people in areas affected by the smoke should keep doors and windows shut to minimise their exposure.”

RealNZ, owners and operators of Walter Peak station, said the area was south of the Walter Peak Homestead.

“Following a controlled scrub burn last week, the site has reignited this afternoon,” a spokesperson said.

They said the Homestead and nearby treeline were not impacted at this stage.

“As a precaution, guests, contractors and staff have been evacuated to the amphitheatre while the burn area is dampened down. Our 1pm and 3pm Walter Peak experiences have been cancelled.”

Tour guide Kevin Pilbrow told the Herald the blaze was the biggest he has seen in 26 years of taking groups around the South Island.

Pilbrow arrived at Walter Peak aboard the TSS Earnslaw with a group of American tourists about 11.45am and headed into the Colonel’s Homestead Restaurant for a barbecue lunch.

He said the group first noticed a “little bit of smoke” while they were on the water.

“Then, in the course of us having lunch, I popped out a couple of times just to keep an eye on it and it had spread quite rapidly,” he said.

By the time they finished eating, he said there was a “tremendous amount of smoke” sweeping along the mountainside towards Queenstown.

Pilbrow estimated the fire was about 200m from the restaurant but said he did not believe the group was in danger.

“Even the smoke never came because there was a very, very strong southerly wind and it was blowing away from the restaurant itself and all the way up the hill. So we never even got a smell of the smoke.”

Tour guide Sinchan Haria, who was at Walter Peak with a group for lunch and a farm show, told the Herald there were initially only a few wisps of smoke and it appeared to be a routine controlled burn.

About an hour and 15 minutes later, when the group headed outside after lunch, large plumes of smoke were rising from the hillside.

“We were then told to evacuate the homestead by the Real NZ staff who were very calm, courteous and collected. People were outside and taking photos and videos but there was no panic.”

Haria said a helicopter soon began drawing water from the lake to fight the fire.

A RealNZ catamaran then arrived to take visitors back to Queenstown, with the remaining visitors following aboard the TSS Earnslaw.
