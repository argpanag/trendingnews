Trump launches new effort to oust Fed Governor Lisa Cook | CNN Business

TheTools · 09.10.2026 14:26 · source · permalink

President Donald Trump is mounting a fresh offensive in his battle to oust Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, forming a committee of his own appointees to investigate allegations against her.

The committee will investigate alleged false statements Cook made in connection with mortgages on two properties and hold an in-person hearing at the White House on November 5, the White House announced on Friday.

Analysts say the formation of the committee and setting of a hearing date indicate Trump officials are addressing procedural issues addressed by a June Supreme Court ruling that initially blocked the administration from removing Cook.

Newly installed Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling, who is also serving as acting director of the Office of Government Ethics, is one of the three members. So is National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett, a staunch defender of Trump’s economic agenda, whom the president considered for Fed chair. The third member is Andrea Lucas, chair of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission.

Cook’s attorneys, Abbe Lowell and Norm Eisen, said they have been in touch with the White House “to evaluate whether the process proposed has a possibility of being a genuine one and not simply a box-checking exercise.”

“Governor Cook welcomes the opportunity to present the facts so she can clear her name and demonstrate there is no legal basis to fire her,” they said in a statement provided to CNN on Friday.

The formation of the committee marks the latest salvo in Trump’s ongoing attempts to reshape the Fed and comes just weeks ahead of its next monetary policy meeting, where officials will consider whether interest rate changes are merited.

“This seems tailor-made to address the Supreme Court’s biggest criticism in its last attempt: that Governor Cook didn’t have a chance to defend herself,” said Tim Mahedy, a former official at the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, who is now CEO of research firm Access/Macro.

Mahedy said the outcome of this review “is clear” given the makeup of the committee. He expressed surprise that Hassett, a White House economist, would be on a committee investigating a legal matter about paperwork.

Trump has long berated the politically independent Fed because it hasn’t lowered interest rates to his liking, publicly targeting Cook and former Fed Chair Jerome Powell. But Trump has spared the current chairman, Kevin Warsh, whom he appointed to the position earlier this year after Powell’s term at the head of the central bank expired. Instead, Trump has insisted that Warsh is blocked by a “political” Board of Governors.

The central bank’s eight-member Board votes on interest rate decisions, along with regional Fed presidents. While the Fed leader often plays an influential role in shaping the discussion on policy moves, decisions are ultimately the result of the 12-person committee process.

At its September meeting, all 12 members, including Warsh, voted to raise rates for the first time in three years in an effort to contain inflation that has heated up largely due to the US war with Iran.

Trump said later that Warsh shouldn’t have greenlit a rate hike. “I don’t blame Kevin Warsh. I probably would have voted against the Board if I were him,” Trump said in an interview Time Magazine published earlier this month.

Separately, Trump reaffirmed he wants Warsh to “vote the way he wants.”

Trump vs. Cook

Trump previously sought to fire Cook over unproven allegations of mortgage fraud. Ultimately, in a 5-4 decision earlier this year, the Supreme Court ruled Trump could not immediately remove her.

“The 5-4 Supreme Court decision did not prohibit President Trump from firing a Fed Governor, it gave him a roadmap to do it,” Ed Mills, Washington policy analyst at Raymond James, told CNN in an email.

Chief Justice John Roberts wrote in his majority opinion released in June that Cook was entitled to sufficient time and notice to respond to a termination and that she wasn’t given a fair opportunity to respond last year when Trump initially sought to remove her.

But that doesn’t mean Trump can’t lawfully fire her, he said. The law allows a president to remove Fed governors “for cause,” which is generally interpreted to mean negligence or malfeasance in the role.

The “ultimate question of whether the presi­dent can remove Cook for cause will depend in part on the underlying facts,” Roberts wrote. “We have not addressed the facts, as they have yet to be found or analyzed under the relevant legal standards.”

Mills said Cook is “very clearly at risk of being fired, and it is unclear if the Supreme Court would block President Trump in Round 2.”

He added that two of Cook’s colleagues, Powell and Governor Michael Barr, are also on the list for “potential ‘for-cause’ firing.”

Kurt Lewis, a former special adviser to Powell who is now head of central bank policy at Piper Sandler, said this will ultimately come down to how much deference the Supreme Court gives the president on “cause” once justices think he has “followed a semblance of procedure.”

Cook was appointed by former President Joe Biden in 2022 and is the first Black female Fed governor, with a term set to expire in 2038.
