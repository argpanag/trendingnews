Canadiens vs. Predators: Game preview and how to watch

TheTools · 08.10.2026 10:00 · source · permalink

The Habs break up their homestand with a trip to Nashville.

Game 4: Montreal Canadiens vs. Nashville Predators

Start time: 7:00 PM EDT / 4:00 PM PDT
In the Canadiens region: TSN2 (English), RDS (French)
In the Predators region: SCRIPPS
Streaming: ESPN+, RDS, TSN+

After a couple of good results on the road to begin the season, the Montreal Canadiens returned home for a disappointing game at the Bell Centre. It wasn’t so much the end result of a 6-4 regulation loss that included an empty-netter, but how the team played in that defeat. The talk of the off-season was about needing to make some changes to better handle the Hurricanes, but it didn’t appear that anything had changed since they lost Game 5 of the Eastern Conference Final.

The countdown now begins to the net time the two teams meet: November 19 in Raleigh. Montreal will be about a quarter of the way through this 84-game season at that point, and will have had plenty of time to revisit their tactics and get accustomed to the new style of play Derek Lalonde is trying to implement.

You can’t get much more of a swing than going from the team that eliminated Montreal with four consecutive wins in the playoffs to the Western Conference’s Nashville Predators, a team that hasn’t made the post-season in two years. But all of the games are important, not just the three measuring-stick matches versus the defending champions, and the lack of emotion in tonight’s game is a good chance for the coaching staff to make sure everyone is sticking to the plan.

The Predators also had a goal for the new season: a fast start to avoid falling behind in the Central Division. Through three games they’ve had a bit of success on that front, recovering from a season-opening loss to the Minnesota Wild by beating the Dallas Stars two days later. Most recently they blew a 3-0 lead on the Toronto Maple Leafs to lose in overtime, and they won’t be happy about that lost point.

To keep pace in their division, they will need more goals. Maybe more accurately, they need more goal-scorers. Through three games, only three players have hit the scoresheet: Steven Stamkos three times, Nils Höglander the same, and Roman Josi once. It will probably just be a matter of time until Filip Forsberg gets on that list, though he hasn’t managed to do so versus Montreal in the past two seasons. The team scored a total of three goals against Montreal in 2025-26.

That’s the type of shutdown the Habs coaching staff will be looking to see from the team once again. Offence has been relatively easy to come by for Montreal, even last game when they struggled to get shots. If the Canadiens don’t give the puck away under pressure and mess up their assignments the way they did on Saturday, they should be able to recover from their first regulation loss of the season and work on getting back to a style of play more befitting a contender.
