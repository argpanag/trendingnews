Did Lizzie Borden Ever Have A Romance With Maid Bridget Sullivan? An Expert Unpacks the True Story Behind ‘Monster’ Season 4

TheTools · 18.09.2026 20:49 · source · permalink

Kimmy Yam

In Netflix’s “Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story,” domestic servant Bridget Sullivan is portrayed as a seductive manipulator who plays a substantial role in the dynamics that lead to Lizzie Borden’s infamous trial for the gruesome Aug. 1982 murders of her father and stepmother.

But the sensational nature of the series has viewers wondering if the maid, played by actor Vicky Krieps, was truly the figure who was stirring the pot in the real-life Borden family all along.

It turns out, the true story of Bridget Sullivan was far less salacious. And though the season, which was released on Sept. 17, depicts her as entangled in a romance with Lizzie Borden herself, experts and historical accounts show that the pair had a professional relationship — one that never crossed into the realm of lust.

As Cara Robertson — author of the book “The Trial of Lizzie Borden,” which is largely considered the definitive account of the case — tells TODAY.com, it “sounds as if they want to make this a sexy story and it just isn’t.”

How is Bridget Sullivan Portrayed in ‘Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story?’

Bridget makes her mark not long into the first episode of the series.

After the Borden household goes through a slew of maids, Bridget interviews with the family and immediately impresses matriarch Abby Borden, Lizzie’s harsh, hard-to-please stepmother, with the way in which she is able to remove a stubborn blood stain from a prized fainting couch. Patriarch Andrew Borden is also struck by Bridget’s presence, drawn to her lack of queasiness around blood — something that’s important to him as a woodworker who builds coffins for a living.

But perhaps the person most captivated by Bridget is Lizzie herself, particularly after the new maid takes a special interest in her, introducing Lizzie to some more risqué things, including a hand-cranked vibrator. Bridget also attempts to ingratiate herself with Lizzie but emphasizing Abby’s poor treatment of her.

“I don’t know why she belittles you like that,” Bridget says to Lizzie in the first episode, entitled “Bloodbath.” “She’s the most cruel woman I’ve ever seen.”

Though Lizzie appears to be more sympathetic to her stepmother’s temper, Bridget’s tactic works. And before long, there are secret bedroom visits and intimate meetings between the two.

Their sexual tension-filled bond is cemented by the end of the premiere episode when the two share a bath of cherry juice, inspired by Elizabeth Báthory, a 16th century Hungarian countess who is said to have bathed in the blood of young women to preserve her beauty, though Bathory’s infamous blood baths are likely myth rather than truth.

Earlier in the episode, it is Bridget who gifts the story of Báthory to Lizzie.

What Was The Real-Life Bridget Sullivan Really Like?

The real-life Bridget Sullivan was indeed a maid for the family. But aside from that detail, the similarities between Sullivan and her “Monster” character are few and far between.

Sullivan, who hailed from Ireland, worked for the Borden family for a number of years, starting in 1889, according to the Lizzie Borden House. However, according to Robertson’s book, she was not always happy with her employment, telling an officer that she had attempted to leave the position on multiple occasions.

As the book points out, most of the members of the Borden family did not bother to learn Sullivan’s name, calling her “Maggie,” simply because that was the name of the family’s domestic servant before her.

Sullivan also wasn’t particularly entrenched in the family’s drama, Robertson says.

“Bridget was an observer of the family tensions. She lived in the house and negotiated the dynamics,” the author says.

Nor is there any evidence that Bridget was a master manipulator, she adds.

The 25-year-old Sullivan did end up becoming a key witness in the trial, as she was in the home at the time of the murders. Though Sullivan testified over the course of two days, she was ruled out as a possible suspect.

So Did Bridget Ever Have A Romance With Lizzie?

Over the decades, many have attempted to romantically link Lizzie Borden with Sullivan, especially because the former was never married and considered a spinster for the time. But the truth is that there’s just never been any evidence that points toward a steamy affair between the two. It was all just strictly professional, Robertson explains.

“This is simply a newer version of a longstanding desire to give Lizzie a fictional romance,” Robertson says. “It’s as if writers and adapters of the story can’t make their stories work without inserting a love interest.”

What’s more, the real-life Sullivan had a rather sunny view of Abby Borden. Robertson says that Abby Borden was the only person in the household who bothered to call her by her real name.

“We really don’t know much about her relationship with Abby,” Robertson says. “Bridget spoke positively of her after her death, calling her ‘a lovely woman.’”

Bridget Sullivan herself did eventually marry. After the murders, she eventually found her way to Anaconda, Montana where she tied the knot with laborer and fellow Irish immigrant John Sullivan, according to the Lizzie Borden House. With the middle-class living that John Sullivan was able to provide, Bridget Sullivan left behind her work as a domestic servant. She refused, however, to speak of the murders ever again.
