Craig Bellamy will end his 25-season tenure as Melbourne Storm's head coach at the end of the 2027 NRL season. (Getty Images: Daniel Pockett)

In short:

Craig Bellamy will depart from his role as Melbourne Storm coach at the end of the 2027 NRL season.

Bellamy, 67, hopes to remain involved with the club in various non-coaching roles after his exit. 

He has previously said that Melbourne's next coach should come from within.

Melbourne can officially begin planning for life after Craig Bellamy, after the veteran coach confirmed the longest tenure in NRL premiership history would end in 2027.

After years of speculation about his future, Bellamy will officially finish with the Storm next year at the end of his 25th season in charge of the club.

The 67-year-old's call to walk away comes after he was diagnosed with a neurodegenerative disorder this year.

But he still hopes to play some role around the club after 2027, with the five-time premiership-winning coach the most powerful figure in the Storm's history.

"I love this club and everything it represents, the people, our history and our fans," Bellamy said.

"I still have huge passion for the job, and I'm excited about what we can achieve together in 2027.

"While 2027 will be my final season as senior coach, I'm determined to make the most of the opportunity and give everything I can to our club and our players.

"I've always said I'll continue coaching while I believe I can make a positive contribution, and I feel I can still do that.

"I also feel the time is right to make 2027 my final year."

Cameron Munster is one of several big-name Storm stars who are off-contract at the end of the 2027 season. (Getty Images: Pete Dovgan/Speed Media/Icon Sportswire)

Bellamy's decision to officially call stumps comes after chief executive Justin Rodksi admitted on Friday the uncertain future had complicated contract talks with the club's biggest names.

Harry Grant, Cameron Munster and Stefano Utoikamanu are all off-contract at the end of 2027, with the Storm facing an uphill fight to retain all three.

Melbourne hasn't been able to provide clarity on who will coach the club in 2028, unable to officially move on appointing a successor until Bellamy had decided his future.

Billy Slater remains the biggest name linked with Melbourne after he walked away from the Queensland State of Origin job in August.

Billy Slater is the highest-profile name tipped to be Bellamy's successor for the Storm. (Getty Images: Darrian Traynor)

Slater does sit on Melbourne's part-time staff, but has so far suggested he does not want to coach in the NRL.

Bellamy also declared in August that Melbourne's next coach should come from within, with Marc Brentnall, Ryan Hinchliffe and Bellamy's son Aaron his full-time assistants.

Brad Arthur, Michael Maguire, Anthony Seibold and Jason Ryles are among coaches to have come out of Bellamy's system in Melbourne.

Arthur is currently coaching Leeds in the Super League, but has signalled a desire to return to the NRL, while Maguire is under pressure at the Broncos.

Ryles is on the verge of signing an extension at Parramatta, while Seibold is a free agent after being sacked by Manly last season.

"Craig is one of the greatest coaches in the history of rugby league, and we're proud of everything he has achieved in the game," Storm chairman Matthew Tripp said.

"We're also delighted he'll have one final season in charge in 2027.

"While we would obviously love to have Craig coaching our team forever, we understand how significant this decision is and are incredibly grateful that we will have another season with him leading our club."
