India defeated Pakistan by 19 runs to win their second successive cricket gold medal at Korogi Sports Park in Nisshin on Saturday. Chasing a daunting 212-run target, that was built on Abhishek Sharma’s blistering 61 and Tilak Varma’s quickfire 51, Pakistan almost pulled off a thrilling victory.

Hasan Nawaz, who walked to the crease with Pakistan in a spot of bother at 36/2 in 4.5 overs, almost pulled off an incredible victory for his team.

Nawaz kept Pakistan in the hunt with a swashbuckling 51-ball 96 that was studded with 7 sixes and 5 fours.

Nawaz, who was dropped twice during his innings – once by Vaibhav Sooryavanshi when he was on 4 in the ninth over and then by Washington Sundar when he was on 59 in the 15th over, made the Indians pay for their sloppy work in the outfield.

But in the end, Nawaz’s lone warrior act wasn’t enough to take Pakistan to victory and they had to settle for a silver medal.

Who is Hasan Nawaz?

Hasan Nawaz was born in Southern Punjab, Pakistan and started his cricketing journey in the unforgiving world of tape-ball cricket before moving on to the hard ball. His natural talent and aggressive style saw him get picked in the Pakistan Super League where he made his debut for Islamabad United in 2023 before moving to Quetta Gladiators for the 2025 season.

Pakistan’s side was going through a big rebuild with a new crop of players emerging after a disappointing early exit in the 2024 T20 World Cup. Hasan was one of the few who got the opportunity to show the world what he was capable of.

He made his T20I debut against New Zealand away from home, but his introduction to international cricket was anything but smooth. He began with back-to-back ducks before exploding in his third appearance, smashing a blistering century to announce himself on the big stage. He was unable to build on that innings, however, failing in his next two outings.

Hasan's batting is built around risk. He is willing to take the aggressive option, attack bowlers and attempt shots that can leave spectators on the edge of their seats. When it comes off, the result can be spectacular; when it doesn't, the dismissal can arrive just as quickly.

That volatility is both his appeal and his challenge. As he settles into international cricket, Hassan may eventually need to find a balance between his daredevil instincts and the kind of calculated approach that gives his team a greater safety net at the crease.
