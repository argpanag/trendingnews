REVIEW: Pierre (S1 E3/6)

TheTools · 19.10.2026 21:00 · source · permalink

This week’s instalment of Pierre shifts some of its attention away from the central mystery and towards the inner workings of Pierre’s law practice, giving Charlotte, Danny and Alecia a chance to take centre stage. It’s a welcome change of pace, particularly as the series continues to build up the people surrounding its titular character.

There’s a more detailed look at the realities of running a small legal practice, and the difficulties of making ends meet while trying to accommodate Pierre’s idealism. Charlotte finds herself presented with a potentially lucrative solution when an old university flame, now a senior figure at a much larger firm, offers her some overflow business. There is, however, a catch: he wants something in return, and it’s something Charlotte is unwilling to give. So, rather than taking the easy route, it’s back to finding ways of keeping the practice afloat.

Meanwhile, Pierre’s investigation into Michael’s death continues to gather pace. He discovers the identity of the young man Tanner was handing money to and remembers seeing him at the crime scene. The obvious question is why Tanner was paying him – and, more disturbingly, whether he was being paid to plant drugs on Michael’s body (this is my speculation).

But Pierre’s determination to keep digging is beginning to have consequences. Ronnie warns him off the case, while his son is beaten up in what looks like an attempt to frighten Pierre into backing down. Then, as if that wasn’t enough, a complete stranger approaches him with another warning. It raises the stakes considerably and, more importantly, suggests that Michael’s death may reach much further up the chain than Pierre initially imagined.

There’s a real sense now that Pierre is poking around somewhere he really shouldn’t. The balance between the practice’s everyday struggles and the increasingly dangerous investigation works well, and the episode leaves us with a tantalising question: just how high up does this case go?

Paul Hirons
