For Adam Fan, also known as Fan Chengcheng, going global is not simply a matter of changing languages. His debut full-length English-language album, SKYFALL, arrives September 16, 2026, as an intentional expansion of an artistic identity already established across Chinese music and entertainment.

Released alongside the official music video for the focus track “Make It Make Sense,” SKYFALL moves through melodic rap, trap, rage, rock, and dance while keeping its central ideas close to ambition, pressure, independence, and persistence. The result is an album designed to introduce Adam Fan to international listeners without reducing his identity to the crossover itself.

Adam Fan’s “SKYFALL” Defines His International Sound

Adam Fan’s SKYFALL is a debut English-language hip-hop album that combines melodic rap, trap, rage, rock, and dance influences while connecting his Chinese entertainment background with an international-facing musical direction.

Why Does “SKYFALL” by Adam Fan Stand Out?

SKYFALL stands out through its range: Adam Fan moves between atmospheric melodic rap, trap-driven songs, Rage production, rock crossover, and dance-oriented material rather than committing to a single contemporary hip-hop lane. The album also uses English-language songwriting and international production to establish a new phase of his artistic identity for audiences beyond China.

“Skyfall” Establishes the Album’s Emotional Center

The album begins with “Skyfall,” a cinematic title track that establishes a more introspective side of Adam Fan.

Co-written by American artist Lawson, whose writing credits include The Weeknd and Playboi Carti’s “Timeless,” the song combines ethereal melodies with melodic rap. Its subject matter turns inward, examining pressure, loneliness, setbacks, self-discovery, and the determination to keep moving.

That opening matters because it gives SKYFALL an emotional foundation before the project moves into more aggressive and energetic territory.

The song is less concerned with presenting success as a finished destination than with the internal experience of pursuing it.

“Make It Make Sense” Turns Confidence Into the Focus

“Make It Make Sense” shifts the album into a more composed and assertive mode.

Written and produced by Puku, whose credits include “SOMETHING ABOUT YOU” by PARTYNEXTDOOR and Drake, the track is built around trap-driven production and a confident lyrical perspective. Rather than leaning on spectacle alone, its central message emphasizes personal growth, ability, and dedication over superficial appearances.

The accompanying music video extends that perspective through cutting-edge fashion and an international rap aesthetic. Adam Fan’s presentation becomes part of the song’s argument: confidence comes from knowing what you bring to the room.

“SKYFALL” Moves From Introspection to Self-Definition

The sequencing of SKYFALL gives the album a sense of movement, beginning with introspection before gradually shifting toward confidence, energy, and collective ambition.

“Skyfall” establishes that emotional starting point, exploring pressure, loneliness, self-discovery, and the determination to keep moving despite setbacks. From there, “Make It Make Sense” turns inward reflection into self-assurance, while “Go Girl” brings a more energetic melodic dance-rap approach centered on confidence, independence, and female empowerment.

The album then pushes into heavier territory. “TTTM” and “Error” embrace the explosive energy of modern Rage rap, while “Make It Rock” combines rock with Rage to expand the project’s sonic range. These tracks give SKYFALL a more aggressive edge without abandoning its broader emphasis on momentum and personal ambition.

“Pink” shifts the mood again, pairing psychedelic textures and melodic songwriting with Rage production. Its focus on clarity and self-belief after periods of struggle echoes the emotional concerns introduced on the opening track, creating a connection between the album’s softer and harder moments.

“Heard of Us” brings that journey to a close with a message of collective ambition and perseverance. Its emphasis on building quietly and allowing achievement to speak for itself provides a fitting final statement for an album centered on growth, self-definition, and forward movement.

Taken together, the songs make SKYFALL feel less like a showcase of disconnected genres and more like an evolving portrait of an artist exploring different sides of his identity. Adam Fan moves between melodic rap, trap, Rage, rock, and dance while keeping the album anchored in the same underlying ideas: pressure, ambition, confidence, and the determination to keep moving forward.

The Album Connects Chinese Pop Culture With Global Hip-Hop Language

Adam Fan enters SKYFALL with an established identity in Chinese entertainment rather than as an unknown artist attempting a first introduction.

He first rose to prominence through the Chinese survival program Idol Producer, later debuting as a member of NINE PERCENT before developing his solo career. His work across music, television, and film has made him a recognizable figure in Chinese and Asian entertainment.

SKYFALL changes the context rather than erasing that history. English-language songwriting gives Adam Fan another way to communicate internationally, while the album’s production draws from sounds already circulating prominently across global hip-hop and popular music.

The project is produced by international music production companies Hits Rhythm and A WW Productions. Outside China, its distribution is supported through a partnership between Hits Rhythm and Pacific Music Group, with an international promotional focus.

Why This Matters

SKYFALL marks Adam Fan’s debut full-length English-language album and a new international chapter in his music career.

Its significance is tied to the way the project approaches that transition. Instead of relying on one sound, Adam Fan draws from melodic rap, trap, Rage, rock, and dance to build an album capable of moving between different corners of contemporary global music.

For listeners discovering him outside China, SKYFALL provides an entry point into an artist whose career already spans music, television, and film. For Adam Fan, the album establishes English-language music as another dimension of an evolving artistic identity.
