Legendary Costume Designer Bob Mackie Dies at 87

TheTools · 14.09.2026 23:48 · source · permalink

The Emmy and Tony winner dressed divas such as Cher, Carol Burnett, Bernadette Peters, and Liza Minnelli.

September 14, 2026 By Diep Tran

Legendary fashion and costume designer Bob Mackie died September 14 at the age of 87. The news was announced on his official Instagram page: "It is with a heavy heart that we share the news that Mr. Bob Mackie passed away today. He lived his 87 years to the fullest, fulfilling his dream of being a Fashion and Costume Designer, which is all he ever wanted to do. His genius and extraordinary designs will live on forever, continuing to bring beauty into this world."

Known for his extravagant, sparkling creations, which made his divas shine as they stepped under the stage lights or on the red carpet—Mr. Mackie's career encompassed film, television, and the stage. He designed for such iconic artists as Cher, Carol Burnett, Liza Minnelli, Bernadette Peters, Barbra Streisand, Dolly Parton, Elton John, and many more. His designs have also become popular with the current generation of entertainers, such as Ariana Grande, Taylor Swift, Sabrina Carpenter, and Miley Cyrus.

Mr. Mackie was born Robert Gordon Mackie March 24, 1939, in Monterey Park, California. As a boy, Mr. Mackie lived with his grandmother following his parents' divorce. He spent his spare time watching classic Technicolor musicals, becoming inspired by Meet Me in St. Louis and An American in Paris to become a costume designer.

Mr. Mackie wanted to design for Broadway, but because he was in Los Angeles, it was easier to begin his career in Tinseltown. Though he studied briefly at Pasadena City College and Chouinard Art Institute, he was mostly self-taught. He began his career working as an assistant to Oscar winners Edith Head and Jean Louis. Mr. Mackie was hired to be a sketch artist for Louis; he drew the original sketch for the dress that Marilyn Monroe wore when she sang "Happy Birthday" to President John F. Kennedy.

In 1966, Mitzi Gaynor needed a designer for her Las Vegas stage show. Mr. Mackie's frequent collaborator and romantic partner Ray Aghayan recommended Mr. Mackie. That began a five-decade-long partnership between Gaynor and Mr. Mackie—who went on to design her television specials; he won two Emmy Awards for them. 

Carol Burnett saw Mr. Mackie's designs for Gaynor and asked him to design for The Carol Burnett Show, beginning in 1967 and for the entirety of the show's 11-year run. It was a large-scale undertaking that saw Mr. Mackie designing an average of 60 costumes a week. One dress, a parody of the green velvet curtain dress from Gone With the Wind, that included an actual curtain rod and tassels, is now on display at the Smithsonian’s National Museum of American History.

Mr. Mackie's work on the Carol Burnett Show attracted the attention of the recording artist Cher, and so began one of the most eye-catching collaborations in entertainment history. Mr. Mackie designed Cher's outfits for her various television specials from 1970 and onwards, her red carpet looks, as well as the costumes for her numerous tours—including her farewell tour in the early 2000s and her 2010s Las Vegas shows. Together, the two presented some of the most iconic looks on the Academy Awards red carpet, such as the all-black look of black pleather pants, knee-high boots, matching sparkling loin-cloth and bra top, and a sky-high Mohawk headdress that Cher wore to the 1986 Oscars.

Mr. Mackie's work with Cher earned him his first Tony Award in 2019, when he won for The Cher Show, the musical based on the singer's life. Mr. Mackie's design for the show featured work from his own archives as well as new stage creations, including a finale dress made of sequins, feathers, and illusion netting worn by Stephanie J. Block.

Though his first Tony Award didn't come until 2019, Mr. Mackie periodically designed for the stage. He made his Broadway debut with the 1971 revival of On the Town starring Bernadette Peters (he would design dresses for Peters throughout her career), and went on to design the costumes for Lorelei (he just did Carol Channing's costumes), Platinum with Alexis Smith, The Best Little Whorehouse Goes Public with Dee Hoty, Moon Over Buffalo with Carol Burnett, and the Sondheim revue Putting It Together (again with Burnett); and Minnelli On Minnelli with Liza Minnelli. 

Mr. Mackie also designed the costumes for musical films: Funny Lady starring Barbra Streisand, Pennies from Heaven starring Steve Martin and Peters, Gypsy starring Bette Midler, and Once Upon a Mattress starring Burnett.

Mr. Mackie also launched his wearable collection with QVC in the 1990s, which continue to be sold to this day. Mr. Mackie's collections have also been worn on the biggest diva of all: Barbie; he's designed collectible Barbies for Mattel since 1982.

    Mr. Mackie was a nine-time Emmy Award winner, a Tony winner, and recipient of three Oscar nominations. In 2007, he was awarded the TDF Irene Sharaff Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007 for theatrical costume design. In 2023, Mr. Mackie was presented with the inaugural Giving Us Life-time Achievement Award by RuPaul on RuPaul's Drag Race. Mr. Mackie was the subject of a 2024 documentary called Bob Mackie: Naked Illusion, a reference to his pioneer use of flesh-colored mesh to create the illusion of nudity when worn by performers. The documentary is now streaming on HBO Max.

    The Museum of Broadway in Times Square currently houses a number of Mr. Mackie's design. The late artist once said of his work: "A woman who wears my clothes is not afraid to be noticed."
