Inside the Scottish origins of GTA as Rockstar prepares for huge release

TheTools · 14.11.2026 10:02 · source · permalink

GRAND Theft Auto (GTA), the blockbuster gaming franchise created in Dundee, has racked up an estimated 10.3 million pre-orders ahead of the release of the game’s latest instalment next week.

The video game franchise was born in Dundee 29 years ago by a small team of developers known as DMA Design. It has become a Scottish gaming success story with around 480 million copies of GTA sold worldwide.

DMA Design also had early breakthroughs such as Lemmings in 1991, which sold millions of copies, before developing the hit Grand Theft Auto in 1997.

GTA, which was originally titled Race ‘n’ Chase, became an incredible success. The company was acquired by UK publisher Gremlin Interactive for £4.2m. Following the release of Grand Theft Auto 2 in 1999, Gremlin was acquired by Infogrames.

After the Infogrames acquisition, the DMA Design was sold to the current American owners, Take-Two Interactive, for $22m.

In 2001, after the release of Grand Theft Auto III, DMA Design moved to Edinburgh and was ultimately renamed Rockstar North to become part of the Rockstar Games label.

Rockstar’s parent company Take-Two Interactive has described advance orders as unprecedented.

Strauss Zelnick, the company’s chairman and chief executive, said this week that the launch was expected to deliver a historic year for the business.

The new game has been developed by Rockstar’s international network of studios, with Edinburgh-based Rockstar North continuing to play a central role.

Grand Theft Auto VI is rumoured to have cost Rockstar more than $1.5 billion to develop, making it the most expensive game ever developed. However, this has not been confirmed by Rockstar. The game arrives more than 13 years after the release of GTA 5 and saw resources reallocated from Red Dead Online.

Rockstar was also accused of union busting in the lead-up to the release date after firing 34 employees, including some from Rockstar North, due to the alleged distribution of confidential information regarding the latest release. An employment tribunal examining the claims is ongoing.

(Image: Suppleid)

While acquisitions have brought increased investment into Scottish game development, enabling companies to expand their operations and meet the growing demands of players, they have also raised questions about the future of the nation’s gaming industry.

As Scottish studios have been acquired by international publishers, many of them American-owned, the industry has become increasingly reliant on overseas investment.

A lack of investment, industry infrastructure and representation has contributed to a steady brain drain, with some of Scotland’s most promising talent forced to look elsewhere to pursue opportunities. It is a problem the Scottish Government is now seeking to rectify.

Brian Baglow, founder of the Scottish Games Network, an advocacy organisation supporting the development of Scotland’s games industry, is among those working to address these challenges.

He is leading the Games Action Plan, unveiled at the start of the year, which aims to strengthen the sector and create greater opportunities for Scottish developers. Scotland aims to boost the games sector to more than £1bn by 2030 through the Scottish Games Action Plan to build the nation into a “games supercluster”.

Alongside the £1bn target, the Games Action Plan aims to establish 20 new studios and 50 intellectual properties with prototype funding by 2028.
