'I met the Queen and I was so embarrassing it made me squirm - I went against etiquette' - AOL

TheTools · 21.10.2026 15:06 · source · permalink

Christian Horner has reflected on the time he winked at Queen Elizabeth II as she added sugar to his coffee. The ex-Red Bull team principal admits he was overcome with etiquette-breaking embarrassment. It has been 15 months since Horner last held a position in Formula 1. Having made a mark in Formula 3000, the 52-year-old was given a shot by Red Bull energy drink co-founder Dietrich Mateschitz when the Austrian purchased Jaguar Racing in 2004. Horner became the outfit's first team principal - and F1's youngest ever - when he assumed his position in 2005. In the two decades to come, he would create a dynasty.

Red Bull won six Constructors' Championship titles under his leadership. Both Sebastian Vettel and later Max Verstappen were guided to four Drivers' Championships apiece. Yet Horner was sacked in July 2025 after subpar performances and an internal power struggle. According to accounts filed in October, he was paid a total compensation package of £100.48million.

He has been linked with a return to the sport, with Ferrari mooted. Now, Horner is set to release a new book titled Drive. In it, he details a lunch he was invited to by the late Queen Elizabeth II and Prince Philip. Horner writes: "As the afternoon wore on, and we moved on to coffees in a lounge, the Queen offered me some sugar.

"I never took sugar in my drinks. But when offered by the most famous woman in the world, I couldn't say no. Then, when she dropped it into my cup, I did something that still makes me squirm with embarrassment. I winked at her. Whether she noticed or not, I have no idea, but it went against all etiquette, and I regretted it immediately."

On the lunch, he said: "I had no idea what the event was about or what was expected of me. The scene was unlike anything I'd experienced before. Sitting in a small room were seven other guests, all of them from different walks of life.

"The only thing that connected us was the fact that we were guests of Her Majesty who, on occasion, enjoyed entertaining small, eclectic groups of people - some of them famous, some of them not. Among the invite list was a farmer, a mathematician and the most senior female member of the Royal Navy.

"As we waited nervously for our hosts to arrive, a side door opened and the Queen entered. Wow, she's a lot smaller in real life, I thought. As we walked through the corridors to the dining room, the atmosphere felt relaxed and very homely. I remember thinking that it was like having lunch with your grandparents."

He added: "I met the royal family on numerous occasions, including when I was awarded an OBE in 2013 and a CBE in 2024. Visiting Buckingham Palace was a surreal experience and an incredible honour that reinforced just how proud I was to be British. I remember feeling very fulfilled, knowing that I was making my parents proud."
