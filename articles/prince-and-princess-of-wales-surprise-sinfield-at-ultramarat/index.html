Prince and Princess of Wales surprise Sinfield at ultramarathon finish line

TheTools · 29.09.2026 20:33 · source · permalink

Former rugby star greeted at end of third gruelling long-distance run raising funds for MND research in memory of Rob Burrow

The Prince and Princess of Wales have surprised Sir Kevin Sinfield at the end of his third ultramarathon to raise funds in memory of Rob Burrow, the late rugby league star.

In emotional scenes in Wakefield, the Prince and Princess watched as Sir Kevin welled up speaking to supporters, and told him he was “so inspiring”.

The former professional rugby league player now coaching England is raising money for motor neurone disease (MND) research.

“Well done you,” the Princess told him, at the end of his third of seven ultramarathons in memory of his friend. “It’s been very special to watch,” said the Prince.

Burrow’s three children, Macy, Maya and Jackson, were there to watch along with his mother Irene, greeted by Sir Kevin with a hug.

Seven-year-old Jackson scored a try in a charity rugby match on Tuesday, with Sir Kevin pointing at him and shouting “I saw your try!” and a smiling Prince quizzing him on how good he was at rugby.

The Prince claimed he was “dreading” getting a call from Sir Kevin asking him to join the ultramarathon effort, laughing “I can’t hear you!” when he joked about it.

“I can’t do Lycra,” said the Prince. “That’s my line.”

As Sir Kevin took to the microphone to thank his team, MND charity supporters and fans at the DIY Kitchens Stadium, the home of Rugby League club Wakefield Trinity, he briefly became tearful before delivering a three-minute speech without notes.

He told the Prince and Princess: “To have you here today is incredible and brings real credibility and awareness to the MND community.”

Hearing how Sir Kevin had been dreaming of an ice bath throughout Tuesday’s run, the Prince told him afterwards: “Make sure you hold together for another few days.”

“We’re all thinking of Rob all the time,” he said.

The Prince has met Sir Kevin on several occasions. In 2024, he presented CBEs to both Burrow and Sir Kevin during a meeting at Headingley Stadium, the home of the Leeds Rhinos rugby team.

He later opened the Rob Burrow Centre for Motor Neurone Disease, where he met Burrow’s widow and children, alongside Sir Kevin, to hear about the difference the facility would make to families living with MND.

On Tuesday, the Princess – who had temporarily stepped back from public duties while undergoing cancer treatment in 2024 – joined her husband in Leeds and Wakefield for two engagements relating to MND, and to cheer Sir Kevin on in person.

The couple also met families affected by MND at the stadium.

Sir Kevin set off on Sunday on what he has announced will be his final fundraising challenge: completing an ultramarathon every day for seven days while visiting all 12 English Betfred Super League grounds. He will finish at Old Trafford before the Super League Grand Final.

On Monday, Sir Kevin spent time with the Princess Royal at the University of York athletics track after completing the second day of the challenge.

He joked that he would have liked the weather conditions to have been worse, otherwise “people see it on the telly and think it’s a fun run”.

“Fun run is a contradiction in terms,” replied the Princess, adding: “I will stick to horses.”

Sir Kevin is aiming to run at least 45km each day in 7km blocks.

This is the seventh annual endurance event he has taken on to raise awareness and funds for MND in memory of his former Leeds Rhinos teammate Burrow, who died aged 41 in June 2024, four and a half years after his diagnosis.

Since his first challenge in 2020, Sir Kevin has raised more than £11m for the cause.

This challenge will raise money for six charities: the MND Association, Leeds Hospitals Charity, My Name’5 Doddie Foundation, Darby Rimmer Foundation, MND Scotland and the Irish MND Association.
