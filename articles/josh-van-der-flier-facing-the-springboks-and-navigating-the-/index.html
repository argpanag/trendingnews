Josh van der Flier: Facing the Springboks and navigating 'the performance arousal curve'

TheTools · 20.11.2026 07:00 · source · permalink

JOSH VAN DER Flier is drawing graphs in the air, explaining the theory behind “the performance arousal curve”.

This is the guide the Ireland flanker uses to chart his emotional state as the week leads into matchday. If he feels his mental state is at the low end of the curve, he’s not at the correct emotional pitch for what’s coming. Move up the curve, and you hit the sweet spot, where motivation and alertness are right where you want them to be. Push it too far, and you slide down the curve again, entering the zone where stress and anxiety can come into the picture.

“Sometimes, games like this (against South Africa) you’re almost like, right I’m too hyped up, I need to calm myself down,” explains Van der Flier, who has warned his audience the chat is about to get a bit ‘nerdy’.

“Sometimes you just find you’re getting a bit ahead of yourself or you find you’re a bit too emotional, sometimes you’re too pumped up for a game. You don’t want to be at a stage – and you see it sometimes in sport – where someone’s way too hyped up, they’re too pumped up, and they give away stupid penalties.

The flanker was speaking to the media in Abbotstown this week. Ben Brady / INPHO Ben Brady / INPHO / INPHO

“To find the middle ground, sometimes morning of a game, night before a game (if) I’m not really feeling as up for it, I might watch some motivational video or some rugby highlights, or I watch some of my own things I’m doing really well, it gets me excited to play. For this weekend, I’ll probably just try and relax once captains (runs) are undone.

“I’ll try not to really think about rugby until two hours before the game because I don’t need to be getting overhyped or burning too much energy too early. Whereas there’s obviously other games where you might not feel it as much, you might have a few bumps and bruises, you might not be as keen to play in.”

Sportspeople speak about treating every game the same, but naturally, the importance of any given fixture can impact how you’re feeling leading into it.

“If you’re being really honest, like say you have a big match you don’t really need to win; you could be playing a friendly or something in the club, or you could be playing for say Leinster A or something, and you know you’re likely to play the next week, you might be like, ‘I just want to get through this game.’

“Those games I’d try and get myself really pumped up and then this weekend I’d be probably thinking ‘try and stay as calm as possible,’ and I know I’ll be at the right level by the time we get to the game.”

The 33-year-old’s ability to land into the game with the right mindset is something his teammates have picked up on. Last month, Ryan Baird revealed that Van der Flier is someone he would turn to for a word of advice ahead of big games.

Van der Flier is happy to reveal what’s involved in those chats.

“I was rooming with him, so I was chatting to him and he said – we are quite open with each other – he was feeling a bit nervous. We had a bit of a chat. It’s always helpful when you have a game where you’d be, ‘right I’m feeling a bit off, keep me going; if you see me slacking off or losing concentration, give me a bit of feedback or whatever.’

“Some people respond really well to a bit of positivity. Tell them they’re great and they’re going to have a great game.

“It kind of depends on the people. One thing the lads are quite good at is (they) get around each other quite well in terms of that.

Van der Flier has a close relationship with Ryan Baird. Dan Sheridan / INPHO Dan Sheridan / INPHO / INPHO

“(I remember) Jamie Heaslip saying it before, it might have been my first cap, in the huddle before the game. ‘We’re playing at Twickenham, it’s going to be unbelievably tough. We have set plays, but the lads are going to forget them. Just talk to each other, talk to the man beside you. You’ll get through it. There’s going to be 80,000 people there. It’ll be intense. Chat to each other if you’re feeling nervous. If you forget something on the field or feel like you’re off, chat to the lad beside you.’

“That’s probably what it’s like now. I thought that was good advice from Jamie and probably how we like to do it.”

Van der Flier has felt a bit more heat for his Ireland place recently, with Nick Timoney’s consistent form making him a viable option in the starting back row. Timoney started the opening November game against Argentina, before moving to number eight against Fiji as the Leinster man came back in at openside. Against South Africa on Saturday, Van der Flier keeps his place alongside Ryan Baird and Dan Sheehan in a new-look back row.

It promises to be the most physically demanding challenge Ireland have faced this year, but one they’ve been preparing for. 

Van der Flier continues in the starting Ireland back row this weekend. Tom Maher / INPHO Tom Maher / INPHO / INPHO

During the summer Nations Championship games, Farrell’s squad used some of their time in Australia to work with Sione Lemoto, a former player who now runs a ball-carrying rugby clinic called Core Collisions. Lemoto’s 19-year-old son, Heinz, is a promising back row on the books at Toulouse.

 

“They definitely challenge you physically,” Van der Flier says of the Springboks.

“Like some other teams, like France, the skill set of the forwards, they’re going to move the ball around, look for offloads, whereas I would have said South Africa, it’s very much a big physical test, a collision test.

“Sometimes I find with, obviously the All Blacks probably experience it a bit more, but (they) have good strike plays, there’s some good coaches, some good plans. But at the end of the day, you have to front up physically with South Africa and that’s probably the big thing I take from it anyway.

“You have to win collisions, if you win collisions, if you win the game line, you stand a very good chance against them. It’s obviously not as simple as that, but that’s probably the area I find you’re challenged the most against South Africa.”
