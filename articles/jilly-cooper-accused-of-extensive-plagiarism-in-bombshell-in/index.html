Jilly Cooper accused of extensive plagiarism in bombshell investigation

Jilly Cooper has been accused of extensive plagiarism, with the late author’s estate now potentially facing legal action.

Cooper, who died in 2025 at the age of 88, was considered one of the originators of the “bonkbuster” novel, with the successful TV adaptation of her 1988 bestseller Rivals sending her profile skyrocketing in recent years. More than 11 million copies of her books have been sold, and she was awarded a damehood in 2024 for her services to literature and charity.

But a newspaper investigation has threatened to take apart her legacy, after reporters found scenes, descriptions and dialogue from her 1978 novel Prudence had been lifted wholesale from two earlier novels: The Serial by Cyra McFadden, which was published in book form in 1977, two years after it was serialised in a US newspaper, and The Franchise Affair by Josephine Tey, which was published in 1948. McFadden died in 2024, while Tey died in 1952.

McFadden’s daughter Caroline told The Observer that she is considering legal action against Cooper’s estate. “It absolutely boils my blood to think of someone stealing my mother’s work because I know how hard she worked at her craft,” she said. “I will be looking at legal options as this can’t be allowed to stand. I sincerely hope that all of Dame Cooper’s work will be scrutinised to see if this was a pattern of hers and other talented authors’ work stolen.”

Prudence, about a woman from the Lake District who becomes entangled with three brothers, includes a character called Berenice who serves as a feminist, American foil to the eponymous heroine. But the investigation found that almost all of Berenice’s behaviour and lines of dialogue were lifted from The Serial.

In one of 35 examples of alleged plagiarism of The Serial found by The Observer, Berenice describes her ex-husband as such: “I wanted an open living relationship based on trust and growth, and all he wanted was his jockey shorts ironed. I mean we weren’t coming from the same place at all.” In The Serial, a character named Kate recalls her own ex-husband, saying: “I wanted an open, loving relationship, based on growth and trust and sharing, and Harvey wanted his jockey shorts ironed. I mean we just weren’t coming from the same place at all.”

An entire subplot involving the collapse of a court case in Prudence was also found to be strikingly identical to the description of the collapse of a court case in Tey’s The Franchise Affair, a mystery novel about a kidnapping of a young woman. For example, in Tey’s novel, the narrator declares: “Against my will, my eyes went to Mrs Wynn, and came away at once. It was crucifixion.” In Prudence, Cooper’s heroine says: “Against my will, my eyes went to Ricky Wetherby and came away at once. It was crucifixion.”

While Cooper never acknowledged The Serial’s direct influence on Prudence, she wrote in a 1982 newspaper column that McFadden’s novel was “a marvellously funny send-up of American trendies in the late Seventies”. She then quoted a passage from the novel.

The Independent has contacted Cooper’s publicist and her publisher at Penguin for comment. Neither responded to The Observer’s own request for comment when they were approached about the findings of the investigation.

Cooper was one of Britain’s most famous authors, best known for her steamy fiction focusing on scandal and adultery in upper-class society. Her 1988 novel Rivals, the second book in her Rutshire Chronicles series, depicts the sordid power struggle between rival television companies in the Cotswolds.

Cooper was heavily involved in the Disney adaptation of Rivals during the production of its first season in 2024, regularly visiting the set. She died in October 2025 after a fall at home.

The Queen was among the many famous figures to pay tribute to the author, describing her as a “legend” and a “wonderfully witty and compassionate friend to me and so many”.
