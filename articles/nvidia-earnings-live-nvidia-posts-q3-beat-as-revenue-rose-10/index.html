Nvidia earnings live: Nvidia posts Q3 beat as revenue rose 103% YOY to $115.8B, board says it will not reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia (NVDA) reported its Q3 results after the bell Tuesday, beating Wall Street's expectations on the top and bottom lines and offering a better-than-anticipated Q4 outlook. The company also announced a $150 billion stock buyback. Nvidia stock rose on the news.

For the fourth quarter, Nvidia expects revenue between $127.4 billion and $132.6 billion. Analysts were anticipating $125.25 billion

For the quarter, Nvidia saw earnings per share of $2.68 on revenue of $115.8 billion. Wall Street analysts were projecting EPS of $2.47 and revenue of $109.15 billion, based on Bloomberg consensus estimates.

(NVDA )

This price reflects trading activity during the overnight session on the Blue Ocean ATS, available 8 PM to 4 AM ET, Sunday through Thursday, when regular markets are closed.

The company reported EPS of $1.30 and revenue of $57 billion in Q3 last year.

Data Center revenue topped out at $107.6 billion versus an anticipated $101.85 billion. The company's Edge Computing segment brought in $8.2 billion versus an expected $7.49 billion.

This is Nvidia's first earnings report since CFO Colette Kress took over as interim CEO, following Jensen Huang's resignation last week.

Huang left the company after revelations regarding a series of affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and another with an alleged Nvidia employee.

  • AMD, Broadcom, CoreWeave stocks rise on Nvidia earnings beat

    Semiconductor and neocloud provider stocks rose on Tuesday in after-hours trading as AI chip-heavy Nvidia (NVDA) reported Q3 results that beat Wall Street's expectations. Peers AMD (AMD) and Broadcom (AVGO) shares rose more than 1% in post-market trading. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSM), which manufactures Nvidia's chips, also saw its stock rise 2%.

    The semiconductor trade has been volatile since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last Thursday following reports of extramarital affairs. Wall Street's questions over a succession plan have kept shares of the AI chip heavyweight and the AI trade under pressure.

    "I hope that this helps to calm some jitters in the market about the transition," Brian Colello, Morningstar analyst, told Deirdre Bosa Live on Tuesday.

    "Seems like business as usual and the forecast is really encouraging as well," he added.

    The analyst said there are still plenty more questions but that Nvidia reiterating its long-term financial forecast is helpful to the market.

  • The Bank of Nvidia's tab nears $670 billion

    The Bank of Nvidia needs a bigger vault.

    Everything Nvidia (NVDA) has promised to buy, back, or invest in climbed to nearly $670 billion in the quarter that ended Oct. 25, up from about $530 billion in July, according to Yahoo Finance calculations. Most of it is chips locked up from suppliers, mainly memory.

    The bank-like part is the backstops. Nvidia now guarantees $118 billion of other companies' obligations, including nearly $10 billion in new ones for partners it didn't name. It also bought nearly $16 billion of stakes in other companies, mostly private.

    And customers are taking their sweet time paying. Nvidia's unpaid bills hit $81 billion, or 64 days of sales, the slowest pace since the 2022 chip slump.

    Don't expect the vault to keep growing at this pace. "As the market matures and additional sources of capital become available, we are rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees," interim CEO Colette Kress said on Tuesday's call.

    CFO and Interim CEO Colette Kress says Nvidia's cloud industry revenue backlog is now a staggering $2.5 trillion. That's up from $2 trillion three months ago.

    "Our ambition will not change. Our passion to innovate will not change. And our commitment to customers, partners, and shareholders will not change," longtime Nvidia CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress said in her opening remarks as interim CEO.

  • Harvey Jones on CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO

    We have an incredibly talented leadership team, and we have every confidence in Colette's leadership, integrity, and ability to deliver as interim CEO.

  • Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones says the company has no plans to bring Jensen Huang back

    Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones says Nvidia is an exceptionally strong company.

    Says Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned from his positions as CEO, board member, and president, and that the board has no intention of bringing him back.

    "On November 12th, our Board of Directors announced that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as President, CEO, and board member. The NVIDIA Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO. The Board appointed Colette Kress, NVIDIA's longtime and well-respected CFO, as interim CEO. In addition, the Board announced it was initiating a search for a permanent chief executive, and we have since retained Haydrich and Struggles to lead the search. While this was an unexpected transition, the Board acted deliberately and in the best interest of the company's employees, customers, partners, and shareholders. This morning, the company announced that our Board has established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee that will include a select group of our independent Board directors, senior external advisors, and legal counsel," Jones said, according to a live transcript of the earnings call.

    Jones added that the company will not respond to questions about former CEO Jensen Huang. "We will not be addressing or taking questions on today's call regarding the circumstances of Jensen's resignation and the ongoing investigation.

  • Colette Kress and Harvey Jones host earnings call

    Nvidia CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress and the company's new board chair Harvey Jones are hosting their first earnings call since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO.

  • Nvidia's Edge Computing segment sales climb, but PC sales continue to take a hit

    Nvidia reported Edge Computing revenue of $8.2 billion for the third quarter, up 42% year-over-year on Blackwell workstation sales and physical AI hardware.

    But the memory shortage hit PC sales, something rivals Intel and AMD have also noted.

  • Nvidia's GTC events a 'much stronger signal to watch' than earnings: Uniphore CEO

    Umesh Sachdev, Uniphore CEO, said that Jensen Huang's departure as CEO has changed how he views Nvidia's (NVDA) earnings.

    "Earnings have played out exactly as expected," Sachdev said on Deirdre Bosa Live, adding that "this is as good a quarter as any has been for Nvidia."

    However, he added, "I personally will pay a lot more attention to the upcoming GTC conferences because, for me, the innovation engine — that is what Jensen was very intimately involved in and driving hands-on for the company."

    Sachdev said that what Nvidia announces at GTC "is an indication of what happens 15 to 18 quarters out for the company. Is their innovation going to remain as strong as it was with Jensen? So for me, the GTC conference is a much stronger signal to watch in the absence of Jensen more than the earnings."
