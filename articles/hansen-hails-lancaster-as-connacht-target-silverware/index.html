Hansen hails Lancaster as Connacht target silverware

TheTools · 09.09.2026 14:40 · source · permalink

Connacht back Mack Hansen has paid tribute to the early groundwork Stuart Lancaster has done at the province, as they target silverware in the upcoming 2026/27 season.

The Ireland and Lions star only featured once for his province last year due to a series of injuries, but now in year two of the Lancaster project, the focus is on further progression following a return to the URC play-offs last year.

Speaking at the launch of Canterbury's new Speed Falcon 2.0 boot designed by Hansen, the 28-year-old was effusive in where he believes the men from the west are heading.

"We're going for silverware and we're going to win something", he told reporters on Wednesday.

"It's a great feeling around the place and we're all on the same page for that."

Connacht finished in eighth place in the URC last season, losing in the quarter-final stages away to Glasgow Warriors. They also suffered quarter-final elimination in the EPCR Challenge Cup where they were dispatched by eventual champions Montpellier.

It was widely viewed as a successful first year at the helm for the former England, Leinster and Racing 92 coach, and although Hansen barely featured in the green of Connacht, he has been more than impressed with what he has seen.

"Stu's everything that we asked for as players," he said.

"He's come in and he's just turned the place upside down in the best way possible. He came in and said, 'look, this is what I want to do. This is how we're going to do it.' And either you're on board or you're not, pretty much.

"He's come in and as I said, look, he's just been unbelievable. The training standards, the standards we hold ourselves in everything pretty much have just gone up through the roof. And again, that definitely showed towards the end of the season."

The primary aim for the Australian-born winger is to get a consistent run of games, having seen his 2025/26 season halted due to injury. A late returnee due to his involvement on the Lions tour, Hansen lined out just once for Connacht and twice for Ireland, before an injury sustained in the loss to South Africa ended his campaign.

Undeterred but motivated, Hansen explained in his typical charm that the foot injury was a rather obscure one and not the most exciting.

"It wasn't very exciting at all. I wish I had done it skydiving or fighting a shark or something. It was just as simple as stepping on a bloke’s foot the wrong way", he said.

"Unfortunately just the way I landed on it, it gave out and I ended up doing a few of the ligaments, which were holding together my midfoot more than anything. That’s where all the power in your foot goes. So, I had a lot of power going through there with not much stability and I ended up having to get a bone fusion, six screws and a plate in the end. Fair number on it."

He has since returned to action however, featuring in both of Connacht's pre-season games as they lost away to Vannes, and defeated Ealing at home.

It means that Hansen's next run out in Galway will be his first in front of a packed out crowd since the redevelopment. Already starting to become a bit of a cauldron-like atmosphere, having won their last five home games of the season last year against Glasgow, Scarlets, Ospreys, Sharks and Munster, Hansen feels it can make a special impact.

"It's some atmosphere," he said.

"I think the way that we came home at the end of the season is super exciting. Obviously, it didn't end the way that we wanted, but I think if we actually look back at last year as a team, we had a pretty bad run of injuries throughout the whole squad and everything.

"So I think if we can keep healthy and especially adding the new people that we have in to the squad. We've got a deep squad, which in probably a lot of years has been a strong point of ours, like you could pull up 30, 35 blokes and you know they're going to do a job.

"So it's super exciting and I think that that's just going to keep getting bums on seats pretty much. So that's what we're trying to do and trying to build something special at Connacht.

"And look, the only way you can do that at the end of the day is winning games. So I think, yeah, we're really looking forward to it."
