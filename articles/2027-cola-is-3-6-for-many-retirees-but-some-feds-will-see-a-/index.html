2027 COLA is 3.6% for many retirees, but some feds will see a smaller adjustment - Federal News Network

TheTools · 15.10.2026 09:05 · source · permalink

A 2027 COLA of 3.6% will be added to Social Security payments and retirement benefits in January. But FERS annuitants will see a smaller 2.6% adjustment.

Many federal retirees will see a 2027 cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) of 3.6% added to their Social Security benefits and retirement annuities beginning in January.

The Social Security Administration’s announcement Wednesday of the 2027 COLA amount follows the Bureau of Labor Statistics‘ release of the September Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W). About 76 million Americans, including retirees and individuals with disabilities, receive an annual COLA added to their Social Security benefits.

It’s a larger adjustment than what retirees and beneficiaries saw for 2026. Most annuitants received a 2.8% COLA this past January. It’s also slightly higher than the Senior Citizens League’s projection of a 3.5% COLA, but on par with AARP’s forecast of a 3.6% adjustment.

The annual COLA is meant to keep federal retirees’ and Social Security recipients’ benefits on pace with rising inflation. But not every beneficiary receives the same amount.

Civil Service Retirement System (CSRS) annuitants receive the full COLA added to their federal retirement payments, but those in the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS) often receive a reduced adjustment.

The exact difference depends on how large the COLA is in a given year, based on the following formula:

  • COLA over 3%: FERS annuitants receive 1% less than COLA
  • COLA between 2% and 3%: FERS annuitants receive 2% COLA
  • COLA less than 2%: FERS annuitants receive full COLA

For 2027, the above formula means FERS retirees will see a 2.6% adjustment to their annuities. CSRS retirees will get the full 3.6%.

While the impact of a smaller COLA may appear minimal in any given year, federal advocacy organizations have argued that over time, the difference can add up. After many years, reduced COLAs can create a difference of thousands of dollars.

The National Active and Retired Federal Employees Association (NARFE) said the reduced COLA for FERS retirees doesn’t keep pace with inflation or rising federal health insurance premiums.

“FERS retirees will have their COLAs reduced by an entire percentage point, reducing the real value of their annuities,” NARFE National President William Shackelford said. “Inflation impacts these FERS retirees the same way as all other retirees, yet they are forced to accept a diet COLA.”

At the same time, some lawmakers have been attempting to eliminate the difference for FERS retirees. A pending House bill called the Equal COLA Act would allow for a full COLA each year on FERS annuities. The legislation was introduced in January 2025 and currently has 90 cosponsors, but the bill has so far not seen any movement.

“The Equal COLA Act would remedy this inequity,” Shackelford said. “FERS retirees deserve full protection to maintain the purchasing power of their annuity throughout retirement.”

COLA increases have averaged to about 3.1% over the last 10 years, but retirees have seen a wide range of percentages for the annual cost-of-living adjustment. Most notably, the COLA spiked to 8.7% in 2023, marking the largest increase in more than four decades.
