    Aadam Patel
    Football Reporter

    You get the feeling that the schedule and length of turnaround between fixtures will be a common topic of conversation with Andoni Iraola.

    Liverpool were brilliant last Wednesday against Atletico Madrid but followed that up with a poor display against Fulham, less than 72 hours later.

    Iraola pointed out the lack of freshness on Saturday as a factor and now just over 72 hours after that game, Liverpool will play at Anfield for the third time in less than a week.

    As Iraola indicated, expect to see a heavily-changed Liverpool line up that will see Giorgi Mamardashvili in net and a chance for fans to “see some players we haven’t seen yet” and for Iraola to “see whether they can help us”.

    Iraola insists he is “very happy” with his squad and a cup game against Premier League opposition tomorrow will provide a strong indication about how reliable his squad options really are.

  8. 'We will study it case by case' - Iraola on working with his playerspublished at 12:47 BST 14 September
    12:47 BST 14 September

    Liverpool v Tottenham (20:00 BST)

    Liverpool

    Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola, asked whether he has had ample opportunity to work with and learn about his players amid a busy schedule:

    "I have. If you ask me how new players, for instance Jeremy Jacquet, are going to deal with playing every three days for two months, I don't know because he hasn't done it.

    "We have some other players, experienced, have done it, I know they can do it. Virgil [van Dijk] can do it, because he has done it before, they have the experience, he has performed, his performance isn't so affected.

    "With the younger ones and those coming from abroad, there are some question marks, for them and for me and everyone. We will have to study it case by case."

  9. 'We have to get used to' intense schedulepublished at 12:44 BST 14 September
    12:44 BST 14 September

    Liverpool v Tottenham (20:00 BST)

    Liverpool

    Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola, asked how workload will affect team selection:

    "I think it has to affect, definitely.

    "It's the third game, what's the scenario? Game, two days [rest], game, two days [rest], game. In terms of the schedule we haven't been lucky, because it's true that after this one we have a little bit more before we play Bournemouth. Also we had a little bit more between Ipswich and Atletico, you notice, when it's five days in comparison to two or three days.

    "It's a big difference, but it is what it is. After the international break we have this period where we are playing four games in nine or ten games. We have to get used to this."

  10. 'When the team plays well, everyone looks better'published at 12:42 BST 14 September
    12:42 BST 14 September

    Liverpool v Tottenham (20:00 BST)

    Liverpool

    Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola, asked whether the issue he identified with duels can be fixed through coaching, since it has been a long-term issue in the squad:

    "Yes, I said and accepted and agree with what [the reporter who raised the issue] said. But it's not a matter of just the midfielders.

    "For me, it's something more collective than just the midfielders. When we've been good collectively, the midfielders have looked a lot better, won more duels and recovered more balls, but it's not because they have played better today. It's because we are helping them more to win the duels.

    "It's like the defenders. If the ones upfront do their job very well, the defenders look very good because all the balls that arrive, they arrive not clean. If we are allowing them to defend one v one all the time, be comfortable on the ball, not being aggressive - the defenders will struggle. The players in front of you are doing their job, the defenders and midfielders will win more duels because they are easier duels. The ball arrives not in the best condition and then you have more chances to win duels.

    "It's much more collective than just because, 'if I change this for this because this one wins duels...' No. There's something more collective happening. That's the help we have to give to our defenders and midfielders.

    "When the team plays well, everyone looks better. The key is that we all play collectively well. Everyone looks smarter, better, in better position. That's from the collective side, for me."

  11. Iraola says Liverpool won the ball back too deep vs Fulhampublished at 12:39 BST 14 September
    12:39 BST 14 September

    Liverpool v Tottenham (20:00 BST)

    Liverpool

    Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola, asked about his players' duels and ball recovery numbers against Fulham:

    "Comparing to previous games, we won the ball a lot lower on the pitch. We didn't concede a lot, the only chance we conceded was the goal kick that we messed up after we defended well but we recovered the ball always very low. When this happens you have to attack always against 11 players, and it is more difficult to make a difference.

    "In previous games, we were able to win it a lot higher, then use our quick transitions to attack against fewer players, use the space better. This is key in our system, to recover the ball as high as possible because it makes the offensive side a lot easier. Situations to attack in one v ones, or against fewer, are less than if you recover in your half of the pitch."
