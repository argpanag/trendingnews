US sends 2,000 Marines, carrier strike group to Mideast in Iran buildup

More than 2,000 US Marines and the Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group are heading to the Middle East as Washington expands its military presence around Iran, an unnamed US official told reporters on Thursday.

The official said the aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt and its accompanying warships had departed San Diego for the region, while a Marine expeditionary force left the base earlier this week.

By the end of November, the United States is expected to have three aircraft carriers and two amphibious groups deployed around Iran, according to the official.

The official said concentrating that level of US military power in the region would give Washington “many options” in dealing with Tehran.

Iran defense chief rejects US claims defense industry destroyed

Majid Ebn-e-Reza, acting head of the Iranian defense ministry, pushed back Thursday against the US claims that the Islamic Republic’s defense industry had been destroyed.

Ebn-e-Reza said on X that such claims “change nothing,” adding that Iran’s capabilities and initiative would be seen on the battlefield and would have political consequences.

He also accused the White House of seeking to mask what he described as confusion over the war, saying public pressure, troop exhaustion and rising costs had disrupted Washington’s calculations.

Iran sentences protester to death over alleged aid to hostile governments

Mehdi Nami, detained during Iran’s January 2026 protests, has been sentenced to death by a Tehran Revolutionary Court on charges of “operating in favor of hostile governments,” the HRANA human rights website reported Thursday.

Iran summons UK ambassador over Fairford plot accusations

Iran summoned Britain’s ambassador in Tehran on Thursday to protest what it called “baseless” accusations linking Tehran to a suspected sabotage plot targeting RAF Fairford, the Iranian Foreign Ministry said.

British Ambassador Hugo Shorter was summoned by Deputy Foreign Minister Majid Takht-Ravanchi, who accused the British government of pursuing a “destructive approach” toward Iran and rejected allegations of Iranian involvement in the suspected plot. Shorter said he would convey Tehran’s protest to London, according to the ministry.

Takht-Ravanchi also accused Britain of complicity in US and Israeli military action against Iran by allowing British bases and facilities to be used for operations against the country. The UK has previously acknowledged permitting US forces to use British bases for what it described as limited defensive operations against Iranian missile facilities.

Saudi Arabia gives Yemen government $60 million budget support

Saudi Arabia has provided Yemen's internationally recognized government with a new budget support payment of more than 224 million Saudi riyals, about $60 million, Saudi Ambassador to Yemen Mohammed Al-Jaber said on Thursday.

Al-Jaber said the payment was made at the Yemeni government's request and was intended to help maintain government cash flows, support fiscal policy and bolster Yemenis' purchasing power.

He said the support came as Yemen faced attacks by the Houthis on government resources, property and economic interests.

The funding was provided through the Saudi Development and Reconstruction Program for Yemen, he added.

Netanyahu says flydubai co-pilot underwent Islamist radicalization

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Thursday that the co-pilot accused of trying to crash a flydubai passenger jet bound for Israel had undergone Islamist radicalization.

“We know that he underwent Islamist radical indoctrination,” Netanyahu told Fox News.

Netanyahu also said Israeli authorities expected to know within days whether the co-pilot had connections to Iran.

The flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv was diverted to Saudi Arabia after an incident in the cockpit. Saudi authorities said both pilots were injured, while all passengers were safe.

Trump says renewed bombing of Iran after midterms 'possible'

US President Donald Trump said renewed large-scale bombing of Iran after the November midterm elections was "possible," while rejecting Tehran's latest ceasefire proposal as insufficient, according to an interview with TIME published on Thursday.

Asked directly whether Washington could ramp up bombing after the midterms, Trump replied: "Possible."

He declined to discuss potential targets but said the United States had spent the past six months replenishing its weapons stocks. "We're stocking up at levels we've never done before," he said.

Trump said he rejected an Iranian ceasefire proposal presented over the weekend because its terms fell short of what Washington now wanted.

"They made an offer to open up the Strait," he said, referring to the Strait of Hormuz. "It's just, it's just, it's not good enough. Nearly."

Iran's leadership struggling to sustain combined military, economic pressure - Reuters

The combined strain of US military pressure and economic restrictions is becoming increasingly difficult for Iran's leadership to sustain, Reuters reported on Thursday, citing an insider close to the country's decision-making process.

The assessment comes as Tehran prepares for a broader response if the United States launches another major attack, while continuing diplomatic efforts that Iranian officials privately see as unlikely to produce an agreement.

Three senior Iranian officials and another person familiar with the discussions told Reuters that commanders were reviewing plans to widen potential targets beyond US-linked assets, including countries supporting US military operations and possibly locations outside the Middle East.

Two officials said Iran had also told allied groups in Lebanon, Yemen and Iraq to prepare for a coordinated response stretching "from the Strait of Hormuz to US interests in the region and outside the Middle East."

Reuters said a key concern for Tehran was whether Iran's economy could withstand a prolonged confrontation under a US blockade that has cut oil exports, one of the government's main sources of revenue.

The economic pressure is also feeding concern within the leadership over the risk of renewed unrest, according to the report, after nationwide protests in January were triggered in part by deteriorating economic conditions.

Pezeshkian says Iran remains open to dialogue despite disputes with US

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said on Thursday that Tehran remained willing to negotiate with Washington, while accusing the United States of attacking Iran after signing the June Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding.

“We have never walked away from dialogue, and we will not do so,” Pezeshkian told a meeting of entrepreneurs and business leaders. “Even though the United States targeted our country three times after signing the agreement.”

The memorandum, signed by Pezeshkian and US President Donald Trump in June, called for an end to military operations and committed both sides to refrain from using force against each other while negotiations continued.

Japan urges Iran to show flexibility in indirect talks with US

Japan’s Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi urged Iran to show maximum flexibility in its indirect exchanges with the United States and called for safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, Japan’s Foreign Ministry said on Thursday.

In a 20-minute phone call with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, Motegi said Japan would continue to work for de-escalation, stability and a diplomatic solution, and wanted to build diplomatic momentum with other countries, the ministry said.

Motegi also stressed the importance of international discussions on restoring free and safe passage through both waterways and on the future of the Strait of Hormuz. He called on Iran to respond constructively.

Araghchi briefed Motegi on the exchanges with Washington and Iran’s position. The two ministers agreed to stay in close contact to support a diplomatic resolution, the ministry said.
