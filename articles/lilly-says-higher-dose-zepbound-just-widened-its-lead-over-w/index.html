A new indirect analysis found that patients receiving Zepbound 15 mg and 10 mg had 4.5 percentage points and 3.2 percentage points more weight loss, respectively, over 72 weeks than patients taking high-dose Wegovy.

The difference was even more pronounced among people reaching larger weight-loss goals.

Patients receiving Zepbound 15 mg were three times more likely to lose at least 20% of their body weight compared with those receiving Wegovy HD, according to Lilly.

Discontinuation rates because of adverse events were comparable across the Zepbound 10 mg, Zepbound 15 mg and Wegovy HD groups.

There is an important caveat. The results did not come from a direct head-to-head trial. Lilly's analysis compared data from its Phase 3 SURMOUNT-1 study with Novo Nordisk's STEP UP studies.

The findings nevertheless add another data point to the increasingly competitive obesity-drug market, where Lilly and Novo are trying to differentiate their treatments through greater weight loss, higher doses and new formulations.

Novo shares were down about 1.3% in Tuesday premarket trading, while Lilly was up roughly 0.4%.

For investors, the next question is whether the apparent advantage seen in the indirect comparison holds up as Lilly and Novo generate more direct clinical evidence for their next generation of obesity treatments.
