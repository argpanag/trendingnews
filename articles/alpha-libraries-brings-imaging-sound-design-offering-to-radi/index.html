Alpha Libraries Brings Imaging, Sound Design Offering To Radio | Radio & Television Business Report

TheTools · 20.10.2026 16:59 · source · permalink
  • Tech Roundup

SourceAudio’s Alpha Libraries, which sees veteran radio programmer Steve Wall serve as Vice President of Radio and Library Services, has launched a new imaging and sound design product featuring 750,000 elements designed to help radio stations create “distinctive, custom-branded audio.”

Alpha Imaging for Radio provides imaging shells, beds, drones, impacts, sweeps, risers, transitions, artist IDs, listener drops, and other sound design elements for stations across all formats.

“Great imaging isn’t just what happens between the songs, it’s part of what makes a station recognizable, memorable, and uniquely its own,” said Wall, who is also a VP for SourceAudio’s Alpha Imaging for Radio. “We created Alpha Imaging for Radio because every radio station has its own personality, audience, and identity, and its sound should reflect that every time a listener tunes in. We’re thrilled to be giving producers and programmers an incredible palette of sounds and creative tools to do just that and more.”

Music licensing and distribution technology platform SourceAudio has more than 700,000 users with access to millions of tracks from various rights holders. Its technology powers music discovery, licensing, and distribution across television, film, radio, advertising, and digital media.

Learn more: AlphaImagingForRadio.com.
