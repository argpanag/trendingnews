Igor Thiago, who scored 27 goals last season for club and country, netted for just the second time in the Premier League this season

Igor Thiago continued his return to form as his second-half strike helped Brentford end their five-game wait for a win against Nottingham Forest.

The Bees had taken the lead on the half-hour mark when Nikola Milenkovic inadvertently steered past his own goalkeeper.

Thiago, converted Brentford's second in the final 15 minutes for his second goal in three days - after finding the net at Sunderland in the Carabao Cup - and only his second in the Premier League this season.

His goal, in the 76th minute, was also the game's first shot on target.

In a tense affair, with defeat leaving Forest 19th in the Premier League table, Brentford winger Dango Ouattara's moment of magic broke the deadlock.

The Burkina Faso international skipped past Forest defender Murillo on the right wing before drilling a cross into the six-yard box.

In his attemps to divert the ball, Milenkovic's block looped over his own goalkeeper Matz Sels and into the net.

Brentford captain Mathias Jensen nearly added the game's second before half-time with a free-kick that hit the crossbar.

Visitors Forest had significantly more shots than the hosts but never looked likely to beat home goalkeeper Caoimhin Kelleher.

Their best chance came early in the second half when substitute Ryan Yates headed over from close range.

But Thiago sealed three points for the hosts with his fourth goal of the campaign in all competitions.

Winger Jaidon Anthony started a counter-attack, moments after coming on as a substitute, before squaring the ball to Thiago whose shot did enough to beat Sels.

Three points for Keith Andrews' Brentford moves them up to sixth.

Brentford analysis: Thiago rediscovers scoring touch

Keith Andrews - post-match interview - Brentford vs Nottingham Forest

Only Manchester City's Erling Haaland (27) netted more Premier League goals than Thiago's 22 last season. But this season, it has been a different story.

Until four days ago, Thiago had scored just twice in 10 matches in all competitions, one of which was against Championship side Birmingham City in an EFL Cup second-round drubbing.

But, after scoring on Wednesday at Sunderland, again in the EFL Cup, the Brazil forward now has four goals in all competitions from 12 outings and with two in three days, showed a huge sigh of relief after sealing victory for the Bees.

"It means a lot to me. I was waiting for this goal for so long this season," Thiago, whose only other league goal came at home to Chelsea, told Premier League Productions.

"Thank you Lord, to score one more goal at home. I am really happy."

In the summer, Thiago went to the World Cup with Brazil. But, after starting in their opening match against Morocco, he was an unused substitute for the remainder of the campaign.

With back-to-back goals, his form will only complement a Brentford side who despite missing Thiago's goals from last season, are showing early season signs of being in the European mix.

Nottingham Forest analysis: Is Glasner under pressure?

Oliver Glasner arrived at the City Ground in the summer, replacing Vitor Pereira

More dropped points for Forest leaves them second-bottom before a meeting with league leaders Manchester City next weekend.

Forest have just one Premier League win in nine games since the arrival of Oliver Glasner as head coach in the summer and have picked up a single point from the last 15 available will raise some eyebrows.

Only Manchester United (18.6) have managed more shots per game in the Premier League this season than Forest (16.9), but they are the division's lowest scorers.

In nine games, they have mustered just six goals. Five of those matches saw them fail to score.

With a further 15 shots at Brentford, and just one on target coming in the 78th minute from James McAtee, their failure to convert attempts into goals could prove costly for both their chances of success this season and Glasner's hopes of staying on as head coach.
