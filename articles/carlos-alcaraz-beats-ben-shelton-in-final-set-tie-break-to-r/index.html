The man himself was not present in Turin on Saturday afternoon, but one of the most intriguing developments on and off court in men’s tennis this year was Ben Shelton’s surprise decision to hire the former No 1 Juan-Carlos Ferrero as his new coach alongside his father, Bryan. Ferrero is one of the most highly respected coaches on tour, but he also knows Carlos Alcaraz better than any other coach in the world.

However, there is only so much that anyone can do against one of this sport’s greatest talents. Shelton, the third seed, played a spectacular match, serving monstrously, attacking without hesitation and particularly volleying so well in decisive moments, but it still wasn’t enough to defeat the second seeded Alcaraz, who maintained his focus after an uncertain start as he reached the final of the ATP Finals with a 3-6, 6-3, 7-6 (1) win over Shelton.

The indoor hard court swing has long been the most difficult part of the season for Alcaraz, both because of his difficulties with fatigue at the end of a long season and his relative vulnerability in the faster indoor conditions. However, at the end of a turbulent season he has made significant progress indoors. Alcaraz will attempt to win his first ATP Finals title on Sunday, where he will face the winner of the second semi-final between old rivals Alexander Zverev and Daniil Medvedev.

One of the interesting developments of this indoor season has been the continued trend of hard courts across the tour speeding up. Both Paris and Turin represent two of the fastest courts of the past decade and these are exactly the conditions a big server like Shelton would most prefer against Alcaraz. However, Alcaraz has made great improvements in rapid conditions. His level has been supreme throughout the tournament.

Following his loss to Shelton at the quarter-finals of the US Open two months ago, one issue Alcaraz felt he needed to address was the quality of his own serve. He made a number of changes to his service motion once he returned home, particularly simplifying the start of his motion to find greater balance and lowering his toss. After starting slowly and conceding his opening service game, the quality of Alcaraz’s serve was perhaps the defining point of the match, particularly in these fast conditions.

It improved consistently throughout the match and by the final set he simply did not seem at risk of losing his serve. That security behind his first serve allowed him to start the final set tie-break completely relaxed. After Shelton fired down an ace on the first point, Alcaraz immediately followed him with two unreturned serves. While Alcaraz’s confidence skyrocketed, a forehand error from Shelton and a rare missed volley on consecutive service points quickly sealed Shelton’s fate.

Despite ending his year with a defeat and understandable frustration about his wasteful errors at the close, Shelton played an excellent match himself. Along with his monstrous serving, the most impressive part of Shelton’s performance was his netplay. Time and time again, he made excellent volleys on key points, particularly on his serve in the final set.

Shelton still has very clear weaknesses that he, Ferrero and his father must continue to work on, but he ends his 2026 season having put together by far the best season of his career. His highlights included a first grand slam final at the US Open, four titles including the Montreal ATP 1000, and the solidification of his position as the No 4 player in the world.

This match was accompanied by ample intrigue. The added dynamic of Ferrero’s presence in Shelton’s team now makes their matches a must-watch but there were also question marks over Alcaraz’s fitness after he felt pain in his left thigh during his win over Daniil Medvedev on Wednesday. Alcaraz underwent an MRI scan after that match.

His thigh heavily taped, he responded to that setback by dismantling his good friend Flavio Cobolli to advance to the semi-finals without dropping a set and then he rallied from behind here to reach the final in Turin for a second consecutive year. He now stands on the brink of securing one of the few significant achievements that have eluded him so far in his historic career.

So he’s happy, happy, happy. After Shelton’s fast start, it was all about Alcaraz’s fast finish. Alcaraz lacked energy at the beginning, probably because he was worried about his hamstring, but as the match went on he seemed to find that flow state. It was a clutch tie-break from him and another sign – if he can stay healthy – that it’s only a matter of time before the world No 3 is back at the top of the game. He can certainly send a message to the No 1 Zverev tomorrow if they do meet in the final. Shelton, meanwhile, can at least head off on his holidays knowing he’s just had best season of his career. He may may seeing that tie-break in his sleep for a few nights first though.

I’m just really happy. I was frustrated at the beginning, I didn’t start the match how I wanted to. Ben pushed me to the limit and I didn’t start well, with the early break, but I’m happy I came back in the second set, playing great tennis, step by step. I think he raised his level in the third set, me too, and I’m just happy to play great tennis in the tie-break when it matters. I’m excited for tomorrow and happy for today.

Preamble

Buongiorno! Only two months after Carlos Alcaraz’s comeback tournament ended with a five-set, 3.33am defeat by Ben Shelton in the US Open quarter-finals – and only two weeks after Alcaraz gained some revenge over the American in the Paris final – the pair are at it again, this time for a spot in the ATP Finals, erm, final (maybe we should go back to the days of calling it the Masters Cup).

Since losing to Shelton in New York, in a modern classic that ended with the dethroning of the champion and a coming-of-age victory for the eventual runner-up, Alcaraz has looked like a man determined to make up for those four months he missed with his wrist injury. His late-season charge includes a 20-1 win record, three titles and A+++ form this week despite a hamstring problem, topping his group with a perfect record of three straight-sets wins. He may have only one fit leg (and even that one has some tape on it too), but no one has been able to get near him.

So where does this leave Shelton’s chances of reaching the season-ending final for the first time? His form has been more mixed than Alcaraz’s this tournament, with a loss to Alexander Zverev bookended by wins over Arthur Fils and Rafael Jodar, but he does have an ace in his pack today in the form of Juan Carlos Ferrero, Alcaraz’s former coach who, after their surprise split at the end of last year, joined Shelton’s team this month.

It’ll be fascinating to see if Ferrero makes any tweaks to Shelton’s tactics against a player Ferrero knows like no other, having coached Alcaraz to six of his seven slam titles. If Alcaraz’s hamstring holds up, we could be in for another blockbuster in this late-season trilogy (hopefully without the 3.33am finish).
