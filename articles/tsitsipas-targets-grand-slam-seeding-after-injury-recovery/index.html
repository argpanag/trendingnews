Tsitsipas Targets Grand Slam Seeding After Injury Recovery

TheTools · 20.10.2026 09:19 · source · permalink

Stefanos Tsitsipas is increasing his tournament schedule as he seeks a stronger finish to the 2026 season and a seeded position at the 2027 Grand Slam events. The Greek tennis player, ranked No. 43 and seeded No. 7 at the Almaty ATP 250 in Kazakhstan, says improved physical condition has restored his confidence and ability to compete regularly.

Speaking on Sunday after signing autographs for fans, Tsitsipas explained that the physical problems he experienced last year had affected both his ranking and his belief in his game. He now feels capable of maintaining a demanding schedule without the same concerns.

"Last year, I didn’t have a healthy body, and it affected my ranking and my confidence," Tsitsipas said. "But now suddenly I can have long runs without being affected by it, so that is a big relief for me. I know I can get back to the top.

I genuinely believe that. If I want to play every single week, now I know I can without any concerns."

The 2026 season has included signs of progress for Tsitsipas, who won the title in Gstaad. His improved fitness has encouraged him to enter more tournaments during the final stretch of the year, with ranking points and Grand Slam seeding among his main priorities.

"Normally, I wouldn’t be playing that many tournaments, but I’m trying to accumulate as many points as possible at the end of the season," he said. "I want to close the season strongly in order to be seeded at the Grand Slams and avoid big names in the early rounds of the big tournaments. I feel next season will be more about me being hunted than hunting, like this year when I was genuinely hunting to get back to where I belong."

Tsitsipas has also credited Thomas Perrin, who joined his coaching team from the Mouratoglou Academy, with helping him regain stability. The Greek player highlighted the importance of a collaborative working relationship and a calmer environment within his team.

"Thomas brings calmness to the team, and that’s what I need," Tsitsipas said. "I need someone who understands me and my decision-making process. He’s a very collaborative person, and he’s open-minded and sees things exactly how I see them."

Tsitsipas also discussed his decision to keep his father outside his coaching role, explaining that separating their professional and personal relationships had improved their bond. He additionally identified Patrick Mouratoglou as an important member of his wider team.

"I love my father as a father, and I want to keep the coaching part out of my relationship with him because now it has never been better."

Tsitsipas added that Patrick Mouratoglou "is a big asset that is also part of the whole Tsitsipas team."

The Greek player has previous experience of reaching the final of the tournament now held in Almaty. In 2022, when the event took place in Astana, he lost to Novak Djokovic 6-3, 6-4.

Tsitsipas is scheduled to open his campaign against American player Aleksandar Kovacevic. The winner will meet Kazakh wildcard Mikhail Kukushkin in the second round.
