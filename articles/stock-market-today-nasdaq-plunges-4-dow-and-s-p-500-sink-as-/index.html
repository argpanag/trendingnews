US stocks accelerated their losses on Friday after Anthropic's (ANT) successful initial public offering failed to quell broad uncertainty about artificial intelligence safety and financing.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) slipped 1% after the blue-chip index fell below 50,000 and neared a correction. The S&P 500 (^GSPC) declined by 2.6%. Both the Dow and S&P 500 were poised to close in correction territory.

The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) fell more than 4.2%, with the tech-heavy index on pace to close negative for the year.

Investors went risk-off on Friday as semiconductor stocks led a tech sell-off. Nvidia (NVDA) stock fell 6%, extending a six-day decline. AMD (AMD), Broadcom (AVGO), and Micron (MU) stocks suffered double-digit drops.

A day after its record-breaking debut, Anthropic shares fell 10% from a closing price of $97, though they remain narrowly above their IPO price of $83. After a big pop on its IPO day, Anthropic faces a test in the markets to see whether it can sustain the momentum.

(^DJI )

Other assets slid on Friday as well. Gold futures (GC=F) dropped nearly 5%, while the price of digital gold — bitcoin (BTC-USD) — dropped below $59,000 to trade at its lowest price since July.

On the economic front, meanwhile, the University of Michigan's consumer sentiment index ticked up as Americans grew more optimistic about business conditions after the midterm elections.

  • Bitcoin falls below $60K as November woes deepen

    Bitcoin (BTC-USD) plunged below $60,000 on Friday, deepening a November sell-off that is shaping up to be the largest cryptocurrency's worst monthly decline in more than four years.

    Bitcoin fell as low as $59,540 in late Friday morning trading. At its current price, the digital coin is down 23% so far in November, putting it on pace for its worst monthly performance since June 2022 when it fell 38%, according to Yahoo Finance data.

    The sell-off comes as chips and other tech stocks are tumbling on the heels of Anthropic's public debut, which investors largely viewed as the AI trade's 'Super Bowl moment' for 2026.

    The monster listing has also ushered some soul-searching among crypto's longtime believers.

    "I said back in May that most of the crypto industry was dead and it was never coming back. I believe that even more today," investor Anthony Pompliano said in a Friday post on X, where he remarked on the "enormous benefits" and " impressive revenue growth" of AI companies.

    At its current pace, November is shaping up to be bitcoin's eighth-worst month since Yahoo Finance's daily records began in September 2014. Along with AI enthusiasm, which has prompted some bitcoin mining firms to sell their holdings at a faster pace, analysts have pointed to the toll that higher interest rates are taking on investor appetite for riskier assets.
