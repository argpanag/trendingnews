The "odd" 2000 co-star Morgan Freeman "was concerned" about working with

TheTools · 16.09.2026 16:15 · source · permalink

Only the biggest stars in Hollywood are allowed to pick and choose their co-stars, and since Morgan Freeman has never been in that bracket, he’s got to make the best of what he’s given.

That’s not to say he isn’t a big star, a household name, and an indisputable icon of the industry, because he is, but he nonetheless resides on a rung beneath the A-listers who call their own shots, and there aren’t many of those, with former colleagues Clint Eastwood and Tom Cruise among the very few.

He did draw the line at sharing the screen with TI, though, who was booted from the ensemble of the forgettable comedy Last Vegas at Freeman’s behest when he started dozing off during a table read of the script, so the rapper really only had himself to blame for looking so unprofessional in front of the veteran.

Other than that, and by his own admission, the Academy Award winner is a mercenary. He’ll go where the money is, and while a lot of his choices are driven by the who, what, when, where, and why, at the end of the day, he’ll always consider the size of the paycheque before officially signing on the dotted line.

The exposition machine does prefer when the material is up to standard, as any actor would, which explains why he agreed to star in director Neil LaBute’s 2000 crime comedy, Nurse Betty, despite admitting that he thought the filmmaker’s previous work had “sucked deeply.”

The farcical plot follows Renée Zellweger’s title character, who convinces herself that she deserves to be with the protagonist of her favourite soap opera, a delusion brought on by the nervous breakdown she experiences after witnessing her husband being tortured to death.

What she doesn’t know is that, when she packed her belongings to pursue the love of her life, Betty also brought a cache of stolen drugs along for the ride, with Freeman and Chris Rock’s father-an-son hitmen dispatched to retrieve the narcotics and make sure there’s no trace of anything tying back to their boss.

The studious, composed, and gravitas-laden Freeman sparring with the charismatic, motor-mouthed, and comedically-inclined Rock was an unusual pairing, and the former wasn’t sure about it. “I thought it was an odd combination, and I was concerned,” he admitted. “I like his act, but someone you don’t know, whose rep is built on another medium, is this going to work out?”

Even though Rock had been in almost 20 movies by then, most of his roles were extensions of his stand-up persona, which is why Freeman was trepidatious about playing opposite him in something that, while comedic in some respects, required some dramatic chops, too. Did it work out? “It did,” according to the man himself, which is just as well, because if it didn’t, he knew exactly who to blame.
