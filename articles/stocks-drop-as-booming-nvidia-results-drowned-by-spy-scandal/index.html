Asian equities failed on Wednesday to capitalise on blockbuster earnings from Nvidia that soothed concerns about the AI boom but were overshadowed by concerns about a spy scandal engulfing the chip titan that has shaken global markets.

The tech sector -- already hit by questions about profitability -- has been strafed over the past week by claims that boss Jensen Huang had had a number of affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy who might have funnelled restricted chips to Beijing.

The scandal forced Huang to step down on Thursday and has fanned worries about the chip titan, which is at the centre of a buildout that has helped propel world markets to record highs and boosted economic growth.

In a highly anticipated earnings release Tuesday, the firm said third-quarter revenue topped expectations and doubled to more than $115 billion while net profit hit $65.3 billion.

The firm also increased its forecast for next year's revenue, predicting 75 percent growth, compared with a previous forecast of 70 percent.

The figures suggested the AI boom remained on track despite concerns that the vast sums pumped into the sector might not see returns any time soon, if at all.

In a bid to reassure investors, interim CEO Colette Kress said it was "business as usual" at the firm, even after the loss of Huang, a pivotal player in the eye-watering explosion in the AI industry.

The news sent Nvidia's shares surging nearly five percent in after-hours US trade, while Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom were also sharply higher.

However, after a healthy open, Asian markets struggled to maintain momentum Wednesday as traders continued to fret over the outlook for the sector.

Adding to the nervousness was news that the United States moved to tighten restrictions on Chinese access to chips, while Huang has agreed to meet US lawmakers on December 1 to answer questions about chips and national security concerns.

Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei were well down, while there were also losses in Hong Kong, Shanghai, Sydney and Singapore, though Wellington, Manila and Jakarta rose.

Regional tech firms were broadly under pressure, with Japan's Kioxia and Tokyo Electron down more than three percent, while TSMC was also lower in Taiwan. South Korean chipmakers SK hynix and Samsung were mixed.

There are "still lots of unknowns for the stock that create the overhangs that investors are worried about. But the earnings show it's still in very good shape", wrote Neil Wilson at Saxo Markets.
