The "outrageous" 1986 role Christopher Lloyd couldn't refuse

TheTools · 29.10.2026 07:00 · source · permalink

The Back to the Future legend that is Christopher Lloyd recently turned 88 years old, but, thankfully, he didn’t then immediately vanish into thin air, leaving just a spinning number plate and some fire on the ground, because that would have been terrifying.

Instead, he marches on as an older but very recognisable reminder of one of the greatest movies of all time, the 1980s time-travelling masterpiece that finds new fans every year and represents the pinnacle of popcorn-fed, special effect-laden, retro excitement in movies, true escapism in cinema.

As Doc Brown, one half of the iconic duo with Michael J Fox’s Marty McFly, Lloyd ensured he would be recognised by generations of film fans to come, let alone the army of obsessives who have been born in the more than 40 years since Back to the Future was first released. 

Despite Brown’s ‘crazy old scientist’ persona in the movie, Lloyd was, in fact, just 47 when he made the film, which is ten years younger than Jennifer Lopez is now. It was 1985, and he had already had a long and successful career as a performer on Broadway for many years before making his film debut in Jack Nicholson’s astonishing One Flew Over the Cuckoo’s Nest a decade prior.

That led to a main role on the US sitcom Taxi alongside Danny DeVito, Lloyd playing the erratic cab driver ‘Reverend’ Jim Ignatowski for some 84 episodes, earning two Emmy awards in the process. Then, the year after he had become world famous thanks to the massive success of Back to the Future, he was brought in for an episode of the fantasy sci-fi anthology series Amazing Stories, which was created by Steven Spielberg as a kind of more family-friendly Twilight Zone and featured stand-alone tales of monsters, freaky events, and aliens.

Lloyd recalled making his episode, titled ‘Go to the Head of the Class’ about an English teacher cursed by his students with horrifying results and helmed by an old friend, to AV Club back in 2012, saying: “Yeah, I loved doing that Amazing Stories. That was just so outrageous. I love working with Bob Zemeckis. I think he’s amazing and wonderful to work with. And it was such a crazy character to play. There was no question in my mind that I would do it.”

It was Spielberg who acted as an executive producer on the Back to the Future trilogy, and he fulfilled the same role on Amazing Stories, which ran for two seasons and 45 episodes, eventually being brought back in 2020 for an Apple TV reboot where he again served in the same role. Between the first two BttF movies, Zemeckis and Lloyd would team up again, this time on the part-animated, part-live action noir Who Framed Roger Rabbit, with Bob Hoskins in the lead role.

That would be another huge hit for the duo, with the fantasy comedy bringing in $350 million at the box office against a production spend of just $50 million, and being nominated for seven Oscars, winning four. Lloyd played the imposing Judge Doom in the movie, the evil rule-maker of Toon Town, and the part for which he beat Tim Curry after the audition process.

A year later, though, he was back in the role for which he will always be remembered, as Back to the Future Part II almost did as well as the first movie, becoming one of the highest-grossing films of 1989. It was filmed back-to-back with Part III, which was released the next year, but didn’t quite reach the same heights.
