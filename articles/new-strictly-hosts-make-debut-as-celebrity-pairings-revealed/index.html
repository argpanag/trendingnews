  17. One step, two step: A quick guide to Strictly Come Dancingpublished at 19:15 BST
    19:15 BST

    Don't know a Charleston from your elbow? For the unitiated, we've put together a whistle-stop guide to how Strictly Come Dancing works.

    In a nutshell, celebrities (15 this year) are partnered up with professional dancers.

    Each Saturday, they perform a dance routine - mainly in Ballroom or Latin style - in front of the four judges. Each judge awards them a score from a woeful one to a terrific 10.

    It's then time for the public to vote. This gets combined with the judges scores, and the two lowest-performing couples compete in a dance-off on Sunday evening. It's up to the judges who to send home.

    Couples are knocked out weekly until the grand finale, where the final four battle it out. A final public vote decides who wins the glitterball trophy.

    Sound simple enough?

    It is. But expect twists and turns along the way. From musical relays to "instant dance" challenges, last series served up plenty of curveballs.

  19. A comedian, a presenter and a former Strictly pro walk onto a dancefloorpublished at 19:05 BST
    19:05 BST

    Strictly bid adieu to long-running hosting duo Tess Daly and Claudia Winkleman at the end of last series.

    Taking up the presenting baton are TV presenter Emma Willis, comedian Josh Widdicombe and professional dancer Johannes Radebe.

    Ahead of starting, Willis said she was a "huge fan" of the show, while Widdicombe said he was "giddy with excitement". Radebe - who is a former pro on the show - said returning in a presenting role was "beyond anything I ever imagined".

    It has not yet been announced which role each presenter will take on, but it's understood the new trio will have equal billing and hosting duties may be different from the Tess and Claudia era.

    Willis told Richard Osman on The Rest is Entertainment podcast earlier this month "no one's replacing anybody - at the minute it's all very kind of open".

    Widdicombe added that they would "be like Total Football" [where players can take the role of anyone on the team], adding: "We're going to be moving everywhere".

    Radebe admitted he felt a "little bit of a tinge" of FOMO [fear of missing out] at seeing the pros in rehearsals.

  20. Football to glitterball: How last year's winner Karen Carney tackled Strictlypublished at 18:56 BST
    18:56 BST

    Former Lioness Karen Carney swapped her football for the glitterball trophy last year, as she was crowned winner in a glitzy final alongside her professional dance partner Carlos Gu.

    She is the first and only footballer to have lifted the trophy.

    "I can't believe it, it's been the biggest privilege and honour," Carney said, adding that she was "lost for words".

    She beat Love Islander Amber Davies and social media star George Clarke, who were also finalists in the 2025 series.

    Carney's season got off to a roaring start last September when she topped the leaderboard in week one with 31 points for her Jive, dancing to Blondie song One Way or Another.

    But she faced challenges along the way - particularly in her ballroom numbers, where a spinal condition created challenges with posture.

    Carney's partner Gu told her she had "changed my life," adding: "To the girls and boys watching at home, dream big and be proud, and never give up."

    Keep your eyes peeled during tonight's launch show, as Karen and Carlos will be returning to the dancefloor.

(L-R): Johannes Radebe, Emma Willis and Josh Widdicombe

Strictly bid adieu to long-running hosting duo Tess Daly and Claudia Winkleman at the end of last series.

Taking up the presenting baton are TV presenter Emma Willis, comedian Josh Widdicombe and professional dancer Johannes Radebe.

Ahead of starting, Willis said she was a "huge fan" of the show, while Widdicombe said he was "giddy with excitement". Radebe - who is a former pro on the show - said returning in a presenting role was "beyond anything I ever imagined".

It has not yet been announced which role each presenter will take on, but it's understood the new trio will have equal billing and hosting duties may be different from the Tess and Claudia era.

Willis told Richard Osman on The Rest is Entertainment podcast earlier this month "no one's replacing anybody - at the minute it's all very kind of open".

Widdicombe added that they would "be like Total Football" [where players can take the role of anyone on the team], adding: "We're going to be moving everywhere".

Radebe admitted he felt a "little bit of a tinge" of FOMO [fear of missing out] at seeing the pros in rehearsals.

Football to glitterball: How last year's winner Karen Carney tackled Strictlypublished at 18:56 BST
18:56 BST

Carney wore a sequinned football kit as she lifted the glitterball trophy

Former Lioness Karen Carney swapped her football for the glitterball trophy last year, as she was crowned winner in a glitzy final alongside her professional dance partner Carlos Gu.

She is the first and only footballer to have lifted the trophy.

"I can't believe it, it's been the biggest privilege and honour," Carney said, adding that she was "lost for words".

She beat Love Islander Amber Davies and social media star George Clarke, who were also finalists in the 2025 series.

Carney's season got off to a roaring start last September when she topped the leaderboard in week one with 31 points for her Jive, dancing to Blondie song One Way or Another.

But she faced challenges along the way - particularly in her ballroom numbers, where a spinal condition created challenges with posture.

Carney's partner Gu told her she had "changed my life," adding: "To the girls and boys watching at home, dream big and be proud, and never give up."

Keep your eyes peeled during tonight's launch show, as Karen and Carlos will be returning to the dancefloor.
