A Rivalry 130 Years in the Making: Why this Ole Miss-LSU Magnolia Bowl is unlike any before it - The Rebel Walk

TheTools · 16.09.2026 20:17 · source · permalink

Posted by Billy Kuhl | Sep 16, 2026 | Football, Ole Miss

OXFORD, Miss. — The rivalry between the Ole Miss Rebels and LSU Tigers has spanned more than a century, producing unforgettable finishes, championship implications and plenty of bad blood along the way.

Saturday night, another chapter will be added to that history — and this one has all the ingredients to become one of the most memorable yet. The series dates all the way back to 1894, when LSU defeated Ole Miss, 26-7, in Baton Rouge.

Since 1945, the two programs have met every season, and this past offseason, Ole Miss and LSU renewed their agreement to continue the annual rivalry through the end of the 2029 season.

Even before Ole Miss and LSU became annual opponents, the two programs had already built a rivalry stretching back to their first meeting in 1894. The series had occasional gaps during its early decades and was interrupted during World War II, when Ole Miss did not field a team in 1943 or 1944. But beginning in 1945, the Rebels and Tigers became annual opponents, meeting every season since.

The birth of the Magnolia Bowl

Despite the long history between the two programs, the matchup didn’t officially become known as the Magnolia Bowl until 2008.

The name came from a student-led effort involving both universities, with students ultimately settling on the Magnolia because it is the state flower of both Mississippi and Louisiana. A trophy featuring the magnolia became part of the rivalry, awarded to the winner of the annual meeting.

Saturday will mark the 115th installment of a rivalry in which there has rarely been much love lost between the two sides.

This year, however, the fire perhaps burns even brighter.

A familiar face returns to Oxford

Lane Kiffin spent six regular seasons as the head coach at Ole Miss, compiling a 55-19 record and helping transform the Rebels into a program capable of competing on the national stage.

His final season in Oxford took Ole Miss somewhere it had never been before.

The Rebels finished the 2025 regular season 11-1 and earned the program’s first berth in the College Football Playoff. But before Ole Miss ever took the field in the postseason, Kiffin made the decision to leave Oxford for SEC West rival LSU.

Throughout the final weeks of the regular season, Ole Miss made a strong push to retain its head coach as speculation surrounding his future intensified. Ultimately, Kiffin chose LSU, announcing his departure on Nov. 30, 2025. The timing of the decision — with Ole Miss preparing for its first CFP appearance and still in contention for a national championship — made an already painful departure even more difficult for Rebel fans.

He pushed Keith Carter to allow him to remain and coach the team throughout the playoffs; however, it was never going to be a viable option for the new, sitting LSU head coach to be in the Ole Miss athletics facilities for another month-and-a-half, with access to players he would try to take with him to Baton Rouge.

The emotions surrounding Kiffin’s exit were evident. As he prepared to leave Oxford for Baton Rouge, a group of Ole Miss fans even gathered at the Oxford airport as Kiffin boarded a plane bound for his new home. Let’s just say it, understandably, wasn’t a friendly goodbye.

But Ole Miss’ season wasn’t finished.

With Pete Golding taking over as head coach, the Rebels continued their historic run through the College Football Playoff, eventually advancing all the way to the semifinals. There, Ole Miss’ remarkable season came to a heartbreaking end in the Fiesta Bowl with a 31-27 last-second loss to Miami.

Nine months later, Kiffin is coming back to Oxford. This time, he’ll be standing on the other sideline.

A Top-10 Magnolia Bowl awaits

The circumstances surrounding Kiffin’s return would make Saturday’s matchup one of the most anticipated Ole Miss-LSU games in recent memory on their own.

But the stakes on the field make it even bigger.

Both programs enter SEC play with 2-0 records and separated by just one spot in the Associated Press Top 25. LSU comes to Oxford ranked No. 7, while Ole Miss sits directly behind the Tigers at No. 8.

Then came one more addition to an already enormous weekend.

ESPN announced that College GameDay will return to Oxford for Saturday’s showdown, marking the show’s third trip to Ole Miss and its first since 2022. The Grove will become the center of the college football world Saturday morning before attention shifts across campus to Vaught-Hemingway Stadium that evening.

More than 130 years after Ole Miss and LSU first met, the rivalry has evolved from a regional battle into one of the SEC’s most enduring annual matchups. It has a trophy now, a name and generations of history behind it.

But rivalries are ultimately remembered for individual games — the nights, plays and moments fans continue talking about decades later.

With two undefeated Top-10 teams, College GameDay in town, the beginning of SEC play and a former Ole Miss head coach returning to Oxford wearing LSU colors, Saturday has everything necessary to become one of those nights.

Ole Miss and LSU will write the next chapter of the Magnolia Bowl Saturday at 6:30 p.m. CT on ABC.
