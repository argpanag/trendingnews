    18:16 BST

    Katie Williams
    Live reporter

    The second storm in two days has brought wet and windy weather to the UK, causing travel disruption and event cancellations.

    Scotland, Northern Ireland and the north of England are bracing for the worst of Storm Boelo, with gusts of up to 80mph forecast.

    Three yellow weather warnings are already in place for wind and rain, impacting areas from Scotland right down to southern England.

    Another three warnings will kick in this evening from 21:00, including two more serious amber warnings for wind impacting Northern Ireland, Scotland and northern England. We've got full details on those here.

    Halloween events have been cancelled in Scotland and Northern Ireland, a marathon has been called off in North Wales and Knowsley Safari Park in Merseyside, north-west England, will be closed tomorrow.

    National Rail has announced disruption on a number of lines, flights to and from Dublin Airport have been cancelled, and highway authorities are urging drivers to take particular care and avoid non-essential journeys.

    A number of power cuts in the Glasgow and Edinburgh areas have already been reported to SP Energy Networks, while power outages continue to affect large parts of south-west England following Friday's Storm Austen.

    We're pausing our live coverage now, but you can read the latest in our news story.

  2. Could we see three storms in a row?published at 17:53 BST
    17:53 BST

    Matt Taylor
    BBC Weather

    Storm Boelo follows hot on the heels of Storm Austen, but forecasts also show the potential for yet another deep area of low pressure to arrive later on Monday and into Tuesday.

    It's too early to say if this one will be named, but it looks likely to follow a similar track to Boelo, with the strongest winds focused towards the north of the UK.

    A succession of storms is perhaps not as unusual as you think.

    When you have a strong jet stream roaring across the Atlantic as we do right now, areas of low pressure can easily be developed and steered towards the UK in quick succession.

    In February 2022 three named storms had big impacts in Northern Ireland - Eunice, Dudley and Franklin.

    A fourth storm is looking unlikely though as our weather quietens down through the week.

  7. Dublin Airport cancels flights and warns of further disruptionpublished at 16:40 BST
    16:40 BST

    Storm Boelo is continuing to cause travel disruption this afternoon.

  8. New flood warnings for Cumbria, Greater Manchester and Merseysidepublished at 16:23 BST
    16:23 BST

    New flood warnings and alerts have been issued since this morning.

    There are now six warnings in place, indicating flooding is expected, and 39 alerts, indicating it is possible. A warning is the more serious of the two categorisations.

    • Cumbrian coastline from St Bees Head to Haverigg, along the coast from North Head to Haverigg
    • Irish Sea at New Brighton in Merseyside
    • Lee's Bridge at Astley Bridge Central, Bolton, Greater Manchester

    People in these areas are encouraged to avoid seafronts and harbours, as well as coastal roads, footpaths and carparks which may be flooded.

  10. Northern half of UK expected to be worst affected by Storm Boelopublished at 15:52 BST
    15:52 BST

    Annie Flury
    BBC News

    Strong winds and heavy rain are forecast to continue across the UK over the weekend as Storm Boelo moves eastwards, bringing the risk of travel disruption, power cuts and localised flooding.

    The worst-affected areas are forecast to be across the northern half of the UK.

    The Met Office has issued an amber wind warning for much of Scotland and parts of northern England from 21:00 BST on Saturday to 21:00 on Sunday.

    In Northern Ireland, a second amber warning for wind runs across parts of the north of the region from 21:00 on Saturday to 10:00 on Sunday.

    While the worst of Saturday's weather is forecast to be felt in areas covered by warnings, heavy and persistent rain is expected across larger parts of the UK.

    Spray and surface water could make driving conditions hazardous, while further downpours increase the risk of localised flooding.

    Strong winds are forecast to continue into Sunday, although rain should gradually ease across most of the country.

    Sunny spells and blustery showers are expected elsewhere, while north-east Scotland is likely to see the most persistent rainfall.

As a reminder: The Met Office has issued an amber warning for wind over Scotland and northern England from 21:00 on Saturday to 21:00 on Sunday.

The following rail line closures are planned:

• Glasgow Central – Ayr will close between Kilwinning and Ayr from 21:00 on Saturday. After 21:00, a half hourly shuttle service will operate between Glasgow Central and Kilwinning in both directions

• Glasgow Queen Street – Oban / Mallaig will close between Fort William and Mallaig and between Crianlarich and Oban from about 22:00 on Saturday

• Inverness – Kyle of Lochalsh will close between Kyle of Lochalsh and Achnashellach from the start of service on Sunday

BBC News NI

Police in Northern Ireland are advising drivers that traffic disruption is possible due to the weather conditions.

They also advise motorists to “take extra care” if they must travel and to reduce their speed “because of the possibility of surface water” and to avoid driving through flood water.

“It may be necessary to close some roads or put temporary restrictions in place - road users are reminded to adhere to road signs and any temporary traffic lights,” a police statement says.

The storm has also caused a number of road closures in the Republic of Ireland.

Meanwhile, in Londonderry, Foyle Bridge will remain closed until further notice due to high winds.
