Watch: Tottenham’s Rodrigo Bentancur sent off for butting Luke Shaw

TheTools · 10.10.2026 22:25 · source · permalink

Gary Neville calls Uruguayan’s indiscipline ‘madness’; plus, how late show of grit at Old Trafford can galvanise Roberto De Zerbi’s team

Tottenham midfielder Rodrigo Bentancur was sent off for butting Manchester United’s Luke Shaw, prompting a touchline brawl between the two sides.

The Uruguayan reacted furiously to being pushed over by Shaw, who was upset at Bentancur’s lunging tackle on him with 12 minutes to go of the 1-1 draw.

As he got to his feet, Bentancur moved his head into Shaw’s nose, with the full-back clutching his face in apparent agony.

That led to players and coaches from both sides getting involved, with numerous individuals having to be pulled away from each other.

And when referee Stuart Attwell showed Bentancur the red card he was enraged, trying to confront Shaw before being led to the tunnel by a member of the Tottenham coaching staff.

On commentary for Sky Sports, Gary Neville criticised Bentancur’s actions, although his side did come back to take a point as Dominic Solanke equalised after Bryan Mbeumo had put United in front.

“Madness from Bentancur,” Neville said. “Lack of discipline is not what he [Tottenham manager Roberto De Zerbi] needs. It’s stupidity. It really is.”

Shaw and Tottenham defender Jan Paul van Hecke were both shown yellow cards for their role in the incident.

After the match, Tottenham head coach Roberto De Zerbi jumped to the defence of Bentancur and revealed he kissed his player in the dressing room at Old Trafford.

The coach insisted it was the type of passion he wants to see from his players, who are still looking for their first Premier League win of the season.

“I kissed Rodrigo and said thanks,” said De Zerbi. “It is clear it is a mistake from Rodrigo, it is not the right reaction in football but now Tottenham needs to react, sometimes the reaction are not nice, not positive for the team but in that moment maybe with 10 players we played with more courage more energy and from a long time Tottenham played without this blood, this character.

“Now I am the coach and I want to see this character every game because Manchester United in the second half showed character. With the qualities, the character, the right energy we are going to win many games and score many goals.”

Analysis: How late show of grit can galvanise Spurs

Roberto De Zerbi’s squad are jetting to Marbella for a team-bonding camp this week, but nothing brings players together like a stoppage-time goal. It felt like a big moment when Dominic Solanke scored at Old Trafford.

It is October and Tottenham Hotspur have yet to win a Premier League game this season, although snatching a point off Manchester United was celebrated like a victory and the sangria will taste all the sweeter for it.

That is especially the case for Solanke, who was in the England squad at the back end of last season, but missed out on the World Cup and was overlooked by Thomas Tuchel at the start of this campaign.

Such was the criticism aimed at him, De Zerbi made him captain against United in a show of support to his striker. This was despite Archie Gray, part of the leadership group at Spurs, being in the starting line-up.

De Zerbi is trying to use these setbacks to galvanise this Tottenham team. Rodrigo Bentancur’s red card meant they had to fight their way to a result. “For the first time this season we showed we are a team,” said De Zerbi.

“We played for the team-mates, we played together, we stayed in the game in every situation and we attacked as a team and defended as a team and had the right football behaviour.”

It is still only three points this season and no wins, but there will be opportunities to break their duck at home to Coventry City and Crystal Palace later this month, with free midweeks for De Zerbi to build on this point.

In Spain, De Zerbi wants his players to be socialising and eating together, which he believes is as important as the warm-weather training. “We’ll stay together, work together, go for dinner together, to know better each other, because to be a team you have to know the team-mates.

“Now the target is to build the team. We are a great group of players but we are not a team yet and to be a team is to play for the team-mates to suffer together.”

De Zerbi’s messages are clearly getting through to the Spurs players as they put in their best performance of the season, with the Italian saying they could have been four goals up at half-time rather than going in goalless.

Conor Gallagher had the biggest influence on the match in the first half, then Spurs finished strong with James Maddison off the bench. Sandro Tonali put aside his struggles with Italy to set up the equaliser, with Solanke scoring his first Premier League goal of the season.

“Dominic deserved to be a captain,” De Zerbi said. “He suffered too much because Tottenham didn’t win and score, people saying ‘Solanke is not good enough’. It is not true. He is good enough to play, maybe he is the best striker for us. He is a complete player, he is a cool guy. He has to be good in terms of physical condition and I wanted to show him my confidence.

“After the last game, the criticism, I wanted to put him captain to show my confidence and the confidence of the team, all the other players were happy when I said he was captain.”

When Solanke ran off to celebrate in front of the away end, it felt like there was a connection returning between the players and fans. Those who had travelled up the M6 were dancing like Spurs had just won rather than earned a point – but surely that win will eventually come.

“We can’t accept to stay in that situation, we have to react and we have to win and then when we play three four five 10 games like today we can say we got out of the problems but so far we are in it together and have to suffer together,” De Zerbi said.

“But I am happy for the performance, for the qualities of the play especially in the first half, for the defensive phase together, we didn’t suffer too many chance considering we played at Old Trafford.”

Solanke, meanwhile, wants to use the point as a foundation to build from. “We’ve got so much to work on. The mentality and character was great. We’re going to put things right eventually and we’ll be flying sooner rather than later,” he said.
