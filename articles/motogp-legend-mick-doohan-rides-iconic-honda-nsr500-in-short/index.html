MotoGP legend Mick Doohan rides iconic Honda NSR500 in shorts and a T-shirt

TheTools · 17.11.2026 13:54 · source · permalink

Former MotoGP champion Mick Doohan has been spotted warming up his iconic NSR500 before taking it for a ride in shorts and a T-shirt.

Mick Doohan, who won five MotoGP titles in a row between 1994 and 1998, has been spotted riding his championship-winning Honda NSR500 in an unusual manner.

Wearing the famous Repsol Honda colours from the 90s era, and with the number one plate attached to the front and rear fairings, Doohan was filmed warming up his machine in Australia.

But the unusual element to all this is that Doohan is seen wearing nothing but a T-shirt, shorts, helmet and trainers.

There are very few motorcyclists you would trust more to ride a GP bike than Doohan, even at his current age of 61. However, seeing the MotoGP legend warming up his race bike before riding it in that sort of clothing is not something we would ever have expected. 

Doohan overcame serious leg injuries to win all five of his MotoGP titles riding the Honda NSR500, and remains the most successful Australian rider in terms of championship victories in MotoGP.

In 1999, Doohan suffered a career-ending accident at the third round of the season at Jerez, putting an end to hopes of a sixth consecutive title, something only Giacomo Agostini has managed to achieve in the 500cc/MotoGP class.

Despite his career ending sooner than it should have, Doohan served as an inspiration for many Australian riders, including none other than two-time MotoGP champion Casey Stoner. 

Stoner called it a dream of his to ride for Repsol Honda following the success Doohan enjoyed with the Japanese manufacturer. 

Stoner would go on to win the 2011 title with Repsol Honda, four years after securing his first championship win with Ducati.

A winner of 54 Grand Prix during his illustrious career, Doohan was presented with his MotoGP Hall of Fame medal at this year’s Australian Grand Prix, the last to take place at Phillip Island following the championship’s decision to move to Adelaide in 2027.
