Netflix's New Streaming Hit is 'Weapons' Meets 'Silence of the Lambs' - AOL

TheTools · 05.09.2026 00:49 · source · permalink

In recent years, Adam Scott has become a new kind of scream king. Following this year’s Hokum, the Severance actor has added The Whisper Man to his list of accomplishments. The Netflix original movie is a callback to many horror greats of the past, including Zach Cregger’s Weapons and the iconic The Silence of the Lambs. The Whisper Man combines what works in both movies to create a tragic tale of child abandonment wrapped up in a fascinating mystery.

Cregger’s 2025 movie worked so well because of the themes at the center of the story. A small town is rocked when an entire elementary school class walks out of their respective homes to seemingly vanish into thin air. All seemed to leave of their own free will, which made the occurrence all the more disturbing. Weapons pops off in the last half of the film, offering answers to this confounding tale, just like The Whisper Man.

‘The Whisper Man’ Hones In On Toxic Home Lives

One of the most haunting aspects of Weapons is the ending and the realization of what lone boy, Alex, is enduring at home. While the rest of his classmates disappeared, he becomes caretaker for them. The same goes for his parents, who have become hollowed-out husks of who they were. A metaphor for alcoholism, the themes of Weapons resonated with viewers.

The Whisper Man follows this trend with the story of Tom (Adam Scott) and his son Jake (Acston Luca Porto), who move to a small community after a personal tragedy. Jake doesn’t fit into a certain box of what a child should act like and imagines a girl in a blue sweater, exasperating his father. This tense household leads to Jake’s abduction by the titular serial killer. Like The Whisper Man’s previous victims, Jake isn’t necessarily accepted at home. He isn’t supported, and the child predator thinks that they can fill a void for them.

This picture of child neglect also affects Tom, who has an estranged relationship with his father, Pete (Robert De Niro). Child abandonment is a visceral piece of this horror film, but it wouldn’t be complete without the necessary addition of the serial killer thriller angle. Michelle Monaghan plays police detective Amanda, who appears as a proxy for Clarice Starling of The Silence of the Lambs fame.

She almost single-handedly discovers the identity of the killer in a sequence that is remarkably close to the finale of the Thomas Harris adaptation. The Whisper Man revives the '90s serial killer trend and includes a surprise role from Michael Keaton as Frank Carter, the Whisper Man himself. Though only in a few prison scenes, Keaton steals the show, channeling his inner Hannibal Lecter to deliver some genuinely chilling scenes.

The Whisper Man hits on these familiar trademarks to deliver a solid mystery with compelling themes. This stacked cast leaves the audience wanting more in a typical open-and-shut thriller. This is a return to form for Netflix, which has recently been struggling to find its niche in the heyday of streaming wars. The more macabre content brings audiences to the platform with familiar setups that are a comfort to horror fans.
