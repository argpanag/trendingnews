Federal judge proposes former DC US attorney to investigate government lawyers in golf course case

TheTools · 10.11.2026 00:05 · source · permalink

Judge Ana Reyes of the US District Court for the District of Columbia said Saturday that she intends to appoint former US Attorney Jessie Liu as a special master to investigate possible ethical violations by government lawyers in litigation over President Donald Trump’s plans to redevelop East Potomac Golf Links.

In a November 7 minute order, Reyes proposed that Liu investigate whether Department of Justice (DOJ) or Interior Department attorneys committed ethical violations in the case through October 31. The parties have until 10 AM Friday to submit written responses to the proposed appointment, which would be at the government’s expense. Reyes said a detailed appointment order would follow if she selects Liu.

The proposal follows an October 30 hearing at which Reyes announced an inquiry into the government’s conduct. The appointment remains pending, and the minute order does not resolve whether any attorney committed an ethical violation.

Reyes’s October 26 opinion detailed conflicting accounts of the redevelopment plans. Trump publicly announced that the course would close November 15, while government witnesses testified that they were unaware of plans for that closure. Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward also told the court there was no plan to close the course on that date.

The opinion further criticized the government’s removal of hundreds of trees without meaningful advance notice. Reyes barred further tree removal or damage and required 10 business days’ written notice before any irreparable modification to the forms or features of East Potomac Park.

The DC Preservation League and two golfers brought the lawsuit challenging the redevelopment. In granting preliminary relief, Reyes concluded that they were likely to succeed on their claim that the government violated the National Environmental Policy Act by improperly dividing its environmental review of the project.

The inquiry concerns attorneys’ obligations of candor to the court. Rule 3.3 of the DC Rules of Professional Conduct prohibits lawyers from knowingly making false statements to a tribunal and, subject to its confidentiality exception, requires correction of their prior materially false statements.

Liu served as US attorney for the District of Columbia from 2017 to 2020, during Trump’s first administration.
