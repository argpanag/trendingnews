  9. From Bury to AC Milan: Bournemouth's journey to 'dreamland'published at 19:54 BST
    19:54 BST

    Bournemouth v AC Milan (20:00 BST)

    "I'm pinching myself," says Bournemouth fan Kirk Tovey.

    "It's not long ago we were playing Oldham Athletic and Hartlepool United in League One. Now I'm about to watch us play AC Milan.

    "It's absolute madness."

    Fifteen years to the day after Bournemouth lost 2-1 at home to Bury in the third tier of English football, the Cherries host seven-time champions of Europe AC Milan in the Europa League on Thursday (20:00 BST).

    It is a game between the club that refused to die against the 11-time European Cup finalists on what promises to be a momentous night in the history of the 127-year-old Dorset club.

    "Over the last decade, my hometown club has achieved dreamland," Tony Pulis, who played for Bournemouth before managing them from 1992-94 and still lives in the area, tells BBC Sport.

    "It's an unbelievable story, when I think back to what the club was like when I was player, coach and then manager there in the 1980s and early 1990s, but one that hopefully just keeps going."

  12. Palace's defensive strugglespublished at 19:51 BST
    19:51 BST

    Besiktas v Crystal Palace (20:00 BST)

    Sami Mokbel
    BBC Sport Senior football correspondent

    Crystal Palace's recent DNA is rooted in defensive stability. Predecessors Oliver Glasner and Roy Hodgson produced well-organised teams with a solid base for its attacking players.

    Conceding close to 2.5 goals per game is so far removed from what the team's recent successes has been built upon that it has raised major concern from the club's powerbrokers.

    Palace are currently on course to concede 92 goals this season. No club has conceded more since Sheffield United shipped 104 goals to finish bottom in 2024.

    Personnel is contributing to those defensive deficiencies. Losing Guehi, Lacroix and Munoz in the space of six months represent significant losses.

    From the three centre-backs that started in Sunday's 5-0 loss to Brighton, Jaydee Canvot is 20 years old and Honest Ahanor only 18.

    Nevertheless, there is a clear sense from inside the club that Pierre Sage's system and approach has been a major contributing factor towards their troubles in defence.

FULL-TIMEpublished at 19:35 BST
19:35 BST

Lech Poznan 1-1 Sunderland

All over.

Sunderland remain unbeaten in the Europa League, but fail to win for the first time.

They are fifth in the table as it stands.

Sunderland registered eight shots on target during a dominant first half

Sunderland missed the chance to make it three wins from three in the Europa League as they were held to a draw by Lech Poznan.

After victories over AZ Alkmaar and Portuguese second-tier side Torreense in their opening two games, the Black Cats were reminded of the challenges of European football as they surrendered their lead in the final 20 minutes against the Polish champions.

Having slipped to the foot of the Premier League last weekend after suffering a fourth consecutive defeat, Sunderland initially showed little sign of feeling the pressure and took just two minutes to open the scoring in Poland.

Winger Nilson Angulo capitalised on a loose pass from goalkeeper Mateusz Lis before crossing for Timur Tutierov to head home his first goal for the club, with the striker deputising for the injured Wilson Isidor and Brian Brobbey.

Lech Poznan had a couple of chances to equalise during a chaotic opening period, but an impressive save from Robin Roefs kept Sunderland in front before the hosts' Daniel Hakans fired wide after being sent through on goal by Pablo Rodriguez.

Sunderland manager Regis le Bris was left wondering how his side had not extended their advantage before the break, with the visitors registering eight shots on target and Malick Fofana striking the woodwork.

The hosts should have punished Sunderland for their failure to convert earlier chances when the ball fell to an unmarked Leo Bengtsson at the back post shortly after the hour mark, but the substitute could not keep his volley on target.

Bengtsson made amends with 20 minutes remaining, finding space inside the area after some sloppy Sunderland defending to score Lech Poznan's first Europa League goal of the campaign with a sweetly struck half-volley.

After weathering a brief spell of pressure, Sunderland looked to have snatched a late winner for the second European away game in a row, but Chris Rigg's curling effort from outside the area whistled just past the post.

Next up for Sunderland in the Europa League is a home match against Dinamo Zagreb on November 5 (20:00 GMT), while Lech Poznan will travel to Benfica at the same time.
