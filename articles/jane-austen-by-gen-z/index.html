Jane Austen, by Gen Z

The new Sense and Sensibility lacks the subtlety of the novel – and of previous adaptations

By David Sexton

The classics always need reviving; what keeps them classic is their adaptability. Even so, making a new Sense and Sensibility is a bit of a poser. The 1995 version, written by and starring Emma Thompson and directed by Ang Lee, was a corker, rated by many as the best Austen adaptation ever. Thompson’s Englishness melded wonderfully with Ang Lee’s more purely cinematic approach. The cast was fabulous: Hugh Grant, Alan Rickman, Kate Winslet. It still seems just right, and not yet itself a period piece.

Here comes a new take, generationally different: a Gen-Z Sense and Sensibility, scripted by the young Australian novelist Diana Reid – whose 2021 debut, Love & Virtue, saw her hailed as the new Sally Rooney – and directed by Georgia Oakley. The film establishes its differences immediately. It opens on an extreme close-up of a girl’s face, revealing her to be lying in long grass in a loose white gown, adoring a caterpillar on her finger. Deer run by. We’re at one with nature – if not quite at Malickian levels, still pretty Hamnet-y.
