The one director Val Kilmer would never dream of turning down

TheTools · 01.10.2026 00:15 · source · permalink

When you’re an actor as successful as Val Kilmer was, you find the space to turn down some big directors. In fact, he rejected many classic movies during his career, which taught him a lot about the importance of being mindful of the choices he made.

I mean, how do you know if a movie is going to be a success? He rejected the part of Johnny in Dirty Dancing back in the 1980s, deeming it too “vapid,” but the movie became a major hit, with Patrick Swayze rising to significant acclaim for his performance. The movie wasn’t exactly vapid, either; it was actually a pretty poignant exploration of class told through the lens of a romantic drama.

He even turned down David Lynch’s Blue Velvet, with Kilmer put off by the movie’s surreal approach to sex and violence. That was another ‘80s classic that could’ve significantly altered the course of the actor’s career, yet he opted for Top Gun that year instead. Everything worked out in Kilmer’s favour, though, and he was soon appearing in everything from The Doors and Tombstone to Batman Forever and Heat.

Kilmer was a ‘90s icon, taking on enough major roles to establish himself as one of Hollywood’s most well-known stars – even if he did acquire a rather difficult reputation. The decade was a pivotal one for him because he learned a lot about the art of picking roles.

Most were great, but then there was the box-office bomb The Island of Dr Moreau, which saw Kilmer act incredibly hostile behind the scenes, while his co-star, Marlon Brando, wasn’t cooperating well, either.

That was probably the biggest mistake of his career, with the movie failing to win anyone over. The whole thing was a mess. When he looked back at certain choices, though, Kilmer could admit where he went wrong. “I’m old enough to be able to see patterns in my own behaviour and [I can tell if I’ve] done something awful,” he told TV Guide Online.

“It’s embarrassing, but I pretty regularly turned down work with really fine directors, of which there just aren’t that many.” 

Val Kilmer

“It’s embarrassing, but I pretty regularly turned down work with really fine directors, of which there just aren’t that many.” 

With stinkers like The Island of Dr Moreau under his belt and great directors turned down, Kilmer learned a valuable lesson. “I would never do that again,” he said. He got to a point in his career where he realised that, above anything, it was stupid to turn down directors who excited him. Kilmer was now down to work with his favourite filmmakers, regardless of what they were making. 

“If Ang Lee called me up and said, ‘I want to make a comedy about, I don’t know, a dinosaur,’ I’d say, ‘Okay!’ I wouldn’t even read the script, just because I think the films he does are so interesting. Whatever he wanted me to do, I’d like to do it,” Kilmer revealed.

Lee has been responsible for movies as wide-ranging as the Jane Austen adaptation Sense and Sensibility to the martial arts film Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon – oh, and don’t forget his Oscar-winning Brokeback Mountain and the superhero film Hulk, too. He’s done it all, apart from work with Kilmer, which sadly never happened in the actor’s lifetime.
