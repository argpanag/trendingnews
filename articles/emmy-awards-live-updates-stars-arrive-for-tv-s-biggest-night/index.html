It's almost showtime

Jaclyn Smith rocks her mother-of-the-groom dress to the Emmys

“Charlie’s Angels” star Jaclyn Smith graced the Emmys red carpet wearing a light blue, feathered Monique Lhuillier gown that she’s already worn once before.

Not only is the gown comfortable, she said, but it’s sentimental, too: Smith had previously worn it to her son’s wedding, she told Elizabeth Wagmeister and Variety co-president Ramin Setoodeh on CNN and Variety’s live coverage from the Emmys carpet.

Smith had no concerns about wearing it again for another special occasion she said, taking inspiration from the Princess of Wales, Kate Middleton, who is known to re-wear items at Royal engagements.

“If Princess Kate can rewear something, I can rewear something and I think it makes sense,” Smith said.

Smith is appearing on Sunday’s telecast alongside her former “Charlie’s Angels” costars Kate Jackson and Cheryl Ladd to commemorate the 50th anniversary of the classic 1970s TV show.

Zendaya's stylist Law Roach teases more details about her wedding to Tom Holland

Law Roach, Zendaya’s longtime stylist, is weighing in again on the “Euphoria” star’s wedding to “The Odyssey” actor, Tom Holland.

“I actually lied, she wasn’t married,” the stylist told Variety co-president Ramin Setoodeh during CNN’s Emmy Awards red carpet coverage tonight, referring to when he first claimed the couple was married earlier this year.

“They actually haven’t had an official ceremony yet. It’s going to happen after the ‘Dune’ press tour,” Roach said, adding that Zendaya has “been his wife for a really long time now, but it’s not official yet.”

Meg Stalter is the prize on the red carpet — literally

“Hacks” star Megan Stalter stuns on the red carpet every award season and this year is no exception.

Her co-star and co-creator of the show, Paul Downs, was right by her side.

“And my own, as I deserve,” Stalter quipped.

The comedian is nominated tonight for her role as Kayla Schaeffer in “Hacks” in the outstanding supporting actress in a comedy series category.

Zendaya stuns on the Emmys red carpet

Zendaya has arrived at the Emmys, wearing a stunning silver beaded gown on the red carpet.

She is once again nominated for outstanding lead actress in a drama series for her portrayal of Rue Bennett in HBO Max’s “Euphoria,” adding another chapter to her history-making run.

Zendaya became the category’s youngest winner in 2020 when she won for the show. Two years later, at 26, she became the youngest two-time acting Emmy winner and the first Black woman to win the award twice.

Colman Domingo says he was "emotional" about that shocking Euphoria ending

Colman Domingo can remember the moment he heard about how HBO’s “Euphoria” would end. He was having lunch with creator Sam Levinson, who told him the penultimate episode of the last season would feature Domingo’s own narration – in contrast to typical episodes, which featured lead Zendaya’s narration.

“He just looked at me and I said, ‘Oh no, no,’” Domingo told CNN’s Elizabeth Wagmeister and Variety’s Ramin Setoodeh. “From there, I knew what was going to happen.”

The episode leads up to Zendaya’s character, Rue, dying from an accidental drug overdose at the end of the series.

But then, Domingo’s character – her sponsor – “takes on the banner,” he said.

“When I found out about that, I was emotional,” the actor, who has Emmy nods tonight for his roles on both “Euphoria” and “Four Seasons,” said. “I thought it was beautiful.”

In an earlier interview with CNN’s Kate Bouldan, Wyle — who also serves as a writer, director and executive producer on the series — said it was important for the show to address all aspects of the healthcare system this season.

Stephen Root of "Widow's Bay" calls filming locations "a beautiful place"

Apple TV’s horror-comedy “Widow’s Bay” was filmed across multiple locations on the East Coast in what actor Stephen Root called “a beautiful place.”

On CNN and Variety’s live coverage from the red carpet tonight, Root told host Melissa Rivers that one of the remote filming locations was “very lonely out there” and recalled one instance in which a fellow castmate was spooked.

“Dale Dickey decided to take an Airbnb right on the river, nothing around it. Kind of got freaked out,” he said.

“Widow’s Bay” received 19 Emmy nominations for its first season.

"Pluribus" star Rhea Seehorn says she's "terrified" ahead of ceremony

How is Rhea Seehorn, nominated for lead actress for her starring turn in “Pluribus,” feeling ahead of the Emmy ceremony?

“I’m a terrified person,” she told Elizabeth Wagmeister and Variety co-president Ramin Setoodeh on CNN and Variety’s live coverage from the awards carpet.

Seehorn said it was a privilege just to be nominated. “That list of the other nominees – like I’m still like, wow, I’m on this list, and I’m getting to do this incredible job,” she said. “This is all icing on an amazing cake.”

She added that she was “forced” to frantically write an acceptance speech – just in case she wins – while getting her makeup done earlier today.

“I don’t want to, God forbid, forget to thank anybody,” she said.

“Pluribus” is Seehorn’s latest collaborator Vince Gilligan, who previously worked with the actress on “Better Call Saul,” for which she scored two previous Emmy nods. Seehorn said she was proud to stand alongside other collaborators from both series at the Emmys: “I wear it with pride that we’re all here tonight and that so many of them got nominated as well.”

Jason Segel says "Shrinking" costar Harrison Ford is the ultimate teammate

Jason Segel doesn’t take costarring with Harrison Ford for granted.

Segel told Elizabeth Wagmeister and Variety co-president Ramin Setoodeh on CNN and Variety’s live coverage from the Emmys that Ford is the “best example” of an on-set costar.

“Harrison is one of the best actors I’ve ever been around,” Segel gushed. “He approaches this job like a carpenter. He could walk into the room and command awe but he breaks through that right away and says, ‘I’m your teammate. We’re here to build a show together.’”

Ford – who got his start in the industry as a carpenter – is nominated for supporting actor in a comedy series for “Shrinking,” in which he stars alongside Segel, who hopes to see the “Indiana Jones” star take home a statuette at tonight’s ceremony.

“I’d love for him to have any honor that they would like to give him but I know for him, that work is what it’s all about,” Segel added.

Segel is also nominated for lead actor in a comedy series for “Shrinking,” marking his sixth nod but, to him, he already considers himself a winner.

“When you’re standing across from Harrison Ford, it’s hard to feel like you’ve done anything but won.”

A theme at this year's Emmys: Characters trying to keep it together

This year’s Emmy nominations have been dominated by characters just trying to get by.

Whereas the so-called Golden Age of Television was once overrun by stories of mostly White male antiheroes up to no good, many of this year’s frontrunners across some of the biggest categories in comedy and drama are regular people with good intentions trying to survive in increasingly dire times.

Traumatized doctors on “The Pitt” navigate Trump era cuts and crackdowns. The city officials on “Widow’s Bay” struggle to protect their small town from a force seemingly out of their control. And on “Abbott Elementary,” teachers try to educate their students in an underfunded public school.

On “Pluribus,” a prickly writer named Carol (Rhea Seehorn) and the reclusive storage facility owner Manousos (Carlos-Manuel Vesga) cling to the last vestiges of a lost world. On “Paradise,” the medical-student-turned-tour-guide Annie (Shailene Woodley, now an Emmy-winner) hides out in Elvis Presley’s Graceland mansion following another doomsday event.

Even on shows that aren’t as literally post-apocalyptic — “Margo’s Got Money Troubles,” “Your Friends & Neighbors” and “The Diplomat,” among them — we’ve seen people resorting to extraordinary means to keep the lights on as things fall apart economically, socially or politically.

TV Academy chairman hopes In Memoriam segment will do "justice" to those lost this year

It’s been a tough year for Hollywood this year with many beloved celebrity deaths, and tonight’s In Memoriam portion of the award show will try to honor those lost, according to Television Academy chairman Cris Abrego.

“This year unfortunately there’s been so many big iconic names and so we really want to do it justice in terms of honoring those names. We wish we could get everybody in on the telecast. It’s not feasible,” he told CNN correspondent Elizabeth Wagmeister and Variety co-president Ramin Setoodeh on the red carpet tonight.

Abrego teased singer-songwriter Noah Kahan as one of those performing as part of the tribute tonight, and said there are other surprises to look forward to during that segment.

Remember: The talents of late stars like Eric Dane, Hayden Panettiere, Catherine O’Hara, James Van der Beek, Vincent Pastore and television director James Burrows gave audiences seasons upon seasons of iconic television — work that was met with gratitude this year especially upon their deaths. And while people like Dolly Parton and Tim Curry may have been best known for their work in other mediums, their small screen contributions have also been remembered in the wake of their deaths.

Award-winning fashion designer Bob Mackie dies at 87

Nine-time Emmy award winner and renowned fashion and costume designer Bob Mackie has died at age 87, according to his social accounts.

Mackie became known for his glamorous designs after collaborating with Cher, dressing her on television and on tour. He also served as the costume designer for “The Carol Burnett Show,” responsible for Burnett’s entire wardrobe.

Nicknamed the Sultan of Sequins, the Rajah of Rhinestones and the Guru of Glitter, Mackie’s ostentatious, flattering looks became instantly recognizable over the course of his career. His popularity continued into the modern day, with his archive coveted by a new generation of stars.

For his stage and screen work — and in addition to the nine Emmys — Mackie was inducted into the Television Academy Hall of Fame and also earned three Academy Award nominations. In 1999, he received a retrospective at the Museum at FIT.

More recently, he won a Tony Award for the musical “The Cher Show” and a lifetime achievement award from the Council of Fashion Designers of America.
