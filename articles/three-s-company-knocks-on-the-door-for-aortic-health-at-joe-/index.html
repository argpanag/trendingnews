‘Three’s Company’ knocks on the door for aortic health at Joe’s Pub event

TheTools · 14.11.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

On Nov. 2, Joe’s Pub on 425 Lafayette St. hosted The John Ritter Foundation for Aortic Health’s celebration of the 70s sitcom “Three’s Company’s” 50th anniversary. 

Twenty-three years ago, Amy Yasbeck, founder of The John Ritter Foundation for Aortic Health, lost her husband, John Ritter, to an aortic dissection. Additionally, Yasbeck expressed that Ritter, who starred in “Three’s Company,” was undiagnosed and further misdiagnosed. 

According to The John Ritter Foundation for Aortic Health website, 20,000 people suffer from aortic dissection in the US annually. The Foundation aims not only to raise money to support those suffering from the disease, but also to raise awareness of the illness and available preventive actions. 

Yasbeck said, “I knew that if there was any way in the world that I could gather myself and my family and everybody, John’s friends, fans, that we could make a difference about this. And we have. So the John Ritter Foundation for Aortic Health started right after John passed away in 2003.” 

The event was more than a reunion of fellow “Three’s Company” cast members and fans, it also included a live table reading of the “Three’s Company” pilot episode featuring Jason Biggs as Jack Tripper, Christine Taylor as Chrissy Snow, Bellamy Young as Janet Wood, Richard Kind as Stanley Roper, Susie Essman as Helen Roper, and original cast member Richard Kline (known for his role of Larry Dallas) as the night’s narrator. 

Joyce DeWitt,  original cast member of “Three’s Company,” said, “You can imagine 50 years ago this show birthed itself and America kind of went, ‘hello’ and then they hung in with us all these years, lucky us!”

Although DeWitt starred as the original Janet Wood, for the celebratory table read she passed the baton of the iconic character to Bellamy Young. “It became clear to me that I would be the ghost of Christmas past in my own life. And I was like, I don’t think so,” DeWitt said. 

Young said, “I was such a ‘Three’s Company’ fan when I was growing up. I’m such a Janet. And so I was over the moon when they asked me to be a part of tonight for this, for this 50th anniversary.” Upon being asked how it feels to step into DeWitt’s shoes as Janet Wood, Young said, “No, the shoes are too big. I’m just borrowing the shoes cause Joyce is here.” 

Similar to Young, actress Christine Taylor stated that she, too, was a fan of “Three’s Company.” “’Three’s Company,’ to me, was the epitome of comedic perfection,” Taylor said. “I didn’t know what episode it was. I just said, I’m in. I’m honored. I hope I can do it justice.”  

And on all accounts, justice was indeed done. The John Ritter Foundation for Aortic Health aimed to raise at least $20,000 that night and ended up leaving with over $56,000 worth of generous donations. 

“It’s trying to spread awareness, create money for research, for aortic health, in particular aortic dissection, which is what took John out,” DeWitt said. DeWitt joined another original cast member, Richard Kline, in honoring Ritter’s comedic legacy and celebrating Yasbeck’s continuous work with the foundation. 

Young said, “So I hope for tonight, we have a lot of joy. We raise a lot of money, and we raise a lot of awareness. I hope the word gets out that there is community around aortic dissection. I hope that caregivers and people who are suffering this feel less alone. I feel like people who are doctors learn more and can diagnose early. I just hope we hit on all fronts tonight, joy and progress and learning and community.” 

This is only the beginning; the event is officially on tour, planning to perform in Cleveland, Dallas and Los Angeles in honor of Ritter and aortic health advocacy. For more information on the Foundation’s mission, visit johnritterfoundation.org.
