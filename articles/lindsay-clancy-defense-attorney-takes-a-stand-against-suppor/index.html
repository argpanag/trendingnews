Lindsay Clancy defense attorney takes a stand against supporters blaming husband Patrick - AOL

TheTools · 22.09.2026 21:56 · source · permalink

Lindsay Clancy's attorney, Kevin Reddington, came to Patrick Clancy's defense as conspiracy theories continue to swirl on social media.

Speaking to NBC News’ Laura Jarrett on Sept. 9, Reddington condemned the online chatter on social media accusing Patrick, Lindsay Clancy’s ex-husband, of killing his children.

“His life has been turned upside down into a nightmare that he doesn’t deserve,” Reddington said. “Period. Full stop.”

Reddington also added that he has “said it repeatedly, unequivocally, that there is absolutely no way that this guy deserves what he has been living (through).”

“I respect the fact that people are invested and that they’re doing their due diligence and they’re investigating and God bless them,” Reddington said. “But the bottom line is, he did not deserve what he went through.”

Clancy's case resulted in a mistrial after jury deliberations concluded with no unanimous verdict.

Clancy, a 36-year-old former labor and delivery nurse, never disputed that she killed the children. Her attorney said she had lost control of her mind due to a condition called postpartum psychosis, a rare mental illness linked to the stress, sleep deprivation and hormonal changes that follow childbirth.

Though Clancy's defense has not denied she killed her children, the internet has been filled with conspiracy theories about Patrick being the one who killed his children, and forming an elaborate plot to frame his ex-wife.

On Sept. 8, Patrick's attorneys finally spoke out against the accusations.

Howard Cooper, Patrick's attorney, slammed the “campaign of misinformation,” accusing internet conspiracy theorists for “publishing what they know to be outright falsehoods that Patrick was somehow involved in the death of his young children and other vile claims.”

“This campaign, now fueled by insatiable media coverage, remains at a fever pitch and has resulted in real life threats to his reputation, livelihood, and life,” Cooper said in the statement.

"Enough is enough -- this spread of blatant and baseless falsehoods must stop. Those responsible should understand that there will be consequences, and every appropriate measure will be pursued to hold them accountable, including legally. Law enforcement has been notified."

Three of the 12 jurors told NBC Boston that the jury was prepared to find the mother not criminally responsible for the deaths but one male juror did not agree, resulting in a deadlock.

“He admitted he had reasonable doubt, and I started filling out the forms,” Roni Carlson, a former elementary school teacher who served as the jury foreperson, told the outlet. “I was so excited. There were three forms I had to fill out, and I started filling them out. I wrote my signature on each one, and then he said, ‘But I’m still not going to say that she’s not guilty by reason of insanity.’”
