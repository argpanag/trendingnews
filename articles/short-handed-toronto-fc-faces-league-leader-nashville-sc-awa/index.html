Short Handed Toronto FC Faces League Leader Nashville SC Away

TheTools · 27.09.2026 00:44 · source · permalink

A severely short-handed Toronto FC travels to GEODIS Park on Saturday evening to face Supporters' Shield leaders Nashville SC, as the host side continues its push toward an all-time Major League Soccer points record.

Nashville SC enters Matchday 28 sitting atop the overall league standings with 57 points from a 17-3-6 record, having already clinched a playoff berth with eight matches remaining in the regular season.

The Tennessee club features the top defensive record in North America, conceding just 21 goals this season, alongside an undefeated home mark of 12 wins and one draw at GEODIS Park.

In contrast, Toronto FC sits 12th in the Eastern Conference with 29 points from a 6-9-11 record, remaining four points behind FC Cincinnati for the ninth and final postseason spot.

The visiting side will be missing key defensive personnel, including center back Walker Zimmerman, who is suspended due to yellow card accumulation after picking up his fifth caution against St. Louis CITY SC.

Defenders Richie Laryea, Richard Chukwu, and Benjamin Kuscevic are unavailable due to senior national team duty, while midfielder Markus Cimermancic received his first career international call-up with the Slovenia under-21 team.

Designated Player midfielder Djordje Mihailovic will also miss his fourth consecutive fixture as he continues to recover from a hamstring injury.

Despite the roster depletion, Toronto FC manager Robin Fraser expressed confidence in his depth options during pre-match media availability.

"I think we have a good group of guys, and [there’s] a reason why the team has been built the way it is — because obviously we need to have depth, and we have defenders that we like and we believe in. It's different to not have the guys who we see week in week out, but we certainly have a lot of belief in the rest of the group," said Fraser.

Fraser also addressed the scheduling challenges posed by international windows operating during the regular MLS season calendar.

"It is what it is, and we have to deal with it. And we've dealt with this for 30 years in the league now, so it just has become part of what we do. We're certainly looking forward to a time when we won't have these [conflicts] anymore.

Is it frustrating this time of year? Probably, based on where we are. If we were sitting at the top [of the standings], it probably wouldn't be that frustrating," stated Fraser.

Toronto FC previously secured a 2-1 victory over Nashville at BMO Field earlier this month, handing the league leaders one of their three regular season defeats.

Reflecting on that previous result, Fraser emphasized that past success provides momentum rather than a structural template for Saturday's contest.

"I don't think [that win] serves as a blueprint so much as it gives us belief. Meaning, they'll change things. They'll have different personnel, so it's not like we can do the exact same things," said Fraser.

"But I think it gives us a measure of belief. Obviously, beating a team that's at the top of the table, I think, will definitely have a positive effect. Having said that, there's a reason they're at the top of the table. It's because they're really good, and obviously we need to do a lot of things well, and we need to win our duels and that sort of thing," Fraser added.

Off the field, Nashville SC is hosting a Harry Potter theme night at GEODIS Park, handing out 12,000 house banners to early-arriving fans prior to the 8:30 p.m. ET kickoff.
