Contractors accused of failing to prevent CFMEU ‘army’ from attending brawl site

Accusations that police and major contractors failed to prevent a CFMEU blockade of Brisbane rail project sites from escalating into violence have been aired at Queensland’s inquiry into the union and construction sector.

The public probe has returned for a second day of evidence this week surrounding the May 2024 incident, which resulted in what Commissioner Stuart Wood described as a “riot” at the Dutton Park Cross River Rail station site.

Some two dozen CFMEU-associated men involved – some previously subject to a public callout by the inquiry – have also been identified, with Wood questioning the police response and the actions of firms employing the men.

“One of the things that’s come up over the past couple of days has been the failure of the police to ensure there was lawful access to this site,” Wood said on Wednesday.

“But if each of the employers – and I think Hutchinson got five of them – had either not employed these people as delegates, or had ensured they stayed at the site at which they were nominally employed, you wouldn’t have had that group of people at the Cross River Rail site for the police to have to be called.

“So there is a sort of upstream problem. It’s all very well to criticise the police for their failure, but what are these tier-one contractors doing, allowing this army of delegates to run around causing havoc?”

Counsel assisting the inquiry, Eddy Gisonda KC, reminded Wood that the brawl, which emerged as a group of workers tried to enter one of the project sites being obstructed by the CFMEU, was happening at the same time as a separate union campaign.

This one, relating to the Centenary Bridge upgrade in Brisbane’s west, was subject to evidence during a recent hearing that ousted union leader Jade Ingham had sent a message to all delegates requiring them to attend that site.

“And then you saw the outcome of that. There was at least 30, if not more, delegates, and there was other evidence where he threatened to have 2[00]-300 people attend the site at a moment’s notice,” Gisonda said.

“But in order for those people to be able to follow those instructions, they need to either have the permission or acquiescence of their employer.”

Gisonda also took the inquiry through details of the 15 men who the inquiry wanted to hear from in a public callout in April so “all relevant perspectives are heard”, along with others also linked to the event.

Most of the men were not employees of the project, and had roles in other parts of Brisbane and south-east Queensland. A number were delegates with major contractors, including Hutchinson Builders, John Holland, and Multiplex.

Others, including Dean Rielly, Dylan Howard, Andrew Blakeley, Cody Budgeon, Dennis Mitchell, Hayden Turner-Davey and Matthew Vonhoff, were CFMEU organisers. Mitchell and Turner-Davey were described by Gisonda as the two “main men” involved in the brawl.

Others identified by the inquiry as being present around the site on the day of the brawl, or the day prior, included delegates Desmond Savage and Anthony Perrett.

Perrett, who the inquiry has heard is related to Ingham, drove some union officials to meetings with barrister Geoffrey Watson’s early investigations into the CFMEU’s Queensland activity, and has separately been charged with murder.

Turner-Davey was also identified by Australian Workers’ Union organiser James Downie, who appeared before the inquiry on Wednesday, as having threatened him and named his two sons during a separate incident at the Woolloongabba Cross River Rail station site in 2023. Downie was also bumped onto a road and only just managed to avoid being hit by a passing truck.

Downie outlined a series of other incidents in which he was blocked from accessing various work sites by CFMEU figures, or subjected to what appeared to be intimidation aimed at stopping him from trying to do so.

Wood said it seemed Downie had been “terrorised” by the union. Two other AWU figures also outlined the CFMEU’s actions against them.

Earlier on Wednesday, a human resources manager on the Cross River Rail project, Matt Papworth, said the situation around the industrial action that led to the 2024 brawl was difficult for the Unity Alliance, including the CFMEU-targeted CPB, to manage.

WhatsApp messages among senior project figures shown to the inquiry, and Papworth’s evidence, show discussion of the union’s actions being in breach of an eventual court injunction issued in the days after.

The CFMEU argued at the time that CPB had brought in “hired muscle” to break their strike.

Two of the subcontracted workers among the group who managed to push through the CFMEU human chain amid the altercation had either worked on the site for years, or were recently recruited CFMEU members.

Timothy Carlton, a senior safety adviser with CPB who was present on the day of the brawl, said in the aftermath he witnessed a group arrive on motorcycles and join the CFMEU group, including one with a Hells Angels outlaw motorcycle gang logo on their jacket.

One message shown to the inquiry included a copied-and-pasted response from police to concerns raised about union action at one of the sites with a strong CFMEU presence during that period.

It appeared to show police saying they were aware of “planned industrial political activity involving CFMEU members” but “will not be responding to any industrial incidents unless criminal activity occurs or there is serious public disorder”.

Wood said the situation resembled a matter in Tasmania, with a “misunderstanding” by the police commissioner on how to respond to an issue at the Burnie Paper Mill.

Gisonda said it was “the exact issue”, and there seemed to be confusion “as to whether criminal activity is occurring in the first place” and that public disorder had to be of a “serious” nature.

“What are they supposed to do if the police don’t act?” Wood said. “I mean, they’ve got the option of self-help, try and push through the picket line – which then turns into a riot – or give in. I mean, what do you do?”

The $95 million multi-year inquiry, in its 15th block of public hearings, will continue to hear evidence on Thursday.
