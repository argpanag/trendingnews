Francesco Farioli admits Porto struggles: “For us, not a great night”

Francesco Farioli acknowledged FC Porto’s difficulties in reaching the last 16 of the Portuguese Cup, having beaten USC Paredes on penalties at the Estádio do Dragão. The Dragons’ coach credited the opposition’s attitude, admitted his team’s mistakes and defended Francisco Moura after his own goal. Progression to the next round did not change his assessment: “It wasn’t a brilliant night.”

Between the relief of going through and the need to play better, Francesco Farioli made no attempt to portray qualification as an accomplished performance. After a night in which FC Porto needed penalties to get past USC Paredes, the coach acknowledged the difficulties while stressing the need to return to their usual level, with the Champions League already on the horizon.

USC Paredes’ resistance did not surprise the Porto coach. In his view, the gap between the teams did not diminish the importance of the opposition’s mentality or the unpredictability of a cup tie.

“We were expecting a difficult game, not just because of USC Paredes’ quality, but because of the mentality and effort they brought to the pitch,” he explained. “Besides, anything can happen in a cup game, and we’ve seen several examples of that in the recent past. It’s always difficult, and unusual situations can arise during games that make things even harder, but that’s part of football.”

Assessing FC Porto’s response, Farioli went further in his self-criticism. He recalled his warning before the match and refused to take credit away from the opposition because they play in a lower division.

“Yesterday, when I began the press conference, I said it wouldn’t be an easy game, despite the two-division gap between the teams. They wanted it badly and gave everything they had. For our part, it wasn’t a brilliant night after the international break, with players returning over the last few days,” he acknowledged. “We have to look at our mistakes and give USC Paredes credit for what they did here. In other games I’ve complained that we deserved more, and today we didn’t deserve much, but I’m pleased we’ve gone through in the Portuguese Cup.”

Qualification was therefore the positive to take away, but it did not serve to disguise what had gone wrong. The coach separated his satisfaction at achieving the objective from his assessment of the performance, without using the international break as an excuse to absolve the team of responsibility.

When it came to Francisco Moura, his message was one of support. Farioli described the own goal as an unfortunate incident, preferring to highlight the response of his teammates and the fans, as well as the player’s contribution beyond that moment.

“What happened to him was unfortunate, but all the players on the pitch and the fans showed him kindness. He had already played at centre-back against RC Deportivo, and today he had a very good first half. Apart from the own goal, he had a positive impact on the game,” he stressed. “We can’t focus on that, and we’re all behind him. We’re a family, and at times like these we have to stick together. Some mistakes can hurt more than others, but today we had a hero in yellow who helped us enormously with a great performance.”

The demands placed on the team did not, therefore, translate into blaming one individual for the night’s struggles. Alongside his praise for the unity shown around Moura, the coach acknowledged a decisive performance that helped secure a favourable outcome for the Dragons.

With the tie now behind them, attention turns to the Champions League. The first step, Farioli said, is to recover from the exertion, without losing sight of the need to return to the standard of football he considers normal for the team.

“In football, even in an ideal scenario, things can happen that you want to avoid, but they’re part of the game. Now we’ll recover from today’s exertions, but I feel we’re in a good position in that respect,” he said. “Bad games can happen, but I’m happy we secured qualification, and that’s what we have to take forward. Now we have to get back to our usual level.”

Farioli set out a clear course for the next steps: build on qualification, recover and make corrections. Reaching the last 16 keeps their cup campaign alive, but the coach himself made clear that a different level of performance is required.
