Every year, the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) surveys exercise professionals to find out which fitness trends they see as most widespread. The survey includes 1,433 valid U.S. responses coupled with survey data from other countries. Respondents were mostly exercise physiologists (30%), personal trainers (25%), and group exercise instructors (17%), with an average age of 46. Here are some of the highlights from the survey:

Biggest Risers

Group fitness classes were one of the biggest risers from 2026, going from #13 to #7 overall. Influencer-led fitness programs also took a big leap, entering the top 10 after being off the list completely the previous year. Traditional strength training and mobile exercise apps also ticked up modestly.

Hot and cold therapies like saunas and cold plunges rose six spots to #11, their highest ranking ever. Some new additions to the top 20 were competitive fitness events (e.g. Hyrox) and massage/vibration/compression therapies.

Biggest Fallers

Adult recreation and sport clubs, like pickleball leagues, fell from #9 to #17. Its overall rating did not change much; it's just that other trends scored higher. Exercise for the purpose of weight management fell three spots.

The Clear Champion

Wearable technology was the number one overall trend. It has been in the top three every year since 2016, has been #1 ten times, and was the only trend in the top three for every job role, work setting, and experience level. As technology advances further and further, it's hard to imagine wearables being bumped from the podium any time soon.

Other Notable Findings

As mentioned, this survey encompassed data from multiple countries. Different countries have different interests. For example, Pilates is the number one trend in Australia, while group exercise classes are number one in Poland.

Social connection is considered important to exercise enthusiasts. One cited survey found that 57% of consumers join fitness communities mainly for social reasons, which helps explain the rise of group fitness.

Recovery is also becoming a part of structured programming, rather than just an amenity. Thermal therapies and massage/compression tools are growing, despite lacking formal guidelines for use.

Final Thoughts

While this was a relatively large survey, it still only represents a specific part of the population in the form of fitness professionals. There is the possibility that they rate trends they know best more highly. It was also self-selected, meaning the participants chose to take part.

The trends were also rated independently, not against one another. Still, it's an interesting snapshot of where people who do this for a living see the fitness industry going in the near future. The authors say they will work to broaden participation and improve representation across roles and settings in future surveys.
