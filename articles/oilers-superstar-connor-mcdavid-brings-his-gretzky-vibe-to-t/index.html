Oilers superstar Connor McDavid brings his Gretzky vibe to Toronto

TheTools · 13.11.2026 16:44 · source · permalink

“There was a stat a couple of years ago, where he had less games with no points (12), than he had games with three or more points (13). Like, I don’t get that many points in practice. In a season.” — Oilers defenceman Connor Murphy.

TORONTO — There will never be another Wayne Gretzky, hockey’s all-time leading scorer who had more career assists (1,963) than second place Jaromir Jagr has career points (1,921).

But as Connor McDavid rolls into Toronto for a Saturday night game against the Toronto Maple Leafs, a 20-game, season-opening points streak in tow, there’s definitely a Gretzkian vibe around McDavid.

He is, as Gretzky was, the Toronto-area kid who landed in Alberta’s capital, and the envy of Leafs Nation. And as he readies himself to set a new personal mark by extending his points streak to 21 games on Saturday (Sportsnet, Sportsnet+, 7 p.m. ET / 6 p.m. ABT), like almost any new milestone that McDavid can imagine, it inevitably pales to something Gretzky once did.

In this case it was Gretzky’s 51-game points streak in the 1983-84 season in which Gretzky averaged exactly three points per game. That season, Gretzky’s first pointless game came on Jan. 27, at home against New Jersey, his shoulder injured and his spirit waning just five games short of Joe DiMaggio’s iconic 56-game hitting streak in baseball.

“We knew he was always going to take care of business, that he was always going to get his chances,” said Paul Coffey, the Evan Bouchard to Gretzky, back in the day. “We never had to say. ‘Okay, let's get Gretz a point tonight.’ Never. But I tell you, if he gives it to you, you’d better (expletive) finish.”

Today, the eldest Oiler — 1990-born Mattias Ekholm — came into this world two years after Gretzky’s last Stanley Cup. The Great One never played in the 2000’s. To today’s player he is, as Gordie Howe and Maurice Richard were to the generation of players before them, a name in the record books more than an actual memory.

“For a lot of us,” said the ’95-born Jason Dickinson, “Connor is the best player of all-time.”

In true Gretzkian fashion, at the quarter mark of the season, McDavid has accumulated more assists (38) than any other NHL player has points. In his Age 29-30-year-old season he’s on pace for his best year ever, which has even a league full of NHLers quietly shaking.

“Your buddies, your family, they’re the ones watching from the outside. They’re like, ‘Are you watching this?’” Dickinson said. “Of course I am. And I'm sitting there going, ‘Why can't I do that?’ But that's what makes him him.

“He's not thinking about, ‘I need to get a 21-game point streak.’ He's thinking, ‘I need to go win this game for our team,’ and the byproduct is him putting up points. Because he plays like such a psycho that you can't keep up with him.

“It’s not a conscious thought, ‘Points, points, points.’ It’s just him dominating.”

As one who has watched both in their prime, there’s no denying it. Where McDavid is today. Well, you can see Gretzky from here.

In a game where time is currency, Gretzky found an extra second or two with that patented buttonhook inside the blue-line. Nowadays they track back much harder, so McDavid finds his space by other means, like attracting a defender, only to spin away at the last second.

The time the defender takes to find his bearings, to re-establish close proximity with McDavid, is all the time McDavid requires to extend a points streak, by goal or assist.

Night, after night, after night.

“How many times do you see guys play really well, but it just doesn't go in,” wondered defenceman Connor Murphy. “A goalie makes a great save, or they make the pass to someone who just misses. But the execution on the plays that (McDavid) makes … when he sets up guys they have such a good chance of scoring because of the way he's deceived the goalie and the (opposing) players. Because of how high quality the pass is.

“That's the extra detail that gets those daily points.”

The Oilers had to rent a ballroom at the team hotel in Toronto back in 1980 to accommodate all the media requests after Gretzky had lit the league on fire in his first NHL campaign.

“My memory is, there were 40-50 people there, and it was one of the greatest news conferences I've ever been in,” said then-Oilers media relations man Bill Tuele, now 80 and living on Vancouver Island. “Because it was the first time I ever saw Wayne really perform. In the sense that he was so composed and so young at the time. And Wayne really handled himself like a pro.”

The next night at Maple Leaf Gardens, having collected only 38 tickets for the 60 people he had coming in from his hometown of Brantford, Ont., Gretzky sent one of little brothers into the street to trade a signed stick for more tickets.

“I think it was Brent,” recalled Tuele. “He got 20 tickets, and pretty good ones. There were some greens, but they were mostly reds. So he did okay.”

This might be the one area where McDavid has already surpassed Gretzky. As part of his quest to become lead architect of the firm McDavid and Bedard, McDavid secures the CIBC luxury box for all of his visits to Scotiabank Arena.

“Very fortunate,” McDavid said.

Very fortunate, indeed. Are we not?
