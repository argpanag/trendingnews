The Defensive Adjustment the Blazers Need to Make vs. LaMelo Ball, Wolves

TheTools · 16.11.2026 16:05 · source · permalink

In this story:

The Portland Trail Blazers are continuing their six-game road trip as they travel to the Twin Cities to take on LaMelo Ball and the Minnesota Timberwolves.

The Blazers began their road trip on the right foot after beating the Sacramento Kings at the Golden 1 Center in their second NBA Cup game. Meanwhile, the Wolves are coming off their worst loss of the season at home against the Chicago Bulls, who improved to 3-9 with the win.

That game provided the blueprint for how to beat the Wolves in their own building and the Blazers need to try and execute that in order to come out on top. It all starts with defending Ball, who has taken on the responsibility as the primary offensive engine with Anthony Edwards on the sidelines nursing a right knee injury that required arthroscopic surgery on Oct. 29.

Blazers Have to Force LaMelo Ball Into Poor Shots

One thing about Ball he is going to shoot and shoot and shoot and shoot, even if the ball isn't going in. During the loss against the Bulls, Ball shot 7 of 25 from the field, including 4 of 19 from downtown. His inconsistency in shooting is ultimately what cost the Wolves in a 117-106 loss.

Ball's success shooting the ball is directly tied to how well the Wolves play. In nine wins this season, Ball shoots nearly 50% from the field, including 45% from downtown. He averages 21.6 points per game, and the team's offensive rating is close to 125.

Those numbers take a steep decline if the Wolves are on the losing end of an outcome. In losses this season, Ball is shooting under 30 percent and just under 19 percent from downtown. He only averages about 12 points per game, and the team's offensive rating is close to 78, which is nearly 50 points lower than during wins.

This makes Portland's job pretty simple: keep a strong defender on Ball throughout the game, and the rest will follow. This likely means Toumani Camara will be assigned to defend Ball at least for part of the game. Camara is Portland's best stopper, and having him defend Ball is a tactic that could lead to the Blazers' win and keep the winning streak going.

The tip-off between the Blazers and Wolves is scheduled for 5 pm PT inside the Target Center in Minneapolis. Fans can watch the game on Rip City Sports Network or stream it on NBA League Pass.
