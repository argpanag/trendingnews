PREVIEW: Oilers at Lightning

TAMPA, FL – An electric feeling surrounds the Edmonton Oilers as they prepare to put their road-warrior mentality and current six-game winning streak on the line Thursday night at Benchmark International Arena against the Tampa Bay Lightning.

Given how they're playing, the Oilers have every right to feel confident heading into an arena where they've had their fair share of struggles against the Lightning over the years, winning only once on their last 13 visits (1-10-2) dating back to 2011-12.

The Oilers stretched their streak to six straight on Tuesday, improving to an NHL-best 17-3-3 overall and 10-0-1 on the road with a 5-2 win over the Panthers, where Vasily Podkolzin and Kasperi Kapanen each put in three-point performances along with Leon Draisaitl, who extended his point streak to 10 games with a goal & two assists.

The three forwards were realigned on the Oilers' second line together after the opening faceoff for the first time this season, and they picked up where they left off in last year's playoffs by scoring twice at even strength and continuing their collective dominance of their opponents, having outscored opposition 25-8 for a 74 percent goal share when on the ice together.

Edmonton went 2-for-4 on the power play and have scored with the man advantage in 14 of their last 16 games, sitting with the second-best PP in the NHL at 35.1 percent behind the Anaheim Ducks (36.0 percent).

Not flying under the radar, Connor McDavid added a goal & an assist to extend his historic season-opening point streak to 23 games, totalling 56 points (13G, 42A) to match Wayne Gretzky (1988-89) for the third-longest such streak in NHL history. His power-play goal in the opening frame moved him past The Great One for the most power-play points in Oilers franchise history, and he's showing no signs of stopping with an 11-point lead on Draisaitl for the League's scoring lead.

The Oilers win their sixth straight win over the Panthers on Tuesday

McDavid can move into sole possession of fourth on the all-time season-opening point streak leaderboard with a point against the Lightning on Tuesday and is on pace for 200 points, joining Wayne Gretzky and Mario Lemieux as only players to be on pace for that mark beyond the quarter mark of their season.

Devon Levi had 20 saves to win his sixth straight start, improving to 10-1-0 to set a new franchise record for the fewest games (11) to reach 10 wins, while having a 2.44 GAA, .907 save percentage and one shutout this season. The 24-year-old was on track for his second career shutout, but Matthew Tkachuk added two late third-period goals as consolation before Draisaitl added an empty-netter for his 21st goal in 23 games.

Despite their red-hot form, it's never been easy in West Florida for the Oilers.

Edmonton fell 2-1 in a tough overtime decision almost a year ago before the Lightning took the season series with a 5-2 win at Rogers Place later in the season on Mar. 23, so the challenge will be great for them, but not one they're going to be shy about when they meet for the first of two meetings.

The Lightning earned their own 5-2 win over the Capitals on Tuesday, powered by former Oilers prospect Sam O'Reilly's first NHL goal. The 20-year-old O'Reilly has a goal & assist in his first four games for the Lightning, who are 14-5-2 this season and 6-2-0 during the month of November.

On the other side of the experience spectrum, Nikita Kucherov had two assists in his 900th career NHL game and leads the Lightning with eight goals and 22 assists in 22 games this season. The Russian has 16 points (5G, 11A) over an eight-game point streak against the Oilers.

Something will have to give on Tuesday, as the Oilers' best road record in the NHL at 10-0-1 will go head-to-head with the Lightning's best home record this season at 9-1-0. Tampa Bay is also 11-0-1 when scoring first for a points percentage of .958, which is better than the Oilers, who are 15-0-2 with a .941 this season.

BY THE NUMBERS

  • The Oilers can tie the 2023-24 Kings (lost to Edmonton in the first round) and the 2012-13 Blackhawks for the second-longest road point streak to begin a season if they can come away with a win on Thursday.
  • Edmonton has the NHL's top three players when it comes to plus-minus in Evan Bouchard (+32), Connor McDavid (+29), Mattias Ekholm (+29) and Leon Draisaitl (+25). The last team that had top-four players in plus/minus, including ties, was the 2013-14 Bruins (Krejci, Bergeron, Marchand, Iginla), and the last team that had top 4 players outright was the 2011-12 Bruins, who had the top five (Bergeron, Seguin, Chara, Kelly, Marchand) 
  • Evan Bouchard can tie Paul Coffey for the NHL record for the longest season-opening road point streak by a defenceman with a point against the Lightning. The defenceman has five points (1G, 4A) over their past six meetings.
  • Connor McDavid has recorded a multi-point effort in 6 of his last 11 games vs. the Lightning (8G, 7A) and has five points (3G, 2A) over his last five games against them. He scored a career-high four goals versus Tampa on Feb. 5, 2018 in Edmonton.
  • Leon Draisaitl is on the 12th double-digit point streak of his career, and he will be facing only one player born outside North America with more in Nikita Kucherov (13). The German has recorded at least a point in 11 of his last 14 games against the Lightning (8G, 10A), with goals in five of the last eight meetings.
  • Ryan Nugent-Hopkins has points in seven of their last 11 meetings (2G, 9A).
  • Zach Hyman has three goals over his last seven games versus the Lightning.
  • Trent Frederic has goals in back-to-back games and four goals over his last six against them.
  • Mathieu Joseph has five points (4G, 1A) over his eight career games vs. Tampa.
