Rod Stewart confirms final tour after heart procedure and health scares

Rock legend Rod Stewart revealed 'The Final Encore Tour' after undergoing a heart procedure and a string of health setbacks

Fox News Flash top entertainment headlines of September 23

Fox News Flash top entertainment and celebrity headlines are here.

Rod Stewart is ready for one final encore.

The rock legend announced "The Final Encore Tour" Monday, revealing that next year will mark his last-ever tour after facing a series of health issues.

"Well, here we go… one last time!" Stewart wrote on Instagram. "Come and see me on my The Final Encore Tour for one more round of rock ’n’ roll. I’m really looking forward to it."

Rod Stewart announced Monday that his 2027 "The Final Encore Tour" will be his last-ever rock tour, sharing an emotional message thanking fans for decades of support while clarifying that he is not retiring. (Christopher Polk/Penske Media via Getty Images)

Stewart opened up about his decision to make the tour his last in an emotional video message.

"Ladies and gentlemen, after much deliberation and sadness, I have to announce next year will be my last tour ever, ever, ever," he told fans.

"I’m not retiring, but this will be the last rock and roll tour," he clarified.

"I wanna thank you for everything you’ve given me, and hopefully I’ve given you something in return," Stewart said before singing, "Please come out and see me on the tour. It’s gonna be great!"

Rod Stewart's announcement comes after a series of health setbacks, including battles with strep throat, COVID-19, the flu, laryngitis and a coronary stent procedure that forced him to cancel the remainder of his 2026 tour. (Kevin Mazur/Getty Images for FIREAID)

In the comments, fans said they were "heartbroken" and thanked Stewart for his music.

"Sir Rod, After 53 years and nearly 300 shows across four continents, I am heartbroken that I just have one more year of your AMAZING live shows. Can’t wait to see ALL of them. Thank you for a lifetime of music that created incredible moments and memories. You live will be missed," one fan wrote.

"You have given me so much joy, a real good reason to travel the world and the chance to met wonderful people (some of whom I can call my friends now). Gratitude, Respect and Love forever," another wrote.

Another fan added, "Love you Sir Rod!! Thank you for all the Rock N Roll you given all of us!!" 

"You have given us so much there are no words. Wishing you all the very best," one fan wrote.

Stewart has faced a string of health setbacks in recent years.

He has canceled shows after battling strep throat, COVID-19 and the flu.

His health troubles continued in 2026, when a sinus infection and an acute upper respiratory infection that led to laryngitis forced additional cancellations. 

In August, Stewart canceled the remaining dates of his "One Last Time Tour" after undergoing a coronary stent procedure.

"Doctors are pleased with his recovery, and Rod is doing very well and has returned to his normal daily activities," Stewart's team shared on Instagram at the time. "On the advice of his doctors, he will take the next four weeks to recuperate and regain full fitness before returning to the stage."

Rod Stewart's wife, Penny Lancaster, recently revealed that doctors ordered the singer to stop touring for four weeks because of the intense physical demands of his performances as he recovered from heart surgery. (Denise Truscello/Getty Images for Wolfie's Whisky/The Barbershop Las Vegas)

Stewart's wife, Penny Lancaster, later explained why doctors recommended that the singer take a break from touring to recover from his heart procedure, describing his intense routine.

"So, he’s on the cycling machine warming his legs up, he’s warming his voice up, he’s getting prepared with his outfit, it’s like pumping his chest, you know? And he comes out the dressing room, and it’s that walk down the corridor to his stage," she said during an appearance on the "At Home with Vanessa" podcast. "And once he gets onstage, he doesn’t just walk on, like he runs on. And the first song, it’s like go, go, go. And he does that for like an hour and a half, two hours."

Lancaster said Stewart's demanding routine was why doctors wanted him to give his body time to heal.

"So of course, [the] doctor’s recommendation was not touring, not doing those shows for four weeks to have a full recovery so that he is fit to go back. But he’s already back in the gym," she shared on the podcast.

Lancaster admitted that keeping the energetic rocker from overdoing it had become a team effort.

"Between me and my brother, his trainer, and doctors and everyone, you know, we’re not letting him get away with it ... he has to do as he’s told," she revealed about the singer's recovery.
