Ian Huntley 'beaten to death at Frankland jail in cold blood'

TheTools · 24.11.2026 17:13 · source · permalink

Soham double child-murderer Ian Huntley (pictured in 2002) was killed at HMP Frankland in February 2026

  • Published
    23 November 2026

A triple murderer beat Soham child-killer Ian Huntley to death with a metal bar in a "cold-blooded" attack at the jail where they were both serving life sentences, a jury has heard.

Anthony Russell, 44, denies murdering Huntley, 52, in a recycling workshop at the high security Frankland Prison, near Durham, in February, but admits manslaughter.

Opening the case at Newcastle Crown Court, prosecutor Matthew Bean KC said Huntley would be "known to many if not all" the jurors as the murderer of Holly Wells and Jessica Chapman in Soham, Cambridgeshire, in 2002.

Russell, who killed three people in 2020 and was an inmate on A-wing with Huntley, planned the attack weeks earlier, the court was told.

Shortly after 09:00 GMT on 26 February, Russell "murdered Ian Huntley by striking him repeatedly to the head with a metal bar", Bean said.

"The prosecution case is this was a cold-blooded murder that had been planned weeks in advance of its execution."

Anthony Russell and Ian Huntley were both inmates at HMP Frankland in Durham

Jurors were told Huntley's offences "aroused feelings of disgust and anger" and prosecutors were not asking for sympathy for him.

Bean said: "Ultimately, this case is not about the appalling actions of Ian Huntley during his life but the manner of his death."

Prison records showed there had been previous tension between the two murderers and, in March 2025, officers had separated the pair as they argued and threatened one another, Bean said.

"They continued to make threats towards one another as they were taken back to the wing," the prosecutor said, but both men were later spoken to and "agreed nothing further would come of it".

Anthony Russell is on trial at Newcastle Crown Court

Russell was seen with a black eye at the beginning of 2026, the court heard.

He claimed he had injured himself falling over but staff believed he had been assaulted, with some evidence indicating Huntley was the perpetrator, Bean said.

Then, on 26 February, the prison workshop reopened after several months out of action, with both men among a cohort of 15 or so inmates going back to work there.

The atmosphere was "happy", Bean said, but moments later a "commotion" broke out.

Russell, who had been told to mop the floor, attacked Huntley shortly after prisoners were issued with tools and clothing, the court heard.

Bean said there had been no raised voices and "no suggestion of any tension" before the attack.

'Nothing to lose'

A prison officer and civilian worker ran over and saw Russell hit Huntley, who was lying on the ground, at least six times in the head with a 9lb (4kg) 4ft-long (1.2m) metal bar, the court was told.

The attack was "brutal" and those who saw it were of the view Russell was "out to intentionally kill" Huntley, who was left lying unconscious in a pool of blood, the court heard.

Russell told prison officers Huntley had been "pushing my buttons" and "winding us up", the court heard, with the Soham child-killer described as "arrogant" and "cocky".

"Hopefully I've killed him", Russell told guards, adding he had "smacked" Huntley six or seven times with a bar and "caved" his head in.

The court heard Russell told the prison's governor he was "sick" of Huntley, adding: "When he sees weakness in people he tries to bully them, and he has been getting in my face the last few weeks."

He said he had deliberately chosen the bar, one of several used for loading pallets, and picked his moment when no guards were looking before he "wrapped it round [Huntley's] head", the court heard.

As a "natural lifer" who was serving his sentence without any chance of parole, Russell said he had "nothing to lose" and was "not getting out" anyway, with a "little fight" not worth losing the privileges he had earned in prison for.

"So I thought 'I'll kill him if I can'," the court heard Russell had told police, with Bean saying the plan was "driven by nothing more" than Russell's "personal sense of grievance" towards Huntley arising out of "wholly trivial incidents".

Huntley was taken to Newcastle's Royal Victoria Infirmary, where he was found to have multiple skull fractures and brain injuries from which he died at 08:46 on 7 March, the court heard.

'No emotion or prejudice'

Bean said Russell had admitted manslaughter on the grounds of diminished responsibility, with the killer claiming he was hearing voices and under the influence of childhood post-traumatic stress disorder when he attacked Huntley.

But the prosecutor said Russell had not mentioned any symptoms in the weeks before the attack and his account was "wholly different" to what he told prison staff and police in the aftermath.

Jurors heard the room had previously been a kitchen and there was no CCTV inside, but it had an office from where prison guards monitored inmates.

Judge Mr Justice Hilliard KC told jurors the trial "must be a fair one" and "not driven by emotion or prejudice".

He said he could "quite understand" if the jurors' "first inclination" was to look up details of those involved, but they must not.

The trial is listed to last until 11 December, but the judge said all parties hoped it would be concluded "well before" then.

The trial continues.
