TROY, Ohio (WKEF) — Testimony resumed on Tuesday, marking day four of the Caleb Flynn murder trial.

40-year-old Flynn is accused of shooting and killing his wife, Ashley, in their Tipp City home in February.

Family members, including Ashley Flynn’s parents, sister, and brother-in-law, all testified about their relationship with Caleb Flynn and the days leading up to and following Ashley’s death.

It was an emotional day in court as jurors heard from each family member. Ashley's mother, Jill Smith, said she had a wonderful relationship with her daughter.

Smith said the last time she saw Ashley was February 13, when the family went to lunch. The following night, Ashley was preparing a “Galentine’s” celebration for her daughters, doing facials, painting nails, and making special food. Prosecutors showed jurors a photo of Ashley with the girls from that night.

"We were best friends. We talked every day, encouraged each other every day, and her whole life, we just had a wonderful relationship. She was an excellent daughter," said Smith.

Smith later testified about the moment she learned her daughter was gone. Smith described receiving a phone call from Caleb around 3 a.m. February 16. She testified Caleb told her, “She’s gone,” and said someone had broken in and murdered Ashley. When Smith arrived at the house, she saw police lights and later hugged Caleb outside.

Prosecutors also asked family members about the family business and inheritance, including what Ashley stood to receive and what Caleb could have been in line for.

Prosecutors used Kayla Keyt, Ashley Flynn’s sister, testimony to establish a possible financial motive. She said Ashley and Kayla were expected to become equal owners of the family business when their father retired.

Keyt testified that Caleb was present for discussions about that plan and saw projections showing he could potentially earn $300,000 to $400,000 a year if business sales continued growing.

The defense also questioned family members about what Ashley’s two daughters may have known about Caleb’s affair and the police investigation.

On cross-examination, the defense emphasized that the family initially believed Caleb was innocent. Keyt agreed she told investigators they had arrested the wrong person before she learned Caleb had allegedly been having an affair. She said learning of the alleged affair shocked and devastated her.

“I remember hearing rumors that there was an affair, and I was so mad because it was tarnishing his reputation,” Keyt said

Keyt said she did not learn about the alleged affair until after Caleb’s arrest, when she was interviewed by the FBI.

Keyt was also questioned by the defense on Caleb's liaison with a woman who led worship at the Flynn's church.

Alleigha Botner was identified as the woman who sang with Caleb at the church. According to Keyt, the family knew the other woman from Christian Life Center.

Jurors later heard from Captain Tony Smith with Tipp City Police, who testified that Botner’s cell phone and laptop went missing while she was away on a trip in January of 2026.

Ashley's father, Todd Smith, told the court he was on his way to a ski trip in Canada when he learned of his daughter's death, recalling the phone call he received from his wife.

"I told my buddies, I said, ' You guys, my daughter has been murdered in her house and they said, ' There's no way that had to be a nightmare. Call Jill back and see if it's the truth. So I called her right back. Babe, was I dreaming? And she said, " No, you weren't," said Smith.

Testimony continued in the afternoon, hearing from a special agent with ATF and forensic scientists with BCI.

The trial is expected to continue for all of this week.
