Representatives Visit Alliance on Campaign Stop - Alliance Times-Herald

TheTools · 04.11.2026 07:06 · source · permalink

The Alliance Airport played host to several legislators last Thursday morning, as they made their way across Nebraska encouraging citizens to vote on November 3.

Senator Pete Ricketts, Congressman Adrian Smith, Congressman Mike Flood, Treasurer Joey Spellerberg and State Senator Paul Strommen were in Alliance on October 29 for a final push for yesterday’s vote.

Senator Pete Ricketts and Congressman Adrian Smith gave an update to current happenings within the Senate and Congress.

Ricketts stated that the accomplishment that he is most proud of in the Senate is the Working Families Tax Cut.

“The biggest thing that we passed that is going to be generational and impact is a working families tax cut. Not only do we avoid a $2,400 tax increase on your average Nebraska family, but we were able to add in things like no tax on tips, no tax on overtime. We made it so that 88% of seniors aren’t paying federal income tax, just the way like when I was Governor, we got rid of the state income tax on Social Security. In addition, we increased the child tax credit and made that permanent. We made the death penalty or the death tax exemption permanent, so that we can make sure we pass family farms and ranches on to the next generation, and we have many other things to be able to help businesses invest. Really a lot of great things in that bill that are just going to impact so many families across the state,” said Ricketts.

Ricketts noted that the most pressing issues he is currently focusing on are the Farm Bill, the National Defense Authorization Act and the Match Act.

“Right now, the most pressing things we have to get done is get the Farm Bill passed. Right now, it’s got E15 in it all year round. That’s going to be a huge deal for our corn farmers to be able to generate demand for ethanol. So that’s a big deal. We’ve got to get the Farm Bill passed, and then we also have to focus on getting the National Defense Authorization Act passed. This is how we defend our military, and in that, I’ve got my Match Act, which is a bipartisan bill to prevent Communist China from buying high-end semiconductor manufacturing equipment, so that we can stay in the lead as far as technology in front of Communist China,” stated Ricketts.

While traveling through Nebraska, Senator Ricketts has met with many constituents and has heard some of their most prevalent issues.

“The biggest thing people talk about generally is cost of living. Again, this is why it’s so important that we pass the Working Families tax cut to be able to put more money back in people’s pockets, whether it was seniors, people working with tips, overtime, all that, helping put more money back in there, and then also what we did with passing the Road to Housing Act, which will help make housing less expensive as well, and then we’re going to continue to look for ways to be able to bring down costs, like for example, bringing more fertilizer plants to the United States, getting more refineries here to bring down the cost of gas and diesel. Those are some of the things we can do to help impact the prices for everybody,” said Ricketts.

Smith stated that the accomplishment he is most proud of in Congress is the ethanol 15% sale year-round.

“Most recently, it’s the ethanol 15%, to allow that sale year-round. We got that through the House, a very bipartisan bill. I worked with my colleagues on both sides to get that passed. I know our Senators, both Ricketts and Fischer, are working to get E15 through the Senate. Looks like right now there’s little different language, but ultimately we need to get any language to the President. I think he’s eager to sign that. That would deliver relief at the pump. Also, you know, obviously work on tax issues and helping victims of the wildfires and drought to get the assistance that they need as well,” said Smith.

Smith stated the two most pressing issues he is currently working on are input costs for agriculture and the price of oil.

“Right now the cost of inputs on agriculture are especially challenging, and this has been some time. You know, when Russia invaded Ukraine, that was a big hit on input costs for agriculture. So there’s a lot of fronts to that, and so we need to address those just everywhere we can. Trade has a lot to do with that. That’s important. Obviously, the price of oil that has been very volatile lately. We want to develop more energy here at home, and E15, for example, is on strategy for that,” said Smith.

Nebraskans are voicing their concerns to Smith while he travels throughout the state. The most prevalent issues being agriculture input costs and Nebraskans who want to be left alone.

“One, back to the agriculture input costs; those concerns get mentioned a lot. Also, as it relates to the Federal Government, most Nebraskans in the Third District want to be left alone to do their thing. And you know, there’s a concern about the government forcing things on folks that it’s unwelcome. It’s oftentimes unnecessary. But you know that a lot of ag producers they see the Farm Bill as something they may need some time. They know that it needs to get passed so that we can have some stable policy as well. That’s some feedback I get,” said Smith.

At the event, representatives spoke about getting out to vote and then met with citizens afterwards.
