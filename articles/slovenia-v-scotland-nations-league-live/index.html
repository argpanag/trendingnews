Sebastien Pocognoli: “We had a good camp, we reached all the aspects that we wanted to reach on the pitch but also off the pitch,” said Scotland’s head coach during his press conference at the Stozice Arena.

“Regarding the first game for Scotland, I’m excited because we want to see what we worked on this week. I’m very curious to see if we can produce this. I hope we are well balanced, well focused and well concentrated in the good moment, but also in the challenging moments.

“The mental aspect to have a first game with a new gaffer, with new ideas, it’s a consideration, the mental aspect. We try to plan the games on the physical aspect, the tactical aspect and on the performance aspect. These will be the last details.”

After today’s game against Slovenia, Scotland are scheduled to play Switzerland at Hampden Park on Tuesday. Following that, they’ll face North Macedonia next Saturday before wrapping up the lengthy internatinoal break with a home game against today’s opponents the following Tuesday.

Andy Robertson: This isn’t the Scotland skippper’s first Slovenian rodeo, as both he and the home team’s head coach Bostjan Cesar were on the pitch when last these two sides met in 2017. “When the international break comes we always get excited for it,” he said yesterday. “This one’s no different and we look forward to the challenge ahead that we’ve got. It’s been enjoyable. We’ve had a good week. Obviously, the manager’s trying to get his ideas across.

“It’s a really new squad, we’re trying to get to know each other, trying to make sure we keep the values that have worked well for us but also adapting to the new squad and the new characters. It’s been a really important week. I think the manager’s enjoyed it. I think the staff have enjoyed it. It’s all about preparing for the games now. We’ve got four games, which is quite unique. It’s something that we’re all looking forward to.

“We’re going to be tested. Saturday, Tuesday, Saturday, Tuesday is intense when you’re at club level. We’re not really used to that kind of schedule at international level. It’s going to be intense, but at the end of the day, footballers want to play football. I suppose it will be a learning curve for us, but one that we want to learn on the job. We still demand results and we still expect results. It’s up to us to try and produce them.”

Scotland: The national team’s unproven head coach represents a potentially shrewd gamble made by an organisation that could do without another embarrassing episode, writes Ewan Murray.

Oli McBurnie starts for Scotland

Scotland: Sebastien Pocognoli picks his first Scotland team and has handed debuts to Swansea City centre-back Stephen Welsh and Sevilla striker Robbie Ure. Oli McBurnie also gets the nod and will make his first appearance for Scotland since March 2021.

Early Slovenia team news: The headline story from the home camp is that Benjamin Sesko has withdrawn from their squad. The Manchester United striker has featured in six Manchester United games this season, mainly off the bench, but is suffering from a shin injury.

“I am very sorry that I will have to miss the next matches of the national team,” he posted on Instagram. “I have always played for Slovenia and will always play with heart and pride. The most important thing now is to fully dedicate myself to recovery. “I am already working on getting back on the field as soon as possible and helping the national team and the club again.”

Slovan Bratislava striker Andraz Sporar could lead the Slovenia line in the big man’s absence. Defenders Kenan Bajiric and Marcel Ratnik have also been forced out of the squad with injury. Goalkeeper Jan Oblak will skipper the Slovenia side and is set to earn his 85th international cap.

Early Scotland team news: After his omission from Scotland’s World Cup squad caused no end of consternation, Oli McBurnie earns the recall his early-season Premier League performances for Hull City deserve. He is joined by a host of untried talent, including Luis Binks (Brondby), Luke Graham (Stoke City), Luke Graham (Stoke City), Josh McPake (Hearts), Robbie Ure (Sevilla), Colby Donovan (Celtic), John McCracken (Bradford City) and Stephen Welsh (Swansea City).

There is no place in Pocognoli’s squad for World Cup particpants Lyndon Dykes, Ross Stewart, George Hirst, Grant Hanley, John Souttar and Anthony Ralston, while goalkeeper Craig Gordon has retired.

Scott McTominay, Che Adams, Lawrence Shankland are all unavailable through injury, while Kenny McLean and Kieran Tierney also withdrew from the squad earlier this week. Along with McBurnie, Aaron Hickey, Lewis Ferguson, Josh Doig and Kieron Bowie return to the Scotland squad after missing out on World Cup selection.

Group B1: Slovenia v Scotland

New campaign, new coach, clean slate. Scotland are in Ljubljana to play their Nations League opener under their freshly appointed head coach Sebastien Pocognoli. The former Belgium defender was appointed Steve Clarke’s replacement, following the 63-year-old’s decision to step down in the wake of Scotland’s underwhelming performances at the World Cup and says he is “excited and curious” to learn about his charges.

Pocognoli has introduced eight uncapped players to a squad from which several members of the squad that went to North America have been culled. A full fifteen of the players in the original Scotland squad for this game were not at the World Cup under Clarke. Slovenia have nevewr beaten Scotland in five previous meetings but have drawn their two most recent encounters. This is their head caoch Bostjan Cesar’s fourth game in charge and he is looking for his second win.

The last time Scotland played in Slovenia, a 2-2 draw in October 2017 ended their hopes of qualifying for Russia 2018 and prompted the then coach Gordon Strachan to question Scottish football’s DNA. They have qualified for three major tournaments since and with their qualification for Euro 2028 all but assured before a ball is kicked (caveat: this is Scotland we’re talking about), it will be down to Pocognoli to try to get his team over the historically insurmountable hurdle of getting them past the group stage. Kick-off at Stadion Stozice is at 2pm (BST) but we’ll have team news and build-up in the meantime.
