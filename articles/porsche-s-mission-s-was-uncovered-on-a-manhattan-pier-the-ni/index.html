Porsche’s Mission S Was Uncovered on a Manhattan Pier the Night Before Its Debut

TheTools · 15.10.2026 22:10 · source · permalink

A white mid-engined Porsche with a glass engine cover and a Porsche crest set into the bodywork beneath that glass was uncovered on a Manhattan pier on Wednesday evening, and a phone camera somewhere above it caught the whole thing.

The 21-second Instagram reel went up at 8:26 p.m. Eastern on October 14 as a joint post from the accounts kingofthecrest and dougswheels, location-tagged Manhattan, New York. The caption reads "Sneak peek at the Porsche Misson S !!!" (the typo is theirs) and tags Porsche and the Porsche Nashua dealership in New Hampshire. By Thursday afternoon Instagram showed about 60,000 likes.

Porsche had set the date itself. Its October 7 Capital Markets Day release says that "on 15 October, Porsche will provide a preview of a potential mid-engined super sports car platform for the future," one that would make room for a model line above the 911. The name comes from the company's Mission S preview page, which calls the car "our tribute to the sports car" and lists no specifications at all. As of 1:20 p.m. Eastern on Thursday, Porsche's newsroom had not posted the reveal, a time or a venue.

What the video shows

The clip opens on a car under a black cover in the middle of a long concrete pier, with roughly a dozen people standing around it and a lighting stand and road cases near the water's edge. The cover is off within about ten seconds. Parked at the far end of the pier is a silver open-top vintage racer with "Fletcher Aviation" lettered across its nose, which suggests whoever booked the pier was staging more than one car.

Because the camera is high and behind the car, the rear is what you get. The engine cover is glass, split lengthwise by two ribs into three panes. A round element wearing the Porsche crest sits at the base of the center section, and a thin red light strip runs across the cover's leading edge. Two buttresses run back from the cockpit into the rear fenders, a slim light band crosses the tail, and there is an opening at the front of the roof. The paint is plain white, with no livery, decals or visible badging beyond the crest.

Nothing in the footage identifies the engine. You can see a shape under the glass, not a cylinder count.

Porsche's own teasers

Porsche has been handing out pieces for a week. On October 9 it posted a clip of an analog tachometer on Instagram, captioned "Mission S. Everything starts with the driver." The dial's numerals stop at 8, and its red band begins before 7. That is a lower ceiling than Porsche's current naturally aspirated 911 GT3, which revs to 9,000 rpm, though a tach only shows what the engineers chose to print on it.

Porsche's Mission S wheel teaser. The center-lock hub is marked "FAN," "LOCK" and "LÖSEN." (Teaser image: Porsche AG)

The teaser image on the preview page that says the most is a center-lock wheel lit in red. Printed on the hub are "FAN" and "LOCK," with "LÖSEN," German for release, and an arrow running around the edge. Porsche hasn't said what the fan is or where it sits. The round, crest-bearing element under the leaked car's engine glass is the obvious place to look, but nothing Porsche has published connects the two.

What Porsche hasn't committed to

Read the October 7 release closely and every noun is hedged. It is a "preview," of a "potential" car, built on a "platform." It names no engine, no cylinder count, no hybrid system, no output, no price and no production date. The talk of a flat-eight, which we covered when the rumored flagship would end Porsche's 64-year run without a road-going eight-cylinder boxer, still has no Porsche document behind it. The closest thing to a flat-eight Porsche with plates so far has come from outside Weissach, in RUF's 1,000-hp B8.

What the release does commit to is money. The same Sportwagenschmiede '35 plan targets an average selling price for top-of-the-range models about 20 percent higher, at least one "brand-defining" new product a year by 2030, and six times the current Sonderwunsch (custom-order) sales. A car above the 911 fits all three targets. The same document also cuts development costs for future model lines by up to 20 percent.

One of the Mission S teaser images from Porsche's preview page. (Teaser image: Porsche AG)

Porsche's official preview is due today, October 15. The pier footage gives a checklist for it: the glass engine cover, the crest beneath it, and whether the "FAN" on that wheel hub turns out to be the round part under the glass. Then there is the powertrain, which no Porsche document has named yet.

Should Porsche's first car above the 911 be pure combustion, or is a hybrid the right call for a flagship meant to last into the 2030s?
