Wyndham Clark left country for much-needed golf hiatus ahead of Presidents Cup

TheTools · 24.09.2026 00:17 · source · permalink

Wyndham Clark took a much-needed break from the game of golf following his Tour Championship push, but now the U.S. Open champion is refreshed entering the Presidents Cup at Medinah.

Clark had an incredible season as he pulled his name into player of the year conversations thanks to his win at the BMW Championship against World No. 2 Rory McIlroy. He said he felt like he could be the best player in the world before heading to the season finale, where Scottie Scheffler reigned successful.

Clark finished tied for 21st at East Lake, and he was ready for the PGA Tour season to be over after competing in 23 events.

“Right after East Lake, I went to Cabo with friends,” said Clark ahead of the Presidents Cup. “There was a country music festival. That was a lot of fun. I was kind of waiting for that after the BMW, I really was kind of getting a little sick and tired of golf. It's the end of the season. So I was just looking forward to Mexico.

“Then I've been home in Colorado enjoying the good weather there, played in a pro-member that Peyton puts on called the Smith-Cole at Cherry Hills. So that was a lot of fun. And now we're here.”

Three wins during the 2026 season, a relationship hard launch and six top 10 finishes meant it was time for a break, but now the 32-year-old is back playing team golf alongside the world's best and a 21-year-old rookie.

“I feel like I'm adaptable,” said Clark. “I like team sports. I played team sports growing up. I just think it's so much fun.

“I want this amount of time to do this, but you make sacrifices for the team. Whatever's best for the team is what you want to do.

“There's never more fun in golf than doing what we do this week and at the Ryder Cup because it's something -- you know, it's something that's not really in golf. We all have a lot of fun. Normally we're trying to beat each other's heads in, and now we're good buddies trying to root each other on and for one goal, to win the cup.”

Looking ahead to the upcoming competition in Chicago, Clark is scheduled to partner with Morikawa in Thursday's opening session at Medinah. Throughout the event, he aims to bring high energy, team spirit, and patriotic enthusiasm to the squad.

“JT is probably our most passionate guy, especially during these events,” said Clark. “I think it helps. Any time you can bring the crowd and get them involved, you gain that momentum, and momentum is so big in golf.

“This is now my third team event, and I just -- I really want to have a couple of those moments that are just iconic and really bring the team together and get the crowd going. Hopefully that happens this week.”
