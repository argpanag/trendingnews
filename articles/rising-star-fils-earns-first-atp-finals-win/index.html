Rising star Fils earns first ATP Finals win

TheTools · 17.11.2026 21:40 · source · permalink

Rising star Arthur Fils claimed his first career win at the ATP Finals by battling to a comeback victory over Rafael Jodar to remain in contention in Turin.

Fils, 22, beat 20-year-old Jodar 4-6 6-2 6-2 to become the first Frenchman to record an ATP Finals victory since Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in 2011.

It was the youngest match by combined age at the season-ending tournament - which features the eight top-performing men - since a 21-year-old Novak Djokovic defeated a 20-year-old Juan Martin del Potro in 2008.

Fils has risen to a career-high ranking of seventh in the world during a season in which he became the youngest French ATP Masters 1000 champion in Cincinnati.

All four debutants at this year's tournament lost their opening match, with Fils beaten by American third seed Ben Shelton and Spain's Jodar losing to Alexander Zverev in the John McEnroe Group.

But Fils, a finalist at the Next Gen ATP Finals three years ago, will fight for a place in the semi-finals against world number one Zverev on Thursday after keeping alive his hopes of reaching the knockout stage on his debut.

"It's going to be tough but I know that I can battle. I'm not afraid," said Fils of his showdown with Zverev, who plays Shelton later on Tuesday.

"It will be a nice match against the world number one. I don't have anything to lose, I'm just going to enjoy myself."

Fils was second best in the first set but gradually found his rhythm to outperform a fading Jodar over sets two and three.

Jodar made an ideal start, striking at the perfect moment when he broke for the first time to clinch the opening set after 10 games.

Fils particularly struggled on serve, landing just 38% of first serves in, and produced just a single winner in a flat first set.

But the Frenchman reset impressively in the second, twice breaking Jodar's serve on his way to forcing a decider.

Jodar appeared to be fading physically and took a medical timeout after falling a double break behind in the second set.

The Spaniard was unable to rediscover his previous level, however, and Fils powered to victory by winning six of the final seven games.

"It means a lot to me after a tough start," Fils said.

"I played a great game against Ben [Shelton], I was just unlucky. The first set here was frustrating and I was struggling - but I'm very happy with the second and third."

In the men's doubles, Britain's Henry Patten and Finnish team-mate Harri Heliovaara fell to defeat in their second match.

The world number ones and defending champions won the first set but were beaten 6-7 (5-7) 6-3 10-4 by fourth seeds Marcelo Arevalo and Mate Pavic.
