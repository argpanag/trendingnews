Melanie Lynskey addressed a few things about her time on Two and a Half Men, including what she was actually being paid, because apparently the internet completely inflated the real amount.

The Yellowjackets star recently set the record straight after an Instagram user claimed she was earning between $200,000 and $300,000 per episode toward the end of the hit CBS sitcom's run. Except, according to Lynskey, those numbers couldn't be further from the truth.

"Hey! Sorry, but this insane number is floating around on the internet, and I must tell you how wrong it is," she wrote in response to a user on Instagram Oct. 25. "I made literally one tenth of that stated number per episode."

Melanie Lynskey at The Actors Night hosted by The Actor Awards & ELLE held at Chateau Marmont on December 11, 2025 in Los Angeles, California. (Photo by Michael Buckner/Variety via Getty Images)

Lynskey explained that she actually started out making $20,000 per episode, with what she believes was a 5% raise each season. Her biggest paycheck? Approximately $43,000 for an appearance during the final season. She appeared on the show from 2003 to 2015.

"But more than half of the episodes I did were in the first two seasons," she added. "The highest paycheck I got on the show was, I think, $43,000 for one episode in the final season. This is before agents, managers, business managers' cuts, and taxes, so you take home around 50% of what you are paid."

"There is this insane idea that I made millions of dollars, and it's so ridiculous. I was extremely grateful for the job and for the money," Lynskey, who was just 25 at the time she was hired, explained. "It allowed me to pay my small mortgage, pay bills, and be able to work in indie movies and build a career. It wasn't my show, and I felt fine with what I was paid," she noted. "I just get annoyed when it's misreported."

The conversation came amid renewed attention surrounding fellow Two and a Half Men star Holland Taylor, who recently revealed that she declined to renew her contract after six years because she felt she wasn't being fairly compensated. Taylor, who played Evelyn Harper, said she received four Emmy nominations for her work on the series without ever getting a raise.

While Lynskey stressed that she was comfortable with her own compensation, she made it clear that she believed Taylor deserved better.

"If Holland's talking about it, I'll talk about it too," Lynskey said. "Also, Holland is and was a legend and a queen and deserved many raises!!!!"
