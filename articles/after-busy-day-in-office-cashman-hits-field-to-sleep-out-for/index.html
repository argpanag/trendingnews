After busy day in office, Cashman hits field to Sleep Out for good cause

TheTools · 20.11.2026 11:04 · source · permalink

NEW YORK -- Even as Brian Cashman prepared to spend a mostly sleepless Thursday evening on Yankee Stadium’s damp turf, the Yankees' general manager expected there’d be plenty to keep him busy.

Arriving on the field for Covenant House’s Sleep Out event, Cashman said he had just completed a two-hour Zoom call with an agent. His office remained open, as the Yankees’ baseball operations staff continues to pursue every available avenue to upgrade their roster.

“I would describe it as drinking out of a fire hose right now,” Cashman said. “There’s engagement with my opposing general managers with other teams to see if there’s any trade matches, and full throttle on player-agent engagement. We’re doing everything we can to try to improve our team.”

At last week’s General Managers Meetings in Las Vegas, Cashman said it would be his “main priority” to address the bullpen. They have made progress, with closer David Bednar accepting a $23.15 million qualifying offer on Tuesday.

“I can tell you, David Bednar wants to be here,” Cashman said. “He loved his time with the Yankees and his experience here. That’s not going to be a surprise to anybody. We knew David wanted to be here, and so I was hoping he would take it. Ultimately, he did.”

Left-hander Tim Hill also agreed to a one-year, $2.25 million deal that includes a $2.5 million club option for 2028, a deal that has not yet been formally announced. A potential Paul Blackburn reunion is still possible, and Cashman indicated he remains on the hunt for additional relievers.

“Free agency hit that area hard,” Cashman said. “We pride ourselves in having a very strong bullpen, and obviously with our great starting pitching, you want to be able to finish it off.”

Managing general partner Hal Steinbrenner pointed to offensive underperformance as a key factor in the club’s elimination in the American League Division Series, and Cashman indicated there are several areas where the club may upgrade before Opening Day.

Most candidly, Cashman suggested the team is considering all possibilities at third base, where Ryan McMahon is owed $16 million next year. McMahon turns 32 in December and is coming off a disappointing season in which he batted .213 with a .636 OPS in 124 games.

“We’re having those conversations,” Cashman said. “Ryan McMahon, I’m sure he’d be the first to tell you he’s a better player than what we saw last year, and that’s true of a number of different guys that we have. We’ll assess the opportunities that are available out there.”

Though published reports have connected the Yankees to free agents Luis Arraez and Brandon Lowe, they haven’t closed the door on re-signing Jazz Chisholm Jr. Still, Cashman said he could envision a spring competition between Anthony Volpe and Jose Caballero at second base.

In the outfield, Cashman has stopped short of anointing Spencer Jones as the starting center fielder, noting that he profiles better defensively in a corner spot. With Cody Bellinger and Aaron Judge set to return in left field and right field, respectively, the Yankees are weighing choices, while remaining encouraged by Jones’ progress.

“It makes no sense to guarantee anybody anything right now,” Cashman said. “I know that in 2025, for instance, Spencer Jones had a lot of work to do and ground to cover. In 2026, he covered all that ground and came in and took a position. So, good for him.”

Unprompted, Cashman brought up Jasson Domínguez's performance in the Dominican Winter League, where “The Martian” has been hitting well for the Águilas Cibaeñas. Domínguez's performance has prompted interest around the league, Cashman suggested.

“I think the entire industry is noticing, based on the phone calls I’m getting,” Cashman said.

Domínguez has struggled defensively in the Majors. In September, he was optioned to the Minors after his second misplay in three games, and did not return. Asked if he would consider Domínguez an option in center field, Cashman replied: “I’m not ruling anything out.”

This marks the 15th year that Cashman has participated in the Sleep Out. He is a board member at Covenant House, a nonprofit charity that provides housing, support services and advocacy for youth facing homelessness.

“Brian is a real champion for Covenant House,” said Bill Bedrossian, president & CEO of Covenant House International. “Sleeping out every year demonstrates his passion, but he’s active behind the scenes, too. He’s deeply involved. Tonight is a culmination of what he’s been doing for the last 15 years, and a public display of how much the Yankees support Covenant House and care about young people.”

More than 500 participants took part in Thursday's event at Yankee Stadium, the first at the ballpark since 2019. Cashman said Thursday's effort should raise close to $3 million, and he thanked the Yankees and the Steinbrenner family for their continued support.

“This is probably going to be the most impactful day at Yankee Stadium in the entire season,” Cashman said. “A lot of good stuff happens here, but this is going to be the best day of the year.”
