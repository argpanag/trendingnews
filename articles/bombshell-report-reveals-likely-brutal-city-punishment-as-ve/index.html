Bombshell report reveals likely brutal City punishment as verdict on ‘doomsday’ scenario emerges

TheTools · 20.10.2026 04:05 · source · permalink

Manchester City could be set for a multi-year stay in the Championship as the Premier League prepares to hand down punishments for the cheating scandal, according to a shock new report.

The English powerhouse has been bracing for severe sanctions since news broke in September that they had been found guilty of over 100 severe financial rules breaches from 2009 to 2018, including artificially inflating their revenue by more than $1 billion with “sham” contracts.

The club won eight trophies, including three Premier League titles, during the cheating period, with the guilty verdict sparking a torrent of outrage across the league and around the sporting world.

Punishment calls have ranged from enormous points deductions across multiple years, relegation to the Championship, long-term transfer bans and forcing the controversial City Football Group to sell the club.

However, City, who have made a stunning seven-win start to the season despite dealing with the scandal, have consistently denied all wrongdoing, and have launched an appeal challenging the verdict.

A report from The Daily Mail‘s Oliver Holt, however, claims the Premier League’s sanctions announcement is imminent, with the league keen to reveal their decision before the result of City’s appeal, which is expected by no later than January next year.

The report claims the likely punishment will be a “swingeing points deduction” for the season that will make City’s relegation to the Championship a certainty, with the possibility of another points deduction for the 2027/28 season to force them to remain in the second tier for at least another season.

However, the EFL would have to approve the latter penalty for it to take effect.

City would also face a substantial fine.

However, Holt said a “doomsday scenario” of expulsion from the Premier League to the fourth-tier League Two is unlikely.

“There is very little point in pursuing a Treaty of Versailles-type punishment,” Holt wrote.

“The desire to humiliate the defeated might satisfy a base instinct but it does very little for the future wellbeing of the body politic.

“City must be punished, of course, but they must also be given a pathway towards rehabilitation.”

Holt also suggested the club’s owners would be untenable in the event of their appeal failing, which may prove to have more dire consequences for City than mere relegation.

“It is hard to see how a regime found guilty of such egregious deception could possibly be allowed to remain as custodians of the club,” he wrote.

“But then how do City survive without the Abu Dhabi United Group? How do they survive without the hundreds of millions in revenue that City now insist came directly from the Abu Dhabi government?

“Would another buyer step forward to rescue a club whose image has been so badly tainted?”

In contrast, talkSPORT host and former Arsenal defender Martin Keown has claimed that the punishment could be financial only.

“I’m hearing rumours that this will be all over in a few weeks and it won’t be nearly as bad as we think it might be,” he said.

“Maybe they get off with a big fine and we return to the football pitch.”
