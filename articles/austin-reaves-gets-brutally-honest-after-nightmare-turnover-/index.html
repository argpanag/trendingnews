Austin Reaves took full responsibility after committing eight turnovers in the Lakers' 112-95 loss to the Warriors, calling himself "terrible again" and saying the second unit was "pretty terrible" while refusing to use unfamiliar lineups as an excuse.

Austin Reaves took full responsibility for his turnover-heavy performance after the Los Angeles Lakers suffered a 112-95 loss to the Golden State Warriors on Friday night.

Reaves finished with 21 points, four rebounds, and five assists over 37 minutes, but committed a game-high eight turnovers as Los Angeles gave the ball away 22 times overall. Golden State turned those mistakes into 27 points.

Through the first six games of the season, his turnover average now sits at 4.8 per contest.

Asked afterward about his eight lost balls against the Warriors, Reaves kept his assessment extremely simple.

"I was terrible again," Reaves said. "That's it."

The Lakers opened the game with a dominant 38-15 first quarter, but Golden State completely erased the advantage by winning the second period 38-15 itself, sending the teams into halftime tied.

Reaves was particularly critical of the group that opened the second quarter and immediately pointed the finger at himself.

"Just bad. We were bad," Reaves said. "That first group to start the second unit was bad. Like I said, that starts with me, so I'll be better."

The Lakers have been forced to use combinations that have spent limited time together, but Reaves refused to lean on that as justification for the team's struggles.

"Yeah, I mean, it's real, but it doesn't give that second unit an excuse to be pretty terrible," Reaves said. "And that starts with me. I'll watch the film and try to be better."

"But you've got to learn on the fly and pick up things quickly. As I said, I have to be better."

The turnovers proved particularly costly against Golden State. The Warriors overcame a 25-point deficit and eventually pulled away behind Stephen Curry, who scored 31 points and knocked down eight three-pointers.

Lakers coach JJ Redick, meanwhile, took a jab at the referees after the game.
