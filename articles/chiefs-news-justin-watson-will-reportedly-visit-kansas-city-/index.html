The Chiefs need wide receiver help. A familiar face is visiting Monday: Source | The Kansas City Star

Wide receiver Justin Watson will visit the Chiefs on Monday, a source told The Star.

Watson, 30, entered the league as a fifth-round pick of the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in the 2018 NFL Draft. The 6-foot-2, 215-pound receiver beat the Chiefs in Super Bowl LV along with Tom Brady. Watson signed with the Chiefs in 2022 and stayed with the club through 2024. Over that span, he appeared in 50 regular-season games, compiling 64 receptions for 1,064 yards and seven touchdowns.

4 Chiefs Questions That Will Define the Rest of the Season | Heavy Sports

Who Is Going to Be the Chiefs’ Big-Play WR?

Tyquan Thornton being out for most, if not the remainder of the season is a big blow to the Chiefs’ offense. Thornton has been its only legitimate downfield threat that can beat man-to-man coverage, averaging 19.9 yards per catch. The rest of Kansas City’s receiving corps is made up of short-to-intermediate route runners who play better against zone coverage.

The only other receiver the Chiefs have who possesses top-end speed is Xavier Worthy. However, Worthy has been inconsistent so far this season, and hasn’t been the deep threat that he was expected to be when he was drafted back in 2024. If nobody can step up, Kansas City may have to acquire someone via trade or free agency.

Chiefs Cornerback Nohl Williams Escapes Penalty for Forceful Hit | Athlon Sports

The 24-year-old was flagged at the 5:30 mark of the second quarter. Williams was penalized for a forceful, high hit on Nailor, who was assessed for a concussion and had to miss the remainder of the game.

The NFL reviewed the incident and decided not to fine Williams. For context, a hit on a defenseless player would have carried a $17,911 fine for a first offense. Alternatively, a fine of $23,882 is issued for impermissible use of the helmet/launching, under the 2026 NFL fine schedule.

Marvin Harrison Jr. trade: Cardinals send former top-5 pick to Texans for multiple draft choices | CBS Sports

The trade was announced during the Cardinals’ game against the Detroit Lions, a game in which Harrison was inactive due to the pending trade. Rumors swirled over the weekend that a trade could be imminent, and now Harrison will get a fresh start after his time with the Cardinals didn’t go as expected.

Texans quarterback and Harrison’s former Ohio State teammate C.J. Stroud was asked about the trade after his game.

“You want to know more? I don’t know what more I can tell you,” Stroud said with a smile. “I think he’s a great guy, think he’s a great player, and I’m excited to get him here.”
