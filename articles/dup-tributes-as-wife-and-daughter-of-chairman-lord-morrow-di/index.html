DUP: Tributes as wife and daughter of chairman Lord Morrow die hours apart

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:10 · source · permalink

Sympathies have been expressed to a senior Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) politician following the death of his wife and daughter within hours of each other.

The party said Lord Morrow, who is chairman of the DUP, lost his wife Lady Jennifer Morrow and their daughter Rachel Burrows at the weekend.

DUP leader Gavin Robinson said that "words cannot adequately express the heartbreak and sorrow felt across our party".

"There are no words that can ease the pain of such an unimaginable loss," he added.

Lord Morrow is a former Stormont minister and a former assembly member for the Fermanagh and South Tyrone constituency.

A funeral notice for his daughter Rachel said she was a married mother of two children. It said she died on Sunday "peacefully surrounded by her loving family".

Robinson added that Lord Morrow's wife and daughter died "within hours of each other" on Sunday.

"On behalf of the Democratic Unionist Party, I extend our deepest sympathy to Maurice, to Rachel's husband John, and to the entire Morrow and Burrows families at this most difficult of times," he said.

"Maurice Morrow has been a rock of strength and support to so many of us through difficult moments in our lives. Now, in his darkest hour, it is our turn to be a rock for him.

"Throughout Jennifer and Rachel's long battles, Maurice has demonstrated extraordinary love and strength."

Speaking at Stormont, Robinson said the last couple of weeks for Lord Morrow would "have been incredibly difficult".

"But to lose your daughter and know how gravely ill your wife is, and to lose your wife some four hours later yesterday, is a deep personal shock and tragedy for him, for his surviving daughter Victoria, for Rachel's husband John and their two children, and for the wider Morrow and Burrows family."

Former DUP leader Baroness Foster, who has known Lord Morrow and his family for many years, said the deaths of his wife and daughter were "completely devastating".

The former first minister described Lady Jennifer Morrow as "a lady in so many ways" and said his daughter Rachel was "such a lovely person".

"As a political spouse it's sometimes very difficult, but she [Lady Jennifer Morrow] was always very strong and supportive in a very quiet way, and always there for Maurice," she told BBC Radio Ulster's Evening Extra programme.

Baroness Foster said Lord Morrow had expressed to her how "it was the saddest time of his life".

Traditional Unionist Voice (TUV) leader Jim Allister also extended his sympathies to Lord Morrow and expressed "sincere Christian condolences".

First Minister Michelle O'Neill also expressed her sympathies to Lord Morrow and his family "at this horrendous time for them".

"Such a tragedy to be visited upon any family. I can't imagine what they're going through," the Sinn Féin vice-president told reporters at Stormont.

Northern Ireland Secretary Sir Chris Bryant offered his "profound condolences" to Lord Morrow.

North-South Ministerial Council postponed

Taoiseach (Irish prime minister) Micheál Martin offered his "deepest sympathy" to Lord Morrow and his family following their "devastating loss".

"To lose two loved ones within hours of each other is particularly heartbreaking," he said in a statement.

"We offer our thoughts and prayers to the Morrow and Burrows families and their friends at this most difficult of times."

A meeting of the North-South Ministerial Council (NSMC) this Friday has been postponed following a request from the first and deputy first ministers.

It had been due to take place in Dublin, but it is understood Lord Morrow's wife's funeral will take place that day and the deputy first minister Emma Little-Pengelly is expected to attend.

The taoiseach has agreed to postpone the NSMC meeting following the request.

The DUP has faced calls from the TUV to boycott the meeting in protest at Michelle O'Neill's participation in blocking the Garvaghy Road in Portadown during the Drumcree dispute.
