Greta Gerwig Details Led Zeppelin Choice for Narnia Trailer

TheTools · 13.10.2026 22:30 · source · permalink

Greta Gerwig explained how she secured Led Zeppelin's Stairway to Heaven for the first trailer of Narnia: The Magician's Nephew during a conversation with Stephen Colbert at AMC Lincoln Square IMAX theater before the Oct. 13 trailer release. The film adapts C.S. Lewis' 1955 prequel and will arrive in theaters Feb. 12, 2027, before streaming on Netflix starting April 2, 2027, according to the report.

The trailer features Digory and Polly encountering Aslan, voiced by Meryl Streep, with Led Zeppelin's song accompanying the character's transformation. Gerwig said she chose the track because of its connection to the story's themes.

"It's this epic song," Gerwig said. She explained that the song reflects themes of a spiritual journey and the choices characters face in the story.

Gerwig said Led Zeppelin had previously not allowed Stairway to Heaven to be used in films. She met with Robert Plant and Jimmy Page before receiving approval for the song's inclusion. "I was so moved that they allowed me to use this song," Gerwig said.

She added that the band understood the film's emotional foundation and connection to C.S. Lewis.

Gerwig also described her personal connection to the Narnia books, recalling that she imagined a world beyond her parents' wardrobe when she was young. She later pitched the adaptation after learning Netflix held the rights. "I only know how to make personal things," Gerwig said. She said her goal was to bring emotional depth to the friendship between Polly and Digory while maintaining the story's larger scale.

Gerwig said she studied Lewis' life while preparing the adaptation, including rereading his autobiography Surprised by Joy and visiting places connected to him in England. She also explored the role of faith in her filmmaking. "Film and faith have always gone hand in hand," Gerwig said.

She linked that idea to the shared experiences created by movies and other communal spaces. The production includes Daniel Craig, Carey Mulligan and Ciarán Hinds, with Mark Ronson and Andrew Wyatt composing the score. During filming, Gerwig encouraged creative experimentation among collaborators.

"This book is about creation," Gerwig said. She also referenced the story's depiction of a lion bringing a world to life.

"You need to give people permission" to use their imaginations, Gerwig said. She said the film aims to encourage audiences to create after experiencing the story. "I hope they want to make something: a song or a cake or a film or a painting," Gerwig said. "Making something is an act of hope."
