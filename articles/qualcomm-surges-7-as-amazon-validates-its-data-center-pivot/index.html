Qualcomm (NASDAQ:QCOM), the wireless and computing-chip designer, rode its Amazon AI-infrastructure partnership into Monday's semiconductor rally. Shares jumped approximately 7% to $190.085, putting fresh momentum behind Qualcomm's push beyond smartphones and deeper into data centers.

Amazon may purchase as much as $60 billion of Qualcomm products, while receiving purchase-linked warrants valued at roughly $4 billion. The companies are also building custom inference silicon and optical connectivity capable of reaching 1.6 terabits per second. That combination gives Qualcomm something investors have been waiting for: a credible path to becoming a larger supplier inside hyperscale AI infrastructure rather than remaining overwhelmingly tied to mobile chips.

The valuation picture now demands a little more discipline. At $190.085, Qualcomm trades 8.3% above its $175.52 GF Value estimate, suggesting the market is already pricing in part of the Amazon opportunity. The $4 billion warrant value is equivalent to roughly 6.7% of the potential $60 billion purchasing ceiling, creating a meaningful incentive for Amazon to buy Qualcomm hardware, but not guaranteeing order volume, timing or margins. The next test is simple: Qualcomm has the strategic endorsement; now it needs the data-center revenue to catch up with the stock price.
