Donovan Clingan’s 25-under-25 snub undersells how much he’s anchoring Blazers

TheTools · 20.11.2026 21:21 · source · permalink

The FanSided team recently released its 25-under-25 NBA Player Rankings for the 2026-27 season. Despite a promising young core, the Portland Trail Blazers weren't represented on the list. Deni Avdija and Toumani Camara were too old anyway, but 22-year-old big man Donovan Clingan certainly wasn't!

FanSided's Ian Levy did name Clingan the first player to just miss the list, ranking him at No. 26 overall. But that still feels too low after seeing Clingan's importance to this Blazers 10-5 start to the season.

Donovan Clingan is anchoring a top-ten team in Portland

Portland is top ten in net rating, offensive rating, and defensive rating, and Clingan's interior presence is a huge part of that roster balance. It would be one thing if this was projecting the ceilings of players under 25, given Clingan's offensive limitations despite a developing three-point shot. But with these ranking criteria being their immediate impact for this season, Clingan deserves more credit for Portland's early season success.

Memphis Grizzlies center Zach Edey was ranked No. 19 overall, recognizing his impact on Memphis' surprising start to the season. However, the Blazers aren't too far behind them in the standings, while Clingan has Edey beat in every major statistical category.

Clingan is averaging 10.7 points, 10.7 rebounds, 1.8 assists, and 2.2 blocks per game on 56/36/59 shooting splits. Say what you will about his limited perimeter mobility, but this is the type of defensive anchor and interior presence every team covets.

We just saw how much the Los Angeles Lakers paid for Walker Kessler this offseason and the impact he's having on Luka Doncic and their 13-3 start. Looking at the Western Conference standings, every team currently in the playoffs has an established frontcourt presence, whether that's Kessler, Clingan, Victor Wembanyama, Alperen Şengün; the list goes on. It shows not only the advantage of having a frontcourt piece like Clingan, but also why his defensive presence will be essential if the Blazers have any hope of a deep playoff run.

Clingan has been key to Blazers' early-season success

Clingan may not put up the flashy statistics of some of these players to make the cut. But there's a reason he's a back-to-back NCAA champion with UConn and why that winning has translated to Portland. The impact of a top five rebounder and rim protector in the league can't be overstated.

Levy points that out in the "Why we might look stupid" part of Clingan's snub:

"His size is his most important attribute, but that's because he's really, really big — 7-foot-2 and 280 pounds. He's an intimidating rim protector, and while he's vulnerable in space, his length makes him a significant impediment in drop coverage... Maybe it doesn't matter that he only does a few things if he does those things really, really well."

The Blazers have a surprisingly balanced roster this season, with equal contributions on both ends. That's a credit to first-year head coach Micah Nori, who has players like Clingan buying into their specific roles. Clingan rarely tries to do too much on offense, and his limitations have been mitigated by the number of playmakers now on this roster who can set him up. He's also shooting the three ball better than ever, at 36.1 percent to start the season.

You may not think of him as a traditional modern NBA center because he's 7-foot-2, 280 lbs, and, with all due respect, moves like some dinosaur. But when you consider what the modern NBA actually means -- the analytical importance of the three-ball and protecting the paint -- there are few centers better at both than Clingan.

He's just in his third year but is already anchoring a playoff-caliber team for a second consecutive season. Now Clingan's strengths fit perfectly with what Nori has emphasized this season. Portland has a good shot profile, rebounds, and defends. Clingan is at the center of it all, proving he's the type of big man who can anchor a contender.

Center has been the least of the Blazers’ problems throughout this rebuild thanks to the stability Clingan offers at the position. That may not be reflected in these rankings. But Blazers fans know Clingan’s true value and importance, as we’re seeing firsthand in his contributions to this strong start.
