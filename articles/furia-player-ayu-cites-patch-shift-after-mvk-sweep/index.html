FURIA Player Ayu Cites Patch Shift After MVK Sweep

TheTools · 17.10.2026 06:39 · source · permalink

Preparation on a new game patch emerged as a central issue for FURIA after the Brazilian team was swept 3-0 by MVK Esports in the League of Legends World Championship play-ins in Los Angeles, according to Hotspawn and comments from FURIA player Ayu published on October 16, 2026.

According to the source article, the loss left FURIA needing a quick reset before a lower-bracket meeting with Cloud9 the next day. Hotspawn reported that FURIA entered the event after a strong CBLOL finish but struggled through the series and rarely found chances to recover.

Hotspawn framed the interview around FURIA's unexpected defeat and the team's limited time to prepare after last weekend's CBLOL final and travel to the United States. Ayu said the team still had significant review work ahead after the one-sided result.

"It sucks to lose 3-0. The games felt strange. I think we played badly in the early game and in a lot of team fights.

It felt like we were losing everything around the map and everything was burning. There’s a lot to review, but I don’t have a general opinion on the games yet," said Ayu, FURIA player.

He then pointed to the patch transition as a likely factor in the defeat. The source said FURIA had little to no scrim time before the match, leaving the roster with limited room to adjust to champion balance changes.

"Yes, for sure. We need to look for new things. Many champions were nerfed or buffed, and we didn’t have much time to adapt. So, probably, yes," said Ayu, FURIA player.

Even with little turnaround time, Ayu said the team remained confident before facing Cloud9. According to Hotspawn, he said FURIA planned to review the MVK series immediately and rely on prior strong showings against C9.

"We lost, but I think we’re still confident. Today we’re going to review the game and see what we can do better next time. We’ve had good matches against C9, so I think we have a lot of confidence going into tomorrow. I think we’ll be fine," said Ayu, FURIA player.

The interview also turned to Ayu's broader time with the organization. He said his happiest career moment was FURIA's first title, while correcting the length of his stint with the team.

"Three years, I think. The first title we won was probably the happiest moment of my career, so I’d say that," said Ayu, FURIA player.

Asked what success means outside competition, Ayu described a simpler standard centered on daily life and team relationships. Hotspawn also published his self-assessed player card, which listed an overall rating of 81, with champion pool the highest mark at 88 and laning the lowest at 70.

"That’s a hard question. For me, it’s simple things, like eating good food and having fun with my teammates and other people.

This life is very stressful. We lose, we win; there are highs and lows. So, for me, a good life is about simple things.

You can use money and other stuff to have fun. I think it’s like that," said Ayu, FURIA player.
