Bitcoin (CRYPTO: $BTC) was trading below $70,000 U.S. on Nov. 13 and headed for a weekly loss of nearly 10% as the selling in cryptocurrencies continues. Bitcoin was changing hands at $69,100 U.S. at week's end, having dropped 9% in the past five trading sessions. Ethereum (ETH), the second largest cryptocurrency, also continues to struggle and is now trading below the key support level of $2,000 U.S.

Crypto prices have declined for six consecutive weeks amid persistent volatility in the stock market as investors grow increasingly nervous about the artificial intelligence (A.I.) trade.

Bitcoin is also being pressured by steady outflows from exchange-traded funds (ETFs) that track its spot price. Market data shows that outflows from Bitcoin ETFs have topped $1.56 billion U.S. for the year.

Pressure on cryptocurrencies has increased since crypto exchange Binance disclosed on Oct. 30 that it had lost $6.3 billion U.S. worth of digital assets in a hack of its trading platform. The Binance theft has shaken investor confidence in crypto and is largely responsible for the continued decline in digital asset values, say analysts. Expectations for higher interest rates and the resignation of Nvidia (NASDAQ: $NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang are also pressuring risk assets such as crypto.

Here's what else happened with digital assets over the past week…

U.S. Seizes $1.2 Billion Of Crypto Stolen From Binance: The U.S. government seized $1.2 billion U.S. worth of cryptocurrencies stolen from Binance. The U.S. Department of Justice announced the seizure along with a pledge to tackle cyber crimes originating from North Korea.
American authorities have accused North Korean hacker group "TradeTraitor" of carrying out the hack at Binance that saw $6.3 billion U.S. stolen from the crypto exchange. TradeTraitor is said to be linked to the North Korean military.

Strategy's Bitcoin Holdings Top 850,000: Crypto treasury firm Strategy (NASDAQ: $MSTR) bought more Bitcoin, pushing its holdings to more than 850,000 digital tokens. Strategy acquired another 1,036 BTC during the week ended Nov. 6 at a cost of $81.9 million U.S. The company bought more Bitcoin at an average price of $79,012 U.S. per token. Strategy now owns a total of 850,364 Bitcoin, representing more than 4% of its circulating supply.
