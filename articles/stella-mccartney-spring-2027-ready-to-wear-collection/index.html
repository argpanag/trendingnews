SPRING 2027 READY-TO-WEAR

By Stella McCartney

Any show that allows you to place Jennifer Lopez and Philomena Cunk together in the intro is fine by me: by that standard, Stella McCartney delivered this afternoon. The seat between these two icons was filled by Alasdhair Wills, McCartney’s husband, and watching them commune was the chief distraction among many in our long wait for this show to begin.

We were in the British Ambassador’s residence, just along from the Élysée Palace: unfortunately, unlike the also-chaotic Vivienne Westwood show held here back in 2012, this show was held in the gorgeous garden—and today it was raining. Cue many clients discovering that high heels are not easily extricated from soft turf. Angelo Flaccavento wisely opted to watch the show from under a nearby tree. When the proceedings eventually began, staff forced guests to close their umbrellas: happily, the heavens did not too significantly further open.

The show mixed environmental polemics related to ocean conservation with a gentle cruise through many of McCartney’s greatest hits that resurfaced archival silhouettes, prints and techniques from previous collections. There were some great old-school castings too, including Miranda Kerr and Karlie Kloss.Stone-cold classics it was great to see again included McCartney’s loose, cool tailoring, here with technical drawstring detailing at the waist. Knit skirts, dresses and bustiers built up from heaped whorls of furled panels were appropriately coraline; elsewhere transparent fish wire was used to build crinoline hems and oyster-like sculptural forms. There were airbrushed dolphins on jersey and denim. A look that combined a mixed-wash denim trucker jacket over basketball shorts in shirting cotton and built-up ’90s-style skater trainers was poignantly true to the North Kensington cool-girl origins of the brand: a visual rewind straight back to Subterania, Notting Hill Arts Club, KCC and The Globe.

The more ornate, occasion-facing, evening-inclined pieces hewed closer to the more contemporary manifestation of brand Stella evidenced by those turf-stuck clients here today. There were Grecian flourishes, sculpted peplums and architectural bodices in washed-out pastels. Combined with David Attenborough’s warm but warning ocean commentary, these pieces made me think of Daryl Hannah in Splash.
