The numbers back the buyers, and TSMC's 2027 guidance in mid-January is the next test.

Big money was busy buying Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM:NYSE) last quarter. Twenty-five big funds opened a position or added more than 20% to one, according to their 13Fs for the quarter ended Sept. 30, filed between Oct. 14 and Nov. 16.

Since Sept. 30, though, the stock has fallen 21%, and it's now 26% below its 52-week high. So did the funds walk into a trap, or did the sell-off just put the stock on sale?

Some of these were huge bets

GQG Partners made the biggest move in dollars. The firm manages $163.86 billion. It raised its TSMC stake 562% to 5.74 million shares, worth $2.62 billion at quarter-end, or 4.9% of its 13F stocks. Andreas Halvorsen's Viking Global added 23% to reach 3.90 million shares worth $1.78 billion, and TSMC ended the quarter as its largest holding. Lone Pine Capital raised its stake 1,595% to 1.72 million shares worth $783 million.

Some smaller funds went further. TSMC was 15.1% of Greenwoods Asset Management's 13F stocks and 14.8% of Carrhae Capital's, which made it Carrhae's biggest position. Five funds, Castle Hook Partners among them, opened new positions.

Plenty of these funds were adding to the AI chip trade more broadly. GQG also raised its NVIDIA (NVDA:NASDAQ) stake 376%. Lee Ainslie's Maverick Capital added 21% to NVIDIA, its largest holding, and 41% to TSMC. Avantyr Capital Partners moved the other way on NVIDIA: it sold all of its NVIDIA shares in the quarter and opened a new TSMC position.

TSMC's business hasn't slowed

TSMC fell along with the rest of the chip group. On Nov. 5, TSMC sank in a chip rout after Democrats' midterm wins, amid fears of tighter AI rules and slower US data center construction. TSMC had no news of its own that day.
