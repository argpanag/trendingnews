Company behind major Canterbury solar farm developments placed into receivership - Chris Lynch Media

TheTools · 24.11.2026 07:14 · source · permalink

The company behind two major solar farm developments in Canterbury has been placed into receivership.

Companies Office documents show Far North Solar Farm Limited was placed into receivership on November 18, with Stephen Keen and Stephen White of Teneo Financial Advisory New Zealand appointed joint receivers.

The receivership covers the company’s business interests, property and assets secured under those agreements.

Far North Solar Farm Limited, a renewable energy developer based in Kumeu, Auckland, was behind proposed large scale solar farms in Waipara and the Mackenzie Basin.

The receivership comes little more than a month after independent hearing commissioners granted partial resource consent for the company’s controversial North Canterbury development.

The company originally sought permission to construct and operate a solar farm across 181 hectares at 380 Waipara Flat Road, north of Amberley.

Under the original proposal, approximately 250,000 solar panels would have generated up to 144 megawatts of electricity at peak capacity, enough to power an estimated 30,000 homes.

The development attracted significant community opposition, with Hurunui District Council receiving 158 submissions, 125 of which opposed the proposal.

Concerns raised during the consenting process included the development’s visual impact, potential effects on neighbouring properties, glare, fire risks and the ability of solar panels to withstand strong winds.

On October 13, independent commissioners granted consent for the development on the property’s upper terrace but declined permission for solar panels on the lower terraces, reducing the scope of the development by approximately 25 percent.

The consent also included requirements for extensive planting to reduce the visual impact on neighbouring properties.

On November 3, the Hurunui District Council received an appeal against the decision from Waipara Valley Community Collective Incorporated.

Far North Solar Farm Limited is also behind The Point Solar Farm, a proposed development covering approximately 670 hectares on the northern shores of Lake Benmore, between the Pukaki River and the Ohau C hydro canal.

The project was intended to generate electricity for the national grid.

The company lodged its application under the Government’s fast-track approvals legislation on September 1, 2025, with the application deemed complete on October 16 that year.

However, on August 21 this year, the Environmental Protection Authority suspended processing of the application because the company had not paid costs associated with the consenting process.

The Government’s fast-track approvals website said processing will resume when those costs were paid.

The receivership follows the end of a joint venture between Far North Solar Farm and Aquila Clean Energy earlier this year.

On January 5, Aquila announced the two companies had mutually agreed to conclude their partnership and pursue their respective business interests independently.

The joint venture had successfully commissioned the Pukenui Solar Farm in Northland in 2025.

As part of the separation, Aquila announced it had taken full ownership of several solar developments, including the operational Pukenui facility and the Omeheu Solar Farm in Edgecumbe, Bay of Plenty.

Far North Solar Farm retained a number of projects still under development, including those in Greytown, the Mackenzie Basin and other parts of the South Island.

Less than 11 months after that announcement, Aquila Clean Energy NZ Limited appointed receivers to Far North Solar Farm Limited.

The appointment gives the receivers control over the company’s secured assets and the power to exercise their rights under the security agreements and the Receiverships Act 1993.
