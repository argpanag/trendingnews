What to watch as high-stakes midterm elections unfold across DC region – WTOP News

TheTools · 03.11.2026 10:33 · source · permalink

Who's on your ballot? Here’s how to find out.

Follow WTOP’s team coverage of Election 2026 online, on air at 103.5 FM or on the WTOP News app. Stay with WTOP on election night for the latest results.

Voters across the D.C. region are headed to the polls Tuesday to elect future leaders and decide on ballot measures with a nationwide battle over Congress staged in the background. 

Democrats are fighting to pick up enough seats to flip the U.S. House and U.S. Senate as Republicans aim to maintain their majorities with hopes of advancing President Donald Trump’s agenda during his remaining two years in office.

The spotlight may be on Congress, but pivotal local and statewide races are also up for grabs. Voters in D.C., Maryland and Virginia are voting on referendums as well, which could make changes to their state constitutions or District law.

The biggest shift in leadership will likely come out of D.C., where voters are picking a new mayor and congressional delegate.

In Maryland, Gov. Wes Moore is up for reelection and all eight congressional seats are on the ballot. Voters will also have a chance to weigh in on redistricting following similar initiatives around the country that set out to sway state maps ahead of the midterms.

Virginia is operating under the same congressional boundaries that went into effect in 2022, after a mid-decade redistricting effort was struck down by the state’s supreme court. All 11 seats occupied by Virginians in the U.S. House are on the ballot, and Sen. Mark Warner is running for his fourth term in Congress, which he says will be his final.

Down ballot, Virginians are voting on proposed amendments on reproductive healthcare, same-sex marriage and voting rights.

Polls are open in Virginia from 6 a.m. to 7 p.m.; from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. in Maryland; and from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. in D.C.

Maryland

Maryland voters on Tuesday select a governor, attorney general and eight members of Congress. They also tackle three statewide ballot questions, including one that could have an impact on the 2028 congressional election, if approved.

According to the state constitution, legislative districts are to be compact and connected. Question 3, the redistricting amendment, would clarify that the constitutional requirements apply only to state senate and House of Delegates districts. In other words, U.S. House districts would be exempt. The state’s Democratic leaders have pushed for the amendment in response to mid-decade redistricting efforts in other states aimed at giving Republicans an edge in House elections.

The district Maryland Democrats wish to capture encompasses the Eastern Shore and parts of Baltimore County. Rep. Andy Harris is an eight-term incumbent and the only Republican in the state’s eight-member congressional delegation. Democrat Dan Schwartz is challenging Harris for the seat but the odds are not in his favor. Cook Political Report was still rating the 1st District a “solid R” as of its Nov. 2 update.

The other seven U.S. House districts were rated “solid D,” with Democratic incumbents running for reelection in all but Maryland’s 6th District, where Democrat Adrian Boafo and Republican Chris Chaffee are vying for the seat being vacated by retiring Rep. Steny Hoyer.

GOP gubernatorial candidate Dan Cox faces similarly long odds in his second-chance challenge against Democratic incumbent Moore. A UMBC poll conducted in late September found Moore with a 27-point lead over Cox, with 51% of likely voters favoring the current governor. Fourteen percent of voters were undecided or said they didn’t know. The remaining 6% of likely voters were split between Green Party candidate Andy Ellis and Cathy White of the Working Class Party.

Local races in Maryland

Outside the statewide races, scores of local lawmakers and other officials are on the ballot in parts of Maryland.

Democrats are expected to breeze to victory in the county executive races in Montgomery and Prince George’s counties. In Montgomery County, council member Will Jawando faces Republican Esther Wells. In Prince George’s, incumbent Aisha Braveboy does not have a challenger from any party.

Voters in Prince George’s County are also wading through a whopping 10 county-specific measures, including a proposed charter amendment that would shrink the county council from its current 11 members to nine beginning in 2031.

Virginia

Congress

In the D.C. region, most Congressional representatives are expected to win reelection, and voters tend to favor Democratic candidates and incumbents.

Polls indicate Warner is favored to win a fourth term as the senior U.S. Senator from Virginia. Republican Bert Mizusawa, a retired major general of the U.S. Army, is taking on the longtime senator in the midterms.

There’s only one congressional seat in Northern Virginia that’s considered competitive by the Virginia Public Access Project. Rep. Eugene Vindman narrowly won House District 7’s seat after it was vacated by now-Gov. Abigail Spanberger.

Some analysts believe the first-term congressman could be vulnerable; he’s been challenged by Republican Douglas Ollivant and a handful of third-party candidates. Vindman’s constituents live in Prince William, Spotsylvania and Stafford counties, as well as Fredericksburg, and to the west, Orange and Culpeper counties.

Three incumbent congressmen are also running for reelection in Northern Virginia, all in districts that are considered to lean in favor of Democrats, according to the Virginia Public Access project.

Democratic Rep. Don Beyer is looking to secure a seventh term in District 8, which includes Arlington County, the cities of Falls Church and Alexandria, and parts of Fairfax County. His opponent, Republican Tony Sabio, is a security expert who worked at the Pentagon.

Rep. Suhas Subramanyam, a Democrat, is defending his seat in District 10 against Republican Dave Beckwith, an Air Force veteran.

James Walkinshaw won a special election last fall to fill a congressional seat held by his former boss, the late Congressman Gerry Connolly. The titan Democrat served in the U.S. House from 2009 until his passing in May 2025. He represented residents of Fairfax County and Fairfax City for more than a decade.

Republican Arthur Purves and Green Party candidate Dianne Blais are running against Walkinshaw in District 11.

Outside the D.C. area, two Virginia races could be competitive. Democrats believe they have a chance to add to their majority in Virginia due to the same challenges facing Republicans nationwide — the war with Iran, fuel prices and affordability.

Near Virginia Beach, Republican Rep. Jen Kiggans is running against Democrat Elaine Luria in District 2. Some analysts believe the results in District 2 could be a bellwether for Democrats’ prospects nationwide.

Out in the Richmond suburbs, Republican Rep. Rob Wittman is defending his seat against Democrat Shannon Taylor, Henrico County’s top prosecutor. Wittman has served as District 1’s representative since 2007.

Changes could be ahead for Virginia’s constitution 

Virginians will be asked three questions on proposed constitutional amendments Election Day.

Voting “yes” on the first proposed amendment would enshrine reproductive rights in the state constitution, following the Supreme Court ruling that overturned federal protections for abortion in 2022. If passed, the amendment is expected to face legal challenges in the state supreme court next week.

The next amendment asks about removing an existing ban on same-sex marriage from the state’s constitution. If passed, the amendment would swap the ban with language that requires all legal marriages to be treated equally under the law, regardless of either person’s sex, gender or race.

The third question deals with voting rights, namely for people convicted of a felony. If voters approve the amendment, people who serve time for a felony would automatically regain their right to vote after leaving jail or prison.

The same amendment would narrow an amendment that prevents people from voting who are considered incapacitated. Under the proposal, a court would need to decide that a person lacks the capacity to understand the act of voting for their right to be stripped away.

Special election, local offices

One special election is taking place Tuesday to fill a seat in the Virginia General Assembly vacated by Del. Michelle Maldonado, who stepped down in May. Democrat Sonia Ruth Vasquez Luna and Republican Nathaniel Fritzen are competing for the open seat to represent District 20, which includes Manassas City, Manassas Park City and parts of Prince William County.

Candidates are campaigning for local offices throughout the commonwealth, including mayoral races in the towns of Haymarket, Lovettsville, Purcellville and Herndon.

Fairfax City is electing a new mayor, new city council members and school board members.

DC

When the dust settles Tuesday, the District will have a new mayor, delegate to Congress and new members of the D.C. Council as part of one of the largest shake-ups to local government in the nation’s capital in decades.

While the changes will be momentous, there likely won’t be any surprises on Election Day, given the overwhelming support for Democrats in the city.

A new wave of Democratic leadership

Ward 4 Council member Janeese Lewis George, a self-described Democratic Socialist, is the front-runner in the race to succeed three-term Mayor Muriel Bowser. Her fellow council member, Robert White, is expected to become the District’s next delegate to Congress, taking the reins from Del. Eleanor Holmes Norton, who held the position for 36 years.

One of D.C.’s eight wards is set to elect a new representative to the council, and self-described Democratic Socialist Aparna Raj leads the pack of contenders as the Democratic nominee. Another potential newcomer, Oye Owolewa, secured the Democratic nomination to fill the at-large seat of outgoing member Anita Bonds. Independent Elissa Silverman is the favorite to win the vacant at-large seat previously held by Kenyan McDuffie, as she recently won a special election to complete the few months left on McDuffie’s term.

Council Chair Phil Mendelson, Ward 5 Council member Zachary Parker, Ward 6 Council member Charles Allen and Attorney General Brian Schwalb all face long shot challengers to their reelection bids.

Ballot question sparks debate

The one item on the ballot that has sparked some debate ahead of Election Day is a measure that would ban the sale of foie gras in D.C.

The ballot question asks voters to bubble in “Yes, to approve” or “No, to reject” the ban. It would also prohibit the force-feeding of a bird for the purpose of enlarging its liver, which is how the dish is usually made.

Among those who’ve weighed in on the issue is prominent chef and humanitarian José Andrés, who, in reference to the ballot question, said on social media, “just lets concentrate on things that matter,” such as human rights issues and world hunger.

Others advocating for the ban say the practice of force-feeding ducks or geese to produce foie gras is blatant animal cruelty.
