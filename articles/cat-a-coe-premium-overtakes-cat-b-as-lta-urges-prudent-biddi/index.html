Cat A COE premium overtakes Cat B as LTA urges prudent bidding ahead of car exhibition

TheTools · 21.10.2026 09:19 · source · permalink

Smaller cars now cost more to bid for than bigger ones, and LTA wants your views on how car COE categories should work.

SINGAPORE — Category A Certificate of Entitlement (COE) premium for smaller cars rose $2,003 to $132,004 in the bidding exercise that closed on Wednesday (21 Oct), edging above Category B for larger cars, according to results from the Land Transport Authority (LTA). Premiums rose in three of the five categories, while motorcycle and Open category premiums fell.

"We note that there is an upcoming car exhibition which may lead to elevated demand and COE prices during this period," the LTA said in a statement, adding that COE prices remain elevated across most categories. "We urge car buyers and dealers to be prudent in bidding for COEs," the authority said.

Cat B, for bigger and more powerful cars, rose $1,801, or 1.4 per cent, to $131,901, leaving it $103 below Cat A, according to the LTA results. Cat A previously closed above Cat B in the second February exercise, the second April exercise and on 17 June, according to Motorist, and it is now $1,005 below the record S$133,009 it set on 9 Sept.

Cat C, for goods vehicles and buses, rose $311 to $92,200, while Cat D, for motorcycles, fell $234, or 2.1 per cent, to $10,778, according to the LTA results. The Open category, which can be used for any vehicle except a motorcycle, fell $1,210, or 0.9 per cent, to $135,790.

Fewer certificates on offer

The exercise offered 3,231 certificates, 39 fewer than the first October round, with Cat A down 41 to 1,191 and Cat B down 22 to 923, based on quotas set in a Government Gazette notification on 16 Oct. Figures for bids received in the latest exercise were not available at the time of writing.

Public consultation on car COE categories

The LTA said it has launched a public consultation on possible changes to the COE categorisation framework for cars, with differentiating more effectively between mass-market and higher-end cars among the key considerations. The consultation runs until 11.59pm on 2 Nov, ahead of the next bidding exercise in early November, and the LTA said it will carefully consider the feedback received before deciding on any changes.

What is COE?

The COE gives the owner the right to register and use a vehicle in Singapore for 10 years. It can be obtained through an online open auction conducted twice a month. Bidding exercises usually start at 12pm on the first and third Monday of the month, and last for three working days. Each bidding will end at 4pm on Wednesday, if there is no public holiday in between.

The vehicle quota in each category will be announced before the start of each bidding exercise.

After 10 years, when the COE expires, an owner can choose to de-register the vehicle or renew the COE.

How is the COE quota counted?

Since 1 February 2023, the number of COEs available for bidding in the corresponding vehicle category in each quarter is the rolling average of the number of vehicles deregistered over the previous four quarters.

Will the COE price drop?

COE prices, of course, can drop – it is dependent on supply and demand.

Since 2017, the Ministry of Transport has been freezing vehicle population growth for all categories except Category C for goods vehicles and buses. The current rates will be maintained until 31 January 2028.

Singapore is one of the costliest places in the world to buy a car – in fact, in 2022, the Global Wealth and Lifestyle Report 2022, released by Swiss private bank Julius Baer, found Singapore cars were the most expensive globally.
