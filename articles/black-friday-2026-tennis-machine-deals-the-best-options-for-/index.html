Black Friday 2026 tennis machine deals: The best options for indoor training this winter

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:12 · source · permalink

With Thanksgiving and Christmas soon approaching, Black Friday sales on tennis ball machines are here.

For those living in cold areas, indoor tennis courts provide a break from being cooped up all day long playing GTA VI away from the wind, rain, and snow. Tennis ball machines from the following three categories can help maintain a fit body despite eating too many turkey legs and stuffing.

Traditional ball machines

Tennis ball machines such as The Lobster have been in production for over 40 years. These traditional ball machines are controlled by a panel of buttons and dials, allowing for precise spin control and placement. They are reliable and repairable, with many units still in use after many decades.

Although they do offer remote controls to control speed and spin, these mechanical ball machines do not come with training programs for different strokes or advanced phone or smart watch apps.

From personal experience, these machines are reliable for coaches or tennis players who do not want to deal with complex apps or wireless connectivity issues. They are simple to set up and use, but do not provide the player with tips on improvement unless a coach is present.

These machines weigh about 35 to 44 lbs (16 to 20 kg).

The Lobster is now on sale from $1,079 (versus a $1,179 MSRP) for the Elite Liberty entry-level model to $1,749 (versus a $1,999 MSRP) for the Elite Three top-end model.

AI-powered ball machines

A variety of ball machines now have AI-powered features, including preset drills, player tracking, drill analysis, and voice control, including modesl from Pongbot and Lumistar. They can be significantly lighter than machines from the other two categories while providing more functionality and feedback on the courts thanks to their vision systems.

Since they can be controlled by smartphone apps, these AI-powered machines offer many dozens of preset drills to train all the major strokes without the need to adjust the machine by hand prior to each drill. They can also be optionally controlled by smart watches or a wristband voice controller (Lumistar).

Most of these models come with cameras to track the player and balls, enabling these machines to provide AI analysis and tips for improvement. This allows beginning players to hone their strokes when a coach is not available, and more proficient players to focus on specific areas of improvement such as shot placement. 

These machines weigh around 26 lbs (12 kg) for the Lumistar Teros.

The Lumistar Tero is now on sale for $799 (versus a $1,099 MSRP), and the Tero Pro is on sale for $1,499 (versus a $1,899 MSRP). They are also offered through Amazon.

Robotic AI ball machines

At the upper-end of tech-powered machines are robotic AI ball machines like the fun Acemate S10 tennis machine reviewed by Notebookcheck that can move across the entire court while catching returns. Coupled with the just-released AI virtual opponent feature, the Acemate can simulate matches while keeping track of scores and calling balls out thanks to dual 4K cameras.

The Acemate machines also come with many dozens of preset drills, allowing players to train everything from lobs to smashes to cross-court baseline returns. Each session is tracked, with maps of where each ball landed, and post-session AI analysis provides feedback for further improvement.

Although the Acemate S10 does have a net to catch returns, these leading-edge machines are a work-in-progress with frequent software updates, so it won't catch every last ball. Also, the AI software running the robot is constantly improved, so not every feature will work perfectly out of the box.

Still from personal experience, the company is responsive to fixing bugs and handling customer service requests, and they made substantial improvements during testing to keep the S10 fun for use as a tennis drill partner.

Tennis ball machines like the Acemate S10 are ideal for tennis players who are used to working with techology and software updates, and also enjoy using a device that constantly surprises them with new features and improved play capability. Its ability to move across the court like a real player while playing matches and provide post-match tips for improvement is also a key advantage.

These machines weigh 39 to 51 lbs (18 to 23 kg).

The AceMate S10 is on sale for $1,799 (versus an MSRP of $2,499) and the just-released S10 Pro with faster ball speed and longer runtimes is on sale for $2,499 (versus an MSRP of $2,999).

Gear

No tennis player is complete without a solid racket, and this reviewer has long used rackets like the Yonex Vcore 95 8th Gen for its Isometric head design and crisp shot response.

It is also best to buy enough tennis balls like the Penn Championship Extra Duty to run tennis machines for several minutes between refills. Sadly, none of the ball machines have the ability to pick up tennis balls off the court, so players will still need a good metal ball hopper like the Wilson pick-up basket.
