In 2025, four out of 10 small event organizers said they were concerned that inflation, travel costs and U.S. trade tariffs may negatively impact their events, according to CEIR figures reported by the International Association of Exhibitions and Events. When venues, artists and ad budgets cost more, the fee taken from each ticket affects margin directly.

That pressure changes what a ticketing platform has to do well. Selling seats is the easy part. Protecting profit takes fees that stay predictable at checkout, payouts that arrive on a schedule matching real bills, and a way to bring last year’s attendees back without paying to find them again. Secondary revenue matters too, because a sponsorship package or a parking add-on can make up the difference when ticket sales fall short.

This ranking compares six event ticketing platforms on the things that decide whether an event makes money, from per-ticket fees and card processing to payout timing, marketing tools and sponsorship sales, with all details updated November 2026.

The Numbers Behind This Market

Buyers want to see the full price of a ticket before they reach checkout, and polling shows broad agreement on that point. In May 2026 polling by Breakwater Strategy, published by the National Consumers League, 95% of voters called upfront price transparency important to fixing the ticketing industry. Support for requiring it runs nearly as broad. Nationally, 82% of voters back an industry-wide mandate for all-in pricing, and 87% of registered voters nationally support legislation explicitly designed to mandate transparency and fairness in the ticketing marketplace.

For a ticket seller, these figures point to checkout behavior. Surprise fees can lead a buyer to abandon the cart or skip a future event. A simple, visible fee structure keeps the organizer’s accounting clean and protects buyer trust at the moment they’re most likely to walk away. Clear fee design can reduce abandonment and help revenue.

Repeat sales follow the same logic: segmented emails generally earn better click-through rates than unsegmented sends. For an event business, the most useful segment is people who have already bought a ticket.

These numbers lead to a few practical habits. Fix the event’s costs before the on-sale date, well enough to name the ticket count that covers them. When comparing platforms, separate the card processing charge from the platform fee, because bundled quotes and split quotes look different and the gap widens as ticket prices rise. Weigh the payout schedule against the event’s real bills too, since money that arrives after the event can’t cover a venue deposit due weeks before it.

1) Loopyah

Why it ranks here: Loopyah is built for organizers who run paid events as a business, and it treats ticketing, profit tracking and promotion as one job. Enter the event’s costs and the CRM shows the break-even point, revenue, payouts and profit, so the event’s financial position is visible before tickets go on sale. That pre-sale visibility can affect how to price tickets, whether to add a second date and how much ad budget the event can carry. It’s what makes Loopyah the best event ticketing platform for organizers who need every event to make money. Pricing follows the same logic. There’s no ticketing subscription, and one fee of A$0.95 + 4.25% per paid ticket includes card processing, while free events carry no fee at all. Payouts land every Monday starting from the first event, so cash keeps moving while the next event is still on sale.

The marketing tools build on that profit data, so the system that sells tickets also holds the information needed to sell the next event. Every ticket buyer becomes a contact in the CRM, one record per buyer across all events, with their spend, the events they’ve attended and their permission to be emailed. The next event starts with an audience already in the system. The email builder works by dragging in images, text, buttons and social links, and an automation is built from four choices, covering what starts it, how long it waits, what it does and what ends it. Email marketing costs US$25 per month for 10,000 marketing emails, while the CRM itself, the promoter tools and the AI Ads Manager come with ticketing. The AI Ads Manager runs the organizer’s own Meta, Google and TikTok ad accounts against tickets sold, with no markup on ad spend, so the full budget goes to media and no part of it is kept as a platform charge. Promoters can be paid for the tickets they sell, which turns word of mouth into a tracked sales channel. Sponsorship packages get their own online page, where brands buy or apply, pay by card, and the money arrives with the ticket revenue. Loopyah takes 4.5% only when a brand pays, card processing included, so the sponsorship page costs nothing unless a brand pays.

Loopyah addresses two common profit problems. It shows the break-even point before sales begin, and it lets organizers reach past buyers without paying to acquire them again. It also includes a sponsorship channel with its own online page, where brands buy or apply, pay by card, and the money arrives with the ticket revenue.

Event fit: Loopyah is designed around running events as an ongoing business, with costs to track, audiences to bring back and sponsors to sell. The CRM, promoter tools and sponsorship pages are available to every event, and the same per-ticket pricing works for a single event. Free events carry no fee.

Pick it if the plan is to run paid events as a business, whether that’s an independent venue, a festival, a promoter operation, a conference or a paid workshop, and profit tracking, ad automation and sponsorship revenue belong in the same system as the tickets.

2) Ticketbud

Why it ranks here: Ticketbud is the pay-as-you-go option for organizers who want control over when money arrives and a ready-made set of integrations around it. There’s no subscription, free events cost nothing, and paid tickets carry a platform fee of 1.75% + 99c or 2% + 99c, with payment processing at 2.9% + 30c per ticket on top. Organizers can choose daily, weekly or monthly payouts, the widest choice of cadence on this list. For anyone paying suppliers as tickets sell, daily payouts can provide money while sales are still coming in, which helps cover costs before the event.

Ticketbud connects to Salesforce, Facebook, Zoom and Zapier, which suits teams that already keep customer data in an external system and want ticket sales feeding straight into it. Add-on products and merchandise sales are built in, so there’s room to grow the order beyond the ticket, and free events carrying no fee make the platform an easy test for a first event. Ticketbud lists event categories running from festivals, concerts and comedy shows to class reunions, conferences, nonprofit events, races and church gatherings. On Capterra, Ticketbud holds a 4.6 rating across 37 reviews.

Limits: The fee structure stacks two charges on every ticket, since the platform fee and the processing fee each carry a percentage plus a fixed cent amount. On a premium ticket, the extra fixed amounts may be small. On a low-priced ticket, they take a visibly bigger share of the price, so organizers running low-cost, high-volume events should compare the total fee at their actual price points before committing.

Pick it if daily payouts and pre-built connections to Salesforce, Zoom or Zapier sit at the top of the requirements list.

3) TicketSpice

Why it ranks here: TicketSpice pairs a low flat per-ticket fee with tools designed to raise what each attendee spends. Standard ticketing starts at $0.99 per paid ticket, with per-ticket rates running from 49¢ to $1.49 depending on the setup, and card processing at 2.9% + 30¢ per transaction. Because the platform fee is a flat cent amount, it shrinks as a share of the price as tickets get more expensive. A percentage fee moves the other way, rising with the ticket price. Payouts arrive weekly as fast, direct event funding, which keeps cash predictable across a long on-sale window.

The revenue tools work with that pricing model. A built-in Customer CRM and event email marketing with automation handle repeat outreach, and add-ons and upgrades let organizers attach extras to the order. The CRM and email tools also let organizers send follow-up messages and offers to those attendees. The platform targets festivals, farms, fairs, concerts and museums, high-volume events where a few dollars of upgrades per order can multiply across thousands of attendees. On Capterra, TicketSpice holds a 4.8 rating across 107 reviews. A flat fee keeps platform costs predictable at scale, and upgrades can raise average order value without changing the fee structure.

Limits: With per-ticket rates that vary by setup and a separate processing charge on every transaction, the total cost per ticket depends on setup and ticket price. To compare it against all-in pricing, calculate the blended fee at the event’s own ticket prices first.

Pick it if the event is a high-volume festival, fair, concert or attraction where flat base fees and add-on upgrades matter more than bundled processing.

4) Afton Tickets

Why it ranks here: Afton Tickets is aimed at live-event producers, and its features address common festival budget needs. Published rates run $0.79 + 2.00% or $1.29 + 3.00% per ticket, with a schedule-a-call option for custom pricing and no hidden monthly fees. Early payout options release ticket money before the event to help cover event costs. That timing lines up with bills that arrive weeks before gates open.

Tools also cover revenue beyond the ticket. Title sponsor placement puts a sponsor’s name directly on tickets, and unlimited add-on items cover extras such as camping, parking and rides. These tools fit multi-day events with parking fields, campsites and sponsors to keep happy. Email and SMS marketing tools handle engagement and upsells. Its audience includes independent festival producers, county fair organizers, rodeo promoters, venue operators and event promoters. On Capterra, it holds a 4.9 rating across 58 reviews.

Pick it if the event is a festival, county fair or rodeo that needs early access to ticket money, SMS upsells and sponsor branding on the tickets themselves.

5) TicketSource

Why it ranks here: TicketSource offers a choice in how fees and payouts work, on a platform with no contracts, subscriptions or complicated access tiers. The first option is a percentage-plus-fixed booking fee per paid ticket, with all payment card processing costs included. On that model, ticket money is sent to the organizer’s bank on the Monday after the event and should arrive by Wednesday. The second option is a fixed booking fee per paid ticket, with Stripe charging card processing directly when the organizer connects their own Stripe account, so ticket money goes to that account each time someone books. The organizer can choose a single fee with post-event payout or a lower fixed fee with payments handled through a Stripe account to manage. Both options avoid a subscription.

The rest of the toolkit leans toward seated and community events. The platform handles specific seats for theaters, general entry for gigs and festivals, and timed sessions for tours. Email marketing runs through Mailchimp or Brevo, connecting the organizer’s existing email account. Extras like food, programs and merchandise can be sold alongside tickets, and customers can add a donation when they purchase, which can suit charity and community events.

Limits: Its own processing pays out after the event finishes, so organizers who want money as it comes in need the Stripe option, which runs card processing through the organizer’s separate Stripe account with Stripe’s processing fees charged directly.

Pick it if the event involves assigned seats, timed entry or general admission and the organizer wants no subscription with Mailchimp or Brevo email sync.

6) VBO Tickets

Why it ranks here: VBO Tickets suits organizations that sell more than admission. Its CRM and communications tools are fully integrated, and merchandise moves through the same system as tickets, so jerseys, hats and playoff-only drops sell through the ticketing system itself or via VBO’s point of sale software at the venue. For a venue or a production company with a busy season, keeping ticket and merchandise revenue in one place simplifies the back office.

The same system covers concerts, theater productions, festivals, timed-entry attractions, conferences, classes, workshops and virtual events. A single organization can run different event types through it without changing tools. It can handle online sales and venue sales for the same organization. Pricing is transaction-based and tied to ticket volume.

Limits: VBO’s pricing has more parts than the pay-as-you-go platforms ranked above it, combining a quoted platform fee, a transaction fee tied to volume and processor or gateway charges identified separately. That structure suits organizations that want a quoted, ongoing platform relationship, and it makes like-for-like cost comparison take a little more work.

Pick it if the organization runs multiple event formats and sells merchandise online and at the point of sale under one roof.

Afton and VBO center extras, with Afton pairing email and SMS marketing tools with title sponsor placement and unlimited add-on items, and VBO moving merchandise through its ticketing system and point of sale software. Common Questions

These answers cover fees, payouts and how to choose among the six.

Which Event Ticketing Platform Is Best for Running Profitable Events?

Loopyah is the top pick for running profitable events, because it tracks each event’s break-even point, revenue, payouts and profit from entered costs before tickets go on sale, then backs that with email automations to past buyers, an AI Ads Manager that runs Meta, Google and TikTok accounts with no markup on ad spend, and online sponsorship pages. Ticketing sits under one fee of A$0.95 + 4.25% per paid ticket, card processing included, with no subscription, while email marketing costs US$25 per month for 10,000 marketing emails and sponsorship sales carry a 4.5% charge only when a brand pays. Organizers who need a Salesforce connection and daily payouts should look at Ticketbud, and high-volume events optimizing for a low flat fee on higher-priced tickets should consider TicketSpice.

How Do Per-Ticket Fees and Card Processing Affect Event Margins?

Pricing across this list falls into two models. All-in pricing bundles card processing into the platform fee. Loopyah charges A$0.95 + 4.25% per paid ticket with processing included, and TicketSource’s US all-in option works the same way. Split pricing lists the charges separately. Ticketbud adds 2.9% + 30c processing to its platform fee, TicketSpice adds 2.9% + 30¢ per transaction, and VBO Tickets identifies processor and gateway charges apart from its platform fees. Ticket price decides which model keeps more margin, because flat cent amounts and percentages behave very differently on a low-priced workshop seat and a premium festival pass. Compare the total fee at the event’s actual price points.

How Can Ticketing Tools Boost Revenue Beyond Ticket Sales?

Loopyah gives organizers a dedicated sponsorship channel, with packages on their own online page where brands buy or apply and pay by card, and it takes 4.5% only when a brand pays. Its CRM email automations and AI Ads Manager then work on bringing past buyers back. Afton Tickets also supports sponsor revenue, placing title sponsors directly on tickets and allowing unlimited add-on items like camping, parking and rides, backed by email and SMS marketing. VBO Tickets sells merchandise online and through its point of sale software. Ticketbud, TicketSpice and TicketSource cover add-on products, upgrades and merchandise, and TicketSource also lets customers add a donation at checkout.

When Do Organizers Receive Ticket Payouts to Cover Event Expenses?

Payout timing affects whether ticket revenue can pay the event’s bills before the event happens. Loopyah pays out every Monday starting from the first event, which keeps cash flow predictable across a season. Ticketbud offers the widest choice of cadence, with daily, weekly or monthly payouts. TicketSpice funds weekly, and Afton Tickets offers early payout options specifically to help with upfront event costs. TicketSource sends money on the Monday after the event, arriving by Wednesday, unless the organizer connects their own Stripe account, in which case payments route directly through Stripe each time someone books.

Choosing the Right Platform for Profitable Events

Profitability starts before doors open, in the fee structure buyers see at checkout, the date ticket money arrives, and whether last year’s attendees hear about this year’s show. Ticketbud offers payout flexibility and integrations, Afton provides early funding and sponsor placement, TicketSource supports seated and timed events, TicketSpice suits high-volume add-on sales, and VBO Tickets combines admission with merchandise. For organizers running paid events as a business, knowing the break-even point before the on-sale date and keeping the buyer relationship afterward is a strong position. Loopyah builds around that, with profit tracking and ad management with no markup under one per-ticket fee, plus sponsorship packages sold online where Loopyah takes 4.5% only when a brand pays. The right platform is the one that fits the event’s fee model, payout schedule and marketing needs, and Loopyah ranks first in this list.
