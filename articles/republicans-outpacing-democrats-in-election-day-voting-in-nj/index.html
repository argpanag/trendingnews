Republicans outpacing Democrats in Election Day voting in NJ-2, but Democratic turnout lead holds - New Jersey Globe

TheTools · 03.11.2026 19:39 · source · permalink

Van Drew benefits from stronger GOP turnout, while Mullock retains a 7,471-vote Democratic registration advantage among ballots cast

By David Wildstein, November 03 2026 2:39 pm

Republicans are outvoting Democrats on Election Day in New Jersey’s 2nd congressional district, but Democrats continue to hold an overall turnout advantage as Rep. Jeff Van Drew (R-Dennis) seeks a fifth term against Democrat Zach Mullock, the mayor of Cape May.

An analysis by Umichvoter shows that 25,669 Republicans have voted on Election Day, compared with 19,243 Democrats and 12,996 unaffiliated voters.

Republicans account for 44% of Election Day turnout, compared with 33% for Democrats and 22% for unaffiliated voters, giving the GOP a 6,426-vote advantage among those who have voted today.

But Democrats entered Election Day with a substantial lead in advance voting, and Republicans have not yet erased it.

Of the 168,239 ballots cast so far, 71,223 came from registered Democrats, 63,752 from Republicans, and 33,264 from unaffiliated voters.

That leaves Democrats with a 7,471-vote advantage among voters who have already participated.

Republicans are running particularly strong in Ocean County, where they account for 54% of Election Day voters, compared with just 22% for Democrats. The GOP has a 4,622-vote advantage there.

Republicans are also outvoting Democrats in Atlantic County, 42% to 37%; Cumberland County, 40% to 38%; and Gloucester County, 40% to 37%.

The figures remain incomplete. Cape May and Salem counties, both Republican strongholds, have not yet reported meaningful Election Day turnout figures.  Atlantic County’s numbers are based on county-reported totals rather than the same Election Day reporting feed used for most other counties.

The remaining pool of potential voters includes 138,005 Republicans, 107,089 Democrats, and 183,289 unaffiliated voters.

That gives Republicans a 30,916-voter advantage among registered partisans who have not yet cast ballots, potentially significant as Van Drew seeks to overcome the Democratic advance-voting lead.

The numbers reflect voter registration, not votes for individual candidates, and do not establish which candidate is ahead.
