Ontario teachers flag higher prices on Ford government's new Staples portal

TheTools · 04.09.2026 08:40 · source · permalink

EDITOR’S NOTE: This article originally appeared on The Trillium, a Village Media website devoted to covering provincial politics at Queen’s Park.

Ontario teachers logging on to the Ford government’s new classroom supply portal are finding some products priced significantly higher than what their school boards previously paid for the same supplies — but the education minister says teachers are missing a key discounted part of the new system.

The online portal operated by Staples officially went live on Sep. 1.

A Toronto District School Board teacher who contacted The Trillium shared screenshots comparing the portal’s prices with the TDSB’s purchasing catalogue, showing multiple items that will now cost them more than on its previous platform. For example, a case of paper that TDSB teachers could previously purchase for $49.85 is now listed for $84.49 on the Staples portal. Duotangs were listed at 96 cents each, compared with 38 cents through the board. A package of 20 notebooks on the portal was listed at $35.63, compared with $17.25 through the board.

“We are being absolutely swindled,” the teacher, who wished to remain anonymous, wrote in an e-mail.

The Trillium was sent the same prices and screenshots from at least three other teachers.

Education Minister Paul Calandra said teachers are missing the list of more than 200 priority products identified by teachers as classroom essentials that are being offered at the lowest prices available.

The government released the full list late Tuesday, which contains a wide range of products from pens and pencils, to construction paper, to sensory fidget tools at discounted prices. 

You can find the full list here. 

“Those 200 products, they are the absolute cheapest that you can find across Staples or across any platform,” Calandra said at an unrelated announcement in Hamilton.

Calandra pointed to one example of pens that he said were priced at about $8 on the regular Staples website compared to 81 cents through the teachers' portal. The problem, he said, is that teachers logging onto the portal can also see a wider selection of Staples products, including products outside the list of specially priced essentials. The government is now working to make the cheaper products easier to find, according to Calandra.

“So teachers, when they go on the portal, they will still see some of the other products that are available to all customers,” Calandra said. “Is there going to be a couple things there that shouldn’t be on it? Yes, but we’re working on that.”

He said they are also working to add more to the list of 200 as they hear back from teachers.

“I am absolutely, positively confident that there is no one who will find a cheaper price on those 200 things," Calandra said. 

Homeroom elementary teachers have been given $750 each to spend on supplies through the portal, which has faced scrutiny for several reasons. The Ford government has been questioned over its decision to award the $66 million program to a single vendor, and the website itself was not ready in time for the start of the school year. Some Ontario school boards have already welcomed students back to class this week before teachers had an opportunity to order supplies.

“This is a minister of education who cannot even deliver paper and pencils to classrooms without something messing up,” said opposition NDP Leader Marit Stiles. “This big American corporation was given this (single vendor) contract, and guess what? They’re exploiting the hell out of it.”

In a statement, Staples Canada wrote "there are more than 200 core items in the program, identified by a green symbol on the Classroom Supplies Fund portal at eway.ca.

Those core items were determined through Supply Ontario’s survey of approximately 25,000 Ontario elementary teachers, who identified the products they need most. Those prices are the lowest available at Staples.

Staples complemented the core items with other products teachers may find helpful in their classrooms. Staples is committed to ensuring those complementary items are priced the same or less than can be found at Staples in-store or online."

Staples said there is a new hotline teachers can call if they find cheaper prices elsewhere. 

"If a teacher happens to find a product that costs more on the portal than they find at Staples in-store or online, Staples has set up a dedicated line to respond to any price differences. Teachers are encouraged to call Staples at 1-866-839-9901, and Staples will ensure prices are matched."

Calandra acknowledged the new system is still a work in progress and invited teachers who find pricing mistakes to flag them to their schools.

“Are you going to be able to go on that portal and find mistakes? 100 per cent,” he said.

“If you find a mistake, then let your principal know, so that they can tell the superintendent, so that they can then tell us, so that we can work with Staples to fix it.”

He also pushed back on teachers posting complaints about the portal on social media before raising them with the government.

“Don't just run to TikTok and say, ‘Oh my gosh, look what they're doing to us.’ Help me make it better,” Calandra said.

The education minister said the portal represents a new way for teachers to purchase classroom supplies without paying out of pocket, and insisted the government will continue making changes based on feedback.

“It's not meant to be perfect from day one, but it will get there,” he said.
