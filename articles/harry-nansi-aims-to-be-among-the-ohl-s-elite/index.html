Harry Nansi aims to be among the OHL's elite

TheTools · 03.09.2026 14:58 · source · permalink

The Toronto Maple Leafs prospect is primed for a breakout season in the Scenic City and could be one of the league's top players.

When Harry Nansi is on his game, it looks like he’s conducting the action on the ice.

He may as well be wearing a formal black tuxedo with long tails and a white tie. His hockey stick could be a baton, ordering the game around the ice like a maestro who controls the tempo, pace and volume of the orchestra.

In a game that’s naturally chaotic and quick, his ability to slow it all down and pause, to let the play unfold around him and open up a seam for a passing lane, catches the eye.

It also helps that he’s six-foot-four and 200 pounds.

His physical size lets him hold players off while he processes his next move. His skating has improved to the point where he isn’t so easily pushed off the puck anymore. He’s a bull who has become nimble enough to dance through the china shop.

All this to say, Nansi is on the cusp of a major breakout season for the Owen Sound Attack.

“Coming back to the OHL, I just have a little more poise. I realize how much time I have. It’s pretty nice,” Nansi said.

The Brossard product who grew up in the Ottawa area will turn 19 on Sept. 10.

In a league where talented 19-year-olds are becoming a rarer species as older players migrate to the NCAA, Nansi will be one of the more experienced veterans of the OHL this year. He’s already played 175 OHL games, having made the Attack as a 16-year-old out of the Nepean Raiders under-18 program.

His head coach back then in Nepean is now Owen Sound’s head coach — Keenan Reynolds.

Reynolds said he thinks the key to a good offence is attacking through the neutral zone, drawing defenders out of position, and finding the space they’ve vacated with a deft pass.

“He (Nansi) might be the best player in the league at it,” Reynolds said.

Really, last season was Harry Nansi’s breakout year. He recorded 56 points in 67 games, including 43 assists after coming back from a summer spent with the player development staff of the Toronto Maple Leafs.

But he had long periods of inconsistent play and was a part of a disastrous playoff performance that no one in Owen Sound wants to remember.

Reynolds thinks there’s untapped potential there.

“We’re going to challenge him to shoot a lot more this year. I keep telling him he only had 13 goals last year. It’s getting under his skin, so he’s going to be shooting a lot this year,” he said after a preseason game in which Nansi had a goal and three assists.

A position change may also be on the table. Nansi has played on the wing for much of his junior hockey career, but lined up in the middle for Owen Sound’s first preseason game at the Bayshore.

“At the next level, being able to play all three positions and do it at a high level is something I know his NHL team is going to be looking for. We’re going to hold him to a high level,” Reynolds said.

Nansi said he’s open to anything and knows how highly valued centremen are in the National Hockey League.

“It doesn’t matter, really. I’ll do whatever to help the team. If coach wants me at centre, I’ll play centre. If he wants me on my off-wing, I’ll play on my off-wing,” he said.

“Sometimes you have to support your D, but if you get caught down low when the offence goes, you’re not in the play. So, you’ve really have to think a lot and if you support or anticipate if the puck is going the other way,” he said about playing the pivot position.

If he does end up playing in the middle of the ice alongside Pierce Mbuyi, who scored 32 goals last year, and Cole Zurawski, who has one of the best shots in the circuit — look out.

That trio combined for 12 points in its first time ever being put together on OHL ice for any length of time Wednesday night against North Bay.

It’s just the preseason, and there’s a long way to go, but Nansi seems primed for a performance of a lifetime under the bright lights of the Bayshore this year.
