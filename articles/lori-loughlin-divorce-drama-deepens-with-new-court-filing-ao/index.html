Lori Loughlin Divorce Drama Deepens With New Court Filing - AOL

TheTools · 23.10.2026 14:48 · source · permalink

Lori Loughlin and Mossimo Giannulli’s divorce has taken another step forward. Giannulli has formally responded to the Full House star’s petition after 28 years of marriage, agreeing that the couple should divorce over “irreconcilable differences.”

However, their court filings differ on several financial matters. Giannulli asked the court to determine whether either spouse should receive support, while Loughlin previously referenced the couple’s prenuptial agreement in her petition.

Mossimo Giannulli has responded to Lori Loughlin’s divorce petition after 28 years of marriage

As per Page Six, Giannulli filed his response in Los Angeles Superior Court on Thursday. Like Loughlin, he cited “irreconcilable differences” as the reason for ending their marriage.

Loughlin filed for divorce earlier this month. In her petition, she asked the court to divide their property, assets and debts according to a prenuptial agreement they signed before their November 1997 wedding and later amended in August 2015.

She also asked the court to determine spousal support at a later date. Loughlin requested that each party cover their own legal fees and costs.

Giannulli took a somewhat different approach in his response. He did not mention the prenuptial agreement and instead asked the court to decide “whether spousal support shall be payable to either party upon a request of either party.”

The fashion designer also asked the court to determine whether one spouse should contribute toward the other party’s legal fees.

Another difference appears in their separation dates. Loughlin listed August 31, 2025, as the date of separation. Giannulli reportedly left that section of his filing blank.

The former couple’s separation first became public in October 2025. At the time, Loughlin’s representative confirmed that she and Giannulli were living apart but said they had not started legal proceedings.

Loughlin and Giannulli married in 1997 and share two daughters, Isabella Rose and Olivia Jade. Their marriage later faced intense public scrutiny following their involvement in the 2019 college admissions scandal. Both ultimately served federal prison sentences after pleading guilty in the case.

Giannulli’s new filing does not establish that either spouse will receive support. Instead, it leaves the issue for the court to determine as their divorce proceedings continue.
