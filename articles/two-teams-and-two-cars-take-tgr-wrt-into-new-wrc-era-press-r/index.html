Two teams and two cars take TGR-WRT into new WRC era

World champions and rising stars form six-strong driver line-up

2026.11.03 (Tuesday)

TOYOTA GAZOO Racing World Rally Team (TGR-WRT) will field two distinct teams with two different cars and a star-studded driver line-up as the FIA World Rally Championship enters a new era in 2027.

The two squads will compete as TOYOTA RACING, debuting the GR Celica Rally1 car, and GAZOO Racing TOYOTA, campaigning the new 2027 GR Yaris Rally1 car.

The combined driver line-up across the two teams will feature at least three world champions, with the 2026 title winner Elfyn Evans joined by two returning heroes in Ott Tänak and Kalle Rovanperä. Completing an impressive mix of rally-winning experience and proven potential are the WRC’s three newest rally winners: Oliver Solberg, Sami Pajari and Takamoto Katsuta.

In 2026, TGR-WRT secured a record-equalling sixth consecutive manufacturers’ championship, with all five of its official crews claiming multiple rally wins and contending for the drivers’ and co-drivers’ titles. Three drivers continued the fight all the way to a thrilling final stage of the season, where Evans ultimately prevailed by just five points ahead of Solberg and six ahead of Pajari.

Under new Rally1 technical regulations for 2027, the ethos of providing all drivers with a car they can win with remains central, with preparations underway to field up to six factory cars on each rally. These will be entered across two teams that will compete as equal challengers for victory, further enhancing competition and creating new stories for fans to follow and enjoy.

TGR-WRT will continue to operate both teams under the same roof at its headquarters in Jyväskylä in Finland. Supporting the competition between them and the development of the two cars will strengthen its efforts to make ever-better cars, drawing on feedback from strong driver line-ups assembled to give both teams the opportunity to compete for victory.

The TOYOTA RACING drivers will be entrusted with the new GR Celica Rally1 car, returning one of the most iconic names in Toyota rally history to the WRC.

Its line-up will be led into the 2027 season by the defending drivers’ and co-drivers’ champions, Elfyn Evans and Scott Martin. Entering their eighth successive full season with the team, the experienced and ever-consistent Welsh-English duo have started more rallies for Toyota than anyone else in the company’s long WRC history and secured their hard-earned first world titles this year having finished runners-up on five previous occasions.

Alongside them will be Oliver Solberg and Elliott Edmondson. The spectacular Swedish driver and his British navigator finished as runners-up in their first full season with TGR-WRT this year – scoring three victories and more stage wins than anyone else – after winning the WRC2 title with the GR Yaris Rally2 car in 2025.

Completing the TOYOTA RACING trio will be another up-and-coming force in the WRC, Sami Pajari and Marko Salminen. The Finns finished third in the standings and took three wins in what was their second full season competing at the highest level, demonstrating exceptional consistency with nine podiums from 13 events.

The GAZOO Racing TOYOTA drivers will take the wheel of the 2027 GR Yaris Rally1 car, aiming to build on the remarkable success already achieved by the GR Yaris under the outgoing ruleset.

Its line-up includes Ott Tänak and Martin Järveoja, who renew their successful WRC partnership with GAZOO Racing seven years after winning the 2019 world championship aboard the Toyota Yaris WRC. In their previous spell with the team, the Estonian duo won 10 rounds of the WRC across two seasons.

Joining them at GAZOO Racing TOYOTA are Takamoto Katsuta and Aaron Johnston, following a breakthrough 2026 campaign in which they achieved their maiden WRC victories in Kenya and Croatia and Katsuta became the first Japanese driver to ever lead the championship standings.

Also making a highly anticipated return to WRC competition with GAZOO Racing TOYOTA following a year away will be Kalle Rovanperä and Jonne Halttunen. Rovanperä became the youngest ever World Rally Champion as the Finnish pairing won back-to-back titles with TGR-WRT in 2022-23, and they will contest a partial season of selected events.

This will provide the team with the possibility to welcome other drivers into its line-up during the season. This could include selected appearances for experienced drivers such as nine-time world champion Sébastien Ogier, who remains part of the GAZOO Racing family. The team will also continue to explore opportunities for young drivers, like those in the GR WRC Challenge Program, who show potential to compete at the top level.

The TGR-WRT name will be retained by the Jyväskylä-based high-performance development company as it moves into an exciting new era. The presentation of the driver line-up at the Jyskä HQ followed an official groundbreaking ceremony the previous day (November 2) at the site of its new technical center now under construction at nearby Majajärvi.

Meanwhile, in the surrounding forests of central Finland, the team is today (November 3) concluding six days of rally simulation testing with its 2027 prototype. The GR Celica Rally1 and GR Yaris Rally1 cars will run to the same new WRC Car regulations – aimed at increasing competition by making the top Rally1 category more accessible – with equal power and performance. With the homologation process due for completion before the end of this year, further details regarding the two cars and their liveries will be released prior to the 2027 season.

GR WRC Challenge Program drivers take the next step

The GAZOO Racing WRC Challenge Program continues to nurture and develop young athletes with the potential to compete on the world stage.

Founded in 2015, the Program played a key role in Takamoto Katsuta’s journey from rally beginner to WRC event winner. It continues to support and develop young Japanese drivers with ambitions of following in Katsuta’s footsteps, and was expanded for 2026 to give opportunities to exceptional young talent from around the world.

Yuki Yamamoto, from the Program’s second generation, took on an expanded schedule of WRC events in 2026 driving the GR Yaris Rally2 car. In the highly competitive WRC2 category, he continued to demonstrate progress with his pace and achieved a landmark first podium on home roads at Rally Japan. Established as his country’s leading contender at Rally2 level and proving the pathway available for aspiring Japanese drivers, Yamamoto will target more strong results in 2027 when he returns to WRC2 for a championship campaign across selected rallies.

In 2026, a trio of drivers made their debut in the GR Yaris Rally2 and gained valuable experience across a range of national, European and World championship events. Young Estonian Jaspar Vaher, who joined the Program ahead of this year following its first-ever selection for global talent, impressed with his performances after stepping up from two-wheel drive machinery, achieving his first overall victories at the Rallye Ciudad de Pozoblanco in Spain and Jyväskylä Ralli in Finland as well as a strong fourth place in WRC2 at Rally Finland. Japan’s Takumi Matsushita, from the Program’s third generation, also claimed an overall win at Rali Terras d'Aboboreira in Portugal and showed promise and progress across different surfaces in what was only his third season of rally competition.

For 2027, Vaher and Matsushita have been chosen to take the next step and gain more experience through an expanded programme of WRC rounds across Europe and in Japan. Their preparation will include competing in the Rallye du Dévoluy in the French Alps this December. Shotaro Goto’s participation with the Program has concluded following the end of his 2026 season. During his three years with the Program he has shown significant growth, and all involved wish him every success for the future.

Rio Ogata and Kanta Yanaguida, the Program’s fourth generation of drivers, made history in 2026 as they finished first and second in the Finnish Rally Championship’s SM2 class, with Ogata becoming the first Japanese driver to ever win a Finnish rally title after a close battle between the pair.

With both showing strong promise while still early in their rally careers, having only contested their first events with the Program in mid-2025, Ogata and Yanaguida will continue at the wheel of Rally3 machinery into next year, making more WRC3 appearances to gain further experience through longer and tougher rallies, while also testing Rally2 cars to prepare for the next step.

Hiroya Minowa, the Program’s newest and youngest driver, made his rally debut in June and has shown encouraging progress across his first six events. For 2027, the 17-year-old will step up from Rally4 to Rally3 machinery, initially continuing to compete in Finland and the Baltic region due to his young age, with the potential for his schedule to be expanded later in the season.

Tomiya Maekawa, the only co-driver in the Program, continued to develop his experience across a variety of events in 2026, competing alongside Jarkko Nikara in Rally3 cars and joining Jari-Matti Latvala for selected historic rally outings. In 2027, Maekawa will become Latvala’s navigator on a more regular basis, focusing on the FIA European Historic Rally Championship.
