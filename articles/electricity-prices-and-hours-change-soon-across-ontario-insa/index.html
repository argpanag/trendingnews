Electricity prices and hours change soon across Ontario | insauga

TheTools · 19.10.2026 16:11 · source · permalink

By Ryan Rocca

Published October 19, 2026 at 12:11 pm

Ontario residents will see changes to electricity prices in the coming weeks.

The Ontario Energy Board has announced new rates that are set to take effect on Nov. 1.

At the same time, winter price periods will begin, which changes when cheaper electricity hours are for those on time-of-use plans.

The changes apply for those who are regulated price plan (RPP) customers, which includes most households, small businesses, and farms in Ontario. RPP customers can choose between time-of-use, ultra-low overnight, and tiered pricing.

Here’s a look at what is changing as of Nov. 1:

Time-of-use prices

  • Off-peak: 9.1 ¢/kWh (down from 9.8 ¢/kWh)
  • Mid-peak: 14.6 ¢/kWh (down from 15.7 ¢/kWh)
  • On-peak: 18.9 ¢/kWh (down from 20.3 ¢/kWh)

Ultra-low overnight

  • Ultra-low overnight: 3.7 ¢/kWh (down from 3.9 ¢/kWh)
  • Weekend off-peak: 9.1 ¢/kWh (down from 9.8 ¢/kWh)
  • Mid-peak: 14.6 ¢/kWh (down from 15.7 ¢/kWh)
  • On-peak: 37.7 ¢/kWh (down from 39.1 ¢/kWh)

Tiered

  • Tier one, which in the winter is the first 1,000 kWh/month for residential customers and the first 750 kWh/month for non-residential: 11.2 ¢/kWh (down from 12 ¢/kWh)
  • Tier two, which is for electricity used above the threshold: 13.2 ¢/kWh (down from 14.2 ¢/kWh)

Changing price periods / thresholds

As mentioned, winter price periods also go into effect on Nov. 1, which means time-of-use customers will see the cheaper times to use electricity change.

The summer price periods are in effect from May 1 to Oct. 31, while the winter period is from Nov. 1 to April 30.

Ultra-low overnight price periods stay the same all year.

Ontario Electricity rebate decreasing

Also on Nov. 1, the Ontario Electricity Rebate (OER) is decreasing to 15.7 per cent from 23.5 per cent.

“The OER provides a rebate on the electricity bill of residential, small business and farm customers,” the Ontario Energy Board said.

“Electricity prices under the OEB’s RPP are decreasing year-over-year, which allows for a reduction in the OER. The overall rate of increase for customer bills will remain consistent with previous years. It appears as a pre-tax credit at the bottom of electricity bills. For a typical residential customer who uses 700 kWh of electricity per month, the OER will provide a rebate of approximately $23 each month.”

The Ontario Energy Board sets prices annually in October.

“These prices are based on the forecast cost of supplying electricity to RPP customers over the next 12 months. Any surplus or shortfall between forecast and actual costs from the prior period is also factored into the price-setting process,” the board said.

“TOU, ULO and Tiered prices are designed to recover the same forecast average cost of supply for a typical residential customer who uses 700 kWh/month.”
