Gukesh vs Sindarov: Everything said at the World Championship 2026 press conference

TheTools · 23.11.2026 10:36 · source · permalink

The Salesforce FIDE World Chess Championship 2026 starts on 24th November in Geneva. It is D Gukesh vs. Javokhir Sindarov, the youngest World Championship match in history, as both players are 20! Before the first game, both players sat down with the media at a press conference on 22nd November. They spoke about their preparation, their teams, the pressure and what they expect from the match. What Gukesh and Sindarov said in the Press Conference

The first game of the Salesforce FIDE World Chess Championship 2026 will begin tomorrow at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny. The match will run until 12th December. Fourteen classical games will decide the World Champion. Both players, Gukesh and Sindarov, are 20 years old, making this the youngest ever duo to play a World Championship match. Before the match, both players faced the press yesterday.

FIDE President Timur Turlov and H.E. Erzhan Kazykhan, Permanent Representative of Kazakhstan to the UN in Geneva, joined the players on stage.

Here is everything that was said at the press conference:

Maurice Ashley: (To Gukesh) You were the challenger just two years ago. The two years must have flown by very quickly. What is it like now? What is the difference between now and then? You were a starry-eyed first-timer coming in. Now you're the seasoned veteran, almost of the two of you. Even though your opponent is older than you are, you're the veteran. How does it feel now as the champion coming into this event? (watch)

Gukesh: Thank you, Maurice, and good evening, everyone. I'm first of all really glad to be here. I'm glad to be in Geneva, part of this match. Compared to the last time, it feels like I'm more experienced coming into the match. But whatever has happened till now, I think, just gets erased when we sit at the table for game one. It's a completely different match, completely different opponent, different place. So I'm not really thinking about other things, just giving my best for these 14 games.

Maurice Ashley: Following up on that, different opponent, different circumstances. Tell us a little bit more: how is it different this time? (watch)

Gukesh: Well, I think Sindarov has just been in very consistent and very good form, and surely one of the very top players at the very top of his game. So for sure it's going to be a real test. But I have prepared the best way that I can, and I'm very enthusiastic for this match, to test myself against such a strong player and to see what I'm capable of.


Maurice Ashley: You are coming in as the challenger. It's your first time in a World Championship match. What has the feeling been like the last six months since you secured your place in the Candidates, and now knowing that the moment is finally here? (watch)

Sindarov: Thanks for the question, Maurice. Hello, everyone. After Cyprus, I don't know, I played, as I said in interviews also, a lot of tournaments in a row. These six months, I thought I had a lot of time, but it went very fast. I was travelling a lot and played many tournaments, but still today I'm here. I'm very happy to fight for this crown, and I'll try to enjoy the match. I know it will be very hard. It's my first experience, and we'll see. But anyway, I'm happy that many chess players play chess their whole lives and don't have a chance to play for this. So I'm happy that today I have a chance to fight for this.

Maurice Ashley: Your opponent had great words, warm words to say about your level. There have been some questions about his level, but we have seen him at his peak level and it's been incredible. What is your anticipation about the challenge that will lie ahead against such a tough opponent? (watch)

Sindarov: Yeah, of course. Gukesh's performance in 2024 is something fantastic. We know how well he played. But of course, if you look at his last year, he's been unlucky. Still, this year I played against him in Tata Steel, and I feel he's really very strong. Sometimes you have some bad periods, but I think it doesn't mean a lot, because we also saw Ding in 2024. He played very bad tournaments, but still, in a match, he played well. So I don't think we need to think about all of this stuff. I'm just trying to play against a strong opponent and trying to enjoy the match.

Robin Millard: (To Gukesh) It's often said in many sports that it's much more difficult to defend a title than having won it in the first place. So I wanted to ask you, what is your perspective on entering this match, not just competing to win the title but competing to defend that title? (watch)

Gukesh: Well, I don't think I'm here to defend anything. I'm here to win it all over again. I think that's the mindset I'm in here.

A content creator: (To Sindarov) I remember after the Candidates, you said in some interviews that going into the event, you really didn't expect to have such a performance. You actually, I believe, estimated your chances to be around 15% before the event. I was curious to hear, what do you estimate your chances to be to win the title now and to actually become the new World Chess Champion? (watch)

Sindarov: I have had many interviews about this, but I think the chances are like 50/50. I don't know. I don't want to say anything because this match will be very tough. I think it's normal to give 50/50 because it's a hard match and 14 games; everything can happen.

Abhyudaya: Something apart from chess. We know that food is a very important part when it comes to the preparation for the World Championship. As you know, Magnus Carlsen brought a private chef for the 2013 World Championship. So this question is for both Gukesh and Sindarov. What does your day-to-day diet look like for a World Championship like this? Is there something different, or is it just your regular diet? (watch)

Gukesh: I generally prefer just food that I'm comfortable with. Usually I eat a lot of Indian food. Some people in Geneva have been very kind to prepare food for me. So I'm getting home-cooked Indian food, which I'm very comfortable with, and I'm really grateful for it.

Sindarov: Actually, I'm the guy who can eat any cuisine. It doesn't matter. But here I'm happy; we have a chef especially for our team who makes food for us every day. Our government also brought some chefs from Uzbekistan. But as I said, I'm really happy to go for any cuisine. If someday I want to go for some Japanese cuisine, I'm very happy to go. So for me especially, it doesn't really matter a lot. I'm happy with everything.

Mike: Javokhir, you had one of the most active run-ups to the World Championship, playing in so many events. What went into your thinking about doing that? And Gukesh, were you surprised that he played so many games and gave you so much to look at before the World Championship? (watch)

Sindarov: We talked about this a little bit with Roman. First, I had the invitation for the Grand Chess Tour before I won the Candidates. Of course, when you get the chance to play such a strong tournament, when you have the opportunity, I immediately agreed to play. But my goal was that, even after I won the Candidates, I wanted to have more experience because I didn't play that many strong tournaments in my career. So probably from this year I really started playing such strong tournaments like Tata Steel. That's why I chose to play many tournaments, and I'm happy with my decision.

Gukesh: Not really. My team and I kind of expected that he'd play more than me. We had decided to slow down the tournaments quite a bit this year. But basically, for the same reason that Sindarov had said, this is kind of the first year that he has been playing at the very elite level. We just thought it made sense that he'll make use of the good momentum and he'll get more experience against the top guys. So it was not a surprise at all.

Amit: My question is to both of you. Yesterday you saw the playing hall for the first time, and also tested the chairs a little bit. What is it that you're looking for when you're testing chairs, and what is your opinion about the playing hall? It's a very unique setup. No glass cage; it's surrounded by books. I don't know how much you read, but what's your opinion about those things? (watch)

Gukesh: I checked the playing hall yesterday. It was basically checking whether I'd be able to feel comfortable for four or five hours every day. I checked the chairs, the lighting, the floors, different kinds of things that can bother you during a game, I guess. But I was happy with pretty much everything. About the books, I just saw there were a lot of books. I didn't really bother to check what books there are, but I don't think I'll be reading in between the games.

Sindarov: It's my first time when there is nobody playing around us, only the two. It can be boring sometimes because you don't know whom to watch. Even in some other tournaments when I played, there should be someone whom I always watch. But here, okay. In general, we saw our rooms, everything. The playing hall is really very beautiful. I was thinking I would play in this box, but it still looks very fine. I hope for a great match; what can I say.

Andrea: (To Sindarov) Cyprus is a very special country for you. You have already visited three times this year. After winning the Candidates tournament, you even got a suite named after you at Cap St Georges. Could you tell us how your training camp in October in Cyprus was going? (watch)

Sindarov: It was a great memory. I came back after the Candidates to the Cap St Georges hotel, and I want to say my thanks to the managers of the hotel. We had a great camp there. Somehow I met Hikaru there also. He was also resting. I don't know what he was doing there. He said he was preparing for the Clutch Chess. But in general, I had a very good rest there and good preparation. Our team was happy, so I'm really happy. Maybe soon I will also come back to this place. I enjoy it.

Himank Ghosh: My question is to both Gukesh and Sindarov. As we know, Gukesh is here with his longtime second, Gajewski, and Sindarov with his coach Roman Vidonyak. But apart from these two people, are there any other team members or seconds you would like to reveal before the World Championship match? (watch)

Gukesh: I'm here with Gajewski, and other team members I would like to reveal later.


Sindarov: On my team, here is Roman, and everyone knows Mukhiddin is also here. Also, Nikita Vitiugov is helping me here. I have other team members, but maybe after the match I will also reveal.


Jonathan: A question to both of you. What does it mean to prepare for a chess World Championship match? And I see all these logos behind you. What changed in the era of AI? So many digital options to prepare: databases, solutions. How does it feel and what does it mean, to prepare for a World Chess Championship match? (watch)

Sindarov: In my opinion, let's take the last six years; engines have changed a lot. Sometimes it becomes boring to prepare such long lines because before, engines were showing some lines as very bad, but now it's a completely different evaluation there. So that's what changed in the last six years. In my opinion, chess has become stronger. In general, openings mean a lot, but still you have to play very good chess. I will say, compared to other matches ten years ago, opening preparation has changed really a lot, in my opinion. So that's the main point right now.

Gukesh: I guess the clear answer is that opening prep has become much more computer-based, and it's very hard to disagree with the computers sometimes. But also, for example, the general level keeps rising. The more people work with computers, they also learn a lot more ideas. Not just opening ideas, but in general they understand structures better, defence gets better, because the computer is always very resourceful. I think just working with computers has increased the level of chess by humans and made it much harder to win games, I guess. But also I think it makes it more of a sport. Everybody has access to the same or similar things that help them, like engines. Top players mostly have seconds, coaches, stuff, but you need to find something that would help you be slightly better than the rest, which is what sport is about, I think.

Theo: My question is for Javokhir Sindarov. First of all, congratulations because you became number one on Lichess in the bullet leaderboards. I wanted to ask you a question about preparation as well. Mainly, as this is your first time in a match format, can you go into some depth about how different this experience of preparation against one opponent is versus something like a Swiss tournament or a round robin, where you have multiple opponents? (watch)

Sindarov: The most different part of preparation, from Swiss tournaments or small mini-matches to a big match, is opening things. You need to work really a lot. I was thinking six months; I have enough time, but maybe even these six months, okay, I played many tournaments, but still it's also maybe not enough to prepare for such a hard tournament. It's my first time, and I'm starting to understand that. When someone told me it takes three or four months to prepare for a World Championship match, I never believed that it could be normal. But now I understand how preparation is going. It was really hard but exciting, I will say. I'm enjoying our part of training.

Susana: I have a question for both of you guys. Previously, Gukesh, you said, if I win and I become a World Champion, it's kind of on a lighter note, okay, you said you would do bungee jumping and you kept your word. In the past, we have seen chess players jumping in the pool dressed. You might have promised to show the trophy to the president. Is there any other bet or personal promise you have made, both of you, that you're going to keep once you become the World Champion this time? (watch)

Sindarov: I think I had the same question today in interviews: what will you do? But I really don't think about this. I don't have any bets about this, really. I tried many things in life, so I have no idea what I will do. But I want to really focus and not think about this, like, if you win, blah blah blah. The first thing I really want to do is focus on my next 20 days and try to play my best chess, and then we'll see. I don't know. I don't want to say anything.

Gukesh: I have a couple of things in mind. I'll do them. I think you'll get to know in time.

Sophia: My question is for both of you. Is it your first time in Switzerland, and how do you like it here? Or maybe do you plan to visit something on the free day? (watch)

Gukesh: No. This is my, I don't know, third or fourth time, actually. I played a few tournaments here. Also went on a little vacation to Interlaken. That was pretty nice also. I don't have any plans for the rest day, but just the hotel we are staying in is quite nice, just in front of the lake. Maybe after the tournament I'll do something here.

Sindarov: For me also, my first time was in 2024. I went to many places here. I recommended these places to my family also. My coach Roman lives in Munich, it's very close to Switzerland, and we came here by car many times, maybe not many times, once. He has a lot of students here, and I met many of his students. It's my third time here. I have never been in Geneva. Geneva is very beautiful. I like the lake, everything here, and the weather is also very good right now because I thought it would be colder. But somehow it's very good weather. I'm enjoying the city. For the rest day, I will probably play more video games. (laughs)

Journalist, Spanish news agency: I would like to ask on a more personal level. What important things do you think you had to give up as children, teenagers or young people, as you are now, to be at the level that you are and to compete for the world title? (watch)

Sindarov: I don't know. All of my life I never worked seriously until 2025. I know that I always have a great talent in chess. Not in chess, maybe in every game, because I have something that if I play some games, I always try to win. It was in any game. When I started working on chess, I became stronger. So I recommend that everyone work on anything they like. If you love to play football, work on football, because with hard work I think everything will also come, in my opinion. I will say, really, the last two years I worked a lot and I won a lot of tournaments. Also, I'm lucky a little bit, but still I work a lot. So I recommend to all the children everywhere, in any sport, to work a lot on yourselves. That means a lot, in my opinion.

Gukesh: The things I gave up were probably just a lot of time for different things. Maybe in childhood I didn't really go to school or anything like that. But also I'm grateful that this is a kind of life that not many people get to live, and surely many people wouldn't mind. So I'm grateful for it. There are some things maybe that I did miss, but it all felt worth it. Now also through chess I developed a lot of other interests that I make time for these days. So no, I don't really feel like I would have it any other way.

Journalist, German newspaper: You mentioned the rise of chess and the interest in chess worldwide. Now we are here in Geneva, surrounded by chess countries. At the last championships in Singapore and in Dubai, there were large live audiences which made a chess feast. So why is FIDE excluding a live audience here from the playing hall? (watch)

Turlov: I think we found a beautiful playing hall here, but it's also very small. We definitely have already successfully sold some tickets for the audience to be able to participate. But the format and the number of people who will be able to participate, of course, are very limited here. We decided that for us now it's much more important to create good conditions for players, while also fitting this championship to Geneva. Frankly, we found a very beautiful location, but it was not so easy to book a location on relatively short notice. Maybe in further preparation we will have more opportunities and more options to, at the same time, build the same level of comfort for participants and still for observers and fans. I hope that will be our task for future championships.

Reporter, Swiss radio: A question for both players. How did you prepare yourself mentally and physically for this game? (watch)

Gukesh: Physically, in general, I play a lot of different sports. I was also surfing quite a bit. Some mild workouts, but mostly I just stayed active, very much outdoors, sports or running and stuff like that. Mentally, I'm continuing to work with my sports psychologist Paddy Upton. That has also been going great. He has been very helpful since the time we started working together. Other than that, I read from time to time about different kinds of stuff. The usual stuff, nothing special.

Sindarov: Physically, I also love to play many sports. Probably before Cyprus I started, but during Cyprus I also played; I started playing padel and I started loving this sport. I have played a lot in my camps also. So I think you also have to do some physical things. I feel okay with this, and also I'm young for such a hard match. If I were 30 or 40, maybe it would be tough, but because I'm young, I think it will not be very hard physically. But mentally I think it's important, and I work on that with my coaches. Everything is fine.

Maurice Ashley: Who wins when you play padel?

Sindarov: Mostly, you know my character, I always try to win. We played a lot in summer camp, my grandfather and I against both Nodirbeks. Sometimes it was our day, sometimes it was their day. So basically I think it finished at the end with maybe the same result.

Maurice Ashley: How old is your grandfather?

Sindarov: He's good. He's really good at tennis. He loves to play tennis.

Theo: You heard your challenger Javokhir saying this is his first time preparing to this extent, in a match format, taking three or four months of preparation. Obviously this isn't your first time doing that. But given you already had the experience of preparing for a match, was it easier to do it a second time, or in a way was it harder to do it because you kind of knew the difficulty of the work ahead of you? (watch)

Gukesh: I think the preparation kind of differs from match to match. Also I think in 2024 I was playing a lot more than I did this year. It depends. Sometimes it just feels like hard work when you do certain kinds of work. Sometimes it just feels so much like fun and creativity flowing around. Also I think just working with the team that I enjoy being around is one of the most important things for me, so that even when the work is exhausting or kind of stressful, you're still surrounded by people you like being around, right? So that gives you a lot of strength. In that sense, I feel like the best part about this whole World Championship journey is the training camps. It's so much fun, so much good work, but so much laughing, so many jokes. With Gajewski I share a very close personal bond. The training camps are just probably the things that I will most remember after the match.

Maxim, Memorable Stories Magazine: What is one value, one piece of advice, one lesson that you will keep from your mentors, from your family, with you on the chessboard and off the chessboard? (watch)

Gukesh: I think I've gotten a lot of advice from my team, my friends, my family. Probably the ones that stick the most with me are in difficult moments when somebody says, just go there and express yourself; it doesn't matter what happens, we are there for you. Also, one thing that the last couple of months, a few months, has stuck with me is somebody said, "Remember who you are." I think in this match I will remember that.

Sindarov: I don't have anything that I always take for every moment of my life. But during a hard period, I think it's important, and my friends and my family always support me during tournaments if I have some problems. That's why I really say my big thanks to them, because in every hard moment in my life they always give me a hand to help me. I think it's very hard to have such strong results if you don't have good friends and family in front of you. So I think it's very important.

Robin Millard: I'd like to ask both of you how well you think you know each other's games. Have you been putting a lot of work into studying each other's strengths? (watch)

Sindarov: I think we don't play too many games against each other. So I learn from his games also a lot. I will say he has very good strength in some typical positions, and his calculation is very good. He has something that he can fight, Gukesh can fight until the end. I can remember the game, how he pushed against Wei Yi, a very important game in the Olympiad 2024. That's really a very good strength in chess, in my opinion.

Gukesh: When you play a World Championship, it's very nice that it's just one opponent, so you can put full attention on that one specific player. There's a lot of analysing your opponent going on, but also only to an extent, because most of the guys, especially Sindarov, are very much universal. You can play any position at a decent level. It's just sharpening your skills, understanding your opponent to a good degree. I don't think there's more than that.

Journalist, South Korea: You said chess will matter more in the age of AI. Korea is still a small chess market, but I think it's going to grow, I hope. For countries like that, what does FIDE think actually builds a chess culture? (watch)

Turlov: I'm sure that AI can open up a lot of opportunities for the chess industry, not just for preparation. It's already been there for a long period of time, so AI started from chess. I'm also sure that AI can help to improve chess broadcasts and help to build broadcasts more interesting for beginners, for players below 1000 Elo, which is the majority on chess platforms. It's definitely hundreds of millions of people who actually play chess but are just beginners. For them now sometimes it's difficult to understand the chess broadcast, very difficult to understand the beauty of the game. But I'm sure that with the help of AI we can let them better understand what happens in a grandmaster's mind during the game and how beautiful this work could be, and thinking about.

I'm sure that may dramatically increase the popularity of the sport and drive growth of the commercial value of chess, growth of chess broadcasts in general, and at the end of the day inspire even more people to play chess. I'm sure that also the impact of chess on society is great because it helps to build concentration. In this fast-moving world, in the world of social media, it really helps to keep up the minds of many people. In my opinion, it's a great combination because the popularity of chess will continue to grow because of AI. At the same time, the popularity of the game will continue to grow because it's beautiful, it's interesting, and it's getting better explained every next year, which equalises opportunities for multiple people to study chess, to improve themselves, to get a first-class chess scholarship almost for free because of AI. In my opinion, that can open up a lot of new stars in different emerging countries who had very limited access to grandmaster coaching before.
