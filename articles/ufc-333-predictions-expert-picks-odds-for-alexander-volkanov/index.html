UFC 333 predictions: Expert picks, odds for Alexander Volkanovski vs. Movsar Evloev fight card in Abu Dhabi

TheTools · 25.10.2026 01:08 · source · permalink

Check out who the experts at CBS Sports are taking in the pair of title fights on Saturday

A big night of fights is set to go down in Abu Dhabi on Saturday. The UFC 333 card is headlined by a pair of title fights, including undefeated Movsar Evloev challenging Alexander Volkanovski for the UFC featherweight championship in the main event.

Evloev holds a perfect 20-0 professional record, but he'll face the biggest test of his career in Volkanovski, who is on his second run as 145-pound champion at age 38.

In the co-main event, Petr Yan and Merab Dvalishvili will clash in a rubber match for the bantamweight championship. Dvalishvili won their first meeting en route to winning the championship and a lengthy title run, but dropped the belt to Yan in their December rematch.

Every fight on the main card has meaningful implications for the UFC rankings. If Alexander Volkov wants the UFC heavyweight title shot he petitioned UFC President and CEO Dana White for, he'll need to make a statement against Rizvan Kuniev. That's especially true with Ciryl Gane vs. Josh Hokit and Sergei Pavlovich vs. Alex Pereira coming up at UFC 334 and UFC 335, respectively. 

Lone'er Kavanagh and Ramazan Temirov could be major flyweight players, but only one promising fighter will take a step forward in their division on Saturday. Lastly, Aaron Pico is running short on time to prove he's everything analysts predicted he'd be when he debuted in MMA in 2017. Nearly a decade later, after several ups and downs, Pico is a ranked bantamweight looking to fend off Losene Keita. It's now or never for Pico to make good on lofty expectations.

With so much happening on Saturday night, let's look more closely at the full fight card with the latest odds before we get to our staff predictions and picks for the main card that you can consider before hitting the sportsbooks.

UFC 333 expert picks

With such a massive main event on tap, the crew at CBS Sports went ahead with predictions and picks for the main card from Brian Campbell, Brent Brookhouse, Shakiel Mahjouri, Michael Mormile and Brandon Wise.

Alexander Volkanovski vs. Movsar Evloev predictions

Campbell: Volkanovski's longevity has been remarkable to see, especially after he bounced back from stoppage losses to Islam Makhachev and Ilia Topuria to regain the featherweight title. But it must be said that both of his victories since those damaging defeats came against the all-action but technically limited Diego Lopes, whom Evloev defeated by decision in 2023. For whatever Evloev lacks as a finisher inside the Octagon, he has size, an incredible motor and a physical grappling style that has the potential to wear down the 38-year-old Volkanovski in the championship rounds. He also has a better fight IQ than what Lopes showed against Volkanovski over 10 rounds. 

Brookhouse: There are few fights where I will pick against Volkanovski until I've truly seen time catch up to him. Evloev has tremendous wrestling and a willingness to use that wrestling to grind out ugly wins, but Volkanovski has a well-rounded game that also features strong takedown defense. Volkanovski has been here before and he's got the kind of gritty, gutsy game that should require Evloev to do more than just wrestle. While Evloev has shown some strong skills when forced to fight on the feet, Volkanovski has brilliant timing and distance management tools that should let him edge out the fight on the cards.

Mahjouri: Volkanovski says he'll deliver such a sublime performance that fans will think he's de-aged. I'm inclined to believe him. The champion doesn't mean to disrespect his challenger. In fact, he believes Evloev is supremely talented. Volkanovski's confidence stems from the style matchup. He sharpened his grappling defense in preparation for two lightweight fights with Islam Makhachev. On the feet, it's his fight to lose. Volkanovski has a deeper, more nuanced striking arsenal and can fight without fear of being knocked out. Evloev has stopped three opponents by strikes, and none were in the UFC. Evloev is tenacious and will make Volkanovski work early. However, as the fight goes on, expect Volkanovski to break ahead and deliver exactly the performance he predicts.

Wise: Evloev has seemed like a "champion-in-waiting" for a few years now. He has one of the most well-rounded games in the sport that just lacks the finishing blow with all his fights going the distance. While Volkanovski has long been the gold standard at 145 pounds, age is bound to catch up with him, as is the damage absorbed over the course of a long UFC run. While Diego Lopes was unable to crack the code in a pure standup battle across two fights, Evloev has the ability to mix things up well and give Volkanovski a different look than he has seen in his recent fights at featherweight. Expect high-speed chess in this one, but for the challenger to walk away victorious. 

Petr Yan vs. Merab Dvalishvili predictions

Campbell: After Yan rebounded from a series of losses and injuries, he returned against Dvalishvili a new fighter in their December rematch. Yan took "The Machine" down five times, shut down Dvalishvili's wrestling attack and proved he could match him from the standpoint of pace and cardio. And if Yan can do the same and make their trilogy bout into a kickboxing match, it's hard not to imagine the same scenario from 10 months ago happening again. Dvalishvili was also hurt multiple times in their rematch, including on a pair of body kicks in Round 3 that likely would've finished any other opponent. If Yan can continue to widen the gap between them in terms of striking variety and potency, a stoppage isn't out of the question.

Brookhouse: This feels like an impossible fight to pick. Yan fought brilliantly in the rematch, defending against takedowns and using great striking, including a huge body kick that badly hurt Dvalishvili. However, it's hard to know if that fight was a product of Yan hitting a new level -- or at least finding his way back to his old form -- or Dvalishvili seeing a wildly ambitious 2025 schedule catch up to him and impact his performance. My best guess is that Saturday's rubber match is an extremely close fight, and one Yan edges by his best moments on the feet being more eye-catching than what Dvalishvili produces.

Mahjouri: Dvalishvili earned the nickname "The Machine" because no human could keep up with him. Eventually, his pace broke everyone. What happens when someone can match that pace for 25 minutes? That's exactly what Yan did in his major upset win over Dvalishvili. Yan was always the more technical, powerful striker. By denying Dvalishvili's relentless takedowns and keeping pace, he chipped away with his strikes, most notably hurting Dvalishvili to the body. Maybe Dvalishvili pushed his body too far in attempting to make four title defenses in one year. But I'm inclined to believe Yan is uniquely equipped for this task. Dvalishvili's success depends on a physicality that Yan has proven he can match. I don't think the former champion has the offensive threats to change the outcome in this trilogy.
