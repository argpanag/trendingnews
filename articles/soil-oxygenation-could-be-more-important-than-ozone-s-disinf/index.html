"Soil oxygenation could be more important than ozone's disinfection effect"

TheTools · 14.09.2026 16:57 · source · permalink

In recent years, ozone use in agriculture, especially for treating irrigation water, has increased, as Benito Navarro of Green Ozone notes. Its strong oxidizing ability, which plays a decisive role in sustaining life on Earth by accumulating in the stratosphere to form the ozone layer, makes ozone an effective disinfectant.

© Green Ozone

"Chemically speaking, ozone is a molecule made up of three oxygen atoms, and that extra atom is extremely unstable, so it eventually breaks away and oxidizes nearby substances," explains Navarro. Due to its strong oxidizing properties, careful regulation of its production, incorporation, and application is crucial.

"Ozone is applied directly to irrigation water in the form of micro- and nanobubbles for different purposes. One purpose is to disinfect water contaminated by an agent or pathogen. Another is to eliminate the biofilm that develops inside pipes, which can reduce the effective diameter of the pipes and cause blockages in drippers," he said.

"When ozonated water reaches the soil, a notable agronomic phenomenon occurs. Because ozone is highly unstable, it quickly reverts to oxygen, increasing oxygen availability in the root zone—especially important in heavily tilled, compacted, or low-porosity soils," he added.

© Green Ozone

Oxygen for soils subjected to years of cultivation
Loss of aeration frequently occurs in certain agricultural soils that face prolonged machinery traffic, regular irrigation, and diverse cultivation practices, whether in intensive horticulture or woody crops. "As the pore space that holds air diminishes, the conditions around roots can deteriorate. Therefore, oxygen from irrigation water may sometimes be more important than the initial oxidizing effect of ozone itself," he explained.

Interest in soil oxygenation has extended to adapting the approach for production systems that want to prevent ozone's oxidizing effect. For example, some farms, including biodynamic farms, use oxygen-enriched water directly for this purpose.

© Green Ozone

Ozone use depends heavily on how the gas is produced because it cannot be stored for later use, unlike many agricultural inputs. "Its high instability means it must be produced right before use, which is why Green Ozone equipment is typically set up in irrigation huts to generate and inject ozone when it is operated."

"Because ozone has no residual effect, we recommend maintaining ozone injection throughout irrigation cycles to maximize its disinfecting and oxygenating effects. However, caution is needed when applying humic or fulvic acids and certain plant-protection products, as ozone may react with these substances and diminish their effectiveness," Benito Navarro advised.

From greenhouses to citrus, vineyards and olive groves
Green Ozone is primarily used in protected horticulture areas, where high production, continuous irrigation, and soil compaction often cause multiple problems at once. However, it is also being applied to open-air crops such as citrus, stone fruits, and woody plants.

© Green Ozone

"Contaminated water, biofilm, or oxygenation issues affect all crops. For example, we collaborate with an Italian distributor serving vineyards in the Modena region, whose grapes are used to make vinegar," he stated.

© Green OzoneThe business model depends on distributors who can design and advise on local installations. Green Ozone is actively collaborating with them in markets like France, Portugal, Italy, Mexico, and Morocco, and has recently started operations in Algeria where, Benito notes, "there is great potential for us to expand."

Ozone is also introduced into the fumigation tank
However, irrigation is not the only agricultural application Green Ozone is pursuing; in recent years, they have also developed systems to directly ozonize water used in spray treatments.

"Ozone has shown effectiveness against pests when used in spraying strategies. Ozonated water can damage bacterial cell membranes upon contact. Additionally, our tests indicate it has promising ovicidal effects on pests like red spider mites. Beyond its disinfectant and oxygenating qualities, ozone could also help manage specific pests within integrated pest management systems," Navarro stated.
