Morrisey order allows red-dyed off-road diesel on highways, cutting costs about 20 cents

CHARLESTON, W.Va. (WCHS) — Off-road diesel, also known as red-dyed diesel, has a variety of uses.

"Farmers, construction industry, railroad industry, generators, things like that," Oliver Fuels and Oils owner Jill Oliver said.

With Gov. Patrick Morrisey's new executive order allowing the use of off-road diesel on the highway, it's opening the door for more uses.

Morrisey said doing this will provide temporary relief to industries like coal, trucking, agriculture and more, saving drivers around 20 cents per gallon.

Oliver has been in the fuel industry for decades. She said when it comes to the difference between regular diesel and off-road, it all comes down to two things.

"Both diesels are ultra-low sulfur diesel fuels," Oliver said. "The off-road diesel has a red dye in it to show that you did not pay the West Virginia and federal excise taxes."

Red-dyed diesel typically comes in cheaper than regular highway diesel. However, it cannot be found everywhere. It can be found at commercial fuel stations, some farm suppliers, bulk distributors as well as other places.

Oliver said she worries in the long run the governor's executive order could be costly.

"The pink dye is very potent," she said. "The red-dye pink is very potent. It's going to be hard to get out of your filters, your lines, your tanks in the future when this is over. I just think it's going to cost you more money in the long run."
