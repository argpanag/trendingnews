“Those determined to go will go anyway” ... 230,000 Head to Jeju, Airlines See a Windfall

A steady flow of tourists to Jeju Province is expected during this year's Chuseok holiday as well. Despite recent controversies, including the long-term disappearance of a woman in her 30s, tourism demand has not been significantly affected. Airlines are moving to expand capacity on flights to Jeju, targeting the holiday travel boom.

According to the Jeju Tourism Association on the 25th, a total of 229,000 people are expected to visit Jeju Province by air and sea over the five days from the 23rd, the day before the Chuseok holiday, through the 27th, the final day of the holiday. Although that figure is 4.4% lower than the 239,596 visitors recorded during last year's Chuseok holiday, as many as 192,000 of this year's visitors are expected to travel by air, keeping demand for flights to Jeju solid.

The aviation industry recognized this trend and began preparing to expand capacity several weeks ago. Korean Air has added 20 special mileage flights on the Gimpo–Jeju route, covering the Chuseok holiday through next month's National Foundation Day and Hangeul Day holidays. The measure reflects the significant number of people planning trips to Jeju using the airline's mileage program and provides about 3,160 additional seats. In effect, the airline is flexibly increasing capacity in response to peak-season demand for Jeju.

Eastar Jet has also added 32 flights on the Gimpo–Jeju route. From the 19th of this month through the 11th of next month, it will add a total of 434 flights across five routes to Jeju, Japan, Taiwan and other destinations, providing about 82,000 seats. The strategy is to increase seating across short-haul routes overall in preparation for the peak autumn travel season, including the holiday period.

Jeju Air has moved beyond short-term increases in flights, extending the operating period and frequency of its Jeju route itself. The airline has extended the operating period of its Incheon–Jeju route, which resumed last May, from next month through March next year. Starting in November, it will also increase service from two flights a week to three. The route's average load factor stood at 87.1% through the end of last month.

According to the aviation and tourism industries, demand among visitors to Jeju was not significantly affected despite controversies last month, including the “false closure of a missing-person case.” A total of 7,464,545 domestic and foreign visitors traveled to Jeju last month, an increase of 0.8% from the same period a year earlier. The numbers of both domestic and foreign visitors rose.

An aviation industry official analyzed the situation, saying, “Office workers who want to spend the holiday without taking extra leave appear to prefer nearby destinations that can be visited easily during the roughly four-day Chuseok holiday. As airlines' fuel surcharges rise again, there is demand from travelers who want to head to Jeju, where airfares are at least somewhat cheaper than those for overseas destinations.”

Jeju International Airport is also preparing for the holiday demand. A total of 2,548 flights are scheduled to operate from the 23rd through the 27th of this month, and the number of passengers using the airport is expected to exceed 430,000. With an average of more than 87,000 passengers a day, airport congestion during the holiday period is expected to be heavier than usual.

The aviation industry is also taking note that recent demand for travel to Jeju has not been concentrated solely among domestic travelers. Jeju Air carried 160,000 foreign passengers on its Gimpo–Jeju and Incheon–Jeju routes from January through August this year, an increase of 28.8% from the same period last year. The share of foreign passengers on the Incheon–Jeju route has also risen to 30.4%.

As foreign tourists add to domestic travel demand, the potential to reduce the seasonal volatility of flights to Jeju is growing. Competition among airlines for seats is therefore expected to intensify further.
