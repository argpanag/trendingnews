Vinícius Júnior went from transforming Real Madrid's attack to being sent off following a VAR review. The Brazilian faces a potential multi-game suspension that could rule him out of El Clásico against Barcelona.

From the Bench to the Spotlight at the Santiago Bernabéu

What initially looked like a night of redemption for Vinícius Júnior turned into one of the biggest controversies of the LaLiga matchday. The Brazilian forward was sent off during Real Madrid's 1-0 victory over Villarreal after VAR detected an act of violent conduct before a play that had ended in a goal.

The story began on the bench. José Mourinho decided to leave the Brazilian out of the starting lineup, bringing him on in the 52nd minute to replace Trent Alexander-Arnold, with the score still tied at 0-0.

His introduction immediately changed the attacking dynamics of Los Blancos. In one of his first contributions, Vinícius cut inside and delivered a spectacular outside-of-the-foot pass to Yan Diomandé, although his teammate failed to capitalize on the opportunity.

The Brazilian began creating problems down the left flank and quickly became one of Real Madrid's most dangerous attacking players.

From Scoring a Goal to Receiving a Red Card in Minutes

The major controversy arrived in the 71st minute. After an extraordinary individual run by Jude Bellingham, Vinícius Júnior received the ball and found the back of the net for what appeared to be Real Madrid's second goal of the night.

However, while the Santiago Bernabéu celebrated, VAR began reviewing an incident that had occurred before the counterattack.

The footage showed an altercation between Vinícius and Nathan-Dylan Saliba, a Villarreal player, in which the Brazilian pulled his opponent's hair and made a striking motion.

Referee Isidro Díaz de Mera went to the pitchside monitor to review the incident and made two crucial decisions: disallowing the goal because of the earlier foul and showing the Real Madrid forward a straight red card.

The decision sparked enormous controversy inside the stadium. What had seemed like a perfect night for Vinícius, after changing the pace of the match from the bench, ended with the Brazilian leaving the pitch before the final whistle.

 

How Many Games Could Vinícius Miss? El Clásico Is at Risk

Real Madrid's concerns do not end with the sending-off. The club must now wait for the disciplinary ruling to determine how many matches its forward will be suspended for.

According to initial reports from the Spanish media, the incident could be classified as violent conduct away from the play, an offense that could carry a two- to three-match suspension under Article 103.2 of the disciplinary regulations. Other assessments suggest the punishment could reach four games, depending on the final classification of the incident.

The schedule makes the situation particularly concerning.

Real Madrid will host Sevilla on October 18, while El Clásico against Barcelona is scheduled for Sunday, October 25, at Camp Nou.

This means that a suspension of at least two LaLiga matches would rule Vinícius out of the showdown against the Catalan giants.

Although Real Madrid also has Champions League fixtures against Roma and RB Leipzig, those matches would not automatically count toward serving a suspension imposed in the domestic competition.

For now, the length of the suspension has not been officially confirmed. The referee's match report and the disciplinary committee's ruling will determine the Brazilian's immediate future.

A night that began with Vinícius waiting for his opportunity on the bench could end up having much greater consequences for Real Madrid in its pursuit of the LaLiga title.

Key Takeaways From Vinícius' Red Card

  • Vinícius Júnior started on the bench, entered the match in the 52nd minute, and transformed Real Madrid's attack against Villarreal.
  • The Brazilian scored in the 71st minute, but VAR disallowed his goal and led to his dismissal after identifying an act of violent conduct against Nathan-Dylan Saliba.
  • Vinícius could face a multi-game suspension and miss El Clásico against Barcelona on October 25, although no official disciplinary decision has been announced.
