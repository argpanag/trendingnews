Bruce Campbell says he has five years to live, "give or take"

TheTools · 05.09.2026 00:45 · source · permalink

In March, the iconic Evil Dead star shared he has been diagnosed with cancer.

After disclosing his cancer diagnosis earlier this year, Bruce Campbell has elaborated on just how serious his diagnosis is on a recent episode of The Adam Carolla Show. Fans of the actor may want to brace themselves. According to Campbell, he has five years to live, “give or take.” 

Campbell has death on his mind, not only because of his recent diagnosis, but ironically, his new film Ernie & Emma, which he wrote, directed, and stars in, follows a widower (Campbell) spreading his wife’s ashes. So the Evil Dead star tells Carolla about the experience of his grandfather’s passing, which inspired him to invite his kids to watch his own father’s death in hospice. “It was a beautiful exit,” he says. “None of it disturbed them in any way.” It’s a rather graceful way of looking at this painful experience that, as Campbell points out, many people don’t want to confront. This prompted Carolla—and we promise not to spend too much time on this—to bring up that, like death, “a lot of the pedophilia stuff gets by because people don’t want to think about it.” The host then name-checked Penn State, Jerry Sandusky, and Joe Paterno. Anyway, Campbell steers the interview back on the road to discuss finding humor in the Bryan Johnsons of the world who try to cheat death. “You’re not really living,” Campbell said. “You just have a new hobby that you’re obsessed with. What is the quality of these extra years?” This jogs something in Carolla’s mind, and he asks, “Didn’t you have a health scare some time ago?” 

“I wouldn’t even call it a ‘scare.’ It was an arrival.” Campbell says. “It’s a cancer arrival that is still existing. It’s a very fascinating time of having a movie about a guy who’s spreading his wife’s ashes who died, made by a guy who’s dying. The current thing is a five-year deal […] Give or take.”

For his part, Campbell doesn’t seem uncomfortable or offended that Carolla evoked a strangely specific child sex abuse scandal in the middle of discussing his limited mortality. But that’s how Campbell is rolling these days. He’s not running from his diagnosis, and instead he’s living his life the way he wants. “I’ve decided, pretty specifically, to live with it, not die from it,” Campbell said. “You can go down a massive rabbit hole. The cancer rabbit hole is enormous. You can go into focus groups of 10,000 guys who have what you have. I wouldn’t touch it with a 10-foot pole.”

Campbell is currently on tour with Ernie & Emma, traveling to Alamo Drafthouses across the U.S. The tour begins today in Los Angeles and ends on November 13 in Littleton, Colorado. The If Chins Could Kill author shared his diagnosis in March, saying he has “a type of cancer that’s ‘treatable,’ not ‘curable.'” He also said that if “treatment goes well,” he’d be on tour with the film this summer, so we guess it’s a positive update that the 67-year-old is doing just that.
