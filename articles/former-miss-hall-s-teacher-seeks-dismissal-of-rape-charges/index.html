Former Miss Hall’s teacher seeks dismissal of rape charges

TheTools · 29.10.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

MISS HALL'S SCHOOL ALLEGATIONS

The allegations date to 2009 and 2015. A hearing is scheduled for Nov. 12 in Berkshire County Superior Court.

PITTSFIELD — A former Miss Hall’s School teacher accused of rape wants all three charges dismissed, arguing that two are too old to prosecute and that prosecutors withheld evidence from the grand jury about the third.

Matthew Rutledge was indicted in March on three counts of rape after two former students, Melissa Fares and Hilary Simon, publicly accused him of grooming and raping them. Rutledge pleaded not guilty to the charges.

His attorney, Ryan Schiff, filed the motion to dismiss on Oct. 13. The prosecution has not yet filed a response, according to online court records.

In the motion, Schiff argues the first two counts of rape, which stem from allegations dating back to 2009, must be dismissed because they are outside of the standard 15-year statute of limitations.

Prosecutors argue that the 15-year statute of limitations was paused for a total of 994 days while Rutledge vacationed outside Massachusetts. According to Schiff, those trips averaged about five days each and were spread over 17 years.

Schiff argues that Rutledge remained a Massachusetts resident during those trips, so the clock should not have stopped. He also claims prosecutors relied on incomplete travel records and had to speculate about the length of roughly half his trips.

The third rape charge stems from an alleged encounter in 2015. Schiff argues that charge should be dismissed because the prosecution did not inform the grand jury that, during a police interview, "the alleged victim … did not allege that the encounter occurred without her consent or was rape." 

According to Schiff, she "first made" the allegation about the 2015 encounter after the Berkshire District Attorney's Office publicly announced that it would not pursue criminal charges against Rutledge.

Berkshire District Attorney Timothy Shugrue initially declined to prosecute Rutledge in October 2024, stating that Fares and Simon were at least 16 when the alleged sexual activity began, making them old enough to legally consent under Massachusetts law at the time.

Fares and Simon, however, maintained that the encounters were nonconsensual. Prosecutors later reconsidered the allegations and sought the indictments after a separate investigation commissioned by Miss Hall’s School that identified additional evidence of alleged abuse by Rutledge.

The case built support for the Trusted Adults Act, which was signed into law in July and makes it a crime for teachers and other adults in positions of authority to engage in sexual activity with 16- and 17-year-olds under their supervision.

Schiff did not say in his filing when the police interview that pertained to the 2015 encounter occurred. However, both Fares and Simon had publicly accused Rutledge of sexual abuse by April 2024, months before the Berkshire District Attorney's Office first announced it would not bring charges.

The revelations thrust the prestigious private school into turmoil, and led to two more indictments, with former head of school Jeannie Norris and former teacher Matthew Johnson also facing charges to which both pleaded not guilty.

Norris is charged with reckless child endangerment for what prosecutors say was inaction on allegations of "inappropriate behavior" between a teacher and student.

Johnson was charged with assault with intent to rape and two counts of indecent assault and battery against a person over the age of 14. The charges stem from his tenure as a science teacher during the 1993-94 school year.

Arguments on Schiff's motion to dismiss are scheduled for Nov. 12 in Berkshire County Superior Court.
