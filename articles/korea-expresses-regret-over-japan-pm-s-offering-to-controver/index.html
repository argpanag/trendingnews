Korea expresses regret over Japan PM's offering to controversial war shrine - The Korea Times

TheTools · 18.10.2026 10:43 · source · permalink

Takaichi plans not to visit the shrine in person during the festival. Seoul urged Japanese leaders to confront history and demonstrate remorse through action to build trust-based Korea-Japan relations.

Key Facts

  • Yasukuni Shrine honors about 2.46 million Japanese war dead, including 14 Class A criminals convicted by international tribunals for their roles in World War II.
  • Takaichi sent the offering in her capacity as prime minister, while Kyodo News reported that she would refrain from visiting the shrine during the festival.

A wooden plaque, left, bearing the name of Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi is seen beside a tree that she sent as an offering to the Yasukuni Shrine on the first day of the Autumn Festival in Tokyo, Oct. 17. AFP-Yonhap

The Korean government expressed regret Saturday after Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi sent a ritual offering to a war shrine seen as a symbol of Japan's imperialistic past.

Takaichi sent the offering earlier Saturday in her capacity as prime minister to mark the start of the Yasukuni Shrine's three-day autumn festival. But she plans to refrain from visiting the shrine in person during the festival, Japan's Kyodo News reported, citing sources close to the prime minister.

The Yasukuni Shrine honors some 2.46 million Japanese war dead, including 14 Class A criminals convicted by international tribunals for their roles in World War II.

In a statement, Seoul's foreign ministry said it "once again expresses deep disappointment and regret" that responsible Japanese leaders have "repeatedly sent offerings to or visited Yasukuni Shrine, which glorifies Japan's past war of aggression and enshrines war criminals."

It urged Japan's leaders to "face history squarely and demonstrate humble reflection on and sincere remorse for the past through action."

"We once again stress that such steps are an important foundation for building a future-oriented Korea-Japan relationship based on trust between the two countries and their peoples," it added.
