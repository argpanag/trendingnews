‘Be Kaleb’ mindset has Kaleb Johnson finding his stride

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:46 · source · permalink

Second-year running back gaining comfort, yards for Packers’ offense

GREEN BAY – Kaleb Johnson hasn't exactly had much downtime since being traded to the Packers a week before the start of the regular season.

The second-year running back really wouldn't have it any other way.

Despite Green Bay having shorter bye week than usual due to its Thanksgiving eve matchup with the Los Angeles Rams this upcoming Wednesday, Johnson was counting down the days until the team reported back to work last Friday.

The brief amount of time that Johnson had off consisted of just three things: Family, football and Grand Theft Auto VI.

"Staying home, over-studying – slash playing GTA 6," said Johnson, who's already through the third chapter of the recently released video game. "Just allowing your mind to relax, but at the same time stay on top of the game."

The 23-year-old running back rode a wave of momentum into the Week 11 bye after providing a needed spark for a Packers run game that struggled to get going during the first half of the season.

Acquired in a late August trade with Pittsburgh, the 6-foot-1, 224-pound running back was mainly off the radar before rushing for 110 yards and a touchdown on 24 carries in Green Bay's last two games before the bye.

That production has propelled Johnson into becoming the Packers' leading rusher with 214 yards on the season. Yet, NFL Next Gen Stats has credited him for even more yards after contact (216), the fifth-most among running backs with at least 50 rushing attempts this season.

"The one thing about Kaleb is you can feel him attack the line of scrimmage," offensive coordinator Adam Stenavich said. "He was coming off the ball really well. He was running hard. So that was really encouraging, to see him go out there and do that. I'm very excited about him as we move forward, for sure."

Johnson owns Green Bay's two longest carries of the season – a 34-yard run against the New England Patriots in Week 9 and a 31-yarder versus Tampa Bay in Week 4. Both plays contributed to Packers victories and aided the team's two best overall showings on the ground this season.

That part has come naturally for Johnson, a former All-American at Iowa whom the Steelers drafted in the third round in 2025. The challenge has been picking up the nuances of pass protection at the next level, a task he rarely handled in the Hawkeyes' run-heavy offense.

That learning curve and a couple early-season fumbles kept Johnson mostly on the sideline during the first two months of this season. He was a healthy scratch in back-to-back games before MarShawn Lloyd sat out against New England with a knee injury.

Although his confidence never wavered, Johnson admits he had some rust to knock off after carrying the ball only 28 times in 10 games during his rookie season with the Steelers.

"I really haven't faced adversity, and I felt like during that time when it happened, it was like, 'Oh (shoot), here we go,'" said Johnson, who had 508 carries for 2,779 yards and 30 touchdowns in three years at Iowa.

"I feel like it was me just believing in God and just keeping going and just keep striving and keeping my head down and working. That was really it and being me. Got comfortable with the system and went out there, be Kaleb."

While biding his time, Johnson was intentional about staying in the playbook, working with position coach Ben Sirmans and being ready when his next opportunity was presented.

It happened to come when Josh Jacobs broke his finger in the first quarter against the Patriots, leaving Johnson and Pierre Strong Jr. as the only healthy running backs. He responded with 14 carries for 68 yards and his first NFL touchdown.

Most importantly, Johnson garnered praise from Head Coach Matt LaFleur and the coaching staff for his growth with his pre-snap recognition and pass protection.

"Running the ball, I think that's the easy part for him. That's what he does," quarterback Jordan Love said. "I think it's just all the little things – the pass pro, understanding our system, picking stuff up day in and day out – that's going to keep making him better. But he's made some really fantastic runs, a lot of explosive plays. He runs very hard, and that's what we like to see."

The Packers may need more of that on Wednesday with Jacobs unlikely to play against the Rams. The three-time Pro Bowler has been practicing with a modified cast over his right hand but gripping the ball could be an issue.

If Johnson is summoned to make his first NFL start against the Rams, he's ready and willing to help Green Bay find a way through a Los Angeles defense that ranks third in total yards, fourth in passing yards, fifth in scoring and 10th against the run.

"I feel like I really am built for this," Johnson said. "I really am ready to go whenever my number's called – starting or not starting – I really don't care. Just want to go out there and play and help the team win."
