Jeremy Bergman's takeaways:

  1. Cincy's stars sizzle in España. Few teams have had better 10-quarter stretches than the Bengals over the past three weeks. Since being shut out in the first half against Baltimore in Week 7, Cincinnati has responded with 112 points over two-and-a-half games. Leading the way is Joe Burrow, staking a strong claim in the MVP race. Burrow was lethal again on Sunday in the first half, leading Cincy's offense to four scoring drives on 9.3 yards per play and completing 82% of his passes for 239 yards. Eight of those completions went to Ja'Marr Chase, who set a career high with 142 yards in the first half (finishing with 155 and a score on 10 catches), showcasing his dynamic ability on an international stage for the first time. Tee Higgins boxed out C.J. Henderson and Atlanta's defensive backs for two TDs. Even Chase Brown (season-high 6.3 YPC) and Cincinnati's anemic run game (season-best 123 yards) showed some explosion. After Sunday's romp, Cincinnati has now won four straight games with at least 30 points scored for the first time in franchise history. If the Bengals are capable of this type of production weekly, do they even need to swing big for a piece, possibly at running back, at the trade deadline? That's a question for Tuesday. Maybe Atlanta will have an answer for Cincy's offensive onslaught by then.
  2. Another big Bijan day undone by Atlanta errors. A week after losing to a Tampa team that didn't complete a pass, the Falcons found a more traditional way to fall Sunday morning: play leaky defense, commit penalties, repeat. In pristine, climate-controlled conditions inside Madrid's Bernabéu, there were no excuses for Atlanta's performance. First, the positives. Michael Penix Jr. threw three fewer interceptions than he did a week prior and scrambled for a career-high 50 yards, looking svelte and nimble on the Spanish pitch. Bijan Robinson, who loves an exclusive window, rushed for 100 again, totaling 147 yards from scrimmage and one TD on the day. The elusive dynamo was right at home at the home of Real Madrid's galácticos. But Jeff Ulbrich's defense was a sieve against the NFL's hottest attack, allowing 7.1 yards per play and forcing just two punts on the day. Atlanta also committed 12 accepted penalties for 88 yards, repeatedly setting back the offense. The Falcons O-line committed two fouls in the red zone on their second drive, leading to the first field goal of a touchdown fight. Its most critical error, though, came at the start of the fourth quarter, when down two TDs at the Bengals' 10-yard line, Atlanta was called for offensive holding on a third-and-2 incompletion. Cincy accepted the penalty and forced a fourth-and-12. Settling for cutting a two-score lead to a ... two-score lead, Nick Folk instead shanked a 38-yard field goal, and the Bengals' lead held at 14. The Falcons never got closer.
  3. A tale of two divisions. Despite the result, Cincinnati and Atlanta both might return to the States tied in the win column for leads in their respective divisions. The Bengals' victory moved them into a 6-2 tie atop the AFC North with the Steelers, setting up a critical "Sunday Night Football" showdown on the banks of the Ohio River in Week 10. Having already lost this season to Pittsburgh, the Bengals need a home win against Aaron Rodgers and Co. to improve their division record and ensure no favor in the head-to-head tiebreaker. Down in the cursed NFC South, the Falcons, at 3-6, are nowhere near out of it but are at risk of falling behind hotter clubs. Entering the rest of Sunday's slate, Atlanta is two games behind Carolina in the loss column and one behind Tampa Bay. Last-place New Orleans is still alive, as well. A needed bye awaits for the Falcons after a Week 10 matchup with Patrick Mahomes' Chiefs.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Bengals-Falcons (via NFL Pro): Joe Burrow was pressured on nine of 32 dropbacks (28.1%) against the Falcons, completing 20 of 23 passes for 237 yards and two touchdowns when kept clean. His time to pressure of 3.26 seconds was the longest of his career, and the Falcons defense had just one quick pressure in the game.

NFL Research: Joe Burrow and Ja’Marr Chase connected for their 19th touchdown of at least 40 yards in Sunday's win, tied with Joe Montana and Jerry Rice for the third-most by any QB-receiver duo since 1980. The only duos with more are Peyton Manning and Marvin Harrison (21 TDs) and Steve Young and Jerry Rice (20 TDs). Their first-quarter touchdown was the 58th score by the duo overall (including playoffs), which passed Daunte Culpepper and Randy Moss for secnd-most by any QB-receiver duo before age 30, trailing only Dan Marino and Mark Clayton (68 TDs).

Highlights from the matchup between the Denver Broncos and the Carolina Panthers in Week 9 of the 2026 NFL season.

Nick Shook's takeaways:

  1. Broncos win a game of runs. It wasn't quite an avalanche, but as is often the case in basketball, Sunday's game became a tale of runs. What began as a one-sided contest in favor of Denver reversed course just before halftime Sunday when the Panthers turned an interception into a scoring possession to claw back into the competition. That shift in momentum saw Bryce Young enter a flow state, dotting the field with precise, confident passes and leading a second scoring drive to double up between halves and put the Panthers in a 14-13 lead. He appeared poised to extend that lead in the fourth, too, but a wacky interception snagged by Denver safety Devon Key stopped the Panthers in their tracks and swung momentum back in favor of the Broncos, who rattled off two straight touchdown drives to take a 26-14 lead in the final period, good for their latest fourth-quarter comeback effort. Denver had to hang on for dear life in the final minutes after Carolina mounted one final push, and it's not exactly a resounding victory, but it's the latest example of a contending team finding a way to win a back-and-forth contest by coming through in the most important moments and finishing in a style that fits the team's strengths, with edge rusher Nik Bonitto strip-sacking Young to stop the Panthers' final drive in its tracks. Bo Nix looked more like the passer we've known him to be, too, a small but encouraging sign as the calendar creeps toward Thanksgiving.
  2. Panthers' defensive changes fail to produce improvement. Defensive coordinator Ejiro Evero became the fall guy when coach Dave Canales relieved him of his duties following Carolina's Week 8 loss to Green Bay, installing an interim DC with the hopes it might improve the Panthers' inconsistent defense. In short, it didn't. The Panthers allowed Denver to break 300 yards of total offense and mount two touchdown drives that appeared vastly different but produced the same result. The first fourth-quarter scoring march covered 80 yards over 14 plays, while the other needed just six plays to gain 61 yards and finish with a wide-open touchdown pass to Pat Bryant as a result of a blown coverage, a symptom of a defense that is still scrambling to take care of the details. Carolina stood tall late, buying its offense a chance to compile a game-winning drive, but the earlier breakdowns represent a persistent issue. Had cornerback Corey Thornton not made an incredibly athletic play of the ball to pick off Nix, this game might have snowballed out of control in the first half, long before the Panthers' offense was able to climb back into the game. Had Jaylen Waddle not exited due to a head injury, Denver might have continued to rely on the playmaker to break open for big gains. This unit was never going to be completely fixed in one week, but Sunday told us there's still plenty to correct.
  3. Nix begins to break out of his shell. It didn't last throughout the entire game, but seemingly for the first time in ages, Bo Nix took the training wheels off his own bicycle and let himself play a bit more instinctively. Proof existed in his decisions made while rolling out, in which Nix twice opted to throw back against his body to open targets underneath instead of accepting a small scramble gain or throwing it away. His bullet ripped through traffic to Courtland Sutton for a touchdown was another encouraging sign because he was willing to take a minor risk, and when Bryant streaked open through the Carolina secondary, Nix didn't hesitate to put the ball on him for a score. The results are evident in his stat line: 25 of 37, 237 yards, two touchdowns (one interception). It's not a massive change in performance, but it's a start Broncos fans have been yearning to see for weeks. We'll see if it's a sign of things to come.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Broncos-Panthers (via NFL Pro): Bo Nix posted a 124.2 passer rating against single-high coverage (20 of 26 for 202 yards and two touchdowns), but a 15.3 passer rating against split-safety coverage (5 of 11 for 35 yards and an interception). His 15.3 passer rating against split-safety looks was his lowest in a game since his NFL debut in Week 1, 2024.

NFL Research: The Broncos won, 26-21, in Week 9 against the Panthers after trailing by one point entering the fourth quarter, their fifth win of the season after trailing at any point in the fourth. That is tied with the 2016 Lions and 2022 Vikings for the most wins after trailing in the fourth quarter in a team’s first nine games of a season since 2000. Both the 2016 Lions and 2022 Vikings lost their first playoff game that season in the Wild Card Round.

Kevin Patra's takeaways:

  1. Cowboys survive collapse to secure a much-needed win over Colts. Brian Schottenheimer’s squad tried to give the game away. Luckily, the Cowboys' opponent did a better job. Dallas built a 10-point lead deep into the third quarter. Mistakes then gave Indianapolis life. CeeDee Lamb fumbled late – the Cowboys' second turnover of the day – to wipe out a chance to put the game away.  Despite putting up stats with George Pickens out, Lamb had a mistake-filled day. In addition to the fumble, he was called for offensive pass interference twice, and couldn’t toe-tap a TD that cost the Cowboys four points in the red zone. Dallas couldn’t get out of its own way for much of the contest. Dak Prescott was far more off the mark than we’re used to, leading to stalled drives. Schotty’s club was called for 14 penalties for a whopping 153 yards, turned the ball over twice, and missed a late go-ahead field goal. That’s how you let an opponent, amid a fire sale, stay in a game despite being outgained 508-271. Dallas should have run away with the game. Instead, it trailed late, until Ryan Flournoy -- the best No. 3 WR in the league -- made a massive play for a 46-yard gain to set up the game-winning TD. A loss would have all but doomed the Cowboys, who still have a big hill to climb in the NFC East.
  2. Colts' offense can’t kick it into gear until late. Facing a bad Cowboys defense, Daniel Jones and Co. couldn’t take advantage for long stretches. Indy went three-and-out five times, including on four of its first five drives -- the other was a 19-yard TD drive following a Dak Prescott fumble. Jones was inconsistent and often off the mark, completing 20 of 34 passes for 180 yards with a TD and an interception. The Cowboys D entered the game allowing a league-high 385.4 yards per game and 5.9 yards per play (29th). Indy generated just 271 yards and 4.6 yards per play while going 3 for 13 on third down. Ashton Dulin's 55-yard catch-and-run TD was Indy’s only pass play longer than 16 yards. Jones averaged 2.6 air yards per completion in the game, third-fewest of his career (minimum 10 completions), per Next Gen Stats. Against a Dallas D that’s been ripped up, Jones needed to do more. The rushing attack did some solid work, generating 105 total yards on the ground, with Seth McGowan earning 7.5 yards per carry on six totes with a TD. If Sunday was Jonathan Taylor’s final game in Indy, McGowan flashed some upside to take over the bulk of the workload.  
  3. Downs calls game. When Javonte Williams scored the go-ahead touchdown with a little over a minute remaining, the big question was whether a sieve Dallas D could hold on. Caleb Downs ensured it would by stepping in front of a Daniel Jones pass, sealing the win over his brother Josh Downs' squad. The INT capped a game in which Downs generated a sack, three tackles, a QB hit, a tackle for loss, and two passes defended, allowing just one reception on four targets. Sunday marked the first time all season that the Cowboys defense allowed fewer than 318 total yards. Doing so against a team that’s begun a mid-season teardown is one thing. The Cowboys will have a stiffer test against Kyle Shanahan and the 49ers in Week 10.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Cowboys-Colts (via NFL Pro): CeeDee Lamb caught 6 of 12 targets for 180 yards and a touchdown on downfield throws (10-plus air yards) in Week 9 against the Colts, the most receiving yards he has recorded on downfield throws in his career.

NFL Research: Sunday marked Dak Prescott’s first game with 300-plus passing yards, multiple passing touchdowns, no interceptions, and a game-winning drive since 2018 in a Week 17 win against the Giants. Prescott had one such game in each of his first three career seasons, but none from 2019-2025.

Highlights from the Week 9 matchup between the New York Jets and the Kansas City Chiefs during the 2026 NFL season.

Bobby Kownack's takeaways:

  1. K9 scores III touchdowns. Kenneth Walker III's reign of terror over the rest of the league took a couple weeks’ break, as the running back was held to 105 rushing yards and no touchdowns over the previous two contests after torching opponents for 665 rushing yards and eight total scores in his first five outings as a Chief. Unfortunately for the Jets, his dominance’s brief hiatus ended Sunday. Walker was a man on a mission versus Gang Green, almost singlehandedly keeping Kansas City afloat amid a slow start that included two fumbles, a three-and-out and a missed field goal over the opening two quarters. Kansas City’s other two first-half drives ended a bit differently -- on 7- and 25-yard touchdown receptions by Walker. He provided the offensive spark while Patrick Mahomes worked to piece together a consistent passing game using anyone else, and after the rest of the operation caught up to Walker’s heroics to start pulling away from the Jets, he added a two-yard TD run in the third quarter. Walker finished with 102 rushing yards, a 5.7 per-carry average and four runs of 10-plus yards, as well as four catches for 41 yards. That’s after he was able to take a rest midway through the fourth quarter due to a blowout he helped create. He now has five games with multiple scrimmage touchdowns, the most in the NFL this season and the most by a Chiefs running back since Larry Johnson (eight) in 2005.
  2. Wilson is sorely missed by Jets. There was a lot of intrigue surrounding New York’s offense heading into Week 9. How would the Jets mix Breece Hallback into the rotation after Braelon Allen excelled to such a degree during the starter’s four-game absence? How would the passing offense operate without Garrett Wilson after Omar Cooper Jr. and Adonai Mitchell recently took turns breaking out for New York? As far as the running backs, Hall saw 18 touches to Allen’s 10, with Hall turning his into 87 yards and Allen totaling 54. Both running backs averaged 4.1 yards per carry. As for the passing offense? It was ugly. Wilson had appeared frustrated in recent weeks as he produced smaller numbers than his counterparts, hauling in 12 catches for 153 yards without a score over his previous three games before sustaining a concussion, but it’s clear he makes the offense go. Without the star there to demand attention from Kansas City’s secondary, the Chiefs had little problem shutting down Geno Smith. Smith threw for only 165 yards. Cooper led the way with 58 yards on eight receptions, while Mitchell went from 142 yards on seven catches last week to zero catches on seven looks a game later. New York got nothing going, settling for four field goals in the first half -- tied for the most over the last 10 seasons. In the second half, the Jets didn’t even get the opportunity to settle. Smith and Co. managed 58 yards over five drives on the other side of intermission, forced to watch their two-point halftime deficit unravel into a 23-point loss.
  3. Chiefs come alive for much-needed blowout. Kansas City rode a two-game losing streak into Week 9, with a -22 fourth-quarter point differential that ranked 31st in the league over the past two games. If there’s one thing Patrick Mahomes is going to do, though, it’s beat up on bad teams. By defeating the Jets, he moved to 18-0 in his career against teams entering a matchup four-plus games under .500, the best by any quarterback since 1970 (minimum 15 starts), per NFL Research. He went 23 of 31 for 242 yards and two touchdowns with a 117.9 passer rating, but most importantly seemed OK after a strip-sack by David Bailey late in the second quarter bent his elbow awkwardly and forced him to receive medical attention on the sideline. He returned and overcame a frustrating offensive stupor to blow the doors off the Jets, while the Chiefs defense minimized damage early and inflicted plenty of it throughout. Kansas City pressured Geno Smith on half of his dropbacks. Chris Jones and George Karlaftis led the team with six pressures and two sacks apiece, while Drue Tranquill logged 13 tackles and Nick Bolton had 12 plus a forced fumble. Detractors will point out it’s only the Jets, and there are still some issues to work out -- think Harrison Butker missing a 56-yard field goal and the subsequent 51-yarder after getting a reprieve due to an offsides penalty just before half -- but the Chiefs can only play who is in front of them. In this case, they demolished them.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Jets-Chiefs (via NFL Pro): Travis Kelce caught four of six targets for 72 yards, accounting for two of the Chiefs' five explosive passes (40.0%).

NFL Research: Kenneth Walker had his first career game with multiple receiving touchdowns to go along with a rushing touchdown in the Chiefs’ win over the Jets, his second career game with three touchdowns.

Highlights from the Week 9 matchup between the Detroit Lions and the Miami Dolphins during the 2026 NFL season

REWATCH: Lions-Dolphins on NFL+ Premium

Bobby Kownack's takeaways:

  1. Usual suspects lift Detroit to tightly contested win. Sunday’s victory over Miami was not your typical Lions affair. Jared Goff and the offense took forever to get going, and it seemed as if they knew things weren’t quite clicking. The Lions pulled out all the stops against their one-win opponent. They went for it on fourth down on the first drive. A few players later, Jahmyr Gibbs took a direct snap and faked a pitch to Goff before streaking around the edge for 16 yards. Two plays into the second quarter, Detroit converted a fake punt throw by Jack Fox on fourth-and-7 from its own 30. The Lions constantly stalled out, failing to score a touchdown until 1:20 remained in the third quarter -- and that was a run by Gibbs on fourth down, too. Luckily, they had a couple stars to lean on to help guide them through their struggles. Amon-Ra St. Brown was the main source of the team’s passing offense. Deep into the final frame (at the start of the Lions’ seventh drive of the game), he had seven catches, while the rest of Detroit’s pass catchers had combined for six. St. Brown drew a third-down pass interference penalty early on that possession, and a few players later hauled in a key 17-yard catch on third-and-8 to keep the chains moving with a perilous seven-point lead. He eventually finished with 86 yards on eight receptions. Every other Lion combined for 10 catches and 95 yards. And though Gibbs was frequently met by a pod of Dolphins in the backfield, he still broke off a number of clutch gainers, including the aforementioned 8-yard TD run. He finished with a game-high 74 rushing yards. That was about it for offensive highlights, but it was enough.
  2. Miami wastes strong defensive performance. Jeff Hafley’s defense fought valiantly against the league’s No. 3 scoring offense. If only the rest of the team had been able to repay the group’s efforts. The Dolphins consistently crashed into Detroit’s backfield to locate Jahmyr Gibbs, containing the electric back who had been averaging 4.9 yards per carry entering Week 9 to 3.7 yards per attempt on 20 rushes. Jared Goff struggled to find and hit open passers, throwing for a season-low 170 yards. He was sacked three times and tossed just his second pick of the season to rookie corner Chris Johnson after Josh Uche hit his arm during the throwing motion. The Dolphins didn’t bend to the point of allowing a touchdown -- the only one of the game -- until the waning minutes of the third quarter. Malik Willis and the offense could do nothing with their opportunities, however. Willis threw for only 128 yards and an interception and seemed hesitant to open it up on the ground (two carries for 7 yards), while Ollie Gordon II was held to 55 rushing yards. When the Fins had a chance to make it a tie game heading into halftime, they instead committed a holding penalty and missed a 38-yard field goal on the final two plays of the second quarter. Toward the start of the fourth quarter, down 10 with the ball at Detroit’s 7-yard line, Willis took two sacks over the next three plays before Miami settled for a three points. Gut-wrenching mistakes are no way to beat a contender like the Lions, and the Dolphins certainly made their share of them.
  3. Ugly game all around. One of the biggest concerns for any 5-2 team traveling to face a 1-6 opponent is typically avoiding playing down to the lesser squad’s level. The Lions absolutely joined the Dolphins in the doldrums for a messy matchup that will have neither side coming away feeling particularly happy. The Lions and Dolphins combined for 20 accepted penalties, with Detroit committing nine to Miami’s 11. They went 7 for 24 on third down, collectively. Detroit managed only 270 yards, nearly 100 fewer than its previous low on the season (355); Miami posted its second-lowest yardage total of the year with 228. Thanks to the offensive ineptitude, a few of the most exciting plays of the matchup were actually fake punts. Both teams ran one, and both teams converted on passes -- one by Lions punter Jack Fox and another by Dolphins punter Bradley Pinion. It was the first time since at least 1950 that multiple punters have completed a pass in the same game, per NFL Research. Give credit to the Lions defense for showing up and making life miserable for the Dolphins, but Detroit didn’t exactly put an exclamation point on this one despite running its winning streak to four.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Lions-Dolphins (via NFL Pro): Aidan Hutchinson matched up against Austin Jackson 16 times in Week 9 (all one-on-one), recording six pressures (37.5% pressure rate), including two quick pressures under 2.5 seconds.

NFL Research: Jahmyr Gibbs recorded his 60th career touchdown, tying Hall of Famer LaDainian Tomlinson for the second-most by any player in their first four seasons in NFL history, trailing only Hall of Famer Terrell Davis with 61.

Nick Shook's takeaways:

  1. Saints come alive to earn much-needed win. By all accounts, Sunday was an ugly display of football between two middling teams for most of the afternoon. A field goal fest (including a Charlie Smyth first half miss) set up for what appeared to be a likely 9-6 finish until the Browns finally hit a big play for a touchdown when Shedeur Sanders connected with rookie KC Concepcion for a 64-yard passing touchdown. The score seemed to position the Browns for another fourth-quarter comeback win, but instead it awoke the Saints with just enough time for chaos to ensue. New Orleans covered 55 yards in six plays and 50 seconds to take a 16-13 lead, allowed the Browns to tie the game with 0:08 left, then embarked on an efficient, relentless 74-yard scoring drive that required 11 plays and ended with a pretty touchdown pass from Tyler Shough to Juwan Johnson for a 23-16 lead in overtime. The stop of Cleveland's two-point try in overtime sealed the win, but it was only made possible by some late success that must have felt for Saints fans like the thawing of an offense that spent far too long frozen within a glacier. At 3-5, the Saints are back in the thick of a race for an NFC South crown that might only require nine wins to secure. If they're going to get there, Sunday's late push toward a victory was essential.
  2. Browns show clear weaknesses in brutal defeat. Cleveland has lost its ability to stop the run since losing Maliek Collins to injury and delivered an embarrassing effort against the Saints' low-ceiling rushing attack Sunday, allowing Audric Estimé -- who was a member of the Browns less than a month ago -- Tyler Shough and Alvin Kamara to run for 155 yards on 31 attempts, pacing an offense that came to life thanks to this newfound balance in the second half. Even worse, Cleveland's pass coverage unit struggled, losing one-on-one matchups all afternoon (Grant Delpit, Ronnie Hickman Jr. and Tyson Campbell each struggled in such matchups) and especially in the game's most crucial moments (e.g., Juwan Johnson's overtime touchdown, and a 33-yard completion from Shough to Chris Olave late in the fourth quarter). Paired with an offense that wasn't threatening for most of the afternoon with Sanders at quarterback, Cleveland was guaranteed to be locked in a four-quarter battle with a team most expected it to defeat without a major struggle. Instead, the Browns fell in a fashion that is a strong competitor with their Week 1 blowout loss to Jacksonville for worst defeat of the season. That's not a good place to be in in Week 9.
  3. Shough finds his stride. New Orleans' offense has lacked juice for some time now, draining the Saints of their early season optimism that was tied to the trajectory of Tyler Shough in his second NFL season. That finally changed in the fourth quarter Sunday. When Cleveland stunned the Superdome crowd with an explosive touchdown pass from Shedeur Sanders to KC Concepcion, New Orleans' offense woke up, stringing together well-designed passing plays with a rushing attack that finally showed some life to move down the field with relative ease against a Browns defense that had limited it all afternoon. Shough was at the center of all of it, calmly dropping dimes on Chris Olave, Juwan Johnson and even rookie Jordyn Tyson to propel the offense to two clutch touchdown drives in a wild fourth quarter and overtime period. We saw the return of a composed, accurate Shough, whose clutch gene reappeared after going into hiding over the last month. The timing couldn't be better for this Saints team, which will be in a weekly fight for survival in an NFC South that remains wide open. There's no guarantee their rushing success carries over, but getting Shough back on track was a massively important development for coach Kellen Moore, who was able to demonstrate some of his expertise in how he called the game in the final period plus the extra frame.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Browns-Saints (via NFL Pro): Shedeur Sanders completed three of his six downfield attempts for 154 yards and a touchdown against the Saints, with all six attempts occurring in the second half and overtime. Sanders' fourth-quarter deep pass to Jerry Jeudy set up a game-tying field-goal attempt that sent the game to overtime. Sanders added +55.5% win probability on downfield attempts, a career-high mark.

NFL Research: This is the fourth game in NFL history, including playoffs, with 12-or-fewer combined points in the first three quarters to feature at least 33 combined points in the fourth quarter and overtime. Philadelphia Eagles-Cleveland Browns in 1982 (35 points), New York Yanks-Chicago Bears in 1951 (35 points) and Green Bay Packers-Rochester Jeffersons in 1925 (39 points) are the others.

  1. Giants get first win in Philadelphia since 2013. Big Blue snapped a 12-game road losing streak to its division rival. J.J. McCarthy made some impressive third-down throws, but it was Jameis Winston who led the Giants' only touchdown drive, giving New York a third-quarter lead it would cling to through the end. McCarthy got popped on a QB keeper and exited in the third quarter with a hip injury. He’d return to help close out the road win. A stymied run game led to a trove of third-and-long situations. McCarthy went 6 of 7 on the key down for 88 yards with two sacks. It was an ugly offensive affair, with neither team reaching the 250-yard mark. McCarthy didn’t flash as well as he had in his last start, but didn’t make a big mistake and layered some pretty balls. Notching another win to keep a significant NFC East lead is all that matters.

The QB added a few good runs, leading the Eagles with 64 yards on nine rushes. Milton completed 18 of 32 passes for 169 yards while taking seven sacks. Milton owns a whip, but the issue is his accuracy. Too often, the young QB was off the mark, leading to stalled drives. Reese gobbled up three sacks, a team-high 12 tackles, three tackles for loss, a forced fumble, four QB pressures, including three quick pressures, and three QB hits. Like a hound, Reese has a nose for the football and constantly puts himself in position to make a play. No matter where defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson lines up his rookie, Reese proves he can hunt the pigskin. Reese became the only player in the NFL this season with two or more sacks aligned at ILB in a game, per Next Gen Stats. The linebacker’s play opens up the rest of the defense to feast. Four other Giants had a sack on Sunday, and Big Blue ate up 10 tackles for loss.

Newsome II recorded a pass defensed and forced tight windows on three of his five targets. Banks allowed just one reception on six targets. 

NFL Research: Braden Mann’s 86-yard punt in the second quarter was the longest punt in the NFL since 2001, when Rodney Williams of the Giants had a 90-yard punt and the second-longest punt in Eagles history, behind Randall Cunningham’s 91-yard punt in Week 13, 1989 vs. the Giants. Mann leads the NFL with 49 punts and 22 punts inside the 20-yard line this season.
