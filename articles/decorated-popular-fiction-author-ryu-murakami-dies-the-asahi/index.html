Author Ryu Murakami in 2014 (Asahi Shimbun file photo)

Award-winning writer Ryu Murakami who captured some of Japan’s top literary prizes has died. He was 74.

Born in Sasebo, Nagasaki Prefecture, in 1952, Murakami wrote “Almost Transparent Blue” while still a student at Musashino Art University in west Tokyo.

His debut novel about youth living and partying near a U.S. military base was published in 1976 and netted him the prestigious Akutagawa Prize.

He went on to write popular novels that include “Coin Locker Babies” and “Ai to Genso no Fascism” (“Fascism of love and fantasy”) as well as a 2003 book aimed at getting junior high schoolers interested in the job market.

Two years later, he was awarded the Mainichi publishing culture prize and Noma Literary Prize for “From the Fatherland, with Love,” which explores an alternate timeline where Japan is invaded by North Korea.
