Cahair O’Kane: To beat Scotstown, Crossmaglen must attack their greatest strength

TheTools · 25.11.2026 12:23 · source · permalink

WITH a bit of luck, Kieran Donnelly would already have had an Ulster Club in the bag and be preparing for another final next weekend.

When the Scotstown team he managed came up against Gaoth Dobhair in the 2018 decider, Donnelly got a huge amount right tactically.

Shane Carey was harshly sent off with 14 minutes of normal time remaining.

Scotstown didn’t score in the remainder of normal time.

Had he stayed on, there’s a good chance that game goes the other way.

On Saturday evening, Carey stuck away one the best penalties ever seen in Gaelic football.

In doing so, he helped end his former manager’s hopes of taking St Eunan’s to the promised land.

It should never have gone to penalties.

Kieran Hughes’s free was clearly wide.

The strange thing about the decision to give a score was the deliberation. If it’s over, the decision is straightforward. If you’re in any doubt, the officials can’t give the score.

It was a huge stroke of luck for Scotstown but that’s the thing with luck. It’s random.

St Eunan’s did so much right.

On commentary, Vinny Corey spotted pretty quickly how they were manipulating Scotstown’s defence.

Their tactic of transferring the ball rapidly from one side to the other through the boot will have been nothing new.

That was exactly how the Monaghan team Corey played on shocked Donegal in the 2013 Ulster final.

That facet of St Eunan’s play illustrated the value of a proper gameplan.

Scotstown’s win illustrated the value of great players.

Rory Beggan’s free from almost right on the 65-metre line was ridiculous even for him.

Even though he coughed up a couple of costly turnovers, Conor McCarthy’s good far outweighed the bad.

Nicky Sherlock continued his fine season. Darragh Murray, as he’s done repeatedly over the last two seasons, popped up with an important goal.

But really, it was about their big men who showed that no matter how good the opposition’s gameplan is, sometimes there’s very little you can do about good players.

On their kickouts, Scotstown spread their fetchers out all over the shop.

It plays into a policy that places great trust on the individual.

When the new rules first came in, Armagh and Donegal both made early advancements with a similar ploy. They looked ahead of the curve.

Then on one weekend, Kerry wiped Armagh out and Donegal were left so spooked by Monaghan that they both effectively abandoned it thereafter.

It is bold to the point of reckless to isolate players in the way Scotstown do – unless you have the players Scotstown do.

For 52 minutes before Kieran Hughes came on, every time An Bhoth were under any duress, Rory Beggan simply hung the next kickout up above Micheal McCarville.

St Eunan’s flailed and flapped and clumb and swung but he was just too big, too imposing, too well-set on the turf to be moved.

Then Hughes came in and Beggan had two options, not one.

The Donegal champions were not surprised by these kickouts. They would have spent a lot of the last fortnight planning for them.

It did not matter.

Crossmaglen will do the same.

I’d bet my bottom dollar that the next fortnight for Shane Lennon and his coaching team are all about taking a few off Scotstown in the air.

Because nothing inflates a team’s presence in a game quicker than striking a blow to their opponent’s greatest strength.

Exhibit A was minute 26 in the Aviva on Saturday evening.

Everyone knows the power of the South African scrum. Leading 10-3, Ireland won one against the head.

The roof almost came off the place.

It was not just a scrum. It was the outworkings of all the time Ireland had devoted to that area on the training ground over weeks and months in preparation for that exact moment.

Ireland were lifted by it precisely because South Africa were hurt by it.

Attacking the thing your opposition trusts most, the thing they turn to when everything else is failing, can be a powerful weapon.

Crossmaglen are a side that have the capacity to go from very ordinary to brilliant at the drop of a hat.

A forward line containing the three O’Neills (Aaron has been a quiet star of this run) and Cian McConville could cover any cracks.

McConville hasn’t found himself yet in Ulster. Oisin O’Neill was off-colour on Sunday. They still won.

Edendork had the right idea. They built the foundations of their plan around stopping Rian and Oisin in particular.

Outside of Rian’s stunning early off-balance two-pointer, they managed it pretty well in the first half. But his greatness came to the surface in the second period.

As good as Edendork’s plan was, they, like St Eunan’s, just couldn’t do anything about a really good player turning it on in a way that led to his manager describing his performance as “superhuman”.

For the next two weeks, Scotstown’s training sessions will revolve around stopping those four players.

For Cross, the whole game is striking blows on that Scotstown kickout when it matters.

St Eunan’s troubled it in the first half but when the soup thickened, McCarville and Hughes won everything in the air.

If it was easily broken, someone would have done it by now.

But that is the whole ball game for Crossmaglen.

To beat Scotstown, they must do what St Eunan’s and Magherafelt couldn’t and really strike at their heart.
