Irish trainer has licence suspended after welfare concerns identified during yard inspection

TheTools · 16.10.2026 20:46 · source · permalink

Click here to add us to your Google preferred sources or find out more here

Trainer Jerry Murray has had his licence suspended by an Irish Horseracing Regulatory Board (IHRB) referrals committee due to "serious welfare, care and management concerns" identified during an inspection at his yard in August.

Murray, who last had a winner with Top Of The Pops at Gowran Park in April 2025, had his Offaly yard inspected on August 19, when IHRB officials noted "serious deficiencies in the health, condition, care and welfare of horses". Four horses recorded as being in his care were not present at the yard, while seven at the yard were not returned as being in his care.

In her evidence, Dr Lynn Hillyer, IHRB chief veterinary officer, explained concerns regarding the management of the training establishment, among them "continuing issues concerning the welfare of certain horses and the conditions in which they were kept". 

She noted that all horses present at the initial inspection required veterinary intervention and three required urgent attention, including one who had to be euthanised.

The yard was inspected again on September 3 and October 7. Hillyer said that Murray engaged with the IHRB and remedial steps had been taken, but welfare concerns remained and continuing IHRB veterinary oversight and intervention was necessary.

The IHRB submitted to the referrals committee that the seriousness of the welfare and management concerns and the level of oversight required to achieve the necessary standards were unsustainable and could not properly form the basis upon which a licence could remain operative.

Murray accepted he was in breach of the rules and outlined the steps he has taken to improve conditions at the yard.

The referrals committee was satisfied that material concerns remained and the level of continuing regulatory oversight required was unsustainable, while acknowledging the personal circumstances of Murray, which were put forward by John Fitzgerald of the Restricted Trainers Association.

Murray's licence was suspended by the committee with immediate effect until it determines otherwise. Murray may apply for the suspension to be reviewed, so the committee can consider whether the concerns that gave rise to the suspension have been addressed.
