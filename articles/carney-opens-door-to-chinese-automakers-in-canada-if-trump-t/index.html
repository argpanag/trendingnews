Carney opens door to Chinese automakers in Canada if Trump trade talks fail, putting 27,000 jobs in play

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:55 · source · permalink

For decades, Canada's auto industry has relied on open trade with the United States — a relationship that supports both the vehicles Canadians drive and the jobs of those who build them. Over the last 18 months, that relationship has been under significant strain.

It's why Prime Minister Mark Carney has been casting Canada's net further afield — in an effort to shore up business opportunities to keep Canada's automotive industry running on all cylinders.

On Nov. 16, Carney clearly indicated that if current trade talks with U.S. President Donald Trump failed to protect an integrated North American auto industry, Canada could turn to Chinese automakers. Carney's comments came just days before a planned meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping.

For most Canadians this tariff issue could shape what your next car costs, which rebates you qualify for and how secure jobs are in Ontario's auto towns.

What did Carney actually say about Chinese carmakers?

While on a cross-country visit that took Carney to Cambodia, he told reporters that U.S. negotiations remain his priority, but their outcome could decide whether Canada pursues partnerships with European, Chinese or Japanese automakers.

Asked whether Chinese investment was a bargaining chip, he replied: "It's not leverage. But action, reaction."

But Carney isn't willing to take scraps — and set a condition for any deal, where a Chinese investment would need real manufacturing in Canada, not just the assembly of imported parts.

His comments build on a January 2026 deal with China, when Canada removed tariffs to allow up to 49,000 Chinese-made electric vehicles (EVs) into the country each year at a 6.1% tariff. Prior to this updated agreement, the tariff for Chinese EV imports was 100%, imposed in 2024.

But not everyone is on board with China becoming the alternative partner for Canada's automotive industry. Ontario Premier Doug Ford criticized the plan and warned that Chinese EVs could threaten Canadian jobs and jeopardize access to the U.S. market.
