(Brief) Introduction

Until around a decade ago, Liverpool were refereed fairly normally. Then, things went very wrong; and then, got much worse.

This is all in the data.

With help from data specialists and analysts who subscribe to the site (and compiled from official sources), The Tomkins Times Refereeing Database now has umpteen columns of info on every single Premier League match going back to the year 2000 (and in even more columns of data since 2014). And also granular detail on every single VAR decision in the league since its introduction in 2019, and the strange gaping holes in the data where no decisions were given; like looking at a year of childbirth statistics and finding not a single baby born in the month of June.

At times, such as in 2013/14, the Reds were reffed fairly abnormally (in a good way). This is clear, looking back.

With a very British team and manager, the Reds won more league penalties between January 2014 and April 2014 (10) than in any full season since. (In the last season-and-a-third, the Reds have been awarded just three league penalties, out of 10-12 clear cases of a penalty being the correct outcome.)

I also found in the past that the more Brits the Reds had in the team over a 20-year period, the more penalties the team won. That should not be the case.

Following the virality of my more ranty reaction to the utter shitshow of officiating at the weekend, and upon gaining lots of new readers (perhaps the first time my analysis of this subject has had a positive impact and broken out of the Substack ecosystem), I wanted to lay out a facts-based overview of what I’ve been doing on this issue for a few years now, for anyone new to my work on this.

(And believe me, I much prefer analysing the actual football, but someone has to do this. I loved a lot about the game at the weekend, for example, but the outcome, and Liverpool’s season, was jeopardised by bad refereeing. The ref, Andy Madley, had a decent first half, then lost the plot in the second.)

One issue faced by fellow Reds, that I have experienced a lot myself, is that complaining about officials is “typical Liverpool victim behaviour”. I get also resistance from other Liverpool fans for this reason; to complain is to risk being called a victim, and the word has a nasty ring to it when aimed at Reds.

But sometimes you have to complain, if that’s what the facts suggest. I’m big enough, and ugly enough, to deal with the blowback.

It may be also be seen as a bit “small time”.

But big data says it’s a big problem, that cost Liverpool league titles under Jürgen Klopp and cost Arne Slot his job. (Last season was horrific for Big Decisions for and against, unlike 2024-25, which was more normal.) It’s already likely put Liverpool out of this title race by November.

And obviously criticising referees is automatically seen as conspiratorial; so my aim is to have as much irrefutable data as possible, even as I wear my tinfoil hat.

Big Decisions – like penalties and red cards – are massively game-changing; while even Foul Balance (if all other match data is equal) affects your points-per-game, as my research shows.

A penalty is almost worth a goal (80%), and a single goal massively increases your chances of winning. As does an opposition red card. And of course, vice versa if it goes against you.

With various qualified sports data analysts, and with the support of other academics who subscribe to TTT, I am working on our biggest database yet, to track everything referee (and VAR) related. I have combined all my previous work with masses of new Premier League data. We will be spending the next few months combing through the data, and looking how each different club is officiated against the logical ‘norms’ of how football works.

At some point I also want to create a series of ‘Expected Decisions’ (similar to expected goals).

Plus, I’m in the middle of creating a “days since” tracker, to contrast how long it’s been since Liverpool got some major types of Big Decision, seeing as it’s literally been decades in some cases.

There are different time periods covered in the data I will quote, depending on when I ran the data, and where possible, using the data to show how things have got measurably worse in three stages: the arrival of Jürgen Klopp; a change in 2020, which coincides with David Coote’s rant about Klopp and Liverpool down the pub, which may have reflected a wider refereeing viewpoint or may just be coincidental; and 2023, when Klopp had a major beef with the then-named PGMOL, and things instantly changed in a negative way in terms of refereeing, from which the Reds are still reeling.

So, that’s the waffling over; albeit I always have to caveat and explain, rather than just spout random data.

As such, I will keep this simple, and only add a few graphs and tables, to keep it cleaner. A more in-depth version will appear in time, with more footnotes and tables of data (albeit much will be on the site already). I’ll also link to some previous work that involves video analysis of the Big Decisions (or lack thereof) that we produced last season.

So, let’s briefly explain Domination.

‘Domination’ is my stat for shots+corners minus shots+corners conceded, and it correlates very closely with Foul Balance; at 85%, far more than possession actually does (70%), and it correlates very similarly to xG Difference in terms of results. But I have 26 years of Domination data, and less than 10 years of xG data. So, Dominant teams get better refereeing outcomes, as you’d logically expect.

Conclusion

My aim, as ever, is to make refereeing fairer, and to hold Pro Refs and their ever-increasing PR machine to account. I really appreciate a good referee – it’s a great thing to witness someone doing their job well – and as such, it would be nice to see some, as a Liverpool fan.

You will not get perfectly distributed outcomes in life; but you should not get loads of totally illogical outcomes either. That proves some bias is at play, even if it’s not conscious.

All the data we have already collected exists on the past 26 years’ of games, and we have more detailed data on the last 14 years, and even more on the VAR era.

It’s all there. It exists, as hard facts on each match.

So we can look at patterns that referees cannot fudge, or explain away. Once you get past a dozen “one-off” mistakes (such as Stuart Attwell not giving Liverpool a call as the VAR on increasingly obvious fouls), it’s well beyond being ‘just one of those things’.

After all, data in sport, and life, is used to show patterns otherwise dismissed as unconnected. It joins the dots.

And clearly, decisions do not even themselves out. There is no refereeing god, alas, handing down equitable outcomes.

So I’ll be adding to the database, and keeping it up to date. I will work with data analysts and academics to highlight all relevant issues. We aim to undertake an academic study (including neutral contributors), but that could take 1-2 years to complete.

(A senior academic who subscribes to the site noted that it’s best to keep sharing the findings, rather than just wait until then.)

I would love nothing more than to never have to write about the officials again – GENUINELY – but this season alone they have cost the Reds in big games against Man City and Arsenal at home, and made a title challenge, however unlikely, almost impossible.

Indeed, they cost points at home to Nottingham Forest too, with a type of penalty never, ever given (keeper collides slightly with striker after shot is taken, with shot going wide), which occurred when the official could not work the substitute board for more than three minutes, and got angry and stroppy at the Liverpool staff asking him to do his job. That’s three home games, eight points dropped, and we’re only five games in. All with serious refereeing errors on Big Decisions: penalty to Forest, two yellows to Ryan Gravenberch, and a lack of a penalty and DOGSO red-card (for no attempt to play the ball) just after going behind to Arsenal.

There was a period of about a decade where Everton had won more penalties in Anfield derbies (two to none, both won by Dominic Calvert-Lewin) and the Everton manager at the time, a couple of years ago, moaned that “you never get penalties at Anfield”. That’s the kind of bias that pervades the game. This is not the Kop of the 1970s. Calvert-Lewin won more Anfield penalties than a lot of Liverpool attackers.

In 2024/25, while Liverpool’s Foul Balance data was crazy (and led to Arne Slot losing his mind with the officials), the Big Decision Balance was, for once, pretty okay.

Yay!

It reflected being the best team in the country, as the Reds were by some distance, on the underlying data. Slot’s second season saw the Reds’ Big Decisions go crazily bad; they were still a good team, but getting terrible decisions, until they ran out of energy.

Alas, most seasons, neither the Big Decisions nor the Foul Balance reflect how good Liverpool actually are. Both cost points. For Man City the data looks fair, reflecting that, on the pitch at least, they were a very good side.

So Liverpool should be close behind on refereeing data; not the polar opposite.

Either City are being treated ultra-generously, if Liverpool are being treated merely fairly; or if City are only being treated fairly, Liverpool are being treated ultra-negatively. Over periods of six, 10 or 14 years, the officiating data should match on-pitch performances, as it does for all good teams other than Liverpool (other than Manchester United, but to a lesser degree).

And no one can explain to me why Aston Villa’s Foul Balance is always massive high, even after Jack Grealish left.

Liverpool’s refereeing data used to be much better; again, in 2013-14 Liverpool won loads of fouls and loads of penalties. With a mostly British team and a British manager.

I don’t want a return to 2013-14; or even to the 1970s when referees shat themselves at the sound of the Kop howling. I don’t want every half-hearted shout for a handball to be given as a penalty.

I just want a return to referees not pandering to the masses, and just reffing on incidents. As made clear by the video. From various angles.

I (foolishly) assumed VAR would correct for this, but instead we now get bullshit like “not wanting to re-referee games”, when the whole fucking point was to see what referees cannot humanly see in a fast-moving game with angles blocked by up to 20 outfield players, in gale-force winds and spitting rain.

VAR is there to show us what the ref missed, or could not possibly see; not there so they can say “well, the ref didn’t see it, so we can’t give it”, despite what the actual evidence shows.

Instead, VAR usage just gaslights us, by showing us a clear incident, then saying something like “they both came in high” – when one guy has both feet on the ground, and has the other guy’s foot in his ribs.

It’s far more maddening than pre-VAR, where you knew the ref – often a rotund man in his early 50s, with sweat in his eyes and a dicky knee – could not be expected to see everything, especially if there were players in the way.

We’ve ended up with another ex-ref writing a column saying that it was “arguable” if Alexander Isak was going to head the ball on Sunday; and him, and others, saying that the pull was not sustained for long enough, as if one sharp, hard pull of the shirt – where the shirt is stretched by literally three feet in the freeze-frame – does not make any impact in stopping the striker who is already only millimetres from the ball.

It’s bullshit and they know it. It’s insulting.

This ex-ref said Isak simply turned and walked back, to get on with the game without a murmur, which itself was a blatant lie; he turned and swore and Jurrien Timber, who had just fouled him. If you can’t even see that part then you should register yourself as legally blind.

And it’s arguable that we’re all in a simulation.

Anything is arguable.

It’s arguable that all referees are taking bungs, but I’m not going to argue that. But it is arguable. (It’s happened before, all over the world.)

I’m also not saying that I could do better … but I’m also not being paid £200,000 a year.

In truth, I think most referees are honest and try to be fair, and are probably pretty decent blokes, all told. But as in any group of people (and especially within certain styles of authorities), you will get the less honest and more deceitful.

Then you will also get those who do not even realise what they are doing is wrong. Maybe Andy Madley genuinely thought Liverpool made a ton of fouls in the second half and Arsenal almost none, as reality told us otherwise, as the Reds did all the attacking (which normally means winning more fouls; certainly not getting absolutely hammered by the officials).
