Brad Pitt's New Near-Perfect Action Thriller Officially Beats His Own 26-Year-Old Gangster Classic

TheTools · 07.10.2026 23:01 · source · permalink

Former co-stars and fellow Hollywood icons Brad Pitt and Tom Cruise are duking it out in theaters this week with ambitious, high-stakes passion projects. However, the outcomes couldn't be more different, as Cruise's auteur-helmed cinematic spectacle, Digger, unfortunately missed the mark with critics and fumbled its opening weekend. Meanwhile, Pitt's intimate action thriller has garnered overwhelmingly positive reviews, and while its box office performance is up for debate, its domestic debut was not only way better than Digger's; it has also earned enough to outpace one of Pitt's cult classics.

Brad Pitt's 'Heart of the Beast' Dethrones a Guy Ritchie Classic

Brad Pitt's Heart of the Beast is getting a massive boost with a solid 85% critical score, earning $20 million on debut, and has now grossed $99.5 million worldwide against its $82 million production spend. While it's still a ways off from the break-even point and commercial success, it has at least charted inside the actor's top 30 highest-grossing films, nudging his 2000 gritty crime comedy Snatch down a notch.

Snatch, Guy Ritchie's sophomore outing after Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels, is a gem that gets better with each rewatch. It cemented Ritchie's signature aesthetic and knack for disjointed narratives, which worked well thanks to its eclectic cast, starring Pitt, Benicio del Toro, and then-up-and-coming actors Vinnie Jones, Stephen Graham, and Jason Statham. Pitt lent star power to the film and bolstered its success, expanding its commercial appeal outside the UK. Produced on a modest $10 million budget, the film was a breakout hit, earning $83.5 million from the global market, alongside a certified fresh rating of 74% from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, and a near-perfect 93% rating from audiences.

'Heart of the Beast' Is a Survival Thriller that Tugs at the Heartstrings

Grossing eight times its budget like the Guy Ritchie gangster flick might be a long shot for Heart of the Beast, but it's still succeeding on its own merits. It has an overwhelming 95% audience rating on Rotten Tomatoes and an A- grade on CinemaScore. It's also holding strong with a 45% drop in its second week and is showing good legs, especially in the international market, where it has so far grossed $60.3 million against $39.2 million domestic. Part of its accessible appeal is its universal premise — a survival drama about a man and his dog forced to brave the elements. It's a four-quadrant movie that effortlessly tugs at the heartstrings, much like Togo, Call of the Wild, Arthur the King, and I Am Legend.

Pitt in Heart of the Beast is a cross between his performances in Fury, Ad Astra, The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford, and Seven Years in Tibet. Heart of the Beast is a unique two-hander with minimal exposition and dialogue — its emotional beat is the bond between James Belmont (Pitt) and his retired combat canine Odin (played by Uber the dog for the most part), both battle-scarred war veterans who have now retreated to private life. It's a gripping film that makes you root for their survival, before being rewarded with a gut-wrenching payoff that's both heartbreaking and cathartic.
