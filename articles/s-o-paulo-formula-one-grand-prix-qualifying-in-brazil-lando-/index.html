That’s all from me. Tomorrow afternoon, Daniel Harris will be with you for the race proper, however long it lasts, with the lights due to go out at 5pm GMT. Giles Richards’ report will be with us shortly, but here’s a taster:

Lando Norris took pole position for McLaren at the São Paulo Grand Prix at Interlagos, with George Russell setting the second fastest time. Norris put in a strong lap in very tricky wet conditions during a qualifying session that took two and a half hours to complete because of heavy rain. However, Russell has a grid penalty, promoting the new world champion, Kimi Antonelli, to second and the Racing Bulls’ driver Liam Lawson to third and Audi’s Nico Hülkenberg to fourth.

Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton were also amongst a raft of drivers taking a grid penalty for taking a new engine and the Dutchman opted to not set a time after Q1, to save his tyres for the race and will start from 21st.

Hamilton also had to take a full range of engine components, having had a new internal combustion engine for final practice which then suffered a problem and he will start from 20th place. His Ferrari teammate Charles Leclerc finished in fourth but has a ten-place penalty for taking a new ICE and will start from 13th.

Well, that was fun. At times it did not look as if we would get Q1 done, never mind Q3.

The weather is on his mind. “We’ll try to be ready for anything … And thank you for all the fans for staying out with us.”

“I struggled a little bit in the inters, I made a big mistake and almost went off at turn five. Still was enough for P3.”

Now Antonelli, greeted as the new world champion. Did it feel any different? “It was very tricky … I didn’t know what to expect, I have never driven here in the wet.”

His hopes, given the penalty, depend on the weather. “I feel more confident in the wet than the dry.”

Now Russell, asked about his frustration: “Obviously congrats to Lando, but that was definitely the pole lap. A misjudgment on our side, we need to understand what happened … The rain was coming, we should have just finished the lap.”

He continues: “I’m very happy … It was about getting two laps in and not making any mistakes, and that’s what we did.”

Norris speaks to Naomi Schiff: “It’s been a long day … It’s been a great qualifying, to be on pole again here, two years in a row.”

This will be it. Norris’s 1:27.470 will take pole and Antonelli will be alongside him, as Russell drops to seventh. Leclerc has a 10-place penalty, so Lawson will be third, with Hulkenberg alongside him.

Everyone back in the pits. The clock ticking, barely four minutes left.

Russell furious at being told to back off, because Albon was in the way, but then the rain came. Toto Wolff trying to console him.

Norris, Russell, Antonelli, Leclerc, Lawson, Hulkenberg, Hadjar, Bearman, Alonso, Albon.

Ted Kravitz says the heavy rain is here. That could be that.
