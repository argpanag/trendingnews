Who is Terence Tao, the ‘Mozart of Math’ who learnt calculus at 7, earned a PhD at 20 and became UCLA’s youngest full professor at 24? - The Times of India

TheTools · 02.11.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

Terence Tao, widely known as the ‘Mozart of Math’, is one of the world's most respected mathematicians. He attracted attention at a very young age because of his exceptional mathematical abilities.

He began studying calculus when he was 7 years old, earned his PhD at 20 and became a full professor at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) at 24.

Tao was born in Australia and as a youngster became fascinated with numbers. He has made significant contributions to mathematics over the years and won several prestigious awards, including one of the highest awards in mathematics, the Fields Medal.

His journey from a young mathematics enthusiast to a globally recognised professor has made him an inspiration for students, researchers and mathematics lovers around the world.

Terence Tao, nicknamed the ‘Mozart of Math’, is an Australian-American mathematician, professor at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). He is noted for his outstanding mathematical talents and work in number theory, harmonic analysis, combinatorics and partial differential equations. Due to his successes, he is one of the most renowned mathematicians in the world.

Terence Chi-Shen Tao was born on 17 July 1975 in Adelaide, Australia, and had a remarkable fascination with numbers and learning from a young age.

From his early days as a child, he was also endowed with great mathematical skills and became fascinated with the abstract problem he was able to solve. His first educational experiences were interesting because he knew about mathematical things in a very early age.

Tao's father says he taught himself calculus when he was seven and he started college-level math at nine. His parents supported his education, and encouraged his curiosity.

It would take years of study, practice and dedication for his childhood achievements to help him garner the recognition he sought as a mathematics prodigy.

After that, Terence Tao went on to study at Flinders University in Australia; he earned a Bachelor's and Master's degree at the age of 16. Later on, he immigrated to the USA to study at Princeton University. In 1996 he earned his PhD in mathematics at the age of 20, which was another great achievement in his academic career.

After completing his doctorate, Tao joined UCLA as a faculty member. In 2000 he became a full professor, the youngest in the university's history. His research and academic work have helped build his reputation as one of the leading mathematicians and academics around.

The Fields Medal, the pinnacle of mathematics awards, was one of the greatest successes of Terence Tao's career when he was awarded the prize in 2006.

He was honoured for his exceptional service to several fields of mathematical studies. He is also recognized for his ability to apply concepts from various areas of mathematics and to join researchers in tackling difficult problems.

Students who have an interest in mathematics, science and research will find inspiration in the life of Terence Tao from a child prodigy to a world-renowned mathematician. His story is a reminder to be curious, keep learning and thinking creatively.

Tao is a very influential mathematician and professor, and has been known as the ‘Mozart of Math’.

Terence Tao's educational journey was different from that of most students. He began attending university mathematics classes at a young age and later pursued higher education in Australia and the United States.

Tao won bronze, silver and gold medals at the International Mathematical Olympiad in 1986, 1987 and 1988, respectively.

His gold medal achievement at the age of 13 made him the youngest gold medallist in the competition's history at that time.

He moved to the United States for graduate studies and completed his doctoral research under mathematician Elias Stein at Princeton University.

Terence Tao's research covers multiple branches of mathematics. His work explores difficult problems involving equations, patterns, numbers and mathematical structures.

Some of his key research areas include:

1. Number theory: The study of numbers, their properties and relationships, including prime numbers.

2. Harmonic analysis: A branch of mathematics that studies functions and signals using techniques related to waves and frequencies.

3. Combinatorics: The study of counting, arrangements, combinations and mathematical patterns.

4. Partial differential equations: Equations used to understand changes in quantities such as heat, waves and fluid movement.

His research has contributed to mathematical understanding across different disciplines, and his collaborative approach has encouraged researchers to work together on challenging problems.

Terence Tao was awarded his PhD from Princeton University in 1996 and became a professor at UCLA's mathematics department in 1997.

He was first an assistant professor and later he continued his work in advanced mathematics.

At age 24, Tao was made a full professor at the University of California, Los Angeles, in 2000, breaking the record for the youngest person ever made a full professor at the university.

He kept advancing with his academic career, tackling mathematical challenges and engaging in interdisciplinary research.

Tao is also affiliated with UCLA's Institute for Pure and Applied Mathematics, where he has participated in research projects and collaborations.
