CFB FanDuel DFS Breakdown for Week 7

We cross the halfway point of the college football season in Week 7, but there is still plenty of college football DFS remaining. We've got a number of juicy matchups to look at this week, and a number of under-the-radar games that are more fun to watch from a fantasy perspective.

Headlining the fantasy realm is the Big 12 this week, with West Virginia-Cincinnati (63.5) and Houston-Oklahoma State (62.5) leading the way in expected scoring totals, the only two games to surpass 60. Ole Miss-Missouri (58.5) is next in line, while Miami (38.5) and LSU (36.5) sport the two highest expected totals to do the heavy lifting with their game totals.

Georgia (34.5) is third on the expected scoring list, while Texas (31.5) rounds out the expected 30-plus point scorers.

Week 7 CFB DFS Plays on FanDuel

Quarterback

Michael Hawkins, West Virginia ($12,200) vs. Cincinnati

Hawkins is giving Denard Robinson in the Rich Rodriguez system, putting up video game fantasy numbers on a weekly basis. It was a bit more of a struggle last week against Arizona, but he should find Cincinnati's defense much easier to generate offense against. The Bearcats rank fifth in most points per game allowed to opponents on the slate (20.4) and have yielded 17-0 rushing yards and three scores over five games to opposing QBs. That's music to the ears of Hawkins, who has racked up  117 carries for 672 yards and 12 rushing touchdowns through six games.

 Conner Weigman, Houston ($11,300) vs. Oklahoma State

Weigman is another dual-threat option under center, though he hasn't quite relied on his legs as much as he did a season ago, when he racked up 171 carries for 700 yards and 11 touchdowns over 13 games. That's okay, though; he's still found pay dirt regularly, chalking up five rushing TDs over the last three games, and he's on pace for his best season yet as a passer with 15 touchdown passes through the first six games and a career-best 8.6 YPA. 

Oklahoma State boasts a high-octane offense as well, so I don't mind the Drew Mestemaker ($10,900) as a pair with Weigman if you want to stack here. The Cowboys' defense has also allowed 24.9 and 32.6 FD points against the two Big 12 quarterbacks it has faced, so there's reason for optimism that Weigman can find success. 

 Bear Bachmeier, BYU ($9,300) vs. Notre Dame

This is definitely more of a GPP play, but Bachmeier will be counted on heavily in this game. Star running back LJ Martin remains sidelined with injury, and there just aren't any major options behind him. On top of that, the Cougars boast a solid contingent of weapons in the pass game, and Bachmeier can certainly scramble for yardage. I would be surprised if he's not the leading rusher in this contest (assuming he can avoid a heavy dose of sacks).

 Notre Dame has certainly been stingy to date, but the Fighting Irish have yet to face a ranked opponent this season and haven't faced any top-tier offenses to date either. North Carolina dropped 26 on the defense, with Billy Edwards racking up three touchdown passes, so there could be some vulnerability there to exploit with the talent in this offense.

Running Back

The Top Dogs

 Cam Cook, West Virginia ($10,800) vs. Cincinnati

This is one of the rare teams where stacking the quarterback and running back here is not a bad move. I harken back to the days of Pat White and Steve Slaton here in Morgantown, so give me my shares of this backfield. Cook is a workhorse, reaching 29 or more carries in four of the six games to date. That's enough said right there, and the matchup was highlighted a bit above. The Bearcats yield 4.6 yards per carry on the ground and 1.6 rushing touchdowns per contest. This offense could put up five or more.

 Jamal Roberts, Missouri ($10,500) at Ole Miss

Like Cook, Roberts is a volume hog, claiming a 67-plus percent carry share in every game but the opener against FCS Arkansas-Pine Bluff. It doesn't hurt that this matchup features an Ole Miss front allowing 23.4 FD points per game to opposing running back rooms. To be fair, most of the damage came via the hands of Florida star back Jaden Baugh, but Roberts should be afforded a heavy dose of carries again in this one and has put up big numbers and is coming off back-to-back games of 200-plus rushing yards.

Mid-Tier Targets

 Bo Jackson, Ohio State ($7,600) at Indiana

Jackson was fully unleashed last week versus Maryland to the tune of a season-high 23 carries for 107 yards and a pair of touchdowns. With Ja'Kobi Jackson ruled out for Saturday, it should be the Bo show this week in the Buckeyes' backfield, though Legend Bey and Isaiah West could snag some carries along the way as well. This is too important of a matchup for Ohio State to let slip, so expect heavy usage of the most trusted back here, and he comes at an affordable rate.

 Caleb Hawkins, Oklahoma State ($7,500) at Houston

I still can't understand the Hawkins pricing here. Sure, he did have a down week last week versus UCF last week, but that included a pair of rushing touchdowns from other sources. That's a pretty random result, especially when one of them was Morgan McPhaul, who has one carry all season. Drew Mestemaker tallied the other, but Mestemaker isn't exactly a scrambler: that was just his second touchdown of the campaign. Hawkins still touched the rock 25 times in the contest, including a pair of catches for 32 yards. 

Bargain Options

 Gi'Bran Payne, Cincinnati ($6,100)

Payne has laid a clear claim to the top spot in Cincinnati's backfield, boasting 42 and 39 percent carry share over the past pair of tilts. That equated to only 27 combined carries, but the Bearcats also fell way behind and got blown out, so it makes sense the run was largely abandoned. This contest is expected to finish with a much closer result, and the Mountaineers have allowed 4.6 yards per carry and 163.8 rushing yards per contest on the year, making this a prime matchup for the running back room to get involved.

In the four games against FBS teams this season, the Mountaineers have allowed 42, 28.7, 54.4 and 34.8 FD points to opposing RB rooms. That's enough on its own to get me interested, and this is an extremely cheap price tag.

 Abu Sama, Wisconsin ($5,700) at UCLA

Speaking of cheap price tags for starting running backs. Sama has been dinged up early this season, but he's coming out of a bye with a probable tag for Saturday's clash. Wisconsin doesn't figure to put up the same figures as Cincinnati in terms of points, but the Iowa State transfer is the most explosive option here, averaging 6.8 yards per carry on the year and 6.8 yards per carry on his five totes against Penn State before injury ended his night. Darrion Dupree is a fine back, but he's much more capable as a receiver than a runner.

Having watched this team regularly, I'm also aware that it changed the offensive line alignment prior to the Michigan State game into a much more solid unit. Expect Sama to see plenty of run in this one, and he's on the cheap.

Wide Receiver/Tight End

The Top Dog

 Jeremiah Smith, Ohio State ($12,500) at Indiana

If you can find a way to fit Smith in your lineup, of course you want to. He's the biggest target hog on the slate (37.6 percent) and puts up otherworldly numbers with it, with 13.7 yards per target and 11 touchdowns in six games. It's just a question of if you can fill out an optimal lineup around him while including him. It would likely require bypassing a second quarterback and utilizing Smith as your Superflex, which is certainly a reasonable decision, given that he's tallied 31.6 or more fantasy points in three of his six games to date.

 Charlie Becker ($8,700) and Nick Marsh ($8,200), Indiana vs. Ohio State

Indiana's wideout room is arguably as talented as the Buckeyes, though the clear No. 1 of the bunch is generational wideout Jeremiah Smith ($12,500). The Hoosiers remain underdogs here by 2.5 points, but they are going to need to throw the ball. Hoover wants to his his targets out wide, something he did when he was at TCU and continues to do at IU. Becker and Marsh both boast 30.7 percent target shares on the season, and they both average an identical 12.6 yards per target (Becker has one more receiving yard). Becker has hit pay dirt twice more than Marsh, but Marsh has four in the last three games compared to Becker's two.

The matchup is obviously not great, but this is a prolific passing attack, and at least one of the two here will need to put up numbers if the Hoosiers are going to keep it close, and don't be shocked if both do. I'd lean slightly toward Marsh here, given the $500 price disparity, but so too will likely those taking a wideout. If you want to go against the grain and have the budget left over, taking a whirl with Becker doesn't hurt either.

Mid-Tier Targets

 Patrick Overmyer, Houston ($7,800) vs. Oklahoma State

Overmyer has become the favorite target for quarterback Conner Weigman, amassing 10 or more targets in three of the last four games. He leads the team in receptions (33), receiving yards (399) and receiving touchdowns (six). This game could turn into a shootout, and Overmyer is the man to bet on in this Cougars offense. Bonus: you can get him for less than $8,000 on the salary front.

 JV Gibson, Cincinnati ($6,900) at West Virginia

Gibson's 19.1 percent target share plays in comparison to some of the others above here, but it mostly all comes as a result of getting blanked last week versus Arizona. I'd expect a much better outcome this week and a lot more offense out of the Bearcats in the highest expected scoring contest of the week. Only Isaiah Johnson boasts more targets than Gibson, but he works out of the slot and averages just 5.3 yards per target to Gibson's 10.7, and Gibson leads the team with three receiving touchdowns.

Bargain Options

 Jelani Thurman, North Carolina ($6,300) at Duke

Thurman has impressed for the Tar Heels this season and, like Overmyer, is one of the few tight ends to lead his team in target share. In fact, the tight ends are the only ones to reach paydirt through five games for UNC, with Thurman finding the end zone four times and Shamar Easter doing so twice. The team total isn't particularly impressive, but the Tar Heels hung with Notre Dame and seem to have found a bit of a groove offensively.

 Derek Meadows, Alabama ($5,400) at Tennessee

I mean, Ryan Coleman-Williams is out this week. That is the big absence that leaves a void in this offense. On top of that, Keelon Russell has been at his best this season on the deep balls. That may partially be due to throwing a lot of those to Coleman-Williams, but it's also indicative of where Russell is seemingly most comfortable throwing them. Well, Meadows leads the team with a 15.3 ADOT on limited targets and seems the likely candidate to claim a starting role at the "X" with Coleman-Williams out. In a potential high-octane contest versus the Vols, sign me up. 

Note: Noah Rogers ($5,600) is another possible candidate to start for Coleman-Williams, so it will be worth monitoring who is set to get the nod. If it's Rogers, he's also a worthy target.
