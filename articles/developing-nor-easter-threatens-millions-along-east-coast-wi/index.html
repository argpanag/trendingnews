Significant beach erosion and dune scarping will develop from the Outer Banks through the Mid-Atlantic and Long Island as the surface low-pressure system forms.

HOW TO WATCH FOX WEATHER

The FOX Forecast Center said moderate coastal flooding is expected during high tides, particularly in vulnerable back bays and low-lying coastal roadways.

Coastal storm emerges Thursday into Friday

The storm will emerge off the coast and begin to strengthen sometime Thursday or Friday.

The Outer Banks are forecast to see major coastal flooding, extreme surf and heavy, continuous rain. Some areas could experience wind gusts of 40 to 50 mph, with offshore waves reaching 15 to 18 feet.

The region will also see widespread rainfall, although widespread flash flooding is not currently forecast.

However, pounding waves across multiple high-tide cycles could lead to coastal flooding and severe beach erosion.

Wind gusts along immediate coastal areas could reach 40 to 50 mph, pushing ocean water inland.

Nor'easter takes aim at New England this weekend

By Saturday and Sunday, the low-pressure center is expected to track north toward the I-95 corridor and New England, solidifying the nor’easter.

As the strengthening system tracks into or near New England, the strongest pressure gradient will impact the region, including Boston.

However, the FOX Forecast Center said there are a few scenarios that could unfold as the storm moves north.

One scenario is if the storm shifts closer to Boston. This could turn the storm into a major weather event, featuring 60 to 70 mph wind gusts, heavy rainfall, major storm surge and significant disruptions to Boston Harbor and the Massachusetts coastline.

On the other hand, if the storm shifts closer to New Jersey or Long Island, the strongest impacts could shift farther south, potentially putting New York City on the fringe of the storm.

Finally, if the area of low pressure remains offshore, coastal New England could still experience rough surf, elevated tides and breezy conditions, while the heaviest rain and strongest winds remain displaced to the south and east over open water. The FOX Forecast Center said this scenario is appearing less likely.

What is certain, according to the FOX Forecast Center, is that wind gusts could reach over 60 mph as the peak of the storm shifts into southern New England, Massachusetts Bay and the Gulf of Maine.
