UK set for 27C heat before wet and windy weather brings 50mph gusts

TheTools · 30.09.2026 06:24 · source · permalink

Temperatures are set to reach up to 27C in parts of the UK, marking an unseasonably warm spell.

The heat is expected to peak in London on Tuesday, before a shift to wet and windy conditions midweek.

Stephen Dixon, spokesman for the Met Office, said: "A high of 27C is expected in London on Tuesday, which is notable for this time of year and will feel fairly unseasonable for many."

The warm weather is being driven by a low-pressure system over the Atlantic, drawing warmer air from Europe toward the UK.

However, the settled conditions are not expected to last.

Mr Dixon said: "By later on Tuesday evening, we start to see the arrival of rain to western areas, where some western upslopes could see rainfall totals of between 20 and 30mm of rain.

"Particularly thinking about areas to the south west of England, parts of Wales, Northern Ireland, and Scotland, that front of rain will gradually move eastwards Tuesday night and into Wednesday."

It will remain warm overnight, with minimum temperatures of 19C in some areas.

Mr Dixon said: "An overnight low of 19C again would be notable for the time of year and could feel a touch uncomfortable for some, particularly as we’re in late September."

Much of the UK will see rain on Wednesday, with gusty winds and the potential for large waves along western coasts.

Some areas could see more than 40mm of rain and wind gusts exceeding 50mph.

Mr Dixon said: "It’s going to be a fairly wet and blustery day for many."

The Met Office has also warned of a risk of "some very large waves for some western coastal areas" as the front moves through.

Conditions are expected to improve later in the week.

Mr Dixon said: "Thursday and Friday will be days of sunshine and showers and mixed conditions."

Higher pressure is forecast to build over the weekend, bringing more settled weather.

The Met Office described the week ahead as bringing "remarkable temperatures, big waves and a bit of heavy rain too."

Despite speculation, forecasters say it is unlikely the UK will see its first named storm of the autumn during this period.
