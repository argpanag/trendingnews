Yankees-Rays ALDS Game 1 (LIVE on TBS/HBO Max)

TheTools · 03.10.2026 23:38 · source · permalink

ST. PETERSBURG -- The Yankees and Rays have shared the field for plenty of memorable moments, tightly contested games and deeply rooted disagreements over the years. They are natural rivals in the American League East, pitting New York’s history and high-payroll dominance against Tampa Bay’s ingenuity and lower-budget success.

They’ve shared the top spot in the standings, too. Since Tampa Bay’s worst-to-first turnaround in 2008, the Rays have won the division five times and the Yankees have won it six times.

But they’ve never shared the postseason spotlight quite like they will in this American League Division Series.

Believe it or not, the Rays and Yankees have only met in the playoffs once before. That matchup came in the 2020 ALDS, the unforgettable back-and-forth affair capped by Mike Brosseau’s game-winning home run off Aroldis Chapman, a distillation of the clubs’ “David vs. Goliath” narrative. But that series took place in San Diego’s Petco Park, a neutral site, during the pandemic.

Now, these two AL powers will square off in their home ballparks with a chance to advance to the AL Championship Series. The series begins Saturday night at Tropicana Field, where the Rays went 55-26 this season in their return to their home dome. It will move to Yankee Stadium, where the Yankees went 46-34 but couldn’t stop their rival from celebrating a division title on their field.

“I feel like every time we play them, it's a tight game, it's a dogfight,” Rays starter Drew Rasmussen said. “I'd say that's probably what should be expected.”

What will the latest installment of this rivalry bring? We’ll soon find out.

When is the game and how can I watch it?

Game 1 is underway at Tropicana Field. The game is being broadcast on TBS, truTV and HBO Max. 

The ALDS will be available in Spanish on UniMas, while Univision’s TUDN Radio will provide Spanish language audio coverage. ESPN Radio will provide live national coverage of all 2026 MLB Postseason games.

Who are the starting pitchers?

Yankees: Gerrit Cole, RHP
Key stats: 4-1, 2.09 ERA (9 ER, 38 2/3 IP) in his last six starts vs. the Rays

Gerrit Cole will start Game 1, the Yankees announced Friday. Carlos Rodón was also in contention for the series opener, according to pitching coach Matt Blake.

Had the AL Wild Card Series advanced to Game 3, Cole was in line to start; Rodón was in the bullpen, awaiting a potential starting assignment in Game 1. This will be Cole’s 23rd career postseason start -- and his first since Game 5 of the 2024 World Series.

“Carlos and I, we’re all just taking the ball,” Cole said. “We’re all just a little bit unique, I think, in the way we do it. I’m stoked. I’m really stoked about it, to be honest. I couldn’t have done it without a lot of the people that are pouring into it behind the scenes. It just means a little bit more to me.”

Looking ahead to Game 2, the Yankees will turn to Cy Young favorite Cam Schlittler, who threw 6 1/3 scoreless innings in a 9-0 win over the Red Sox in Game 1 of the AL Wild Card Series.

Rays: Drew Rasmussen, RHP 
Key stats: 5-2 with a 1.81 ERA and 0.86 WHIP vs. Yankees in regular season (11 games, 10 starts)

Rasmussen is “the standard” for Rays pitchers, one of the best arms in franchise history and undoubtedly the starter Tampa Bay wants on the mound to begin a short series. The last time he pitched in the regular season was at Yankee Stadium, where he carried a no-hitter into the seventh inning and struck out 11 batters in the Rays’ division-clinching victory over the Yankees.

That kind of success against the Yankees was nothing new for Rasmussen, who -- for reasons even he doesn’t fully comprehend -- has had their number over the years. Granted, the 31-year-old right-hander pitches well against pretty much everyone he faces. He led all AL pitchers in WHIP and strikeout-to-walk ratio, finished second in wins and ranked fourth with a 2.67 ERA this season.

Looking ahead to Game 2, the Rays have yet to decide who will start, but two options are right-handers Griffin Jax and Freddy Peralta, both of whom have pitched well at home this year. Peralta compiled a 1.69 ERA and 0.75 WHIP in his last four home starts, while Jax, the converted reliever, allowed one run over 18 innings in his final four starts.

How will the bullpens line up after the starter?

Yankees: After carrying 10 pitchers for the Wild Card Series, the Yankees added one arm for the ALDS. Ryan Weathers touched 96 mph during his recovery from a left flexor strain and is back on the roster. David Bednar, Paul Blackburn and Brent Headrick should all be leaned upon for high-leverage outs.  

Rays: We won’t know for sure until the Rays reveal their rotation plans beyond Game 1. But it’s probably easiest to start at the back end, where All-Star closer Bryan Baker was a rock in the ninth inning (and occasionally in the eighth, if needed). They have a solid setup crew in front of Baker in Tyler Wells, assuming he returns from a forearm issue, and the righty-lefty duo of Kevin Kelly and Cam Booser. Those four seem likely to get the biggest outs of the game, but it’s possible manager Kevin Cash could turn to one of his other starters -- perhaps a lefty like Ian Seymour, who performed well against the Yankees this season -- to build a bridge from Rasmussen to the high-leverage arms.

Any injuries of note?

Yankees: For the second straight playoff round, Judge was not added to the roster. He has yet to run the bases as he recovers from a right calf strain, though he has been performing outfield running. He has also taken numerous live at-bats while throwing. Weathers was added to the roster and will be an option out of the bullpen. Clarke Schmidt has been ruled out for the remainder of the postseason.

Rays: Quite a few. Wells finished the season on the IL due to a forearm issue, as did lefty reliever Garrett Cleavinger. Same goes for left-hander Shane McClanahan, whose status is unclear except that he will not pitch in this series. Simpson sat out the last two games of the regular season after fouling a ball off the top of his right foot. Wells, Cleavinger and Simpson should be available for Game 1 after getting through a week of workouts in good shape.

Who is hot and who is not?

Yankees: Rice is swinging a hot bat -- including the postseason, he is batting .365 (23-for-63) with seven homers and 15 RBIs over his last 17 games, dating to Sept. 10. He crushed the Rays this year, belting seven homers in 13 games. Wells drove in two runs in the Game 2 win over Boston and has 17 RBIs over his last 24 games after logging just 19 RBIs in his first 100 games. Lombard has not wilted under the postseason spotlight, collecting four hits and an RBI in the Wild Card Series. Bellinger drove in four runs in Game 2, including a homer. 

Rays: If you want to pick a potential breakout star this postseason, keep an eye on Mesa. The super-talented rookie outfielder hit .355 with a 1.015 OPS in September, and he’s got game-changing ability in the outfield. Palacios finished strong, too, posting a .981 OPS with five homers in September. Aranda doesn’t get as much love as the other members of the “Big Three,” but his left-handed bat is key -- and he hit better than either Caminero or Díaz in September. Tampa Bay’s entire rotation surged into the postseason, highlighted by Rasmussen and September AL Pitcher of the Month Freddy Peralta, but also including Griffin Jax and Seymour.

Mullins didn’t contribute much at the plate down the stretch, nor did Williamson in his return from a significant hamstring injury. Vilade’s power output also declined in September, and it wasn’t quite the month you’d expect out of Díaz (.666 OPS) or Hicks (.668).
