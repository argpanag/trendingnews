By Dhara Ranasinghe and Jiaxing Li

LONDON, Nov 9 (Reuters) - World stocks nudged up from six-month lows on Monday, recovering slightly from last week's tech-led rout, though a sharp fall in AI bellwether Nvidia was already shaping up as a fresh test of sentiment.

Nvidia shares fell just over 4% in ‌pre-market trading after an internal investigation into whether CEO Jensen Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort put his future at the AI ‌chipmaker under scrutiny.

Oil prices, meanwhile, fell over 3% with President Donald Trump set to meet the prime minister of key mediator Qatar on Monday as efforts to end the Iran war gather pace following last week's ​midterm elections in the United States.

US stock futures recovered from early falls in a positive sign for the Wall Street open. European shares rallied as bank stocks rose after Credit Agricole proposed merging its Italian subsidiary with Banco BPM, a move that would create Italy's third-largest bank.

The oil price fall supported sentiment, and Asian shares also rebounded as last week's hefty selloff attracted buyers.

Global stocks have been under pressure in recent weeks with the AI trade crumbling amid rising interest rates, and sweeping Democratic wins in the US midterm elections deepening worries about ‌increased regulation of AI and data centres.

Analysts said the outlook ⁠remained cautious with the selloff in Nvidia shares the latest test of investor sentiment.

"Markets are trying to figure out what this means for arguably the world's most important company right now," said Neil Wilson, UK investor strategist at Saxo.

"If Huang is forced to leave, what happens ⁠to Nvidia? As others have said, Huang is kind of irreplaceable for US leadership in AI."

MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan rose 1.25% after sliding 4.6% last week. Taiwan's benchmark rose almost 5% after notching up its steepest weekly drop in two decades last week.

MSCI's World Stock Index rose 0.5%, holding above six-month lows hit last week.

Earnings reports due this week from tech ​companies ​including CoreWeave, Applied Materials and Cisco Systems are set to put the profitability of AI ambitions ​to the test amid the massive costs of the build-out.

CoreWeave has ‌become a barometer of investor anxiety over AI, and the cost of insuring its debt has spiked into distressed territory recently.

"The only fundamental this week that matters is price," said Chris Weston, head of research at brokerage Pepperstone.

"You could easily see a lot of people going, 'You know what, now's the time to buy — blood on the streets. It's not as bad as everyone thinks,'" he said. "It will be driven by flow, and fundamentals will mean very little."
