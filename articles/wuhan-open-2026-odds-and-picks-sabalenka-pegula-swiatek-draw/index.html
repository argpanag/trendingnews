Wuhan Open 2026 Odds And Picks: Sabalenka, Pegula, Swiatek Draw

TheTools · 12.10.2026 11:17 · source · permalink

The final WTA 1000 tournament of the year is underway in Wuhan with 15 players missing, among them world No. 1 Elena Rybakina and defending champion Coco Gauff. Top seed Aryna Sabalenka and second seed Jessica Pegula lead what remains. This preview breaks down each half of the draw, compares first-round prices and picks three bets for Tuesday.

The Wuhan Open has a withdrawal list long enough to fill a seeded draw of its own. Elena Rybakina (foot), Coco Gauff (fatigue), Amanda Anisimova (wrist), Madison Keys (abdominal injury), Naomi Osaka and Emma Navarro were already out before the draw. Third seed Mirra Andreeva, fourth seed Linda Noskova and 2024 finalist Zheng Qinwen have since joined them, taking the total to 15.

What is left is a 56-player WTA 1000 with Aryna Sabalenka on top, Jessica Pegula at the bottom and a lot of open road in between. The tournament runs through Sunday, October 18 on outdoor hard courts, with $665,000 and 1,000 ranking points for the champion. The position here is simple: respect Sabalenka, be wary of Pegula, and bet the players who showed form in Beijing while their prices are still modest.

Wuhan Open First-Round Odds: Tuesday's Matches

Prices follow the order in which the players are listed. The best price on each player is in bold, and a dash means no price was posted on Monday morning. The matches are scheduled from 10 p.m. ET on Monday.

Outright prices for the tournament were not yet posted at these online sportsbooks on Monday morning, so the betting case below is built on match odds.

Top Half: Sabalenka's Record Against Her Draw

Sabalenka has won this tournament three times, in 2018, 2019 and 2024, and no other former champion is in the field. She has a bye and then plays American Ann Li or lucky loser Camila Osorio; Li is -120 at FanDuel in that first-round match.

The trouble starts after that. Elise Mertens, who beat both Gauff and Iga Swiatek at the China Open, could be waiting in the third round, and Swiatek, seeded seventh, is the projected quarterfinal. Sabalenka herself lost in the third round in Beijing. The market will still make her the favorite when outrights appear, and that is fair, but this looks like a draw to watch for two rounds before paying a short price.

Andreeva's withdrawal reshaped the second quarter. Iva Jovic, the 10th seed and a title winner in Guadalajara, now has a bye and a section with only one top-eight seed in it, Marta Kostyuk. For a player ranked 11th in the world, a semifinal is a realistic target, and Jovic is the name to look for at a price in this half.

Bottom Half: Pegula Returns Cold

Pegula lost last year's final here to Gauff, 6-4, 7-5, and she is the second seed. She is also playing for the first time since the US Open after missing the China Open. Her opening match is against Jelena Ostapenko or Daria Kasatkina, who came through qualifying, and Ostapenko is a -133 favorite at BetRivers to be that opponent.

The price to oppose will be Pegula's. A layoff of more than a month is a real handicap against an opponent who has already won a match on these courts. Karolina Muchova, the sixth seed, is in the same quarter, and Elina Svitolina and Belinda Bencic head the other one after Noskova pulled out. Canada's Leylah Fernandez is live in Pegula's section too: she beat Ekaterina Alexandrova 6-4, 3-6, 6-3 on Monday and plays Alexandra Eala or qualifier Shi Han next.

Tuesday's Best Bets

The value on Tuesday is with form over ranking. Mertens is -204 at DraftKings against Daria Snigur. After wins over Gauff and Swiatek in Beijing, that is a fair price on the 14th seed, and the main risk is the flat match that often follows a big week.

Nikola Bartunkova is -256 at DraftKings against Lilli Tagger. The 20-year-old Czech beat Muchova and Sabalenka at the China Open, where she collected two of her three career wins over top-10 players. The moneyline is short for a single, so the better play is to pair her with Mertens, a parlay that pays close to even money.

The third bet is a small one. Katie Boulter is +315 at FanDuel against 11th seed Diana Shnaider. Shnaider deserves to be favored, but Boulter took Muchova close in Beijing, and that is enough to make the underdog price worth a modest stake. I would not go beyond that. Samsonova against Siniakova and Yastremska against Bucsa are close to pick'em prices with nothing to separate the players, and both are passes.

Wuhan Open Schedule

The first round ends Tuesday, October 13. The second round is Wednesday, the third round Thursday, the quarterfinals Friday, the semifinals Saturday and the final Sunday, October 18, not before 6:30 a.m. ET. Total prize money is $4,088,211.
