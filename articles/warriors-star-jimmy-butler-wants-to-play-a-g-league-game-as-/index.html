Warriors star Jimmy Butler wants to play a G League game as he rehabs from ACL tear

LIVE

Jimmy Butler says he wants to suit up for the Santa Cruz Warriors as part of a rehab assignment. Ezra Shaw / Getty Images

HOUSTON — As Golden State Warriors star forward Jimmy Butler continues to rehab from a torn ACL in his right knee, he has set his sights on a new goal on his path back to an NBA floor: a stop in the G League.

“I’m going down to Santa Cruz to hoop,” Butler told The Athletic recently.

Butler, who suffered the ACL injury on Jan. 19, proclaimed his desire to experience the G League with a big smile on his face. While his exact return date remains unclear, he is adamant that he will get at least one game with the Santa Cruz Warriors in the NBA’s developmental league before returning to action with his NBA teammates.

“It may not be for long, but I’m going to play in Santa Cruz,” he said. “I’m playing in a f—ing game.”

Butler acknowledged that his former Chicago Bulls teammate and current Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy might not like the plan, but he said he’s set on the experience. One person who is on board with Butler’s G League desire, however, is Warriors coach Steve Kerr.

“Oh, hell yeah,” Kerr told The Athletic prior to Wednesday’s game against the Houston Rockets. “I love that. Jimmy told you that? I love that. It’s funny because I’ve always thought about that given my baseball fandom; you see these guys go to the minors all the time for a rehab stint and they get several games. It makes perfect sense, but I’ve never really seen a star player go to the G League. Has it happened?”

The answer to that question is … no.

Many famous players, including Butler’s current teammate Stephen Curry (who practiced with the Santa Cruz Warriors before returning from a broken hand in March 2020), have participated in G League practices. Serge Ibaka, a three-time All-Defensive First Team player during his career, completed a four-game G League assignment in 2021. But it’s safe to say Butler would be the biggest star to play in a G League game on a rehab assignment.

“Well, y’all gonna get your opportunity come Santa Cruz,” Butler said. “Because I’m definitely coming down there to play. So good luck to everybody. Y’all ain’t gonna get too many minutes out of me, but I’m definitely going down there. I want to hoop.”

Kerr, a lifelong Los Angeles Dodgers fan, said he appreciates the idea of a basketball player taking a similar approach to a rehab assignment as a baseball player.

“I think it makes such perfect sense,” Kerr said. “But most guys in the league in Jimmy’s position are, like, too vain to do it — and Jimmy’s the opposite. I think he would love giving the fans down there a great surprise. That’s amazing. I love that.”

The idea for Butler’s G League appearance came, at least in part, from an Instagram post Butler recently saw about how many players had played in both the NBA and the G League. Later, during a conversation about his NBA career, Butler was asked a question about young players looking up to him and he referenced the stat that had been sticking in his mind.

“I take a lot of pride in that, man,” Butler said. “And honestly, I saw this thing on Instagram the other day about (how) there was like 275 guys in the NBA that had G League experience. It’s finna be 276 because, baby.”

Has Butler cleared that with Dunleavy?

The smile that was affixed to Butler’s face throughout the G League conversation only got bigger.

“I don’t got to clear s— with nobody,” Butler said. “I’m going. Me and Mike will fistfight. He’s gonna be like, ‘Jimmy, no.’ I’m gonna be like, ‘Mike, yes. I’m going to G League, baby!’ Y’all know where to find me. Santa Cruz, baby.”

One person who will definitely be in attendance, as long as the schedule permits, whenever Butler decides to play for the Santa Cruz Warriors, is Kerr.

“I’ll be there,” Kerr said. “As long as we don’t have a game, I will be there. Hell yeah.”
