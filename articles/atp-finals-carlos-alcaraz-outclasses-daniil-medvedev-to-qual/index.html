ATP Finals: Carlos Alcaraz outclasses Daniil Medvedev to qualify despite injury concern

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:19 · source · permalink

Carlos Alcaraz was denied a first ATP Finals triumph by Jannik Sinner in last year's final

  • Published
    18 November 2026

Carlos Alcaraz continued his sensational late-season form as he outclassed world number five Daniil Medvedev to reach the knockout stage at the ATP Finals.

But the 23-year-old Spaniard later said he had felt "a little problem" with his hamstring.

Alcaraz produced another sparkling display to control the contest throughout, despite ending the match with heavy strapping on his upper-left leg.

The issue did not disrupt the world number three, who led by a set and a break when he received treatment on the area, as he cruised to a 6-2 6-4 win in just 72 minutes.

"Right now I'm just feeling OK," said Alcaraz, whose qualification for the semi-finals was confirmed by Frances Tiafoe's win against Flavio Cobolli.

"We will see with my team and physio, but I think the rest of today and tomorrow are really going to be important days ahead of me."

It was an 11th consecutive victory for the seven-time major winner, following back-to-back titles at the Vienna Open and Paris Masters.

Alcaraz is scheduled to face Italy's Cobolli - who cannot reach the knockout stage following his 7-6 (8-6) 6-0 loss to Tiafoe - in his final group match in Turin on Friday.

Asked what pleased him most about his performance, Alcaraz replied: "Everything".

"We could see in [Medvedev's] first match that he was serving very well. I returned really well, really aggressively, and really deep," he added.

"I think I just hit the ball in a sweet spot every time. I'm really happy about it."

Given the outstanding levels at which he is currently operating, it is easy to forget that Alcaraz spent four months out of action in the middle of the season.

The former world number one's rapid return from that wrist injury to his scintillating best has been astonishing.

Alcaraz has won three titles and lost just twice in 24 matches since making his return at the US Open in August, when his lack of match fitness appeared to catch up with him in a four-and-a-half hour epic against Ben Shelton.

Just 10 weeks on, he delivered one of the best indoor performances of his career in sweeping past 2020 champion Medvedev.

That came two days after he began his latest bid for a first ATP Finals title with a statement victory over Tiafoe - and later warned his rivals he has never felt better at this stage of the season.

Medvedev beat home hope Cobolli in straight sets in his opening match for his first win against a top-10 opponent since defeating Alcaraz at Indian Wells in March.

The Russian was no match for the Australian Open champion on this occasion, however.

A dominant seven-game streak ultimately decided the match, as Alcaraz ran away with the first set and then broke immediately in the second to take firm control.

The second seed did not face a single break point as he outmanoeuvred Medvedev, firing 16 winners and committing just 13 unforced errors.

It was a display which scored 9.5 out of 10 on the ATP's performance rating, which combines several statistics to measure a player's overall game, following a 9.6 in his opener.

That was despite Alcaraz playing the final seven games with the issue in his hamstring, which he will now work to overcome in the next 48 hours.

Tiafoe keeps semi-final hopes alive

Frances Tiafoe claimed his sixth win against top-10 opponents in 2026 - the third-most on the tour behind Jannik Sinner (10) and Carlos Alcaraz (eight)

Tiafoe will go head-to-head with Medvedev for a place in the semi-finals after earning his first career win over home favourite Cobolli at the ATP Finals.

The importance of this match to both players' aspirations of progressing on their tournament debut was evident in a tense opening set which featured several momentum swings.

But it was Tiafoe, 28, who managed to maintain his composure in key moments, the American world number eight recovering from 3-1 and a break point down in the set, and two set points down at 6-4 in the tie-break, to take control of the match.

Cobolli's frustrations were clear as he missed significant opportunities on Tiafoe's serve to lead 4-1 and 5-4, before conceding the tie-break by losing four consecutive points.

With Cobolli unable to shake off that disappointment, Tiafoe cruised to victory by winning 28 of the 40 points played in a one-sided second set to book a winner-takes-all meeting with Medvedev.

In the doubles, Briton Neal Skupski and American team-mate Christian Harrison, seeded second on their tournament debut in Italy, bounced back from their opening defeat with a 6-3 6-4 victory over Italians Simone Bolelli and Andrea Vavassori.
