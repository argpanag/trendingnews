Lucia Caminos in Grand Theft Auto VI at 4K with DLSS 5 and MFG 6X at 280 FPS on an RTX 5090

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:56 · source · permalink

We started playing Grand Theft Auto VI on a workbench with a processor AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 graphics card. A PlayStation 5 Pro console was connected to the computer via an AVerMedia Live Gamer ULTRA 2.1 GC553G2 external video capture card, and the game was run at 4K resolution with maximum graphics settings. Image output was organized using OBS Studio, and video recording was done using the NVIDIA App. To transform the image and achieve maximum smoothness, we enabled NVIDIA DLSS 5 (3D-Guided Neural Rendering) real-time generative neural rendering with the Natural profile and DLSS 4.5 frame generation in 6X mode using NeuralScreen v2.6.4.

In the opening scene in a correctional facility featuring protagonist Lucia Caminos, we directly compared standard console graphics and neural rendering. NVIDIA DLSS 5 technology works on top of the standard image to add photorealistic visual effects, radically changing the perception of the scene. With the neural network enabled, Lucia's face, skin texture, strands of hair, the folds of her prison uniform, and the soft light falling from the window all became realistically rendered. Simultaneously, six-times frame generation boosted performance to stable 262 and 280 FPS, with peaks of 286 FPS, ensuring incredibly smooth movement.

The RTX 5090 graphics card utilized 62% to 75% of its system capacity while running DLSS 5 generative neural rendering, maintaining a core clock of 2857 to 2917 MHz at temperatures ranging from 57° to 65°C and power consumption ranging from 305W to 368W. Without active neural rendering, with frame generation only, the GPU utilization dropped to 37% and 40%, while power consumption dropped to 189W and 214W. The system utilized approximately 5,3GB of video memory, and just over 12GB of RAM. AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D It ran stably at 5225 MHz, with temperatures ranging from 57° to 63° C and power consumption ranging from 41W to 48W, handling real-time streaming of Grand Theft Auto VI without issue.
