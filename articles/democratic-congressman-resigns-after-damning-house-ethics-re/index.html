Democratic congressman resigns after damning House ethics report | CNN Politics

TheTools · 23.11.2026 19:51 · source · permalink

Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez is resigning from Congress on the heels of a damning ethics panel report that found he “made sexual contact” with a then-congressional aide without her consent as part of a series of incidents that culminated in her departure from Capitol Hill.

The California congressman said that while he disagrees with parts of the House Ethics Committee’s findings, his resignation would be effective on December 1.

“I also have serious concerns about the process. In my interview, I answered the Committee’s questions only to see my statements selectively quoted, disparaged, and disregarded throughout the final report, to which I was given no opportunity to respond,” he said.

“With this in mind, I see no other alternative than to resign, effective December 1, to allow for my staff to close out important constituent casework and needs.”

The ethics panel concluded in a report Monday that “Representative Gomez’s conduct caused this House staffer significant and ongoing distress and contributed to her decision to leave House employment altogether.”

The report, the culmination of a months-long probe, does not name the woman, who “repeatedly referenced a toxic and predatory culture” on Capitol Hill and alleged Gomez made sexual contact with her without her consent in 2024, according to the report. CNN previously reported that the former staffer, who was in her mid-twenties and worked for a House leadership office at the time of the incidents, had spoken with the committee.

“Representative Gomez failed in his obligation to behave in a manner that reflects creditably on the House, and his conduct was exacerbated by his failure to appreciate the inherent power imbalance between House Members and House staff,” the report states.

The report was made public by the committee as part of a tranche of investigative findings released Monday.

CNN has reached out to Gomez for comment.

The women’s attorneys told CNN in a statement that the panel’s report “confirms that Representative Gomez sexually harassed and assaulted our client while she was a staffer on Capitol Hill.”

“Representative Gomez abused the power given to him by the voters when he violated this young woman, effectively destroying her dreams of a career in public Service,” attorneys Lisa J. Banks and Sarah E. Nesbitt said.

Gomez previously acknowledged in a statement to CNN that he had made mistakes that caused his family pain but said he had not violated the law or any House ethics rules.

“Years ago, I made personal mistakes outside my marriage that have caused real pain to my wife and family. Although my actions were consensual in nature and haven’t violated the law or House ethics rules, that doesn’t diminish the impact that these mistakes have made on those I care about the most,” he said at the time, adding that he took “full responsibility” and had “sought professional assistance to help re-center and heal” relationships.

Gomez will not return to Congress in January after losing reelection to his Los Angeles-area seat earlier this month.

According to the report, the woman, identified as “Witness 1,” told the investigative panel that Gomez had sexually assaulted her at a bar in Washington, DC, and later groped her in his office.

CNN has independently spoken with the woman and individuals close to her, as well as reviewed text messages that corroborate what she told the ethics panel.

The woman told CNN that she first met Gomez at a birthday party in February 2024, and that they later met with acquaintances at a downtown bar. There, the woman said, Gomez told her that he had noticed her around the US Capitol.

The woman told lawmakers that she asked Gomez when men start focusing on how good women are at their jobs, rather than how they look. “Usually when a woman turns 40 is when they get respect,” she recalled him saying.

Gomez, according to the woman, “put his hand down [her] skirt and then put his fingers inside of [her].” Describing the incident as “nonconsensual whatsoever,” she recalled that she “probably flinched” and “was shocked by it.” She ultimately ran to the bathroom, where she began to cry, according to the report.

Later, the woman said she allowed Gomez to kiss her outside of the bar because she hoped that it would help her to leave the situation.

Gomez claimed the exchange was consensual in his testimony to the committee.

In another encounter that summer, the woman said Gomez grabbed her and tried to kiss her in his office before she told him “no” and left the room.

CNN has also spoken with the woman’s friend about the toll it took on her physical and mental health after she left Washington following that second incident. According to this person, she seemed “distraught and depressed” and started losing hair.

The woman’s attorneys said their client “sacrificed her own sense of safety and privacy to help reveal the ongoing misogyny and debauchery that permeates Capitol Hill” and called for Congress to take “immediate steps” to rein in that culture.

“Democracy cannot function properly when women must endure harassment and abuse as a condition of public service,” they said.

Recent high-profile allegations of sexual assault and harassment against lawmakers in both parties have brought renewed scrutiny to how Capitol Hill handles the issue and members of Congress hold those within their own ranks accountable.

Last week, the House Ethics Committee took steps to speed up probes into sexual misconduct, including changing how the bipartisan panel issues subpoenas and codifying its policy of publicly disclosing findings when lawmakers’ behavior violated House rules.

The report about Gomez was one of several made public Monday.
