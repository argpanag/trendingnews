Build-up of rain and thunderstorms (in blue and green) over South Peninsula and Sri Lanka and East and North-East India on Thursday. | Photo Credit: www.meteologix.com/in

The South Peninsula is beginning to show classic signatures of approaching north-east monsoon, with an expansive spell of rain, thunderstorms, lightning and gusty winds building, including over Andaman and Nicobar Islands, the gateway for easterly waves to typically move towards the Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka coasts.

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has forecast isolated heavy rainfall over Kerala and Mahe and Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal for three days from Thursday. At the same time, the stalled withdrawal of the south-west monsoon could resume, marking a sharper turn in the seasonal pattern.

The IMD said conditions are likely to become favourable for the south-west monsoon to withdraw from more parts of Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh and some parts of Telangana and North Interior Karnataka during the next 2-3 days. The weather pattern is being shaped on Thursday by cyclonic circulations over Lakshadweep and the adjoining south-east Arabian Sea, along with a trough extending from the circulation towards Rayalaseema across north Kerala and South Interior Karnataka.

IMD Update: Thunderstorms to strike TN, Puducherry, Karaikal for next six days

Together, these systems are creating a corridor for moisture from both the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal, raising the prospect of sharp showers accompanied by thunderstorms, lightning and squally winds across parts of the South Peninsula. Rainfall is expected to broaden and intensify over the coming days. Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam, Rayalaseema, Coastal Karnataka and South Interior Karnataka are likely to see isolated to scattered rainfall for up to seven days from Thursday, while thunderstorms and lightning could affect Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal through the same period.

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are also showing signs of the seasonal transition. Isolated to scattered rain is expected there on Thursday, before becoming fairly widespread to widespread from Friday and continuing for six days. This development is significant as the Andaman Sea is the usual entry route for easterly waves that subsequently feed the north-east monsoon over Tamil Nadu and adjoining parts of the South Peninsula.

Over the next several days, isolated to scattered rain is forecast over Lakshadweep and Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal, while Kerala and Mahe are likely to remain wet through the weekend and into early next week. North Interior Karnataka is also expected to receive isolated to scattered rain for three days from Friday. Fairly widespread to widespread rainfall is forecast over Lakshadweep and Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Karaikal on Friday, and over Kerala and Mahe for three days from Thursday.

The rain-bearing systems will also keep the atmosphere electrically active. Thunderstorms, lightning and gusty winds are likely over Kerala and Mahe and Lakshadweep on Friday and Saturday. Gusty winds are also expected over Kerala and Mahe on Thursday and Sunday, while Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam and Rayalaseema could experience gusty winds on Thursday and Friday. Strong surface winds are forecast over Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam and Rayalaseema on Thursday and Friday.

The expanding rain shield, increasing thunderstorm activity, strengthening winds and growing activity over the Andaman Sea point to a South Peninsula increasingly entering the north-east monsoon transition zone. The next few days could therefore provide an early taste of the easterly-driven weather regime that is expected to take firmer shape through next week.
