South Korea leaves APEC with an FTA upgrade, Japan with only a handshake from Xi

Xi Jinping shook hands with Japan's Sanae Takaichi in Shenzhen on November 18, their first contact since her Taiwan remarks, hours after sealing a trade deal with South Korea.

Set side by side, the two encounters at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit show how differently Beijing now treats Seoul and Tokyo. South Korean President Lee Jae Myung left with a concluded upgrade of the bilateral free trade agreement (FTA) and, by Seoul's account, Xi's endorsement of renewed talks between North Korea and the US. Japan's prime minister came away with a photograph.

A greeting, not a meeting

The handshake came at the leaders' welcome banquet. Takaichi stepped forward, offered her hand and said a few words; Xi smiled faintly and nodded without answering, according to The Japan Times and Bloomberg. Tokyo's foreign ministry press secretary, Toshihiro Kitamura, played it down as normal protocol. Beijing's own account of the evening says Xi and his wife Peng Liyuan greeted every arriving guest cordially and names none of them.

No bilateral meeting was arranged. Beijing had also sent a relatively junior party to receive Takaichi at the airport, South Korea's Nocut News noted. The freeze dates from November 2025, when she told parliament that an attack on Taiwan could threaten Japan's survival; China answered by telling its citizens to stay away from Japan and then curbed seafood imports and dual-use exports. She has retracted nothing, and says only that she is open to dialogue.

What Lee took home

Lee's 35-minute session with Xi earlier that day had substance. Seoul's trade ministry declared the follow-up negotiations on services and investment substantially concluded, eight years and eight months after they opened in March 2018, with only technical wording left to settle. China's readout confirms it, quoting Xi as saying the deal would 'further consolidate the win-win situation'.

The package is weighted towards Korea's strengths. China grants most-favoured-nation treatment to Korean films and games and, for the first time in any trade agreement, promises non-discriminatory pre-screening of such content. Korean travel agencies in 23 Chinese regions may sell foreign trips to Chinese customers, Korean retailers may run chains of 30 or more stores without a local partner, and forced technology transfer is barred as a condition of investment, the ministry said.

There is room to grow. China accounted for 20.7% of South Korea's goods trade in 2024 but only 12.4% of its services trade, $38.8bn, according to the ministry. Chinese customs figures already put South Korea ahead of Japan as a trading partner, at $331.7bn last year against $322.5bn.

North Korea, and the price of entry

On the peninsula, National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac said in a release: 'President Xi said he supports various efforts aimed at easing tensions on the Korean Peninsula, including North Korea-US dialogue.' Lee had described the issue as a 'do-or-die' matter for Seoul and asked China to play 'a constructive role', Yonhap reported.

China's version is silent on North Korea. What it records instead is Lee affirming that Seoul's commitment to the one-China policy 'has not changed and will not change'. The contrast is striking, but the summit record does not establish that Lee's one-China assurance secured the trade outcome or that Takaichi's Taiwan remarks caused the distance.

Russia at the edges

Moscow added its own pressure on the eve of the summit. On November 17 Russian military aircraft entered Japanese airspace off Okinoshima and passed through South Korea's and Taiwan's air defence zones. Tokyo lodged a protest; Seoul and Taipei kept to routine statements. A day later Vladimir Putin sat down with Xi in Shenzhen, so Japan ended the summit challenged by one neighbour and kept at a distance by another.

Seoul now speaks easily to both Tokyo and Beijing. Lee has accepted an invitation to visit Japan in January, and South Korea and China mark 35 years of diplomatic ties in 2027. For Takaichi, a handshake without words is a start of sorts; turning it into a meeting will cost something she has so far declined to pay.
