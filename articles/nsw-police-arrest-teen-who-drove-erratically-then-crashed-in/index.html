NSW police arrest teen who drove 'erratically' then crashed into crowd before NRL final

TheTools · 03.10.2026 12:25 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

An 18-year-old P-plate driver has been arrested after crashing into a crowd of Newcastle Knights fans, leaving two people, including a 5-year-old girl, in critical condition.

The incident happened as onlookers waited to farewell the team bus before the NRL grand final.

Officials said in a press conference this afternoon that 10 people were injured and were all taken to hospital.

A girl aged 5 and a 67-year-old man were in critical condition.

The eight other people remain in stable condition and include a second 5-year-old girl, a 2-year-old girl, two women aged 31 and 63 and four men aged 48, 24, 25 and 57, ABC reported.

ABC has also reported 10 ambulances and three helicopters are treating a number of people at the scene.

NSW Police Assistant Commissioner Dave Waddell said, unfortunately, a young 18-year-old P-plate (Provisional plate) motorist driving “erratically” lost control of the vehicle and went into the crowd.

“The male has been arrested. There was two other persons in the car. We had to get them out of there because the crowd did turn on them,” Waddell said.

He said the young man has been taken for mandatory drug and alcohol testing and charges are likely later today.

Video shared of the incident appears to show a BMW after it has collided with the crowd, with a helicopter later flying in and emergency service vehicles surrounding the scene.

New South Wales police said emergency services were called to Newcastle Link Rd at the Transfield Drive roundabout near Wallsend in the west of Newcastle.

Officers attached to Newcastle City Police District are at the scene, being assisted by Traffic and Highway Patrol Command officers and NSW Ambulance paramedics.

Newcastle Link Rd has been closed in both directions and is expected to remain that way while inquiries continue.

Police said this has impacted the planned route of the Knights’ grand final procession, which was diverted on an alternative route and has left Newcastle, which was confirmed by the Knights in a social media post.

“We apologise to anybody who was waiting and missed the boys, but public safety is our priority.”

Members of the public have been asked to safely disperse from the area and keep roadways clear.

Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said he has offered support to the NSW government.

“My thoughts are with everyone injured or involved in the horrible incident this morning in Newcastle,” Albanese wrote in a social media post.

“I want to acknowledge and thank emergency services and police for responding so quickly, and their ongoing work in treating the injured.

“Rugby league brings people together, and this should have been a weekend of excitement and pride for Newcastle fans.

“I have spoken to the NSW Premier Chris Minns, and offered our support.”

NSW Premier Chris Minns said his thoughts are with the families impacted by the incident.

“It’s heartbreaking to see fans who were sending off their grand final team involved in such a terrible incident,” he wrote in a social media post.

“My thoughts are with the families and friends of loved ones who have been involved or injured in the crash. I also want to thank emergency services including police and ambulance who are responding to this tragedy.

“We’ve seen how this grand final has brought Newcastle and Hunter communities together, and I hope the whole community remains united as we continue to respond.

“As large numbers of fans and families make their way down to Sydney for the grand final please drive safely and take caution on roads.”

Meanwhile, Australian Rugby League Commission executive chairman Peter V’landys said he and the commission were “deeply saddened” by the incident.

“We’re currently working with police to understand the circumstances.,” V’landys said.

“Our thoughts are very much with those injured and anyone impacted by these events, not to mention the entire Knights community who have come together in jubilation to support their team.”

Knights captain Kalyn Ponga said on Instagram that the team’s heart was with everyone affected.

“We’re thinking of you, your families and everyone in our Newcastle community.”Sending our love and support," Ponga wrote.
