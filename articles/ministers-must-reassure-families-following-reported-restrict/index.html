Ministers must reassure families following reported restrictions on residential disability supports - Social Democrats

TheTools · 06.11.2026 12:17 · source · permalink

Social Democrats TD Liam Quaide has called on Ministers Norma Foley and Emer Higgins to assure families that they will be provided with urgently needed residential disability supports following alarming reports of HSE restrictions on the approval of placements.

Deputy Quaide, who is the party’s spokesperson on disability, said:

“An internal HSE memo has instructed regions not to approve further residential placements or increases in residential support requiring additional funding for the remainder of 2026, including emergency cases.

“This memo is shocking given what the Government and HSE already know about the number of disabled people awaiting residential support and the circumstances of their families.

“Minister for Disability Norma Foley has now said she is engaging with the HSE on this matter. She must explain what this directive means for people awaiting a placement and assure families that the HSE will provide the support they need.

“The Before We Die campaign has highlighted that more than 2,000 adults with an intellectual disability live with parents aged over 70, including around 500 whose parents are over 80. These parents fear becoming seriously ill or dying without knowing who will care for their son or daughter.

“The HSE’s own National Disability Services Needs Analysis, which it initially refused to publish, recorded 1,649 people awaiting residential services in early 2026, including 330 emergency cases.

“It estimated a residential capacity shortfall of 4,288 places across adult and child services. The report acknowledges gaps and possible overlap in the data, but the scale of unmet need is clear. It also identified nearly 2,500 people whose respite needs were not being met.

“Yet the HSE’s National Service Plan provided for just 72 new planned residential placements across the country this year, alongside 80 emergency placements. Minister Foley and Minister of State for Disability Emer Higgins must explain how many of these placements the HSE has delivered and whether this directive has delayed any that remain outstanding.

“The Government’s provision in Budget 2027 was already nowhere near enough. It funded 212 new residential places, of which 112 are for emergencies, leaving just 100 planned placements for the entire country. That falls abysmally short of the level of need.

“Now families learn of a HSE instruction restricting further approvals, even for people assessed as needing emergency residential support.

“What happens if a parent dies or becomes too ill to continue providing care? What happens to someone whose existing residential support is no longer sufficient to keep them safe?

“The HSE has told its regions to prepare contingency plans to manage these risks. Ministers must explain what those arrangements involve and how the HSE will fund the support people need.

“Without Before We Die’s persistence, the Government and HSE could easily ignore these families. Parents who are already providing care around the clock should not have to campaign relentlessly to get the Government to plan for their children’s futures.

“Ministers Foley and Higgins must explain when they became aware of this directive, whether they approved it, and if it remains in force. They must assure families that the HSE will not refuse an urgently needed placement or increase in residential support because additional funding is required.

“Disabled people are entitled to the same choices as anyone else about where and with whom they live. The Government must fund the homes and supports that make those choices possible, so that disabled people can live and participate fully in their communities.”
