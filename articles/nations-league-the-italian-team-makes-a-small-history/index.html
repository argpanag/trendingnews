Nations League: The Italian team makes a small history!

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:22 · source · permalink

After beating France 3-1 following an early goal deficit, the Italian national team moved to the top of Group A in the Nations League ahead of the final round of matches.

The Italian national team has revived under coach Mancini.

The situation in Group A is still unclear, but the Azzurri have at least demonstrated their ability to compete with the strongest opponents. Looking ahead, the Italian team is in high spirits for their away match against Belgium, where a draw would be enough to secure qualification.

That's a huge difference from when Italy were eliminated from the 2026 World Cup by Bosnia, missing out on the World Cup for the third consecutive time (previously, Italy were eliminated from the 2018 and 2022 World Cups by Sweden and North Macedonia).

Coach Roberto Mancini returned to manage the Italian national team just over three months ago.

PHOTO: REUTERS

Having hit rock bottom in terms of disappointment, significant change doesn't happen overnight. Coach Roberto Mancini returned to lead the Azzurri just over three months ago. In their opening match of the Nations League, Italy lost 0-2 to Belgium at home, further fueling speculation about a bleak future for the four-time World Cup champions. But Mancini made eight changes to the starting lineup, and Italy won convincingly 4-1 away against Turkey in the next match, then drew 1-1 with France after dominating possession. Mancini's team then beat Turkey 3-1 in the return leg to demonstrate their progress. And now, a 3-1 victory over France, giving Zinedine Zidane his first defeat since taking over the French national team. This also marks the first time in 64 years that the Italian national team has beaten France at home.

Coach Zinedine Zidane suffered his first defeat since taking charge of the French national team after the 2026 World Cup.

PHOTO: REUTERS

These days, there's only one other noteworthy story concerning the already dilapidated state of Italian football. That's the miraculous rise of Como – a small club that was playing at an amateur level just a few years ago. Now, Como is soaring as the leading flagbearer of Italian football in the Champions League, currently sitting 4th in the standings, having just won 9-1 away at Lens in their last match. A key characteristic of Como: they hardly ever use Italian players. This can be linked to the personnel characteristics of the Azzurri under coach Mancini. He called up many players currently playing outside Serie A, and Italian players of foreign origin.

Throughout history, the Italian national team has only had a total of 6 players playing for foreign clubs when participating in the World Cup. The current Italian team has 17 players playing for foreign clubs since Mancini took over, with 8 of them in the squad for the recent match against France alone. Clearly, Mancini has a more independent vision and a more open-minded approach compared to the traditional player selection methods of Italian football.

The Italian national team under coach Roberto Mancini has many young players.

PHOTO: REUTERS

Regarding personnel, the Azzurri under Mancini are different from before in that they now have a large number of young players. In 2025, Serie A teams will only allocate 1.9% of their total playing time to Italian players under 21 years old (to reiterate: only 1.9% - the lowest rate in all of Europe). Only one player under 24 will have played for the Italian national team between 2025 and 2026 (as of February). Now, there are 14 players aged 23 or under in the Italian squad that just played France and is preparing to play Belgium.

There was experimentation and change before achieving stability. Mancini showed he dared to choose his own path, independent of Italian football tradition, but without being extreme enough to completely change things. The Azzurri's playing style in recent matches has focused on ball control (although most of the players are... relatively unknown). The Azzurri also play an attacking style, quite different from the defensive tradition of Calcio. Mancini is also good at motivating his players. He told them before the match against France: "This is a match we will talk about when we are old." The result: the Italian national team made a small history!
