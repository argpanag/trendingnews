Leonna Mayor Rides for Autism in Racing Fundraiser

TheTools · 16.10.2026 14:59 · source · permalink

Former jockey and broadcaster Leonna Mayor will return to competitive racing at Nottingham on October 28, joining eight other riders in a 1m2f fundraising race for Autism in Racing to support autism-friendly racedays, mentoring opportunities and community activities.

Mayor is taking part after being invited by Bobby Beevers of Autism in Racing, a cause she said has a personal connection because her younger sister has autism. Her sister is now 17, and Mayor said crowded spaces and social interactions can present daily challenges.

Autism in Racing works with racecourses to provide support and access to quiet and sensory spaces, according to Mayor. The initiative is intended to help people with autism and their families enjoy racing together.

Mayor has prepared by riding out on Gun Carriage and Battlefield Boy at Dylan Cunha's yard in Newmarket. Cunha is allowing her to ride Asgard's Captain, a six-year-old gelding whose listed successes include last year's Great Metropolitan Handicap at Epsom and this summer's Shergar Cup Challenge at Ascot.

Racing to School, where Mayor serves as an ambassador, has also loaned her a mechanical horse for training. She has continued riding but has not competed professionally as a jockey since 2013.

Returning to a racing saddle and short stirrups has required renewed cardiovascular fitness and leg and core strength, according to Mayor. Her preparation is taking place alongside raising her young son Teddy, working in broadcasting and other professional roles, and caring for her own horse and dog.

Mayor said the race represents both a return to competition and an opportunity to support a sport that has shaped her career.

"Riding in this race is another way of giving back to a sport that has given me so much," said Leonna Mayor, Racing to School Ambassador.

She has already exceeded her £3,000 fundraising target, according to the source. The event will also be used to raise awareness of autism and the work Autism in Racing does to improve access to the sport.

Mayor also promotes racing through Racing to School, which is marking its 25th anniversary in 2026. The charity provides free, curriculum-linked learning days at racecourses, training yards and stud farms across Britain.

In a recent visit to Derby College, Mayor met equine students to discuss career opportunities across racing. Racing to School uses hands-on activities including Equicisers, virtual reality headsets and jockey equipment to introduce young people to the industry.

Racing to School is expected to engage 18,500 young people in 2026, according to the source. Some participants may later need additional support to access racing through initiatives including Autism in Racing and existing racecourse sensory rooms.
