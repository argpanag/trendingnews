In short: 

Former Wagga Wagga MP Daryl Maguire has been sentenced to jail after being found guilty of a conspiracy charge for his role in a fraudulent visa scheme.

A jury earlier this year heard he recruited participants to the initiative, which involved Chinese nationals seeking permanent residency.

His three-year sentence has a non-parole period of one year.

Former Wagga Wagga MP Daryl Maguire has been jailed for his role in a fraudulent visa scheme.

The disgraced former Liberal Party politician, who held the seat for almost 20 years, was found guilty of a conspiracy charge after a jury trial in July. 

Judge Ian Bourke today sentenced him to three years in prison, with a one-year non-parole period. 

Maguire's defence team indicated an appeal against his conviction would be lodged based on the conviction being unreasonable, and made an immediate application for bail. 

That was denied and Maguire was taken into custody.

Daryl Maguire (left) was the member for Wagga Wagga between 1999 and 2018.  (ABC News: Sam Foster)

Throughout the trial, held in June and July, the court heard the former member for Wagga Wagga knew false or misleading information would be provided to the Commonwealth on applications for visas, permitting non-citizens to remain in Australia between 2013 and 2015, while he was a sitting MP.

Maguire was involved in the visa scheme with his associate Maggie Wang, also known as Maggie Logan, and migration agent Monica Hao.

Logan was sentenced in August to an aggregate prison term of two years and 10 months, plus 240 hours of community service, on two charges relating to her involvement in the crime.

Judge Bourke today told the court he was satisfied Maguire "performed an important and pivotal role in the conspiracy".

"In my view, it was likely a joint effort, however the evidence is insufficient to reach any clear conclusions on the matter," he said.

"I do not, however, accept that it was Ms Wang or Ms Hao that was solely responsible for cooking up the idea and that Mr Maguire had no input.

"On the contrary, I am satisfied beyond beyond reasonable doubt that the offender was an active participant in the planning and execution of the scheme."

Throughout the trial, the jury heard Maguire's role was to recruit participants to the initiative, which involved Chinese nationals seeking permanent residency.

The court also heard he had long-standing links to China and visited the country with some of the business owners involved in the visa scheme.

He also met Chinese President Xi Jinping in 2014.

Daryl Maguire's trial earlier this year heard he met with Chinese President Xi Jinping in 2014. (Supplied: NSW District Court)

Multiple business owners, primarily in the former MP's electorate of Wagga Wagga, were called to give evidence throughout the trial and described receiving cash payments for their involvement in the initiative.

During a sentencing hearing last month, lawyers for Maguire argued his offending was on the lower end of the range and his role in the fraudulent scheme was limited to introducing prospective employers to Logan, who then took over the process. 

The Crown argued he was part of a detailed, profit-driven, long-running fraudulent "cash for visa" scheme as a member of parliament and a prominent member of the regional business community.  

Judge Bourke said that despite a lack of remorse or contrition, Maguire's rehabilitation prospects were "reasonably positive".

The maximum penalty for the offence was 10 years, but Judge Bourke said the crime committed by Maguire was at the lower end of the scale.

Maguire was previously in the national spotlight due to his one-time relationship with former NSW premier Gladys Berejiklian, who has not been accused of any wrongdoing.
