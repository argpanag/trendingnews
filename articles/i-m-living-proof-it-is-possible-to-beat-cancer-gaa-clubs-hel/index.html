‘I’m living proof it is possible to beat cancer’: GAA clubs help detect cancer for 40 people

TheTools · 09.11.2026 06:17 · source · permalink

Con Shanahan said: 'I’m really grateful that my cancer was detected early through the Lung Health Check'. 

A Limerick man among 40 people whose lung cancer was identified in a “life-saving” pilot project run in GAA clubs has joined calls for a national screening programme.

In Ireland, 60% of lung cancers are only found at a late stage when there is a lower chance of surviving.

However, the pilot project, funded by the Irish Cancer Society, has already found almost 40 cancers with 80% identified at Stage 1 since March last year.

Those people then had “lifesaving curative treatment”, according to a doctor leading the project.

Con Shanahan, 73, is from Askeaton, Limerick. He was invited onto the pilot because of his history with pneumonia and smoking.

When the team called, he said: “I remember asking her, ‘Are you sure it’s necessary?’ She said it could be worth my while.”

Now living in Louth, Con was then referred to Beaumont Hospital for further tests. To his shock, he was diagnosed with lung cancer in December.

“To my generation, the words ‘lung cancer’ always meant death,” he said.

“When I was younger and I heard lung cancer mentioned, I’d think, ‘How long have they got?’ It was feared; We spoke about it in whispers.”

Instead, he had treatment including surgery in January. Surgeons removed the top lobe of his right lung to take out the tumour, with a CT scan in July confirming he was cancer-free.

“The word ‘cured’ wasn’t in my vocabulary with lung cancer, but I’m living proof that it is possible to be cured of it, if it’s caught early,” he said. 

“I’m really grateful that my cancer was detected early through the Lung Health Check.”

Irish Cancer Society chief executive Nikki Gallagher said: “We are calling for the development of a free national lung screening programme for people at high risk of getting lung cancer.

“It is so important we don’t lose the momentum of this successful Lung Health Check pilot," said Ms Gallagher. 

'Early detection can save lives' 

“Con’s story is a good example of how early detection can save lives.”

She called on the Government to support this type of community testing.

“Bringing cutting-edge technology to accessible settings — in this case local GAA clubs — has ensured very high participation rates,” she said.

“It also shows that high-quality screening can be done cost-effectively in the community, and it offers the State a model for a value-for-money approach.”

The Irish Cancer Society has invested almost €5m in the pilot, she added.

Professor Daniel Ryan, respiratory consultant at Beaumont RCSI Cancer Centre and clinical lead for the pilot, said of the 40 people: “These patients have gone on to have lifesaving curative treatment. Our cancer detection rates and outcomes are comparable with anywhere in the world in this screened population.”

Over 98% of participants have already gone through a second round of screening, he added.

The campaign follows similar calls from West Cork patient Gillian Ryan. An active non-smoker, the mother of two was devastated at her diagnosis days after turning 40 in 2021. Just last week, she underwent another gruelling surgery taking six hours.

“I want to see my kids get married and have kids of their own and be a nana and have the kids dropped to me on the weekends. And I just want to live,” she previously told the Irish Examiner.
