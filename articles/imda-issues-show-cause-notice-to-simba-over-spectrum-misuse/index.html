IMDA issues show cause notice to Simba over spectrum misuse

The regulator is considering possible suspension or cancellation of spectrum right

Benjamin Cher

    • Simba says it intends to respond to the show cause notice, which has a deadline of Nov 16. PHOTO: ST

    [SINGAPORE] Simba’s parent Tuas Ltd announced on Monday (Oct 26) that the Infocomm Media Development Authority of Singapore (IMDA) has issued a notice to show cause over its misuse of telecommunications spectrum.

    The notice was sent to Simba on Friday, said Tuas, and confirms that IMDA has not reached a conclusion on whether action should be taken against the telco.

    Simba has been requested to respond to the following allegations:

    • That it deployed dynamic spectrum sharing to about 133 sites for intermittent periods between 2022 and 2023 to allow 2,100 megahertz (MHz) band spectrum for 4G and 5G coverage, when the telco was only authorised to use the spectrum band for 5G.
    • That the 2,340 to 2,360 MHz band was used at about 92 sites for intermittent periods between 2023 and 2026 when Simba did not have authorisation to do so.
    • That the deployment of dynamic spectrum sharing on the 2,100 MHz band and use of 2,340 to 2,360 MHz spectrum was intentional and approved by Simba’s senior management.
    • That the telco deployed 5G indoor radio access network equipment at two locations in 2023 prior to IMDA’s authorisation on Nov 14, 2023.
    • That Simba did not comply fully with IMDA’s production order, and that the failure to produce documents was deliberate and intentional.
    • That IMDA has concerns over deficiencies in Simba’s governance and compliance culture which the regulator considers should require further regulatory obligations in the telco’s licence to address.

    IMDA has identified a number of regulatory actions, and has asked Simba to make representation on why these actions should not be taken.

    These actions include suspending or cancelling the grant of spectrum right, reducing Simba’s licence period, and a fine of either 10 per cent of Simba’s annual turnover or S$1 million, whichever is higher.

    IMDA is also considering intervening and imposing regulatory obligations to address governance deficiencies and prevent similar conduct from recurring.
