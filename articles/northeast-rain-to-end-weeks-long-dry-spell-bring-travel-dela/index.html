Northeast rain to end weeks-long dry spell, bring travel delays and localized flooding

Rain is forecast to end a weeks-long dry stretch across much of the Northeast Wednesday and Thursday as a storm draws on moisture that contributed to recent flash flooding in the Southwest.

A slow-moving weather pattern has kept high pressure over the Northeast for much of the past couple of weeks, bringing an extended stretch of dry, sunny and generally calm winds.

Sunny days and cool nights have supported the development of fall colors in some areas, while the generally dry, calm weather has provided favorable conditions for preserving and viewing the foliage.

The clock is ticking on the dry weather as a storm is forecast to spread showers and thunderstorms eastward from the Plains to the Mississippi Valley into Election Day evening and will pivot into the East, spanning Wednesday and Thursday.

Dry weather is forecast to last through much of Wednesday along the mid-Atlantic coast and across New England. Temperatures across much of the eastern United States will be several degrees above the historical average.

Anyone planning to store patio umbrellas, cushions or other seasonal outdoor items may want to do so before the rain arrives, while the items are still dry.

Roads may become especially slippery when rain first mixes with oil and other residue that accumulated during the dry spell. Wet leaves can also make roads and sidewalks slick.

As the storm and its trailing cold front move through the region, moisture from the Atlantic could help enhance downpours. Localized street flooding is possible, especially where fallen leaves clog storm drains.

Even where flooding does not occur, rain could slow travel during the Thursday commutes.

Gusty winds will accompany the storm, followed by lower temperatures behind the cold front. Temperatures will be more typical of early November, with a smaller drop along the Atlantic coast than in the Appalachians and Upper Midwest.

In New York City, sunshine will return Friday, with highs in the low 60s, down about 10 degrees from the low 70s at midweek. Detroit will have a larger temperature drop, from the mid-70s Tuesday to the low 50s Friday and Saturday, when clouds will dominate.

Gusty winds will bring down more leaves and scatter raked piles, adding to yard cleanup.

More persistent and seasonably familiar cloud cover is forecast from the Midwest to the Appalachians this weekend into next week, marking a change from the recent stretch of sunny weather.
