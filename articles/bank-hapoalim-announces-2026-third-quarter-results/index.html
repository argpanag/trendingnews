Bank Hapoalim Announces 2026 Third Quarter Results

TheTools · 16.11.2026 09:13 · source · permalink

News provided by

16 Nov, 2026, 10:25 IST

  • Net profit in 3Q26 totaled NIS 2,364 million; return on equity 14.0%. 
  • Net profit in the first nine months of 2026 totaled NIS 6,976 million; return on equity 14.0%.
    • The annual impact of the special tax on banks is 1.3%–1.4%. Excluding the tax impact, ROE for both 3Q26 and 9M26 was approximately 15.4%.
  • Total income decreased 6.7% vs. the previous quarter, mainly due to less favorable macroeconomic factors and softer capital-market activity. However, income from regular financing activity excluding the CPI impact increased by 1.7% in the nine-month period.
  • Total expenses were down 1.8% QoQ and 3.4% YoY; the cost-income ratio was 32.2% in the quarter and 32.9% in the nine-month period.
  • Credit growth was strong and diversified across all segments of operation, at 3.2% QoQ and 15.4% YoY.
  • Credit quality metrics remained strong: the NPL ratio was 0.49% and the NPL coverage ratio was 292%. The credit-loss ratio decreased to 0.12% in the quarter, reflecting a lower collective provision driven by improved risk parameters and more favorable macroeconomic forecasts.
  • Shareholders' equity continued to grow organically, up 5.9% over the last twelve months, despite the impact of the increase in U.S. and Israel government yield curves. The CET-1 capital ratio was 11.62%, vs. a minimum internal target of 11.0%.
  • The board of directors declared a total distribution of 50% of net profit, of which a cash dividend of NIS 946 million, with the remainder through the execution of the first tranche of a newly launched share buyback plan (NIS 236 million).
  • The annual impact of the special tax on banks is 1.3%–1.4%. Excluding the tax impact, ROE for both 3Q26 and 9M26 was approximately 15.4%.

TEL AVIV, Israel, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Bank Hapoalim (TASE: POLI) today announced its financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

Income statement 

  • Net profit in the third quarter of 2026 totaled NIS 2,364 million, compared with NIS 2,488 million in the preceding quarter and NIS 2,758 million in the same quarter last year. Net profit in the corresponding quarter included one-time income of NIS 380 million, net, from an insurance reimbursement. Excluding this income, 2025 third-quarter net profit amounted to NIS 2,378 million. 
    The decrease in 3Q26 net profit vs. the comparable quarters was mainly driven by a less favorable interest-rate and inflation environment and a decrease in non-regular financing activity, partially offset by continued growth in business activity, higher fees, lower operating expenses, and lower credit-loss provisions.
  • Income from regular financing activity in 3Q26 (NIS 4,819 million vs. NIS 5,109 million in 2Q26) was negatively impacted by lower interest rates (3.45% vs. 3.91%, on average) and CPI (1.0% vs. 1.3% known CPI), and lower income from regular non-interest financing activity, due to lower volatility and trading volumes in capital markets.
  • The financial margin from regular activity was 2.55% in the third quarter, vs. 2.70% in the preceding quarter, reflecting lower interest rates and CPI, as well as lower regular capital-market activity. 
  • Income from non-regular financing activity totaled NIS 28 million in the third quarter, vs. NIS 325 million in the preceding quarter. The decrease was driven by substantially lower income from equity investments, amid softer equity markets, as well as realized losses on AFS bonds.
  • Fee income totaled NIS 1,277 million in the third quarter, compared with NIS 1,145 million in the previous quarter and NIS 1,117 million in the same quarter last year. Third-quarter fees benefited from financing-related fee income recognized following a reimbursement from insurers in respect of Sale Law guarantee insurance. Credit-handling fees also grew, reflecting the recognition of syndication fees from infrastructure transactions, as the bank led the financing of one of Israel's largest energy infrastructure projects in recent years during the quarter.
    Fees for the nine-month period grew 6.5% to NIS 3.5 billion. Excluding one-time income recorded in both periods, growth was broadly similar and distributed across all fee types.
  • Operating and other expenses totaled NIS 1,982 million in the third quarter, compared with NIS 2,019 million in the previous quarter and NIS 2,052 million in the same quarter last year, a decrease of 1.8% QoQ and 3.4% YoY. On the longer view of the nine-month period, operating and other expenses declined by 2.2%, due to a decline in both other expenses and salary expenses. Salary expenses declined by 3.1% due to lower bonus provisions, while base salary expenses remained stable, supported by efficiency measures and employee retirements.
  • The cost-income ratio remained low at 32.2% in the third quarter of 2026 and was 32.9% for the first nine months of 2026.
  • The provision for credit losses in the third quarter of 2026 totaled NIS 167 million (a credit-loss ratio of 0.12%). The low provision for credit losses is due to a decrease in the collective provision, which reflects improved risk parameters and more favorable macroeconomic forecasts. The individual provision remained low at NIS 31 million, with no exceptional recoveries during the quarter.
  • Profit before tax for the nine-month period remained stable at NIS 11.8 billion, and even grew by 3.7% excluding the aforementioned one-time income in the corresponding period, despite a decline of approximately 70 basis points in interest rates and lower CPI (2.1% in the first nine months of 2026, vs. 3.0% in the corresponding period last year). This reflects continued growth in the bank's business activity, lower credit loss provisions, and a 2.2% reduction in operating expenses. 
  • Profit after tax for the nine-month period was negatively impacted by the special tax imposed on Israeli banks. The special tax on the banking industry increased from NIS 1.3 billion in 2025 to approximately NIS 3.0 billion in 2026. For Bank Hapoalim, the special tax is approximately NIS 950 million in 2026 and approximately NIS 40 million in 2027.

Balance sheet

  • Net credit to the public in the third quarter of 2026 totaled NIS 553.2 billion, compared with NIS 536.2 billion in the preceding quarter, an increase of 3.2%. Compared with the corresponding quarter, net credit to the public increased by 15.4%. The growth in the credit portfolio was broad-based across all segments of operation and diversified across several economic sectors. Corporate credit increased by 3.4% in comparison to the preceding quarter, and by 27.5% in comparison to the corresponding quarter; commercial credit increased by 5.3% QoQ and 16.7% YoY. In the retail banking division, the housing loan portfolio grew by 1.8% QoQ and 6.3% YoY, small business credit increased by 2.0% and 8.9%, and consumer credit grew by 1.8% and 7.0%, respectively.
  • Total deposits reached NIS 637.2 billion, an increase of 3.1% vs. the last quarter and 7.9% vs. the same quarter last year. Retail deposits stayed fairly flat vs. comparable periods, at NIS 318.1 billion, constituting 50% of total deposits.
  • LCR and NSFR are at 126% and 115% respectively, vs. the minimum regulatory requirement of 100%. 
  • The allowance for credit losses stood at NIS 9.1 billion as at September 30, 2026, of which NIS 8.7 billion attributed to the collective allowance. The total allowance constitutes 1.61% of total credit.
  • Credit quality indicators remained strong; the NPL ratio stood at 0.49% and the NPL coverage ratio (balance sheet allowance for credit losses/NPL) was 292%.
  • Shareholders' equity grew by 5.9% in the last year, to NIS 67.5 billion, supported by our strong organic capital generation, partially offset by a decrease in the balance of the capital reserve in respect of the AFS bond portfolio. The CET-1 capital ratio stood at 11.62%, vs. the 10.24% minimum regulatory requirement and 11.0% minimum internal target, as at September 30, 2026. The total capital ratio as at September 30, 2026, stood at 14.09%, vs. a 13.5% minimum regulatory requirement.
  • Dividend distribution and share buyback: The board of directors declared a distribution at an overall rate of 50% of net profit, of which a cash dividend of NIS 946 million and the rest through the exercise of the first tranche of a new share buyback plan (NIS 236 million). The dividend will be paid on December 1, 2026.

Financial targets

Financial targets for 2026–2027, based on baseline assumptions as of March 5, 2026, and following the completion on March 30, 2026, of the legislation regarding a special bank tax in 2026 and 2027:

  • Net profit in the range of NIS 8.5–9.5 billion for 2026 and NIS 9.5–10.5 billion for 2027;
  • Return on equity in the range of 13%–14% for 2026 and 14%–15% for 2027;
  • Growth of the credit portfolio at an average annual rate of 8%–9%;
  • Profit distribution at a rate of 50%–60% of net profit, through cash dividends or buybacks, subject to the guidelines of the Bank of Israel.

Recent developments

  • Annual general meeting of shareholders
    On October 29, 2026, the bank held its annual general meeting of shareholders for 2026, at which the following resolutions were passed: (1) the Financial Statements and the Report of the Board of Directors and Board of Management of the Bank for 2025 were discussed; (2) the reappointment of Somekh Chaikin (KPMG), CPA, and Ziv Haft (BDO), CPA, as joint auditors of the bank until the end of the next annual general meeting was approved; (3) the appointment of Michal Halperin and Tamar Schechter as external directors pursuant to the Companies Law (for a second and first term of office, respectively) was approved; (4) the appointment of Israel Trau as an other (non-external) director (for a third term of office) was approved; (5) the remuneration policy for officers of the bank was approved; (6) updated terms of service for the bank's directors, in connection with the grant of fixed equity compensation, were approved; and (7) updated terms of service and employment for the Bank's CEO, Yadin Antebi, were approved.
    As part of the approved remuneration resolutions (items 5 and 7), and as part of a bank-wide initiative to discontinue early retirement arrangements, the option for senior executives to convert their accrued retirement rights into bank shares was also approved.
    The bank estimates that the conversion of the accrued retirement rights will result in a one-time expense of approximately NIS 8 million. At the same time, discontinuing the accrual of early retirement and enhanced severance rights and converting them into the bank's shares is expected to reduce future expenses (service cost and interest) by approximately NIS 10 million per year.

  • Bank shares buyback plan
    On November 19, 2025, the board of directors of the bank (after receiving the approval of the Banking Supervision Department) approved a plan for a buyback of shares of the bank, at a volume not to exceed NIS 1 billion, until November 5, 2026, or until the full amount of the plan was acquired, whichever was earlier. Within this plan, which was completed on September 17, 2026, the bank purchased a cumulative 13,128,375 ordinary shares of the bank, constituting approximately 0.98% of the issued and paid-up capital of the bank, at a total cost of approximately NIS 985 million.
    On November 15, 2026, the board of directors of the bank approved a new plan for a buyback of shares of the bank at a volume not to exceed NIS 1 billion, from November 17, 2026, until November 1, 2027, or until the full amount of the plan is acquired, whichever is earlier.
