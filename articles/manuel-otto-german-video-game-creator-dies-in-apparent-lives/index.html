Manuel Otto, German video game creator, dies in apparent livestreamed suicide

TheTools · 23.10.2026 15:01 · source · permalink

German video game designer and influencer Manuel Otto apparently died by suicide while he was livestreaming on Twitch, according to the streaming platform and his colleagues. He was 30.

Otto was a 14-year-old high school student when he created his most famous game, “Stick Run.” The free Facebook game, later an app for Android and Apple, was played by tens of millions of people.

His body was found Tuesday after authorities responded to reports of a blaze in an attic room on a property in the Bavarian village of Breitbrunn am Chiemsee, Bavarian police said in a statement to NBC News.

“The fire was quickly extinguished thanks to the courageous intervention of a resident,” police said. “Despite the resident’s courageous intervention, it was too late to save one person still inside the apartment.”

Colleagues confirmed his death on social media.

“It is with a heavy heart that we confirm and announce the passing of our creator, developer, and fellow Stick Runner, Marc-Manuel Alexander Otto,” the Stick Run Team posted Wednesday on Facebook. An earlier post from the Stick Run Team said Otto “appeared to take his own life” in a livestream.

Videos of the livestream later posted online appear to show Otto setting himself on fire.

Meanwhile, a Twitch spokesperson said in an emailed statement that Twitch “moved quickly to remove the channel and Video on Demand (VOD) to prevent it from being viewed by others.”

“What took place is horrible, and the mental health and wellbeing of our community matters deeply to us,” the spokesperson said. “We encourage anyone who may be struggling to seek support.”

Otto launched his signature game, in which players control a stick figure running through obstacle courses, on Facebook in 2010.

Since then, “Stick Run” has been played by more than 43 million people worldwide, according to Dexerto, a digital media company that covers video games.

“Thank you, Manuel, for being a part of our childhood,” the Stick Run Team said in its post. “You will never be forgotten.”
