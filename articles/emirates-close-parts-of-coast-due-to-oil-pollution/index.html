Environmental authorities in the United Arab Emirates have closed all beaches and coastal access points in the emirate of Umm al-Quwain due to oil pollution.

The oil reportedly leaked from a tanker. Clean-up operations were under way, the authorities said. The statement did not specify which vessel was involved. Fishermen were also urged not to fish in the affected area and to comply with instructions issued by the authorities.

During the course of the Iran war and the mutual blockade imposed by the United States and Iran in the Strait of Hormuz, tankers have repeatedly come under attack in recent months.

Several oil slicks have spread off the coasts of Oman and Iran, some threatening protected natural areas, including Oman's Musandam National Park. The rugged coastline is considered an important breeding ground for birds. Environmental organizations have expressed concern.

However, a tanker believed to be part of Russia's so-called shadow fleet also recently caused an oil slick in the region. The Caroline Bezengi ran aground off Oman's southern coast.

Vessels in the Russian shadow fleet are often considered to be outdated and a risk to shipping.
