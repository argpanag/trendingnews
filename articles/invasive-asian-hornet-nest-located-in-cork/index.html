Invasive Asian hornet nest located in Cork

TheTools · 02.10.2026 15:39 · source · permalink

An Asian hornet nest has been located near Ringaskiddy in Co Cork following an intensive search operation involving the use of miniature radio transmitters attached to live hornets.

The Minister of State for Nature, Heritage and Biodiversity Christopher O'Sullivan confirmed that conservation rangers from the National Parks and Wildlife Service (NPWS) successfully tracked the invasive insects back to their nest.

The breakthrough came just hours after the search team managed to attach a tiny transmitter to the back of a live Asian hornet yesterday.

The insect was then released and its movements tracked by radio signal in the hope that it would lead the rangers directly back to the nest.

The operation followed several sightings of Asian hornets foraging among ripened ivy around a disused building in the Ringaskiddy area over recent days.

The rangers had initially used traps to confirm the presence of Asian hornets before deploying bait stations containing sweet syrup and meat to attract live insects.

At least one hornet was marked with a coloured tracking spot and observed flying away before the team succeeded in attaching the miniature radio transmitter to another.

The Minister said speed was critical because of the threat Asian hornets pose to honeybees and other native pollinating insects.

"In an operation like this, speed is critical. Once reports of Asian hornets were received, it was essential that every effort was made to locate the nest as quickly as possible," he said.

"The use of this new technology has significantly strengthened our rapid response capability, allowing teams on the ground to track hornets back to their nest with remarkable efficiency."

The immediate priority is now to safely destroy and remove the nest.

Specialist teams are expected to treat the nest with a pesticide to kill the colony before it is removed for examination and DNA analysis.

This could help establish where the hornets came from and whether they have any connection with the Asian hornets found in Cork last year.

The NPWS will also continue monitoring the surrounding area to establish whether this is the only nest in the vicinity.

That will be particularly important because young Asian hornet queens leave their nests at this time of year to overwinter before potentially establishing new colonies of their own in the spring.

Asian hornets are an invasive predator which feeds on honeybees and other pollinating insects, including bumblebees, solitary bees and hoverflies.

It is estimated that a single Asian hornet nest can consume around 11 kilograms of pollinating insects during a season.

Niall O’Donnchú, Director General of the NPWS, said the speed with which the nest had been located demonstrated the effectiveness of the response procedures developed following last year's Asian hornet incursion.

"I want to commend the entire team, led by Regional Manager Daniel O’Keefe of the NPWS, for the alacrity with which they were able to find this nest. And all within a matter of hours of tagging a subject Asian hornet," he said.

"This is testament to the skill and tenacity of the team involved, including colleagues from the National Biodiversity Data Centre, led by John Kelly, and is proof again that the time invested in developing the protocol last year was time well spent."

He described the Asian hornet as a "very aggressive invasive alien species" and said the importance of locating the nest quickly could not be overstated.

"Our teams will continue to work to establish that this is the sole nest in the vicinity," he said.

Minister O’Sullivan also thanked Cork County Council, the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine, the National Biodiversity Data Centre and the local community in Ringaskiddy for their assistance during the operation.
