Utah State at Texas State: How to watch, listen to or stream the game

TheTools · 23.10.2026 16:00 · source · permalink

Utah State (2-4, 1-1 Pac-12) at Texas State (3-3, 1-1 Pac-12)

  • Kickoff: Saturday, 5:30 p.m. MDT
  • Venue: UFCU Stadium
  • TV: CBS Sports Network
  • Radio: Aggie Sports Network (KZNS 1280 The Zone)
  • Series: Utah State has only played Texas State once, back when the Aggies and Bobcats were both members of the Western Athletic Conference in 2012. Utah State dominated that game 38-7, and went on to win the WAC.

The trends

For Utah State: After Utah State won its last game against Washington State, it needed its bye week in the worst way. As usual, the offense was sloppy and disorganized.

The defense and Asher Cunningham came to the rescue. Cunningham forced two fumbles and recovered one.

Aggies coach Bronco Mendenhall has now fully embraced Grady Brosterhous as the starting quarterback. Through the first six weeks, the quarterback position was up in the air between Brosterhous and McCae Hillstead.

“Grady’s our starting quarterback as we speak today,” Mendenhall said in Monday’s press conference. “That does not mean McCae won’t play or is not available, nor have we ruled him out. But Grady, to this point, I think has earned the job.

“I’ve been impressed with Grady, the grit, the determination, the resolve, how he plays the game. He’ll be our starter for this week.”

For Texas State: Texas State had a strange start to its Pac-12 play. The Bobcats opened conference play on the road against San Diego State. They played well and had a 17-3 lead at halftime.

However, the Aztecs outscored Texas State 28-12 in the second half.

Similar to Utah State, it then got a bounce-back win. But unlike the Aggies, the Bobcats completely ran through Colorado State.

Texas State has had one of the most lethal offenses in the country, especially on the ground. The Bobcats ran for 503 yards en route to a dominant 66-27 victory over the Rams.

The offense averages 562.2 yards per game, which leads the entire FBS. It also averages 303.5 rushing yards per game, which is third in the FBS. Finally, Texas State averages 40 points per game, which is ninth in the country.

Inversely from Utah State, the Bobcats’ defense hasn’t been great. Outside of its dominant win over FCS Incarnate Word, Colorado State’s 27 points was a season low for the Texas State defense.

What to watch for

Stop the fiery offense: As mentioned, Texas State is among the hottest offenses in the country. Its offensive line is big, can create wide lanes, and has the rushing ability to take advantage.

The biggest caveat to all of Texas State’s offensive success is that, outside of Texas, it hasn’t played great defenses. Excluding Texas, the best defense Texas State has faced this year is North Texas.

Utah State’s defense is already riding a high after its clutch win over Washington State, and the Aggies need to continue the momentum to cause the Bobcats some problems.

An opportunity for Eli Wood? The defense has an opportunity to give Texas State some fits and one of its biggest challenges of the year, but that only matters if the offense can get going.

Yes, Texas State has generally played poor defenses. However, it still has a large offensive line and deep offensive capabilities. Even if the defense plays spectacularly, Texas State may still put up a decent amount of points.

Through six games, Utah State’s season high has been its 21 points scored against Troy. But has the offense adapted enough with a bye week to work on things and focus on Brosterhous as the starter?

Texas State has been very poor with its pass defense. It gives up an average of 297.3 yards per game, which is 137th in FBS.

This could be a great opportunity for Brosterhous to gain more confidence in his passing and for his favorite target, Eli Wood.

Wood’s been by far the brightest spot for the Utah State offense. He leads the team with five touchdowns and could cause some serious issues for the Bobcats’ secondary.

Another opportunity for turnover margin: It’s no secret that Utah State has had one of the worst turnover margins in the country this season. The Aggies especially had an interception problem and currently sit with a -6 turnover margin.

That was a problem that plagued the offense early on, and while it’s certainly not fixed, the Aggies have slowly improved. Brosterhous has now gone two consecutive games without an interception, and the defense has started to force more turnovers.

Meanwhile, Texas State has nearly an identically poor turnover margin at -5. Even in its rout of Colorado State, it did so without forcing any turnovers and giving up a fumble.

For an offense as hot as Texas State, the Aggies’ defense has a real opportunity to cause some havoc by forcing turnovers and ending drives early.

Quotable

“It remains to be seen what you think you addressed in the bye week and then what’s actually addressed. Through execution, time tells with that. Love the chance to self-scout. Love the chance to identify areas of strength, areas of weakness. Love the chance to just confirm statistically and through objective measures where we are. Again, that’s all happening every week. When you have a bye week, it gives you more time. Really love the chance for our team to have had some time away from us and us away from them and even the staff away from each other, as many of our staff members are recruiting. It’s just healthy. I think I’ll have a motivated, excited, and eager team for the stretch run with many of their goals still available. I think statistically, analytically, and emotionally, all those things were addressed.” — Utah State coach Bronco Mendenhall

They want to bloody your nose, and we better match the physicality.” — Texas State coach GJ Kinne
