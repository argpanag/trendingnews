    19:49 GMT 8 November

    FT: Man Utd 4-4 Aston Villa

    • Manchester United will end tonight 18th in the Premier League, their lowest position 10 games into a top-flight season since 1986-87 when they were 20th. They've gone eight Premier League games without a win for just the second time, also doing so in April-May 2025.
    • They have failed to win in their last eight games in the Premier League, their last joint-longest winless streak was from 1 April 2025 to 16 May 2025.

  8. 'Man Utd giving themselves a mountain to climb in every game'published at 19:46 GMT 8 November
    19:46 GMT 8 November

    FT: Man Utd 4-4 Aston Villa

    Dion Dublin
    Former Coventry and Aston Villa striker on BBC Sport

    Manchester United clearly still have issues that need sorting. Over the last two games they have overturned two-goal deficits on three occasions to get something out of matches against Roma and Aston Villa.

    But there's only so many times you can do that because it's quite tiring, physically and mentally. The players today were walking around after the game like they had lost, even though they got a point.

    By starting so slowly they are giving themselves a mountain to climb in every game. The emotions we saw at full-time were relief. I don’t think they are going into the game thinking about Michael Carrick and his job, they are just trying to get three points. But they just don’t seem to be gelling at the moment. There’s not enough energy.

    Right at the end, we saw the United their fans want to see. And when they came back from 2-0 down, they also had some momentum. But it didn’t last and they quickly reverted to their default way of playing, which is slow and negative – and before long they were 4-2 down.

  11. 'It's a learning experience today'published at 19:37 GMT 8 November
    19:37 GMT 8 November

    Aston Villa

    Aston Villa captain John McGinn, speaking to Sky Sports: "Obviously, in a very strong position to win the game. We've experienced it enough here, you need to defend until the 95th minute and beyond. We didn't manage the first half enough. We have a very young team and it's a learning experience today.

  15. 'I don't like situation but we can do something about it'published at 19:24 GMT 8 November
    19:24 GMT 8 November

    FT: Man Utd 4-4 Aston Villa

    Manchester United

    Manchester United boss Michael Carrick, speaking to Sky Sports:

    "Disappointed not to win the game. We were the better team for large parts. Made it difficult for ourselves with the goals we're giving away. Not just one but two in succession to put us in a difficult position. We did it the other night, did it again today, come back into the game and then could've won it at the end.

    "It's just moments we need to tighten up on, different moments in the game and we're giving teams goals that they don't normally get in the game. We tighten up on that then there's a lot of good in that performance. I thought we were the better team but conceding the goals that we are is not helping us at all.

    "No goal is a good goal to concede but there are ones you can do a bit more about and the ones we gave away today we have to tighten it up. It's making it difficult for ourselves and giving ourselves too much to do in games but we did a lot and still feel we should have won the game."

    On conceding so many goals: "There's been too many - that is clear and we know that. Something we have to put right.

    "I liked the character, the quality of football. We had most of the game, attacked the box we had shots and created chances. Coming from a place where you're 2-0 down and haven't had a good run of results the boys played good football - pressed well. A lot of the game was where we want it but if you look at it overall there's a lot [to like] and to finish the game and never give up it shows the character in the boys."

    On fans: "They are right behind the players, they are desperate to get behind us and for us to give something. There's frustration and disappointment at times and we all feel that but they're right behind the boys, they were until the end today and we all feel that."

    On potential for relegation fight: "We're in November so I don't like the situation and the results we've had but we can definitely do something about it."

Man Utd score twice in stoppage-time to share points in eight-goal thriller

Manchester United twice came back from two goals down to earn a point against Aston Villa in an eight-goal thriller at Old Trafford, although they will go into the international break in the Premier League relegation zone.

The Red Devils had dropped into the bottom three following Nottingham Forest's win against Manchester City on Saturday, and the weekend continued to get worse for Michael Carrick's side as they found themselves 2-0 down against Villa after just four minutes.

United fought back to be level at the break, but then conceded two goals in eight minutes at the start of the second half.

That looked to have sucked the fight out of the home side but remarkably - and for the third time in a week after recovering to beat Roma on Tuesday - they came back from two goals behind again.

Joshua Zirkzee sparked it when he struck two minutes into stoppage time to make it 4-3, before Patrick Dorgu dramatically levelled in the sixth minute added on when he headed in from a corner.

They almost won it, too, but ultimately had to settle for snatching a draw from the jaws of defeat - leaving them 18th and one point from safety going into a two-week break.

It was a result that will not quite ease the pressure on Carrick, given their at times chaotic defending, but he will be the happier of the two managers.

Unai Emery's side had made a dream start when Alysson opened the scoring inside the opening minute as his shot squeezed under Senne Lammens, who moments later parried a second Alysson shot - with Nicolas Jackson there to fire in the loose ball.

But Manchester United were awarded a penalty in the 14th minute when Boubacar Kamara took down Matheus Cunha inside the box, and Bruno Fernandes made no mistake from the spot.

That seemed to galvanise the hosts and they were the better side for the rest of the half, deservedly drawing level just before the break when Youri Tielemans - who joined Manchester United from Villa in the summer - drove in from Noussair Mazraoui's cutback.

However, all that work was undone as Manchester United conceded two further goals early in the second half.

First, Aston Villa were given a spot-kick a minute after the break when Diogo Dalot took down Alysson and John McGinn stepped up to confidently convert.

Leon Goretzka then restored Villa's two-goal advantage in the 53rd minute following some haphazard defending following a free-kick. Leny Yoro headed the ball back towards Lammens, who punched it on to his own crossbar and Goretzka was there to prod in at the back post.

From then on it seemed like Villa would take a win that would lift them up to eighth, but then came the stoppage-time drama that will have provided some sense of relief for Carrick.

Man Utd analysis: Defensive frailties remain a concern

Fans will have certainly left Old Trafford entertained and, while this was a memorable game, Carrick will know Manchester United cannot keep putting themselves into positions of needing to stage fightbacks.

Their defensive frailties have repeatedly left them in difficult situations, and Lammens should have done better for the first two goals against Villa, while defenders Dalot and Yoro were at fault for the third and fourth goals.

United have to be commended for not giving up, both in this game and against Roma in the Champions League, but their defending has to improve if they are to end their search for a first Premier League win since August anytime soon.

They have shipped 30 goals in all competitions this season - no side in Europe's top five leagues have conceded more.

Aston Villa analysis: Game management a concern

'I'm absolutely speechless, that feels like a loss!'

Emery was clearly exasperated at how his side twice gave up strong positions at Old Trafford, and he headed towards the tunnel the moment Manchester United equalised for the second time.

The performance leaves big question marks over Aston Villa's game management, and it will have been concerning for Emery just how swiftly his players wilted after conceding the first and third goals.

Villa should really have been celebrating a first win in four games but instead this extends their recent stumble, following an unbeaten seven-match run through September and October.
