Businesses supporting Ukraine told to 'act now' to protect against Russian sabotage

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:46 · source · permalink

The UK government has issued a warning and advice for any companies that support Ukraine's armed forces

  • Published
    24 November 2026

The UK government has warned that any businesses providing support for Ukraine's armed forces could become a target for Russia, and should "act now" to protect themselves.

An alert issued on Tuesday warned of an increased risk of Russian sabotage attacks such as arson and threats to staff, premises and operations.

Any businesses that are providing support to Ukraine's armed forces, including organisations in the supply chain, have been urged to take extra precautions, both physically and online.

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and there has been a recent increase in Russian sabotage attempts against defence related facilities in Europe.

In September, a man appeared in court accused of planning to "torch" UK drone factories on behalf of Russia's intelligence services in an alleged sabotage plot.

Estonia has also accused Moscow of being behind an attempted arson attack at a drone factory last week, and Germany blamed it for a drone attack at Leipzig's airport in August.

The joint alert, external between MI5's National Protective Security Agency (NPSA) and the Ministry of Defence, has advised businesses to remove unnecessary detail that can be found online, emphasising that hostile reconnaissance usually begins with internet research.

It also suggested that companies install visible protective security measures "such as CCTV, secure entrances and robust access controls" to "deter hostile activity". These security measures should be "actively promoted... without giving away information that could be used to plan an attack".

When it comes to personal security, any staff members - not just those at the top - should "remain alert to their surroundings, avoid predictable routines where practicable, and consider how personal information, movements and behaviours could be used to build a picture of them, their role and their organisation".

Leaders should ensure their staff know how to manage their personal security, and know what to do if they notice anything suspicious or concerning.

The government said it will give closed-door threat briefings to critical national infrastructure and defence sector CEOs in Whitehall on Tuesday and Wednesday.

Gen Sir Richard Barrons, who co-authored last year's Strategic Defence Review, said the latest warning was "significant but not new" and the relevant companies will be "quite savvy" about the risks of cyber attacks.

He told BBC Radio 4's World at One that while there had been a recent "uptick" of these kinds of attacks across Europe, "we are not expecting an epidemic".

"These actions so far are significant but not strategic. They cause disruption, dismay and some damage but they have not reached the level of them being a strategic threat to the security of Europe," Gen Barrons added.

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband has warned that threats from Russia are aimed at deterring the UK's support for Ukraine, which "must not" happen.

Speaking to the Commons Foreign Affairs Committee earlier on Tuesday, Miliband said the threat from Moscow will endure beyond the Ukraine war and reiterated that any peace settlement must not reward Russia.

Security Minister Dan Jarvis said recent sabotage attempts across Europe "demonstrate the seriousness of the threats we face".

Minister for the Armed Forces, Louise Sandher-Jones, added that Russia and other hostile actors "pose a growing threat", while companies have a responsibility to strengthen their security "and help protect Britain".
