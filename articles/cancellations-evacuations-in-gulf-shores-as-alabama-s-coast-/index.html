GULF SHORES, Ala. (WKRG) — The Gulf waters in Gulf Shores are already showing signs that something tropical is headed this way.

 Gulf Coast school closures: List of schedule changes due to tropical weather 

For the annual National Shrimp Festival, the timing couldn't have been worse. The four-day festival, which brings 250,000 people to Alabama's beaches, was canceled Wednesday morning.

"One of the hardest decisions we have ever had to make," said Steve Jones.

Now, instead of setting up, "You put a lot of money and effort into it, to not, you know, even be given a chance," said first-time participant Brittany Bullion.

Vendors are scrambling to break down and get out of town.

"It's just not safe, and everybody's got to do the prep to get out of here. So it makes good sense," said local vendor Terry Rinehardt.

Two blocks from the beachfront, an evacuation is already underway.

"We are asking everyone to vacate this park by noon tomorrow," said Brad Newton with Luxury RV Resort.

The park is surrounded by water, and if they don't get out now, they may not.

"Everybody has been so understanding, and they know we have their safety as the main concern," added Newton.

Wednesday afternoon, a line of Gulf Shores residents trying to get hurricane re-entry decals before warnings go up along the coastline stretched outside city hall.
