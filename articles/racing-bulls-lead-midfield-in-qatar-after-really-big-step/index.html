Racing Bulls lead midfield in Qatar after 'really big step'

TheTools · 28.11.2026 01:21 · source · permalink

Major changes between the two sessions on Friday proved crucial for Racing Bulls to find performance in Qatar.

Racing Bulls led the midfield pack after Friday practice ahead of the Qatar Grand Prix, as Liam Lawson and Arvid Lindblad finished Free Practice 2 in P9 and P10.

Both drivers hadn’t been entirely happy in FP1, but major changes – particular adding front wing to counter understeer – brought a significant improvement for the second session.

Despite the encouraging start to the weekend Lawson believes that there’s more to come ahead of Qualifying on Saturday at the Lusail International Circuit.

“We definitely didn't maximise," he said. "There's some stuff we can improve to put our lap together, just on some performance stuff. But overall, we have a strong car.

“Straight line speed would do it. So yeah, let's obviously see tomorrow. We were really lacking it in FP1, and we made a good step for FP2. It's all stuff we can see, and should be able to fix.

"We made a really big step with the car between the sessions, and it's not often you make big changes on a race weekend, but we did, and the car was a lot better.

“So we really had quite a good car in FP1, and now we have an even faster car. We'll try and fine tune it for tomorrow. So far it's all worked."

Racing Bulls Team Principal Alan Permane was encouraged by the clear improvement between sessions.

“Yes, it's been a good day. I have to say, it didn't start off the best. Liam had a little power unit issue, which they cleared up for FP2, and our balance wasn't quite perfect in the first session.

“But again, we made some changes, some reasonable changes between the sessions, and both cars are much happier.

“We've just added a bit of rotation to the car. We had a bit too much understeer. I think if you'd have listened to almost any of the drivers – certainly we were listening to some – they were all complaining of the same thing.

“So understeer in that first session, and I suspect most people did something similar, to put more front end into the car for the second session.”
