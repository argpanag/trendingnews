Audiences are spoiled for choice this week, with several new movies debuting theatrically and others premiering on digital platforms. The holdover hit The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is dominating the box office and is expected to retain the number-one spot over the lucrative Thanksgiving period. It does, however, have fresh competition from the animated film Hexed, the comedy sequel Focker-in-Law, and the biopic Hershey. Meanwhile, a trio of recent theatrical releases has become available to rent and purchase at home. Among them is a sleeper-hit romantic fantasy headlined by an Oscar winner.

Starring Olivia Colman and Alexander Skarsgård, the film puts a unique spin on the Frankenstein archetype. It debuted on the PVOD market alongside another box-office hit, the A24 documentary You Can See Everything, which has generated nearly $100 million in its theatrical run so far. The two hit films were joined on digital platforms this week by the underperformer The Social Reckoning, writer-director Aaron Sorkin's follow-up to the Oscar-winning hit The Social Network.

The Social Reckoning grossed less than $20 million worldwide against a reported budget of $40 million and is nowsitting at a 53% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes. The film will hope to redeem itself at home, where the older audiences it's targeted at are more likely to discover it. According to FlixPatrol, You Can See Everything has already emerged as a home-video smash, instantly claiming the number-one spot on the domestic iTunes leaderboard. The Social Reckoning came in at number nine.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

  • When Harry Met Sally made the case that the best romance is an argument you enjoy, conducted over a decade, by two people refusing to admit what is happening.
  • You want to be known rather than swept away. Somebody who remembers your worst story is doing better work than anybody arriving with flowers.
  • The film is unusually honest about timing. These two are wrong for each other repeatedly until suddenly they are not, and neither of them engineered it.
  • You would still be talking about everything at eighty. That is the entire promise.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

  • This is the rare romance where both people have a real career and the film takes both of them equally seriously.
  • You would rather be pushed than protected. The right person for you is also a bit of a rival, and that never entirely goes away.
  • It understands the actual problem in ambitious relationships. Not whether you love each other, but whose turn it is this year.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

  • Heated Rivalry runs on the oldest engine in romance: two people on opposite sides, meeting in secret, getting far more attached than either of them planned.
  • You keep your private life genuinely private, and something in you finds hidden things more real than public ones.

Here's How Much the Sleeper Hit Grossed at the Box Office

But we're talking about the romantic fantasy film​​​​​​​ Wicker, which took the 11th spot on the iTunes chart upon its digital debut. The movie holds a "Certified Fresh" 94% critics' score and a "Verified Hot" 92% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. The aggregator website's consensus reads, "Wicker is a charming fable that strikes the right balance between naughty and nice, drawing much of its appeal from deftly deployed salty humor, seamless creature effects, and Olivia Colman's eminently lovable performance."

Also featuring Elizabeth Debicki and Peter Dinklage, Wicker emerged as a sleeper hit, grossing $40 million worldwide against a reported budget of $10 million. Following a solid opening-weekend haul of $5 million from 1,400-odd theaters, Black Bear Pictures expanded the film's theater count to more than 2,300 in its sophomore week. Wicker is now playing in roughly 400 locations nationwide, now that it's available at home as well.
