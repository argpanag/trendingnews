The UK has warned British nationals travelling to Saudi Arabia that their travel insurance could be invalidated if they travel against official advice, as security concerns and the risk of further attacks continue across the Kingdom.

The FCDO warned that further attacks on airports, along with travel disruption and flight cancellations, were very likely. British nationals seeking to leave Saudi Arabia have been advised to consider travelling by land to a neighbouring country and flying from there, while checking entry and exit requirements before setting off.

The FCDO also warned that the possibility of renewed Iranian attacks remained amid continuing regional tensions, despite a memorandum of understanding announced by the US and Iran on June 17.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said Egypt was ready to defend Saudi Arabia "if requested", as fighting flares between the kingdom and Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen.

Speaking at a graduation ceremony at the Egyptian military academy, Sisi said Egypt was "not looking for a role to play" in regional conflicts, but added that it was "inconceivable" for Cairo to stand by while Saudi Arabia was "harmed or its security and stability threatened".

"Egypt, of course, with the directives of His Majesty King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, will carry out any measure to protect you or defend you," he said.

He added that any deployment would require parliamentary approval and said he hoped lawmakers would convene in an emergency session to grant authorisation "if it is requested of us".

"I'm not talking about fighting anyone or attacking anyone," Sisi said. "We are speaking only about defence."

An LNG tanker came under fire from a small boat carrying seven people about 140 nautical miles southeast of Al Mukalla, Yemen, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said on Saturday.

According to UKMTO, the tanker’s master reported that the small boat approached the vessel and fired two shots. The tanker’s armed security team responded by firing two warning shots, forcing the boat to change course.

The incident was reported at about 10.30am UTC on October 17.

UKMTO advised vessels to transit the area with caution and report any suspicious activity as authorities investigate the incident.

IMF member countries have called for greater financial support for nations facing short-term funding pressures from conflicts, high energy prices and economic disruptions, warning that global economic risks remain tilted to the downside, Reuters reported.

In a statement following a meeting in Bangkok, the International Monetary and Financial Committee also warned that rapid technological change, rising bond yields and geopolitical fragmentation could increase financial vulnerabilities. Members pledged support for countries pursuing reforms, particularly low-income nations, vulnerable middle-income economies and conflict-affected states.

The statement referred to the risks and opportunities of “advanced intelligence technologies” but did not explicitly mention artificial intelligence.

Yemen's Houthis announced the release of 159 pro-government fighters under an amnesty announced after they seized swathes of land in a major offensive last month.

The prisoners had "voluntarily surrendered themselves without fighting" as the Iran-backed group captured strategic positions along the Red Sea, Houthi official Abdelqader al-Murtada told a press conference in Sanaa.

The International Committee of the Red Cross confirmed the unilateral release of 159 prisoners and said it had given support by organising interviews and health checks, contacting families and helping arrange transport.

Murtada said 323 prisoners were released under the amnesty last month after surrendering in Hodeidah governorate.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Saturday that Tehran supports a "political process" to resolve the conflict between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis.

The Houthis have repeatedly attacked Saudi targets including energy facilities and Riyadh's international airport, where 13 people were killed in a major attack last week.

"We support an inclusive and comprehensive political process as the only practical and sustainable way to resolve the issue," Araghchi said in a speech at the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation in Baghdad.

Araghchi repeated Iran's offer to mediate between the two sides. Deputy Foreign Minister Saeed Khatibzadeh previously said Riyadh has not responded to Iran's overture.

On Friday, Iran denied having anything to do with the Houthi attacks, after Saudi Arabia accused Tehran of encouraging the group's escalation.

In his speech, Araghchi also said "either we build security together, or we will endure insecurity together," adding that Tehran was willing "to cooperate with all its brothers and neighbours to achieve the first option".

The Houthis and Hezbollah in Lebanon are part of Iran's "Axis of Resistance" against Israel and the United States, which also includes armed groups in Iraq.

“The consequence of ships violating Iranian regulations in the Strait of Hormuz is burning in the fire of the American terrorist army,” the navy said, referring to a supertanker that caught fire after reportedly striking a naval mine in the strait.

Ashley Milner told The Associated Press that the finger on her mother's left hand was cut to the bone and a tendon was visible. She said Levine also has a broken rib and multiple broken vertebrae as well as lacerations across her body.

Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian villages across the occupied West Bank on Friday, setting fire to cars and trees and beating residents as well as Levine in the latest violence to disrupt the territory's annual olive harvest this month.

Levine said the perpetrators - armed with bottles and slingshots - "were kicking me for like 10 minutes and I was trying to hold onto my cameras and my bag." She said her helmet and protective vest, which was marked "PRESS," likely saved her from more serious injuries.

Iranian-backed Houthi rebels say that airstrikes from Saudi Arabia killed three children in Yemen's southwestern province of Dhamar.

The Houthi-run health ministry said Saturday that the strikes a day prior hit houses in al-Ar village in the Dawran Aness district, killing one child aged 8 and two children aged 10 and wounding more than a dozen people.

The Houthis blamed Saudi Arabia for the strike, which comes amid heightened fighting between the group and the kingdom and its Yemeni allies. Saudi Arabia didn't immediately comment on the strike.

The children are among 124 people the Houthis say have been killed in their territories since fighting resumed in September, according to the ministry.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said an oil tanker was on fire in the Strait of Hormuz after hitting a mine in the waterway at the heart of the Middle East war.

Tehran seized control over Hormuz after the start of the war in late February, choking off the strait that normally carries about one-fifth of global oil consumption.

"A supertanker in violation of regulations, carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil and attempting to pass illegally through the Strait of Hormuz, struck a mine, triggering an explosion and a fierce fire," the Revolutionary Guards said.

The statement did not specify the exact location of the incident. Flanked by Iran to the north and Oman to the south, the Hormuz strait is about 30 kilometres (20 miles) wide at its narrowest point.

Since taking control of the strait, Iran has demanded that all ships seek its authorisation before transiting.

"The tanker attempted to cross the strait with US support... by deactivating all its tracking systems," the Guards added, also posting a video of a vessel in flames during the night.

In response to Tehran's blockade on the Strait of Hormuz, Washington has imposed its own naval counter-blockade on Iranian oil exports.

This week, the Guards' naval commander Admiral Ali Ozmai warned that any vessel that deactivated its navigation systems would be considered "hostile".

Saudi Arabia’s Civil Defence briefly issued early warning alerts over a potential threat to Khamis Mushait and Abha in Asir Province, in the country’s southwest near the Yemen border. Authorities later confirmed that the threat had passed.

They can also rebook from alternative GCC airports served by their respective airlines, subject to availability. IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva said the global economic outlook was marked by both “optimism and worry” as the organisation’s Annual Meetings concluded in Bangkok. While AI investment and Asia’s economic momentum offered growth opportunities, inflation, rising debt, high bond yields, wars and climate risks remained major concerns.

Georgieva said the IMF had a responsibility to support low-income countries hit by price shocks linked to the US-Israel war on Iran, particularly oil-importing nations facing higher energy costs. She said debt repayments now account for 14 per cent of government revenue in low-income countries, up from 8 per cent in recent years.

She urged governments to prioritise economic growth and protect vulnerable populations, while calling for debt restructuring, stronger domestic revenue mobilisation and more international financing to support struggling economies.

Georgieva also cautioned against relying too heavily on AI as a driver of global growth, comparing such dependence to “standing on one leg”. She welcomed cooperation among AI companies, governments and other stakeholders on discussions about safety and regulation.

CENTCOM reported financial shortfalls across several sectors of Iran’s economy, including energy, oil, steel, manufacturing, automotive, aviation, construction, security and the military.

It also cited reports that Iran-aligned militia groups had withheld payments to fighters to redirect funds to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), which was reportedly struggling to pay its own personnel.

According to CENTCOM, the financial strain was restricting Iran’s ability to fund allied militia groups, criminal networks and attacks against the US and its allies.

The command also claimed Tehran was facing difficulties replacing destroyed weapons and factories, describing the IRGC as showing signs of increasing desperation.

The commander of US Central Command (CENTCOM) said more than 50,000 US military personnel are currently deployed across the Middle East, adding that 400 commercial vessels had passed through the Strait of Hormuz over the past week.

In a fact sheet, CENTCOM said Iran was facing mounting economic pressure, claiming Tehran could not access $25 billion in oil export revenues and that the US had frozen about $350 million in cryptocurrency. The command also claimed Iran had no imports or exports by sea.
