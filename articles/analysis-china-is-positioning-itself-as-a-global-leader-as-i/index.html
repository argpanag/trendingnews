ANALYSIS | China is positioning itself as a global leader as it hosts Trump, Putin | CBC News

TheTools · 17.11.2026 21:36 · source · permalink

Ukraine, Iran and Nvidia loom over this week's APEC gathering in Shenzhen

As China welcomes world leaders to the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) forum this week in Shenzhen, it presents a very different nation than the one that last hosted the event in 2014.

Amid global conflicts, tariff disputes and economic instability, Beijing is using the summit to cast itself as a reliable global power capable of uniting leaders and brokering international economic co-operation.

Trump, Xi, Putin

Beijing's current standing is perhaps best captured by a proposed tripartite meeting hosted by Chinese President Xi Jinping with U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, the world's three largest nuclear powers.

Choosing Shenzhen places the talks in the heart of China's drive for technological self-reliance. Once a small fishing village, it has transformed into a sprawling metropolis of skyscrapers and factories, and home to companies like electric vehicle maker BYD and telecom giant Huawei.

Some observers compare the gathering to the 1945 Yalta Conference, where the U.S., U.K., and Soviet Union formalized their post-war spheres of influence.

"Who else could broker this three-way right now?" said Greg Chin, associate professor of political economy and director of the Global China Research Lab at York University in Toronto. 

"It's really only China and President Xi."

Trump arrives in a weakened domestic position following a devastating midterm election defeat, with voter anxiety over the cost of living and the U.S.-Israel war with Iran taking centre stage. That conflict is expected to come up, given that both China and Russia maintain strategic partnerships with Iran. 

Another likely agenda point is the war in Ukraine — where Russia has made only slow, incremental gains — notably with neither Ukraine nor other European states seated at the table.

A Russian drone hits a car in the frontline town of Kramatorsk, Ukraine, on July 15. Russia has recently made only incremental progress in its invasion. (Iryna Rybakova/Press Service of the Ukrainian Armed Forces/Reuters)

"Both the U.S. and Russia are at war with someone," said Henry Huiyao Wang, the founder and president of the Center for China and Globalization, a think-tank in Beijing.

"China is the only one without a war." 

Wang says both Washington and Moscow are growing fatigued, leaving an opening for China to step in as a mediator.

As he departed the White House for his Asia trip, Trump even suggested a meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un was possible.

"He may come. I don't know. That's up to President Xi," he said. 

And although China appears to be wary of the discussion, Trump says he is planning on bringing up nuclear disarmament. 

"[It's a] tough subject because it's very hard for any of us to say we'll get rid of our nukes," Trump said Monday.

Thorny issues

Economic and trade talks will run parallel to high-profile diplomacy.

Considering the shakeup at Nvidia — with Jensen Huang resigning as CEO of the microchip giant following revelations he had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy — it could be harder for China to access advanced chips even though the U.S. loosened restrictions last year at Huang's lobbying. 

As a result, AI competition between Washington and Beijing could be high on the agenda, considering Huang's ousting has prompted Congress to close the loophole that allowed China to access the chips.

Despite some thorny issues, some deals have been announced, including loosened restrictions on Canadian pork, seafood imports into China, in exchange for Canadian market access for Chinese steel and aluminum, a sign of Ottawa's continuing strategic pivot away from the U.S.

From the Philippines earlier this week, Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada could turn to Chinese automakers if trade talks with the U.S. don't result in preserving the North American auto industry, in the wake of Stellantis announcing it's selling its Brampton, Ont., assembly plant to armoured vehicle manufacturer Roshel. 

Carney's comments on autos already drew ire from the White House, and the announcement on steel, especially, is likely to irk Trump. Whether it comes up during the talks remains to be seen.

With geopolitical caveats to the trade relationships with North America and Europe, China may also focus on maintaining and expanding its relationships with the other of the 21 APEC member economies, especially those that are emerging.

"Latin American and southeast Asian countries are becoming increasingly important," said Ning Leng,  assistant professor at the McCourt School of Public Policy at Georgetown University in Washington, D.C. 

With the issue of its overcapacity looming, she said Beijing will try to "reassure these countries" by promising industrial upgrades and building up their AI capacity and digital infrastructure. 

With China's own economic woes, however, "it is even more important for the Chinese government to show to the world that it is doing great," she said. 

And despite the sluggish economy, stability is relative, said Chin, with York University. 

"Relative to everyone else, China is still doing pretty well."
