Belichick’s football program remains a Rorschach test

TheTools · 20.11.2026 18:00 · source · permalink
  • UNC Football

Quick thoughts looking back, looking ahead

5-5 will do that to a program. Add a true freshman QB with obvious athleticism coached by the guy who coached Lamar Jackson in college. While the pain of the 2025 season continues to recede, the murky present remains precariously balanced between skepticism and hope. Some see a developing team that will compete for a playoff spot next season as the post-season tournament expands to 24. “The team’s just a few plays away from 8-2 right now.” Others see something much less than that. “This team’s just a few plays away from 2-8.” Both sentiments have some evidence to support their perspective.

Quick thoughts, in no particular order…

Louisville came into the game as one of the most explosive offenses in college football. They averaged 7.76 yards per play, which led the nation. The Cardinals averaged 7.6 against conference competition, so their numbers weren’t boosted by gargantuan stat padding against a couple of creampuffs. UNC held them to just 4.7 yards per play, a full three yards below their season average. That’s a huge accomplishment.

How did they do it? It was a mix of things. For the first time all season, UNC brought pressure from unexpected directions. Greg Smith, usually an eraser on the back end, notched his first sack of the season. UNC didn’t log a ton of pressures, but the combination of varied rushes and coverages had Cardinals QB Lincoln Kienholz struggling to know where to go with the ball. A team with a 17% explosive rate on the season managed 4.6% against UNC.

Teams that live on a diet of explosive plays can start to press on that front if a defense frustrates them. Louisville had one drive where they simply fed their big back, Keyjuan Brown, between the tackles. Brown finished the game with 11 carries for 78 yards — 7.1 per carry — with seven of those carries on that one drive. The rest of the night, Louisville kept trying to get their speedy playmakers into space and force missed tackles, which the Tar Heels usually smothered. UNC made a bet Louisville would be too impatient to grind out the game between the tackles, and it worked.

The one win came last season against a 4-8 Stanford team that finished the year as one of the worst in the P4 and got their coach fired. The nine losses started out as wipe-outs — 34, 23, and 28 point drubbings — but since then, the remaining six have been by a combined 23 points. Two of those came courtesy of Notre Dame and Miami. Still, 1-9 at home against FBS competition is a brutal record. Which brings us to….

This sort of thing needs to end:

No college football team in the history of the sport willed itself into a winning program via game attendance. Win games, and fans will follow. Lose games, and fans will find other ways to spend their precious time and money.

While this team has shown marked improvement from last season (very low bar) and played admirably in spurts (encouraging), it’s failed to seize two huge opportunities this season. A win over what has turned out to be an awful Clemson team had a real chance to get College Game Day to Chapel Hill. A win over what has turned out to be a poor Syracuse team also had a real chance to get College Game Day to Chapel Hill. UNC squandered big leads in both of those games to lose to bad teams.

Yet, there seems to be a new strain growing within the Belichick Apologia that blames attendance, and specifically students, for failing to do their part to help UNC football level up. Nope. Lose eight of nine home games, and people understandably get frustrated seeing the same result over and over again.

The last home game of the season, NC State, feels increasingly like another inflection point for the program, a must-win to head into 2027 with some measure of positive vibes.

Until Saturday, Edwards was leading a late-season offensive renaissance -- relatively speaking, at least. UNC averaged 31.8 points in a four-week run that included two wins and a near-upset of Miami, but he was poor against Louisville and then left the game because of injury in the third quarter. He’s evidently day-to-day, so I won’t worry about including backup Travis Burgess here, but if Edwards is out, UNC’s odds of grabbing a sixth win diminish.

The three big metrics on average now rate UNC the 43rd-best team in the country, static from the Louisville result. The UNC defense over-performed in that game, while the offense underperformed. The offense remains ahead of the defense, although last week’s showing narrowed the gap considerably.

Against FBS competition, UNC now has a positive point differential of +5 (223 points for, 218 points against). It’s a vastly improved team compared to last season, but still with a narrow margin for victory. Nine more total points could have this team at 8-2. 16 less could have it at 2-8.

SP+ was much higher on UNC last season than both FPI and FEI, mostly due to vastly different ratings for the defense. This season, SP+ is the most skeptical of UNC, mostly due to vastly different ratings for the offense. Belichick’s style of play doesn’t necessarily get rewarded more by one metric of the other. No, I haven’t done a deep dive yet on why SP+ has such a different view of the offense than FPI or FEI, but the two primary culprits are probably yards per play (70th nationally, raw stat) and lack of explosive plays (122nd nationally in plays of 20 or more yards, raw stat),

FPI rates UNC’s difficulty of schedule so far 20th, while FEI puts the SoS at 15th nationally and SP+ at 14th. FEI thinks so far that the offense has faced a tougher challenge (16th nationally) than the defense (42nd). Reminder: it’s been decades since UNC faced a top 20 strength of schedule. Caveat: Miami and Notre Dame (both well played but losses) elevate these rankings considerably. Syracuse and Clemson increasingly look like missed opportunities, while the W over Duke still looks better with each passing week. So far, UNC under Bill Belichick is 5-13 against P4/ND schools.
