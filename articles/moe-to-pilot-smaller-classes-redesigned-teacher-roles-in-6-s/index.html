MOE to pilot smaller classes, redesigned teacher roles in 6 schools from 2027

TheTools · 16.09.2026 08:50 · source · permalink

The Ministry of Education (MOE) will pilot new approaches to redesigning classrooms in six schools from January 2027, including exploring more flexible learning spaces, smaller classes and changes to how teachers' work is organised to better support learner-centred teaching.

The pilot will involve three primary schools and three secondary schools, as part of efforts to strengthen students' holistic development.

Education Minister Desmond Lee outlined the plans at this year's Schools Work Plan Seminar on Wednesday (Sept 16), where he announced two shifts to reimagine learning and redesign classrooms.

"To support these new ways of learning, we must redesign how teaching and learning take place in our classrooms," he said.

MOE said the pilot will explore how learner-centred teaching approaches and more purposeful feedback can be better incorporated into lessons, while giving teachers more time and space to focus more on students' learning and development.

The six schools participating in the pilot are St Anthony's Canossian Primary School, Woodgrove Primary School and Zhangde Primary School.

The secondary schools are Commonwealth Secondary School, Deyi Secondary School and Jurongville Secondary School.

MOE will work closely with the schools to provide guidance and support as they explore approaches suited to their needs and operating contexts, before considering how they could be applied more widely across schools. 

Classrooms to be adapted for flexible learning

One of the strategies MOE will explore is adapting existing school spaces to support different learning activities.

This includes classrooms with mobile and modular furniture that can be easily rearranged, allowing spaces to accommodate both whole-class and small-group learning.

MOE will also look into technology-enabled learning environments that allow smoother transitions between physical and digital learning.

Lee said: "Tomorrow's classrooms will be different. Teachers will facilitate more discussions, observe how students think and collaborate, give timely feedback, and adapt teaching to their needs."

MOE will also explore whether more students could benefit from smaller classes, particularly where they can better support learner-centred teaching approaches.

It will consider whether more students could benefit from smaller classes, particularly where closer teacher attention and more timely feedback could make the greatest difference to teaching and learning.

Lee added that smaller classes do not automatically mean a lighter workload for teachers, as they could end up teaching more classes.

MOE will therefore consider smaller classes as part of a broader redesign of teaching and learning, alongside other measures to create capacity for teachers.

The ministry will also study the possibility of allowing teachers to focus on two of three responsibilities beyond classroom teaching: form teachership, co-curricular activities (CCAs) and committee work.

The time freed up could then be channelled towards teaching and strengthening classroom practices.

Teacher Workspace to be rolled out to all schools by 2027

MOE will also use technology to reduce the time teachers spend on administrative processes.

Teacher Workspace, a platform developed by the ministry, brings together student information, tools and everyday workflows in one place.

The platform aims to reduce the need for teachers to navigate between different digital systems, while giving them a more holistic view of each student's needs and development.

The platform has been piloted in about 20 schools.

Lianhua Primary School principal Pamela Kiew said teachers have responded positively to the platform, particularly its Student Insights feature.

She explained that the feature allows teachers to access information from different channels in one place, giving them a more complete picture of each student.

"Teachers really appreciate that holistic view of each child," she said.

Kiew added that having the information readily available allows teachers to take action more quickly, instead of spending time searching for details across different platforms.

"Teachers want most time in the classroom and not be bogged down by trying to look for things or to understand the thing, because it's already provided to you," she said.

She also said the platform has helped teachers make more informed decisions when planning programmes for students.

Kiew said: "The data really helps us to make good strategic decisions based on real data student needs."

She added that the platform has helped teachers consider students' academic, emotional and home support needs, allowing them to reach out in a more appropriate manner.

"A child can only thrive when we look at them holistically, and the data helps us with that," she said.

Teacher Workspace will be rolled out to all MOE schools by 2027, with additional features introduced progressively.
