Super in dangerous territory after minister's 'extraordinary' investment idea

TheTools · 07.10.2026 00:30 · source · permalink

Opponents of the Labor government have warned a recent suggestion by the country's trade minister is taking Australia's compulsory retirement system into "dangerous territory".

It was the latest suggestion by Trade Minister Don Farrell who floated the idea of super funds investing in the US lamb industry in order to avoid further tariffs by Donald Trump. And it came just a few months after Anthony Albanese argued superannuation was a national asset that could be invested in projects beneficial to the national interest.

NSW Senator Andrew Bragg, the federal shadow minister for housing, labelled Farrell's comments as "quite outrageous" on Monday, arguing they reveal a growing "risk" of superannuation. "He's effectively saying that he wanted to breach the sole purpose test," he said, referring to the legislation that mandates investments must be maintained exclusively to provide retirement benefits to members.

Bragg argued the government was trying to push super funds into housing and corporate landlordism, clean energy and, and now, US lamb. 

If the government continues to push these ideas, "people will lose confidence in it very quickly," he told 2GB radio this afternoon.

"People think that Canberra is effectively playing politics with their money.

"I just think it's extraordinary that a senior Cabinet Minister would actually go on the record and say these things."

Industry super funds did quickly push back on Farrell's US lamb industry-investment idea, with Super Members Council chief Misha Schubert calling his comments "ill-informed and ill-advised". 

"Super funds invest completely independently of governments for very good reason – they invest only in the best financial interests of their members," she said. 

"The role of super is to deliver the strongest possible investment returns for members. It is not the job of super funds to solve other policy problems – nor should it ever be."

Bragg pointed to concern over such investment ideas as driving a "huge explosion in self-managed super funds" as more Aussies take control of their retirement through a self-managed super fund (SMSF).

Bragg pointed to former Deputy Prime Minister Wayne Swan, who is now the chair of Cbus Super, as an example of the close ties between leading Labor figures and the super industry.

"...maybe they think that they can direct the investments in accordance with their political objectives, whether they be for or against the national interest," he said.
