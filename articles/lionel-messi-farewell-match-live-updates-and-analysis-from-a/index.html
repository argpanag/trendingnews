44 Min: Benin with another effort, again easily handled by Martinez.

Guys, what if he doesn’t score?

26 Min: Messi lines up for a free kick 30 yards from goal and puts it on target at the near post, but it’s dealt with easily by Benin goalkeeper Marcel Dandjinou.

Similar distance to one of his all-time greatest efforts – his free kick goal against the United States at the 2016 Copa America. Different result, though.

24 Min: Messi laughs after he misses a golden chance. Messi’s Inter Miami teammate Rodrigo De Paul found him at the edge of the six with a driven cross. Messi, fully outstretched, redirected the cross just wide of the near post. Feels like it’s only a matter of time before the inevitable.

19 Min: Benin midfielder Junior Olaitan gives The Cheetahs their first real chance of the game, streaking towards the byline and driving his shot from eight yards wide of goal – Argentina keeper Emi Martínez (who has a tribute to Messi shaved into his hair tonight, by the way) seemed to have it covered regardless.

Still, impressive fight from a vastly outclassed opponent.

14 Min: Messi receives the ball 25 yards from goal above the semicircle and deftly plays Nicolás Otamendi through with a one-touch service. Otamendi, though, is offside. Predictably enough, Argentina with the bulk of the action in this one thus far.

10 Min: The match is stopped at the 10th minute for a one-minute ovation. More fireworks, more tears as Messi salutes the crowd, which are serenading him – “Olé, Olé Olé Olé, Messi, Messi.” Not sure what Ifab or Fifa have to say about any of this but honestly who cares at this point.

Absolutely electric atmosphere at the Estadio Monumental, the home ground of Argentine side River Plate and the largest stadium in the country – it is filled to capacity well ahead of the opening kick.

Argentina are near-unbeatable at the venue and Messi himself has never lost a match at the stadium, having won 22 of the 28 matches he’s played there with Argentina and drawn the other six, scoring 19 goals along the way.

Something tells Benin will not be breaking that streak tonight.

Argentina’s starting XI is out and Messi, unsurprisingly, is in it, as are many of the players who helped guide Argentina to the 2026 World Cup final.

Messi, like any other megastar, has his haters and believers. I can’t say I fall into either side, but I find myself a bit sad watching the early scenes at Estadio Monumental.

Hi folks, Pablo Iglesias Maurer here, settled in and ready for Lionel Messi’s swan song as Argentina welcome 93rd-ranked Benin to El Monumental for an international friendly.

First things first – if you’re wondering why Argentina is playing a team like Benin, you likely haven’t been paying much attention to the types of friendlies the Albiceleste typically schedule. It’s far from their first encounter with a bottom-rung opponent; the average FIFA global ranking of their friendly opponents over the past several years is 85, at the moment, having faced other minnows like Burkina Faso and Mauritania.

This dynamic offers little in the way of competition but offers the Argentine Football Association a huge amount of control in terms of logistics and financial terms. Tonight, it may lead to all of us seeing Messi score a dozen goals or more. Who knows.

Messi’s international career falls into two distinct halves. There was the first decade when there was a sense that he didn’t quite fit, was somehow insufficiently Argentinian, didn’t care for his country. That was always absurd and can be recognised now as a manifestation of the Argentinian public’s frustration at their trophy drought despite the presence in their forward line of one of the best three to play the game.

Then Messi retired, distraught after a missing a penalty in the shootout against Chile in the Copa América final in 2016. Suddenly, Argentina, rather than just lashing out at their biggest star, was forced to confront what it would mean to live without him. The prospect was so appalling it provoked a mass campaign to encourage Messi to reverse his decision.

Since then he has been loved and, despite a shambolic World Cup in 2018, has delivered with two Copas América, a World Cup and another World Cup final. His career was dogged by the accusation that he never matched Diego Maradona’s achievements in a national shirt; he ended up having won more.
