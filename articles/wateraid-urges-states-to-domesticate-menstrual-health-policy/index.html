WaterAid urges states to domesticate menstrual health policy

TheTools · 27.11.2026 20:18 · source · permalink

WaterAid Nigeria has called on state governments to domesticate the National Policy on Menstrual Health and Hygiene Management to ensure its effective implementation across the country.

The Country Director of WaterAid Nigeria, Evelyn Mere, made the call yesterday in Abuja during the official launch of the National Policy on Menstrual Health and Hygiene Management (MHHM).

Reflecting on the development and approval process of the policy, which lasted 44 months, Mere said the approval of the policy marked an important milestone but stressed that its success would depend on effective implementation, adequate funding and commitment from relevant stakeholders.

The Country Director who was represented by the acknowledged the contributions of the Federal Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development, National Technical Working Group on Menstrual Health and Hygiene Management as well as commended the Population Services International (PSI) for supporting the process,.

Mere said the policy development process involved extensive consultations across Nigeria’s six geopolitical zones to ensure inclusiveness, national ownership and consideration of the country’s diverse social and cultural contexts.

Mere however called for effective monitoring and evaluation to track progress and ensure that the policy delivered tangible results for women and girls across the country.

She said the real test of the policy would be its ability to provide accessible and affordable menstrual products, safe sanitation facilities, accurate information and quality menstrual health services.

Other expected outcomes, she said, included supportive schools and workplaces, as well as an end to stigma, discrimination and exclusion associated with menstruation.

She urged government institutions, development partners, the private sector and civil society organisations to move beyond policy endorsement to resource mobilisation, budgetary allocation and coordinated implementation.

Mere said every woman and girl, regardless of location, income, disability or circumstances, deserved to manage menstruation safely, confidently and with dignity.

The Minister of Women Affairs and Social Development, Hajiya Imaan Sulaiman-Ibrahim, in her remark stated that the policy was aimed at addressing inequalities in access to menstrual hygiene products, sanitation facilities and essential health services across the country.

The minister who was represented by the Chairman of the Governing Council of the Maryam Babangida National Centre for Women Development Princess Jummai Idonije emphasized the need to protect women’s dignity and address structural inequalities affecting their health, education and social inclusion.
