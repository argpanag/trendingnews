AC/DC is slated to play Toronto next week. Will storm-battered Rogers Stadium be ready? | CBC News

TheTools · 11.09.2026 10:58 · source · permalink

'I don't think that's going to happen,' says expert on repairs being done before show on Sept. 16

AC/DC’s Toronto concert is cancelled: Live Nation

A week after a storm damaged parts of Rogers Stadium there is still no word on whether the venue will be up and running in time for a sold-out AC/DC show scheduled for Sept. 16.

The open-air venue in the Downsview area of North York seats up to 50,000 people and is operated by Live Nation Canada. It was significantly damaged after a storm last Wednesday, which ripped out bleachers and scattered debris across the grounds.

Doug Perovic, a professor in the University of Toronto's department of materials science and engineering, told Metro Morning Wednesday he is skeptical the repairs will be finished by next week.

"I don't think that's going to happen before AC/DC," he said.

He listed assessments, extensive repairs and an independent inspection from a professional engineer as things that need to get done.  

"I would think that would probably take a couple weeks, so this timeline looks very compressed," he said.

The City of Toronto issued an "unsafe building" order to the operators on Sept. 3, following an inspection. The city gave Live Nation until Sept. 8 to submit a report outlining the damage and timeline for repairs, which it confirmed in a statement Wednesday morning it had received.

The city did not share details of the report's finding but says it will continue to work with the stadium owner "throughout this process."

According to the order, the operators will now need to file an update by Friday on the progress and status of the repairs.

A Live Nation spokesperson wrote in an email Tuesday the company hopes to have an update on the AC/DC concert this week. As of Wednesday the show has not been cancelled or rescheduled.

'Stay tuned': City manager

Toronto city manager Paul Johnson told Metro Morning Tuesday that he couldn't definitively say whether the venue would be ready in time for the concert.

"The short answer is, Live Nation and their teams are working hard to get it up and going. Our job is to make sure the work is safe and the work is done and they're ready to go," he said.

"I think stay tuned … we have a little bit of time before they have to make some decisions."

Live Nation has has said the damage from Wednesday’s storm was being considered a 100-year event. 

"Structures affected by significant weather events, like a red-level storm of this nature, is a standard process," a Live Nation statement read. "Safety for employees, fans, artists and vendors is always paramount for any part of our operations." 

Perovic said there is a need to revise and expand building codes, especially in the face of increasingly extreme weather events. He noted the fatal 2012 stage collapse ahead of a Radiohead concert, where he participated as an expert in the investigation, as another good reason for extra precautions.

"We hear about this one-in-100-year storm term. To me, I don't buy that. It's somewhat of an excuse," he said.

"We've got more work to do to make these safer."

'We've heard nothing,' fan says

Pam Pardy, an AC/DC fan from Newfoundland, grew up listening to the band and has tickets for the Sept. 16 show with her husband.

"I've never seen them, hubby has never seen AC/DC live, so this is a big thing for us," Pardy said.

She said they have plane tickets secured through points, made supper reservations, arranged accommodation and have tickets to see a Blue Jays game.

Pardy said they were "pretty excited" until Sept. 2, when the storm hit and damaged the stadium.

"Now we're exactly a week away from the concert and we've heard nothing."

Pardy said she and her husband will go on the trip even if the show is cancelled, but she believes there is a plan and that Live Nation Canada can find another venue.

"We all want this show to go on and we want also to feel safe," she said.
