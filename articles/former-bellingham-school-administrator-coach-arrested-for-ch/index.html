Former Bellingham school administrator, coach arrested for child rape | Cascadia Daily News

TheTools · 30.10.2026 11:01 · source · permalink

Victim was a Squalicum High School student in 2013

  • Oct. 30, 2026 11:01 a.m.
  • Updated Oct. 30, 2026 4:41 p.m.

No Q&A content found.

A former Bellingham Public Schools administrator and coach was arrested Thursday for allegedly raping and molesting a student in 2013. 

Russ Robinson, who was the district’s director of safety and security until his resignation this June, was reported to Bellingham police on Oct. 8 by Assistant Superintendent of Human Resources Matt Whitten, according to the probable cause statement. In an unrelated conversation, a former Squalicum High School student, now an adult, told Whitten he was sexually assaulted by Robinson when he was a freshman. 

The assault allegedly occurred when the former student was on the Squalicum wrestling team and Robinson was the head coach. Robinson, who was 28 at the time, formed a “very close” relationship with the victim, 14 at the time, and two other students on the team and would “hang out with them on weekends, go on camping trips and sometimes take them to school,” according to the probable cause statement.

During spring break of 2013, the family of the two other wrestling students asked Robinson to house-sit for them to watch their dog. The victim was asked to check in on the dog, too. When the victim went over one night to the house, Robinson allegedly assaulted him, according to the probable cause statement. The victim told police that he was afraid to report the alleged assault in fear of retaliation from Robinson.

In Whatcom County Superior Court on Friday, Robinson pleaded not guilty to one count of rape of a child in the third degree and one count of child molestation in the third degree. He was booked into the Whatcom County Jail on Thursday night. Commissioner Ann Vetter-Hansen set bail at $100,000 Friday.

Robinson is not allowed to contact the alleged victim or anyone under the age of 18, cannot leave the state of Washington without court permission, cannot purchase a firearm and must surrender any weapons to the Snohomish County Sheriff’s Office. (He currently lives in Snohomish County).

Robinson has no prior criminal history, his defense attorney said.

This fall, he started a new job as director of athletics and safety at Mukilteo School District in Snohomish County. Upon hearing of the arrest, the district immediately placed him on paid administrative leave, that district’s communications director, Julianne Repman, said in an email. 

“We take student safety and employee conduct seriously,” Repman said. “We remain committed to maintaining safe learning environments and will take any additional steps necessary to protect students and staff.” 

Dana Smith, spokesperson for Bellingham Public Schools, said Robinson was a wrestling and football coach from 2000 to 2024 and worked as a campus monitor from 2009 to 2021. He was given additional “districtwide health and safety responsibilities” in 2021 and was hired as director of safety and security in 2024.

Smith said before this report, the district hadn’t received any reports regarding Robinson of alleged sexual assault or misconduct. When district staff were told of the allegation Oct. 8, they “immediately” reported it to the police and worked with them as officers conducted an investigation.

“This is deeply concerning and upsetting news, and we are focused on student safety and are taking appropriate steps to support our students and community,” Smith said in an email. “We have compassion for anyone affected by sexual abuse and honor all victims and survivors. Our staff is committed and trained to take action and address concerning behavior.”

Robinson received a Bellingham Public Schools “Promise Award” in 2024. The district praised him for his collaborative nature, and his ability to “work with strategies that are approachable, age appropriate and strike the ideal balance between school operation and student safety.” An article on the school district website also notes his “special gift connecting with students to bring out the best of them, understand them and work together for the best outcomes.” 

Anyone can report or share a concern about sexual abuse or assault, Smith said. Community members or students should talk with a principal or trusted adult at the school or use the district’s safety tip line.

Other resources in our Bellingham community include the Domestic Violence and Sexual Assault Services 24-hour helpline at 877-715-1563. (Se habla espanol. на русском говорят.) You can also call the Lummi Victims of Crime 24-hour hotline 360-312-2015 or reach out to the Brigid Collins Family Support Center.
