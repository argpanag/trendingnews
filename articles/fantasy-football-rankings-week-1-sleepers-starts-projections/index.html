Week 1 notes, starts, sits

Ranks need context. This year, each skill position will still have notes for potential changes based on news, injuries, weather, etc. Rankings are updated all week and always before kickoff on game day (except Monday, because they’re locked).

  • S-Tier (1) — Elite. If they fail you, it would be surprising.
  • A-Tier (2) — Rare. Need a really good reason to bench.
  • B-Tier (3) — Good. Better than most, but chance to disappoint.
  • C-Tier (4) — Common. Replacement-level with a rare surprise.
  • Hail Mary-Tier (5+) — Damaged. You might get lucky with a broken-hit huge bonus.

Quarterbacks

  • Numerous reports have the Commanders’ offensive line as a massive concern. I’m not knocking Jayden Daniels too much, but that’s enough to keep him from the S-Tier.
  • Jaxson Dart has top-six upside, but if possible, I’d like to wait a week to see 1) how healthy Malik Nabers is and 2) how functional the new offense looks.
  • If not for facing the Rams, Brock Purdy would be in the top 10.
  • The Broncos allowed just four QB scores of 20+ points last year, three involving a rushing touchdown. You likely didn’t draft Patrick Mahomes as your only QB, so I’d wait a week to see how healthy everyone is, especially since reports suggest he’ll run a bit less this season.
  • Malik Willis is the ultimate boom/bust play, which means his ranking is either too low or too high.
  • Similar to Willis, Daniel Jones still has a QB1 ceiling, but similar to Dart, I would like to see how healthy he looks and would prefer him not facing the Ravens.

Running backs

  • Rankings assume Ashton Jeanty plays in Week 1, but given the risk of workload uncertainty, he’s not an S-Tier option, despite that upside against the Dolphins. If out, rookie Mike Washington would jump into the A-Tier behind David Montgomery, given the workload and matchup.
  • If Keaton Mitchell is out, Omarion Hampton would enter the S-Tier, as Kimani Vidal doesn’t look likely to siphon as many touches.
  • With Bucky Irving reportedly 100%, he’s a great RB2 play, and while I also like Kenneth Gainwell, the third-down usage concern is overblown in fantasy. The majority of receiving value actually comes on first and second down.
  • Bhayshul Tuten is the Malik Willis of RBs — if he gets 60% of the backfield work or more, this is too low, but if Chris Rodriguez is in a 50/50 split, it’s likely too high.
  • If D’Andre Swift is out, and Kyle Monangai is ready to go, Monangai would slide in just in front of Rhamondre Stevenson. If both are out for Week 1, Roschon Johnson would slot into the mid-upper 30s, as a high-volume play not without decent risk.
  • Yes, I’m on record as “hating” Cam Skattebo. He might run like Master Chief, but the John Harbaugh-Greg Roman mix and other backfield options worry me that he’ll be the Giants’ version of Mark Ingram.
  • Jeremiyah Love is a lot like Jeanty, but with more risk and a tougher matchup — heck, even if he’s good to go, Tyler Allgeier could lead the way for a few games.
  • MarShawn Lloyd will be one of the biggest risers or fallers after Week 1 — bell-cow work moves him into the B-Tier, if not A-Tier, but half the touches or less and it’s C-Tier City.
  • Jonathon Brooks’ rank hinges on his and Chuba Hubbard’s health. If Hubbard is out, Brooks is B-Tier quality. If Hubbard is active and Brooks doesn’t sound 100%, Brooks likely slides down to C-Tier. If Brooks is surprisingly out and Hubbard starts, he’d be B-Tier ahead of Tony Pollard.

Wide receivers

  • Drake London will slide behind Zay Flowers in the rankings if Tua Tagovailoa starts, but that’s still S-Tier. You can’t outright bench him … yet.
  • If Emeka Egbuka doesn’t sound 100%, he’d fall to the top end of the B-Tier, ahead of Terry McLaurin.
  • Malik Nabers is ranked given the risk — yes, he has top-five upside, but he also feels like trusting your N64 controller not to drift.
  • If we didn’t have a new injury for Mike Evans every other day, he’d be in the A-Tier, and if we get reports of zero limitations, I’d put him ahead of Rashee Rice (facing the Broncos).
  • Alec Pierce, anyone? I can’t get him much higher in the rankings even with full practices, but obviously, if he’s limited heading into Sunday with an uncertain snap count, he’d fall to C-Tier as a boom/bust play. Josh Downs and Keenan Allen would get slight upticks, but not tier-breaking jumps.
  • Already need a flier? Hopefully not; it’s only Week 1! But Devaughn Vele was a target hog late last year, and with rookie Jordyn Tyson sidelined, Vele could be a nice surprise.

Tight ends

  • George Kittle is ranked as though playing, and not at 100%. Kittle would be in the top-three conversation if we knew he had zero limitations.
  • Tucker Kraft would rank ahead of Tyler Warren if we knew he was 100%, as well.
  • Juwan Johnson had nearly 900 receiving yards last season, and again, the Saints are without Tyson. Vele isn’t the only one to benefit from his absence.

Fun with ranks

As mentioned, and because I rank everything from cereal to games to the coolest athletes to hang out with, I like to have some fun with random rankings. Feel free to suggest yours, as most all of them have … “Made the list!” (Yes, I ranked wrestlers and their intros too). To kick things off since it’s been a while (Aaron Lewis, Staind voice), I start Week 1 each year with the best movies since we’ve last ranked things.

Best Movies of 2026 — According to me, and what I’ve seen

  1. Spider-Man: Brand New Day — If you know me, you knew this was coming. Did it get misty in a superhero movie? Yep.
  2. Project Hail Mary — Thought this was the 2026 movie to beat (after “Spider-Man”), and it wasn’t beaten.
  3. The Odyssey — Great, but someone let Christopher Nolan know our ears don’t have to bleed out for it to be a great movie.
  4. Obsession — It takes a lot for me to be impressed by a horror movie, and kudos are well deserved.
  5. Send Help — How do I talk about it without spoiling anything? It’s not your typical survivor movie. Watch it.
  6. The End of Oak Street — Take the 1980s Steven Spielberg movies and make one today. There you go … with dinosaurs.
  7. Super Mario Galaxy — Fun? For sure. Sensical? Not at all. My ’90s kid heart? Happy.
  8. Hoppers — For a kid’s movie, I enjoyed it more than I thought I would.
  9. The Sheep Detectives — My wife Nicole loved it. I went in with low expectations and left recommending it as a good family film. Nicole ranks it fourth.
  10. Crime 101 — Thor and Hulk back together, but for crime things in a movie that many compared to “Heat.” It’s not that good, but where “Heat” was S-Tier, this is B-Tier.
  11. Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu — Could have been another season of the series. Fun, but nothing super special.
  12. Toy Story 5 — Better than 4? Yeah. Good topical theme, and still pretty funny, but mid-level of “Toy Story” movies.
  13. GOAT — It’s your underdog story, but it surprises you in ways. Solid kids movie.
  14. The Breadwinner — If you like Nate Bargatze, you’ll enjoy this and laugh quite a bit.
  15. Normal — If you liked the “Nobody” movies, this is in the same vein.
  16. Disclosure Day — Half-decent summer flick, though the most entertainment came from bumping into a friend as it got out, and he complained and complained about how nonsensical the timeline of events was.
  17. Mortal Kombat II — Better than the first in terms of action and Mortal Kombat-iness, but also more corniness.
  18. Backrooms — I expected … more? Lulled a bit in the middle, in my opinion, and the “big bad” was more humorous than anything.
  19. Office Romance — Date-night time. It’s weird seeing Roy Kent in this role, but I enjoyed it more than I want to admit because I wanted Nicole to “owe me one.”
  20. One Night Only — Date-night movie, but wouldn’t make it your first (or even second) date night. The trailer does not let you in on what’s really happening. Nicole puts Office Romance and this fifth and sixth — she’s mostly okay with the rest of the ranks.
  21. Minions & Monsters — Best “Minions” movie yet? Possibly, but that’s not a high bar.
  22. The Rip — Crime thriller. Decent twist. Fun time-waster.
  23. The Dog Stars — It was … fine. Another movie where the trailer tricks you — way less action than it suggests. Decent story, just, OK and fine.
  24. Cold Storage — Hokey and kinda fun. If you’re looking for a random rainy Saturday movie.
  25. Moana — Complete remake of the original, so, I guess that’s good? Maybe?

Plan to see: “Coyote vs. Acme,” “By Any Means,” “Runner,” “Heart of the Beast,” “Resident Evil,” “Street Fighter,” “Clayface,” “Godzilla Minus Zero,” “Violent Night 2,” “Avengers Doomsday”
