$5 Into $1 Million: One River Card Creates an $880,000 PokerStars Swing

TheTools · 03.09.2026 09:21 · source · permalink

A PokerStars player needed only 18 minutes, a $5 buy-in and one unforgettable river card to become an online poker millionaire. The extraordinary Spin & Go ended with a British player collecting $1 million, while his Hungarian opponent was left wondering how close he had come to claiming the life-changing top prize.

Turning $5 into $1 million sounds more like a lottery fantasy than a poker result, but that is precisely what happened when UK-based player “Gwaugh1” entered a special-edition $5 Spin & Go at PokerStars. The three-handed tournament generated the maximum $1.2 million prize pool, an outcome listed at odds of just one in 10 million, and suddenly all three players were competing for sums almost unimaginable at these stakes.

“Gwaugh1” eventually claimed the $1 million top prize, while Hungary’s “Kepes897” earned $120,000 as the runner-up and Danish player “g3treal” received $80,000 for third place. Every participant therefore secured an exceptional return on a $5 entry, but the final hand created an enormous $880,000 difference between first and second place.

One River Card Worth $880,000

The decisive hand produced a particularly dramatic ending. “Kepes897” committed his chips with top pair and was ahead when the money went into the middle. “Gwaugh1” still had a gutshot straight draw and a flush draw, but needed help from the final card to complete the comeback.

That help arrived in the form of a jack on the river, completing the British player’s straight and immediately ending the tournament. Instead of being reduced to a short stack and continuing the battle, “Gwaugh1” secured the full $1 million prize. For “Kepes897”, the same card meant finishing second for $120,000 - still a remarkable result, but $880,000 short of the jackpot.

Third Million-Dollar Spin & Go of the Summer

Even more remarkably, this was already the third time PokerStars had awarded its maximum Spin & Go jackpot during the summer of 2026. Denmark’s “dillerfar19”, who reportedly usually played tournaments costing only $0.50, became the first of the three winners after moving up to the $5 level. Argentina’s “Anatsuk334” then repeated the feat only a few days later.

Spin & Go tournaments combine three-handed poker with a randomly selected prize pool. Players begin with short stacks and most games finish within minutes. The majority offer relatively modest multipliers, but the exceptionally rare maximum jackpot transforms a low-stakes sit-and-go into a seven-figure contest before the first hand is even dealt.

For “Gwaugh1”, the entire journey from a $5 registration to a million-dollar victory took approximately 18 minutes. For “Kepes897”, it came down to one river card. Few hands illustrate the extraordinary swings of jackpot poker more clearly.
