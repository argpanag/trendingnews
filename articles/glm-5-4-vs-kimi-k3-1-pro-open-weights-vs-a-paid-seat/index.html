One of these models is a 1.56-terabyte download. The other is a model name you type into a subscription's settings box. Kimi K3.1 Pro published its weights on Hugging Face on 4 November 2026, twelve days after M​oonshot opened the API on 23 October, and with them a licence, a benchmark grid, an architecture table and a per-token price. G​LM-5.4 went live on Z.ai's Coding Plan on 23 November 2026 with vision support, a 50% quota increase and no documentation page at all. Comparing G​LM-5.4 and Kimi K3.1 Pro is therefore less a capability contest than a contest of disclosure — and for anyone choosing what to build on this quarter, the disclosure gap is itself the finding.

One table, two kinds of blank

Line the two releases up and the asymmetry is stark enough to be the section.

• Weights — Kimi K3.1 Pro is downloadable, ungated, in MXFP4 at roughly 1.56 TB (~5.6 TB in BF16) vs G​LM-5.4 has no repository and no announced plan for one.

• Licence — a custom "Kimi K3.1 Pro License", MIT-style with a clause requiring a commercial arrangement above $20M in model-as-a-service revenue vs nothing to license, because there are no weights.

• Price — $2.00 input and $10.00 output per million tokens, cached input $0.20, with a Highspeed tier at $4/$20 vs no per-token rate; G​LM-5.4 is sold only as part of a $18 / $80 / $168 monthly plan.

• Context — 1,048,576 tokens with no long-context surcharge vs a "1M" tag reported from the Coding Plan interface, not documented.

• Benchmarks — a published grid including Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7% vs no benchmark table, no model card.

• Parameters — 2.8 trillion total with 104 billion active, disclosed vs undisclosed, with Chinese press reporting around 700 billion.

• Modality — text and image input, with video through the Files API vs image input added to the flagship for the first time.

• Reasoning — always on, effort low/high/max vs unconfirmed, though Z.ai's line has always defaulted to maximum effort.

None of those blanks prove G​LM-5.4 is worse. They prove that a comparison cannot be completed, which is a different statement and a more useful one. A reader who needs a number to put in a procurement document has one side of this table filled and the other side empty.

K​imi's numbers, and which ones to trust

The parts of K​imi's table that hold up are the independently produced ones. Artificial Analysis measures Kimi K3.1 Pro at 55 on its Intelligence Index — fourth of 215 models in its class, two points above GPT-6 Astra at 53 and three below Claude Opus 5.5 — at roughly $2.11 per completed index task. That cost figure matters because the model is verbose: about 55,000 output tokens per task, which erodes the advantage of a $10 output rate. Per-token, it is cheap. Per task, it lands within a rounding error of models charging more per token and thinking less.

M​oonshot's own grid is more flattering and should be labelled as vendor-reported: Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7%, a small lead over Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4% on the independent run and a much larger one over the 58.2% attributed to G​PT-6 Astra. BrowseComp 92.1, HLE with tools 70.0, SWE-Marathon 51.7. These are real results, honestly produced, and they are also the rows a vendor chooses to publish.

The rows it publishes less loudly are where the model has a shape rather than a score. Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 at 38.1, Program Bench at 31.5, HLE-Diamond at 39.4. Kimi K3.1 Pro is an agentic-workhorse model that is excellent at long-horizon coding and retrieval and unremarkable at scientific reasoning and benchmark-integrity tasks. If your workload looks like a repository and a browser, it fits. If it looks like a physics problem, the headline number is not about you.

Vision is the reason G​LM-5.4 exists

Strip away the quota bump and the Z.ai release has exactly one capability change: the flagship can see. GLM-5.3 was text in, text out. Its smaller sibling, GLM-5.3-Flash, already handled images and video. G​LM-5.4 closes that gap in the main plan model, and Z.ai's own framing is unusually concrete — reading interface screenshots for front-end development and page debugging.

That is a narrow claim and a meaningful one. It is also the claim most in need of evidence, because "accepts images" and "uses images well" are different products. Kimi K3.1 Pro has had image input since launch, plus video through the Files API, and its vision encoder is documented in the architecture rather than asserted in a changelog. On modality breadth, the open-weight model is strictly ahead. On the specific task of looking at a rendered UI and changing the code behind it, nobody outside Z.ai has tested G​LM-5.4 at all.

The self-hosting arithmetic

Kimi K3.1 Pro's open weights are real, and so is their cost. At 2.8 trillion parameters with 104 billion active, a self-hosted deployment wants around eight B300-class GPUs; data-parallel sharding on smaller hardware is possible but pushes latency into the wrong range for interactive coding. Day-0 support landed in vLLM, SGLang and TokenSpeed, so the software path exists. The hardware path is a capital decision, not a download.

G​LM-5.4 has no such arithmetic because it has no weights. The entire cost is the plan: $18 Lite, $80 Pro or $168 Max, unchanged by the release, with the weekly credit pool 50% larger when the model is set to g​lm-5.4. For a solo developer that is a rounding error next to any GPU budget. For a team running sustained agent traffic, it is the difference between an operating expense and a capacity plan — and the fact that Z.ai has not priced the model per token means you cannot yet compute the crossover.

One key, two very different bets

There is a middle path that does not require choosing between a subscription and a datacentre. Kimi K3.1 Pro is on OrcaRouter's catalogue at the provider's $2/$10 with no markup, and GLM-5.3 sits beside it at $1.26 input and $3.96 output per million tokens with cache reads at $0.234, against Z.ai's own $1.40/$4.40 card. That is the honest version of "try both": one key, provider list prices passed through, and a routing DSL that can send a task to whichever model your own logs say finishes it cheaper.

G​LM-5.4 itself is not on the catalogue, and this page will not pretend otherwise. Z.ai has not exposed it as an API, so there is no endpoint to route to. What you can do today is measure the documented GLM-5.3 against Kimi K3.1 Pro on your own traffic — same prompt, same scaffolding, count output tokens — and use that as the baseline that a future G​LM-5.4 endpoint gets compared against. Failover matters here too: an agent that can fall back from K​imi to a second provider does not stop working when one host degrades.

Who should take which

Take Kimi K3.1 Pro if the decision has to survive an audit. You get a published price, a published licence, a downloadable checkpoint, independent scores on the same index as its competitors, and a documented weakness pattern you can plan around. The licence's $20M revenue clause is the one thing to read before building a product on it; the weights being open does not mean the terms are permissive for a large API business.

Take G​LM-5.4 if you are already inside Z.ai's plan and the missing capability was vision. It costs nothing extra, it raises the headroom by half, and for screenshot-driven front-end work it removes the workaround of switching to the Flash model. What you should not do is treat it as a frontier contender on the strength of a launch post. M​oonshot published 65.7 on Terminal-Bench before asking anyone to believe it; Z.ai has published nothing, and the fair reading of that silence is "unmeasured", not "unbeatable".

The comparison will be worth rerunning the week Z.ai posts a rate card and a model card. If the shape of the G​LM line holds, the interesting question will not be whether G​LM-5.4 beats Kimi K3.1 Pro on the aggregate — the open-weight model's margin on agentic work is large — but whether it gets close enough on the coding tasks the plan is sold for, at a subscription price that no per-token endpoint can match.
