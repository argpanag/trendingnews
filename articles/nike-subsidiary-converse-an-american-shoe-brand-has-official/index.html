Nike subsidiary Converse, an American shoe brand, has officially apologized after controversy erupted over an advertising photo reminiscent of the white supremacist group KKK.

According to the BBC and Yonhap News on the 19th (local time), an opinion was raised that a sneaker advertisement photo posted on Converse Instagram account recently showed an image like a white hood in the form of a cone hat, the symbol of KKK.

The white headscarf is a symbol of racism worn by the KKK when it committed mass violence and terrorism against black people while hiding its identity. It was also pointed out that a pair of sneakers hanging in the photo reminded people of the lynching victim.

As criticism intensified, Converse deleted the photo in question from social networking services. Other platforms also said they are working on removing this photo.

"This should not have happened," Converse said in a statement released the day before, "and we understand why this image is deeply regrettable and admit we were wrong."

Although deleted from Converse's official account, the photo is rapidly spreading on social networking services.

Meanwhile, the photo of the advertisement was taken for Converse's worldwide promotion campaign for "Chuck 70 X" on the 27th of last month. Singer Aespa's Karina also participated as a model in the promotional campaign.
