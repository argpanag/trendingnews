Gates Foundation Shifts Strategy to Focus on AI Anti Poverty Efforts

TheTools · 25.09.2026 16:19 · source · permalink

The Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation shut down a $500 million anti-poverty initiative to focus on artificial intelligence, committing $1 billion over two years to use tech solutions to expand access to public safety-net benefits.

The philanthropy giant ended its Economic Mobility and Opportunity program, which had unlocked $2.2 billion in safety-net benefits and improved job quality for 100,000 workers since 2016. In October, the foundation joined other donors to fund NextLadder Ventures with $1 billion over seven years to deploy personalized AI tools for career transitions, health crises, and housing needs.

Bill Gates defended the technological pivot in a progress report, emphasizing the need for quick investment to ensure the technology is applied safely across society.

"AI, if shaped well, could expand who has access to solutions, opportunities, and knowledge that has too often been out of reach," wrote Bill Gates, co-chair of the Gates Foundation.

Foundation leadership maintains that using artificial intelligence allows the organization to lean into data-driven solutions while avoiding lengthy public policy debates. NextLadder Ventures will focus on cutting bureaucratic hurdles for individuals navigating public assistance systems.

"It's so critical that there's capital to ensure that AI is not a tool just for the wealthy, that it is empowering," said Ryan Rippel, leader of NextLadder Ventures.

Critics argue that complex socioeconomic challenges require sustained, multifaceted investments rather than rapid technological deployment. Former program leaders cautioned against seeking simplified interventions for long-term systemic problems.

"I don't know if this is a human thing or an American thing, but particularly in philanthropy, we want that magic pill," said Nisha Patel, executive director of the U.S. Partnership on Mobility From Poverty. "Unfortunately with economic mobility, there are too many factors in play to say one thing is going to be the cure-all."

Former foundation executives noted that traditional anti-poverty campaigns often encounter institutional resistance, whereas targeted technology can directly streamline access to existing public benefits programs.

"Something like poverty is very difficult to do," said Allan Golston, former director of U.S. programs at the Gates Foundation. "If you try to launch something that's too big or too complicated, you just naturally encounter friction."

Other major philanthropic organizations, including the Ballmer Group, Robin Hood Foundation, and the McGovern Foundation, are also shifting funding into artificial intelligence social mobility initiatives.

"Nothing has caught fire that fast," said Emary Aronson, chief knowledge officer at the Robin Hood Foundation. "We see this as just a natural evolution of philanthropy. And philanthropy, like everything else, has to change."
