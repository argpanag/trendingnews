CMA updates Code of Ethics to address discrimination, advance reconciliation

TheTools · 11.10.2026 08:34 · source · permalink

The Canadian Medical Association has updated its Code of Ethics and Professionalism, adding stronger expectations around racism, discrimination, culturally safe care and reconciliation with Indigenous Peoples.

The CMA says the changes follow an extensive consultation process and are part of its Reconciliation Action Plan.

The update also follows the association’s apology for the role of the medical profession in past and ongoing harms to Indigenous Peoples, including Indian hospitals, forced medical experimentation, forced sterilization, child apprehensions and systemic discrimination within the health-care system.

New expectations for physicians

The updated code includes clearer expectations for physicians to provide culturally safe, trauma-informed and equitable care.

It also calls for respect for Indigenous Knowledge and healing practices and sets expectations for how physicians treat colleagues.

CMA president Dr. Bolu Ogunyemi said racism and discrimination continue to exist within health care and the profession has a responsibility to address them.

“Racism and discrimination have no place in health care, yet we know that they persist, sometimes with tragic consequences,” Ogunyemi said.

“We can and must do better, and this updated Code of Ethics and Professionalism is an important step in that process.”

The CMA says longstanding principles including respect, fairness and putting patients’ health first remain central to the code.

Code available in six languages

The updated Code of Ethics and Professionalism is available online in six languages: English, French, Inuktitut, Southern or Heritage Michif, Northern Michif-Cree and Anishinaabemowin.

Jenny Tenasco, an Algonquin Elder from Kitigan Zibi Anishinabeg, said health professionals have an obligation to provide care without racism or discrimination.

“All health professionals have an obligation to help all people with professionalism, no racism or discrimination,” Tenasco said.

“Kindness and understanding must be there for everyone.”

Part of broader reconciliation work

The CMA says updating the code is one part of a broader effort to create a more equitable and accessible health-care system.

The association says it plans to continue working with physicians, health organizations and other health-system partners on changes aimed at reducing discrimination and improving care.

The Code of Ethics and Professionalism is used to help guide ethical medical practice across much of Canada.
