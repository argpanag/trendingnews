The Oval Office Clock Kept Moving While Nobody Said a Word

TheTools · 09.10.2026 20:50 · source · permalink

Trump said he could do whatever he wanted. Beside him stood a $6.25 billion donation, a $9.7 billion contract, and a face the internet could not stop dissecting.

The clock in the Oval Office moved through ten minutes.

Trump stood behind the Resolute Desk. His right index finger tapped the wood. Three taps. Stop. Two taps. Michael Dell stood in front of him, dark blue suit, tie knot pulled tight, as if the tie had just come out of the box. Susan Dell stood half a step to his right. Red dress. Red with no other color in it. Her heels sank into the carpet. Her ankles tilted slightly outward. She held herself straight. Shoulders back. Chin up. Someone behind her might have been pulling a string.

Peter Doocy of Fox News asked a question about the midterms. Trump listened. The corner of his mouth moved first. Then he began to talk about Hillary Clinton, about Joe Biden, about Barack Obama. He said he could have done very bad things to them, but he did not, because you have to respect the office of the presidency. Then he said, "I can do whatever I want, if I want."

Doocy's pen stopped in the air. A man in a gray suit looked at the door. Susan Dell did not blink. Her lashes were long and thick, like a row of small brushes. Her lips pressed together. The corners of her mouth lifted a little, then fell back.

The camera stayed on.

The event continued. Trump turned to Michael Dell. He said the Dells had given six billion two hundred fifty million dollars to Trump Accounts. He called it historic. He said people should go buy Dell computers. He laughed. Michael Dell laughed. Susan Dell did not laugh. Her chin stayed up. The tendons in her neck stood out.

Six billion two hundred fifty million. That number was announced in December. The Dells' statement said they would give twenty-five million children one thousand dollars each. Michael Dell said that when a child gets a small financial start, his world expands. The press release carried that sentence. The backdrop at the White House event carried it too.

Four months later, Dell Technologies received a contract. The Department of Defense. Nine point seven billion dollars. Five years. Microsoft 365. Cloud subscriptions. On-premise licenses. The Pentagon. Intelligence agencies. The Coast Guard. The Defense Department said Dell won in a competitive process. It said the contract would save four hundred twenty-two million dollars a year in licensing costs.

A little earlier. February tenth. Trump's disclosure forms showed he bought Dell Technologies stock. One million to five million dollars. The date was on the form. February tenth. Before that, his accounts had already made twenty-four Dell trades. Sixteen buys. Eight sells.

None of those numbers appeared on the screen in the Oval Office. The screen showed a children's account webpage. A cartoon piggy bank. Blue. An American flag printed on its side.

Susan Dell stood beside the screen. Her face was smooth under the lights. Her jawline looked cut. Her cheekbones were high, high enough to make her eyes seem sunken. The skin around her eyes was pulled tight. The corners tilted up. People on social media began taking screenshots. They asked what had happened to her. They asked if her eyes had been stitched open. They asked why she was standing like that.

Ross Perry, Gerard Lambe, and Shervin Naderi later analyzed the photographs for the press. One said full facelift. One said fillers, eyelid surgery, Botox. One said fifty thousand to one hundred thousand dollars. One said it was called a "Mar-a-Lago face." High cheekbones. Thick lips. Tight jaw. Flawless skin. A mask for an elite circle.

Susan Dell did not respond. She did not need to respond. Her face had already been looked at millions of times.

After the event, people walked down the hall. Susan Dell's heels struck the marble. Click. Click. Click. Michael Dell walked beside her, phone against his ear. Trump stopped at the door. He asked Michael Dell whether Susan might go into politics. He said he would give her "complete and total support." Michael Dell laughed. Susan Dell did not look back.

At the end of the hall there was a window. Outside was the lawn. On the lawn was a helicopter. The rotors turned, but there was no sound. The glass was too thick.

That night, Dell Technologies stock rose. Michael Dell's net worth reached sixth in the world. Trump's accounts still held Dell stock. The Defense Department contract was still in effect. The children's account webpage was still up. The cartoon piggy bank was still there.

The clock in the Oval Office was still moving.

No one mentioned the sentence. No one mentioned the contract. No one mentioned the stock. No one mentioned Susan Dell's face.

Doocy later stood in the hallway, scrolling on his phone. He saw a post that said, "She looks like she's being held up by sticks." He locked the screen. He put the phone in his pocket. He walked into the parking lot. It was already dark.

The White House press secretary later stood behind the podium, hands flat on the lectern. Reporters shouted questions. He pointed at one. That person asked about the Dell contract. He said Dell won in a competitive process. He pointed at another. That person asked about the stock. He said the president's finances were managed by an independent trust. He pointed at another. That person asked about Susan Dell. He paused. He said, "Today we are talking about children's accounts." Then he left.

Susan Dell had said one sentence at the event. She said, "We believe every child should have a start." Her voice was light. She looked at the teleprompter as she spoke. Her fingers pinched the edge of the lectern. Her nails were French. The white tips were narrow.

That was the ten minutes.
