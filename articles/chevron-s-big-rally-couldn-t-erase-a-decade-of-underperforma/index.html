The energy major's stock has gained sharply over the past year, and the rally changes how its full decade looks. Put $1,000 into Chevron (NYSE:CVX) ten years ago, reinvest every dividend, and you'd now hold more than three times your money. Even so, you'd still trail the S&P 500.

Oil Crashes, Oil Spikes and a Hess-Sized Push for Scale

Chevron works across the whole energy chain. Upstream, it finds and produces oil and gas. Downstream, it refines, markets and makes chemicals. Results swing with crude prices. The 2020 COVID crash hit the whole industry hard, though Chevron kept paying its dividend. Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 then brought a boom.

Chevron closed its Hess acquisition in 2025. That year production hit a record 3,723 MBOED, up 12%. In 2026, military action that began February 28 effectively shut the Strait of Hormuz, and Brent averaged $117 per barrel in April. Second-quarter revenue rose 51.4% to $67.2B. Over the whole decade, the quarterly dividend climbed from $1.07 to $1.78.
