Live: Multiple Irish media outlets receive bomb threats

TheTools · 06.11.2026 10:20 · source · permalink

LAST UPDATE | 14 mins ago

MULTIPLE MEDIA ORGANISATIONS have received bomb threats this morning, disrupting news coverage and forcing staff to evacuate.

Buildings at RTÉ’s Montrose campus, the Irish Independent’s Dublin city centre building, and the offices that house Newstalk and Today FM in Dublin were evacuated this morning amid bomb threats. 

Newstalk’s staff have been permitted to return to the building and is broadcasting as normal. RTÉ remains evacuated from its main studios but is back on air through back-up channels. 

It follows a week that saw multiple bomb threats sent to airlines across Europe, including Aer Lingus and Ryanair.

Speaking on RTÉ Radio 1, Taoiseach Micheál Martin said authorities will pursue those responsible for the threats causing today’s disruption, but declined to speculate on whether Russia was involved.

Speaking to David McCullagh via phone, Martin said it had not yet been established whether individuals or a coordinated operation were responsible.

“However, we will go after this to identify who’s responsible and to hold them accountable,” Martin said.

Asked about security ahead of next week’s European Political Community summit in Dublin, he said agencies had been preparing for such risks since before Ireland’s EU presidency.

“The security stakes are much higher in this period of time than they have been for a long time in Europe.”

Martin said any threats received should be taken seriously and that gardaí should be contacted over them. Asked directly about possible Russian involvement, he said:

“I’m not going to make any comment at the stage as to who is responsible.”

It’s understood the threat sent to RTÉ claimed to be from the same group that were behind the threats to airlines earlier this week. 

It referred to European authorities “incompetent fools” and warned of further action to come. 

David McCullagh is now back on air on Radio 1. 

“Irish society is being discommoded,” the current affairs host told listeners as his broadcast began. 

Marty Whelan, who had been evacuated from his Lyric FM studio along with the rest of the RTÉ staff this morning, made a surprise appearance as the first guest on the show. 

“Time for my bed,” Whelan, said, tongue-in-cheek.

He shared that he had been forced to leave his jacket and keys in the studio building in Montrose as he was ushered out.

“It is a scary moment. In all the years I’ve been here, I’ve never come across this ever.”

McCullagh just said about the people who did this:

“Presumably they might feel that they have achieved their aims as we’re sitting in the Roadcaster.

“But we’re on air, up yours whoever you are.”

Yesterday, Defence Minister Helen McEntee described this week’s wave of hoax bomb threats against European flights as “extremely concerning”, ahead of Ireland hosting 55 heads of state next week.

At least 27 flights were targeted yesterday, following threats against around 10 flights on Tuesday.

Among yesterday’s affected services was Aer Lingus flight EI402 from Dublin to Rome, which landed safely at Évreux military air base in France after being escorted by fighter jets. Cork Airport also received an anonymous telephone threat concerning a specific flight. Nothing of concern was found and that service resumed operations after a 90-minute delay.

All affected flights across Europe landed safely and no bombs were found. Authorities are investigating who is responsible and whether the threats are connected.

Speaking to reporters yesterday, McEntee said the Defence Forces and gardaí were preparing to ensure the safety of those attending next Thursday’s European Political Community summit in Dublin.

“Everything that needs to be done, and should be done, will be done, to ensure their security and safety,” McEntee said, adding that gardaí would work with services across the EU to identify those behind the threats. 

Statement in from Garda Press Office: 

“An Garda Síochána is aware of a threat made against media organisations here and in Europe.

“An Garda Síochána has been liaising with the Irish media organisations and the threats will be investigated.”

Returning to air this morning Clare Byrne told listeners: 

“Everybody left. We were advised that we could return to the building, and that that warning was a hoax. So apologies for the interruption. It was beyond our control.

“We wanted to make sure that everybody was safe and sound. But I’m happy to say that we are back on air.”

It’s understood RTÉ Radio 1 is also due to recommence broadcasting from the Roadcaster mobile studio parked outside the broadcaster’s HQ. 

RTÉ have just issued this statement: 

“RTÉ, along with a range of media organisations, received a threat this morning. In line with our security and safety protocols, the campus has been evacuated and some services are affected. Gardaí are on site in Donnybrook.”

RTE Radio 1 has been playing music since its regular progamming was taken off air, with a continuity announcer telling listeners efforts were being made to resume scheduling. 

Newstalk’s Claire Byrne Show was also off air, and switched to a playback of a ‘best of’, but the presenter has now resumed her show. 

Bomb threats were sent to RTÉ earlier this morning, and all the main buildings at the campus were evacuated. 

Gardaí are now at the site and are investigating. 

As news of the threat to the national broadcaster emerged, sources confirmed threats were also sent to the Irish Independent and Bauer Media, the group that controls Newstalk and Today FM.
