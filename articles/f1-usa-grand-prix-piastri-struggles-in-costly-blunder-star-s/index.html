F1 USA Grand Prix: Piastri struggles in costly blunder; star slams ‘s*** show’ as title leader takes huge step — Quali Wrap

TheTools · 25.10.2026 01:02 · source · permalink

Oscar Piastri qualified in seventh thanks to a late blunder from McLaren teammate Lando Norris as Kimi Antonelli took another step towards securing his maiden Formula One world title.

Antonelli is on the cusp of glory after beating Max Verstappen to pole position - for the first time since July’s Belgian Grand Prix - for the United States Grand Prix on Saturday.

The 20-year-old Italian will wrap up the drivers’ championship on Sunday if he wins the race, his Mercedes teammate George Russell finishes outside the top eight and Lewis Hamilton finishes outside the top four.

Briton Russell could only qualify sixth-fastest and will join Hamilton on the third row of the grid.

Verstappen missed out on a third consecutive pole but will line up alongside Antonelli on the front row.

The Dutchman’s Red Bull teammate Isack Hadjar qualified third, with Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc fourth.

Piastri was initially eighth-fastest, but Norris dropped two places after his final lap time was deleted for track limits.

Seventh is a place Piastri has become all too familar with - he has qualified in that spot on the fourth row for half of this year’s races.

Norris was fastest in Q1, while Leclerc was fastest in Q2, but Antonelli came to the fore when it mattered.

The two Mercedes and Piastri were the only cars to head out on medium tyres in Q1.

The Australian battled to be 12th fastest in the first session, and then was sixth fastest in Q2 after a scare, sitting in tenth as he began his final lap.

In Q3, Antonelli’s lap of one minute and 34.035 seconds gave him his seventh pole position of the season, but his first since the Belgian Grand Prix in July.

“We got pole, so we will try to win tomorrow,” Antonelli said.

“Max has been very quick and Isack did an incredible job, so it won’t be easy.

“We will try to get a good start and hopefully we lead into Turn 1, then set the pace, even though it will be very tight.

“Very happy with qualifying. Q3 was very tough with the wind. Very gusty and unpredictable but happy to put it on pole.”

Verstappen, meanwhile, was complaining about his tyres and his car being “tricky” to drive on Red Bull’s team radio, but still managed to earn a place on the front row.

“Just Q3 was not good,” he said.

“Q1, Q2 was nice but then, for whatever reason on both sets [of tyres], just didn’t have a good balance.

“There were no mistakes but just no grip and started to slide a lot and then the final lap, you can see that it’s not really happening with the tail wind in places so you try to push a bit more, you hit the bump wrong the car is like a kangaroo and you have a massive slide so you know the lap is over.

“Just not very happy with Q3, to be honest. Not what I wanted, not what I expected but if it’s still a P2, I take it.”

The most heated moment of the day came from Alex Albon after he and teammate Carlos Sainz were eliminated Q3.

“What a s*** show. Why did we go so early?” he said over the team radio of Williams’ double early exit.

Remarkably in Q3, Aston Martin’s Fernando Alonso had a fire at the back of his car, but still managed to progress to Q2.

Earlier, one of Australia’s fan favourites, Valtteri Bottas, is reportedly facing the axe with the United States Grand Prix shaping as a huge opportunity on Sunday morning.

Bottas, who has earned his honorary Aussie status by becoming a fan favourite over the years and spending plenty of time in the country due to having an Australian wife, has struggled this season behind the wheel for Cadillac.

With Haas reportedly sacking Esteban Ocon, Cadillac could shape as a new home for the Frenchman after a fresh report cast doubts on Bottas’ future.

According to Racer, the Finnish driver’s teammate Sergio Perez has held talks regarding a new deal — while Bottas could make way for IndyCar convert Colton Herta.

Bottas finished down the bottom of the rankings during both of Saturday’s practice sessions, while Aussie Oscar Piastri slotted into seventh and eighth.

On the track, Lewis Hamilton topped the times for Ferrari ahead of Red Bull’s Max Verstappen with Mercedes’ championship leader Kimi Antonelli fourth in Saturday’s third and final free practice.

The 41-year-old seven-time champion clocked a best time of one minute and 34.893 seconds to outpace his old rival, four-time champion Verstappen, by 0.168sec at the Circuit of the Americas.

Hamilton’s Ferrari team-mate Charles Leclerc was third after having several laps deleted -- like many of the drivers -- for exceeding track limits, finishing ahead of Antonelli and his Mercedes team-mate and nearest title rival George Russell.

Antonelli leads Russell by 102 points in the championship and can become Formula One’s youngest champion, at 20, and Italy’s first since Alberto Ascari of Ferrari in 1953, if he wins Sunday’s race and his rivals fail to stay within reach of his points total.

Russell needs to finish in the top eight and Hamilton in the top four to keep their respective title hopes alive if Antonelli wins.

Hamilton’s fastest lap ensured Ferrari had topped the times in all three practice sessions after Leclerc topped both on Friday. Leclerc was fast again in this session but had five laps deleted due to exceeding track limits as a total of 42 laps were ruled out overall.
