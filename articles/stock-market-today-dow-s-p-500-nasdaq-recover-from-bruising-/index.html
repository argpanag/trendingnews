US stocks climbed on Monday as investors regained risk appetite ahead of another packed week of earnings, midterm elections, and a key jobs report.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) rose 1.1%, while the S&P 500 (^GSPC) added 0.6%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) gained 0.6%, attempting a comeback after a tech rout dragged stocks last week.

(^DJI )

Markets were poised to bounce back after an AI-fueled sell-off that saw the Nasdaq Composite lose 5.2% during the month of October. Chipmakers and other tech names rebounded ahead of the market open, though Apple (AAPL) shares dipped slightly in the lead-up to the iPhone maker's earnings report after the bell on Monday. Investors also got a fresh look at Anthropic on Monday morning with the AI startup disclosing third quarter earnings in an amended version of its S-1 filing.

Bitcoin (BTC-USD) was little changed at $78,000 per token after cryptocurrencies took a hit following a $6.3 billion hack of Binance, which investigators are calling the biggest crypto hack in history.

Crude oil prices, meanwhile, dropped amid reports that the US and Iran are seeking a diplomatic resolution to the war in the Middle East. Brent crude futures (BZ=F), the international benchmark, traded near $105 per barrel.

On the economic data front, US manufacturing expanded in October as inflation pressures persisted, according to recent surveys. The calendar for the rest of the week will feature several pieces of labor market data, culminating in the government's October jobs report on Friday.

  • Fed has a long way to go to rein in inflation: Apollo's Sløk

    The Fed may be farther away from its inflation goal than the market currently expects, according to Apollo Global chief economist Torsten Sløk. (Disclosure: Yahoo is a portfolio company of funds managed by affiliates of Apollo Global Management.)

    Consequently, more substantial rate hikes may be needed to achieve the Fed's 2% inflation target.

    "A macroeconomic model based on the historical relationship between interest rates, GDP and inflation suggests the Fed would need to raise the Fed funds rate by about 275 basis points, from roughly 4% today to 6.75% by the end of 2027, to bring CPI back to its 2% target," Sløk wrote in a blog post on Monday.

    Fed funds futures show traders are pricing in 76% odds of a 25 basis point rate hike in December, per CME Group. Markets have put a less-than-2% probability on the Fed raising its benchmark rate above 5% in 2027 (the rate is currently in a range of 3.75%-4.00%).

    Sløk added that even if the Fed began hiking aggressively, inflation would not likely come down until at least the second quarter of 2027.

  • Musk is throttling Grok because its cost is 'unsustainably high'

    Elon Musk said on Sunday that Grok (SPCX) users would run up against usage limits more quickly than normal because servicing Grok Bot has become too expensive, Yahoo Finance's Hamza Shaban reports:

    "The cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue," Musk wrote on his social media platform, X.

    The sudden throttling highlights one of the biggest questions swirling around the AI revolution: whether demand from users will be enough to sustain a business. It turns out, at least in Musk's case, that demand isn't the problem. The cost is.

    User demand is just one side of the AI market equation, and the businesses churning out sophisticated and expensive models have to — or will have to, eventually — generate revenue to cover their heightened costs

  • A lasting AI sell-off is one of the biggest risks to the economy, BofA warns

    Yahoo Finance's Jared Blikre reports:

    Wall Street hedge funds have already backed away from AI. BofA's economists warn the rest of the economy may not be able to.

A slowdown would hit growth directly through spending, and indirectly through the wealth effect — when falling stock prices make households feel poorer and spend less.

    The warning follows a three-week stretch in which AI stocks lost about $5 trillion in market value. The Nasdaq 100 (^NDX) ended Friday about 9% below its Oct. 6 record, though stocks are rebounding early Monday.

  • Anthropic reveals amended S-1 outlining huge spending commitments, rising sales

    Yahoo Finance's Dan Howley reports:

    Anthropic (ANTH.PVT) released an amended S-1 investor prospectus on Monday, updating its total revenue and spending through the first nine months of the year, showing increased sales and massive spending numbers.

    The company said revenue grew $1,026% to $33.2 billion through the first nine months of the year that ended Sept. 30.

  • US manufacturing expands in October as inflation pressures persist

    US manufacturing activity expanded in October, though two closely watched surveys released Monday offered differing signals on the sector's momentum as factories grappled with persistent inflation pressures.

    The Institute for Supply Management's manufacturing index rose to 55.5 from 54.5 in September, exceeding economists' expectations of 54.6 and marking the gauge's 10th consecutive month in expansion territory.

    Factory production rose to its highest level since November 2021, with ISM's production index jumping 2.3 points to 59.0. New orders accelerated slightly (55.5 from Sept's 55.3) while employment growth slightly slowed (52.4 from Sept's 52.7).

    Meanwhile, a measure of prices paid by manufacturers eased to 75.2 from 77.9, though it remained elevated as businesses grappled with higher energy prices, tariffs and supply chain bottlenecks.

    A separate survey from S&P Global released Monday also pointed to continued factory expansion, though at a slower pace. Its manufacturing index fell to 54.9 in October from 55.9 in September, below an earlier preliminary reading of 56.0.

  • Hyperscaler debt offerings are back: Oracle and Google join Microsoft in new bond issuance

    While stocks generally rose in early trading, Oracle (ORCL) stock is getting dented after the company announced a new $20 billion capital raise, suggesting that concerns about AI spending continue to churn beneath the surface.

    Oracle plans to sell $10 billion in investment-grade bonds and a similar amount of stock, according to Bloomberg, to fund AI capital spending and dividends.

    It's not the only hyperscaler looking to issue debt at this time to pay for massive AI investments.

    Bloomberg also reported that Alphabet (GOOG) is set to raise 6 billion euros (roughly $6.7 billion) in its second euro bond sale in 2026. The report stated that the Google parent company plans to offer higher yields than its bond offering in May.

    The announcements also come just days after Microsoft (MSFT) announced a $25 billion bond offering of senior unsecured notes. Microsoft said it intended to use the proceeds to fund working capital, capital expenditures, repurchases of capital stock, acquisitions, and repayment of existing debt.

    But the divergence in stock prices highlights the difference in the companies' balance sheets. While Google stock rose on Monday following its announcement, Oracle stock fell 5%, as investors view the company as more leveraged.

  • Binance sees more than $4.6 billion in withdrawals after largest crypto hack in history

    Binance has seen $3.64 billion in outflows since it gradually restored user withdrawals following the largest crypto hack in history, which the FBI linked to North Korea.

    The world's biggest cryptocurrency exchange said that it processed $4.649 billion in withdrawals and approximately $1.01 billion in deposits. Binance detailed that the resulting net outflows represent just 2.1% of its $171 billion in total assets under management.

    Binance was hit late Thursday by a hack that investigators said impacted roughly $6.3 billion in funds, with roughly half of those assets frozen.

    On Monday, the exchange said it has covered approximately 95% of the impacted amount. The outstanding 5% consists of smaller tokens outside of the top 10 cryptocurrencies, whose assets are less liquid and will take longer to source.

  • SpaceX looking to acquire Boost Mobile in pursuit of wireless market: Report

    In pursuit of plans to enter the US wireless market, Elon Musk-led SpaceX (SPCX) is reportedly exploring a potential acquisition of Boost Mobile or access to its network.

    Verizon (VZ), AT&T (T) and T-Mobile (TMUS) have reportedly rejected SpaceX's requests to use their wireless networks, according to a report from the Washington Post.

    SpaceX has since held discussions with EchoStar (ECHO), Boost Mobile's parent company, about a potential partnership or acquisition, the report said. Shares of EchoStar are up roughly 5% in premarket trading.

    A deal could give SpaceX access to Boost Mobile's network agreements, providing an entry point into the estimated $235 billion US wireless market.

    The move would expand Musk's business empire beyond rockets and Starlink, opening another potential revenue stream for SpaceX.

  • Apple has turned into Wall Street's hedge against the AI trade: Chart of the Day

    When AI stocks get hit, investors have found a place to hide. It's the company that has mostly sat out the AI spending race — Apple (AAPL).

    Chip stocks have had plenty of rough days this year, and Apple has mostly shrugged them off. On the 46 days the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (^SOX) fell 2% or more, Apple rose on 30, about two out of every three.

    That's new. From 1994 through last year, Apple rose on only about 1 in 6 of those days, and no year came anywhere close to this one, by Yahoo Finance's calculations.

    Apple and chip stocks have basically stopped moving together. Apple's correlation with the chip index — a measure of how closely two assets move in step — turned negative in July and hit its lowest level on record in September.
