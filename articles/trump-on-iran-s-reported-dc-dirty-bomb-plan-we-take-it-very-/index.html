Trump on Iran's reported DC dirty bomb plan: 'We take it very seriously'

TheTools · 17.11.2026 16:47 · source · permalink

President Donald Trump said Tuesday that if a report about Iran developing plans to target top administration officials with a "dirty bomb" in Washington is true, Iran would face consequences.

"Well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before," Trump said when asked if there is any imminent threat to Washington, home to close to 700,000 people in addition to the seat of government.

"I hope it's not, but we take it very seriously, because they happen to be crazy," Trump said during a photo op with world leaders at the summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations in Manila, Philippines.

"This is why they can't have a nuclear weapon, by the way, in case people had any questions," Trump added, reiterating his main rationale for launching a war with Iran in late February.

Late Monday, CNN cited two U.S. officials in reporting that new U.S. intelligence has intercepted "very specific" plans, including "locations and targets," related to a potential Iranian plot to set off a dirty bomb, which combines conventional explosives with radioactive material.

Officials have debated whether Iran would actually attempt to detonate such a device in the U.S., or even if it could, CNN reported, citing seven U.S. officials in total.

CNBC has not verified the report. Trump administration spokeswoman Lauren Bis told CNN in a statement that the U.S. is "monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland."

Trump was asked about imminent Iranian threats during the first leg of his tour through Asia, which began at ASEAN and is set to include high-stakes meetings with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Asked about the dirty bomb report on CNBC's "Squawk Box" Tuesday morning, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin suggested the Iranian plot was not new and described Tehran as "aspirational" in its threats.

If Iran were to take such an action, "you would cause an all-out war" with the U.S., which would attack at such a level that the Islamic Republic's allies, such as China and Russia, would not come to its aid, Mullin said.

It is a threat, and "we take it very serious," he said. "But the homeland is secure, I can tell you that. But every day we addressing new threats."

CNN reported later Tuesday morning that NSA Net, the classified communications network used by the National Security Agency, is down, citing six people familiar with the matter.
