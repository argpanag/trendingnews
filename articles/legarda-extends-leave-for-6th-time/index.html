Legarda extends leave for 6th time

TheTools · 21.09.2026 16:15 · source · permalink

SEN. Loren Legarda extended her medical leave for the sixth time, keeping her away from the Senate until the second week of October, Senate Secretary Rey Bantug said Monday.

Bantug said Legarda is on leave again from Sept. 21 to 23, Sept. 28 to Oct. 2, and Oct. 5 to 9.

Legarda first filed medical leave from Aug. 3 to 5 and has since repeatedly sought extensions, mostly covering Mondays to Wednesdays, the days set by the Senate impeachment court for the trial of Vice President Sara Duterte.

Senate President Sherwin Gatchalian, Senate Majority Leader Juan Miguel Zubiri and Sen. Panfilo Lacson have separately said Legarda is being treated for a lung problem.

Legarda has not publicly disclosed details of her condition.

The senator reportedly left the country on Aug. 2 only a few days after the Office of the Ombudsman announced that she and her son, Batangas Rep. Leandro Leviste, would be subject to a preliminary investigation over P10-billion “ghost” solar power projects.

Legarda has denied any connection between her health issues and the ongoing investigations by the Ombudsman.

Her spokesman, Tony La Viña, maintained that Legarda and Leviste have not “done anything illegal” amid investigations into the latter’s solar panel company.

“Against that backdrop, the subsequent inclusion of Senator Legarda in an Ombudsman preliminary investigation, and the timing with which that proceeding emerged, inevitably raise questions about whether the scrutiny against her is grounded in evidence of her own acts or is being driven by the wider political issues surrounding her son,” La Viña said in a statement posted on his Facebook page last week.

He also said Legarda “does not own, run, or take part” in Solar Para sa Bayan or any other solar energy companies. There was also no evidence showing her connection with Leviste’s businesses, rather than simply being his mother.

“Saying she’s connected to the business only because she’s the mother of a private businessman is pure invention. Being a parent and being a senator does not in any way prove she benefited, acted improperly, or committed a crime,” he added.

Legarda and Leviste already filed their counter affidavit this week as ordered by the anti-graft court, according to the spokesman.

He added that he will represent Legarda and her son in cases that may be filed against them.

Ombudsman Jesus Crispin Remulla insinuated that the two may not return to the country to evade the issue.

“The Ombudsman’s public pronouncements convey the unmistakable impression that liability has already been assumed, notwithstanding that the matters involving them remain at the preliminary investigation stage,” La Viña said.

The case stemmed from more than 30 service contracts that Leviste obtained from the government in 2013.

The Ombudsman alleged that the number of service contracts “created a virtual monopoly of solar energy projects.”
