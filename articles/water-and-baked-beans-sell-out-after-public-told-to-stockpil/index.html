Water and baked beans sell out after public told to stockpile food

TheTools · 27.10.2026 14:15 · source · permalink

Sales of emergency rations surge after the Defence Secretary urged country to prepare for cyber attacks

Baked beans, bottled water and batteries are selling out at supermarkets after the Government warned households to stockpile survival packs in case of cyber attacks.

Shoppers were unable to buy several brands of bottled water and other durable essentials from Ocado, the online grocer, on Monday.

It comes after Wes Streeting, the Defence Secretary, urged Britons on Thursday to prepare “be ready” emergency kits that include three days’ worth of non-perishable food and bottled water.

In the days since, essential items have sold out. Product lines from Volvic, Highland Spring and Brecon Carreg were listed as unavailable, while various Heinz and Baxters soup ranges were also out of stock.

An Ocado spokesman told The Times: “We’ve seen an increase in demand for some products over the weekend and are working with our supply-chain partners to increase availability as soon as possible.

“We’d encourage customers looking for a specific product to keep checking back as stock is replenished daily or arrange a delivery for later in the week.”

Iceland, the frozen-foods seller, added it was “clear consumers have listened to the Government’s message as we have seen a 40 per cent increase in sales of tinned products over the weekend and strong sales of bottled water too. Our supplies of these products remain healthy”.

Booths, the supermarket chain based in the north of England, said it had seen “some year-on-year sales increases” among categories such as tinned goods, bottled water and batteries.

However, they said those upturns may also be related to the Austen and Boelo storms.

Mr Streeting’s advice was part of his launch of a “national resilience conversation” that asked the public to get ready for disruption caused by natural disasters or cyber attacks by hostile states.

It was issued as websites across the world were shut down after the discovery of a vulnerability in PHP, a crucial piece of website architecture that left the Government susceptible to cyber attack.

Mr Streeting’s warning was also preceded by a Telegraph investigation that revealed Russian-deployed fishing trawlers had been spying on the UK’s undersea cable network, which connects Britain to the outside world.

As well as durable food and bottled water, he also told people to keep an FM radio and blankets.

Just two days after Mr Streeting’s warning, retailers reported that their wind-up radio stocks were selling out after a fivefold surge in orders.

Maplin, an online electronics retailer, said torch sales had risen by 284 per cent between Thursday and Saturday compared with the same period the previous month, while its £18.99 Ionos Almere wind-up radio sold out on Friday.

On Amazon’s best-sellers page, bottled water and kitchen roll made up three of the four best-selling grocery products on Monday evening.

The term “survival pack” has ballooned in Google search results since Mr Streeting’s speech, with Google Trends reporting a more than 5,000 per cent increase in October compared with the same period last year.
