Cher's 1973 Hit 'Dark Lady': The Grisly Story Behind Her Halloween Classic

Cher took center stage in her 1973 pop song, which topped the charts with its grim tale and has captivated generations of fans during Halloween season.

"Dark Lady" became one of the singer's biggest hits of the early '70s, landing her at No.1 on the Billboard Hot 100 chart and No.3 on the Adult Contemporary chart. It was the lead single of her eleventh album of the same name that became memorable for a reason.

Cher uses her astounding vocals to tell a grisly story of deception, heartache, and murder. "Dark Lady" has Cher singing as its lead narrator about a gypsy fortune teller in New Orleans who was known for sleeping with her clients. The song's narrator follows the fortune teller to her lair and pays to have her fortune told.

She soon learns that her lover has been cheating with someone close to her. The fortune teller tells the narrator to leave, never return, and forget her entirely. Shaken by what occurred, the narrator realizes she smells the same perfume the fortune teller wore in her very room.

Enraged, she sneaks back into the fortune teller's lair to find her and the narrator's lover together. "Dark Lady" takes a dramatic turn when the narrator shoots them both dead.

The Ventures' keyboardist, Johnny Durrill, was the one who wrote and composed the track alongside Snuff Garrett, as its producer. According to SongFacts, Cher wasn't too pleased with her vocals, but they all knew "Dark Lady" would be an instant success.

"Everybody knew it was a hit the minute they heard Cher's vocal on the playback, though she didn't particularly like it," he explained.

When it came to the track's inspiration, Durrill has never fully explained where he got the story from or what inspired him. He was touring with the Ventures at the time and Garrett had given him a helpful idea on how to end the song's storyline.

"I spent a week in his [Snuff Garrett's] office playing him songs, one of which Cher recorded. Later, when I was on tour in Japan with the Ventures, I was writing an interesting song. I telegraphed the unfinished lyrics to Garrett. He said to 'make sure the bitch kills him.' Hence, in the song both the lover and fortune teller were killed," he explained.

Cher once told Vox in 1991 that the song was a "pain in the a*s" to record "because there was no place to take a breath" due to it having so many words. The song got two music videos, one of which aired in the third season of The Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour, and the other was an animated cartoon by John David Wilson. The animated version portrays the song's entire story.
