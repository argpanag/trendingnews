(WJW) -- Don't let your trick or treat night cause a fright; plan to keep children safe this holiday by preparing ahead.

Mercy Health said parents shouldn't take the fun out of Halloween in order to keep their children safe. Here are some tips to keep them safe and healthy.

Stay visible

Before little ones walk through neighborhoods or cross intersections on the night of trick or treat, parents can add reflective materials to a child's costume so they can be spotted easily. Children can also carry glow sticks or a flashlight as the sun goes down.

"Halloween is an exciting time for children and families, but it is also a night when kids may be distracted, visibility can be limited and familiar safety rules can be easy to forget," said Melia Strodtbeck, a physician assistant with Mercy Health, is quoted in a news release.

Check candy

Before children dive into their pillowcases, be sure to review candy labels to be sure it won't cause an allergic reaction. Mercy Health recommends supervising young children around potential choking hazards.

Choose costumes carefully

To make sure children have a safe and memorable trick or treat, make sure their costumes fit properly, allow them to see and walk without tripping hazards.

Carve pumpkins with caution

As you prepare to decorate your porch, it's advised that adults be the only ones handling sharp tools when children carve pumpkins. To reduce burns, Mercy Health suggests using battery-operated lights instead of candles.

Stay home when sick

If you or your child are feeling unwell, stay home to rest to prevent spreading illnesses to other children and their families.

During this time of year, the seasonal cold or flu can spread easily. Mercy Health said cold viruses can spread through the air, direct contact or surface contact.

Families should seek medical attention for serious or potentially life-threatening symptoms like difficulty breathing, severe allergic reactions or major injuries. In an emergency, call 911.
