'Disgusting': Reform Scotland leader snubs Holyrood debate on violence against women

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:34 · source · permalink

THE SNP have hit out at Reform Scotland leader Malcolm Offord for snubbing a Holyrood debate on tackling violence against women and girls, despite being a "self-proclaimed defender of women".

Leaders from every party in the Scottish Parliament spoke in Wednesday's debate on tackling violence against women and girls (VAWG), yet Offord was notably absent.

Reform Scotland has frequently framed anti-migrant rhetoric through the lens of protecting women and girls, with party figures often presenting themselves as advocates for women's safety.

Just three of Reform's 16 MSPs were present in the chamber for the entirety of the debate – Amanda Lindsay, Senga Beresford and David Smith.

Reform Scotland's deputy leader Thomas Kerr was seen leaving the chamber at the beginning of the debate and was absent for more than an hour.

Lindsay spoke on behalf of her party in her capacity as Reform Scotland's spokesperson for women and equalities.

SNP MSP Alex Kerr, who is also the party's national secretary, intervened during Lindsay's speech to say: "I don't disagree with a lot of the thrust here.

"But I know that every single party leader is in the room other than Malcolm Offord and your deputy leader Thomas Kerr, self-proclaimed defenders of women."

"I find it really disgusting that they've not even shown up for this debate on violence against women and girls," he added.

Lindsay responded: "Thank you for that intervention, but I have the portfolio for women and equalities and that's why I'm speaking to this."

The debate, lodged as a motion by First Minister John Swinney, coincided with the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women and marks the start of the UN’s 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence campaign.

The First Minister's motion paid tribute to the UN-led campaign and recognised that "tackling this violence and abuse requires commitment and collaborative action across society".

Swinney also announced nearly £10 million of additional funding to support frontline and prevention services for victims and survivors of gender-based violence and abuse.

“Overwhelmingly, it is men who perpetrate this violence," Swinney told MSPs.

“It is men whose behaviour and attitudes need to change, and it is men who, when they see those behaviours and attitudes in others, need to act.”

During Reform's contribution to the debate, the party's MSPs repeatedly linked VAWG to immigration and asylum policy.

Lindsay went on to state that "Glasgow has more supported asylum seekers than in any other UK local authority", saying that "constituents are entitled to ask whether the impact of these policies has been properly assessed".

Meanwhile, Smith referenced a specific case involving a man from Afghanistan.

He faced an intervention from SNP MSP Clare Haughey, who challenged him to state "how many white Scottish, white British men were arrested for sexual assault" during the period he was referring to, receiving applause from the chamber.

Smith responded: "I do not disagree with what she's saying, however we're saying the things that aren't being said."

During the debate, several party leaders pointed towards the case of Russell Brand, who was jailed for 10 years and six months earlier this month after being convicted of rape and sexual assault.

The First Minister said that VAWG is a “public health problem” as he set out multiple initiatives the Scottish Government is undertaking as part of its Equally Safe Delivery Plan, which was published on Wednesday.

This includes public campaigns to tackle misogyny and domestic abuse, along with £1m investment in the next year to provide schoolchildren with access to age-appropriate learning on VAWG and misogyny.

The debate comes after hundreds of people marched through Glasgow city centre on Tuesday evening for the “Fight for the Night” rally, which called for an end to gender-based violence.

The Scottish Government is also committed to tackling online misogyny, Swinney said, adding: “Increasingly, we are seeing both boys and girls being negatively impacted and targeted by toxic content and bad faith actors online.

“Those who direct outrage and vitriol at women and girls, both online and in life, with very real and dangerous consequences, and those who frame the problems of men not as a result of societal challenges but as a result of women.”

All the amendments to the First Minister’s motion were moved by male leaders of opposition parties, with the exception of the Greens and Reform who did not submit an amendment.

Scottish Labour leader Michael Marra called for more funding for vital services for women and girls, while Scottish Tory leader Russell Findlay said the justice system had "routinely failed" survivors of abuse.

At the end of the debate, Thomas Kerr raised a point of order to accuse Alex Kerr of "inadvertently misleading Parliament", saying that he "briefly left" the chamber to attend an event hosted by the Scottish Police Federation.

The Scottish Parliament TV website shows the Reform Scotland deputy leader was absent for more than 60 minutes of the debate.

The motion passed as amended by 107 votes in favour to 11 against.
