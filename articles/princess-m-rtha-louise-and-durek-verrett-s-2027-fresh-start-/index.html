Princess Märtha Louise and Durek Verrett's 2027 fresh start after turbulent year

TheTools · 22.11.2026 12:00 · source · permalink

Princess Märtha Louise of Norway and her husband Durek Verrett will be looking forward to next year after a difficult time

Princess Märtha Louise of Norway, 55, and her husband Durek Verrett, 52, have had a difficult few years. 

Durek underwent a kidney transplant shortly before the passing of King Harald V earlier this year. Plus, they have been battling to move into a property together since 2023. The couple are hopeful that 2027 will bring happy news and they'll finally be able to move into their dream home. 

The residence they purchased together in Stabekk, Bærum (just outside Oslo) in 2023 has been plagued by a string of issues, causing significant delays to their move in date. 

The property cost an estimated £1.4 million but has been embroiled in issues after structural faults were uncovered and Martha and Durek started a dispute with the sellers.

While these issues were ironed out by 2025, the royal couple's planned renovations also held up the process as the local authorities put the works on hold. 

In early 2026, Martha revealed that further faults have required insurance claims delaying the process once more. Now, 2027 is hopefully the year that they will get the green light to move in. 

Princess Märtha Louise of Norway's previous home 

Princess Märtha Louise of Norway's former home with late ex-husband, Ari Behn, and their three daughters, Maud Angelica, Leah Isadora, and Emma Tallulah, was sold in 2025. It was initially listed for £1.5 million and later relisted at £1.4 million before selling. 

The jaw-dropping residence consists of four bedrooms and is surrounded by 5.7 acres of private land, along with beautiful forest views. 

Durek's health struggles 

Earlier this year, Durek underwent a successful kidney transplant, a year after revealing he required dialysis several times a week "just to stay alive". 

At the time, they released a joint statement which read: "First and foremost, we feel an enormous sense of gratitude toward the person who was willing to become a donor. It is difficult to put into words what such a gift means.

"The new kidney has given Durek a second chance, and that is something we will never take for granted."

Durek called the medical staff that cared for him during his recovery "little angels" in a moving message. "The way the doctors and nurses have cared for me, both as a patient and as a person, has deeply touched me. They have been my 'little angels' through a very challenging time."
