How can Ohio State football beat Texas? 3 keys for Buckeyes' massive Week 2 showdown

TheTools · 11.09.2026 14:54 · source · permalink

Here are three keys for No. 1 Ohio State at No. 3 Texas on Sept. 12:

Find support for Jeremiah Smith

It’s no secret Texas has so far contained Smith, limiting him to seven catches for 46 yards across two games. But it didn’t keep the Buckeyes from prevailing in either last season’s opener or their College Football Playoff semifinal in 2024. Their supporting cast made the Longhorns pay for devoting so much attention to Smith, especially Carnell Tate, who combined for nine catches for 136 yards and one touchdown in the wins. Tate's departure to the NFL makes UTSA transfer Devin McCuin the most likely complement to Smith on the perimeter. While the Buckeyes would benefit from getting Smith going against Texas, they’ll still need help from others, and McCuin, who had six catches for 97 yards and one touchdown in the Buckeyes' season-opening win over Ball State, can provide another reliable weapon.

Win in the red zone

The X-factor in the last two meetings came inside Ohio State’s 20-yard line. The Buckeyes dug in near their end zone, holding the Longhorns to just one touchdown in four red-zone trips. Texas came up empty three times, most memorably on Jack Sawyer’s scoop-and-score that sealed Ohio State’s semifinal win. But the Buckeyes also produced a goal-line stand last year and broke up a pass in the end zone on fourth down. Texas’ struggles aren’t limited to matchups with Ohio State. It has not finished higher than 61st in the Football Bowl Subdivision in red-zone scoring since 2022, while the Buckeyes have had one of the nation’s best red-zone defenses in recent years. If the Buckeyes find their new pieces equally stout near the goal line, it could again make the difference.

Account for aggression

When Texas hired Will Muschamp to replace Pete Kwiatkowski as defensive coordinator, it pointed to the potential for a more aggressive style of defense. The Longhorns blitzed Texas State quarterback Brad Jackson on 44% of his dropbacks in Week 1, according to Pro Football Focus. The pressure generated three takeaways and two sacks. The Longhorns finished with a havoc rate of 20%. The prospect of pressure makes it all the more important for the Buckeyes to tighten up their pass protection, which surrendered two sacks against Ball State, and for quarterback Julian Sayin be decisive from the pocket. If Texas brings the heat, there will be opportunities for Ohio State to exploit the aggression for big plays.

Key matchup

Ohio State pass rush vs. Texas pass protection

The Buckeyes rattled Arch Manning when they spoiled his debut last season, using a mix of blitz packages and disguised fronts to make him uncomfortable in the pocket. It was the first of a series of protection issues that would plague the Longhorns throughout the year, as Manning was under pressure on 35.6% of his dropbacks, per PFF. Only three Power 4 quarterbacks were pressured more times in 2025. Texas upgraded its starting offensive line during the offseason as a result, adding Wake Forest transfer Melvin Siani at right tackle and Oregon State transfer Dylan Sikorski at left guard, and the moves paid dividends in Week 1. Manning was under duress on just 13.3% of his dropbacks against Texas State. But if defensive coordinator Matt Patricia is again creative with the pass rush, the Buckeyes could overwhelm the Longhorns up front, tilting things in their favor.

Key stat

21: The combined margin of Ohio State’s recent wins over Texas, winning 14-7 last season and 28-14 in the College Football Playoff semifinals in 2024.
