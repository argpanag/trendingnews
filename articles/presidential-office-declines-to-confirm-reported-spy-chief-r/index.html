Presidential Office Declines to Confirm Reported Spy Chief Replacement

TheTools · 23.11.2026 01:25 · source · permalink

Office Gives Standard Response to Report That Former Justice Minister Kang Kum-sil Is Front-Runner

The presidential office said on the 23rd that it could not confirm reports that National Intelligence Service Director Lee Jong-seok would be replaced, saying "we cannot confirm matters related to personnel."

Presidential spokesperson Kim Sung-wan gave that response at a briefing at the Chunchugwan press center when asked whether the office was actually considering replacing Lee. Earlier in the day, the presidential office had also said in a notice to the media that "it is difficult to confirm matters related to personnel." The office maintained a general position without explicitly denying the reported replacement.

One news outlet reported the same day that President Lee Jae-myung was considering replacing the intelligence chief, whom he appointed early in his term, after one year and five months, and that former Justice Minister Kang Kum-sil was the leading candidate to succeed him. A prolonged period of friction between South Korean and U.S. intelligence authorities and the lack of a clear breakthrough in improving inter-Korean relations were cited as factors behind the deliberations.

Kang, named as a possible successor, served as the first justice minister of the Roh Moo-hyun administration and was the first woman to hold the post in the country's constitutional history. She led a special presidential delegation to France shortly after last year's presidential election and has served as global climate and environment ambassador since early this year. If appointed to lead the National Intelligence Service, she would become the first woman to head the country's intelligence agency in the 65 years since the Korea Central Intelligence Agency was founded in 1961.

Original reporting by Song Jong-ho for Seoul Economic Daily.
