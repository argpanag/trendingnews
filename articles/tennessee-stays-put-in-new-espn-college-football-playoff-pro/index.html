Tennessee stays put in new ESPN College Football Playoff projection after beating LSU

TheTools · 22.11.2026 06:41 · source · permalink

Volquest Football

Tennessee’s 27-13 win over LSU on Saturday at Neyland Stadium didn’t help the Vols move up in ESPN’s new College Football Playoff projection. ESPN’s Heather Dinich had Tennessee at No. 8 last week, after the 22-7 win at Texas A&M, and at No. 8 again this week.

ESPN has Georgia, Utah, Texas and Alabama as its top four teams, respectively, while Notre Dame is No. 5, Miami is No. 6 and UCLA is No. 7.

The new ESPN projection would have No. 9 Wake Forest playing at Tennessee in the first round, with the winner advancing to face Georgia. The other first-round matchups would be No. 12 Boise State at No. 5 Notre Dame, No. 11 Indiana at No. 6 Miami and No. 10 BYU at No. 7 UCLA.

Last week ESPN projected Tennessee as the No. 8 seed, hosting No. 9 Indiana in the first round.

“The committee has made it clear it thinks two-loss Alabama is better than two-loss Tennessee in spite of the Oct. 17 head-to-head result,” Dinich wrote, “and it seems unlikely to change given Saturday’s results. There will likely continue to be enough teams ranked ahead of Tennessee that the separation between the Vols and Bama continues to exist during the voting process.

“The Vols have won three straight since their Oct. 24 loss at South Carolina, and beat unranked LSU on Saturday in convincing fashion.”

If the Vols are ranked lower than No. 8, Dinich cited Tennessee’s three turnovers against LSU, the Tigers being penalized 16 times for 98 yards and the questions at quarterback with Faizon Brandon and George MacIntyre splitting reps on Saturday.

“The Vols were 4-of-17 on third downs and had 308 yards of total offense,” Dinich added. “Beating an unranked, five-loss LSU team doesn’t carry much weight, though the committee does reward teams for wins against opponents .500 or better.”

On Tuesday night, Tennessee dropped one spot from No. 8 to No. 9, after being ranked No. 13 in the first CFP Top 25 of the season on Nov. 3. Indiana jumped the Vols, going from No. 9 to No. 8 after a 27-13 win over USC.

Indiana lost 27-24 in overtime at Washington on Saturday and UCLA, which was ranked No. 4 last week, lost 18-16 in overtime at Michigan.

Tennessee (9-2, 6-2 SEC) could punch its ticket to the College Football Playoff with a win at Vanderbilt (4-7, 1-7) on Saturday (7 p.m. Eastern Time, ESPN).

Josh Heupel makes Tennessee’s case for the College Football Playoff

That’s where Josh Heupel started when asked Saturday night what he would want the College Football Playoff selection committee to know about his team.

“All we’ve got to do (is) our job, coaches and players, go win,” Heupel said. “That’s it. Exclamation point. It’s really hard to do that inside of this league. Vandy’s a good football team … we’ll have to be at our best.”

Tennessee beat LSU by doing what it does best this season: Running the football and playing suffocating defense.

Quarterback Faizon Brandon ran 12 times for 112 yards, including touchdowns of 18 and 55 yards in the fourth quarter, and the Vols finished with 203 yards on 43 attempts as a team.

Tennessee’s defense held LSU to 265 total yards, forced three turnovers and had eight sacks, 13 tackles for loss and six quarterback hits. The lone touchdown for the Tigers came after the Vols muffed and lost a punt inside the 10-yard line.

“As people assess and look at this football team,” Heupel said, “it’s a different style than maybe I’ve played historically as a head coach. I would think some of the old-time coaches would have a great appreciation — they’re on that committee — the style of play, the physicality that we play.

“We play field position a little bit more than I have historically and play all three phases of the game together and play it pretty good.”

Tennessee’s history in the College Football Playoff rankings

Before this season, Tennessee had been ranked 28 times in the College Football Playoff Top 25 dating back to 2015. The highest the Vols have been ranked is No. 1 (2022) and the lowest is No. 25 (2015, 2025).

Last season Tennessee started at No. 25 in the first CFP Top 25 and moved up to No. 19 in the final update.

The Vols started at No. 7 in 2024, dropped to No. 11 after a November loss at Georgia and finished back at No. 7. They were there the No. 9 seed when the bracket was announced and lost at No. 8 Ohio State in the first round, with the Buckeyes starting their run to the national championship.

Tennessee started at No. 17 and finished at No. 21 in 2023 and started at No. 1 and finished at No. 6 in 2022, when it was four-team playoff. The Vols started at No. 19 in 2016 and finished at No. 21 and started at No. 25 in 2015 and finished at No. 23.
