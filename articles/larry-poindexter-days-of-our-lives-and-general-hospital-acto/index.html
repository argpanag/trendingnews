Poindexter had two stints on Days of Our Lives among his dozens of TV credits.

Larry Poindexter, the character actor best known for stints on soap operas like Days of Our Lives and General Hospital, has died. He was 66.

Deadline reported that Poindexter died on Wednesday, days after being struck by a car while crossing the street in his Studio City neighborhood. The actor was reportedly at the hospital and scheduled to undergo surgery for broken ribs when he died suddenly.

Entertainment Weekly has reached out to representatives for Poindexter for more information.

Poindexter was born in Dallas in 1959 to H.R. Poindexter, a Tony Award-winning light and set designer, and Sue Ann Erdman, an opera singer. He made his stage debut in Dallas Summer Musicals, where his parents first met, and received his first TV credit for a 1982 episode of A New Day in Eden. His first screen acting project, the TV movie Thursday's Child, premiered a year later and starred Rob Lowe, Gena Rowlands, and Jessica Walter.

Poindexter said that he landed the role by accident after interrupting a casting session for the TV movie while dropping off paperwork for the producer he worked for at the time.

"I had just walked out, and the door flies open and she strides up to me and asks; 'Are you an actor?" he recalled on his website. "I say; 'Yes.' 2 weeks later I auditioned for a part in the movie I was a production assistant on - and got it!"

Poindexter's first soap stint came when he played Ben Welch on eight episodes of Days of Our Lives in 1984. The actor appeared on individual episodes of shows like Happy Days, CHiPs, Silver Spoons, and Growing Pains throughout the 1980s before his 23-episode stint as Justin Moore on Santa Barbara in 1986.

He went on to appear on seven episodes of Duet and a pair of Quantum Leap episodes in the late '80s, and also acted in movies like Number One With a Bullet, Blue Movies, and Invaders From Mars.

In the '90s, Poindexter appeared in all 22 episodes of Fox's Too Something, also known as New York Daze, and played Dalton Lowne on seven episodes of JAG. He also acted in episodes of Friends, Lois & Clark: The New Adventures of Superman, ALF, Party of Five, and Murphy Brown.

Poindexter played Dr. Asher Thomas on 12 episodes of General Hospital in 2005. He acted on several prominent series throughout the 2000s including CSI, Will & Grace, Nip/Tuck, Weeds, and Bones. He also acted opposite Samuel L. Jackson and Colin Farrell in 2003's S.W.A.T., and played small supporting role in 17 Again.

The actor appeared on almost every episode of MTV's The Hard Times of RJ Berger, which ran from 2010 to 2011, and returned to Days of Our Lives to play a different character, the priest Father Louis. He also performed in episodes of The Mentalist, Scandal, Castle, Hawaii Five-0, K.C. Undercover, and NCIS.

In the 2020s, Poindexter appeared on three eps of Station 19 and guest starred on Curb Your Enthusiasm, 9-1-1, The Lincoln Lawyer, and The Rookie.

Poindexter also acted and produced for the stage throughout his career, performing in productions of Fatherland, In the Valley of the Shadow, Boys in the Band, The Night of the Iguana, and Tender Is the Night. He produced and developed Broadway's The Cher Show, which won two Tonys in 2019.

In addition to acting and producing, Poindexter sang lead in an alt-country band called The High Lonesome, which released the album Feel Free To Do So in 1995.

Poindexter is survived by his wife, casting director Carol Kritzer, and their twin sons.
