USDA Specialty Crops Ad Activity Falls 3% Weekly - News and Statistics - IndexBox

TheTools · 12.09.2026 04:26 · source · permalink

The USDA Agricultural Marketing Service reported that advertised specialty crop activity at major grocery store outlets declined in the week ending during the period from September 5 to September 17, 2026, according to the agency's weekly specialty-crops retail feature activity report. The report, published by USDA AMS, reflects prices advertised by major grocery retailers to consumers through circulars and digital promotions and gathered through a weekly survey.

Total ad numbers for the week were 317,902, a 3 percent decrease from the prior week's total of 326,494. The comparable week a year earlier was 8 percent lower at 293,624.

Commodity Group Breakdown

Fruit accounted for 178,581 ads, or 56 percent of the total. Vegetables represented 107,662 ads, or 34 percent. Onions and potatoes totaled 22,014 ads, or 7 percent. Herbs accounted for 1,415 ads, ornamentals 1,412, hemp 3,211, and honey 5,022.

Organic produce ads numbered 39,804, equal to 13 percent of all ads.

Fruit Price Movements

Among major advertised items with at least 3,000 ads, significant fruit price increases compared with the same week last year included white seedless grapes in a 2-pound container at 51 percent, honeydews sold each at 23 percent, Bartlett pears per pound at 23 percent, and cantaloupes sold each at 11 percent.

Significant fruit price decreases included Navel oranges in a 3-pound bag at 37 percent, blackberries in a 5.6 to 6 ounce package at 15 percent, and avocados sold each at 10 percent.

Potatoes, Onions and Vegetables

The only significant change for potatoes and onions was a 16 percent decrease for Russet potatoes in a 5-pound bag. The report noted no significant price increases or decreases for vegetables during the week.

Seasonal Promotional Activity

The report described an eventful week at grocery stores, with multiple produce promotions. National Hispanic Heritage Month was recognized in several flyers, featuring sales on tomatillos, onions, Roma tomatoes, cilantro, jalapeno peppers, plantains, cactus pads, mangos, papayas, and sweet corn. Pumpkins made a prominent entrance, joining displays of hard squashes, mums, and apple cider as the season moved toward autumn. Stores also offered deals on candy, costumes, and decor ahead of Halloween.
