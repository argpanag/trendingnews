India’s road to the World Test Championship final has become tougher after South Africa beat Australia by 176 runs in Cape Town and sealed the series 2-1. The outcome has also shaken up the table and left Shubman Gill’s side with very little room for mistakes across their last seven Tests.

India are fourth on 68 points with a points percentage of 51.52 after 11 matches. They still have two away Tests against New Zealand and a five-match home series against Australia. With only the top two making the final, India need a near-perfect finish.

The New Zealand tour could be decisive. India Today reported that if India win both matches there, their PCT will rise to 58.97. If they lose both, it would fall to 43.59, according to the same report.

Even a 5-0 home series win over Australia after those defeats would take India only to 59.26 per cent, leaving them dependent on results elsewhere.

How Bad Is It?

The equation is simple. India must keep winning and hope other results go their way.

If India win both matches against New Zealand, their PCT will rise to 58.97. Losing both Tests would bring that down to 43.59, leaving them with a huge task in the last five matches against Australia.

A clean sweep in Australia would still not be enough if India lose both Tests in New Zealand. Even then, five straight wins at home would lift them only to 59.26 per cent, which could leave their fate tied to other results. Seven wins from seven would take India to 70.37 per cent and put them in a strong position for the final. Six wins would still give them 64.81 per cent, while two or more defeats would make the route far harder.

What Changed?

Cape Town altered the picture. South Africa’s 176-run win over Australia in the third Test reshuffled the WTC standings, and Temba Bavuma’s side moved into second place after clinching the series 2-1. Their PCT went to 71.42 after the victory.

Australia fell from first to third after their PCT slid from 75 to 69.23. New Zealand now sit on top with 72.22 per cent and 52 points from six Tests. India moved up to fourth after Bangladesh lost to West Indies in Mirpur and dropped to fifth. South Africa, New Zealand and Australia now fill the top three spots, so Gill and Co. must win their own matches and wait for rivals to lose points in the rest of the cycle.
