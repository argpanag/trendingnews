Ukraine war briefing: Russian attacks on Kyiv bridges cause traffic chaos

TheTools · 03.10.2026 01:52 · source · permalink

Bridge across Dnipro River hit a day after another linking two sides of capital struck, forcing traffic suspensions. What we know on day 1,683

  • A bridge across ⁠the Dnipro River in Kyiv was hit in a Russian air attack ⁠on Saturday, the mayor said, amid an escalation in strikes on infrastructure in the Ukrainian capital. Emergency services ⁠were deployed to the scene, Vitali ⁠Klitschko said, and traffic across the Northern Bridge from ⁠the east to the west bank was suspended ​after damage to the ‌road surface ‌and the overhead trolleybus cables.   • The strike came a day after Russia hit a different bridge linking two sides of Kyiv for the second successive day, officials said on Friday. An ⁠evening drone strike on the ​closed Southern Bridge was among a series of strikes since Thursday that forced the full or partial suspension of traffic across it and two other bridges spanning the Dnipro. Long traffic jams snaked through the city in the morning and evening. Before ⁠the Friday attack Klitschko held a city security council meeting, describing the situation in Kyiv as “very dramatic”.

  • The strikes on the bridges and other sensitive infrastructure appeared to be linked to an order Vladimir Putin made in September to scrap constraints on Russia’s air campaign, a senior Ukrainian official said. About 500,000 people commuted from the Dnipro’s left bank to the right bank daily, an economic analyst said. Earlier on Friday, one person was killed and two injured in a Russian drone strike that caused a fire on the ‌upper floors of an apartment building, officials said. In the north-eastern city of Kharkiv, Ukraine’s second-biggest, two people were killed and 32 wounded in a guided bomb strike, the regional governor said.

  • Kyiv is entering its most difficult period since the early days of the invasion, as intensifying Russian attacks reshape everyday life in a capital that Moscow is seeking to plunge into darkness. For weeks, the main worry in the Ukrainian capital had been the coming winter, Pjotr Sauer reports. But a sharp escalation in Russian attacks has left many residents feeling that some of the hardship they were bracing for is already here.
