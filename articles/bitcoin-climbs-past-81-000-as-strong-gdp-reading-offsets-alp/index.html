Bitcoin Climbs Past $81,000 as Strong GDP Reading Offsets Alphabet-Led Tech Rout — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 29.10.2026 16:55 · source · permalink

Bitcoin pushed back above $81,000 on Thursday, shaking off a sharp selloff in technology shares that dragged the Nasdaq Composite lower, after U.S. growth and inflation data came in at or above forecasts.

The largest cryptocurrency fell as low as $79,607 following Wednesday's close before rallying to $81,547 on Bitstamp, according to TradingView data. The move higher coincided with the release of the September Personal Consumption Expenditures index and the final estimate of third-quarter gross domestic product.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis reported that the PCE price index, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge, rose 0.5% from August and 3.6% from a year earlier. Core PCE, which strips out food and energy, climbed 3.1% year over year. Third-quarter GDP expanded at an annualized 3.4%, topping the 3.2% consensus forecast.

Chris Rupkey, chief economist at Fwdbonds, said in comments to CNBC that growth was "powering ahead despite the uncertainty of geopolitical risks and the damage posed to many sectors of the economy by higher energy costs." He added that real GDP has climbed back above a 3% run rate for the first time in a year.

The macro backdrop did little for equities. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.64% to 26,117.47 in early trading, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.51% to 7,704.55 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 0.31% to 52,311.92. Alphabet led the decline, falling roughly 13% and threatening to erase more than $500 billion in market value.

The Google parent reported quarterly revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25%, with Google Cloud sales more than doubling to $30.7 billion. But advertising results missed expectations and capital expenditures reached $51.4 billion, contributing to negative free cash flow of about $9.1 billion. The company kept its full-year capital spending outlook at $195 billion to $205 billion.

Philipp Schindler, Google's chief business officer, said the rapid adoption of AI search features, which monetize at lower rates than traditional search ads, weighed on search advertising results.

The losses spread across software. ServiceNow tumbled about 23% and Fortinet fell nearly 22%, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 1.8%, ending a four-session rally. Microsoft slipped about 0.5% even after reporting solid results and Azure growth, as the company prepared its first U.S. bond sale since 2017, aiming to raise at least $25 billion.

Note: Bitcoin figure reflects the intraday high on Bitstamp; equity moves are as of late morning Eastern Time.

The Federal Open Market Committee held its benchmark rate in the 3.75%-4% range on Wednesday, as markets had expected. Chair Kevin Warsh said economic activity was "expanding at a strong pace" while describing inflation as "elevated."

"While uncertainty remains elevated owing, in part, to geopolitical developments, domestic spending has been resilient," Warsh said in a statement. "Productivity growth is strong, and capital investment is robust. Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little."

The combination of resilient growth and sticky inflation reinforced expectations that policymakers will keep policy restrictive, a headwind for companies that depend on cheap financing for long-dated investments.

On the technical side, crypto analyst Rekt Capital flagged $81,400 as the level bitcoin needs to reclaim by the weekly close. He noted the price was oscillating around weekly levels near $80,400 and $81,400 and expected that choppiness to persist into the close. October is on track to deliver a 3.3% decline for the token.

Separately, Steve Eisman, the investor made famous by his bet against the U.S. housing market before the 2008 financial crisis, told CNBC's "Squawk Box" that he has cut portfolio risk substantially and built hedges against declines in several holdings, though he stopped short of taking outright short positions.

Eisman questioned the profitability and capital efficiency of companies developing frontier AI models, citing what he said were gaps in Anthropic's IPO filing and a ratio of revenue to computing infrastructure costs that fell from 2.08 in the second quarter to 1.7 in the third. He said AI investment is approaching an inflection point but that timing a downturn is difficult because the spending is woven through the broader market and economy.

For crypto traders, the session reinforced bitcoin's recent tendency to trade on macro data rather than tech earnings, even as the 50-day simple moving average near $81,831 and the $81,400 weekly level remain caps. A close below $80,565 on the four-hour chart would lower the odds of a retest of that resistance, according to Keith Alan, co-founder of Material Indicators.
