Scathing report into FENZ recommends reviewing board and truck procurement

TheTools · 17.09.2026 07:26 · source · permalink

A scathing select committee report into Fire and Emergency is recommending the government looks at changing the organisation's board and whether it should be allowed to buy its own fire trucks.

National MP Tim Costley, who is the deputy chair of the Governance and Administration committee, said the report laid bare years of failings within FENZ.

FENZ had bought 30 new trucks in 2019 which still were not being used, Costley said.

"The evidence shows they haven't been able to deliver. They've consistently had delays, they've consistently had issues with trucks and it looks like they have a culture of changing the design halfway through the process regularly."

Ladder trucks FENZ bought had also suffered significant delays, he said.

"Despite a final inspection in July 2025, they are still sitting with original manufacturers, not yet delivered to FENZ and far from being put into operational use."

The report recommended the government "consider whether FENZ is best placed to conduct its own procurement, or if further support is required".

Costley said the report also raised issues with the governance of the organisation.

National MP Tim Costley

RNZ / Samuel Rillstone

"The board could not produce any evidence of asking about these new fire engines between December 2023 and the inquiry. Really concerning... not a lot of confidence in the board right now."

He said bringing in new board chair Raveen Jaduram in June had been the right move, and he had been "far more transparent".

Other recommendations in the report included "that the government direct FENZ to shift its focus from generating corporate documents to efforts that will deliver tangible improvements at frontline stations".

FENZ responds

Fire and Emergency chair Raveen Jaduram said he acknowledged the significant shortcomings identified in the report, and was committed to transparent action in response.

"The report identifies unacceptable delays and weaknesses in setting procurement requirements, contract management and governance oversight.

"Firefighters and communities are entitled to expect better."

Jaduram said since coming into the role in June, he had taken several steps to improve things.

"Fire and Emergency is introducing several initiatives to strengthen fleet procurement, reporting and management practices including clearer accountability, stronger oversight of delivery and risk."
