LIVE UPDATES: 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila

TheTools · 14.11.2026 16:30 · source · permalink

WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

The global energy crisis, food security, and transnational crime are among the top issues on the table as Southeast Asian leaders and their allies converge in Manila.

The Philippines, chair of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations for 2026, is hosting the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits from Sunday, November 15, to Tuesday, November 17.

Aside from leaders and representatives of ASEAN’s 11 members, other world leaders will be in attendance, including United States President Donald Trump, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, and newly reelected Brazil President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who is embarking on a state visit to the Philippines before the summit.

During the summit’s closing ceremony, the Philippines will also turn over the ASEAN chairmanship to Singapore, which will lead the bloc in 2027.

How did Malaysia, Singapore react to haze issue?

Southeast Asian foreign ministers on Saturday, November 14, reaffirmed their commitment to the full implementation of the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution and relevant existing mechanisms.

Haze from forest fires in Indonesia spread to its neighbors in recent months. How did Malaysia and Singapore react on Saturday, especially when Indonesia reported that it has exerted all efforts to address the issue?

“Very cordial. I surmise there had been discussions before the formal meeting this morning. Singapore remarked positively and Malaysia, the same thing,” said Philippine Foreign Secretary Maria Theresa Lazaro.

Lazaro added that Singapore’s tough stance against companies contributing to transboundary haze was not discussed.

United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres arrives in Manila on Saturday, November 14, 2026, for the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits.

This is Guterres’ last engagement with ASEAN as the world’s chief diplomat. His term ends on December 31.

No easing of ASEAN’s peace plan for Myanmar

There is no easing of the ASEAN Five-Point Consensus (5PC) for Myanmar, said Philippine Foreign Secretary Maria Theresa Lazaro on Saturday, November 14.

However, proposals to review the plan are being considered. Lazaro said ASEAN foreign ministers started an “extended informal consultation” on the plan’s implementation and discussed positions concerning Myanmar.

The 5PC is ASEAN’s peace plan that seeks to address the Myanmar crisis following the country’s 2021 military coup.
