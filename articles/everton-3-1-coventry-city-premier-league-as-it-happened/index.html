    Preamble

Neither manager seems particularly happy with the state of officiating, then. Which should give the referees pause, but, well, y’know. “We saw it last weekend at Anfield and elsewhere, and again this weekend, and all evidence suggests that Howard Webb’s reponse to VAR criticism is to abdicate all responsibility,” writes David Dein. “He was a weak referee so it’s absolutely no surprise to see him weak with the top position in the referees body. The whole body is not fit for purpose.”

David Moyes speaks to Sky Sports. “The efforts were good … the performance didn’t reach what we’ve been doing recently … getting three points in the Premier League, you take it any way you can … hugely important for us, it keeps us nearer the top end … I have to give Coventry credit … we had no divine right to come here and think you’d beat Coventry … I didn’t … I don’t think we did … things could have gone the other way for them … it’s not a night for being negative, I just know we can play much better … I’m bothered [about VAR mistakes] because we want it to be right … but we’ve had a few decisions recently that we don’t think have gone [our way] … Newcastle, Man City last season, which was hugely important for us getting into Europe and didn’t go in our favour … sometimes things go for you, and sometimes they don’t … it’s easier to talk about it when they go for you … if it had gone against me I’d have probably been complaining a bit more.”

The Premier League table. Everton leapfrog their neighbours and rivals into fifth spot; Coventry remain in 16th. Both teams will take heart from tonight’s performance, albeit in very different ways.

Lampard tries to leave for the international break on a high: “We’re getting better, and tonight’s result doesn’t change that.”

Lampard: VAR 'didn't do its job'

Frank Lampard speaks to Sky … and he is fuming over the three VAR checks that didn’t go Coventry’s way. “Proud of the players … up until scoring the deserved first goal of the game, thought we were the better team to that point … then they had a spell … two goals in five minutes … it knocked us back … the third goal was poor from us but so many things had happened in the game that contributed to the scoreline being what it was … big decisions … the first-half decision with [James] Tarkowski got checked … seeing it back, he pulls on Caleb’s [Yirenkyi] hair while competing with Ethan [Pinnock] … I thought it was clear, the rules … it’s a foul and a penalty … the one on Taiwo [Awoniyi] running through … it’s a clear foul the referee doesn’t give in game … I can’t believe that … it’s clear … maybe he waited for VAR because it’s on the borderline … but as I see it his foot’s entering the box … on the line … that goes against us with VAR … and Barry’s second goal comes off his hand … big decisions change games and that’s disappointing … VAR’s there for that, right? … and it didn’t do its job … Barry’s last touch flicks it in the net … it was a really quick check as well.”

Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall’s turn to talk to Sky. “Very important … it’s been a fantastic week … three games, three wins … so, so happy with the way the lads responded to going down tonight … really happy we’ve managed to get the three points … happy, happy days! … we never know when we’re out of a game … every time we’ve gone behind we’ve acted in a positive manner … their goal has kicked us into gear … that shouldn’t happen really but it did and after they scored we turned it up a notch … we were fantastic for the last 30 minutes and they couldn’t deal with it.”

Thierno Barry talks to Sky Sports. “I am very happy … we wanted to win … and I scored two goals! … I just try to do my best … I want to score in every game … I am in good form in this moment … I will try to keep on.”

He’s also asked about the potential use of an arm in his second goal, but unsurprisingly straight-bats that line of questioning: “A goal is a goal.”

And then the final gambit from Sky interviewer: “I have the player of the match award but you have to do your dance first before I pass it on, come on.”

No, you come on. Let’s maintain some decorum, shall we? Barry politely declines; he’d have been well within his rights to tell the Sky dude to bugger off and file the award where the sun doesn’t shine.

Everton celebrate to the soundtrack of Elton John taking a guess as to why they call it the Blues. And well they might! They responded magnificently to falling behind early in the second half. Having been practically somnambulant up until that point, they awoke with a start, Thierno Barry scoring twice and setting up Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall for an absolute thriker to decide a hard-fought game. Full marks for all of that … though they were given a big hand by VAR, who denied Coventry two possible penalties, and waved through the role of Barry’s left arm in Everton’s second goal. Really fine margins decided all three moments, and it’ll be interesting to hear Frank Lampard’s view. But tonight’s about David Moyes, Mr 592, whose Catterick-equalling feats have been rewarded with a win that takes the Toffees into the international break on a high. Coventry will go away to lick their wounds, but theirs wasn’t the performance of a relegation team, and they will surely play worse and win.

FULL TIME: Everton 3-1 Coventry City

David Moyes celebrates his record-equalling 592nd game in charge of Everton with a win that takes his side above Liverpool and into fifth!

90 min +6: Mykolenko dinks a cross in from the left. Hackney tries to guide a diving header across Rushworth and towards the bottom right, but only finds the keeper. Barry, on a hat-trick, standing just behind, may have notes.

90 min +4: Barry launches into an enthusiastic 50-50 with Pinnock, and even though Coventry win the throw, he celebrates with his fans. It’s been his evening, even if Dewsbury-Hall scored the pick of the goals. Two goals and an assist, it’s hard to top!

90 min +2: Hamer, quarterbacking from deep on the left, releases Tchaouna into the box with a glorious sliderule pass. Tchaouna spins and fizzes a low shot-cum-cross through the six-yard box. The pass deserved a more composed finish.

90 min +1: The first of six additional minutes.

90 min: Branthwaite showed a stud or two in that challenge, but it wasn’t high enough for a red. David Moyes hooks him nonetheless, sending on Keane in his place. Alcaraz also comes on, for George.

89 min: Branthwaite takes a heavy touch and, in lunging to retrieve the situation, catches Yirenkyi. He goes into the book … as does Yirenkyi, who had hauled back George earlier in the passage of play.

87 min: Coventry make a quadruple change. Torp, Sakamoto, Rudoni and Awoniyi make way for Grimes, Tchaouna, Cherif and Hamer.

85 min: George shoots from a tight angle on the left. Rushworth kicks clear. Barry heads the rebound straight back at the keeper … but inches wide of the right-hand post. Barry probably should have done better, and yet you can hardly pick fault with a man who has scored two and set up another.

83 min: It’s been an evening of the finest margins, and that time Coventry played Dewsbury-Hall onside by the length of a toenail. When it’s not going your way, it’s not going your way.

GOAL! Everton 3-1 Coventry City (Dewsbury-Hall 81)

Barry turns provider, dropping deep before sending Dewsbury-Hall romping into space down the inside-left channel. He’s got Hackney free in the middle, but instead of crossing low, lashes a shot from a tight angle past Rushworth at the near post and in! He absolutely skelped that! No stopping it. What a strike!

79 min: George has a whack from distance. Well over the bar. “Coventry hss been screwed by VAR today,” writes Everton supporter and all-round good egg Mary Waltz. “And that is why BIN VAR is on my stationary.”

78 min: Coventry enjoy a bit of sterile possession in the midfield. The Everton support respond by cranking up the volume in the hope of getting their lads over the line. Speaking of which … “The swing in the xG in Everton’s favour to ‘1.24 to 1.12’ is hardly overwhelming, and I’m an Everton fan,” writes Barry McAuley. “Still, will be delighted if they see this particular banana skin out. Seem to slip on most of them!”

76 min: Thierno Barry is on a hat-trick … and is now into double figures for the season. His ninth and tenth goals tonight. This is also his third consecutive scoring game. No wonder he’s got his dancing shoes on.

74 min: Coventry respond by replacing their goalscorer Gboho with Thomas-Asante. “Absolute joke VAR!” exclaims Robert Jones. “Three gross decisions by Timothy Wood!”

73 min: A triple change for Everton, as Dibling, Rohl and Armstrong make way for Johnson, Hackney and O’Brien.

72 min: Nothing comes of the corner.

71 min: Torp launches himself at Dewsbury-Hall, a frustrated swipe. He’s very fortunate his opponent sashays clear of the challenge and away, eventually winning a corner. Had that attempted challenge connected, the referee would have had some work to do.

69 min: … but having said that, what a response since falling behind. Everton have hauled themselves back into this game with verve and passion, and they’ve got their reward. The xG is now 1.24 to 1.12.

67 min: Replays do suggest the ball brushed Barry’s left arm as he brought the ball down before forcing it over the line from close range. Everton have certainly had the benefit of some generous VAR calls tonight; it’s going to be very interesting to hear Frank Lampard’s take.

66 min: Some space for Dewsbury-Hall down the inside-left channel. He power-curls towards the top left, but his shot is turned around the post by Rushworth. No corner, as there had been a foul earlier in the move.

65 min: Rudoni crosses from the right, but Awoniyi can’t power a header goalwards. It doesn’t feel like the scoring is over.

64 min: That did look like a handball, but perhaps the evidence wasn’t 100 percent clear. Either way, Coventry won’t be happy with VAR Timothy Wood at all this evening.

62 min: There was a question of handball as Barry jumped with Dasilva. But VAR doesn’t see anything untoward, and the goal stands! Everton looked on the ropes, but how they’ve responded!

GOAL! Everton 2-1 Coventry City (Barry 61)

Another cross into the Coventry box from the left. George this time. Coventry don’t deal with it. A scramble at the far stick. Barry rises above Dasilva and the ball dribbles into the bottom right in slow motion!

60 min: Dewsbury-Hall swivels just inside the Coventry box and shoots. Pinnock blocks. All of a sudden, Everton look really lively in attack. Going behind really woke them up. And then …

59 min: A free kick for Everton out on the left. Dewsbury-Hall curls it in. Barry competes for a header but can’t get an effort on target this time. Coventry counter quickly through Awoniyi, who should release Gboho, but his pass into the centre from the left is no good. The ball’s shuttled on to Rudoni, who wins a corner from which nothing comes. This match is wide open now, and great fun!

GOAL! Everton 1-1 Coventry City (Barry 57)

Mykolenko crosses from the left. Cleared. He tries again. This time Barry rises at the far stick, and guides an unstoppable downward header into the right-hand portion of the net! The HDS, so frustrated seconds earlier, explodes in joy!

56 min: The game suddenly unfolds into a basketball match. Did Armstrong handle a left-wing cross in the Everton box? Nope! Did Pinnock impede Tarkowski up the other end? Nope! Should Gboho have done better upon spinning Mykolenko down the right? Yes! And then …

54 min: VAR has a good look for offside, but there isn’t one. The goal stands. Coventry would have been fuming had another decision gone against them!

GOAL! Everton 0-1 Coventry City (Gboho 52)

Rudoni dribbles right to left across the face of the Everton box. He lays off to Sakamoto, who feeds Gboho down the left channel and into the area. Gboho takes a touch and spins, putting Branthwaite on his backside before flicking a slow-motion shot across Pickford and into the bottom right. It had been coming.

51 min: Tell you what, that Mykolenko clip on Awoniyi was very close to being on the line. One angle looks like just outside, another the striker’s toe on the line. Who’d be a referee, huh?

50 min: Armstrong scuffs a shot goalwards from a tight angle on the right. It’s easy for Rushworth, but Everton’s first on-target effort for nearly 40 minutes. Coventry then counter through Dasilva, whose cross from the right only just evades Awoniyi in the middle.

49 min: VAR deems the clip on the ankle to be just outside the box. It looked on the line to the naked eye, but that vile jelly can play tricks on you. Be interested to see that again, mind. Everton very close once again to conceding a spot kick.

48 min: Torp slips a ball down the inside-right channel for Awoniyi, who enters the box and feels a light shove from Mykolenko on his back. Or is there a clip on his heel? Either way, he goes over, and VAR is definitely going to take a look at this.

46 min: Gboho immediately strides into space down the middle and, with options either side, opts to shoot himself. The shot’s deflected and bobbles through to Pickford.

Coventry get the second half started. Neither side has made a change.

On Sky Sports, studio pundits Jamie Redknapp and Callum Wilson both suggest James Tarkowski got away with a big one for tugging on Yirenkyi’s hair (39 min). Never mind planting his arm across Pinnock’s face in the same challenge! It should have been a penalty to Coventry, no question. No idea why VAR didn’t get involved.

HALF TIME: Everton 0-0 Coventry City

Everton started brightly, but Coventry ended the half much the stronger; the visitors have been the better side over the piece. A smattering of boos as Everton trudge off. Nothing super-dramatic, to be clear, a frustrated minority in a quiet stadium. But a smattering nonetheless.

45 min: Rohl shanks a right-wing cross out for a goal kick, and it’s the Coventry fans you can hear as they make fun of the Everton player. The home fans are pretty subdued at the moment. One minute of bonus first-half time to come.

44 min: … and so Torp sends a dipping long-range rake over the wall and towards the bottom left. Pickford deals with it. To be honest, it’s just as well the keeper makes the save, because Dasilva had quite a handful of Dibling’s shirt in the tussle that led to the free kick. Everton would have been livid had that decision led to a goal.

43 min: Coventry win another throw in Everton territory, deep down the left. It’s launched long and only half cleared. Everton can’t get out. Dibling and Dasilva grapple, 25 yards out, and Coventry get the decision. A free kick in a dangerous position, just to the left of centre.

41 min: Rudoni shimmies his way down the inside-right channel before unleashing a low bobbler across Pickford and inches wide of the left-hand corner. Goal kick. Not sure Pickford was getting to that. Everton were on top for a while back there; now it’s all Coventry, and the xG is now 0.28 for Everton to the visitors’ 0.57.

39 min: George and Torp collide in the midfield. Free kick to Coventry, whose player was taken out in mid-air. The resulting free kick is launched towards the Everton box. Tarkowski goes up with Yirenkyi, and gives the Coventry man’s hair a snide tug. He also manages to slap his arm across Pinnock’s startled face. VAR isn’t interested in looking at either part of that challenge; Tarkowski has got away with one two there.

37 min: … and now a long throw into the Everton box from the left causes minor bedlam. The ball drops to Rudoni, on the right-hand corner of the six-yard box … but he can’t sort his feet out to get a proper shot away. The home crowd getting a bit agitated now as Coventry get closer and closer.

36 min: Branthwaite’s poor clearing header is intercepted and sent back into the Everton box down the inside-right channel. Sakamoto shoots from a tight angle. Pickford parries. Coventry growing and growing.

35 min: Yirenkyi, Rudoni and Sakamoto combine well down the right to earn Coventry’s first corner of the evening. Rudoni swings it in. Pickford half-punches, half-slaps the ball clear, doing just enough. The visitors are getting on top.

33 min: Torp slides Awoniyi into acres down the right. He should stride into the box and have a whack, but tries to find Gboho in the middle with a low cross that the back-tracking Dibling cuts out. Torp and Dibling come out of this looking good; the lads either side of their contributions, not so much.

32 min: A bit of space for Torp down the inside-left channel. Hoick! Goal kick! Just beforehand, Sakamoto and Dewsbury-Hall traded misplaced passes. This has got a wee bit scrappy.

30 min: Yirenkyi crosses from the right. The ball’s deflected, so Pickford does extremely well to gather in the middle of a crowded six-yard box.
