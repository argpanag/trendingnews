Take a look inside the iconic house from Notting Hill on sale for £7,500,000

TheTools · 11.11.2026 13:24 · source · permalink

‘I’m just a girl, standing in front of a boy, asking him to love her.’

Rom-com fanatics the world over will instantly recognise this line, spoken by Julia Roberts to Hugh Grant, in Notting Hill.

And now, the home that Will Thacker shared with Spike, with its iconic blue door, is up for sale.

As this is Notting Hill, the price tag isn’t small, currently on the market for a cool £7,500,000.

But potential buyers be warned: 280 Westbourne Park Road looks a lot different than it does in the film.

There’s bespoke art on the walls, shiny marble counters, open-plan living and a mezzanine floor.

Confused? While the front door of the property was used in Notting Hill, the indoor scenes were filmed on a sound stage.

This means the real interiors of 280 never seen in Notting Hill.

It’s still got some impressive movie credentials though. As well it’s recognisable door, back in 1999, it was also screenwriter, Richard Curtis’ real life home.

The home is even listed on Google Maps as ‘The Blue Door of [the] Notting Hill movie,’ which isn’t exactly off the radar.

Property hunters on r/SpottedonRightmove Subreddit, have also pointed out this downside.

‘You wouldn’t get a moment’s peace with all the people outside for photos all the time,’ @CampMain wrote.

Meanwhile, @Briglin had a much more practical solution, suggesting ‘if you’re bored you can let them in for a fiver.’

Over the last few years, many of the area’s most iconic houses, with their large front doors and idyllic paths, have become a posing ground for tourists.

In March, selfie hunters descended on Stanley Crescent, where flats can command as much as £1 million, and entire homes almost £20 million.

Visitors were spotted setting up ring lights and professional cameras, while some were even spotted changing outfits in front gardens, Metro reported at the time.

Likewise, on Lancaster Road, residents are increasingly fed up with visitors taking pictures on their doorsteps.

Last year, some even took to painting their houses, famously bright in colour, black, to deter people from snapping to their heart’s content.
