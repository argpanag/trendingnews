Department-store chain closing half its locations after 188 years in business

TheTools · 22.11.2026 03:00 · source · permalink

One of the world's oldest department store chains is preparing to shut down half of its physical retail network as mounting financial pressures threaten its future after nearly 188 years.

The major restructuring will affect 19 stores and put approximately 1,500 jobs at risk.

Founded in Sydney, Australia, in 1838, David Jones is a premium department store chain selling luxury and upscale brands across fashion, beauty, homewares, and other categories.

The retailer's latest troubles come after years of financial losses, growing competition, and mounting pressure to restructure its operations.

David Jones is closing half of its store network

David Jones is closing 19 of its 38 stores across Australia and New Zealand, equivalent to half of its physical retail footprint.

The closures will significantly reduce David Jones' presence in suburban and regional shopping centers, while the retailer also prepares to shut its Hay Street Mall flagship in Perth.

The decision follows a review of the company's store portfolio, with the receiver determining that a smaller physical network is necessary for the business to continue operating.

Most of the affected locations are expected to cease trading by the end of 2026, although the final closure dates have not been confirmed. Until then, the stores will continue trading, with remaining inventory expected to be discounted or transferred to other locations.

The shutdown will leave David Jones without a physical retail presence in New Zealand and significantly reduce its footprint across Australia.

According to the Shop, Distributive and Allied Employees Association (SDA), 1,689 directly employed workers are affected by the closures. Of those, approximately 170 have been offered redeployment, leaving around 1,500 jobs at risk.

"As part of this restructure, the receivers have commenced an immediate consultation process for all staff associated with 19 stores that have been identified for closure," FTI Consulting said, as reported by the Guardian.

"For the immediate term these stores will continue trading and while there is no final date for the closures, we anticipate they will cease trading by the end of the year."
