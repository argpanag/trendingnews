Hundreds of flights cancelled at UK airports as air traffic control issue causes widespread disruption

TheTools · 08.09.2026 18:37 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Hundreds of flights are cancelled at airports across the UK due to an issue with air traffic control

  • Nats - the air traffic control provider - says the glitch is with its "flight processing system" and that a fix has been deployed

  • Heathrow, Manchester and East Midlands airports are among those reporting issues - airports in Scotland and Ireland are also experiencing delays

  • Phil White, travelling to Lanzorate, has been stuck on the runway at Gatwick for hours. "The pilots have been on every 20 minutes saying they are waiting for information," he tells us

  • Meanwhile, Chloe Davis, an aspiring pilot, takes the opportunity to explore the cockpit on her delayed Virgin Atlantic plane to Orlando

  • Ryanair says 65,000 of its passengers have been delayed by up to eight hours while British Airways warns of a "significant knock-on impact for customers and colleagues"

  • There's now a backlog of flights and aircraft across Europe in the wrong place - sorting all of this out will take time, writes our correspondent

Hundreds of flights are cancelled at airports across the UK due to an issue with air traffic control

Nats - the air traffic control provider - says the glitch is with its "flight processing system" and that a fix has been deployed

Heathrow, Manchester and East Midlands airports are among those reporting issues - airports in Scotland and Ireland are also experiencing delays

Phil White, travelling to Lanzorate, has been stuck on the runway at Gatwick for hours. "The pilots have been on every 20 minutes saying they are waiting for information," he tells us

Meanwhile, Chloe Davis, an aspiring pilot, takes the opportunity to explore the cockpit on her delayed Virgin Atlantic plane to Orlando

Ryanair says 65,000 of its passengers have been delayed by up to eight hours while British Airways warns of a "significant knock-on impact for customers and colleagues"

There's now a backlog of flights and aircraft across Europe in the wrong place - sorting all of this out will take time, writes our correspondent

  3. My flight has been cancelled on the way to my own weddingpublished at 19:10 BST
    19:10 BST

    Charlotte Simpson
    Reporting from Gatwick Airport

    I'm at Gatwick and was due to be on the 17:15 flight to Bari, Italy, as I'm due to get married In Puglia on Saturday.

    I’ve got my wedding dress with me, I’ve got my fiancé with me, but sadly we are not going to be going anywhere tonight.

    Our flight is among those that have been cancelled this afternoon because of this disruption with air traffic control.

    It's pretty chaotic at the moment, people have just been wondering around looking for help - but from what we've seen they've just had to wait around to be told over the public address system - like us - that their flight is cancelled.

    Everyone has been rushing on the phone, trying to call various airlines to try and get re-booked.

    Luckily our wedding isn't until Saturday, so we do have a bit of time.

    But it isn't the tranquil start to our wedding celebrations that we had imagined unfortunately.

  4. Frustration among passengers in Malta as flights to UK cancelledpublished at 18:57 BST
    18:57 BST

    Manish Pandey
    Reporting from Malta International Airport

    I’m at Malta International Airport, where my flight home to London Gatwick has been cancelled.

    The departures board initially showed “delayed” to all UK-bound flights when we arrived, before changing the 21:30 BA flight to cancelled.

    The official at the British Airways counter has told us all flights to the UK have been cancelled because of the air traffic issues.

    There are a significant number of travellers heading home to the UK.

    We’ve been told that accommodation is being worked on, and we will be told through the BA app what the next steps are.

    There is frustration from lots of passengers, and the coffee shop in the airport is filling up quickly with exhausted travellers.

    For now, the wait continues.

  5. British Airways says fault will have 'significant knock-on impact' for customerspublished at 18:45 BST
    18:45 BST

    British Airways says it has been informed by the air traffic controller that the "fault has been resolved" but due to the severity of the issue, there will be a "significant knock-on impact for customers and colleagues".

    A spokesperson for the airline says it was forced to cancel or divert more than 100 flights this afternoon, "inconveniencing tens of thousands of customers".

    This incident was "entirely out of our control and incredibly frustrating for our customers and colleagues," it says, with aircraft and crews ending up out of place.

    "We ask customers whose flights have been cancelled not to travel to the airport and to bear with us if they’re trying to get in touch, as our call centres are incredibly busy at the moment.

    "Customers due to travel today or tomorrow are able to make changes to their booking on ba.com," it adds.

  7. Government must improve resilience of air traffic control - shadow transport ministerpublished at 18:24 BST
    18:24 BST

    Shadow transport minister has criticised the government and Nats for allowing another air traffic control incident after a major outage brought UK airports to a standstill last year.

    “Yet again passengers are stuck on the tarmac wondering why Britain cannot keep its own skies open," Greg Smith says.

    “The Government and Nats must explain what has happened and how they will make sure resilience is improved.”

  11. Disruption likely to persist for hours to come due to flight backlogpublished at 17:32 BST
    17:32 BST

    Theo Leggett
    International business correspondent

    Nats says the problem with its flight processing system - which provides air traffic controllers with essential information about aircraft flying through UK airspace - is being resolved.

    But the disruption is likely to persist for hours to come.

    Here’s the problem. Hundreds of flights have been cancelled and many more delayed across the country.

    Although the technical issue affected departures, arrivals were also hit as space on the ground became increasingly restricted. So planes from overseas airports were held on the tarmac as well.

    The result is that now there’s a backlog of flights, and aircraft across Europe are in the wrong place. Sorting all of this out is likely to take time.

About 200 flights have been cancelled this afternoon due to the UK air traffic control issue, and that number is expected to rise, according to flight monitoring site Flightradar24.

Air traffic controller Nats says the issue with its “flight processing system” is causing flight disruption across UK airports, adding that “recovery will take some time”.

Gatwick is one of many airports across the UK reporting delays
