1. EMIRATES NBA CUP GROUP PLAY FINALE: WHAT’S AT STAKE

Four weeks of Group Play have settled just one of the eight spots in the Emirates NBA Cup Knockout Round.

The Mavericks have claimed West Group A. The other seven berths? They’ll all be decided tonight.

Never before in the tournament’s four-year history have this many Knockout Round spots been up for grabs entering the final night of Group Play, with 20 teams still in contention heading into an 11-game Friday slate. That includes 15 teams that can still win their group.

Just like your Thanksgiving Day plate, there’s a lot to digest. So, before diving into Friday’s clinching scenarios, let’s take a quick look at the standings and refresh on the Group Play tiebreakers:

  • 1. Head-to-head record in Group Play
  • 2. Point differential in Group Play
  • 3. Total points scored in Group Play
  • 4. Regular-season record from 2025-26

So, who can punch their ticket tonight, and what will it take? Let’s break it down.

Win-And-In Group Scenarios

  • 76ers at Pacers (7:30 ET, League Pass): Philly claims East B with a win
  • Hawks at Celtics (8 ET, League Pass): Winner claims East C
  • Grizzlies at Wolves (8:30 ET, League Pass): Winner claims West B

More Clinching Group Scenarios

  • East A: Orlando claims the group with a Pistons loss, while Detroit (vs. Milwaukee) and Toronto (vs. Brooklyn) can each claim the group with a win and some help
  • East B: Miami claims the group with a win over New York (7 ET, Prime) and a Sixers loss. But if both the Heat and Sixers lose, all five teams finish 2-2, opening the door for the Knicks or Pacers to win East B via tiebreaker
  • West C: The Lakers visit the Spurs (9:30 ET, Prime), while the Blazers host the Warriors (9:30 ET, League Pass), with all four teams needing a win and some help to claim the group

2. ON PRIME: LUKA’S LAKERS VISIT WEMBY’S SPURS (9:30 ET)

Sean M. Haffey + Chris Gardner/NBAE via Getty Images

Lakers (2-1, -9 point diff) at Spurs (2-1, +48) | West C | 9:30 ET, Prime

They are the top two teams in the West. They’re led by the top two players on the Kia MVP Ladder. And tonight on Prime (9:30 ET), the Spurs and Lakers square off for the first time this season, with both teams’ Cup hopes on the line. | Shaun Powell On Luka’s Historic Start

  • Spurs Stakes: San Antonio wins West C with a win and a Blazers loss to the Warriors
  • Lakers Stakes: Los Angeles wins West C with a win and a Warriors loss

How rare is this matchup? The last time the Lakers and Spurs met with the two best records in the West (min. 15 GP each) was in February 2011, back when Kobe Bryant and Tim Duncan had led their respective franchises to a combined three titles in four years.

Now, a new generation of superstars headlines the rivalry – another prolific scorer in purple & gold and another dominant big man in silver & black: Luka Dončić and Victor Wembanyama.

  • Luka Magic: Dončić (586 pts, 123 ast) is just the second player in NBA history to tally 575+ points and 100+ assists through his first 16 games of a season, joining James Harden (2019-20)
  • Alien Takeover: Since blocks were first tracked in 1973-74, only one player has racked up 550+ points, 225+ boards and 60+ blocks through his first 18 games of a season: Wembanyama (552 pts, 225 reb, 69 blk)

Behind Luka and Wemby, the Lakers (15-3) and Spurs (15-4) are a combined 30-7. But as staggering as their numbers are, some things are best appreciated with your own eyes.

So, ahead of tonight’s showdown, let’s take a moment to marvel at what makes these two so special.

Wemby is on pace for 298 blocks this season, which would be the most by any player in 23 years. One reason why? Sequences like this: contesting a drive, denying a poster, then swatting the follow-up (plus tapping the board to Dylan Harper to top it off).

Dončić is averaging an NBA-best 36.6 ppg but also ranks 7th in assists (7.7). That keeps defenders guessing – and more often than not, Luka makes them guess wrong.

Speaking of dimes, how about a Wemby lob to himself … off the glass and down with the left.

Then there’s 77 … from 62 feet.

3. MORE ON PRIME: GIANNIS’ HEAT FACE KNICKS AT MSG (7 ET)

Mitchell Leff + David Sherman/NBAE via Getty Images

Heat (2-1, +25 point diff) at Knicks (1-2, +12) | East B | 7 ET, Prime

The Knicks have been here before. A win-or-go-home matchup in their Group Play finale. At Madison Square Garden. Facing a Giannis Antetokounmpo-led East rival.

On Black Friday last year, New York needed a win over Giannis’ Bucks to advance to the Knockout Round. Behind 37 points from Jalen Brunson, it did just that, claiming East Group C. Eighteen days later, the Knicks were NBA Cup champions.

Tonight on Prime (7 ET), the reigning champs need another Group Play finale win – and this time, some help – to keep their title defense alive. And once again, Giannis stands in their way, only now in different colors.

  • What New York Needs: The Knicks are eliminated with a loss, but can still win East Group B via tiebreaker with a victory by 7+ points and a Sixers loss. They can also advance as a Wild Card
  • What Miami Needs: The Heat have a wider path. They win East Group B with a victory and a Sixers loss, but can still advance to the Knockout Round even with a loss

Nathaniel S. Butler/NBAE via Getty Images

Adding to the Cup stakes, tonight’s matchup features two of the East’s hottest teams, separated by just 1.5 games in the standings. The Knicks have won six straight, while the Heat had won seven in a row before falling to the Sixers on Wednesday.

What’s fueled their success? For Miami, it’s been a high-powered offense. For New York, a relentless defense.

  • NY Stops: Only one team in the NBA has held its opponents below 120 points in all but one game this season: the Knicks, who rank 2nd in points allowed (103.2 ppg)
  • The Key? Closeouts and cleanup on the glass, as the Knicks rank T-7th in shot contests (40.2 per game) and 3rd in rebounds (46.7)
  • Heat Check: While the Knicks boast the NBA’s 2nd-best scoring defense, the Heat feature the league’s 2nd-best scoring offense, pouring in 119.7 ppg
  • The Key? Ball movement and capitalizing on quality looks. The Heat rank 2nd in assists per game and 5th in secondary assists, while their top three scorers – Giannis (28.4 ppg), Bam Adebayo (19.1) and Davion Mitchell (17.2) – are all shooting above 50% from the field

Now, two of the league’s top units collide at the Garden – with their Cup hopes on the line.

4. ON LEAGUE PASS: TWO WINNER-TAKE-ALL MATCHUPS

Kevin C. Cox + Maddie Meyer/NBAE via Getty Images

For the Hawks, Celtics, Grizzlies & Wolves, the path to the Emirates NBA Cup Knockout Round is simple: win and they’re in.

Ahead of tonight’s two winner-take-all matchups on League Pass, let’s look at one key for each team to come out on top.

Hawks (2-1, +21 point diff) at Celtics (3-0, +68) | East C | 8 ET, League Pass

With Boston on a four-game skid, Jayson Tatum delivered a 41-point outburst to beat Washington on Tuesday and keep the C’s unbeaten in Cup Play. Now, they face the surging Hawks, who have won four straight and eight of their last 10.

  • Boston Blueprint: The Celtics lead the NBA with 16.4 made 3s per game. They’ve drained 20+ triples a game, a league-best six times this season, and when they hit 15+ treys, they’re 8-2 (.800)
  • Atlanta’s Edge: While Boston can fill it up from deep, the Hawks can wear teams down in the open floor, leading the NBA in both pace and fast break scoring (18.9 ppg)

Jordan Johnson/NBAE via Getty Images

Grizzlies (2-1, +18) at Wolves (3-0, +39) | West B | 8:30 ET, League Pass

Something’s got to give in Minnesota. While the Wolves rank 1st in defensive rating this season, the Grizzlies rank 2nd in offensive rating, averaging an NBA-best 120.1 ppg.

Tonight, they meet for the second time in six days – with a Knockout Round berth hanging in the balance.

  • Grizz Range: Memphis leads the league in 3-point percentage (38.3%), with four of its top six scorers shooting above 40% from deep. That doesn’t include Ty Jerome, who is pouring in a career-high 24.5 ppg
  • It does, however, include Cam Spencer (12.1 ppg), with the Grizzlies going 6-1 when he hits 3+ triples and 9-2 when he scores in double figures
  • Wolves’ Wall: On the other side, Minnesota allows just 12.1 triples per game, the 3rd-fewest in the NBA
  • That perimeter defense shined last Saturday, holding Memphis to a season-low eight 3s – at a 25% clip (8-for-32) – in a 104-95 win
  • After suffering a late-game ankle injury in Minnesota’s win over the Thunder on Wednesday, LaMelo Ball is listed as questionable for tonight’s game

5. KIA MVP LADDER FIRST LOOK: WHAT TONIGHT COULD MEAN

Michael Gonzales/NBAE via Getty Images

It’s still November, but Shaun Powell’s Kia MVP Ladder already has a pair of frontrunners. And for the first time this season, we’ll get to see them share the floor.

With Luka Dončić and Victor Wembanyama holding down the top two spots, respectively, tonight’s showdown in San Antonio could shift the balance of the early MVP race.

In his latest edition, which drops later today on NBA.com and the NBA App, Powell examines what’s at stake – beyond NBA Cup aspirations and the West standings:

“There are a smattering of regular season checkpoints where a single game carries a bit more weight, and one such tipoff has arrived: Lakers at Spurs, for first place in the West, featuring Luka Dončić and Victor Wembanyama on a get-your-popcorn-ready Friday.

“The aftershocks could also cause the KIA MVP Ladder to sway at the very top, where the two players remain engaged in a competition that finds them separated by a hair.”
