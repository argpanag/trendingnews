Week 1 Mailbag: Panic, Concern and Optimism

TheTools · 12.09.2026 02:57 · source · permalink
  • Washington Huskies Football

Washington hosts Utah State tomorrow but before we transition to Saturday’s gameday coverage. Here is the Week 1 mailbag!

Chicken Little: Is it time to panic?

I wouldn’t say the sky is falling, but there is certainly plenty of reason to be concerned. The Huskies’ struggles on offense are excusable, but they make you seriously consider a complete shift in expectations now that their two best running backs will miss time. Washington is in desperate need of a hot start to stay as healthy as possible for the toughest games on the schedule, which come in the second half of the season. I get that rivalry games can be funky, but there is zero reason why the Apple Cup should’ve been as close as it was. Wazzu’s offense was horrid, and Washington’s was equally so until the fourth quarter. For a team wanting to compete with the Big Ten elite… that’s bad. I’m not panicking just yet, but I am considering lowering my win total because I’m not sure if UW will be healthy enough to be the best version of itself.

iadawg: Ellinneus Davis was supposed to be the best defensive lineman but he only had the third most snaps of defensive linemen. Can you explain this? Thanks.

Definitely strange, but I wouldn’t spend too much time thinking about it unless it happens against Utah State and Eastern Washington. There’s a good chance he had a minor injury bugging him, or head coach Jedd Fisch and defensive coordinator Ryan Walters saw opportunities with other lineman and took advantage. In the end, it worked out for the Huskies. His lower-than-expected usage rate is not a trend that I think will continue through the remainder of September.

MIKERYANCLASSOF67: How concerning was the OL performance, and how likely is it to be fixed by B1G play.

Very concerning. Throughout that game, my main takeaway was that the offense played without intensity or joy. They felt very stale and unprepared, which is strange considering the defense’s complete opposite approach to the game. For whatever reason, UW’s offense just didn’t bring “rivalry game energy” to the Apple Cup. Washington State’s defense did, and I think it had an impact in the trenches. Again, Washington State should not have been able to match up with the Huskies. Defensively, UW knew that. Offensively, UW looked like it was going through the motions. There is no excuse for a performance like that in a game of such high importance to the program. I think it will improve in Big Ten play, but the opponent sack totals will be high if Fisch continues to try to establish quarterback Demond Williams as a pocket passer.

No Username: Is one head better than two? Are the Huskies really at an advantage versus other teams that have both a head coach and an offensive coordinator? How is the UW team better off, schematically, with teaching and game preparation, tactically?
It doesn’t make sense to me. At all. The way I see it, if Seattle Seahawks head coach Mike Macdonald has a defensive coordinator on his staff despite calling plays, Fisch should have an OC. Why wouldn’t Fisch want additional insight? Why wouldn’t he want to alleviate a portion of the responsibility off his shoulders? Did the Apple Cup feature a Husky offense that looked ready to play? Absolutely not.

Rhaego: If you were an AP voter, where would you rank UW after week 1? Feel free to go beyond Top 25 if you think it’s warranted.

Though I’ve been critical of the Week 1 performance throughout this article, I think the voters got it right by keeping Washington as a Top 20 team. There certainly was a desire to see more, but college football can’t obsess over how special rivalry games are, then punish a team for winning a close game against one.

BathAveCrewDawg: Gun to your head….how loud is the alarm going off about the RB health? I think Bonner and Sanoe both improved from camp to camp. At times Bonner really showed his 10.4 100m speed. I’m a big Sanoe fan as I think he offers legit size/speed combo not on the roster. I get that Cooley is rb1 but how many carries do the true frosh get a game? I’m guessing pass pro is a big question as it is w most true frosh. I can envision an effective thunder/lightening duo but it’s so early. Should I be panicked about what the running game looks like during the heart of league play?

The alarm is deafening. I think we’ll see a lot of all three running backs you mentioned because the last thing Washington can afford is an injury to Cooley. I mentioned in my prediction that it is incredibly important for the Huskies to win this game by a blowout so they don’t have to lean on Cooley late. He has an injury history and is still shaking off the rust after minimal action against Wazzu. I think the success of Bonner or Sanoe depends on Fisch figuring out how to use Williams’ legs without putting him at risk. On any sort of option play, the focus will be on Williams, regardless of down and distance. He is just that dynamic. He is like a lethal three-point shooter in the way that his gravity opens up opportunities for teammates. I’d be panicked about the running game even if UW’s best two ball carriers were healthy because both will need to make signficant strides to live up to the conference standard. If you can’t run the ball in the B1G, you won’t go far in the B1G.

Otis: Defense was excellent. Offense however looked lost. Do you feel this was simply rust or a symptom of Jedd’s play calling. When the offense struggles, I see this as an opportunity for in-game coaching adjustments to get some momentum. I did not see many plays that would take advantage of our talent advantage and get things going in the right direction. It’s early, but I have to admit- I’m a little worried. Please make me feel better with an optimistic explanation.

My optimistic explanation: There’s a reason we see teams open the season against an FCS or low-tier FBS opponent. Since there’s no preseason, those games are the best opportunity to replicate three preseason games in one. Opening the season against a rival could potentially result in a blowout, but there is also real danger in those games. Washington looked like a team trying to shake off the rust while playing against an opponent that didn’t allow it. As for the playcalling, it’s only a bad thing if Fisch doesn’t learn from it and improve against Utah State. Week 0 and Week 1 tend to be incredibly sloppy, and now we’re in an era where a lack of roster continuity makes finding cohesion as a whole tougher because so many key players haven’t played a snap together. That isn’t the case with Washington as much this year, but I’m sure the Huskies would’ve looked sharper if Adam Mohammed was still donning purple and gold. Replacing impact players like him isn’t easy. I don’t think you should be worried yet. If the Huskies can’t move the ball against Utah State… WORRY!

Thanks for the questions!

Next week, this will run on Thursday. The SoCal heat wave scrambled my brain for a few days.
