  4. Could Potter be England's next breakthrough star?published at 19:56 BST
    19:56 BST

    Emma Sanders
    BBC Sport women’s football news reporter

    When Sarina Wiegman named her squad for England's upcoming World Cup qualifying matches, one uncapped inclusion caught the eye - Lexi Potter.

    The 20-year-old Chelsea midfielder's first senior call-up follows her standout start to the season.

    The Chelsea academy graduate broke through last season, scoring the winner on her first WSL start against Brighton in March.

    It came after a two-year spell on loan at Crystal Palace where she gained regular first-team football.

    This season Potter has stepped up, starting alongside England midfielder Kiera Walsh in three of Chelsea's four WSL matches.

    Their partnership is flourishing and it has allowed Walsh to push further up the pitch and provide a goalscoring threat for the Blues.

    Potter has shown immense composure on the ball and maintained a 93% passing accuracy in the WSL so far this season.

    She ranks fourth among her team-mates in tackles and interceptions and has the third-best passing success rate at Chelsea.

    The midfielder has already played 207 minutes, made 21 recoveries in the WSL and is yet to be on the pitch while her side have conceded a goal this term.

    It's clear she is making an impact.

  7. Chelsea show flickers but fragilitypublished at 19:46 BST
    19:46 BST

    Joe Bradshaw
    BBC Sport senior journalist

    A brilliant goal from Wieke Kaptein handed Chelsea victory on their opening night in what was a promising attacking performance from the Blues.

    Summer signing Melvine Malard was bright in her pre-planned 45-minute appearance, while Alyssa Thompson's electric display showcased what she could offer in her second season at Stamford Bridge.

    With Ellie Carpenter playing behind Thompson, Chelsea's right side could be a potent weapon as they strive to return to the summit of English football - and, in reality, they ought to have added a couple more goals against Austria Vienna.

    However, there were concerns for Sonia Bompastor. Shorn of injured captain Erin Cuthbert and with Keira Walsh substituted early, they ceded control in midfield to the Champions League debutants.

    Indeed, Vienna came close on a number of occasions to taking a deserved point, hitting the post and having more shots than their esteemed opponents.

    Although Chelsea have only conceded one goal in four matches this season, the fragility on display could cost them against more clinical opponents.

Paris FC 2-2 Arsenal

Lucy Ward
Former Leeds striker on Disney+

We have seen this in the men's game with Aston Villa and Tyrone Mings. You can understand why the referee has done what she's done.

Arsenal will be kicking themselves.

A miscommunication between Arsenal goalkeeper Misa Rodriguez and England captain Leah Williamson gifted Paris FC a late equaliser as the sides drew 2-2 in the Women's Champions League.

Arsenal had taken a two-goal lead through Alessia Russo and Georgia Stanway, scoring their first goals of the season either side of half-time before Clara Mateo halved the deficit for Paris with a brilliant counter-attacking move.

With five minutes left, Williamson secured the ball with her hand in order to take a goalkick - but the referee deemed her team-mate Misa had already taken it, and awarded a penalty, which Maeline Mendy scored to level the game.

It was a bizarre turn of events which Arsenal manager Renee Slegers and her team could have done without, having won just one of their opening four Women's Super League (WSL) games and only narrowly winning their Champions League opener against HB Koge amid struggles in front of goal.
