As the final score would indicate, Clark was far from the only U.S. player who struggled against the Italians. The United States' overall output was its lowest at the World Cup since 1971, and it shot 28.6 percent overall while having more turnovers (14) than assists (11).

For as much talent as Team USA has from top to bottom, A'ja Wilson's absence looms large. The score might've looked much different if the four-time WNBA MVP had been stationed in the paint.

The United States closes out the group phase Monday against Czechia, and it could be a great opportunity for Clark and others who lack a lot of international experience.

The Americans have already qualified for the quarterfinals by virtue of winning Group D. With little at stake, coach Kara Lawson may choose to rest her usual starters ahead of the knockout stage. In that case, Clark would be getting plenty of playing time.
