Strongman ‘friends,’ AI’s potential and other takeaways from Trump’s China trip and sitdown with Putin and Xi | CNN Politics

TheTools · 19.11.2026 11:51 · source · permalink

Officials were frank heading into Thursday’s unprecedented three-way talks: US President Donald Trump, Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian leader Vladimir Putin were unlikely to leave Shenzhen with any major agreements.

Diplomatic breakthroughs, it turned out, were not the point. The talks themselves were the deliverable.

Gathered at a round table inside a state guesthouse on the outskirts of this modern metropolis, each man cited the “responsibility” on them to jointly navigate a complicated world.

“History tells us that when great powers are at peace, the world is secure; when great powers clash, the world is in chaos,” Xi said as the talks were starting.

Each was also looking to gain something from the power projection the meeting afforded.

For Putin, it was a chance to reemerge on the biggest global platform, with vastly more powerful nations, after being shunned nearly five years ago for his invasion of Ukraine. A joint statement released after the meeting listed immunity for state officials as “of fundamental importance,” a matter of significance for a leader who is wanted for war crimes by the International Criminal Court.

Xi was able to portray himself — and China — as a diplomatic power player, even as economic problems dog him at home.

And Trump, whose lame-duck era was hastened after this month’s midterm election battering, was able to show he’s still in the game with two men whose power in their own countries goes mostly unchecked.

“No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement and speech together,” Trump said as the meeting began. “We are choosing a different course.”

“Let’s see if we can get something done,” he said before the press was dismissed and the meeting got underway.

Here are five takeaways from the summit in Shenzhen.

What was the point?

Whether Thursday’s meeting will be remembered as a historic pivot point — or just another photo-op — isn’t yet clear. Eighty-one minutes was hardly enough time to resolve all the major differences between the three countries, on wars, economics and cutting-edge technology.

Heading into the talks, the leaders’ objectives seemed to differ.

The summit was initially proposed by Putin as a way to discuss the war in Ukraine without representatives from Ukraine at the table — a prospect that sent shivers through Kyiv and led to comparisons to the 1945 Yalta conference, when world powers carved up the postwar world into spheres of influence.

Both Xi and Putin referenced the post-World War II moment in their opening remarks as a time when powerful countries joined together to shape the world, a hint of how they viewed Thursday’s encounter.

Initially, American officials said Ukraine would not be on the agenda — though Trump later said he would bring it up.

“I think things are happening. I think that (Putin) and I hope the other side would like to see an end to that war,” he said at the start of the meeting.

More important, in Trump’s mind, was a discussion on nuclear arms control among the world’s largest nuclear states. Trump wants to negotiate a new nonproliferation pact with Moscow and Beijing, though he was candid about the difficulties of reaching an agreement.

“Once you have the nuclear weapons, it’s very hard to not have them,” he said. “It’s the way life works.”

Xi has appeared uninterested in entering a nuclear agreement with two countries whose arsenals of warheads are many times the size of China’s. And he was initially reluctant to host tripartite talks at all that could overshadow his APEC summit, according to US officials.

Speaking in the hours before the sit-down, Trump was asked about the advantages of meeting the two autocrats together.

“They both like Trump,” he said. “Now, what happens after me? That I can’t tell you.”

Relationship building

The competing objectives for the summit didn’t seem to matter once the men arrived at the Kylin Villa, alongside Shenzhen’s tiger safari park.

They convened at a round table, spaced apart equally with Xi in the middle.

“We are really making history,” Trump said proudly. “The three of us have a great deal to discuss.”

The relationships on display are complicated. Trump has worked ardently to cultivate both Xi and Putin over the last two years, hoping deeper personal ties will translate into smoother geopolitics. He’s employed flattery and showy summits to fete and elevate his counterparts.

He called both men “friends” in individual meetings beforehand.

“I think as long as you and I are in charge of things, we’ll never have a problem,” Trump told Xi.

The flattery has yet to yield significant breakthroughs. Trump has complained his relationship with Putin, helped along by a summit in Alaska last year, has so far failed to end the Ukraine war. Disputes over trade and shipments of rare earths continue to act as irritants in the US-China relationship. And both countries are providing assistance and technology to Iran.

Xi and Putin, who proclaimed five years ago they were partners “without limits,” have met dozens of times. After another meeting here on Wednesday, Chinese state media declared the relationship was “steadfast as a rock through wind and rain.”

Xi and Putin have long been united by a shared view that the global balance of power is undergoing a fundamental shift in their favor, as the East rises and the West is in decline. Or, said another way, that the age of the American superpower is ending.

With this unprecedented three-way meeting with Trump, Xi and Putin have clinched what both are sure to use as a new, landmark testament to that view.

Countries at war

Two of the three men in Thursday’s meeting are embroiled in wars against smaller adversaries that have stretched on longer than anticipated. Trump’s war in Iran and Putin’s war in Ukraine have each sapped military resources and led to global price increases — and frustrated Xi, who sees the conflicts as a threat to global stability.

Both wars were topics Trump hoped to discuss with Xi and Putin, each of whom maintains ties with Tehran. Trump has not applied pressure on Moscow or Beijing to cut off their ties. In fact, he loosened sanctions on Russian diesel, applied after the invasion of Ukraine, in an attempt to bring down costs that spiked as a result of the Iran war, indirectly allowing that conflict to enrich Russia.

Trump has grown exasperated by all the war. The conflict in Iran, which he initially said would last six weeks, is highly unpopular at home and, by his own admission, led to sweeping Republican losses in the midterms. He is now weighing military options to restart a new, intensive round of bombing.

Meanwhile, his campaign pledge to resolve the Ukraine war within 24 hours of taking office is now 667 days overdue.

How much help Xi and Putin will be in ending the wars isn’t clear. And how much a summit without Ukraine or Iran at the table can do toward bringing peace seems limited.

Still, Trump appeared hopeful.

“A lot of people weren’t sure would something come out of it. I think something … will come out,” Trump said beforehand as he met with Xi. “I mean, very very strongly. I think something will come out.”

Over in Berlin, Jared Kushner said that Trump dialed into the meeting of the “Coalition of the Willing” and gave his assessment of the talks in Shenzhen.

The president’s son-in-law — speaking alongside the leaders of Germany, France, Ukraine and the United Kingdom at the summit — said Trump had conveyed “a very strong message to both of them [Xi and Putin] on the desire for peace and would like to engage both of them very aggressively to get there.”

He didn’t elaborate but said Trump “came away believing that peace is achievable.”

Steve Witkoff, the president’s special envoy, said that Trump had made “Ukraine and a peaceful end to this” a central part of his message in Shenzhen.

Battle for AI supremacy

Even before Trump arrived in Shenzhen, the scandal involving former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and an alleged Chinese intelligence asset was overshadowing the stop. So, too, were cascading service outages in the United States — caused in some cases by AI updates — causing anxiety the administration at first was slow to address.

Questions of how the US will compete in the rapidly developing AI industry seemed more urgent set against the backdrop of this gleaming hub of China’s tech and chip-making. And the revelations about Huang served to expose a deep rift within Trump’s cabinet over how — or whether — to restrict the sale of high-powered AI chips to China.

Sitting alongside Xi at dinner on Wednesday evening, Trump raised the matter only briefly, he said afterward. Heading to China, he said he was wary of “insulting” China by pressing the spying issue too hard.

Many in his administration are more hawkish, and want to cut off China’s access to the most advanced AI chips. Trump, however, sounded opposed to any steps that could “stifle” AI’s advances.

“We do not want to do anything to destroy the tremendous potential of this technology,” he said. “We oppose any form of globalist regulation.”

Still, as he was speaking to Asian leaders, Trump did seem to acknowledge there could be danger ahead.

“We have to be careful. We have to cherish it. It’s almost completely good, but it’s got some bad elements,” he said. “And we have to be very careful in managing those bad elements.”

Xi as a diplomatic power player

Beijing will see this summit as a major success for Chinese diplomacy.

Trump’s praise of China and his assurances that as long as he and Xi are in charge they’ll “never have a problem” are welcome words for a Chinese leadership that’s closely watching for a tone shift in US policy toward China amid growing calls from US lawmakers to tighten controls on Chinese access to US high-tech.

Beijing was clearly interested in using Trump’s visit to capitalize on the US president’s goodwill towards China — especially ahead of a change of power in Congress and as Trump enters the final leg of his final term. Xi’s precedent-breaking airport welcome of Trump and his apparent warmth toward the American president in their interactions would have been carefully calibrated with that in mind.

The lack of concrete outcomes from the trilateral meeting also suited Beijing’s agenda, as Xi was widely seen to be wary of a Yalta 2.0 set of optics when it comes to discussions on the fate of Ukraine. That would have clashed with China’s avowed push for more multilateralism on the global stage.

Instead, Xi got to show himself as international convener — and a powerbroker whose diplomatic stature allows it to stand shoulder-to-shoulder with the US — without making any complicated commitments. That’s good timing for Xi, who is looking to bolster his own profile ahead of a key Communist Party conclave next year where’s he’s expect to cement his fourth term atop the China’s power structure.
