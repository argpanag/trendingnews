IS BIGGER REALLY BETTER?

Now then, which one of you hipsters decided to put Porto 0-1 PSV on? Oh dear. This was another night of Bigger Cup at its Bigliest self, comebacks everywhere, 40 goals in nine games, six of those in a best-v-best clash as Paris Saint-Germain rallied late against Barcelona. Even in defeat, Lamine Yamal found space to decorate the room with a ludicrous toe-poke equaliser, and there was a wholesome tale in Italy as Scott McTominay scored twice for Napoli in their 4-3 win against Bodø/Glimt. These were his first strikes since returning from heart surgery, taking his tally at the club to 29 goals in 86 appearances. No wonder Erik ten Hag was peeved with his bosses at Manchester United for shipping the midfielder off to Naples.

But even after all that, Football Daily feels hollow, as it does for most of Bigger Cup’s group stage. The absence of jeopardy in the 36-team table remains a sore point, as PSG magnificently demonstrated during their back-to-back title-winning campaigns. In 2024-25 they finished 15th, taking seven points from their first six games; in the old days this meant a third-place finish in your group and a humiliating retreat to Big Vase. In 2025-26 Luis Enrique’s side settled for 11th and, once again, embraced their punishment of an extra knockout round. To be fair, this season they look as if they want to put their feet up in February, sitting pretty at the top with three wins from three.

Liverpool, too, would have been OK without a special night at home, having won their first two games, but try telling that to the Kop. They got the thrills of coming back from two goals down to beat Villarreal, under-the-pump Florian Wirtz hit the decisive goal, and Rio Ngumoha received plenty of love. The visitors got the full Anfield experience, with Villarreal’s manager, Iñigo Pérez, confessing: “We weren’t able to handle the emotion.”

Having said all that, try telling Michael Carrick that the match in Como doesn’t really matter, that it’s perfectly fine if his United side make it two wins in 11 games this season. Cesc Fàbregas’s side are the opposition, which should make you feel really, really old. The boy who infamously flung some pizza in an Old Trafford melee, splattering Sir Alex Ferguson, is enjoying himself on the touchline. All grown up, he could make life seriously messy for another Manchester United manager.
