How a Hotel Room Fire Stick Enabled a Massive Security Breach of Rockstar Games - AOL

TheTools · 17.11.2026 03:32 · source · permalink

Arion Kurtaj, the hacker responsible for the 2022 Rockstar Games hack that led to nearly an hour of unfinished Grand Theft Auto 6 footage being leaked, has pled guilty to a number of cybercrime offenses. The 21-year-old pled guilty to the Rockstar hack and several other attacks at Woolwich Crown Court on Monday, November 16.

Kurtaj carried out the Rockstar hack while on bail over previous hacking offenses. He was staying at a Travelodge in Bicester under police protection at the time after receiving threats from rival hackers. He was able to access Rockstar’s systems using an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into his hotel room TV, along with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, after his computer was seized.

The Rockstar Hack Cost the Company Millions

Kurtaj stole internal company data that included 90 video clips with nearly an hour of gameplay from the highly anticipated GTA 6. He then logged into Rockstar’s internal Slack messaging system and threatened to release the game’s source code unless the company contacted him on Telegram.

Rockstar refused to cooperate, and Kurtaj later published the stolen material online under the name TeaPotUberHacker. The leak revealed several details related to the unfinished game, including the two protagonists, Jason and Lucia. It also revealed that a female playable protagonist was featured, something Rockstar had kept secret.

The company said the breach cost it $5 million to recover from, as well as thousands of hours of staff time.

Kurtaj was previously found unfit to stand trial because of the severity of his autism. But in 2023, a jury held a trial on the facts, deciding whether he did the alleged acts, not whether he had criminal intent. He was later ordered to stay in a secure hospital indefinitely unless doctors decided he no longer posed a danger.

He was later assessed as fit to face a criminal trial but pled guilty instead. He also targeted BT/EE, Revolut, and Uber, with the prosecution estimating damage of around £10 million from his other hacking activities.

Kurtaj will be sentenced at a later time. His guilty plea is another chapter in the activities of the Lapsus$ hacking group, which has targeted several major companies.
