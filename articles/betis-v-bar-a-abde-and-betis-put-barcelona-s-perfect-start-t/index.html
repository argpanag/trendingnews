Betis v Barça: Abde and Betis put Barcelona's perfect start to the test

Eight wins from eight matches for Barcelona, 19 points for Betis: the clash at La Cartuja could tighten the top of the table. Ez Abde faces his former club.

Barcelona have won their first eight La Liga matches. Betis have already collected 19 points. Their meeting at La Cartuja this Saturday, 17 October, on matchday 9, is therefore a genuine test of the top of the table, and not just another step for the leaders. Kick-off is at 16:30 UTC, which is 17:30 in Brazzaville.

Five points apart, a chance to close the gap

Barcelona's 24 points are the measure of a flawless start. Betis, for their part, have six wins, one draw and one defeat. A victory for the Seville side would cut the gap between the two teams from five points to two. That prospect gives the match immediate significance, without making it a premature title decider.

Both sides arrive with convincing attacking form. Barcelona beat Getafe 3–0 in their last league outing and Galatasaray 4–1 in the Champions League. Betis won 5–2 against Osasuna and took their first two European games, against Lille and then Porto, both by 3–2.

Betis must attack without losing their shape

These scores promise chances, but our reading calls for a note of caution: scoring plenty does not excuse leaving certain doors open. Betis have the tools to unsettle Barcelona, but they will also have to manage the moments when an attack breaks down. A disorganised turnover can cost more against this Barça side than against a less efficient opponent.

For the Catalans, the challenge will be to avoid letting Betis's momentum turn every ball recovery into a clear chance. The winning run brings an added demand: to keep solving new problems. La Cartuja will ask not just for a repeat of previous scorelines, but for an ability to adapt to a team with its own reasons to believe it can win.

Abde: a special occasion and a concrete role

Ez Abde, the Morocco international who previously played for Barcelona before settling at Betis, played a part in the last La Liga win, scoring against Osasuna. His return against his former club should not, however, reduce his game to a revenge story.

The sporting question is more specific: if he plays, can he turn his attempts to take the initiative into useful chances while also contributing to his team's balance? A spectacular burst of pace is only worth something if it improves the attack. Conversely, Barça will have to respect that threat without forgetting Betis's other attacking routes.

The setting is already strong enough: a perfect leader in the table, a challenger capable of closing the gap and a former Barcelona player who can influence the game.
