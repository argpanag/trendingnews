Review: Grand Theft Auto 6: A Landmark Achievement That Redefines a Console Generation

TheTools · 18.11.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

Even more impressive than we expected - despite performance compromises.

Grand Theft Auto 6 marks a pivotal technical milestone for Rockstar Games, delivering arguably the most detailed, dense and convincing recreation of an urban environment yet seen in interactive media. Built entirely from the ground up for modern hardware, the sprawling depiction of Vice City and its surrounding state of Leonida leaves behind the generational compromises that constrained its predecessor. The result is an extraordinary simulation where sheer scale matches an obsessive attention to minute visual detail.

Both the base PlayStation 5 and PS5 Pro render at a base resolution of 2560x1440 (1440p), yet the final presentations differ markedly. On PS5 Pro, Sony's PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR) machine learning upscaler reconstructs a clean image that convincingly mimics native 4K. In contrast, the standard PS5 relies on conventional spatial upscaling, resulting in a noticeably softer 1440p image that struggles to resolve fine, distant details with equal clarity.

The choice to target a strict 30fps update has proved contentious, but after spending dozens of hours in Leonida, it's clear that it was a deliberate design decision, a trade-off that allows for an extreme focus on simulation. Performance on the PS5 Pro remains exceptionally robust across typical gameplay, sustaining an unwavering 30fps lock with perfect frame pacing. The base PS5 generally holds to that target during routine exploration too, though it exhibits minor frame dips during intense driving - and sustained drops down into the mid-20s are possible during prolonged encounters with explosive material.

Central to the title's visual splendour is its heavy reliance on hardware-accelerated ray tracing, deployed with unmatched levels of investment and cohesion into the game's artwork. Rockstar has implemented full ray-traced global illumination and ray-traced reflections across both smooth and rough surfaces, which both contribute to a nearly photorealistic look. The PS5 Pro benefits further from ray-traced shadows, which eliminate the coarse cascade boundaries of traditional shadow maps to produce natural penumbra transitions and pin-sharp contact shadows.

This is arguably the best implementation of ray tracing we've seen to date, with very little of the artefacts that are characteristic of many RT implementations in Unreal Engine 5, for example, and help to transform even ordinary city blocks in graphics showcases. As we've seen from this generation, just supporting RT features isn't enough to achieve great-looking visuals. Instead, it's that union between well-implemented RT and artist work that makes GTA 6 so striking.

The resources that Rockstar has at its disposal are evident in the construction of the world, which features a believable architectural layout, detailed road infrastructure and true-to-life sightlines. Rather than feeling like a fabricated gaming playground, navigating strip malls, complex highway junctions and coastal boulevards evokes the eerie sensation of viewing Street View images or real-life footage. Even distant level-of-detail transitions remain imperceptible, preserving active traffic, pedestrian crowds and functional street lamps right up to the horizon, which is particularly convincing when visualising major thoroughfares by the trail of headlights and taillights in the distance. It's quite a feat that the largest Rockstar world map ever is also its most dense and detailed.

Atmospheric rendering sees a massive leap forward through a physically based sky and procedural volumetric cloud model evolved from Red Dead Redemption 2. Dynamic, localised weather systems form visibly over distinct districts, allowing players to watch storms gather, cast dark shadows and unleash rain across specific city blocks. The high dynamic range presentation supports specular highlights beyond 1500 nits too, delivering dramatic contrast between blinding Florida sunlight and deep ambient shadows.

Interiors feature unprecedented accessibility, with physically-based materials that capture grime, dust, glass refraction and authentic wear. Tinted glass accurately projects coloured transparent light and tinted shadows into fully furnished buildings, from story-centric strip clubs and courtrooms to bars and other local businesses. While subtle screen-space reflection layering and denoiser noise can occasionally appear on rough metallic surfaces like elevator doors, the overall indirect bounce lighting remains jaw-dropping - and key to that photorealistic look.

Rockstar has deployed a cutting-edge, physics-driven strand hair system across all characters too, unlike most games that constrain this expensive tech to their protagonists. Every bystander features individual hair strands that react realistically to momentum, wind and water immersion. The rendering overhead of this strand pipeline at such enormous scale provides further technical justification for why a 60fps mode was unfeasible on current-generation console silicon.

Everyday interactive fidelity extends to granular life-simulation mechanics that ground the player within Vice City. You can choose food and drinks from fully-stocked shops, vending machines or your own fridge, consume them, then toss the wrappers onto the floor or into the bin. Working televisions feature bespoke shows, sports and ads; at home, you can play surprisingly deep games on Jason and Lucia's console. ATMs even feature privacy screens that obscure the view from an angle. Vehicles exhibit a range of security measures, from simple hotwiring to key cloning; they have a fuel gauge and need to be filled up at gas stations; damage is handled with realistic panel deformation that directly alters handling dynamics.

NPC interactions take inspiration from classic immersive titles by introducing a dedicated contextual focus menu. Players can engage in dialogue, issue threats or exchange greetings with virtually any civilian, uncovering bespoke vocal reactions and unique personality traits. Pedestrians demonstrate remarkable behavioural autonomy too, spontaneously starting altercations after traffic accidents or fleeing into nearby shops during street shootouts. We've even been shouted at by cops for standing in the street, lining up a screenshot - how often does that happen in games?

That same realistic behaviour applies to the game's revamped law enforcement response mechanics, which abandon instant omniscience in favour of a proper emergency services network. Officers despatch from actual stations across the map, requiring line-of-sight visual confirmation before initiating an active pursuit, and use alternate entrances to surround and overwhelm. Clothing, getaway vehicles, disguises and visible weaponry heavily govern suspect recognition, letting players to alter their appearance or swap cars to evade escalating search zones.

Combat encounters exhibit striking biological and mechanical nuance through an advanced damage model. Gunshot reactions correspond precisely to impact placement, causing targets to limp, drop their weapons or collapse clutching specific wounds rather than triggering generic death animations. When firefights erupt indoors, dynamic ballistic destruction obliterates furniture and punctures drink containers, which drain appropriately-coloured liquid at a rate proportional to the number of holes they have in them.

Outside the city centre, the countryside embraces diverse motorsport disciplines that operate with dedicated gameplay depth. Venues like the Thrillbilly Mud Club offer dirt bike and off-road circuit racing driven by rich surface deformation, tactile vehicle physics and rival combat that rivals dedicated racing franchises. Street racing leagues, demolition derbies and asphalt circuits play host to a wide range of two and four-wheeled vehicles, with handling that strikes a balance between the weight of GTA 4 and the responsiveness of GTA 5. The racing and car simulation here is in many ways on par with dedicated games like Gran Turismo or Forza, though the non-infringing car designs often mix elements from multiple vehicles despite a recognisable silhouette. You even get stuff like heat haze, a mirage visible on roads that stretch out before you on a hot summer day.

The accompanying sound design matches the visual complexity through comprehensive surround sound propagation and meticulous acoustic detail. Distinct footsteps resonate differently across diverse floor materials, crowds produce layered conversational murmur and dynamic vehicle audio responds to mechanical damage. The original score is augmented with an incredible licensed soundtrack, with original music contributions from artists such as Justice, Blackpink's Jennie and Dua Lipa, with multiple fleshed-out music stations to choose from in typical Rockstar fashion.

Ultimately, Grand Theft Auto 6 represents a landmark achievement in visual realism, simulation density and world design for the current console generation. While the 30fps baseline compromises absolute responsiveness, the unprecedented simulation scope, cutting-edge ray tracing and unmatched environmental fidelity showcase an uncompromising technical vision that sets a towering standard for future open-world titles. The almost limitless budget and decade of development has somehow not gone to waste - and that's incredible.
