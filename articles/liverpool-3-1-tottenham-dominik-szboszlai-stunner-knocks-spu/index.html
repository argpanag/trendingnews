    FT: West Ham 2-3 Fulham

    Jude Winter

    Oscar Bobb struck his first Fulham goal to seal their place in the fourth round of the Carabao Cup after a thriller at West Ham.

    On an emotional night, Hammers fans paid tribute before kick-off to their former goalkeeper Ludek Miklosko - who made almost 400 appearances between 1990 and 1998 - after he died from cancer at the age of 64.

    And the hosts twice fought back in a first half containing four goals with Morato and Pablo - his first for the club - cancelling out efforts from Fulham's Rodrigo Muniz and Cesar Palacios.

    Substitute Bobb's 78th-minute strike delivered a much‑needed win for Fulham boss Alvaro Arbeloa following a winless start in the Premier League.

    Defeat ended a six‑match unbeaten run for Championship leaders West Ham, who pushed for a late leveller that never came.

  2. Dowman, 16, scores twice as Arsenal prove too good for Ipswichpublished at 22:57 BST
    22:57 BST

    FT: Ipswich Town 2-4 Arsenal

    Emlyn Begley
    BBC Sport

    Max Dowman scored twice as Arsenal eased to a Carabao Cup third-round victory at Ipswich Town.

    The 16-year-old was one of two youngsters making their first appearance of the season from the start, alongside centre-back Marli Salmon, 17.

    Dowman opened the scoring with a fine solo goal in the seventh minute. He won the ball in midfield, played a one-two with Eberechi Eze and ran through, leaving two defenders on the ground, before sending the goalkeeper the wrong way.

    Noni Madueke rifled in their second effort following Mikel Merino's flicked ball over the top.

    And after the break Dowman showed good composure to control Martin Zubimendi's pass before rolling the ball into the far bottom corner. Merino fired in Arsenal's fourth goal off the inside of the post.

    Ipswich scored twice, through Anis Mehmeti's finish from Jack Clarke's pass and Chuba Akpom's tidy turn and finish - but it was far too little and too late.

  3. Hard-fought win takes Brentford throughpublished at 22:55 BST
    22:55 BST

    FT: Reading 1-2 Brentford

    Charlotte Coates
    BBC Sport

    Brentford secured their spot in the Carabao Cup fourth round with a hard-fought win at League One Reading at the Select Car Leasing Stadium.

    Callum Wilson and Fabio Carvalho both found the net in the first half to put Brentford into a two-goal lead, before Paudie O'Connor's second-half header ensured the visitors had to work for their victory.

    It took just three minutes for the Bees to take the lead when Dango Ouattara's pass evaded the home defence and Wilson finished calmly for his second goal for the club since his summer arrival.

    The hammer blow for Reading came in the 46th minute when Ouattara grabbed his second assist of the evening to set Carvalho free and he finished past Joel Pereira with ease in his first game since rupturing his anterior cruciate ligament in November 2025.

    Reading were fortunate not to have gone three goals down when Ouattara's header from close range forced Pereira into a save and Michael Kayode made a mess of the rebound.

    The League One side had strong appeals for a penalty turned down in the 71st minute, before O'Connor headed into the bottom corner from the resulting set-piece. He had earlier had a goal from an overhead kick ruled out for a high boot in the box.

    Bees substitute Igor Thiago missed a golden opportunity to wrap up the win late on, while Jannik Schuster's header was ruled out in stoppage time for a foul.

  5. 'We've gone away from allowing referees to officiate'published at 22:50 BST
    22:50 BST

    FT: Reading 1-2 Brentford

    Brentford

    Brentford boss Keith Andrews on reaching the fourth-round of the Carabao Cup: "We took it very seriously in the last round and we did again tonight. It's a serious competition for us, we want to go as far as we can. There's no reason why we can't. We're going to attack it as hard as we can."

    On the officiating in the match: "It's been a tough week for officials as everybody is probably aware. I think we just need to let them ref the game and be human beings and make decisions because we have gone through a period in the game where I feel like they weren't actually making decisions in both boxes because if the technology we have in the game and I feel like that is a real shame.

    "I always felt as a player when there was a relationship with an official that was built on respect and being able to speak to each other in a certain way, we've gone away from allowing them to officiate and allowing them to make mistakes and I think it is a real shame. Now they are having to go back to that and it's not the habits they have had in recent years."

    On how alarming it is that there wasn't a check on Enzo Fernandez being offside for Manchester City's goal in the Manchester derby: "It's pretty alarming. The first thing was whether [Erling] Haaland was offside and then obviously [Enzo] Fernandez was. It's a shame."

  6. Peterborough edge through on penaltiespublished at 22:48 BST
    22:48 BST

    FT: Peterborough 3-3 Barnsley (7-6 on pens)

    Peterborough moved into the fourth round of the Carabao Cup for the first time since 2009 thanks to a 7-6 penalty shootout victory over Barnsley that followed a thrilling 3-3 draw between the two League One sides.

    Barnsley were gifted the lead after 61 seconds when Peterborough goalkeeper Alex Bass was charged down by Cameron McGeehan, with the Tykes captain nodding in after the ball had come off the bar.

    Peterborough equalised in the 19th minute when Lucca Mendonca's cross was pushed out by Barnsley goalkeeper Kieran Flavell as far as Harry Leonard, who prodded in.

    The hosts then completed the turnaround after 27 minutes when Harley Mills' free-kick was headed in by the unmarked Danny Ormerod.

    But the Tykes were level five minutes before half-time when Reyes Cleary beat Mendonca with ease down the left before placing his shot into the bottom corner.

    The visitors looked to have won it when Cleary bent in his second in the 74th minute, but Ormerod sent the tie to penalties by heading in Mills' cross with just two minutes remaining.

    After 12 successful spot-kicks, a tame effort from Barnsley's Luke Alker was saved by Bass, giving Harrison Jones the chance to send the hosts through, which he did.

FULL-TIMEpublished at 21:53 BST
21:53 BST

Reading 1-2 Brentford

All over and Brentford go through, but Reading certainly gave the Premier League side a game.

Dominik Szoboszlai scored his third goal of the season for Liverpool

Tottenham's difficult start to the season continued as they were knocked out of the Carabao Cup by a largely second string Liverpool side.

Roberto de Zerbi's team at least managed to score their first goal this season against Premier League opposition - having failed to find the net in four league games.

But goals from Alexis Mac Allister, Cody Gakpo and Dominik Szoboszlai helped Andoni Iraola's Liverpool progress to the fourth round at Spurs' expense.

Both managers named heavily-changed lineups with Iraola making 10 changes from the side that laboured to a goalless draw against Fulham on Saturday, with only Ronald Araujo retaining his place and Joe Gomez captaining the team on his first appearance of the season.

De Zerbi made eight changes from Spurs' goalless draw against Everton with Andy Robertson on the bench on his first return to Anfield since leaving Liverpool in the summer.

And, after Giorgi Mamardashvili had denied Spurs an opener with a great save from Lucas Bergvall, Liverpool broke the deadlock on 21 minutes when Mac Allister side-footed the ball into the top corner as it broke to him on the edge of the box.

Spurs should have levelled shortly after the half-hour mark when Mohammed Kudus blazed over from a few yards out and Liverpool made them pay after the break as they doubled their lead when Gakpo finished off a lovely move after being set up by Mac Allister.

Robertson came on to a standing Anfield ovation shortly after as De Zerbi turned to his bench and Spurs had some hope when Conor Gallagher turned the ball into the net from Mateus Fernandes' corner after 69 minutes.

It was their first goal against Premier League opposition this season and their first since August 26 in the previous round.

The 6,000-strong travelling support should had an equaliser to celebrate but Mamardashvili made another superb stop to keep out Omar Marmoush before Savio fired wide from inside the box.

And Szoboszlai ensured a Liverpool win when he chested the ball down in injury time and found the back of the net with a Steven Gerrard-esque thunderbolt from 25 yards out.

Spurs have now lost three of their six games in all competitions this season, with their only win coming in the previous round of the Carabao Cup against Charlton.

Liverpool analysis: Iraola's changes work as Reds entertain

As expected, Iraola made a number of changes for Liverpool's third game in less than a week at Anfield and he was rewarded with an energetic display from a largely second-string side.

"I made a lot of changes but I think I needed to do it," said Iraola. "We lacked a bit of energy the other day and we needed fresh legs and I am happy that all the players have given us energy and aggressiveness."

The goals may have come from familiar names but Iraola will have been pleased with how well his youngsters coped against Premier League opposition.

Rio Ngumoha, 18, was superb while Lewis Koumas, 20, was relentless in leading the press from the front and the 20-year-old came close to scoring too. Trey Nyoni, 19, and James McConnell, 22, were impressive as well.

"It is very clear what he needs to do what the team needs and the energy he shows is contagious for the rest," said Iraola, when asked about Koumas.

With Florian Wirtz rested, along with Virgil van Dijk, and watching on from the hospitality suites at Anfield, the performances from the players on the pitch ensured that Iraola was not left to regret his decision.

Mamardashvili had a nervy start but made a number of crucial saves and showed exactly why Iraola was so keen to sign him at Bournemouth.

It was a solid evening for Liverpool who will travel to Bournemouth in the league this weekend - where Iraola will be reunited with his former club - with a spring in their step as they look to continue their unbeaten start to the season.

Tottenham analysis: Spurs punished for their wastefulness in the final third

For the 20 minutes between Gallagher pulling one back for Tottenham and Szoboszlai wrapping things up for Liverpool, the travelling Spurs support and De Zerbi grew in belief but ultimately they were wasteful in the final third and that is what cost them.

Bergvall, Kudus, Marmoush and Savio all had great chances but failed to convert and they only had to look at the other end, with all three of Liverpool's goals coming from quality strikes.

"I don't know if the [moment Spurs' attack will] click is on Saturday because we have enough qualities to score many goals with these players," said De Zerbi. "It is a tough moment but we have to be stronger."

It was generally a positive display from Spurs, who had more possession, more touches in the opposition box and more shots on target than Liverpool, but their horrid start to the season continues.

If they fail to win against Aston Villa on Saturday, they will go into October without a victory against a Premier League side this season.
