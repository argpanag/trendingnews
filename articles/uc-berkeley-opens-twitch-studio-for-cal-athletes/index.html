UC Berkeley Opens Twitch Studio for Cal Athletes

TheTools · 17.10.2026 01:05 · source · permalink

Cal student-athletes now have their own professional broadcast studio on campus, built to help them stream everything from workouts to cooking contests straight to a dedicated Twitch channel. The facility, opened by the college sports livestreaming network StreamU, is loaded with real TV-production gear and a PlayStation 5 gaming lounge, marking one of the most tangible steps yet in Cal's push to turn athlete personal branding into a revenue stream.

StreamU, which is run by the NIL technology platform TheLinkU in partnership with Twitch, opened its first on-campus content studio at the University of California, Berkeley, according to Net Influencer. Student-athletes across all of Cal's sports programs can now use the space to produce content for the school's own StreamU Twitch channel, with roughly 10 hours of weekly programming already scheduled. That lineup is expected to include athlete workouts, teammate challenges, gaming sessions, cooking contests, campus tours, watch parties and game-day coverage, per the outlet's report.

The studio itself was built by UnlimitedIRL, serving as StreamU's technology partner, and it is not a bare-bones setup. It features Canon and Sony cameras, professional lighting and audio, and a Blackmagic Design video switcher that allows for multi-camera streams, alongside a portable Sony FX3 camera and LiveU encoder so athletes can broadcast from locations around campus rather than just the studio itself.

Rivera Frames Streaming As an NIL Priority

Cal football general manager and athletic director Ron Rivera said the university is excited about its venture with StreamU. “Providing student-athletes resources and infrastructure to tell their stories and enhance NIL potential is a significant priority for the football program and athletic department,” Rivera said, according to the same report. Rivera, a former NFL head coach and Cal All-American linebacker, was initially hired as the football program's general manager in March 2025 before his contract was extended through March 2028 and his role expanded to Athletic Director of Football in July 2026, as Sports Illustrated has reported, giving him oversight that runs directly to the university chancellor over football operations and revenue strategy.

Taylor Webb, chief of operations at UnlimitedIRL, said the studio lets athletes share their stories with fans and go live with professional, multi-camera shows. Webb added that the Berkeley facility is the model being brought to every StreamU campus, the Net Influencer report notes.

A Network Built On Compliance and Payout Infrastructure

Behind the cameras sits TheLinkU, the NIL technology platform founded by Austin Elrod in 2022 that manages contracts, fund distribution and reporting for the entire StreamU network. TheLinkU already handles compliance, contract management and delayed-payment services for more than 1,100 college athletes nationwide, according to Profluence Sports. Landon Goesling, chief revenue officer of StreamU, said the goal is to make athlete-driven content a regular part of Cal Athletics and to give student-athletes more ways to build audiences and create value, per Net Influencer's report.

The StreamU and Twitch partnership itself dates back to March 2026, when the two companies established a centralized platform giving university athletic departments school-controlled Twitch channels supported by subscriptions, advertising and shared sponsorships, according to a GlobeNewswire release. Cal's new facility is now positioned as the launch site for that broader network, with StreamU planning to expand its studio footprint to Texas A&M University, the University of Arkansas, Baylor University and the University of Houston in the weeks following the Berkeley opening, the release states.

Why Cal Needs New Revenue Streams

The studio's arrival lands against a backdrop of real financial strain for Cal Athletics, which posted total operating expenses exceeding $165 million against $153 million in revenue during the 2024–2025 fiscal year, producing a $24 million operating deficit in its first full season in the Atlantic Coast Conference. Increased travel costs tied to ACC membership and long-term stadium debt contributed to that gap, Hoodline previously reported. Cal has also leaned on corporate sponsorship to close the budget gap, striking a field naming-rights agreement worth up to $22.8 million with Databricks in September 2026 that renamed the playing surface Databricks Field at California Memorial Stadium.

Industry analysis from Tubefilter has framed dedicated student-athlete streaming studios as an emerging recruiting incentive, letting universities compete for top high school prospects by offering professional digital brand-building infrastructure alongside traditional training facilities. Creator content has become an increasingly popular secondary focus and backup career path for college athletes, the outlet notes.

The Bigger Picture on Athlete Pay

The Berkeley studio also arrives amid a much larger shift in how college athletes get paid. College athletes received nearly $1.8 billion in direct revenue-sharing payouts during the 2025–2026 academic year under the House v. NCAA settlement, which let participating Division I institutions distribute athletic department revenue directly to athletes up to an annual cap of roughly $20.5 million per school, according to Forbes. More than 300 Division I schools are now distributing revenue across dozens of sports under that settlement.

California has been at the forefront of this shift since well before the House settlement. The state became the first in the country to grant college athletes the legal right to earn money from their name, image and likeness when Governor Gavin Newsom signed the Fair Pay to Play Act, Senate Bill 206, into law in September 2019, per legislative records on file with LegiScan. That law barred California universities and athletic associations from revoking scholarships or athletic eligibility for athletes who earned NIL income.

Power Four conferences have since moved to formalize those arrangements. The SEC voted to adopt league-wide standardized NIL and revenue-sharing contracts in 2026, following a similar standardized memorandum model the Big Ten had already established, CBS Sports has reported. How revenue generated from Twitch channel subscriptions and ad placements will ultimately be divided between individual Cal athletes, the university and platform partners like TheLinkU and Twitch remains an open question as the new studio ramps up its programming.
