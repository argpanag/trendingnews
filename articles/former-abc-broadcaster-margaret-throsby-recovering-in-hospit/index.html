Former ABC broadcaster Margaret Throsby recovering in hospital after being hit by ebike

TheTools · 29.09.2026 07:21 · source · permalink

The 84-year-old underwent surgery after a collision with two teenagers on an electric ‘fat bike’ in the Illawarra

Former ABC broadcaster Margaret Throsby is recovering in hospital after she was struck by an electric “fat bike” while walking on a shared path in the Illawarra, south of Sydney last week.

Her daughter, musician and author Holly Throsby, said the 84-year-old required surgery after a high impact collision with two teenagers on a fat bike left her with an injured hip, thumb and head.

“The boys seemed to impact her on one side, and she went down on her other side and broke her hip,” Holly told Guardian Australia.

“It was really quite a sickening scene to witness, just because there was so much blood. Her whole face was really covered in blood, and she had two massive haematomas on her head which was super alarming.”

The speedy e-bikes with wide, thick tyres and seats big enough for two are commonly seen in coastal regions such as the Illawarra.

Holly, who was contacted by bystanders who came to Margaret’s aid, said police attended the scene at Sandon Drive, Bulli, and have kept in touch about the condition of her mother.

“When the paramedics arrived and tried to move her, she was in so much pain from the broken hip,” Holly said. “So they gave her some pain relief and got her on a gurney and got her into the ambulance, and she had surgery the next day on her hip.”

She says the teenagers looked “completely mortified and scared” and she wouldn’t want them charged.

A NSW Police spokesperson said officers spoke with the rider, a 16-year-old boy and his passenger, who remained on scene and assisted the woman. “Inquiries are ongoing into the incident”, police said.

The next day police conducted Operation Spoiler, targeting the use of illegal e-bikes and trail bikes within the Wollongong and Lake Illawarra districts.

“The high visibility operation ran from 8.30am to 6pm, and saw traffic and Highway Patrol officers, assisted by local general duties police, and specialist Trail Bike police, bicycle police, and Polair.

During the operation, officers conducted 37 e-bike stops and issued four infringement notices and two cautions in addition to 15 youth warnings.

An officer said the operation was unrelated to Throsby’s accident.

Holly called for legislation to govern the use of e-bikes by kids.

“I think the more appropriate measure would be to look at e-bike legislation, education and training and some kind of licensing system,” she said.

“For kids to be riding those bikes [which are] massive and heavy… it does seem inevitable that this kind of thing will happen.”

Margaret Throsby AO joined the ABC in 1967 and was the first woman to read national radio news in 1975 and, in 1978, the first woman to present national television news.

She hosted a high-rating morning program, The Margaret Throsby Show, on ABC Radio Sydney and in more recent years hosted various programs on ABC Classic FM and Radio National.

Holly said her mother was in excellent health and she expected her to recover well although it may take some time with rehabilitation needed for her hip.
