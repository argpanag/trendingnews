Nigella Lawson brings a new lease of life to The Great British Bake Off

TheTools · 29.09.2026 20:36 · source · permalink

With glamour, intelligence, poise – and the show’s trademark innuendo – the new judge proves perfect for the role

5/5

Are Bake Off ratings going to go through the roof now that Nigella Lawson is on board? Paul Hollywood thinks so. “She’s a breath of fresh air, certainly for my male mates. They’ve told me they’ll be tuning in for the first time in 16 years,” he said. And they’re not the only ones who will love the new-look show. Bake Off and Nigella are a match made in heaven.

She brings glamour, intelligence and poise. And, of course, a hefty dollop of innuendo. “The flavours are giving me a lot of pleasure,” she purrs. “You like neatness, I like a bit of goo,” she tells Hollywood after sampling an “irresistible” stout cake oozing with salted caramel and peanut buttercream.

Lawson is supportive in her judging but not afraid to tell the bakers where they can improve. She gives helpful advice. She declares herself thrilled to be on the Bake Off team: “I’m loving it. I’m full of cake and hope and expectation,” she says at the outset.

If Hollywood’s nose has been put out of joint by Lawson’s arrival, he’s doing his best not to show it. But she is clearly the star of the show in the bakers’ eyes. They no longer covet a Hollywood Handshake. “I want something from Nigella. I don’t think I will be satisfied by a little handshake off Paul. I want a little Nigella air kiss,” declared Tom, a “self-proclaimed northern diva” (uh-oh).

There’s also a “self-styled daddy of Birmingham drag” in the line-up. Can everyone stop with the self-styling? Gen Z loves this sort of thing – and will definitely love law student Mo, who describes himself as having “lots of baggage, but the good baggage, like Louis Vuitton baggage”. 

I prefer the quieter types, such as 67-year-old Gary. He is one of the few older contestants, who set about producing some faultless bakes. Gary’s tip is to add mayonnaise to your buttercream mix. Yannis impresses with his artistic skill in the showstopper challenge, which is to bake a self-portrait cake. His creation is a tribute to pointillism. He does talk about himself in the third person, though, which is a worry at this early stage.

The quirkiest character is Danni, who says: “Indeed-a-roonie!” and appears utterly guileless. “I’m so, so, so excited to show you my bog water!” she trills. It involves magnolia petals, stinging nettles and something called “Sticky Willy”. Nigella’s face remains impassive in the face of someone else’s innuendo. Danni explains that Sticky Willy is a weed that sticks to your clothes. I daren’t Google this, so will have to take her word for it.

It’s a chaotic first show for the bakers, with disasters everywhere. More than one cake ends up on the floor. Poor Danni watches in horror as her vegan showstopper slowly, agonisingly collapses (not helped, presumably, by the fact the show was filmed during the heatwave). She cries. “It’s just cake, my love,” someone says. But it isn’t, is it? This is Bake Off. It’s cake and hope and expectation.

The Great British Bake Off continues on Channel 4 on Tuesdays at 8pm
