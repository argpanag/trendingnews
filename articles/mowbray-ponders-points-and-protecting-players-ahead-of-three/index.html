Mowbray ponders points and protecting players ahead of three-game week for Rovers

TheTools · 10.10.2026 13:24 · source · permalink

Tony Mowbray faces a selection headache ahead of Blackburn Rovers' return to Championship action against Cardiff City, with the Rovers boss weighing up the need to protect his international stars against the importance of building momentum at Ewood Park.

After a three-week break between domestic fixtures, Rovers head into a demanding spell of three Championship games in seven days. But several members of Mowbray's squad already arrive with heavy mileage in their legs after representing their countries.

The Rovers head coach is mindful that three of his four internationals - Balazs Toth (Hungary), Andri Gudjohnsen (Iceland) and Tom Atcheson (Northern Ireland) played four matches during the break and fears the fixture schedule could leave players vulnerable to injury, while their a concerns of Kristi Montgomery's lack of game time for Scotland.

And Mowbray says communication will be key in determining who should play, when and for how long.

"The bottom line is people skills really, I think. You've got to talk to the players, how they're feeling, how is the camp, how did you do it?," he explained.

"Three of them played all four games and one lad didn't have a minute. So there are different issues around one's almost de-trained, needs to play games, but if you throw him in for three games in a week he's probably going to get injured having not done anything for three weeks other than jog up and down the pitch after a game.

"And with the others, if they've already played four games there's a danger of them playing seven games in very, very short period of time, with three midweeks back to back.

"It seems a bit tight to me.

"I'm pretty sure along the way some of them players who have been away on international duty will pick up injuries if they attempt to play all three games next week."

That leaves Mowbray walking a fine line between preservation and ambition as Rovers look to make Ewood Park a difficult place to visit once again.

"I need to protect the group but I need to win the game as well," he said.

"The club, we want to get to a point where any team who comes to Ewood Park knows they're in a really tough game and a difficult fixture for them."

With Cardiff first up before trips to Bristol City and West Bromwich Albion, Mowbray must decide where risk becomes reward as Rovers look to restart their campaign on a winning note.

But he is hoping to have as much information at his disposal as possible in order to assist that decision-making process.

"We're trying to get the data from the national teams and seeing how much sprinting they did, how much running they've done and making a call really on what we think the team this weekend is," he explained.

"I'm constantly following my phone when the games are being played and seeing who was still on the pitch, who was getting substituted off or substituted on. But generally they've all come through it okay. We've just got some decisions to make, because obviously playing Saturday, Tuesday, Saturday, Tuesday, and then to come back to work for two days and thinking they're going to play a Saturday, Tuesday, Saturday again, is a big ask for a few of them, so we'll wait and see.

"What you don't want to do is get any of these important players for us injured. So we'll make some calls on who's going to play, who's going to sit on the bench, who's going to play potentially 60 and who's going to play 30 and we'll see how it goes."
