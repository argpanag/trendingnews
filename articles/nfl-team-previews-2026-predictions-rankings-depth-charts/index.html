NFL team previews 2026: Predictions, rankings, depth charts

TheTools · 07.09.2026 11:49 · source · permalink

The 2026 NFL season kicks off Wednesday with a Super Bowl rematch featuring the reigning champion Seattle Seahawks hosting the New England Patriots (8:20 p.m. ET, NBC). Then, on Thursday, the NFL will have its first-ever regular-season game in Australia, where the Super Bowl-favorite Los Angeles Rams will take on the San Francisco 49ers (8:35 p.m. ET, Netflix).

To welcome back football, we're previewing every single team in the league. Our NFL Nation reporters identified strengths and concerns for all 32 rosters, and then they laid out what would make 2026 a successful season. Analyst Matt Bowen gave a fantasy nugget for each team, analytics writer Seth Walder made 32 bold predictions, and ESPN Research provided QB stats to know.

Plus, our Football Power Index predicted chances for every team to win its division, make the playoffs and more. The model even suggested best- and worst-case scenarios for all 32 teams, based on the 90th- and 10th-percentile outcomes across thousands of simulations, respectively.

This is everything you need to know for the start of the season, which will conclude Feb. 14 at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California, for Super Bowl LXI on ESPN.

1. Los Angeles Rams

Chances to make the playoffs: 84.9%
Chances to win division: 50.4%
Projected wins: 11.3
Strength of schedule: Third hardest
2025 record: 12-5

Biggest strength: The passing game. There are several options to choose from on this star-studded roster, but it's hard not to pick the reigning MVP. Quarterback Matthew Stafford is coming off the best season of his career and will again have one of the best receiver pairs in the NFL: Davante Adams and Puka Nacua. Last season, Adams led the league with 14 receiving touchdowns, and Nacua led the NFL with 129 catches. -- Sarah Barshop

Biggest concern: Offensive tackle. The Rams don't have much depth at tackle, which could matter if left tackle Alaric Jackson is suspended. The NFL is conducting its own investigation into an alleged incident of domestic violence by Jackson, who is not facing criminal prosecution because his case was assigned for diversion. Third-round pick Keagen Trost, who was expected to back up Jackson and Warren McClendon Jr., left the preseason opener with a hamstring injury. Coach Sean McVay said he's hopeful Trost will return to start the season. -- Barshop

QB stat to know: Stafford is coming off a career-high 46 passing touchdowns -- his third season with 40 or more passing scores. Those three seasons are tied for the most by any QB in NFL history along with Aaron Rodgers and Tom Brady. However, Stafford has seen major regression following each season of 40 passing TDs -- including going from 41 to 20 in 2012 and going from 41 to 10 in 2022. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 14-3
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 9-8

This season is a success if ... the Rams win the Super Bowl. The Rams took steps to maximize the remaining time that Stafford wants to play, so expectations are sky-high for Los Angeles. -- Barshop

One key fantasy nugget: The Rams regularly isolate Adams in scoring position given his ability to win off the release. His 14 touchdown receptions were the second most in a season this century (Randy Moss had 16 in 2007). -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Tight end Terrance Ferguson will catch eight touchdown passes. I'm buying the hype here because it just makes too much sense. The Rams need a third pass catcher behind Nacua and Adams, and with a year of experience in the league after being a 2025 second-round pick, Ferguson could emerge as that guy. If either of those two receivers misses time, his ceiling could be even higher. -- Walder

2. Buffalo Bills

Chances to make the playoffs: 84.1%
Chances to win division: 67.1%
Projected wins: 10.7
Strength of schedule: 14th easiest
2025 record: 12-5

Biggest strength: Josh Allen. It's pretty simple. The Bills have an opportunity to win a Super Bowl once again this year, despite a first-time head coach, and that's because of who is playing quarterback. Allen, 30, is going into his ninth NFL season and has a new No. 1 receiver in DJ Moore. Breaking records has become commonplace for the 2024 MVP, and Allen gives the team a chance in every game. -- Alaina Getzenberg

Biggest concern: It is a transition year for the new defensive scheme. Coordinator Jim Leonhard has been open about the fact this year's defense will not fully look like his vision for the unit as it transitions from a 4-3 to 3-4 base. There is a mix of new players who fit the scheme specifically and carryovers from the previous regime. How the group comes together will go a long way in determining the unit's success. -- Getzenberg

QB stat to know: Allen's dual-threat ability is unlike anything the NFL has seen. Allen already has 299 passing and rushing TDs combined in his first eight seasons, 40 more than the next-closest player (Patrick Mahomes, 259). In fact, he already has the most through a player's first nine seasons (next closest: Peyton Manning, 288). -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 13-4
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 8-9

This season is a success if ... the Bills reach the Super Bowl. Unfortunately for new coach Joe Brady, that's the bar. Maximizing Allen's tenure is the priority, and anything comparable to or less than what Sean McDermott accomplished won't make for a fully successful season. -- Getzenberg

One key fantasy nugget: Allen gives fantasy managers a major boost when the Bills have the ball on the doorstep of the end zone. He converted 22 of 28 true goal-line carries (78.6%) over the past three seasons. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Wide receiver Keon Coleman will record 750 or more receiving yards and at least five touchdowns -- both career highs. Earlier this offseason I thought there was a decent chance he'd be on a different team by now. But Coleman also hasn't been that bad in his first two years, posting a 49 overall score in ESPN's receiver score (just below average) and 1.6 yards per route run. Even though the Bills added Moore, I could see Coleman having a mini breakout in Year 3. -- Walder

3. Seattle Seahawks

Chances to make the playoffs: 68.0%
Chances to win division: 26.3%
Projected wins: 10.2
Strength of schedule: Sixth hardest
2025 record: 14-3

Biggest strength: The defense remains the strength even though it lost three players -- safety Coby Bryant, cornerback Riq Woolen and outside linebacker Boye Mafe - from the unit that allowed the fewest points in the NFL last season. The Seahawks still have plenty of difference-makers on that side of the ball, and they still have one of the game's brightest defensive minds, coach Mike Macdonald, calling the shots. -- Brady Henderson

Biggest concern: It's more of a question mark than a concern, but Sam Darnold and the offense will be relying on a first-time coordinator in Brian Fleury. Macdonald's choice to replace Klint Kubiak, Fleury will be running an offense that's similar to his predecessor's, but he will almost certainly have to endure some growing pains as he calls plays for the first time. -- Henderson

QB stat to know: Darnold has won 14 games in each of the past two seasons, making him one of two quarterbacks in NFL history with consecutive 14-win seasons along with Tom Brady in 2003 and 2004. No quarterback has had three consecutive 14-win seasons. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 13-4
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 8-9

This season is a success if ... Seattle at least gets to the NFC Championship Game. We'll set that as the bar for success, since only seven defending champions since 2000 have reached the conference title game. -- Henderson

One key fantasy nugget: Jaxon Smith-Njigba was the best receiver I studied last season, showing the ability to uncover at every level of the field. Plus, he can take the top off the defense with a league-leading 18 receptions on throws of 20-plus air yards in 2025. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Jadarian Price will finish the season as a top-eight running back in terms of fantasy points. He's a first-round pick at a position that doesn't usually have them, and Zach Charbonnet is coming back from a serious injury. The opportunity -- and talent -- is there for Price. -- Walder

4. Baltimore Ravens

Chances to make the playoffs: 79.8%
Chances to win division: 50.3%
Projected wins: 10.7
Strength of schedule: Fifth easiest
2025 record: 8-9

Biggest strength: QB Lamar Jackson. The two-time MVP is the biggest barometer for success. In the four seasons in which Jackson has played more than 13 games, the Ravens have captured three AFC North titles and two No. 1 seeds in the AFC. In four seasons in which he has played 13 or fewer games, Baltimore has one division title and has missed the playoffs twice. So, the key is to keep Jackson healthy. -- Jamison Hensley

Biggest concern: Center. The Ravens have gone from a three-time Pro Bowler in Tyler Linderbaum to a big question mark in front of Jackson. It looks like Baltimore will start the season with Jovaughn Gwyn, who has played 11 offensive snaps in three seasons. But the Ravens could eventually turn to Ethan Pocic, who has 97 career starts but is coming off an Achilles injury that he suffered in December. Either way, it's not an ideal situation. -- Hensley

QB stat to know: Jackson is coming off a losing record as a starter for the first time of his career (6-7 in 13 starts). Over the last eight weeks, Jackson had a 6-6 TD-to-interception ratio and was off target on a league-worst 22% of his pass attempts. However, he did average 8.4 YPA (third in NFL) over the season. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 13-4
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 8-9

This season is a success if ... the Ravens advance to at least the AFC Championship Game. Baltimore fired coach John Harbaugh because of a lack of deep playoff runs. The Ravens need to avoid quick exits under Jesse Minter. -- Hensley

One key fantasy nugget: With Jackson's electric dual-threat ability and a play-action-heavy system under new coordinator Declan Doyle, the Ravens quarterback is primed to produce big numbers. Jackson completed a league-best 80% of his play-action throws last season. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: The Ravens' defense will rank in the top four in EPA per play. That's a pretty big jump from last season, when it finished 16th. But in Minter's first season with the Chargers, he took a defense that ranked 29th and brought it to seventh best. And Minter probably has more talent to work with in Baltimore. -- Walder

5. San Francisco 49ers

Chances to make the playoffs: 63.8%
Chances to win division: 23.2%
Projected wins: 10.0
Strength of schedule: 11th hardest
2025 record: 12-5

Biggest strength: Offensive talent and continuity. Eight starters return from an offense that ranked ninth in scoring, eighth in offensive EPA and seventh in yards last season. In the spots with new additions, the 49ers brought in future Hall of Fame receiver Mike Evans and intriguing rookie wideout De'Zhaun Stribling. Left guard is a question, and injuries always loom, but this is a group capable of bigger and better in 2026. -- Nick Wagoner

Biggest concern: The pass rush. In 2025, San Francisco finished last in the NFL in sacks (20). It brought in defensive tackle Osa Odighizuwa, but it's still fair to wonder if the 49ers have enough on the edge with Nick Bosa and Mykel Williams coming off ACL injuries and rookie Romello Height replacing Bryce Huff. If the pass rush isn't markedly better, it could be another long season for the defense. -- Wagoner

QB stat to know: Brock Purdy is coming off a season that saw him start just nine games because of injury but post his highest interception rate of his career; throwing an interception on 3.5% of his pass attempts. Purdy has seen his interception percentage increase in each season of his career from 2.4 to 2.5 to 2.6 to 3.5. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 12-5
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 7-10

This season is a success if ... the Niners return to the Super Bowl. Perhaps unfair given their division, but this is the Niners' standard, and the clock is ticking on their veteran stars. Since 2020, San Francisco has made four NFC Championship Games and two Super Bowl appearances. -- Wagoner

One key fantasy nugget: Deebo Samuel Sr.'s play speed has declined, but in coach Kyle Shanahan's offense, the 49ers can give him a long runway to find space on crossers. He also still has the ability to win weeks, with four games of 20-plus points last season. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Rookie running back Kaelon Black will record at least 650 rushing yards as the 49ers -- for real this time -- take it easy on Christian McCaffrey's workload. That would be 250 more rushing yards than last year's backup running back, Brian Robinson Jr. -- Walder

6. Los Angeles Chargers

Chances to make the playoffs: 66.2%
Chances to win division: 36.1%
Projected wins: 9.8
Strength of schedule: Seventh hardest
2025 record: 11-6

Biggest strength: Offensive tackle. This is one of two position groups (edge rusher is the other) on the Chargers' roster with multiple Pro Bowlers, with Joe Alt and Rashawn Slater both in their prime and bookending the line. With these two together, the Chargers are a different offense, and they are a big reason coordinator Mike McDaniel took this job. Behind Alt and Slater is fourth-round pick Travis Burke, who has emerged in the preseason as what coach John Harbaugh called a "Game 1 starter" and might serve as the team's tackle if veteran Trey Pipkins III starts at guard. -- Kris Rhim

Biggest concern: Interior offensive line. Chargers fans are all too used to this concern. This group has been this team's Achilles' heel for what feels like forever. General manager Joe Hortiz brought in an entirely new interior this season, highlighted by the signing of center Tyler Biadasz. But Biadasz suffered a season-ending ACL injury in his left knee, moving rookie Jake Slaughter, who was in contention to start at left guard, to starting center. Now, Harbaugh says the team might platoon at left guard, raising more questions about the weakest position group on the roster. -- Rhim

QB stat to know: Justin Herbert was sacked a career-high 54 times last season and contacted 206 times, the most of any QB in the NFL. The Chargers ranked last in the league in pass block win rate, according to ESPN Analytics, and the team used 32 different offensive line combinations, tied with the Browns for most. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 12-5
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 7-10

This season is a success if ... the Chargers get a playoff win. Since their last playoff win in 2019, the Chargers have been a team whose success has largely been rooted in hypotheticals. Instead of reaching their potential, they've been on the wrong end of some of the most embarrassing losses in NFL history. A playoff win would signal that the paradigm has finally shifted for this seemingly cursed franchise. -- Rhim

One key fantasy nugget: Running back Omarion Hampton is one of my top breakout candidates this season. With a healthier front and a new system under McDaniel that uses heavy two-back personnel, Hampton can build off his 15.2 points per game in 2025. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Alt will win Protector of the Year. This was my bold prediction in this space last year, and I feel like I was on track for the six games he played, so I'm trying my hand again. If the Chargers improve on offense, deserved credit will go to the return of Alt and Slater. -- Walder

7. Green Bay Packers

Chances to make the playoffs: 61.9%
Chances to win division: 31.3%
Projected wins: 9.9
Strength of schedule: 14th hardest
2025 record: 9-7-1

Biggest strength: Jordan Love's connection with his pass catchers. Last season, he had too many mouths to feed, so they streamlined the receiver room, and it appears to have helped Love to develop chemistry with his top receivers -- Christian Watson, Jayden Reed and Matthew Golden. Throw in tight end Tucker Kraft, and Love should be able to thrive. The Packers haven't had a 1,000-yard receiver since Davante Adams' last season (2021). They might have one (or two) now. -- Rob Demovsky

Biggest concern: Can they survive without Josh Jacobs and Micah Parsons to start the season? Jacobs had the most carries out of the Green Bay running backs last season, and who knows when -- or whether -- he'll come off the commissioner exempt list and be available. The defense will have to find a way to generate pass rush without Parsons for at least the first month of the season. That will be new defensive coordinator Jonathan Gannon's greatest challenge. -- Demovsky

QB stat to know: Among 26 quarterbacks who made at least 10 starts last season, Love ranked second in QBR (73), trailing only Drake Maye (77). -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 12-5
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 7-10

This season is a success if ... the Packers can do better than the No. 7 seed in the playoffs and make a legitimate run. They have been the last wild-card team three years in a row and have only one playoff win to show for it. Nothing less than the second weekend of the playoffs will be considered a success. -- Demovsky

One key fantasy nugget: Watson's injury history could create some pause, but he brings a high ceiling to fantasy lineups given his vertical speed and expanding route tree. In 10 appearances last season, Watson scored 13.2 points per game; he topped the 20-point mark three times. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Love will finish in the top 3 in MVP voting. Love has always been a tad underrated relative to his numbers, but I suspect if he puts up a high-end QBR like he did last year (third best), he'll rise up ballots, too. That he has an elite offensive scheme designer in Matt LaFleur helps, too. -- Walder

8. Detroit Lions

Chances to make the playoffs: 71.4%
Chances to win division: 40.4%
Projected wins: 10.5
Strength of schedule: Second easiest
2025 record: 9-8

Biggest strength: The offense. Although Detroit enters the season under new offensive coordinator Drew Petzing, it still has veteran QB Jared Goff running the show along with an elite group of playmakers -- including Jahmyr Gibbs, Jameson Williams, Amon-Ra St. Brown and Sam LaPorta -- capable of scoring against any opponent. Despite a 9-8 finish last season, Detroit still finished top five in points per game (28.3). -- Eric Woodyard

Biggest concern: Injuries. Several key players will begin the season on the injured reserve list, including RB Isiah Pacheco (back), center Cade Mays (wrist) and safeties Brian Branch (Achilles) and Kerby Joseph (knee), meaning they'll miss a minimum of the first four games. After finishing last in the NFC North, the Lions could benefit from an easier schedule, but they'll have to adjust until at least Week 5. -- Woodyard

QB stat to know: Improved pass protection for Goff will be key. Last season, he had a QBR of 76 from a clean pocket, but that declined to 14 when he was pressured. That was the second-largest drop-off in the NFL ahead of only Mac Jones. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 13-4
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 8-9

This season is a success if ... the Lions return to the postseason. Detroit missed the playoffs for the first time since 2022 last season. The organization has committed over $1 billion in contracts and extensions over the past two years and is seeking a return on its investment. -- Woodyard

One key fantasy nugget: St. Brown remains one of the league's most consistent receivers with at least 1,000 receiving yards in four straight seasons. And he has averaged 18.8 fantasy points during that stretch. St. Brown will again be the top target in an explosive Lions offense. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Edge Aidan Hutchinson will win Defensive Player of the Year. Hutchinson ranked fourth in pass rush win rate last season, plays a ton of snaps and the Lions face the second-easiest schedule. Put that all together, and the result should be a ton of sacks for Hutch. -- Walder

9. Kansas City Chiefs

Chances to make the playoffs: 68.0%
Chances to win division: 37.7%
Projected wins: 9.9
Strength of schedule: 16th easiest
2025 record: 6-11

Biggest strength: Beyond Patrick Mahomes, the Chiefs have revitalized their rushing attack with Kenneth Walker III, Emari Demercado and rookie Emmett Johnson. All three are an upgrade from the position a year ago. And all three have the skills to break tackles for explosive gains to better support Mahomes. The Chiefs need to run more often behind one of the NFL's best interior group of linemen -- center Creed Humphrey, right guard Trey Smith and left guard Kingsley Suamataia. -- Nate Taylor

Biggest concern: A lack of depth could sink the Chiefs. Unlike previous years, the drop off in talent on the offensive line is drastic. Jaylon Moore, a five-year veteran, appears to be the team's only hope at right tackle. The team also cannot afford to have a significant injury to linebackers Nick Bolton and Drue Tranquill since the rest of the players at that position are inexperienced. The same could be said at safety. -- Taylor

QB stat to know: While Mahomes completed a career-worst 63% of his passes and threw just 22 touchdowns last season -- his lowest mark as a starter -- he made up for it with his legs. Mahomes set career bests in rushing yards per game (30.1) and rushing touchdowns (5), generating 25% of his fantasy points on the ground, the fifth-highest percentage among QBs with at least 12 games played. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 12-5
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 7-10

This season is a success if ... Mahomes stays healthy after suffering a season-ending knee injury last season. The Chiefs will also need their rookies -- cornerback Mansoor Delane, defensive end R Mason Thomas and defensive tackle Peter Woods -- to improve throughout the season. -- Taylor

One key fantasy nugget: Walker has real upside in coach Andy Reid's offense as a dual-threat running back. Walker caught 31 passes last season in Seattle, and that number should elevate through screens and schemed targets in Kansas City's passing game. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: The Rashee Rice season of fantasy managers dreams finally happens. Rice will stay healthy and collect 1,300 receiving yards, establishing himself as one of the game's top wideouts. Last year Rice had a 68 open score but just a 23 catch score. The latter is less stable year-to-year, so if Rice can just get a little contested catch luck to go his way -- and he stays on the field -- he should be in for a big year. -- Walder

10. Philadelphia Eagles

Chances to make the playoffs: 62.8%
Chances to win division: 46.3%
Projected wins: 9.8
Strength of schedule: 16th hardest
2025 record: 11-6

Biggest strength: The defense. Entering Year 3 under Vic Fangio, this group is well-versed in the scheme and has the talent to maximize it. There are All-Pro caliber players at all three levels, from Jalen Carter up front to linebacker Zack Baun in the middle to Cooper DeJean and Quinyon Mitchell on the back end. The secondary has a chance to be special with cornerback Riq Woolen, the former Seahawk, joining the fray. This defense has top-three potential. -- Tim McManus

Biggest concern: How Jalen Hurts & Co. will take to the new offense. The system has been overhauled under Sean Mannion to a largely under-center operation featuring heavy play-action throws over the middle and a timing-based passing attack. The results were mixed this summer, as Hurts leans into elements of the game he has not majored in previously, and the group overall works to sync up. Expect some boom or bust early. -- McManus

QB stat to know: Since his second year as the Eagles' full-time starting quarterback in 2022, Hurts has led the team to more than 10 wins each season. This season, he is looking to become the seventh QB to win double-digit games in 5 consecutive seasons. The only active QB with a longer streak is Josh Allen with seven. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 12-5
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 7-10

This season is a success if ... the offensive line gets back to its dominant ways and Saquon Barkley once again leads a highly efficient ground game. If that happens, this team will be a contender. -- McManus

One key fantasy nugget: With A.J. Brown now on the Patriots, DeVonta Smith elevates to the top role in Philly's pass game. A slippery route runner with vertical ability, he caught 77 passes and topped 1,000 receiving yards last season. -- Bowen

Bold prediction for 2026: Jonathan Greenard will record at least 0.8 sacks per game. I'm framing it that way because it's unclear when Greenard will be back from a pec strain in the offseason. Once he's on the field, I expect big things from Greenard playing alongside the rest of the defense, even after a down year in Minnesota. For context: 0.8 sacks per game translates to 13.6 sacks if he were to play all 17 games. -- Walder

11. Dallas Cowboys

Chances to win division: 33.9%
Projected wins: 9.2

2025 record: 7-9-1

Biggest strength: The offense led by QB Dak Prescott. The Cowboys can beat defenses in multiple ways with the big pass plays to wide receivers CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens, by the run game with Javonte Williams, and if they need to paper-cut a defense and execute down the field, they can do that. Parker is a first-time defensive coordinator. There is not a dominant edge rusher. Linebacker depth is a question, as is cornerback depth. There will be games when the offense is stifled, so can the defense win low-scoring games? -- Archer

Since he entered the NFL in 2016, the only other players with those numbers in 4 different seasons are Patrick Mahomes and Jared Goff. -- ESPN Research

Best-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 12-5
Worst-case scenario for 2026, per FPI: 7-10

This season is a success if ... they make the playoffs. The Super Bowl drought might continue, but the postseason is doable with the talent on hand. If this happens, it means Schottenheimer's program is going in the right direction. -- Archer

One key fantasy nugget: Williams shouldn't be overlooked in an offense that just racks up points. A downhill hammer as a runner, he scored 15.2 fantasy points per game last season. Plus, he found the end zone 11 times. -- Bowen

13. Houston Texans

Chances to make the playoffs: 58.9%
Chances to win division: 40.5%
Projected wins: 9.3
Strength of schedule: 10th hardest
2025 record: 12-5

Biggest strength: How could it be anything but Houston's defense? It's arguably the best in the NFL, and it brought back nine of 11 starters. The unit holds three All-Pros in cornerback Derek Stingley Jr., to pair with their defensive end duo of Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter. There are also three other Pro Bowlers on the unit with linebacker Azeez Al-Shaair, safety Calen Bullock and cornerback Kamari Lassiter. -- DJ Bien-Aime

Biggest concern: Well the general consensus would be quarterback C.J. Stroud, but in reality it's the people protecting Stroud. When he's protected, he has produced a QBR of 66 in his career (15th since 2023), but when he's under pressure, it drops to 27.
