Utah Jazz vs Memphis Grizzlies: Is Ty Jerome Man or Machine?

TheTools · 26.10.2026 18:48 · source · permalink
  • Utah Jazz Game Coverage

The Jazz have the chance to get some revenge against the NBA’s leading scorer

The Jazz have an opportunity for some early season revenge against the Memphis Grizzlies, who took them down on opening night. While at the time the loss seemed rather disappointing (and a win still obviously would’ve been preferred), it has become less surprising in hindsight - the Grizzlies, led by the greatest shotmaker to ever grace the court in the form of Ty Jerome, are one of the 6 3-0 teams left in the association. They haven’t played a murderers row of opponents thus far, but Memphis has looked the part nevertheless. Jerome has taken a leap (that was easier to predict than you might think), Cameron Boozer has looked as NBA-ready as expected, and Zach Edey has quietly been perhaps the best defensive player in the league up to this point. I projected this team as a sneaky play-in contender, and I still think that is their likely range, but Grizzlies fans have to be feeling pretty good about the longer term outlook of this core.

What will the Jazz to do to right the opening night wrongs and end the Grizzlies early-season win streak? I’m curious to see what the starting lineup looks like. Jaren Jackson Jr. may legitimately be eaten alive by Zach Edey on live TV, but starting Ace Bailey over Jaxson Hayes does allow us to put the most talent on the court at one time. Additionally, Ace showed more life than any other Jazz defender when slotted against Ty Jerome, so fitting him into the starting lineup somehow seems beneficial. I would be curious to see a Keyonte-Ace-Lauri-JJJ-Jaxson lineup. It would allow Ace to best use his additive, role player skills next to the stars, and would allow Darryn to potentially take on some more on-ball usage with bench lineups.

Giving Darryn opportunities should be a point of emphasis in this game. He’s found ways to provide marginal value here and there, but he hasn’t seemed to have found a flow offensively. Perhaps all that DP needs to gain the confidence necessary to really let it fly from long-range is one game where he doesn’t feel constrained by the offensive games of the stars around him. He’s young, but Darryn was drafted to make decisions and power offense, and he has not done that yet. Hopefully, coach Hardy recognizes that the ceiling of this iteration of the Jazz is dictated by Peterson, so even if there is a learning curve early on, he needs to be given reps in a variety of contexts.
