Critical incident declared at Nottingham hospitals amid long waits at QMC A&E | West Bridgford Wire

TheTools · 23.11.2026 19:10 · source · permalink

Nottingham University Hospitals (NUH) has declared a Critical Incident today, Monday 23 November, following a sharp increase in cases of flu and COVID-19. 

The increase in cases of flu and COVID-19, alongside sustained pressures and high admissions, has resulted in significant demand and delays, in particular at the Queen’s Medical Centre’s (QMC) Emergency Department (ED).  

 

  • The Trust has seen an increase in flu cases in its hospitals in the last week, rising from 14 to 60. COVID-19 cases have increased from 53 to 73. 
  • This follows a week of high attendances into our ED, causing significant pressure on our staff and long waits for our patients.  
  • The Trust currently has around 200 patients in its ED, with patients waiting in ambulances for up to four hours. 
  • Almost 100 patients are waiting for beds on wards, and the Trust has no available capacity in resus or major trauma.   
  • Nottingham healthcare partners across the system, including the East Midlands Ambulance Service, have also been experiencing extremely high levels of demand and this has compounded issues with the flow of patients through our hospitals.  

Andrew Hall, Chief Operating Officer at NUH, said: “We have seen flu and COVID cases rising significantly in the last week, creating further pressure on an already difficult situation, particularly for those staff and patients in our emergency department. These numbers have surpassed the predicted flu figures for this time of year and are higher than we saw this same time last year. 

“We have high numbers of patients waiting to be seen and waiting for beds on our wards, which carries with it an increased risk of harm. 

“Our staff are doing everything they can to mitigate these pressures by creating more space for patients who need our care, asking colleagues to support with additional shifts, looking for every opportunity to discharge patients safely and working with our partners to improve flow out of the hospital. I want to thank all of our colleagues who are working incredibly hard to do this, while managing patient safety and continuing to provide the best possible care.  

“But there are things that the public can do for us too. Getting patients out of hospital when they are ready is vital to improving our position. If you have a family member in our hospitals who is ready to return home, we urge you to collect them as soon as possible. This helps those patients returning home, who will recover better, and those who urgently need our care.”  

How you can help:  

  • If your relative is due to be discharged from hospital and needs to be collected, please do so as early as possible. This will help our teams and free up a hospital bed for someone waiting to be admitted.  
  • Only call 999 or attend ED for serious accidents and for life threatening emergencies.  
  • Where the situation is not life-threatening, alternative support will be available through NHS111 online or by calling 111.  
  • Urgent Treatment Centres (UTC) treat injuries including sprains, strains, suspected fractures, bites, cuts, scalds and other non-emergency conditions. Waiting times are usually much shorter than ED  
  • Pharmacies can help with allergies, constipation, headaches and many other ailments, over 200 pharmacies in Nottingham and Nottinghamshire can provide NHS medicines for seven common conditions through the Pharmacy First service. Depending on your age, this includes Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs) in women, earache, shingles, sinusitis, impetigo and sore throats. See the website for details.  
  • Please do not visit your loved ones in hospital if you have any flu or other respiratory illness symptoms – please wait until you are better to visit them.
