Scottsdale police arrest man accused of targeting homes of people on vacation

TheTools · 08.10.2026 00:16 · source · permalink

SCOTTSDALE, AZ (AZFamily) — Scottsdale police have arrested a man in connection with a string of home break-ins. The suspect is believed to have targeted the homes of people who had posted on social media that they were out of town or on vacation.

Suspect allegedly scrolled social media to find targets

According to court records, 41-year-old Tyler Johnston would scroll his social media feed, and when he saw someone post they were out of town, he would target their home and break in.

Sean McGraw had his central Phoenix home broken into this past Sunday. His front door is currently boarded up after it was shattered to pieces.

“It is a very violating feeling having a criminal in your master bedroom and bathroom and closets. Its indescribable. I’ve never felt this way before,” McGraw said.

Surveillance video shows break-in

Surveillance video shows a man wearing a ski mask and dark clothing throwing rocks at McGraw’s home early Sunday morning, then walking up to his front door and smashing the glass with a big rock to get inside. Police believe the man in the mask was Johnston.

McGraw thinks a social media post his wife made, saying they were out of town, is the reason their home was hit.

“He’s targeting people that are out of the house on vacation. It does seem like he’s using social media to target his own friends and network and rob them,” McGraw said.

Suspect arrested after tracking to Scottsdale office

Johnston was arrested shortly after Sunday’s break-in, after police tracked him to his office in Scottsdale. He has been charged with that burglary and another in Scottsdale, but authorities suspect he may be involved with more than a dozen other break-ins across the Valley, all involving people who were out of town and posted about it on social media.

Johnston is accused of stealing more than $100,000 worth of cash, jewelry and other valuables.

McGraw is hoping what happened to him will serve as a warning to everyone to be extra careful about what they post on their social media feed.

“Wait to post about your vacation until you are back from vacation. You are telling somebody, ‘I’m not home, come on in,’ and that’s just putting yourself and your family at risk,” McGraw said.

Police tracked suspect’s car near break-in locations

Court records indicate police were able to catch Johnston after spotting his car near or around a number of homes that were broken into.

Johnston has been released on a $40,000 bond while he awaits his next court hearing and the possibility of additional charges filed.
