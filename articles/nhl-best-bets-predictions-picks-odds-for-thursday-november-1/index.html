NHL Best Bets, Predictions, Picks, Odds for Thursday, November 19

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:08 · source · permalink

We went 0-for-2 last night, but tonight, we have an NHL jumbo 12-game slate, and here, I’ll be dishing out five of my favorite picks.

Tonight, we’ll be covering the Los Angeles Kings vs Toronto Maple Leafs, St. Louis Blues vs Pittsburgh Penguins, Minnesota Wild vs Calgary Flames, Anaheim Ducks vs Colorado Avalanche and Vancouver Canucks vs Seattle Kraken.

Let’s take a look at my NHL best bets, predictions and picks for Thursday, November 19. And make sure to download the Action Network app to follow all of my picks!

Los Angeles Kings vs Toronto Maple Leafs

The Maple Leafs are a mess, and they really dug themselves into a hole after signing Sergei Bobrovsky to a three year deal. Now, they have to rely on Anthony Stolarz to not be as bad as him.

The thing is, Stolarz is a good goaltender. But he’s had a lot of injury problems throughout his career, and so he’s not the most reliable.

In comes the Kings, who just ended their four game losing streak to shut out the Boston Bruins a couple of days ago. Perhaps a road trip was all the boys needed?

All things considered, I’m still pretty bearish on the Kings. This is an old roster, who’s best player is 35-year-old Artemi Panarin. Don’t get me wrong, Panarin can still go at a high level. But the way this roster is built has been brutal.

Quinton Byfield has been invisible all season, and Adrian Kempe has been mildly disappointing. The only net-positive outside of Panarin has been defenseman Brandt Clarke, who’s second in points.

I’m very bearish on both teams. But the Leafs have lost seven in a row including a 6-3 blowout to the Blackhawks. The Blackhawks!

At least Los Angeles has had some recent success, and its total defense has actually ranked second behind the Hurricanes, with a 56.64 xGA. I’ll take the Kings to win this.

Pick: Los Angeles Kings ML

St. Louis Blues vs Pittsburgh Penguins

It was announced that Joel Hofer will start for the Blues, who has been one of the best goaltenders in hockey.

The last time I rocked with Hofer, I was burned pretty bad, when he dropped a 4-1 stinker to the Panthers. Regardless, I’m still a believer!

His .921 SV% and 8.2 GSAx this season has been a valuable and consistent part of this roster, along with Jimmy Snuggerud’s 10 goals.

Meanwhile, the Penguins are one of the NHL’s most curious cases. Could it be that age is starting to get to Sidney Crosby? His statline has been remarkably quiet all season, with the exception of a two goal game he posted in L.A.

However, Pittsburgh is starting to get healthier, which is always nice. It’s still missing Elmer Soderblom and Bryan Rust, and Nick Robertson is a game-time decision.

But the one problem that has been bothering Pittsburgh has been goaltending. And that seems to be a major pain point for a lot of teams this season. Arturs Silovs is getting the nod, but his outlook has been less than ideal.

In his last five games, Silovs has let up 19 goals, and before that let up 21 goals in his first six starts.

So, I’m not particularly bullish on Pittsburgh’s outlook this season, especially if he’s the guy it plans on running with.

I like the Blues’ offense though. It ranks 11th in expected goals, and they have the talent to light up an already-vulnerable Silovs. Take the Over at plus-value. 

Pick: Over 6.5

Minnesota Wild vs Calgary Flames

I would like to keep this short and sweet.

The Flames have been real solid over their past 10 games. Over their past six, they’ve traded a win for a loss, and given how this season has started, I consider that an upgrade, don’t you?

The problem is, the Wild come into town and they’re one of the NHL’s most dominant teams right now, winning seven of their last eight. Not only that, Jesper Wallstedt was just announced as the starter.

Wallstedt has been hockey’s top performer this season, playing to a top-ranked 17.5 GSAx with his .924 SV% and 1.99 GAA.

This is the moment Wallstedt has been waiting for, and he’s doing it with flying colors, as a number-one netminder. 

I don’t see Calgary making much noise against Wallstedt, given how well he’s played. The Flames play at a bottom-10 xGF and xGA rates, while the Wild drive play at an elite level.

I like Minnesota to cover the puck line here. Calgary is prone to blowouts at home.

Pick: Minnesota Wild -1.5

Anaheim Ducks vs Colorado Avalanche

What is happening in Anaheim is insanely unsustainable. This is a team that is fourth in goals scored, but 30th in goals against.

As of now, the Ducks have five players who are averaging over a point per game, with Leo Carlsson and Cutter Gauthier leading the pack. Gauthier has been on a tear that I don’t think we’ve seen since the 80s, posting 19 goals in his past 10 games.

In those 10 games, he’s scored a goal in each of them, including four hat tricks. Four!

With those kinds of numbers, including the supporting cast around him, you’d think that this would be one of, if not the best teams in the NHL. But they’re not. In fact, they’re eighth in the Western Conference because of absolutely horrendous goaltending.

Anaheim has rolled out Lukas Dostal, Ville Husso and Laurent Brossoit, and they’ve each played at least five games. The highest save percentage belongs to Brossoit, who holds an .867%. That is so unbelievably bad.

Now the Ducks head to the Mile High City to face the Avalanche, who lost to the Utah Mammoth in an intense, heated battle.

Colorado’s shorthanded, with both Nathan MacKinnon and Cale Makar out. Its top remaining players, Martin Necas and Artturi Lehkonen, were banged up last night, but fortunately were able to continue the game.

I still believe in the Avalanche, even without their top two players. It’s a long season, and injuries are going to happen, and I think Joe Sakic has done a great job at building this team’s depth.

Scott Wedgewood will likely take the crease after Mackenzie Blackwood started last night and Wedgewood has had a rough go this season, with an .863 SV% and a -7.3 GSAx — good enough for fourth worst amongst all netminders.

The stars are lining up for this to be a high-scoring bout. With all the talent up front for both teams, and the abhorrent goaltending, this is the perfect opportunity to take the over.

Pick: Over 6.5

Vancouver Canucks vs Seattle Kraken

This will be the Kraken’s first game back from Finland, and they couldn’t leave that country fast enough.

They were blown out by the defending Stanley Cup champion Hurricanes, after getting outscored 9-2 in two games.

And you would think looking at the schedule, a “get-right game” would be against the Canucks right? Wrong!

The Canucks have climbed out of the pit of misery they were in, by winning two in a row against the Golden Knights and the Mammoth.

It seems as if coach Manny Malhotra has finally figured out that Kevin Lankinen is a bad goaltender and a big reason as to why this team has been a total disaster. Because Leevi Merilainen will start tonight in the Emerald City, against Seattle’s Philipp Grubauer.

And contrary to popular belief, Grubauer hasn’t been a total disaster all season, playing to a serviceable .893 SV% and a -0.2 GSAx. A rapid improvement from what we’re used to seeing from him.

Seattle will continue to be a “blah” team for me. Its offense has never been good. The defense can hold its own. But even when the defense holds its own, the goaltending fails it.

I knew that the Kraken were going to get blown out by the Canes. I correctly predicted that they would lose by at least two goals in each game, and now after a 10 hour trip back to the PNW, they have to face a Canucks team that’s feeling their oats?

Call me crazy, but I like Vancouver outright.

Pick: Vancouver Canucks ML
