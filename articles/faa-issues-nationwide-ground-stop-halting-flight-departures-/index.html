FAA issues nationwide ground stop, halting flight departures until 10 p.m. Friday

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:01 · source · permalink

An aviation monitoring account reports the FAA has halted flights across the United States until 10 p.m. Central Time, citing frequency issues affecting the National Airspace System

The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop Friday afternoon, Nov. 13, halting departing flights at airports across the United States because of unspecified safety concerns.

According to an advisory issued by the FAA’s Air Traffic Control System Command Center, the ground stop took effect at 2:54 p.m. Central time and will remain in place until further notice.

“Due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” the advisory states, “all airports are ground stopped.”

The advisory lists an estimated end time of 10 p.m. Central time, with an effective period extending to 10:30 p.m. However, the FAA has not confirmed when flights will be allowed to resume.

International flights arriving in the United States will be handled on a case-by-case basis.

What caused the nationwide FAA ground stop?

The FAA has not officially identified the cause of the nationwide ground stop.

Reports from aviation monitoring accounts and news outlets suggest the disruption may be connected to radio communication problems affecting the nation’s air traffic control system, including reports involving the Kansas City Air Route Traffic Control Center.

However, the FAA’s advisory does not mention a communications outage or confirm that those reported issues prompted the nationwide order.

The agency cited extraordinary circumstances and safety concerns but did not provide additional details.

How are Oklahoma City flights affected?

The nationwide ground stop applies to departing flights across the country, including those at Will Rogers International Airport in Oklahoma City.

Travelers departing Oklahoma City or connecting through other airports could experience delays and cancellations as airlines respond to the restrictions.

The number of flights affected at Will Rogers International Airport has not been confirmed.

Passengers are encouraged to check directly with their airlines or visit the Will Rogers International Airport flight information page before traveling to the airport.

When was the last nationwide FAA ground stop?

A previous nationwide FAA ground stop occurred Jan. 11, 2023, when a failure of the agency’s Notice to Air Missions system, known as NOTAM, temporarily halted domestic flight departures.

The system provides pilots and flight crews with critical safety information, including runway closures, equipment outages and other potential hazards.

The FAA ordered airlines to pause domestic departures for approximately 90 minutes while officials worked to restore the system.

The disruption resulted in more than 10,000 flight delays and over 1,300 cancellations nationwide.

The 2023 incident was widely reported as the first nationwide ground stop since the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks.

Unlike the 2023 disruption, which was tied to a computer system failure, the cause of Friday’s nationwide ground stop has not been confirmed.

Friday’s advisory also lists a substantially longer estimated disruption window, extending into the evening.

When will flights resume?

The FAA’s advisory identifies 10 p.m. Central time Friday as the estimated end of the ground stop, but the agency has directed all departing aircraft to remain grounded until further notice.

Restrictions could be lifted earlier or extended depending on the circumstances.

The total number of flights and passengers affected nationwide remains unclear.

Travelers can monitor updates through the FAA’s National Airspace System Status website.
