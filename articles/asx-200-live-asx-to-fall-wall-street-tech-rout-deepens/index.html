ASX falls after Wall Street wipeout; ALS dives

The sharemarket opened lower on Monday after an aggressive retreat from the artificial intelligence trade triggered the biggest sell-off on Wall Street since March 2020 and sent the Nasdaq tumbling into a correction.

The benchmark S&P/ASX 200 Index dropped 55.2 points, or 0.6 per cent, to 8537.4 by 10.02am (AEDT). Ten of the 11 sectors opened in the red, led by a sell-off in the technology and property sectors. The AFR Real Economy Index fell 11.6 points, or 1.1 per cent, to 1007.6.

A wave of selling hit Wall Street on Friday as traders ramped up bets for higher interest rates from the US Federal Reserve to stamp out inflation and amid growing doubts about the sustainability of the AI boom, even after Anthropic’s blockbuster float last week – a debut widely hailed as a defining moment for the technology sector.

“Two separate stories are converging: a slowing, but still-solid, economy is changing market leadership, while the AI theme that has carried markets is facing its own reassessment,” said Magdalena Ocampo, market strategist at Principal Asset Management.

“Each is potentially manageable on its own, but together they are testing the market.”
