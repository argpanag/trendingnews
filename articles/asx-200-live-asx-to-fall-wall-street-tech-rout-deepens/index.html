Mining rebound keeps ASX losses in check

The Australian sharemarket edged lower on Monday as a rebound in the mining sector and higher US futures helped offset an aggressive unwind of the artificial intelligence trade on Wall Street.

The benchmark S&P/ASX 200 Index fell 22 points, or 0.3 per cent, to 8570.6, after falling as much as 0.6 per cent in early trade. While just two of the 11 sectors closed higher, bargain hunters in the mining sector protected the bourse from further losses. The AFR Real Economy Index was 11.4 points lower, or 1.1 per cent, to 996.2.

Local investors shrugged off the heavy selling in the US sharemarket that sent the Nasdaq 100 into a correction as traders ramped up bets for higher interest rates from the US Federal Reserve to stamp out inflation and amid growing doubts about the sustainability of the AI boom.

But after early pressure, Australian shares found some relief as firmer US stock futures pointed to a rebound on Wall Street, with Nasdaq 100 contracts up almost 3 per cent and S&P 500 up more than 2 per cent.

On the ASX, miners rebounded after weeks of heavy selling that sent BHP and Rio Tinto into a bear market last week.
