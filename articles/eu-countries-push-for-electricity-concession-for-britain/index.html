EU countries push for electricity concession for Britain

TheTools · 10.11.2026 16:53 · source · permalink

Britain should be offered an “appropriate” deal to join the European Union’s electricity market, member states have urged Ursula von der Leyen.

Germany and the Netherlands have urged Brussels to offer concessions to Britain in negotiations to bring the UK and EU markets closer together.

In a letter to the bloc’s top officials, seen by The Telegraph, the two countries, as well as Luxembourg, Belgium and Denmark, called for the talks to be concluded “swiftly”.

It reads: “An agreement on electricity market integration is crucial, in order to provide clarity and predictability for stakeholders from both the UK and the EU.

“This is necessary to unlock the full potential of the North Sea area and transform it into the largest clean energy hub in the world.”

The precise terms of the UK-EU deal to link electricity markets are unclear, but they are expected to be discussed when Andy Burnham, the Prime Minister, meets Ms von der Leyen, the president of the European Commission, and António Costa, the European Council chief, later this month.

A statement expected to be signed off at the summit said Britain would be expected to pay into the bloc’s development fund as the price for a deal.

While the five nations believe an “appropriate” contribution should be made, The Telegraph understands that they are working to prevent maximalist demands crashing the negotiations.

The member states have previously criticised that the Commission, which negotiates on behalf of the bloc, has followed French calls to charge Britain substantial levies for joining EU schemes, such as its €150bn (£125bn) Safe defence loans programme.

An EU diplomat told The Telegraph the bloc should also work to sell an electricity pact to the British public as Mr Burnham’s string of Brexit deals have generated mixed views.

In recent years, the UK has been a net importer of energy, with the EU providing 5-10 per cent of its electricity and 4-12 per cent of its gas.

The source said the sales pitch should focus on lower prices, the economic benefit of North Sea projects and energy security.

The diplomat added: “This will lead to more efficient electricity trading, resulting in lower costs for UK consumers since Brexit.

“A larger electricity market will ultimately lead to lower electricity prices in the UK. And, of course, the UK is currently facing very high electricity prices.”

Sweetener for UK will lower prices for all

For EU nations sceptical about giving Britain a discount, the five nations argue that it will have similar effects across the bloc.

“The UK’s integration into the EU electricity market will reduce dependency on fossil fuels,” their letter reads.

“It will also improve the efficiency of electricity trading on the market, while fostering stable and competitive electricity prices. An agreement will thus enhance Europe’s energy security and contribute to a competitive economy that centres on access to clean and affordable energy.

“These benefits underline the importance of swiftly concluding the ongoing negotiations.”
