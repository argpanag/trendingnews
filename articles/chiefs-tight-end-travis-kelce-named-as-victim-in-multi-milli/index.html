Chiefs’ tight end Travis Kelce named as victim in multi-million dollar fraud case

TheTools · 15.09.2026 16:33 · source · permalink

ST. LOUIS (First Alert 4) -- One of the victims in a multi-million dollar scheme is Kansas City Chiefs player Travis Kelce, according to prosecutors in court. The case was prosecuted in St. Louis and the defendant was sentenced Tuesday to 11 years in prison.

Very little was said about Kelce in court, other than briefly naming him as a victim. Prosecutors did not comment further about Kelce’s connection, saying they do not comment on individual victims. We have reached out to the Chiefs for comment but haven’t yet heard back.

A 2021 Forbes article states Kelce was an investor in a fund created by Swiftarc, which the defendant, Sid Jawahar, co-founded.

Jawahar pleaded guilty in January to federal wire fraud charges related to an investment scheme. Prosecutors said Jawahar used money made from new investors to repay older investors “and to fuel an extravagant lifestyle that included flights on private planes, stays at luxury hotels and expensive outings at fancy restaurants.” He is currently in the Ste. Genevieve County jail. In court documents holding Jawahar behind bars, a judge noted he is from India and has been in the U.S. illegally since 2005.

Missouri Congressman Sam Graves asked a federal judge for leniency for an “illegal immigrant” who engaged in a Ponzi scheme that cost victims more than $25 million.

Prosecutors say Jawahar also paid a consultant to ensure a positive article was written about him that could then be used to “influence the judge” set to sentence him. Their conversations were recorded on jail phone calls.

Graves wrote a letter to U.S. Judge Zachary Bluestone, recommending a sentence of four years for Jawahar. The letter mentioned no personal connection between the congressman and Jawahar.

Bluestone did not heed the congressman’s recommendation. Instead, he sentenced Jawahar to 11 years in prison to be followed by three years of supervision.

Prosecutors allege that on August 24, Jawahar engaged with a political consulting firm called Axiom Strategies for $10,000 to “assist with the development and coordination of approximately one to two advocacy or support letters for consideration in connection with Client’s forthcoming sentencing.”

According to a docket entry from September 10, Missouri Congressman Sam Graves submitted a letter on Congressional letterhead, urging the federal judge to consider a 48-month sentence for Jawahar, a fraction of the maximum sentence. The Congressman stated he was writing in his personal capacity.

We reached out to Congressman Graves’ office multiple times but have not yet heard back. Axiom Strategies and its CEO Jeff Roe are connected to Congressman Graves. His campaign has paid Axiom $1.77 million since 2006, according to Federal Elections Commission records analyzed by First Alert 4 Investigates. We also have not heard from a representative of Axiom Strategies.

Jawahar’s attorney, Doug Passon, said his client was remorseful for his actions.

In a recorded jail phone call on August 31, prosecutors say Roe and Jawahar discussed the letter. Jawahar asked whether the letter will be from a “state senator,” to which Roe responded “its gonna be someone good.”

Graves, according to the filing, then signed a letter September 3, which first referenced the judge, writing: “I know your professional pedigree—shaped by elite analytical training, rigorous appellate oversight and a deep grounding in our state’s legal infrastructure.”

The letter then went on to advocate for Jawahar, who the Congressman claims has chosen accountability. “A structured, measured sentence enables him the eventual opportunity to re-enter society as a productive economic agent capable of continuing that restitution.”

During the sentencing hearing, Judge Bluestone said he was troubled that no restitution was being paid by Jawahar. In court, Jawahar indicated he was committed to making restitution.

Prosecutors say Jawahar is an illegal immigrant who is expected to be deported. He is expected to serve his prison term before any potential deportation.

Prosecutors, in their filings, also point to an article published about Jawahar in the Jefferson City News Tribune, which praises his supposed work of creating “a comprehensive, peer-to-peer academic and vocational academy” inside the facility.

Officials at the Ste. Genevieve County Detention Center told First Alert 4 Investigates: “We have never had an incarcerated individual, including this particular detainee, start any programs within our facility.”

Prosecutors, in their court filings, point to the recorded jail call in which Jawahar told Roe that the purpose of the article is “to influence the judge.” Recorded jail calls were played in court at Jawahar’s sentencing.

Roe, prosecutors say, then told Jawahar he could geographically target the article to the judge’s home.

In a phone call transcript filed in court, Roe said: “I’m gonna take that and I’ll put a little money around it and geofence his house and so he’ll see it…”

Prosecutors, in their filing, wrote that Jawahar paid a political consultant thousands of dollars to influence the Court, instead of paying his victims back the money he stole.

Judge Bluestone called the attempts to influence him a “ludicrous idea” during the sentencing hearing.
