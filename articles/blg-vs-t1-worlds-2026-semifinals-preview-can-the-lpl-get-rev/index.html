BLG Vs T1 Worlds 2026 Semifinals Preview: Can The LPL Get Revenge? - Hotspawn

Bilibili Gaming (BLG) and T1 will meet in the Worlds 2026 semifinals on Sunday, November 8, with a place in the final on the line.

Both teams entered the semis with convincing quarterfinals runs. BLG swept Dplus KIA, showing a much higher level compared to the Swiss stage, while T1 beat Karmine Corp 3-1 thanks to some impressive teamfighting.

Who will join Gen.G for the grand final in New York? Let’s break down the two teams in our preview.

Based on what we’ve seen from the tournament, the current meta is rewarding the teams whose mid-jungle duo can accelerate the game well.

Whether it’s giving a strong mid laner or jungler to snowball a lead, or having picks that can transfer that lead to the bot lane, both have seemed to be the optimal way of playing games.

Bot lane can help swing a game by winning the 2v2, but the gold and map pressure generated through mid and jungle can often outweigh that advantage.

With that in mind, the mid-jungle duo will have a lot of responsibilities in this series. Knight is in great form, so he will be a big problem for T1 to contain.

Top lane could be another deciding factor. Bin may have the upper hand against Doran, who spent much of T1’s quarterfinal against Karmine Corp playing weak side. Even when Doran had a lead on Jayce, he had a few questionable moments. If Bin wins the lane matchup, T1 may have to rely more heavily on its bot lane to keep pace.

The two ADCs’ champion pool has been quite different so far at Worlds. Viper has shown comfort on Ashe and Ezreal, and BLG also used Cassiopeia bot against DK. He’s known for being versatile, so this could play in BLG’s favor later in the series.

Peyz, on the other hand, has either relied on hyper carries like Zeri, Yunara, and Kai’Sa or lane bullies like Calista, Caitlyn, and Jhin. One notable gap: Peyz has yet to play Ashe at Worlds, while Viper hasn’t picked Zeri.

As always, the bot lane duo will also depend on which support picks are available, but both teams have got plenty of picks to work around, and we could see some heavy banning in Games 4 and 5. In the first few games, the two will probably just trade the usual combinations, so we shouldn’t really see any big surprises draft-wise.

BLG has looked way more convincing against Dplus KIA, but you can never count out T1 at Worlds. The positive news for fans is that a one-sided result is highly unlikely, something that we’ve witnessed over the past few days.

So far, I’m inclined to think BLG has a greater chance thanks to Knight’s incredible form and the top lane gap that Bin might find in the top lane. If T1 cannot find a way to get the bot lane rolling, BLG could take control through mid-jungle and top.
