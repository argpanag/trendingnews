Iowa weather: Severe thunderstorm warning issued

TheTools · 08.09.2026 09:55 · source · permalink

A severe thunderstorm warning has been issued for parts of central Iowa until 7 p.m.

This includes parts of Poweshiek, Polk, Marion, Mahaska, Jasper and Warren counties.

Hazards include 70 mph wind gusts and quarter-sized hail.

A flash flood warning has been issued for eastern Dallas County and Polk County until 12:30 a.m. Wednesday due to the heavy rainfall on Tuesday evening.

Power outages

Thousands of metro residents are without power after heavy rains and strong winds blew through the area on Tuesday evening.

As of 6:55 p.m., MidAmerican Energy Co. reports nearly 17,000 customers in the Des Moines area without power.

Forecast

Warm air will surge into central and southern Iowa today, pushing high temperatures into the low 90s with heat index readings near 100 degrees. At the same time, a cold front will push into the northwest corner of the state sparking thunderstorms throughout the day. That front pushes into the metro this evening bringing storms and then cooler weather for the rest of the week.

Showers are already widespread across Iowa this afternoon, which has held some of our temperatures for the northern half of the state in the middle 70s. South of this area of showers and storms, instability has had no problem building, with temperatures ranging from the upper 80s to the upper 90s.

As the front moves across the state, storms will begin to fire around 4-5 p.m., making it to the metro around 7 p.m. The initial storms will be capable of damaging winds, heavy rain, possibly hail, and possibly a tornado, though the risk for that is relatively low. Surface winds will be out of the southwest ahead of the front, which will limit turning in the atmosphere. So, if we do see an isolated tornado, it's not expected to be long-lived.

Winds from the southwest will help pull in continued moisture, however, and with their parallel orientation to the front, could continually fire storms in the same locations, leading to heavy rain totals between 2-4 inches. This may lead to some localized ponding and possibly some localized flash flooding.

Showers and storms will continue into Wednesday morning before tapering off by mid-morning. Winds will turn out of the north/northwest, bringing in cooler, drier air. While we'll likely only drop into the low 70s Wednesday morning thanks to the lingering clouds and rain/storms, Wednesday afternoon we'll top out in the low 80s.

It'll be the start of a much more comfortable stretch of weather that'll last into the weekend, with highs in the low to mid 80s through Saturday and the mid to upper 70s on Sunday. High pressure noses in Thursday and most of Friday, bringing comfortable sunshine.

An area of low pressure will start to push into the state late Friday, kicking our winds back up ahead of it during the day Friday and bringing our next potential for strong to severe storms late Friday evening into Saturday. More details will be worked out with this as we get closer, but if you have camping or outdoor plans during that timeframe, you may want to rethink those.

Saturday afternoon looks mainly dry, but we may start to see some scattered showers and isolated storms Sunday ahead of another system that'll bring more showers and thunderstorms to kick off our next workweek. This will keep our temperatures cooler yet again, with highs in the upper 70s to low 80s Monday and Tuesday, with some showers lingering Tuesday morning.

Yet another wave of energy looks to push through next Wednesday, though with how far out that system is, little more can be said except it's something to keep an eye on as we get closer.
