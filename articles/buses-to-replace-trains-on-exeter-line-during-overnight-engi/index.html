Buses to replace trains on Exeter line during overnight engineering works

TheTools · 13.11.2026 14:13 · source · permalink

Train services will be disrupted due to overnight engineering works.

South Western Railway is carrying out the work between Yeovil Junction and Pinhoe from Monday, November 23 to Thursday, November 26, with services replaced by buses from 10.46pm each night.

Passengers travelling between London Waterloo and Exeter St Davids are advised to plan ahead as journey times will be longer.

Simon Milburn, chief engineering and technology officer at South Western Railway, said: "The record-breaking conditions of the summer dried out the clay soil in our embankments, causing the ground to shift below our tracks.

"This disturbed the precise track levels that we need to run trains at full speeds, which is why we have speed restrictions between Salisbury and Exeter.

"We’ve already carried out a lot of tamping to ensure trains can continue to run safely and reliably, and this additional overnight work will help us get to the track quality we need to bring us another step closer to restoring the normal timetable.

"We’re sorry for any disruption this may cause.

"We recommend customers plan carefully and allow extra time to complete their journeys, and once again thank them for their continued patience."

The engineering work involves extensive tamping to re-pack ballast beneath the sleepers and level the rails.

This follows months of disruption caused by record-breaking temperatures, which dried out clay embankments and caused the ground to shift, affecting track alignment.

The work aims to lift current speed restrictions and restore the full timetable between London and Exeter.

Tamping is being carried out overnight to minimise passenger disruption, but buses will replace trains between Yeovil Junction and Exeter St Davids from 10.46pm each night.

Journey planners have been updated and passengers can check their journeys at southwesternrailway.com/plan-my-journey.

The West of England line has been running on an amended timetable since August due to the track issues.

South Western Railway is using the New Measurement Train to monitor track conditions and identify areas needing tamping.

Passengers are encouraged to check the South Western Railway website for the latest updates on the line and the revised timetable.
