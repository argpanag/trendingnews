Festival In Focus: Marrakech’s Atlas Workshops Nurtures Bespoke Approach As Project Incubator Goes From Strength-To-Strength

TheTools · 16.11.2026 16:13 · source · permalink

Breaking News

Marrakech’s Atlas Workshops heads into its ninth edition this year with upcoming films by Palestine’s Leila Abbas, Morocco’s Meryem Benm’ Barek and Sudan’s Amjad Abu Alala in the selection and Brazilian Oscar nominee Kleber Mendonça Filho as its official patron.

The legacy of the project incubator, focused on films by Arab and African directors, is impressive as it approaches its 10th anniversary. To date, it has supported 242 projects, with completed films making their mark across the international festival circuit.

“We calculated that since the creation of the workshops in 2018, 42 films have gone to Cannes, Venice or Berlin,” says program director Hédi Zardi, who took up the role in 2023.

Recent participants have included Laïla Marrakchi’s Strawberries and Marie-Clémentine Dusabejambo’s Beni’mana, which both played in Cannes, with the latter title winning the Caméra d’Or, as well as Tala Hadid’s Bardi, which was selected for New York; and Rami Kodeih’s Furies, which debuted in Venice and Toronto.

They are among 12 films playing in Marrakech’s main festival program this year that have gone through the Atlas program alongside Yassine Marco Marroccu’s Alkebulan, Rani Massalha and Salem Massalha’s Cairo Shadows, Abdallah Al-Khatib’s Chronicles from the Siege, Rafiki Fariala’s Congo Boy, Elisé Sawasawa’s Enough Is Enough, Mohammed Hammad’s Safe Exit, Sara Ishaq’s The Station and Mohamed Zineddaine’s Until Dawn.

This year’s selection spans 30 feature films, selected out of 410 submissions, from 14 territories spanning Algeria, Cabo Verde, Egypt, Ethiopia, Jordan, Lebanon, Libya, Morocco, Palestine, Rwanda, Senegal, Sudan, Syria, and Tunisia.

Projects in development include upcoming films from a host of directors who have already made their mark, including Abu Alala (You Will Die at Twenty), Abbas (Thank You for Banking with Us), Benm’Barek (Behind the Palm Trees), Sofia Alaoui (Animalia), and Yasmine Benkiran (Queens).

Abu Alala will present A Beautiful Land To Die, about a man on death row who suddenly finds himself free when Kober Central Prison is broken open in the early days of Sudan’s ongoing war, and Abbas attends with New York-set America Is Behind The Door about a Palestinian woman trying to carve out a new life in the U.S.

Alaoui is in the selection with The Sleepers about a doctor on a mission to get to the bottom of a mysterious sleeping sickness afflicting her town, while Benm’ Barek brings Ashes, an allegorical drama exploring the legacy of human rights abuses during Morocco’s Years Of Lead.

Benkiran will present Waltz of the Savages about a July 14 Bastille Day imposed upon the Pasha of an Arab country under French colonial rule in 1955, as its citizens clamor for independence outside the palace walls.

Newcomers to the lab include Syrian-Germany director Dyaa Naim’s Chronicles of an Announced Death, about an undertaker living as a refugee in Germany on a mission to return his father’s body to Syria following the fall of the Bashir Al-Assad regime, while Zardi also highlights Studio Kébé, the first film from Senegalese director Dyana Gaye in more than a decade.

Four of the 11 projects being presented at the production or post-production stage attended while in development, spanning Rwandan director Samuel Ishimwe’s Seasons of the Weary Kind, Senagalese Yoro Mbaye’s Fagadaga and Alaa Eddine Aljem’s Eldorado Ismaël El Iraki’s Wolfmother, which both hail from Morocco.

Discoveries

Discoveries include the workshop’s first Libyan production, 1986 by Mouayed Zabtia. The coming-of-age tale follows a young man who has to hide his love of Western music when hostilities breakout between Libya and the U.S.

“We like to nurture strong links with directors but we also like discoveries,” says Zardi, explaining that Zabtia is a self-taught director who learned about the workshops on the social networks and sent in his project on spec.

“He identified two labs which included us. I couldn’t believe what I was seeing. I asked myself, “Does he actually exist?” I felt the need to call him, just to ask, “Is it a real person?,” recounts Zardi

“I learned that he had worked in television doing a lot of series following the fall of the Gaddafi regime and that he had set aside some money and bought equipment to make his film.”

Another five projects will be presented on in the Atlas Close-Ups section dedicated to Moroccan cinema, including B514, The Man Who Forgot To Die by Jihane el Bahhar, who achieved box office success at home with last film Triple A.

Eight prizes will be awarded across the three sections, with the development jury comprising producers Nadim Cheikrouha (The Voice of Hind Rajab), Marie-Ange Luciani (Anatomy of a Fall) and Linda Mutawi (Eagles of the Republic).

Distributor Eddie Bertozzi at Be Water Film, director Mohamed Kordofani and BFI London Film Festival Artistic Director Kristy Matheson will decide the post-production awards.

With the Atlas Workshops at its heart, Marrakech’s industry offering restructured last year under the Atlas Programs banner to now encompass Atlas Station, focused on up-skilling 10 Moroccan professionals, and the Atlas Distribution strand.

The latter, aimed at encouraging the circulation of films in the Middle East and Africa, comprises the Atlas Distribution Awards feting proactive distributors in the region, and the Atlas Distribution Meetings, bringing some 60 distribution professionals from Africa, the Arab world, and Europe to Marrakech to screen films in the festival program and the Atlas Workshops post-production showcase.

Zardi is also quietly looking to expand the attendance of private investors, with a handful checking out th event as observers this year. Its a move that makes sense, he says, with funding out of Europe under pressure, while opportunities are growing in MENA and Africa.

Bespoke Approach

Amid the growth, Zardi is determined to keep the workshop activities boutique and bespoke, with much of the activity increasingly revolving around one-on-ones and small group meetings, while all of the attendees will participate in sessions with The Secret Agent director Mendonça Filho.

He follows in the footsteps of Cristian Mungiu and Jeff Nichols, who previously took on the recently created role of patron under which he will mentor the attendees, who will then take on the mantle of being of “The Year of Kleber Mendonça Filho”.

“I think of the reasons the workshops work is because we try to avoid too many generalized, theoretical panels to focus more on one-on-ones or small group meetings,” says Zardi.

The 11 mentors this year will include director Clémentine Dusajembo, John Trengove and actors Nisrin Erradi, Majd Mastoura and Lyna Khoudri, while experts and consultants include editor Nadia Ben Rachid, colorist Micheal Derrosett and sound editor Rana Eid.

“We are pushing more and more towards this way of working. A number of directors with films in the festival program will be coming to meet with the projects,” explains Zardi.

“So, we’ll have a director and an actor, a director and a cinematographer, or two directors in conversation with one another. And a new step we’ve taken this year is organizing preparatory sessions with all the consultants, briefing them on the material that has been sent in.”

The actors, for example, will be working with the directors on questions around the dynamics of a character’s personality.

“I feel it’s more valuable, within the broader industry dynamic, where one-on-ones are often geared toward future project financing to place a strong emphasis on making these interactions concrete, hands-on working sessions.”
