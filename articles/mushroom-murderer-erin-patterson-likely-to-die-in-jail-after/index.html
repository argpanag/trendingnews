Mushroom murderer Erin Patterson likely to die in jail after failed appeal

TheTools · 30.10.2026 01:48 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / World

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._f0.YIZs2-J2FpwgcBcJJb29s1vXmFAWHLh_p1oUyv-l-RA/world/2237274%2Fmushroom-murderer-erin-patterson-likely-to-die-in-jail-after-failed-appeal

  • Victoria's highest court has increased the jail term for mushroom cook Erin Patterson.
  • The court removed a non-parole period that was set last year when Patterson was sentenced to life in prison for triple murder and attempted murder.
  • It is unclear whether Patterson's legal team will mount a challenge in the High Court.

Erin Patterson has lost her appeal against her murder convictions over the deaths of three relatives who ate beef Wellingtons that contained death cap mushrooms.

A separate appeal by the prosecution to impose a harsher sentence was upheld and Patterson will be re-sentenced.

The decision today by the Court of Appeal means it is likely Patterson will die in jail.

Patterson was last year sentenced to life in prison with a non-parole period of 33 years for the murders of Don and Gail Patterson, and Heather Wilkinson, along with the attempted murder of Heather's husband Ian.

She had mounted an appeal on seven grounds, all but two of which were dismissed by the Court of Appeal judges.

The appeal was heard on the remaining two grounds, but was dismissed.

Patterson, who watched the proceedings on a video link from a women's prison, sat back in her seat and appeared to stare blankly when the decision was announced.

It is unclear whether her legal team will mount a challenge in the High Court.

She is being held in the maximum security wing of the Dame Phyllis Frost women's prison.

Erin Patterson pictured in 2025.

AFP / Martin Keep

Offending was 'careful and deliberate'

The Court of Appeal judges upheld prosecutors' appeal against the non-parole period that was included in her original sentence.

Delivering the court's judgment, Justice Stephen McLeish said the crimes involved "shocking criminality" and Patterson's conduct deserved to be "denounced in the strongest terms".

He said Patterson's decision to plan and then poison her relatives with a beef Wellington laced with poisonous mushrooms involved an "elaborate series of deceptions".

"The offending was carefully planned and deliberate," he said.

Justice McLeish described Patterson's crimes as "extraordinarily cold and calculating."

"The applicant's intent to murder all four victims was sustained for nearly three weeks from when she invited them to lunch, through to preparing and serving the poisoned meal and then throughout the protracted suffering and ultimate deaths that resulted," he said.

It was therefore inappropriate for parole to be an option, he said.

The decision to reject Patterson's appeal leaves her with few legal options, including appealing to the High Court of Australia.

Patterson's leave to appeal was granted by the judges on two of the seven grounds her lawyers put forward in arguments.

The first of those grounds related to a mistake that meant the jury hearing her case stayed at the same hotel as a police witness.

However, the appeal judges said today there was no reason to think the jury wouldn't have carried out its sworn duty.

The second related to the use of expert evidence derived from Patterson's mobile phone data used by the prosecution to show she had visited areas identified in internet posts as death cap mushroom sites.

The court decided that this evidence was admissible as part of the wider circumstantial case put forward by the prosecution.
