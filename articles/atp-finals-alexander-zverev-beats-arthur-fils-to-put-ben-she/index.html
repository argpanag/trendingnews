ATP Finals: Alexander Zverev beats Arthur Fils to put Ben Shelton through to semi-finals

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:11 · source · permalink

Alexander Zverev reached three of the four Grand Slam finals this year, winning the French and US Opens

  • Published
    19 November 2026

World number one Alexander Zverev said he would do "everything I can to be pain-free" after hurting his ankle during his ATP Finals victory over Arthur Fils.

Germany's Zverev ensured he finished top of the John McEnroe Group with a 6-4 7-6 (8-6) win over Fils - a result which also sent Ben Shelton through to the semi-finals.

However, the French and US Open champion's serve speeds dropped throughout the match and he repeatedly gestured and grimaced.

Zverev said afterwards he felt pain in his right ankle - the same place where he tore ligaments in a career-threatening injury in 2022.

"It's a tendon that goes over my bone, and it moved when I broke my ankle," the 29-year-old explained.

"The ankle is a topic that probably will stay until the end of my career.

"I'm going to do everything I can to be pain-free on Saturday, but this is something I have to live with nowadays.

"I can still move well, but you can see immediately my serve speeds go down. I hope I'll be 100% on Saturday."

US Open runner-up Shelton needed Zverev to beat Fils to be assured of a place in the semi-finals.

The American earlier had to fight for just over two hours to beat fast-rising Spaniard Rafael Jodar 7-6 (10-8) 7-5.

Zverev will play either Daniil Medvedev or Frances Tiafoe in Saturday's semi-finals, while Shelton will face Carlos Alcaraz.

Seven-time major champion Alcaraz practised on Thursday after having a scan on "a little problem" in his hamstring that he picked up during his win over Medvedev.

Zverev will hope to brush off his own injury concerns as he bids to win the tournament for a third time.

He has yet to lose a set in Turin or have his serve broken, and his match against Fils mirrored his previous wins over Shelton and Jodar.

After a strong start, Fils played an error-strewn game at 3-3 in the first set to hand the initiative to his opponent.

With such a huge first serve Zverev is one of the most difficult players to break, and he kept Fils at bay before serving out the opener in one minute flat.

In the second set Fils played a superb return game at 6-5 to force two break points on the Zverev serve, but they were calmly saved by the top seed.

Zverev led the tie-break 6-1 but, distracted by his ankle and a re-energised Fils, five match points went by before he finally closed out victory.

Shelton through to semi-finals for first time

Ben Shelton reached his first Grand Slam final at this year's US Open

After closing out his victory over Jodar with three aces in a row, Shelton wrote "I'll be watching, Arthur" on the camera lens - a reference to the final game in the group.

It is a different scenario to 2025, where Shelton lost all three of his group matches on his tournament debut.

This year, he arrived in Turin with a Masters 1000 title and a first Grand Slam final under his belt, and a determination to add more to his serve-based game.

Shelton was under pressure against Jodar in the first set, saving two break points and missing a series of routine shots when his opponent was struggling.

However, after taking a tight tie-break, Shelton quickly broke to take control of the third set.

Jodar, competing in his first professional season on the ATP Tour, was strong defensively and wrestled the break back to level at 4-4.

However, he was visibly fatigued and unable to keep Shelton at bay in a series of lengthy rallies, with Shelton breaking for 6-5.

Shelton slipped at the back of court as he served for the match, but recovered quickly to close out victory.

In the doubles, Britain's Henry Patten and Finnish partner Harri Heliovaara missed out on the semi-finals after the defending champions lost to Theo Arribage and Albano Olivetti.

The Wimbledon champions and world number ones were below their best in a 7-6 (7-5) 7-6 (7-3) loss to the French pair.

Patten was visibly unwell and at one point asked for a bucket to vomit into during a changeover.
