Our top tipster Lewis Jones, aka Jones Knows, provides his analysis and betting insight ahead of the weekend's Premier League action.

Tottenham vs Aston Villa, Saturday 12.30pm

There is a striking amount of evidence pointing towards a low-scoring game.

Tottenham's problems in front of goal are well documented - zero scored in four Premier League matches to a backdrop of just 0.95 expected goals per game. Roberto De Zerbi's teams averaged 1.92 goals scored per game at Brighton and Marseille, so to average just 0.73 per game since taking the Spurs job showcases a worrying trait for the environment at the club. There seems a "mental block" holding this football club back.

Villa have scored just one goal themselves this season in the Premier League. Both teams have genuine attacking-process problems, but for different reasons. Tottenham have possession and territory without enough penetration while Aston Villa are struggling to generate much shot volume at all.

Under 2.5 goals at 10/11 with Sky Bet looks too good to turn down.

SCORE PREDICTION: 1-1 | JONES KNOWS' BEST BET: Under 2.5 goals (10/11 with Sky Bet)

Brighton vs Arsenal, Saturday 3pm

Fabian Hurzeler has yet to taste victory over Mikel Arteta across five meetings but his Brighton team have certainly bloodied the nose of the champions. In games that have produced plenty of needle - even between the managers - Brighton have won four of the five expected goals battles which points towards this being a tricky encounter for the visitors despite their perfect start to the season.

I'd be wary of taking the 7/10 with Sky Bet for an away win, with the double chance for Brighton at 11/10 much more appealing.

Olivier Boscagli should be lining up against Bukayo Saka, who looks back to his dangerous best with the ball. The winger has drawn 13 fouls in three Premier League games, with three of those fouls leading to a booking for the opponent. Boscagli is 5/4 with Sky Bet to make two or more fouls and is 4/1 to be carded - a price given further edge by his two previous cards in two games with Arsenal.

SCORE PREDICTION: 1-1

Everton vs Ipswich Town, Saturday 3pm

Nightmare. Absolute nightmare.

We were on Leif Davis to score or assist the week before last, then decided not to follow him into the Crystal Palace game last weekend - and, of course, he went and did both. That's betting for you. It makes us resilient.

I'm happy to go back in.

Davis is playing a role that makes him far more than your traditional left-back. He is getting extremely advanced for Ipswich, constantly pushing into the final third and becoming a genuine attacking outlet. His two goals and an assist in the opening four Premier League games underline just how involved he is.

There is also a lovely little momentum angle here. If we include pre-season, Davis has scored four times in his last seven games. That is some output from a player starting from left-back.

The shot-on-target price is the interesting bit. The 100/30 with Sky Bet feels big for a player whose positioning means he is regularly getting himself into attacking areas.

SCORE PREDICTION: 2-1 | JONES KNOWS' BEST BET: Leif Davis +1 shot on target (100/30 with Sky Bet)

Newcastle United vs Hull City, Saturday 3pm

There is something about a team getting absolutely battered that makes me interested in opposing them in their next game. It is not just the physical exertion of chasing shadows for 90 minutes - there is a psychological hangover that can linger.

Newcastle certainly got a beating at Leeds. They were 4-0 down before eventually losing 4-1, with Matthias Jaissle admitting afterwards that the result was "difficult to digest" and that his young side could not match Leeds' physicality.

When looking at teams conceding four or more goals in a Premier League game, excluding matches against Arsenal, Manchester City and Liverpool, their record in their next game is horrible. Just one win from the last 15, alongside five draws and nine defeats.

That makes Hull or draw at 11/8 with Sky Bet interesting.

SCORE PREDICTION: 1-2

Narrative is always worth listening to in football betting. Andoni Iraola returning to Bournemouth with Liverpool feels like one of those occasions where the emotional temperature at the Vitality Stadium could be turned up a few degrees.

Iraola spent three seasons on the south coast, taking Bournemouth to their highest-ever Premier League finish before moving to Anfield this summer. He returns on Sunday for the first time.

There are some interesting historical parallels, too. Managers returning to former clubs for the first time can find the occasion awkward. David Moyes lost at Everton with Manchester United, Graham Potter lost at Brighton with Chelsea, while Thomas Frank drew at Brentford with Tottenham, Brendan Rodgers drew at Swansea with Liverpool and Mauricio Pochettino drew at Southampton with Tottenham.

These results highlight the unusual dynamics of these fixtures. The home crowd knows the manager, the players know the manager and there is an extra edge to the occasion.

Bournemouth are 19/10 with Sky Bet for the win.

Leeds have lost just one of their last 13 Premier League matches dating back to March, winning six and drawing six. That's the fewest defeats of any side in the division over that period. Their latest performance was another reminder of the formula, too, as they dismantled Newcastle 4-1 at Elland Road.
