Stars thrill the "best-ever" crowds as BBC festival brings thousands to city

TheTools · 16.09.2026 11:25 · source · permalink

Midway through his band’s electrifying after-sunset performance on Sunday night, Simply Minds frontman Jim Kerr struck up the opening notes of Someone Somewhere in Summer Time.

That Somewhere was City Park, with the stunning backdrop of Stirling Castle and the Sometime Sunday, September 13, 2026, when Scottish international superstars Simple Minds closed the Radio 2 in the Park festival in front of a capacity crowd.

This was one of these pinch-me moments.

Soaking up the applause, Kerr, who shook many hands, asked the crowd: “Are you ok?” and then said: “thank you” as he soaked up the scenes, drinking in the applause.

In years to come some of the youngsters attending, some not even yet in their teens and adults of all ages, aged up to at least 70 or beyond will recall this moment as Scottish concert crowds were as much stars of the show as the performers themselves, belting out banger after banger.

In short, they helped make this festival the rousing success it was.

Moments after this stirring anthem the band switched tempo to the haunting melody of Belfast Child, written to chronicle the fight to end of the Troubles in Northern Ireland and you could have heard a pin drop as Kerr delivered the emotional opening segment of the song.

Other hits delivered with great energy and amazing guitar riffs from Charlie Burchill, included anthem Promised You A Miracle, Love Song, the opener, Waterfront, Alive and Kicking and Beginner Boys.

We ended with Don’t You Forget About Me, complete with the most amazing mass chorus you could imagine.

There could not have been a better band to bring the curtain down than Glasgow and Scotland’s finest and this will long remain etched in the memory.

The praise kept coming as veteran DJ Gary Davies described the Radio 2 in the Park festival in Stirling as “the best ever” in the 15-year history of the event.

The current Drivetime host and Sounds of the Eighties presenter was bowled over by the atmosphere at City Park as thousands lapped up the spectacular event.

He told the audience they had made it the “biggest festival ever” as he spinned the discs as warm-up man for Simple Minds.

Speaking between some of his many gigs, he said: “The people have given us such a welcome and what warmth they have shown. The crowd was fantastic.

“Deacon Blue were one of the highlights and The Script were fantastic.

“This is a phenomenal setting for concerts, especially with the backdrop of the festival.”

While in Scotland, Davies did not miss out on the opportunity to play his beloved golf in the area.

He said: “I teamed up with DJ Spoony to play golf at Gleneagles on Saturday and we tied our match, that was fun.”

Fellow DJ Jeremy Vine added: “The weather is as good as it can be in September and I am enjoying the festival.

“I am back for the Bloody Scotland Festival this weekend. I should just have stayed!

“It’s great to have the festival here as you get out of London and have an opportunity to meet your listeners.

“The warmth of the Scottish audience is incredible. I was excited by ABC and thought Texas were great.”

After great DJ sets on Friday, Saturday got underway with a superb performance from Texas, featuring Say What You Want, Summer Son and Inner Smile, before ageless 77-year-old Lulu in a pair of cool shades belted out classic Shout and others including her new single, I’m In Love Again. Also starring on the opening day were ABC and Chaka Khan.

But the Saturday stand-outs were The Script, as charismatic front-man Danny O’Donoghue, so energised by the audience that he went crowd-surfing, quipping: “Someone stole my phone, someone stole my wallet!” as he was helped through the crowds. Songs like Man In The Arena, Break Even and For the First Time had everyone jumping around and when Rain was performed he asked people to put up their umbrellas, joking that the band had kept the rain away.

Chaka Khan headlined and ABC and Jack Savoretti sparkled, along with inspired Scottish songstress, Aberdeen’s own Emeli Sande.

Sunday saw Deacon Blue excel with hits like Real Gone Kid and Cast and Blue performing memorable sets, while James Blunt also went crowd-surfing. Level 42, complete with a superb big band earned a deserved slot as second on the bill.
