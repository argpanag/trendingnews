First Fall storm brings heavy rain, gusty winds and a big cooldown to Oregon, SW Wash.

Our first fall storm is set to arrive Thursday evening, bringing widespread rain, gusty winds and a major temperature drop to northwest Oregon and southwest Washington.

A strong cold front will push through the region overnight Thursday into Friday morning.

LATEST FORECAST

Rain will first spread along the Oregon Coast before becoming widespread across the Coast Range and Willamette Valley overnight.

Wind picks up overnight

Along with the rain, winds will increase as the front moves through. Gusts could top 50 mph in spots along the Oregon Coast, while a few locations around the Portland metro could see gusts approach 40 mph.

Those winds could be strong enough to bring down small branches and lead to isolated power outages. The strongest winds are expected overnight into Friday morning before gradually relaxing Friday afternoon and evening.

The combination of rain, wind and slick roads could also make for a slower Friday morning commute.

Scattered storms possible Friday

The widespread rain will become more scattered Friday afternoon as the main front moves east. However, the atmosphere will remain unstable enough for isolated thunderstorms to develop.

Any stronger shower or thunderstorm could briefly produce moderate rain and gusty winds. There is also a chance for a few funnel clouds to develop with some of the stronger cells, especially as showers move off the Coast Range and into communities on the west side of the Portland metro.

Big temperature drop follows the front

You'll also notice a major change in temperatures.

Portland will go from around 70 degrees Thursday afternoon to just 62 degrees Friday. That cooler air sticks around through the weekend, with afternoon highs struggling to reach 65 degrees.

Saturday and Sunday are looking mainly dry at this point, but the cooler temperatures will stick around. After a mild start to fall, this system will bring our first real taste of the cooler and wetter weather that's typical this time of year.
