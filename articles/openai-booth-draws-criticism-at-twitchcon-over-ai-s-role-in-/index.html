OpenAI booth draws criticism at TwitchCon over AI’s role in creative work | Межа

TheTools · 15.11.2026 21:14 · source · permalink

Photo source: NBC News

OpenAI’s Codex booth at TwitchCon drew criticism from attendees uneasy about AI’s growing influence on creators, even though AI was largely absent from the convention’s official programming.

OpenAI promoted its Codex coding tool with a hackathon on the expo floor at Twitch’s annual convention in San Diego, which brings together streamers, fans and industry experts. Some attendees described the booth as “Tone deaf,” “Bland and boring” and “stupid.” A Twitch user also posted on X that “No one wanted this, and we made that clear,” linking to a community forum calling on Twitch to end its partnerships with AI companies.

This is reported by NBC News.

AI remains a divisive issue among streamers. Some want to experiment with new tools, while others worry about the effects on human creativity and jobs. At the convention, some attendees said tools such as speech-to-text, automatic captions and clipping could be useful if they did not generate content or replace human creativity.

Twitch drew criticism from users a few months ago when it introduced an option for streamers to opt out of having their content used to train AI models owned by its parent company, Amazon. Critics said they had never consented to their content being collected and that the setting should have been opt-in, not opt-out.

Chloe Van Ryn, 31, of Arizona, said some uses of AI could help with accessibility and moderation, but she was unsure it belonged in creative work on Twitch.

I do think it’s useful in a sense of accessibility for people who may not be able to do certain things,

- Chloe Van Ryn

I do think it’s useful in a sense of accessibility for people who may not be able to do certain things,

- Chloe Van Ryn

Van Ryn said Twitch’s opt-out setting made her suspicious of the company’s approach to streamers’ content. She would not be comfortable with her own content being used to train AI models and said users should know whether they have opted in.

I think it’s definitely something like you have to know you’re opting into it. And I think it’s really important that your people, your community, know whether you’re opting in or not.

- Chloe Van Ryn

I think it’s definitely something like you have to know you’re opting into it. And I think it’s really important that your people, your community, know whether you’re opting in or not.

- Chloe Van Ryn

Rummeko, who asked to be identified by his Twitch username, opposed making content scraping widespread and requiring users to navigate several layers of settings to turn it off.

But I think to make [AI content scraping] widespread for all, and we all have to go in several layers deep to turn it off, I think that’s an entirely wrong and disrespectful way to go about it.

- Rummeko

But I think to make [AI content scraping] widespread for all, and we all have to go in several layers deep to turn it off, I think that’s an entirely wrong and disrespectful way to go about it.

- Rummeko

Twitch CEO Dan Clancy told NBC News that being automatically opted in did not mean a user’s data had already been scraped. He said the setting was intended as a preventive measure in case Amazon planned to train its AI on Twitch content. Clancy also said he was not involved in what Amazon was doing.

And that way, wherever this is going, our creators are able to express their wishes.

- Dan Clancy

And that way, wherever this is going, our creators are able to express their wishes.

- Dan Clancy

It is unclear whether Amazon has used Twitch content to train its models. Amazon did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Twitch Chief Product Officer Mike Minton declined to discuss the issue at TwitchCon, citing lawsuits filed by streamers in response to the controversy.

In terms of what’s happened in the past, due to ongoing litigation, I have nothing to say,

- Mike Minton

In terms of what’s happened in the past, due to ongoing litigation, I have nothing to say,

- Mike Minton

Clancy and Minton said most other services do not let users opt out of having their content used for AI training. Minton said Twitch’s decision to offer the option drew attention to the company and prompted discussion of creators’ concerns about generative AI.

There’s a trade-off there, and we felt it was important to offer the option,

- Mike Minton

There’s a trade-off there, and we felt it was important to offer the option,

- Mike Minton
