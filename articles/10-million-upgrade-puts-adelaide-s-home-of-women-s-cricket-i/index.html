$10 million upgrade puts Adelaide’s home of women’s cricket in shape for summer - The Indian Sun

TheTools · 16.11.2026 02:00 · source · permalink
  • Sports
  • Top Story

Karen Rolton Oval in Adelaide has undergone a $10 million upgrade, with new spectator terraces and improved lighting completed ahead of Tuesday night’s Women’s Big Bash League clash between the Adelaide Strikers and Sydney Sixers.

The redevelopment, jointly funded by the South Australian Government and the South Australian Cricket Association (SACA), is intended to improve access for spectators and strengthen the venue’s capacity to host professional and community cricket.

The most visible change is the removal of the steep eastern embankment, which has been replaced with terraced seating to make the ground easier to navigate and more comfortable for spectators.

New floodlights providing 1500 lux of illumination have also been installed to meet Australian standards for domestic cricket. The previous 750-lux lighting system has been moved to Oval 2, giving the precinct another floodlit playing surface for cricket and other community sports.

The works form part of a long-term masterplan for Karen Rolton Oval, which serves as South Australia’s principal venue for women’s cricket while accommodating junior development programmes, football and community events.

South Australian MP Rhiannon Pearce said the improvements would make the centrally located ground more welcoming to spectators.

“Karen Rolton Oval is such a great venue, centrally located in the western Parklands, and these works make it even an even more accessible for fans,” Pearce said.

She said the timing was particularly important as Adelaide prepared for another summer of women’s cricket, with improved facilities needed to accommodate growing participation and spectator interest.

“With women’s cricket continuing to grow at an extraordinary pace, it’s fundamental we have appropriate facilities to cater for this increase in participation,” she said.

The redevelopment comes as cricket administrators seek to ensure facilities keep pace with the development of the women’s game, particularly at grounds that serve both professional competitions and emerging players.

Karen Rolton Oval has become an important part of South Australia’s cricket infrastructure, providing an alternative venue when Adelaide Oval is unavailable for domestic or international fixtures.

Its ability to accommodate major matches was demonstrated during the 2024–25 Sheffield Shield final, when South Australia defeated Queensland by four wickets to claim its first title in 29 years. The match attracted a packed crowd to the ground.

SACA chairman and president Will Rayner said the investment would benefit players across different levels of the sport.

“This investment in Karen Rolton Oval is a major step forward for women’s cricket in South Australia — not only for the professional game, but for community and junior cricket,” Rayner said.

He said the lighting improvements would benefit evening fixtures and television coverage while allowing the venue to accommodate future generations of players.

“The upgraded lights will enhance night matches and lift the experience for players, fans and broadcasters alike, while ensuring this venue serves the next generation of cricketers,” he said.

The project has been financed through equal contributions of $5 million from the state government and SACA, reflecting the shared responsibility for developing sporting infrastructure used by professional teams and the wider community.

For the Adelaide Strikers, Tuesday’s WBBL encounter with the Sydney Sixers will provide an early opportunity to use the upgraded facilities in a competitive setting.

The improvements also give cricket administrators greater flexibility in scheduling matches after dark, with two illuminated ovals now available within the precinct.

Pearce said the partnership with SACA recognised the importance of the ground to women and girls playing cricket across South Australia.

The completion of the works ahead of the WBBL fixture places Karen Rolton Oval at the centre of Adelaide’s summer cricket programme, with the upgraded ground expected to serve professional competitions, developing players and community sport.
