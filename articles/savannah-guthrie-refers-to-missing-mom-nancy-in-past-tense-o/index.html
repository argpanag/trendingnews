Savannah Guthrie Refers to Missing Mom Nancy in Past Tense on ‘Today’ 7 Months After Disappearance

TheTools · 10.09.2026 17:35 · source · permalink

Savannah Guthrie referenced her missing mother, Nancy Guthrie, in a rare on-air remark during Today.

During the Thursday, September 10, episode of Today, Savannah, 54, mentioned her family's connection to Ireland. "My mother was Irish," she said. "Her grandmother — they're from Galway."

Her use of the word "was" stood out as the search for Nancy, 84, remains ongoing. She was last seen at her home in the Catalina Foothills area near Tucson, Arizona, on January 31 and was reported missing the following day. Authorities previously released footage showing an armed individual approaching Nancy's home and appearing to tamper with a camera at her front door around the time she vanished.

MPI28/Capital Pictures / MEGA

The comment came as Savannah and her Today colleagues discussed their plans to broadcast from the Emerald Isle in October. Savannah revealed that despite her family roots, she has never visited the country.

Savannah used similarly somber language in an August 1 Instagram post marking six months since her mother disappeared. "Our hearts are in ruins," she wrote, adding that her family was determined "to find out what happened to her, so that we can give her the dignity of a proper goodbye — a celebration of her remarkable life that she so deserves." Savannah ended the emotional plea by writing, "We will never stop looking for answers — and we will never stop looking for the light."

SteveSands/NewYorkNewswire/MEGA

She has also received support from her longtime friend and former Today cohost Hoda Kotb. As First for Women previously reported, Kotb, 61, returned to the anchor desk alongside Savannah in April. "I feel like I get to be in the seat to watch what strength looks like," Kotb said of her friend, per People. "There's nobody like her."

People with any information regarding the case are asked to contact the FBI at 1-800-CALL-FBI (1-800-225-5324). You may also contact your local FBI office, the nearest American Embassy or Consulate, or you can submit a tip online at tips.fbi.gov.
