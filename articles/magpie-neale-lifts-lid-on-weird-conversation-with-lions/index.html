Magpie Neale lifts lid on ‘weird conversation’ with Lions

TheTools · 03.10.2026 03:26 · source · permalink
  • Sport
  • AFL
  • AFL

October 3, 2026 - 3.26am

Lachie Neale has opened up about his blockbuster move to Collingwood, just days after securing a third consecutive premiership with the Brisbane Lions.

Speaking on Triple M’s The Rush Hour with Dobbo & Elliott, Neale admitted that informing outgoing coach Chris Fagan and footy boss Danny Daly of his departure was both “uncomfortable” and “weird”, even though his exit had been anticipated for some time.

Lachie Neale joins Collingwood. @CollingwoodFC

“It’s still a little bit uncomfortable, but I think everyone knew for a while now that it was on the cards. Both of those guys (Fagan and Daly) have been in contact with me throughout the year,” Neale said.

“Especially Danny – I’ve spoken to him so much and he’s guided me a lot through the last 12 months. I’ve had a number of conversations with Fages as well, so it wasn’t a shock for anyone.

“But it is still a little bit weird saying it to two guys you’ve been working alongside for a long time, having achieved some pretty special things and been through some highs and lows with. It is a weird conversation to have.”

Neale joins the Magpies as an unrestricted free agent, following eight years at the Lions that included three premierships and two Brownlow Medals.

The trade caps off a wild few weeks for Neale, who faced a tense wait at the tribunal in the week leading up to the grand final.

He was banned for one match after striking Hawthorn’s Jack Ginnivan during the preliminary final.

His ban was successfully overturned later in the week, with the 30-year-old crediting his personal growth over the past year for giving him the resilience to compartmentalise the chaos and take the field.

“I probably thought I was going to get off on Tuesday, and then when that didn’t happen, I thought we might be in a bit of trouble here,” he admitted.

“Early on I thought I would get off, but I didn’t in the first one. Luckily enough, I got off at the appeals board on Thursday night, which is pretty late to find out you’re playing on a Saturday afternoon in a grand final – the biggest game of the year.

“I think the last 12 months have taught me a fair bit about myself as I try to improve and get better as a human.

“I think a strength of mine is my resilience, so it was just showcasing that again. I didn’t have my best game on Saturday, but I felt I contributed.”

While Neale is admittedly reserved when it comes to the media, his transition to one of Victoria’s powerhouse teams means he will be stepping directly into the AFL’s “fishbowl”.

“I don’t know if it excites me,” Neale laughed.

“Being part of a powerful club like Collingwood, and seeing the amount of fans they have across Australia, not only at the home games at the MCG, but everywhere we travel, I can’t wait to play in front of the Magpie Army.

“Even playing against them can be a little bit intimidating with how many fans they have, so that part is exciting, but maybe not so much the media.”
