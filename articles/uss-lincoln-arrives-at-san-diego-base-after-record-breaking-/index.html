The US has imposed additional sanctions on entities including ships with ties to what the US treasury department has described as Iran’s “shadow fleet.”

In a statement on Thursday, the justice department said it is targeting “the aging network of tankers the regime relies on to move billions of dollars’ worth of petroleum and petroleum products to foreign markets.”

Treasury secretary Scott Bessent said that the US will “continue exposing those who enable the regime’s oil sales,” adding: “No enabler of Iranian sanctions evasion is safe from the full force of Treasury’s authorities.”

The US has imposed sanctions on 17 shadow fleet vessels that are responsible for transporting millions of barrels of Iranian crude oil and petroleum and petrochemical products to a variety of South and East Asia markets, the treasury department said.

Eric Leventhal knows he’s going to vote for a Democrat in Congress when he goes to the polls in Missouri this November – the problem is he has no idea which candidate is going to be on his ballot.

In the state’s August primary, he voted for Fred Wellman, a Democrat running in the state’s second congressional district. But after a long and complicated legal battle sparked by Donald Trump’s desire to gain every advantage in the upcoming midterm contest, the districts have changed for the general election that will determine which party controls Congress. Leventhal said he lived right near the border of the first and second congressional district, so now he’s not sure which one he’s in.

“It is dizzying and embarrassing, frankly,” said Leventhal, a 35-year-old stay-at-home father.

Leventhal’s experience crystallized the mix of anger and confusion felt in Missouri, where an effort by Republicans to redraw the state’s congressional map devolved into a saga that seemed to have as many twists and turns as the gerrymandered districts themselves.
