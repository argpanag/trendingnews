Forsberg won his only start last season versus Chicago, as he made 22 saves on 23 shots in a 3-1 victory here at United Center.

Storyline Of The Day – Second Period Discussion
Coming into last night’s game in New York, the Kings had been second best in the second period. For the bulk of the season, really, but definitely on the trip. The starts have been fine, in my opinion. But the second period just feels like a completely different team.

Goals were only 2-1 against but over the first three games of the trip but the Kings had been out-attempted 84-47 in the middle stanza overall. This season, only the Columbus Blue Jackets had scored fewer second-period goals than Los Angeles, entering the Garden yesterday.

When asked about the second period struggles, captain Drew Doughty answered the question very matter-of-fact before the Rangers game.

“We’ve addressed it and we’re going to fix it next game.”

Fair enough. But talk is cheap. What was happening was not a secret. Everyone saw it. Several players spoke about wanting to play with a more aggressive mindset in second periods. Joel Edmundson observed – correctly – that the Kings were getting pucks out to center, but not in deep and not with any sort of sustained pressure when it did get in. It was, at best, one and done. At worst it came right back the other way. Erik Gustafsson felt it started in the defensive zone, needing to close quicker, before breaking out better as a “five-man unit” with possession.

Even at the first intermission last night, in speaking with Brandt Clarke, he brought up, unprompted, the struggles the team has had in the second period and the focus on turning things around beginning that night. There was already a thought towards the second period and needing to keep playing the same way.

The start in New York was great, but would it carry over? Easy to say it, harder to execute it.

The Kings came out and put up 15 shots on goal in the second, by far their highest-event second period of the trip. They scored twice and had a third goal disallowed. After seeing the play dictated by their opponents throughout the trip, last night it was the Kings who did more of the dictating. They sacrificed a bit the other way in being more aggressive, as the Rangers had 13 shots on goal of their own, but the Kings often had two if not three forwards engaged on the forecheck, which plays into what Edmundson said. It helped to create more sustained time in the offensive zone and when you’re attacking, you’re not defending.

“I think we tried to stay with what we did in the first, play quicker, don’t bring so many pucks back,” defenseman Mikey Anderson said. “If you don’t have a play, just keep advancing it, win the blue line, win the red line, get it in. I thought, overall, it was pretty solid, or at least better than it’s been, which is good.”

You could see a team that kept the foot on the gas, after seeing it taken off to often in past games. It almost felt like there was a determination to play aggressively, maybe not at all costs, but certainly with an understanding there could be chances against as a result. When needed, Darcy Kuemper made the saves, stopping 12 of 13. There were chances in both directions and the Kings were largely the team getting the better of the looks.

Good to see an area which was a clear problem addressed with a different approach.

3 To Watch For –
– Step on up Taylor Ward.

Ward hadn’t played an NHL game all season. He was recalled on October 17 but has been a healthy scratch each game he’s been up. Against the Islanders and Devils, Jared Wright, who had just been recalled, was selected over him. It’s a tough spot to be in. Last night, with the team struggling to produce lower in the lineup, Ward got his chance. Took just 75 seconds to deliver.

I’ve long heard that Ward is very effective in breakaway situations in the AHL, to the point of when it felt like the Kings couldn’t win a shootout last season, I heard Ward’s name suggested by those who watched him in Ontario. He showcased why last night, after taking a good pass from defenseman Drew Doughty in stride. A composed finish. I think that any goal a minute and 15 seconds in would’ve spurred on the group, but I think Ward being the guy to get it went a bit extra. He’s a hard worker who doesn’t complain. He’s a good teammate. When you have a guy sitting out for a while and he gets one that early in his first game back, that sparks the group.

“He’s been patient the last little bit,” Anderson said of Ward. “He’s super skilled. Every time he gets sent down, he scores a bunch of goals, comes back and instant contribution. Good play by him. Good play by Dewey. That was a good start for the game.”

Frankly, I think the Kings were begging for a depth player to take that opportunity. How many times has someone not taken it this season? Too many games decided by a single goal when a breakaway would’ve changed the outcome. When a Grade-A chance would’ve changed the outcome. Good on Ward for taking his. It should earn him another game and a longer look if he can continue to produce a bit.

Nice to see an honest, hardworking player get the reward.

– All 18 skaters were on the ice for at least one even-strength goal versus the Rangers last night.

That’s the definition of spreading the scoring.

The top line still got theirs. Multi-point games for Artemi Panarin (1-1-2) and Alex Laferriere (0-2-2) helped to lead the way up front. Adrian Kempe couldn’t get one to go but he led the team with seven shots on goal. Feels like Kempe is close to an explosive kind of run, the ones he’s been known for throughout his career. Since the trip began, Panarin is tied for second in the NHL in goals (4) and tied for third in points (7). Laferriere is tied for fourth with five assists. The big guys contributed.

The scoring wasn’t just those players, though, which is what’s more important. Ward got his line’s goal, while the Laughton line was on for defenseman Brandt Clarke’s goal. The fourth line was on the ice for defenseman Mikey Anderson’s goal. All in all, it was good to see things so balance and that includes the blueline.

The Kings got four goals from their defensemen last night, just the third time in franchise history that four different defensemen have scored in the same game. FWIW, no team in NHL history has had five, per NHL PR. As a result of one night, the Kings jumped to a tie for fourth in the NHL in goals by defensemen this season. Last year, they ranked 32nd. They’re already 35 percent of the way towards last season’s final number. While Clarke’s goal was his third of the season, a team-best, it was the first strike for Anderson, Drew Doughty and Joel Edmundson. Each of the team’s most-used six have at least one goal, with Clarke leading the way.

Offense comes in a variety of ways. Last night, the Kings got maybe their best balance of getting shots through from the point with traffic in front. Panarin’s power-play goal was obviously not a defenseman but Laferriere’s screen is one of the best I’ve seen in some time. There was no chance to see that puck. A good mix of all the different concepts coming together at one. It’s just the one night, but that’s a proof of performance that it can work when everyone is contributing.

– Tonight’s game is the first of 10 back-to-back sets this season. Nine of those ten games will be like this one, between a pair of road cities. The Kings played a league-high 16 back-to-backs last year, posting middle-of-the-road results. The Kings collected 17 points in the 16 second-half games, with 1.06 points-per-game, good for 18th in the NHL.

If there’s one item in their favor tonight, it was that last night’s win allowed the Kings to run the full bench for most of the night. No forward played in the 20’s in terms of minutes, while no defenseman was over 22. The top four each began with a 20 or a 21. That’s good balance. Panarin had his second-lowest, which is good when it’s a 6-3 win. Same with Kempe. Good to see the minutes spread, with a game the following night.

“When you go into games that are back-to-back, you do anything to win the first game, but ideally, you’re thinking in your head if you can really run the bench and manage the minutes and keep a lot of guys under 20 minutes, it pays, I think, benefits for the next day and the next night,” Head Coach Peter Laviolette said. The game played out well for us for that scenario.”

With players Quinton Byfield returning from injury and a group of older players, I think not leaning too heavily in any one direction is really important in these scenarios. If the Kings had to empty the tank to win last night and had nothing tonight, I think you ultimately accept that if you get the win. It was good that the Kings could spread the wealth, though, especially with a pretty long flight after the game between two cities you don’t usually see on consecutive nights.

See if it pays off at all this evening.

Final game of a five-game trip, with a very good record on the line. Kings are 3-1-0 and have the chance to come home a game shy of .500, but I think this game will be tougher than your usual Chicago game, with the travel and schedule, plus an opponent that will be motivated to avoid a winless homestand.
