Tiger Woods' golf comeback gathers pace at championship event with son Charlie

TheTools · 27.10.2026 10:27 · source · permalink
  • Alex Worth, US Sports Writer
  • Published: 10:27, 27 Oct 2026
  • Updated: 13:36, 27 Oct 2026

It appears Tiger Woods is nearing a comeback to professional golf, alongside his prodigious son Charlie.

Woods, arguably the greatest golfer of all time, has not played in a PGA Tour event since missing the cut at The Open Championship in 2024.

Further injuries, and a car crash in March, have de-railed his comeback, but it appears he is ready to step back into the limelight once again.

Woods took to the range at Monterey Peninsula Country Club earlier in October while hosting another golf clinic.

With his swing looking as good as ever, talk is intensifying over a comeback and one tournament is reserving a space for him and his son.

PNC Championship holding place for Tiger and Charlie Woods

PNC Championship organizers are holding a place for Woods and his 17-year-old son, per reports.

The 2026 PNC Championship takes place from December 19-20 at the Ritz-Carlton Golf Club, Orlando, Grande Lakes, with only three of the 20 teams confirmed so far.

Reports add that the tournament is prepared to welcome the father-son pairing if Woods decides he is physically ready to compete.

The 36-hole scramble format allows Woods to use a golf cart and share the workload with his son, reducing the physical demands.

The Woods' duo have competed together five times at the PNC since 2020, but are yet to win the event.

Their best finish has been second place in both the 2021 and 2024 editions of the tournament, but they have delivered the usual outstanding plays.

In 2024, Charlie stunned the crowd recording the first hole-in-one of his career.

However, the duo just fell short despite reaching 28-under par, losing a playoff round to Bernhard Langer and his son, Jason.

Woods - Koepka to open TGL 3rd season

If Woods decides not to compete at the PNC, he could see his first action just a week later to open the TGL's third season.

The two opening matches will include the debut of Brooks Koepka and Motor City Golf Club (Detroit) vs. Rory McIlroy's Boston Common Golf.

That match will be followed by Woods and his Jupiter Links Golf Club vs. the defending champion Los Angeles Golf Club in a rematch of last season's title match.

Last season proved to be hugely successful, with 21.782 million people tuning in, a figure that has led ESPN to agree a multi-year extension to its TV deal and a $1 billion-plus valuation for the league's parent company.

2026 will also mark the first season of the WTGL league, despite criticism from four-time major winner Nelly Korda.

Between defending champions, bitter rematches, and the expansion into women's golf, TGL is cementing its status as a permanent fixture on the professional golf calendar.
