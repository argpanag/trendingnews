Horror as Emirates flight from Dubai to London declares mid-air emergency - AOL

TheTools · 01.10.2026 16:08 · source · permalink

A flight from Dubai to London has declared a mid-air emergency and diverted to Frankfurt. Emirates flight EK31 (UAE4PY), which departed Dubai International Airport (DXB) at 11.25am local time on Thursday (October 1), squawked 7700 - the code for a general emergency - on its journey to Heathrow Airport and began descending near Frankfurt Airport (FRA). The airline later confirmed this was due to a passenger on board suffering a medical emergency, which required an immediate diversion.

The Airbus A380-861's scheduled arrival time was 4.10pm local time. The aircraft appears to have left the runway without incident. The current status of the flight and its passengers is unknown. According to Flightradar24, the aircraft is scheduled to depart from Frankfurt for Heathrow Airport later today. Its scheduled departure time was 5.30pm local time, but this has already been delayed until at least 6.33pm.

An Emirates Airbus A380 carries an average of 484 to 615 passengers, depending on the chosen cabin layout.

Squawk code 7700 is the international universal transponder signal used by pilots to declare a A Pilot's Guide to Squawk Codes general emergency. It instantly highlights the aircraft on Air Traffic Control (ATC) radar scopes, notifying controllers of an abnormal situation without requiring a verbal radio call. The flight then receives priority routing, clearance from surrounding traffic and expedited vectors to the nearest suitable landing site.

Ground crews and airport emergency services are placed on standby before the aircraft arrives. Common triggers include technical issues such as engine failure, smoke, or sudden loss of cabin pressure. It is also declared during medical crises, when severe passenger or crew medical emergencies require an immediate diversion.

The 7700 code differs from other codes, including 7600, which alerts to a total loss of radio communications, or 7500, which covers unlawful interference or hijacking, which occurred on the FlyDubai flight FZ1073 from the UAE and bound for Tel Aviv.

The Express contacted Emirates for comment and a spokesperson said: "Emirates can confirm that flight EK31 from Dubai to London Heathrow diverted to Frankfurt on 1 October due to a medical emergency involving a passenger onboard.

"Our cabin crew provided assistance during the flight, and medical support was arranged on arrival. The aircraft landed safely in Frankfurt, where the passenger disembarked to receive further medical attention.

"The flight is expected to continue its journey to London Heathrow at approximately 18:00 local Frankfurt time.

"We apologise to customers for the delay. The health and safety of our passengers and crew is always our highest priority."
