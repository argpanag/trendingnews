The 2026 AFL Draft Class runs deep, with a series of high-quality talents continuing to rise up draft boards as tonight's draft approaches.

One of the latest risers is Gippsland Power talent Keenan Boi.

An explosive talent, the 177cm small forward is one of the most damaging prospects in the upcoming pool, threatening both on and ahead of the ball.

With flair and speed that few possess, the Gippsland best and fairest winner has steadily earned some serious comparisons.

“I've never been compared to Will Ashcroft before, but I'd like to say so,” Boi told SEN’s Summer Run.

“The way he showed in that last five minutes of the Grand Final and all throughout his career how he can burst away and just be another level above from all the other mids and players in the comp.

“I definitely see a little bit of Will Ashcroft in me.”

With several mock drafts floating across numerous platforms, it’s hard to ignore the range that many experts see Boi landing come draft night.

A late rise saw Boi elevated into Cal Twomey’s top 30 in October’s Phantom Form Guide on AFL.com.au, while ESPN’s Jasper Chellappah ranked Boi at 29 in his September Draft Power Rankings.

“It's a bit strange,” Boi said.

“There's one guy that have you in the 20s and one that doesn't even have you in the 50s, so it's weird.

“I try not to take it in, because at the end of the day, it's just opinions, and they're just doing what they think and putting their opinions first.

“I disregard it all and just focus on what I can control and at the end of the day, all I need is for my name to be called out on the 19th or 20th.”

While Boi still isn’t confident in where he will land at the draft, he remains tied to Hawthorn as an NGA prospect.

Having spent time training with the club, Boi has engaged in a conversation with officials to gauge their interest heading into the draft.

That said, the club hasn’t rewarded the talent with extreme clarity.

“I met with them about a month, two months ago maybe,” he said.

“They said they'd keep in touch. I haven't heard too much yet but I'm assuming they'll reach out in the next week or so to give me an idea on what their views are like and how they see the draft.

“But at the minute, I haven't heard too much.”

Having the Hawthorn connection certainly hasn’t done any harm to his draft preparation.

“I think going into the club in the pre-season throughout December and January was just a huge gain,” he said.

“(It) really helped kickstart my 2026 year. I got to work alongside the likes of Nick Watson, Jack Ginnivan, Connor Macdonald and Dylan Moore, and the quality of those footballers is insane.

“It's arguably the best in the comp, so to surround myself with that and improve my forward craft and educate myself on how they dominate games was really, really helpful for me.

“I think it set me up really well for this year.”

Outside of his own talents, who would Boi select if he had the number one pick?

“Everyone says Dougie Cochrane, but I've never seen him play,” he said.

“So from first-hand experience, I'd put a bid on Ethan Drever.

“I think he stood out to me the most this year. Clearly one of the most dominant mids in the draft.

“When he played for Vic Country, playing alongside him, the way he burst through with his pace, his run and carry and speed was just a real standout and to do that at 190 centimetres is pretty impressive.”
