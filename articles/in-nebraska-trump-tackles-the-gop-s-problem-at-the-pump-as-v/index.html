In Nebraska, Trump tackles the GOP’s problem at the pump as voters decry ‘very high’ costs

TheTools · 06.10.2026 04:25 · source · permalink

GRAND ISLAND, Neb. — President Donald Trump knows he has a problem with gas prices. On Monday night, he tried to do something about it.

With less than one month to go until Election Day, Trump traveled to Grand Island, Nebraska, to rally with incumbent GOP Sen. Pete Ricketts, who is fending off an unexpectedly competitive challenge from independent Dan Osborn.

His visit took place as Nebraskans grapple with high fuel costs: The average price of gas was $4.21 per gallon in the Cornhusker State on Monday, according to AAA, while diesel averaged $5.99 per gallon, squeezing farmers who rely on the fuel to power their machinery.

At the rally Monday night, it was clear the GOP’s problem at the pump was top of mind for Trump.

In the middle of his remarks, Trump signed an executive order waiving the off-road requirement for tax-free red-dyed diesel, a move he said would save Nebraskans money. Trump aide Natalie Harp brought the executive order on stage for the president to sign.

“So, Pete and Jim, this should absolutely assure your election, I guarantee you,” Trump said as he prepared to sign the document, referring to Ricketts and Gov. Jim Pillen, who is running for another term.

The president also mentioned gas prices minutes into his speech, defending the war in Iran — even though it has driven up fuel costs — and insisting the conflict would end soon and bring prices down.

“That will end very quickly, one way or the other, very soon, maybe a little after the election, maybe a little before, but it’s going to end very soon,” Trump said. “When that happens, the oil will come tumbling down.”

He also recalled seeing gas stations advertising gas for $1.95 and $1.85 a gallon while driving through Iowa, and promised Nebraskans lower prices in the near future.

“We’ll have it lower than that in a very short period of time,” Trump said.

The last time U.S. gas prices were that low was during Covid-19 lockdowns in 2020 and, before that, in February 2016.

Trump’s visit to Nebraska was his latest stop on a swing through traditionally red states as he tries to boost struggling Republican candidates in competitive Senate races — seats the party needs to defend to maintain its majority in the upper chamber.

Last week, Trump traveled to Texas to appear with Ken Paxton, who is in a tough battle with Democrat James Talarico, and stopped in Ohio to rally with Sen. John Husted, who is fending off a comeback bid by former Democratic Sen. Sherrod Brown.

The president’s Nebraska visit comes as recent polls have shown Ricketts, the former governor, within a few percentage points of Osborn, who is backed by Democrats. While Trump won Nebraska by 20 percentage points in 2024, Sen. Deb Fischer, R-Neb., beat Osborn by just under 7 percentage points that same year. The Cornhusker State last elected a Democrat to the Senate in 2006.

High fuel costs have emerged as one of the defining factors in the race. But whether Trump’s rescue mission will help Ricketts will be up to voters.

While Trump insisted Monday night that costs are on the decline — “all your prices have been tumbling down,” he declared — voters at the rally described a different economic reality.

Wendy Munsen, 50, a Nebraska resident whose husband is a farmer, said gas prices are “bad” and “very high.” She said she wants that to change.

“The cost to put the diesel in the trucks to haul the corn, I mean, it’s really putting a burden on them,” she told MS NOW.

Munsen, who was wearing a sequin MAGA jacket and declared, “I love Donald Trump,” said she wants the president to do more to address high costs. Asked what her message to Trump would be, she responded, “Bring us back to affordable living.”
