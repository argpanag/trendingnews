Apple's stock drops on weak margin forecast in Ternus’ first earnings report: Live updates

TheTools · 02.11.2026 15:00 · source · permalink

This is CNBC's live coverage of Apple's 2026 fourth-quarter earnings.

Apple reported better-than-expected revenue but came up short of estimates on iPad sales for the fiscal fourth quarter and issued a disappointing margin forecast. The stock slid in extended trading.

Here's how the company did compared to analysts' estimates, according to LSEG.

Here's how Apple's most important product lines performed:

It's Apple's first earnings report since CEO John Ternus took the helm on Sept. 1, succeeding Tim Cook.

On the earnings call, Apple said it expects revenue to increase between 7% and 10% on an annual basis in the December quarter, compared to the 6.1% average analyst estimate, according to LSEG. The company's gross margin forecast for the current quarter is 45% to 46%, trailing the 46.3% average estimate.

Revenue in the past quarter rose 12% from $102.5 billion a year earlier, Apple said in a statement on Monday. Apple's iPhone revenue in the quarter only included eight days of new iPhone 18 Pro sales. And Apple's new flagship device, the iPhone Duo, didn't hit store shelves until the period was over.

Monday's report includes a full quarter of higher-priced Apple products, as the company raised prices across the board in June after it signaled challenges with memory supply and other parts. Apple increased prices on iPhones by $100.

The iPhone maker is reckoning with a global memory crunch and a rush for computer components. Apple said on Monday that its memory costs were higher this quarter than in the June period. 

Still, Ternus said in a statement that Apple had just capped off its "best fiscal year ever." Ternus told CNBC's MacKenzie Sigalos that Apple's growth came in spite of supply constraints and foreign exchange headwinds. 

"I think at this point, with everything we've achieved, it just feels like we've never been more confident that the best is yet to come," Ternus said.  

Ternus said that Apple "reluctantly" raised prices, which helped it offset component costs. Apple said tariff refunds added 5 cents to earnings per share.

At the end of prepared remarks, Apple CFO Kevan Parekh provided a guide for the December quarter.

He said that Apple's revenue would grow between 7% and 10%. Analysts polled by LSEG were looking for 6.1%.

Apple said that it expected services sales to rise between 8% and 9%, signaling slowing growth.

Parekh also said that gross margin would be between 45% and 46%, lower than in the fourth quarter.

— Kif Leswing

Ternus is leading Apple's quarterly earnings call for the first time.

He started with prepared remarks highlighting the company's packed schedule of product launches since he took over, including new iPhones, Home products, and touch screen laptops.

"This is an amazing time at Apple," Ternus said. "We just completed one of the most prolific launch periods in our history."

— Kif Leswing

Apple released an updated Siri AI during the quarter, a free upgrade for people who own iPhones new enough to support the software.

Investors want to know if Apple will offer more AI features through subscriptions or services. Ternus said on Monday that Apple has a "lot of opportunities" for monetization.

"One example is on the hardware side, right?" Ternus said. "It offers a compelling reason for upgrading or switching."

— Kif Leswing

Apple remains synonymous with the iPhone, which accounted for just over half of Apple's sales in the quarter.  

Sales of iPhones jumped over 17%, capping off four quarters of strong growth behind last year's iPhone 17 models.  

Ternus said industry research firms reported market share gains for Apple during the quarter.

— Kif Leswing

 

The company's profitable services business grew less than 10% during the September quarter, the slowest expansion since 2022.  

The category includes a wide variety of products and subscriptions, including iCloud storage, App Store fees, AppleCare warranties, and licensing revenue from Google for using its search engine on the Safari browser.  

"We saw strong performance in many service categories, with all-time records in video, cloud services, payment services, and advertising, and a September quarter record in music," Ternus told CNBC.  

— Kif Leswing

Compared to its megacap peers, which are pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into AI capital expenditures, Apple is a light spender.

Apple reported $2.24 billion in capex during the quarter, even lower than the $3.33 billion estimated by LSEG.

— Kif Leswing

In August, Apple announced a new home product called HomeView that uses AI and a display to show personalized information to multiple users in a family. It's potentially a sign that Apple is going to take its home accessories more seriously under Ternus. 

For the September quarter, the category, which includes headphones, speakers, and the Apple TV hardware, was a laggard, with sales declining nearly 8% to $8.3 billion, trailing the $9.06 billion average analyst estimate, according to LSEG.

"With the home products, we're really establishing a new kind of plan for our home," Ternus told CNBC.

— Kif Leswing

Apple's Greater China business grew 21% on an annual basis to $17.53 billion in sales. The region includes the mainland as well as Taiwan and Hong Kong.

Apple said the growth was due to iPhone and Mac sales. 

— Kif Leswing

Apple's Mac division grew the most of any of the product categories during the quarter, with revenue surging nearly 24% on an annual basis to $10.79 billion, topping analyst estimates.

Apple has been positioning its Mac lineup of desktops and laptops to provide the company a foothold in AI. It also announced new chips, Mac Studio and Mac Mini models in August.

Last week, Apple introduced its first MacBook with a touch screen, although it hasn't hit store shelves yet.

Ternus said that Mac growth was attributable to strong sales of its MacBook Neo, its low-cost laptop, and the MacBook Pro.

— Kif Leswing

Apple's iPad division contracted over 10% on an annual basis during the quarter to $6.22 billion in sales. It was the worst performing of all of Apple's product categories by growth.

The company released a new iPad Mini last month, and updated the iPad Air back in March, but the product line has not had many significant updates this year. Apple's iPad division declined 6% year-over-year during the June quarter, too.

Ternus blamed supply constraints and a difficult comparison to last year as reasons for the iPad's performance.

— Kif Leswing

Apple's gross margin is being closely watched as the company is being battered by higher prices for key components, such as storage, memory, and chips.

The gross margin was 47.9%, including a favorable impact of approximately 1 percentage point from tariff refunds. It's a major drop from last quarter's 50.1% gross margin.

It's unclear if analysts factored any potential tariff refund impact in their projections. Had they not, gross margin would have been short of the 47.5% consensus estimate from LSEG.

"We saw higher memory costs as compared to the June quarter, and that was partly offset by the benefit of carrying inventory and lower costs on non-memory components," Ternus told CNBC.

— Kif Leswing

Needham analyst Laura Martin told CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" that Apple is facing strong competition from AI companies and that it has "bet the farm" on the smartphone's continuing relevance even as companies like Meta and Google experiment with new hardware form factors.

"Almost every other AI company is going to try to change that form factor," said Martin, who recommends holding the stock.

— Kif Leswing

Monday is an unusual day for Apple to report earnings. The iPhone maker typically schedules earnings for Thursday, although it has mixed in Tuesdays and Wednesdays.

The last time the company reported earnings on a Monday was on April 27, 2015.

— Kif Leswing

Apple spent the weekend as the most valuable publicly traded company, after taking the crown from Nvidia at market close on Friday.

But Apple shares slipped about 2% on Monday, and the two are neck and neck at around $4.91 trillion. The two companies have been swapping at the top of the table for years. Before this month, Apple passed Nvidia in July, but Nvidia quickly reclaimed the top spot a few days later.

— Kif Leswing

While Monday's earnings report won't include sales from the iPhone Duo, execs may still be pressed on the device's launch in October.

There hasn't been much supply of the folding iPhone, with first-day preorders quickly slipping into estimated ship dates in 2027.

But there are also a number of reports that the Duo can crack under normal use, which is a tough hit to Apple's brand under Ternus. Apple acknowledged the issues last month and said they affected a "small number" of devices.

— Kif Leswing

Analysts at Barclays wrote in an earnings preview over the weekend that they "remain cautious on the stock given iPhone uncertainties this cycle, limited monetization of AI, and renewed fears on the App Store."

The firm has an underweight rating on the shares, and said the "pricing benefits AAPL has been enjoying from the memory backdrop due to its scale and preferential pricing are fading."

—Ari Levy

Bank of America analyst Wamsi Mohan said on Friday that his team had a "conservative outlook" on earnings because of gross margin concerns and price increases. 

The analyst pointed out that Apple's gross margins in the September quarter will be boosted by a positive impact from tariff refunds, and that margins could drop in the coming quarters.

Apple reported a gross margin of 49.3% in its June quarter, but warned that it would be between 47.5% and 48% in the September period.

"Investor focus will likely be on component cost inflation, durability of gross margin and services revenue growth," Mohan wrote in the note. 

Bank of America maintains a Buy rating on the stock and a price target of $370.

— Kif Leswing

Apple is competing in a smartphone and PC market that has "never been worse," IDC's Ryan Reith told CNBC.

Consumer electronics companies everywhere are grappling with surging prices for computer parts, including memory and flash storage that's used in every smartphone and laptop.

Intel on Friday had its worst day on the stock market since the 1970s, dropping 29%, after the company cited PC softness for a disappointing forecast.

Many companies have responded to component shortages by raising prices or reducing specifications for current devices. Consumers are holding onto their old computers for far longer, driving sales down. 

IDC says the smartphone market is set to drop nearly 17% this year based on shipments. PCs could be down 11%. 

Apple isn't immune. The company raised prices on almost all of its products since executives warned over the summer that the market was in a "thousand year flood."

— Kif Leswing

Apple's new $2,000 foldable phone won't have any sales for the quarter, which ended last month. While Apple and Ternus announced new iPhones on Sept. 9, the foldable Duo didn't go on sale until a month later, on Oct 23. 

Some analysts have theorized that Apple's most committed fans may have been waiting to see the Duo come out before buying it.

There will be a few weeks of sales from the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max, which went on sale on Sept. 18.

— Kif Leswing

Apple investors and customers are still getting to know Ternus, but since he took over for Tim Cook on Sept. 1, the new CEO has embraced his new role as the face of the iPhone company.

Ternus' first major appearance came nine days into his tenure, when he unveiled the iPhone Duo at Apple's headquarters. A week later, he was walking the red carpet at the Emmys. He then flew to New York, where he opened the doors at an Apple store to commemorate the new iPhone Pro going on sale. He also went to Austin, Texas, this month to kick off sales of the iPhone Duo, and appeared at an elementary school and an F1 race while on the trip. 

And he's had a busy media schedule, including an appearance on the cover of Time magazine.

Last week, Ternus continued a new style of Apple product introduction in unveiling the first MacBook with a touchscreen using a format he first tried out in the spring. That's when Apple launched its low-cost MacBook Neo. Ternus addressed a crowd of media and analysts, giving a five minute speech interspersed with a few short videos. 

Investors will get their first in-depth look at Ternus on Monday. He's been on the past few calls, but hasn't given extended remarks, and largely deferred to Cook when asked questions.

— Kif Leswing

Heading into its earnings report, Apple has emerged as the preferred megacap tech stock on Wall Street. The shares are up 27% this year as of Friday's close, by far outpacing Apple's leading peers.

It's also the last among the group (other than Nvidia, which reports next month) to issue quarterly earnings. Investors weren't kind to the hyperscalers over the past two weeks, scrutinizing their hefty expenses and dwindling cash flows.

Apple is in a much different category as the iPhone maker has avoided running up huge capital expenditures bills, taking a more measured approach to its AI investing.

—Ari Levy
