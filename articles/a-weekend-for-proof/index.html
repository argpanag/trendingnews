A Weekend For Proof

TheTools · 28.11.2026 08:25 · source · permalink

The mock accreditation exercise on Saturday offers eligible young voters in selected polling units an opportunity to help test the process through which their votes will eventually be recorded.

 

INEC tests its systems on Saturday. Opposition leaders face their own test on Monday. Campaign violence makes both exercises more consequential.

By Tuesday, Nigerians should have fresh evidence about two institutions seeking their confidence. INEC will have conducted its nationwide mock accreditation and result-upload exercise. Opposition parties will have had their scheduled opportunity to formalise cooperation in Abuja. The first must demonstrate operational readiness. The second must demonstrate an ability to act together. Both approach those tests amid a worsening concern: whether citizens can participate in the campaign safely.

Wednesday’s attack on Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) supporters in Benin City, Edo State, confirmed by the police, followed Monday’s deadly violence in Delta. Edo police reported one person injured and ordered an investigation. The attackers’ political affiliations and any sponsors have not been established. That development changes the context of the coming days. Electoral preparation now requires visible progress on protection, accountability and public confidence alongside equipment and political agreements.

Saturday: Let The Test Produce Evidence

 

 

INEC’s mock exercise is scheduled for November 28, from 8:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. Nigerian time, in 17,676 selected polling units. It will test accreditation, election devices, and the uploading and viewing of simulated results. Registered voters in the selected units are expected to participate.

The most useful outcome would be a public account of performance.
How many locations opened on time? How many participants were successfully accredited? Where did devices fail? How long did uploads take? How were exceptions resolved?

A rehearsal can reveal problems that announcements cannot. Finding a fault early allows INEC to correct it. Explaining that correction gives citizens a reason to have greater confidence.

The exercise also has practical support from state governments. Lagos has cancelled Saturday’s sanitation exercise, while Anambra has postponed its own, to avoid obstructing participation. Those decisions remove an immediate obstacle. The remaining test concerns delivery at the selected polling units.

 

 

Monday: What Will Opposition Unity Require?

 

 

Atiku Abubakar, Peter Obi, Seyi Makinde and other opposition leaders are expected at the November 30 summit. Their attendance and any signed commitments remain forthcoming. The APC says it is untroubled by the proposed gathering. The parties’ task is to define cooperation precisely.

Joint election monitoring could be valuable. So could common procedures for documenting intimidation, sharing verified information and coordinating legal responses.

Presidential cooperation presents more difficult choices. Supporters need to know whether their parties intend to campaign separately, endorse an existing candidate or pursue another lawful arrangement. A declaration of unity leaves those questions open unless it specifies obligations.

The summit should therefore be assessed through what participants authorise, who carries out the decisions and when implementation begins. Any agreement must reach state organisations, constituency candidates and polling agents to become electorally useful.

 

 

Obi’s Campaign Faces An Organisational Dispute

 

 

A fresh disagreement within the support-group environment complicates the NDC’s preparations. A group led by John Ughulu announced on Friday that it was withdrawing campaign and mobilisation support under the OK Movement name. The NDC rejected its claim to represent the authentic movement, while Obi’s spokesman said his campaign did not recognise Ughulu’s group as one of its support organisations.

This establishes a dispute over authority and affiliation. It does not establish how many voters, organisers or functioning structures have changed allegiance. Its practical significance depends on identifiable capacity: people organising locally, maintaining campaign operations and preparing for election day.

For Obi and Kwankwaso, the broader challenge is to coordinate their recognised organisations and give volunteers clear responsibilities. For anyone claiming to withdraw support, the test is whether the claimed structures can be independently demonstrated. Political labels alone cannot measure electoral strength.

 

Delta’s Aftermath, Edo’s Warning

 

 

The violence has continued to demand attention beyond Tuesday’s edition.

Reporting records visits by Obi and Kwankwaso to a hospitalised survivor of the Delta attack. Separately, the House of Representatives called for investigations and action against perpetrators and sponsors. Its discussion of possible emergency measures was conditional; it did not declare a state of emergency.

In Edo, the police account confirms an attack and a gunshot injury. The NDC alleges a broader pattern of political intimidation. The APC rejects allegations that it is coordinating attacks, and the Edo governor has denied government involvement. Those accusations and denials require examination against evidence.

The immediate standard is equal protection. Campaign organisers need reliable security arrangements. Victims need care and access to justice. Investigators need to identify individual responsibility and any sponsorship. Repeated condemnation without visible investigative progress risks teaching citizens that intimidation carries little consequence. That perception can discourage participation even where no attack occurs.

 

 

The Government’s Performance Remains On Trial

 

 

Campaign disputes must leave room for scrutiny of governing choices.
Atiku has questioned a reported decline in oil receipts, while Makinde’s campaign has challenged the relationship between expenditure plans and borrowing. The figures being debated concern an earlier reporting period; their renewed prominence does not make them newly incurred losses.

For Tinubu’s administration, the response should explain revenue performance, spending priorities and what citizens receive from public expenditure. For challengers, criticism should lead to a credible alternative. Promises on security, jobs, electricity and education require resources. Voters deserve to understand the proposed trade-offs.

The governing party seeks another mandate on its record and plans. The opposition seeks one on its diagnosis and proposed remedies. Both should face questions of cost, delivery and accountability.

 

 

 

APC/Tinubu: The immediate test is whether the governing camp can combine its campaign strength with confidence in public institutions and equal protection for political competitors.

ADC/Atiku: The leadership and nomination appeal remains consequential. The ADC has sought a stay of execution; the latest material reviewed does not establish that a stay has been granted. Its postponed Jigawa rally was attributed by a party spokesperson to mobilisation and logistical preparations, rather than a verified government prohibition.

NDC/Obi–Kwankwaso: Campaign safety and coordination among support organisations require attention. The disputed OK Movement announcement cannot yet be translated into a quantified electoral loss.

APM/Makinde and other opposition partners: Monday will test their willingness to accept defined responsibilities within a common arrangement.

State contests: The Court of Appeal has reserved judgment in the dispute over Isa Pantami’s PDP governorship nomination in Gombe. A reserved judgment is a pending decision, rather than a victory for either side.

Recent local and off-cycle elections remain useful indicators when turnout, competitiveness and local conditions are considered. They provide evidence about organisation and participation, without automatically predicting a presidential result.

Election Watch assessment: The coming weekend offers measurable tests of readiness and cooperation. No fresh, credible polling reviewed for this edition establishes a national swing.

 

Give Participation A Practical Purpose
Saturday offers eligible young voters in selected polling units an opportunity to help test the process through which their votes will eventually be recorded.
Their experience matters: the time taken, the clarity of instructions, the treatment of participants and the response to difficulties.

The information environment also deserves attention. INEC says it is strengthening verification tools and partnerships against digitally manipulated content and electoral misinformation. The effectiveness and fairness of those measures remain to be demonstrated.

Young campaigners can contribute through verification, voter education, observation and policy scrutiny. Party leaders should give them responsibility and protect them from intimidation.

The economic test remains equally concrete: do proposals offer realistic routes into work, affordable enterprise and useful skills?

Youth Metre assessment: Watch participation, safety and confidence in the process. Claims of a generational swing still require credible age-specific evidence.

 

• Published findings from Saturday’s mock exercise.
• Monday’s attendance, signed agreements and implementation timetable.
• Investigative progress on the Delta and Edo attacks.
• Any court order affecting the ADC or disputed state nominations.
• Evidence clarifying the reach of organisations claiming to deliver or withdraw support.

 

The Signal
INEC can use Saturday to show how its systems perform. Opposition leaders can use Monday to show what cooperation means in practice.
The next few days should give voters something more substantial than assurances: evidence they can judge.
