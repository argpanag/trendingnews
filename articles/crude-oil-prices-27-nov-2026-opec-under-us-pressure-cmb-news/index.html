Crude Oil Prices, 27 Nov 2026: OPEC Under US Pressure — CMB News

TheTools · 27.11.2026 07:49 · source · permalink

Crude oil trades around recent highs as US pressure on Venezuela and Iraq to leave OPEC raises cartel cohesion risks while near term prices look volatile but supported.

Prices

Brent and WTI have rebounded from early week lows, with intraday quotes on 27 November showing Brent fluctuating in the low- to mid-90s USD/bbl range and WTI in the mid-80s USD/bbl, after a sharp rally on 25 November lifted Brent above USD 94/bbl and WTI back into the low 80s.

Technically, Brent remains in a corrective downtrend from early November highs, trading below its 20- and 50-day moving averages, but short-term momentum has turned positive again after this week’s bounce.

Supply & Demand

According to multiple reports, the United States encouraged Venezuela and Iraq earlier this year to consider leaving OPEC and, in Venezuela’s case, blocked payment of its OPEC membership dues from US-controlled oil revenues. This represents a direct challenge to the cartel’s cohesion and to its ability to manage aggregate output.

Venezuela, exempt from current OPEC+ quotas, is gradually rebuilding production and targets further gains toward 2027, while Iraq is already locked in a dispute with OPEC+ over an external review that pegs its sustainable capacity below Baghdad’s own estimate. If either producer were to loosen adherence to OPEC+ guidance – inside or outside the group – medium-term upside capacity to add supply would increase, potentially capping prices once current geopolitical risk premia fade.

On the demand side, recent analysis and official projections point to slower global oil consumption growth in 2026 compared with 2025, as high prices and weaker macro data in key consuming economies temper usage. This softer demand backdrop makes cartel discipline more important: any additional uncoordinated barrels from Iraq or Venezuela would more quickly translate into downside price pressure.

Fundamentals & Geopolitics

The reported US maneuvers exploit Washington’s financial leverage over both Caracas and Baghdad, including control over Venezuelan state oil revenues held in US accounts and oversight of Iraqi oil proceeds routed via the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The goal appears to be higher production and investment outside OPEC’s quota framework, aiming to restrain global prices and ease domestic fuel costs.

Within OPEC+, tension over capacity baselines is already visible. Iraq is pushing back against an external study that placed its maximum sustainable capacity below 5 million bpd and is seeking recognition of a significantly higher figure ahead of Sunday’s online policy summit, where 2027 quotas will be calibrated. This dispute, coupled with US pressure, increases the risk of a looser, more fragmented quota system going forward.

For Venezuela, any move away from OPEC discipline could accelerate its production recovery, but only if sanctions flexibility and investment materialize. For Iraq, the primary leverage remains within the group: threatening to leave is a bargaining chip to secure a higher quota rather than an imminent policy shift, but even talk of exit is enough to add a geopolitical premium to prices.

Short-Term Outlook & Trading Takeaways

  • Headline risk premium: Into and immediately after the 29 November OPEC+ meeting, crude prices are likely to remain highly sensitive to leaks or statements on Iraq’s quota and any comment on Venezuela’s status. Expect intraday swings rather than a one-way trend.
  • Upside risks: If OPEC+ reaffirms strong cohesion and avoids visible splits, current geopolitical noise could support Brent holding in the 90s USD/bbl, especially if broader risk sentiment stays constructive.
  • Downside risks: Clear signs of quota slippage, hints that Iraq may disregard future limits, or progress toward bringing more Venezuelan barrels to market without offsetting cuts elsewhere would point to a softer medium-term price path.
  • Positioning bias: Short-term traders may look to fade extreme spikes driven purely by exit headlines, assuming OPEC+ retains at least a loose coordinating function, while longer-horizon hedgers in consuming countries could use rallies toward the upper 90s in Brent as opportunities to layer in coverage.

Across US, Venezuelan and Iraqi crude streams, differentials to Brent are likely to remain more sensitive to perceived policy risk and sanctions dynamics than to local weather or near-term demand changes over the next three days.
