Lamine Yamal stars again as Spain see off Czechia and maintain perfect start

TheTools · 03.10.2026 21:13 · source · permalink

Lamine Yamal says he feels like Superman or Spider-Man sometimes, and Spain’s fans are starting to wonder if he just might be. There is something comic book hero about what the teenager is doing right now, some unexplained super power swelling inside him. For the third consecutive game he scored inside six minutes, curling in another outrageous goal as Spain completed their World Cup homecoming in Oviedo with a 3-1 win over Czechia that leaves them a single point from the Nations League quarter finals already. Spain’s manager, Luis de la Fuente, put it simply: “Lamine Yamal is on course to become a legend.”

And yet in a game which seemed to have been done at half-time, when Spain led 2-0 after Rodrigo Hernandez had added the second and Lamine Yamal was withdrawn, his show seemingly over, the danger returned. Czechia, vastly improved after the break, pulled one back through Jiri Slama on 69 and then were denied a 79th-minute equaliser when Vassilis Fotias, the referee, who rescued Spain, harshly ruled out a Ryan Naderi goal – apparently for a foul on Robin Le Normand as the free-kick that led to it was swung in.

Reduced to 10 men soon after, still furious, Czechia finally fell with two minutes left when Mikel Merino made Spain safe. The seleccion’s first visit to the Carlos Tartiere in 11 years had ended the way they had hoped. Welcomed back as world and European champions once more, they were applauded off as winners again. A night that had begun with Rodri being presented with the World Cup by Santi Cazorla and Juan Mata, academy products at Real Oviedo and members of the generation to which all others would forever be compared, ended with a jeopardy no one anticipated when it started in a way that has become implausibly, absurdly familiar.

Lamine Yamal had scored after 104 seconds at Wembley and 70 in Seville, as Spain defeated England and Croatia. And the silly thing was that here, his goal might have come even sooner. Fifteen seconds in, the first frisson of excitement went round this place as he chased down the goalkeeper and after just fifty-seven Oviedo almost exploded. But, running on to a Dani Olmo pass, Lamine Yamal calmly guided a shot past the goalkeeper Antonin Kinsky … and past the post.

He would just have to wait until the sixth minute was up instead. Playing a quick one-two with Dani Olmo, Lamine Yamal took possession on the top right corner of the area and curled another outrageous shot into the far corner, the ridiculous becoming routine. They had been playing five minutes 20 seconds, another stadium letting out a roar that spoke of delight but also a kind of disbelief. 320 seconds he had been out there, taking him to 494 seconds to score three.

Spain weren’t hurrying but they were controlling, and the chances were accumulating: by half-time they had taken 13 shots to one, and enjoyed 70% of possession. They had also added a second on 32 minutes. Rodri collected near the centre circle and did something he rarely does. The man who sometimes seems to be walking, effortlessly controlling the game with his mind and without fuss, happy enough to drop the ball off to others to do their thing, went off on a run. Advancing through the midfield, he slotted to Pedri whose return, with the help of a defender’s touch, set him up to shoot high into the net.

Next Lamine Yamal slipped in Alex Baena, denied by Kinsky. A little dinked pass from Baena then found Lamine whose shot, slightly scuffed, went wide. At the break Baena, who had drawn “oohs” with one glorious touch, was withdrawn and so too was Lamine Yamal, his work here done, another show over. The game, on the other hand, was not. That at least was Czechia’s intention and it should probably have been their reward too, the visitors taking a decisive step forward and a degree of control. They also reduced the deficit with 20 minutes to go.

The move began deep: as it progressed up the right and through the middle, Jiri Slama was sprinting to catch up. Adam Hlozek saw him coming and played the ball into his path for him to steer it low and true beyond David Raya.

A first error from Spain’s keeper almost gave Czechia an equaliser when Raya stepped out to play and his misplaced pass only found Ondrej Lingr, and a second error really should have done soon after. But if there was relief for Spain when Lingr’s shot drifted wide of an unguarded goal, it was even greater when the referee ruled out Ryan Nadel’s effort.

For Czechia there was disbelief. Michal Sadilek’s long cross had been headed goalwards by Ladislav Krejci, and Raya, reaching by the bar, could not hold on. The ball dropped on the line, where Nadel nudged it in. The referee, though, had blown. For Santi Denia on the touchline it was hard to take and it was equally difficult not to feel sympathy. All the more when Krejci was soon sent off and then Víctor Muñoz was sent clear, dashed into the area where Kinsky blocked and Merino, forever alive, finished off the festivities.
