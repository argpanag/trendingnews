Men's Results and Upcoming Schedule

No. 11 Frances Tiafoe def. Alex Michelsen, 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6 (6)

Frances Tiafoe staged a stunning comeback, rallying from two sets down against Alex Michelsen to secure his place in the US Open semifinal for the third time in his career.

Michelsen came out firing, using fearless aggression to quickly take control and build a commanding two-set lead.

Tiafoe looked tentative, managing just 8 winners in the first two sets as he struggled to find his rhythm.

Michelsen was on the verge of becoming the first unseeded man this century to reach a major semifinal without losing a set.

But everything changed in an instant.

As Michelsen served for the match, Tiafoe finally found his form—unleashing his signature intensity and athleticism, breaking twice in a row to seize the set and dramatically alter the match's course.

Fueled by that surge, Tiafoe broke Michelsen two more times in the fourth, forcing a deciding fifth set and sending the crowd into a frenzy.

Michelsen quickly recaptured the momentum in the fifth set, seizing the first service break.

But Tiafoe remained undeterred, ultimately breaking right back.

Both players continued to hold, sending the final set to a 10-point tie-breaker, where Tiafoe hit an incredible cross-court return at 4-4 to claim what would be a crucial mini-break.

The quarterfinals continue Wednesday on ESPN, with all matches also being available on ESPN Unlimited.

Women's Results and Upcoming Schedule

No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka def. No. 6 Linda Noskova, 7-6 (1), 3-6, 7-6 (7)

Aryna Sabalenka outlasted Linda Noskova in a three-set thriller to keep her quest for a US Open three-peat alive.

Sabalenka started strong, taking a 5-2 lead, but Noskova regained her composure, breaking Sabalenka and forcing a tiebreaker.

Sabalenka quickly regrouped and dominated the tiebreaker to take the opening set.

However, Noskova was just beginning to find her rhythm. The 21-year-old delivered a commanding performance in the second set, winning 83 percent of points on her first serve and hitting 14 winners.

In the third set, both players exchanged service breaks until Sabalenka faltered in an error-filled game, falling behind 3-2.

It appeared that Sabalenka was starting to lose her composure; however, the World No. 1 remained resolute, maintaining her aggressive play and breaking back to equalize the match at 4-4.

The third set was sent to a tie-breaker, where Sabalenka found another gear to avoid the massive upset.
