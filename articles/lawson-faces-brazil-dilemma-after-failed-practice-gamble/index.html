Lawson faces Brazil dilemma after failed practice gamble

TheTools · 06.11.2026 22:14 · source · permalink

The Racing Bulls driver dropped from 10th in FP1 to 13th in FP2 on Friday, finishing a place behind team-mate Arvid Lindblad as a change of direction on his car failed to deliver the hoped-for improvement.

As temperatures dropped at Interlagos, Lawson found himself battling considerable understeer, leaving the team with a decision to make before qualifying.

“We started a little bit stronger this morning, and then we tried something that didn’t really work in FP2, and when it got a lot colder, the stuff we changed was in the opposite direction to that, so it didn’t really help,” Lawson said.

“I think we’re pretty surprised by how much of an effect what we changed had because the car was probably one of the worst balances we’ve had this season, or at least in the recent races in FP2.”

Lawson said he favoured a return to the earlier configuration for final practice, although the prospect of rain on Sunday had complicated matters.

“We’ll probably go back on that,” he said.

“I think we had a better car [in FP1], so we’ll try to start with something similar [in FP3], and then we’ll see what happens with the weather because it is probably going to rain.”

“But obviously, we can’t change anything drastically because if it rains, you need to have a good car in the rain as well.”

With the race set-up largely determined once qualifying gets underway, Racing Bulls must weigh up its dry-weather pace against how the car might perform in wet conditions on Sunday.

Lawson said the cooler track presented another challenge, with drivers struggling to get enough temperature into their tyres before reaching the first corner.

“Any traffic queuing in the pit lane on track can really, really affect Turn 1, so it’s going to be interesting, especially Q1 when everybody’s on track together,” he added.

Lindblad experienced a different trajectory. After finishing 14th in FP1 on his first visit to Interlagos, the rookie improved to 12th in FP2 and reported greater confidence after set-up changes of his own.

“We made some changes ahead of FP2, where I felt much more comfortable and confident behind the wheel, so that’s a definite positive,” Lindblad said.

Racing Bulls team principal Alan Permane acknowledged the team had fallen short of its usual Friday performance, but remained encouraged by its wet-weather potential.

“We know the car runs well in the wet, so we’re optimistic as we look ahead to Qualifying tomorrow,” Permane said.
