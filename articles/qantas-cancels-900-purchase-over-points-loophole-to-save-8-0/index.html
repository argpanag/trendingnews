Qantas cancels $900 purchase over points loophole to save $8,000 on flights: 'Unheard of'

TheTools · 08.11.2026 14:21 · source · permalink
  • QAN.AX
    0.00%

Business class flights to Europe are probably not an expense most Aussies are considering at the moment. But for Sydneysider Andrew Stavrou, spending $1,000 on them was a no brainer — even if it meant he ended up with ten pairs of earphones he didn't need. 

Stavrou, who calls himself The Point Exec online, had created a personalised tracker that alerts him in real-time to any deals that come out regarding frequent flyer points. He recently got a notification about a Qantas points deal with Sony earbuds on the airline's Marketplace and, without hesitation, went on and snapped up a bunch. 

"I knew [the ten purchases] would get me about 150,000 Qantas points, which is about a one-way business class ticket to Europe," he told Yahoo Finance.

"That's the way I saw it."

Stavrou explained that the deal saw each pair of earphones, which sold for $99, bringing in 15,000 Qantas Points – working out to be 0.65 cents per point. 

Multiplying that by ten pairs would equal 150,000 Qantas Points (for $990), and a Sydney to Dubai flight with Emirates Business can be purchased with 143,000 points. 

If a frequent flyer were to purchase points directly from Qantas (with no promotion running), it would cost $3,999. That works out to be 2.67c per point. 

By comparison, the cash fare could be more than $9,000 for the same Sydney-Dubai route, so the $990 earphone-deal demonstrated savings of around $8,000. 

"That's almost unheard of," Stavrou said. 

For most people, if they are earning Qantas points on every 2c, then they are happy to go down that path of spending, rather than the higher cost of purchasing points directly, Stavrou said. 

It would mean that spending $200 equals 10,000 in Qantas points, making anything under that 2c mark a "great deal". 

"So when it was 0.65 cents… It was amazing. It was almost too good to be true," Stavrou said. 

Unfortunately for him, it was too good to be true. His purchase was cancelled at the last minute by Qantas, with the airline claiming the deal was an error on their behalf. 

Stavrou isn't sure about the validity of that claim, considering the deal was promoted all weekend until they suddenly pulled the pin, coincidentally after a video he posted about the deal went viral online. 

Qantas has been contacted by Yahoo Finance.
