Victorian Liberal party uses AI to write Jess Wilson’s tribute to Molly Meldrum

A statement on the death of Molly Meldrum attributed to the Victorian opposition leader, Jess Wilson, was written using AI, the Liberal party has confirmed.

Meldrum, the Australian music journalist, broadcaster and producer, died on Monday morning. He was 83. Shortly after his death, Wilson issued a statement describing Meldrum as a “true Australian icon who will never be forgotten”.

“His famous cowboy hat, his larger-than-life personality and his lifelong love for his beloved Saints made him unmistakably Molly,” the statement read.

“But above all, he will be remembered for his generosity, his warmth and the enormous affection Australians held for him.”

When submitted to AI-detector service Pangram, the 177-word statement came up as 100% AI-authored. Pangram maintains its algorithm is so accurate that it incorrectly identifies text as an AI output only about one in every 10,000 times.

In response to questions from Guardian Australia, a Liberal party spokesperson confirmed the party used AI to compile research on Meldrum’s career and draft the statement.

“Like plenty of workplaces, we use search and AI to help with drafting and researching a variety of material, which was the case here,” the spokesperson said.

“The initial draft was done using a combination of search and AI to compile Molly’s extensive career.”

It is understood the initial AI-generated draft did not include references to Meldrum’s time on TV variety show, Hey Hey It’s Saturday or the two times he was crowned as the king of Melbourne’s Moomba festival. That information was added by a member of the team.

“The statement was then fact checked and edited by a member of the team before it was provided to the Leader for approval,” the spokesperson said. “The final version pays tribute to a true legend of the Australian entertainment industry.”

A tribute by the premier, Ben Carroll, came up as 100% human-authored when submitted to Pangram.

In it, the Victorian Labor leader described the Sunday night tradition of tuning into Meldrum’s Countdown program and then heading to a record shop on Monday morning to ask for the song he recommended.

“Molly gave young Australian musicians a chance to be heard in lounge rooms across the country,” Carroll’s statement said.

“Years before Countdown, he helped start the St Kilda cheer squad. Supporting others was in his nature. A giant of the Australian music industry and a true character.”

Carroll said Meldrum would be honoured with a state funeral.

AI has been used extensively during the Victorian election campaign by the major parties and One Nation, as well as third-party campaigners. Labor’s release of a deepfake video of Wilson trick-or-treating alongside One Nation’s Victorian leader, Warren Pickering, was widely criticised and prompted the Greens to call for the practice to be banned altogether.

Timothy Koskie, a media researcher at the University of Sydney, said authenticity is becoming a “harder currency to build” due to the prevalence of AI.

“The public is doubting the credibility, authenticity, reliability of just about everything in the shared social space,” he said.

Last month, the Australian Electoral Commissioner, Jeff Pope, told the federal parliamentary inquiry on artificial intelligence that there would be challenges in requiring political communications to be labelled if AI was used in its creation.

“The challenge with that is that, if AI is not already ubiquitous, it’s not far away,” he said. “If you’re labelling everything with AI, then, at the next election or not too long thereafter, every bit of political communication could have an AI label on it, and you’ve got to wonder about the effectiveness of that.”
