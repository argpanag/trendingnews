Fact check: Did Ben Shelton actually break Grand Slam record with 158 mph serve?

TheTools · 12.09.2026 09:16 · source · permalink

One of Ben Shelton’s humungous serves went viral during his US Open clash with Frances Tiafoe for breaking the all-time record. Here’s a look at what actually happened.

Ben Shelton has made the first Grand Slam final of his career, in which he will be facing off against Alexander Zverev. Perhaps the most important aspect of his game that brought him this far is his serve.

Shelton took the match against Carlos Alcaraz with a monster ace. Against Frances Tiafoe in the semi-final, he was at it again.

One of his services was clocked at a whopping 158 mph (254 kmph) during the game, which would blow the Grand Slam record for fastest serve ever out of the water. However, there is a twist in the tale.

Ben Shelton’s 158 mph serve was actually 144 mph and the reading was wrong

Shelton’s big serve in the third set saw Tiafoe barely able to return the ball, with Shelton winning the point with his next forehand. The moment went viral as the speed was shown to be 158mph.

Giovanni Mpetshi Perricard holds the record for fastest serve at a Grand Slam, he managed a 151.6 mph serve at Wimbledon in 2025.

However, it seems that Shelton did not, in fact, break this record. Soon after the moment, the official US Open account issued a correction about it.

Shelton and his fans may have been hoping to get the record, but he broke plenty of others as he won his first ever Grand Slam semi-final at the age of 23.

The US Open final is set to take place on Sunday, and it promises to be an absolutely epic clash. Shelton will enjoy support of the likes of which only a few have experienced before.
