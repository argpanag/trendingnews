Morgan Geekie was drafted by the Hurricanes in 2017. On Tuesday, he returns with the first-place Bruins. - The Boston Globe

TheTools · 27.10.2026 23:38 · source · permalink

Morgan Geekie’s “Boston” story, now in its fourth regular-season chapter, began upon his signing here as an unrestricted free agent in the summer of 2023. On the whole, the story has turned into a pretty good read.

On Tuesday night, the hard-shooting winger will be with the Bruins in Raleigh, N.C., which is where his hockey story began in June 2017, when the Hurricanes picked him at No. 67 in the NHL Draft.

Geekie was 18, his pro career about to unfold, and yet to realize in full that hockey, though a sport, is first and foremost a business at the pro level, often with parts deemed interchangeable. It all came into sharp focus four years later when the Hurricanes didn’t protect him in the Seattle expansion draft.

The once-promising Hurricane was suddenly, and unexpectedly, a member of the Kraken’s inaugural roster.

“It’s always tough, especially at a young age,” said Geekie, recalling how he felt when his Carolina tenure abruptly ended. “You start to question things a little bit — especially the type of player you are — when you’re not established, it’s a lot tougher to get your feet under you.”

More than five years since shipping off to Seattle, Geekie will face the Hurricanes again, in the Bruins’ first meeting this season against the defending Stanley Cup champions. The sizzling 9-2-1 Bruins will enter with the leading points percentage (.792) in the NHL, with the high-tempo 9-2-2 (.769) Carolinians barely a stick blade’s distance away in the standings.

Through the dozen games, Geekie has contributed 9 points, a pace just off the career-high 68 he posted in 81 games last season. His goal total remains stuck at one, but with so many others in the lineup scoring — eight Black-and-Gold forwards have three goals or more — his output as a top-six winger fits just fine in the team’s winning mosaic.

“I know it’s cliché,” mused Geekie, riding of late on right wing on a trio with Fraser Minten and Marat Khusnutdinov, “but I [couldn’t] really care less, honestly. I truly do not care one bit about it.”

Geekie has proven the goals will be there. He has suited up for 246 regular-season games for the Bruins, and he has 90 goals. He owns one of the league’s hardest shots, can play either wing, and there is still a plan, conceived in the offseason, for him to post up in the bumper position (a.k.a. Patrice Bergeron’s office) on the power play.

It’s that history of production that gives Geekie the confidence that goals will come.

“We’ve got the two best records in the East going at it tomorrow,” Geekie noted after Monday’s workout in Brighton. “It’s tough to complain when [the team is] sitting at the top. So, just try to continue and pitch in in other ways. Every line is pitching in offensively, and if you can get a little bit from everybody, that’s better than a lot from just a few.”

As a first-year pro in the Hurricanes’ organization, Geekie won the AHL’s Calder Cup with Charlotte. He was a rookie with a championship title, and he finally played his first two NHL games the following season. He then played another 36 in the COVID-shortened 2020-21 season, leading up to his departure in the expansion draft.

“The championship in Charlotte was probably one of the biggest things to help me out a lot,” said Geekie. “You know, confidence-wise, just to prove you can play at a high level. You go through all of those grindy camps with a lot of guys kind of the same age. A lot of guys are trying to make the jump. And it seems with that home [Charlotte] team, only one or two guys ended up playing with Carolina after that.”

Seattle, noted Geekie, ended up being “a fresh start for everybody.”

“And I think going [there], for everyone involved, was maybe a blessing in disguise,” he said. “It’s one of those things, I don’t think I’d be where I am without it.”

Veteran Mikey Eyssimont, waived to AHL Providence this season without suiting up for the Boston varsity, has nine goals and an assist in his six games with the Baby Bruins.

Coach Marco Sturm on Monday again praised the hard-working winger for having an impressive training camp and did not dismiss the idea that he could be called back.

“Unfortunately, he was the odd man out,” said Sturm, referring to the late-September roster cuts. “But I do believe he’ll get his chance again. With one injury [to the Boston roster], he’s right back at it. I’m just happy that he didn’t just drop his head about being in the minors. No, he’s been a really good pro. I’m very happy for him.”
