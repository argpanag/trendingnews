Crime drama follows journalist's quest for truth

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:48 · source · permalink

Actress Liu Shishi initially struggled to understand why the protagonist in the crime drama Dream would choose to remain in her hometown rather than leave her troubles behind. But as the story unfolds, a series of murders draws her back into a past she can neither escape nor ignore, turning a personal dilemma into a deeper exploration of duty, human nature and the lives caught up in changing times.

Set in a small city in Northeast China, the series follows Liu's character, an experienced television journalist who returns home after more than a decade of struggling to build a career in Beijing. Initially, she plans to sell her property and start afresh elsewhere. But she is drawn into a series of murders linked to a local brewery, about which she made a documentary in 1999.

"I felt confused about her choice. If she has an opportunity to clear her name and leave the city, why does she choose to stay?" Liu recalled at a recent symposium in Beijing.

She took the question to director Yang Yang, whose explanation — that the protagonist's decision was rooted in her sense of duty as an investigative journalist — helped her understand the role.

Liu describes the character as "a hedgehog trapped in fog", an ordinary person whose old wounds are reopened by fate.

"She is no halo-clad hero. What is admirable is that, despite her fears and doubts, her innate goodness enables her to confront herself and bravely point to the truth," Liu said.

Yang said the drama uses the rise and decline of an old brewery over more than a decade as a backdrop to explore how individual lives intersect with broader social changes.

"Suspense is only the shell of the story," she said. "I hope it can lead audiences to see the real people, human nature and emotions behind the events, as well as the joys, sorrows and choices of ordinary people caught up in the tide of the times."

Yang added that film and television productions should go beyond presenting social issues, encouraging audiences to reflect on the relationships and deeper causes behind events through the characters' experiences. For creators, she said, curiosity about and respect for everyday life remain essential to understanding the times.

Wang Yuren, the chief producer, said that, despite the growing impact of AI on the film and television industry, the creative principles and realist approach underpinning long-form dramas should not be neglected or abandoned. He also encouraged young creators to carry these values forward and continue developing them.
