GALLERY: The 'reset button', the reply exam and the return of Eli

Newcastle Jets coach Mark Milligan admits that he's pushed the "reset button".

Amid a dozen squad changes and first-up defeats across two separate competitions, the last month or so has given the Hunter-based club a chance to somewhat take stock.

It comes ahead of a blockbuster AFC Champion's League Elite fixture, with the Jets welcoming Japanese powerhouse Gamba Osaka to Newcastle's McDonald Jones Stadium on Tuesday (6:45pm).

"Obviously we've had four weeks [since round one] to get some work under the player's belts and get a few more games in, which is very important," Milligan told media after training at the Turton Road venue on Monday.

"Everyone now also has a very good idea of exactly the challenges that we face and it's been a good learning experience for me as well.

"Obviously the way that we finished last season and the football that we were playing, we had to push the reset button a little bit again.

"Especially in terms of the amount of changes that we've had through the squad, so the last month has been fantastic being able to build into the team that we want to be."

The Jets, who claimed a domestic double last season by holding aloft trophies for both the Australia Cup and A-League Men's premiership, opened their current AFC Champion's League Elite campaign with a 7-1 loss away to Kashima Antlers last month.

"it's obviously never nice to have a result like that," Milligan said.

"But from my point of view, when I look at the players and what I ask them to do, it was always going to be extremely difficult. And it was a good indicator of where we were at.

"It was important as well, if you take the result away, that we use that to continue to emphasise the direction we want to head in and the way want to play football.

"We've been through this before. We went through this at the start of last season. I think the group now has a very good mentality and they understand why, where and how we fell short.

"The last three or four weeks have helped get us to a point where we can be more competitive from a physical point of view, which has probably been the most important."

There's every chance the Jets will be up against a familiar face on October 13, with former star Eli Adams now part of the opposing Gamba Osaka roster.

Newcastle captain Max Burgess knows all too well the attacking threat Adams poses, but with the boots now firmly on the other foot, the locals will now be trying to nullify the impact of a recent teammate.

"He's got a great left foot, so we need to stop that," Burgess said.

"We obviously know Eli well. He's got a lot of quality and deserves to be playing at that level.

"As Millsy [coach Milligan] just said before, it's a collective thing. They've got a lot of threats.

"But as always our attention and our focus is on ourselves and what we do first and foremost and I know all the boys are very excited to play tomorrow night."

In terms of leading out the first XI at home in this tournament, Burgess says it's "very special on a personal level."

"It's a huge honour to be captain of this side and this club," he said.

"To bring the Asian Champion's League to Newcastle, to the Hunter, is a really special thing.

"Football is massive in this region. We got a taste of that with the fans last year and how special the community is.

"Looking forward to leveraging off that and hopefully delivering great football for our fans."

Milligan hinted at a "few changes" on game eve, confirming new recruit Alex Grant would return to the main group while Alex Badolato and Oscar Fryer were both continuing rehabilitation from ACL surgeries last year.

The Jets, who begin A-League commitments with a derby against the Mariners on the Central Coast this weekend, were eliminated from the Australia Cup by Preston Lions (3-2) in the round of 32.

Newcastle is facing a jam-packed schedule of seven matches in the space of 25 days.
