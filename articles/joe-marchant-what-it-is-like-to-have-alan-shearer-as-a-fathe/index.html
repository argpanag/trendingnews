Joe Marchant: What it is like to have Alan Shearer as a father-in-law

TheTools · 17.09.2026 13:11 · source · permalink

Sale centre returns to Prem after three-year spell in France hoping to catch eye of new extended family and England selectors

After three years in France, Joe Marchant returned to England this summer with a new assertiveness, a point to prove and a footballing father-in-law.

It proved a busy couple of months for the 30-year-old centre who swapped a fashionable arrondissement in central Paris for Hale, a village south of Manchester, after signing for Sale Sharks from Stade Français. Marchant has also tied the knot with Hollie Shearer, daughter of the great England striker Alan, although there still remains a divide between the round and oval ball elements of the extended family.

“I have conversations [with Alan] about the differences between rugby and football,” Marchant said. “My football knowledge is terrible and Hollie’s brother reminds me of that a fair bit. The things which are most unrealistic are when we’re playing football here and people expect me to be good. I’m like, ‘Why would that make me good in any way?’ I’m not the best footballer.”

There are vague plans for Shearer to attend Sale’s match against Newcastle Red Bulls in October. “Hopefully he’s supporting us,” Marchant says, slightly hesitantly.

Former Newcastle forwards are not the only spectators whose eye Marchant is hoping to catch this season. Marchant makes no bones about the fact he is targeting a place at the 2027 World Cup having started all of England’s knockout matches in the 2023 tournament in France. A rotating cast of players have cycled through England’s No 13 shirt ever since. The early signs at Sale, albeit in training and pre-season, have been highly encouraging.

“He looks world class,” Alex Sanderson, the Sale director of rugby, says. “He is as good defensively as he is in attack, you just notice his involvements in attack more. His ability to hold feet as the second-to-last man or last man is brilliant. That’s where I have been most surprised: his talk, his awareness, his alignment, his line speed defensively. He is a complete 13.”

Marchant only ended up in France after a peculiar chain of events that he calls the “Eddie chapter”. In 2022, Eddie Jones, then England head coach, told him in no uncertain terms that he would not play for England under him, so Marchant explored options away from Harlequins, opting to sign for Stade Français. Less than a month later, Steve Borthwick, who most certainly does rate Marchant, had replaced Jones.

“There were those conversations [with Eddie Jones] just before the autumn internationals that I wasn’t fitting the mould and I wasn’t going to play in the World Cup,” Marchant says. “And the Stade offer I thought was actually quite good timing then because obviously it was a good offer that, on the back of that with the England stuff, it seemed like the right thing to do. And then three weeks later, Eddie wasn’t with England any more.

“I picked up with Steve before the Six Nations and we had a really honest chat. He brought me in just before and we had a really honest chat about who I am as a player and my decisions to go abroad and all that, and who I am as a person as well. And they were all really positive. From that, we just got pretty aligned where he felt like he knew what he wanted from me and how to get it.”

Hence when the rest of England’s squad headed home after their third-place finish at the 2023 World Cup, Marchant remained in Paris to join Stade. “It was a bit of a strange one, everyone was going home and for me it was my new home,” he says. Nevertheless, Marchant made the most of his time in the City of Light, exploring the nooks and crannies of Paris, even if he could never get his head around the abysmal French tea-making process of providing a cup of warm water and a separate teabag. “No milk as well and you’re like ‘What’s going on here?’ And no Yorkshire tea,” Marchant said. “In all seriousness, I think the home comforts are the big ones [I missed]. It sounds crazy, but just a cup of tea and some chocolate digestives, and a roast on Sunday.”

Marchant is one of just a handful of English players – including James Haskell and Andy Goode – who have played in both Top 14 and Super Rugby after a brief loan spell with the Blues in New Zealand. If the rapid speed of Super Rugby was the lightning then the Top 14 brought the thunder. “Every weekend, there’s someone who is at least 150kg [23st 9lb] who always sits out of a five-man line-out and you just have to get ready for that coming every week.”

Another benefit from playing in France was that it helped Marchant find his voice. Playing outside Danny Care and Nick Evans for the majority of his Harlequins career, Marchant let his rugby do the talking whereas a language barrier forced him to be more assertive.

“You can’t be shy,” Marchant says. “You have just got to be not afraid of making mistakes and you have got to pick it up on the run and communicate with everyone on the pitch and I think trying to get clarity on what you are trying to say, I think has helped loads especially coming in here. There’s no translation in my head as I am going to call something.”

Borthwick had told both Marchant and Sale team-mate Courtney Lawes that they will need to prove themselves in the Prem before he considers handing recalls to his French émigrés. “Regarding England, I want to play for England, that’s what I want to do,” Marchant says. “I’m proud to have played for England and I don’t want it to be my last time at that World Cup, I want to play again.”
