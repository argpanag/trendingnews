Donegal is par for the course in Bill Murray’s new BBC series - Donegal Daily

TheTools · 17.10.2026 19:11 · source · permalink

Bill Murray sporting Cruit Island Golf Club colours in HLINC golfwear. Photo: HLINC. Inset: Bill with Eddie Doherty during his visit to Eddie Doherty Handwoven Tweeds in Ardara via Facebook

Hollywood star Bill Murray’s Donegal golf adventures are set for the small screen next week, with Cruit Island, Ardara and Lough Eske all featuring in his new BBC series.

A Course Called Ireland is a new six-part series following the Ghostbusters and Groundhog Day star on a golfing road trip around Ireland and Northern Ireland.

Inspired by Tom Coyne’s bestselling book of the same name, the series mixes golf with travel, chance encounters and plenty of humour along the way.

Murray is joined throughout the series by Coyne, members of the Murray family and a string of celebrity guests including Chris O’Dowd, Joanne McNally and former NFL star Jason Kelce.

The Donegal leg saw Bill, Tom and Bill’s brothers Joel and Johnny play golf at the spectacular Cruit Island Golf Club.

They also travelled to Ardara, where they visited Eddie Doherty’s Handwoven Tweeds, before overnighting at Lough Eske.

Bill Murray and Joel Murray with Sean Hyland, John Boyle, Cathal Doherty, Robbie O'Donnell, Joel Murray, John Murray, Tom Coyne at Cruit Island. Photo: BBC/Stellify Media

Bill Murray, Joel Murray at Cruit Island Golf Club. Photo: BBC/Stellify Media

Murray had been spotted in Ardara in September 2025 while travelling around Ireland for the production.

The upcoming series is as much about the places and people encountered along the way as it is about golf.

Bill Murray at Eddie Doherty's shop in Ardara. Photo: BBC/Stellify Media

Bill Murray, Eddie Doherty and Conor McCrossan at Eddie Doherty's Handwoven Tweeds Ardara. BBC/Stellify Media

Across the six episodes, Murray and his companions take on some of Ireland’s most memorable courses while also going “off course” to enjoy the craic, local characters and unexpected moments that come with the journey.

The first episode, Feels Like Home, airs on BBC One Northern Ireland next Friday, October 23 at 10.40pm.

It will also be shown on BBC Two on Sunday, October 25 at 9.30pm, with another airing on BBC Two on Tuesday, October 27 at 10pm.

Episode two, The High Notes, airs on BBC One Northern Ireland on Tuesday, October 27 at 10.40pm, with Chris O’Dowd joining Murray for more golfing chaos.

 

The series is being made with funding from Northern Ireland Screen.

A Course Called Ireland is co-produced by independent production company Stellify Media and Skydance Sports.
