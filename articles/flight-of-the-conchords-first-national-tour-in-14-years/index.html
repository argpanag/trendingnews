Flight of the Conchords first national tour in 14 years

TheTools · 17.09.2026 01:15 · source · permalink

The popular New Zealand comedy duo is coming home for a string of sets in November.

Bret McKenzie and Jemaine Clement will return to Aotearoa this November for their first New Zealand tour since 2012.

After an eight-year pause from performing live, Flight of the Conchords surprised the capital in April with five shows at Wellington’s Meow Nui. They sold out in minutes, sparking a frenzy among fans.

Flight of the Conchords announced on Monday they will perform at Michael Fowler Centre in Wellington on 10 and 14 November, Christchurch’s Wolfbrook Arena on 12 November, and The Civic in Auckland on 18 November and 19 November.

Tickets go on sale 18 September at 1pm.

McKenzie and Clement formed the musical comedy act in 1998, soaring to worldwide fame off the back of their HBO comedy series of the same name with tunes including 'Business Time' and 'Hiphopopotamus vs. Rhymenoceros'.

While taking a break from performing together recently, Clement has kept busy writing on comedy horror TV shows Wellington Paranormal and What We Do In The Shadows. He most recently played Dr Garvin in the last two Avatar films.

McKenzie won an Oscar for his song writing on The Muppets. He released two solo albums Songs Without Jokes in 2022 and Freak Out City in 2025, and has been touring New Zealand, Australia, the US and the UK.

McKenzie told RNZ in March that the pair were in "hysterics" trying to remember their own songs for the Wellington shows.

"We ended up watching ourselves. We have to pull up YouTube clips to remember how to play our songs.

"We're like a bad covers band of ourselves."

The shows were a warm up for the band as they prepared for the Netflix is a Joke Festival in Los Angeles in May, where they will performed two shows.

Many fans were gutted to miss out on tickets for the Hiphopopotamus and Rhymenoceros' Wellington shows.

One posted on popular Wellington Facebook page Vic Deals saying they were "not above selling an organ or two" in order to get tickets.

Another person offered to "sell a limb", and several people are considering heading to the venue anyway.

"We should all band together and just have a massive listening party outside the venue".
