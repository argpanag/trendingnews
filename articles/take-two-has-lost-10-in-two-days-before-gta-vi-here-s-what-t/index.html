Take-Two Has Lost 10% in Two Days Before GTA VI. Here's What the Stock Did After the Last Five Grand Theft Auto Launches

TheTools · 18.11.2026 14:07 · source · permalink

This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.

Grand Theft Auto VI goes on sale at midnight local time on Thursday, Nov. 19, after two delays and a 13-year wait since GTA V. Take-Two Interactive (NASDAQ: TTWO), the publisher behind Rockstar Games, has lost 10% in two days: it closed at $203.93 on Tuesday, 10.5% below Friday’s close, and traded at about $214 before the open on Wednesday, up about 5%, after Sensor Tower estimated that digital pre-orders on PlayStation and Xbox had passed $1.5 billion.

This is the sixth mainline Grand Theft Auto, and the stock’s record after the first five is specific enough to be useful. We pulled Take-Two’s split-adjusted closing prices around each North American launch date, measured from the last close before release, and set them against the S&P 500 over the same days.

Read as a pattern, the record is a caution against buying for launch day itself: the first three releases were followed by gains within a month, and the last two, bigger and longer hyped, left the stock about flat for one to three months before it moved. The limit is the sample: five launches in five different markets, one of them months before the financial crisis.

Five launches, one table

Change in Take-Two’s split-adjusted close from the last close before each release, in trading days (5, 21, 63 and 252). Prices from Alpha Vantage; the S&P 500 is measured with the SPY fund. Take-Two has had one split since 2001, a 3-for-2 in April 2005, and the adjusted prices account for it.

2001 to 2004: the launches that paid at once

GTA III arrived on Oct. 22, 2001, six weeks after the Sept. 11 attacks, and it is the game that turned the series into an open-world franchise. Take-Two had lost 44.5% in the three months before the release. It then gained 19.9% in a week, 39.6% in a month and 136.6% in a year, while the S&P 500 fell 15.4% over the same 12 months.

Vice City followed a year later, on Oct. 29, 2002. The stock rose 25.7% in the month after launch and gave it all back, sitting 16.8% below its pre-launch close three months out. A year on it was up 61.2%.

San Andreas, on Oct. 26, 2004, was the first launch where the stock slipped on the day, by 0.7%. It gained 7.3% in a month and 11.0% in three, then faded: a year later it was down 1.3% while the S&P 500 had gained 11.2%.

2008: a takeover bid, then the crisis

GTA IV is the launch that looks least like the others, because a bidder set the price before the game did. Electronic Arts went public with a $26-a-share offer for Take-Two, about $2 billion, on Feb. 24, 2008, roughly 50% above the $17.36 close the Friday before. Take-Two’s board said the offer undervalued the company and that EA was trying to buy it ahead of the game, and it put off talks until after the release. By the last close before launch on April 29, 2008, the stock had run up 68.1% in three months.

The game sold about $500 million in its first week, according to Take-Two, and the stock did almost nothing: up 0.6% on launch day, 2.2% a month later, and 9.3% lower after three months. EA let its tender offer lapse in August 2008 and ended talks in September. Then the financial crisis took over. A year after launch Take-Two was down 65.6%, against a 35.8% drop in the S&P 500.

2013: a record launch and a flat stock

GTA V set the pattern most investors remember. Take-Two said the game took in more than $800 million on its first day, Sept. 17, 2013, and more than $1 billion in three days, the fastest any entertainment product had reached that mark. The stock fell 2.0% on launch day, from $17.35 to $17.00. A month later it was down 1.9%. Three months later it was up 0.1%.

The payoff came later. A year after launch the stock was up 32.5%, against 20.2% for the S&P 500, as GTA Online turned a single release into a recurring business.

What is different this time

GTA VI was first expected in the fall of 2025, moved to May 26, 2026, and then, on Nov. 6, 2025, to Nov. 19, 2026. Sensor Tower’s tracker counted 15.88 million digital pre-orders on PlayStation and Xbox by Nov. 17, worth an estimated $1.54 billion, nearly twice what GTA V took in on its first day. Take-Two has not published a pre-order figure. CEO Strauss Zelnick told analysts on Nov. 9 that pre-orders were far in excess of expectations and that the numbers would speak for themselves soon enough.

That same report showed the quarter before launch was soft. Net bookings for the fiscal second quarter were $1.60 billion, down 18% from a year earlier. For the launch quarter the company guided to $3.35 billion to $3.5 billion, about double the $1.76 billion of a year earlier, and it kept its full-year range of $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion. The stock jumped 8.3% the next day and closed at $231.31 on Nov. 11, its high for the month. Six sessions later it had lost 11.8% from that close, and it sits 21.1% below its 52-week closing high of $258.41 in July.

The two-session drop into launch is the sell-the-news trade arriving early, and it rhymes with 2013, when the stock fell on the day itself. Bank of America has said the game needs about $1.9 billion in its first three days to meet investor expectations. Rockstar reported GTA V’s first-day total the day after launch and the three-day total three days later, so the first hard numbers could land before the weekend.

The bottom line

Across five launches, the stock beat the S&P 500 over the following year three times, and the median one-year change was a gain of 32.5%. The two misses were San Andreas, which drifted, and GTA IV, which met the financial crisis. The month after launch has been the weak spot in the modern era: after GTA IV and GTA V the stock was within 2.2% of its pre-launch close 21 trading days later, and after GTA V it took a year for the gain to show.

For anyone who owns Take-Two at Tuesday’s $203.93, the record says the launch is a sales event before it is a stock event. The first-week numbers will decide the headline on Friday. The last two launches say the stock’s verdict took longer.
