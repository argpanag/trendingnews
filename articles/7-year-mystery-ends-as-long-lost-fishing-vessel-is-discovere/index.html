BLOCK ISLAND, R.I. - The wreckage of a fishing vessel is believed to have been found seven years after the ship sank near Block Island, Rhode Island.

According to Green Oceans, the shipwreck, believed to be the F/V Mistress, as well as apparent human remains, were found during a Green Oceans-sponsored investigation.

F/V Mistress was a 52-foot wooden trawler operating out of Pt. Judith, Rhode Island. The vessel tragically sunk on January 1, 2019 in proximity to the Block Island Wind Farm, claiming the lives of two fishermen.

The commercial fishing vessel F/V Mistress sank on Jan. 1, 2019 near the Block Island Wind Farm. Two fishermen died.

The U.S. Coast Guard and other agencies searched for the ship and its two missing crew members for days, but the search was called off and the ship, along with the captain and another crew member, were never found.

A third person, the captain's son, was saved by a nearby boat from a life raft, Green Oceans reported.

Seven years after the wreck, a multidisciplinary team of marine professionals was assembled to search for and document the wreck, Green Oceans said.

In late July, a sonar scan revealed a wreckage site, and in late August, scientific divers performed an underwater assessment of the area.

"The divers documented numerous features consistent with the F/V Mistress, including its propeller and shaft, fuel tanks, net drum, deck hatch, processing equipment and other characteristics to support its identity," Green Oceans said.

The final identification of the wreckage will be confirmed by the appropriate authorities.

Green Oceans said the wreck is in approximately 92 feet of water and is 50 feet from the base of Block Island Wind Farm turbine No. 1.

The nonprofit said the discovery highlights the need to examine how permanent offshore infrastructure may affect visibility, radar navigation, maneuverability, emergency access and search and rescue operations.

"We hope this discovery provides answers and some measure of closure for the fishermen’s families," Michael Lombardi, president of Green Oceans, said. "We have provided the new
evidence to federal authorities and requested that the wreck site be secured immediately for an appropriate forensic analysis."

Green Oceans is a Rhode Island-based nonprofit dedicated to protecting the ocean and all the life it sustains, from the largest whale to the smallest plankton, and pledges to share scientific facts and truthful information with the public.
