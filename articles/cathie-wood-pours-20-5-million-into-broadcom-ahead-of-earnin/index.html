Cathie Wood Pours $20.5 Million Into Broadcom Ahead of Earnings, Trims AMD — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 28.08.2026 05:25 · source · permalink

Cathie Wood's ARK Invest made a decisive bet on Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) just days before the chipmaker's fiscal third-quarter earnings report, committing roughly $20.5 million to the stock while continuing to scale back a position in rival Advanced Micro Devices Inc. (AMD) that has more than doubled this year.

The Wednesday transactions, disclosed in ARK's daily trading update, show the firm acquiring 57,705 Broadcom shares across its three largest ETFs. At the same time, it sold 37,977 AMD shares valued at approximately $18.1 million, extending a reduction that has been underway for several months.

ARK paired the Broadcom purchase with fresh allocations to two other AI-focused names: 69,585 shares of Cerebras Systems Inc. (CBRS), the newly public AI chip startup, worth about $12.8 million, and 47,794 shares of Cloudflare Inc. (NET) at roughly $13.3 million.

Figures are ARK Invest's disclosed trades across its ETFs on Wednesday, Aug. 26, 2026. Exact share counts and dollar values for the CrowdStrike, Robinhood, Roblox, and Brera Holdings sales were not disclosed.

The moves reflect a broader reshuffling of ARK's semiconductor exposure rather than a wholesale retreat from AMD. In the flagship ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK), AMD remains the 11th-largest holding at $193.3 million, accounting for about 3% of fund assets. That still exceeds the fund's stakes in Cerebras at $149.5 million and Broadcom at $95.3 million.

Source: ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK) holdings disclosure as of Wednesday, Aug. 26, 2026.

Broadcom's AI test

Broadcom is scheduled to report results for its fiscal third quarter on Sept. 2 after the market close, and expectations are elevated. Analysts project revenue of $29.24 billion, an 83.3% jump from a year earlier, while adjusted earnings per share are forecast to climb to $3.21 from $1.69 in the prior-year period.

Investors will be watching the AI semiconductor segment closely. Broadcom previously guided for AI chip revenue of $16 billion in the quarter, more than double the year-ago figure. RBC Capital analyst Srini Pajjuri said he expects a modest beat and raise, driven by strength in the networking business.

The bullish case for Broadcom is not without complications. MediaTek and Marvell Technologies Inc. (MRVL) are expanding their presence in the TPU market, and Alphabet Inc.'s Google (GOOGL) has already struck a supply agreement with Marvell. Still, Pajjuri argued that Broadcom's long-term contracts and the gradual expansion of TPU adoption beyond Google should support growth for several years.

TipRanks data shows Broadcom carries a Strong Buy consensus rating from 24 Buy and three Hold recommendations, with an average price target of $509.96 implying roughly 43% upside.

Beyond the chip trade

ARK's Wednesday activity extended well beyond semiconductors. The firm sold 181,919 shares of Tempus AI Inc. (TEM) for about $12.5 million and 53,280 shares of Twist Bioscience Corp. (TWST) for $8.1 million. Smaller disposals included CrowdStrike Holdings Inc. (CRWD), Robinhood Markets Inc. (HOOD), Roblox Corp. (RBLX), and Brera Holdings PLC (SLMT).

The reallocation took place on the same day Nvidia Corp. (NVDA) reported second-quarter results that exceeded Wall Street estimates. The AI bellwether posted adjusted EPS of $2.22 versus expectations of $2.09, with revenue climbing 106% year over year to $96.2 billion. Nvidia guided for $108 billion in third-quarter revenue, above the $103.9 billion consensus.

The Cerebras gamble

Cerebras represents one of the more aggressive positions in ARK's AI portfolio. The company went public in May at $185 per share, opened its first trading day at $350, and touched an intraday high of $386 before tumbling 53% from that peak. Its first quarter as a public company in June featured 94% revenue growth, but thin margins and heavy customer concentration, with two UAE clients accounting for nearly three-quarters of revenue, gave investors pause.

Second-quarter growth slowed to 74%, though core revenue more than doubled and the cloud business nearly quadrupled — core cloud and services revenue alone surged 287% to $127.7 million, per Cerebras's own quarterly results. The company closed the period with $25.4 billion in remaining performance obligations, a striking figure for a business with less than $700 million in trailing revenue.

Cloudflare, by comparison, has been a steadier performer. The cybersecurity and infrastructure provider is down 14% from its all-time high reached just two weeks ago. Its latest quarter delivered 36% revenue growth, the strongest top-line performance in three years. The company says it blocks more than 300 billion cyber threats daily.

Wood's positioning suggests she views the recent pullbacks in all three names as entry points rather than warning signs. Broadcom is down 28% from its June peak, while Cloudflare's decline has been swifter but shallower. For ARK, the common thread across the purchases is exposure to AI infrastructure at prices that look more reasonable than they did at the highs.
