What history tells us about the Premier League table after 10 games of a season

TheTools · 20.11.2026 08:00 · source · permalink

Past years suggest it is tough to make big positional gains after slow start but that someone now safe will be relegated

The Premier League is about to enter the most intense phase of its season. After 10 rounds of fixtures across the past three months there are 10 in the next seven weeks. Yet if you hibernated for winter, most clubs would be in roughly the same position no matter when in spring you emerged.

Across the past decade, Premier League teams’ positions changed by an average of 2.6 places between matchweeks 10 and 38. Only 5.5 clubs per season moved by more than three places over the final 28 games, with almost half of the teams (89 out of 200) shifting by a single spot at most. Every club other than Arsenal will be looking to improve their position but they need not trouble themselves too much. History shows they will probably finish 2026-27 close to their current standing.

There have inevitably been outliers. Tottenham have tumbled by at least 10 places after matchweek 10 in the past two seasons and Newcastle (in 2021-22), Crystal Palace and Everton (both 2017-18) have risen from the relegation zone at this point to the safety of mid-table by May.

But even moving one position across the remainder of the season can make an enormous difference to a team on the fringe of the Champions League qualification spots, or to those that sit 17th or 18th after 10 games. Not since 2006-07 have the bottom three clubs with 28 matches to go all been relegated.

This fact feels especially relevant this season given two of the three teams in the drop zone have changed their manager since the previous round of matches. Sunderland have stood by Régis Le Bris but he is the favourite to be the next Premier League manager to leave their post. If we can reasonably assume at least one of the clubs currently 18th to 20th will be in the top flight in 2027-28, which is best placed to survive?

It is going to be very tough for Crystal Palace. Opta’s underlying data model implies they are where they deserve to be in the table, whereas Manchester United and Sunderland should be much higher. The largest improvement Palace’s new manager, Thomas Frank, engineered between weeks 10 and 38 at Brentford was two places, which will not cut it this season. Palace have to take inspiration from the 2024-25 season, in which they also had seven points after 10 games yet rose to finish 12th.

Mauricio Pochettino’s Chelsea were 11th going into their 11th game in 2023-24 before ending sixth. As they were also 11th in early April, they made a much shorter dash to the finish line, though. Pochettino’s teams usually improve as campaigns wear on, with Tottenham rising three places after week 10 in each of his first three seasons with the club. A similar lift in his new role would not make this season a rousing success for United but it would stave off the unthinkable idea of relegation.

For someone to escape, someone else must take their place. Ignoring the possibility that the answer to “who?” may be Manchester City, the team in 17th are inevitably the ones most at risk. Of the 13 sides in the past decade who were outside the drop zone after 10 games but went down, six were fourth-bottom going into matchweek 11.

Given that the points gap between first and 20th (20) was last lower at this stage in 2015-16, those figures may be more pertinent than position this season. Leeds in 2022-23 and Bournemouth three years earlier had 13 points after 10 games but were relegated, as did Blackpool in 2010-11 and Middlesbrough two seasons before them.

Only nine clubs are now above that bar, with Aston Villa, Newcastle and Tottenham among those who are not. The table is unlikely to change too much before the end of the campaign but there is a distinct chance that a secure-looking side will perish.
