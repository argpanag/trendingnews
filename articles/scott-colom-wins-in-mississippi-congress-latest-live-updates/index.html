NAACP members in Jackson, Mississippi celebrate Colom's historic victory

Members of the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP), the largest civil rights organization in the country, erupted in cheers and embraced each other at their historic state headquarters in Jackson soon after CNN projected Scott Colom’s Mississippi Senate win.

In unseating GOP incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith, Colom becomes the first Black person elected to statewide office in Mississippi since the Reconstruction era.

Members were gathered at the M.W. Stringer Masonic Lodge, the building where Medgar Evers — an NAACP field secretary and voting rights leader who was assassinated in 1963 — once worked.

Colom will also be the first Democrat to represent Mississippi in the Senate in almost 40 years. Democrat John Stennis, an avowed segregationist, retired in 1989 after decades in the chamber.

A simply stunning outcome

Scott Colom’s victory in the Mississippi Senate race is the most extraordinary result of a midterm election that was already a stunner.

There’s the history: He’ll be the state’s first Black senator since the end of Reconstruction — the period after the Civil War when Confederate states were reintegrated into the union amid bitter divisions over the status of freed slaves.

No one ever thought Mississippi was in play — apart perhaps from Colom, Georgia Sen. Raphael Warnock and Democratic operatives who’ve been quietly building infrastructure in the deep red Deep South in recent years.

The win is also a rebuke for complacent MAGA Republicans who thought they’d coast to victory however unpopular President Donald Trump got. By the time incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith realized she was in a contest, it was too late.

Colom, meanwhile, is a vibrant new political talent. His “get another senator” campaign commercial went viral, defining the 2026 midterm zeitgeist.

An unpopular Trump will never be on the ballot again — so lightning may not strike twice. But Democrats will now have senators from Mississippi, Texas, South Carolina, Kansas, Georgia and North Carolina. No one saw that coming.

This foundation opens the tantalizing possibility of a new battleground in presidential elections that goes far beyond the handful of recent swing states and could transform the political map.

Democrat Scott Colom will win US Senate seat in deep-red Mississippi, CNN projects

Scott Colom will defeat GOP Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in a major upset to become the first Democrat that voters in deep-red Mississippi have elected to the chamber in more than four decades, CNN’s Decision Desk projects.

Colom, a local district attorney, centered his campaign on affordability issues, labeling Hyde-Smith “Cindy High Prices.”

The campaign also produced one of the most attention-getting ads of the midterms by mocking a Hyde-Smith comment that there are “so many proteins to choose from” when she was asked about rising beef prices.

As the race tightened, a parade of high-profile Democrats, including former Vice President Kamala Harris, campaigned with Colom in the final weeks of the election, and Democratic super PACs began spending money on his behalf.

President Donald Trump also traveled to the state to rally Republican voters.

Colom led Hyde-Smith in the days after the midterm elections by a razor-thin margin as vote counting continued in the state. Local officials had to vet thousands of provisional ballots cast by voters in the high turnout election.

Colom campaigned relentlessly as he sought to energize Black voters, who make up roughly 35% of the state’s electorate. He also worked to build a coalition that includes White, working-class Mississippians.

The win comes just a few years after Hyde-Smith blocked Colom’s nomination to the federal bench, although the Democrat had the backing of other Republicans, including Sen. Roger Wicker, Mississippi’s senior senator.

Hyde-Smith became the first female US senator from Mississippi when she was appointed in 2018 to fill a vacancy. She went on to win a special election later that year and reelection in 2020.
