Klay Thompson praised Miami's "real" Heat Culture after scoring 30 points and hitting the game-winner in a 117-116 overtime victory over the LA Clippers, indirectly pushing back on Stan Van Gundy's recent claim that the concept is "overblown."

Just days after Stan Van Gundy questioned whether "Heat Culture" was anything special, Klay Thompson provided a very different view following his biggest night yet with Miami.

Thompson delivered 30 points and eight three-pointers, including the game-winning three with one second remaining in overtime, as the Heat erased a 20-point deficit and stunned the LA Clippers 117-116 on Wednesday.

"I enjoy having big nights, but I don't go into games with the mindset that I have to prove myself," the 36-year-old said. "I know what I'm capable of, and as long as I stay in great condition, I'm going to have plenty of nights like this throughout the season."

The veteran sharpshooter then pointed directly to Miami's environment as a primary reason he chose to join the franchise.

"That's why I was so attracted to Miami. The culture is real," Thompson affirmed. "They really get the most out of you, especially at this stage of my career."

"That really attracted me to being a player for this organization."

Former Miami head coach Stan Van Gundy publicly questioned the reputation surrounding "Heat Culture" earlier in the week.

"I don't think Heat culture is anything special," Van Gundy said on Nov. 3. "I think it's overblown, the whole Heat culture thing."

Van Gundy, 67, spent more than a decade in the Miami organization as an assistant (1995-03) and head coach (2003-05).

Miami, meanwhile, has now won three of its last four games after beginning the season 0-4. The Heat face the Warriors next.
