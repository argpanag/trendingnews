ICC: No ‘political objective’ in looming Duterte trial

TheTools · 24.11.2026 13:09 · source · permalink

NATIONAL

by

Joyce Ann Rocamora

Nov 24, 2026

9:09 PM GMT+8

Former President Rodrigo Duterte during his third status conference on Sept. 16, 2026. (Photo courtesy of ICC)

MANILA – The International Criminal Court (ICC) is not pursuing any “political objective” in the upcoming trial of former President Rodrigo Duterte and will ensure the proceedings are “conducted fairly,” its spokesperson said on Tuesday.

 

“Judges will ensure that the proceedings are conducted fairly and that the rights of all trial participants are respected. The Court's role is not to pursue any political objective,” ICC Spokesperson Oriane Maillet said in a statement.

 

“Rather, its role is to determine, through independent and impartial judicial proceedings, whether the charges have been proven according to the law.”

 

Duterte is accused of the crimes against humanity of murder and attempted murder, alleged to have been committed in the Philippines between November 2011 and March 2019 during the conduct of his campaign against illegal drugs.

 

His trial will open on Nov. 30 and will be presided over by Judge Joanna Korner.

 

On opening day, the ICC judges will check that Duterte understands the charges against him and ask him to enter a plea.

 

The Trial Chamber III will then hear the opening statements of the Prosecution and the Legal Representatives of Victims.

 

The Prosecution currently anticipates calling about 100 witnesses over the course of the trial, while more than 500 victims have been authorized to participate in the current proceedings through common legal representatives, their lawyers, who will present their views before the court.

 

Following the Prosecution's case, the Defense will have the opportunity to present its own evidence and witnesses.

The judges will deliberate and decide whether to acquit or convict Duterte once all the evidence has been heard. (PNA)
