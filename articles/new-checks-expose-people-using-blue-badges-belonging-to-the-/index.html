New checks expose people using Blue Badges belonging to the dead

TheTools · 28.11.2026 09:57 · source · permalink

Investigations into Blue Badge fraud in Suffolk have soared after a new database exposed people using badges belonging to dead people or reported lost or stolen.

New figures released by Suffolk County Council show there were 42 blue badge investigations during 2026 up to October 31, a rise from 34 in the equivalent period the previous year.

Of those cases, 41 involved badges used for parking and one involved a badge application.

Blue Badge cases made up two-thirds of the 63 referrals investigated by Suffolk County Council’s Counter Fraud Service during the period.

The council said the increase was linked to West Suffolk civil enforcement officers being given access to the Manage Blue Badge database.

The new database found people were using disabled badges that were lost, stolen or belonged to deceased people. (Image: Archant)

According to the report, this has led to more cases being identified where blue badges on display belonged to people who had died or had previously been reported stolen or lost.

But the council said the rise reflected better detection and did not necessarily mean fraud itself had increased.

Eight warning letters were issued for Blue Badge misuse during the period.

The report also revealed one person had been prosecuted for displaying an expired blue badge which did not belong to them at Cattle Market Car Park in Bury St Edmunds.

They were found guilty at Ipswich Magistrates’ Court on August 24, fined £660 and ordered to pay £1,171.25 in legal and investigatory costs, plus a £264 victim surcharge.

A spokesperson for Suffolk County Council said: “Blue Badge fraud and misuse is not a victimless crime. It can prevent disabled people from accessing the parking spaces they genuinely need and undermines a scheme designed to support them.

“We take misuse seriously and work with local parking enforcement teams across Suffolk to identify cases and take appropriate action. Where necessary, cases can be referred to our Counter Fraud Service for investigation, which can result in a warning or, in the most serious cases, prosecution.

“The vast majority of people use Blue Badges appropriately, but we would urge anyone considering misusing one to remember that accessible parking spaces are there for people who genuinely need them.”
