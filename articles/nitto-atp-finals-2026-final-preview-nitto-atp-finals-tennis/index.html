Nitto ATP Finals 2026 Final Preview | Nitto ATP Finals | Tennis

TheTools · 22.11.2026 18:25 · source · permalink

Entering the Nitto ATP Finals, there were two clear favourites: World No. 1 Alexander Zverev and Carlos Alcaraz. Now, Sunday evening not before 6 p.m. local time, the two superstars will clash for the prestigious trophy at the season finale.

It will be a clash of titans inside Inalpi Arena, where Zverev will try to secure his third Nitto ATP Finals crown, and Alcaraz his first. One thing is certain: The fans will be in for a treat between two men plenty used to the big moment.

Zverev has shone brighter than ever in 2026, claiming his first two major trophies, ascending to No. 1 in the PIF ATP Rankings for the first time and now standing one win from glory in Turin. But look up “showtime” in the dictionary and it might very well read “Carlos Alcaraz”. The Spaniard loves competing in the biggest moments in our sport.

Alcaraz leads 7-6 in their Lexus ATP Head2Head series, with victories in four of their past five meetings. The good news for Zverev is that the one of those five clashes came two years ago at the Nitto ATP Finals, where he won in straight sets.

“It’s quite fast, so I think the aggressive style of tennis helps. Although he’s very, very aggressive, especially this week,” Zverev said of Alcaraz. “His serve plus one is absolutely amazing. But the matches that we’ve played were always very difficult and I look forward to another difficult one. And hopefully it’s going to be entertaining.” 

One year ago, Alcaraz reached the final at the season finale for the first time, falling short against two-time Nitto ATP Finals champion Jannik Sinner in a clash between the top two seeds at the event.

The crowd was understandably behind Sinner, but Alcaraz loved playing in front of the Italian fans, who he explained are “really passionate about the game”.

“Even when I'm playing against Jannik obviously here in Italy, I just feel it's not the whole stadium against me,” Alcaraz said. “But yeah, I feel really loved every time that I come here. It's crazy, the energy that I receive in every practice, in every match. I've been really successful during the tournaments every time that I came here to Italy, not only in the ATP but in the juniors and under-14 and everything.

“Every time that I came here, I've been really successful, which means how good I feel here in Italy.” 

The court has been quick this week in Turin, making the serve even more important. Neither finalist lost serve — or a set — in round-robin play. Zverev faced six break points in the group stage and saved them all, before defeating Daniil Medvedev in the semi-finals without facing a break point.

And Alcaraz, who dropped serve just once in his gripping final-set tie-break victory against Ben Shelton in the semi-finals, has noticeably dialed up his aggression. Part of that is because he received treatment for an upper-left-leg injury, which has been wrapped since his second round-robin match.

“I'm realising that I just need to be careful with it, and sometimes I don't have to push myself to the limit in some movements, in some shots, in some balls. I don't have to run every ball, which I'm just understanding right now,” Alcaraz said after his semi-final victory. “But, yeah, just feeling better, feeling good. Today was great feedback for me. Right now I'm going to go with my physio, try to recover, and be ready for tomorrow.”

Zverev can become only the eighth player to win the Nitto ATP Finals title at least three times, while Alcaraz can become the first Spanish champion at the year-end championships since Alex Corretja in 1998.

Alcaraz, the recent Vienna and Paris champion, is on a 13-match winning streak and is now 11-2 against opponents in the Top 10 of the PIF ATP Rankings this season, according to the Infosys ATP Win/Loss Index.

Zverev is flying higher than ever, freshly into the ATP No. 1 Club and with two major titles to his name. But is he allowing himself to enjoy it yet?

“After tomorrow, I’ll start.”
