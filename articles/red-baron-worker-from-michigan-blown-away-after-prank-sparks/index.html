Red Baron worker from Michigan 'blown away' after prank sparks fundraiser

TheTools · 31.10.2026 06:55 · source · permalink

A Michigan man has drawn widespread attention after a video of him emotionally confronting a bothersome prankster went viral, prompting a fundraising campaign and conversations about social media pranks.

In the video, a prankster approaches the man and pretends to steal $10 from him through Apple Pay. As the prankster continues to provoke him, the worker grows increasingly frustrated and explains his financial struggles and demanding work schedule.

People touched by the man's plight have stepped up and donated hundreds of thousands of dollars to help make his life a little easier. The man, who has been identified online as Walt Z., a delivery worker for frozen pizza manufacturer Red Baron, told the fundraiser organizer that he has been "blown away" by the outpouring of support.

The Detroit News reached out to the worker to get his take on the viral clip and the subsequent fundraiser. His wife said they are declining media interviews for now.

What happens in the video

The approximately 2-minute video shows Walt stocking frozen pizzas in a grocery store when the prankster approaches him and asks for the time.

As the worker pulls out his mobile phone, the prankster, who is not visible throughout the clip, plays a sound mimicking an Apple Pay transaction and tells Walt that he just took $10 from him.

The employee initially is calm and but says he's going to report the prankster. He then walks away from the refrigerated case where he was working.

The clip then cuts to an interaction at a checkout counter, where Walt grows increasingly angry and agitated as the prankster continues to needle him. He explains that he is struggling to pay his mortgage while supporting four children, says he is already an hour late getting home and describes his demanding work schedule.

"Life is hard enough as it is," he says. "I don't need this crap."

A spokesperson for Minnesota-based Schwan's, which is Red Baron's parent company, told the New York Post that the worker is a direct store delivery employee and that the incident occurred in August.

The video appears to have been originally posted to Instagram by the account @thea.ctiveworld, also known as ThreeOfAKind. The account has since been deleted.

In recent days it has spread wildly on several social media sites, garnering millions of views.

Thousands of dollars raised for harassed worker

In response to the viral video, a GoFundMe campaign has been organized to help Walt. As of Oct. 29, the fundraiser had raised more than $335,000 from more than 9,000 donations.

The effort aims to help Walt continue to support his family and cover expenses, including his mortgage.

The person who launched the fundraiser, identified on the website as Luke Oakenshield of Arizona, said he did not know Walt personally when he launched the campaign, but wanted to "give this employee some breathing room while everything unfolds, especially so they can keep supporting their family and have some protection if they are fired."

Oakenshield said he has since made contact with the worker and identified him online as "Walt Z out of Michigan."

"Walt Z out of Michigan sends a heartfelt thankyou, and is absolutely blown away by how much people have cared over this," Oakenshield posted on GoFundMe.com. "They are requesting privacy, and GoFundMe is taking the lead in transferring all of this to him. You're all Rockstars, and it shows."

A spokesperson for GoFundMe confirmed that the organization is working with Oakenshield to ensure all of the funds safely reach Walt.

What people are saying about the prank

Red Baron appeared to address the incident on social media with a post indicating that Walt was still with the company and had their support.

"He's still here," the company posted. "He's got a fan club. Including us."

Online reaction has largely favored the worker, with viewers criticizing the prank as cruel and accusing social media creators of humiliating working people for clicks.

Conservative commentator Benny Johnson amplified the story on X, and the video has fueled broader criticism of prank culture and calls for greater respect for people simply trying to do their jobs.

"Why did this make me cry. The struggle is so real and people want to stick their phone in your face to get the next viral video," one commenter said on Facebook.
