Coles puts antitheft tags on meat to combat shoplifting

TheTools · 21.10.2026 23:06 · source · permalink

Supermarket giant Coles is adding bright yellow antitheft tags to meat in a bid to decrease theft.

Meat is now getting the same security treatment as lipstick and mascara, as supermarket giant Coles brings bright yellow antitheft tags to Queensland stores.

The tags, seen attached to premium meat in Gold Coast stores this week, work in a similar way to security devices found on health and beauty items, with staff able to remove them for customers at the checkout.

Coles is introducing yellow security tags to its meat in a bid to decrease theft. Photo: NewsWire/ Gaye Gerard

While they were trialled in Victoria in 2024, they have only been rolled out in the Sunshine State this year.

“While most of our customers do the right thing, unfortunately a small number don’t,” said a Coles spokesman.

“Measures like this help us deliver better availability of products that our customers want.”

The Australian Retail Council estimates retail crime, including shoplifting, costs the Australian economy around $9b each year.

“We are working hard to address retail crime and create a safe environment for our team and customers,” the Coles spokesman said.

“We work closely with police on this issue, and we have a range of security measures in place across our stores, including additional security guards, CCTV, access-controlled entry doors and duress pendants.”

The heightened security measures come as soaring interest rates, rising rents and mortgage repayments, and cost of living expenses put increasing financial pressure on consumers.

Finder’s Consumer Sentiment Tracker (CST) revealed 41 per cent of Aussies listed their groceries as one of their top three most stressful expenses in September.

The data showed the average Aussie spends $207 per week on groceries for their household.

Sarah Megginson, personal finance expert at Finder.

“When we have supermarkets putting security tags on everyday items like meat, it shows just how many Australians are struggling to keep up with the rising cost of living,” said Sarah Megginson, personal finance expert at Finder.

“For a lot of families, the weekly shop has become a balancing act.

“They’re being forced to weigh up what they can afford and what they have to go without.”

Finder’s research shows 14 per cent of Australians regularly run out of food before payday, while 12 per cent of parents have to prioritise their children’s meals over their own.

“That goes beyond tightening the belt,” said Ms Megginson.

“Those families are in real hardship.”

Finder data revealed the financial strain had become so severe that four per cent of Australians – equivalent to around 870,000 people – admit they had deliberately scanned items as cheaper products at supermarket self-serve check-outs.

A further two per cent – around 435,000 Australians – admit they have stolen food to get by.

Ms Megginson said Aussies struggling with grocery costs should review all of their major expenses, and not just their supermarket bill.

“Comparing your mobile phone plan, insurance, energy, internet bills and even your mortgage could free up hundreds or even thousands of dollars each year, easing pressure on the household budget,” she said.

The financial squeeze is no longer confined to mortgage repayments, with struggling households tightening their belts across hospitality, retail and construction. Picture: iStock

Experian’s latest Housing Pressure Report revealed the financial squeeze was no longer confined to mortgage repayments, with struggling households tightening their belts across hospitality, retail and construction.

The report revealed almost one third of Australians had cut their grocery bills.

It paints a troubling picture of the ripple effects of Australia’s housing affordability crisis, as households increasingly prioritise keeping a roof over their heads.
