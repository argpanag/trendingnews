'Chilling' if political violence has come to Australia, professor says

C

By Claire Campbell

By Claire Campbell

Deakin University's Greg Barton said there has been a "rising problem" of violence towards MPs in the UK and US.

In 2021, Sir David Amess, a British MP of 38 years, was stabbed to death during a constituency meeting.

In 2016, Labour MP Jo Cox was killed in Yorkshire in northern England just days before the Brexit referendum.

Professor Barton said there had also been "major incidents" in the US this year.

While the attack on Helen Haines appears to be intentional, according to the prime minister, determining the motive would be important, according to Barton.

"If it's just a personal animus, that's terrible but if it's somebody thinking they're going to change the system by using violence, the worry is that opens up a pandora's box," he told ABC’s News Channel.

"If this problem of political violence of elected officials is coming to Australia now that's chilling for everyone."

"If it's just a personal animus, that's terrible but if it's somebody thinking they're going to change the system by using violence, the worry is that opens up a pandora's box," he told ABC’s News Channel.

"If this problem of political violence of elected officials is coming to Australia now that's chilling for everyone."

Barton said it was a situation Australia had "to brace ourselves for".
