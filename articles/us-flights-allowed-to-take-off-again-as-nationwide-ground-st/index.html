US flights allowed to take off again as nationwide ground stop lifted but delays expected

TheTools · 14.11.2026 02:06 · source · permalink

Summary

  • US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says a nationwide ground stop has been lifted after an outage halted all flights

  • Duffy says the travel chaos is going to take time to unwind, with many passengers still stranded at airports

  • White House officials said earlier the ground stop was due to an air traffic control communications issue - here's what we know so far

  • There's zero evidence this was a cyber-attack against the US, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin says - what else did he say?

  5. US has 'incredibly old air traffic control system', Duffy sayspublished at 01:48 GMT
    01:48 GMT

    Duffy has given us a timeline as to how the incident unfolded.

    He says that at 3:36 pm local time, “we experienced instability for our radar and our communications and our centres” across the country. Once inconsistent data from routers started registering, the FAA issued a nation-wide ground stop.

    “What we have is an incredibly old air traffic control system,” Duffy told reporters, explaining that the routers got “overloaded with data”, which led to “inconsistency” in the data received from those routers.

    He went on to say that Congress needs to “give us the resources to build a state-of-the-art air traffic system”, and he needs $50bn to revamp the system.

    Currently the Republican Party controls both chambers of Congress.

  12. Communications outage caused by a network update, CBS reportspublished at 00:44 GMT
    00:44 GMT

    Multiple sources have told the BBC's US media partner, CBS, that the technical fault behind the nationwide ground stop was caused by an update to the private communications network that connects America's air traffic control system.

    The network, which CBS says is operated by L3Harris, rebooted as a result of the update, and subsequently crashed.

    On its website, L3Harris says it is the "premier provider" of communications systems for the world’s most advanced airspaces, weapon systems and Command and Control platforms.

    L3Harris has not immediately responded to the BBC's request for comment.

  13. Longest US air traffic grounding event since 9/11, expert sayspublished at 00:35 GMT
    00:35 GMT

    Jake Horton
    BBC Verify

    Some more comments coming in comparing the current groundstop to previous events.

    During a press conference earlier about the nationwide ground stop, US Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin repeatedly said that this "isn't another 9/11".

    BBC Verify has spoken to Dr Guy Gratton, an Associate Professor of Aviation and the Environment at Cranfield University, who agreed and said: "This looks like the worst since 9/11, but clearly not on that scale."

    When asked about comparable incidents, Dr Gratton pointed to a US grounding in 2023 due to a failure in the Notice to Air Missions (Notam) system.

    "That lasted 1 hour and 52 minutes, delayed about 9,500 flights and cancelled about 1,300," he said.

    The outage meant air traffic safety notices could no longer be published, and flights were grounded until that system was restored.

    That means the current US-wide grounding - which has been in place since 15:54 ET (20:54 GMT) - has now lasted for more than three hours, making it far longer than the 2023 event.

  16. What we know so far about the US flight groundstoppublished at 00:05 GMT
    00:05 GMT

    Grace Eliza Goodwin
    US reporter

    The nationwide ground stop affecting departing flights across the US is still in effect. It's only the second time - and the longest - such a stop has been issued since right after the 11 September 2001 terrorist attacks.

    Here's what we know:

    • The stoppage of all flights in the US was ordered a couple hours ago "due to extraordinary circumstances and reasons of safety"
    • The problem is related to a telecommunications outage affecting air traffic control centres, US authorities say
    • Airports around the country have confirmed the ground stop as some plane passengers report being stuck on the tarmac
    • Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin says there's no indication of an attack, all flights are accounted for and "everybody's safe"
    • The problem is not connected to the global Microsoft outage that began on Thursday, Mullin says
    • American Airlines says it expects the stoppage to last all day while United Airlines says no domestic flights will take off while the ground stop is in place
    • Travellers around the country remain stranded as they wait to hear for flight updates

    We still don't know what specifically caused the issue, how long the stoppage will last, or for how long flights will be delayed or cancelled once the ground stop ends.

  18. Biggest ground stop since 9/11, says former NTSB chairmanpublished at 23:53 GMT 13 November
    23:53 GMT 13 November

    The former chairman of the US National Transportation Safety Board says there hasn't been a ground stop of this magnitude in the US since the 11 September attacks in 2001.

    "This is absolutely unbelievable that this could happen in this day and age," Robert L Sumwalt tells the BBC's US media partner CBS News.

    He mentions that there was a short-lived ground stop in 2023 that only lasted a few hours.

    Sumwalt says he doesn't yet have a sense of what went wrong but notes that there are 22 air traffic control centres in the United States that are at high altitudes and control airplanes once they are above about 10,000 or 12,000ft ( 3,048 to 3,657m).

    "That's where these outages appear to be happening. It looks like the FAA realises they've got an issue, so they are they're doing the right thing by saying nobody can take off until we get this thing straightened out," he says.

    The FAA has said that the ground stop is due to "reasons of safety",

    "Obviously anytime you have a diminished ability to contact air traffic control, that creates a problem right there," he says. "So again, I think the FAA is doing absolutely the right thing by not ingesting more traffic into a system that is already strained under the current conditions."
