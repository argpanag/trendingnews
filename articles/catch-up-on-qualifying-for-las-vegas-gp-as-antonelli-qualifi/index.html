  1. Report: Antonelli fastest but penalty leaves Verstappen on polepublished at 05:58 GMT 21 November
    05:58 GMT 21 November

    Andrew Benson
    BBC F1 correspondent in Las Vegas

    Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli was fastest in qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix but a grid penalty will hand pole position to Red Bull's Max Verstappen.

    Antonelli, running at the back of the pack of cars in qualifying and maximising his tow, beat Verstappen by 0.258 seconds.

    But the new world champion has a five-place grid penalty for using more than permitted number of engine components and will start sixth.

    The Italian's team-mate George Russell was just 0.073secs behind Verstappen and will start alongside the Dutchman on the front row.

    McLaren's Lando Norris qualified fourth, and will start third, ahead of Red Bull's Isack Hadjar.

    Both Ferraris were eliminated in the second session and qualified 11th and 12th with Charles Leclerc ahead of Lewis Hamilton.

    05:55 GMT 21 November

    That was an interesting qualifying session for stupid o'clock in the morning, wasn't it?

    World champion Kimi Antonelli secured pole position in Las Vegas but the Italian won't be starting there thanks to his five-place grid penalty.

    While the Italian drops down the order to sixth, Max Verstappen and George Russell will line-up together on the front row. The two drivers made contact in third practice, with Verstappen escaping a penalty from the stewards. Given the interview we've just read from Russell, it could be a lively start at lights out on the famous Strip.

    As for Ferrari, it's another qualifying to forget this season. Charles Leclerc couldn't hide frustrations about how poorly he thought his team had executed the session, but he and team-mate Lewis Hamilton do gain a place each tomorrow because of Oscar Piastri's 10-place grid penalty.

  3. 'Somebody has to stand up to that guy' - Russellpublished at 05:52 GMT 21 November
    05:52 GMT 21 November

    Mercedes' George Russell speaking to Sky Italia on the incident with Max Verstappen in FP3: "Well, I mean, the warning was fine. There's nothing in the rules to say that you cannot overtake someone who is starting their lap but when you do this, you are screwing this person over.

    "He does this week in, week out, with many drivers. Everybody accepts it and I am not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they are bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody has got to stand up to that guy."

  4. 'No clue' how Antonelli got pole - Wolffpublished at 05:49 GMT 21 November
    05:49 GMT 21 November

    Mercedes' boss Toto Wolff, speaking to Sky Sports, on his drivers getting P1 and P3 in qualifying: "We have absolutely no clue [how Antonelli got pole]. And you as a strategist, you know we thought Kimi was either going to miss the lap or he’s going to be way too close to [Arvid] Lindblad.

    “He crossed the line 1.4 seconds behind and that was basically it. So, I don’t understand how being that close you can even do this time. If you’re 1.4 seconds behind the car in front, you would think this is too close, especially with a car that went eight tenths slower, but for whatever reason, that works.”

    On why Mercedes did not give Russell a tow from Antonelli: "Actually, George, for me, had the perfect position. He was four seconds behind the car in front. We need to find out because I think there was some kind of, Turn Seven, Eight, Nine, whether that was a mistake or something else happened, we need to - I don’t know, to be honest, but it looked like the ideal position for a pole lap, because he was quickest all the time."

    On what he expects to happen in Sunday's race: "Well a big cinema, if they try to go through the first corners together, we shall be watching."

  5. Ferrari execution has been very poor - Leclercpublished at 05:46 GMT 21 November
    05:46 GMT 21 November

    Ferrari's Charles Leclerc, speaking to Sky Sports, after also being eliminated in the second part of qualifying in P13: "We didn't do a good job, I did the mistake, the only lap I was given, basically.

    "At the same time, the execution has been very poor. I don't know, we'll review... I don't know... I'm very angry now, so I'd rather not speak too much. But I think there were plenty of things we could've done much better."

    On whether Ferrari can recover positions in tomorrow's race: "I don't think so, because we're losing a second down the straights, so there's is literally nowhere where we'll be able to overtake."

  6. 'When it rains, it pours' - Hamiltonpublished at 05:41 GMT 21 November
    05:41 GMT 21 November

    Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, speaking to Sky Sports, after being knocked out in Q2 in P12: "We work hard to do a good job, and I really didn't know where we would be today.

    "We knew we'd struggle a little bit with these long straights, but I was optimistic going in - I thought the car would be OK. But on the first or second lap, we took a hit on the floor, debris or something, and we lost a bunch of downforce and a few tenths.

    "I was giving it everything, my lap was as good as it could be in the end. but I didn't have anyone ahead of me, and then we lost... we had a lack of deployment in the end, and I lost like six tenths.

    "When it rains, it pours, We were scrapping just to get into Q3. Without the deployment loss, without the downforce loss, I think I could've got into Q3, but it still would've been tough.

    "It's definitely not a nice feeling for the team, but we'll try and move forward tomorrow."

    05:28 GMT 21 November

    Red Bull's Max Verstappen, who qualified P2 but will start on pole position in Las Vegas because of Kimi Antonelli's grid penalty: "We've just struggling with some things from Brazil and just trying to get my head around it and make the best out of it again.

  10. 'Come back stronger tomorrow' - Antonellipublished at 05:23 GMT 21 November
    05:23 GMT 21 November

    Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli, who claimed pole position in Las Vegas but will start the race in P6 because of a five-place grid penalty: "It felt like going back to 60 mini cars, where you do the qualifying all together, all super close.
