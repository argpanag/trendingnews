'Get my son back': Mum's plea after police fear missing Christchurch man is dead

TheTools · 08.10.2026 00:53 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Police have confirmed this morning that missing Christchurch father, Braydon Hepworth, has died.

But they are unable to shed any further light on the investigation into his disappearance and how he met his fate.

The 21-year-old was last seen in September and since then, police and his family have been searching for him.

His mother has also made increasingly desperate appeals for information about the missing man on social media.

Police today confirmed the worst fears of Hepworth’s family.

Detective Inspector Scott Anderson said a number of search warrants had been executed at properties in Linwood and Edgeware this morning.

“Our inquiries to date suggest Braydon is no longer alive,” he said.

“We know there are people in the community that know what has happened to Braydon and request that they come forward with information.

“Braydon is a father and a son and police are determined to get answers for Braydon’s family. We are continuing to appeal to anyone who may have information in relation to Braydon’s disappearance.

“Any information, no matter how small, could be a piece of the puzzle we need to locate Mr Hepworth.”

Anderson said that “at this time” police were “unable to share details on the findings of the search warrants”.

A large police presence is at a Hereford St house this morning, just 100m from where the last search was conducted at a property on the corner of Hereford and England St.

Police have cordoned off the property - a one-bedroom flat connected to a larger house.

A forensic tent was set up and mobile police units have been parked on the street.

The property police are searching is made up of two dwellings. The main three-bedroom house is not thought to be connected to the investigation.

Police are focused on the flat connected to the side of the villa, which has its own driveway.

Around midday, a large amount of police forensic equipment, including special lights, was carried into the cordon.

More scene examination experts arrived around the same time.

Hepworth’s mother Char Huntley posted on Facebook this morning after the police announcement

“Please help me get him home,” she said.

“Please speak up. Someone knows something. Remain anonymous if you need - but let me get my son back.

“Searches are underway to give us anything to locate him.

“Our family is broken with not knowing what happened to him.”

The last confirmed sighting of Hepworth was at a Linwood service station on September 3.

Security footage showed him wearing a Monster Energy-branded beanie with a pair of headphones over the top.

The search for Braydon

Police first appealed for help to find Hepworth on September 23 after he was reported missing.

On Friday, they issued a fresh appeal for information and confirmed they were searching a property in Linwood in connection with his disappearance.

The house is located on the corner of Hereford and England Sts and police said it was believed to be Hepworth’s last known address.

It is understood the house runs as a backpackers of sorts and has several residents of varying lengths of stay.

During the search, police cordoned off the corner property, which has caravans at the back.

Officers stood guard outside while investigators worked

“Staff have made extensive inquiries to date, and Braydon has not been located. We are very concerned for Braydon,” police said in a statement.

“Police continue to appeal for anybody with information regarding Braydon’s whereabouts to please get in contact with us.

“Police and Braydon’s family have serious concerns for his wellbeing, as this is extremely out of character for him.”

Earlier this week police refused to comment on the status of the investigation, including who Hepworth last spoke to or contacted, when he last accessed his bank accounts or used his phone, if he has accessed medication he uses daily, whether they believe the young father has been a victim of foul play, and if so, whether any persons of interest had been identified.

On Tuesday Detective Senior Sergeant Karen Simmons issued a statement.

“A scene examination at the address believed to be Braydon’s last known address has now been completed,” she said.

“The operation to understand the circumstances surrounding his disappearance remains ongoing.

“Police continue to appeal for anybody with information regarding Braydon’s whereabouts to please get in contact with us.”

Huntley, has been appealing for information on Facebook since September 22.

“Please help me keep looking for my boy … Braydon Hepworth. He’s 21 and Type 1 diabetic. He needs insulin daily to stay alive,” she wrote four days later.

“This is devastating, not knowing where he is, if he is safe, if he needs help but can’t get it, if he is still in Christchurch or has gone further willingly or not.

“He has a 9-month-old daughter and two siblings as well. He’s loved and very missed by many, and we are all concerned for his health and wellbeing. He is not in any trouble; we just need him home.”

Huntley revealed that as of August 4, Hepworth had “a month’s worth” of insulin.

“So we are desperately needing to find him to help him,” she said.

She had called hospitals “all over the South Island” as well as bus, shuttle and ferry companies in case Hepworth was sick, injured or had travelled away from Christchurch.

“Have been in the city where he would frequent and been showing his photo in multiple shops. No luck,” she said.

“Messaged friends and our extended family that know him or he may go to. No luck.

“His phone is off and all social media are non-active and everything is only delivered but not received his end. This is unnaturally out of character for him to not be heard from for this long. I am very scared for his safety, and the not knowing is the worst.”

Huntley said she was in a situation “no parent should ever go through”.

“I won’t stop looking until I find him safe … I am heartbroken not knowing where he is,” she updated later in September.

“His health and safety is a serious concern. Someone must know something that will lead to his location. Please help me find him. I love him, and I need him home.”

In her most recent update last week, 27 days after the last confirmed sighting of Hepworth, she begged anyone with information to “please speak up”.

“Remain anonymous if you need, but let me get my son back safe,” she said.

“Our family is broken with the uncertainty of his whereabouts and well-being. We have searched many places, made multiple calls and have no solid leads yet. I’m needing any information leading to his discovery.”

The grandfather of Hepworth’s daughter also posted online urging people to come forward with any information that could help the family.

“Although Braydon is not my biological son, he was in a relationship with our daughter and they have a beautiful 9-month-old daughter together. From the very first day we met Braydon, he called us Mum and Dad, and we called him our son. Over time, we built a very strong bond with him, and he has become an important part of our family,” he wrote.

“I received a message from Braydon on 2 September telling me that he was planning to get his car licence and was also sorting out other appointments. He had been doing so well, which is why everything that has happened over the past few weeks is so concerning and completely out of character for him.

“The search will continue, and we are asking everyone to please keep sharing the missing-person posts and keep an eye out for Braydon.”

He thanked everyone who had supported Hepworth’s family during “an extremely difficult and stressful time” and helped to search for him and spread the word about his disappearance.

“It truly means more to us than we can put into words,” he said.

“Thank you from the bottom of our hearts for continuing to support Braydon, our family and especially his little daughter during this incredibly difficult time.

“We are desperately hoping and praying that we receive good news soon.”

Over the weekend, he posted a message directly to Hepworth, revealing he had spent “countless hours driving and walking around looking for him”.

“I’m just lost for words, Braydon – please let us know where you are. You’re not in trouble; we all just want you back home,” he wrote.

It is unclear if Hepworth was working at the time he went missing.

In June 2025, he posted on Facebook looking for fulltime work “urgently”.
