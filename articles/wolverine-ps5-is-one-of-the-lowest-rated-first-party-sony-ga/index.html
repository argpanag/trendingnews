Wolverine PS5 Is One of the Lowest Rated First-Party Sony Games of the Generation

TheTools · 10.09.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

How Logan you go

Reviews for Marvel's Wolverine have dropped, and it's fair to say that many of them are a bit disappointing.

At the time of writing, Insomniac Games' big budget action title sits at an eyebrow-raising 77 on Metacritic, based on just over 100 evaluations.

Now I should stress that a 77 is not a bad score, despite the growing sentiment that anything below an 80, or 8/10, is unworthy of your time. Even Metacritic itself states that a 77 is "generally favourable".

But given the largely excellent track record of PlayStation Studios, that 77 looks surprisingly low.

For context, Wolverine is now counted amongst the lowest rated first-party Sony games of this console generation.

And by first-party, I mean major development outfits owned by Sony. The likes of Naughty Dog, Santa Monica Studio, Sucker Punch, and, of course, Insomniac Games.

To compare Wolverine with other first-party PS5 releases, we've got...

  • Astro Bot (94)
  • God of War Ragnarok (94)
  • Demon's Souls (92) (developer Bluepoint technically wasn't a first-party studio yet when Demon's Souls released)
  • Marvel's Spider-Man 2 (90)
  • The Last of Us Part 2 Remastered (90)
  • The Last of Us Part 1 (89)
  • Horizon Forbidden West (88)
  • Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart (88)
  • Saros (87)
  • Uncharted: Legacy of Thieves Collection (87)
  • Marvel's Spider-Man Remastered (87)
  • Ghost of Tsushima: Director's Cut (87)
  • Gran Turismo 7 (87)
  • Returnal (86)
  • Ghost of Yotei (86)
  • Marvel's Spider-Man: Miles Morales (85)
  • Horizon Zero Dawn Remastered (85)
  • Astro's Playroom (83)
  • Days Gone Remastered (78)
  • Marvel's Wolverine (77)
  • Concord (62)

Again, this is only taking first-party studio titles into account. If we're talking PS5 exclusives published by Sony, then games such as Destruction AllStars and God of War: Sons of Sparta all rank lower than Wolverine.

In any case, it's a fairly steep drop-off for Insomniac. The Burbank-based outfit has gone from releasing Spider-Man 2 in 2023 — rated at a very impressive 90 — to giving us a 77-rated Wolverine four years later.

Indeed, the studio hadn't crafted a game with a Metacritic score lower than 85 across the whole generation — and Insomniac's been busy over the last seven years or so, delivering more titles than any other Sony-owned developer in that time.

Wolverine makes for an interesting outlier, then. And I'm left wondering how the game will end up being perceived in the future — especially once players have had their say.

For what it's worth, our own reviewer Liam really enjoyed Wolverine, despite some pretty clear flaws. It got an 8/10 in our Marvel's Wolverine PS5 review.
