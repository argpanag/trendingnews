After her Governors Awards appearance sparked speculation, the star is calling out the scrutiny of her changing face and body.

Florence Pugh has spent years defending her body against criticism. Now, she's defending her face — and her right to have her appearance change. 

After her appearance at the Academy's Governors Awards on Nov. 15 sparked speculation about cosmetic procedures, the 30-year-old pushed back in a lengthy Instagram post. She denies facial surgery and Ozempic use, questioning why strangers feel entitled to dissect her appearance.

"I find it so stressful waking up and [having] thousands of people talking about you and your body in ways that you can't even comprehend," she wrote. "I woke up the other morning and quite a lot of you were discussing my face and body in such a disturbing way."

The commentary ranged from accusations of the actor getting cosmetic surgery — an upper or lower bleph? Buccal fat removal? — to guesses that she might have lost weight with the help of a GLP-1.

"I have never been on ozempic. I have NEVER done any face surgery. And don't EVER say that I didn't make my own nose, I've always had that beautiful ski slope. Even as a baby!" wrote Pugh. 

The East of Eden star's statement comes after speculation swept across social media. One Instagram video posted by Evie magazine on Tuesday compared older and newer photos of Pugh and suggested she had "fallen victim to Hollywood's obsession with sculpted faces." 

Pugh responded directly beneath the video later that day.

"Guys. I turned 30. I'm a woman. Body's and faces change. It's not that deep, the fact that people can't even tell between what's aging and what's surgery is disturbing," she wrote. "Sadly puppy fluff doesn't last forever."

Still, onlookers continued to discuss her changing looks — and their skepticism that natural aging is responsible for it. "I love her but the Botox and fillers are so obvious, I miss her old face so much," wrote one Redditor. 

By Wednesday, Pugh had more to say. 

"I have spent my whole career defending and protecting bodies that just grow the way they grow. I was subjected to fat shaming in the business from the moment I started and I have loved everytime I get to tell someone to f*** off when they are being inappropriate about my weight, and I'm not changing that now just because you've all said so."

"Here's the truth," Pugh added, sharing that turning 30 prompted resolutions to change her relationship with alcohol and to train with the goal of completing a pull-up. "For the first time in my ENTIRE career, I have had the opportunity to be rested, to sleep, to wake up and work out and to eat regularly and healthily. I've WANTED to move my body and not take it for granted," she wrote. "Guess how much weight I've lost in all of that? 2 pounds."

More importantly, she's gained strength and has surpassed her pull-up goal with the help of a trainer. "I can now do 6 pull ups," she continued. "I'm looking after myself, and that's literally it."

The Dune: Part Three star went on to describe years of contradictory criticism: strangers calling her fat, telling her to take Ozempic and insisting that she shouldn't show her midriff. 

"I've always been aware that seeing a confident woman be happy in her skin is triggering to a lot of people and for [a] long time just accepted that I will receive commentary like that. But now this…?"

She also revealed she had "sued a few publications" earlier this year for publishing AI images that seemed to show her getting injectables. "They lied to sell their story. But it damaged me," she wrote. 

Even commentary presented as concern about beauty standards subjects her body to scrutiny, she reminded followers. "I understand that we are in a warped part of image history right now, with beauty standards changing, getting harder and harder to keep up with," she wrote. "But please be gentle with yourselves and others when you go in on people's physical appearance."
