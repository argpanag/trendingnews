Nudity, a new twist, and Jack Dee dancing make Last One Laughing a total riot: review

When it returned for its second season earlier this year, Last One Laughing was proclaimed the funniest show on British TV by this newspaper and several others. Producers must have been beside themselves with glee. You could hardly think of a simpler, cheaper format than filming a group of comedians trying not to laugh in a studio for a few hours. And it wasn’t just a hit with critics; this gem of a show (which also has many popular iterations around the world) was bringing in more than 6 million viewers. It’s no wonder, then, that ahead of its third run next year, they’ve brought it back for a four-part Halloween special with a whole new twist.

Not only are the comedians banned from laughing and smiling, they also can’t “scream, screech, shriek or squeal” as their fellow players – and producers – try to scare them and make them jump. First in the door this time is Inside No 9’s Reece Shearsmith, the “king of creepy”, followed by Lou Sanders (back from series one and “ready to lose again”), self-proclaimed scaredy cat Phil Wang, series two returnee Daisy May Cooper, panel show regular Tom Allen, up-and-coming comic Harriet Dyer and deadpan master Jack Dee. There are also cameos from Charlie Cooper (brother of Daisy), Catherine Tate, Steve Pemberton and more.

Allow me to get one minor quibble out of the way (the only thing that brings this rating down from five stars to four). I didn’t think it was possible for a show’s set to be more aesthetically wrong – all those garish colours are enough to make a Farrow & Ball paint consultant promptly vomit and faint! – but now it’s had a “spooky makeover”. The studio is full of all the plastic bits and bobs that creep onto the shelves at Sainsbury’s earlier and earlier every year. Dead dolls lurching out of the walls. Knobbly chairs “made from the skeletons of previous guests”. Shiny black crows perched on sofas. Host Jimmy Carr, like Blunty on The Celebrity Traitors, eerily and inexplicably resembling an embalmed estate agent. Oh wait, that last one isn’t a new prop. But still… the eyes bleed!

Now, onto why this show continues to be brilliant. The no-shrieking twist brings an extra dimension to the game that’s very fun. Previous seasons have seen the stars doing lots of anxious pacing, and they still do this, but now it’s in between bouts of sitting very still, darting their eyes around nervously, and refusing to open the fridge in case the decaying head of David Mitchell pops out from a pile of iceberg lettuce (which it does).

In a sentence I never thought I’d write, one highlight of the new episodes is an excruciating half-naked performance from Allen, where he wrestles with a man called Martin who looks like he’s just walked off Love Island and is definitely not really called Martin. “Is Martin straight?” asks Cooper. Another moment I defy you not to weep laughing at sees all the comedians butchering regional British accents in a strange, job-seeking-themed game overseen by Pemberton. Also, Jack Dee dances. It’s sort of like a tap dance meets a line dance. It’s decades’ worth of repressed enthusiasm and joie de vivre unleashed in one go. And it takes everyone a while to recover.

Those looking for a fright won’t really find anything scary in this Halloween twist on the format – that was never the point – it’s still just simply very, very funny. And that’s fine, because the world is a scary enough place as it is at the moment. This show is an excuse to think about nothing and laugh until our sides hurt. As viewers at home, we can do this all we like, with the restrained comedians in the studio the only ones who aren’t allowed to really let go and enjoy it. As Allen puts it: “It’s all been a lot of fun so far. I wish I could have let my face know.”
