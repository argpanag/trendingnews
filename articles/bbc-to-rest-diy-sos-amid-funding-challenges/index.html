BBC to ‘rest’ DIY SOS amid funding challenges

TheTools · 18.10.2026 14:21 · source · permalink

Renovation programme will be replaced by ‘cost-effective’ spin-off show revisiting past projects

The BBC has confirmed that DIY SOS, its long-running home-renovation TV series, will be “rested” next year because of funding challenges.

The programme, hosted by Nick Knowles, helps transform the homes of families facing severe illness, disability, bereavement or building disasters.

First aired in 1999, it began as a low-budget show in which Knowles and a small team helped homeowners fix botched DIY projects.

In 2010, the TV series relaunched as DIY SOS: The Big Build and shifted to larger-scale projects that saw the team repair and transform people’s homes across Britain with help from local communities.

The BBC has confirmed the show will be “rested” after the series currently being broadcast, and replaced by a spin-off show revisiting past projects.

A BBC spokesman said: “In light of the BBC’s savings challenges we have taken the decision to rest DIY SOS in 2027 and commission a new spin-off show instead from producers South Shore.

“DIY SOS Rebuilt My Life will revisit successes from the show’s past and expand the brand on the BBC in a more cost-effective way next year.”

The BBC has also confirmed its annual Children In Need instalment of DIY SOS, which involves a large-scale charitable build often focused on supporting vulnerable young people, has been rested.

The decisions come amid a series of recent cuts by the corporation as part of the first phase of its effort to save £500m in the next three years, which have included the cancellation of TV shows Blankety Blank, Celebrity Mastermind and Live At The Apollo.

DIY SOS has taken on many ambitious projects through the years. In 2015, Knowles worked with the Prince of Wales, the Duke of Sussex and hundreds of volunteers to transform a derelict street in Manchester into housing for veterans.

The Prince of Wales returned to the programme in 2018 for its two-part special focused on supporting the survivors and community affected by the Grenfell Tower fire, which killed 72 people.

The episodes also showed the DIY SOS team building a community centre and restoring the Dale Youth Boxing Club, which had been destroyed in a fire in June 2017.

The programme launched its 35th series earlier this month with an episode in which Knowles broke down in tears during a “physically and emotionally draining” house renovation.

The TV host’s team helped 16-year-old Dan, who had spent the last three years in hospital after life-saving surgery to remove a brain tumour that left him with complex disabilities.

DIY SOS is available on BBC iPlayer, with new episodes airing weekly on BBC One.
