Yellow alerts issued for tomorrow as 5,000 without power

TheTools · 24.10.2026 23:01 · source · permalink

Status Yellow weather warnings will come into effect for 11 counties tomorrow, as around 5,000 homes and businesses remain without power following Storm Boelo.

The second named storm of the season brought some very disruptive weather across the country over the last 24 hours, with impacts including power outages, spot flooding, fallen trees and road closures.

Air and ferry travel were also affected, with disruption to services continuing into today.

Dublin airport operator daa said gusting winds in Ireland and poor weather at airports abroad saw 30 flights cancelled at Dublin Airport today.

"While wind speeds have now dropped considerably in the Dublin area, poor weather conditions at other airports mean further disruption is possible," an update from the airport said.

The head of the National Directorate for Fire and Emergency Management (NDFEM) Keith Leonard thanked all the local teams from across the country "who worked throughout the last few days to help us to prepare to meet the challenges of Storms Austen and Boelo, mitigate their effects and remedy any impacts afterwards".

"Work is ongoing, particularly in the northwest to help those who have been impacted."

The emergency coordination group said although Storm Boelo has now moved away, more rain and strong winds are expected on Monday - with yellow warnings issued for the west, south and southeast.

Following a meeting this afternoon, it said: "Soils are also saturated and more rainfall may cause some localised flooding, particularly in the Southwest and west.

"River levels are also expected to remain elevated in the southwest and west on Monday and into Tuesday.

"The National Directorate for Fire and Emergency Management will continue to actively monitor the situation and will reconvene the National Severe Weather and Flood Coordination Group as and when required."

In an update, ESB Networks said that as of 5.15pm, approximately 5,000 premises were without power, mostly in the northwest.

The number of premises without power is down from 41,000, which was recorded at 7.45am.

Earlier, the company warned that further outages were possible as weather warnings remain in place.

It also said that if any member of the public comes across fallen wires or a damaged electricity network, people should not approach them and instead call its emergency number on 1800 372 999.

ESB Networks said that crews "will work late into the night to restore power to as many customers as possible".

However, the utility said some customers will remain without supply overnight.

A Status Orange wind alert for Donegal expired earlier as well as a national Status Yellow wind warning.

Eleven counties under a yellow warning tomorrow

A new weather system is going to hit the country next week with Met Éireann issuing a Status Yellow wind warning for 11 counties.

The warning, valid from 5pm tomorrow until 11.59pm, is for counties Carlow, Clare, Cork, Donegal, Galway, Kerry, Kilkenny, Mayo, Waterford, Wexford and Wicklow.

Difficult driving and cycling conditions as well as some fallen trees and branches are expected.

A Status Yellow rain warning is also in place from 1am until 9am tomorrow for Cork and Kerry and a separate yellow rain warning for Cork, Galway, Kerry and Mayo from 4pm tomorrow until 10pm.

Difficult travelling conditions are expected and localised flooding is also possible. Met Éireann also said some outdoor events may be impacted.

Weather system does not meet named storm threshold, forecaster says

There were fears during the week that the weather system could build and become a named storm. According to Ciara O'Hare, a Met Éireann forecaster, though, the dull and blustery conditions it will bring will not meet the threshold.

"There will be some impactful weather from this system moving in, but it's not a named storm," she said.

"So the impactful weather that we're going to see will be more rain and wind, but not at the levels required for a named storm."

Ms O'Hare said that the major issue with the new weather system is the compounding affect it will have on areas already impacted by Storm Austen and Storm Boelo.

"There's already a lot of rain having fallen, so the ground could be waterlogged or saturated, and so it could potentially impact rivers. And if there's already been some wind, there could be damage to buildings, and then more wind could exacerbate that," she said.

"There is more rain coming and more wind coming."

Met Éireann has issued a yellow wind warning for Antrim, Down and Derry for all day on Tuesday.

Fallen trees reported in Donegal, Leitrim

There are a lot of fallen trees reported in Donegal, as well as debris on the road. Road users are being urged to be careful on the roads.

ESB area manager for Donegal, Mícheál Ó Dubhgáin, told Nuacht RTÉ that up to 25,000 customers in the county were without power this morning due to Storm Boelo.

That figure was reduced to 10,000 by lunchtime, and he hopes that most places affected will have power restored by the end of today, although some places might have to wait until early tomorrow

Many trees were down in exposed areas in Co Donegal, particularly in the Fánaid Peninsula, Churchill, Ramelton and Inishowen.

In Ramelton, protective barriers at a building site were blown to the ground and there was some signage torn by heavy winds in Letterkenny and An Tearmann.

Restoration work by ESB Networks and Eir have been going on all day.

Garry Martin, Director of Human Resources, Economic Development and Emergency Services with Donegal County Council, said that in some instances electricity lines are entangled in trees.

He said people need to stay back and call the ESB's emergency number.

Mr Martin said that there is also some minor flooding.

In Leitrim, there are a number of fallen trees but it appears the county has escaped any major damage.

Meanwhile, Dublin Airport has said that strong winds will continue to impact flight operations today.

In a post on social media, it said that 12 inbound and 12 outbound flights have been cancelled. It said that as wind warnings remain in place, further disruption is possible.

In Northern Ireland, several thousand homes are also without power. The power cuts are localised with the overall number affected at this stage believed to be fewer than 7,000.

A Status Amber warning is in place for counties Derry and Antrim until 9pm this evening.

A Status Red marine warning was also issued from Bloody Foreland to Malin Head to Belfast Lough. However that has been downgraded to a Status Orange warning, which was in place until 9am.
