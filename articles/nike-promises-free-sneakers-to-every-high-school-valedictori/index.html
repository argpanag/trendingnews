Nike promises free sneakers to every high school valedictorian in America after viral request

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:59 · source · permalink

The announcement came after Mississippi Senator-elect Scott Colom requested custom Air Force 1s for his state

You don't have to be a star athlete to score free Nike sneakers. Apparently, being a straight-A student will do the trick, too.

Nike announced Tuesday that it plans to give free shoes to high school valedictorians across the United States for the 2026-27 school year.

The idea started with Mississippi Senator-elect Scott Colom, who took to X on Monday with a request for the sportswear giant: Give every high school valedictorian in his state a custom pair of Nike Air Force 1s.

Democratic U.S. Senate candidate Scott Colom, wearing an "I Voted" sticker, walks across the campus of Jackson State University on Election Day, Nov. 3, 2026 in Jackson, Miss. (Aron Smith/Jackson State University via Getty Images)

Colom suggested the giveaway would encourage students to "hit the books" while celebrating his historic election. The Democrat defeated incumbent Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith earlier this month, becoming the first Black U.S. senator elected from Mississippi since Reconstruction — nearly 150 years ago.

But Nike decided to take things a step further.

"The athlete mindset makes things possible, including this idea," Nike's official store account wrote on X Tuesday. "@scott_colom, we'll do you one better… For this year, 2026/2027 high school valedictorians from across the U.S., let's go!"

The company added that more information would be coming.

A Nike swoosh logo at Nike by Eugene. (Kirby Lee-USA TODAY Sports)

The giveaway comes as Nike works to broaden its appeal and rebuild its brand after years of leaning into politically charged marketing campaigns. Under CEO Elliott Hill, the company has made a concerted effort to return to its roots, focusing on sports, achievement and connecting with consumers. And rewarding students for academic excellence seems like a pretty easy way to win some goodwill across the political spectrum.

For now, Nike hasn't specified which sneakers the students will receive, whether they'll be custom-designed or how graduating seniors can claim their free kicks.

Regardless of which shoes Nike ultimately hands out, it's a pretty sweet deal for students who have spent four years working their way to the top of the class.
