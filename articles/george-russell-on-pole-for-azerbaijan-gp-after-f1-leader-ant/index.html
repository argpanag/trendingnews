Giles Richards' qualifying report

Here’s Giles’s verdict:

Kimi Antonelli was left ruing an uncharacteristic error in qualifying for the Azerbaijan Grand Prix which eliminated the championship leader in the first session after he hit a wall in Baku. His Mercedes teammate George Russell claimed pole with a fine lap and will be in a strong position to make up ground in the title chase in Saturday’s race …

The error was striking because Antonelli had shown such composure in stepping up to control the championship in only his second season in the sport. The Italian has barely put a foot wrong this season …

Russell in is every position to take advantage but Antonelli knows that the race is far from a write-off. At Monza he came back from 19th to take victory and Baku, with its long straights, has similarly generous opportunities for overtaking. It will nonetheless be another serious test for the youngster who must maintain his nerve to close out a title that is within his grasp.

Gabriel Bortoleto and Nico Hülkenberg will remain as the Audi driving lineup for the 2027 season, the team principal, Mattia Binotto, confirmed in Baku on Friday.

Aston Martin’s Fernando Alonso and Lance Stroll have yet more power-unit penalties and will start at the back in Baku, however they fare in qualifying. Stroll would have started there come-what-may, of course.

Lando Norris’s hopes of another pole look slim. He said the McLaren was “miles off” the pace after placing 14th and sixth in the two sessions and he was down in eighth in FP3.

“The car didn’t feel that bad, so it’s just a little knife in the heart when you see how far off you are,” he added.

Ten minutes to go.

Azerbaijan’s Remembrance Day was instituted in December 2020 to mark the outbreak on 27 September that year of the second Nagorno-Karabakh war. By F1’s 2026 standards, having a race weekend Thursday-Saturday is fairly small political potatoes.

The Middle East war led to the cancellation of the Saudi Arabian race and the rescheduling and displacement of the Bahrain GP, now taking place on Sunday 4 October in Malaysia. Now, the escalating conflict continues to threaten the climax to the season, scheduled for Qatar and Abu Dhabi but with Imola on standby should those races be postponed.

Of course there are far more important matters hanging on the US-Israel-Iran-Saudi-Arabia-Houthis conflict but Lewis Hamilton has been talking about his hopes that the scheduled season climax can go ahead:

“I personally want to go. I love going to the Middle East,” he said. “From what I have read, things seem OK out there, but I don’t have all the information and I don’t know what the risk levels are. I trust in Stefano [Domenicali, F1’s chief executive] to make the right decision for the sport. And I do hope we get to go there.”

Preamble

Welcome to Friday F1 qualifying. No, this is not Las Vegas, but the result of Sunday being Azerbaijan’s Remembrance Day. So from 1pm BST the 11 teams and 22 drivers will be chasing grid positions for a race that starts at noon BST on Saturday.

Two weeks ago in Spain, Lando Norris took pole but had a near-certain victory snatched away by the timing of a virtual safety car, instigated just after he passed the pit entrance and allowing pretty much everyone else to take a free pit stop … including Kimi Antonelli, the driver least in need of a slice of luck but whose championship lead was extended to 81 points with his victory.

Can Norris bounce back from the disappointment of his Madrid misfortune and claim a fourth pole in five races? Talking of Spanish pain, can Lewis Hamilton get his outside title shot back on track after his DNF? Will George Russell, back in second but with litt;;e hope of closing the gap to his Mercedes teammate, be able to show the kind of form that made him pre-season favourite? Or will Antonelli enjoy yet another stellar weekend? Join me from 12.30pm for all the buildup.
