Wait times rise for temporary residence applicants

TheTools · 16.09.2026 18:08 · source · permalink

ByAmara Okonkwo·Published September 2026

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) has published updated temporary residence processing estimates, showing that wait times are largely increasing. The most significant increases include a one-week rise for U.S. work permit applicants, a one-week increase for in-Canada and Nigeria-based study permit applicants, and a substantial rise of over seven weeks for U.S. super visa applicants. Philippines-based super visa applicants also saw an increase of over three weeks.

These changes are part of a broader trend in temporary residence processing times, which have been fluctuating in recent weeks. Compared to the previous update on September 9, the latest figures show a mix of increases and decreases across different application types and countries. For example, work permit wait times for U.S. nationals had held steady for over a month before increasing by one week, while in-Canada applicants continued to see declining wait times. Study permit processing times increased by one week for applicants from within Canada and Nigeria, but remained unchanged for other featured countries.

The specific wait times for each application type and country are as follows: work permits range from 3 weeks for U.S. applicants to 10 weeks for India and Nigeria-based applicants, with a service standard of 120 days for in-Canada submissions and 60 days for outside Canada submissions. Study permits range from 5 weeks for India and Philippines-based applicants to 10 weeks for Nigeria-based applicants, with a service standard of 120 days for in-Canada submissions and 60 days for outside Canada submissions. Visitor visa processing times saw minimal movement, with increases of 2-3 days and declines of no more than 2 days. Super visa wait times rose in every country but Pakistan, which saw a decline of over a month, with the U.S. posting the steepest climb at over seven weeks.

According to IRCC, "processing times provide an estimate of how long a newly submitted application could take to finalize" and should not be interpreted as deadlines or guarantees. The department calculates these estimates using historical or forward-looking approaches, taking into account factors such as application complexity and available processing capacity. Service standards, on the other hand, are departmental performance targets that represent the period within which IRCC aims to process a particular application type under normal circumstances.

These changes in processing times affect various groups of applicants, including those from the U.S., India, Pakistan, Nigeria, and the Philippines. For example, U.S. nationals applying for work permits or super visas will face longer wait times, while Pakistan-based super visa applicants will see a significant decrease in processing time. Applicants from other countries, such as India and Nigeria, will also be affected by changes in study permit and visitor visa processing times.

Applicants can check the official IRCC website for the latest processing time estimates and service standards. It is also recommended to check the Express Entry draw tracker for updates on Express Entry draws and other immigration-related news. The next update on temporary residence processing times is expected to be published on or around September 23, 2026. In the meantime, applicants can use the IRCC website to estimate their processing time and plan accordingly.
