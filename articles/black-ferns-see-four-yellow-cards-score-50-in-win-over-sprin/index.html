Black Ferns see four yellow cards, score 50 in win over Springbok Women

Follow the action as the Black Ferns and Springbok Women clash at FNB Stadium.

Match preview

It took 15 years for the Black Ferns to play a second test against South Africa.

The sides first met in 2010, when the New Zealanders cruised to a 55-3 win. Their next clash was in last year’s World Cup quarter-final, where the then-Allan Bunting-coached Black Ferns claimed a 46-17 win, fighting back from falling behind early and a 10-all halftime score.

Almost a year later, the sides will meet again at FNB Stadium in Soweto as the first part of a doubleheader featuring the third test between the All Blacks and Springboks.

“That World Cup match was a tough one. We learnt a lot of lessons in that first half and we were able to apply what we learnt in that second half,” Black Ferns lock Laura Bayfield said.

“Going into this match we’re prepared really well, we’ve done our homework and the team’s coming together really nicely.”

Bayfield’s inclusion on the bench is one of several changes to Whitney Hansen’s side from the group that beat Australia’s Wallaroos in Auckland late last month.

There are two changes to the starting side, with Mia Anderson starting at blindside flanker and Hannah King steering the ship in the No 10 jersey with Ruahei Demant on the bench.

Anderson joins the starting side in place of Kaipo Olsen-Baker, who was unavailable with a shoulder injury suffered late in the win over the Wallaroos, which led to Liana Mikaele-Tu’u shifting from blindside to the back of the scrum.

On the bench, Bayfield provides locking cover and Amarante Sititi, who was the lock cover last time out, is the loose forward option.

Halfback Tara Turner, who was unavailable for the test against the Wallaroos because of injury, returns to provide cover for Maia Joseph. Hooker Atlanta Lolohea and utility back Justine McGregor are also included on the bench, in place of Vici-Rose Green and Shyrah Tuliau-Tua’a respectively.

The test will be the second of eight on the Black Ferns’ end-of-year schedule, with tests away to Scotland, France and England to come, before hosting France for a three-test series later in the year.

“It’s really making our rugby identity come alive and allowing us to challenge ourselves more against the best in the world,” King said of the Black Ferns’ extended test schedule this season.

“Coming across to South Africa, it’s pretty insane to be part of this historic test so we’re really excited to go out there, have some fun and go against this physical Springbok Women’s team.”
