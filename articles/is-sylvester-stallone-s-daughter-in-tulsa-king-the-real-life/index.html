Is Sylvester Stallone's Daughter in 'Tulsa King'? The Real-Life Manfredi Connection Explained

TheTools · 17.10.2026 12:25 · source · permalink

When television mastermind Taylor Sheridan and writer Terence Winter developed ‘Tulsa King’ for Paramount+, they built a distinctive playground for Hollywood royalty. The gritty crime drama brought cinema icon Sylvester Stallone into his first major scripted television venture as Dwight “The General” Manfredi, an exiled New York capo forced to carve out a new empire in Oklahoma.

Throughout its run, the series has leaned heavily into the culture shock of contrasting classic East Coast mafia traditions with the rugged terrain of the American heartland. While the show thrives on underworld turf battles, sudden violence, and high-stakes criminal diplomacy, it also relies on subtle character moments that flesh out Dwight’s loyal circle of Midwestern outcasts.

Among the eclectic faces navigating Dwight’s expanding enterprise, observant viewers frequently notice a familiar screen presence sharing quiet moments with the veteran action star. Sylvester Stallone’s real-life youngest daughter, Scarlet Rose Stallone, is indeed an active cast member in ‘Tulsa King‘, portraying the recurring character Spencer across multiple seasons.

Spencer’s Evolution Across ‘Tulsa King’

Scarlet Rose Stallone first stepped into the narrative during the drama’s inaugural season as an unassuming barista working at an Oklahoma coffee shop visited by Dwight. Recognizing her gentle demeanor with animals and strong work ethic, the hardened mobster promptly offered her a new position caring for his beloved white horse, Pilot, at Fennario Ranch.

Over time, Spencer transitioned even deeper into Dwight’s inner circle, eventually moving behind the counter as a bartender at the syndicate’s headquarters, the Bred-2-Buck saloon. Her narrative footprint expanded as she crossed paths with crew members like Tyson Mitchell and found herself caught between high-stakes power plays involving rival local factions.

Despite their genuine biological kinship, Scarlet does not portray Dwight’s estranged onscreen daughter, Christina “Tina” Manfredi, a pivotal role played by Tatiana Zappardino. Showrunners intentionally kept Scarlet in a separate supporting role because the series required an older daughter who had spent 25 long years enduring the emotional fallout of Dwight’s prison sentence.

Working Together On and Off the Screen

Sharing a set with a celebrated Hollywood parent can be an intimidating challenge, but the Stallone family already possessed plenty of experience with creative collaboration. Before joining ‘Tulsa King’, Scarlet shared brief screen time with her father in the 2014 ensemble indie ‘Reach Me’ and later appeared alongside her sisters on the Paramount+ reality series ‘The Family Stallone’.

Stepping onto the set of a prestige drama demanded a far higher degree of discipline, yet the experience allowed father and daughter to build a refreshed artistic relationship. The shared workplace dynamic gave both performers an opportunity to bridge generational divides, trade acting advice, and cultivate a professional bond away from the comforts of home.

As Variety reported, the series quickly established itself as one of streaming’s premier powerhouse franchises, securing a durable foundation where the ensemble could steadily evolve. That production stability offered a rare environment for newer performers like Scarlet to sharpen their craft directly alongside seasoned industry veterans who have shaped American screen acting for decades.

Addressing the Nepotism Conversation Head-On

In a modern industry where familial connections routinely spark passionate debates regarding privilege and access, the Stallone patriarch has refused to dance around the subject. Sylvester Stallone has been remarkably candid about creating professional openings for his children, arguing that leveraging career success to help one’s family is a natural parental duty rather than an unfair shortcut.

Addressing the ongoing conversation regarding family casting choices, Stallone openly embraced his role in helping Scarlet secure an audition for the show. “Of course it’s nepotism, you idiot. That’s why we’re parents. We want the best for our children,” Stallone bluntly remarked in an interview with USA Today Entertainment.

The action star was quick to clarify that while parental influence can unlock an initial audition, genuine longevity in show business rests entirely upon personal talent. He noted that once the director yells action before millions of television viewers, no family surname can protect a performer who lacks the presence or work ethic to deliver believable work.

As ‘Tulsa King’ continues pushing Dwight Manfredi into increasingly volatile turf wars and regional conflicts, Spencer remains an endearing and stabilizing element of his everyday world. For dedicated viewers who appreciate rich storytelling both in front of and behind the cameras, watching a real-life family dynamic quietly enrich the Midwestern crime drama adds an extra layer of enjoyment.
