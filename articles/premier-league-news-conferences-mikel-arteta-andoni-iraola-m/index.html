    The long, long international break is almost done. Premier League football will return tomorrow when Arsenal host Leeds at 12:30 BST.

    Today we had 15 Premier League news conferences and, unsurprisingly, Manchester City's guilty verdict around their financial breeches have dominated the headlines.

    Manchester City manager Enzo Maresca said City's titles are "absolutely not" tainted despite club being found guilty of breaking the majority of the 115 financial rules they were accused of breaching.

    Maresca also said he "completely trusts the club" and will speak about his future "when we win the appeal".

    But Liverpool boss Andoni Iraola said the "uncertainty" about Man City's appeal is "not good for anyone".

    And Manchester United boss Michael Carrick believes the case "throws up a lot of questions".

  2. 'Maresca cut a composed and positive figure'published at 15:24 BST
    15:24 BST

    Liverpool v Man City (Sun, 1630 BST)

    Dan Roan
    BBC sports editor

    This was an accomplished performance by Enzo Maresca, who, notably, was accompanied by City’s senior communications official at a packed news conference at City’s training complex.

    Unlike some other Premier League clubs who say they will not answer questions about the cheating scandal, Maresca faced a fifteen minute-long barrage, with all but one of the inquiries focused on the controversy.

    If Maresca is worried by the prospect of punishment, or the hostility that lies in wait at Anfield on Sunday, he did not show it, cutting a composed and positive figure throughout. And although he would not be drawn on whether he had read the commission’s findings, and also avoided confirming whether he would stay at the Etihad regardless of the punishment City face, he seemed well prepared. Sticking to the script, he repeatedly spoke about his trust in City’s hierarchy, and referred to the “noise” surrounding the club, often returning to the football that he insists is his focus.

    One reporter suggested that the club’s chief executive or chairman should be fielding questions, rather than the manager. There was no sense that Maresca was frustrated about having to address the issue, but the scrutiny is set to continue.

  19. 'If somebody cheats they need to get punished'published at 14:33 BST
    14:33 BST

    Crystal Palace v Nottingham Forest (Sun, 14:00 BST)

    Nottingham Forest

    Nottingham Forest head coach Oliver Glasner on Manchester City: "First of all I thought, because we have many TV screens in our ground. 24 hours a day it was Man City. Is this is the only problem in the world we have right now? It's so big, in England especially.

    "I haven't been here when these things happened, or did not happen. There's no final verdict right now. That's why I don't have any comment on Man City because I don't know. It looks like everybody is saying they are guilty but there is an appeal.

    "I can react on the final verdict, but in general, if somebody cheats they need to get punished, if not, then not. I think we all hope for the integrity of the game and of the Premier League that the final verdict is spoken soon. But there are thousands of pages to read, I read a book every night and it takes a long time to read a thousand pages. So it takes a bit of time, we have to be patient.

    On the impact of the verdict: "I don't think there is a big impact. If there is a points deduction then there will be, but right now I don't think, especially not for us. I'm not surprised that Enzo said something in that direction, that's why they appealed so they feel quite confident that they have done right. The manager can't say 'I'm in doubt' that makes no sense, they have to be aligned. But I don't think that there is a huge example this weekend. It's not a big topic at our training ground. For us it doesn't matter."
