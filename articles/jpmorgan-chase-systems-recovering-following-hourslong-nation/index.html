JPMorgan Chase systems recovering following hourslong nationwide technical outage

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:58 · source · permalink

JPMorgan Chase’s systems were coming back online Monday with no indication of malicious or external cyber activity

Chase, Wells Fargo among banks reporting user outages

Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America and U.S. Bank are reportedly among the major banks experiencing widespread online outages.

JPMorgan Chase’s systems were coming back online Monday after an hourslong nationwide outage left customers reporting trouble withdrawing funds and accessing their bank accounts.

The disruption was traced to a software release the bank rolled out earlier Monday that affected a subset of its systems, according to an FBI official. There was no indication of malicious or external cyber activity, the official said.

The impact was limited to JPMorgan Chase and transactions at other financial institutions in which JPMorgan Chase was a party, the official said.

The bank also said publicly that the incident was not malicious, and its systems were coming back online.

"Due to a technical issue, some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete but customers still have access to their accounts and funds," Chase said. "We are working to resolve this as quickly as possible. This is a technical issue, not cyber-related."

Customers began reporting problems conducting transactions Monday morning. Chase earlier acknowledged an online banking issue in a response to a customer through its Chase Support account on X.

"We saw your post and want to reach out. I do apologize, it appears that we are currently working to resolve an issue with online banking," Chase Support said. "Please try again at a later time. We're sorry for any inconvenience, and appreciate your patience."

A teller at a Washington, D.C., Chase branch told FOX Business that in-store and online banking issues began around 10 a.m., and had been resolved just after 3 p.m. A branch manager said they had some glitches at the branch but can process most transactions there, on app and online. ATM machines were working as normal, the manager said. 

At another branch, a Chase bank employee said the ATMs and banking app were working, but the computer system at the branch had "been up and down."

A JPMorgan Chase bank branch as customers experience disruptions due to a banking technology outage.  (Ava Palschakov for Fox News Digital)

"It is taking longer than usual to service some customers," a company spokesperson said. "This is not a systemic issue – other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us."

Bank of America said its systems were operating normally.

"Bank of America systems have been operating normally today. Clients experiencing delays with other providers may wish to contact them directly for the latest information," the bank said in a statement.

A spokesperson for US Bank also said its users were not experiencing issues.

"U.S. Bank systems are not directly impacted," the spokesperson told Fox News. "However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delay."

PNC Financial Service and Wells Fargo told FOX Business they were not impacted by Monday's events. 

Zelle, the digital payment network, said its system was working and operating as normal. 

"Zelle is not down. Zelle is operating as it should," the company said in a statement. "Consumers use Zelle through participating banks and credit unions, which manage their accounts and hold their funds. Consumers do not have a separate Zelle account, and Zelle does not hold consumer funds."

A JPMorgan Chase ATM is pictured outside the bank as technology disruptions impact banking services.  (Ava Palschakov for Fox News Digital)

Zelle’s network operator is a company called Early Warning Services, which is owned by financial institutions including Bank of America, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, PNC Bank, Truist, U.S. Bank, and Wells Fargo, according to The Associated Press. 

The outage happened just a week after an historic FAA ground stop of air travel nationwide Friday and a Microsoft Outlook email disruption Thursday.

Last week’s widespread Microsoft 365 outage was driven by three factors, including a software bug undetected since July, Charles Lamanna, Microsoft’s executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, said Monday.

The trend continued Monday evening, with United Airlines reporting a system-wide computer outage. 

"We sincerely apologize for the inconvenience," the airlines told one person on X. "We're aware of the widespread system outage, and our teams are working hard to get things back up and running as soon as possible. We appreciate your patience while we work through this."

United said the issue was resolved later Monday evening.
