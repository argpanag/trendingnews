Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson has ended speculation about his political plans by launching a reelection campaign. While the mayor seeks to highlight his progressive achievements concerning affordability, crime and standing up to President Trump, opponents are pointing to a rocky relationship with other elected leaders during Johnson's first term.

Brandon Johnson launches reelection bid, highlighting economic policy and Trump fights

In a 2-minute video launching his reelection campaign, Mayor Johnson repeatedly stated his ongoing goal to make Chicago "the safest, most affordable big city in America." He highlighted efforts such as building affordable housing units, declaring that the city was on track to build 10,000 such units. Johnson and others highlighted the mayor's background as a teacher and a union organizer. Echoing popular progressive talking points, Johnson called for "a full moratorium on data centers in our city" while boasting that "taxing the rich" was not just a "theory" in Chicago but something that his administration had actually accomplished. In terms of the safety and security of Chicago's residents, Johnson highlighted "the fewest amount of homicides in 60 years" and boasted of standing up to President Trump by denying Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents the use of Chicago government property.

Overall, Johnson's campaign is painting the mayor's first term as one of significant achievements that would continue into a second term. To get to that second term, Johnson will have to avoid the same fate as his predecessor Lori Lightfoot, who was defeated by both Johnson and candidate Paul Vallas in her 2023 reelection bid. Since achieving that upset and beating Vallas in a runoff, Johnson has seen achievements such as falling murder rates, part of a trend of violent crime rates falling in cities being run by Black Democratic mayors. Johnson also clashed with the Trump administration over immigration policy, with Johnson issuing an executive order to make Chicago an "ICE Free Zone" and Trump in turn calling for Johnson to be arrested.

Johnson hopes progressive momentum will help overcome reelection challenges

Despite the points that Johnson has highlighted in his newly launched reelection campaign, the mayor's term has also been marked by fierce clashes with the Chicago City Council and local businesses over tax and budget policies, and the city's voters rejected a real estate tax meant to fund services for homeless individuals. Mayor Johnson's reelection announcement ends months of speculation concerning whether or not he would seek reelection amid low approval ratings, campaign finance deficits and a competitive field of challengers. Johnson's approval rating in the city was recently measured at less than 14 percent. The low approval rating comes after a first term in which Johnson clashed with aldermen on the Chicago City Council, including over a city budget that faces a shortfall of nearly $900 million for 2027. Johnson's campaign also faces a funding disadvantage. Recent campaign financing declarations showed Johnson's campaign with less than $1 million cash on hand, in contrast to Illinois Secretary of State Alexi Giannoulias, whose campaign has nearly $22 million, as well as state Comptroller Susana Mendoza's $1.6 million and Rep. Mike Quigley's roughly $375,000.

The mayoral race is shaping up to be one not only of money but also of ideology. Giannoulias is one of several top Democratic challengers, alongside Rep. Mike Quigley (D-Illinois) and state Comptroller Susana Mendoza, who are running as more centrist candidates against the progressive mayor. In the race, Johnson is hoping to ride the wave of progressive Democrats that has swept across the party. Although Johnson is not a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, he has aligned with much of the DSA's positions and rhetoric. "It's clear that the progressive energy that we ignited back in 2023 has impacted the rest of the country," Johnson said, pointing to his first election win. "People are tired of corporate greed at the expense of everyday people." The Chicago DSA has not endorsed any candidate in the mayoral race, with Chicago DSA co-chair Sean Duffy explaining that the organization was focusing instead on electing progressive aldermen to the City Council, which would potentially strengthen Mayor Johnson's hand to push through policies in a second term. "If I'm Brandon Johnson," Duffy explained, "I'm thinking about 'How do I get to a strong progressive majority?'"

Johnson's reelection campaign against several prominent challengers may serve as a continuation of the competition between progressive and centrist Democrats that has both divided and energized the party. But despite how the Chicago race fits into larger national narratives, the race will ultimately be a referendum on Mayor Johnson's leadership over his first term in office.
