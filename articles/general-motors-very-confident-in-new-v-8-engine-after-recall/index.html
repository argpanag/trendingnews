General Motors 'very confident' in new V-8 engine after recall issues

TheTools · 19.09.2026 17:05 · source · permalink

FLINT — General Motors Co. is "very confident" in newly designed V-8 engines for its reimagined light-duty pickups after being dogged by engine recalls last year.

GM's new L76 5.7-liter and L78 6.6-liter V-8 engines will outfit the light-duty versions of the 2027 Chevrolet Silverado 1500 and GMC Sierra 1500, high-selling and profit-rich models for the Detroit automaker.

The architecture of the small-block engines that power new GM light-duty trucks is also used in the 2027 Corvette Stingray, Grand Sport and Grand Sport X, said Norman Peralta, executive chief engineer of GM global electrical propulsion systems.

"That does not happen if you don't have a robust engine family," Peralta told reporters at a Wednesday briefing at Flint Engine Operations. "So we're very confident."

GM is rolling out revamped engines, years in the making, after recalling nearly 600,000 trucks and SUVs fitted with its L87 6.2-liter engines for potential failure last year, attributing the issue to a supplier.

GM directed dealers to check the engines of affected vehicles. If the engine passed testing, GM recommended switching to thicker engine oil. Faulty engines were replaced.

"For us, foundationally, we're going to stand by our customers," Peralta said. "It's all about customer safety and customer satisfaction, and that's why for those customers, we've got a 10-year, 150,000 (mile) special coverage."

Peralta said the redesigned engines feature new agent architecture, new lubrication systems, new fuel systems and new heads.

"I can go on and on," he said.

Production of the engines is starting out at GM's Flint Engine Operations. Tonawanda Propulsion in Buffalo, New York, and St. Catharines Propulsion Plant in Ontario have additional capacity to make the engines as the rollout of next-gen trucks ramps up.

V-8s, diesels and EVs

GM's investment in V-8s follows ambitious commitments by the company to electric vehicles and as competitors shift focus to hybrids as the powertrains of the future.

GM's strategy has been on maximizing efficiency and range in both EVs and gas-powered engines. For example, the new light-duty trucks can get more than 900 miles of highway driving range when outfitted with another new engine — the Duramax 3.0-liter diesel — and an optional 34-gallon fuel tank.

"No one has the efficiency on the diesel that we have and have had and retained and now make even better," Reuss said. "And so, if you look at the performance in efficiency, but also in all performance and range, hybrids don't do it. And so this is a pretty segment-exclusive deal here."

"And it's expensive to do to have this and develop it and refine it," he added. "And I think it's a bit different adding a hybrid system to an existing powertrain and trying to get the efficiency out of something like that. At the end of the day, we have focused on something that the customer wants, and that's what we have here."

The automaker pulled back on EV production last year amid slowing consumer demand and policy reversals by the Trump administration and Republican-controlled Congress, although it continues to offer its extensive electric lineup of pickups, crossovers and SUVs.

"We spent effort and time on EVs, and we will continue to do that," said Mark Dickens, GM's executive chief engineer of full-size trucks and SUVs. "But we never let our foot up off the throttle on our naturally aspirated V-8s or any of our powertrain lines."

"These engines didn't just happen overnight," he added. "This is years of development that's gone into these engines, and you can see that based on the results. So I think that's the statement: We didn't turn our back on these on ICE engines."

Specs

GM's next-gen V-8 engines contain cylinder bore centers spaced apart in 4.40-inch intervals. Light-duty truck V-8 engines are larger than the previous versions, up to 5.7 liters from 5.3 liters and 6.6 liters from 6.2 liters.

The 5.7-liter V-8 produces 402 horsepower and 428 pound-feet of torque, and the 6.6-liter engine has 481 horsepower and 501 pound-feet of torque.

Engineers boast a "higher compression ratio for more efficient combustion," "more airflow via larger throttle diameters" and "new intake manifolds with tuned runners to manage the additional airflow" in the new design.

The engines use a dual-fuel system that blends direct injection and port fuel injection for "expandedoperation in optimal power, efficiency and emissions windows." The lubrication system includes a "continuously variable" oil pump, on-engine oil cooler and new oil feed locations "for additional oil pressure consistency."
