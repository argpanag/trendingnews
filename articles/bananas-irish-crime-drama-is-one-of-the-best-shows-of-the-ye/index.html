'Bananas' Irish crime drama is one of the 'best shows of the year'

TheTools · 02.11.2026 19:55 · source · permalink

Viewers have found themselves immediately captivated by a 'totally nuts' series. Some have even declared the new show to be the finest of the year, just one day after its premiere.

Tall Tales and Murder first aired in its Ireland a few months ago, and now even more viewers can discover what all the excitement is about. While the first episode is scheduled to broadcast on Tuesday (November 3) at 10.40pm on BBC One, eager viewers needn't hold off.

This is because the complete series is already available to watch on iPlayer and RTE player. Consequently, it's entirely possible to binge-watch the six-part thriller over just a couple of evenings.

The show was co-created by Chris Addison, who was once a familiar face as a comedian panellist on Mock the Week. He also starred in and penned episodes of The Thick of It before shifting his focus to work behind the camera, reports the Mirror.

He directed several episodes of the American sitcom Veep, widely regarded as a US equivalent of The Thick of It. He also co-created the comedy Breeders starring Martin Freeman, and has joined forces with Irish writer Stuart Carolan for this latest project.

Addison took to social media to express his enthusiasm ahead of its BBC release, describing the series himself as "bananas" and "bonkers". Tall Tales and Murder is loosely inspired by the Dublin 'trilogy' collection of novels penned by Caimh McDonnell.

Based on its storyline, an unidentified patient emerges from an extended coma in a Dublin care facility, claiming to be a criminal who possesses knowledge of hidden treasure. The programme also centres on unconventional nurse Bridget, who covers the overnight shift at the residential home.

She recites horror tales to the elderly residents at bedtime, organises evening dances and provides euthanasia if requested politely. Entering this clandestine environment is Paul, a fragile young man trapped stealing vehicles to settle a debt owed to crime lord Ma Baker.

Paul is frantic to avoid imprisonment and requires a genuine job to protect himself, so he approaches his guardian angel - a bad-tempered detective named Bunny Ryan. Bunny exploits his personal connection with the matron of Bridget's care home to call in a favour - a favour that will prove costly for him.

During Bridget and Paul's initial shift working together, the enigmatic comatose patient identified solely as 'Anonymous' regains consciousness and discloses he is a long-missing gangster possessing a concealed cache of Celtic gold. Could this treasure provide the solution to all of Bridget and Paul's problems? Will they gamble everything to locate it?

The series is yet to accumulate a substantial number of critical reviews, but viewers have been quick to share their enthusiasm online.

One fan posted: "This show is a black comedy. Brilliant and a breath of fresh air. Likeable and loveable characters involved in crime with a 'Crime Ma'.

"I love Bridget with her wee book about her patients and a lovable AOD. Paul and Bunny hard hapless and loyal to their women. I hope there a second series as great fun, totally nuts."

Another wrote: "Refreshingly original, competently funny and a very likable cast. I didn't know what to expect going in and oh boy was I pleasantly surprised. The humour is on point, the story fun and entertaining and the both lead actors are amazing with a great cast all around. Definitely on my list of best shows of the year."

A third viewer commented: "This was a rollercoaster of a adventure so many ups and downs tied to the characters. Fabulous by all involved in creating this. Well done to the actors, thank you for making this so enjoyable."

Tall Tales and Murder is streaming on BBC iPlayer and RTE Player.
