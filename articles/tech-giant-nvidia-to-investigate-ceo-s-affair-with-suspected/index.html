Tech giant Nvidia to investigate CEO's affair with suspected Chinese spy

TheTools · 09.11.2026 05:32 · source · permalink

Summary

  • US tech giant Nvidia opens an independent investigation following reports its CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy

  • The company, one of the most valuable in the world, says its board has no reason to believe Nvidia's secrets or national security were compromised

  • US intelligence officials believe Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who had a close relationship with Huang, was a spy for Beijing, according to the New York Times

  • Huang admits to having been "unfaithful" to his wife and apologises for hurting her and their children

  • Nvidia, which is seen as central to making the hardware for emerging AI technologies, says Huang is committed to continuing to lead the company

  • The tech world has been stunned by the news, which could have enormous consequences, writes our tech and AI editor Zoe Kleinman

US tech giant Nvidia opens an independent investigation following reports its CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy

The company, one of the most valuable in the world, says its board has no reason to believe Nvidia's secrets or national security were compromised

US intelligence officials believe Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, who had a close relationship with Huang, was a spy for Beijing, according to the New York Times

Huang admits to having been "unfaithful" to his wife and apologises for hurting her and their children

Nvidia, which is seen as central to making the hardware for emerging AI technologies, says Huang is committed to continuing to lead the company

The tech world has been stunned by the news, which could have enormous consequences, writes our tech and AI editor Zoe Kleinman

Live Reporting

Edited by Peter Hoskins with reporting from Zoe Kleinman and Laura Bicker

  1. Huang had been asked to brief Congress on spy reportpublished at 05:19 GMT
    05:19 GMT

    Top Republicans in the House of Representatives last week asked Jensen Huang to brief Congress about the NYT report that US officials believe he had a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy.

    Three House Republican committee leaders also asked the tech boss to discuss Nvidia's "security protocols against foreign adversary espionage" in a closed-door meeting on 18 November.

    They asked that he accept the invitation or propose an alternative date by 11 November.

    "Recent reports raise significant national security concerns regarding the PRC's [China's] access to sensitive US technology and information," they said in their letter to Huang.

    "The issue of PRC infiltration and access to cutting-edge US technology is core to the investigative work of our three committees this Congress."

    The letter was signed by House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Brian Mast, Select Committee on China Chairman John Moolenaar, and House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence Chairman Rick Crawford.

  2. Spy claims come at sensitive moment ahead of Trump-Xi talks next weekpublished at 04:58 GMT
    04:58 GMT

    Laura Bicker
    China correspondent

    Beijing routinely dismisses allegations of targeted intelligence-gathering operations as politically motivated, and has rejected claims that it is involved in industrial espionage against Nvidia.

    Last week, Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the accusations unsubstantiated and "nothing but smears against China".

    The allegations also come at a particularly sensitive moment.

    Donald Trump has confirmed he will travel to China next week for a high-stakes summit with President Xi Jinping - a meeting that could help finalise a trade deal between the world's two largest economies and prevent a return to escalating tariffs. The current truce expires in January.

    Chinese officials are likely to view the timing of these accusations with suspicion. Beijing could portray the claims as an attempt to undermine delicate negotiations ahead of the summit.

    The dispute also touches on one of the most contentious issues in the US-China relationship: advanced technology.

    China has repeatedly called for Washington to lift export restrictions on Nvidia's advanced chips, which are critical for AI development. At the same time, it is investing heavily in domestic alternatives through technology giants such as Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance.

    The message from Beijing is that while US restrictions may slow China's technological ambitions, they will not stop them.

    And China enters next week's talks with leverage of its own, including its dominant position in the global supply of rare earth metals, which remain vital for everything from electric vehicles to advanced defence systems.

  3. Singapore business records show Huang Le links to Megaspeedpublished at 04:47 GMT
    04:47 GMT

    Tessa Wong
    Asia Digital Reporter

    I've just done a check of Singaporean business registration records, and found that a Chinese national named Huang Le was the managing director of Megaspeed between March and November 2023.

    As a reminder, Huang Le is the Chinese name of Alice Huang, the woman reported to be a Chinese spy who worked at Megaspeed and had an affair with Nvidia's Jensen Huang.

    A Singaporean man, Tan Yong Pong, is now listed as Megaspeed's director. Business records show he took over the company from Huang Le back in 2023.

    There have been no suggestions that Tan is involved with the most recent allegations of Chinese espionage.

    Megaspeed however is under investigation by the Singapore government regarding previous allegations that the company was circumventing US sanctions on the export of Nvidia's high-end chips.

    The BBC has contacted Megaspeed for comment.

  4. What we know about Megaspeedpublished at 04:10 GMT
    04:10 GMT

    Megaspeed is a data centre company based in Singapore that buys Nvidia chips.

    US authorities had investigated Megaspeed last year for allegedly helping Chinese firms bypass trade restrictions to access Nvidia chips.

    Megaspeed has again found itself in the spotlight after Nvidia announced it was investigating CEO Jensen Huang for allegedly having a relationship with a Chinese spy.

    The New York Times has identified the alleged Chinese spy as Alice Huang, a former executive of Megaspeed.

    Megaspeed, in a statement to The New York Times, said that “current leadership” takes the matter “extremely seriously and is committed to getting to the facts behind these allegations”.

    It said it has commissioned an internal investigation to be done by outside counsel.

    Megaspeed has bought $4.8 billion in Nvidia chips in the last two years, according to reports, making it one of Nvidia's biggest customers in South East Asia.

  5. Some Chinese social media users think this is just an affairpublished at 03:40 GMT
    03:40 GMT

    Fan Wang
    Live page reporter

    Chinese social media users woke up to a shock this morning when they saw reports that Jensen Huang had admitted to having an affair.

    Hashtag "Jensen Huang admits affair" has generated nearly 10 million views within just hours, while "The person Jensen Huang cheated with" and several other hashtags are also gaining traction.

    But as is often the case in tightly controlled China, there are already signs of censorship and, in some cases, self censorship.

    Although these topics are generating more engagement than most other hashtags, they remain relatively low on the trending chart.

    Chinese media is unsurprisingly muted on the spy allegation. Those outlets that did report on Huang's statement omitted mention of national security concerns, as well as accusations that Alice Huang is a Chinese spy.

    Many social media users appear to think this is no more than a marital scandal. "He wants to ge a divorce and protect some of his assets," one Weibo user wrote.

    "He just made a mistake every man could have made," wrote another, referencing a phrase used by movie star Jackie Chan when referencing his own extramarital affair in 1999.

    Some "self-media" accounts, which are not affiliated with state media, were not oblivious to the espionage allegation, though they stopped short of discussing it in details.

    "Is she a Chinese spy or American spy?" one user asked. "It's just political maneuvering," another user wrote.

  6. What is Nvidia?published at 03:15 GMT
    03:15 GMT

    US tech giant Nvidia is the world's most valuable publicly-traded company - worth some $4.5tn (£3.4tn) - and a leading provider of advanced computer chips.

    AI relies on large banks of servers, run on chips like these, to operate.

    Nvidia provides this technology to major firms such as OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, and Meta, and has grown amid the AI boom.

    Its chips have been a major focus of the rivalry between the US and China, who are locked in an AI race - with America having long been concerned about Beijing's access to advanced computing technology.

    Washington has restricted exports of the most advanced Nvidia chips to Beijing since 2022, citing national security concerns and the risk that they could strengthen China's military operations - although there has been some loosening of restrictions under President Trump.

  8. Who is the suspected spy at the centre of Nvidia's investigation?published at 02:26 GMT
    02:26 GMT

    The New York Times (NYT) reported last month that US intelligence officials believe Alice Huang, who had a close relationship with the Nvidia boss, was a spy for Beijing.

    The paper reported that American intelligence agencies had been monitoring the Chinese businesswoman - who is of no relation to Jensen Huang - and concluded that she was acting on behalf of Beijing, citing unnamed sources.

    Surveillance reportedly revealed communications between the suspected spy and a US citizen, who was subsequently identified as Jensen Huang through a process known as "unmasking".

    Alice Huang has not responded to the paper's requests for comment.

    She is linked to Singapore-based data centre firm Megaspeed. The firm told the NYT last month its current leadership took the allegations seriously and had appointed external legal advisers to conduct an internal investigation.

  9. Jensen Huang is a 'fine person' who has done 'incredible job', says Trumppublished at 02:07 GMT
    02:07 GMT

    US President Donald Trump says he has not spoken to the Nvidia boss since news of the investigation emerged.

    "I just know he's a fine person, but you never know what's going on," Trump told reporters late on Sunday, adding that he had "done an amazing job for our country".

    The New York Times reported in late October that Trump had been briefed last year on intelligence alleging Alice Huang was spying on China's behalf.

    Asked by reporters whether he now wished he had "acted on the intelligence" at the time, Trump responded: "No, I don't think so. I think he's an honourable guy."

    China has previously rejected allegations of an espionage operation targeting the Nvidia chief, describing the claims as "groundless speculation".
