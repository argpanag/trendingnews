Teale Phelps Bondaroff aims for 2nd term on Saanich council - islandsocialtrends.ca

TheTools · 17.10.2026 03:59 · source · permalink

Teale is widely known across Saanich for his leadership in many initiatives.

Friday October 16, 2026 | SAANICH, BC

Political news feature & analysis | by Mary P Brooke | Island Social Trends

The energy and momentum that Teale Phelps Bondaroff brings to municipal politics in Saanich is hard to miss.

He’s been a municipal councillor on District of Saanich council for one term (2022-2026) and is hoping to achieve success for another term in the October 17 election this coming weekend. 

In his grassroots campaign, Teale has been chatting with a lot of people on the doorstep and the conversations have been excellent, he reports. “It’s hard to pin down a single topic or even a couple of key topics, as conversations have covered a wide range of subjects,” says Phelps Bondaroff who has an infectious way of stirring up discussion on many issues that are important to communities.

He does more than sit on Saanich council. He heads up or has instigated a number of active community initiatives including getting more doctors in Saanich, activating little street-side book boxes (little libraries) across the Greater Victoria area, and making strides with an organization that supports free contraception (something that found success provincially through the BC NDP government).

Bondaroff officially launched his campaign in a local Saanich park on July 11. Since then, on the campaign trail, the most prominent discussions have been about the need for primary health care, affordability and the cost of living, traffic and transportation, and housing.

One of the key elements of the Teale Bondaroff for council platform is supporting strong and healthy communities. He has found that many of the people he talks to are not attached to primary care.

“This term, I worked hard to explore ways Saanich might be able to encourage better access to healthcare services for our residents. This included things like density bonusing and other incentives to get more clinics built, permissive tax exemptions for not-for-profit clinics, and advocacy towards the province,” says Bondaroff.

“One proposal that has been really resonating with folks on the doorstep is exploring the possibility of setting up a municipal clinic, along the lines of Colwood, Langford, View Royal, and Esquimalt,” says Bondaroff, even though those four municipalities have taken different approaches to the same problem. 

“Affordability is on everyone’s minds and this connects to both housing and transportation. Folks are concerned about growing traffic challenge and limited transportation options,” says Bondaroff.

“One of the solutions I’ve been talking about a lot is working to provide people with a wider range of safe transportation options including transit, walking,and cycling to help reduce traffic and transportation costs,” he notes, as fuel at the pump hovers around or exceed $2.00 per litre.

“We can really help with the cost of living situation by providing folks with fast, frequent, accessible, and reliable transit, and safe, convenient, and connected active transportation infrastructure,” says the District of Saanich second-term hopeful.

Housing also remains a major concern in Saanich, a suburban/rural community with a population of now about 115,000. “We’ve made progress towards tackling the housing crisis this term, but too many people are still struggling with high rents and few options when they need a home.”

“I’ve knocked on a lot of doors of folks who are struggling with difficult landlords or housing situations but who cannot move because there are no other options<,” says Bondaroff.

“This underscores to me the importance of continuing out work delivering a diverse range of housing options for folks close to amenities and transportation options,” he told Island Social Trends during the campaign. 

When not directly engaged in the community, Bondaroff is working as Director of Research for OceansAsia, a marine conservation organization dedicated to protecting our oceans and combatting wildlife crime. He is a lifelong resident of Saaanich, where he and his wife engage broadly in the community.

Bondaroff is one of 27 people seeking a seat on the 2026-2030 municipal council. His approach to politics is likely to appeal to those who think and care deeply about how Vancouver Island communities have unique needs for development, care and support.

Voting on October 17:

Saturday October 17 is the general municipal voting day across BC.

In the 2026 District of Saanich General Local Election eligible voters will cast their ballots to elect:

  • One (1) Mayor [4 candidates]
  • Eight (8) Councillors [27 candidates]
  • 4 CRD Directors
  • Two (2) School District 61 Trustees [4 candidates]
  • Two (2) School District 63 Trustees [4 candidates]
  • One (1) Public Opinion Question (non-binding): “Do you support Saanich and Victoria becoming one municipality?”
