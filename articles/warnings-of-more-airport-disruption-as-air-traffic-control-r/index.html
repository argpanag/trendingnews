TheTools · 09.09.2026 04:01 · source · permalink

Summary

  • There are warnings of further disruption after an issue with air traffic control saw hundreds of flights across the UK on Tuesday cancelled or delayed

  • Nats, the air traffic control provider, has said that recovery from the incident is "taking longer than hoped"

  • Heathrow, Manchester and East Midlands airports are among those that reported significant disruption, while airports in Scotland and Ireland have also experienced delays

  • Some airports say they are expecting a full schedule of flights to run on Wednesday, but warn of airports being busier due to rescheduled flights

  • Ryanair says 65,000 of its passengers have been delayed by up to eight hours, while British Airways warns of a "significant knock-on impact for customers and colleagues"

  • There's now a backlog of flights and aircraft across Europe in the wrong place - sorting all of this out will take time, writes our correspondent

    22:50 BST 8 September

    Tara Mewawalla
    Live reporter

    There are warnings of further disruption at airports after hundreds of flights were cancelled and delayed due to an issue with air traffic control.

    Nats - the air traffic control provider - has fixed the issue this evening, but said earlier that recovery is taking "longer than we had hoped".

    Europe's busiest airline, Ryanair, earlier reported that more than 65,000 passengers had been delayed by up to eight hours, and British Airways warned of "significant knock-on" impacts.

    Chief executive of Nats Martin Rolfe defends the body as "fit for purpose," but says there is a "huge backlog" of flights and passengers.

    Bristol Airport said flights are now arriving and departing and London Heathrow said arrivals had been suspended for the rest of the day "to ensure safety", but departures had resumed. Both said disruption was expected to continue

    Manchester Airport and East Midlands Airport both issued statements saying they expect a "full flight schedule" to operate tomorrow, though - again - disruption is expected for the rest of the day

  2. Nats is fit for purpose, says air traffic control bosspublished at 22:18 BST 8 September
    22:18 BST 8 September

    Katy Austin
    Transport correspondent

    Martin Rolfe, the chief executive of Nats, tells the BBC that his organisation's systems are fit for purpose, and has apologised for Tuesday's issue.

    Speaking to the BBC, he says that while the system was now operating normally, “of course, there's a huge backlog” of flights and passengers.

    When pushed on how long systems weren’t working properly, Rolfe wasn’t sure but agrees it was “probably” in the ballpark of six hours.

    Rolfe says the focus is "about recovery" and they are "having a full investigation" into what went wrong.

    Asked if an incident like this showed Nats’ systems aren't fit for purpose, he responds: “I don't think so, no."

    He says: “Whilst we never want these things to happen - and I absolutely apologise for the fact it has happened today - I do think we're fit for purpose."

    Asked if he is considering his position, Rolfe says: "I’m obviously focusing on making sure that we get this solved.

    "My focus today has been making sure the team's in the right place, we have the right resources, the technical issues are being investigated, and the recovery happens.

    "There'll be lots of time for that later, but that's not now,” he adds.

  13. Wizz Air brands air traffic control provider Nats 'not fit for purpose'published at 20:13 BST 8 September
    20:13 BST 8 September

    Wizz Air has branded air traffic control provider Nats "simply not fit for purpose in its current form" after a technical issue caused hundreds of cancelled flights.

    In a statement the airline says it is "extremely disappointed" that "another Nats technical failure" has caused widespread disruption.

    "A critical national infrastructure provider should not repeatedly bring the aviation system to a standstill," it says.

    It says "urgent reform is needed" and that airlines, passengers and airports "cannot continue to bear the consequences of recurring system failures".

    "We sincerely apologise to affected passengers, but today’s disruption originates from the UK air traffic control system and is entirely outside Wizz Air’s control," the statement concludes.

    Nats declined to comment on Wizz Air's statement when contacted by the BBC.

  20. British Airways says fault will have 'significant knock-on impact' for customerspublished at 18:45 BST 8 September
    18:45 BST 8 September

    British Airways says it has been informed by the air traffic controller that the "fault has been resolved" but due to the severity of the issue, there will be a "significant knock-on impact for customers and colleagues".

    A spokesperson for the airline says it was forced to cancel or divert more than 100 flights this afternoon, "inconveniencing tens of thousands of customers".

    This incident was "entirely out of our control and incredibly frustrating for our customers and colleagues," it says, with aircraft and crews ending up out of place.

    "We ask customers whose flights have been cancelled not to travel to the airport and to bear with us if they’re trying to get in touch, as our call centres are incredibly busy at the moment.

    "Customers due to travel today or tomorrow are able to make changes to their booking on ba.com," it adds.

We’ve been told that accommodation is being worked on, and we will be told through the BA app what the next steps are.

There is frustration from lots of passengers, and the coffee shop in the airport is filling up quickly with exhausted travellers.

For now, the wait continues.

British Airways says fault will have 'significant knock-on impact' for customerspublished at 18:45 BST 8 September
18:45 BST 8 September

British Airways says it has been informed by the air traffic controller that the "fault has been resolved" but due to the severity of the issue, there will be a "significant knock-on impact for customers and colleagues".

A spokesperson for the airline says it was forced to cancel or divert more than 100 flights this afternoon, "inconveniencing tens of thousands of customers".

This incident was "entirely out of our control and incredibly frustrating for our customers and colleagues," it says, with aircraft and crews ending up out of place.

"We ask customers whose flights have been cancelled not to travel to the airport and to bear with us if they’re trying to get in touch, as our call centres are incredibly busy at the moment.

"Customers due to travel today or tomorrow are able to make changes to their booking on ba.com," it adds.
