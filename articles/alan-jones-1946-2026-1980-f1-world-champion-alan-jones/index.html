Alan Jones: 1946-2026 | 1980 F1 World Champion Alan Jones

TheTools · 05.11.2026 07:30 · source · permalink

Posted in:

  • Australian GT
  • Obituaries

5 November 2026, 8:30 AM

News broke overnight in the UK of the passing of 1980 F1 World Champion Alan Jones MBE, just days after his 80th birthday.

Jones is best remembered for his long and successful Formula One career, which peaked in 1980 with a World Championship for Williams, fending off a near season-long challenge from Nelson Piquet’s Brabham and sealing it at the last F1 Grand Prix at Watkins Glen.

Those five Grand Prix wins formed part of a 12-race winning F1 career over 12 years, in a wide variety of machinery, including scoring the only Grand Prix win for Shadow at the Austrian Grand Prix in 1977.

Beyond that, though, there were regular outings in the World Sportscar Championship (between 1974 and 1986) in Chevron, Lola, Porsche, Jaguar and Toyota machinery, in touring cars and, notably, in a title-winning run in the Australian GT Championship in 1982.

That season saw Jones in imperious form, winning all nine races in a works-backed Porsche 935; the season was marked by several races where Jones went head-to-head with fellow Aussie legend Peter Brock in his Chevy Monza.

Jones also succeeded in Can-Am racing, emerging as the 1978 Champion with Carl Haas’s team; he’d later return to F1 with the Haas squad. He also finished runner-up in the supporting BMW Procar Championship in his F1 championship-winning year.

Two runs at Le Mans saw a best finish of sixth overall in 1984 in a Kremer Porsche 956B.

And there was a long association with Australian touring car racing too, as a driver and team owner. That run included two Bathurst 1000 podium finishes and race wins in Peter Jackson-run Ford Falcons in 1993 and 1994, finishing runner-up in the Championship in 1993 to teammate Glenn Seton. He won again in 1997 in the penultimate race of his team at Oran Park, again in a Falcon.

And there was success in the Bathurst 12 Hour too, back in its production car days, with second overall and a class win in 1992 in a BMW M5, followed by an overall win the following year in a Mazda RX7.

An absolutely no-BS character, ‘Jonesy’ sometimes perplexed the establishment but was adored by the fans, a sentiment that only grew in his later years as a TV commentator, where there were no holds barred in his analysis.

His passing was marked by a family statement issued by his wife via Motorsport Australia:

“We are extremely saddened to announce the passing of our beloved Alan, who passed away on 5 November following a short illness.

“He will be dearly missed and fondly remembered by those closest to him, as well as the many fans whose support he was always grateful for.

“Alan dedicated his life to motorsport, building a legacy that will live on through the countless people he touched along the way. Through his achievements, passion and unmistakable character, both on and off the track, Alan left an enduring mark on the sport both nationally and across the globe.

“As a family, we would like to take this opportunity to thank everyone who was part of Alan’s journey for the many years of friendship, love and support. He was incredibly fortunate to have so many wonderful people around him.”

Motorsport Australia President Alan Fraser said:

“Alan Jones was one of the most significant figures in Australian motorsport history, whose achievements on the world stage brought immense pride to our nation.

“Alan’s place in the history of Australian motorsport is assured, having become the second Australian to win the Formula 1 World Championship in 1980.

“With 12 Grand Prix victories and a career that spanned the highest levels of international and domestic competition, his was a record built on determination and a fierce and fearless competitive resolve.

“Alan was a singular character, formidable on and off the race-track.

“His contribution to our sport will be remembered for generations, and we extend our deepest sympathies to his family and loved ones.”

Alan is survived by his wife, Amanda, and his children, Christian, Amber, Zara, Jack, Camilla and Emma.
