Orange County Increases Mosquito Surveillance, Encourages Residents to Take Preventive Action

TheTools · 04.09.2026 22:10 · source · permalink

Following two recent health advisories from the Florida Department of Health in Orange County, one regarding increased West Nile virus activity and another confirming a locally acquired case of dengue, Orange County Mosquito Control has increased surveillance and targeted mosquito treatments in affected areas to help protect residents.

Those efforts will continue until surveillance data indicate the elevated risk has passed.

While Orange County crews work to reduce mosquito populations, officials say one of the most effective forms of mosquito control begins at home.

“Our team is actively monitoring mosquito activity throughout the County and responding quickly where increased populations or mosquito-borne illnesses have been identified,” said Steve Harrison, manager of Orange County Mosquito Control. “Targeted treatments are an important part of protecting public health, but residents are our most valuable partners. The simple steps people take around their homes can make a tremendous difference.”

Mosquitoes lay their eggs in standing water, and it takes very little for them to multiply. In fact, as little as a teaspoon of water left standing for more than a week can become a breeding site.

  • Inspect their property at least once a week and empty or cover anything that can collect water, including birdbaths, buckets, pet dishes, children’s pools, coolers, tarps, gutters, tires, recycling bins and outdoor toys.
  • Use an EPA-approved insect repellent according to label directions.
  • Wear protective clothing whenever possible. Clothing and outdoor gear may also be treated with products containing 0.5% permethrin, although permethrin should never be applied directly to the skin.
  • Keep windows and doors closed when possible.
  • Ensure window screens fit properly and repair any holes that could allow mosquitoes inside.

“Mosquito control is a shared responsibility,” asserted Harrison. “When residents eliminate standing water, use insect repellent and report mosquito concerns, they’re helping protect not only their own families, but their entire neighborhood. Working together is the best way to reduce mosquito populations and lower the risk of mosquito-borne illnesses.”

Residents experiencing mosquito problems at their homes or within their neighborhoods are encouraged to report them by calling 407-836-3111 or 3-1-1.
