Record income for Mayo GAA after All-Ireland winning year

TheTools · 19.11.2026 17:17 · source · permalink

Mayo GAA's sensational 2026 was not confined to the pitch as the Connacht county has welcomed record income and profits off the back of a first All-Ireland SFC title since 1951.

Mayo's 2026 financial report, covering 12 months to the end of September, makes for fine reading for the county with net profits seeing a massive rise of 212% to €1.42m off revenue of €6.3m.

It leaves Mayo in rude financial health with a balance sheet that stands at €6.8m at year-end, including €3.3m of cash reserves.

As a result, all clubs in the county are set to receive a €2,500 appreciation grant while a 'One Mayo' initiative will be launched next year to investigate further ways to offer financial support to clubs from fundraising proceeds.

Commercial income (€1.74m), fundraising income (€1.49m), gate receipts and matchday income (€800,000) were also up on the previous year, significantly so for the first two.

Total income from the GAA's central council, the Allianz Football League and provincial allocations amounted to €1.6m in 2026 – a 43% increase on the previous year.

Expenditure for 2026 rose by 30% year-on-year to reach €4.9m. This was primarily driven by a sharp increase in team preparation costs to €2.2m as a result of that extended All-Ireland campaign.

During the year, the county also completed an €800,000 investment on the development of an all-weather training pitch at Mulvey Park, which is adjacent to Hastings Insurance MacHale Park in Castlebar. This capital project was completed out of cash reserves and no bank debt was required.

Mayo GAA treasurer Valerie Murphy said that their 1-20 to 1-17 final win over Kerry was key to the positive numbers.

"The past year has been an unprecedented 12 months for Mayo GAA on all fronts. Winning our first All-Ireland Senior Football title (since 1951) in July was a hugely significant moment for the county and the people of Mayo.

"That historic result has also helped our county to achieve a record financial performance in 2026 thanks to very strong growth in commercial revenues and fundraising income.

"The current strength of Mayo GAA’s finances means we are in a position to continue investing in our teams and young players coming through, while also moving ahead with some significant infrastructure projects such as our new centre of excellence facility.

"With a multi-year strategic investment plan well underway, our goal in Mayo GAA is to continue growing the county’s commercial operations and to build on the success of this year’s historic All-Ireland win."

Other factors attributed to the record profits were fundraising events in the UK and America - Andy Moran and Colm Boyle visited New York, Chicago and London with county board officials - while Mayo GAA anticipate that increased attendances at the club championships in the wake of Mayo's Sam Maguire success will be reflected in next year's financial report.

Looking ahead, work at the county's planned centre of excellence in Bohola is due to begin early 2027 with Lally Chartered Engineers appointed to lead the design team.

Having hosted the rugby inter-pro meeting of Connacht and Munster at Hastings Insurance MacHale Park in March 2025, Mayo GAA are also keen to explore further "opportunities for similar sporting or musical events in the future".
