Volatility gripped the stock market last week amid concerns about the sustainability of the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure spending boom. The S&P 500 experienced a modest decline, but it continues to trade at a historically expensive valuation, so strong corporate earnings will be necessary to keep this bull market going.

As a result, all eyes are on the current reporting season. Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) will release its operating results for its fiscal 2027 first quarter (ended Sept. 30) on Oct. 28, and Wall Street will be squarely focused on the Azure cloud platform and Copilot virtual assistant, which are central to the company's AI strategy.

Microsoft stock is attractively valued heading into the report, so here's why I predict it will climb in the weeks and months that follow.

Positioned to lead the enterprise AI race

AI is a powerful tool in an enterprise because it can dramatically improve productivity by automating tasks, analyzing large volumes of data, and even generating content like images and computer code. Microsoft is helping businesses tap into the power of AI through Copilot and Azure, and both represent enormous opportunities for the company.

The Copilot assistant is embedded in Microsoft's legacy software products like Windows and Edge for free. But enterprises can also add it to the 365 productivity suite (which includes Word and Excel) for an additional monthly subscription fee. As of June 30, organizations worldwide were paying for 30 million Copilot licenses for 365, a staggering 50% increase from just three months earlier.

However, organizations pay for more than 400 million 365 licenses for their employees, so Copilot adoption is still in the very early stages. Investors should keep an eye out for some updated numbers on Oct. 28.

Then there is the Azure cloud platform, which provides every tool enterprises need to deploy AI software applications. That includes computing capacity from specialized data centers, more than 11,000 ready-made foundation models, and a platform called Foundry where they can design and manage custom AI agents.

Microsoft built 88 new data centers during fiscal 2026 as part of a two-year plan to double its global infrastructure footprint. This will help Azure convert its enormous $678 billion in remaining performance obligations (RPO) into revenue, which effectively represents the platform's order backlog from customers who are waiting for more infrastructure to come online.
