Timberwolves loss gives Blazers further evidence it’s time to trade Scoot Henderson

TheTools · 18.11.2026 22:10 · source · permalink

The Portland Trail Blazers lost a heartbreaker Tuesday, falling 106-105 to the Minnesota Timberwolves. Many will point to Donovan Clingan's failed inbound pass, but Portland wouldn't have been in that late-game situation if they had played better. One player in particular they needed more production from was Scoot Henderson, whose struggles have only gotten worse as the season has progressed.

Tuesday's loss was especially a chance for Henderson to step up as a playmaker with Deni Avdija sidelined with a foot injury. Unfortunately, it was more of the same underwhelming production: nine points, one assist, one turnover on 4-of-13 shooting from the field and 0-of-4 from beyond the arc.

Scoot Henderson's struggles are holding the Blazers back

This season, Henderson is averaging a career-low 8.7 points, 1.8 rebounds, 1.4 assists, and 2.0 turnovers per game. The playmaking isn't there for a point guard, yet the floor spacing isn't reliable enough in an off-ball role.

Henderson's stats may be down because of his diminished role in this offensive hierarchy, but even then, that should lead to easier looks and increased efficiency. Instead, he's now shooting 31.7 percent from the field and 20.0 percent from beyond the arc.

It's now been 14 games, which is becoming a large enough sample size to say Henderson's struggles are holding this team back. Head coach Micah Nori realized that, to an extent, when he swapped Henderson for Sidy Cissoko in the starting lineup, and the Blazers have been better off because of it.

But with so many injuries in the backfield at the start of the season, Portland is still relying on Henderson more than they'd like in that 20-plus minute role off the bench. If these struggles continue, Nori may have to consider getting that even below 20 minutes per game when key pieces like Ja Morant and Shaedon Sharpe return.

Blazers have to consider trading Scoot Henderson at this point

The Blazers will have to seriously consider moving on from their former No. 3 overall pick for good. GM Joe Cronin seems to have made the right decision not to extend Henderson before the season with the level he's playing at. Cronin did previously call Henderson a "high priority" player, adding that Portland will have another opportunity to retain him next offseason. But Henderson certainly isn't playing like a high-priority player in Portland, and in fact he's becoming a liability.

A combo guard who can't effectively create and can't consistently space the floor is essentially just out there doing cardio. The defense has improved to his credit, and it does help Nori's offensive system to have another ballhandler out there. But that system largely works because all of Portland's backcourt pieces are good decision-makers with the ball in their hands, too. That lets the Blazers move the ball in half-court or initiate the fast break with multiple weapons. It doesn't work as well with Henderson's limitations at this stage of his career. He still struggles to process the game at NBA speed, as someone who plays like he's constantly five steps ahead of his dribble.

Henderson is 22 and can still improve these key aspects of his game, but it's looking like that window may no longer align with the Blazers, who have accelerated their timeline after making the postseason. With his contract now set to expire and Portland facing a restricted free agency dilemma, a trade before February's deadline suddenly makes more sense than ever.
