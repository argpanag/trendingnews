Ranking the Top 30 prospects from the '26 Fall League campaign

TheTools · 21.11.2026 01:12 · source · permalink

A year ago, the Rangers planned for Sebastian Walcott to refine his bat path and defensive consistency during six weeks in the Arizona Fall League, laying the groundwork for him to make his big league debut at age 20 in 2026. Instead, he played just one game before getting shut down with arm fatigue that was a precursor to internal brace surgery this February.

Walcott missed the first 3 1/2 months of this season before returning as a DH and raking at Double-A. He enjoyed his second trip to the Fall League much more than the first one, resuming playing shortstop and living up to his status as the highest-rated prospect on the developmental circuit. Ranked No. 8 on MLB Pipeline's Top 100 list, he headlines our annual appraisal of the AFL's best prospects.

This year's rankings include 14 Top 100 prospects but also four players not included on our Top 30 lists for their organizations. Another Top 100 prospect, Scottsdale shortstop Bryce Rainier (Tigers), didn't play enough to qualify and went 4-for-27 (.148) with 11 strikeouts. Scouts considered the crop of pitchers as the worst in Fall League history, with teams averaging 6.54 runs per game and only one arm appearing among the 19 best prospects below.

As always, these ratings are based more on long-term value than AFL performance, though both matter. Several scouts who track the league contributed to our evaluations.

1. Sebastian Walcott, SS, Surprise (TEX No. 1/MLB No. 8)
Walcott's two loudest tools are his double-plus raw power and arm strength. The latter was more evident, as he boasted the Fall League's best infield arm, and he also posted some of the best exit velocities despite homering just once in 16 games while batting .303/.351/.455. He can get aggressive at the plate, though his pop overshadows solid hitting ability.

2. Eduardo Quintero, OF, Glendale (LAD No. 2/MLB No. 15)
Quintero turned in one of the more baffling performances, controlling the strike zone and stealing seven bases in 17 games but batting .154/.375/.173. He's still a five-tool center fielder with a .305/.432/.495 line in four pro seasons, hence his ranking, but he looked tired for much of the fall season.

3. Welbyn Francisca, SS/2B, Surprise (CLE No. 19)
Francisca went 5-for-5 on Opening Day and never let up, leading the AFL in batting (.427), slugging (.733), OPS (1.227), hits (32), total bases (55) and RBIs (29) to capture MVP honors. He's a switch-hitter with at least solid power and speed, and he offers more defensive value than the Top 100 prospects right behind him on this list, with a better chance to stick at shortstop than Arjun Nimmala and at least as good a shot as Tate Southisene.

4. Caleb Bonemer, OF, Glendale (CWS No. 2/MLB No. 27)
Bonemer led the Minors with 69 extra-base hits and ranked fifth with 37 homers this season while reaching Double-A at age 20, then slashed .286/.364/.633 with three homers in 13 games in Arizona. He's a capable shortstop and a better third baseman than Aidan Miller, but he played center field this fall because the White Sox are in more immediate need of outfield help. While he's destined for a corner, he has the power to become a star in left or right field.

5. Aidan Miller, 3B, Surprise (PHI No. 2/MLB No. 59)
After back issues prevented him from playing in the Fall League last year and limited him to four regular-season games in 2026, Miller batted .327/.557/.481 and topped the circuit in on-base percentage and walks (25 in 16 games). He's a complete offensive player with sneaky basestealing savvy and he played a steady third base after spending his entire career at shortstop.

6. Arjun Nimmala, SS, Mesa (LAA No. 1/MLB No. 34)
Nimmala continues to display plus power and improve his approach at the plate, and he hit .304/.455/.519 with six steals in 20 games. He's slowing down as he fills out, which likely will prompt a move from shortstop to third base, where his pop and strong arm will profile well.

7. Tate Southisene, SS, Glendale (ATL No. 3/MLB No. 58)
Southisene is similar to Nimmala, with less power and track record but more quickness and a better bet to remain at shortstop. After reaching Double-A at age 19 during the Minor League season, he batted .345/.500/.418 with eight steals in 17 games and more walks (16) than strikeouts (12).

8. Angeibel Gomez, OF, Surprise (KC No. 2/MLB No. 76)
The second-youngest player in league history, Gomez made the jump from the Rookie-level Dominican Summer League to the AFL at age 17 and showed the potential to develop into a plus hitter with power to match by slashing .311/.415/.444. There are varying reports on his speed, ranging from solid to well above average, and scouts who are convinced he'll become at least a plus runner and stay in center field would rank him several spots higher on this list.

9. Jarlin Susana, RHP, Salt River (WSH No. 4/MLB No. 66)
The AFL Pitcher of the Year, Susana unleashed the 65 hardest pitches in the league, sitting at 101 mph and topping out at 104 with his fastball. He backed up his heat with a wipeout 85-88 mph slider that generated swings-and-misses at a 68 percent clip and topped the circuit in strikeouts (26 in 18 2/3 innings) and groundout/airout ratio (3.4). He'll need to improve his changeup, overall control and fastball command to become a frontline starter, but his floor looks to be as a dominant closer.

10. Tai Peete, OF, Peoria (STL No. 17)
The Fall League's most electric player and Offensive Player of the Year, Peete hit .351/.441/.662, set an AFL record by tripling in four consecutive games, paced the circuit with 20 steals and placed second in the Home Run Derby. He has exceptional bat speed, well-above-average foot speed and plus raw power and arm strength. He comes with chase and swing-and-miss concerns, but if he can maintain his 18 percent strikeout rate from this fall, he'll be a star.

11. Dax Kilby, SS, Salt River (NYY No. 1/MLB No. 78)
At age 19, Kilby showed off one of the league's most polished left-handed swings and advanced approaches, which translated into a .275/.350/.565 line with eight steals in 18 games. He's a future 20-20 player, though his average arm likely will dictate a move from shortstop to second base or center field.

12. Nathan Flewelling, C, Scottsdale (TB No. 4/MLB No. 63)
Like Kilby, Flewelling stood out as a lefty-swinging teenager with a promising bat. His .309/.423/.346 line belies his 25-homer upside, though he'll need to make more consistent contact to get there. He's athletic yet raw behind the plate, with a chance to stick at catcher but also the possibility of becoming a corner outfielder.

13. Zyhir Hope, OF, Scottsdale (DET No. 1/MLB No. 26)
The headline prospect in the Tarik Skubal trade this summer, Hope wasn't as dynamic as he was in his first Fall League stint in 2024. He batted .241/.405/.293 and looked fatigued, but he's still primed to hit for plenty of power while offering average-to-solid speed and quality defense on the outfield corners.

14. Zion Rose, OF, Surprise (KC No. 6)
Rose went No. 6 overall in the 2026 Draft because he accepted a below-slot deal, but he's also a talented player who hit .322/.494/.508 with 14 steals in 16 games in Arizona. He combines advanced bat-to-ball skills with plus raw pop and flashes of well-above-average speed once underway, though he's a so-so defender in left field.

15. Ike Irish, OF/1B, Peoria (BAL No. 5/MLB No. 95)
Irish is similar to Rose, not as athletic but having the advantage of batting left-handed. He controls the strike zone well and hits balls hard to all fields, producing a .328/.439/.507 line with five steals in 18 games. He's still learning the outfield after catching for much of his amateur career but should be a capable defender in right.

16. Charles Davalan, 2B, Glendale (LAD No. 6/MLB No. 79)
Though he underperformed (.221/.325/.353), Davalan is an efficient left-handed hitter with sneaky power and solid speed. An outfielder during his Draft year in 2025 and his first full pro season, he manned second base in Arizona and should be able to handle the keystone as he gets more experience there.

17. Chase Harlan, 3B, Glendale (LAD No. 9)
Harlan showcased some of the best raw power in the Fall League while slashing .290/.408/.581 with five homers in 17 games, and he crushes balls without overswinging. He does struggle against non-fastballs (40 percent swing-and-miss rate in Arizona) and needs to tighten up his consistency at third base.

18. Demetrio Crisantes, 2B, Salt River (AZ No. 1/MLB No. 84)
Crisantes uses a simple right-handed swing and nifty hand-eye coordination to make repeated contact, enabling him to bat .312 in four pro seasons and .286/.419/.443 with nine steals in 19 games and more walks (16) than strikeouts (10) in the Fall League. He doesn't hit balls especially hard or stand out with his speed or defense, so his floor is more notable than his ceiling.

19. Dante Nori, OF, Surprise (PHI No. 7)
Nori, who batted .271/.421/.441 with five steals in 17 games, ranks No. 19 on this list for the second straight fall. Once again, he showed some of the best speed in the Fall League and looked like a potential leadoff man with gap power and good center-field defense.

20. Miguel Mendez, RHP, Scottsdale (SF No. 16)
Mendez recorded a spectacular playoff start with six strikeouts over four no-hit innings after logging a 4.91 ERA with 22 K's in 14 2/3 frames during the AFL regular season. He misses bats with a 96-98 mph fastball that reaches 101 with explosive carry and run, a wicked mid-80s slider and a lively upper-80s changeup. He lacks polish and comes with reliever risk, but it's a terrific arm.

21. Briggs McKenzie, LHP, Glendale (ATL No. 8)
The results (6.53 ERA, albeit with 25 strikeouts in 20 2/3 innings) may not have been what McKenzie wanted. He's still a projectable left-hander who dodges bats with his four-seam fastball and generates weak contact with his two-seamer, which both park in the low 90s and top out at 95. His low-80s slider features plenty of horizontal action yet got roughed up by Fall League hitters, while his strike-throwing should improve as he adds strength.

22. Luke Holman, RHP, Peoria (CIN No. 23)
The AFL's most efficient pitcher, Holman led the circuit in ERA (0.69) and average-against (.114), fanned 20 in 13 innings and won the championship game with five shutout frames. While he's not overpowering, he's a high-floor starter who commands his low-90s fastball well and backs it up with a pair of effective breaking balls, an upper-70s curve and a mid-80s slider.

23. Wyatt Sanford, SS, Glendale (PIT No. 5)
Known more for his glove when Pittsburgh made him a second-round pick out of a Texas high school in 2024, Sanford began hitting balls significantly harder this season. He has traded some contact for power and needs to find a happy medium after hitting just .268/.366/.380 with five steals in 18 games in Arizona, but he's a quality athlete and defender with 20-20 upside.

24. Patrick Reilly, RHP, Peoria (BAL)
Reilly led the Fall League in strikeouts per nine innings (14.4) while ranking second in K's (25) and fourth in ERA (2.87), thanks to a mid-90s fastball with big carry and a sharp mid-80s slider. He still has to prove his control and command are consistent enough to make it as a starter, but at worst he should be a weapon as a reliever.

25. Justin Lamkin, LHP, Surprise (KC No. 8)
One of the league's more polished left-handers, Lamkin compiled a 3.86 ERA, an AFL-best 0.92 WHIP and 14 strikeouts in 16 2/3 innings. He compensates for a lack of velocity on a low-90s fastball with movement and command, and he has nice feel for an upper-70s curveball, low-80s slider and mid-80s changeup.

26. Vance Honeycutt, OF, Peoria (BAL)
The AFL Defensive Player of the Year, Honeycutt arguably had the best all-around tools in the league and built on a late-season surge at Double-A by slashing .361/.507/.522 with three homers and nine steals in 18 games. He led the Fall League with a 32 percent walk rate while cutting his strikeout percentage from 40 in three years in pro ball to 25 in Arizona. That's a promising sign for a guy with well-above-average raw power and speed, not to mention a reputation as the best defensive center fielder in the Minors.

27. Carlos Sanchez, SS, Peoria (CIN No. 14)
One of the more underrated Fall Leaguers, Sanchez did a little bit of everything while batting .300/.425/.571 with five homers and eight steals in 20 games. He could be a 15-15 guy who's capable of playing all over the infield and outfield.

28. Kyle DeBarge, 2B/3B/CF, Salt River (MIN)
After struggling at Double-A, DeBarge rallied to hit .347/.480/.653 with five homers and nine steals in 21 games while exhibiting his versatility by starting games at second base, third base, center field and shortstop. He possesses plus speed and has a steep right-handed stroke that enables him to get the most out of his fringy raw power.

29. Owen Carey, OF, Glendale (ATL No. 21)
The lowest-drafted player (15th round, 2024) on this list, Carey doesn't have loud tools but he projects to have at least average hitting ability along with similar power and speed. Best suited for the outfield corners, he batted .328/.432/.492 with the second-best strikeout rate (10 percent) in the Fall League.

30. Kevin Bazzell, C, Salt River (WSH)
One of the better all-around catchers in the AFL, Bazzell has a good approach and average power that resulted in a .341/.500/.591 line and a league-best 7 percent strikeout rate. He gets the job done behind the plate and has a strong arm.
