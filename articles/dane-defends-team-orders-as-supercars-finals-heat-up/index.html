Dane defends team orders as Supercars Finals heat up

TheTools · 06.11.2026 03:13 · source · permalink

By Andrew Clarke

Date posted: 6 November 2026

Roland Dane has defended team orders in Supercars, declaring that attempts to eliminate them from motorsport are naive and arguing that teams have every right to act in their own interests.

The PremiAir Racing boss addressed the issue at Sandown following renewed debate over team tactics during the championship’s elimination-based Finals series.

Dane says the regulations distinguish between a team managing its own drivers and outside parties attempting to manipulate race results.

“Look, a team is entitled to do what it wants in its own team. The rules are very clear, and anyone who thinks you can eliminate team orders within a team in motorsport is naive,” Dane said.

He pointed to the Gold Coast, where Ryan Wood and Chaz Mostert’s late position change highlighted how championship considerations could influence the running order.

“You can’t stop, for instance, if Chaz Mostert saw on the big screen, and he’d obviously know the equation, that it was advantageous to back off, even if the team told him not to.

“How can you stop him doing it? Let’s be realistic about it.”

Why the team orders rule exists

Dane explained that the existing Supercars regulations were primarily intended to prevent outside manipulation, rather than teams managing the relative positions of their own cars.

“The rule that exists there is to stop manipulation by outside people, and particularly manufacturers, but not only,” he said.

“It could be the sponsors across teams out there as well.

“That’s why the rule was written as a direct result of what happened at Phillip Island in 2006.”

He acknowledged that team orders were not always popular with spectators but argued that they have been part of professional motor racing for decades.

“Was it always great to see? No, it isn’t,” Dane said.

Ryan backs the wingman approach

Erebus Motorsport boss Barry Ryan agreed, recalling how Brodie Kostecki and Will Brown worked together during their 2023 championship campaign.

“There were plenty of times in ’23 when Brodie and Will worked together because one was on fresher tyres or vice versa,” Ryan said.

“They go back and take that position if there’s not cars in between. There was a lot of that in 2023, and it worked really well with those two drivers and our team.

“You’ve got to have a wingman if you can.”

Ryan also referenced the pressure Wood applied to Broc Feeney during last year’s championship battle, arguing that drivers can influence outcomes without breaking the rules.

But both team bosses rejected the idea of sacrificing their own results to assist another team aligned with the same manufacturer.

“If we’re up for a result, we’re going to take every result we can get,” Ryan said.

“Like Anton, we’re aligned with GM, but we’re not going to do anything that’s going to sacrifice our results. Definitely not.”

Dane said PremiAir would take a similar approach.

“We’ll be racing for ourselves as a team,” he said.

“We’ll be respectful of the people who are taking part in this final thing … and we’ll be respectful of all the people in it, in so much as you can be when you’re running your own program.”

With the elimination format increasing the importance of individual positions and points, the role of teammates is likely to remain a talking point throughout the Sandown weekend.
