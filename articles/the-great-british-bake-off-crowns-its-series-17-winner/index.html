The Great British Bake Off crowns its series 17 winner

TheTools · 24.11.2026 21:54 · source · permalink

The winner of The Great British Bake Off has said they were "in shock" after they were crowned as the winner of the 17th series of the hit show on Tuesday evening.

Warning: Spoiler alert - this article contains the results of the final of The Great British Bake Off

22-year-old Moyin Odeniran from Essex described it as a "very busy time for me" as he juggled competing in the Channel 4 baking competition with completing his university studies.

Despite his dissertation being due a month after filming wrapped, Odeniran achieved a first in his political economy degree at King's College London.

He beat fellow finalists Tom Whittaker and Mo Mahir to the title.

He said: "I don’t know how that happened, I don’t know how I got here. I can’t believe it; I am in shock.

"I have made friends for life and I have just loved every single second of it, and I don’t think I could have asked for anything more."

After witnessing Odeniran’s jubilant celebrations with his family and friends, judge Paul Hollywood said he had "never seen anything like it", adding: "It was pure magic!"

"He has done an amazing job because out of all the bakers he struggled at the beginning, and he actually got better and better," he said.

Fellow judge Nigella Lawson praised Odeniran as a "worthy winner" but also described the other finalists as "sensational".

The high-stakes final saw the three contestants tasked with making a citrus celebration cake, crème brûlée donuts and a "spectacular tribute to iconic British landscapes".

Reflecting on his performance in the final, Odeniran said: "I didn’t want to put too much pressure on myself, but I knew I had put my best foot forward.

"I couldn’t have done anything better than I did - and I had a great time doing it."

He said he got "quite exhausted" while filming the series and praised his mother, sister and close friends, adding: "They were all a great support system."

Odeniran said: "I always hoped I would get to the final and then I surprised myself as the weeks went through and thought, 'Yes - I might have a chance’.

"Earlier on, some of the challenges were quite difficult for me and I was shocked that I kept going through.

"Before you get there you think you know how to bake, and by week two you question whether you know anything about baking."

During the series, Odeniran was named star baker during caramel week and won the technical challenge in week six for his Polish karpatka, a custard-filled choux pastry dessert.

He also received the first Hollywood handshake of the series alongside his mother after they made a baked cheesecake together during the signature challenge in week three.

"That will be a moment we will remember for the rest of our lives," he said.

Reflecting on what he learned from the competition, Odeniran said: "I think the biggest thing for me throughout was the learning and listening every single day to the advice from Nigella and Paul.

"So, you improved each time, taking little steps, and that gave you more confidence."

He added: "What was important to me was that you were appreciated as the person you are and allowed to be free.

"I will never forget my time there and the friendships made which will always continue."

He also said his dream would be to become "one of the best bakers in the world", but in the meantime he will continue baking and "just see what happens for me out there".
