Léa Seydoux Says Young Actors May Lack The “Gravitas” For James Bond, Recalls Secrecy Around Daniel Craig’s Death

TheTools · 20.11.2026 19:16 · source · permalink

The ‘No Time To Die’ actress reflects on Daniel Craig’s emotional depth, the secrecy surrounding his final film, and the challenge of finding the next 007.

Few roles in Hollywood carry as much baggage or scrutiny as James Bond. With every new incarnation comes the inevitable debate over who should wear the tuxedo and whether the next actor can measure up to his predecessor. As the franchise prepares for its next chapter under Denis Villeneuve, Léa Seydoux, who starred opposite Daniel Craig in both “Spectre” and “No Time To Die,” has some thoughts about what the next Bond needs—and why younger actors might struggle to fill his shoes.

Speaking with Deadline’s The Actor’s Side, Seydoux was asked who she thought might make a good successor to Craig. While she stopped short of suggesting any particular actor, she offered a pointed assessment of what the role demands.

“I think it’s difficult to find a James Bond,” she said. “It’s not easy to find the right actor because I think he needs to be layered, you know, needs to be someone who has, like, a certain maturity, right? And young actors today, they maybe lack, you know, they lack this gravitas.”

For Seydoux, Craig’s greatest strength was the emotional weight he brought to the character, something that distinguished his interpretation from previous incarnations of the British secret agent.

“That is something that Daniel did so well, you know, is that he really, like, there is a weight, you know, and I think it made the character more interesting.”

Her comments arrive just as Amazon MGM Studios has officially announced Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond. Seydoux presumably spoke with Deadline before the November 16 announcement, so her remarks shouldn’t be interpreted as criticism of Pattinson. Still, her emphasis on maturity and emotional depth offers an interesting counterpoint to Villeneuve’s own description of the qualities he wants in 007, including charisma, sensuality, and the believable presence of an assassin.

Seydoux also reflected on the extraordinary secrecy surrounding Craig’s farewell in 2021’s “No Time To Die,” particularly the decision to kill off James Bond. Although her character, Madeleine Swann, played a central role in the film’s emotional conclusion, she revealed that most of the people working on the film had no idea how the story would end.

“Yes, they told me the end. I was not allowed to say it,” Seydoux explained. “And some people in the crew didn’t know the end. I mean, most of the crew didn’t know.”

“We were only, like, a few to know the end,” she continued. “It was a big pressure, you know, for me because I knew that I was in the last scene. And actually, it made me really sad.”

Craig’s Bond became the first cinematic 007 to receive a definitive on-screen death, closing out a five-film arc increasingly concerned with his emotional scars and personal relationships. Seydoux’s Madeleine Swann gave Bond the prospect of a family and a life beyond espionage, even if he never got to enjoy it.
