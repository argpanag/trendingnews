Zverev clearly thinks he’s going all the way in this one if he’s already talking about playing all eight days. Confidence from the world No 1.

Zverev beats Jódar 6-4, 6-4!

Game, set and match thanks to another rapid service game from Zverev. He has given Jódar nothing in this one and earns match point with piercing volley at the net to causes the Spaniard to stumble. One unreturnanle serve later and it’s all over.

Second set: Zverev* 6-4, 5-4 Jódar (*next server)

Jódar races to 40-0 but a silly drop shot lets Zverev back in at 40-15. The Spaniard rights that wrong on the next point with a well-contructed rally and the world No 1 will have to serve this out.

Second set: Zverev 6-4, 5-3 Jódar* (*next server)

Three serves and a forehand get Zverev to 40-0, another huge first serve and Jódar is serving to stay in the match.

Second set: Zverev* 6-4, 4-3 Jódar (*next server)

Jódar hits his third ace of the match to go up 30-15 but the next point Zverev finds the corner with a forehand winner and when Jódar misses long with a forehand down the line it’s another break point. Jódar doesn’t hold back and saves it with a huge forehand winner that Zverev can’t get a glove on. Bad follows good and decent but not unplayable return is misshit by Jódar to cough up another break point. That’s saved but a double fault gives Zverev another break chance, but a good serve outwide shuts that down. Jódar finally closes his serve out, the 20-year-old is battling admirably here.

Second set: Zverev 6-4, 4-2 Jódar* (*next server)

Very little for Jódar to work with here. Brutal serving.

Second set: Zverev* 6-4, 3-2 Jódar (*next server)

It looks like we’re hading for a rare routine hold for Jódar, until Zverev whips a cross court forehand for a winner to get to 40-30 and then another winner on a weak second serve makes it deuce. Jódar shows some fight to get the next point and secures the hold when Zverev loses control of a forehand

Second set: Zverev 6-4, 3-1 Jódar* (*next server)

No issues for Zverev on serve until he gets 40-15 when Jódar sticks a return on a second serve and overpowers his opponent to get it back to 40-3-0. Normal service resumed on the next point, a hold.

Second set: Zverev* 6-4, 2-1 Jódar (*next server)

Zverev looks to be in control of the rally at 15-15 but another forehand miss sees him waving his arms around. There’s more posturing on the next point with a missed backhand down the line but Jódar lets Zverev back into it with a big miss at 40-15. Two points later it’s break point and after a long exchange of forehands Jódar is reeled into the net where he is ultimately outwitted. Zverev now a set and a break up.

Second set: Zverev 6-4, 1-1 Jódar* (*next server)

A rare point when Jódar takes charge on the Zverev serve gets it to 15-30 and sharp backhand return makes the German have to focus at 30-30. Two power blasts later and that’s the game.

Second set: Zverev* 6-4, 0-1 Jódar (*next server)

Early chance to get a break for Zverev at 15-30 but an unforced error lets Jódar off the hook. Jódar’s box only has his dad in it and he looks unruffled as his son closes out the game.

Zverev wins first set 6-4!

A mistake from Zverev at 15 all gives Jódar a chance and then a wonderful return on the next point gives him the chance to break back at 15-40. An ace kills one chance and the next is evisverated by a huge first serve that is unreturnable for Jódar. Another ace brings Zverev set point and even his second serve proves to be too much. First set done in 40 minutes.

First set: Zverev* 5-4 Jódar (*next server)

First ace for Jódar sees him go up 30-15. He needs more cheap points like that if he is to find a way back into this one. Zverev seemed less tenacious in that game, probably knowing he’ll be serving for the set next.

First set: Zverev 5-3 Jódar* (*next server)

Shall I just repeat my entry from Zverev’s last service game? This was almost identical, 17 of 18 first serves in and eight aces now.

First set: Zverev* 4-3 Jódar (*next server)

The contrast between how hard Jódar is working on his serve to Zverev is huge. There’s a double digit rally just to make it 15-15 and then a tough volley to go up 30-15. The next point is dominated by Zverev and when Jódar misses down the line at 30-30 he coughs up more break points. This time Zverev turns the screw in the rally and finally makes the pass to take charge of the first set.

First set: Zverev 3-3 Jódar* (*next server)

Zverez has already nailed seven aces and missed one first serve. Tough for Jódar to get a foothold when the serving is this good from the other end.

First set: Zverev* 2-3 Jódar (*next server)

Zverez goes up 0-15 when Jódar goes wayward with a forehand but the Spaniard battles back gamely to make 30-15. A piercing early backhand from Zverev makes it 30-30 and we have our first sniff of danger for Jódar. An unforced error from Jódar gives Zverev his first break point but a loose forehand, loopy and long, from the German is a gift to get back to deuce. Zverev gets another break chance when Jódar goes long with a cross court backhand, but a steely second serve and follow-up backhand see off the danger. Zverev can only find the net with his return on the next point and Jódar escapes with hold after wicked approach pins his opponent on the baseline.

First set: Zverev 2-2 Jódar* (*next server)

Routine hold for the world No 1.

First set: Zverev* 1-2 Jódar (*next server)

Jódar hits a lovely forehand winner to take control of the game at 15-0 and looks pretty nerveless as Zverev tries to get on the front foot. A backhand winner down the line from the 20-year-old at 40-15 seals it.

First set: Zverev 1-1 Jódar* (*next server)

Zverev’s serve is on and he rattles through to level.

First set: Zverev* 0-1 Jódar (*next server)

Zverev is aggressive from the off, coming into the net on the very first point but his approach isn’t the best and Jódar finds the pass. After getting out to 30-0 lead, Jódar dumps a forehand into the net and suddenly we’re at 30-30. A couple of sloppy errors from Zverev to release the pressure and the Spaniard has the first game on the board.

First set: Jódar wins the toss and will serve first.

Preamble

Ok, let’s get into it. Alexander Zverev gets the first chance to state his credentials at this year’s ATP Finals. Zverev has thrived in the absence of the top men’s players, winning the French and US Opens but with Carlos Alcaraz back and lurking on the side of the draw in Turin, we will see how the 29-year-old handles the gaze of coming into this glitzy event with the added weight of being world No 1.

On the other side of the court we Rafael Jódar, a player who has rapidly ascended the rankings. The 287 days between his ATP Tour-level debut and Jódar reaching the top 10, a run which featured his first title in Morocco, obliterated the previous record held by Gustavo Kuerten. Zverev is favourite but Jódar is coming. Play begins shortly.
