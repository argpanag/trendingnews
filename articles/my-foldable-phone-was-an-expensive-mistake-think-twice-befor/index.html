Apple’s iPhone Duo looks incredible, and it will no doubt be one of the best foldable phones you can buy. However, it also costs an eye-watering $1,999 and is far more awkward to handle than a traditional iPhone. After using a similar Samsung Galaxy Z Fold for over a year, I've found far fewer reasons to unfold the screen than I originally imagined. Take it from me: Foldable phones simply aren't for everyone. Here's why I think you'll vastly prefer carrying around a regular iPhone every day.

The Ergonomic Reality: Bulky, Awkward, and Unpleasant to Hold

The iPhone Duo is Surface Duo-like in appearance and a massive departure from the traditional iPhone design. And while I haven't touched it, it simply looks awkward to hold with a single hand from what I saw during Apple’s September event keynote. If it's anything like my Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 6, I expect it to be cumbersome when folded. My phone is unusually narrow rather than extra-wide like the Duo, but the extra thickness when folded is just unpleasant.

More problematically, I've never actually wanted to unfold my phone and use it while I'm walking down the street or standing in line somewhere. It's too fragile and easy to drop. If I'm sitting down and reading something in an app, I also prefer it to keep my phone folded, so I can quickly slide it into my pocket without folding it back up first. And if I’m at home, I don’t need a foldable phone. I can simply grab an iPad or a laptop with an even larger screen.

Essentially, as the months went by, I found myself increasingly using the Z Fold 6 like a normal phone. And that meant I got to experience all the irritating aspects of a foldable device without any of the supposed benefits.

Multitasking Friction: Two Screens Don’t Mean Double the Speed

Yes, foldable phones let you use two apps at once, but how often do you really need to run multiple apps simultaneously on a phone? I like watching videos while using other apps, but all iPhones have a picture-in-picture feature for that. Multitasking sounds powerful on paper, but I almost always need just a single app at a time.

Dual-screen multitasking is also just a slower way to switch between apps. On an iPhone or a typical Android phone, moving between open apps is lightning-fast. Setting up an app on each side of your phone's screen simply isn't as efficient. The iPhone Duo will undoubtedly have a polished software experience, but even iPad multitasking remains somewhat awkward.

Don't Believe the Hype: Foldable Phones Aren't for Everyone

I have no doubt some people will immediately click with Apple's first foldable phone. I also expect the iPhone Duo to be one of the most polished foldable devices on the market to date. However, it’s still a foldable phone, and they aren’t right for everyone.

If I were buying an iPhone, I’d get the iPhone 18 Pro and save the $800. I learned my lesson the hard way that traditional phones remain far more practical in the long run.
