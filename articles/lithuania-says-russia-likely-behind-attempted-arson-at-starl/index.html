Lithuanian Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevičius said on Sunday that Russia was probably behind an attempted arson attack on a company storing equipment for the Starlink satellite service used by Ukraine.

Speaking to Lithuanian radio on Sunday, the prime minister of the EU and NATO-member country said the incident was clearly an attempted act of sabotage and part of Russia's hybrid war. Similar incidents had been seen across Europe, including in Poland and Bulgaria, he said.

Sinkevičius reported the incident on Facebook earlier on Sunday, saying an attempt had been made that morning to set fire to a Lithuanian company that stores Starlink equipment. He said the plan failed because authorities had warned the company of possible attacks and security measures had been intensified.

The firm provides connectivity for nine Starlink antennas used by Ukraine, according to the prime minister.

"At around 2 am (2300 GMT Saturday), an attempt was made to set fire to satellite antennas and a control unit. Flammable liquids and containers with a capacity of 3 to 10 litres were found," Sinkevičius said, adding that he had called a meeting of the National Security Council for Monday.

"We will learn more about who gave the order, how everything happened and who issued it. It is probably already known who ordered it - it is the Russian side," Sinkevičius said. He expressed hope that those involved in the arson would soon be detained.

Defence Minister Robertas Kaunas confirmed the information, but said he could not yet provide details. "The incident is under control and could be prevented, but it is still being investigated," he said, according to a radio report.
