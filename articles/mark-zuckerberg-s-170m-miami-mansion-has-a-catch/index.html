Mark Zuckerberg’s $170M Miami mansion has a catch

TheTools · 30.10.2026 14:49 · source · permalink

Mark Zuckerberg paid $170 million for a Miami mansion next to Jeff Bezos, but a Meta board agreement keeps him a California resident for eight years.

Table of Contents

American billionaire Mark Zuckerberg has bought a $170 million mansion on Miami’s Indian Creek Island, but he cannot make it his primary home for at least eight years.

The Meta CEO, 42, agreed with the company’s board to remain a California resident for the next eight years, The Wall Street Journal reported. The arrangement holds as long as he stays CEO or executive chair, barring material unforeseen circumstances. He is free to visit the Florida property. He just cannot switch his primary residence there.

The March purchase was the most expensive residential sale in Miami-Dade County. The house was listed at $200 million and sold for $30 million less. The estate covers about two acres of waterfront land and belongs to Zuckerberg and his wife, Priscilla Chan.

The 28,000-square-foot home has nine bedrooms, with plans for a swimming pool, private dock, gym, hair salon and massage room. It will also have a 1,500-gallon aquarium and a library with a concealed passageway.

Indian Creek Island, nicknamed the “Billionaire Bunker,” is home to some of the richest people in the US, and security patrols it closely. Amazon founder Jeff Bezos has reportedly acquired three properties there worth a combined $234 million, which will make him Zuckerberg’s neighbor. Tom Brady, Ivanka Trump and David Guetta also own homes on the island.

Tax politics add another layer. California voters will decide on Nov. 3 on Proposition 40, a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of qualifying billionaires who lived in the state on Jan. 1, 2026. Zuckerberg reportedly considered leaving California, where he has spent a sizable sum renovating his home.

Work may keep him there regardless. Meta wants him close to its Menlo Park headquarters and its growing AI operations. The company plans to create a new class of nonvoting shares that Zuckerberg can sell to raise cash while keeping his voting control.
