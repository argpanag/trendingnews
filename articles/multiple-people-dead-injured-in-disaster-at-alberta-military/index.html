Multiple people dead, injured in 'disaster' at Alberta military base training area | CBC

TheTools · 18.10.2026 05:34 · source · permalink

Deaths, injuries after fire at Alberta military base training area, officials say

The Latest

  • Emergency personnel are responding to a "significant and tragic" fire that started at a training area on a Canadian Forces Base in east-central Alberta.
  • Minister of National Defence David McGuinty says there have been fatalities and injuries from the incident at CFB Wainwright but did not confirm how many. The RCMP is investigating.
  • A "code orange" disaster response protocol has been activated in Edmonton to help care for a high number of patients being transported to the city's hospitals from Wainwright. 
  • Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said the incident is "affecting many of our brave military personnel." Prime Minister Mark Carney also described the fire as "serious."
  • Officials confirmed paramedics had responded, but have released few additional details.

    Disaster response involves more than just hospitals: doctor

    Emily Rae Pasiuk

    As my colleague Wallis Snowdon confirmed earlier, a code orange has been called in Edmonton hospitals — but Dr. Warren Thirsk says the current situation affects most other emergency services too. 

    “I know that our EMS colleagues are activated. I know that our fire colleagues are activated. I would expect that our police services are activated and the RCMP,” Thirsk told us.

    “So I would expect that everyone that would typically respond to any type of emergency is both aware of the disaster situation and mobilizing extra teams to respond as quickly as possible.”

    Thirsk also says that the number of patients in the situation “quickly exceeded” the threshold for calling the code. 

    “This wasn’t a hard decision to call a disaster code.”

    Cameron MacCuish

    The wildfire burning south of the town of Wainwright is now being held, according to the Municipal District of Wainwright.

    In a social media update just after 10:45 p.m. MT, officials urged residents to continue avoiding the incident area so roads remain clear for emergency crews and equipment.

  • 2 hours ago

    Union offers support after ‘devastating’ fire

    Cameron MacCuish

    The national president of the Union of National Defence Employees June Winger issued a message of solidarity and support for those affected by today’s incident. 

    “Our thoughts are with everyone affected by the devastating fire at CFB Wainwright, especially the families, military personnel, civilian workers and community members impacted by this tragic incident,” Winger stated.

    She added that the union stands “in solidarity with the entire Wainwright community and will continue to update and support our members during this difficult time.”

  • 2 hours ago

    ‘It’s a disaster,’ Edmonton doctor says

    Wallis Snowdon

    I’m a reporter with CBC News Edmonton. 

    I just learned some stark details about the emergency response called in the wake of the incident in Wainwright.

    I just spoke with Dr. Warren Thirsk, an emergency department physician at the Royal Alexandra Hospital in Edmonton.

    Thirsk said a code orange has been declared in Edmonton, a disaster response protocol that he said was required to help care for a high number of patients being transported to the city’s hospitals from Wainwright.  

    He said patients are being diverted to hospitals across the city and additional medical teams have been called in to deal with the onslaught of patients.

    He said Wainwright’s health-care facilities were “completely overwhelmed” by the number of patients requiring care.

    “This number of patients would overwhelm the city of Edmonton and that is why we are clearing our decks and mobilizing to respond as if it was our own city.

    “It is more patients than any of us want to count right now. It’s a disaster. It’s a one in every 15 to 25-year disaster.”

    Thirsk said his thoughts are with the victims and their families and said the focus now is on the immediate needs of the patients being brought to the city from Wainwright. 

  • 2 hours ago

    Dramatic wind shift at Wainwright as gusts reach 69 km/h

    Johanna Wagstaffe

    CBC meteorologist Johanna Wagstaffe here. I've been looking at the wind observations from CFB Wainwright, and there's been a dramatic change in conditions this afternoon.

    A cold front moved through between 3 and 4 p.m., shifting winds from southwesterly to northwesterly. That's a significant change for fire behaviour, as shifting winds can quickly change the direction a wildfire spreads.

    Temperatures also plunged from 23C to 13C in just one hour as the front passed, while winds strengthened, gusting to 69 km/h at 4 p.m.

    Northwesterly winds remained gusty into the evening but gradually eased, with gusts down to 41 km/h by 7 p.m.

    Looking ahead, the strongest winds appear to have passed, although gusty conditions could persist overnight.

    Unfortunately, there will be little help from rain. There's only a slight chance of showers, with mainly dry conditions expected overnight.

  • 2 hours ago

    Alberta NDP says it is ‘deeply saddened’ by fire

    Emily Rae Pasiuk

    Alberta NDP Opposition Leader Naheed Nenshi released a statement this hour, along with his colleague Nicole Goehring, armed forces and veterans affairs liaison. In it, the pair says they are monitoring the situation closely. 

    “We are deeply saddened by reports of the incident at CFB Wainwright.

    "Our thoughts are with the brave Canadian Armed Forces members affected, their families, loved ones and the broader military community. We are grateful to the emergency personnel responding and supporting those involved.”

  • 2 hours ago

    Elections Alberta closes Wainwright voting station

    Cameron MacCuish

    Elections Alberta says the Wainwright voting station for the advance vote for the Alberta referendum has closed due to a fire at the local Canadian Forces base.

    The agency says it was informed that a fire near the base is spreading rapidly into the Municipal District of Wainwright due to high winds, forcing the closure of the Wainwright Communiplex advance polling station.

    Elections Alberta said that ballot boxes and materials from the site “are being moved securely.”

    Today is the final day of advance polling in the province ahead of the Oct. 19 referendum.

  • 2 hours ago

    Non-profits supporting those affected by incident

    Amir Said

    The Wainwright community is reeling from this incident. Residents tell me it's the largest emergency response they've ever seen in the community. 

    Armor of Light is a Wainwright non-profit dedicated to helping people recover from crises. The society's president Hailey Jones tells me the incident has taken a heavy emotional toll on the community.

    "It’s an incredibly difficult and frightening night for our community," she said.

    "Wainwright is a close-knit community, and an incident of this magnitude affects so many people, including families waiting for news about their loved ones, first responders, hospital staff, and the wider community."

    Armor of Light is providing cots for anyone who needs somewhere to stay tonight. Jones says she's seeing other organizations and community members step up as well.

  • 2 hours ago

    CFB Wainwright principal base for live fire training

    Murray Brewster

    I've been covering the Canadian military for the past 20 years, and I can say it's unprecedented to have the defence minister give a statement like this about a serious incident.

    CFB Wainwright is the principal base for live fire training exercises. Military personnel describe it as essentially the 'finishing school' because it is the premier training base where all military elements—infantry, artillery, and air support—train together in combined arms training. 

    The base has 690 military personnel and 300 civilian workers. So we're talking about a community of about 1,000 people on a regular day, with more during special training sessions. 

    It's very important to the Canadian military because it's been around since the Second World War. But it became the premier training base for Canadian troops that were heading to the Korean War in the 1950s. And it has continued to expand since then. 

    Many troops for Afghanistan would go to this base just prior to deployment overseas. But over the last few years, a lot of the Canadian combined arms training has been overseas as opposed to at Wainwright. But because of the influx of money coming into national defence and because there are more opportunities to train, there's been a lot more activity at Wainwright in the last year or so.
