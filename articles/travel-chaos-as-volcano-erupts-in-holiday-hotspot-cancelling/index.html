Travel chaos as volcano erupts in holiday hotspot cancelling flights

TheTools · 14.09.2026 09:13 · source · permalink

Mount Etna erupted causing delays(Image: Getty)

Sicily's Mount Etna exploded on Monday morning triggering travel mayhem across the region. The eruption forced Catania Airport to suspend flights as a safety precaution.

Travellers are now being redirected to Palermo, roughly 200km away. This follows initial volcanic activity reported last week.

The Express reports there were "continuous ash emissions" billowing from the volcano's Northeath Crater last week. On Monday morning, a 4km high eruption plume was observed above Mount Etna.

The INGV Etneo Observatory has now declared a red flight alert (VONA, Volcano Observatory Notice for Aviation). This means aircraft bound for Catania Airport will be rerouted until at least 4pm today as the facility's C1 sector remains shut.

This isn't Mount Etna's first eruption this year either. In July, the volcano also triggered disruptions at Catania Airport.

(Image: Fabrizio Villa, Getty Images)

The volcano is renowned for its regular emissions and ash activity. Italy's Institute of Geophysics and Volcanology states Etna is experiencing "variable-rate degassing".

"A rapid evolution of the ongoing phenomena towards more energetic activity with the formation of eruption columns, ash clouds, and collapse of portions of the summit cones with the formation of pyroclastic flows cannot be ruled out," the institute said in a statement.

Travellers bound for Sicily are being advised to verify details before departure. "A new VONA will be issued if conditions change significantly or colour code changes," the Etna Volcano Observatory warning stated.

The chaos comes just a week after an air traffic control incident caused mayhem across airports in the UK.

Cancellations and delays were seen in major airports such as Heathrow, Gatwick, Manchester and more over several days.
