    Preamble

It was another high-profile game when Thomas Tuchel’s England let a lead slip in the second half and went on to lose. But unlike the last time, it was not one for recriminations. The England support can accept losing when their team plays with cut and thrust. More to the point, they can accept losing to Spain who, at times, played football from a different stratosphere.

England were game. They were driven by Jude Bellingham, who ran on adrenaline and played to the point of maximum expression. They had positive surges either side of the interval, especially the one towards the end of the first half when goals from Anthony Gordon and Harry Kane put them in charge, hauling them back after Lamine Yamal’s second-minute goal.

It was our best 15 minutes after the [half-time] whistle, but we couldn’t convert the penalty. Had we done that I thinik Spain would have felt their unbeaten run was ending.

Nobody will ever write the book on England’s greatest defeats, but that was one of the better ones. They went toe to toe with the world champions and would probably have gone 3-1 up but for Harry Kane’s Standing Foot (best prog-rock band ever). There are obvious concerns about the defence, but with the ball England were really good.

“Bit late for this,” says Paul McGrory, “but is Bellingham the first player to wear the armband who has never played top-flight English football. It might be a disgrace, but we’ll have to await the view of various social media disgraces.”

You’re a disgrace mate. (It must be, right.)

Full time: England 2-3 Spain

Bellingham thrashes a rising shot from the angle that is pushed over by Simon. The corner is cleared and the referee calls time on a richly enjoyable game at Wembley. It confirmed what we already knew. England are a very good football team; Spain are the best.

90+1 min Jude Bellingham has been offensively good in the second half.

81 min Now Spain are sitting a bit deeper, inviting England to show them what they’ve got. Bellingham finds Saka, who goes over under a challenge from Cucurealla in the area. Cucurella got a slight touch on the ball, so no penalty. Spain beak and Bellingham is booked for a tactical foul.

79 min “Pedri, Rob, just Pedri,” says Ruth Purdue. “He made that goal. Just an invisible force of technique. The interception when it was fired at him at the start of the buildup for the goal… wow.”

77 min “After being put on the back foot by Spain, England have shown spark but Harry Kane’s missed penalty and a second defensive blunder is taking its toll,” writes Colum Fordham. “As the excellent Anthony Gordon said prior to the match, England have to play to their strengths. Playing the Spanish way ‘is not in us’. It might be time to give Trent AA a chance to show his skills with his crosses and long-range passing.”

What a delicious goal. Baena found a pocket of space just outside the D and guided a perfect angled pass into the area for Oyarzabal. He took a touch on the run and teased the softest of chips over Trafford with his left foot. The defensive gaps were probably too big, and Laporte probably shouldn’t have been allowed to punch a pass through to Baena, but the creation of the goal was beautiful.

GOAL! England 2-3 Spain (Oyarzabal 74)

It’s Spain’s game now!

73 min This is still anyone’s game, but Spain have been so much better since that missed penalty from Kane.

70 min: Spain substitution Pedri on, Fabian Ruiz off.

70 min: Double substitution for England Morgan Rogers and James Garner replace Anthony Gordon and Myles Lewis-Skelly, who were both excellent. To these admittedly tired eyes, Lewis-Skelly looks a proper midfield player.

69 min This game is so open. Yamal’s long-range shot is blocked at one end; Lewis-Skelly’s long-range curler is comfortably saved by Simon at the other.

68 min “It looks like the City players are affected by the charges,” writes Colin Greener. “Errors from O’Reilly, Guehi, and even Rodri. Doesn’t explain the Cucurella one though.”

He does have the same initials. Bit like Jarvis and Jesus.

67 min O’Reilly is booked for tattooing Yamal’s right calf.

66 min Saka, found by the increasingly influential Bellingham, whips a trademark curler just wide of the far post. Lovely effort.

62 min Olmo’s shot from the edge of the D is well blocked by Guehi. Spain have woken up.

Spain are level after another mistake from England. Nico O’Reilly’s errant pass went straight to Olmo on the right wing, just outside the area. He fired a pass into Baena, who made room to shape an emphatic shot past Trafford from 20 yards. That’s a cracking finish from a player who has made a great chance and scored a goal within a few minutes of coming on.
