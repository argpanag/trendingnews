Norris reprimanded over Lap 50 Safety Car incident | Grandprix.com

TheTools · 04.10.2026 16:46 · source · permalink

News October 4, 2026

Lando Norris received his first driving reprimand of the season for a Lap 50 near miss behind the Safety Car in the closing stages of the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, but the stewards ruled that his rejoin was safe and left the final classification unchanged.

The clock and lap number matter in a race whose start had been delayed and then suspended. Race Control declared the eventual standing-start procedure at 4:36 p.m. local time. Norris's incident came at 6:08:52 p.m., on Lap 50 of the 55-lap race, while the field was circulating behind the final Safety Car.

George Russell had stopped ahead and the cars in front slowed sharply. As the queue compressed approaching Turn 1, Norris closed rapidly on McLaren teammate Oscar Piastri, took evasive action, left the track at speed, crossed the gravel and rejoined the line of cars.

Norris told the stewards that the low sun had affected his visibility. The panel accepted that the conditions contributed to the incident, but concluded that he had misjudged his closing speed and driven erratically. That brought a driving reprimand, his first of 2026.

The stewards found that Norris had rejoined safely and had not illegally overtaken Piastri or Lance Stroll behind the Safety Car. He therefore remained ninth in the final result, 13.993 seconds behind winner Max Verstappen.

Alex Albon and Piastri were called over an incident at 4:53:01 p.m., at the end of Lap 9. Albon slowed sharply on the approach to the pit-entry road and Piastri, following closely, had to brake and take avoiding action.

The stewards took no further action. Albon explained that he had realized he was below the required delta time and slowed to comply. Piastri said the explanation was understandable, and the stewards accepted that Albon's unusual reduction in speed was an attempt to meet the delta rather than erratic or unnecessarily dangerous driving.

Nico Hulkenberg's case arose during his stop at the end of Lap 32, at 5:35:56 p.m. Audi's automated release system initially showed green while a mechanic was still in front of the car near the left-front wheel. The team's manual backup check returned the signal to red, and Hulkenberg stopped before his car had formally left its pit-stop position.

The stewards therefore ruled that it was not an unsafe release under the regulation's technical definition. They did, however, find that the premature green signal had created an unsafe situation. Audi was fined €30,000, with €20,000 suspended for 12 months provided the team does not commit a similar pit-stop infringement.

Gabriel Bortoleto's collision with Carlos Sainz occurred at 5:01:01 p.m. on Lap 13. Bortoleto received a 10-second penalty after the stewards found that he attempted to pass at Turn 2 without establishing the required overlap, failed to hold the inside line and made contact with Sainz. The penalty was served during the race.

With the Norris decision complete, none of the post-race cases altered the finishing order.
