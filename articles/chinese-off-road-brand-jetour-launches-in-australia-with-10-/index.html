Chinese off-road Brand Jetour Launches In Australia With 10 Models Planned

TheTools · 26.11.2026 12:09 · source · permalink

Key Points

  • Chery owned Jetour has entered Australia with plans to introduce 10 models over the next three years.
  • The first model will be the Jetour 20 plug in hybrid soft roader, due in early 2027.
  • Jetour has appointed 11 dealer partners across major Australian cities, with prices for most models expected between $50,000 and $100,000.

Chinese automotive brand Jetour has officially entered Australia, bringing plans for a new range of SUVs, plug in hybrids and battery electric vehicles over the next three years. The Chery owned brand will begin its local rollout in early 2027 with the Jetour 20, a plug in hybrid soft roader, before expanding into more rugged off road models, utes and fully electric vehicles.

Jetour has already appointed 11 Australian dealer partners and is preparing its local sales and aftersales operations ahead of the first vehicle launch. The company plans to introduce 10 models in Australia over the next three years, targeting buyers who want a practical daily vehicle that can also handle weekend road trips and outdoor adventures.

The brand made its Australian debut at an event in Sydney this week, outlining its product strategy, dealer expansion and plans to establish a local service and parts network.

Jetour enters Australia

Jetour is a subsidiary of Chinese automotive giant Chery and has sold more than 2.6 million vehicles across over 100 international markets.

Unlike brands focused mainly on urban SUVs, Jetour plans to build its Australian identity around vehicles suited to touring, camping and recreational driving. Its target customers are families looking for a comfortable weekday vehicle that can also take them beyond sealed roads on weekends.

The company says its Australian range will largely sit between $50,000 and $100,000, positioning the brand in the mid to upper price segments while allowing room for dealer profitability.

Jetour also says it intends to establish a strong aftersales operation and parts supply chain, an important consideration for a new automotive brand entering an increasingly crowded Australian market.

Upcoming models and powertrains

The first Australian model will be the Jetour 20, scheduled to arrive in early 2027.

It is described as a soft roader powered by a plug in hybrid system, combining electric driving capability with a combustion engine for longer journeys. Further technical details, including battery capacity, electric driving range, performance and pricing, are expected closer to launch.

The Jetour 20 will be followed by a broader selection of vehicles, including:

  • More rugged off road focused SUVs
  • A ute
  • Fully electric vehicle models

Jetour plans to introduce 10 models locally within three years, although it has not yet confirmed the individual launch dates or specifications for the full range.

The brand's focus on plug in hybrids and battery electric vehicles gives it an opportunity to appeal to buyers considering electrified vehicles but who may not yet be ready to switch to a fully electric model.

Dealers and launch plans

Jetour has appointed 11 dealer partners as it begins establishing its Australian retail network. Initial showroom coverage will include major cities such as Sydney, Canberra, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Perth.

The network is expected to expand into additional markets during the second half of 2027 as more vehicles arrive.

Dealer representative Andrew Doyle from Peter Warren said the group would work with Jetour to prepare showrooms, train staff and establish aftersales services. The aim is to ensure the dealer network can support the initial launch and keep pace as more models are introduced.

Jetour Motor Australia general manager Johnny Fang said preparing local sales and servicing operations was the company's immediate priority. Work is already underway on staff training, vehicle maintenance and parts supply.

The company has also been testing its first model in Australian conditions. Testing has included city streets, country roads, long distance highway driving and gravel surfaces, as well as high temperature conditions.

These tests are intended to help assess and tune ride comfort, handling stability, cabin noise and air conditioning performance for local conditions.

What this means for Australia

Jetour's arrival adds another Chinese automotive brand to an Australian market that has seen a rapid expansion in new manufacturers and electrified models.

Its strategy is somewhat different from brands concentrating primarily on affordable electric passenger cars. Jetour is aiming at families and recreational drivers, with a mix of plug in hybrid, electric and more capable off road vehicles.

The planned $50,000 to $100,000 price range also suggests the brand will compete in a segment where buyers expect more than basic transport, including strong equipment levels, practical interiors and dependable aftersales support.

However, much will depend on the specifications and pricing of the Jetour 20 when it arrives in early 2027. The brand will also need to demonstrate that its dealer network, servicing facilities and parts availability can support customers across Australia.

More details about the first model are expected closer to its local launch.
