All 26 Starlink satellites deployed, Starship Flight 15 now coasting

SpaceX's Starship Flight 15 Ship vehicle has successfully deployed all 26 Starlink satellites and has now entered an extended coast phase of its mission that should last well into tomorrow. Here's our launch wrap with video and photos from Josh Dinner at the launch site.

The Ship 42 spacecraft deployed all 26 Starlink V3 internet satellites by the 1-hour, 17-minute mark of the mission.

Flight controllers then decided to forgo an "early return" option to land Starship early with an ocean splashdown off the coast of Hawaii. The option, at the 90-minute mark of the flight, is the first of three "early return" options fopr Starship in case something goes wrong.

So far, Ship 42 is performing well in orbit. The next early-return checkpoint is about 7.5 hours into the mission, which will occur around 1 a.m. EST (0600 GMT).

"That means this is now going to be the longest Starship mission we've ever flown," SpaceX's Dan Huot said. "We're going to surpass the point that we had to come home on Flight 14. So that's already exciting."

Huot and other SpaceX commentators will return in a few hours with a status update. So for now, Starship is just coasting in space.

Starship Flight 15 Ship deploys 12 of 26 Starlink satellites

It's been one hour since SpaceX's Starship Flight 15 launch and Starlink satellite deployment is underway.

So far, the Ship 42 upper stage has deployed 12 of 26 Starlink V3 internet satellites, with more popping out of its Pez-dispenser like payload bay every few minutes.

Ship is flying over a brilliant blue Earth, with the camera views from the spacecraft relayed to Earth via SpaceX's Starlink satellite constellation.

Starship begins Starlink satellite deployments

SpaceX's Starship Flight 15 Ship upper stage has begin deploying its 26 Starlink internet satellites into orbit.

Three satelllites have already been deployed, with the Ship performing a slow rotation to allow the cameras on one of the Starlinks capture imagery of the spacecraft's heat shield.

"It will be about 40 minutes all told to get these satellites out the door," SpaceX's Dan Huot says.

Starship enters orbit on 22-hour flight after launch success

SpaceX's Ship 42 has successfully performed its orbital insertion burn and is now orbiting the Earth. It is only the second time SpaceX has reached orbit with Starship. The first time was September's Starship Flight 14 test mission.

"We’ve got a nominal orbit insertion, so Starship now in orbit around Earth for the second time ever," SpaceX’s Dan Huot says. "That’s a really great moment."

The next major milestone will be Starlink satellite deployment, which should start at the 35-minute mark and last about one hour.

Starship Flight 15 'GO' for orbit, SpaceX says

SpaceX's next big milestone after today's mostly successful Starship Flight 15 launch with be orbital insertion, when the Ship will fire up a Raptor engine to enter orbit around Earth.

That engine burn is scheduled for about 26 minutes after liftoff and will last about 20 seconds.

"We are go for orbit," SpaceX's Dan Huot says.

If all goes to plan, Ship 42 should end up in an orbit about 275 kilometers above Earth and spend up to 22 hours orbiting Earth 14 times before returning to Earth tomorrow.

Deployment of the 26 Starlink V3 satellites in Ship's payload bay is scheduled to begin at the 35 minute mark.

SpaceX aborts Starship Flight 15 Super Heavy booster catch, Ship in orbit

SpaceX's Booster 22 has splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico after it aborted the planned launch pad catch. It was unclear the exact cause of the landing catch abort.

"We'll dig in to what caused that abort during its flight back to the tower," SpaceX's Dan Huot says.

The Ship upper stage, meanwhile, successfully completed its ascent and has shut down its 6 Raptor engines. It is now in orbit, with the payload deployment of 26 Starlink satellites to come next.

Starship Flight 15 Stage Separation, Booster heads home

The Starship Flight 15 Super Heavy Booster 22 has successfully separated from the Ship upper stage and is beginning its return to Earth. One of its 33 engines may have dropped out, but its upcoming 13-engine boost back burn is coming up.

Ship 42 is continuing its ascent with all 6 engines firing.

SpaceX now fueling Starship Flight 15 Super Heavy and Ship

SpaceX has officially begun fueling its massive Starship Super Heavy Booster 22 and Ship 42 for today's Flight 15 test launch. Liftoff is on track for 5:47 p.m. EST (2247 GMT).

The Super Heavy and Ship require nearly 12 million pounds of liquid oxygen and liquid methane for launch.

Super Heavy Booster 22 will be the first V3 Starship booster to attempt a landing, though SpaceX has performed the feat several times before. Ship 42 will be the first upper stage to attempt a landing when it returns to Earth on Saturday.

"We are not currently tracking any technical issues, any range issues and definitely no weather issues for today," SpaceX spokesperson Dan Huot said.
