Ukraine war briefing: Nato ‘foils Russian undersea cable sabotage drill’

TheTools · 11.09.2026 02:31 · source · permalink

Swindon drone factory was target of Russia plot involving British man, court hears; Poland says Ukraine border crossing came under threat. What we know on day 1,661

  • Nato forces chased away Russian ships in Arctic waters that were training to attack undersea cables with a secret sabotage weapon deployed by submersible craft, Reuters has reported, citing western officials. Reuters said its sources declined to detail how the technology works. The confrontation took place near the remote Svalbard archipelago. British and Norwegian officials took their findings to Moscow to show they were aware of the new technology. CIA director John Ratcliffe’s visit to Moscow in August was partly to warn ‌Russia against escalating sabotage operations in Europe, Reuters said, citing two people familiar with the matter. The US has not publicly explained Ratcliffe’s trip.

  • The Norwegian defence minister, Tore Sandvik, told Reuters: “Through our joint operation, we sent a clear message to Russia that they cannot operate covertly … It is in no one’s interest to escalate tensions in the High ‌North.” Britain’s defence ministry pointed to previous statements about attempted covert activities in and near Norwegian and British waters by Russia’s “deep-sea research directorate”, known by the acronym GUGI, which is under international sanctions. The White House did not respond to a comment request, Reuters said, while the CIA declined to comment.

  • The British man Joshua Cammidge, arrested as an alleged Russian agent, was part of a plot to sabotage a drone factory in England, prosecutors said on Thursday in a London court. Cammidge, 31, is charged with engaging in conduct to materially assist a foreign intelligence service in carrying out activities in violation of the National Security Act. He did not enter a plea and was returned to custody until due in court again on 25 September. Cammidge was in contact with a suspected representative of the GRU Volunteer Corps, a group controlled by Russia’s military intelligence agency, the prosecution told Westminster magistrates court. Cammidge allegedly provided information on four drone manufacturing sites in his home town of Swindon.
