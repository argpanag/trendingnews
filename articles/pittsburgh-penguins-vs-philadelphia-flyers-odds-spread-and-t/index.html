It's the battle of Pennsylvania between the Pittsburgh Penguins and Philadelphia Flyers. The puck drops on Wednesday night at 7:30 p.m. ET.

Last season, the Penguins finished with a record of 41-25-16, totaling 98 points, second in the Metropolitan Division, and they ended a three-year playoff drought. Under head coach Dan Muse, the Penguins overachieved and beat the low expectations. They had an explosive offense and a steady veteran core. The future Hall of Fame core continued to carry the workload. Sidney Crosby led the team in scoring, Evgeni Malkin enjoyed a 61-point campaign in 56 games, and defenseman Erik Karlsson anchored the blue line as the primary playmaker. Crosby, Malkin, and Karlsson are all entering the final years of their contracts, and the front office attempted to balance one final competitive run for its legendary trio while integrating top young talent.

Last season, the Flyers finished with a record of 43-27-12, totaling 98 points, third in the Metropolitan Division, and they made their first playoff appearance since 2020. The team was fierce, had a young core, and was a come-from-behind type of group. Forward Trevor Zegras revitalized his career in Philadelphia, racking up 67 points, while free-agent goaltending addition Dan Vladar held down the crease with a stellar 29-14-7 personal record. In the postseason, Philly beat these Penguins in six games and then were swept by the Carolina Hurricanes in the Second Round.

Last season, Pittsburgh was third in scoring, 24th in goals against, seventh on the power play, and sixth on the penalty kill. Anthony Mantha led the team in goals. The Penguins were 21-12-8 on the road and 5-5 in their last 10 games. The veteran-heavy Penguins are looking for revenge after their early postseason exit. The team brought in forwards Nick Robertson and defenseman Trevor van Riemsdyk to support the roster. The long-time "Big 3" of Crosby, Malkin, and Kris Letang will lead the offense.

Last season, Philadelphia was 21st in scoring, ninth in goals against, 32nd on the power play, and 22nd on the penalty kill. Owen Tippett led the team in goals. The Flyers were 20-13-8 at home and 7-3 in their last 10 games. This team returns to the ice with elevated expectations. They added goaltender Joseph Woll, defenseman Simon Benoit, and former Penguins forward Noel Acciari. Philly is led by a solid young forward unit in Tippett and Travis Konecny.

Best Bet: Penguins Spread

Pittsburgh has the edge in scoring, while Philly is the better defensive team. The Pens have a huge advantage on the power play and the Flyers were last in that category last season. Should be a close one between the old veterans in the Penguins and the youth movement of the Flyers.
