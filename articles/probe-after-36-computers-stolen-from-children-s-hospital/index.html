Probe after 36 computers stolen from Children's Hospital

TheTools · 27.10.2026 15:19 · source · permalink

Children's Health Ireland (CHI) has reported the theft of 36 computers from its unfinished National Children's Hospital over two days in July to gardaí.

The computers are estimated to be worth around €1,000 each.

CHI said the PC units were stolen from the hospital's Early Access areas on 30 and 31 July.

A garda investigation is currently under way, it said.

It was replying to a parliamentary question from Aontú leader Peadar Tóibín.

CHI said the theft of the computers was identified by its ICT team and reported to gardaí at Kilmainham.

CHI said the site remains under the control of developer BAM until the hospital reaches substantial completion.

It said CHI "can only comment on incidents affecting property belonging to CHI or the National Paediatric Hospital Development Board".

CHI said since the incident there has been a review of security, "with all access routes into the Early Access area".

A spokesperson for BAM said: "As this matter is currently the subject of an ongoing investigation by An Garda Síochána, we are unable to comment further at this time."

Gardaí said they received report of theft at an organisation in Dublin 8 "that occurred between Thursday, 30 and Friday, 31 July 2026".

They added: "A number of electronic items were reported as stolen. Investigations ongoing."

Deputy Tóibín said that "this is the latest debacle in the shambles that is the National Children's Hospital".

"Not only is it running years over time and millions over budget, now we have established there is a serious IT theft there, being investigated by gardaí," he said.

Mr Tóibín added that "if the hospital is unused, there is a greater chance that there will be more items stolen".

He added: "It's a data risk to have computers stolen. There are questions to be asked about oversight and security during this construction phase with no end date."
