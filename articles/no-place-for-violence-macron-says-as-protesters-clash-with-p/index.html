'No place for violence,' Macron says, as protesters clash with police in cities across France - follow live

TheTools · 17.10.2026 19:15 · source · permalink

Summary

  • There is "no place for violence", French President Emmanuel Macron says, as protesters clash with police across France over falling education standards and the cost of living

  • About 550,000 people have taken part in the protests, according to Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez

  • Nuñez also says about 400 people have been arrested and 60 law enforcement officers injured, including two who were taken to hospital unconscious

  • A tourism office has been set on fire in Lyon, while protesters have broken into and damaged town hall buildings in Lille and Marseille

  • Many of the buildings surrounding us have been smashed into, and we've seen some protesters looting and throwing glass bottles, our reporter in Paris writes

  • It follows three weeks of student-led protests across the country, with calls for workers' unions and key figures from the "yellow vest" movement to join the students

    The mayor of the east-central city of Saint-Étienne has confirmed that a young man "had his hand blown off by a grenade" during today's demonstrations.

    Régis Juanico writes on social media that "such a tragedy should never have happened", as he expresses his support for the man and his family.

  2. Hundreds arrested and widespread looting: What we heard from Nuñezpublished at 19:25 BST
    19:25 BST

    France's interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, has given an update on events across the country during today's protests.

    Here's what we've learned:

    • An estimated 550,000 people took part in demonstrations across France, including 110,000 in Paris
    • About 400 people were arrested. Nuñez said most were adults and were not connected to the student movement
    • During clashes, 60 law enforcement officers were injured, including two who were taken to hospital unconscious
    • Nuñez blamed the violence on individuals associated with the "hard left and extreme left", adding there was a "clear desire" to attack public buildings
    • The interior minister also said looting or attempted looting was recorded in several cities, including Paris, Rouen, Nantes, Toulouse and Marseille

    Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez tells reporters: "There is a break-in and arson inside the Limoges town hall. Firefighters are currently responding."

    18:34 BST

  10. 'We felt safe but then the mood shifted' - Leeds man in Parispublished at 18:13 BST
    18:13 BST

    Rozina Sini

    Mick Tickner, from Leeds, is in Paris.

    He recalls how riot police were running in the Le Marais district.

    "A protester shouted 'no violence please!' towards the police. We felt safe but then the mood shifted," he tells BBC Your Voice.

    Tickner says there was "a lot of tear gas used which we got caught in which was scary".

    He says that when he and his girlfriend, Dannii, were struggling after the tear gas, a young protester gave them some eye wash and helped them get back on the right road.

    Tickner says they knew about the protests before going to Paris and were prepared to stay away, but then they moved to the street where they were staying.

    "It’s calm here now and the fires are out. Everyone was clapping the fire brigade when they were doing their jobs."

  11. French PM says country will not be 'intimidated' by violencepublished at 17:53 BST
    17:53 BST

    France’s Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu says the country will not be “intimidated” by violence during today’s protests.

    People have the right to protest peacefully, but there is no place for “arson, looting, and attacks”, he writes in a social media post.

    "To those who clearly want to use this violence as a 'warm-up' before the presidential election, I say clearly: We will not let ourselves be intimidated. No one will impose their political agenda through violence."

    The French government has blamed the “far left” for mobilising today’s protests, ahead of the presidential election in April.

    Lecornu also praises police officers for the “extreme violence” they have faced, adding that they deserve the public’s support.

  13. Protesters loot and throw glass bottles in Parispublished at 17:34 BST
    17:34 BST

    Adina Campbell
    Reporting from Paris

    There’s a constant ring of emergency sirens sounding and the air is thick with tear gas.

    Many of the buildings surrounding us have been smashed into, and we’ve seen some protesters looting and throwing glass bottles, which is adding to the chaos.

  14. Police officers injured in Toulon, steamroller used in Caen - local authoritiespublished at 17:26 BST
    17:26 BST

    Amid the protests across France, fuelled by calls for greater investment in schools, there have also been outbreaks of violence and looting.

    In Toulon, local authorities say there were "several acts of violence" at the head of the procession.

    Two police officers were injured by projectiles, while "violent individuals" also dismantled barriers at a Christmas market and used them against police, the authorities add.

    Violence is also reported on the fringes of a protest in Caen, where groups were engaged in "looting and vandalism", according to local officials.

    Stones were thrown and graffiti sprayed on several public buildings in the city. A steamroller was used to force open a police gate, and two police vehicles were damaged, officials say.

    Meanwhile, France's Union of Senior Staff of Internal Security (Syndicat des cadres de la sécurité intérieure, SCSI) says a Hugo Boss store in Lyon was looted and set on fire after other businesses were also "ransacked".

    17:06 BST

    Protests across France are continuing into the afternoon, as students demand better school conditions and yellow vests call for measures to tackle the spiralling cost of living.

    • Our reporter says "it does feel a lot more heated and hostile" in the last few hours, while our correspondent notes there is "a clear contrast" between the official march and small groups "clearly bent on making trouble"
    • Officials say 272 people have been arrested nationwide so far, with the interior ministry reporting attacks on police officers and looting

    • Marine Le Pen, leader of the hard-right National Rally, accuses "rioters" of "poisoning" the protests, while Jean-Luc Mélenchon, leader of the left-wing La France Insoumise, says the media have been focusing on the violence while "everything is unfolding in calm"

  18. There is violence but also peaceful protesters - Manchester exchange studentpublished at 16:25 BST
    16:25 BST

    Rozina Sini

    Benjamin Woolfe, 20, is a University of Manchester student spending an exchange year in Paris.

    He lives near Place de la République, where he’s witnessed skirmishes between protesters and police.

    "I haven’t taken part in the protests but I have been down to the edge of the square where I saw riot police charges. There's lots of people with signs and a big police presence," he tells BBC Your Voice.

    "One of my friends got tear gassed but many international students have kept a distance because they're on visas and don’t want to risk it."

    Benjamin says he doesn’t have strong opinions about the reasons for the demonstrations, having lived in Paris for only a few months.

    "A lot of coverage has focused on the violence but there are also lots of peaceful protesters," he adds.

  19. Rapid-response riot police deployed in Parispublished at 16:18 BST
    16:18 BST

    A rapid-response riot police force has been deployed in Paris, the city’s prefecture confirms.

    “Like other mobile force units reinforcing the police prefecture’s staff, CRS 8 is intervening in the Les Halles area,” it says in a statement.

    “Given the expectation that the processions would be highly mobile, an agile deployment strategy was adopted, with units intervening systematically to restore calm.”

    CRS 8 is a specialised force trained to combat urban violence.

  20. Loss of tourist office is heartbreaking, says Lyon mayorpublished at 16:11 BST
    16:11 BST

    The mayor of Lyon says the burning of the tourist office is "a tragedy" for the city.

    Pictures from the scene show protesters wearing masks breaking the windows before a fire broke out inside the building. Local police said "hostile individuals" had prevented firefighters from putting out the blaze.
