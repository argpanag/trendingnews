With the hype the way it is, it’s extremely challenging these days to figure out fact from fiction when it comes to AI. On one extreme, we have people claiming AI is going to save the world—it’s going to cure cancer, make us so efficient we don’t have to work, and be a panacea for all of our ills. On the other hand, we have people saying AI is going to doom us by either enslaving us or using its intelligence to cause mass casualties in some deadly manner. What is an average Joe supposed to do? I mean, one day an engineer says it’s just too powerful of a tool, and the next day we learn he’s hired a PR firm! Argh!!! Thankfully, I can bring much-needed assurance.

If you are roaming the proverbial streets of social media and are interested in AI, you might have come across a young feller named Husk IRL, who makes comedy with and about AI. I know, I know—you’re thinking: How’s that going to help beyond a few laughs? We’re talking either utopia or total annihilation!

The thing is, Husk’s brand of comedy only works because we’ve used AI. We know it can do really stupid things. And he exploits that knowledge to make us laugh. It’s brilliant.

So, if you’re ever feeling absurdly optimistic or super anxious because of what AI might become, look no further than the Husk-O-Meter to snap you back to reality.

By now, you’re asking yourself: What’s wrong with the writer of this article? Plenty, but let’s delve into the Husk-O-Meter, if only for our sanity.

The Husk-O-Meter

With all these unbelievable stories about AI in the news, we need a reliable, solid way to ground us to reality. We need something that’s gonna cut through the BS and tell it like it is, and the one person that can do that is Husk.

The other day, he released a video of him asking an AI how many E’s are in the word seventeen. The AI kept repeating three, even after spelling the word out.

Did an AI actually do this? Are we back to the R’s in strawberry? I dunno. It could be scripted; it might not be scripted—I have no idea. But what I do know is it’s still funny, and it’s funny precisely because I use AI. I know what the companies claim, and I also know that AI sometimes falls flat on its face. Amazingly. And then it gets up and does it two more times.

So I can laugh at Husk’s comedy precisely because I’ve been there. I can relate to that. And if I can relate to that, I can feel pretty confident that AI isn’t going to create heaven or hell on earth in the immediate future. So as long as Husk’s comedy makes people laugh because of their current experiences with AI, you know we’re good, if only momentarily.

I have no doubt someone more talented than me could tie his popularity to a digital gauge that indicates the current state of whether AI is going to save us, kill us all, or be somewhere in the middle. I imagine it as a circular, digital meter. At one end, we have a little icon of a nuclear bomb. On the other end, we have an icon of heaven. In the middle is a photo of Husk’s smiling face. All of it is tied to Husk’s popularity—clearly an infallible metric.

So remember, as long as we’re laughing, we’re still in the middle.
