Magic the Doberman becomes owner's second dog rescued from mineshaft

Magic the Doberman has been rescued after falling down a disused mineshaft in the Ballarat region. (Supplied: Invermay CFA)

In short:

A Doberman has been rescued from a disused mineshaft in bushland near Ballarat.

Magic is her owner's second dog to fall into one of the region's thousands of disused mineshafts.

What's next?

Rescuers advise people unfamiliar with the area to stick to formed paths.

It was a moment of deja vu for a Country Fire Authority (CFA) firefighter when he helped rescue a Doberman from a mineshaft in western Victoria earlier this week.

Magic the Doberman became her owner's second dog rescued from a mineshaft after she fell into one while chasing a rabbit in bushland at Nerrina, near Ballarat, on Monday.

Fire Rescue Victoria (FRV) was called, and a specialist high-angle rescue team hoisted Magic nearly six metres to safety.

A high-angle rescue team member entered the mineshaft to harness Magic and help bring her to safety. (Supplied: Invermay CFA)

"There were no reported injuries, and the dog was happy to be reunited with its owner," an FRV spokesperson said.

Invermay CFA firefighter Daryl Rowe, who was part of the rescue team, said it was a familiar scene.

"Interestingly, the same owner, we rescued another one of his dogs about 10 years ago [from a mineshaft]," Mr Rowe said.

But he said it was unsurprising, considering the number of disused mineshafts in the area.

"There are thousands of mineshafts up there,"

"They're everywhere, and we've had animals and people [fall in mineshafts] where we've had to try and arrange to extricate them.

"It's not feasible to cover all the mineshafts up there; locating them would be another issue, and the cost would be prohibitive.

"You don't want to stop people from walking through the bush."

Mr Rowe said disused mineshafts could often be identified by a slight depression in the ground and advised people to stick to pathways, bike tracks, and roadways.
