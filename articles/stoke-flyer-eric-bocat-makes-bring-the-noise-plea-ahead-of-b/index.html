Stoke flyer Eric Bocat makes bring the noise plea ahead of Boro showdown

TheTools · 13.10.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

Stoke City flyer Eric Bocat is hoping to help turn up the noise at the bet365 Stadium in a run of five out of eight games at home.

A clip of Bocat has gone viral as he was filmed walking towards Stoke’s 4,600-strong sell-out away end at Bolton Wanderers on Saturday. “They said they were down to 10 men,” he said to the camera. “Look how many people. Not 10, never.”

Now the fourth-placed Potters have returned ready to take on a Middlesbrough side (8pm) sitting two places and two points better off and is well fancied to go the distance in the promotion race this season.

Stoke are looking to make it four wins in a row – and four home wins in a row – and Bocat wants to be carried again by a team that seems increasingly united with supporters.

“To be honest I just hear them the whole game,” he said. “Most of the time during games, I am that focused that I am not standing there thinking, ‘Oh they’re loud’ but I was hearing them all through the game and I think that really pushed us.

“(If we can get that kind of noise in the home matches) that’s going to help us. I think it’s one of, if not the most, noisiest crowds in the league. I hope they can bring the same energy again against Middlesbrough because it’s going to be a tough game.

“I hope we can be together. That’s the word, togetherness.”

Middlesbrough, last season’s beaten play-off finalists, are seven unbeaten and beat Wolves 1-0 at home at the weekend.

Bocat said: “It’s going to be a good test, a good game because since I’ve been here Middlesbrough have always been a good side with good players.

“Now we’re playing at home so we expect to do our best and give our best performance so let’s see.

“I think being consistent in the league is really important. Now we’re on a high but a quick turnover can happen and there will be times we have to get through harder periods but we have to stick together and get through it. Every training session, every game we have to do our best to deliver a result.”

Bocat has been one of the more eye-catching performers in the Stoke side so far this term, enjoying a new licence to attack – particularly since the change of shape to a back three that saw him move to wing-back.

He has scored twice, set up two more and is emerging as one of the main attacking threats in a new-look side under Mark Robins. That has been important especially after the loss of last year’s main creative force Sorba Thomas on deadline day.

Bocat said: “To be honest when we started the season I didn’t know we would change to a back five but obviously the gaffer and his staff knew I could play in this position. It came naturally and ever since they’ve put me there I’ve really enjoyed it.

“Every season is different. Sorba helped us a lot last year – I mean he was one of the best players in the league – and now we needed to find something else because Sorba is not there anymore. It came naturally and I have more freedom to get forward.”
