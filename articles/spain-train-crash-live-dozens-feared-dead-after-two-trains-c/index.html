Spain train crash live: Dozens feared dead after two trains collide south of Madrid

TheTools · 28.10.2026 11:08 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Dozens of people are feared dead after a Spanish high-speed train collided with another train south of the capital Madrid

  • More than 500 passengers were travelling on the trains involved, and an initial death toll of 10 "will almost certainly rise", the spokesperson for the regional government says

  • Spain's Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez says he is "shocked by the serious train accident" and is in constant contact with the transport minister

  • Rail operator Adif says the incident occurred between a Renfe train and another of the Iryo company, which covers the Madrid to Seville route

  • "It was a catastrophe" - a 17-year-old, who was travelling on one of the trains, tells a Spanish broadcaster there was "chaos" at the scene

  4. Dozens feared dead in high-speed crashpublished at 10:40 GMT
    10:40 GMT

    Paul Kirby
    Europe digital editor

    The death toll in this morning's high-speed rail crash near Urda south of Madrid has risen to "a minimum" of 30, regional government sources have told Spain's Efe news agency.

    Officials in Castilla-La Mancha say 147 passengers were on a train run by private operator Iryo and 397 people were on the high-speed train.

    So far, they say they cannot confirm that the high-speed train went into the back of the Iryo train when it had stopped.

    There are fears that the number of casualties could be even higher, and sources have told Spanish media that the disaster could be "worse than Adamuz" last January, when 46 people died.

  5. Concerns number of casualties could be much higherpublished at 10:35 GMT
    10:35 GMT

    Guy Hedgecoe
    BBC News in Madrid

    Two trains have collided in Spain causing at least 10 deaths, although there are concerns that the true number of casualties could be much higher (some reports are putting the death toll at 46).

    The crash took place near the town of Urda, around 140 km (100 miles) south of Madrid.

    Emergency services are at the scene helping those injured and looking for survivors.

    The accident happened when a high-speed train travelling from Madrid to the south of the country collided with another train that was apparently stationary. The crash caused at least two carriages to fall down an embankment.

    A passenger on board one of the trains told local media there was a heavy impact about 40 minutes into the journey.

    Firefighters, paramedics and other emergency personnel are at the scene, along with three helicopters.

    According to reports, the government believes that human error linked to signalling is a likely cause of the crash.

    The national rail operator Renfe has set up a help line for relatives of those who were on board the trains.

    And Parliament has cancelled events for the day. Prime minister Pedro Sánchez posted on social media that he was “upset” at the news and offered his solidarity and support to those hurt and their families.

    In January, a collision between two trains on the same route killed 46 people.

    10:27 GMT

    A 17-year-old girl named Eduarda, who was travelling on one of the trains, tells broadcaster Cadena Ser that there was "chaos" at the scene and that she saw casualties under the trains.

    "A lot of people couldn't get out. I heard screams... It was horrific," she says.

    "It was a catastrophe... I could hear people screaming in panic."

  7. 'Major tragedy' and fears that casualties will risepublished at 10:23 GMT
    10:23 GMT

    Paul Kirby
    Europe digital editor

    Health Minister Mónica García has told Spanish radio that the images from the scene of the crash show there has been a "significant impact [that] points to a major tragedy".

    Some of the casualties have been taken for treatment at a health in Urda not far from the crash, and Spanish TV reports that those needing hospital treatment will be taken to the city of Toledo to the north.

    Meanwhile, there are fears the number of casualties will rise.

    The mayor of Los Yébenes, not far from Urda, says he's spoken to local police about what they have seen. Jesús Pérez said they had described "truly nightmarish scenes".

  10. Another tragedy on Spain's high-speed rail networkpublished at 09:56 GMT
    09:56 GMT

    Paul Kirby
    Europe digital editor

    For Spaniards, this rail crash near Urda brings back very painful memories.

    It was only last January when a high-speed train derailed in southern Spain, and its rear carriages went into the path of a state-run Renfe train, claiming the lives of 46 people.

    The Adamuz collision was Spain’s worst rail tragedy since 2013 and cast doubts on safety on the country’s prized high-speed rail network.

    This latest disaster is further south but again it involves a Renfe train and another run by private operator Iryo.

    The circumstances are very different as at Adamuz there was a fracture on the track, while at Urda there appears to have been a failure involving safety mechanisms.

    But again, there are new concerns about safety on Spain’s railways.

  11. Spanish PM Sánchez 'shocked' by the rail collisionpublished at 09:47 GMT
    09:47 GMT

    Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez says he is "shocked by the serious train accident in Toledo".

    "I remain in constant contact with the Minister of Transport, and with the president of Castilla-La Mancha," Sánchez said on X, adding that emergency services were working on the ground “to assist those affected and ensure their safety, in coordination with all the involved administrations".

    “My affection, solidarity, and support to all the victims and their families.”

    Sánchez and other Spanish politicians have cancelled all public engagements today, including campaigning for next month’s parliamentary elections.

    Two high-speed trains have collided in Spain just after 09:00 (08:00 GMT) between municipalities Mora and Orgaz, just south of Madrid. Here’s what we know so far:

    • A least 10 people are dead, regional government spokesperson for the Junta de Castilla-La Mancha region Esther Padilla has told local television.
    • Padilla has told Castilla-La Mancha Media the deaths could potentially increase
    • Rail operator Adif says the incident occurred between a Renfe train and another of the Iryo company, which covers the Madrid to Seville route
    • All trains on this line have been stopped at stations along the route, Adif says on X
