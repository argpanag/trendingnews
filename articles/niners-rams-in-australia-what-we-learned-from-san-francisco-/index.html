Niners-Rams in Australia: What We Learned from San Francisco's 27-7 win

TheTools · 11.09.2026 05:53 · source · permalink

REWATCH: 49ers-Rams on NFL+ Premium

FULL BOX SCORE

  1. Niners burst Rams’ bubble. Lying in wait for an upset in Australia, the 49ers stunned the NFL’s most-hyped squad -- and their division rivals. New faces (Mike Evans), returning talents (Fred Warner) and familiar standouts (Brock Purdy) came to play and make a statement. Kyle Shanahan largely carved up the Rams' revarnished defense with Purdy throwing three touchdowns passes and for 205 yards in front of 100,021 fans attending the NFL’s first-ever regular season game in Australia. Though they only collected three points, the 49ers' opening drive set a stellar tone, with Purdy guiding them 65 yards on 15 plays, completing 9 of 12 passes with Christian McCaffrey, rookie Kaelon Black, George Kittle (returning from an Achilles injury), Deebo Samuel (returning after spending 2025 with the Commanders) and Evans all catching balls. An absolute beauty of a 39-yard TD pass to former Rams WR Demarcus Robinson led to a precarious 10-7 halftime lead for the 49ers, but in the second half, Purdy and Co. squeezed the life out of the Rams. Purdy’s 15-yard score to Deebo Samuel culminated an 11-play, 99-yard TD march to open the fourth quarter and close out a statement-making Week 1 win. You can’t hold a funeral or throw a party based off a Week 1 result, but this was a dominant win for San Francisco against a Super Bowl favorite that has been everyone’s offseason darling. The 49ers aren’t to be overlooked in the NFC.
  2. Rams fall flat in much-anticipated opener. When the Rams received the opening kickoff, they did so as the first team in league chronicle with the reigning AP NFL MVP (Matthew Stafford) and the reigning Defensive Player of the Year (Myles Garrett). By the time the game ended, both symbolized the Rams' shocking lack of production from their galaxy of all-stars. Stafford was dismal, unable to get the Rams -- the league's top scoring offense last season -- into any rhythm. He threw for just 155 yards with no touchdowns, an interception and a 61.2 rating. The seven points in the loss marked just the second Stafford start for the Rams with 10 points or fewer scored – the other was a Week 4 loss to the 49ers in 2022. Having won the MVP after leading the league with 46 touchdowns, Stafford had at least one TD pass in every game last season. Stafford’s zero TDs were matched by Garrett’s tackles, as the longtime Browns standout recorded just his fourth career game out of 135 with zero tackles. Niners offensive tackle Trent Williamsboat-raced Garrett in their heavyweight showdown. Garrett was credited by five QB pressures, one coming in 11 matchups with Williams, per Next Gen Stats. With Aaron Donald back home in Los Angeles, he had just about as much statistical impact for the new-look Rams D as Garrett. A long season still lies ahead, but this is L.A's first loss by more than seven points since Week 12, 2024. There were few positives to take from this one other than there being 16 games left in the regular season for the Rams to prove they're not paper champions -- which will surely be the too-quick, too-hot take as they’re flying home.
  3. Evans makes phenomenal introduction. There was no shortage of shiny new additions for the 49ers or Rams to unveil in the season opener. Mike Evans’ 49ers debut proved to be the most pivotal, as the 33-year-old looked every bit like the six-time Pro Bowler he was with the Buccaneers in his first game playing in a different uniform. Evans was clutch and in classic form, highlighted by a 2-yard touchdown reception in the third quarter off a Brock Purdy fade. It was Evans' 37th career TD catch of 5 yards or fewer -- tying him for second all time, per NFL Research. He finished tied for the game high with six receptions for 49 yards. He had three third-down conversions on the 49ers’ opening drive. San Francisco wasted little time letting it be known Evans will be a big part of its plans, but Shanahan likewise showcased balance that was missing last year. Christian McCaffrey (10 carries for 68 yards) and Kaelon Black (14 for 65) split reps, while three 49ers caught TD passes and six had multiple catches. New and old faces meshed quickly.
  4. McVay’s flux capacitor fails him for first time. The Rams departed Southern California on Tuesday night and arrived in Melbourne, Australia, on Thursday morning, roughly a day before kickoff on Friday morning Aussie time. Sean McVay and the Rams' travel plan in comparison to Kyle Shanahan and the 49ers taking ample time to arrive and get acclimated drew criticism and will garner more. For the first time, McVay’s travel itinerary failed him. The Rams did a quick turn and burn for a 35-7 win over the Jacksonville Jaguars last season in London, arriving just a day ahead of the game, with McVay improving to 3-0 in international games. But McVay lost for the first time in an international game. L.A. looked sluggish to start, punting on its first two drives after never having multiple punts in the first quarter last season. Once Matthew Stafford hit Puka Nacua for a 41-yard gain late in the first, the Rams seemed to be ignited with Kyren Williams scoring two plays later. However, they never got out of the offensive mud. They left late and they left for home early as Stafford hit the bench when L.A. took over possession late in the fourth, conceding the loss.
  5. Defensive returns make difference. On the first play from scrimmage, Matthew Stafford was pressured by Nick Bosa before throwing a pass that was almost picked off by Fred Warner. Bosa (torn ACL) and Warner (ankle) were absent for much of last season and their returns, beyond just the stat sheets, so clearly buoyed San Francisco. The Rams were the top offense in scoring and yards last year, but were held to seven points and 290 yards in this one, had two turnovers and were stuffed on two of three fourth-down attempts. Warner had a game-high 11 tackles, while Bosa had a four tackles (two for loss). San Francisco got gashed a few times on the run, but overall this was a marvelous performance in Raheem Morris’ first game as defensive coordinator and no doubt bolstered by the return of two of the NFL’s elite talents.

NFL Pro Insight for 49ers-Rams: Brock Purdy’s 39-yard touchdown to Demarcus Robinson traveled 44 yards relative to the line of scrimmage for the longest completion by air yards in Purdy’s career.

NFL Research: Sean McVay lost his first international series game, falling to 3-1. His three international victories as a head coach trail only Doug Pederson’s four.
