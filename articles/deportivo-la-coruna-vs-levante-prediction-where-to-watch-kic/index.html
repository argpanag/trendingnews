Deportivo La Coruna vs. Levante - Head-to-head record

The historical rivalry between these clubs spans 43 competitive matches across all competitions, with Deportivo holding 20 victories and seven draws while suffering 16 defeats against the Granotas.

At the Riazor, Deportivo have established traditional dominance, winning 15 of their 22 home meetings against Levante and losing only four times.

In top-flight action, the Galicians are unbeaten in their last seven LaLiga clashes against Levante (three wins and four draws), although the Valencia outfit completed a league double over Deportivo when both sides competed in the Segunda División in 2024/25.

Recent meetings have frequently produced goals at both ends, including consecutive 2-2 draws during their previous shared top-flight season.

With both managers organizing attack-minded approaches, supporters can expect an entertaining contest where fine defensive margins prove decisive.

Deportivo La Coruna - In need of a result

Antonio Hidalgo’s team return to the Riazor aiming to convert encouraging attacking displays into three points after dropping a tight contest in San Sebastián. Below is an overview of the hosts’ current form, team news, and projected starting XI:

Recent form

Deportivo sit in a respectable eighth position in LaLiga with 10 points from eight matches (two wins, four draws and two defeats).

While their overall return remains impressive following promotion from the second tier, a four-match winless league run has slowed their early-season momentum.

Clean sheets have eluded Hidalgo’s side entirely this campaign, marking the club’s longest wait for a top-flight shutout to start a season since 1966/67. Despite those defensive concerns, the Riazor has provided a reliable base on Friday evenings, with Deportivo winning seven of their last 10 Friday home fixtures in the top division.

Hidalgo must deal with several absences across his squad. Midfielder Lorenzo Amatucci serves a suspension following his late dismissal against Real Sociedad, while top scorer Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang (five league goals) is out until November with a meniscus injury. Marc Casadó and Zakaria Eddahchouri also remain sidelined.

Leo Román starts in goal behind central defenders José María Giménez and Bright Ede, while Mario Soriano and Diego Villares anchor midfield, supporting David Mella, Luismi Cruz and Yeremay Hernández behind central forward Bil Nsongo.

Levante - Struggling for consistency

Luís Castro brings his Levante squad to Galicia seeking their first away victory of the season after an entertaining six-goal thriller against Sevilla. Here is an examination of the visitors’ tactical setup, defensive frailties, and expected starting lineup ahead of kickoff:

Recent form

Levante occupy 17th place in the standings with six points from seven matches (one win, three draws and three defeats).

While Castro’s men have displayed plenty of attacking threat, scoring 11 goals across their last five outings, their defensive record is a major concern. The Frogs have conceded 15 times in seven matches—including 10 goals in their last three league fixtures alone.

Travel form has offered little relief for the visitors, who remain winless in four road matches this term (two draws and two losses) while failing to score in three of those outings. Furthermore, Levante are one of three LaLiga sides yet to hold a halftime lead in 2026/27.

Castro has to cope with the absences of midfielder Hugo Sotelo (knee sprain) and attacker Karl Etta Eyong (thigh).

Australian international Mathew Ryan anchors the defense behind central pairing Adrián de la Fuente and Ifeanyi Ndukwe. Jon Ander Olasagasti and Oriol Rey look to contain Deportivo’s runners in midfield, while Roger Brugué (three goals) and Iván Romero (three goals) lead the offensive charge alongside Yéimar Musuayi.
