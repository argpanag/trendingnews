Eric Cantona: I would never have signed for Man Utd under Sir Jim Ratcliffe

TheTools · 23.09.2026 20:01 · source · permalink

Ineos founder’s favourite player says the billionaire’s remarks about immigration would have scuppered his move to Old Trafford

Eric Cantona says he would not have signed for Manchester United under Sir Jim Ratcliffe’s ownership as he hit out at the Ineos founder’s stance on immigration.

The former United striker – whom Ratcliffe described previously as his favourite player – has been a vocal critic of the way the British billionaire has run the club.

Now Cantona has made the admission that he would not have joined Manchester United from Leeds United in 1992 had Ratcliffe been the owner, after criticising the 73-year-old’s views on immigration.

Ratcliffe provoked condemnation in February when he told Sky News that the UK had been “colonised by immigrants” and in an interview with the BBC this week he said the country was “on the slide” and “no one is dealing with the immigration problem and no one is tough enough to deal with the benefits problem”.

“Someone needs to address this, otherwise the country ends up in a bad place and goes broke,” added Ratcliffe, who also said he will not return to Britain because of the country’s culture of envy towards wealth. The Manchester United co-owner has been a tax resident in Monaco since 2020.

Asked by well-known United fan Pete Boyle whether he would have signed with Ratcliffe in charge, Cantona replied bluntly: “No.”

And when asked to expand on that point, he added: “Of course [I would not have signed]. You know what I think about all of this, with this kind of politics. And I cannot understand that someone like this can say something like this when he’s one of the owners of the club.”

‘All the greatest players come from different waves of immigration’

Ratcliffe’s remarks have angered Cantona, one of the club’s most famous and successful foreign imports.

“Football is a very popular sport. Football is made – all the greatest players come from different waves of immigration. Everywhere, in France, in England, everywhere,” said Cantona, who is on tour for An Evening with Eric ‘The King’ Cantona events in Ireland.

“In France for instance, the greatest players in France, they all come from different waves of immigration. I made a documentary on that, French Football: An Immigrant History.

“Raymond Kopa, in the fifties, was the son of Polish immigrants who came to France, worked in France, and worked hard in France.

“And then the Italians, Italians like [Michel] Platini [born in France but the son and grandson of Italian immigrants]. And then Spanish, like me, my mother came from Spanish refugees. I cannot accept this kind of thing [Ratcliffe’s views].”

This is not the first time Cantona has spoken out against Ratcliffe. In April last year, Cantona said he would choose not to support Manchester United after accusing Ratcliffe of “destroying” the club and running it “more like an economy”.

It followed a brutal cost-cutting drive that resulted in about 400 staff losing their jobs.

Cantona said at the time: “I support United because I really love United, but now if I was a fan and I had to choose a club I don’t think I would choose United.

“Because I don’t feel close to these kind of decisions. They have another strategy, another project. Do you feel close to this project? I don’t think so.

“For me, it is very important to respect these people like you respect your manager and your team-mates. I think since Ratcliffe arrived it’s the complete opposite and this team of directors, they try to destroy everything and they don’t respect anybody.”

Cantona is the latest high-profile figure to criticise Ratcliffe. Gary Lineker, the former England captain and Match of the Day host, claimed Ratcliffe was worse than the Glazer family – the club’s long-standing owners who are reviled by the fanbase – and “not particularly pleasant”.

Speaking on The Rest is Football podcast, Lineker said: “I look at Manchester United and I see problems coming down from the top. I must congratulate the Glazers on finding someone even worse than them that they can blame and getting him in there and getting all the stick: Jim Ratcliffe.

“I don’t think he’s a particularly pleasant human being and it’s a bit of shambles of a football club, the way it’s being run at the moment. So, well done to the Glazers on that one, deflecting everything away from them.”

Manchester United’s Muslim supporters’ club said this week Ratcliffe “must go” after branding his latest remarks on immigration “deeply disappointing and completely unacceptable”.

The club have endured a challenging time since Ratcliffe took over the day-to-day running of operations at Old Trafford from co-owners the Glazers in February 2024. Two managers have already been sacked in Erik ten Hag and Ruben Amorim, and Michael Carrick is under pressure after a poor start to this season, despite having guided the club back to the Champions League last term.
