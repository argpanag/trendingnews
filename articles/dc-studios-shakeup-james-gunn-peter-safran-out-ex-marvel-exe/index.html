DC Studios Shakeup: James Gunn, Peter Safran Out, Ex-Marvel Exec In

TheTools · 09.11.2026 17:16 · source · permalink

In a seismic executive shuffle unlike anything Bruce Wayne could pull off at Wayne Enterprises, James Gunn and Peter Safran are leaving their posts as co-CEOs and Chairmen of DC Studios, jobs which they’ve held for the past four years. The dynamic duo will continue to produce on various DC projects and are returning to their full-time creative work. They’re currently in post on the Superman sequel, Man of Tomorrow which will open in theaters on July 9, 2027.

Taking over the comic book entertainment label as President and Chief Creative Officer is former Marvel SVP of Production and Development, Jeremy Latcham. He starts in the position on Dec. 1 and reports to Skydance Motion Picture Group co-chairs Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein. Note the tweak here in the reporting structure of DC: Initially the DC Bosses reported straight into the CEO (previously Zaslav, post-merger David Ellison and Ynon Kreiz). Now DC — like the other Warner film labels — reports into Goldberg and Greenstein.

Latcham worked with Gunn previously on Guardians of the Galaxy. Latcham shepherded such mega MCU franchises as Iron Man, Guardians of the Galaxy, Spider-Man and The Avengers. He will lead DC’s film, television and animation slate, overseeing the creative pipeline, along with production, talent and strategy.

The news hits today as DC’s new parent, Skydance, is reporting its third quarter earnings.

For some, the dynamic duo’s departure is a bit of a shock as sources told us that they were staying after Paramount’s acquisition of Warner Bros. However, Safran’s contract was expiring in the spring. His name was floated for the film boss job at Netflix under newly installed Film Content Chief Peter Rice. From what we know, Safran and Rice haven’t sat down.

Gunn and Safran were put in charge of DC by former Warner Bros Discovery CEO David Zaslav as he strove to create a motion picture production label strategy on par with Disney’s. The hiring of Gunn as a creative production executive was arguably an anomaly: Few filmmakers have led studios, with Charlie Chaplin being one of the most notable exceptions.

Gunn and Safran took over DC at a time when the label was waning under the Zack Snyder universe. Zaslav wanted a universe that was built out across film, TV and games. The former WBD boss was adamant about making a Superman movie, and former WB Motion Pictures bosses quietly contracted Gunn to start writing his version before he landed the co-CEOs job with Conjuring producer Safran.

During their tenure, they saw the debut of the Matt Reeves’ produced 9x Emmy-winning HBO Max series, The Penguin, a spinoff of the filmmaker’s The Batman starring Colin Farrell. That series was well in the works before Gunn and Safran arrived at DC. They also had two seasons of the Emmy-nominated HBO Max series Peacemaker, which was a spinoff of Gunn’s 2021 Suicide Squad movie starring that pic’s anti-hero played by John Cena. There was also the animated HBO adult series Creature Commandos. Gunn’s Superman rebooted the franchise a fourth time since the Man of Steel hit the big screen in 1978, with a $618.7M global gross. Their HBO Max series, Lanterns, was a viewership success and their lower budget Clayface with a net $44M production cost was No. 1 worldwide for two weeks in a row. That James Watkins-directed pic’s cume now at $109M WW. Upcoming is Reeves’ The Batman II which has Robert Pattinson returning in the title role and Steven Soderbergh finishing in London for a Feb. 18, 2028 release. Also upcoming for DC in 2027 is The People v. Gorilla Grodd, the eight-episode HBO Max series starring Skyler Gisondo as Jimmy Olsen and Jimmy Tatro as Gorilla Grodd has completed filming. Gunn and Safran serve as executive producers.

A big miss for Gunn and Safran was the Craig Gillespie directed Supergirl which cost a net $186M and grossed only $126.4M WW. A graphic novel which mirrored True Grit in its story, the Superman spinoff arrived a year after Gun’s Man of Steel movie, and was perceived by fanboys as being too similar to other fantasy movies — i.e. Mad Max and Guardians of the Galaxy.

Gunn and Safran had a big plan for DC, announced as “Gods and Monsters” in January 2023. It will be interesting to see what projects Goldberg, Greenstein and Latcham keep intact. There was early buzz of James Mangold circling a Swamp Thing movie, and Andy Muscietti penning a version of Batman Brave and the Bold. Of the projects Gunn and Safran announced which wound up fizzling after being long in development was the Peacemaker spinoff series Waller starring Viola Davis, Paradise Lost a series set in the Wonder Woman-verse, Booster Gold and an Authority movie about a bunch of DC antiheroes. Gunn said in February 2025 on social that Authority “is a very big movie, if you’re going to do it properly. Some of the story elements perhaps that are in Superman and some of the other films we fast-tracked, step a little on The Authority … [It’s] the one that got messed with through all of the other things that were happening. The script had a harder time coming along.”

In fact, Gunn announced recently he was working on a screenplay for a new feature project. What’s the status of that mystery project?

It’s been a while since Latcham has been in the superhero space. He left the Kevin Feige led Marvel in 2017 to found Latcham Pictures, which held first-look deals at 20th Century Fox, Hasbro and Wonder Project. He produced Bad Times at the El Royale and Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves. Recently, he held an overall deal at Hasbro Entertainment, where he was developing a Dungeons & Dragons sequel and an Action Man film. He’s also producing Lionsgate and Sony’s big screen take of anime Naruto which has Spider-Man: Brand New Day‘s Destin Daniel Cretton directing.

David Ellison, Chairman and CEO, and Ynon Kreiz, Co-CEO, of Skydance said in a statement about today’s news, “James and Peter launched a new era for DC Studios and led it through a period of real transformation, resetting the foundation and building a slate we’re incredibly proud of. We’re grateful for their passion, contributions and commitment to this legendary brand. Jeremy has been a longtime creative partner to both, and we have complete confidence that James and Peter will ensure a smooth, collaborative transition and continue this work with care. We look forward to working with Jeremy to build the next generation of DC storytelling and to doubling down on our commitment to fans.”

Said Gunn and Safran: “Four years ago, we set out to unite DC under one creative vision. In that time, we helped restore Superman to the heart of DC, while opening new doors and telling new stories in film and television with Peacemaker, Creature Commandos, Lanterns and Clayface. We’re immensely proud of the team that turned that ambition into something real, including the actors, crews and executives we had the opportunity to collaborate with. We’ve shared a creative history with Jeremy going back to Marvel, and we’re eager to see the perspective he brings to DC’s next chapter. We’re excited to return to what drew us to this job in the first place, which is telling the stories we love, including the next chapter of Clark and Lex’s journey in Man of Tomorrow.”

“Jeremy brings a deep reverence for DC and its passionate fanbase, recognizing that they are the very foundation of this storied, multigenerational brand,” said Goldberg and Greenstein. “Under his leadership, every film and television show will be ushered from start to finish through the prism of the legions of fans who love and champion it. Jeremy’s track record exemplifies the excellence he will bring to building out DC and the many beloved characters within it.”

“DC’s pantheon of characters is among the most iconic in American popular culture. I’ve had the privilege to help bring some of the most celebrated superhero stories to life on the big screen, and I’m excited and honored for the opportunity to now lead DC Studios into its next era. The chance to steward the incredible cast of DC characters to the screen in a way that excites lifelong fans while introducing their timeless stories to the next generation is a massive responsibility that I do not take lightly,” said Latcham. “I am grateful to Dana and Josh for putting their trust in me. I can’t wait to work night and day alongside filmmakers, comic book creators, artists and storytellers of every kind, across film, television and animation, to build something ambitious for longtime DC fans and newcomers alike.”
