Lachie Neale is hoping to overturn a one-match ban for striking so he can play in the grand final. (Getty Images: Chris Hyde)

The Brisbane Lions head to the AFL tribunal to challenge Lachie Neale's ban for striking Hawthorn's Jack Ginnivan, in the hopes of freeing the dual Brownlow medallist for the grand final.

Neale's "gut-punch" on Ginnivan could mean the former Brisbane co-captain has played his last game for the Lions, as the team prepares to face Fremantle on Saturday seeking a second premiership three-peat in club history.

Neale 'a man always at the coalface': Anderson says Lions star's career meets exceptional and compelling circumstances

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

Anderson says its appropriate to consider Neale's career, saying everyone can make one minor mistake.

"He averages 27 disposals a game, and his contested possessions is nearly as many as Mr Pendlebury, despite 320 games (compared to more than 400 for Pendlebury)," Anderson says.

"Here is a man always at the coalface, involved in thousands and thousands of contests."

He argues that this record with no previous suspensions is worthy of meeting the exceptional and compelling circumstances.

Tribunal chair says no history of exceptional and compelling circumstances for intentional act

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

Jeff Gleeson KC speaks to Adrian Anderson about the use of exceptional and compelling circumstances.

"There's never been any exceptional and compelling circumstances that has been applied or upheld for an intentional act," he says.

"If it's an off the ball strike, no matter the career, no matter how unblemished the career, why is it nonetheless inappropriate to get a week's suspension?"

Neale has been 'fairest and best' in the AFL twice

Anderson arguing exceptional and compelling circumstances to turn ban into a fine

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

Anderson has officially turned to the question of exceptional and compelling circumstances.

He goes back to Neale's record, of 320 games played (and the dual Brownlow medals).

He says there are 114 players who have played 300+ games.

Of those, 72 have been suspended, for a total of 356 weeks. That's an average of 4.94 weeks.

He is also one of only 25 players to have played 320 games or more without being suspended.

Key Event

Issue of Grand Final consequences relevant to outcome of charge

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

Adrian Anderson talks about the issue of the Tribunal being "comfortably satisfied".

He refers to a case of Brigginshaw, where there is reference to satisfaction "not independently" of the nature and consequences of the facts to be produced.

This includes the gravity of the consequences, including "a defining moment in the career if he’s to miss a historic opportunity to play in a threepeat grand final", Anderson says.

He says he is not suggesting the normal rules are not to apply just because it's a Grand Final coming up.

"All we are saying is that to be comfortably satisfied when you take into account the consequences on the line ... taking into account the fact that the vision is inconclusive."

Key Event

Tribunal chair Jeff Gleeson says incident is 'well off the ball'

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

The Tribunal chair Jeff Gleeson KC is asking Anderson about the policy implications of the "gut punch" bans.

He asks him if he agrees with the concept the guidelines were made to "deter players from intentional striking".

"This is well off the ball, not in play," Gleeson says.

"More tellingly, this is not a strike that happens in the course of a grapple or a scuffle."

While not really dealing with the question, Anderson returns to the idea that this incident does not fit the idea of a "gut punch". 

"This is not a punch to the guts, which this guideline is designed to address," Anderson says.

By Andrew McGarry

Key Event

Ginnivan incident not the same as 'gut punch' incidents, says Anderson

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

Anderson continues to argue the incident is not the same as the normal "gut punch" incidents this season.

First, he says that Ginnivan's left arm and elbow are in the way of Neale's arm as it comes near.

The second difference, he argues, is that Ginnivan sees it coming.

The next difference is that Ginnivan is not doubled over, gasping, or remaining on the ground for a time.

Ginnivan gets up reasonably quickly and gives no indication of having been winded, according to Anderson.

Key Event

Anderson argues again it is not a strike

A

By Andrew McGarry

By Andrew McGarry

Adrian Anderson is arguing again, repeating the position it is not a strike.

"Whilst at first blush it does look like there's a punch to the guts, a careful, close analysis of the vision and and of what Lachie Neale says, the conclusion is more the impact to the body is more (like Neale's recollection), more like a bump or body-check.

"The impact from the left arm is insufficient to amount to a strike," Anderson continues.

"If it is enough, it is of negligible impact."
