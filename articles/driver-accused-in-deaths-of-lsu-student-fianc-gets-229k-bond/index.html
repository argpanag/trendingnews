Driver accused in deaths of LSU student, fiancé gets $229K bond, house arrest

TheTools · 19.10.2026 19:11 · source · permalink

BATON ROUGE, La. (WAFB) - A woman accused of hitting and killing an LSU student and her fiancé as they walked along Nicholson Drive was given a $229,000 bond with house arrest during a court appearance Monday, Oct. 19.

Mia Dawson, 20, faces multiple charges, including vehicular homicide and operating while impaired, in connection with the crash that killed 21-year-old Leah Victoria Fonte and her fiancé, 21-year-old Shane David Hirstius.

Dawson’s next bond review is scheduled for Dec. 16.

According to the Baton Rouge Police Department, Dawson was driving a 2016 Nissan Rogue southbound around 2:40 a.m. Sunday, Oct. 18, when she veered off the road in the 5000 block of Nicholson Drive and struck the couple as they walked along the right shoulder.

Fonte died at the scene. Hirstius was taken to a hospital, where he later died from his injuries.

Police said officers located Dawson shortly after the crash in the 1900 block of Nicholson Drive.

She was booked into the East Baton Rouge Parish Prison on charges of:

  • Vehicular homicide
  • Operating while impaired
  • Hit-and-run driving
  • Reckless operation of a vehicle
  • Vehicular negligent injuring

Victims remembered as LSU student, firefighter

Fonte was a senior at LSU studying biological sciences on a pre-dental track, according to the Third District Volunteer Fire Department.

Hirstius served as an operator and firefighter with the department, which remembered him as a dedicated first responder.

The fire department said the couple had been celebrating a friend’s birthday before the crash.

Officials call for pedestrian safety improvements

The deaths have prompted renewed calls for improvements along Nicholson Drive, where pedestrian safety has been a longstanding concern.

Mayor-President Sid Edwards said a project already underway includes a new 10-foot-wide shared-use path along the east side of Nicholson Drive between Brightside Drive and Gourrier Avenue, additional lighting and improved pedestrian crossings at Gourrier Avenue and Bob Pettit Boulevard.

Edwards said the city’s Transportation and Drainage Department plans to meet with the Louisiana Department of Transportation and Development on Tuesday, Oct. 20, to discuss additional safety measures that could be implemented before construction is complete.

Gov. Jeff Landry also called for action following the deaths.

“I am sick of seeing students and others killed while walking along Nicholson Drive at night,” Landry said in a statement.

Landry said he would work with state transportation officials, Baton Rouge leaders and LSU to explore ways to protect pedestrians.

“Enough is enough,” Landry said.

The crash remains under investigation.
