“Undriveable” – Hadjar looking for answers after “massive” struggles in Austin practice

It was a day of contrasting fortunes for Red Bull during Friday practice at the Circuit of the Americas.

Verstappen was largely satisfied with the early signs of the RB22, although he stressed that the balance between high-speed and low-speed corners still needed improvement, with technical director Pierre Waché indicating that Red Bull needed to make gains in the slower sections of the track.

Verstappen used the word ‘fine-tuning’, but things are certainly not at that stage on the other side of the garage. Hadjar had a largely positive feeling during FP1, despite finishing 14th in the standings, but decided to explore a slightly different set-up direction for the second session of the day.

“I did [make a lot of changes after FP1]. I was happy-ish with how it went this morning. Afterwards, I thought I was heading in the right direction, but it only brought a lot more problems”, the Frenchman concluded.

“I still felt okay on the medium, even though we changed the car. But on the soft, the car was completely gone for me. It was very difficult to drive, so I need to turn things around tomorrow.

“We need to sit down and see what we can do because the car made a massive step down. We need to bring it back, at least to the level it was this morning.”

Isack Hadjar, Red Bull Racing

Antonelli hails Austin FP2 "step forward"

F1 championship leader Kimi Antonelli reckons Mercedes has made another step trying to extract performance from its recent upgrade package at the US Grand Prix.

Mercedes arrived at Austin's challenging Circuit of the Americas with a new front wing option to further complement its significant upgrade package introduced in Malaysia two races ago. The initial upgrade raised the performance ceiling of the car, but also made it more peaky and harder to optimise.

With both Antonelli and George Russell now on the latest specification after splitting packages in Singapore, Mercedes' progress from first to second practice in Austin suggested the W17 is still difficult to get in the right operating window. But when the magic happens there is performance to be found, with Antonelli just 0.042s behind Ferrari's FP2 leader Charles Leclerc on Friday.

"I felt better in the car compared to FP1," Antonelli said. "There is still lots of work to do, but it was a good step forward and now we'll try to improve even further. Having both cars on the same package helps, because we can try lots of different stuff and we can have a better understanding of this package.

"Since Singapore we were able to analyse the data and understand better what this package needs. We're still trying to find how to put the car in the right window, but we've been taking steps forward and we'll see what we can do to improve even more."

Andrea Kimi Antonelli, Mercedes
