Ex-Lakewoodite Eugene O’Connor AKA Cheetah Chrome Has Died - Lakewood Observer

TheTools · 13.09.2026 22:33 · source · permalink

by Jim O’Bryan –

It was announced today that Eugene O’Connor aka Cheetah Chrome has died. He was 71.

I first met Cheetah when another Lakewood musician and lifelong friend Craig Bell was picking him up for band practice. Gene was the new guitarist. The band was Rocket from the Tombs, a proto-punk band headed by Peter Laughner. After hearing Gene play you realized he was special. Shortly after that Stiv Bators joined the band, and they both left to form the soon to be defined as “PUNK” band The Dead Boys a “Lakewood” band that was nearly as outrageous as Lakewood’s, and the world’s first punk rock band, the electric eels– the band that was listed by the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as having the first punk record ever recorded.

What most people still don’t understand about those early days and real punk bands is that it was impossible trying to harness all the energy of the sun along with youthful angst and keep these bands together. After two years The Dead Boys broke up.

Cheetah with Stiv Bators

Cheetah, along with three other Dead Boys in the band’s early days , lived for a short period in the second apartment north of Detroit on 1389 Giel. Those were indeed crazy times for all that were around. At one point they all also worked in Lakewood at normal jobs.

Cheetah Chrome

Cheetah went on to reform the Dead Boys and Rocket from the Tombs from time to time but it is hard to catch that crazy force again. After that he was a regular playing at the infamous Max’s Kansas City and CBGBs in various incarnations, forming bands with various other rock legends including Joan Jett’s band the Black Hearts.

In recent years he lived and played in Nashville and Las Vegas. In Nashville he was warmly received even doing commercial promos for Gibson guitars, and playing sessions.

The Dead Boys

What made Cheetah so special is his guitar work, which you hear with every “punk” band since Rocket from the Tombs, copying Cheetah’s guitar riffs. Billie Joe Armstrong of Green Day has often given Cheetah credit for influencing his guitar style.

Truly one in a billion. God speed, Gene.
