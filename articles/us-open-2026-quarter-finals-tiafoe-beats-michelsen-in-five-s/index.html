So Tiafoe awaits the winner of Alcaraz v Shelton – which was expected to be the match of day 10, but will now be going some to beat Tiafoe’s win – but before that we’ve got another all-American battle, between Jessica Pegula and Emma Navarro. Tiafoe defeats Michelsen 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6 (6)!

… then 9-6, three match points, as Michelsen misses his return! After four hours and 38 minutes, Tiafoe has match point … and he drills a forehand winner down the line! Tiafoe pulls up his shirt and covers his face, before quickly running to the net and consoling his beaten opponent, who’s in tears, before departing with a towel over his head. It’s gut-wrenching for Michelsen, who may still be replaying that game when he served for the match in the third set in his mind for years to come, but this is Tiafoe’s moment, he’s through to the semi-finals of the US Open for the third time, after a match that will stay with him for ever too.

… and now 7-6, but Tiafoe has the next two points on his serve, and, having turned into an ace machine in set five, he makes it 8-6!

Having not led since the first game of the match, Tiafoe now has a little bit of daylight between him and Michelsen, but at the second change of ends his advantage has been reduced to 7-5 …

Michelsen’s feet don’t move quick enough on the next point either, and Tiafoe is on top at 6-4, make that 7-4, as he flings the balls back to Michelsen’s side of the net, ready to receive …

4-3 Tiafoe becomes 4-4 … and Tiafoe resecures the mini-break with a forehand return winner that leaves Michelsen stumbling to his right in vain!

Tiafoe increases his lead to 4-2 at the change of ends .. with a pile of sweat-soaked shirts by his chair. He’s been pushed to the limit by his younger fellow American .. and they’re back on serve when Tiafoe nets his backhand …

As it’s the final set, this tie-break will be played to 10 points rather than seven, and the first four points are shared for 2-2. And Tiafoe takes the first point against serve with a rasping forehand winner down the line for 3-2!

It seemed for some time that Aryna Sabalenka would be consumed by the rage and anxiety that has smothered her for so much of this year. At 2-2 in the final set of her brutal quarter-final against Linda Noskova, a second double fault of the game damned Sabalenka to a 0-40 deficit. She instantly fell to her knees, bowed her head and screamed. She soon trailed 2-4 in the set.

Over her past five arduous months, Sabalenka has reliably unravelled under pressure. However, at the final grand slam tournament of the year, with her US Open title and No 1 ranking on the line, this was her very last chance to salvage her season. Somehow, the top-seeded Sabalenka finally had a response as she recovered to win an astonishing match and reach the semi-finals with a 7-6 (1), 3-6, 7-6 (7) win over Noskova, the sixth seed.

Before the tournament, it was clear that the intense depth at the top of the women’s game, where each of the top 11 players currently hold a big title, meant there are so many worthy title candidates. The quarter-finals lineup reflects this embarrassment of riches. Each of the top six seeds reached the last eight, with five of them being grand slam champions along with the perennial top-five player Jessica Pegula and the Olympic gold medallist Zheng Qinwen.

A meeting between the defending US Open champion and the new Wimbledon champion was top of the list of the highly anticipated tussles and it developed into one of the best matches of the year. This was a spectacular exhibition of unfiltered first-strike tennis. The quality of serving from both sides of the net was supreme, with Noskova firing down 18 aces and Sabalenka finishing with 46% unreturned serves, including a statement second-serve ace to close out a tight tie-break set. Both players fearlessly forced themselves inside the baseline, Sabalenka’s searing power contrasted with Noskova’s sweet timing and precise changes of direction.
