Bond is heading back to the big screen next month as Daniel Craig's first 007 movie turns 20

TheTools · 17.09.2026 13:17 · source · permalink

Amid the ongoing discussions about who'll be playing the next James Bond (we're still backing our favourite Slow Horse, Jack Lowden, in case you were wondering), a major 007 milestone has — like MI6's finest himself — stealthily snuck up on us and caught us all unawares. Yes, somehow it has been 20 years since Daniel Craig's blue-eyed Bond made his big-screen debut in Martin Campbell's Casino Royale, silencing any naysayers and doubters with a new grit and emotional vulnerability hitherto unseen in the spy franchise. And to celebrate the movie's anniversary, Casino Royale is heading back to cinemas worldwide in October, offering cinemagoers a chance to revisit a modern classic before Denis Villeneuve kicks off a new era for Bond in the coming years. Check out the slick announcement poster below;

From its breathless parkour chase opening in Madagascar to its high-stakes poker-based climax at the high roller tables of Montenegro, Casino Royale ushered in a new era for 007 quite unlike anything that had come before it. Not only was Craig a revelation as a blue steel Bond, but the movie also gave us an all-timer villain in the form of Mads Mikkelsen's ball-whipping, bloody-teared Le Chiffre, and a heartbreaking twist on the Bond girl archetype in the form of Eva Green's Vesper Lynd. And while the Craig era of Bond is (almost) bangers all the way, a chance to revisit the one that kicked the era off, on the biggest screen possible, isn't to be missed.

We don't yet know exactly on what dates — or indeed in what formats — Casino Royale is heading back to our screens. However, with a worldwide rollout in the works and buzz building by the day for Amazon MGM Studios' Bond 26, we fully expect this re-release to be given a licence to thrill for as long and on as big an array of screens as possible. Consider us all in to be shaken and stirred in the best way once again. *Alexa, play 'You Know My Name'*
