Oleksandr Usyk eyeing two new foes for final fight after Wilder talks collapse

TheTools · 16.10.2026 12:38 · source · permalink
  • George Glinski, Senior Boxing & MMA reporter
  • Published: 13:38, 16 Oct 2026
  • Updated: 13:38, 16 Oct 2026

Oleksandr Usyk's plans for his last dance have taken a turn.

The Ukrainian had initially been due to make his final ring walk in March next year against Deontay Wilder.

However, those conversations have since collapsed, with Wilder now locked into negotiations with Rico Verhoeven and Jai Opetaia.

According to Ring Magazine, the Ukrainian is now setting his sights on a trilogy bout with Tyson Fury and a cross-divisional clash with unified WBA and WBO cruiserweight champion David Benavidez.

Usyk has flip-flopped over a third fight with Fury since recording back-to-back victories over 'The Gypsy King' in 2024.

Of course, that fight is dependent upon Fury beating Anthony Joshua on December 11 in Cardiff.

If Fury does get his hand raised, then he will claim the WBA 'super' heavyweight title that Usyk vacated in July, adding an extra incentive to the mix.

Whether there would be as much interest in seeing the pair run it back remains to be seen, particularly with how convincing Usyk's victory was in the rematch.

However, Usyk is running out of viable options for his next assignment.

He has made it clear that he wants to clear out the biggest names from his generation, and Wilder was one of the last to tick off.

The only other standout opponent he hasn't fought is Andy Ruiz Jr, but the marketability of that fight has taken a significant nosedive after the Mexican-American's lopsided defeat to Damian Knyba in September.

A bout with Benavidez comes somewhat out of left field, though it makes for a very compelling matchup.

Benavidez has spoken previously about wanting to move up to boxing's blue-riband division to challenge Usyk.

And while he has plied his trade as low as super middleweight, his walkaround weight is well within the heavyweight bracket.

"I'm actively looking to make that [Usyk fight] happen, too," Benavidez told Ring Magazine.

"I just don't know what's going to happen first. If [Jai] Opetaia is already looking to fight Deontay Wilder, then I'll take my business elsewhere.

"If we can't get that Opetaia fight for Cinco de Mayo, then we'll do it with Usyk … It would be a shame [if the Opetaia fight is not made]."

Benavidez will have to get past Polish WBC cruiserweight champion Michal Cieslak on December 5 first if he hopes to land the Usyk fight.
