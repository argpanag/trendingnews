Yesterday Musical Secures Beatles Songs for West End Stage

TheTools · 19.10.2026 22:50 · source · permalink

The team behind the 2019 film Yesterday is developing a West End stage adaptation featuring music by The Beatles, with producer Emma Freud confirming that the band has approved the use of its songs. Freud is working with her husband, screenwriter Richard Curtis, while Oscar-winning director Danny Boyle is set to direct the production.

Freud confirmed the project in an interview with BBC Radio Suffolk on Monday, October 19, 2026, following her receipt of an honorary doctorate from the University of Suffolk on Saturday. She said the production team had selected a theater for the show but was not ready to disclose its location.

The approval marks a significant development for the musical, as Freud said The Beatles had not previously allowed their music to be used in a West End musical. The production remains in development, with the creative team refining the script, songs and staging before bringing the story to the theater.

Freud described the agreement as "a huge deal," explaining that it would allow audiences to experience The Beatles' music legally in a West End musical for the first time.

The production has already completed two workshops, with a third scheduled for October 2026, according to Freud. These sessions bring actors together to test the script, musical arrangements and staging, helping the team determine how the story will work in a live theatrical setting.

Freud said the script had reached its 11th draft, reflecting the work required to adapt a film into a stage production. Although the central storyline remains the same, the scenes and dramatic progression must be reworked for a different medium.

"We're at workshop stages, so we've already had two workshops and we've got a third one coming up this month, where you sort of ask actors to come into a room and just interrogate the script and the songs and the staging, and see how it's going to work," Freud said.

She also explained that the adaptation required more than transferring the original screenplay directly to the stage, with the creative team reshaping the material to suit live performance.

"Currently we're on the 11th draft of the script, because it isn't just taking a movie script and putting it on to a stage," Freud said.

Freud emphasized that the adaptation preserves the original story while changing how its individual moments unfold theatrically.

"It's the same story, but actually every beat of it becomes different, becomes theatrical rather than filmic," Freud said.

She described the development process as an unprecedented undertaking in her career.

"So it's been an incredible process to be a part of. I don't feel like I've ever done anything as big in my entire life," Freud said.

Released in 2019, Yesterday follows a struggling musician who wakes up in a world where nobody remembers The Beatles. He gains fame by performing the band's songs and presenting them as his own work.

The film stars Himesh Patel and Lily James, alongside Suffolk-based singer-songwriter Ed Sheeran. Production took place across Suffolk, Essex and Norfolk, including a scene filmed in Gorleston-on-Sea that involved 6,000 extras, according to the BBC.

Freud received her honorary doctorate from the University of Suffolk in recognition of her work in film and broadcasting. Her career has also included work addressing poverty and inequality, as well as serving as creative director and executive producer at Comic Relief.

She said the university's recognition held particular personal significance because of her family's longstanding connection to the county. Her family arrived in Suffolk as Jewish refugees from Germany in the 1930s, fleeing Nazi persecution.
