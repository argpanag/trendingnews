Driven: The 2027 BMW iX5 Offers a Taste of Tomorrow, Today

TheTools · 22.10.2026 19:01 · source · permalink

Hey, we all thought BMW’s turn-of-the-century 7 Series was weird, too.

The E65 BMW 7 Series landed like an alien spacecraft when it hit the streets in 2002. Competing in the stuffy executive sedan segment, it looked and functioned like none of its competitors. Like most publications of record at the time, we were heavily critical of it. We called its styling “ungainly,” complained of its “complex” electronic key and push-button start system, and disliked that its iDrive infotainment setup required studying the owner’s manual to operate. Yet despite the criticism, many of those same features would find their way onto nearly all cars—BMW or otherwise—within two decades.

Much like that now-classic 7 Series, we think the industry is going to experience a similar moment once BMW’s next-generation Neue Klasse vehicles hit the streets in full force. And having just driven the new, all-electric 2027 BMW iX5 for the first time, we’re willing to bet the midsize luxury SUV segment isn’t going to look the same going forward.

X5 vs. iX5: What’s Different?

We’ve already covered much of what makes the new 2027 X5 line so revolutionary in our First Drive of the gas-powered X5, which you can read about here. The overwhelming majority of the iX5’s features, from its retro-futuristic design and “Heart of Joy” digital architecture (which synthesizes various functions into a single system) to its panoramic infotainment display setup, are shared with all X5s, no matter what fuels them. The only real differences, then, are the powertrains—and that’s where the focus of this First Drive will be. That’s also where the electric iX5 takes the revolution a step further than the gasoline-powered models can.

Riding on the same updated CLAR platform as other X5s, the iX5 60 xDrive (the only currently available variant) swaps out the engine and associated hardware in favor of BMW’s “sixth-generation” EV tech, which promises faster charging, longer range, and bi-directional charging capability. In the iX5’s case, that means a new 144-kWh battery pack with 800-volt electrical architecture powering the company’s latest homebrewed motors. Those motors—the primary rear electrically excited synchronous motor and ancillary front-mounted asynchronous motor—combine for a healthy 570 hp and 593 lb-ft of torque, good for a BMW-claimed 0–60-mph time of 4.4 seconds.

Charging Flips the Script

More impressive than the power and quickness is the speed at which the iX5 60 xDrive can recharge and BMW’s claims around range. While final EPA results are pending, BMW says it’s aiming for 450 miles of EPA-rated range, which if successful would make the iX5 one of the longest-range EVs on the market. Even more impressive is the iX5’s charging performance. BMW also claims a 22-minute 10–80 percent recharge time on a 500-kW DC fast charger.

That 500-kW figure isn’t a typo. Although there aren’t many chargers in the U.S. capable of delivering that much power (currently just a few dozen are active nationwide, though they’re being rolled out quickly), the iX5’s peak charge rate of 460 kW is designed to take advantage of the next-generation charging systems currently being rolled out across the globe.

To show us what the iX5 is capable of from a charging standpoint, BMW staged a demo with its partners at Ionna (the charging network developer with backing from several automakers) and Alpitronic (a fast-charging tech company), temporarily converting a 400-kW charger into a 500-kW unit for a charging test. During that test, an iX5 recharged from 8 to 50 percent in just 10 minutes, peaking at over 470 kW and adding an impressive 63.4 kWh to the pack, good for about 225 miles of range. That’s a feat we’re eager to independently replicate ourselves, because if it pans out in the real world, it’s a potentially game-changing development that could help change the minds of those who remain turned off by EVs.

Most charging happens at home, though. There, the iX5 can charge at speeds up to 15.4 kW, but it also has a few more tricks up its sleeve. Various adapters will allow you to plug into the iX5’s NACS charge port and turn the SUV into a mobile power bank (the accessory can handle about 20 amps, roughly the same as a typical household circuit), plug into your home to provide power in an outage, or charge another EV should the need arise. BMW also says vehicle-to-grid capabilities, already available in Germany, could be added for the USA in the future.

A Classic BMW Drive Experience

The technical feats continue on the road, where the level of systems integration in the iX5 makes this BMW feel borderline futuristic. There’s a remarkable level of harmony and thoughtfulness between all the various interconnecting systems. The iX5 can, for instance, learn your charging habits and open the charge flap automatically as you back into a stall. Or if you’re using its hands-free driving system, you can intervene with a slight acceleration, braking, or steering correction, and the car will automatically pick up where it left off once you’re done.

Its adaptive braking system is another example. These systems are supposed to use the vehicle’s sensors to detect road conditions and traffic, automatically adjusting regenerative braking on the fly. They rarely work in a predictable manner—but the iX5’s system does. In urban stop-and-go traffic, it would happily slow itself to a full stop just based on the throttle’s position, while on the highway it’ll allow you to coast or gently slow you as you approach a curve or traffic ahead.

Of course, for those who prefer full control of their brakes (Hi there!), the iX5 has options for that, too. Set the energy recovery system into Low, and the iX5 feels just like the gas-powered X5, giving you a sensation similar to light engine braking as you get off the throttle and with a completely natural-feeling brake pedal. Medium, on the other hand, feels like you’ve wrung out a gear in a gas car to near redline and backed off before an upshift, while High feels like you’ve just downshifted while driving sportily. For those who want the true EV experience of one-pedal driving, shifting the transmission into B will get you there. Unlike many of its competitors, BMW allows you to tailor the iX5 driving experience to be exactly as you want it.

That experience, no matter how you set up the Personal default drive mode, is bound to be refined. The iX5 60 xDrive pulls off the line with authority, gently shoving you back in your seat like a V-8 would when you prod the “gas” pedal, and offering plenty of passing power when needed. It rides and handles well, too. Our test car, equipped with both the optional air suspension and four-wheel steering system, happily handled pockmarked roads and freeway expansion joints without upsetting those in the cabin. Meanwhile, on a winding back road, the suspension lowered and steering quickened, turning the iX5 into super competent if not overtly sporty canyon carver. No matter what we threw at the iX5 during our drive—urban stop and go, interstates, winding mountain roads, and long desert two-tracks—it remained a remarkably comfortable and capable vehicle to make miles in.

Speaking of, while those EPA results are still pending, the iX5 seems to live up to BMW’s promises of it being a real road-trip warrior. We weren’t exactly kind to the iX5 yet still managed about 2.5 mi/kWh over 100 miles, with roughly 25 percent of its charge expended. That implies to us that in more forgiving, steady-state conditions like you’d find on a freeway, that the iX5 should crest 400 miles without its driver worrying about that next charging stop or obsessing over their current miles per kWh average.

Taste of the Future?

It’s hard to walk away from our drive of the new X5 line without feeling like it’s a taste of what the future holds. The whole lineup oozes modernity, yet it doesn’t sacrifice the type of classical refinement that makes the midsize luxury SUV space such a popular segment. The iX5 only builds upon that, blending avant garde styling and next-generation infotainment technology with motors that don’t sacrifice performance for efficiency and batteries that can meaningfully recharge in about the same time it took you to read this article.

Like the E65 7 Series before it, the iX5 comes with some unfamiliar and potentially controversial technology, but it won’t stay unfamiliar forever. Features that once left us wondering “why?” are the very same features that we today wonder how we ever lived without. The iX5 almost certainly won’t attain instant popularity among traditionalists, but then again, the 2002 7 Series didn’t, either.
