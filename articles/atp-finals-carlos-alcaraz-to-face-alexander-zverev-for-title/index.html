ATP Finals: Carlos Alcaraz to face Alexander Zverev for title after comeback against Ben Shelton

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:58 · source · permalink

Carlos Alcaraz is through to the 39th tour-level final of his career

  • Published
    21 November 2026

Carlos Alcaraz will face world number one Alexander Zverev for the ATP Finals title after completing a superb comeback victory against Ben Shelton.

The indomitable Spaniard recovered from a flat start to outlast Shelton 3-6 6-3 7-6 (7-1) and secure a return to the showpiece, 12 months after losing to Jannik Sinner.

Alcaraz again competed with strapping around his upper-left leg having injured his hamstring earlier this week, but produced his best tennis when it mattered most.

The seven-time major winner, on a 13-match winning streak, will face Zverev in Sunday's final after the German beat Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (7-0) 6-4.

Asked how he was feeling physically, with little over 24 hours to recover for that match, Alcaraz said: "Today was a really important test. I think it went great.

"I'm probably not pushing myself to the limit. I'm realising that I need to be careful with it and sometimes I don't have to push myself to the limit, I don't have to run for every ball."

Alcaraz, bidding for his first triumph at the season-ending tournament, leads the overall head-to-head against two-time champion Zverev 7-6.

But the 23-year-old has dominated their recent meetings - winning four of the past five, including a five-set epic on his way to this year's Australian Open title.

Zverev said: "Carlos, for sure, the way he's been playing since his comeback, has been one of the best players in the world. Maybe the best player in the world.

"I look forward to tomorrow. I look forward to playing a tough match."

'Our rivalry is just getting better and better'

Alcaraz is through to his seventh final of 2026, a total unmatched by any men's singles player despite his four-month absence with a wrist injury.

The 23-year-old has been in scintillating form since his return to action at the US Open, which concluded in a quarter-final epic against Shelton in September.

He has now won 20 of his past 21 matches, sweeping back-to-back titles at the Vienna Open and Paris Masters prior to this tournament - including taking revenge on Shelton to triumph at the latter.

"I think this rivalry is just getting better and better. It's going to be a great match-up in the future," the third-ranked Alcaraz said after another close match against the world number four.

"Every time we face each other, it's a great battle. It is one of those matches that makes me be a better player and gives me a lot of feedback about what I have to improve."

Victory over Alcaraz in New York marked the biggest win of the 24-year-old Shelton's career, and a repeat of that result appeared within his grasp.

While the hamstring injury Alcaraz sustained earlier this week did not prevent him winning every set in a perfect group campaign, the Australian Open winner was second-best in a subdued start.

Shelton capitalised by blasting in a return to clinch his first break point in Alcaraz's opening service game, before withstanding two break points at 4-2 as he saw out a deserved first set.

Alcaraz's frustration grew as Shelton kept his composure to inch towards victory, maintaining a high first-serve percentage and denying his opponent opportunities.

It took Alcaraz over an hour to launch his fightback - but the former world number one struck at the perfect moment as he broke for a 5-3 lead and forced a decider.

Shelton drew applause from Alcaraz after lifting over a sublime drop shot on his way to holding for a 4-3 lead in the decider - but it was the American who blinked first in the tie-break.

After Shelton missed two forehands to fall a double break and 4-1 behind, Alcaraz guided a backhand return down the line on his first match point and let out a roar in celebration.

Zverev eyes crowning win against 'world's best'

Zverev will end a stellar 2026 as a two-time major champion, after ending his long wait for Grand Slam success at this year's French Open and US Open.

He has, however, been "annoyed" at the reaction to his rise to world number one, which has come in a year in which Alcaraz and Sinner - the dominant pair on the ATP Tour in recent years - have struggled with injuries.

Zverev's win against Shelton earlier this week was his first against an opponent ranked in the top five this season.

After defeating Medvedev, he will have the opportunity to prove his status as the current leading men's singles player.

"I think a lot of you think that I hope that good players always lose so I have an easier path, but I enjoy the challenge of playing the best in the world," Zverev said.

"I think any time it's nice to get a win against these two [Alcaraz or Sinner], because they've definitely been the best players in the world the last four years.

"For sure, they are the benchmark. I look forward to the challenge."

Zverev has not dropped serve so far at the tournament, and saved all six break points that he has faced.

The German hit 16 aces against Medvedev, with a faultless tie-break and a single break in the second set taking him to an efficient win.
