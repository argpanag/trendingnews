8 Gritty Fantasy Movies That Rival Highlander

TheTools · 08.10.2026 22:00 · source · permalink

1986's Highlander is the perfect example of a fantasy film that isn't afraid to embrace the darker, more violent side of the genre. Starring Christopher Lambert and directed by Russell Mulcahy, Highlander combines swordplay and sorcery with an interesting story that spans centuries. Part of the movie's appeal is its willingness to get violent when necessary, and it helped Highlander establish a dedicated fanbase.

For fans of Highlander, the good news is there is no shortage of fantasy movies that take the same gritty approach. The genre has produced plenty of films that take the genre in a more mature and violent direction. For viewers looking for a similar experience, the following are but a few of the best options.

The Green Knight Offers a Surreal Take On Arthurian Legend

Director David Lowery's The Green Knight is one of the best fantasy movies of the 2020s. Released in 2021, the movie is based on one of the more popular Arthurian tales, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight. Dev Patel stars as Sir Gawain, King Arthur's nephew, who accepts a dangerous challenge from a mysterious knight.

The Green Knight blurs the line between fantasy and folk horror. The world feels alive, ancient, and unsettling. Throughout his journey, Gawain has many disturbing encounters, and the film isn't afraid to explore the violence and death surrounding the Arthurian legend.

Solomon Kane Brings Robert E. Howard's Trademark Action to the Screen

Inspired by the tales of the classic Robert E. Howard character, 2009's Solomon Kane brings the author's swashbuckling puritan to life. While fans of Howard's stories will find several differences between Howard's character and James Purefoy's portrayal, the movie retains the action and supernatural horror that made readers love the character. Perhaps the biggest change is Kane's backstory.

Though Solomon Kane makes key changes to the source material, it still pulls heavily from the darker elements of Howard's fiction. The movie was intended to be a trilogy, but suffered a disappointing commercial performance. Still, it is a worthwhile watch for fans of dark fantasy.

Dark City Is a Dark Fantasy and Sci-Fi Cult Classic

Dark City follows Rufus Sewell's John Murdoch as he awakes in a city where it is always dark. The city is controlled by telepathic humanoids referred to as The Strangers. The longer Murdoch remains in the dark city, the more he begins to develop his own powers as he tries to unravel the mysteries of his new surroundings.

Dark City famously struggled at the box office but received critical acclaim, specifically from legendary critic Roger Ebert. The sets of Dark City were so impressive that many of them were repurposed for The Matrix. Over the years, the film's reputation has grown and developed a loyal fanbase.

Excalibur is the Gold Standard For Arthurian Fantasy

For many genre fans, John Boorman's Excalibur is the best King Arthur adaptation. Like many Arthurian movies, Excalibur is heavily inspired by Thomas Malory'sLe Morte d'Arthur.Boorman doesn't shy away from highlighting the darker aspects of the Arthurian myth while delivering one of the most beautifully shot and aesthetically pleasing genre movies of the era.Excalibur features a star-studded cast of established Hollywood heavyweights and future icons. Nigel Terry stars as King Arthur, and he is joined by the likes of Helen Mirren and Nicol Williamson. The movie also features Liam Neeson and Patrick Stewart before their rise to superstardom.

Blade Predates the Comic Book Craze

Wesley Snipes prepares for battle in Blade. 

Long before the rise of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, 1998's Blade brought the comic book anti-hero to the big screen with one of the best-cast leading roles of the time. Wesley Snipes was seemingly born to portray the titular character, so much so that he would reprise the role decades later in 2024's Deadpool & Wolverine. Snipes' action star and martial arts background made him perfect for Blade.Blade was considered a gamble for the studio and was produced on a budget of $45 million. The gamble paid off, as the movie became a massive blockbuster success, leading to several sequels. Blade combines a deep supernatural mythology with martial arts violence that makes it endlessly rewatchable.

The Crow Is a Gothic Tale of Revenge

Directed by Alex Proyas, 1994's The Crow was poised to launch Brandon Lee into the upper tier of action heroes. The well-known and tragic circumstances surrounding Lee's death on sethave become inseparable from the film's legacy. When the movie was eventually released, Lee became a cult icon.The Crow is one of the most recognizable gothic fantasy movies of the 1990s. The movie has a distinct visual style that pulls heavily from German Expressionism. Lee's character, Eric Draven, is murdered alongside his fiancée and returns from the dead to avenge his lover. Despite the continuation of the franchise, the original remains the most respected entry.

Conan the Barbarian Is the Ultimate Sword and Sorcery Film

The genre has been chasing the legacy of Conan the Barbarian since its release in 1982. Based on Robert E. Howard's iconic character, sword-and-sorcery purists can nitpick certain aspects of the movie. Still, Schwarzenegger's portrayal of the Cimmerian is still considered the gold standard of the dark fantasy genre.

Director John Milius simplified the character's backstory and altered a few of his traits for Conan the Barbarian, but it doesn't dampen Schwarzenegger's presence. The film spawned a generation of Conan clones that flooded the Sword and Sorcery subgenre. To this day, it is considered the undisputed blueprint for 80s dark fantasy.

King Arthur: Legend of the Sword Appeals to Dark Fantasy Fans Despite Its Financial Failure

Guy Ritchie's King Arthur: Legend of the Sword was a massive financial gamble. Released in 2017 with a budget of $175 million, the gritty rehashing of the Arthurian legend failed to recoup its budget at the box office. Despite the financial shortcomings, the movie has a lot to offer fans of dark fantasy.

King Arthur: Legend of the Sword stars Charlie Hunnam as King Arthur, and the film takes its fair share of liberties with the source material. For viewers looking for grit, Ritchie's rendition is filled with large battles and brutal sword fights. The movie features Ritchie's signature fast-paced style, and the inclusion of Excalibur offers plenty of supernatural elements to the action.
