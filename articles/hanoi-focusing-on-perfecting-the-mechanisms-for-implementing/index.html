Hanoi: Focusing on perfecting the mechanisms for implementing Resolution No. 57-NQ/TW and other resolutions of the City People's Council.

TheTools · 20.10.2026 14:56 · source · permalink

On the afternoon of October 12th, at the seventh meeting of the Executive Committee of the Hanoi City Party Committee, 18th term, Vice Chairman of the Hanoi City People's Committee Truong Viet Dung reported on the situation and results of implementing Resolution No. 57-NQ/TW dated December 22, 2024, of the Politburo on breakthroughs in the development of science, technology, innovation, and digital transformation.

According to Vice Chairman of the Hanoi People's Committee Truong Viet Dung, in the first nine months of 2026, the Party Committee of the Hanoi People's Committee focused on leading and directing the implementation of Resolution No. 57-NQ/TW, linking tasks with progress, products, and the responsibilities of agencies, units, and heads of departments. The Hanoi People's Council issued 10 resolutions on science, technology, innovation, digital transformation , and related fields; the city allowed two controlled pilot projects in the fields of autonomous vehicles and rare earth processing.

In administrative procedure reform, Hanoi city has approved restructuring plans for 1,557 out of 1,721 procedures, achieving 90.5%; integrating 1,991 procedures into common systems, including 1,608 procedures that are fully online. For 25 key procedures, the restructuring plan is expected to save approximately VND 1,731.48 billion in compliance costs annually, corresponding to an average reduction of 72.4% when implemented synchronously. The city has also announced and made public all 79 specific administrative procedures.

Digital infrastructure has been expanded throughout the city. Hanoi has completed the connection of the dedicated data transmission network to all 126 communes and wards; providing free Internet and Wi-Fi at 350 community centers and cultural houses. With over 7,000 5G BTS stations, the 5G coverage rate for the population reaches approximately 99%; 100% of communes and wards have access to fixed and mobile broadband Internet. The city has handed over 5,013 office computers, 2,375 professional computers, 7,388 licensed Windows software packages, 411 photocopiers, and 306 online meeting rooms to 126 communes and wards.

By October 8, 2026, HanoiWork had established 183 digital workspaces, created approximately 85,500 accounts for officials and employees; cumulatively recorded 156,610 meeting schedules, 9,873 approval process flows, 29,840 internal communication articles, and 2,860,000 work-related messages. The iHanoi application continues to integrate shared services and utilities, with a scale of over 6.2 million verified accounts.

Regarding data, the shared data warehouse has completed the connection and collected 13/13 priority data sources in phase 1. Land data now includes spatial information for 4,827,135 parcels; the database contains 4,781,746 land parcels, reaching 99.1%, of which 2,782,633 parcels meet the criteria of "correct - complete - clean - livable". In the health sector, the system has standardized and managed information for 9,701,297 people; 9,257,035 people have health information, reaching approximately 95.4%. The shared digital learning resource repository on Hanoi Study has nearly 800,000 learning materials, covering the curriculum from grade 1 to grade 12.

Digital transformation continues to be promoted in production, business, and daily life. The digital community model with QR codes has covered 2,734 out of 2,734 villages and residential areas in 126 communes and wards within the 100-day peak implementation period. As of September 25, 2026, 231,026 out of 431,865 beneficiaries received support for digital transformation, reaching 53.5%; 2,601 products were listed on e-commerce platforms; 796 livestream sessions promoted 3,715 products; and 426 out of 432 markets implemented cashless payment, reaching 98.6%.

In the field of science , technology, and innovation, the city has approved the List of Priority Strategic Technologies for the period 2026-2035, comprising 9 technology groups and 25 strategic technology products. By September 2026, the Department of Science and Technology had received approximately 160 proposals for project funding. Two strategic technology development projects were approved through direct assignment: Research, development, and testing of a new generation multi-purpose AI edge camera platform, and research, development, and testing of rescue ladder trucks with a reach of 32m and 56m.

According to estimates up to September 2026, the added value of science, technology, and digital transformation will account for approximately 22.85% of the city's GRDP; the proportion of added value from the digital economy will reach 16.7% of GRDP, of which the core digital economy accounts for 9.4% and the sectoral digital economy accounts for 7.3%. In 2026, the city will allocate VND 9,090.399 billion for science, technology, innovation, and digital transformation; as of October 6, 2026, a cumulative allocation of VND 8,872.40 billion has been made, reaching 97.60% of the plan...

According to Vice Chairman of the Hanoi People's Committee Truong Viet Dung, the implementation of Resolution No. 57-NQ/TW still faces some limitations, such as: some new mechanisms related to ordering technology based on output products, controlled testing, data and intellectual property valuation, and the procurement and leasing of technology services still require specific guidance. Technical requirements and methods for determining costs and evaluating technology products are unique; the capacity for consultation and coordination in implementation in some units remains limited; and the fear of risks in investment, procurement, and acceptance testing remains an obstacle…

Regarding tasks for the fourth quarter of 2026, Vice Chairman of the Hanoi People's Committee Truong Viet Dung stated that Hanoi will focus on perfecting the implementation mechanism of Resolution No. 57-NQ/TW and other resolutions of the City People's Council; clarifying the conditions, procedures, responsibilities, and methods for implementing policies supporting innovative startups, controlled testing, commissioning tasks based on output products, and other content related to data and digital products.

Regarding data and digital platforms, Hanoi aims to complete the remaining three tasks of the Data Lakehouse system before October 30, 2026; and to deploy phase 2 of the shared data warehouse with approximately 80 databases by December 31, 2026. Along with connectivity, the standardization, updating, and exploitation of data must be linked to the responsibility of the managing agency.

Hanoi continues to implement administrative procedure restructuring plans, expanding data reuse, digitizing documents, delivering electronic results, and facilitating online payments; focusing on handling overdue complaints on iHanoi, checking the quality of responses, and monitoring satisfaction levels. Platforms with overlapping functions will be reviewed, integrated, and consolidated based on digital architecture, connectivity standards, security requirements, and operating costs.

For science, technology, and innovation, the city focuses on supporting research results with potential applications, identifying recipient units, and perfecting the process of transferring and exploiting intellectual property, software, databases, AI models, and prototypes. Two controlled pilot projects continue to be monitored according to the approved scope, schedule, and safety requirements.

Hanoi city is also specifying the list of strategic technologies into tasks, products, and pilot projects; prioritizing AI, big data, AI cameras, semiconductors, UAVs, automation, robotics, smart healthcare, information security, computing infrastructure, digital city replicas, and the data economy.

Another key task is to expedite the allocation, disbursement, and effective use of funds.

The city will review and standardize budget estimates, allocation, and disbursement data; resolve obstacles related to investment procedures, budgeting, quotations, norms, technical standards, acceptance of digital products, payment, and final settlement. The responsibility of the unit using the funds will be linked to the progress, quality of implementation, and the ability to put the product into operation.

Regarding human resources, the city aims to complete digital learning materials and question banks before October 30, 2026; organize the remaining training courses and assess digital skills according to the plan. For leaders, civil servants, public employees, and workers at the commune level, ensure that at least 70% of the target group completes the course and participates in the assessment according to the assigned targets. Information security and cybersecurity will continue to be emphasized, linked to access control and protection of personal data.

Hanoi city is also focusing on completing the remaining tasks of the 100-day campaign; supporting businesses, households, and craft villages in applying digital platforms, bringing products to e-commerce sites, and promoting cashless payments. The pilot program of the four Digital Citizen stations will be evaluated based on usage levels, beneficiary groups, operating costs, and service effectiveness.
