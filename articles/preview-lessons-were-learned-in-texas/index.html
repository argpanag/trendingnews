Preview: Lessons were learned in Texas

TheTools · 17.11.2026 03:35 · source · permalink

Short-handed Warriors need a win in New Orleans

Life without Stephen Curry isn’t as fun. The Golden State Warriors have known this for a while, but there’s a fresh coat of pain to this realization as the new era tries to find their way without the Warriors’ biggest star. Like a lot of folks, those first few experiences outside of the comfort zone can really shake you.

So Yaxel Landeborg showed up to practice yesterday with his hair shaved off and a renewed understanding of just how hard finding success in the NBA is going to be.

Up tonight, one of the league’s easier targets, but it is a team that still has Zion Williams. Well… Zion, and an injury report so long that it could be a Tolstoy novel.

Will Podz and Yax be enough to handle the reeling Pelicans? Can Draymond Green emerge as a positive impact leader to help shoulder the burden of Curry’s absence? Only one way to find out!

GAME DETAILS

WHO: Golden State Warriors (8-5) at New Orleans Pelicans (4-10)

WHEN: Monday, November 16th 2026; 5pm PDT

WATCH: Peacock/NBCSBA

Injuries (there’s lots)

Being good at basketball can get you into the NBA if you’re talented enough, but there’s an extra gear that all of the great players tend to find. With all due respect, they’re all a little bit crazy. Whether it be a superhuman dedication to training or a weirdly intense desire to win, the best are just built different.

Going into San Antonio without Curry, the odds were pretty stacked. The results spoke for themselves as the Warriors got completely steamrolled. So it goes in life as it goes in the NBA: things can turn around quickly. The Warriors started the week crushing these same New Orleans Pelicans by 51 points on Monday and then ended it on Friday with an equally lopsided loss of 38 points.

They make movies about moments like this. Whether is be Morgan Freeman telling Kevin Costner’s Robin Hood to “get up. move faster” or John Witherspoon telling Ice Cube that the problem with kids today is that they don’t know how to get [beat up], 1there’s plenty of legends about the power of simply getting back up and trying harder. But we don’t need to go to the movies for this. Curry, Kerr, Draymond Green, everyone has lost. Has played in moments where sheer grit is all that’s left.

Kerr was asked yesterday about Yaxel’s new look, and spoke approvingly about how quickly the rookie understood the assignment here:

“…you have to learn as a pro how to handle this stuff. And he’s learning so many lessons right now, and it’s our job to help him. But the lesson really should resonate with the entire team because that loss was on all of us. So we got destroyed. How are we gonna respond? That’s our challenge.”

That same article (by Nick Friedell) goes on to discuss how Kerr said he spoke to the team, and Luxenborg in particular saying that he “wanted the rookie big man not to feel as if the weight of the world was on him in just his first month as an NBA player.”

“It’s not necessarily learning how to lose,” Kerr said. “It’s learning how to respond to a loss. So that’s the key.”

It’s only been a couple of games in the post-Curry world, and the players and coaches deserve some time to adjust. With the Pelicans up tonight, the Warriors get a rare chance to press the reset button. This team has had non-Curry principles cooked into it since the preseason. There will be a drop off, but there’s got to be a belief that there’s still enough here to tread water.

The Warriors have shown their hand on this already. It will go to Podz, and Yax, and also, to Draymond Green. Green is the team’s third-highest paid player. At $27.6, he makes $21 million more than Lendelborg and Podziemski. We used to post a little pie chart of this team’s salary structure when the anti-Podz noise got too loud, and the ghosts of that chart are starting to waft up from the floorboards at Chase center whispering Green’s name. With Butler and Curry out, this should be an opportunity for him to shine. Not just as a veteran, but as one of this franchise’s cornerstone.

He hasn’t historically shown up well at times like these. None of these are must-win games. The season built up enough padding with that win streak to coast into December picking up the odd wins here and there. It will be okay. Curry and Butler and Moody will come back, maybe there’s a Porzingis trade that helps right away.

In the interim? There’s going to be a lot of opportunities for players to build on their reputations in one direction or another.

Both of these teams tonight have five players out; in each case, those lists include more than one starter. But there’s also some teeth left in both of these mouths. The Warriors are hungry. That eight-win streak showed something special here, and a lot of the players that were part of that tapestry are still playing. Maybe the shots are tougher, the easy looks harder to find, but this team has a non-Steph core. Part of the reason this season has been so fun is looking at the underlying answers to what comes next for this franchise.

Basketball is a crazy sport. Tonight’s game against the Pelicans has some wild parallels. I didn’t even mean to find this quote, but the Pelicans had a four-game win streak of their own prior to that drubbing at the hands of Golden State, now they are deep in the slide.

After Monday's 51-point loss to Golden State, head coach Jamahl Mosley said his team had been "punched in the mouth" and failed to respond. Two nights later, the Pelicans suffered another embarrassing defeat, falling 145-111 to a Brooklyn Nets team that entered the game with just two victories.

Since then, the Pelicans have taken two more losses: a 31-point loss to the Memphis Grizzlies, and then a close loss to the Denver Nuggets. Oopsies.

The Warriors don’t need an answer for the Lukas and Wembys of the world right now, this team just needs to show that they can hover in the middle while waiting for their elite players to come back. A big part of this is getting beat up. The team might not have lost by a hundred, but those monumental losses - where you lose all sense of cohesion on both ends - can be debilitating.

The Warriors just got punched in the mouth. As Kerr says, the key is learning how to respond to a loss. We’ll see what happens.

The Warriors win this one. The Pelicans are one of the worst defensive teams in the league, but after losing to the Warriors by 51 are going to be playing angry.
