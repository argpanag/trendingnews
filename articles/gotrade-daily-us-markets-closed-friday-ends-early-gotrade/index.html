Gotrade Daily: US Markets Closed, Friday Ends Early | Gotrade

TheTools · 26.11.2026 06:45 · source · permalink

Share this article

Wall Street is closed for Thanksgiving tonight. The next session is a half day on Friday, after a sharp swing on Wednesday.

US stock markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early at 1 p.m. ET on Friday, according to the NYSE, a session of three and a half hours.

On Wednesday, the Dow rose 164.25 points, or 0.33%, to 50,182.95. The S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% to 23,585.80, according to CNBC. At their lows, the Dow was down more than 1,100 points, the Nasdaq 4.6% and the S&P 500 3.2%, led by AI-linked stocks.

Friday's Watchlist

Source: closing prices and daily lows from Nasdaq. Nvidia: CNBC. Deere and HP results: company releases. Black Friday: Adobe.

Friday's Key Events

Inflation and new tariffs weigh on stocks

The October PCE price index rose 0.3% for the month and 3.8% from a year earlier, both in line with expectations. Core PCE rose 3.3% year over year, slightly above the 3.2% consensus, according to CNBC.

The White House also proposed new tariffs under Section 301 on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China, the European Union and Japan, CNBC reported.

Nvidia, Deere and HP

Nvidia (NVDA) fell after S&P Global placed Nvidia's AA issuer credit rating on CreditWatch Negative. The agency cited management and governance risks, including board independence and "key man" risk, according to CNBC. Nvidia shares are down 27% in November.

Deere & Company (DE) reported fourth-quarter net income of $1.051 billion, or $3.89 per share, compared with $3.93 a year earlier. For fiscal 2027, Deere forecast net income of $4.50 billion to $5.25 billion, according to the company's release.

HP Inc. (HPQ) reported fourth-quarter net revenue of $16.0 billion, up 9.3%, and raised its quarterly dividend to $0.31 per share, according to the company's release.

Black Friday in focus

Adobe forecasts US online spending of $6.9 billion on Thanksgiving and $12.9 billion on Black Friday, up 9.2% from last year, according to Adobe's forecast released in September. Retail stocks such as Amazon (AMZN) are in focus as shopping data starts to come in.

Pre-Market Pulse 📊

There is no regular session tonight. On Wednesday, the Nasdaq fell 3.3% just after the open before recovering. CNBC linked the rebound to light trading ahead of the holiday and a blog post by Michael Burry saying his portfolio was temporarily almost entirely long.

Macro Note 📝

The PCE data reinforced expectations of Federal Reserve rate hikes in December and January, according to CNBC.

Conclusion

Wednesday showed a market that is still fragile, with indexes falling sharply and then recovering within one session. Inflation data, the tariff proposal and Nvidia news still hang over Friday's short session, and Black Friday shopping data is the next thing to watch.
