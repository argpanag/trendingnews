What are the true origins of Led Zeppelin's 'Whole Lotta Love'?

TheTools · 10.10.2026 01:00 · source · permalink

I’ve said many times that ‘Whole Lotta Love’ is the ultimate Led Zeppelin song.

It’s not their most complex, their most experimental, or their best, but if you’re looking for an entry point into the band, this is a damn good place to start. You see, what made (and still makes) Led Zeppelin stand out when compared to other rock bands is their ability not only to champion everything that makes rock such an exciting genre, but the way that they can stick influences from other styles, cultures and moments in time onto their sound and have it become something totally unique.

They’re the musical equivalent of a child who has torn all of their action figures apart and superglued pieces together to make the ultimate hero. You have Iron Man’s brain, Dr Strange’s hands, Optimus Prime’s body, Superman’s cape, and so on. ‘Whole Lotta Love’ is one of the best examples of all of this.

Firstly, you have that killer Jimmy Page riff. It’s only four notes in total, but that time signature, the melody, the way it’s so effortlessly strung together, really make for an enticing listen. As soon as that intro kicks in, all those within earshot know they’re listening to a serious rock song. Pair that with the screeching and pained vocals of Robert Plant, and one of the most chaotic (and yet effective) rhythm sections in music history, and you have yourself a great piece of rock.

Any other rock band would be happy to just leave it there, but this is a Led Zeppelin song. It wasn’t enough for the band to merely put this combination of musical components together, but they decided to add a strange atmospheric breakdown that takes people out of the rock song and plants them deep into a cinematic tale.

This section, packed with wind sound effects, strange outbursts from Plant, and even a theremin, makes the listener think that they might have accidentally skipped the song, but once you come out of this sonic tunnel, face-first into a Jimmy Page solo and then back to the familiarity of that opening riff, the pay-off is some kind of wonderful.

It begs the question, how the hell do you even come up with a song like this? Well, for a long time, it was widely accepted amongst Led Zeppelin fans that the band had the initial idea as a result of a jam on stage. That would make sense, as the band jammed a lot when they played live, and lord knows magic happened when they improvised and simply let the music flow through them. That’s exactly what they did the first time they ever played together in a small basement in Chinatown, and as bassist John Paul Jones remembered, “the whole room just exploded”.

A jam would also explain why the band were willing to put that improvised and bizarre-sounding breakdown in the middle. Yes, fans were happy to accept this explanation, and did for a long time, until Page came out and dismissed it entirely. During an interview, he set the record straight and said that a jam had nothing to do with it.

“Absolutely incorrect,” he said. “No, it was put together when we were rehearsing some music for the second album. I had a riff, everyone was at my house, and we kicked it from there. Never was it written during a gig. Where did you hear that?” 

Where did we hear that? Page found out that it came from one of the many books that have been written about the band, and told fans that they need to be vigilant when they read those things. “Oh, good. I hope it was that Rough Guide. That’s the latest one, the most inaccurate,” he concluded. “They’re all inaccurate, you know.”
