Borja Sainz wins it for FC Porto: “Focused on what’s next and united”

Borja Sainz secured FC Porto’s victory away at Gil Vicente (0-1) and, after being named MVP in Barcelos, highlighted the unity of a team with nine wins from as many league games. The number 17, who replaced Oskar Pietuszewski in the starting line-up, offered words of encouragement to his teammate and put collective goals ahead of personal milestones. After the match, he said: “We’re focused on what lies ahead and the team is very united.”

A change to the starting line-up put Borja Sainz at the heart of the decisive moment in Barcelos. Having scored the only goal of the match, the reigning champions’ player spoke of his satisfaction with the result, but also of the preparation needed to be ready to deliver when his turn comes. From the decisive strike to his confidence in the squad, that readiness was a recurring theme in his comments.

His first words were for Oskar Pietuszewski, who had been expected to start the match. Sainz coupled his delight at scoring with concern for the teammate he ended up replacing in the starting line-up.

“I’m very happy, but I want to offer Oskar some words of encouragement because, after all, he was the one who was going to start. The truth is that we’re always ready and, thanks to God and my mother, I scored a goal and we got the three points,” he said.

His personal satisfaction was thus tied to the team’s performance. Sainz presented the goal as the result of being prepared, without allowing his moment in the spotlight to overshadow his acknowledgement of his teammate.

His assessment of the trip to Barcelos was straightforward and focused on achieving their objective. Rather than dwelling on the match, the player emphasised securing the three points.

“We knew what we came here to do. We got the three points, which was what we wanted, and we’re going home very happy,” he summed up.

The result gave Porto the outcome they had been seeking on their travels. In Sainz’s view, the satisfaction came from matching the team’s intentions when they took to the pitch with what they ultimately took home.

The goal in Barcelos also added to his personal tally, but the number 17 steered the conversation away from statistics. His priority, he explained, is how he experiences the game and the enjoyment he gets from it.

“I don’t think about milestones. I just want to be happy and enjoy myself, and little by little I think I’m managing that. I hope there are many more to come,” he said.

He is keen to keep scoring, but without making individual milestones the focus of his comments. Sainz preferred to speak about taking things step by step, putting the joy of playing ahead of counting goals.

Turning to the team’s current form, he distinguished their Champions League campaign from their winning run in the league. Even so, he maintained the same expectations on all fronts and stressed the unity between those who start matches and those who come on later.

“We haven’t been having much luck in the Champions League, but we want to win every game. The team is very focused, we’ve won nine out of nine in the league and we always want to win,” he stressed. “We’re focused on what lies ahead and the team is very united. We’re working very well, we’re very united and that shows on the pitch, whether it’s those who start or those who come on. What people say outside doesn’t affect us, and the truth is that we’ve won nine out of nine.”

The strength of the group was his central message: for Sainz, unity translates into performances on the pitch, regardless of the starting line-up. His own decisive contribution in Barcelos brought that message to life, in a victory that extended Porto’s perfect league record.
