'It Had More Nuance'— Sebastian Stan Refuses to Let Box Office Numbers Define Thunderbolts*

TheTools · 27.11.2026 07:20 · source · permalink

After Avengers: Endgame, the Marvel Cinematic Universe entered a difficult stretch. Superhero fatigue began creeping into theaters, and the franchise that once turned every release into a cultural event suddenly found itself scrambling for goodwill. Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania got lost somewhere in the Quantum Realm, while The Marvels struggled to summon its audience.

Amid that creative identity crisis arrived Thunderbolts*, a dysfunctional assembly of emotionally exhausted misfits who seemingly needed a group therapist alongside their weapons arsenal. The film earned considerable affection without setting the box office ablaze.

And Sebastian Stan refuses to let financial figures diminish its accomplishments.

Appearing on the November 23 episode of the Next Best Picture podcast with Matt Neglia, Sebastian Stan addressed the commercial reception of Thunderbolts*, expressing considerable pride in what the team accomplished.

“I still felt for us it felt like a success. I know a lot of care went into that and it had more nuance to it in terms of, I guess, than some of the recent [Marvel movies],” Stan said.

The actor questioned the financial expectations surrounding Marvel, explaining that collecting upwards of $350 million remains an extraordinary achievement. Apparently, the Infinity Gauntlet of box office expectations has distorted Hollywood’s perception of money.

According to Box Office Mojo, the Jake Schreier-directed feature accumulated $382.4 million worldwide. Its opening weekend estimates stood at $76 million domestically and $162 million globally. The reception was encouraging, too. The film secured an 88% critics’ score on Rotten Tomatoes, with opening-weekend audience approval reaching 94% and an A- CinemaScore.

Having navigated Marvel’s collection of psychologically complicated superheroes, Stan is now heading somewhere that could desperately use a therapist: Gotham City.

The Winter Soldier is officially entering the Batcave’s extended neighborhood. Stan has joined Matt Reeves’ The Batman Part II, starring alongside Robert Pattinson, Scarlett Johansson, Brian Tyree Henry and Charles Dance. Colin Farrell, Jeffrey Wright and Andy Serkis will also reprise their respective roles.

Stan’s character remains undisclosed, although speculation surrounding Harvey Dent and Victor Zsasz has circulated extensively. Gotham’s criminal employment agency apparently has several vacancies. Meanwhile, the production has endured its own collection of setbacks.

Production temporarily stopped in October when Reeves stepped away to attend to a family matter. Then, Academy Award-winning filmmaker Steven Soderbergh subsequently joined the production to help complete principal photography while preserving Reeves’ creative vision.
