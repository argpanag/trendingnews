Gold falls to near 14-month low as hawkish Fed comments fuel rate hike expectations

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:18 · source · permalink

AI-translated article with transliterated names. Read original Chinese article here.

Gold prices hovered near a 14-month low on Thursday (19 November). Hawkish comments from US Federal Reserve officials reinforced market expectations that interest rate increases could continue into next year.

Gold traded around US$3,730 an ounce. Prices plunged 3.2% on Wednesday (18 November), closing at their lowest level since September 2025. Minutes of the US Federal Reserve’s latest meeting showed that almost all officials believed further rate increases would be needed in the coming months to bring inflation back to the 2% target.

Higher borrowing costs typically weigh on gold prices because the metal pays no interest.

The Fed held interest rates steady at its last meeting. Since then, officials have broadly signalled further rate increases, though they remain divided over the pace. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said on Wednesday that inflation was moving in the wrong direction and urged the central bank to consider a hefty 50-basis-point increase at its December meeting.

Gold prices have fallen about 30% since war broke out between the US and Iran in late February. Rising energy costs have pushed up inflation, prompting central banks to tighten monetary policy. Markets now expect the Fed to raise rates three times by June next year, by 25 basis points each time.

A stronger dollar has put further pressure on gold prices. The US dollar index rose 0.4% on Wednesday, making dollar-priced gold more expensive for buyers holding other currencies.

Meanwhile, gold prices have also been affected by a sell-off in artificial intelligence-related stocks. Some investors have sold their gold holdings to cover losses on other assets. Nvidia shares initially rose after the company released its earnings, but the gains quickly faded as volatility on Wall Street intensified again.
