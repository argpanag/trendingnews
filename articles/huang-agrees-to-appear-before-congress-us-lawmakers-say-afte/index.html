Huang agrees to appear before Congress, US lawmakers say, after scandal-hit Nvidia CEO resigns

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:34 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Jensen Huang has resigned as CEO of tech giant Nvidia, the company's board says, with chief financial officer Colette Kress appointed interim boss

  • US lawmakers on Capitol Hill say Huang has indicated he is willing to appear before Congress for questioning, according to the BBC's US media partner CBS News

  • The company's shares were down about 4% when US markets closed on Thursday, having recovered some ground after plummeting more than 13% after news of his resignation

  • On Sunday, Huang admitted an affair with a woman, who was reportedly from another company and suspected to be a Chinese spy

  • The New York Times has since reported that Huang had a second affair with an executive at his own company

  • Huang steered Nvidia to enormous success, and in an extraordinary and strangely old-fashioned tale for a future-looking tech firm, he was ultimately undone by his love life, our technology and AI editor writes

  • Meanwhile, Beijing has called allegations of spying a "smear" and warned the US not to use the situation as a pretext to once again restrict tech sales to China

Jensen Huang has resigned as CEO of tech giant Nvidia, the company's board says, with chief financial officer Colette Kress appointed interim boss

US lawmakers on Capitol Hill say Huang has indicated he is willing to appear before Congress for questioning, according to the BBC's US media partner CBS News

The company's shares were down about 4% when US markets closed on Thursday, having recovered some ground after plummeting more than 13% after news of his resignation

On Sunday, Huang admitted an affair with a woman, who was reportedly from another company and suspected to be a Chinese spy

The New York Times has since reported that Huang had a second affair with an executive at his own company

Huang steered Nvidia to enormous success, and in an extraordinary and strangely old-fashioned tale for a future-looking tech firm, he was ultimately undone by his love life, our technology and AI editor writes

Meanwhile, Beijing has called allegations of spying a "smear" and warned the US not to use the situation as a pretext to once again restrict tech sales to China

  1. Nvidia: The chip designer powering the AI boompublished at 22:39 GMT
    22:39 GMT

    Nvidia makes chips used in artificial intelligence (AI) systems.

    It has cemented a reputation as one of the world's premier chip designers, and is one of the most valuable companies in the world.

    A computer chip is essentially a small piece of semiconductor material, or a set of electronic circuits. It processes information and stores data.

    Nvidia is far from the only company that designs computer chips, which more broadly are used in all modern digital technology, from computers to cars to kitchen appliances. But Nvidia chips are used for many cutting-edge technologies, and are widely used in the AI industry.

    The company was founded in 1993. Before the AI boom took off, it was largely known for making the type of chips that process graphics, particularly for computer games.

    It has widely been considered a stock market darling in recent years, with the AI boom driving a meteoric rise in its share price.

    Yesterday - even after allegations that Huang had had affairs caused it to tumble, and with concerns about further AI regulation after the Democrats won Congress - the stock was trading at around $170. That's up from around $30 five years ago - an increase of around 470%.

  4. Going forward, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick will be in the spotlightpublished at 21:57 GMT
    21:57 GMT

    Bernd Debusmann Jr
    White House reporter in Washington DC

    Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia is not the end of the scandal, which will continue to reverberate around Washington for days, weeks and possibly months to come.

    Going forward, much of the focus will be on the Trump administration's Commerce Secretary, former Cantor Fitzgerald president Howard Lutnick.

    According to the New York Times, Lutnick clashed with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent over his handling of Megaspeed, the Singaporean data centre firm that was led, in part, by the woman alleged to be a Chinese spy with whom Huang reportedly had an affair.

    While the commerce department has denied the clash took place, the report starkly highlights tensions within the Trump administration over its AI policy. A particular focus is technology export rules, which are overseen by the commerce department, and the over $4bn of Nvidia technology purchased by Megaspeed.

    Even before today's resignation, House Democrats had publicly demanded that Lutnick be called to answer questions about the administration's AI and export control policies, which they have claimed endanger US national security.

    It has also been reported by Axios was sent a highly classified memo regarding Huang's affair and the alleged connection to Chinese intelligence.

    Lutnick has so far not commented on the issue, and Trump has suggested only that Apple's Tim Cook take the reins of Nvidia.

    21:38 GMT

    US Attorney General Todd Blanche says the Department of Justice has not yet drawn any conclusions about whether or not Alice Huang, the woman Jensen Huang is alleged to have had an affair with, is a Chinese spy.

  7. Nvidia needs to bring in 'innovator CEO,' analyst sayspublished at 20:49 GMT
    20:49 GMT

    Technology analyst Patrick Moorehead tells the BBC the shake-up at Nvidia is more of a "stock shock" affecting governance and the markets, rather than a "business shock" that could affect supply and demand for Nvidia's AI chips.

    The real question, Moorhead says, isn't whether Nvidia survives, it's "about how they keep inventing at Jensen Huang's pace".

    "They need to bring in an innovator CEO to give a higher confidence to investors and their customers for years three, four, and five," he says.

    Moorhead expects a pretty severe effect on the technology export market, along with further restrictions on bilateral trade or advanced AI capabilities.

    "We're going to see a gate shut between the US and China bigger than it is now," he says.

    He also suspects that current laws that allow service to Chinese companies out of remote companies will also be tightened.

    "That door will be closed in the not-so-distant future," he says.

  9. Nvidia caught between US security concerns, China's ambition and the global AI development racepublished at 20:18 GMT
    20:18 GMT

    Michelle Fleury
    New York business correspondent

    With Jensen Huang out as Nvidia CEO, it is worth stepping back and looking at the bigger story. This is about more than one executive or one company.

    The suspected spying scandal raises important questions about the relationship between the US and China, the limits of export controls and the growing influence of big technology companies on government policy.

    Nvidia has become a central part of that debate. The Trump administration allowed the company to resume some chip sales to China in return for a share of the revenue.

    Nvidia agreed to pay the US government 15% of sales of its H20 chips and 25% of sales of its more advanced H200 chips.

    The arrangement raises questions about how much influence Nvidia had over the rules governing its own business, and where the interests of a private company end and those of the US government begin.

    It also shows the risks of putting a company at the centre of a national strategy. Nvidia is now caught between Washington's security concerns, Beijing's ambitions and the global race to develop Artificial Intelligence.

    The wider lesson is that when governments turn private companies into national champions, they also expose them to risks that go well beyond business.

  11. Commerce Department asked to keep all records relating to Nvidia chip decisionspublished at 19:37 GMT
    19:37 GMT

    Rachel Clun
    Business reporter

    Democrats on key congressional committees have asked the Commerce Department and its secretary Howard Lutnick to keep all records relating to decisions to allow Nvidia to sell its advanced AI chips to Chinese companies.

    Ways and Means Committee Ranking Member Richard E. Neal and Senate Finance Committee Ranking Member Ron Wyden are starting investigations over the way Nvidia was allowed to sell these chips.

    18:49 GMT

    US lawmakers on Capitol Hill say that Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang has agreed to appear before Congress for questioning, according to the BBC's US media partner CBS News.

    "I think right now our goal is to get him in as soon as possible, so that we have a full understanding of the facts and get to the bottom of the truth of this situation," Moolenaar says.

    Huang has said he is committed to supporting America's national security concerns, and the Nvidia board has said it has no reason to believe that either national security or company secrets were compromised, and that it is investigating reports that Huang had a close relationship with an alleged Chinese spy.

  15. Singapore probed Alice Huang and banned her from the countrypublished at 17:45 GMT
    17:45 GMT

    Tessa Wong
    Asia Digital Reporter, Singapore

    Days after the Nvidia scandal first broke, the Singapore government has given a statement regarding the suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang and Megaspeed, the Singaporean company she was associated with.

    The statement says that Huang was never investigated for espionage.

    However, she was investigated last year as part of a probe into several companies including Megaspeed, over potential false declarations in relation to the purchase of computer servers.

    Authorities said they took her passport in June 2025 to ensure she could not leave Singapore during the investigations.

    In February this year, authorities concluded there was insufficient evidence that Huang had committed offences regarding the potential false declarations.

    However, they found she had "provided information which she knew to be inaccurate... to downplay her role in Megaspeed".

    Authorities gave her a stern warning and banned her from Singapore. They returned her passport on 26 February, and she left the same day.

    Earlier, Megaspeed published a statement saying the company "takes its compliance obligations under US export control laws extremely seriously".

    The BBC has approached the company for further comment on the statement from the Singaporean government.

  17. Nvidia's next CEO will have more oversight as company chair appointedpublished at 17:00 GMT
    17:00 GMT

    Dearbail Jordan
    Senior business and economics reporter

    Tucked inside the announcement of Jensen Huang’s departure and his temporary replacement, is the news that Nvidia has appointed its first-ever chair.

    This is a big deal. Part of a company chair’s responsibility is to have oversight of the chief executive. Nvidia has gone without one for more than 30 years.

    Huang often lauded the company’s “flat” management structure. “If you want to empower people, you want to make the organisation as flat as possible so that information travels quickly,” he once said.

    Huang had dozens of people reporting to him, which may well have sped up the flow of information and decision-making but also resulted in an extraordinary concentration of power in one man’s hands.

    Now, Nvidia’s new boss, whoever that ends up being in the long term, will have someone overseeing them - in this case, Harvey Jones, a tech industry veteran who was the former boss of Synopsys, a chip design software maker.
