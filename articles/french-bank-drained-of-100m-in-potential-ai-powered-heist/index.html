French Bank Drained of €100M in Potential AI-Powered Heist

TheTools · 15.11.2026 19:55 · source · permalink

French banking and insurance group Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, which includes Crédit Mutuel de Bretagne, Crédit Mutuel du Sud-Ouest, and online bank Fortuneo, has been hit by a major cyberattack. Attackers exploited a compromised third-party service provider to infiltrate the bank’s infrastructure and siphon off approximately €100 million (about $112 million) from the organization’s accounts using instant payments. The bank’s chairman described the incident as a “new type of robbery” and stated that artificial intelligence was very likely involved, though he did not provide specific technical details at this time.

The security breach originated outside the bank’s network. On October 27, Crédit Mutuel Arkéa detected signs of compromise in a third-party provider’s systems, severed the connection, and notified authorities. At the time, the bank had no reason to believe that data had been stolen to facilitate a subsequent breach of its own IT infrastructure. The name of the provider has not been disclosed for security reasons to avoid putting similar companies at risk.

Once inside the system, hackers generated fake Fortuneo IBANs and used them to initiate payment orders from Crédit Mutuel Arkéa accounts to external destinations via instant transfer systems. According to Julien Carmona, these systems have fewer security checks compared to traditional transactions. The transfers began on Sunday, November 1. The funds were sent to legitimate accounts at other European banks that the fraudsters had opened in advance, after which the money was moved to cryptocurrency platforms to cover their tracks.

The scale of the fraud was discovered on the morning of Tuesday, November 3. The bank immediately blocked external access to its information system, disconnected from the interbank payment system, deleted the fake accounts, and updated credentials. Fortuneo systems and customer accounts were not affected. Blockchain experts noticed anomalous transactions as early as Sunday and Monday, and on-chain analyst ZachXBT recorded suspicious withdrawals totaling over $106 million on November 2, later raising the estimate to over $133 million and identifying Crédit Mutuel Arkéa on November 4. Analytics firm Bitquery tracked $108.8 million in USDC moved through Kraken, Crypto.com, and OKX within 21 hours.

Carmona described the attackers as a highly organized and well-equipped criminal group acting with the support of advanced artificial intelligence. No group has yet claimed responsibility for the incident, and the bank has not provided evidence of AI usage. Most of the methods employed—contractor compromise, credential theft, fake payment orders, money mules, and crypto laundering—existed long before the mass adoption of AI. While artificial intelligence could have assisted in reconnaissance, coding, or creating documents to open accounts, there is currently no confirmation of this from the bank’s management.
