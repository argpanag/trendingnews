NPA Ladies Gala backs local students | Cape York Weekly

TheTools · 18.10.2026 20:20 · source · permalink

The 2026 NPA Ladies Gala sent a strong message of support to local students with a more than $9800 donation to the Northern Peninsula Area State College. Photo: Supplied.

The 2026 NPA Ladies Gala has delivered a major boost for local education, raising more than $9800 to support Northern Peninsula Area State College (NPASC) students preparing for life beyond school.

Hosted by Cape York Camping Punsand Bay, the 13th annual event brought together more than 65 women from across the community for a night of dancing on the beach, entertainment and fundraising, with proceeds to support Year 11 and 12 vocational pathways.

NPASC principal Andrea Killen said the funds would be used to give students greater access to opportunities as they prepare to transition from school into the workforce.

While the school is still considering exactly how the money will be spent, she said potential uses included resources for vocational programs and excursions for students to develop practical skills.

“We’ve got four main pathways that we’re really in the process of building and strengthening; some of those pathways are already quite strong, others we’re growing, and it’s been quite a focus over the last couple of years,” she said.

“The pathways at the moment are hospitality and tourism, we’ve got the maritime pathways, community services, which encompass health and education, and agriculture programs.

“I think it’s important for students to feel like what they’re doing in Year 11 and 12 has actually got a purpose for them, and that they can see it is going to be a pipeline into working in local industry, and to have careers within Northern Peninsula Area and beyond.”

Ms Killen said the gala’s fundraising efforts were incredibly meaningful not just for the school, but for the students themselves.

“I think it’s really encouraging to see community support our youth,” she said.

“Those partnerships that the school and community and businesses like Punsand Bay can have, that’s going to enrich their time at school, but also the opportunities they have when they step from school into community roles and being active citizens.

“I think it really sends not only a message to me that we’ve got support out there within the community, it actually sends a really powerful message to our students as well, that their community believes in them, values them and wants to see them succeed.”

Cape York Camping Punsand Bay manager Kahlia Colquhoun said supporting young people was particularly important in the remote region, where workforce challenges made training and skills development essential.

Being a hospitality and tourism operator herself, she also believed the skills could benefit students well beyond the industry in a wide range of careers.

“No matter what sort of career you take, there’s always a little bit of customer service involved with most industries, so I think it’s fantastic that they’ve got some money to use towards building those skills,” she said.

“They’re the next generation of workers coming through, and I think it’s really important to put some time and energy into the local community, and especially the kids.”

Having recently become a mum and juggling returning to full-time work, Ms Colquhoun said she was initially on the fence about putting the gala on this year, but made the decision to go ahead with it after high interest from the community.

“It shows to me that there is a need for this in the community; it gives the ladies something to do, it’s an excuse for us to get dressed up and let our hair down, and raise a bit of money for a good cause,” she said.

“We got a lot of new faces this year, but we also got a lot of return guests to the evening, which for me, shows that we must be doing something right.”

She thanked the generous sponsors and dedicated staff who made the evening possible.

“It’s the end of the season for us, and a few of our staff were planning to head off because it’s gotten quiet for us, but hung around just to help with the event,” she said.

“We’re really grateful to everyone that was involved. A big thank you to all the sponsors and staff, because without them, we wouldn’t be able to do what we do.”
