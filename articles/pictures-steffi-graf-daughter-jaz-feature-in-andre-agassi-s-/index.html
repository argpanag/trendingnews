Andre Agassi recently gave his fans a glimpse into his family life by sharing a photo dump featuring his wife, Steffi Graf, and daughter, Jaz Elle. The post also featured special appearances of Spanish tennis star Carlos Alcaraz and New York Knicks center and forward Karl-Anthony Towns. The carousel of pictures began with his appearance at the 2026 US Open, where he took part in a star-studded exhibition event. He joined hands with Andy Roddick against Roger Federer and John McEnroe. He was seen giving autographs on the US Open posters. He later shared a picture of a stack of books, including his, Billie Jean King’s autobiography, and Roger Federer’s biography. He was seen talking with Carlos Alcaraz, whom he seemed to have met during the Laver Cup. The former World No. 1 also spoke with Billie Jean King and appeared to be shooting with New York Knicks forward Towns. The eight-time singles Grand Slam champion also spent time with the ball kids. He was accompanied by his wife and daughter at the Laver Cup, as seen in the post. His daughter also stole the spotlight, wearing an orange top with ‘Agassi’ written in bold black letters. Andre Agassi served as captain of Team World at the 2026 Laver Cup, held from September 25 to September 27, 2026, at The O2 Arena in London. His team lost to Team Europe, 13–5.

Andre Agassi once opened up about his parenting approach and fatherhood

Andre Agassi has frequently spoken about the difficult childhood he endured as his father relentlessly pushed him towards a career in tennis and sent him away from his family to train at a very young age. When Agassi and his wife Steffi Graf became parents, they promised to give their children Jaden Gil and Jaz Elle a different upbringing, without the pressure he faced growing up.

In a 2019 interview with Virgin Media Television, Agassi opened up about fatherhood, calling it his biggest responsibility. He and Graf wanted their kids to pursue their own paths and never forced them towards tennis.

“Fatherhood is everything,” he said. “My greatest responsibility is raising them. As they get older, the decisions get a little bit more precarious and the worries get less but more intense. I have parented differently from my father — I wouldn’t wish that parenting experience on anyone I cared about, because it came with its pains, despite its intentions. I’ve tried to take the best from it, and teach them the things I held on to that were good.”
