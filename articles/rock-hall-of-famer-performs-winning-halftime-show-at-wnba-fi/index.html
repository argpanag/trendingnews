Rock Hall of Famer performs winning halftime show at WNBA Finals Game 7

TheTools · 01.11.2026 01:25 · source · permalink

Breaking News

MC Lyte performed at Chase Center

MC Lyte, an immensely talented rapper who ranks as one of the true pioneers of hip-hop, was in legendary form at Game 7 of the WNBA Finals on Saturday (Oct. 31).

MC Lyte, who is part of this year’s Rock and Roll Hall of Fame class, delighted fans with a short halftime show during the Halloween contest at Chase Center in San Francisco that would decide the 2026 WNBA champion.

Taking the stage with the score paused at 30–24 in favor of the visiting Atlanta Dream over the hometown Golden State Valkyries, MC Lyte sounded strong as she performed a set that included the big fan favorites “Cold Rock a Party” and “Paper Thin.”

The Brooklyn rapper was the first of the four halftime acts during the WNBA Finals at Chase Center to not hail from the Bay Area — with the other three being San Francisco’s Larry June (Game 1), Vallejo’s LaRussell (Game 2) and Oakland’s En Vogue (Game 5).

MC Lyte’s many claims to fame include becoming the first female rapper to release a full-length studio album — the groundbreaking “Lyte as a Rock” — in 1988. Also, MC Lyte was part of the supergroup known as Stop the Violence (also featuring KRS-One and others). The group’s “Self Destruction” was reportedly the inaugural chart-topper on the Billboard Hot Rap Singles chart.

Over the years, she’s collaborated with Aerosmith, Beyonce, Jay-Z and many other top acts.

Her most recent album is “1 of 1,” her ninth full-length platter, which hits stores in 2024.

Glide Ensemble from Glide church in San Francisco performed a solid version of the national anthem prior to the Game 7 tip off.

Alt-rock great Alanis Morissette, talented Bay Area R&B vocalist Goapele and Fifth Harmony star Dinah Jane sang the national anthem at the previous three WNBA Finals games held at Chase Center.

The national anthem performances at these WNBA Finals have generated much controversy and chatter online, with many people — including President Trump — chiming in that it’s disrespectful for the two teams to leave the court while the song is being sung. The teams were once again not on the court during while the anthem was performed on Saturday.

This practice is nothing new in the WNBA, and for these two teams, but it has obviously garnered more attention than usual during these nationally televised Finals games.
