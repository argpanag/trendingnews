All Blacks v Wallabies, Bledisloe Cup second test, 6pm, Saturday, October 17 at Accor Stadium, Sydney

The All Blacks kept their unbeaten run at Eden Park alive and retained the Bledisloe Cup with a dominant victory over Australia, but will look to keep that form going as both teams meet in Sydney.

Despite being unable to win the Bledisloe Cup for the 24th straight season, the Wallabies can feel positive about facing an All Blacks side weakened by injuries.

Asafo Aumua, Anton Segner, Ethan Blackadder and Leroy Carter were all injured at Eden Park, with replacements being called in to shore up the squad with an already long list of absences.

Richie Mo’unga and Fehi Fineanganofo were ruled out in the run-up to last week’s test, while Ruben Love is having ankle surgery which keeps him out for the rest of the year.

Josh Moorby has been brought in to replace Leroy Carter, while Fabian Holland takes Josh Lord’s place and George Bower plays in place of Ethan de Groot.

Cortez Ratima, Samisoni Taukei’aho and Du’Plessis Kirifi are all expected to feature in Sydney off the bench.

Meanwhile, the Wallabies will be without midfielder Len Ikitau, who suffered a concussion at Eden Park, and outside back Jordan Petaia, who has an ankle injury.

The All Blacks have won their past 12 tests against Australia.

All Blacks v Wallabies referee

England’s Luke Pearce has been promoted from his duties as assistant referee last week and takes centre stage for the Bledisloe test in Sydney. He might be a good luck charm for All Blacks coach Dave Rennie, having taken charge of his first game in the job against France, which the All Blacks won 34-32. His two sideline assistants are Ireland’s Andrew Brace and Damian Schneider of Argentina, while South Africa’s Marius van der Westhuizen is the television match official (TMO) and Mike Adamson of Scotland is the split-screen official.
