Storms to hit both coasts, threatening Thanksgiving travel for millions and Macy’s 100th parade | CNN

TheTools · 23.11.2026 04:57 · source · permalink

Mother Nature could crash Thanksgiving this year by bringing storms on the East and West coasts that will amplify king tide woes as they look to snarl travel and holiday plans.

🌧️ Track the storms in the CNN Weather app

Both will deliver widespread impacts in the form of heavy rain, snow, strong winds and severe coastal flooding. Impacts in the East will come mainly on Wednesday and Thanksgiving Day while the West’s has already begun. By midweek, tides could near records and threaten airports in the San Francisco Bay area.

Here’s what to expect this week.

Impactful storm to rake up the East Coast

The East Coast’s storm will get its act together Wednesday when it forms just off the Southeast. That gives travelers a window early this week to beat its worst impacts.

The new storm will be the second to traverse the East Coast since last week when the first set off disastrous flooding along Florida’s Atlantic Coast. Some residents were still wading through floodwater over the weekend. Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis announced Monday the state has asked for an additional major disaster declaration to free up federal resources.

More rain could bring fresh flooding to parts of Florida still soaked from last week’s storm. A flood watch is in effect for Brevard County on the Space Coast, the Treasure Coast and Okeechobee County from late Tuesday night through late Wednesday night. Flooding also continues along the St. Johns River. The Florida State Guard and National Guard have positioned themselves to help with water rescues, food and water distribution and the placement of thousands of sandbags.

The storm brewing this week won’t produce rainfall as extreme as last week’s, but it will coincide with the highest tides of the month and produce serious coastal flooding from the Carolinas northward. Rain could begin as early as Tuesday night in South Florida but the chances for a wet day in the Southeast and parts of the Carolinas really start to increase throughout the day Wednesday as the storm strengthens. Winds will also ramp up Wednesday across the region as the storm builds.

Major flooding is possible in Charleston, South Carolina, during Wednesday morning’s high tide as winds drive already-swollen water levels even higher. Moderate to Major coastal flooding is also likely as far north as the Chesapeake Bay. Farther north, coastal flood watches stretch from Delaware to New Jersey. Thursday morning’s high tide poses the greatest threat there, with moderate flooding expected and major flooding possible.

The storm will hit its stride Wednesday night, bringing soaking rain and gusty winds to the Carolinas, especially along the coast, as it moves north. Driving could be dangerous in both states with pockets of flash flooding possible. Areas from Charleston north to North Carolina’s Outer Banks are most at risk.

Winds will also start to ramp up Wednesday night north of the storm into more of the Mid-Atlantic and parts of the Northeast. A high wind watch is in effect for most of the New Jersey coast, where up to 60 mph wind gusts are possible.

Wednesday night into Thanksgiving morning will also see wet and windy conditions expand into parts of the Tennessee and Ohio valleys and portions of the Midwest. Periods of snow could also mix in across some of these areas.

Locations from central Missouri to central Indiana could see a few inches of snow as cold air rushes south behind the storm Wednesday evening.

Air travel for any last-minute passengers could be disrupted late Wednesday or on Thanksgiving anywhere from the Carolinas to the major hubs around New York City and potentially as far west as Chicago.

Rain is likely to reach Washington, DC, before daybreak Thursday, making for a wet Thanksgiving morning drive. Philadelphia could stay dry overnight, but rain is expected there on Thanksgiving Day and in New York City mainly during the afternoon. Boston has a longer dry window, with rain chances picking up toward Thursday evening.

Widespread flash flooding is unlikely for this busy portion of the Interstate 95 corridor Thanksgiving Day, but travel could be slow-going on roads as wind-driven rain falls and ponds.

Some areas from Virginia to New York’s Long Island could deal with minor to moderate coastal flooding at each high tide Thursday, outside of any flash flooding concerns. Water could cover low-lying roads even without any rainfall.

In New York City, wind could put the giant balloons in the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade in jeopardy. Widespread gusts of 25 to 35 mph are forecast across the New York area by Thursday morning.

City rules call for grounding the giant balloons if sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts top 34 mph. The question is whether winds along the Manhattan parade route reach those speeds between the 8:30 a.m. start and noon finish. We’ll know more by midweek when the storm forms.

Farther north, Boston could have a breezy but largely dry Thanksgiving. Rain looks to hold off there until Thursday night, but it could become quite heavy overnight and into early Friday morning.

The drive home should turn drier around Washington, DC, by Friday, though it could still be gusty in spots. Rain and high-elevation snow showers could linger into Saturday in northern New England.

West Coast storms and coastal flooding

A potent storm swirling off the Northwest coast on Monday is already escalating coastal flooding concerns for the West Coast as king tides — exceptionally high tides that occur a few times each year — build.

The highest tides along the West Coast are expected Tuesday through Thanksgiving, and in some areas, they could challenge records.

These king tides would be concerning even without the rounds of rain and wind expected to impact the West Coast early this week. The first will drench parts of the Pacific Northwest late Monday. Winds will pick up in coastal and higher elevation areas south into parts of Northern California overnight.

By Tuesday, rain and wind will stretch from western Washington into California’s Bay Area.

The Bay Area could pick up a quarter-inch to an inch of rain through Wednesday. Gusts could reach 40 mph along the coast and through some mountain passes. Extensive coastal flooding is the metro area’s major concern as holiday travelers hit the roads and take to the sky.

A coastal flood warning is in effect there through Thanksgiving, with the greatest threat around each morning’s high tides.

Wednesday morning is the time to watch. Water at the San Francisco tide gauge could come within inches of its 1983 record close to 11 a.m. PT. High tide can arrive up to 90 minutes later elsewhere in the bay.

San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie warned the city faces an “extremely serious” situation and implored residents to rethink their holiday plans and avoid nonessential travel through Wednesday afternoon.

“I know there’s a lot of travel for many of you as you head into the Thanksgiving holiday, but I’m going to ask you all to consider not traveling unless it is absolutely essential starting Monday afternoon and going into Wednesday afternoon,” Lurie said in a video posted to Instagram.

Officials at the San Francisco and Oakland airports, which are both along the bay and low-lying, say they are prepared for the high water.

Bay Area residents have recorded massive waves slamming into the coast. Vincent Erik Roa, a resident of Pacifica, shared a video on Facebook of a wave crashing into a cliff in front of his house, saying his house shook and the glasses in his cabinets rattled with the strength of the impact.

A second storm is set to dive into the Northwest on Thanksgiving Day, bringing another batch of heavy rain to Washington and western Oregon along with some snow in the Cascades. That storm could linger in the region through Friday before pushing into the northern Rockies on Saturday.

CNN’s Elizabeth Wolfe contributed to this report.
