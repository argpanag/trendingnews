Reece Walsh (right) was a constant threat for the Kangaroos. (Getty Images: Ian Hitchcock)

The Kangaroos finished the group stage of the Rugby League World Cup undefeated, closing out the Pool A action with a 66-6 win over Cook Islands in Townsville.

On a sweaty night up north, the Australian men were below their best to start and briefly trailed the Islanders 6-4 before asserting their dominance with 10 unanswered tries.

Wingers Tom Trbojevic and Jack Bostock both finished with hat-tricks while Tino Fa'saumaleaui grabbed a double.

Fullback Reece Walsh was named player of the match after a try, five assists and a pair of line breaks, but his fitness is somewhat concerning.

Walsh grabbed at his foot after converting Kotoni Staggs's 27th-minute try and, despite looking untroubled while finishing off a spectacular team try on the half-hour mark, handed over goal-kicking duties.

Cameron Munster kicked goals for the rest of the game and Walsh closed out an impressive performance that will likely quiet calls for him to be replaced by James Tedesco.

The Kangaroos will play their semifinal on Sunday, November 8 at the Sydney Football Stadium, but their opponent will not be known until after the clash between Tonga and Samoa tomorrow night.

What did we learn tonight?

J

By Jon Healy

By Jon Healy

A 60-point win probably isn't the greatest learning experience for a football team, but I think we got the answers to a few questions.

  1. Reece Walsh will be the fullback. The Kangaroos can carry James Tedesco as a luxury on the six-man bench in case Walsh gets hurt or is running a little cold, but Walsh was electric in attack once again tonight and can create so much out of nothing.
  2. Tom Dearden is the halfback. He was superb tonight and looked like the man of the match after the first half. Even after the attack went away from him a bit, he was still able to impact with his defence. Sorry Sam, but the Cowboys skipper is going to stay in the seven.
  3. The lack of back depth is scary. Every time injury-prone Tom Trbojevic looks a little proppy, a lot of hearts go into mouths because there is zero legitimate wing cover in this squad. He's barely a winger himself.
  4. Ethan Strange could have a role to play. Without Cameron Murray, the debutant came on through the middle and made a real impact. His leg speed and ball playing gave them an extra dimension. Can he co-exist in a team with four locks in it already? Perhaps not, but an excellent debut nonetheless.
