'Tonight, we can be as one…'

Five unforgettable moments from U2's NFL Touch Down. 

Last night the band played to 70,000 football fans at SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles - beamed to a global televised audience - during Netflix's first NFL Thanksgiving Eve halftime show when the L.A. Rams faced the Green Bay Packers.

We asked US music writer, and longtime fan, Kristi York Wooten to send us her highlights.

The opening chant of 'I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For'
As Adam, Bono, Edge and Larry crossed the field to a stage resembling an inverted bottle cap, a ring of congregants dressed in black chanted the opening lines of the hymnlike hit from 1987's The Joshua Tree. The spareness of the words provided a dramatic entree into a ten-minute medley of poignant — and pointed — messages.

'Sunday Bloody Sunday' meets 'American Obituary' (and Motown)
Larry's first drumbeat sounded the clarion call. Then the band charged the gate: 'How long must we sing this song? Tonight, we can be as one'. Edge seared the solo, and a new camera angle followed a drumming corps on the field, snares snapping and lyrics spitting from the Days of Ash EP track, 'American Obituary,' about the ICE killings in Minneapolis earlier this year.
The Womack Sisters appeared, dressed in white and singing The Supremes' 1965 chart topper, 'Stop! In the Name of Love.' I read this as a nod to the American Civil Rights Movement's own Bloody Sunday.


Dolly Parton and the return of the curtain screens
In a breathtaking echo of the 2002 Super Bowl show, fabric panels unfurled from the roof and projections of Dolly Parton's face covered the sheer curtains as the band accompanied a recording of her singing 'Torn,' from the new album Carnaval De Luz. A hush fell over the stadium as her smile stretched across the stands. In the days since her passing in August, the universally beloved Parton has symbolized the power of unity after a decade of division.


'Where The Streets Have No Name' in the round
Has Side A, Song 1 from The Joshua Tree sounded this good, ever? The Sphere in 2024? The 2002 Super Bowl? Arizona's Sun Devil Stadium in 1987? This night was up there with the best. As the chanters marched, counter-clockwise around the stage, a drone swooped from the sky and whirled around the band in time with the circular rhythm of Edge's guitar and Adam's bass thrumming. A thrilling reprise.


The return of 'Pride (In the Name of Love)'
When U2 played the Super Bowl in 2002, America was healing after 9/11. In 2026, the country is healing from political division. U2 performed this Unforgettable Fire standard at the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame ceremony earlier this month, and its reappearance at this NFL halftime show bolstered my theory that 'Pride' is arguably the most influential song in their catalog. The Womacks joined the band for this part of the medley, and Bono changed a lyric to what sounded like "sisters to overthrow".
Sitting in front of my TV in Martin Luther King Jr.'s hometown of Atlanta, I couldn't help but think of the history of this song and how it resonates today, especially after the Nov. 3 elections in which Black women won races for 7 of the 8 top positions in Georgia, including governor.


Thanks, Netflix, for reminding us on Thanksgiving how grateful we are for music with purpose, hope that keeps us going, and fifty years of a band that still has so much to say.
