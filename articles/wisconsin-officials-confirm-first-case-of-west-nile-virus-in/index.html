Wisconsin officials confirm first case of West Nile virus in human this year

TheTools · 16.09.2026 00:13 · source · permalink

BROWN COUNTY, Wis. — One person in Wisconsin is infected with West Nile virus, according to Brown County Public Health.

This is the first WNV case in a person in the state this year, per the health department. The person was hospitalized, according to the Wisconsin Department of Health Services.

Wisconsin DHS reported 27 WNV cases in Wisconsinites in 2025, along with 25 hospitalizations and one death.

Most people who contract WNV do not experience symptoms, per the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Within two to 14 days of being bitten, some patients will have mild, flu-like symptoms. In rare cases, someone may become severely ill and need to go to the hospital for treatment, according to the CDC.

If you think you have WNV, contact your healthcare provider. 

“Anyone can get sick if infected with West Nile virus,” said Anna Nick, health officer and administrator for Brown County Public Health. “However, people who are older, have certain chronic medical conditions or have weakened immune systems are at higher risk of severe illness.”

Public health officials said Wisconsinites should take extra precautions to protect themselves from WNV. Officials recommended using insect repellent, wearing long and loose-fitting clothing, keeping doors and windows closed and staying indoors at night. Additionally, getting rid of areas in your yard with standing water — such as bird baths and clogged gutters — can deter mosquitoes from making a breeding ground near your home.

On Tuesday, Wisconsin officials also confirmed the first case of WNV in a horse this year. The horse is also in Brown County. However, officials said the two cases are likely not connected.

“West Nile virus is transmitted by mosquitoes, and it cannot spread directly between horses and humans,” said Michelle Tipple, community and public health officer for the Oneida Nation Health Department. “While these two cases are not related, they show mosquitoes in the area are carrying the virus and could continue spreading it to humans or animals.”
