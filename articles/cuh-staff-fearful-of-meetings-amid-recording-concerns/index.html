CUH staff 'fearful' of meetings amid recording concerns

TheTools · 15.11.2026 15:30 · source · permalink

The Chief Executive of the Health Service Executive has claimed staff at Cork University Hospital are fearful of attending meetings amid concerns they might be recorded.

Anne O'Connor was speaking as controversy grows around the facility after its CEO Jennifer Kearney warned the HSE, in a letter leaked to the media, how a shortfall of around 1,000 staff meant the hospital would not be safe going into winter.

Speaking on RTÉ's This Week programme, Ms O'Connor downplayed staffing and safety concerns at CUH but did acknowledge staff at the hospital were not working together effectively.

"What worries me probably the most at the minute is the number of staff that I speak to in the hospital who are really very upset about what is going on, who are fearful of going to meetings because they fear they may be recorded. That [applies to] staff within the hospital and staff from outside the hospital going into meetings," she said.

"So, we have reached a point where people are just not working together effectively and my ask of everybody is that we get into open and transparent conversations," she added.

Seventy patients waited on trolleys on Monday at CUH while earlier this month nine clinical directors at CUH wrote to Taoiseach Micheál Martin about ongoing staffing issues.

However, Ms O'Connor dismissed the need for an independent assessment of staff needs at CUH, despite senior clinicians at the hospital calling on the Taoiseach for one.

"To be honest, I don't think we need it. We can look at the staff complements. We can see the census, we can see the figures", she said, while stressing that staffing at the hospital has grown in 2026.

"We know that they have a significant number of staff. The challenge now is to ensure that we have the right staff in the right place at the right time," she added.

Ms O'Connor also expressed confidence in the CEO of HSE South West Andy Phillips after he faced questions over a meeting he held with Ms Kearney.

Mr Phillips told Ms Kearney that he had been "instructed by the Taoiseach's office and the Minister for Health’s office to have some very specific conversations with you".

This was subsequently denied to the point the Taoiseach told the Dáil during Leaders Questions that "false assertions were made about my office" regarding Mr Phillips meeting with Ms Kearney.

However, an audio recording subsequently clarified that Mr Phillips had referenced the Taoiseach's office in his meeting with Ms Kearney, ultimately meaning the Dáil was misled.

"I don't think anybody set out to mislead anybody. It did happen though and it shouldn't have happened. It was a heat of the moment conversation. Andy has subsequently clarified that he did say those things and that he wasn't instructed. I think there was frustration on the day, and I think in the heat of the moment that was said," said Ms O'Connor.

"I have confidence in Andy but I'm very kind of mindful that we have an awful lot of work to do in CUH and in the region and I will be monitoring that closely to see that those plans are being implemented," she added.

Ms O’Connor also said the hospital’s chief executive has not asked to meet her.

Reacting to Ms O'Connor's interview, Sinn Féin's health spokesperson David Cullinane said he was concerned by the "very real patient safety risks highlighted by hospital management but also healthcare trade unions and staff".

"The Government has to fund the priority posts that are necessary to ensure that we have safe staff in that hospital over the course of the next number of months," said the Waterford TD.

"The key issue is staffing. The immediate priority has to be that all of those posts that have been identified as necessary to ensure safe staffing needs need to be funded and put in place as quickly as possible to give the hospital the best possible chance to treat patients as quick as they can during a very difficult winter," he added.

Speaking on RTÉ's The Week in Politics, Minister of State at the Department of Justice Catherine Ardagh said that the CEO of CUH should be permitted to attend the Public Accounts Committee to be questioned about staffing and patient safety issues.

The Fianna Fáil Dublin South Central TD said a HIQA report into the hospital will be on the Minister for Health’s table in a few weeks, which she said will be "considered in great detail".

She said there will be no restriction on frontline staff, adding that "we will always need frontline staff, whether it is our nurses or doctors".

Minister for Health Jennifer Carroll MacNeill has also dismissed safety concerns at CUH, saying she would have no problem recommending the facility to a sick relative.
