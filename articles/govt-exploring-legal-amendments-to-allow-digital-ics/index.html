Govt exploring legal amendments to allow digital ICs

TheTools · 24.11.2026 13:01 · source · permalink

Digital minister Gobind Singh Deo says current legislation does not permit the digital display of identity cards.

He said current legislation does not permit the digital display of identity cards, unlike driving licences issued by the road transport department (JPJ), which are already accessible through smartphones.

“Looking ahead, we are proposing a system that consolidates identity cards, passports, birth certificates and marriage certificates into a single application,” he said in the Dewan Rakyat when winding up the committee-stage debate on the ministry’s 2027 budget.

Gobind said the matter was discussed at a meeting yesterday involving GovTech, MyGOV and MyDigital ID, following Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s directive to expand the digitalisation of government services.

He explained that MyDigital ID would serve as a digital identity verification platform, while MyGOV would function as a centralised gateway for accessing various government applications and services.

“The first phase involves consolidating existing applications into MyGOV. We will then proceed to the second phase, which includes integrating payment systems to enable transactions such as passport renewals to be done online,” he said.

On data centres, Gobind said their development could serve as a catalyst for expanding Malaysia’s artificial intelligence (AI) economy by providing the computing capacity needed by startups and emerging industries.

To support this, the government is developing the National AI Compute Exchange (NACX), which will consolidate information on available computing capacity across data centres while coordinating demand from both domestic and international markets.

“By developing data centres, we are creating an ecosystem or hub with sufficient computing capacity that startups and emerging industries can utilise to access AI services and further strengthen their businesses,” he said.

Gobind also stressed that data centre development must not compromise the country’s domestic electricity and water supplies.

He said the government was encouraging investors to secure their own energy and water sources, citing Google’s US$15 billion (RM63 billion) investment, which includes provisions for its own energy and water supply, as an example.

“Regarding electricity supply, we need to invest in upgrading our infrastructure to ensure that we can meet the demand,” he said.

The government has introduced a Connected Load Charge (CLC) mechanism under which data centre operators face a penalty if their actual recorded maximum electricity demand falls below 85% of their declared phased maximum demand during the first four years of operation.

The mechanism is intended to discourage excessive reservation of electricity capacity and ensure the optimal utilisation of power supply infrastructure.

Gobind said the proposed AI Governance Bill is expected to be tabled in the Dewan Rakyat in the first quarter of next year.

He said the draft had been completed and returned to the ministry for feedback before being submitted to the Cabinet for approval and formal drafting.

Meanwhile, the Dewan Rakyat approved the ministry’s 2027 operating and development expenditure estimates, totalling RM1.362 billion, as part of the 2027 budget.
