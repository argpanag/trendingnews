Andy Burnham oversees new £585 car tax rule change - starts in April 2027

TheTools · 28.11.2026 06:44 · source · permalink

Andy Burnham will oversee new Vehicle Excise Duty (VED) car tax updates in another blow for cash-strapped road users.

Andy Burnham will update Vehicle Excise Duty (VED) car tax fees in a matter of months, with new charges set to hit motorists' wallets from the spring. VED car tax bills are paid by almost every petrol, diesel and electric car owner, with prices rising with inflation every April. The Treasury has confirmed that 2027 is set to be no different, with bills set to increase in price from April 1.

VED rates are dependent on the age of a vehicle and its overall emissions, with higher-polluting models paying more. Among those most affected by the new changes are owners of brand new vehicles, with first-year rates once again on the rise.

According to experts at Pete Barden, owners of new models emitting between 131 and 150g/km of CO2 will pay £585 from April 1, 2027, up from the current £560 fee. The 2027 price rise is set to be slightly higher than the last increase in April 2026, when bills jumped by £20 from £540 to £560.

Vehicles emitting between 131 and 150g/km of CO2 tend to be made up of mid-size family SUVs and non-hybrid turbo petrol hatchbacks and estate models. Some executive saloons and premium SUVs can also fall under this category, with road users set to be impacted by the new bills from April.

VED hikes were expected, with the bills rising in line with Retail Price Index (RPI) inflation every spring. The 2027 increases were confirmed by the Treasury shortly after John Healey’s Autumn Budget statement last month.

The Treasury explained: “The government will uprate VED rates for cars, vans and motorcycles in line with the Retail Prices Index (RPI) from 1 April 2027.”

However, brand-new car owners only pay the expensive first-year rates in year one, with road users then reverting to the standard VED fee.

This is a charge paid by all models registered after 2017, with the current costs sitting at just £200 per year. However, these bills are also set to increase, with Pete Barden suggesting that fees could rise to £210 from April.
