Kim Novak Chooses Human Composting for Her Remains

TheTools · 22.11.2026 18:07 · source · permalink

Actress Kim Novak has chosen human composting for her remains after death, planning to turn them into nutrient-rich soil that friends and family can use to grow trees and plants. The 93-year-old Hollywood star discussed her arrangements in a November 20 interview with PEOPLE while promoting the documentary Kim Novak's Vertigo.

Novak said she documented her wishes several years ago after considering different environmentally conscious burial options. She eventually selected a facility in Washington state that offers natural organic reduction, an alternative to conventional burial and cremation.

Her decision followed concerns about the environmental impact of cremation and difficulties finding suitable locations in the Pacific Northwest for a burial involving a shroud and a newly planted tree. Human composting offered an alternative that aligned with her wishes.

Explaining her reasons for making the arrangements in advance, Novak told PEOPLE, "I'm at an age now, certainly, where I could go at any time, but I wanted to be able to kind of control where I was going,"

She also described her personal outlook on death and the possibility of continuing to contribute to the natural world through the process.

"I don't want to die. I want to continue growing," Novak told PEOPLE.

Under her plans, portions of the resulting soil will be placed in special urns and distributed among relatives and friends. She hopes recipients will use the material to plant trees, although she recognizes that some people may have limited space for gardening.

For loved ones living in apartments, including those in New York City, Novak considers houseplants an acceptable alternative. She has also expressed an interest in incorporating forget-me-nots into the process, according to an October interview with Fox News Digital.

Her preference for supporting new plant growth reflects a longstanding interest in trees, learning and the natural environment. Novak explained that she sees the resulting soil as an opportunity for something living to develop after her death.

"I want what that could grow into, because my whole thing is growing," Novak explained to PEOPLE.

She hopes that discussing her arrangements will introduce more people to natural organic reduction as another option for handling human remains. Novak also emphasized the possibility of giving those remains a role in supporting future plant life.

"There's no reason they have to die," Novak said, explaining that human remains can "bring life" and harmony to what grows next.

Novak became a major Hollywood star during the 1950s through films including Picnic and Bell, Book and Candle. Her career also included Alfred Hitchcock's 1958 psychological thriller Vertigo, in which she starred opposite James Stewart.

She stepped away from Hollywood during the 1960s but continued accepting occasional acting roles. She eventually settled in Oregon, where painting and caring for animals became important parts of her daily life.

In an October interview with PETA, Novak discussed how building trust with animals helped her reconnect with herself after years of portraying different characters on screen.

The documentary Kim Novak's Vertigo, directed by Alexandre O. Philippe, premiered at the Venice Film Festival in September 2025. The film became available for streaming through Kino Film Collection in October 2026.
