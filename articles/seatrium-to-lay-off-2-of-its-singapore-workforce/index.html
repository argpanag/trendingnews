Seatrium to lay off 2% of its Singapore workforce

Around 220 workers are affected by Seatrium’s first retrenchment exercise

Navene Elangovan

    • Tuas Boulevard Yard will continue to be the company’s flagship mega-yard. PHOTO: JANICE LIM, BT

    [SINGAPORE] Offshore, marine and energy group Seatrium is retrenching about 2 per cent of its Singapore workforce, or around 220 workers, in its first workforce restructuring exercise since its formation through a 2023 merger.

    The exercise is part of the group’s ongoing post-merger transformation, which has seen it progressively streamline its global operations as it seeks to reshape its workforce to meet changing business demands. The group was formed through a merger between Sembcorp Marine and Keppel Offshore & Marine in 2023.

    The mainboard-listed group has more than 24,000 employees globally.

    On Tuesday (Nov 24), the company said that the retrenchments were part of its “workforce redesign programme” which involves optimising its Singapore operations, and restructuring its workforce “to support a more integrated and future-ready operating model”.

    The company has also reviewed its operating model, organisational structure and workforce requirements to ensure they remain aligned with its future business needs.

    In line with this, Seatrium will accelerate the optimisation of its Singapore facilities, in line with its “One Seatrium Global Delivery” model.

    Managed centrally from Singapore, the model integrates Seatrium’s global network of yards. This forms part of a broader, ongoing effort to streamline Seatrium’s global operations, which has seen the group progressively consolidate its yard network from 15 facilities in 2023 to 11 currently.

    Tuas Boulevard Yard will continue to be Seatrium’s flagship mega-yard. Meanwhile, Admiralty Yard has been progressively scaled down and the site is scheduled to be returned to the Singapore government as planned in 2028.

    The group’s three remaining Singapore yards – Benoi Yard, Pioneer Yard and Tuas Yard – will see a “sharpened refocus” on best-fit work to support its global projects.

    Seatrium said that before the retrenchment exercise, it had reviewed redeployment opportunities, workforce upskilling initiatives and other workforce optimisation measures wherever feasible.

    However, some roles no longer align with the group’s operating model and organisational structure, Seatrium said.

    Seatrium CEO Chris Ong said: “We are not downsizing or simply cost-cutting. We are repositioning and readying Seatrium to capture new opportunities, ensuring we have the right capabilities, skills and operating models to compete in a rapidly evolving industry and support our ambitions.”

    The Ministry of Manpower (MOM) said Seatrium has engaged the company’s employees’ union. Both parties have worked together to provide a retrenchment package in line with the Tripartite Advisory on Managing Excess Manpower and Responsible Retrenchment.

    “Our priority is to ensure that affected employees are treated fairly and supported in finding new employment,” said the ministry’s taskforce for responsible retrenchment and employment facilitation in a statement on Tuesday.

    The taskforce comprises MOM, Skills and Workforce Development Agency, the National Trades Union Congress (NTUC), and NTUC’s Employment and Employability Institute (e2i).

    Seatrium is also facilitating internal redeployment opportunities and working with e2i and a career transition services provider to provide employment assistance, including job matching and career coaching, added the taskforce.

    Shares of Seatrium were trading 3.9 per cent or S$0.06 higher at S$1.59 at 12.23 pm.
