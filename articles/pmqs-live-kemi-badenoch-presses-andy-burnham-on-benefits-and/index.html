PMQs live: Kemi Badenoch presses Andy Burnham on benefits and Brexit vote claims at PMQs

  • Kemi Badenoch presses Andy Burnham on benefits and his claims of Russian interference in the 2016 Brexit referendum at PMQs

  1. Burnham resists Lib Dem invitation to criticise White Housepublished at 12:40 BST
    12:40 BST

    Chris Mason
    Political editor

    The Liberal Democrats have long seen offering a punchy public critique of President Trump as something it is important for them to do.

    They argue that it is something, in their view, the Conservatives are often unwilling to do and the government is unable to do - such is the need to be diplomatic and maintain a decent relationship with the White House.

    Ed Davey spoke bluntly about President Trump’s diesel deal with Russia - and invited the prime minister to do the same.

    Instead, Andy Burnham’s language about Ukraine’s predicament as a result of that deal was powerful - it was “another dark and difficult moment for him”, Burnham said, referring to President Zelensky, who is visiting Downing Street this afternoon.

    But he resisted any temptation to criticise the White House, saying it was his job "to try to repair bridges so the support comes back” - in other words try to keep Washington on board in its support for Kyiv..

    He added that extra UK support for Ukraine is coming - saying that Zelensky "won’t be going away from Britain today empty handed.”

  2. Burnham says he intends to repair bridges to bring back Ukraine supportpublished at 12:38 BST
    12:38 BST

    Davey now turns to the US, noting that ahead of the mid-term elections in November, President Donald Trump has done a deal with Russian leader Vladimir Putin to buy diesel from Russia.

    The Lib Dem leader asks if Burnham agrees this is a "shameful betrayal" of the European people and their security.

    Firstly, Burnham refers back to Davey's comments on social care, saying he will arrange "a new round of cross-party talks" on the matter.

    Then, responding to his comments on the US and Russia, the PM says he called Volodymyr Zelensky on Saturday because he recognised "it was another very dark and difficult time for the Ukrainian leader" as the war pushes into the winter.

    "What I have to do in my role is to try and repair bridges so support comes back," Burnham says. "That is the role I intend to play. It's the right role."

    He says he will listen to Zelensky closely and that the Ukrainian leader "won't be going away from the UK today empty handed."

  4. Burnham admits building national care service will be difficultpublished at 12:33 BST
    12:33 BST

    Leader of the Lib Dems, Ed Davey, stands to ask a question on social care.

    He says he was "delighted" with Burnham's announcement over the summer that there would be cross-party talks on the issue.

    Davey further cites Burnham saying when he previously tried to address the issue back in 2009, his mistake had been leaving it too close to an election.

    Davey asks whether he still thinks this now - and when the next round of talks will be.

    Burnham says he is "happy to admit that again now" and stresses this is "not an issue for point scoring".

    "It will be difficult but let's get on and build this national care service," he says.

  6. Burnham says he is facing up to difficult decisionspublished at 12:29 BST
    12:29 BST

    On the budget, Badenoch says she met a beauty salon owner who is struggling with the Labour government's tax rises, saying she is "dipping into her pension to pay the young people she employs".

    Badenoch asks whether Burnham is "too busy appeasing his MPs, excusing his own mistakes and avoiding difficult decisions to give the country the leadership it needs".

    Burnham says he used his Labour party conference speech to face up to difficult decisions, including adjusting the triple lock to fund the new national care service.

    Before bringing his exchange with Badenoch to an end, Burnham commends Badenoch for what she said during her party conference speech, when she drew a line with Reform UK for suggesting that her children weren't British.

    "That [what Reform said] is wrong in every single respect, she was brave and she was right to call it out, that's what I want to see more of in the next decade as we go forward".

  7. No 10 insistent Man City investigation is independentpublished at 12:28 BST
    12:28 BST

    Chris Mason
    Political editor

    It’s the first Prime Minister’s Question Time since Manchester City was found guilty of having “systematically” broken Premier League rules - and the first question to Andy Burnham is about it.

    The PM said - of his dealings and conversations with the club - that “I am very happy for everything to be set out in full.”

    And he said the situation the club finds itself in has not been raised with him by the club.

    The Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch suggested Burnham facing questions about Man City “doesn’t sound” like it will be “going away.”

    Downing Street are at pains to insist the investigation, appeal and associated processes involving Manchester City are independent and it is right for him to keep his distance from that.

  8. When will PM will announce date for 3% defence spending target, asks Badenochpublished at 12:27 BST
    12:27 BST

    Badenoch says she didn't, in fact, ask the PM about the Milburn report, but says that it should have been set out before now.

    She then refers to an interview with the BBC on Sunday, in which the defence secretary refused to say when the government will meet the target of spending 3% of GDP on defence.

    "This the money Britain needs to keep us safe," Badenoch says.

    Why won't the PM announce a date for 3% of defence spending in this month's budget, she asks.

    Burnham says he is meeting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky this afternoon and that as the UK's leader he will "honour commitments" to Ukraine and Nato.

    Burham then criticises previous comments the Tory leader has made over defence, saying: "She comes here every week and poses as the world leading authority on defence."

  9. Badenoch accuses PM of delaying decisions to avoid row with backbenchespublished at 12:21 BST
    12:21 BST

    Bandenoch accuses Burnham of delaying a decision on energy to avoid a row with his backbenches - just, she says, as he is avoiding another row on cutting welfare.

    She cites a report in The Times, external that says there are two million claiming sickness benefits who "may not be sick" - and adds that the last time a PM tried to reform welfare, Labour MPs stopped him.

    She asks Burnham if he is "afraid" to cut benefits as he doesn't want a fight with his backbenches.

    Burnham responds saying Labour commissioned the Milburn report, external and adds he doesn't want young people in their 20s to be stuck on benefits.

    He says we "do need better mental health support" and that he has argued for a different education system with parity for technical education.

    We will bring forward the Milburn report soon, he continues, and "we will bring down the welfare bill".

  10. Badenoch next questions PM's stance on UK energy securitypublished at 12:18 BST
    12:18 BST

    Badenoch says Burnham will "say anything" instead of dealing with tough decisions - like on Britain's energy security.

    She then references decisions on the Rosebank and Jackdaw oil and gas fields in the North Sea, and asks when he will stop "faffing about" and give the sites the go ahead.

    Burnham replies that he is waiting for the government process that was struck down by the courts under the Conservative government to be fixed.

    He says he will take a "pragmatic" approach on oil and gas and set out the government's decisions shortly.

    12:16 BST

    11:58 BST

    Within the last month, Andy Burnham has been under pressure to clarify his previous discussions with Man City since the club was found guilty of having "systematically" broken Premier League financial rules - a ruling it has said it will appeal.

    Burnham, who was the mayor of Greater Manchester before becoming PM, has been criticised for saying he would be "really concerned" if the club decided to sell, following the findings.

    His remarks were branded "completely out of touch" and "extraordinary" by the Tories and took many in the football community by surprise.

    Now, it has emerged that Burham had two hospitality dinners with Manchester City football club's chairman while mayor of Greater Manchester - a detail which was not provided in a statement from Greater Manchester Combined Authority about his meetings with the club, which was issued on Monday.

    Downing Street has defended the two dinners with Man City's chairman, saying this was hospitality received at home games, which had been declared, rather than separate meetings with the club chairman.
