Inter Miami Fight to Save MLS Cup Defense Against Columbus

TheTools · 28.11.2026 00:29 · source · permalink

Inter Miami must overcome a three-goal deficit and a hostile atmosphere in Columbus on Friday to keep their MLS Cup title defense alive. Coach Cristian "Kily" Gonzalez is counting on his players to respond in Game 2 of their best-of-three Eastern Conference playoff series after a damaging home defeat. The second-seeded Herons enter the match facing elimination following a 3-0 loss to the seventh-seeded Columbus Crew on Saturday.

According to the series format, Miami must win Friday to force a decisive third game and extend its postseason campaign. If the series reaches Game 3, the teams will return to Miami for Wednesday's scheduled contest. Another defeat on Friday would end Inter Miami's campaign and their attempt to defend the MLS Cup championship.

Gonzalez emphasized the importance of determination and personal responsibility when addressing reporters in Spanish on Wednesday. He believes his squad has the character needed to respond under pressure.

"There are moments when you have to dig deep, tap into that sense of self-respect and the passion that drives you. That's how I see the game, and I truly hope we can demonstrate that this Friday," Gonzalez told reporters. The challenge extends beyond the result of the opening game. Miami also faces an uncomfortable reception in Columbus, where temperatures are forecast to reach the upper 30s, with possible snow flurries.

Relations between the teams have also become increasingly tense following their previous meeting on September 27. During Columbus' 2-1 victory, Miami received five of the nine yellow cards and the match's only red card. The latest confrontation involved Lionel Messi and Columbus player Gonzalo Tapia. During a throw-in in Game 1, Messi threw the ball into Tapia's face, prompting officials to caution both players.

Columbus interim coach Laurent Courtois questioned whether Messi's punishment was sufficient. Speaking on Monday, he also referred to an earlier incident involving the Argentine forward inside the Crew's penalty area. "There's multiple ways to see that situation.

If you talk about purely the soccer part, I think when he dove inside of our box earlier in the game, it should've been a yellow to start with. And this incident is not only the throw-in. It's the whole sequence that leads to it, and you can tell that there's weird intentions at the least, so it is what it is," Courtois said.

Saturday's match featured 36 fouls, with Miami responsible for 22, according to the match statistics. The Herons also collected five yellow cards and one red card, further complicating their attempt to recover from the defeat.

Columbus established control early after Gonzalo Tapia converted a penalty in the ninth minute. Miami defender Gonzalo Lujan received his second yellow card in the 36th minute, leaving the visitors with 10 players.

The Crew capitalized after the interval, with Taha Habroune scoring in the 52nd minute before Tapia added his second goal six minutes later. Lujan's dismissal also means he will be suspended for Friday's second game. Inter Miami forward Luis Suarez acknowledged that his team has reached a decisive moment in the series.

He described the upcoming match as the squad's final opportunity to avoid elimination and bring the contest back to South Florida. "I believe the squad are at a point where we understand that it's time to deliver. This is our final opportunity.

This format has given us another chance to play this Friday, and it's the last shot we have to bring the series back home," Suarez said.
