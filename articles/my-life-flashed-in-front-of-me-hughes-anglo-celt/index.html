‘My life flashed in front of me’- Hughes | Anglo Celt

There has been much discussion online about Kieran Hughes' free-kick late in the game against St. Eunan's on Saturday evening.

With Scotstown trailing by a point in injury time of extra-time, Max Maguire was dragged down between the D and the arc on the right hand side. A textbook free for a left footer in normal circumstances. These were not normal circumstances.

The wind was blowing across the field in the opposite direction, and had picked up dramatically since the game began almost two hours earlier.

Responsibility fell to veteran Kieran Hughes, who had been injured in the build up to the game, and had battled hard to get his body right to play. Even then, he had to settle for a cameo appearance.

The Celt was sure that he'd hit a trademark 'spinner', but no. He went with the conventional in-swinger.

His shot went goalwards, but swerved dramatically to the right at the last minute. Had it gone over? The crowd looked to the umpire, the authority on such matters, who made no effort to shed light on the situation.

After a brief pitch-side summit, the referee awarded the point, sending the game to penalties.

"You know something? I know there's going to be video evidence floating about and stuff," Hughes tells the Celt.

"When I struck it, I thought 'yeah, that's black-spot stuff'. If anything, it's gone over and has come back around.

"People will disagree with me, but I'm at the stage now where I think you make your own luck.

"Max (Maguire) was unlucky, he was dragged down. I think that should be an automatic score, he was straight through on goal."

Hughes is asked about the game as a whole, and his injury struggles in the build-up to the game.

"When you win all the pain eases a wee bit," he explains.

"My life flashed in front of me there, the last free and then missing the penalty. I was thinking, 'it's going to be all on me here'.

"I love the pressure moments, all throughout my career. Unfortunately tonight, the penalty tonight didn't go my way. I knew the boys were going to back me up on that one."

"I've had no work done the last couple of weeks, just letting the leg settle and do whatever is needed rehab-wise.

"Then I was banking on the work I've done on the field all year to get me through. I said it before, just let adrenaline get you through the game.

"When you hear the Scotstown crowd, it will get you through – even if your leg is half hanging off."

At the time, it wasn't clear who Scotstown would meet in the final. It turns out that Crossmaglen await in a pulsating final. For Hughes, that means a lot of recovery and preparation to go to the well once more.

"We’ll get the body settled here now in the next couple of days and let the emotion of it all sort of settle down.

"We’ll see what the craic is, maybe Tuesday or Wednesday maybe do a wee bit. I’d say I’ll not do anything until next week, but we’ll figure it out.

"After these games there’s a lot of emotion about it. You just need to let it settle down and enjoy the buzz, because these days aren’t going to last forever.

"We're not going down without a fight for this Ulster title. I keep saying, as soon as you win a title, you need to make sure the second season you put up a fight. And we’re showing that we’re putting up a fight.

"At times we're playing fantastic football, at times we're a wee bit slack, so we've a lot of work to do."
