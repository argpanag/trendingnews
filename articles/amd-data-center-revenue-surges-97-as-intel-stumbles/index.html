[Key Issue Briefing]

■ AMD posts record quarterly revenue on data center focus: AMD's third-quarter data center revenue jumped 97% from a year earlier to $8.5 billion, lifting the segment's share of total revenue to 64% from 47% a year ago. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $17 billion also topped the average analyst estimate of $16.3 billion, prompting expectations that AMD could overtake Intel for the first time in the fourth quarter.

■ U.S. "blue wave" materializes as Korean stocks swing before rebounding: With the Democratic Party taking control of both chambers of Congress in the U.S. midterm elections, the KOSPI tumbled as much as 406 points, or 6.97%, from its intraday high before paring losses to close up 0.62%. Large-cap chip stocks ended little changed on concerns about tighter regulation of AI data centers, while EcoPro rose 9.54% and EcoPro BM surged 10.96% on expectations of strengthened Inflation Reduction Act policies, marking a clear sector rotation.

■ Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) starts work on 2- and 4-nanometer foundry base in Giheung: Samsung Electronics has begun building an advanced foundry fab on the site of its research institute and engineering college at the Giheung campus and launched a dedicated organization called TF Kairos. The investment is estimated at around 25 trillion won, including 8 trillion to 10 trillion won for EUV lithography equipment, with a 2-nanometer line to support mass production of base dies for eighth-generation HBM5 seen as the centerpiece.

[News of Interest to Stock Investors]

1. Data center share: AMD 64% vs. Intel 44% — speed of business overhaul divided fortunes

Key points: AMD's third-quarter total revenue rose 45% from a year earlier to $13.4 billion, beating the Wall Street estimate of $13.09 billion for a record quarterly result. Surging demand for EPYC CPUs amid the spread of AI agents and the launch of the Instinct MI450 series drove data center revenue to $8.5 billion, up 97% year-on-year. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $17 billion exceeded the average analyst estimate of $16.3 billion compiled by Bloomberg, raising the prospect that AMD could overtake Intel in the fourth quarter. Intel, by contrast, guided to $15 billion to $16 billion for the fourth quarter, falling short of market expectations, and its shares plunged 36% over five trading sessions in a stark contrast.

2. [Exclusive] Samsung to build advanced foundry fab in Giheung

Key points: Samsung Electronics has started construction of an advanced 2- and 4-nanometer foundry fab on the site of its research institute and engineering college at the Giheung campus, with Samsung E&A (028050.KS) taking on the engineering, procurement and construction work. The investment is estimated at around 25 trillion won, including 8 trillion to 10 trillion won for EUV lithography equipment, and some observers say the total could reach 30 trillion won once the cost of converting advanced packaging facilities is included. The Giheung fab will house both a 4-nanometer process for base dies for sixth-generation HBM4 and seventh-generation HBM4E, and a 2-nanometer line for base dies for eighth-generation HBM5. The industry expects Samsung Electronics' HBM supply, measured in bits, to grow at an average annual rate of 52% over the three years through 2028.

3. KOSPI swings on U.S. "blue wave," closes higher at 5,683

Key points: The KOSPI closed on the 4th at 5,683.80, up 35.01 points or 0.62% from the previous session, after extreme volatility that took it above 5,800 intraday before dropping to 5,421.72. Foreign investors led the decline with net selling of 2.51 trillion won, while buying by retail investors of 1.14 trillion won and by institutions led by pension funds of 1.01 trillion won cushioned the fall. Chip stocks ended little changed on concerns over Democratic regulation of AI data centers, but a sector rotation played out clearly as EcoPro (086520.KS) rose 9.54% and EcoPro BM (247540.KS) surged 10.96% on expectations of strengthened IRA policies. Korea Investment & Securities said the election shock would be absorbed within one to two days and that the market's focus would shift back to industry fundamentals.

[Reference News for Stock Investors]

4. "Regulatory pressure on AI data centers is clearly coming" — Samsung, SK face tougher U.S. investment path

Key points: With the Democratic Party controlling both chambers of Congress, tighter regulation of AI data center construction is expected, clouding the U.S. investment environment for Samsung Electronics and SK hynix (000660.KS). Democratic gains in AI data center hubs such as Texas, Ohio and Georgia suggest permitting delays will inevitably affect demand for server DRAM and HBM. On the other hand, the Democrats could check the Trump administration's efforts to scale back CHIPS Act subsidies, improving the chances that Samsung Electronics will secure its $4.745 billion subsidy for the Taylor, Texas plant. Renewable energy companies such as Hanwha Solutions (009830.KS) welcomed the outcome on hopes the IRA will be restored.

5. At 6.55%, Meta's 20 trillion won bond sale drew orders four times the offering

Key points: Meta Platforms issued $15 billion, or about 20 trillion won, of dollar bonds and drew orders of as much as $58 billion, roughly four times oversubscribed. Even after S&P lowered its outlook on Meta's rating, citing the financial burden of expanded AI spending, a 6.55% yield on the 10-year tranche attracted investor demand. Following Oracle's $10 billion, Alphabet's 6 billion euros and Microsoft's $25 billion, big tech bond issuance has come in a steady stream, intensifying competition to fund AI infrastructure. Bloomberg Intelligence projects hyperscalers and neocloud companies will invest about $4.5 trillion over the next three years.

6. Cornered Trump may play a harder tariff card — or seek a breakthrough through talks with North Korea

Key points: Having lost control of Congress, President Donald Trump may look for a breakthrough through a more hardline trade policy. Korea's trade surplus with the U.S. reached $102.3 billion in the January-October period, more than double last year's $49.49 billion, raising concerns that Korea could become a main tariff target. Tariff policy is an executive prerogative that can bypass Congress, so uncertainty is expected to persist for companies heavily dependent on exports to the U.S., such as chipmakers and automakers. Experts said Trump also has room to seek an opening by delivering results on external issues such as talks between the U.S. and North Korea.
