Inquiry hears delegate felt he 'brought danger' to his family after CFMEU comments

By Will Murray

Topic:Unions

A commission of inquiry into the CFMEU in Queensland has heard of clashes with the AWU about visiting sites. (ABC News: Glenn Mullane)

In short:

A commission of inquiry into the CFMEU in Queensland has heard union members made sinister comments about the family of an Australian Workers Union delegate.

James Downie told the inquiry the comments came during a sustained campaign of intimidation by CFMEU members against the AWU.

What's next?

The inquiry continues.

An organiser with the Australian Workers Union has recounted the moment a CFMEU member allegedly made sinister comments about his young family during a confrontation near a Brisbane work site.

James Downie told the Queensland inquiry into the CFMEU the comments came during a sustained campaign of harassment and intimidation by the CFMEU against members of the rival union during visits to work sites.

In mid-2023, Mr Downie and a colleague went to visit the Cross River Rail development in Woolloongabba when they were set upon by a large group of CFMEU members.

James Downie told the inquiry a CFMEU member made sinister comments about his young family. (ABC News)

The CFMEU men allegedly screamed insults at them as they tried to exit the site, ripped at Mr Downie's backpack, and started shoving him as he tried to cross Stanley Street and into the safety of a nearby shopping centre.

As he crossed, he said he was nearly pushed into traffic.

"They charged at me with such force that I was bumped off the pedestrian walkway and there was a large truck … coming at me," he said.

"I had to throw my body back at the pedestrian crossing to get back onto the street, because if I hadn't, I would've been hit by the truck."

He said the truck was so close he felt the wind on his backpack.

"After that I just started running," he said.

Mr Downie told the inquiry the CFMEU mob cornered the two AWU representatives at the stairs leading into the shopping centre. The moment was captured on CCTV and played to the hearing.

During this interaction, Mr Downie said the CFMEU men were trying to goad him into fighting them.

When he refused, Mr Downie said they asked: "Is that what you teach your kids?"

He said he told the men he had no kids, to which he says one replied, "we know you have two sons".

"Then they gave the name of my sons and where they went to school, and to daycare, and my patterns and what I did in my personal life."

Counsel assisting the inquiry asked Mr Downie what he was thinking at that moment.

"That my job had crossed over to my personal life, and I'd brought danger to my family," he responded.

Mr Downie said another member of the CFMEU then told the others the "message has been sent" and they dispersed.

Delegates blocked from visiting members

The inquiry heard about several hostile interactions between the CFMEU and AWU around this time.

On several occasions CFMEU members physically blocked AWU delegates from entering sites to visit their members.

Some of these interactions were captured on phone footage and played to the hearing.

During these incidents, large groups of CFMEU members can be heard yelling insults at the AWU delegates and daring them to try and push past their blockades.

Mr Downie said on one occasion, out the front of the Cultural Centre bus stop upgrade, he was told "take a step forward, you're not getting through, I'm a boxing champion, I'll put you out straight away".

Fearing for their safety, Mr Downie said they decided to leave without visiting members.
