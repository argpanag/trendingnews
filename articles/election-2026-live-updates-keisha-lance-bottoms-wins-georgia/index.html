NBC News Exit Poll in Florida: Sen. Ashley Moody wins with support from conservatives and white voters

Sen. Ashley Moody, R-Fla., edged out Democrat Angie Nixon with strong support from conservatives and white voters in the state, according to early results from the NBC News Exit Poll.

In addition to receiving solid backing from 93% of Republican voters, Moody also won 91% of conservatives and 60% of white voters in Florida. A majority of Latinos cast their votes for Nixon (56%). But among Latinos there was a sharp divide: 58% of Cubans supported Moody, while 68% of Puerto Ricans supported Nixon.

Other groups backing Moody include 62% of residents who have lived in the state for 1 to 6 years, 62% of voters in small towns and rural areas, 57% of voters age 45 and older and 55% with no college degree.

Nixon did especially well with voters under age 45 (66%), moderates (62%), independents (58%) and voters in urban areas (55%).

'Dummymandering' might be the word of the night

I know it’s early, but if we can make the word of the night “dummymandering,” I would like to vote for that.

Some of the districts that were drawn in Florida for Republicans have actually been won by Democrats and it does appear, at least in House races so far, that there is a trend line emerging.

In general, states do not mess with their districts except for in a 10-year window after a census, where they get a sense of how many people live where, and they draw their districts accordingly. This mid-decade redistricting was a super controversial move that came after Trump went to governors in red states and said to them, essentially, “Draw me more districts that are Republican-friendly than they are Democratic-friendly.”

But there’s only a finite amount of voters. You can only move them around so much, and you have to pull those voters out of other districts to put them here. It is backfiring big time in Florida. A new generation heads to the polls in Texas

For 18-year-old Texas resident Daniel Johnson-Baker, voting in his first election was about more than casting a ballot. It was about making sure his voice was heard, especially after his university removed polling locations from campus, making it harder for students to vote.

“If they’re doing that, then I gotta vote,” he said. “I felt good knowing that I can finally make an impact.”

Johnson-Baker said voting also carries a personal meaning for his family. His great-grandmother wasn’t able to vote, and his father reminded him that generations before him fought for that right. “Use your right that you got, you earned as a Black man,” he recalled his father telling him.

In Tarrant County, 19-year-old Tremaine Smith said casting his first ballot felt “different” and “kind of refreshing.” Before he was old enough to vote, he said, he could share his opinions online, but felt that didn’t always make much of a difference. “But even just voting here feels like I have a lot more power than before,” he said.

And for 21-year-old Texan Quentin Bradfield, the motivation came down to wanting change in his community, starting with the cost of housing. “I can’t even afford a decent apartment,” he said. He also sees voting as a responsibility to the generations coming up behind him, saying he wants to help make change “not just for myself, but also the younger generation, kind of carrying the torch of the older ones left for us.”

NBC News Exit Poll: Pessimistic voters are in a sour mood about the U.S.

Voters expressed deep dissatisfaction with the state of the country — and gloom about how life will be for the next generation, according to preliminary results from the NBC News Exit Poll.

Behind voters’ pessimism lie deep economic and financial concerns. Coming in for criticism in this bleak environment: Congress. Voters gave the nation’s legislature just a 24% approval rating, and most voters (71%) said Congress was failing at its constitutional role to provide checks and balances on the presidency.
