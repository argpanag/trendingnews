DHS Finalizes End of Automatic EAD Extensions for Renewal Applicants - Erickson Immigration Group

TheTools · 13.10.2026 17:23 · source · permalink

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has finalized a rule eliminating automatic extensions of employment authorization for many individuals who file timely Employment Authorization Document (EAD) renewal applications. The final rule, scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on October 14, 2026, adopts without change a 2025 interim final rule ending the automatic extension period that previously allowed certain applicants to continue working while renewal applications remained pending.

The change affects a broad range of EAD holders, including adjustment-of-status applicants, certain asylum applicants, and H-4 spouses who rely on EADs for employment authorization. DHS states the rule will allow the agency to conduct updated eligibility reviews and security vetting before authorizing continued employment.

Historically, USCIS has provided automatic extensions for eligible applicants who timely filed Form I-765 renewal applications. During periods of significant processing delays, the automatic extension period was expanded to as much as 540 days to prevent eligible workers from losing employment authorization solely because their renewal applications had not yet been adjudicated.

DHS eliminated the 540-day automatic extension period through an interim final rule issued in 2025. The agency has now made that policy permanent through a final rule, signaling a continued focus on heightened vetting and immigration benefit integrity.

Under the final rule:

  • EAD renewal applications filed on or after October 30, 2025, generally do not receive automatic extensions based solely on a pending renewal application.
  • Individuals must typically wait for USCIS approval before obtaining a new period of employment authorization.
  • Automatic extensions previously granted under earlier regulations remain valid for eligible filings submitted before the policy change.

According to DHS, employment authorization should not continue automatically while an application remains pending because USCIS must first determine that the applicant remains eligible and clear updated security and background reviews.

The rule could create significant challenges for individuals whose EAD renewals remain pending near their expiration dates.

As of June 30, 2026, USCIS reported more than 432,000 pending EAD renewal applications that had been pending for at least six months, including more than 91,000 applications pending for over 18 months. Because most applicants cannot file renewals more than 180 days before expiration, lengthy adjudication times may increase the risk of employment authorization gaps.

For employers, the change may lead to:

  • Temporary workforce disruptions;
  • Increased employee leave or work interruptions;
  • Additional I-9 compliance monitoring obligations; and
  • Greater planning needs for employees who depend on renewed EADs to continue working.

H-4 spouses, adjustment applicants, asylum-related applicants, and others who rely on EAD renewals may be particularly affected by processing delays.

The policy has already faced legal challenges. Earlier this year, a group of H-4 visa holders filed suit challenging the interim final rule, arguing that DHS did not comply with procedural requirements under the Administrative Procedure Act. In September 2026, a federal court in California issued a limited injunction preventing enforcement of the interim final rule against the plaintiffs in that case while litigation continues.

Although the litigation remains ongoing, the final rule is scheduled to take effect upon publication on October 14, 2026.
