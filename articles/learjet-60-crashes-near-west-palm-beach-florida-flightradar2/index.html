Learjet 60 crashes near West Palm Beach, Florida | Flightradar24 Blog

TheTools · 01.11.2026 23:25 · source · permalink

A Learjet 60 crashed 1 November near West Palm Beach, Florida at approximately 17:49 UTC (12:49 local time). According to the Federal Aviation Administration, 9 people were on board the aircraft at the time of the crash. The flight was en route from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas. The National Transportation Safety Board has launched a go-team to begin an investigation.

The flight was operated by N722BD, a Learjet 60 manufactured in 2004. Departing Austin at 15:37 UTC, the aircraft flew east toward the Bahamas. At 17:35 near Naples, FL, the aircraft began a descent down to 35,000 feet. At 17:48, the aircraft briefly climbed, before entering a steep, righthand descent. 

In air traffic control audio captured by LiveATC.net and reviewed by Flightradar24, the crew report icing conditions and moderate turbulence and ask for a “better ride.” The crew then informs ATC they are flying in precipitation and request a climb to 37,000 feet. 

Shortly after receiving their climb instructions from air traffic control, the crew reported descending at a high rate in an overspeed condition. The last altitude data received by Flightradar24 was 8,750 feet at 17:49:22. During the descent, the reported vertical rate exceeded 32,000 feet per minute.

Three files are available for download, a standard format CSV file, a standard format KML file, and a granular data file that includes higher frequency reports from the aircraft.

Aircraft information

N722BD is a Learjet 60 serial number 60-271. The aircraft has positioned to Austin on 31 October from Little Rock, Arkansas.

Investigative updates

2 November — The NTSB held its first on scene media briefing on 2 November, providing updates on the status of the investigation and some early observations. 

  • There is a team of 20 investigators arriving on scene.
  • The aircraft was at one point equipped with a cockpit voice recorder, but the NTSB is working to determine if that was still onboard, and if so, locate it.
  • There is some evidence of in-flight break up of the aircraft, according to NTSB Board Member Tom Chapman, and the agency is still search for all aircraft components.
