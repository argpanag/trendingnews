AGSI calls for public inquiry over protected disclosures

TheTools · 18.09.2026 13:29 · source · permalink

The Association of Garda Sergeants and Inspectors (AGSI) has called on the Minister for Justice to establish a statutory public inquiry into the handling of protected disclosures within An Garda Síochána, following this week's RTÉ Investigates programme.

RTÉ Investigates reported that two people who handled protected disclosures made by garda whistleblowers have themselves blown the whistle, alleging that reforms introduced to protect those who speak out have been undermined by senior management.

In a statement, the AGSI said that they have "serious concerns around the independence of the protected disclosures process" within the garda force.

AGSI General Secretary, Ronan Clogher, said that the revelations in the RTÉ programme show a "clear and concerning breakdown of a process established to protect members".

"The courage to raise a concern within the force is completely undermined when you cannot trust the process," he said.

"In our role as a representative organisation, we seek to ensure that any member who enters into this process does so without fear of penalisation or isolation."

It comes after the Garda Representative Association also called on Minister Jim O'Callaghan to establish an independent statutory tribunal of inquiry.

The association said that the issues with the protected disclosures process was "not a new or isolated pattern" and that it was "one the Association has encountered on previous occasions in its representative work".

The GRA said that it will be writing formally to the minister in the coming days to set out their request for an independent tribunal of inquiry in detail.

Programme removed from RTÉ Player

RTÉ has removed Wednesday's RTÉ Investigates programme on the protected disclosure process within An Garda Síochána from the RTÉ Player.

In a statement this evening, RTÉ said that "following detailed correspondence from the Office of the Director of Public Prosecutions about an upcoming court case referenced, the documentary has been removed temporarily from the RTÉ Player pending the conclusion of these proceedings".

"RTÉ will continue to report on the public interest questions raised in the programme and the programme will be made available again on the RTÉ Player in due course."

The Irish Council for Civil Liberties has also added to calls for an independent public inquiry, saying that any inquiry must be expedient and have terms of reference which "reflect the issues which need to be uncovered".

The Garda Protected Disclosures Office was set up in mid-2017 as part of reforms introduced to protect garda whistleblowers in response to the Maurice McCabe whistleblowing scandal and the Disclosures Tribunal, which was then under way, and published its final report in 2023.

O'Callaghan not looking at establishing tribunals of inquiry

Minister O'Callaghan has said that he is not looking at establishing tribunals of inquiry.

Speaking to reporters in Dublin, he said that they should only be established when there are urgent matters of public importance that require investigation.

"I am not looking at that at this stage," he said.

The minister said that there is a disclosures statutory scheme where people can come forward confidentially, have their complaint assessed and be reported back to them confidentially.

"I am not speaking about any protected disclosures in particular," Mr O'Callaghan said, "but it's necessarily the case that where you have that, people will be satisfied with the outcome, some people won't be.

"I can't have a situation where people aren't satisfied with the outcome of protected disclosures that we then have to have a statutory inquiry."

Earlier, the Labour Party called on justice ministers past and present to answer questions raised in the RTÉ Investigates programme.

The party requested a debate in the Dáil next week, with the support of other Opposition parties, but none was scheduled.

Party whip, Duncan Smith, said: "Government’s refusal to provide time for this debate is completely unacceptable.

"Government cannot treat this as a passing controversy and hope that public concern will fade.

"Both Ministers must come before the Dáil, address the questions raised and provide a clear account of how these matters were handled," Mr Smith said.

"Labour is calling for an urgent, dedicated debate with Ministers O’Callaghan and [former justice minister Helen] McEntee at the earliest opportunity.

"The public, the Dáil and the Garda whistle-blowers at the centre of this scandal deserve clear answers."

Labour's Alan Kelly has called for the immediate intervention of the Minister O'Callaghan to ensure that the independence of the protected disclosure process within An Garda Síochána is protected.

"Minister O'Callaghan has to act and has to intervene in some way to protect the process," Mr Kelly said.
