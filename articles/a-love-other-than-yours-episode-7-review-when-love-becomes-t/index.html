A Love Other Than Yours Episode 7 Review: When Love Becomes Too Painful to Hide | Leisurebyte

TheTools · 10.10.2026 21:35 · source · permalink

Episode 7 is emotionally exhausting as the truth finally comes out. With Mi-do constantly lying and Ho figuring out something is off, the truth isn’t sealed forever. Rather than relying on romantic tension, this episode dives into each character’s emotional damage. With Ho, Mi-do and Tae-seung caught in a complicated love triangle, this is one of the series’ most painful episodes.

A Love Other Than Yours episode 7 runtime is 60 minutes. The series (너 말고 다른 연애) stars Seo Kang-joon, Ahn Eun-jin, Lee Joo-ahn, Jo A-ram, Kim Mi-kyung, Kim Nam-hee and Lee Min-jae. It is directed by Hwang Seung-ki and Lee Ga-ram.

A Love Other Than Yours Episode 7 Review

Failed Reconciliation

Ho finds it hard to reconcile with the woman he loves because he believes she now has another side. He listens to Mi-do’s recorded confession, and it shatters him. He even breaks their photograph, showing that he isn’t just angry but devastated by how his love life of 10 years has crumbled.

We can see how uncomfortable their conversation at the hotel is because both of them don’t seem entirely wrong. Mi-do admits that she got his sympathy due to expressing her pain, and Ho realises that he’s been forcefully protecting her without giving her the room to breathe. He finally says that they should rethink their marriage decision, as their trust is broken and can’t be repaired, not now.

But are they splitting up? No. Ho is still evidently caring for Mi-do despite his struggle to stay away from her. He doesn’t even rant about Mi-do to Yeong-do but only defends her. He is hurt by her, but he won’t let it affect his morals.

Tae-seung Adds Darkness

We see a storyline of Tae-seung and his connection to the suspicious death of Kim Jeong-hwan, access to files that have confidential info, and an attempt to blackmail Ju-hyeok. We also see a flashback to his abusive experience at the orphanage, which provides us with the context behind him not trusting anyone.

His relationship with Mi-do is equally complicated. She recognises his destructive manners yet admits that he makes her feel alive. She confesses that he brought light into her dark life, proving that with Ho she felt loved but not seen. During the car accident scene, when Tae-seung protects Mi-do by taking the impact, we can see that beneath his dark behaviour, he still is a man who has genuine care for someone he loves.

Su-a and Ho

Meanwhile, Su-a’s investigation into the suspicious sweet potatoes adds another layer. However, Ho warns her not to mess with powerful people due to what has happened to him. But she doesn’t let them affect her.

Their relationship also gains emotional depth through quiet, awkward moments. Su-a’s mistaken photograph, her concern for Ho, and her confession that she drops to tell, it all creates another layer. We know she likes Ho, but he is still stuck in his past without realising it is high time to let go of the woman he once loved.

The Playground Scene

The final scene is probably the most moving and annoying one. Mi-do admits to Ho that she only wanted him to see her best parts rather than her ugly ones. She says that around him, she tried her best to hide her flaws because she believes that showing her insecurities will cost her losing him.

Ho’s response is generous. Instead of asking her to run back to him, he tells her to date Tae-seung. Yo, what even? Though the line seems weird, it still is heartbreaking because it shows Ho is letting go and understands that she should explore as well. He believes that she should take care of her destructive dynamics and find herself.

Regardless, I, as a viewer, am done.

Final Verdict

Episode 7 really took us through too many storylines and left us wondering who really is wrong here. Ho and Mi-do’s relationship wasn’t flawless, but it had its depth. Now that both of them agree to move apart, will they realise that with new people they actually won’t get the comfort they gave each other?

Anyhow, the damage has been caused beyond repair, but with two men being Mi-do’s option, I genuinely feel like the writers are making her character seem like the most horrible one. She might not truly be such a person. The series seems to be purposely showing Ho as the bigger person while he has his flaws too.

As Ho finally tells Mi-do to follow her feelings, A Love Other Than Yours leaves us wondering whether letting someone go can sometimes be the most painful experience or a selfish one.
