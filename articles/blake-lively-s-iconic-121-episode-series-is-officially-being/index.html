If you were a teenager in the late 2000s or, at the very least, someone who appreciated a good high school soap opera, then chances are you watched Blake Lively's iconic series that blended wealth, scandal, and romance. Premiering in 2007, the show quickly became one of the defining dramas of an entire generation, and now, it's officially being wiped from Netflix next month.

Blake Lively Stars in a Teen Soap Opera for the Ages

Hitting The CW on Sept. 19, 2007, Gossip Girl is based on the hugely popular novel series by Cecily von Ziegesar. Starring Lively as Serena van der Woodsen, the show follows a group of wealthy teenagers living on Manhattan's Upper East Side whose lives are thrown into chaos by an anonymous blogger who knows everyone's secrets and isn't afraid to make them public.

Also starring Penn Badgley, Leighton Meester, Chase Crawford, Taylor Momsen, and Ed Westwick as the reprehensible Chuck Bass, Gossip Girl became a true phenomenon that embraced scandal, betrayal, love triangles, and rivalries. Premiering at a time when Facebook was still in its infancy, Twitter had just become a thing, and Instagram didn't even exist yet, the series foreshadowed the rise of social media and how (for better or worse) pivotal it would become in our lives.

Developed by Josh Schwartz and Stephanie Savage, Gossip Girl was one of those shows that understood the addictive nature of, well, gossip. Viewers tuned in en masse to find out what juicy secrets would be revealed next in the series, while at the same time staying captivated by all the sex, drugs, manipulation, and blackmail.

It also helped launch several careers, including those of Lively and Badgley, with the latter going on to star in the massively successful Netflix series, You. As for Lively? We all know the trouble she’s been through thanks to the whole It Ends with Us debacle, but before that, she starred in hits like The Town, The Shallows, and A Simple Favor.

'Gossip Girl's Time on Netflix Has Come to an End

Arriving on Netflix in 2024, Gossip Girl has been a regular staple on the streaming service for the past two years, but all that will change next month. Per What's on Netflix, its license is expiring in October, and as such, the series will be wiped from Netflix's streaming library on Oct. 28. This means you only have a short time left to binge one of the most addictive shows of the late 2000s.

Thankfully, the series is still available to stream on HBO Max, so if you don't get a chance to finish it before it's gone from Netflix, at least you have options. Also streaming on HBO Max is the standalone series of the same name that takes place in the same universe as the original, which features a whole new crop of teenagers attending Constance Billard and St. Jude's.
