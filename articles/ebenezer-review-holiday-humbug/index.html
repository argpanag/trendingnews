‘Ebenezer’ Review: Holiday Humbug

Johnny Depp returns as the miser of the Dickens holiday classic. But he isn’t playing Scrooge so much as trying to mount a comeback on a get-up and déjà vu.

The suspense that really hangs over “Ebenezer” is not how the nth retelling of the ghostly Dickens classic “A Christmas Carol” is interpreted, but whether or not this will rubber stamp the comeback of Johnny Depp. It is a sensible vehicle for that mission. For the once-defamed star whose career has been almost singularly associated with the showmanship of gothic and garish personae, playing Ebenezer Scrooge, among the most enduring icons of the holiday season, is about as close as you can get to low-stakes, foolproof-branded IP.

Time will tell if the box office — and therefore, the studios and the public — has reaccepted Depp. But as for the artistry itself, this is a film that feels like the mini-comeback before the comeback; at his worst, Depp appears as if he is in a Scrooge costume in a Christmas-season ad campaign for Verizon. At his best, he is competent.

The latter is fine enough for an adaptation, directed by Ti West, that, among the many iterations of the Dickens tale, toggles mostly between dual modes of the perfunctory and the inoffensive. This is a “Christmas Carol” iteration that is bland in imagination and supremely straightforward in interpretation.

It follows the story structure we’ve all known since elementary school: The greedy miser known as Scrooge is visited by the ghost of his colleague, Jacob Marley, then by three more apparitions on Christmas Eve, each haunting him out of his avarice and into the holiday spirit. It injects little beyond some studio scale, and gives its supporting cast, including Ian McKellen, Tramell Tillman and Daisy Ridley, little room to play.
