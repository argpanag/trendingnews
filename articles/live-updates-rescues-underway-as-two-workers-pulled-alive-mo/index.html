How rescuers are searching for survivors in "Goldilocks zone"

CNN is receiving more information on how the rescuers at the Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station are pushing ahead with their mission, with two people pulled from the wreckage so far.

Army rescuers are digging by hand at a specific point at the top of the stricken tunnel to try to reach any survivors inside, geologist Arnold Dix, who’s advising the operation, told CNN.

Dix calls the area the “Goldilocks zone,” where he believes workers could have survived.

Outside the tunnel’s entrance was completely blocked by the landslide. Rescuers found the entrance to the tunnel on Monday morning – five days after the flood – and have been been methodically digging since then.

The discovery of two men alive in Trishuli 3A on Friday has given rescuers “renewed hope” at other power stations where almost 900 workers remain missing, Dix said.

Tunnel rescues follow period of destruction and heartache

Two people have been rescued from a hydropower tunnel in Nepal, a dramatic breakthrough more than a week after catastrophic floods hit the country’s border with China.

Today’s miraculous rescues are a rare bright spot in what has been a devastating nine days for Nepal and China.

Last Wednesday, just after 8:30 a.m. Nepal time, a large piece of glacier near the countries’ border detached from a mountainside. That glacial collapse triggered a massive landslide and catastrophic flash flooding in both countries.

Entire communities were swept away without warning. Homes, schools, playgrounds, and other markers of life were reduced to a gushing river of “liquid cement.”

Hundreds of people were killed, and thousands more were left missing, many of them foreigners who had visited the Himalayas for a once-in-a-lifetime religious pilgrimage.

In the long nine days since the disaster, authorities have been contending with communication blackouts, road blockages and the threat of further floods, as they try to rescue survivors and recover the bodies of victims.

While thousands of people have been rescued so far, hundreds have been found dead. With morgues at capacity, Nepali authorities were forced to bury hundreds of bodies in a mass grave.

The plight of the nearly 1,000 workers trapped in Nepal’s vast system of hydropower tunnels added a new element of terror and challenge for rescuers, with specialized teams coming in to assist Nepali authorities with the daunting rescue.

Despite a colossal effort, as the days ticked on with no sign of life inside the muddy darkness, hopes had dimmed for recovering the workers alive.

Then, this morning, rescuers heard voices.

Rescued man says he didn't have time to escape after getting others to evacuate

We’re hearing from the first man who was rescued today at the hydropower tunnel.

In a video seen online, Sanjay Sah was heard asking what the date was — he had no idea how many days he was trapped inside the facility.

The clip showed him lying under a blanket and wearing an oxygen mask. The 30-year-old mechanical foreman said it took some time to get others in the tunnel to evacuate and that he didn’t have enough time to escape himself.

Sah said usually there would only a handful of people in the area where he was in the tunnel but there were 40 to 45 people present when disaster struck. He did not say why there were more people.

“It was my responsibility to save everyone’s life,” he said.

He said he thinks there might still be some people trapped inside. “I don’t think everyone was able to escape. The tunnel is very long,” he said.

Sah said he kept his spirits up by chanting Hindu mantras while inside the tunnel.

Here's what it's like inside the tunnels where the rescue is unfolding

Two people have been rescued from a hydropower tunnel in Nepal, a dramatic breakthrough more than a week after catastrophic floods hit the country’s border with China.

Just a reminder of the type of conditions facing rescuers at the Trishuli 3A site: pitch-black walkways with no power; mud-slick floors that threaten to swallow you; and only a few bars for support.

This staggering footage obtained earlier this week by a CNN team on the ground offers a glimpse inside these intricate networks, where desperate rescue efforts have persisted for nine days since floods devastated the region.

The tunnels were built to harness Nepal’s rivers and fuel its booming hydropower industry, CNN’s Hanako Montgomery reported from an earlier rescue mission.

Rescuers digging by hand to reach men in tunnel

Today’s rescue follows days of arduous work. Heavy machinery was used to haul away mud and sludge around the Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station, Lt. Col. Ganga Baral, of the Nepali Army, said in an update Thursday.

Five excavators, compressors and breakers were doing the heavy lifting to bring rescuers closer to any possible survival zone. Blasting work was also carried out to enlarge the tunnel’s entrance, he added.

Inside, explosives were used to clear a path to survivors, though it’s unclear how many are still alive after last week’s devastating floods, triggered by a glacier collapse.

Rescuers have to be careful as they get closer, to ensure no one else is lost in the disaster, said Arnold Dix, a geologist who’s been advising the rescue.

“We can’t do this with machines, we do this by hand,” said Dix.

Rescue crews blew up rocks "the size of cars" to reach survivors

The Trishuli 3A power station, where today’s dramatic rescue is taking place, was unrecognizable after the flood – rescuers didn’t know where the entrance was and had no idea where to start digging for survivors.

Working off satellite data, as-built drawings of the power station and topographic information, they were able to narrow down the location of a tunnel least likely to have collapsed, said Arnold Dix, a geologist advising Nepali rescuers on the ground.

The team started digging through the debris, then found water, which had to be drained, Dix said. Once inside the tunnels, they found their path blocked by enormous rocks.

The tunnel “was full of rocks the size of cars and mud and all the rest of it. So, we have to blow them up in the tunnel, which is incredibly dangerous because we’re in the tunnel, and there might be people on the other side,” Dix said.
