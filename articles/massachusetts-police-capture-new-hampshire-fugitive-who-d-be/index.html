Massachusetts police capture New Hampshire fugitive who’d been on lam for nearly a year

TheTools · 30.10.2026 20:06 · source · permalink

A New Hampshire fugitive who’d been on the run for almost a year was captured in Massachusetts — headed toward his girlfriend’s home — after one last frantic attempt to evade law enforcement, police said Friday.

Daquhan Cromer, 29, was taken into custody shortly after noon Wednesday in Lowell, a day after he dodged police in Salem, New Hampshire on Tuesday night, officials said.

Cromer was “wanted on an escape warrant from New Hampshire State Prison and in connection with multiple felonies in Salem,” authorities in that New Hampshire town said.

His flight prompted a shelter-in-place order in Salem night on Tuesday night, which was lifted by Wednesday morning.

He was surrounded by police almost 15 miles away in Lowell, where he surrendered without incident, officials said.

Salem police had been tipped off he’d been spending time in southeast New Hampshire, authorities said. It wasn’t immediately clear what Cromer might have been doing these past 11 months.

“He was frequenting a residence here in Salem. And as soon as we got that information, we started looking into it,” Salem police Lt. Robert Kirley told NBC News on Friday. “But as as as far as what he was doing in the meantime (these past 11 months), I don’t know.”

Asked if anyone had been assisting Cromer during his flight from police, Kirley said: “To my knowledge, he had a girl. He had a girlfriend that he was with. I don’t know if she was helping him or assisting him in that manner, but he did have a girl.”

“The girlfriend’s place was down in Lowell, where he was ultimately captured,” Kirley added.

Cromer’s attorney declined comment on the case Friday.

Cromer had fled a halfway house in Manchester, New Hampshire, in December 2025, officials said.

Departures from a halfway house are very rare.

“When you’re being transitioned to that type of setting, a minimum security setting where you’re getting some freedoms back and you’re not in state prison, that’s the point where you start to see the light at the end of the tunnel,” Kirley said.

“It’s very odd to me that you would take off if you were fairly close. I don’t know the date (of Cromer’s ultimate release), but again, he was fairly close to being in the free and clear.”
