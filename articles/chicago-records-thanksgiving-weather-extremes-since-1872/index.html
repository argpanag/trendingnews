Chicago Records Thanksgiving Weather Extremes Since 1872

TheTools · 24.11.2026 04:11 · source · permalink

Chicago's Thanksgiving weather history spans dramatic temperature swings, ranging from unusually warm afternoons to subzero conditions and record snowfall. National Weather Service records dating back to 1872 show how temperatures and precipitation have varied across the holiday's recorded history in the city. The historical data identifies November 24, 1966, as Chicago's warmest Thanksgiving, with a high temperature of 69 degrees.

By contrast, the coldest Thanksgiving morning occurred on November 23, 1950, when temperatures dropped to minus 1 degree, according to the National Weather Service. Recent conditions have been considerably different from both extremes. Chicago recorded a high of 34 degrees and a low of 25 degrees on Thanksgiving in 2025.

Thanksgiving precipitation records also show that substantial rainfall has been relatively uncommon. According to the National Weather Service, Chicago has recorded more than 1 inch of precipitation on Thanksgiving only once since 1872.

However, some precipitation has occurred more frequently. Rainfall ranging from a trace amount to more than 1 inch has been recorded in more than half of the 154 Thanksgiving holidays included in the historical record. Snowfall has been less frequent. National Weather Service data shows that Chicago recorded more than a trace of snow on just 17 Thanksgivings between 1872 and 2025.

The city's most significant Thanksgiving snowfall occurred on November 27, 1980, when O'Hare International Airport recorded 3 inches of snow. That remains the highest Thanksgiving snowfall in the available historical records. The National Weather Service compiled the historical measurements using Chicago's official weather recording locations. O'Hare International Airport has served as the city's official recording site since January 17, 1980.

Before that transition, weather observations came from several locations, including downtown Chicago, the University of Chicago and Midway International Airport. The historical figures were compiled from National Weather Service Chicago records, Tribune archives and previous reporting.
