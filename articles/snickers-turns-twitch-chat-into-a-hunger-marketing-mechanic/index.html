Snickers turns Twitch chat into a hunger marketing mechanic

TheTools · 30.09.2026 04:00 · source · permalink

Snickers brings its hunger platform into Twitch, prompting sports fans to rethink heated chat messages before they post during finals week.

Snickers is taking "You're Not You When You're Hungry" somewhere more specific than another sports ad.

During Australia's AFL and NRL Grand Final week, the brand is extending its "Feed The Trolls" campaign into Twitch with a custom mechanic designed to catch a heated comment before it reaches chat. When the system identifies a potentially "hangry" message, the user is prompted to grab a Snickers and reconsider before posting or rewriting it.

That changes the role of the campaign. The brand message is no longer only something fans watch. It becomes part of the interaction at the moment the behavior is happening.

For marketers, that is the interesting shift. Snickers has taken a familiar global brand truth and turned it into a platform-native action inside sports and gaming culture.

Why Twitch is more than a media buy here

The strongest part of the activation is not simply that Snickers bought attention on Twitch.

T&P's extension builds on Twitch moderation technology to identify a "hangry" comment before it is posted. The user is then prompted to reconsider, cool off or rewrite the message.

That gives "You're Not You When You're Hungry" a functional role inside the experience.

A conventional placement could put a Snickers logo beside a live stream. This execution tries to make the brand idea useful at the exact point where a fan's emotional reaction might spill into chat.

The campaign is expected to appear on live streams from Twitch creators Nalopia and Midbeast, while Publicis Groupe OneMars is extending paid activity across sports, gaming and the broader Amazon network.

How Snickers updates an old brand truth

"Feed The Trolls" did not begin this week.

Snickers launched the Australian football chapter in August with creative that turned a frustrated sports spectator into a literal troll. The work brought the long-running "You're Not You When You're Hungry" platform into AFL and NRL culture, with media across television, online video, Kayo and the MCG.

The original campaign also said Twitch activity would follow later.

The finals-week activation delivers that next step. Instead of repeating the same transformation joke, Snickers changes the behavior of the platform while keeping the underlying idea intact.

That matters for a mature brand platform. Longevity does not come from repeating the same execution. It comes from finding new contexts where the same brand truth still makes sense.

Here, hunger still changes behavior, but the proof point moves from a spectator losing control in an ad to a viewer about to type something heated in a live chat.

Why sports and gaming make sense together

The campaign also shows why brands increasingly treat sports fandom and gaming communities as overlapping behaviors rather than separate media categories.

Both environments are live, emotional and social. Fans react in real time, argue over outcomes and follow creators who interpret the action alongside them.

Snickers commissioned research from Lonergan Research to support that behavior. According to the campaign data, 87% of surveyed Australians said hunger affects their mood or behavior in some way. Nearly three in five sports fans, 58%, said they had become more emotional while watching sport because they were hungry.

Mi3 also reports that hungry sports fans were more likely to say they overreact during a close game, at 39%, compared with 26% when they were not hungry.

Those figures do not prove the Twitch mechanic will change behavior. They explain why Snickers believes the moment is relevant to its brand platform.

The brand is choosing a context where the emotion is already present, then using the product's familiar hunger proposition to interpret it.

What marketers can learn from Feed The Trolls

Snickers' finals-week activation offers a useful model for refreshing a long-running brand platform.

  1. Turn the brand idea into a behavior. A message becomes more distinctive when it changes what the audience can do, not only what they see.
  2. Use the platform's native mechanics. Twitch chat and moderation are part of the creative idea rather than just distribution infrastructure.
  3. Extend a campaign instead of repeating it. The August film and the finals-week Twitch execution share one brand truth but perform different jobs.
  4. Follow fandom across contexts. Sports conversations do not stay inside stadiums or broadcasts. They move through creators, gaming spaces and live chat.
  5. Keep the product logic simple. Snickers does not need a new explanation for every channel. Hunger changes behavior, and the execution adapts around that idea.

There is no verified public performance data yet showing whether the Twitch activation reduces heated comments, improves brand lift or increases sales.

What is visible is the campaign design. Snickers has moved a famous advertising line closer to the behavior it describes, turning finals-week frustration into a live moment where the brand can intervene rather than simply advertise around it.
