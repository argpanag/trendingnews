Any retiree hunting for a dividend check they can count on is going to look hard at utilities like Southern Company (SO:NYSE) and American Electric Power (AEP:NASDAQ).

And data centers are driving demand at both, as their July 30 results showed.

Southern pays the bigger yield, 3.7% against 3.3%. But AEP raised its dividend almost twice as fast from fiscal 2020 to fiscal 2025. So which is the better dividend stock for retirees?

Southern Company has the record

Southern raised its annual dividend rate to $3.04 a share in April. Management put the raise in context on its April 30 call: "This action marks our 25th consecutive annual increase, and this will now be 79 consecutive years, dating back to 1948, Southern Company has paid a dividend that is equal to or greater than the previous year."

Its business is growing, too. Data center usage rose 55% in the second quarter of 2026 from a year earlier. Contracted and large-load agreements now top 17 gigawatts, including a 3.2-gigawatt, 25-year contract with OpenAI in Georgia.

Earnings have outpaced the dividend. Normalized EPS rose from $2.89 a share in fiscal 2016 to $4.30 in fiscal 2025, while the dividend went from $2.22 to $2.94.

That took the payout ratio from 79% in fiscal 2019 to 68% in fiscal 2025. Analysts expect 66% for fiscal 2026, which is comfortable for a regulated utility.

The tradeoff is slower growth: 3.0% a year from fiscal 2020 to fiscal 2025. Southern also still plans to raise $1.1 billion of new equity by 2030.

AEP is raising its dividend faster

AEP is riding the same data center wave and turning more of it into dividend growth. Its dividend rose 5.7% a year from fiscal 2020 to fiscal 2025, from $2.84 to $3.74 a share. It pays $3.80 a year at its current rate.

Contracted load additions now reach 69 gigawatts through 2030, up from 63 last quarter. AEP says Texas data centers, hyperscalers and industrial customers are driving most of that. On July 30, it raised its 2026 operating EPS guidance to $6.25 to $6.55 a share, from $6.15 to $6.45.
