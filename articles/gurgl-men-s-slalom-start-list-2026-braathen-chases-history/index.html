Gurgl Men's Slalom Start List 2026: Braathen Chases History

TheTools · 21.11.2026 19:04 · source · permalink

Lucas Pinheiro Braathen / GEPA

GURGL, Austria — Lucas Pinheiro Braathen has won both World Cup races this season. On Sunday, Nov. 22, the Brazilian can accomplish something only American Bode Miller has done: win the opening three events of a men’s World Cup season.

Miller achieved the feat in 2004-05. Braathen arrives in Gurgl with victories in the Sölden giant slalom and Levi slalom.

The official Gurgl men’s slalom start list features 68 racers from 21 nations. Norway occupies three of the top five positions on the slalom World Cup Start List (WCSL), while the Stifel U.S. Ski Team enters Ben Ritchie, Jett Seymour and Cooper Puckett.

The race begins at 10:30 a.m. CET on the Kirchenkar course in Hochgurgl.

What to Know Before the Gurgl Men’s Slalom

  • 🇦🇹 AUT — Gurgl hosts the second men’s World Cup slalom of 2026-27, with 68 racers representing 21 nations.
  • 🇧🇷 BRA — Lucas Pinheiro Braathen draws bib 3 and seeks his third consecutive World Cup victory.
  • 🇳🇴 NOR — Atle Lie McGrath starts bib 1, while three Norwegians occupy the WCSL top five.
  • 🇫🇷 FRA — Defending Gurgl champion Paco Rassat starts bib 7.
  • 🇺🇸 USA — Ben Ritchie (35), Jett Seymour (36) and Cooper Puckett (45) represent the Stifel U.S. Ski Team.
  • 🇬🇧 GBR — Laurie Taylor (23), Billy Major (30) and Zak Carrick-Smith (64) are entered.
  • 🇧🇪 BEL — Sam Maes starts bib 38 in his 100th World Cup appearance.

Can Braathen Match Bode Miller’s Historic Start?

Braathen has already won twice in two different disciplines. His victory in Levi made him the first man to win consecutive World Cup slaloms at the Finnish venue.

According to FIS media statistics, Miller is the only man to win the season’s first three World Cup events. His 2004-05 streak included giant slalom, downhill and super-G victories.

Braathen can match that achievement Sunday. However, the competition remains formidable.

Levi runner-up Clément Noël starts bib 2. Switzerland’s Loïc Meillard, who finished third in Finland, draws bib 6.

Norway’s Atle Lie McGrath, Henrik Kristoffersen and Timon Haugan are equally dangerous. All three rank among the top five on the slalom WCSL.

Five Nations Lead the Early Slalom Standings — Where Is Norway?

After Levi, five countries occupy the first five positions in the season’s slalom standings. Remarkably, none represents Norway, last season’s strongest men’s slalom nation.

  • 1st — 🇧🇷 BRA Lucas Pinheiro Braathen: 100 points
  • 2nd — 🇫🇷 FRA Clément Noël: 80 points
  • 3rd — 🇨🇭 SUI Loïc Meillard: 60 points
  • 4th — 🇸🇪 SWE Fabian Ax Swartz: 50 points
  • 5th — 🇮🇹 ITA Alex Vinatzer: 45 points

McGrath was Norway’s best finisher in Levi, placing seventh. However, the country’s strength remains evident in the WCSL rankings. Gurgl offers another opportunity for the Norwegians to assert themselves.

Gurgl’s Top Seven WCSL Seeds — Official Bib Order

The seven highest-ranked slalom racers hold the first seven starting positions. The Saturday draw determined their bib order.

  • Bib 1 — 🇳🇴 NOR Atle Lie McGrath | YOB 2000 | WCSL 1st, 578 points | HEAD
  • Bib 2 — 🇫🇷 FRA Clément Noël | YOB 1997 | WCSL 3rd, 563 points
  • Bib 3 — 🇧🇷 BRA Lucas Pinheiro Braathen | YOB 2000 | WCSL 2nd, 574 points | Atomic, Oakley
  • Bib 4 — 🇳🇴 NOR Henrik Kristoffersen | YOB 1994 | WCSL 4th, 535 points | HEAD
  • Bib 5 — 🇳🇴 NOR Timon Haugan | YOB 1996 | WCSL 5th, 523 points
  • Bib 6 — 🇨🇭 SUI Loïc Meillard | YOB 1996 | WCSL 6th, 517 points
  • Bib 7 — 🇫🇷 FRA Paco Rassat | YOB 1998 | WCSL 7th, 399 points | HEAD

These seven are the established podium contenders, although Levi demonstrated that younger racers can challenge the elite. Sweden’s Fabian Ax Swartz, bib 19, finished fourth in Finland for his first World Cup top 10.

2026-27 Men’s World Cup Slalom Podiums

Braathen won in 1:48.04. Noël finished 0.56 seconds behind, with Meillard another 0.17 seconds back.

Last Year’s Gurgl Podium — Nine-Hundredths Separated the Top Three

  • 1st — 🇫🇷 FRA Paco Rassat: 1:44.55
  • 2nd — 🇧🇪 BEL Armand Marchant: +0.07
  • 3rd — 🇳🇴 NOR Atle Lie McGrath: +0.09

All three return Sunday.

Rassat earned his first World Cup victory in Gurgl last season. Marchant celebrated his first World Cup podium, while McGrath claimed his second consecutive third-place finish at the Austrian venue.

Rassat draws bib 7, Marchant bib 10 and McGrath bib 1.

🇺🇸 USA — Three Stifel U.S. Ski Team Men Race in Gurgl

Three Americans will race in Gurgl. The other U.S. racers from Levi remained in Finland for the Europa Cup competition.

– Bib 35 — 🇺🇸 USA Ben Ritchie | YOB 2000 | WCSL 37th, 45 points | Green Mountain Valley School | HEAD | Stifel Headgear Athlete. Qualified 20th in Levi before a second-run DNF.

– Bib 36 — 🇺🇸 USA Jett Seymour | YOB 1998 | WCSL 51st, 12 points | Steamboat Springs Winter Sports Club | University of Denver | Atomic | Stifel Headgear Athlete. Finished 38th in Levi.

– Bib 45 — 🇺🇸 USA Cooper Puckett | YOB 2003 | WCSL 60th, 5 points | Steamboat Mountain School | Steamboat Springs Winter Sports Club | Dartmouth College | HEAD, SHRED.

🇬🇧 GBR — Three British Racers, Including Zak Carrick-Smith

Great Britain enters three men, including promising young racer Zak Carrick-Smith.

  • Bib 23 — 🇬🇧 GBR Laurie Taylor | YOB 1996 | WCSL 26th, 104 points | HEAD
  • Bib 30 — 🇬🇧 GBR Billy Major | YOB 1996 | WCSL 35th, 57 points | HEAD
  • Bib 64 — 🇬🇧 GBR Zak Carrick-Smith | YOB 2007

Major arrives with momentum after finishing 15th in Levi. Taylor will seek to convert his experience into another strong result, while Carrick-Smith gets an opportunity to challenge a World Cup field.

🇧🇪 BEL — Sam Maes Reaches His 100th World Cup Start

–Bib 38 — 🇧🇪 BEL Sam Maes | YOB 1998 | WCSL 39th, 35 points | Völkl, Marker, SHRED | Trains with the Stifel U.S. Ski Team.

Maes, who trains with the Stifel U.S. Ski Team, reaches a significant career milestone Sunday.

According to FIS, Gurgl will mark his 100th World Cup start. His career appearances will be divided into 60 giant slaloms and 40 slaloms, the most World Cup starts by any Belgian man.

The milestone adds another dimension to a race already featuring compatriot Armand Marchant, last year’s Gurgl runner-up.

A Smaller Field, but Plenty of International Talent

Gurgl’s 68 entrants from 21 nations represent a smaller field than Levi’s 86 racers from 23 countries. That is 18 fewer competitors, a reduction of approximately 21%.

There are no Canadian athletes entered. Liam Wallace, who earned his first World Cup points with 21st place in Levi, is absent from the Gurgl start list.
