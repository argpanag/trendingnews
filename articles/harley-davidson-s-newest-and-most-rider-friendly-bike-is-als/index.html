Harley-Davidson’s Newest and Most Rider-Friendly Bike Is Also Its Most Affordable (By a Country Mile) | Gear Patrol

TheTools · 02.11.2026 11:01 · source · permalink

The just-unveiled Sprint 440 represents a bold, barrier-lowering new direction for the iconic American brand.

If you buy from a link, we may earn a commission. Learn more

From the electric LiveWire to the adventure-ready Pan America to the fun and funky little Honcho, Harley-Davidson has made some pretty dynamic moves over the past decade.

Beyond the bikes themselves, these nameplates are signs the brand is both deeply in touch with its rich history and free to evolve in forward-thinking ways. 

The latest model also taps into heritage while carving out its own path as the most approachable and affordable gas-powered bike in the lineup: meet the 2027 Harley-Davidson Sprint 440. 

I was one of a small group of American and Canadian journalists who got to test-ride it in Barcelona yesterday, and my full review (spoiler alert: it’s a banger!) will be coming later this week. 

Meantime, here are some fast facts about and photos of the bike I’ve been practically begging Harley to make for a long time. 

The first thing you’ll notice is the engine, as unlike what you’ll find on so many legendary Harleys, it is not a V-twin.

While this will likely turn off some die-hards, the scrappy little single-cylinder nods to the original Sprints Harley produced in the 1960s (heritage!) with Italy’s Aermacchi.

The air/oil-cooled 440cc engine makes 27 horsepower and 28 lb-ft of torque, with a long piston stroke delivering a lively response low in the rev range.

While those specs don’t approach what you’ll get with Harley’s bigger bikes, that’s truly not the point.

Because when your goal is lowering motorcycling’s numerous barriers to entry, here are the numbers that really matter: 26.4-inch seat height, 417-pound wet weight and an MSRP of (wait for it) $5,999.

Those figures alone instantly make the bike much more accessible, welcoming new riders in a way the brand hasn’t in years, while still imbuing Harley DNA and handy modern features.

Taking cues from the Indian-market X440 (as we suspected it might), the Sprint’s silhouette is solid and muscular, with a beefy 19-inch front wheel leading the charge.

Aesthetic highlights include a traditional teardrop tank with a warped checkered center and classic Harley typeface, a black powertrain finish with machined highlights on the engine fins and 10-spoke cast aluminum wheels finished in black.

Beyond the seat height and weight, additional rider-friendly features include comfy mid-controls, ABS and a tapered saddle, making it even easier for shorter riders to plant their feet on terra firma.

Meanwhile, the clutch’s pressure-reducing assist function makes it so easy to pull in the lever and shift among the six gears, you’ll forget all about E-Clutch (sorry, review note sneaking in here).

Additional tech is quiet but solid, led by a retro-round 3.5-inch color TFT display above the handlebar, all LED lighting and a USB charging port above the headlamp.

I’ll dive deeper into details in my full review later this week, but know that (spoiler alert #2) actually riding the thing only made me more excited for this small thunder to hit the streets.

Availability and pricing

The 2027 Harley-Davidson Sprint 440 launches across the pond and in Canada this month.

Because Harley is (admirably) committed to doing final assembly for the American version in York, Pennsylvania, it’s coming in spring 2027.

As mentioned above, the starting price is $5,999.
