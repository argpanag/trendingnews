Man jailed for causing wrong-way police chase crash

TheTools · 25.11.2026 22:13 · source · permalink

The A46 southbound was shut after a crash involving a van and car on 29 June

  • Published
    3 hours ago

A man who crashed into an oncoming car after driving the wrong way along the A46 during a police chase, seriously injuring another driver, has been jailed.

Niks Rogacs, 26, was driving a white Mercedes van which had been reported stolen when the crash took place shortly after 04:30 BST on 29 June.

While high on cocaine, he fled after being spotted by Leicestershire Police, but drove on to the wrong side of the A46 before hitting a blue BMW 1 Series near Glenfield.

Rogacs, of Acacia Close, Leicester, who has pleaded guilty to causing serious injury by dangerous driving and two counts of handling stolen goods, was jailed for four and a half years at Leicester Crown Court on Wednesday.

The court heard how the van had been stolen from the car park in Walnut Street in the early hours of 24 June.

It contained a number of sentimental musical instruments and equipment worth about £3,000.

It was five days later that the vehicle was spotted by three unmarked police cars in the Aylestone Road area of Leicester shortly after 04:30 BST.

Dashcam footage played to the court showed the van persistently driving on the wrong side of the road, reaching 72mph (115kmh) in a 40mph zone.

Rogacs drove the wrong way around a number of roundabouts before finally taking a slip road on to oncoming traffic on the A46.

Footage showed the van driving on the outside lane of the dual carriageway into oncoming traffic.

Seconds later, the car swerved onto the inside lane to avoid a vehicle and crashed into the BMW which was being driven by Jack Riddel, who was on his way to work in Coventry.

The court heard that an officer who had worked for five years in Leicestershire Police's roads team said it was the "worst piece of dangerous driving" he had ever seen.

Niks Rogacs, 26, has been jailed for four and a half years

Bodyworn footage played to the court showed Rogacs tried to run away from the van, which was on fire and later exploded, before being apprehended.

The footage showed Riddel severely injured in the BMW before being dragged away from the scene by officers.

The court heard Riddel was taken to Queen's Medical Centre in Nottingham where he had a cardiac arrest and was revived by medics.

He suffered a bleed on the brain, facial fractures and severe breaks to his pelvis, left hip and both legs.

Since the crash, the victim has undergone a number of surgeries and remained in hospital for 11 weeks.

He is continuing to receive specialist care and attended court in a wheelchair.

In a victim impact statement, Riddel said: "29 June will be etched in my memory as it has changed my life forever.

"I just can't do what I used to do.

"I'm only 24 years old, I should be able to drive and get around freely and do what I want to do.

"I would like to say 'thank you' to everyone who helped me that day, particularly the officers who pulled me away from the car.

"I know I am lucky to be here."

The court heard that blood taken from Rogacs showed he was three times over the unmetabolised cocaine limit and five times over the metabolised cocaine limit at the time of the crash.

Judge Timothy Spencer KC said the case of dangerous driving was "one of the worst this court has ever seen".

"It's all absolutely appalling," he said.

"He had his life ahead of him and his career ahead of him and he was hit full on by you in a stolen van which you were driving the wrong way in that dual carriageway at speed.

"He had absolutely no chance of avoiding the collision.

"Although he regards himself as being lucky to be alive, he still suffered the most awful injury."

Masti Band percussionist, Surinder Sandhu, had his "prototype" dholki drum in the stolen van

The van had been stolen from pop-bhangra act Masti Band and had been carrying "priceless" drums.

At the time of the theft, Aamir Rana, the band's vocalist and keyboard player, told the BBC specialised drums were among the van's contents, which also included mixing desks, amplifiers and speakers.

The 36-year-old said a dhol drum was owned by his uncle and the band's percussionist, Surinder Sandhu, 62, who had played it for more than 40 years and had his name written on the side.

Rana said Sandhu, from Leicester, made several modifications to a traditional dholki, which was a new prototype.
