Men jailed over firebomb attempt foiled by vigilantes

TheTools · 16.11.2026 23:36 · source · permalink

Two Dublin men hired to firebomb a house in Galway City four years ago were foiled by a local vigilante group, a court has been told.

Galway Circuit Criminal Court heard yesterday that Richard Clarke, aged 36, with an address at Walkinstown Parade, Walkinstown, Dublin 12, and Andrew Marlowe, aged 51, with an address at Patrick Henry House, Summerhill, Dublin 1, were involved in a car collision at Joyce Roundabout in Galway city on 11 February 2022.

The court heard that the Saab car they were travelling in was rammed by another vehicle about 400 metres away from a home they planned to burn out in Bohermore, Galway.

They both pleaded guilty last year to possessing items - including a red Jerry can filled with petrol, a face mask, a baseball bat, a metal bar, and a bottle of butane gas - intended to damage property contrary to section 4 of the Criminal Damage Act. Sentencing was delayed to this week due to the solicitors' legal aid dispute.

The court heard that their car - occupied by five men - was rammed by a local vigilante group who were patrolling the area. They received injuries in the collision and were being attacked by vigilantes when Gardaí arrived on the scene.

Detective Garda Marvin Lee said they made themselves known to gardaí for their own protection and were arrested.

Both initially denied the offence but pleaded guilty last November on the day their trial was to proceed. Two other men have already been sentenced and a fifth man is due to come before the court.

Det Lee said that Marlowe and Clarke, who were both addicted to drugs, did not know the intended target of the planned arson attack. He said they were doing it for financial gain or to "write off a drug debt".

Judge Brian O’Callaghan noted that Marlowe, who had 79 previous convictions, was caught red-handed in possession of an iPhone which contained an incriminating message from the person who ordered the attack. It said: "Make sure car and house goes up and send us a video when it’s done so we know the story."

He noted that Clarke, who had eight previous convictions, was in possession of a phone with Google Maps directions from Dublin to the intended target in Bohermore, including an Eircode.

Judge O’Callaghan rejected the suggestion from defence barrister Garry McDonald that the two men were inebriated and "off their heads" on drink and drugs.

They were not "stooges", he insisted, and said they knew exactly what they were doing.

The judge said these were "disquieting and troubling" offences, and the plans to firebomb a house in Bohermore could have resulted in serious damage to property and injury.

He said the actions of the vigilante group, who tried to take over from An Garda Síochána, were not to be commended under any circumstances. He said this was "not a Dublin thing" and it was quite likely that the person or persons who ordered the attack were Galway-based.

Judge O'Callaghan imposed a headline sentence of seven years in prison for Marlowe but reduced it by 12 months after mitigation.

He imposed a six-year sentence on Clarke and reduced it to five years after mitigation. The final year was suspended for three years to promote rehabilitation.
