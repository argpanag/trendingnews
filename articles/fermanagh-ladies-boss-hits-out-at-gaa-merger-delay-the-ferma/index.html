Fermanagh ladies boss hits out at GAA merger delay - The Fermanagh Herald

TheTools · 10.09.2026 14:58 · source · permalink

FERMANAGH ladies manager Barry McKenna says the decision to delay the proposed merger of the GAA, LGFA and Camogie Association is ‘very disappointing’, insisting the county’s players ‘deserve to be on the big stage’.

McKenna said players had been working towards the planned 2027 integration and were now facing uncertainty after the GAA said it could not commit to a definitive timeline.

“A lot of work has gone in and for everybody to have 2027 in their head, and the closer it has got and then it fell apart, it’s very disappointing,” McKenna told the ‘Herald.

“It feels like everything has gone back to square one. I don’t think they deserve that. They deserve to be on the big stage.”

Deep concern

The three organisations had been working towards creating a single association by April 2027, but that target has now been thrown into doubt.

Former President of Ireland Dr Mary McAleese, who chaired the Integration Committee, subsequently stepped down, saying she was “absolutely devastated” by the development.

McKenna believes the delay highlights the need for a change in mindset towards women’s Gaelic football.

“I would safely say the girls on my panel have put in more effort than some men on some county panels, definitely, and that’s not good enough,” he said.

He also believes the women’s game has the potential to attract much larger crowds if it receives greater backing and promotion.

“I saw somebody say they would be surprised to see ladies football ever sell out Croke Park.

“When you see last Saturday that a woman boxer [Katie Taylor] could sell out Croke Park, that backing is there and if it’s properly advertised, you will be able to,” added McKenna.

McKenna said ladies football is continuing to grow but warned that setbacks could impact efforts to retain talented players.

“We seem to be losing a lot of top stars to Australian Rules and enticing girls towards Australia,” he said.

“For the amount of work that has gone into it and then to be told it is maybe back to 2030, I don’t know what will happen to all the work that has been done up to now.”
