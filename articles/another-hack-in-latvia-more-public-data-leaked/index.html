Another hack in Latvia, more public data leaked

TheTools · 03.09.2026 17:51 · source · permalink

Following on from successful recent attacks on Latvian State Forests (LVM) and the Road Transport Safety Directorate (CSDD), this time the target was the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (PTAC).

"PTAC informs about a recently detected data security incident in one of the information systems under the control of PTAC.  The affected information system is the "Remote Statistical Data Retrieval System" (ASDIS). ASDIS is a level C security class system, which is the lowest risk level of an information system. This system is used by the CRPC to ensure the supervision of licensed merchants and meets the minimum cybersecurity requirements set by the country," said PTAC.

The hack is believed to have taken place two days ago on September 1st.

"The current investigation shows that as a result of the incident, contact information of entities licensed by PTAC, namely, providers of consumer credit services, providers of extrajudicial debt recovery services, and providers of complex tourism services, has been obtained," said PTAC. 

Contact information for 697 business representatives and 34 PTAC officials – name, surname, email and phone number – was stolen. In most cases, but by no means all, this information is already available in other public registers, PTAC said by way of attempting to downplay the leak.

Of the leaked data, 106 contacts were not publicly available. The system affected by the incident is currently closed. 

Gints Mālkalnietis, a cybersecurity expert at the cyber incident prevention institution CERT.lv, said the risk posed was relatively minor risk, and that the attack was much simpler when compared to the CSDD and LVM cases.

The hackers who carried out the attack are not from Latvia, Russia, or Europe, but CERT.lv is not disclosing any further information for now. The expert only added that the stolen data was posted on a popular forum for hackers.
