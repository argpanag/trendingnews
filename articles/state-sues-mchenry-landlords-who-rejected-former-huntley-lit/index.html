State sues McHenry landlords who rejected former Huntley Little League treasurer over his theft, fraud arrest

TheTools · 12.10.2026 20:42 · source · permalink

The Illinois attorney general has sued the owners of a McHenry home, accusing them of illegally turning away a former Huntley Little League treasurer because he faced felony charges, including allegedly stealing from the league.

The Illinois Attorney General’s Office filed the lawsuit in McHenry County Circuit Court on behalf of the Illinois Department of Human Rights against John and Morgan Goorsky, the owners of the townhouse.

The lawsuit accuses the Goorskys of violating the Illinois Human Rights Act by refusing to rent the townhouse to Lakemoor resident Philip A. Rizzo Jr., a former Huntley Little League treasurer, because of his arrest record.

The act makes it a civil rights violation to refuse to engage in a real estate transaction with someone because of their arrest record, which includes an arrest that has not led to a conviction, the lawsuit said.

Rizzo submitted a rental application through Zillow on October 25, 2025, for the townhouse in the 2300 block of Evergreen Circle in McHenry and consented to a background check through the site.

The Zillow listing for the two-bedroom townhouse advertised it for $2,100 per month and said the homeowners association requires a background check for each renter that must be completed 10 days before moving in.

John Goorsky told Rizzo on November 3, 2025, that his rental application had been denied after the background check revealed “information indicating pending felony charges,” the lawsuit said.

John Goorsky also searched for Rizzo on Google and found that he had pending cases in both Cook and McHenry counties, according to the lawsuit.

The lawsuit said John Goorsky’s stated reason for not renting to Rizzo was that the decision “was made in accordance with our standard screening practices and is not related to race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status, disability, sexual orientation, or any other protected class under federal or Illinois law.”

John Goorsky also cited the Zillow background check and his own Google search as part of the basis for the decision, the lawsuit said.

Morgan Goorsky, as a co-owner of the townhouse, is jointly responsible for the alleged discrimination against Rizzo, the lawsuit alleges.

Rizzo suffered emotional distress, embarrassment, humiliation, insult, pain and suffering, incidental expenses and attorney’s fees and costs as a result of being turned away, the lawsuit said.

Rizzo filed a charge of discrimination with the Illinois Department of Human Rights later that month and the department found substantial evidence supporting the charge in April.

The department filed a complaint against the Goorskys with the Illinois Human Rights Commission in August but the Goorskys elected to have the case decided in circuit court instead.

That election required the Illinois Attorney General’s Office to file a civil lawsuit on Rizzo’s behalf in circuit court within 30 days after the commission closed the case, according to the lawsuit.

The lawsuit seeks damages, attorney’s fees and costs for Rizzo along with $16,000 in civil penalties to be paid to the state.

It also asks the court to order the Goorskys to stop discriminating against anyone based on an arrest record, adopt anti-discrimination and anti-retaliation policies, attend fair housing training and post notices about the requirements of the Illinois Human Rights Act.

Rizzo, who is representing himself, has since petitioned to join the lawsuit as an intervenor, arguing the act gives him an unconditional right to intervene as the aggrieved party.

Rizzo said he is seeking to take part in the case on the issues affecting his individual rights and relief, including his claim for damages.

Rizzo submitted his own proposed complaint against the Goorskys along with the petition, seeking actual damages in an amount to be determined through discovery and at trial. He demanded a trial by jury on all issues.

Rizzo is charged in the Huntley Little League case with one count of theft over $10,000, a Class 2 felony, and one count of fraudulent use of a credit or debit card, a Class 4 felony.

Rizzo allegedly obtained unauthorized control over $14,297 belonging to the Huntley Little League between August and November 2022 while he served on the league’s board as treasurer, according to the criminal complaint.

The complaint said Rizzo made five unauthorized cash withdrawals ranging from $500 to $3,725 using the league’s credit or debit card, which had been issued to him as a board member.

Huntley Little League President Todd Doyle said in July 2025 that Rizzo was a board member and league treasurer from 2019 until spring 2023, when he was removed from the board.

Rizzo was taken into custody at the McHenry County Jail in July 2025 and released following an Initial Appearance Court hearing where McHenry County Judge Cynthia Lamb ordered him placed on pre-trial services monitoring.

A McHenry County grand jury indicted Rizzo on the charges in September 2025 and he pleaded not guilty. He is represented by the McHenry County Public Defender’s Office.

McHenry County Judge Mark Gerhardt set the case for a jury trial in May after it had been scheduled for a negotiated plea hearing, court records show. The trial is scheduled to begin December 14.

Rizzo was also charged in Cook County with allegedly stealing over $1 million from companies he worked for during the same period he allegedly stole from the Huntley Little League, according to court records.

A scheduling conference in the lawsuit is set for December 8 before McHenry County Judge Joel Berg.
