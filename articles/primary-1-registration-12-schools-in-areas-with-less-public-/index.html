Primary 1 registration: 12 schools in areas with less public housing to get new 2-track scheme from 2027

TheTools · 10.09.2026 10:30 · source · permalink

The Ministry of Education (MOE) will introduce a new two-track scheme for Phase 2C Primary 1 (P1) registration at 12 primary schools from the 2027 P1 Registration Exercise.

The changes will apply to children starting P1 in 2028, said the ministry on Thursday (Sept 10).

Under the new scheme, Phase 2C places at these 12 schools will be equally distributed across two tracks — children living within 2km of the school and those living more than 2km from the school.

This is part of wider changes to the P1 Registration Framework aimed at improving access for children without prior connections to a school and encouraging greater mixing among students from different backgrounds.

There will be no further home-school distance priority within either track.

Said MOE: "For schools located in neighbourhoods with significantly less public housing than the national average, applying the current home-school distance priority may not substantially broaden access for children from different socio-economic backgrounds." 

The 12 schools are:

  • Anglo-Chinese School (Junior)
  • Anglo-Chinese School (Primary)
  • CHIJ (Katong) Primary
  • Methodist Girls' School (Primary)
  • Nanyang Primary School
  • Ngee Ann Primary School
  • Pei Hwa Presbyterian Primary School
  • Raffles Girls' Primary School
  • Singapore Chinese Girls' Primary School
  • St. Margaret's School (Primary)
  • Tanjong Katong Primary School
  • Tao Nan School

If the number of Singapore Citizen (SC) applicants exceeds the number of places available in either track, balloting will be conducted.

If there are remaining places in one track after all SC applicants in that track have been admitted, the remaining places will flow to SC applicants in the other track.

SCs will continue to have priority over Permanent Residents (PR). 

The scheme will apply to 12 schools where public housing accounts for less than 40 per cent of dwelling units within 2km of the school.

MOE said it will periodically review the list of schools under the scheme, taking into account changes in their surrounding housing profiles and the experience of implementing the new arrangement.

Reserved Phase 2C spots up from 40 to 60

Separately, MOE will increase the number of reserved places in Phase 2C from 40 to 60 in all schools, except for schools with a smaller intake.

Schools with fewer than 180 P1 places will continue to have 40 places reserved for Phase 2C.

Phase 2C is open to all children regardless of whether they have prior connections to the school.

The additional 20 reserved places will come from the earlier registration phases.

However, those eligible for the earlier Phases 2A and 2B will continue to have priority admission ahead of Phase 2C applicants.

The number of reserved places in Phase 2B will remain at 20. 

Aiming for more diverse student profiles

Education Minister Desmond Lee said on Thursday that MOE had spoken with parents, school leaders and alumni associations as part of its review of the framework.

There was "broad agreement" that primary schools should remain open and accessible to families from different backgrounds, he said.

However, there were different views on how this could be achieved, with some suggesting more places be reserved for families without prior connections, while others proposed reducing or removing home-school distance priority.

"Parents value a school near home, particularly for their young children, and it's more convenient for siblings to attend the same school together," he said. 

Lee added that simply increasing Phase 2C places may not substantially broaden access to children from different socio-economic backgrounds at schools in neighbourhoods with significantly less public housing, as priority is currently based on home-school distance.

"Whether these schools will in fact see greater diversity will, of course, depend on other factors as well, including parents' preferences and their choices," the minister stated. 

"We will also continue to invest in every school, so that they are well resourced to provide good quality education for all our children."
