Harry Styles-Approved Designer Macy Grimshaw Just Won the 2026 H&M Design Award - AOL

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:09 · source · permalink

Earlier this year, Macy Grimshaw presented her graduate collection from Central Saint Martins at London Fashion Week, styled by Harry Lambert, the mind behind the wardrobes of Emma Corrin, Alexander Skarsgård, and Harry Styles. Since then, the 25-year-old designer has continued to rise, dressing the likes of Anya Taylor-Joy, Simone Ashley, and several of Lambert’s clients—Corrin and Styles among them—in her semi-surrealist separates and top-notch leatherwork. Now, Grimshaw has a major accolade to cap off her standout year: the 2026 H&M Design Award.

At the presentation on Wednesday afternoon in London, Grimshaw was chosen among seven other finalists from top design universities across the globe: Chico Yu, Ennis Finnerty Mackay, Evelina Kryvopust, Fantin Delattre Pirenne, Jessica Lin, Macy Grimshaw, Yichun Liu, and Yvonne Schichtel. Grimshaw will receive a €150,000 ($164,655) endowment, a yearlong mentorship, and will have her collection produced and sold in selected H&M stores worldwide and online in October 2027. Each finalist was awarded a €10,000 ($10,980) endowment.

“She left me with desire and intrigue to see how she can actually develop the work,” said Beka Gvishiani, founder of Style Not Com, who joined H&M’s creative advisor Ann-Sofie Johansson, model Paloma Elsesser, and designer Ib Kamara on the jury. “For the moment, it’s very much the artist’s work,” he continued. “But she has ideas about how to make it wearable for daywear, because some of the pieces are great for an event or a performance.”

Gvishiani highlights the collection’s more experimental pieces, like a rusted, laser-cut mesh minidress ornamented with padlocks or a terracotta-hued molded bustier scattered with discarded cigarette butts. The scenography of the latter was inspired, naturally, by the brick wall where CSM students gather on smoke breaks.

But in between those more experimental moments, it’s easy to see why Grimshaw walked away with the prize, H&M’s first time awarding it since 2020. While fodder for celebrity placements and red carpets, her work also carries “hanger appeal,” clothes that are both wearable and appealing but also evoke something unexpected and slightly “off.” Like a texturized, scalloped suit jacket with a taut waist à la the “New Look” or the almost wet-looking crumpled silk slip dress done in a graphic, almost watercolor effect.

The line took cues from the designer’s college years in London (at 18, she moved there from Hong Kong to attend CSM) and speaks to her introspective way of working. “My whole practice is inspired by my grandmother having Alzheimer’s and trying to represent the fading of memories by using everyday objects and to recontextualize them,” Grimshaw told ELLE after the award was announced. “I want to remind us not to take the everyday for granted; that’s why a lot of my work is pencil shavings, cigarettes, locks.”

Next up, the designer will head to H&M’s home base of Stockholm, where she’ll get started on her collaboration with the brand. “This opportunity is going to be a massive help to make my work accessible to everyone,” she adds. “I’m very excited because this collection was not designed with the idea of commerciality. So I’m really interested to challenge myself and also work with other people to see how it can become that.”
