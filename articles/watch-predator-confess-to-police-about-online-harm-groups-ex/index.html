Watch predator confess to police about online harm groups extorting children

TheTools · 27.11.2026 17:58 · source · permalink

Predatory Beau-Jack Mensforth told police the online harm groups he was involved with would extort girls into cutting themselves. Mensforth is today starting a 14-and-a-half year custodial sentence for a series of horrific offences which left vulnerable youngsters physically and mentally harmed.

After he was sentenced, police released footage of him being interviewed, during which he made frank admissions about the activities of the group.

Newcastle Crown Court heard Mensforth was a leading participant in international "online harm groups" which target youngsters and blackmail them into doing sickening acts, including disfiguring their bodies for them to put online as "content".

His victims, in the UK, USA and Australia, have been left damaged mentally and physically for life. One girl's mum branded him and others involved in the vile offending "cowardly monsters" who were "hiding behind a screen tormenting my baby girl". Another victim told him: "You are what can only be described as a psychopath".

'Calculating predator" Mensforth, 20, of Cairo Street, Sunderland, admitted 17 offences, including participating in the activities of an organised crime group, blackmail, communicating sexually with children and distributing and possessing indecent images of children, between June 2023 and June 2024.

Jailing him, Judge Edward Bindloss said he was a dangerous offender and locked him up for 14-and-a-half years with an extended licence period of a further six years. He told him: "This disturbing case proves you are a calculating predator who groomed and blackmailed victims by making threats to expose them, dox them, swat them or distribute their intimate photos which you had coerced out of them.

"Those threats generated child pornography or images or them self-harming, which you distributed online to like-minded people in the community that amounted to an organised crime group, who shared your taste for sexual violence and enjoyed watching females self-harm."

Robin Patton, defending, said the case involved "rarely encountered depravity" and said a doctor had been instructed to look into "how he descended into this moral squalor". Mr Patton said Mensforth has autism and social anxiety and that he became socially isolated after his dad died when he was 14.

Mr Patton added: "As often happens with online offending, his began with gaming sites then moved on to online forums and he moved onto a group where you have to prove yourself in order to join. He became a victim who became a recruiter."
