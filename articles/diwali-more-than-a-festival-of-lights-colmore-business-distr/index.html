Diwali: More Than a Festival of Lights - Colmore Business District

TheTools · 27.10.2026 09:29 · source · permalink

By Anita Champaneri, Managing Director of Delicious PR

There is something rather special about Diwali. Yes, there are the beautiful lights, the candles, the delicious food, the dressing up and, of course, the coming together of family and friends. But for me, Diwali has a meaning that goes far beyond the celebrations.

At its heart, Diwali is about light overcoming darkness, good overcoming evil, and hope triumphing over adversity. And I think we could all do with a little more of that in our lives.

This year has made me more fearful of dark times prevailing over good more than any other time in my life. International war-mongering, racial tension and anti-immigration rhetoric has made me worry about the future even more. As a mum of two sons making their way in the real world, it seems they have more darkness than light to contend with.

So in 2026, I am determined to spread the goodwill of Diwali than ever before. We have to believe that there is the  possibility of a brighter chapter ahead.

Diwali, also known as Deepavali, is celebrated by millions of people around the world. Its significance varies between communities and faiths, including Hinduism, Jainism and Sikhism, but light, hope, renewal and the triumph of good are important themes across many celebrations.

For many Hindus, Diwali is associated with the return of Lord Rama, Sita and Lakshmana to Ayodhya after fourteen years in exile, and the lighting of lamps to welcome them home. For others, it carries different religious and spiritual meanings, including the celebration of Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity and good fortune.

One of the things I love about traditions is that they connect us to something bigger than ourselves. They link generations, keep stories alive and give us an opportunity to pause in a world that seems determined to keep us busy every minute of the day.

The lighting of diyas, the beautiful patterns of rangoli, the sharing of sweets and the exchanging of gifts are all part of the celebration. But behind every tradition is a feeling: welcome, generosity, gratitude and togetherness.

And those are values worth carrying with us all year round.
