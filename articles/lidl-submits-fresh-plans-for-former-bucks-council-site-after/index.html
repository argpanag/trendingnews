Lidl submits fresh plans for former Bucks council site after store plans refused

Lidl has submitted fresh plans for the former Bucks Council offices in Amersham, after its supermarket proposal for the site was rejected.

But the new application is not for another store. It seeks permission to put up security hoarding around the site for four years while future redevelopment plans are worked on.

The former King George V House, on King George V Road, has now been demolished. Lidl Great Britain wants timber or steel hoarding around the site’s perimeter, with the drawings showing it standing 2.4 metres (nearly 8ft) high.

Plans show a boundary around the plot, with gates for people and vehicles. Two vehicle gates are marked on the drawings, one 4m wide and the other 6m wide. The application site covers about 637 square metres, or 0.15 acres.

Elevation drawings show the hoarding along King George V Road and Chiltern Avenue, with panels designed to give the cleared site a “consistent” frontage. The gates would allow access for site maintenance and inspection.

In a letter submitted with the application, Lidl’s planning agent said the hoarding would secure the vacant site and help prevent unauthorised access, anti-social behaviour and fly-tipping.

They said the boundary would be a tidier option than open fencing or temporary barriers.

It would also not change the site’s use or alter its existing access arrangements. No plans for a new building, supermarket, homes or other redevelopment are included in this application.

The agent said the land was awaiting future redevelopment, but the application gives no details of what might be built there or when.

It says the hoarding would either be removed after four years or further planning permission would be sought to keep it in place.

The move comes after Buckinghamshire Council refused Lidl’s plans for a supermarket on the former offices site in May.

Lidl announced its original proposals in August 2025. They attracted 165 objections and 212 expressions of support.

But the council said the supermarket and car park would make inefficient use of the land, which it said could accommodate a more intensive mixed-use development, such as shops alongside homes.

It also criticised the design, saying the low-rise store would sit too far back from the main street, leaving the car park to dominate the frontage and harm the street scene.

The council offices closed in 2024 and the site was sold before demolition began.

Lidl’s planning agent says the hoarding would provide a “neat and well-maintained frontage”.
