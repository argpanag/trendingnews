ATP Tokyo Day 1 Predictions Including Tabilo vs Paul

TheTools · 29.09.2026 20:24 · source · permalink

Three Top 10 players and Carlos Alcaraz will headline the ATP 500 event in Tokyo, with the tournament notably marking Kei Nishikori’s final appearance before retirement. Before the big names take to the Ariake Coliseum, there are already enticing Day 1 matchups ahead. LWOT has the preview for all of Wednesday’s six opening round matches, with this set of predictions featuring the No. 7 seed Tommy Paul. 

ATP Tokyo Day 1 Predictions

Jiri Lehecka vs Zizou Bergs

Head-to-head: 1-0 Lehecka

Many pundits expected a breakout year for Lehecka, but the Czech hasn’t been able to string together big wins since his French Open semifinal appearance. He did, however, build some confidence when beating Ben Shelton and Learner Tien in the Davis Cup earlier this month. Bergs has also steadily risen the ranks, but the Belgian will aim for a bigger jump into the Top 30. Both players rely on aggressive tennis and strong serves, but Lehecka has the higher ceiling and loves the fast courts. He lost just three games against Bergs, when the pair faced off in Doha this February.
Prediction: Lehecka in 3 

Valentin Vacherot vs Alexander Blockx 

Head-to-head: First meeting

Injuries have hampered Vacherot for portions of 2026, but the World No. 18 did well to reach the US Open third round, falling against the red-hot Frances Tiafoe. Meanwhile, Blockx’s confidence and his firepower elevated him into the Top 30 this year, coming off a US Open quarterfinal appearance. This match should be full of aces and strong forehands, with both players well over six feet tall; Vacherot has comfortably led Blockx in the ATP Tour’s season ace count, with 323. Vacherot will also feel some pressure having to soon defend 1000 points at the Shanghai Masters in October. Vacherot will win this if he can force the Belgian into errors, but I believe the match is on Blockx’s racket.
Prediction: Blockx in 3 

Tommy Paul vs Alejandro Tabilo

Head-to-head: 2-0 Paul

Paul recently faced a crushing US Open loss to another top player this season, this time the formidable Carlos Alcaraz. This is the American’s first time in Tokyo since 2024, while he’s put together a solid 7-4 run since the start of the North American hard court swing, according to Tennis Abstract. Tabilo’s lefty game can always pose challenges, with his forehand likely being the strongest weapon on court. However, the American and his topspin-heavy style rarely falters early in hard-court tournaments, and he should be too much here.
Prediction: Paul in 2
