Former Glentoran captain reflects on being given 'the biggest privilege' by Roy Coyle

TheTools · 20.11.2026 16:16 · source · permalink

Sports Journalist

Former Glentoran captain Pete Batey insists “it was an absolute privilege” to play under Roy Coyle and will always remember being handed the armband by an Irish League icon as “the biggest privilege”.

Batey arrived at The Oval from Bangor in 1995 – two years before Coyle, who collected 31 trophies as Linfield boss, was appointed with the pair going on to celebrate significant silverware success together, including league and Irish Cup glory.

A natural-born winner, Coyle stands alone as the Irish League’s most successful manager having added 17 trophies to his personal tally with the Glens after two at Ards across nearly 1,500 matches in a Premiership dugout.

His passing aged 80 was confirmed by Glentoran on Friday afternoon with tributes pouring in highlighting his significant impact on the game in Northern Ireland.

"He was a big, big personality, a big, big winner,” Batey told the News Letter. “When he first came to The Oval, the belief he instilled...I was a big fan of Tommy Cassidy, but when Coyler came in, he just instilled this winning mentality.

"I’ll never forget the first training session, we were doing laps of the pitch, there was a real buzz about the place with him being there.

"I don't know how far we were off the top of the table, but I remember him just saying, ‘the aim is to win the league’. We were like, ‘wow, we're going to win the league!’.

"Before he came to Glentoran, what he did at Linfield was absolutely amazing, he’s a big, big loss to the Irish League.”

Batey made a majority of his 259 appearances for Glentoran under Coyle, who named the England-born midfielder as his captain – the highlight of his career remains lifting the Irish Cup as skipper after beating Linfield 1-0 in 2001.

Coyle’s brilliance lay in his unwavering winning mentality with Batey clear “mental toughness” was a non-negotiable.

"He simplified the game for me,” added Batey. “If Roy Coyle picked you in his team, you were expected to do what you had been asked, you've got to go and perform.

"A seven or eight out of 10 was the bare minimum he expected, you had to perform every week, not just one in three or five, I enjoyed that.

"His demand on you was enormous, but you were very, very privileged to be in his team.

"He told me ‘run your heart out, win your tackles, win your headers, give it to somebody who can play’ – I turned up every Saturday to do that.

"The biggest privilege was that he actually made me captain of Glentoran after John Devine, who, in my eyes, was a fantastic player, fantastic clubman...John was everything you wanted to be if you were a Glentoran captain.

"I would say he was the nearest to playing under Alex Ferguson – I never played under Alex Ferguson, but I would imagine that Alex Ferguson would have been similar...there's a line in the sand, and if you cross it, no matter who you are, you are out.

"He always said ‘Glentoran is an institution, not a football club’, I'll never forget that. It was an absolute privilege to play for the man.”
