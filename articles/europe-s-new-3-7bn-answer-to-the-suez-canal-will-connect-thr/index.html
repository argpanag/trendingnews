Europe's new £3.7bn answer to the Suez Canal will connect three economies - AOL

TheTools · 28.10.2026 04:53 · source · permalink

A new £3.7billion canal could connect three of Europe's economies in a game-changing move. The Seine-Nord Europe Canal is a wide-gauge canal, and it's the first new French waterway built in over five decades. The canal will have similarities to the busy Suez Canal, a sea-level waterway running north-south across the Isthmus of Suez in Egypt, connecting the Mediterranean and the Red Seas. As the central link in the Seine-Escaut river link, the new canal will cross Hauts-de-France and allow large barges to travel efficiently between France, Belgium and the Netherlands.

The new Canal in France will accommodate large barges, increasing capacity from 650 to 4,400 tonnes, enabling the efficient transportation of goods. On completion, which should be around 2030, the 107km (66-mile) route will connect the Oise River to the Dunkirk-Escaut Canal.

It will reportedly allow trade barges to bypass the bottleneck of the Canal du Nord, making it quicker and easier to transport goods by water. The project also aims to help to reduce road traffic in France by approximately one million heavy goods vehicles per year.

There will be 62 road and rail crossings, as well as three canal bridges. The major project will also contain seven locks, including a junction lock with the Canal du Nord. Over 1,200 hectares of planting and environmental improvements are planned as part of the project.

However, despite what it offers, the plans have faced criticism. France's own version of Extinction Rebellion has criticised the project, denouncing it as putting economic growth over "ecological purpose." Other climate activists have also cast doubt over whether the canal will significantly reduce CO2 emissions as the canal executives claim. Despite the canal's stop-start history, the Société du Canal Seine-Nord Europe is confident its construction timeline can be met and the canal will become operational in 2030.

Since its inception, the Seine-Nord Europe Canal project has been developed in collaboration with residents, businesses, and local authorities in the Compiègne-Noyon area, the Santerre Haute-Somme region, and the Artois-Cambrésis region. On the official website for the project, it says that until the canal is operational, the teams at the Seine-Nord Europe Canal Company aim to keep locals updated on the amenities near them, the benefits of the project, the work program and the location of worksites.
