Fresh supermarket plans on former Buckinghamshire Council site

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:52 · source · permalink

Supermarket chain Lidl has submitted fresh plans for the site of former council offices after a proposal for a new store was refused earlier this year.

The latest application seeks permission to install security fencing around the former Buckinghamshire Council offices site in Amersham.

This comes after the council rejected plans for a supermarket on the former King George V House site in May.

Lidl wants the solid timber or steel hoarding to remain in place for up to four years while longer-term redevelopment proposals are worked on. The fencing would be 2.4m (eight feet) high.

The offices were demolished in August after they were sold by the council as part of a £300m investment programme.

Lidl announced its original proposals in August 2025, which attracted 165 objections and 212 expressions of support.

After planning permission was rejected, the council's Conservative deputy leader and cabinet member for planning, Peter Strachan, said the proposal was "unacceptable" and contrary to local planning policy.

He added that the buyer had indicated at the time of the sale that it planned to bring forward a housing-led development, including affordable homes.

"The council is therefore disappointed that the site was subsequently sold on and an alternative proposal brought forward," he said.

In a letter submitted with the application, Lidl's planning agent said the hoarding would secure the vacant site and help prevent unauthorised access, anti-social behaviour and fly-tipping.

It says the hoarding would either be removed after four years or further planning permission would be sought to keep it in place, according to the Local Democracy Reporting Service.

Lidl's planning agent says the hoarding would provide a "neat and well-maintained frontage".

But the council, which is run by a minority Conservative administration, said the supermarket and car park would be an inefficient use of the land, which could accommodate a more intensive mixed-use development, such as shops alongside homes.
