‘It was the whole team’: Cameron Bancroft blows lid off Australia’s sandpaper scandal

  • Test batter felt ‘betrayed and alone’ after Cummins’ comments

Test batter felt ‘betrayed and alone’ after Cummins’ comments

He says ball-tampering agent discussed by players and coaches in 2018

Former Test cricketer Cameron Bancroft has claimed the entire Australia team knew about the use of sandpaper during the 2018 South African tour, where he was caught on video with the item in his hand in Cape Town and was later one of three players banned.

Bancroft alleged bowlers provided feedback on his attempts to alter the ball and a coach instructed him to do more, in a column written for the West Australian.

The claims were published hours after the Cricket Australia chief executive, Todd Greenberg, and the interim chair, David Boon, announced players would be given an opportunity to discuss the “sandpapergate” incident, without fear of further sanctions.

“It was not just the three of us – Steve Smith, David Warner and I – who knew about me using sandpaper on the ball in South Africa. It was the whole team,” wrote Bancroft, who was banned alongside the Test captain Steve Smith and vice-captain David Warner.

In response, Cricket Australia issued a statement that read: “We respect that Cameron Bancroft has told his story, and we will take time to consider his comments.

“We expect that there will be many dimensions to the events of 2018, which is why we have established the forum for individuals to give their version of events,” the governing body added. “We are finalising the terms of reference and will commence the process as soon as possible.”

Pat Cummins’ management then issued its own statement, which read: “Whilst we feel for Cam and everything he’s been through there’s nothing in the statement that hasn’t been answered by Pat in his press conference.”

The scandal was reignited this month with the release of Usman Khawaja’s book, and his claims that knowledge of the cheating went beyond the three players who were punished for their part in the plot during the third Test at Newlands.

Bancroft claimed the practice began at the second Test at Port Elizabeth, now known as Gqeberha, when Warner raised the possibility of using sandpaper to roughen one side of the ball during a team discussion involving players and coaching staff. The 33-year-old says Australia bowling coach David Saker and then head coach Darren Lehmann were present, while Smith entered the conversation before making clear he wanted no part in the proposal.

Bancroft alleges he subsequently received regular feedback from Australia’s fast bowlers about whether his work on the ball was producing the desired reverse swing.

“I recall conversations with all the fast bowlers Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Mitchell Marsh about whether it was reverse swinging enough,” Bancroft wrote. “All the bowlers gave me feedback if my work on the ball was working. It was helpful.” The bowling quartet of Cummins, Starc, Hazlewood and spin bowler Nathan Lyon released a statement in 2021 denying knowledge of the plan to take sandpaper on the field in Cape Town in 2018.

Bancroft also claims the practice continued for the remainder of the second Test and into the third Test in Cape Town, where television cameras caught him attempting to conceal the sandpaper in his trousers. He says Saker approached him during the Test at Newlands and instructed him to do more with the sandpaper to make the ball swing. “That coach was David Saker and it is true,” he wrote.

Bancroft claims that after he was caught, Saker specifically warned against identifying sandpaper as the material used during the press conference after he was spotted on the field with the ball-tampering agent. “We acknowledged ball-tampering while giving an inaccurate account of the material involved,” Bancroft wrote. “It was another lie. One told while we were supposedly taking responsibility.

“My cricket career has been destroyed and I have never been able to play for my country since Justin Langer was coach in 2019. But I have reached a point where continuing to protect an incomplete account of events feels more damaging than the truth.”

Bancroft says he has remained silent for years to protect others, believing he had taken responsibility for the entire team. “When I was questioned during Cricket Australia’s investigation, I did not give a complete and truthful account,” Bancroft wrote. “I withheld important details, including when the use of sandpaper began, how long it went on for, and the involvement and awareness of others. I did this to protect Cricket Australia’s investigation and not implicate others around me.”

Bancroft said the recent publication of Khawaja’s autobiography Sabr and current Test captain Pat Cummins’ response during a marathon press conference had prompted him to tell his version. “For eight years, I shared the burden of this story for the whole team,” Bancroft wrote. “This was a burden which was heavier than my capacity and I took the bullet for everybody, and it was my choice.

“I have spent eight years speaking about accountability, honesty and the lessons I learned from one of Australian cricket’s darkest moments. However, in 2018 not the entire truth was told. I have never been completely honest about what happened.”

Bancroft said the “final straw” was Cummins’s press conference where the Test captain stood by his earlier denials of having knowledge of the cheating at the time. “When he says ‘we all knew’, if by that he means that we knew sandpaper was going out on the field he’s 100% wrong,” Cummins said on Friday. “I’ve said that consistently for the last eight years. I did not know that was happening. But, I think Usman actually says things that are actually far more serious than that.”
