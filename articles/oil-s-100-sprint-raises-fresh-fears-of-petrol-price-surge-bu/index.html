Oil’s $100 sprint raises fresh fears of petrol price surge - Businessday NG

TheTools · 08.09.2026 14:16 · source · permalink

Oladehinde Oladipo

September 8, 2026

Crude oil surged toward the important $100-a-barrel mark on Tuesday, reviving fears of another round of pump-price increases in Nigeria and raising the possibility that Dangote Refinery could soon revise its gantry price to reflect the higher cost of replacing petroleum products.

Brent crude traded at $98.05 a barrel at 10:15 a.m. West Africa Time, up $2.05, or 2.11 percent, on the day. West Texas Intermediate climbed even faster, adding $2.91, or 3.18 percent, to change hands at $94.39.

Both benchmarks have now gained roughly 4 percent over just two trading sessions, a pace that traders and refiners alike are struggling to keep up with.

“The benchmarks are rising, and they need to factor in replacement cost, so they might review their prices very soon,” an industry source said, speaking on condition of anonymity because the matter is commercially sensitive.

The source said the rally was becoming increasingly difficult for suppliers to absorb without passing costs downstream, but cautioned that any decision at the refinery level would not be automatic. Management, the person said, is also weighing how a fresh increase would land on already-stretched consumers.

“The refinery was also weighing the effect of higher fuel prices on consumers and their purchasing power before making any adjustment,” the source said.

That balancing act, between protecting margins on one side and protecting demand on the other, has become a familiar one for Nigeria’s dominant refiner since it began setting domestic gantry prices in competition with imported fuel. A crude rally of this speed narrows the room for that balancing act considerably.

Depots under pressure

Even before Tuesday’s crude spike fully works its way through the supply chain, depot prices across the country’s major trading hubs were already elevated.

In Lagos, premium motor spirit, or petrol, was quoted at N1,266 per litre at Pinnacle Oil and Gas, N1,267 at MRS Tincan, and N1,270 at both Ascon and Aiteo. Integrated and Sahara depots listed the product at N1,279, while NIPCO was the highest of the group at N1,280 as of September 7.

Diesel, or automotive gas oil, told a similarly firm story. Duport, Integrated, Ibeto, Ibachem, Gulf Treasure and Ascon initially posted AGO at N1,800 per litre before a round of adjustments pulled some depots down to N1,790. Bono and Sahara settled in the same range, near N1,790, while Chipet was slightly lower, between N1,782 and N1,785. Pinnacle stood out on the high side at N1,830, with Aiteo at N1,800.

Warri showed the widest spread of any hub tracked. A.Y.M. Shafa’s diesel topped the list at N1,935 per litre, well above Prudent’s N1,860 and the N1,850 posted by both Rainoil and Zamson. Petrol in the city ranged from N1,280 to N1,300 per litre — already above Lagos levels before Tuesday’s crude move is even factored in.

In Port Harcourt, petrol prices ranged from N1,280 to N1,295 per litre, while Bulk Strategic’s diesel came in at N1,845 — among the highest quoted anywhere in the survey.
