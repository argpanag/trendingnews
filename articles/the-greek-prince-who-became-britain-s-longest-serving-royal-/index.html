The Greek Prince Who Became Britain's Longest-Serving Royal Consort - GreekReporter.com

TheTools · 20.11.2026 08:08 · source · permalink

On November 20, 1947, Princess Elizabeth, the future Queen Elizabeth II, married Philip Mountbatten at Westminster Abbey in London, in a ceremony that offered a moment of celebration to a country still recovering from the devastation of World War II. The bride was 21 years old, while the groom was a 26-year-old former prince of Greece and Denmark whose childhood had been marked by exile, family separation and an uncertain future.

The wedding, which took place 79 years ago, would become the beginning of one of the longest marriages in British royal history. Elizabeth and Philip remained together for more than 73 years, until his death in April 2021.

Behind the royal ceremony was an extraordinary personal story. Born in Corfu in 1921, Philip had been forced to leave Greece as an infant following the political turmoil that accompanied the country’s military defeat in Asia Minor. He would later renounce his Greek and Danish royal titles, become a British citizen and serve in the Royal Navy before marrying the future British monarch.

Their union brought together two members of Europe’s interconnected royal families, but it also represented a new beginning for a young man whose early life had been shaped by the collapse of his family’s position in Greece.

The Greek prince who left Corfu in a wooden fruit box

Philip was born on June 10, 1921, at Mon Repos, a neoclassical villa on the Greek island of Corfu. He was the fifth child and only son of Prince Andrew of Greece and Denmark and Princess Alice of Battenberg.

His grandfather, King George I of Greece, had been born a Danish prince before ascending the Greek throne in 1863. Through his mother, Philip was also a descendant of Britain’s Queen Victoria.

Despite his aristocratic background, Philip’s early years were anything but secure.

In 1922, following Greece’s disastrous military campaign in Asia Minor, King Constantine I abdicated and the country’s political leadership underwent a dramatic upheaval. Prince Andrew was arrested and tried by a revolutionary military tribunal over his military conduct during the campaign.

Although he avoided execution, Andrew was sentenced to exile, forcing the family to abandon Greece.

In December 1922, a British Royal Navy vessel, HMS Calypso, evacuated the family from Corfu. Philip, then approximately 18 months old, was reportedly carried aboard in a makeshift cot fashioned from a wooden fruit box.  The family settled near Paris, relying on the assistance of relatives. Philip later attended schools in France, Germany and Britain.

His parents eventually separated, and family difficulties meant that much of his upbringing took place under the supervision of relatives and guardians.  He attended Gordonstoun School in Scotland, an institution known for its emphasis on discipline, physical endurance and personal responsibility.

Although born in Greece, Philip spent little of his childhood there and was not raised primarily in a Greek-speaking environment. His connections extended across several European royal families, while his education and adult life increasingly centered on Britain.  In 1939, he entered the Royal Naval College at Dartmouth, where he met Princess Elizabeth during a visit by King George VI and Queen Elizabeth.  The two began corresponding, and their relationship continued throughout World War II.

Philip served with distinction in the Royal Navy, including during the Battle of Cape Matapan in March 1941, when British naval forces defeated an Italian fleet in the Mediterranean. He later served in the Pacific and was present in Tokyo Bay during Japan’s surrender in September 1945. By the end of the war, Philip had established himself as an experienced naval officer. His relationship with Elizabeth, however, would eventually require major changes to his nationality and royal status.

In February 1947, Philip became a naturalized British subject, relinquished his Greek and Danish royal titles and adopted the surname Mountbatten, the Anglicized version of his mother’s family name, Battenberg.  His engagement to Princess Elizabeth was officially announced on July 9, 1947.

The 1947 royal wedding that began a 73-year marriage

The wedding took place on November 20, 1947, at Westminster Abbey, before approximately 2,000 invited guests. Millions more followed the ceremony through radio broadcasts around the world.

Britain was still experiencing postwar austerity, with food rationing and economic difficulties shaping everyday life. Nevertheless, the marriage of the heir to the throne became a major national celebration. Elizabeth’s wedding dress was designed by Norman Hartnell and made from ivory silk, decorated with thousands of pearls and crystals. The gown was inspired by Botticelli’s painting Primavera and incorporated floral motifs symbolizing renewal and hope.

Because rationing remained in effect, Elizabeth used clothing coupons to obtain the material for her dress, although she received an additional allocation for the royal wedding. Members of the public reportedly sent their own coupons to help, but these could not legally be accepted. On the eve of the ceremony, King George VI granted Philip the titles Duke of Edinburgh, Earl of Merioneth and Baron Greenwich. He was also accorded the style of Royal Highness.

Among those attending the wedding were members of European royal families, senior British officials and diplomatic representatives. Philip’s mother, Princess Alice, was present, but his three surviving sisters, who had married German aristocrats, were not invited in the aftermath of World War II.  The ceremony was conducted by the Archbishop of Canterbury, Geoffrey Fisher, and the Archbishop of York, Cyril Garbett.

One of the most memorable details occurred when Philip placed the wedding ring on Elizabeth’s finger. The ring was made from Welsh gold, continuing a tradition associated with the British royal family.  Following the ceremony, the newlyweds appeared on the balcony of Buckingham Palace, greeting the crowds gathered outside.

They spent part of their honeymoon at Broadlands, the Hampshire estate of Philip’s uncle, Lord Mountbatten, before traveling to Birkhall in Scotland.  For the first years of their marriage, Elizabeth and Philip experienced a relatively private family life. Their first child, the future King Charles III, was born in November 1948, followed by Princess Anne in August 1950.

Philip continued his naval career, and the couple spent time in Malta, where he was stationed with the Mediterranean Fleet.  Their circumstances changed dramatically on February 6, 1952, when King George VI died while Elizabeth and Philip were visiting Kenya. At just 25, Elizabeth became queen, and Philip’s active naval career came to an end.

For the following decades, he accompanied the queen on official visits, represented the monarchy at public engagements and developed initiatives associated with education, conservation, science and youth development.  The Duke of Edinburgh’s Award, founded in 1956, became one of his most enduring contributions, encouraging young people to participate in volunteering, physical activities and personal development.

Philip became Britain’s longest-serving royal consort, remaining alongside Elizabeth through major political and social transformations, from the decline of the British Empire to the modern Commonwealth.  The couple celebrated their platinum wedding anniversary in November 2017, marking 70 years of marriage.

Prince Philip died at Windsor Castle on April 9, 2021, aged 99. Queen Elizabeth died on September 8, 2022, aged 96.  Their marriage had lasted 73 years, four months and 20 days.

Mon Repos in Corfu, where he was born in 1921, survives as a reminder of the remarkable journey that took a Greek-born prince from exile to the heart of the British monarchy.
