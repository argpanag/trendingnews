A perfect rebound match: All the key stats ahead of Milan vs. Lecce

TheTools · 20.09.2026 11:30 · source · permalink

AC Milan are taking on Lecce in the fifth round of Serie A this evening, desperate to get back to winning ways ahead of the international break. 

Two draws against Juventus and Lazio, as well as a mid-week defeat to Benfica, have put Milan in a somewhat troubling position. The pressure is mounting on Ruben Amorim and since a long international break is coming up, a win is needed today to build some confidence.

Thankfully for the Rossoneri, they have a perfect opponent in Lecce, having won all of their last five games against the Via del Mare side. The overall history of the fixture also speaks in their favour, with 31 wins, 15 draws and only two defeats.

That said, the most recent encounter between the two teams (also at San Siro) was a tight one. Niclas Füllkrug came off the bench and scored the winner late on, though it must be said that Milan wasted some good chances before that.

AC Milan vs. Lecce: Key stats and facts

➤ Milan have won their last five Serie A matches against Lecce – by an aggregate score of 12-2, keeping four clean sheets – and could win six consecutive matches against the Apulian side for the first time in the competition.

➤ Among the teams in this championship, Lecce is the one against which Milan boasts the longest unbeaten streak in Serie A: 16 matches, including 11 wins and five draws; the last defeat dates back to 1 April 2006, at the Via del Mare, with a goal by Axel Konan (1-0).

➤ Since the early 2000s, Milan have never lost at home to Lecce in Serie A, thanks to 12 wins and two draws, with the Rossoneri scoring 30 more goals than the Apulian side (38-8); in particular, the Rossoneri are on a run of four consecutive home wins against the Salento side with an aggregate score of 9-0.

➤ Milan are coming off two consecutive draws (against Juventus and Lazio) and haven’t drawn three games in a row since September 2018, under Gennaro Gattuso; the Rossoneri won their last home game (against Venezia) and have only won two home games in a row in Serie A once this calendar year, last March, against Inter and Torino.

➤ Lecce have won four of their last six Serie A games (D2), scoring nine goals in total (an average of 1.5 per match), which is the same number of goals scored in double the number of previous league games (12).

➤ All of Milan’s goals (seven) this season have come in the final half hour of play, the period in which they have scored the most; by contrast, the Rossoneri have scored only one goal in the first half of their last nine Serie A matches.

➤ Lecce have conceded five of their seven goals in the first half hour of play this season, a league-high; overall, only Monza (seven) have conceded more goals than the Giallorossi in the first half of this Serie A season (five, the same as Genoa).

➤ In his last three appearances in Ligue 1 and Serie A, Diego Moreira has been involved in five goals, three goals and two assists; the 2004-born player, who before his brace as a substitute against Lazio had never scored from the bench in his previous 68 appearances in Europe’s Big 10 leagues, could score in two consecutive appearances in these tournaments for the first time in his career.

➤ Lecce are Christian Pulisic’s favourite target in Serie A, having scored five goals in five games (including a brace on March 8, 2025); the American – who hasn’t started since April 26 – could be involved in at least one goal in two consecutive Serie A appearances after his assist on the final matchday for the first time this calendar year (his last appearance was last December).

➤ Thanks to Lassana Coulibaly (2G+1A) and Ivan Ilic (2A), Lecce have two midfielders with more than one active participation in this championship, with only Inter having more (four); in particular, the Malian player (fresh from a brace on the final matchday) would equal his scoring record in a single season in the competition (three, in 2022/23 and 2025/26) with one goal.
