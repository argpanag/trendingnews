One-Owner 1969 Ford Mustang Mach 1 Resurfaces After 57 Years on Hagerty Marketplace

TheTools · 14.09.2026 20:51 · source · permalink

A 1969 Ford Mustang Mach 1 that has remained with the same owner for 57 years is now up for bid, offering a rare unbroken chain of ownership stretching from the new-car showroom to today's collector market. The SportsRoof fastback is currently listed on Hagerty Marketplace, where bidding sits at $16,000 after ten bids with the auction set to close September 15.

The car's story began in April 1969, when the original owner drove it home from Hubb Motor Company in Chicago, Illinois. Finished from the factory in moss green, the Mach 1 stayed with that same owner through decades of use, a 2014 restoration, and eventually more than 200,000 miles behind the wheel, a detail the seller discloses plainly since the odometer has cycled through its range multiple times and now shows only a fraction of the car's true mileage.

Ford introduced the Mach 1 in August 1968 as a new performance-focused trim that replaced the Mustang GT atop the lineup. Offered only in the SportsRoof fastback body style, the package paired aggressive styling cues with genuine performance hardware, including a matte-black hood, hood pins, bold stripe treatments, and a competition suspension. Buyers responded in a big way, and the Mach 1 quickly became one of the best-selling variants in Mustang history.

Under this particular car's hood is its original 351ci Windsor V8, a two-barrel, 250-horsepower engine coded H in the VIN, rebuilt and paired with a freshly rebuilt three-speed Cruise-O-Matic automatic. The chassis number, 9F02H186162, confirms the details: a 1969 model built at Ford's Dearborn, Michigan plant as a Mustang SportsRoof fastback fitted with the 351 V8.

During its 2014 restoration, the Mach 1 was refinished in dark red metallic over a black vinyl interior with new carpet and headliner. Additional touches include a blacked-out grille, a functional hood scoop reworked with a wire-mesh intake, American Racing Torq Thrust-style wheels, chrome exhaust tips, dual sport mirrors, high-back bucket seats, woodgrain-trimmed dash and console accents, plus an aftermarket three-spoke steering wheel and period-look radio. The car is offered with its original dealer invoice, owner's manual, a thick binder of receipts, and a clean California title, the kind of paper trail that rarely survives with a single-owner car this old. One disclosed flaw is a missing driver's door data plate, worth a close look for any serious bidder.

Where does a car like this sit in today's market? According to Hagerty Valuation Tools, a 1969 Mustang Mach 1 with the 351/250-hp two-barrel setup carries a #3 "Good" condition value of roughly $45,100 as of mid-2026, with a broader range between about $39,300 and $47,700 depending on options and condition. Those numbers have held essentially flat over the past year, though they're down close to 10 percent over three years, reflecting a broader cooling trend among mainstream muscle-era Mustangs. Recent comparable sales show how much condition and documentation matter within the model: a similarly optioned automatic Mach 1 with just 20,000 miles brought $84,000 this past July, while more driver-grade examples have traded in the $60,000s. Given this car's repaint, replaced interior, and true mileage well north of 200,000, bidders will likely value it toward the middle of that range, with its unbroken ownership history serving as the strongest card in its favor.

Bidding remains open on Hagerty Marketplace through September 15, giving buyers a short window to dig into the documentation before the gavel falls.
