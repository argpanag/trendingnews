Ashley Oosthuizen’s Bangkok move brings parole hopes closer

TheTools · 13.11.2026 05:53 · source · permalink

After more than six years in a Thai prison, Ashley Oosthuizen has moved to Bangkok, bringing her closer to her mother and renewing hopes of serving the remainder of her sentence under electronic monitoring.

Image: Facebook

A Bangkok prison transfer has raised hopes that George woman Ashley Oosthuizen could serve the remainder of her Thai sentence under electronic monitoring.

Oosthuizen, who has spent more than six years in prison, was moved on 4 November after a four-month wait. The transfer has also allowed her mother, Lynn Blignaut, who relocated to Bangkok, to visit her more regularly.

The family is now preparing an application for parole under electronic monitoring (EM), which could allow Oosthuizen to serve the remainder of her sentence outside prison under supervision.

Oosthuizen was arrested on October 8, 2020, after signing for a package addressed to her employer. According to Blignaut, police arrived about 10 minutes later and allegedly found MDMA inside.

Oosthuizen, who was 21 at the time, had moved to Thailand after matriculating in 2018 to take up a job as a pre-school teacher on the island of Koh Samui.

In 2021, she was sentenced to 100 years in prison on international drug-smuggling charges.

Following two appeals, a final ruling on March 27, 2025, changed her conviction to possession and reduced her sentence to 13 years and four months. Two subsequent royal pardons further reduced her sentence to nine years and 11 months.

Blignaut said the transfer had brought an improvement in Oosthuizen's living conditions.

"After years on a hard floor, she now has a thin mattress. She says the food is better and more plentiful. Another change she deeply appreciates is the calmer pace. She no longer feels constantly rushed to finish eating or get from one place to another," she said.

The family estimates that Oosthuizen has approximately three years and 10 months left to serve, although the final calculation remains subject to confirmation by prison authorities.

Blignaut said the family was preparing to apply for parole under electronic monitoring.

"Our understanding is that she can now apply, and we have been given an indication of a 50/50 chance of approval. Nothing is guaranteed, but it gives us hope. She will apply as soon as possible," she said.

One of the most significant changes, Blignaut said, was being able to visit her daughter more frequently.

"One of the greatest blessings of this move is that I can now visit Ashley every weekday. The family can also have 10-minute video calls with her."

Relocating to Bangkok, however, has brought additional financial pressure.

"The challenge for both of us is that life in Bangkok is considerably more expensive. Yet the Lord has provided for us up to this point, and we trust Him to continue providing for as long as it is needed," she said.

In a previous letter to friends and family, Oosthuizen reflected on the emotional toll of her imprisonment and the importance of holding on to hope.

She said she drew strength from knowing nothing was permanent.

"Perhaps for this very particular period of my life that causes me, at times, to only see the abyss and forget that this isn't the picture in its entirety.

"To prevent myself from falling into that abyss, I'd very much like to step back, step out and come and take a fresh look at it... And I draw my strength from three simple words; nothing is permanent."
