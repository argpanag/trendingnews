Patriots vs. Bears: Three must-know storylines for Thursday's Week 7 prime-time game

TheTools · 23.10.2026 09:18 · source · permalink
  • WHERE: Soldier Field (Chicago)
  • WHEN: Thursday, 8:15 p.m. ET
  • WAYS TO WATCH: Prime Video, NFL+

Stream live local and prime-time games on mobile with NFL+ .

With midseason in sight, the schedule-makers have gifted us what could be the crown-jewel matchup of Week 7 in prime time. The Patriots and Bears have weathered some early-season storms to arrive at Soldier Field for their final game of October with both teams at 4-2. Thursday’s contest features two of the most exciting quarterbacks in the league, 2024 NFL Draft classmates Drake Maye and Caleb Williams.

The Patriots are riding high, having won three in a row, a streak that started with an upset of the AFC East-leading Bills. Williams’ return from injury fueled Chicago’s road victory over the Falcons last week, and now the Bears have a chance to open a season at 5-2 for the first time since 2020.

Which playoff contender will keep the good times rolling into November? Let’s take a deeper look at some key subplots.

Three must-know storylines

1) The first and third overall picks from the 2024 draft renew acquaintances. Caleb Williams and Drake Maye -- who have known each other since their high school days -- have come a long way since they last met in 2024. That showdown produced a 19-3 Patriots win in a year where both of the quarterbacks' respective teams struggled to sub-.500 finishes, leading to coaching changes. Last season, Maye finished as the MVP runner-up and led New England to the Super Bowl, while Williams helped the Bears take their first NFC North title in seven years, emerging as one of the most thrilling playmakers in the game under Ben Johnson.

Maye has come roaring back from a turnover-plagued start to the current season. Over the past three weeks, he's thrown an NFL-best 10 touchdown passes, while also leading all qualified quarterbacks in passer rating (119.3) and EPA per dropback (0.38). He’s thrown only one interception in that span after tossing a league-high six in the first three weeks. His challenge is to keep the clean play going against the team that leads the NFL in takeaways since the start of last season (42).

Williams performed at near-maximum efficiency as a passer against the Falcons last week (305 yards, TD, 83.3% completion rate) in his return from a hamstring injury that sidelined him for three games. He now leads the NFL (min. 75 attempts) with a 72.5% completion rate in 2026, a vast improvement from his 2025 mark of 58.1%. The Patriots’ defense -- which ranks among the top six in points and yards allowed -- should present a stiffer challenge, though.

2) Will A.J. Brown bounce back from his rough return? Brown -- the Patriots’ marquee offseason addition -- played for the first time since Week 1 last Sunday, and the Patriots have to hope it wasn’t a sign of things to come. He made three catches for just 27 yards on a team-high 10 targets against the Jets, the fewest receptions in any of his 30 career games (including playoffs) with 10+ targets. Brown didn’t have a single grab on his three targets of 10 or more air yards. Drops were a big problem, something he acknowledged after the game.

Will a Bears defense that looked very vulnerable in the secondary a couple weeks ago in Green Bay be the antidote? Maye’s going to be looking his way and hoping for better results, especially with New England’s leading receiver, Romeo Doubs, out with a shoulder injury.

3) Is the Bears' defense for real? Heading into the season, it was popular to dismiss Chicago's defense as a unit that relied too much on takeaways in 2025 and didn’t do enough to improve in free agency/the draft. The chickens haven’t really come home to roost on that one, though. Aside from a horrible day against the Packers, Dennis Allen’s crew hasn’t held the team back. In fact, the Bears rank among the top seven in points and yards allowed, a dramatic improvement from their standing in both categories last year.

If there are still doubts on whether to trust those numbers to hold, I’d understand. After all, Chicago allows 6.0 yards per play, second-most in the league. And the defense has benefited mightily from a running game that keeps it off the field. The Bears are averaging 50.2 defensive snaps per game -- no team has finished a season with a figure that low since at least 2000, per NFL Research.

So, can Chicago's D do enough to bring the turnover-prone version of Drake Maye back out on Thursday night? The Bears will still be without their second-highest paid player -- DL Montez Sweat -- who is serving the second of a two-game suspension, but perhaps we’ll see more of nickelback Kyler Gordon after he recorded three sacks on 16 snaps last week in his season debut.
