Terrence Shannon Jr.'s growth makes the Timberwolves' offense borderline unfair

TheTools · 17.11.2026 20:00 · source · permalink

After adding LaMelo Ball and Jonathan Kuminga this offseason, it was clear that the Minnesota Timberwolves had a plethora of talented offensive players. While it was fair to wonder how everything would mesh, it was also clearly a strength. Amid a 10-4 start, these fit concerns have been irrelevant, and their all-around talent has been on full display.

During Monday night's game, Terrence Shannon Jr. was the story, as he notched 34 points. He carried the Wolves' offense as LaMelo Ball and Ayo Dosunmu both had off nights. In the end, Minnesota took down the Portland Trail Blazers 106-98. Having a gifted scorer off the bench is a massive luxury for the Timberwolves. And when you pair Shannon with the rest of the Wolves' offensive talent, this team feels borderline unfair.

Let's not forget that when the Wolves are at full strength, Shannon is the team's eighth man (give or take). He can scale up and erupt for big scoring nights, as he did on Monday night. Frankly, at times he looks like an offensive star. If having that guy off the bench isn't a luxury, I don't know what is.

Terrence Shannon Jr. has improved a ton this season

While Shannon's 34-point game was a career-high outing, it's also a continuation of his offensive growth. This season, he's averaging 13 points, a sizable jump from his 5.6 points last season.

Shannon has always had blazing speed and solid strength to get to the rim. Yet he has lacked control. Now, though, he can decelerate with ease and has a tighter handle. This makes him a genuinely fantastic downhill scorer. According to Databallr, Shannon ranks in the 92nd percentile for rim attempts per 75 possessions, while shooting 67.7 percent at the rim (a 9.4 percent increase from last season).

Given that LaMelo can be 3-point reliant, having a versatile off-guard/wing who can generate rim pressure at will is especially valuable. Plus, by attacking the rim so often, Shannon draws a ton of fouls. Notably, 15 of his 34 points came at the line on Monday.

His feel as a playmaker and spot-up shooting have also improved. The former allows him to thrive on the ball, while the latter allows him to thrive alongside the Wolves' best offensive players.

Shannon's growth makes the Timberwolves' offense beyond elite

I mentioned it earlier, but what this growth functionally means is that the Timberwolves' offense is borderline unfair to play against. Simply put, they have so many different ways to beat you. Being 7-3 without Anthony Edwards is evidence of this.

During this stretch without Edwards, the Wolves' all-around talent has been on full display. Notably, six players are averaging double-digit points. Add in a healthy Ant to this bunch, and one could argue it's the most talented offense in the league.

Whether it's Edwards' elite three-level scoring, LaMelo's and Bones Hyland's difficult shotmaking, Jaden McDaniels' and Kuminga's ability to punish mismatches, or the downhill scoring of Shannon and Ayo, the Wolves have a bunch of ways to beat you. Furthermore, at some point this season, the Wolves will add Donte DiVincenzo, who is one of the league's best shooters.

Frankly, if you take Shannon out of the equation, the Wolves still would have a ton of offensive threats. Now, with Shannon unlocking his upside as a scorer, Minnesota's offensive talent and scoring threats are simply off the charts. As the Timberwolves gear up for a championship run, this will be a massive advantage, especially paired with their superb defense.
