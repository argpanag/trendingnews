More than 800 students and teachers in Indonesia suffer food poisoning after eating government free meals

TheTools · 03.09.2026 05:54 · source · permalink

Hundreds of students fall ill in three seperate incidents in Java and Sumatra this week as criticism of president’s flagship program grows

More than 800 Indonesian students and teachers have fallen ill in separate incidents of food poisoning linked to President Prabowo Subianto’s free-meals program this week, with dozens hospitalised after they with struck with nausea, vomiting and stomach aches.

The president’s flagship free-meals program was launched last year to tackle malnutrition, with meals distributed to more than 62 million students and pregnant women across the country.

But the multi-billion policy has been plagued by controversy, including recurrent cases of food poisonings, alleged corruption, leadership changes, and complaints it is a major lag on the budget.

This week 566 students fell ill after eating the government’s free meals in Sidoarjo, East Java, with 88 of them hospitalised. In Nganjuk, also in East Java, 49 students fell ill on Wednesday after eating a free meal of fried rice, chicken and tofu.

Another 237 students and teachers in Agam, West Sumatra, also fell ill after their meals on Wednesday, according to local media reports. Eggs were suspected to be the cause.

The regional government in Agam has reportedly suspended distributing the meals, pending an evaluation. The kitchen linked to the Sidoarjo case was also suspended on Wednesday.

In the past year many Indonesians have complained about the program, writing on social media about maggots in tempeh, shards of glass in rice, and food trays washed in dirty water.

Overall, 37,600 people are believed to have suffered food poisoning linked to the free meals as of 8 Aug., according to Network ​for Education Watch, ​a non-governmental watchdog.

Charles Honoris, a lawmaker and member of the sole political party outside the governing coalition, described the situation as a “failure of the system”.

The National Nutrition Agency has blamed previous cases of food poisoning on improper food ​storage and late delivery of cooked meals.

Prabowo has also played down the scale of the problem, saying that of more than a billion meals served, poisoning cases represented just “0.0017% – quite a proud achievement”. He also said the policy had improved school attendance and achievement.

In ​July, a survey by pollster Saiful Mujani Research and Consulting showed only a third of people were satisfied with the scheme, with the reported cases of poisoning cited as a major reason.

Despite the mounting criticism, Prabowo told parliament last month the program would be continued, and promised that improvements would be made.

“We start with the most fundamental aspect: nutrition. Our children must not study while hungry. No child can learn effectively if they are hungry,” he said.

“We cannot accept a situation where one in four Indonesian children suffers from stunting,” he continued, “We cannot accept that.”
