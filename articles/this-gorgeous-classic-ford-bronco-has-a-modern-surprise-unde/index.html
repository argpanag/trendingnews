This Gorgeous Classic Ford Bronco Has a Modern Surprise Under the Hood

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:07 · source · permalink

Looks really can be deceiving, but that is not always necessarily a bad thing.

The black 1969 Ford Bronco currently up for auction on Bring a Trailer is proof. Sure, the SUV may look like any other first-gen example of the iconic 4×4, but crack open its hood and you'll find a modern-day powerplant that makes over 400 horses.

Just look at the early Bronco and it's easy to see why it would go on to become one of the most beloved nameplates in Ford history. The model debuted in 1966 as the Blue Oval's answer to the Jeep CJ-5 and Toyota Land Cruiser, and paired genuine off-road capability with a stylish body. Six decades and five vehicle generations later, this remains the case, though it's hard to view any Bronco but the first as the best-looking of the group.

The Coyote-powered 1969 Ford Bronco

The restomod up for sale shows exactly why this iteration of the Bronco is rated so highly. The SUV is finished in a rich coat of glossy Raven Black, broken up only by a white removable top, bumpers (the front of which includes a winch), and wheels, along with chrome badging. The aforementioned rims are, of course, wrapped in a set of all-terrain BF Goodrich tires. Inside, you'll find period-correct vinyl upholstery, though the cabin has been outfitted with some of the creature comforts today's drivers expect, including a Dakota digital gauge cluster, Kenwood touchscreen infotainment system, and a rearview camera.

The most significant upgrade can be found in the engine bay, though. The first-gen Bronco launched with an inline-six and was eventually available with a small-block V-8, but this example has been equipped with a 5.0-liter Coyote V-8 crate engine. The mill, which debuted in the 2011 Mustang GT, makes 420 hp. Just as exciting is that it comes mated to a Blumenthal five-speed manual transmission. The setup looks to be in good shape, too. The odometer shows just 384 miles since the restoration was complete (the listing says the true mileage is unknown).

The restomod's 5.0-liter Coyote V-8 crate engine

Bidding for the restomod currently sits at $54,000, but don't be surprised to see that number climb before the sale ends later this week.
