Mother, police officer remembered for extraordinary courage after breast cancer battle

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:19 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Tamsyn Hayes, a Dunedin police sergeant and former professional triathlete, died on November 22, aged 41.

A mother, police officer and accomplished triathlete who fought an extraordinary battle against an aggressive form of breast cancer has died.

Tamsyn Hayes died peacefully at Otago Community Hospice on November 22, surrounded by her family, according to a death notice published by Hope and Sons.

She was 41.

Her death has prompted tributes from the sporting and cancer support communities, with Challenge Wānaka describing her as an athlete of remarkable strength, determination and spirit.

Hayes was diagnosed with stage three triple-negative breast cancer in April 2023, aged 37.

The aggressive cancer required extensive treatment, but she eventually received encouraging results and was declared pathologically clear of the disease.

She subsequently returned to competitive triathlon, completing the Wānaka half Ironman in 2025.

However, the cancer later returned and spread to multiple parts of her body.

A Givealittle appeal, established by friend Mel Davidson, was launched in December 2025 to help fund treatment not publicly funded in New Zealand.

A November update said that Hayes had been promoted to sergeant, with the decision made by the Police Commissioner.

A ceremony was held at Dunedin Police Station, attended by colleagues and family, to recognise her service to New Zealand Police.

Davidson said Hayes had been making the most of her time at home, enjoying daily walks, spending time with friends and tending to her tunnel house.

Hayes’ sporting career was also recognised by Challenge Wānaka, where she competed professionally seven times, completing the full-distance event on six occasions and the half-distance race once.

Her highest professional placing at the event was fourth.

She also competed internationally, including at Challenge Roth in Germany, which the organisation said held a special place in her heart.

In a tribute posted following her death, Challenge Wānaka said her return to racing in 2025, after cancer treatment, had seen her finish third in her age group.

“Her courage, honesty and determination inspired so many,” the organisation said.

“She reminded us that strength comes in many forms, and that sometimes simply making it to the start line is an achievement in itself.”

Hayes was also the Cancer Society Otago Southland’s 2024 Dunedin Relay For Life ambassador.

In a tribute, the organisation said she had generously shared her cancer journey with the community and inspired many through her strength, kindness and determination.

Davidson announced Hayes’ death in an update to the Givealittle page on November 23.

She said Hayes had touched the lives of many and achieved more in her lifetime than most could dream of.

“The last few months especially were incredibly tough, but I know Tamsyn would have endured every bit of it all over again if it meant having more time with Anja,” Davidson said.

“Every treatment, every setback, every ounce of fight she had left was for her.”

Davidson thanked those who had donated to the appeal, supported fundraisers or sent messages of encouragement throughout Hayes’ illness.

She said the generosity had helped give Hayes and her family precious time together.

Hayes was the dearly loved wife of Glen, adored mother of Anja and the late baby Aroha, much-loved daughter of Lynley and Mike, and sister of Michaela.

Her family acknowledged the care provided by Mercy Cancer Care and Otago Community Hospice.

A celebration of Hayes’ life will be held at Hope and Sons Chapel at 2pm on Friday, followed by a private interment.
