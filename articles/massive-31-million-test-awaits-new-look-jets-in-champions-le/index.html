Massive $31 million test awaits new-look Jets in Champions League opener

TheTools · 15.09.2026 00:00 · source · permalink

Your digital subscription includes access to content from all our websites in your region. Access unlimited content and the digital versions of our print editions - Today's Paper.

Kashima Antlers striker Yuma Suzuki is worth more on the transfer market than the entire Newcastle Jets salary.

Antlers goalkeeper Tomoki Hayakawa was the J-League player of the year last season, went to the World Cup with Japan and is worth $3.2 million.

The 29-man Kashima squad is valued at $31.5 million on football website Transfermarkt.

The A-League salary cap was $2.55 million last year.

That is just the start of the challenge the Jets face in the Asian Champions League Elite tournament according to club great Jason Hoffman.

Hoffman has been involved in both the Jets' previous Asian campaigns.

He was a rookie in the 2009 side which progressed out of the group stage before going down to eventual winners Pohang Steelers 6-0 in the round of 16.

He was also in the 2019 squad but suffered an ankle injury before the play-off game for entry into the main draw, which the Jets lost 4-1 to Kashima.

They meet the Japanese giants again in the opening group game on Tuesday.

"It is a big challenge on a number of fronts," Hoffman said.

"Japanese football is highly technical. The speed of the ball movement is very different to the way that the game is played here.

"We have a very direct and physical approach in the A-league.

"Japanese teams like to retain the ball and are extremely patient in the build-up of play.

"Our strength is our physical presence and ability from set pieces and transition football.

"Japanese clubs are advanced. They have decades behind them in the playing style and the way they embed it into the youth."

As well as Kashima, the Jets will play Shanghai Port, Johor Darul Ta'zim, Kashiwa Reysol, Gamba Osaka, Vissel Kobe, Beijing Guoan and Kyoto Sanga in the group stage.

Western Sydney Wanderers are the only Australian team to win the Asian Champions League in 2014.

Saudi club Al-Ahli are the defending champions.

The Jets are a different-looking team to the one which won the Australia Cup and Premiers' Plate last season.

Eli Adams (Gamba Osaka), Clayton Taylor (Barnsley), Lachie Rose (Dundee United), Lachie Bayliss (Auckland FC) and Kota Mizunuma (Urawa) head the list of departures.

In their place have come Joe Lolley, Nikos Vergos, Alex Grant and Pat Wood.

Vergos, Grant and Wood featured in the Jets' 3-2 first-round exit to Preston in the Australia Cup in July. The Jets led 2-0 early and were overrun.

"It will be great to see how the team shapes up," Hoffman said. "I thought the Australian Cup game against Preston, considering the squad only had a couple of intense training sessions, the opening 45 minutes was extremely positive.

"I think they will still play a really good brand of football under Mark Milligan.

"It will be really exciting to watch the likes of Joe Lolley, Nikos Vergos and Will Dobson. Hopefully Dobbo can go to another level.

Zach Clough has another preseason under his belt.

Looking at the leadership now that's been placed on Max Burgess and Joe Shaughnessy; I think the foundation of the squad is really strong.

"They have made some really smart signings and hopefully that will put them in good stead."

The Jets have had a low key build up to the $17 million Asian tournament.

They played Melbourne City and Auckland behind closed doors.

There was no fanfare or hype when they released a new playing kit and naming rights sponsor for the tournament.

There has been one media opportunity to announce Max Burgess and Joe Shaughnessy as the new captains.

Otherwise it has seemingly been a case of head down, backside up.

The club did not make public the squad for the trip to Japan.

However, they did post on social media on Tuesday the signing of former Melbourne City teenager Julian Recchia to a scholarship deal.

The squad left on Friday for Japan.

Kashima are in eighth place on the J-League table with four wins and three losses.

The stadium holds 40,000 spectators, which is massive considering the population of the port city is 67,000.

Jets (likely): James Delianov; Daniel Willmering, Will Kenendy, Joe Shaughnessy; Max Burgess, Zach Clough, Will Dobson; Xavier Bertoncello, Nikos Vergos, Joe Lolley.
