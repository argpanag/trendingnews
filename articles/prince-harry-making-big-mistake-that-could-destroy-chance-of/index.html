Prince Harry making 'big mistake' that could destroy chance of royal reconciliation: expert

TheTools · 28.10.2026 18:10 · source · permalink

Commentators say the GQ interview proves Prince William's fear that private matters will end up in print

Several royal experts believe Prince Harry is making a "big mistake" that could ruin his chances of reconciling with his family.

In his first major interview since returning to the U.K. with his family, the Duke of Sussex told GQ he wants to make peace with his father and brother. However, royal commentator Richard Fitzwilliams told Fox News Digital that Harry's willingness to publicly discuss his strained family relationships only makes the royals more reluctant to extend an olive branch.

"The peace offering is already undermined as it is public," said Fitzwilliams. "That continues to be his big mistake. He cannot be trusted. [The Sussexes] had already launched brutal attacks on the royal family [with their tell-alls]. Family disputes are bitter. But numerous interventions from 'friends' and interviews like Harry's in GQ make this highly unlikely."

PRINCE HARRY SAYS ‘MOST IMPORTANT THING IS TO RECONCILE’ WITH ROYAL FAMILY

Prince Harry sat down with GQ for an interview recorded in New York in late September 2026 and published Oct. 20. The Duke of Sussex spoke about grief, the death of his mother, Princess Diana, fatherhood, his family's return to the U.K. and his desire to reconcile with his father, King Charles III, and brother, Prince William. Speaking about the family rift, Harry told GQ: "Some people don't want it to work out. I want it to work out. With everything else that's going on in the world, the most important thing is to reconcile — that's the key point."

"Scars stick around forever," Harry continued. "But what your perception is, and what your mindset is to those things that have happened, letting go of the past. That’s something that I would encourage everybody to do: If there’s certain things from the past that are still affecting you every day, that’s harder to let go of. But, for me, there’s enough drama in the world and enough drama in people’s lives to continue with issues with family."

Harry also told GQ that he has been "really criticized" for sharing his side of the story.

"I find it fascinating because everybody else is allowed to tell their version of my story, but God forbid I should be able to tell the other side of it."

Royal broadcaster Helena Chard told Fox News Digital that although Harry may yearn for reconciliation after years of the Sussexes publicly airing their grievances, "the palace doesn't trust Harry and Meghan." Chard said Harry's decision to speak publicly "proves Prince William's fear — anything said in private will end up in print."

"Vindication on Megxit and absolution without apology do not equate to reconciliation," Chard added.

Fox News Digital reached out to Buckingham Palace and Kensington Palace for comment.

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex stepped back from their roles as senior royals in 2020, citing intense media scrutiny and a lack of support from the palace. After moving to California that year, the couple discussed their struggles in interviews and documentaries. Harry's 2023 memoir, "Spare," further strained his relationship with the royals. Several experts told Fox News Digital that Harry and William are still not on speaking terms.

On Oct. 15, the Sussexes attended a series of charity events in Nottingham, marking their first major joint outing since their reported return to the U.K. The appearances drew attention from royal experts, many of whom compared them to official royal engagements.

In January 2020, Queen Elizabeth II made clear following the Sandringham Summit that there would be no "half in, half out" role for the Sussexes. They couldn't remain working members of the royal family while pursuing independent, income-generating ventures.

"Following conversations with the duke, the queen has written confirming that in stepping away from the work of the royal family it is not possible to continue with the responsibilities and duties that come with a life of public service," Buckingham Palace said in a statement on Feb. 19, 2021.

Prince Harry and Meghan Markle returned to the U.K. six years after leaving for California. (Getty Images)

In response, the couple issued a statement of their own, saying: "We can all live a life of service. Service is universal."

Shortly after the couple returned to the U.K., Buckingham Palace released a letter from the Lord Chamberlain, written on behalf of King Charles III, stressing that there was "no change" to their status. The letter stated that Harry and Meghan are "no longer working members of the royal family" and that their charitable work "is a personal matter for them both and undertaken in their private capacity."

"In short, their position is akin to private citizens with commercial and charitable interests," the letter added.

"'Working members of the royal family' is a really lazy phrase," the friend told The Times. "He hates it, she hates it. They are not 'working' for the institution, but they are members of the royal family, and they work."

It's misleading to call them 'former royals.' It has an impact on how they're perceived and the way people talk about them."
