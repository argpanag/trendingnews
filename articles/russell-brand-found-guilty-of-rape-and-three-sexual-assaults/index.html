Russell Brand found guilty of rape and three sexual assaults

TheTools · 05.11.2026 21:33 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Warning: This page contains distressing details

  • Russell Brand has been found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault - read a summary of his offences here

  • The judge tells him "obviously a custodial sentence of some length will result"

  • The comedian and actor, 51, has been remanded in custody and will be sentenced on Monday

  • Brand faced seven charges: three of rape, three of sexual assault and one of attempted indecent assault. He was found not guilty on three counts

Warning: This page contains distressing details

Russell Brand has been found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault - read a summary of his offences here

The judge tells him "obviously a custodial sentence of some length will result"

The comedian and actor, 51, has been remanded in custody and will be sentenced on Monday

Brand faced seven charges: three of rape, three of sexual assault and one of attempted indecent assault. He was found not guilty on three counts

The seven charges dated from 1999 to 2009 and concerned six women. They are all entitled to lifelong anonymity

It can now be reported that Brand is being investigated over further rape allegations made by two women

  1. On Monday, Brand will be sentenced. For now, there's some closure for victims after verdictspublished at 18:45 GMT
    18:45 GMT

    Helena Wilkinson
    Reporting from Southwark Crown Court

    Today’s verdicts will no doubt bring some closure to the four women who’ve lived with what Brand did to them for years. Some have been left “haunted”.

    They came to court during the nearly four-week trial. They gave their evidence. They relived sometimes harrowing and deeply personal details about what happened. Listening to them, it was clear just how difficult that was.

    The trial also saw Brand - once one of the most in demand presenters - step into the witness box and answer questions about his sexual past. Jurors were taken back to the height of his fame.

    In court, he was a far cry from the extravagant and charismatic entertainer he’s known for being.

    Over weeks the courtroom also heard from different witnesses. Brand’s former friend gave evidence for the prosecution. Laura, Brand’s wife, appeared in his defence.

    The trial attracted huge media interest. Each day journalists - not just the UK, but from around the world - packed into Court 3 for the high-profile case.

    Tonight Brand is heading to prison. On Monday, he’ll be back at Southwark Crown Court where he’ll be sentenced for his crimes.

    We're ending our live coverage here - you can read more in our news story.

  2. Now, questions will be asked over whether a blind eye was turned towards Brand's behaviourpublished at 18:35 GMT
    18:35 GMT

    Sima Kotecha
    Reporting from Southwark Crown Court

    This trial was supposed to last up to 12 weeks - at least that’s what the police and the media initially expected. But it took just a month for Brand to be convicted of four out of seven charges, including rape.

    The victims who gave evidence did so from behind a screen - their voices often trembling as they attempted to remember the horror of what happened to them all those years ago.

    Some of them have described Brand’s conduct as an open secret - he took advantage of these women and used his fame and power to seduce them - sometimes in public settings.

    Questions will now be asked whether his previous employers or colleagues turned a blind eye towards his bad behaviour. Complaints have been made in the past by those who worked with him - were they not taken seriously because of his status?

    Tonight - Brand is behind bars - likely in a box-room sized cell with a sink and toilet. His flamboyant clothing will be replaced by prison wear, perhaps a grey tracksuit.

    He’ll be left alone for hours on end to consider what happened and why, and whether there is any way back from criminal behaviour when he is finally released - probably in many years time.

    Russell Brand has been remanded in custody after a jury found him guilty of rape and sexual assault offences following a weeks-long trial at Southwark Crown Court. Here's what's unfolded:

  6. BBC says 'there can be no place for the abuse of power' as it reiterates apologypublished at 17:41 GMT
    17:41 GMT

    The BBC says the thoughts of everyone at the corporation "are with the brave women who came forward and told their stories which led to a conviction in this case".

    In a statement, a spokesperson says there "can be no place for the abuse of power", adding the industry must work together "to ensure that anyone who suffers at someone else’s hand feels able to report it, that their concerns are taken seriously and that they are listened to".

    It references a "detailed report" published by the BBC in January 2025 into how complaints about Brand's conduct were handled, looking at eight allegations of misconduct while he worked at the BBC between 2006 and 2008.

    "It was of great concern to us that some people felt unable to raise concerns about Brand’s behaviour. We apologised to those impacted, for both the culture and failings at the time, and we reiterate that apology again today," the spokesperson adds.

  8. Channel 4 news director praises women who 'had courage to speak out'published at 17:11 GMT
    17:11 GMT

    Louisa Compton, director of News, Digital and Audience Trust at Channel 4, has released a statement following the outcome of Brand's case.

  9. The four guilty verdicts, and three not guilty verdictspublished at 17:05 GMT
    17:05 GMT

    Warning: This post contains distressing details

    Russell Brand has been found guilty of four offences, and not guilty of three offences. As he makes his way to prison, before sentencing on Monday, here's a recap of the charges:

    Guilty

    • Brand was found guilty of sexually assaulting a woman whose breasts he grabbed at a bar in London in 2004
    • He was also guilty of sexually assaulting a woman at the Choice FM studios in London in either 2004 or 2005, when he grabbed her face and kissed her before touching her breasts and bottom
    • Brand was guilty of anally raping a woman at his then-home in London in 2009 after meeting her at a comedy show in Hammersmith
    • He was also guilty of a sexual assault charge while working on a Channel 4 project in 2009, when he laid his head into the groin area of a woman working with him without her consent

    Not guilty:

    • He was cleared of an allegation that he raped a woman at a hotel in Bournemouth in 1999, during the Labour Party conference
    • He was found not guilty of orally raping a woman in a toilet at a bar in London - the same woman he was found guilty of sexually assaulting when he grabbed her breasts
    • He was also found not guilty of attempted indecent assault - he was accused of grabbing a woman’s arm and pulling her towards a toilet in 2001

  18. 'At the centre of every assault was an abuse of power' - prosecutorpublished at 15:13 GMT
    15:13 GMT

    We can now bring you a statement from the Crown Prosecution Service.

    "Russell Brand was predatory and used his celebrity status to commit sexual crimes," says David Roberts, senior prosecutor in the London rape and serious sexual assault unit.

    "He was a manipulator with an insatiable desire to get what he wanted regardless of whether consent was given or not.

    "As his career took off, he seized opportunities to assault the women around him.

    "Although his tactics varied, at the centre of every assault was an abuse of power and sexual greed, with a complete disregard for the impact of his actions.

    "I would like to thank all the women in this case for their courage in coming forward and seeing this sexual abuser brought to justice."

  19. Brand's attitude to women 'absolutely deplorable', says former colleaguepublished at 15:07 GMT
    15:07 GMTBreaking

    Sima Kotecha

    A comedian and writer who worked with Russell Brand has described him as being "like a wolf" where women were concerned.

    Cole Parker shared an agent with Brand in the 1990s, and the two men often performed on the same comedy circuit. He says they were in contact until 2013.

    "He looked like a wolf. He looked like he was about to jump on them."
