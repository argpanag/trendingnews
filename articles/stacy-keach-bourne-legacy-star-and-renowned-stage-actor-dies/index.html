Stacy Keach, ‘Bourne Legacy’ Star and Renowned Stage Actor, Dies at 85

TheTools · 11.11.2026 02:37 · source · permalink

The actor died after “a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure,” his wife, Malgosia Keach, said

CBS via Getty

NEED TO KNOW

  • Stacy Keach died on Tuesday, Nov. 10, at 85
  • The Tony-nominated actor died following a long illness involving a serious heart condition, his wife, Malgosia Keach, said
  • Malgosia said the renowned actor died at his home in Magdalenka, Poland

Stacy Keach, best known for his work as a character actor on the stage and screen, has died. He was 85.

The actor died on Tuesday, Nov. 10, in his family home in Magdalenka, Poland, his wife, Malgosia Keach, said in a statement obtained by PEOPLE. His death followed “a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure,” Malgosia said.

“Stacy peacefully departed this world,” she wrote. “He fought for so long, with extraordinary courage, determination and strength. He was a true warrior, facing his illness with the same remarkable spirit and resilience that defined his life.”

Małgosia’s statement continued, “Stacy was not only a legendary actor, admired and loved by audiences the world over, but also my beloved husband, my companion and the heart of our family. His passing leaves an immeasurable emptiness in our lives. But his extraordinary spirit, his unforgettable voice and the love he gave so generously will remain with us forever.”

The actor is best known for costarring with Jeff Bridges in John Huston’s Fat City, as well as his work in the action film, The Bourne Legacy. Throughout his career Stacy would garner over 200 credits across film and TV. He earned a Golden Globe nomination for his work as private detective Mike Hammer on the CBS sitcom, Mickey Spillane’s Mike Hammer.

His other credits include Nebraska, If I Stay, Sin City: A Dame to Kill For, The Ninth Configuration, The Heart is a Lonely Hunter, Doc, Up in Smoke, American History and the classic western The Long Riders, in which he starred alongside his brother James Keach. The two, who starred as outlaw brothers Frank and Jesse James, also co-wrote and co-produced the Western.

Stacy was celebrated for his stage work, and regarded as one of the best American Shakespearean actors of his era. New York Times critic Clive Barnes once declared him “one of the great Hamlets of our time.” His stage work earned him a Tony nomination for Best Actor in a Play for his performance in Arthur Kopit‘s 1969 production of Indians.

Stacy is survived by his wife, their children, Karolina and Shannon Keach, and their grandchildren.
