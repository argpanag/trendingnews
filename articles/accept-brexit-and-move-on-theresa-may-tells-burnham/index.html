Accept Brexit and move on, Theresa May tells Burnham

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:57 · source · permalink

Reopening EU negotiations will drain Cabinet’s time and distract Britain’s leaders, warns former PM

Andy Burnham must “accept Brexit and move on”, Baroness May has said.

The former prime minister said she was “aghast” that Mr Burnham was considering rejoining the EU in the wake of his reset summit.

Lady May, who was forced to resign as prime minister in 2019 over her failure to execute a Brexit deal, is the latest of former leaders to warn Mr Burnham to stay out of the bloc.

Speaking to the BBC, Lady May urged Mr Burnham to “just get on with” today’s challenges, rather than reopening conversations around Brexit.

“Brexit is done. Just accept Brexit and move on,” she said.

On Friday, Mr Burnham told the EU he was ready to discuss different options for closer ties between Britain and the bloc over the next year.

Speaking after striking a “reset deal” at a summit in Brussels, the Prime Minister invited EU officials to join him in a “brainstorming process” to decide how Britain could forge closer ties.

At the summit, the UK agreed a deal granting 100,000 Europeans aged 18 to 31 the right to live, work and study in Britain for up to three years, and vice versa.

UK and EU negotiators also agreed that Britain would align with the bloc’s food and drink safety rules and link emissions trading standards.

On Sunday, Sir Tony Blair, another former prime minister, also argued that Britain should only rejoin the EU if it reforms and allows more technological innovation.

“I do think Brexit was a real mistake,” he told The Times, but added that Europe was moving in the wrong direction with technology, and claimed that the debate had changed since 2016.

Lady May presided over three years of Brexit negotiations in No 10 before MPs rejected her deal, which involved a vote on a temporary customs union and the prospect of a second referendum vote.

She was forced to resign over the setback and was replaced by Boris Johnson.

She told the BBC: “What I saw was that the extent of those negotiations, the hours that have to be put in – it’s across the whole of government.

“It’s not just prime ministerial time, cabinet ministers’ time, officials’ time, to-ing and fro-ing on those negotiations. Because you have to negotiate hard in order to make sure that you’re getting out what you want.

“And that’s one of the reasons why I look slightly aghast at what Andy Burnham is talking about further negotiations, of going into the EU. And also we, as a country, we voted for Brexit.”

She added: “When I was prime minister I was very clear, we should deliver on that vote. So I’m afraid my message to him really is, you know, people voted for Brexit. Brexit is done. Just accept Brexit and move on.”

Warning against another vote to rejoin the EU, Lady May said the Prime Minister was “patting” the British public on the head.

“He wants to pat them on their head and say, ‘You know what, remember that vote? Well, you got it wrong, didn’t you? So have another go and come out with the right answer.’

“Or what he thinks is the right answer. But as I say, let’s just get on with making sure the UK is as strong economically, in security terms, and in terms of opportunities for people as it can be.”

Earlier this month, Lord Cameron urged the Prime Minister not to trigger another “decade-long debate about whether we should be in or out”.

In an interview with Bloomberg, Lord Cameron said it would not only spark a “very long and painful” argument, but it would also be anti-democratic as the public voted to leave just a decade ago.

Mr Johnson also warned Mr Burnham against “wasting his premiership” on a futile push to rejoin the bloc.

The former prime minister, who fronted the Vote Leave campaign and took the UK out of the EU in 2020, said Britain would back Brexit again if Mr Burnham forced another vote on the issue.
