As it happened: Election campaign 4 October

TheTools · 04.10.2026 23:01 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Politics / Election 2026

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._Q.VKhexyL5oal5uCV3AUQmI1EOdryHi3hh73RdibV20lY/politics_election-2026/1710400%2Fas-it-happened-election-campaign-4-october

Election 2026 heated up on Sunday, when Labour released its long-awaited fiscal plan and National immediately claimed it fell billions of dollars short, which would be made up in more borrowing and taxes.

Among the promises from Labour leader Chris Hipkins was a pay rise for 65,000 care and support workers, costing $2.5b over four years, while also re-implementing pay-equity legislation.

National finance spokesperson Nicola Willis accused Labour of lying to the country, claiming an $8.5b hole in its policy.

Meanwhile, National leader Christopher Luxon visited the Takanini Gurdwara Sri Kalgidhar Sahib Sikh Temple, which has hosted several previous prime ministers.

National also launched its blue campaign bus and pledged $1.75b to build South Auckland's Mill Road, which NZ First leader Winston Peters claimed his party first announced in July.

National's Mt Roskill MP Carlos Cheung also had billboards burnt down overnight.

ACT leader David Seymour insisted government policies had attacked wealth creation for decades and other parties would "destroy wealth" with their solutions to the current cost-of-living crisis.

Instead, he promised to remove tax on KiwiSaver earnings.

The Green Party pledged to make public transport free for eligible groups, like children, students apprentices, community services cardholders and total mobility users, and cap bus and train fares at $2 for everyone else.

Te Pāti Māori announced its one-person party list, with general secretary Lance Norman the only extra candidate.
