Queen’s death mistakenly announced days before sudden health scare

TheTools · 17.11.2026 23:47 · source · permalink
  • Lifestyle
  • Royals
  • European royals

November 17, 2026 - 11.47pm

Queen Margrethe II has pulled out of a planned royal engagement en route to the event when she suddenly fell ill.

The 86-year-old, who abdicated the throne for her son the now-King Frederik X, was in the car on the way to the Royal Opera House in Copenhagen when she ordered it be turned around.

Denmark’s Queen Margrethe II, seen at Fredensborg Palace on November 3, was forced to cancel an engagement during her journey there after suddenly falling ill. Martin Sylvest Andersen/Getty Images

She had only just left Fredensborg Palace, north of the capital, when she became ill on Tuesday.

Guests waiting to see the queen were waiting inside the venue, thinking Margrethe II was simply late.

But they were soon told the former monarch was sick and would not be making an appearance there after all.

The Royal House of Denmark later said Margrethe II had contracted a serious throat infection.

“Her Majesty Queen Margrethe unfortunately had to cancel today’s presence at the Opera at short notice due to a nasopharyngeal abscess,” the palace said in a statement to the media.

“Her Majesty is now at Fredensborg Palace and is doing well under the circumstances.”

A nasopharyngeal abscess is described as a serious bacterial infection in the back of the throat.

Later that day, Queen Mary was asked about her mother-in-law’s condition when she arrived for an engagement at Copenhagen’s Town Hall.

Queen Mary was asked about her mother-in-law’s condition when she attended an engagement at Copenhagen’s City Hall on Tuesday. The Royal House of Denmark

“I haven’t spoken with her yet, but she is resting at home and is in good hands,” Queen Mary told BILLED BLADET.

“It came very suddenly and unfortunately [her attendance at] the event had to be cancelled.”

Margrethe’s last royal engagement was on November 3 at Fredensborg when she presented the Ebbe Munck Prize. She arrived using a wheeled walking frame but otherwise appeared in good form.

Queen’s death accidentally announced

Last week, a Danish regional television news site was forced to apologise after incorrectly announcing the death of Queen Margrethe II.

Margrethe was last seen on November 3 when she used a walking aide at Fredensborg Palace. Martin Sylvest Andersen/Getty Images

On Friday, a piece was published on the website of TV2 Fyn, which covers the island of Funen.

The obituary, in part, read: “Denmark has lost a queen. A person who, throughout her life, united regions and generations, and played a special role for the people of Funen”.

The story was not removed until Monday.

The site’s editor-in-chief, Thomas Funding, called it an “unfortunate mistake” and said an investigation was underway into how the story was accidentally published. However, just 37 people saw the piece as it had not gone up on the website’s homepage, he said.

The former monarch, pictured on her birthday on April 16, has been living at Fredensborg Palace full-time since her abdication in early 2024. Getty

Recent health setbacks

Queen Margrethe, who turned 86 in April, has suffered a number of health issues recently leading to the cancellation of several engagements.

In September, she was admitted to hospital with a blood clot and dehydration and was forced to miss the funeral of her cousin, Norway’s King Harald V.

Margrethe has been in and out of hospital multiple times this year.

Queen Margrethe II announced her abdication during a televised speech from Christian IX’s Palace, at Amalienborg, on January 31 2023. The Royal House of Denmark

On May 14, Margrethe had a heart attack and underwent a balloon angioplasty to the coronary artery days later.

She was discharged but admitted to hospital again, two weeks later, for a blood clot. A CT scan later revealed a large clot in the hip region, following a bad fall in September last year.

Since her abdication two years ago, Margrethe has continued to carry out official duties but on a much smaller scale to when she was on the throne.

Her decision to step down after a 52-year-reign came after a number of health issues, including major back surgery, in early 2023.

In her New Year's Eve speech announcing plans to step down, in December 2023, Margrethe said the surgery led to "thoughts about the future" and when to pass on the responsibilities of the crown to her son.

One of her most recent high-profile appearances was in Stockholm on April 30 for King Carl XVI Gustaf’s 80th birthday. WireImage

"I have decided that now is the right time," she said in her speech.

"The time takes its toll, and the number of 'ailments' increases... One cannot undertake as much as one managed in the past," she added.
