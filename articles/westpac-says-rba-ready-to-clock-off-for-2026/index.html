Westpac says RBA ready to clock off for 2026

TheTools · 16.11.2026 15:40 · source · permalink

By Kellie Ell

Westpac thinks the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) is ready to call it a year, tipping the central bank will remain on hold for the rest of 2026.

In a recent note, the major bank’s Chief Economist Luci Ellis said the RBA may have reached its peak, despite the central bank itself remaining tight-lipped on what comes next.

"We already believed the RBA cash rate was likely at a peak, even if RBA officials were less convinced," Ellis wrote. "A deeper housing downturn and early signs of cracks in the consumer sector point to an even softer 2027. Fading inflation risks from oil also boost our conviction on rates being at a peak.

"Underlying inflation has been revised down slightly in the second half of 2027, bringing the year-ended figure for calendar 2027 more decisively inside the RBA’s 2% to 3% target range than our previous forecast round," said the economist. "The unemployment rate forecast has been revised up to peak above 5%. Based on this revised forecast profile, an increase in the cash rate from here would be unlikely and — we would argue — undesirable.

"A rate hike in February 2027 cannot be ruled out. But it is looking even less likely given signs in the data and surveys," Ellis added. "The [Monetary Policy Board] will at least discuss a hike in February. The staff and senior officials remain sensitive to inflation risks and have a downbeat view of supply capacity growth. Given what we are seeing in the economy now, though, a rate hike in 2027 would require either a material new inflation shock that has not yet occurred."

Westpac's forecast comes after the RBA delivered five interest rate hikes in 2026, bringing the official cash rate (OCR) to 4.85%. 

During the November press conference, RBA Governor Michele Bullock offered little clarity on the RBA's next move, instead saying the board would wait to see incoming economic data and the impact of previous rate hikes before making any further decisions.

"I don't know what the next move will be. I don't give forward guidance," Bullock said. "There's now five interest rate rises in the system. The last three haven't even started to flow through yet. It is probably a good point to take stock for a minute and see what comes in, in terms of the data, see if we're traveling more or less in line with the forecast. What that means for interest rates in the future, I don't know the answer to that. But all I can say is that the board will be watching very closely. Because there is still a bit of a skew on the upside to the inflation risk. And we need to be alert to those materialising."

A rate hold would be welcome news for mortgage holders and investors nationwide, many of whom are already feeling the pressure of rising living costs, persistent inflation, tighter cash flow and reduced borrowing power. This comes alongside changes to tax incentives under the current federal budget and ongoing global uncertainty.

The RBA's next meeting on monetary policy is scheduled for the 7 and 8 of December.  

With the next decision approaching, Australian Broker spoke to brokers on the ground to get their predictions and find out whether the impact of the previous five rate hikes are already being felt across the economy.

Managing director of Melbourne-based brokerage Loan Studio

"I think the RBA is done for the rest of the year. I don't think it's discounted going into next year. I think, as I always say, we have to wait for the data. But at this stage, I'd be surprised if we saw another rate rise this calendar year. I think the market's felt enough and I think it kind of really just depends how the summer period goes for the economy.

There's a massive flow-on already from the five rate rises this year. I think the numbers speak for themselves with the market sentiment dropping, with the numbers in home lending, new lending applications dropping. There's no doubt that the market is continuing to feel it, because usually spring is arguably the best time of year for real estate and transactions happening. So we're definitely seeing a slowdown.

We're fortunate [in our business] that we've got a really strong client base and referral partners, so we may not be feeling it as hard as other places. But the market is definitely weaker and softening. And I think that there's probably another three to four weeks where there'll be some strength, and then we'll start to see the slowdown come during the Christmas period. And then hopefully sentiment picks up, momentum comes back, people start seeing value in places."

Founder and managing director of Sydney-based brokerage Blue Crane Capital

"I think the RBA will hold rates in December. Despite the RBA acting as completely independent from the government, there is no doubt political pressure. And they don't want bad news pre Christmas for the voters."

Director and mortgage broker at Queensland-based brokerage Green Finance Group

"It's all about inflation. We've got to get inflation under control. Judging by our own business, we're still busy, but the phone calls have certainly slowed down. Whether that's a sign of where the economy is heading or where it's sitting at the moment, I don't know. But my thoughts are, I don't see any further increases this year. I just think, potentially, rates will stay the same for a while until they get inflation back under control. It's that consumer spending.

I do a lot in that pub space. I noticed that in the pub industry, some of the numbers are down. People aren't necessarily going out and going out for meals and spending money on food and drinks. So I think we've probably reached the bottom of the rate increases. If anything, now I think we'll see the rates sit for a while and then, potentially, a rate cut late next calendar year.

The previous rate rises have definitely had an effect [on the market]. In development finance, there are a few larger groups going broke at the moment. So that side of things, there's a lot of projects on hold at the moment. And the investor side and the first-time homeowner [market] has slowed. But also, to be honest, our residential lending hasn't slowed at all. People are upgrading their homes; people are looking for a principal place of residence. So people buying owner-occupied properties, people upgrading, people migrating to Queensland, interstate migration. We're seeing some good numbers through the group even in the last couple of months."
