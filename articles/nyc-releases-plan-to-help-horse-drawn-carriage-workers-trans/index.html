NYC releases plan to help horse-drawn carriage workers transition as industry phases out

TheTools · 17.11.2026 10:24 · source · permalink

NEW YORK (WABC) -- New York City has released a framework to support horse-drawn carriage drivers, stable hands, and medallion owners as the city moves toward ending the horse-drawn carriage industry.

Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani said the plan includes financial support for medallion owners and career training, education, and job placement services for workers affected by the transition. A full transition plan is expected by April 2027.

"As New York City moves to bring the horse-drawn carriage industry to a close, we have a responsibility to the workers who have built careers in this industry. This administration will not leave them behind," Mamdani said. "Our transition plan will provide financial support and pathways to new careers through training, career guidance and education. We will support workers every step of the way."

City officials said they hope to facilitate medallion buybacks and will work with horse carriage workers and the Transport Workers Union as the plan takes shape.

The city also plans to launch a Horse Carriage Worker Industry Program to help about 200 workers move into new careers. Operated by LaGuardia Community College and supported by the Department of Small Business Services, the program will offer career counseling, job training, tuition assistance, and connections to employers.

"LaGuardia will work with each carriage driver and stable hand to ensure they get the workforce training they need to transition to new careers with family sustaining wages," said Kenneth Adams, president of LaGuardia Community College.

Mamdani also directed the administration to study whether electric carriages and electric pedal-assist bicycles could serve as alternatives in and around Central Park.

The mayor still must sign legislation that would ban horse-drawn carriage rides in New York City within two years.

The measure, known as Romanch's Law, passed the City Council last month by a 40-7 vote and now heads to Mamdani's desk.

Named for a teenager from India who was killed after a horse became startled and threw him from a carriage in Central Park, the bill has the mayor's support in principle.

The TWU on Tuesday criticized the legislation, saying in a statement that "all of these planned initiatives could quickly be abandoned in a fiscal crisis as the city scrambles to fund all of its other responsibilities and priorities." The union added that such a scenario would leave "hundreds of horse-carriage workers, owners, and family members on the curb with nothing at all."

The union said it plans to file a federal lawsuit challenging the legality of the measure once Mayor Zohran Mamdani signs it.

The mayor has said he supports the spirit of the legislation but wants protections in place for affected workers and operators.
