Reece Walsh reveals Kiwis act which left him emotional at Rugby League World Cup

TheTools · 17.10.2026 03:41 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Kangaroos fullback Reece Walsh was visibly emotional before Australia’s opening Rugby League World Cup match against the Kiwis because of the haka.

The 24-year-old had tears in his eyes during the national anthem, but later admitted to Australia’s Channel 7 witnessing New Zealand perform the haka made him think of his family.

“It was a special one. Both teams really passionate,” the former Warriors fullback said.

“Sit there face the haka, it was my first time. I was pretty emotional thinking of my family, all my friends who helped me get here.

“To stand out there with such an awesome bunch of boys, it was a really special one.

“Awesome way to start off the 2026 World Cup, was an awesome game to be a part of.”

Walsh has Māori heritage through his birth mother Kahuti Campbell from Hastings, but grew up with his father Rodney and stepmother Jodie in Australia.

Despite struggling this season for the Broncos in the NRL, Walsh started for the Kangaroos and repaid coach Kevin Walters’ faith in him.

An impressive try-saving tackle against Kiwis fullback Kalyn Ponga saw Walsh force his opposition out of bounds and keep his side up by two points with five minutes to go in the first half.

A quick one-two pass with Jack Bostock saw Walsh explode through the Kiwis defensive line from deep inside his own half and sidestep Ponga to bring Australia within touching distance of New Zealand’s score late in the second half.

Photographs captured him vomiting as he prepared to take a kick, with Walsh saying he struggled to run after his calves flared up.

“He’s cramping and vomiting and all sorts of things,” Walters said.

“He didn’t have the energy to kick the goal. He’s a chucker [he throws up]. People get nicknames and his nickname is ‘Chucker’.”

A third try from Bostock with less than five minutes to play sealed a 24-22 win for Australia in Sydney.
