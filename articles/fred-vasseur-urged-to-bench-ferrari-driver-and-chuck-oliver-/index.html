Fred Vasseur urged to ‘bench’ Ferrari driver and ‘chuck Oliver Bearman in’ for Qatar Grand Prix

TheTools · 22.11.2026 14:00 · source · permalink

Sean Fisher

Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur is being told to take drastic action after Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton clashed at the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Charles Leclerc received a 10-second penalty for colliding with Ferrari teammate Lewis Hamilton at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, losing the team valuable points in their quest to secure P2 in the Constructors’ standings.

It is not the first time the Scuderia duo have come to blows on track. Just a few weeks ago, Hamilton was infuriated by Leclerc for costing him a race victory at Mexico City. It falls to Fred Vasseur to draw the line and manage his drivers, and advice is pouring in from all angles.

What was your take on the collision between Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc in Las Vegas? 👀

Fred Vasseur told to bench Charles Leclerc or Lewis Hamilton

Vasseur immediately summoned Leclerc to talk to him after his P10 finish in Las Vegas, and the Monegasque driver accepted blame for the incident. However, Leclerc has since been defended by Anthony Davidson, who analysed the crash in more detail.

Previously, Vasseur has refused to draw any rules of engagement within Ferrari. Suggestions of any ‘scarlet rules’ have been thrown to the side. But now, he admits that action is needed.

According to James Hinchcliffe, speaking on F1 TV, Vasseur should go as far as benching one of the two drivers for the upcoming Qatar Grand Prix.

“Where do you go from here? I mean, you can’t bench one of them? But that’s what you’d want to do,” Hinchcliffe said.

“I mean, I’d chuck Bearman in for a race and sit one of them out and teach you guys a lesson. And let them know there are serious consequences here.

“But I don’t know what else Fred can do, so I feel really bad for him. Here’s the other thing that just makes it so unnecessary: 352 passes. This isn’t Monaco, that wasn’t your one shot to get by, so don’t take an unnecessary risk.”

Should Ferrari drop Charles Leclerc for Ollie Bearman?

In the last two incidents between Ferrari drivers, it is Leclerc who has been blamed. In Mexico City, he aggressively attacked Hamilton and cost the team a victory, in Vegas he caused contact with his teammate.

While Hinchcliffe didn’t specifically name which driver should be dropped, even Vasseur admitted that Leclerc was at fault in Vegas.

Meanwhile, Bearman is locked into contract negotiations with Haas because he wants a guarantee of getting a Ferrari seat in the near future. The biggest message Vasseur could send is to promote Oliver Bearman to the senior seat to remind Hamilton and Leclerc that they are not invincible.

It would be a hugely controversial move, but Ferrari need a shift in team culture and they especially need a line drawn in the sand to stop their intra-team driver battle. Perhaps Hinchcliffe’s idea is something Vasseur should follow.
