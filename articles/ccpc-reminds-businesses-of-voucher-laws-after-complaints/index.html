CCPC reminds businesses of voucher laws after complaints

TheTools · 20.11.2026 00:00 · source · permalink

Getty

The Competition and Consumer Protection Commission has written to businesses across the country to remind them of the laws around gift vouchers.

That follows complaints made to its helpline, as a result of which it identified a number of traders whose gift vouchers may not comply with legislation introduced in 2019.

The consumer's watchdog said traders who sold vouchers valid for less than five years should engage with it and propose remedies for affected consumers.

The letter highlights the consumer law around gift vouchers, including that vouchers must be valid for at least five years, and any expiry date should be given in writing; traders must not limit the number of vouchers a consumer can use per transaction; consumers do not have to spend the full voucher in one go; and traders must refund the remaining value in cash, by card, or as another voucher.

The letter has been sent to the businesses such as hotels, restaurants, takeaways, dentists, sports retailers, clothes shops, salons, and spas.

In July, the CCPC secured refunds of almost €250,000 from JD Sports for consumers after the retailer had sold gift vouchers valid for only one year, instead of at least five years.

CCPC Director of Communications and Consumer Information Aoibhín de Búrca said the organisation is "still hearing from consumers who are experiencing issues with gift vouchers.

"As we approach one of the busiest times of year for the purchase and use of gift vouchers, we are reminding traders of their consumer protection obligations.

"Irish gift voucher legislation is clear, and traders selling vouchers must follow the law. The legislation is there for consumers and we want consumers to know what their rights are, and to get the full protection and value of the legislation."
