U.S. Open quarterfinals — Latest

Ben Shelton (8) is taking on Carlos Alcaraz (2) in the 2026 U.S. Open men’s singles quarterfinals at Flushing Meadows.

Follow along for all our coverage from Arthur Ashe Stadium.

  • Streaming (U.S.): Fubo (Stream Free Now)
  • Watch or listen to The Tennis Podcast

Shelton breaks back! Tiebreak!

Shelton 6-6 (*0-0) Alcaraz

Now then. Errors from Alcaraz and it’s 0-30 on his own serve for the first time in this match.

A crisp forehand makes ground but the next one is wide, and now Shelton has his first two break points of the set at 15-40.

Shelton looks to the sky, twizzles his racket, and sees Alcaraz unleash a wild forehand again.

We’ve got a first set tiebreak!

Bang! Alcaraz breaks

Shelton 5-6* Alcaraz

Business time. Ben Shelton gets loose on his service rallies despite getting his first serves in and Alcaraz senses the opportunity.

Shelton gets a net cord on his forehand and the ball loops up and out for the first break points of the match for anyone, at 0-40.

A short rally, Shelton’s forehand drifts wide, and that is that. The Spaniard breaks to love.

Ridiculously efficient from Alcaraz, who breaks and now gets to serve out the opening set.

Shelton *5-5 Alcaraz

Shake of the head from Ben Shelton as his forehand return goes wide and Carlos Alcaraz is at 40-0 in no time.

A stunning drop shot ends the game and Alcaraz wears an almost comedic smile at his winner, as he walks back ahead of his return game.

You’ve got to love him.

Shelton still hugely involved

After consecutive unforced errors where Carlos Alcaraz dumps a forehand then a backhand in the net, he looks over to his box with frustration. Those are shots that he should make but he missed.

Ben Shelton is hanging around in this set. It has been a strong start for the American.

Shelton 5-4* Alcaraz

Alcaraz gets a little trapped by Shelton’s serve-volley to get out of 15-30, knowing that probably shouldn’t have surprised him as it did.

Alcaraz then nets as Shelton seeks to avoid any issues on his serve at this point in the set.

This is a good test of how sharp Alcaraz is really feeling after such a long time sidelined.

And while it feels like Shelton is hanging on at times, he is being given room by an opponent who isn’t quite at his best — as would be entirely understandable.

Shelton holds. Still no sight of a break point for either player in this opening set.

Consider that bookmarked for later

Carlos Alcaraz was visibly frustrated in Shelton’s last service game, then made a few uncharacteristic misses on his own serve there.

Those are things we don’t often see from Alcaraz — and yet he still finishes out a game with a couple of absurd shots. That’s classic Alcaraz.

And yet, I’ll be keeping those few moments in mind if Alcaraz ends up giving Shelton a chance here.

Remember I mentioned Carlos Alcaraz’s forehand? Well at deuce there, he said, “Watch this!” and crushed one down the line.

It’s fun listening to the Ashe crowd after big shots. That was certainly one the fans who have stuck around for this anticipated encounter enjoyed.

Alcaraz survives service wobble

Shelton *4-4 Alcaraz

Carlos Alcaraz’s drop shot draws in Ben Shelton and although he gets there, Alcaraz’s backhand put-away volley is dismissive enough to hurt his feelings.

Still, there is a little bit of pace off the Alcaraz serve since his wrist injury and this is a slightly wobbly game too. A pair of double faults takes us from 40-15 to deuce.

From there, Alcaraz steps on the gas and pumps his fist at a powerful forehand drive down the line that Shelton can’t get back.

A successful survival mission there from Alcaraz.

Time to target the backhand?

Carlos Alcaraz feeling it with his forehand. In that last service game, he was free wheeling that shot to Ben Shelton’s forehand side and unleashing a forehand winner down the line.

Two of Alcaraz’s four winners thus far have come from the forehand side. It will be interesting to see if Shelton hits towards Alcaraz’s backhand as this set progresses.

Catching a Shelton belter

One of the early tidbits of this match: The crowd is gasping each time Ben Shelton uncorks a particularly powerful, speedy serve.

He hit 138 mph on the radar in that game, and a 145 mph ace in the first game.

The strength means the ball is coming off both their rackets in weird ways. Just now, a ball ricocheted off Shelton’s racket and went high into the air, where it was caught with some juggling by a security guard.

He got some rightful cheers and a replay on the big screens.

Shelton 4-3* Alcaraz

Minor mild peril for Shelton as Alcaraz forces the backhand error to get a look at 15-30 — but Shelton’s serve hasn’t been decoded quite yet by the defending champion, who stands miles back and then goes long off the return as Shelton holds to 30.

Forget about hearing a pin drop

The crowd on Ashe is dialed in tonight, which means it’s quiet enough during points to hear the crack of the ball on Ben Shelton’s serve when he really thumps one, like he did that last point.

One of the best parts of live tennis, in my opinion.

Shelton *3-3 Alcaraz

The serves are starting to come out on top now, as Alcaraz holds to 15. The pace has been pretty ferocious too — and not just from Shelton.

Not much in this already

Shelton 3-2* Alcaraz

Shelton busy acing the serves that aren’t even coming off the middle of the racket.

Still, that is the first hold to love and Shelton has it.

The margins are so fine in this opening set so far, I’m not even sure I can make them out.

Shelton *2-2 Alcaraz

A few gasps as Shelton misses a backhand that could’ve brought him 0-30.

We’ve already had a couple of games reach that point but we’re yet to see a break point.

And this one is the same, once again reaching 40-30 and being served out from there for the hold. This time, for Alcaraz.

Some of the early energy and fun vibes has now been replaced by a dollop of jeopardy and tension — and that exchange in dynamic is likely to continue trending in that direction from here.

Already on for a record?

Tonight’s main event started at 11:05 p.m. ET.

If this match goes the distance (five sets) as the previous three matches have on Arthur Ashe Stadium today, we’re looking at a very late finish into the early morning hours.

The latest finish at a U.S. Open was 2:50 a.m. ET, when Carlos Alcaraz outlasted Jannik Sinner in a classic four years ago.

Should tonight’s match go four hours, we’ve achieved a new record in the late finish category.

Buckle up!

Shelton 2-1* Alcaraz

Shelton did take the opportunity after his first service game to ask the chair umpire to make sure the balls were not too soft already.

Obviously it’s to his advantage that they’re not, but the serve looks OK so far.

Away from the serve, Alcaraz is forcing the issue though. He looks so sharp, Alcaraz. Shelton drifts a backhand wide for 30-30 but a little serve-volley (and another volley) action tees up the hold — which is delivered by a super forehand pass.

Three games in and this is already so much fun!

Alcaraz follows suit

Shelton *1-1 Alcaraz

Carlos Alcaraz with a tweener lob? Yeah, this guys is LOOSE.

Ben Shelton enjoying himself too though, and an outrageous drop shot is too good for the Spaniard as we get another look at 40-30.

But we’re already seeing the potential in this match. Boy, it’s high!

Play! Shelton holds

Shelton 1-0* Alcaraz
