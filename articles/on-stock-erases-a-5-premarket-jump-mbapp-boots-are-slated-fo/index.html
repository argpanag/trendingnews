On Stock Erases a 5% Premarket Jump; Mbappé Boots Are Slated for 2027

TheTools · 18.09.2026 14:08 · source · permalink

On Holding shares erased a premarket gain of about 5% on Friday after the Swiss sportswear company made Kylian Mbappé the centerpiece of its first move into football. ONON traded at $27.13 at 10:04 a.m. ET, down $0.20, or 0.73%, from Thursday’s $27.33 close.

The reversal is a useful distinction for investors: Mbappé gives On an immediate audience in the world’s largest sport, but the first football boots are not expected until 2027 and neither the company nor the player disclosed the cost of the partnership. About 5.31 million shares had changed hands by 10:04 a.m., already 70% of ONON’s roughly 7.54 million-share average over the prior 20 sessions, according to intraday market data.

What On actually announced

On said Mbappé will be a global ambassador and work directly with its product teams on future football footwear and apparel. The company announcement also named Thierry Henry as director of football and expanded FC Barcelona midfielder Sydney Schertenleib’s existing relationship with the brand into the new category.

The product plan starts with LightSpray, a process that creates an upper from a continuous sprayed filament. On introduced the manufacturing technology in 2024 and now intends to adapt it to football boots. The release did not give a price, production target, retail date or expected revenue for the range.

Mbappé’s move ends a Nike relationship dating to 2006, and Nike confirmed the split in a statement reported by Reuters. Reuters said On plans to launch its first football boots in 2027. That timetable leaves the endorsement as a product-development and brand-building expense before it can become a retail sales line.

The revenue hurdle behind the celebrity win

On is entering football from a position of strong growth, but with a business still concentrated in shoes and in the Americas. In the first half of 2026, footwear generated CHF 1.55 billion of the company’s CHF 1.68 billion in net sales, or 91.9%. The Americas supplied CHF 902.3 million, or 53.6% of total sales. Those calculations use figures from On’s August 11 results.

Football therefore fits the existing footwear engine and could broaden the brand outside North America. It also asks On to compete in a category where product credibility, athlete contracts, club relationships and retail distribution have been built over decades. Signing the French captain solves the awareness problem faster than it solves manufacturing scale or consumer adoption.

The best evidence for the bullish case is On’s recent execution in adjacent categories. Second-quarter apparel sales rose 47.7% year over year, while direct-to-consumer revenue increased 26% and reached 45.7% of quarterly sales. A football range sold through On’s own stores and website could preserve more gross profit than a wholesale-heavy rollout. The company’s second-quarter gross margin was 65.4%, up 3.9 percentage points.

The counterargument is in the missing numbers. On has not said how much cash, stock or other consideration Mbappé will receive, nor how quickly football can contribute meaningful sales. A prominent athlete deal can lift awareness while still lowering earnings if the contract and launch costs run ahead of demand. On’s full-year outlook already assumes low-20% constant-currency sales growth and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 19.5% to 20.0%, so investors have a current operating benchmark against which to measure that spending.

The next useful evidence will be more prosaic than Friday’s announcement: a firm 2027 launch date, retail pricing, distribution and any disclosure of partnership expense or equity compensation. Until then, the intraday reversal says the market is treating Mbappé as a potentially valuable entry ticket, not booked revenue.
