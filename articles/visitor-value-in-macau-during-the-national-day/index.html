Visitor value in Macau during the National Day

TheTools · 02.10.2026 05:11 · source · permalink

The Macau Government Tourism Office forecasts more than 150,000 visitors per day during Golden Week, up from around 143,000 last year. Meanwhile, the Macao Travel Agency Association is pointing to hotel occupancy rates of more than 90%.

For McCartney, the central issue is not only visitor volume. “A key issue for the tourism and hospitality sectors will be in terms of the potential revenues generated, so in that aspect, less on the visitation volume but on the spending across sectors, be it on accommodation, restaurants, retail, souvenir shops, bars.” The professor identifies a trend toward visitors who are “value-driven and cost-conscious,” which he says “is not a recent shift but has been occurring for a while.”

The presence of same-day visitors is expected to become “more apparent” during Golden Week. In 2025, 59% of visitors were same-day travelers. “I don’t know if it would mask limited economic contribution from visitors not staying overnight,” McCartney says. “The economic outcomes can be compared statistically to visitation levels,” but there is “certainly the perspective of the value-driven visitor, and how spending will impact across the various hospitality sectors.”

“Gen Z will be part of the visitation,” the academic told PLATFORM, highlighting “the issue of digital use, and how this influences how they navigate around Macao, Taipa and Coloane, visiting community events, watching the fireworks, and influencing non-gaming products choices,” as well as “the content (images/message) being shared.”

Macao’s tourism model “has been shifting for some time.” One example is the possibility for day-trippers to choose accommodation in Hengqin or Zhuhai, while “infrastructure such as immigration and lower cost transportation links increase connectivity and access.”

To respond to this shift, Macau needs to “understand the Chinese (day-tripper) visitor journey more” even before they leave their home, including “social media influences” and choices regarding “accommodation, events and entertainment, places they will visit, products to purchase, transportation, timings” in Macau, Hengqin and Zhuhai.

“Through understanding their expectations and experiences, then it would help in looking at how to adapt” the offer, including ways in which “experiences can be elevated.” McCartney also points to “strategic partnerships through hospitality packaging,” given that “some of the visitor time will be spent in Macao, and some in Hengqin and Zhuhai.”

Beyond arrivals and hotel occupancy, community events should be part of tourism evaluation. “There are community events in Macao planned during the Golden Week and this is great to see. So this should certainly be strengthened,” he says.

McCartney advocates gathering feedback from the community and visitors through what is known as “quality of exchange” (QOE). “Through understanding the resident perspective (including those with less involvement in community events) as well as the visitors,” he says, “then can use this to enlarge and enhance the design, operation and programme of future community events.”

There is a “win-win,” he concludes: revenues generated for small community businesses and, through co-creation, “further visitor spending, longer stays, and positive social media content sharing.”
