'Ted Lasso's Missing Villain Replacement Confirms Its Biggest Season 4 Problem

TheTools · 09.10.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

Ted Lasso Season 4 has been great fun, but now that it's over, the Apple TV show's biggest problem is clearer than ever. Ted Lasso's Season 4 finale, "Being Alive," brilliantly wraps up the fourth installment of the hilarious sports comedy, reuniting us with several familiar faces we didn't think we'd see and keeping us enthralled with some interesting twists.

One of the biggest moments, though, is the revelation that Hannah Waddingham's Rebecca Welton had been aware of the behind-the-scenes chaos of Tracey Ullman's Zelda Southport for some time. It's been heavily implied that Zelda is Rupert's replacement as Ted Lasso's villain for a while. Ted Lasso's Season 4 finale suggests this may still be the case, but it's also clear the show isn't quite sure what to do with the character, and it has some work to do if it wants Season 5 to have a proper antagonist.

Zelda's Fate in 'Ted Lasso's Season 4 Finale Doesn't Quite Make Sense

Zelda's Ted Lasso villain arc has been entertaining; there's no doubt about that, but the anticlimactic note it ends on in the Season 4 finale is definitely strange. Ted Lasso Season 4 built up plenty of hints that Rupert was involved in some form or another, only for the finale to reveal he wasn't behind the plot to buy up Richmond's shares after all.

It's worth noting that Brendan Hunt, who plays Coach Beard, recently spoke about the late Anthony Head and how the production team hoped to involve him in Ted Lasso's Season 4 finale during an interview with Deadline​​​​​:

"We had an outside hope of bringing him back in the last episode. We were told that that was not going to be possible. So yeah, we had an idea for a little thing but it was not going to happen."

"We had an outside hope of bringing him back in the last episode. We were told that that was not going to be possible. So yeah, we had an idea for a little thing but it was not going to happen."

Of course, this didn't end up being the case, sadly, but it makes all the mentions of Rupert throughout the season feel a little out of place. Zelda's motivation for her villainy is to get the men who run the football industry to listen to her, a woman. But rather than attempt to take over another team, Zelda instead infiltrates the one club that is already owned by a woman.

Really, if she wanted to achieve her goal, she should've teamed up with Rebecca, which would have strengthened both of their positions in a sea of men. This motivation doesn't feel like enough by itself; there should be more to it, and her teased connection to Ted Lasso​​​​​​'s original villain, Rupert, would've been the perfect additional layer.

Rebecca's oddly ambiguous "forgiveness" doesn't make a lot of sense, either. There's nothing wrong with being the bigger person or forgiving and forgetting, but I can't help but feel that something was missing from this scene. I believe that Rebecca's decision to see Zelda as a scorpion, essentially taking the high road after all of it, is a callback to Season 4, Episode 1.

In a weird way, I think Rebecca's decision to "forgive" Zelda, in her mind, is her sticking to her feminist morals. Rebecca accepts that Zelda will be a major owner (but not the majority owner) of Richmond going forward, but as women, they ultimately need to be on the same team. Deep down, Rebecca knows how Zelda feels; it's what she had to deal with in every season of Ted Lasso.

While Zelda's story has been a little nonsensical at times, she's definitely been a fun addition to the cast, and I know I'm not the only one who is intrigued to see where Tracey Ullman takes the character next in Ted Lasso Season 5 (presuming the show is renewed and Ullman agrees to reprise her role). However, it could go in either direction: Zelda could return to pull the rug from under Rebecca, with some sort of plan to acquire the rest of Richmond's shares, or she could come back realizing Rebecca isn't as stupid as she once thought and utilize her for a much bigger ploy.
