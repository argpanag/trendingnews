New Zealand reach 119-2 to trail by 233 after India's record 720-9

TheTools · 23.11.2026 00:09 · source · permalink

New Zealand were 119-2 at stumps on day four of the first Test on Sunday, still needing 233 to make India bat again after the visitors' record first innings total.

Devon Conway was unbeaten on 59 and Rachina Ravindra was 27 at the close of play at Wellington's Basin Reserve.

India declared on 720-9 midway through the fourth afternoon, the highest innings total in a Test match in New Zealand.

It surpassed New Zealand's 715-6 declared against Bangladesh in Hamilton in 2019.

It was also India's record score in an overseas Test and the 18th highest innings in Test history.

Rishabh Pant, who resumed on 55, made 128 before falling to the last ball before lunch as India reached the break on 629-6.

The diminutive wicketkeeper brought up his hundred by lofting Glenn Phillips over long-off for six, before kissing the badge of his helmet and pointing to the sky.

It was his ninth hundred in Test cricket and included 10 fours and six sixes.

Dhruv Jurel was the last man out, stumped from the bowling of Phillips for a brisk 78 off 79 balls.

"He's a phenomenal player," Jurel said of Pant.

"I'm always curious about his mindset. Whenever I speak to him I ask him his thoughts of batting.

- Out of the ground -

"You go in, we are thinking that he'll play normally, and suddenly he steps out and hits the ball over covers or whatever.

"He's some kind of player."

Anshul Kamboj took on the role of aggressor at the start of play with a flurry of lofted drives and outright slogs.

He fell for a swift 38 from 42 balls, hitting six fours and a six.

Pant then opened up and whacked Phillips out of the ground, the ball seen running down neighbouring Cambridge Terrace alongside the Sunday morning traffic.

In reply, New Zealand lost Tom Latham second ball without scoring to Jasprit Bumrah, a dreaded "pair" for the captain after he was dismissed by the same bowler in the first innings.

Henry Nicholls, who made 175 not out in the first innings, fell for 31 after an edge onto his pads ballooned to a diving Jurel who took a superb catch at short leg.

Conway and Ravindra then combined for an unbroken 59-run partnership to guide the hosts to stumps.

"We're pretty behind in the game," said Ravindra of New Zealand's position entering day five.

"For us it's about batting as long as we can really. We know it's been a relatively good wicket so hopefully we can apply ourselves for a long period of time."

India set creative fields in an attempt to pry the partnership apart, using a variety of close catchers and leg side lines, while also throwing the ball to part-time bowler Devdutt Padikkal in an attempt to surprise the batsmen.

New Zealand's Ravindra took 2-44 with the ball from 10 overs during India's innings, while Glenn Phillips had 3-154.

Seamer Jacob Duffy conceded the second most runs in an innings by a bowler in New Zealand Test history, returning 1-201 from 33 overs.

Each of India's top five batsmen had scores of 80 or higher, the first time that has happened in a Test.

KL Rahul made 136 opening the batting alongside Yashasvi Jaiswal, who made 85, Padikkal scored 80 and captain Shubman Gill 98 before Pant's century.
