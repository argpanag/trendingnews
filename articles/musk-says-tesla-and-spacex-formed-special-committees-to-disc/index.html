Musk says Tesla and SpaceX formed special committees to discuss merger: Live updates

TheTools · 20.10.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

This is CNBC's live coverage of SpaceX's 2026 third-quarter earnings.

SpaceX shares climbed about 8% in extended trading on Tuesday after the company reported 161% revenue growth for the third quarter, beating Wall Street estimates, with its artificial intelligence business growing ninefold.

On the earnings call, CEO Elon Musk said a merger with Tesla, his automaker, would be a "good idea," adding that the boards of both companies have formed special committees to discuss a tie-up. Tesla reports third-quarter results after the bell on Wednesday.

Here's how SpaceX did compared to LSEG consensus estimates:

SpaceX said in a release that it recorded operating income of $2.8 billion and net income of $4.4 billion. In the same period a year earlier, SpaceX reported a net loss of $1.05 billion.

CFO Bret Johnsen said on the call that revenue in the first quarter of 2027 will be $33 billion to $37 billion, trouncing the average $22.2 billion average analyst estimate, according to LSEG. Johnsen attributed the forecast to "increased visibility into our compute contract pipeline over the next six months."

While SpaceX is best known for its reusable rockets, most of its revenue and profit in recent quarters came from the company's connectivity segment, which consists of its Starlink satellite internet service.

Starlink's subscribers base grew to 13.6 million in the quarter, up from 12 million in the prior period.

Total connectivity revenue jumped 69% to $5.25 billion from $3.1 billion a year earlier. Analysts were expecting $4.84 billion, according to StreetAccount. Revenue from consumer Starlink subscribers amounted to $3.12 billion, while the company generated $2.13 billion from government and enterprise customers.

Buzz has been building about Starlink's plans to build a U.S. mobile network after SpaceX, earlier this month, announced that it's buying 800 MHz of low-band spectrum, and after the Federal Communications Commission approved a plan for the company to build out a 15,000-satellite constellation.

SpaceX jumped into AI with the purchase earlier this year of xAI, Musk's AI lab. The company has been rapidly expanding that business by renting out capacity to Google, Anthropic and others.

AI, which SpaceX now calls "Super Intelligence," has emerged as its largest business. Revenue jumped ninefold to $7.56 billion from $813 million in the same quarter last year. Analysts expected sales of $6.47 billion, according to StreetAccount.

During the third quarter, SpaceX also closed its $60 billion purchase of AI coding startup Cursor.

Meanwhile, SpaceX's launch business was up just slightly in the quarter, with sales rising to $1.77 billion from $1.67 billion a year ago.

Almost all of SpaceX's profit still comes from Starlink. Operating income in the connectivity unit about doubled to $2.37 billion. The AI business swung to a profit of $431 million, while the space division lost $7 million.

Between infrastructure, cloud deals and paying users of the Grok AI models and products, Musk said on the call that annual recurring revenue by the end of next year will be between $500 billion and $1 trillion. Analysts are projecting full-year 2027 revenue of about $110 billion, according to LSEG, which would be up more than 100% from this year.

SpaceX's launch business generated $1.77 billion in revenue in the third quarter.

The company touted its Starship Flight 14 as a major milestone on the earnings call, and Musk said SpaceX will attempt to "catch both the booster and the ship," during its next test flight of the massive rocket.

He added that SpaceX has a "steady stream of Starship flight attempts" scheduled through the end of this year, and accelerating next year.

The company recently announced plans to build a $100 billion spaceport in Louisiana. SpaceX COO Gwynne Shotwell said on the call that SpaceX aims to conduct its first launch from the facility by 2029. Musk said the first launch may be possible by the end of 2028.

The U.S. Department of Justice lost its bid to have a federal court in Mississippi toss a lawsuit filed by the NAACP against SpaceX alleging Clean Air Act violations by Musk's company in the Memphis area.

The DOJ had accused the NAACP of threatening "American national, economic, and energy security" by asking the court to temporarily shut off of the power supply used by SpaceX at its Colossus data centers.

The data centers are powered by methane gas-burning turbines, installed by SpaceX's AI business.

The NAACP, in its suit, said Musk's company built and used gas-burning turbines for power without first obtaining proper permits, and without installing required pollution controls.

In the court's order, published Tuesday, a judge wrote there was "no 'fairly possible' interpretation of the Clean Air Act that would give the United States the right to unilaterally dismiss a citizen suit."

Musk said the boards at Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees to discuss a potential merger "at the request of shareholders," noting that these groups "consist of independent directors."

Because Musk is a "conflicted party," he said he can't be in the discussions.

"I do think there's a lot of merit to combining the companies as we are pretty much in the superintelligence singularity, and there's a lot to be gained from cooperating," Musk said, referring to the development of software, chips and more.

"Do I think it would be a good idea? Yes," Musk said. "But at the end of the day, the shareholders have to be supportive of this."

Musk said that on the Tesla side, "we will need a 50% vote of all shares -- not just those who vote -- but of all shareholders, and that's quite a high hurdle."

Venture capitalist Ira Ehrenpreis is on the boards of both companies.

Musk said on the call that SpaceX aims to reach "volume production" of AI chips, now in development with partners Tesla and Intel, during the third quarter of 2028 at the Terafab factory that's planned in Texas.

Musk thanked ASML and other suppliers for prioritizing equipment deliveries for the factory.

SpaceX secured significant tax incentives in June for the facility despite local opposition from residents concerned about the strain on local resources.

—Lora Kolodny

According to a report from the Wall Street Journal on Tuesday, Musk ordered restrictions on the use of Starshield, a military-grade version of Starlink, in Ukraine in recent weeks, impeding some military operations and the country's ability to defend against intensifying attacks by Russia.

Musk previously pinned responsibility for any restrictions on the U.S. government and President Donald Trump, writing on X that, "Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government."

The Journal, citing a person familiar with the situation, said Musk talked to senior administration officials, including Trump, "before imposing the new speed cap."

The limits caused Ukraine's drones to lose Starshield connectivity when traveling at speed over 100 miles per hour.

—Lora Kolodny

SpaceX said in its shareholder deck that AI growth was spurred by "the activation of new cloud services agreements, the acquisition of Cursor and increased Grok adoption."

Of its $19.6 billion in capital expenditures in the quarter, more than $16.4 billion was committed to the AI business.

Musk said on the company's call that SpaceX aims to "deploy power, cooling infrastructure at a rate that is vastly faster than anyone else."

In its Memphis-area data centers, SpaceX's AI business is on track to be able to bring 200,000 of Nvidia's Grace Blackwell system online "every month for the remainder of this year."

—Lora Kolodny

SpaceX reported that its Starlink subscriber count rose to 13.6 million in the third quarter, with average revenue per user increasing to $69, up from 12 million Starlink subscribers and ARPU of $66 in the prior period.

The company boasted in an investor update that Starlink was activated in over 3,800 aircraft as of the quarter's end. Customers include American Airlines.

Musk recently threatened Delta CEO Ed Bastian, saying he would lose his job because his airline has not yet made Starlink wireless internet available in their planes.

SpaceX is scheduled to hold its third-quarter earnings call at 4:30 p.m. ET. The company encouraged shareholders to submit questions online ahead of the meeting.

—Ari Levy

Musk's other public company, Tesla, is scheduled to post third-quarter results on Wednesday. Analysts expect revenue at the electric vehicle maker to dip slightly from a year ago to $27.9 billion, according to LSEG.

Tesla shares are down about 17% this year as of Tuesday, leaving the company with a market cap of almost $1.5 trillion.

—Ari Levy

SpaceX is relatively new to the cloud computing business. Many of its competitors in the space, the so-called neoclouds, have been getting hammered on Wall Street of late on concerns that rising interest rates are going to make the costly buildout financially burdensome.

CoreWeave and Nebius are down 28% so far in October, while IREN has plunged about 35%. Earlier this month, Australian AI data center operator Firmus withdrew its planned IPO, citing market volatility and conditions.

While SpaceX is far less reliant on that side of its business than are the neoclouds, capital costs still matter, and the company is banking on the AI boom continuing. In the second quarter, debt and finance leases increased to $36.8 billion, up from $22 billion the prior period.

—Ari Levy

Since opening at $150 on June 12, SpaceX shares have edged slightly lower, slipping to $147.50 on Tuesday. That gives Space a market cap of about $2 trillion.

The stock hit a low of $104.83 on Aug. 3, a few days before the first post-IPO lockup expired, making more than 911 million SpaceX shares available for trading.

The lockup lifted on Aug. 6, and the stock rallied, popping 6% initially, then nearly 16% the day after.

—Lora Kolodny

SpaceX continues to serve as a critical launch provider to the U.S. Defense Department and NASA.

The business hasn't been historically profitable, but it's the segment that captured the public's interest in the rocket maker. In the second quarter, SpaceX reported launch revenue of $962 million and a net loss of $542 million.

Future profitability hinges on the success of SpaceX's Starship mega-rocket, which is still being developed and tested.

Since the company's last earnings call in August, SpaceX hit multiple milestones, reaching orbit and using Starship to deploy dozens of its new V.3 Starlink satellites during Test Flight 14. But the rocket is still far from reliable or reusable.

Investors are awaiting an update on pending NASA missions and the future of the older Falcon rocket. SpaceX has reportedly stopped allowing other companies to launch their cargo, including satellites, into orbit on Falcon missions.

—Lora Kolodny

In its first earnings report in August, SpaceX reported capital expenditures of $18.4 billion, with 80% of that going towards its artificial intelligence division.

That number spooked Wall Street, and shares dropped following the report.

SpaceX and xAI merged in February and the company has been pouring money into its effort to become a major player in AI, a market dominated by OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. The AI division also acquired Cursor in a $60 billion transaction and is now known as either SpaceXAI or SpaceXSI, with the "SI" standing for President Donald Trump's preferred "Super Intelligence."

—Lora Kolodny

Wall Street is sounding bullish on SpaceX ahead of its third-quarter earnings report, to be delivered after the bell on Tuesday.

Mizuho has an outperform rating on the stock and a $200 price target, per a Monday note from analyst Brett Linzey. Shares were trading around $148 as of 10:45 a.m. on Tuesday.

"AI is the largest swing factor for 3Q26 revenue and EBITDA. Consensus has AI revenue of $6.30B (+146% Q/Q from $2.56B) and AI adj. EBITDA of $3.32B (from $1.15B)," he wrote.

Analysts are expecting some conversation about deals or competitive plays with cable and telecom companies following SpaceX's acquisition of low-band spectrum earlier this month.

"We expect initial strategic commentary centers around Connectivity and how SPCX can partner/compete its way into global markets, although we think low-band distribution becomes a meaningful intelligence lever over time," Colin Canfield at Cantor Fitzgerald wrote in a Monday note. He has a price target of $246 and an overweight rating on the stock.

Oppenheimer's expectations are more measured. The bank thinks the quarter will be "in-line" with "guidance reiterated."

"Industry subscriber growth is slowing with limited room for pricing action, so focus is shifting from net adds and service revenue growth toward cost-cutting using AI, and lower subsidies/churn," Timothy Horan at Oppenheimer told clients in a Tuesday note. He expects "more rational incumbent behavior" from SpaceX's competitors.

–Tobias Burns

Musk said Monday that he'll give every child in the U.S. one of his SpaceX shares next year.

With 72 million children under 18 in the U.S. as of July 1, 2025, according to official census data, that would amount a giveaway of about $10.5 billion worth of shares as of the current price.

Musk, the world's richest person with a net worth of over $900 billion, said in a post on X that, "This will be the first of many," without providing further details. However, when it comes to fulfilling his philanthropic promises, Musk has a spotty track record.

—Ari Levy

A military tier of Starlink, marketed as Starshield, has drawn scrutiny of late as restrictions on the system in Ukraine hindered the country's ability to defend itself against intensifying attacks by Russia.

Ukrainian officials said SpaceX and the Trump administration restricted Starshield without warning last week, causing Ukraine's military forces to lose control of drones moving over 100 miles per hour, as the craft were relying on Starshield for connectivity at those speeds.

Musk said in a post on X that any changes to Starshield should be attributed to the U.S. government and President Donald Trump, though Trump later denied he ordered any such restrictions.

Ukraine President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told NBC's "Meet the Press" on Sunday that the restraints remained in place over the weekend.

Investors, meanwhile, have poured capital into potential Starlink competitors, including Eutelsat.

—Lora Kolodny

After acquiring xAI in February with its data centers in and around Memphis, Tennessee, SpaceX struck lucrative cloud computing agreements, establishing a new revenue stream.

Deals with Google, Anthropic and Reflection AI, as well as an unnamed customer revealed by SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen at an event last month, amount to potentially tens of billions of dollars in annual revenue for the company.

SpaceX is reportedly in talks to raise up to $40 billion to buy more Nvidia chips to support its AI ambitions.

Investors are looking for updates around this business for SpaceX as it seeks to monetize its AI capacity.

—Lora Kolodny

Ahead of earnings, investors are invited to submit questions for the call. One entry that got a lot of upvotes was about timing for a potential merger between SpaceX and Tesla, Musk's automaker.

Musk's companies have had business and financial relations for years, even sharing personnel and some board members.

Tesla invested $2 billion into xAI, which later converted into shares of SpaceX. The AI unit of SpaceX also spent about $430 million last year on Tesla's Megapacks, which are battery energy storage systems, and $143 million on Cybertrucks.

With SpaceX hitting the public market in June, speculation about a more permanet tie-up has been swirling. SpaceX COO Gwynne Shotwell told CNBC ahead of the IPO that a merger "might make Elon's life a little easier."

A demo of Tesla's next-generation Roadster last week stirred up further merger excitement among Musk fans. The new Roadster was developed with SpaceX and incorporates its technology, including cold gas thrusters, to enable the car to launch, hover and flip in mid-air.

—Lora Kolodny
