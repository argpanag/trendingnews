The Prince and Princess of Wales have shared a public message to King Charles.

In honour of the monarch's 78th birthday, the future King and Queen took to their joint Instagram account to celebrate His Majesty.

"Wishing a very Happy Birthday to His Majesty The King!" the couple penned, sharing the message alongside a recent photo of Charles and Elton John.

The image was taken of the duo as they joined forces to record an hour-long special episode of Sir Elton's Apple Radio show Rocket Hour, which will air this evening in honour of Charles' birthday.

The top-secret recording took place last month, when both men visited Apple Music Studios at Battersea Power Station, and was designed to highlight the close connection of music to the work of the King's Trust, which turns 50 this year.

Why does King Charles have two birthdays?

While Saturday marks the King's 'real' birthday, he also has an 'official birthday' in the British calendar.

This is due to a royal tradition designed to ensure good British weather for outdoor public celebrations.

King Charles has two birthdays (@ Getty)

Because his actual birthday falls in mid-November – when London weather is typically cold, rainy, and unpredictable – his official public birthday is celebrated in June, when conditions are much more reliable for a massive outdoor parade.

Charles will be celebrating his birthday on Saturday privately, but it is still marked publicly by traditional gun salutes in London, 41 guns in Green Park and 62 at the Tower of London, and the ringing of Westminster Abbey's bells.

Trooping the Colour in June is the Monarch's 'official' birthday (@ Getty Images)

Meanwhile, his official birthday is celebrated on a Saturday in mid-June with Trooping the Colour, a centuries-old military parade featuring over 1,400 soldiers, 200 horses, and 400 musicians, culminating in the royal family appearing on the Buckingham Palace balcony for an RAF flypast.

The double-birthday tradition started back in the 18th century when King George II didn't want to risk bad weather ruining his public birthday celebrations. He decided to combine his birthday festivities with the annual summer military parade, establishing Trooping the Colour as the monarch's official birthday celebration.

Then King Edward VII, who was born in November like George II and Charles III, standardised the practice by permanently moving his official birthday to May or June, a custom that British monarchs born in autumn or winter, including Queen Elizabeth II, who was born in April, have followed ever since.
