Maryland Jockey Club's 2027 Dates Dip To 114; Knauf Perceives 'Enormous' National Support For Preakness

TheTools · 18.11.2026 21:50 · source · permalink

By T. D. Thornton

The Maryland Racing Commission on Wednesday approved a slate of 114 race dates for Laurel Park and Pimlico Race Course in 2027, six fewer programs than the commission awarded in 2025 and 2026.

Commissioners did not discuss the date reduction prior to a unanimous voice vote.

Nor did any representatives from the Maryland Jockey Club (MJC), the nonprofit entity that runs Laurel and Pimlico, speak about next year's calendar.

Christopher Merz, the commission's executive director, noted prior to the vote that the Maryland Thoroughbred Horsemen's Association had notified the commission of its consent for the 2027 dates.

There has been no racing at Pimlico since the 2025 GI Preakness Stakes. The track has since been demolished and is being rebuilt.

When the “Pimlico Plus” project began 18 months ago, the goal was for the Baltimore track to be ready to host all commercial-track Thoroughbred racing in the state by 2027, with Laurel, 28 miles southwest, closing.

Delays and other decisions have since led to a revised plan under which Pimlico is now expected to host the 2027 Preakness with temporary infrastructure and will take on the bulk of Maryland's racing sometime in 2028 or 2029.

Laurel, which had been slated to cease all horse operations, is now expected to remain open as a training center once Pimlico opens while also hosting a limited number of race dates to take advantage of its 142-foot-wide turf course, twice the width of Pimlico's.

Later in the meeting, Bill Knauf, the MJC's president and general manager, addressed the commission regarding last week's news that Churchill Downs, Inc. (CDI) and the New York Racing Association (NYRA) are continuing with a Thoroughbred Championship Series (TCS) plan that is widely perceived as an exclusion or marginalization of the GI Preakness Stakes and the traditional Triple Crown.

Both CDI and NYRA have denied that is their intention. They maintain that the Triple Crown remains intact and unaffected by the TCS.

Back in August, CDI and NYRA created the new six-race, bonus-laden TCS series and said it will debut in 2027.

The TCS will include the GI Kentucky Derby, the GI Belmont Stakes and four other stakes at CDI or NYRA tracks, but not the Preakness.

Last week, CDI and NYRA further widened the gap between the TCS races and the middle jewel of the Triple Crown by revealing that for the first time in four decades, there will be no nominations process for the 2027 Triple Crown. The traditional format will be abandoned and replaced by a nominations process for just the TCS stakes.

“Obviously we're disappointed with that,” Knauf said. “But the Preakness, that doesn't change anything. We will have our own Preakness nomination, and that will be coming out in the coming weeks.

“And it certainly doesn't change anything with the Preakness being the second leg in the Triple Crown,” Knauf continued. “People are obviously sometimes getting confused about the [TCS] and the Triple Crown. But if there is a Derby, Preakness and Belmont, there is a Triple Crown. And the Preakness will be the second leg of that, and we intend to put on a great show…

“At the Breeders' Cup [earlier this month], we had a lot of positive conversations with horsemen from all over the country [and] we perceived an enormous amount of support,” Knauf said.

With 186 days between now and the May 23 Preakness, which will be run on a Sunday and 22 days out from the Derby starting in 2027, there is still a long winter ahead.

After racing concludes at Laurel for this year Dec. 27, the track will take a brief break before the 2027 season opens Jan. 8, with racing through the end of that month on Fridays and Saturdays. In February and March Laurel will add Sundays to make a three-day racing week.

Merz told TDN in an email after the meeting that the scheduling for Pimlico's Preakness festival, an expected summer break for the circuit, and the exact dates for the rest of the year would likely not be finalized until the end of March.

The Timonium Fair, which annually hosts dirt racing on its five-furlong track around Labor Day, applied for and was awarded 10 dates but is expected to run only seven (Aug. 27-29 and Sept. 3-6).

Fair Hill, which runs both flat and jump races over its turf course, applied for and was awarded eight dates with a schedule to be determined.  In 2026, Fair Hill was scheduled for two dates in May but lost one because of wet weather.

It is customary for both Timonium and Fair Hill to apply for more dates than they actually intend to run to afford flexibility in scheduling.
