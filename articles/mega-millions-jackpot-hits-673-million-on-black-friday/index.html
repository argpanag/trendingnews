Mega Millions Jackpot Hits $673 Million on Black Friday

TheTools · 27.11.2026 18:00 · source · permalink

The Mega Millions jackpot has been building for 122 days.

Black Friday shoppers could spend it in about half an hour.

The jackpot sits at an estimated $673 million for Friday night’s drawing, with a cash option of $282.6 million, according to the Wisconsin Lottery. That’s up $28 million from Tuesday, when nobody matched the numbers 38, 42, 44, 45, 62 and the gold Mega Ball 16.

The drawing also lands on Black Friday, the most popular shopping day of Thanksgiving weekend, according to the National Retail Federation. That makes for a fun bit of math.

How fast does Black Friday spend a Mega Millions jackpot?

Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion in US online spending on Black Friday this year, up 9.2% from last year. Spread evenly across 24 hours, that’s about $537.5 million an hour. Roughly $9 million a minute.

At that pace, the jackpot’s $282.6 million cash value is gone in about 31 1/2 minutes. The full $673 million annuity lasts longer: about an hour and 15 minutes.

That’s online carts alone, not counting a single store, a single parking lot or doorbuster line.

Meanwhile, the jackpot has been on the clock since July 28, when a ticket sold at a Wawa in Bradenton, Florida, won $800 million. Friday is the 35th drawing since then, and the previous 34 have all missed. The drawing is at 11 p.m. ET.

Where the Mega Millions jackpot ranks all time

If someone hits it Friday, $673 million would be the 11th-largest jackpot in Mega Millions history. It would pass the $656 million prize shared by tickets in Illinois, Kansas and Maryland in March 2012, which currently holds No. 11. Tuesday’s Mega Millions $645 million estimate sat 13th.

The top 10 is still a reach. No. 10 is the $800 million Florida jackpot from July, more than $100 million away.

The jackpot already ranks fourth among the year’s biggest lottery jackpots and second among Mega Millions prizes in 2026, according to Forbes.

Powerball keeps paying while Mega Millions keeps rolling

Here’s the odd part of the year: Since that Florida ticket in July, Mega Millions hasn’t paid a jackpot. Powerball has paid three:

  • $1.04 billion in Illinois on Aug. 12.
  • $847 million in New Jersey on Nov. 4.
  • $114 million in Florida on Nov. 21.

Add them up, and that’s just over $2 billion in advertised jackpots on one side of the counter and none on the other.

The full-year scoreboard isn’t close, either. Powerball’s own list shows seven jackpot wins in 2026 through Nov. 4, and the Florida ticket on Nov. 21 makes eight. Mega Millions has three: $536 million in Illinois on March 10, $60 million in Ohio on March 17 and the $800 million in Florida.

Two of Powerball’s this year rank eighth and ninth in game history.

Powerball goes again Saturday at 10:59 p.m. ET, on what retailers call Small Business Saturday, with a much smaller jackpot of $48 million ($20.2 million cash), according to the New York Lottery.

What a $5 Mega Millions ticket gets you on Black Friday

Mega Millions costs $5 a play. The odds of hitting the jackpot are 1 in 290.5 million, according to the Wisconsin Lottery’s prize chart. The odds of winning any prize are far better, with the smallest payout, $5 for matching just the Mega Ball, at 1 in 36.

Those odds are the same Friday as on any other drawing day. The calendar doesn’t move them.

The NRF expects 126 million people to shop on Black Friday, based on its annual survey. Some of them will grab a ticket on the way out of the store.

And somewhere around 11 p.m. ET, the clock on 122 days of rolling either stops …

… or Black Friday’s biggest number heads to Tuesday.
