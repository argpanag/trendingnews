PlayStation Users Report Outages as ‘GTA 6’ Begins Global Launch

TheTools · 18.11.2026 18:21 · source · permalink

PlayStation, Xbox and internet providers are bracing: 'Grand Theft Auto VI' might literally break the internet.

Grand Theft Auto VI hasn’t even been released in the U.S. yet and PlayStation users are already reporting disruptions.

Outage-tracking site DownDetector has registered thousands of complaints, including reports of login failures and connectivity issues, ahead of GTA 6 launching at midnight. The site’s outage chart suggests that the problems are still spiking.

Which makes sense. The last week has seen disruptions affect Microsoft, Apple, airlines, banks, the FAA and even the NSA. With everything going down lately, does anyone really think the biggest game launch in history will go smoothly?

“Imagine waiting years for GTA 6 only for the PlayStation Network to say, ‘Not today,'” wrote one user on X.

The game launches at midnight on Nov. 19, which means it’s already been released in several countries, such as Australia and New Zealand.

PlayStation and Xbox have been doing what they can to try and limit disruption. Both companies have urged players to pre-download the roughly 212GB game, which is the most pre-ordered title in PlayStation Store and Microsoft Store history (“Given this unprecedented demand, we’ve joined with Rockstar Games to allow players to start pre-loading the game nine days early, so they’re ready to play on launch day,” PlayStation said in a statement).

While Sony hasn’t given a reason for the outages, it’s probably a safe bet that users scrambling to pre-download the game could be causing traffic issues.

Once the single-player game is downloaded, a player should be able to play offline without making further demands on the console companies’ servers.

But that assumes the vast majority of players have planned in advance and didn’t wait until today or tomorrow to get the game — and how likely does that sound? While hardcore gamers have likely been locked and loaded for days, GTA 6 will almost certainly draw legions of casual players reading the game’s positive reviews and media hype. Plus activities like players activating the game, signing into PlayStation accounts, and playing via cloud streaming could cause disruption.

Other potential choke points are internet service providers. Comcast says GTA 6‘s first 48 hours of pre-downloads constituted the largest single event in its network history. The company further predicted that Nov. 19 will its busiest internet traffic day ever. “We’re about to break all-time highs,” Comcast chief network officer Elad Nafshi told Inc.

Surprisingly, for those hoping to play the game who haven’t yet upgraded to a PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series X|S, you’re in luck. The consoles are currently listed as in stock at major U.S. retailers like Target, Walmart and Best Buy. This might not last, however. The Game Business previously reported that at least one major retailer is expecting the consoles to sell out ahead of the holidays. “We’ve been informed that because of the on-going issues around hardware component availability, we won’t be getting the units we want ahead of [GTA 6],” the source said. “Demand will likely outstrip supply during the year end period”). So if you want to return to Vice City, you better buy a console before you have to steal it.
