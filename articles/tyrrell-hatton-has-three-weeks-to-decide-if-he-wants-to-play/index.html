Tyrrell Hatton has three weeks to decide if he wants to play in Ryder Cup

TheTools · 13.10.2026 07:33 · source · permalink

DP World Tour takes hard line with rule change that will make LIV golfers ineligible to represent Europe in biennial event

Tyrrell Hatton has three weeks to decide if he wants to play in next year’s Ryder Cup after the DP World Tour revealed it has introduced a rule that will ban LIV players from being members of the European circuit.

In a move that will surely make joining LIV 2.0 less attractive, officials at Wentworth have abandoned the tour’s former policy and asked its golfers to state up front whether they intend to play in the breakaway league for the forthcoming season.

The DP World Tour said it would expel any player who goes back on their word. Only members are eligible for the Europe Ryder Cup team and Hatton and co have until November 1 to sign up.

“On their membership application for next year, if they said they intended to play on LIV, their application wouldn’t be accepted,” an insider told Telegraph Sport. “If they said they weren’t going to play on LIV 2.0 and they then go on to do just that, their membership would then be rescinded.”

This is a significant setback to LIV as it tries to persuade enough players to sign on for 2027 after Jon Rahm announced last week that he was leaving. There are only 10 events planned on LIV 2.0 – with prize funds slashed from $30m (£22.7m) to as low as $5.5m – and this is clearly not enough for any professional who wants a full schedule.

The PGA Tour has banned LIV players since the league was formed in 2022. If the golfers are qualified, they could play in events on the Asian Tour, but that circuit is also fighting to put a full calendar together after the Saudis withdrew their funding.

With BC Partners – the London-based credit agency which has cast itself as the would-be saviours of LIV with a $300m investment – demanding a “requisite number” of players to sign on before the October 25 deadline, the decision is as stark as it is urgent. DP World Tour applications must be submitted by November 1 and every player must comply, including Rory McIlroy.

This season, the likes of Rahm, Hatton and Adrian Meronk have enjoyed dual membership after the DP World Tour granted “conditional releases”, so long as all the outstanding fines were settled and they agreed to increase their minimum number of events and appear where they are told.

However, after the Saudi Public Investment Fund (PIF) shocked the sport by withdrawing its backing after ploughing in more than £4bn, the DP World Tour indicated that it would be changing tack and returning to its former policy of issuing fines and single-tournament bans for players teeing it up in LIV events. However, it has gone one step further.

The DP World Tour has “a strategic alliance” with the PGA Tour and it is plain to see that with LIV circling the drain, it is a case of foot down on the jugular.

English veteran Richard Bland is the only LIV golfer who has publicly declared that he will play on LIV 2.0 – if it goes ahead – and all eyes are on Bryson DeChambeau, who is viewed as the key individual. The two-time major winner has sounded steadfast in his commitment since the PIF withdrawal, but has latterly gone quiet.

Telegraph Sport last week revealed that DeChambeau’s management had been in touch with Wentworth about the chances of appearing in tournaments, but these would have to be on sponsors’ invites.

Separately, the tour has informed agents of other LIV golfers who have inquired that if they have no status, arrangements could be made to offer a number of starts – presumably, also on invites – but they have also been made aware of the new stringent restrictions.

The DP World Tour is currently involved in negotiating its contract with the PGA Tour – a deal that has been worth tens of millions per annum to Wentworth – and Guy Kinnings, the chief executive, is determined to cement his circuit’s place as the global tour outside the United States.

“Players need to make a choice,” Kinnings told BBC Sport. “We have rules and regulations which are there to protect our tour and which our members must all abide by. Players who are not willing to comply with our rules and regulations should not be seeking to join our tour.”
