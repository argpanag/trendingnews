Live updates: Trump preemptively looks for someone to blame for a potential Texas Senate loss | CNN Politics

TheTools · 15.10.2026 12:02 · source · permalink

On the campaign trail

• Texas blues: President Donald Trump has preemptively blamed outgoing Sen. John Cornyn, whom he spurned in the primary, and GOP strategist Karl Rove for a potential Senate loss in the Lone Star State. His comments underscore GOP anxiety about the race, with Republicans pouring millions of dollars into the reliably red state.

• Fundraising boost: SpaceX founder Elon Musk is doing his part to bolster the GOP’s campaign in Texas, where many of his business interests are based. According to third quarter FEC filings, the billionaire contributed more than $150 million that flowed to a Texas PAC supporting Attorney General Ken Paxton in his Senate race against Democrat James Talarico.

• Pivotal Senate seat: Vice President JD Vance was in Michigan, where the Senate seat remains a prime pickup opportunity for the GOP. Vance renewed attacks on Democrat Abdul El-Sayed as he rallied for the GOP candidate Mike Rogers. At the same time, the Democratic candidate was campaigning in the Upper Peninsula, where New York Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez joined him.

Updates

El-Sayed holds rally alongside Ocasio-Cortez in Michigan's Upper Peninsula

US Senate Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed ended his tour through Michigan’s Upper Peninsula tonight with a packed rally inside Marquette’s Vandament arena, where more than a thousand Michiganders showed up to hear him speak.

Marquette is the bluest part of the Upper Peninsula, home to Northern Michigan University, and voters more likely to support Democrats.

New York Rep. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez took the stage first, delivering a pointed attack against Republican US Senate nominee Mike Rogers, accusing him of doing little for the interests of people in this area while he was in Congress.

“Mike Rogers has already spent 14 years in Congress with the power to do something and what his record actually shows is 14 years of saying one thing to Michiganders and turning around and doing another thing in Washington,” Ocasio-Cortez said.

Ocasio-Cortez, with a chance to test her own political message in a battleground state, focused her speech on access to reproductive care, grocery prices, access to healthcare and the high cost of living.

Ocasio-Cortez is expected to head south to Pontiac, Dearborn and Detroit on Friday, where she’ll campaign for El-Sayed alongside Reps. Ayanna Pressley and Debbie Dingel.

El-Sayed walked out on stage to the tune of Tupac’s “changes” delivering his stump speech to a roaring crowd.

The candidate will continue his campaign through Michigan tomorrow, staying in the Upper Peninsula for a stop in Sault St. Marie for a rally at Lake Superior State University.

Jackson outraises Collins nearly four-to-one in Maine

Republican Sen. Susan Collins was outraised by her Democratic challenger, former state Sen. Troy Jackson, by nearly four-to-one in the third quarter, a concerning signal for the veteran incumbent.

Third quarter reports with the Federal Election Commission showed that Jackson took in $20.4 million since he filed on July 7 to run as the replacement for ex-nominee Graham Platner, whose campaign imploded earlier the same month.

Collins, meanwhile, raised just over $5.2 million to her principal campaign committee during the reporting period.

Entering October, Collins still had an advantage in cash on hand, about $5.4 million to just under $4 million for Jackson.

But Jackson’s ability to quickly energize Democrats behind his bid and Collins’ inability to keep pace with his fundraising entering the general election sprint have kept Democratic hopes alive of flipping the seat despite the turbulence of the race.

Tafoya outraises Flanagan in Minnesota Senate race during Q3

Republican Michele Tafoya outraised Democrat Peggy Flanagan in the third quarter of the year for the US Senate race in Minnesota, a contrast to Democratic fundraising strength in other key races.

The filings showed that Tafoya raised $4.9 million for her principal campaign committee between a pre-primary report covering the first three weeks of July, and a quarterly report through the end of September.

Over the same stretch, Flanagan, the lieutenant governor of Minnesota, raised about $4.3 million for her principal campaign committee. And entering October, and the critical final weeks of the election, Tafoya had about $2.1 million in cash on hand compared to about $1.5 million for Flanagan.

The Senate contest in Minnesota has flown under the radar, with attention focused on marquee races in Ohio, Texas, Michigan and elsewhere. It’s set to see less than $3 million in ad spending over the final three weeks of the election.

But some Democrats have expressed unease over the trajectory of the race there, even as the state hasn’t elected a Republican statewide since 2006. And the Q3 fundraising totals are a yellow light for a party feeling increasingly confident about its midterm prospects.

GOP Rep. Max Miller addresses divorce in new ad

Republican Rep. Max Miller is up with a new ad, which features him talking straight to camera about his divorce amid a bitter custody battle with ex-wife Emily Moreno, the daughter of Ohio GOP Sen. Bernie Moreno.

The congressman and his ex-wife are locked in an ongoing custody battle to renegotiate the terms of their shared parenting agreement. His ex-wife has leveled allegations of abuse against him, which he has denied.

“Despite relentless press and investigations, every allegation against me was proven false,” Miller says in the ad. “As a result, I thankfully share equal custody of our sweet daughter.”

The custody case is expected to go to trial after the midterm election in November.

Miller made the decision to stay in the race in August despite a growing number of politicians in Washington and Ohio called on him to step aside in order to protect a congressional seat in what is supposed to be a safe GOP district. Trump won the district by 11 points in the last election but Democrats have been bullish about their chances there with iron worker Brian Poindexter.

“I stayed in this race because I want the best future for our girl and your family,” Miller says in the ad.

The ad comes the same day as a FEC report showed Miller raised just over $146,000 in the third fundraising quarter and donated a million dollars to his campaign.

El-Sayed fires back at Vance: "We believe in an America big enough for all of us"

Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed fired back at comments made earlier today on the campaign trail by JD Vance, in which the vice president framed the race as “a battle between those who loath the United States of America and those who love it.”

“At his little rally, he wanted to raise questions about my commitment to America,” El-Sayed told a crowd in Marquette, Michigan. “Let me tell you, nobody understands how deep and profound the gift of this country is, like a kid who was born here but for the accident of history that allowed that to happen.”

Vance and El-Sayed have traded barbs throughout the campaign cycle, as Republicans have looked to use the progressive as a Democratic boogeyman. As El-Sayed put it: “Donald Trump and JD Vance can’t seem to keep my name out their mouth.”

At a campaign rally in Michigan in August, Vance called El-Sayed “evil” and incorrectly tried to imply that El-Sayed wants to bring Sharia law to the US.

“They’ve been saying all kinds of things, trying to point to my name and my faith, ‘he’s a Muslim’ — Yeah, I am a Muslim, but I also understand my allegiance is to a constitution,” El-Sayed said.

Rep. Mike Collins' lackluster fundraising hamstrings GOP in Georgia

Rep. Mike Collins, the Republican nominee for US Senate in Georgia, reported raising just $2.8 million in the third quarter and holding just $1.1 million in cash on hand entering October.

Collins’ inability to galvanize Republicans behind his bid against Democratic incumbent Sen. Jon Ossoff has been a significant blow to the GOP’s hopes of flipping the battleground seat.

Ossoff, by contrast, raised more than $33 million for his campaign in the third quarter — and more than $100 million for the cycle so far — and is in such a strong position that he may have leftover campaign cash for future aspirations.

That asymmetry has hamstrung Republicans’ ability to compete with Ossoff on Georgia voters’ screens, even as GOP outside groups have overwhelmed Democrats with money in other races.

Maine Sen. Susan Collins doubles down on Trump criticism over Iran

GOP Sen. Susan Collins doubled down on her criticism of the Iran war as the Maine Republican continues to distance herself from President Donald Trump.

When asked if Congress should have been consulted before Trump decided to go to war, Collins said, “Congress definitely should have been consulted.”

Michigan voter says he would pay $6 or $7 for gallon of gas to improve Iranians' lives

Rob Price, a voter from Pinckney, Michigan, said he would pay even higher prices for gas if that meant the Iranian people could have an opportunity for freedom outside of an oppressive government.

While President Donald Trump initially encouraged the Iranian people to rise up against their government after the first US strikes in February, that hasn’t happened as the war has continued.

Much of the conflict in recent months has focused on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical corridor for the transport of oil and gas. CNN reported today that Trump is weighing new strike plans, as he seeks an end to the deeply unpopular war and high prices that have Republicans bracing for potential backlash from voters in the midterms.

The average gas price in Michigan are $4.58 per gallon, according to AAA. CNN spotted a gas station near Vance’s event that was selling diesel for $6.75 and regular for $4.39 per gallon.

Price told CNN that he’s concerned about the upcoming election.

“I think that the current administration has done a lot of good things for the American people that the American people, the majority, don’t know about,” he said. “I would hate to think that all of that is for nothing.”

Republicans are trying to make people fear El-Sayed, Ocasio-Cortez says

Republicans are trying to make Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed “look big and scary” in advertisements as the GOP tries to win the critical seat, Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez said Thursday.

Ocasio-Cortez, who is campaigning with El-Sayed this week, said Republicans are trying to make people “scared” of the Senate candidate.

Marquette County narrowly voted for former President Joe Biden in 2024, who received just about 4,000 more votes than President Donald Trump.

Ocasio-Cortez encouraged people to break through that fear and tell their friends and family to cast their ballot.

A protester interrupts Maine Senate debate and confronts Collins on data centers

Early in tonight’s Maine Senate debate, a protester shouted at GOP Sen. Susan Collins about the impact of data centers.

The protester said that data centers are “destroying” the climate.

Collins immediately accused Democrat Troy Jackson of planting the protester, but Jackson said he did not know who the person was.

The debate paused briefly to remove the individual and then resumed.

Q3 filings show Musk contributed more than $150 million to bolster GOP in Texas

New filings with the Federal Election Commission on Thursday are shedding light on tech billionaire Elon Musk’s heavy influence on the 2026 midterm elections, especially the blockbuster Senate race in Texas.

After contributing more than a quarter of a billion dollars to support President Donald Trump’s 2024 presidential campaign, Musk is reprising his role as a pillar of GOP operations.

According to the third quarter report from Senate Leadership Fund – the principal GOP super PAC targeting Senate races – Musk donated $75 million on September 9, and another $83 million on September 23.

That injection of more than $150 million appeared to flow directly to another group, Texas PAC, formed specifically for the unusually competitive Senate race in the state, where many of Musk’s business interests are based.

The filing shows that SLF transferred $75 million to Texas PAC on September 10 – exactly matching Musk’s donation to SLF, one day earlier – and another $83 million to Texas PAC on September 24 – also a day after Musk gave the same amount to SLF.

In September, the same time Musk’s money began flowing to Republicans, GOP ad support skyrocketed – Texas PAC spent more than $77 million on ads in the month alone, according to AdImpact data. And in total, including the September surge and bookings through Election Day, Texas PAC is set to spend nearly $160 million on ads backing Paxton.

Abdul El-Sayed sidesteps question about Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez' political future

US Senate Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed is focused on his own race, at least for now.

El-Sayed is campaigning through Michigan’s Upper Peninsula this week alongside Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, who has said she will decide her own political future after the midterms.

Pressed by CNN on whether Ocasio-Cortez should set her sights on the White House, El-Sayed again demurred.

Ocasio-Cortez, who has served four terms in Congress representing parts of Queens and the Bronx, is considering a run for the US Senate or the White House. For now, Ocasio-Cortez has said she is too focused on her own reelection to Congress.

Even as El-Sayed avoided answering the question, it kept coming up.

A nine-year-old town hall attendant in Iron Mountain, who attended with his parents, asked Ocasio-Cortez if she’d consider running for president or vice president.

The question got a big laugh from the audience, El-Sayed and Ocasio-Cortez. Both, again, declined to answer.

Ocasio-Cortez continues stockpiling campaign cash amid questions about her future

In an interview with the New York Times, Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez said she will decide whether to run for president in 2028 after the midterm elections. The 36-year-old New York representative questioned in the interview whether a Latina could be elected in today’s political climate. CNN’s Gloria Pazmino reports.

New York Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez raised $4.8 million in the third quarter and reported a cash on hand total of more than $17 million, according to a filing with the Federal Election Commission.

It’s another strong haul for the progressive favorite, who has built a fundraising operation that rivals national party committees.

But that stockpile can be transferred to a campaign for another federal office – say, a run for US Senate in 2028, with questions swirling about the plans of the seat’s current holder, Minority Leader Chuck Schumer.

It’s also a war chest that could be used to run for president, a possibility Ocasio-Cortez has openly considered. “Life doesn’t wait for you to be ready for things. Sometimes life just makes things ready for you,” she told the New York Times last month in a candid discussion of the subject.

Vance says El-Sayed "obsessed" with conflicts in Middle East rather than Michigan

Vice President JD Vance revived his ongoing feud with Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed as he rallied in Michigan Thursday, casting the candidate as “obsessed” with conflicts in the Middle East rather than issues in the state he is running in.

Framing the midterm election as “a battle between those who loath the United States of America and those who love it,” Vance said Michigan has “become ground zero in that battle.”

“It is a battle between grievance and gratitude, between politicians obsessed with foreign causes and leaders who remember that their first duty is to fight for the people right here at home,” the vice president said as he touted his support for Republican Mike Rogers.

Vance and El-Sayed have traded barbs throughout the campaign cycle, as Republicans have looked to use the progressive, and at times controversial, candidate as a Democratic boogeyman both in Michigan and other battleground races.

Some background: President Donald Trump carried Monroe County, where Vance spoke, by 27 points in 2024.

Democratic Senate candidates show fundraising strength in Q3

Today is the deadline for third quarter reports to the Federal Election Commission, covering fundraising and spending activity between July and September, and announcements and filings are steadily coming in for some key races.

Georgia Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff was another Democratic Senate candidate announcing a huge third quarter, raising $33 million according to a release from his campaign. His GOP challenger, Rep. Mike Collins, has not yet announced or filed a Q3 report.

Meanwhile, in North Carolina, former Gov. Roy Cooper announced raising $19.5 million in Q3, as he appears in a strong position to win his battleground Senate race against former RNC Chair Michael Whatley, who has not yet disclosed his totals.

Filings, which are due by midnight, will shed detailed light on fundraising, spending, and cash on hand entering October.

Meet the Democratic donor funding last-minute Senate ads in deep-red states

A Florida lawyer and his family are plowing millions into a new super PAC to boost Democrats’ chances of flipping four GOP-held Senate seats.

The last-minute gambit by Steve Pajcic, a former Florida state lawmaker and co-founder of a personal injury firm, underscores the optimism among some Democrats that the midterms could deliver big wins for their party.

Pajcic said he will spend at least $7 million on ads to shape US Senate races in Florida, Kansas, Mississippi and South Carolina. Ads began appearing this week, including one targeting Mississippi Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in a state President Donald Trump won by nearly 23 points two years ago.

Scott Colom, the Democratic nominee in Mississippi, has drawn growing interest from big-name figures in his party. Officials in Hyde-Smith’s campaign have said they are confident she will prevail in this deep-red state.

In an interview with CNN on Thursday, Pajcic said he hopes Democrats could win 55 or 56 seats in November and “contribute to a wave of history.”

Pajcic said he could add more to the budget as he and his team evaluate races.

The New York Times first reported on the spending by Pajcic’s super PAC, New Deal for All.

Pajcic said his family’s money can go further in states such as Mississippi with lower-cost media markets. He’s steering well clear of another ruby-red state, Texas, which has seen huge spending to influence the outcome of the battle between Republican Ken Paxton and Democrat James Talarico.

“I would never put any money in Texas, because, Jiminy Cricket, I would be just a drop in the bucket,” the 80-year-old said. “I’m not one of those billionaires, you know. I’m just somebody who happens to have a little extra money, and I think this is a very good cause.”

Trump tries to lay blame for potential Texas Senate loss on Sen. John Cornyn

President Donald Trump on Thursday preemptively blamed outgoing Sen. John Cornyn, whom he spurned in the primary, and GOP strategist Karl Rove for a potential Senate loss in the Lone Star State.

“If Ken Paxton loses, it will be because of WEAK and INEFFECTIVE Political Hacks like John Cornyn and Karl Rove, who have ineffectively opposed me for my entire political career,” Trump said a Truth Social post.

His latest comments underscore GOP anxiety about the Senate race, with Republicans pouring millions of dollars into the reliably red state to boost state Attorney General Ken Paxton, who’s been neck-and-neck with Democrat James Talarico in recent polling.

Trump used some of the strongest words he’s had for Cornyn, whose name resurfaced in recent weeks in leaked audio of Paxton. The president claimed that Cornyn and Rove are “doing everything possible to make it hard” for Paxton to win and asked “all of those who supported Senator John Cornyn to UNITE, and immediately switch your support to Ken Paxton.”

The president said Cornyn and Rove “should be ashamed of themselves,” adding, “They are hurting Texas and, more importantly, hurting the United States of America!”

CNN has reached out to Cornyn.

Earlier Thursday, Trump said he’d return to Texas, if necessary, for Paxton, whom he called “controversial.” He’s already made three trips to the state within one month to bolster his chosen candidate.

Trump has said he has “no regrets” about backing Paxton over Cornyn, but the pair’s relationship has been fraught, with Trump calling the GOP nominee “a pain in the ass sometimes” and making comments on his appearance, while Paxton has privately questioned the president’s political capital.

Protester argues El-Sayed’s message won’t resonate in Michigan’s Upper Peninsula

As voters filed in for Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed’s second campaign stop of the day, a small group of Mike Rogers supporters gathered outside the venue to voice their displeasure with El-Sayed’s visit to the state’s Upper Peninsula.

Donning a “Mike Rogers” t-shirt, Pam Trask told CNN she believes El-Sayed is “unfit to serve” in the Senate and said she didn’t think his message would resonate with voters in Iron Mountain, Michigan, where he campaigned today with progressive Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez.

“If you come to the UP, you realize that we are salt of the earth people,” she added. “We work hard, and we don’t want them taking our money and our land to spread the wealth as they do in socialism, communism.”

Inside the town hall venue, Stacey Peterson said she voted early for El-Sayed and argued that his message could still appeal to residents in the reliably red part of the state.

“I think that they’re not listening to him speak, because his message is very clear,” Peterson said. “He’s running to get money out of politics, money back in our pocket and Medicare for all. And those are three things that we desperately need.”

Embattled Rep. Max Miller raises low numbers going into final weeks before midterms

Embattled GOP Rep. Max Miller of Ohio only raised around $145,000 in the last quarter and personally loaned his campaign $1 million, according to a new FEC filing published Thursday.

The filings indicate the incumbent Republican has struggled to raise funds amidst allegations of physical abuse from his ex-wife, Emily Moreno, though his personal injection of $1,000,000 into his campaign could serve as a useful buffer.

Miller has denied Moreno’s allegations. The parents are also embroiled in an ongoing custody battle over their young child.

Ohio Sen. Jon Husted, who is up this cycle, has also called on Miller to resign and drop his bid for re-election.

While the Republican senators from his home state have slammed Miller, House Speaker Mike Johnson has called Miller a “great member” of Congress and backed his reelection bid, arguing that the courts and the House Ethics Committee have yet to make a determination on the accusations against Miller.
