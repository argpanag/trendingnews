A security researcher claims they dumped game binary on firmware required for GTA 6, but Sony's latest update likely blocks the exploit

TheTools · 17.11.2026 17:34 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Sony pushed out PS5 firmware 26.07-14.33 with security fixes, likely blocking the method used to dump the game.
  • A notable security researcher claims they accomplished a PS5 game binary dump on the firmware version required for GTA 6 (their post has been deleted, and their account has been banned from Twitter).
  • Rockstar hasn't announced an official PC release for GTA 6 yet.

GTA 6 is only days away at this point, so as far as Sony is concerned, the stakes have never been higher in preventing Rockstar's anticipated open-world game from being dumped on the internet, which would in turn allow people to experiment with emulating and porting the console-only title to PC (via Wccftech).

On Twitter, security researcher Gezine posted the following: "Guess I am the first who dumped game binary at PS5 14.22." Their account has since been banned from the platform, likely due to a DMCA takedown from Sony. While Gezine is relatively well-known in the security and PS5 jailbreaking space, they typically don't publicly release how they've circumvented security measures.

GTA 6 being dumped is the first step toward getting the game running on PC, whether through more traditional PS5 emulation — which has made significant strides recently — or by converting the title's x86-64 code into a Windows executable, similar to AstroHybrid and Astro Bot. However, the party seems to already be over for now.

Just ahead of GTA 6's November 19th release, Sony dropped system software update 26.07-14.33, which includes patch notes outlining that the company has "made some security fixes to the system software." While it's impossible to know for certain, it's likely the method Gezine used to dump the game binary tied to the firmware update required for GTA 6, has been blocked by Sony.

It will be interesting to see how quickly PS5 firmware 14.33 gets cracked

Rockstar still hasn't announced an official PC version of GTA 6

It's important to note that in Gezine's now-deleted post, they never specifically mention GTA 6, so there's a bit of inference and wishful thinking going on here. Still, it makes a lot of sense given the game's release is just a few days away, and that their Twitter account seems to have disappeared entirely. Their past work in the jailbreaking space also indicates this is very likely.

It will be interesting to see whether Sony can keep up with the PS5 jailbreaking scene, especially as the rise of AI tools has let people uncover exploits much faster than ever before. It's likely only a matter of time before someone cracks the PS5's 14.33 firmware. In early September, a jailbreak was released that supported all PS5 consoles running firmware 7.00-13.60, opening the floodgates for homebrew since it was compatible with any PS5 that hadn't recently been updated.

While GTA 6 is coming to the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X on November 19th, Rockstar has not announced a PC version. Rumors point to an official PC release arriving somewhere between six months and a year after release, similar to GTA V. With this in mind, it makes sense that people will be hungry for an unofficial PC port of GTA 6. But will one actually arrive? With GTA 6's release just days away, we won't have to wait long to know for sure.
