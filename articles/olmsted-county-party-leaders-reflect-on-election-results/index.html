Olmsted County party leaders reflect on election results

TheTools · 21.11.2026 02:50 · source · permalink

ROCHESTER, Minn. (KTTC) – Minnesota voters made history by electing the state’s first female governor and giving Democrats control of both the state House and Senate. Now, local party leaders are looking ahead to what the results mean for Minnesota and future elections.

The close contest in Minnesota’s 1st Congressional District remains a focus for Olmsted County’s DFL and GOP leaders. Late-reporting precincts helped keep the U.S. House race tight.

John Swanson, the chair for the Olmsted County DFL, said historical results in those precincts suggested the contest would be close.

Swanson said voters shared concerns about affordability, including the costs of gas, health care, child care and property taxes.

Olmsted County Republican Party Chair Aaron Eberhart said affordability was also among the top issues he heard from voters, along with concerns about fraud.

“I’d say the biggest issues we heard about were affordability, fraud, and again, fraud,” Eberhart said.

Eberhart said Republicans had hoped for better results in Olmsted County but are already looking toward the next election.

“We obviously wish we had done better in just about every one of them, but that doesn’t mean that our efforts were futile,” he said.

Swanson said DFL candidates in neighboring areas worked hard and received support from the party.

“We had some of the best candidates I’ve ever seen running in neighboring areas and they really put in the work for it,” Swanson said.

Both leaders also discussed the process of hand-carrying results to the Olmsted County election office. County officials say the practice adds security but can slow down reporting.

As Republicans prepare for the next election cycle, Eberhart said the party will take a closer look at how it communicates with voters.

“We came up short in a lot of places this year that we wish we wouldn’t have,” Eberhart said. “We’ll look in the mirror, figure out how we can … best message our positions to the voters and hopefully be in a better spot at the next cycle.”

While the parties saw different results this year, both say they will continue working to reach voters ahead of future elections.
