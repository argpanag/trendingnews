Verizon CEO puts a $300B price tag on Musk’s wireless ambitions

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:53 · source · permalink

Elon Musk’s plans to turn Starlink into a major U.S. wireless carrier could create another competitor for Verizon and other telecommunications providers, but Verizon CEO Dan Schulman questions whether the investment will give SpaceX a meaningful advantage, Fortune reports. 

Schulman estimates Musk could spend four to six years and roughly $300 billion building a terrestrial network that ultimately resembles existing wireless carriers, without a significant cost advantage or technological breakthrough. 

SpaceX announced its Starlink Mobile ambitions in October, triggering a sell-off that erased approximately $50 billion in combined market value from the three largest U.S. wireless carriers. SpaceX has already acquired wireless spectrum from EchoStar in a $17 billion deal and secured Federal Communications Commission approval for 15,000 additional satellite launches. 

Musk also said SpaceX is exploring the development of smaller ground-based cell towers for the service as early as 2027. Unlike traditional carriers, Starlink Mobile aims to connect phones directly to satellites without requiring dishes or routers. Starlink currently has approximately 12 million paying subscribers and remains the only profitable division of SpaceX.

Schulman acknowledges SpaceX could become a significant competitor but says building a nationwide terrestrial network would require substantial time, infrastructure and capital. JPMorgan analysts similarly see limited near-term risk to established carriers, noting that SpaceX would need to build its own terrestrial network to compete without a partnership with an existing wireless operator. 

Meanwhile, Verizon is preparing for broader industry changes, including the transition to 6G technology and expanded artificial intelligence capabilities across its network. Schulman says the company must take SpaceX’s ambitions seriously and prepare for multiple competitive scenarios rather than become complacent.
