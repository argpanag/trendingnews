NBA Last Night: How the Stars Took Over

TheTools · 22.10.2026 12:13 · source · permalink

Some NBA nights are remembered for the final scores. This one will be remembered for the ways players imposed themselves. Anthony Edwards did not simply score; he stretched Miami until the game broke. Ty Jerome did not hitch a ride on a blowout; he authored one. Anthony Davis looked like the axis of a new Washington attack, Paolo Banchero played the cleanest individual game on the slate and still walked off a loser, and the Toronto Raptors turned a home date with Chicago into a statement about depth and defensive pressure.

Across twelve finals, the story was not who piled up the prettiest box score alone. It was who changed the temperature of a building, and which teams rode that heat all the way to the horn.

Table of Contents

Edwards turned Miami into a runway

The Minnesota Timberwolves went into Kaseya Center and left with a 131–106 win that felt decided long before the fourth quarter. Edwards was the reason. He hunted threes early and often, finishing 9-of-17 from beyond the arc on the way to 45 points, and he stayed on the glass hard enough to grab 10 rebounds. That combination matters: volume scoring from the perimeter usually comes with a tradeoff, but Edwards also crashed, stole two possessions, and blocked one. In just under 33 minutes he finished +25. Minnesota did not need him to play a full star workload because the lead never really threatened to shrink.

What made the night look different from a routine hot streak was the supporting geometry around him. LaMelo Ball kept the offense moving with 21 points and 6 assists, spacing the floor so Edwards could attack closeouts instead of inventing every shot against a set defense. Rudy Gobert cleaned the defensive glass (15 rebounds) and let Minnesota run. When those pieces click, Edwards stops being a volume scorer and becomes a wrecking ball: pull-up threes, transition finishes, and enough defensive activity to keep Miami from building easy answers.

Miami had answers of its own, just not enough of them. Giannis Antetokounmpo attacked the paint the way he always has, converting 13-of-19 from the field for 35 points. It was an efficient, physical night that still ended at −11, which tells you how completely Minnesota controlled the other end and the margins. Davion Mitchell created 11 assists, but the Heat never found a consistent way to slow Edwards once the three-ball started raining. A star can score 35 and still lose the plot of a game when the opponent’s star is rewriting every possession.

The Stat: Edwards scored 45 with nine made threes and a double-double in a 25-point road win.

Jerome’s 45 was a takeover, not a cameo

If Edwards’ night was a star doing star things, Jerome’s was the kind of performance that reorders a team’s hierarchy for one evening. In Memphis’s 123–103 win over the Utah Jazz, Jerome shot the Grizzlies into a different gear. Seventeen makes on 25 attempts is not a player who got hot for a quarter; it is a player who stayed aggressive through every defensive adjustment. He added 7 rebounds and 6 assists with only one turnover, which is the quieter part of the story: he was not hunting empty points. He was running the offense while scoring through it.

That is why the final margin looked so decisive. Memphis did not need a late scramble. Jerome kept the attack flowing, Cameron Boozer won the glass and connected the second side of the floor with 11 rebounds and 5 assists, and the Grizzlies steadily pulled away. Utah got a solid creation night from Keyonte George (22 points), but never found a second stopper for Jerome’s shotmaking. When a guard is both finishing at that clip and finding teammates, help defense becomes a coin flip: collapse and give up the kickout, or stay home and watch him cook.

Two players scoring 45 on the same NBA night is rare enough. Doing it in opposite styles (Edwards as gravitational scorer, Jerome as hyper-efficient creator) is what made the slate feel bigger than any single box score.

Davis looked like Washington’s problem-solver

The Washington Wizards beat the Milwaukee Bucks 128–116, and the win had a clear architectural center. Davis did not float on the perimeter waiting for touches. He lived in the paint, on the glass, and in passing lanes: 34 points, 12 rebounds, 4 assists, 2 steals, and a block. Eleven makes at the free-throw line underscored how often Milwaukee had to foul to slow him down. That is the Davis night Washington drafted into its rebuild: a big who forces help, ends possessions, and still creates for others when the defense collapses.

Trae Young completed the partnership in a Trae way. His jumper was cold (4-of-13, including 0-of-6 from three), yet he still dictated the game with 13 assists and constant pressure at the stripe (12-of-16 free throws). The Wizards did not need him to be a pure scorer because Davis was already warping the floor. Young’s job became reading the second and third defenders, and he did it well enough that Milwaukee’s counters never stuck.

Those counters were real. Tyler Herro ran actions and scored 24 with 9 assists, and Jaime Jaquez Jr. attacked efficiently for 22. Milwaukee had enough individual punch to stay in range, but not enough collective resistance around Davis. When a frontcourt star is finishing, rebounding, and generating free throws at that rate, the visiting defense spends the night reacting.

Banchero played a perfect game in an imperfect result

The cruelest performance of the night belonged to Banchero. In Orlando’s 132–125 loss to the Atlanta Hawks, he spent 41 minutes as the adult in the room: 35 points, 7 rebounds, 10 assists, and almost no waste. Ten makes on 12 shots, including 3-of-4 from three, is the kind of night coaches put on a whiteboard. He scored without forcing, passed without padding, and still could not drag the Magic over the line.

That is the gap between a great individual game and a winning team game. Franz Wagner and Wendell Carter Jr. each scored 22, so Orlando was not stranded. Atlanta simply answered with more voices and more chaos. Nickeil Alexander-Walker poured in 31 and kept shooting with confidence. Jalen Johnson impacted every column that matters in a close road win (20 points, 14 rebounds, 6 assists). Dyson Daniels turned defense into offense with 5 steals and 20 points, the kind of activity that undoes even an efficient star by shortening possessions and creating freebies the other way.

Banchero’s night will show up in highlight packages. Atlanta’s will show up in the standings. On a slate full of scoring explosions, the Hawks reminded everyone that shared creation and disruption can beat a masterpiece.

The Takeaway: Banchero’s efficiency was the cleanest individual work of the night; Atlanta’s balance was the better team performance.

Toronto did not need a hero. It needed a standard.

No club owned the evening quite like the Raptors. A 135–92 demolition of the Chicago Bulls at Scotiabank Arena was less about one scorching star and more about a home team deciding, early, that every possession would be uncomfortable. Kawhi Leonard set that tone without needing a huge minute count. In under 24 minutes he scored 23, went a perfect 11-of-11 at the line, and recorded 4 steals and a block. That is a closer’s toolkit used as an opener: pressure the ball, punish contact, leave before the garbage time starts.

Around him, Toronto looked like a team with multiple primary options rather than a hierarchy waiting its turn. Immanuel Quickley pushed pace and spacing for 23 points and 7 assists. Scottie Barnes attacked as a connector and scorer for another 23. RJ Barrett added 20. Four players at 20-plus in a 43-point win is not randomness; it is what happens when the defense collapses on Leonard and the next pass is already live.

Chicago never recovered enough to make it a game. Caleb Wilson competed (16 points, 3 steals) and Matas Buzelis scored 17, but the Bulls spent the night chasing, fouling, and watching Toronto’s lead grow. For a Raptors group built around Leonard’s two-way identity and Barnes’ versatility, nights like this are the proof of concept: overwhelm early, keep the stars fresh, and turn a regular-season date into a statement.

Close games, different kinds of winners

Not every memorable performance came with a blowout soundtrack.

In Brooklyn, the Charlotte Hornets won 110–103 because they found contributions in different shapes. Naz Reid played the connector-big role to perfection: scoring, rebounding, and passing (20/11/7). Grayson Allen stretched the floor with five threes. Moussa Diabate disrupted possessions with 5 steals. The Nets actually got the louder individual scoring nights. Julius Randle bullied his way to 33 points with 8 rebounds and 7 assists, and Michael Porter Jr. added 26 with five threes and active hands on defense. Brooklyn had star production. Charlotte had the better collective answer in the last stretch.

In New Orleans, the Indiana Pacers survived 123–120 in a game that felt like two offenses refusing to blink. Pascal Siakam carried the scoring load with 30, attacking downhill and living midrange to the rim. Tyrese Haliburton orchestrated with 12 assists and enough scoring (23) to keep help honest. Ivica Zubac gave Indiana a physical anchor on the glass (16 and 15). The Pelicans matched the energy: Dejounte Murray mixed creation and defense, Zion Williamson applied constant downhill pressure for 22, and Yves Missi was everywhere around the rim (13 points, 14 rebounds, 5 assists, 2 steals, 3 blocks). Indiana won by the finer margins, not by silencing New Orleans.

On the West Coast, depth and finishing decided the night

Houston beat the Dallas Mavericks 128–110 with the look of a roster that can hurt you from every level. Kevin Durant scored 30 with his usual midrange and free-throw control. Jabari Smith Jr. matched him with another 30, including four threes, the kind of complementary wing scoring that turns a good offense into a problem. Alperen Sengun tied it together inside: scoring, rebounding, and passing at 23/10/5. Dallas still got a fearless night from Cooper Flagg, who scored 32 and filled the defensive box score with 3 steals and a block. It was a debut-season statement in a loss: Flagg competed like a primary option even while Houston’s three-headed attack pulled away.

The Los Angeles Lakers handled the Golden State Warriors 127–105 behind a different kind of partnership. Luka Doncic ran the show (28 points, 8 rebounds, 7 assists, 3 steals), but the finishing identity belonged to Walker Kessler. Twelve makes on 12 shots is not subtle. Kessler dunked and laid in the opportunities Doncic created until Golden State’s rim protection looked helpless. Austin Reaves added 23 and lived at the line, while Stephen Curry scored 23 in a shortened night that never flipped the game’s direction. When your center is perfect from the field and your creator is also stealing possessions, a rivalry game becomes a runway.

Portland’s 124–83 win over the Phoenix Suns had the same blowout DNA as Toronto’s, only with a different closer. Damian Lillard needed fewer than 24 minutes to drop 25 points and six threes. That is a veteran smelling blood: make enough deep shots to break the visitor’s spirit, then sit with the lead secure. Deni Avdija did the connective work (18 and 10), and the Trail Blazers turned Moda Center into a track meet. Devin Booker scored 23 for Phoenix and still finished −43. Individual scoring without defensive resistance is a lonely night.

The Los Angeles Clippers closed the slate by beating the Sacramento Kings 121–96. Darius Garland set the tempo with 30 points and steady creation, while Brook Lopez controlled the interior with 17 and 11. Sacramento’s best players still showed up. Zach LaVine scored 28, and Domantas Sabonis nearly posted a quiet triple-double shape with 14 points, 18 rebounds, and 8 assists on perfect shooting. The problem was everything around them. When the Clippers’ guard creation and size synced, Sacramento’s stars were producing into a deficit that never closed.

What the night really said

Strip away the raw totals and a clearer NBA picture emerges. Edwards and Jerome proved that 45-point nights can arrive as either supernova scoring or surgical creation. Davis showed Washington a two-way blueprint. Banchero reminded everyone that basketball still keeps score as a team sport. Toronto and Portland showed how quickly a roster with multiple scorers and real defensive pressure can turn a regular-season game into a mismatch. SGA, Doncic-to-Kessler, Durant-Smith-Sengun, and Charlotte’s connected win all pointed to the same early-season truth: the performances that linger are the ones that change how the other team has to play.

Twelve games, a handful of blowouts, and a long list of players who refused to be background noise. That is how an NBA night becomes more than a scoreboard.

FAQs

Who stood out most in the NBA last night?

Anthony Edwards and Ty Jerome both scored 45, but in different ways: Edwards as a three-heavy star scorer in Minnesota’s road win at Miami, Jerome as an efficient creator in Memphis’s win over Utah. Anthony Davis, Paolo Banchero, and Toronto’s balanced attack were right behind them.

How did Anthony Davis play for Washington?

Davis anchored the Wizards’ win over Milwaukee with interior scoring, rebounding, and defensive activity, while Trae Young ran the offense even on a cold shooting night.

Why was Paolo Banchero’s game so notable?

He was nearly automatic from the field, facilitated at a high level, and still lost because Atlanta answered with shared scoring and constant steals from Dyson Daniels.

What made the Toronto Raptors’ win impressive?

Toronto beat Chicago by 43 without leaning on one hero. Leonard, Quickley, Barnes, and Barrett all scored 20-plus while Leonard set a defensive tone in limited minutes.

Which other NBA games had big performances?

Houston got 30 each from Kevin Durant and Jabari Smith Jr., the Lakers rode Luka Doncic and a perfect shooting night from Walker Kessler, Portland blew out Phoenix behind Damian Lillard’s threes, and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander owned Opening Night at both ends for Oklahoma City.
