Lamont, Fazio face off in first televised debate

TheTools · 21.10.2026 19:49 · source · permalink

HARTFORD, Conn. (WFSB) - Gov. Ned Lamont and Republican challenger Sen. Ryan Fazio met Thursday night for their first televised debate, hosted by WFSB and CT Insider.

Lamont and Fazio faced off at 7 p.m. on WFSB and streaming on WFSB+.

The debate came just days after a new poll from Sacred Heart University, WFSB and CT Insider showed Lamont with a growing lead.

November’s election is less than two weeks away, and the first votes had already been cast across Connecticut.

While the debate was civil, Lamont and Fazio did trade the occasional jab.

“Nobody supported your bill. You gotta get things done,” said Lamont.

“Governor Lamont’s living in an entirely different state than the people I talk to on the campaign trail,” said Fazio.

Panelists Susan Raff and Dan Haar pushed the two candidates on Connecticut’s most important and serious topics, including affordability and scandals in the state run colleges and universities.

“Governor Lamont did call the CT ‘Corrupticut’ when he first ran for office. The problem is 8 years later corruption has flourished under his watch,” added Fazio.

In the post debate scrums, the governor continued to be questioned about Kosta Diamantis, the former state official under the governor and current federal fugitive that was convicted of corruption.

Fazio said Connecticut is more expensive than ever and that Lamont had eight years to fix issues in the state and has failed. Fazio promised the largest middle class tax cut ever.

“I would eliminate all those hidden taxes and fees, cut electric bills by 20 percent to make the state affordable,” said Fazio.

Lamont believes he has made progress, including eight balanced budgets in a row.

“Politicians love to make promises they can’t keep. You’re not eliminating the public benefits charge, Ryan. All you’re doing is shifting it over to the backs of the taxpayers. That’s no way to get it done,” Lamont added.

He claimed the heart and soul of the country is on the ballot box this year and took shots at President Trump’s tariffs and the war in Iran.

“We’ve gone from one of the slowest growing economies in the country to one of the fastest. You know what that means? That means jobs and opportunity,” Lamont said.

Fazio called out Lamont’s age and experience as well.

“I respect his public service, but he’s had his chance. The first time he ran for office was the year I was born, the office I currently hold. He’s been in politics for decades and after 8 years CT is more expensive than it’s ever been,” he said.

The debate ended with a handshake, and now it is up to the voters.
