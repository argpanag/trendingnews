Winter Wonderland Hyde Park London Review — Theatre and Tonic

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:13 · source · permalink

We held two thick glasses, carved entirely out of solid ice, in our gloved hands and raised them to our lips to sip our cocktails. My friend and I exchanged surprised glances. A wave of cold swept through us, leaving us feeling almost instantly more alert. The refreshing chill somehow made the cocktails taste even better. After a few more sips, a pleasant warmth began to spread through our throats.

At minus 10°C, this little bar, Bar Ice, is one of the attractions at Hyde Park Winter Wonderland. One of London's most iconic Christmas events, Winter Wonderland is also one of the UK's largest winter festivals. Far more than just a Christmas market, it is a temporary festive wonderland bringing together a theme park, Christmas markets, an icy kingdom, live entertainment, street food and bars. I was fortunate enough to be invited along with a friend for an early preview of this year's event.

Following the Christmas trees and reindeer scrolling across the screens, we made our way into Winter Wonderland. Here, the Christmas trees seemed almost understated compared with the light displays. Everywhere we looked, lights in every colour imaginable illuminated the surroundings, creating a world quite unlike the relatively dimly lit streets beyond central London. The sight of giant plush toys hanging above the carnival game stalls, combined with the tempting aromas drifting from the street food stands, made it almost impossible not to feel cheerful as we wandered beneath the lights. Seeing so many other visitors carrying the prizes they had won at the carnival games also put us at ease. Perhaps these little games weren't quite as fiendishly difficult as they looked!

The thrill rides were equally hard to miss. As we made our way through the grounds, it never seemed to be long before we came across another roller coaster, swing ride, giant pendulum or haunted house, alongside gentler attractions such as the traditional carousel. Beyond the rides, visitors can also enjoy ice skating at the outdoor rink, as well as performances by the Gandeys K-Pop Dragon Circus at scheduled showtimes. My friend and I also climbed aboard the Giant Wheel, which offered an effortless way to take in London's nighttime skyline and the illuminated grounds of Winter Wonderland below.

It was thanks to the bird's-eye view from the Giant Wheel that we managed to locate Neverland, a fairytale-inspired ice sculpture attraction within the Magical Ice Kingdom. Without the coats provided at Bar Ice, the cold here felt even more intense. Yet the ice sculptures looked as though they had been carved straight out of a fairytale. Even as our hands grew so cold that we could barely feel our fingers, we simply couldn't resist taking photo after photo. Once we stepped out of Neverland, a hot chocolate from Café Bavaria was exactly what we needed. The hot dogs, chips and pretzels were all delicious, too. We enjoyed our food and drinks while listening to live singers performing on the nearby stage.

Winter Wonderland was even bigger than I had imagined. There was so much to see and do that we simply couldn't explore everything properly, and it was surprisingly easy to lose our sense of direction as we explored the grounds. Fortunately, the rain held off until the very moment the lights began to go out and we were getting ready to leave. We walked out through the gingerbread-house-like entrance and headed down into the Underground, almost as though we were stepping back into the familiar London of everyday life.

As I watched my friend walk through the station gates, clutching the giant snowflake prize she had won at one of the basketball stalls, I couldn't help thinking how wonderful it was to have a little world like this in the middle of London's winter – a place where, for a few hours, you could escape the noise and bustle of everyday life and simply enjoy a little festive magic.

Winter Wonderland takes place in Hyde Park until 3rd January 2027.

★★★★

 Written by Ziwen for Theatre and Tonic, November 2026.
