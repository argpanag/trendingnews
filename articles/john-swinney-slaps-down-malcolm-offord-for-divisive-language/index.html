John Swinney slaps down Malcolm Offord for ‘divisive language’ during FMQs

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:34 · source · permalink

FIRST Minister John Swinney has called out Malcolm Offord’s “divisive language” in Parliament today.

In First Ministers Questions on Thursday the Reform UK Scottish leader Offord asked Swinney: “Màiri McAllan says the answer to youth unemployment is to rejoin the EU. Really?

“To allow a flood of 100,000 young EU migrants to gobble up the vacancies that our own young people need.

“Presiding Officer, why would the First Minister allow outsiders to take the starter jobs away from our own young Scots?”

In response Swinney said this was a “classic example” Reform’s divisive language in their political discourse.

Swinney said: “Malcolm Offord and his colleagues need to think long and hard about the way in which they raise these issues and how they spread division within our country.”

He continued: “Anyone looking at Scotland today can only come to the conclusion that Scotland has benefited from being an open, outward-looking society, and I'm determined to make sure that always remains the case.”

The First Minister concluded that maintaining Scotland’s reputation as a welcoming country means him not engaging with Reform UK.

Offord came back to Swinney with an offer to help young Scots back to work "because we care a lot about them.

"Will he First Minister meet with me to discuss these ideas together?"

Swinney replied: "I've explained the position my government is going to take. We are not going to seek collaboration with the Reform group in the Scottish Parliament.

"That is a political decision that I have made because I will not associate my party and my government with the divisive ideas that are put forward by Malcolm Offord and Reform.

"Now I appreciate that will not be universally popular amongst members of Reform, but I have a duty to the people that I represent and to Scotland to make sure that I do everything I can to maintain Scotland's position as a welcoming, inclusive country, and part of that means not engaging with Reform UK."

Scottish Labour

Earlier, Scottish Labour leader Michael Marra asked the First Minister about NHS Grampian, after a major review raised serious concerns this week over patient safety and clinical governance.

Marra said: “This must not be a repeat of the government's approach to the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital or Eljamel in Tayside, to be dragged kicking and screaming towards justice.

“Families have waited years for answers. Will the First Minister deliver them?”

Swinney replied: “In all of the cases of correspondence coming into government from Dr Habib there have been appropriate responses issued to ensure that the necessary action could be pursued in relation to these questions, and the advice offered in relation to evidence that could be provided, for example, to the police was given, or advice being given about the support of the National Whistleblowing Service - which is a function that operates independently of government - to ensure that these issues are properly and fully challenged.”

Scottish Labour leader Michael Marra said: “Families have waited years for answers." (Image: Stefan Rousseau / PA)

He added: “The government has made clear its dissatisfaction with the conduct of NHS Grampian, which is why the significant changes in leadership have been required".

Scottish Greens

Scottish Greens co-leader Gillian Mackay asked Swinney about a series of AI-generated GTA “memes”, which the SNP poster but subsequently deleted.

In a now-deleted post, the SNP showed former Conservative leaders Boris Johnson and Margert Thatcher in the style of the game's famous loading screen along with the caption “Grand Theft Alba”. 

However, the posts were short-lived on the party’s social media after hundreds of people called them out over the use of AI. 

Mackay said: “Last night, the SNP angered anti-AI campaigners by posting AI generated GTA memes on its social media, despite huge concerns about the impact of AI and AI data centres on artists, workers and the environment.

“Does the First Minister understand why people are concerned about his government's approach to AI and the big companies behind it?”

In reply, Swinney said: “Well, first of all let me say, I don't want to offend anyone by anything that we get up to, so if we've offended anybody about that, I'm sorry about that. It's not my intention.

“My second point is that artificial intelligence – and I understand the concerns about AI and I share concerns about AI because we are all working our way through significant industrial changes and challenges that are happening before our eyes, so I'm acutely alive to that unease – but equally, I see in a range of different areas and sometimes in our healthcare services the use of AI, which are advancing.

“The ability to identify the risk of cancer to which members of the public are exposed, and that's by AI applications being applied to routine healthcare treatments and assessments that are made.

“So there are advantages that may improve clinical practice as a consequence of AI that we have to consider.

Scottish Green co-leader Gillian Mackay said: "The SNP angered anti-AI campaigners by posting AI generated GTA memes on its social media." (Image: Holyrood TV)

“We have to be alert to the risks and that is what the government will consider as part of the issues around about AI data centres because there will be.

“There will be risks that will come with the development of AI data centres, and those issues will be fully explored in the guidance that we set out.

“And then in broader and wider policy there will of course be substantial issues that have got to be addressed and considered, and the government will do exactly.”

Scottish Conservatives

Scottish Conservative and Unionist Party leader Russell Findlay asked: “Countless patients suffered avoidable deaths at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary. If John Swinney requires surgery there tomorrow, would he have any concerns?”

Swinney responded: “I wouldn't have concerns because we have clinical professionals who are undertaking good work in the National Health Service across the country.

“I think we should have confidence in them to undertake their roles, which they carry significant responsibility in relation to taking forward, and I would encourage people to be confident in the delivery of NHS services in Scotland, and specifically in NHS Grampian.”

Following up, Findlay said: “Patients and their families across North East Scotland are deeply concerned. ARI whistleblowers have been raising the alarm about avoidable patient deaths since 2014 at least.”

Finlay added: “They were ignored by John Swinney's government. Why?"

The First Minister replied: “On a number of occasions, over a number of years where concerns have been expressed, they have been pursued by NHS Grampian and by a range of external bodies.

“I appreciate and acknowledge the depth of concern there is today about the situation in NHS Grampian, and what I said to Parliament last week, and what the Health Secretary said to Parliament on Tuesday, is designed to put in place the steps that are necessary to ensure patient confidence can be at its highest level.

“I give Parliament the assurance that the mechanisms we've put in place and the individuals we've put in place have an obligation to ensure that is the case, and the government will give everything that we need to give to ensure that confidence can be assured.”

Scottish Liberal Democrats

Scottish Liberal Democrat leader Alex Cole-Hamilton asked Swinney about what he described as the “Vision IT scandal”.

Cole-Hamilton said that the faulty IT system used by GPs is silently changing medication dosages on prescriptions and preventing patients from booking appointments.

The Liberal Democrat leader asked: “So given all these issues, why is the First Minister still forcing this system a system that is riddled with bugs onto our GPs?”

In response, the First Minister said that due to changes in the marketplace Vision IT is the only system that is being provided to GP practices.

Swinney said: "The existing system is coming to the end of its life"
