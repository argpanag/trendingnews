Mara Lejins Calls Her ‘Smiling Assassin’ a ‘Morally Complex’ Murderer — JournalArta Global

TheTools · 18.11.2026 12:48 · source · permalink

Mara Lejins, the trivia expert known as the Smiling Assassin on The Chase Australia, says eliminating contestants can be the hardest part of her job. In an interview, the 31-year-old lawyer also recalled surviving a skiing accident that left her trapped upside down beneath the snow.

Mara Lejins, the lawyer and trivia expert known as the Smiling Assassin on The Chase Australia, described her quiz-show persona as a “morally complex murderer” in an interview published Wednesday, November 18, 2026. TV’s smiling assassin admits eliminating contestants can be the hardest part of her job.

Lejins, 31, said her competitive drive takes over when she faces contestants. “It’s quite sad when you eliminate contestants,” she said, “but you can’t just throw a game.”

One contestant she remembered was a foster mother hoping to pay for an interstate holiday for the children in her care. Lejins called her “salt of the earth” and said she was doing one of society’s most important jobs.

A skiing accident beneath the snow

The interview also turned to an accident Lejins described as a near-death experience. While skiing off-piste in heavy snow, she said, she went into a valley, flipped and ended up hanging upside down in a creek hole below the snow line.

One arm was pinned as snow fell around her, leaving her unable to breathe. Her father, who was skiing behind her, helped her out.

“I remember thinking, ‘I’ve had a really nice life,’” Lejins said. The experience made her less afraid of dying, she added.

From quiz contestant to trivia expert

Lejins first appeared as a contestant on the spin-off Beat the Chasers in 2021. She said she learned on the same day that she had secured a federal prosecutor’s job and won 60 grand on the show.

She used much of the money to pay tuition fees at Columbia, where she completed a master’s of laws in New York after taking leave from her job. She also bought a laptop and a championship ring.

Lejins said she applies a version of the Golden Rule when competing on television: “How would I want to be treated if I was a contestant? What level of respect would I want?”

Despite her confident on-screen presence, she said she is not especially confident. “I don’t think it’s wrong to have doubt,” Lejins said. “Doubt is a powerful part of being a human.”
