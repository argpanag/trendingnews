Remembering Freddie Mercury: The Best Vocalist of All Time Who Died 35 Years Ago

TheTools · 25.11.2026 12:19 · source · permalink

Thirty-five years ago today, on November 24, 1991, the music world lost one of its most extraordinary voices when Queen frontman Freddie Mercury died at the age of 45.

Just one day earlier, the famously private singer had publicly confirmed that he had AIDS. According to the History Channel, Mercury died of bronchial pneumonia resulting from the disease at his home in Kensington, London.

Three and a half decades later, his remarkable voice, theatrical showmanship and songwriting continue to earn him accolades, including a particularly meaningful honor in 2026.

Music publication Consequence named Mercury the greatest vocalist of all time in a ranking compiled with input from 50 musicians and the publication's editorial staff.

Freddie Mercury Continues to Inspire Generations of Singers

Among the musicians who championed Mercury in Consequence's survey was Myles Kennedy of Alter Bridge and Slash & The Conspirators.

"He had it all: Charisma, power, range, and above all, an incredible songwriter and performer whose legacy will resonate for generations to come," Kennedy said.

Alissa White-Gluz of Blue Medusa shared a particularly personal connection to Mercury's celebrated performance at Live Aid in 1985.

"My mother was very pregnant with me when that 1985 live aid concert happened and she watched it. And I think I was listening as well at that point. And maybe that inspired me from within the womb," White-Gluz said.

Cody Bowles of Crown Lands credited Mercury with inspiring his own career, saying, "He is the reason I wanted to do what I do today as a singer, and he remains my vocal north star."

It wasn't the first time Mercury had topped such a ranking. In 2016, LA Weekly named him the greatest singer of all time across all musical genres, while Billboard ranked him second only to Bruce Springsteen on its 2015 list of the greatest rock frontmen and women.

Queen's 20-Minute Performance Was Named the World's Greatest Rock Gig

Born Farrokh Bulsara in Zanzibar in 1946, Mercury spent much of his childhood in India before his family relocated to England in 1964.

He formed Queen in 1970 with guitarist Brian May and drummer Roger Taylor, with bassist John Deacon completing the lineup the following year.

In addition to possessing a remarkable vocal range, Mercury wrote 10 of the 17 songs featured on Queen's Greatest Hits album, including "Bohemian Rhapsody," "Somebody to Love," "We Are the Champions," "Don't Stop Me Now" and "Crazy Little Thing Called Love."

Perhaps the most celebrated demonstration of his abilities came on July 13, 1985, when Queen took the stage at London's Wembley Stadium for Live Aid.

In 2005, the BBC reported that a survey of more than 60 musicians, journalists and music industry executives had named Queen's approximately 20-minute set the world's greatest rock gig, ahead of Jimi Hendrix at Woodstock in 1969 and the Sex Pistols in Manchester in 1976.

Mercury's final concert with Queen took place just over a year after Live Aid, on August 9, 1986, at Knebworth Park in England. He continued recording with his bandmates during his illness, and his final vocal recordings appeared on Queen's 1995 album Made in Heaven.

In April 1992, the surviving members of Queen organized a tribute concert at Wembley Stadium to celebrate Mercury's life and raise awareness of AIDS. Among the performers were Elton John, David Bowie, George Michael, Annie Lennox and Robert Plant.

Queen was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001, followed by the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2003. The band received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2018.

But perhaps the most enduring testament to Mercury's legacy is that musicians who were not even born when he delivered that unforgettable Live Aid performance still cite him as the reason they wanted to sing.
