Knicks Notes: Third Center, Dadiet, Rotation, Second Apron

TheTools · 14.10.2026 01:04 · source · permalink

As James Wiseman, Drew Eubanks, and Tony Bradley continue to battle to become the third center on the Knicks’ roster behind Karl-Anthony Towns and Andre Drummond, head coach Mike Brown told reporters on Monday that deciding which player to retain will be difficult, per Kristian Winfield of The New York Daily News.

“They should all be on a roster this year,” Brown said. “There’s no experiments in my opinion. These guys are all legitimate NBA players. They’re all great guys and in some way, shape or form, I can see each of them contributing this way or that way and it’s not gonna be an easy decision.”

Wiseman, Eubanks, and Bradley are all on Exhibit 9 contracts that would convert to standard, non-guaranteed minimum-salary contracts if they make the opening night roster. Technically, the Knicks, who are carrying 13 players on guaranteed contracts, have the ability to keep two of them without making any corresponding transactions, but doing so would push the team’s salary above the second tax apron, which team owner James Dolan has said won’t happen. So, in all likelihood, just one of the three will make the team, and that one spot remains very much up for grabs.

“I’ve been in a lot of training camps and collectively, this is probably — now when I say the best, I’m not saying the most talented — this is the best group of centers I’ve been around,” Brown said. “All of them bring something different to the table.”

We have more on the Knicks:

  • Pacome Dadiet, the 25th overall pick in the 2024 draft, has logged just 247 total minutes since entering the NBA two years ago. If he’s going to play an increased role in 2026/27, the third-year French forward has to be willing to fire away when he gets an open look from beyond the arc, Brown said this weekend (story via Winfield). “The first one is obviously he shoots it, and so he can’t be afraid to let it fly,” the Knicks’ coach said. “He’s got to get his body set, whatever, right away, and as soon as that thing hits his hands, if he has any space at all, at 6-foot-7 or 6-foot-8, whatever he is — boom, let it fly. Have confidence in that.” As Winfield writes, Dadiet was billed as a solid three-point shooter ahead of the draft, but he has converted just 13-of-51 (25.5%) attempts since joining the Knicks.
  • James L. Edwards III of The Athletic previews the Knicks’ potential regular season rotation, predicting that second-year forward Mohamed Diawara will join Jose Alvarado, Landry Shamet, Miles McBride, and Drummond as regulars behind the usual five starters. Edwards also speculates that Shamet will play more minutes than McBride and says he still views Wiseman as the frontrunner for the third center spot.
  • Fred Katz of The Athletic explores how taking a hard-line stance of never crossing the second-apron threshold could impact the Knicks going forward. As Katz points out, treating the second apron as a de facto hard cap and operating right up against it means that the team is already facing some of the same restrictions that a club operating over the second apron would, including not practically being able to use the taxpayer mid-level exception.
