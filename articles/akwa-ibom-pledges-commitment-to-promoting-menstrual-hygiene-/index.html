Akwa Ibom pledges commitment to promoting menstrual hygiene education among adolescent girls, young women - Businessday NG

The Akwa State government says it is committed to the promotion of menstrual health and hygiene by increasing access to comprehensive information and affordable menstrual health products in the state.

Experts say 23% of Nigerian girls cite menstruation for missing school, fear of stain ridicule and no pads as the key drivers while 56% of girls in the country has no pre-menarche education, causing distress and practices reusing cloth rags. Many girls and women can not manage menstruation well and are being held back by gaps in sanitation, healthcare, and by stigma, experts say.

Inibehe Silas Etukudoh, Akwa Ibom State commissioner for Women affairs and social welfare gave the pledge at the close-out ceremony of the Menstrual Health No Wahala (MH-NOW) project in Uyo, the state capital backed by Population Services International Nigeria .

Etukudoh said the state government, with support from PSI Nigeria has trained peer educators and promoted access to Water And Sanitation Hygiene (WASH) programmes as well as encouraged open conversation on menstrual hygiene among girls and women in the state.

According to her, “We have produced and distributed affordable menstrual products, reduced period poverty in rural communities and partnered with schools for menstrual hygiene education,’’ in the state while also promoting sustainable attitude to disposable pads and improved menstrual dignity and confidence.

She thanked PSI Nigeria for the support extended to the state government which made it possible for Akwa Ibom to be the only state in the south south region of the country to be selected for the menstrual health intervention project.

In his keynote address, Pharm. Effiom Effiom, the Country Representative of PSI Nigeria, said MH-NOW began with a simple recognition that knowledge, access and policy must work together, girls need accurate information and a safe place to ask questions explaining that women and girls need affordable products close to home and government systems must be ready to carry the work forward”.

“Today is not an ening, it is a handover. I respectfully urge the state government to protect a dedicated menstrual health budget line to keep the Technical Working Group as the home of this agenda and to deliver menstrual health through schools, primary healthcare and supportive supervision,’’ he said.

Giving a breakdown of the activities, Godwin Udoh, the state programme Officer of PSI Nigeria said the intervention covered three local government areas of the state which included Ikot Ekpene, Nsit Ubium and Esit Eket adding that while the project started in August 2023 at the Country level, it began in Akwa Ibom in January 2025 and ended in July 2026 but with no cost extension to November 2026.

“The MH-NOW worked across Esit Eket, NsitUbium and Ikot Ekpene Local Government Areas through schools, Primary Healthcare Centres, and communities to shift knowledge, behaviour and access and systems around menstrual health. The project addressed stigma and misinformation, promoted menstrual health education in schools and communities , and strengthened provider counselling skills,’’ he said.

He said 41 women producers and distributors were reached, 6,093 MH products were distributed, and N7.31m income was generated by women entrepreneurs, while a total.of 60 MH Champions and 12 MH advocates were engaged in the state.

According to him, 2,501 peer educators in schools were reached, 28,257 girls reached through schools , 11,774 women and girls reached in communities, and 610 men and boys also reached.

On the economic empowerment side, he said 41 women producers and distributors including impact pad operated under the project , distributed 6,093 menstrual health products and earned N7.3m from locally made products demonstrating that a sustainable market for menstrual health was possible at the state level.

Under coordination and policy guidelines development, the project has established and trained MH Technical Working Group while the Ministries of Women Affairs and Social Welfare and Ministry of Health have made provisions for MH programmes and services in the 2927 appropriation bill.

The close-out ceremony which attracted participants from various ministries, department agencies including the state ministry of health, education, schools and top government officials also saw the presentation of awards to those who distinguished themselves while participating in the intervention project.
