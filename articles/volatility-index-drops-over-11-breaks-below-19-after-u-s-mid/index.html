Volatility Index drops over 11%, breaks below 19 after U.S. midterm elections

TheTools · 04.11.2026 23:27 · source · permalink

The Cboe Volatility Index VIX, Wall Street's widely followed fear gauge, fell over 11% on Wednesday, slipping below 19 as investors reassessed market risks following the U.S. midterm elections.

The index traded at 18.72, down nearly 11.4%, at the time of publishing, extending its retreat after trading close to 24 on October 30.

The pullback followed a surge in short-term volatility pricing ahead of the vote, as options markets reflected demand for protection against potential market swings around the election results.

The easing in the VIX came as investors assessed the implications of Democrats securing control of both chambers of Congress. The election outcome has raised questions about the outlook for President Donald Trump's policy agenda, including fiscal spending and potential regulatory changes affecting the technology sector.

The VIX tracks expected 30-day S&P 500 volatility based on options prices, rising when demand for market protection increases and falling as volatility expectations ease.
