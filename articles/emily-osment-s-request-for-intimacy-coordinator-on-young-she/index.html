Emily Osment's Request for Intimacy Coordinator on 'Young Sheldon' Sparks Support from Fans - AOL

TheTools · 27.10.2026 14:12 · source · permalink

Fans are applauding Emily Osment for speaking up about an uncomfortable situation on the set of Young Sheldon, with viewers calling her decision to request an intimacy coordinator an example of Hollywood professionalism.

Osment, who played Mandy McAllister on the CBS sitcom, recently revealed that she requested an intimacy coordinator because of her concerns about filming kissing scenes with co-star Montana Jordan. At the time, Jordan, who played George Cooper, was 18, while Osment was 30.

The actress opened up about the experience in Jessica Radloff’s new book, Young Sheldon: The (Juicier-Than-Meemaw’s-Brisket) Inside Story of the Hit Comedy, explaining why she felt the additional support was important.

And on Threads, fans largely agreed with her decision.

“This is what a responsible actor and adult looks like in Hollywood,” one commenter wrote, while another declared, “100% correct to speak up.”

Others echoed that sentiment, with one person summing up the situation in two words: “Called professionalism.”

“Seems like a logical request,” another commenter added, while others offered their own straightforward endorsements. “Good for her,” one person wrote, and another insisted, “Always speak up.”

Not everyone agreed with the criticism surrounding the situation. One commenter pushed back against the suggestion that Jordan’s age alone made the request necessary, writing, “Nah he was an adult.”

Still, the prevailing sentiment in the discussion was supportive of Osment’s decision to make both actors more comfortable while filming intimate scenes.

In Radloff’s book, Osment explained that she felt uncomfortable because Jordan was so young. “That made me uncomfortable. He was a baby! A baby with a mullet,” she recalled.

Osment said an intimacy coordinator was not available that day, but one was brought in for subsequent episodes as the characters’ relationship developed.

Director Alex Reid also admitted the production had “totally dropped the ball” by not having an intimacy coordinator for the kissing scenes. Co-creator Steve Molaro agreed that Osment had “every right to ask” for one.

Now, with George and Mandy starring in their own spinoff, Georgie & Mandy’s First Marriage, Osment’s comments have reignited conversation about boundaries and professionalism on set.
