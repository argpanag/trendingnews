Former NHLer Craig Anderson says his game slipped following release of GTA 5

TheTools · 18.11.2026 21:15 · source · permalink

Grand Theft Auto IV (GTA 6), the long-awaited next installment in the ultra-popular video game franchise, is finally set to be released on Thursday. While you wouldn’t think the release of a new video game would have any effect on an NHL season, former NHL goaltender Craig Anderson is here to tell you otherwise.

On Wednesday’s episode of the Absolute Non-Sens podcast, Anderson revealed that the release of GTA 5 directly led to a drop-off in his play back in 2013 while with the Ottawa Senators.

“My worst season that I felt I had in Ottawa, was the year that ‘Grand Theft Auto V’ came out,” Anderson said. “Direct correlation. Just a time suck. You’d play after a game, you’d get home 11-11:30, you’d start playing that game and it’d be 4 AM and you’d be like ‘I’m not even tired’. Now Grand Theft Auto VI is coming out so it’ll be interesting to see how that all plays out.”

While the 2013-14 season wasn’t actually Anderson’s worst with the Senators, as his play tailed off pretty dramatically in his age-36 season of 2017-18, his numbers that year do stand out as noticeably worse than those from the seasons surrounding it, lending credence to his GTA 5 theory. In the 2012-13 season, the Park Ridge, Illinois native led the league with a .941 save percentage and a 1.68 goals-against average, finishing fourth in voting for the Vezina Trophy. In 2013-14, he dipped to a .911 SV% and a 3.00 GAA, before bouncing back to a .923 SV% and a 2.49 GAA in 2014-15.

With GTA 6 on the verge of coming out, NHL teams are probably advising players to limit their time with the game to avoid an Anderson-esque tail-off in their play for the rest of the season.

It’s clear, though, that Anderson has a special affinity for video games, as he also desribed his love of playing car racing simulators.

“The racing simulator thing was was something that I had passion for,” Anderson said. “It translates directly into real race race cars, real driving on track. I felt like I had it under control where you could spend a little bit of time on it, but then there was like a stopping point… But it was a way to kind of take your mind away from the game and do something competitive.”
