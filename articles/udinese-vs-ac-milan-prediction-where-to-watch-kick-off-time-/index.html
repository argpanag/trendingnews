Udinese vs. AC Milan - Head-to-head record

The historical rivalry between these two traditional clubs spans 107 official fixtures, with Milan claiming 47 victories to Udinese’s 24, alongside 36 draws.

Recent meetings in Friuli have belonged squarely to the visitors, with Milan winning their last three Serie A trips to the Bluenergy Stadium by a combined 10-2 scoreline.

However, Udinese stunned the Rossoneri with a 3-0 triumph at San Siro in their most recent encounter back in April.

Stalemates have been virtually nonexistent in recent years, with none of the last eight league fixtures between the sides ending level and the away team winning five of the last six.

Given Udinese’s porous backline and Milan’s desire to keep pace with the top three, Sunday’s clash should deliver another decisive, action-packed contest.

Udinese - Struggling for form

Kosta Runjaic’s side enter Sunday night under intense scrutiny after a string of passive displays left them hovering just above the relegation zone. Below is an overview of the hosts’ current form, defensive concerns, and projected starting XI:

Recent form

The Little Zebras sit 16th on four points, with five consecutive league defeats representing their worst top-flight slide since April 2018.

The Bluenergy Stadium has offered little comfort this term, yielding just a single point from four matches. More concerning is Udinese’s tendency to unravel as matches progress: Runjaic’s men hold the worst second-half goal difference in the division (-8), having found the net only twice after the interval all season.

Stopping that slump requires tightening up a leaky backline that has shipped 16 league goals. Maduka Okoye will start in goal behind a three-man defense featuring Oumar Solet, Enzo Ebosse, and Nicolò Bertola, with veteran David Alaba sidelined after suffering a calf strain on his full debut.

Flank protection comes from Mërgim Vojvoda and Hassane Kamara, while Jesper Karlström and Jakub Piotrowski provide the physical screen in central midfield.

In the attacking third, Dutch midfielder Jurgen Ekkelenkamp represents the primary danger man with three goals this term, linking up with Giorgi Chakvetadze to feed target man Keinan Davis, particularly with attackers Idrissa Gueye (ankle) and Vakoun Bayo (muscle) still out of action.

The hosts do welcome back Italian international Nicolò Zaniolo, who is available again following a thigh injury.

AC Milan - Enjoying a good run

Rúben Amorim takes his Milan squad northeast looking to maintain their unbeaten domestic record and erase the memory of Thursday’s 3-0 European collapse on the south coast of England. Here is an examination of the visitors’ tactical setup, attacking firepower, and expected starting lineup ahead of kickoff:

Recent form

The Rossoneri occupy fourth place on 15 points, having navigated their first seven league games without defeat (four wins and three draws).

While their home form has been electric, Amorim’s side have developed a habit of sharing the points on their travels, drawing three consecutive away matches against Sassuolo, Liverpool, and Lecce.

Nonetheless, Milan remain notoriously stubborn on the road in league play, having suffered just three Serie A away defeats since the start of last season.

Amorim’s plans receive a significant boost with club-record signing Gonçalo Ramos declared fit to start after missing the trip to England with a minor calf issue.

The Portuguese striker will lead the line supported by Christian Pulisic and in-form midfielder Adrien Rabiot, who leads the squad with four goals and three assists.

Milan must manage without injured wingers Samuel Chukwueze (hip) and Alexis Saelemaekers (ankle), as well as Ruben Loftus-Cheek. In midfield, veteran Luka Modrić orchestrates tempo alongside the athleticism of Yunus Musah, with Diego Moreira and Pervis Estupiñán providing width.

At the back, goalkeeper Mike Maignan retains the gloves as his European red card does not carry over to Serie A, protected by a back three of Koni De Winter, Strahinja Pavlović, and Mario Gila, with Mattia Gabbia out through a tendon complaint.

Amorim’s men will want to respond decisively after Thursday’s European slip-up, and their track record in Udine is exceptional, having won their last three visits by a 10-2 aggregate scoreline.
