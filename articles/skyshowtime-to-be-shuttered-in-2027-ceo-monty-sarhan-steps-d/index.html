SkyShowtime to be shuttered in 2027, CEO Monty Sarhan steps down

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:21 · source · permalink

Pan-European VoD service SkyShowtime will be shuttered next year after the company’s board confirmed the news in a memo to staff, citing the “competitive dynamics” of the global streaming landscape.

Monty Sarhan

CEO Monty Sarhan is leaving the company, with current chief financial officer Mark Neil taking over as interim general manager to oversee the wind-down period.

The future of the Amsterdam- and London-based company had been uncertain for some time, with rumours swirling that Paramount bosses were unhappy the OTT service had not yet turned a profit.

Staff had feared the worst since September, when the board contacted Sarhan to inform him it was initiating a strategic review of the streamer, which is a joint venture between Comcast and Skydance-owned Paramount.

That communication warned that closing SkyShowtime was one of the options being considered – an outcome now confirmed by the board.

The board’s memo said: “Despite the excellent work and dedication of this team, the shareholders have determined that winding down the business is the most appropriate path forward.

“We are committed to supporting everyone at SkyShowtime throughout this process and sharing information as decisions are implemented.

“In the meantime, SkyShowtime continues to operate as normal for our members. Employee consultations remain ongoing in accordance with local requirements, and timing and process may vary by country.

Mark Neil

“We want to thank each of you for your hard work, professionalism and commitment to SkyShowtime and its members. We are grateful for your contributions and will keep you informed as we move forward.”

SkyShowtime was rolled out in late 2022 following a lavish launch party in Amsterdam. Now active in 22 territories, the SVoD service boasts content from the combined catalogues of Universal Pictures, Nickelodeon, Paramount Pictures, DreamWorks Animation, Paramount+, Showtime, Sky Studios and Peacock.

The platform has also been active in commissioning original programming in markets such as Poland, Sweden and Spain.

However, it has been plagued by rumours that it is not making money and that Paramount would pull the plug on the service to instead focus on the complex integration of Paramount+ and HBO Max.

SkyShowtime will be wound down towards the end of 2027. The company employs around 150 employees in London and a further 50 in its Amsterdam office.

The board’s memo this week suggested that, as a solely European platform, SkyShowtime had struggled to amass the kind of scale and reach it needed to compete with major global streamers.

“Since SkyShowtime’s launch, the streaming landscape has evolved significantly,” the memo read. “Major global platforms have continued to expand their scale and invest heavily in content, technology and sport.

“These competitive dynamics have made it increasingly challenging for regional players like SkyShowtime to achieve the scale needed for long-term success.”
