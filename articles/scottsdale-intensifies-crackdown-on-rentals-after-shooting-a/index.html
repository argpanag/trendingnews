Scottsdale intensifies crackdown on rentals after shooting at Airbnb property

TheTools · 13.10.2026 19:47 · source · permalink

SCOTTSDALE, AZ (AZFamily) — Scottsdale police are stepping up pressure on problem short-term rentals after gunfire broke out at a weekend party, rattling neighbors who say they’ve dealt with this kind of trouble before.

Residents reported hearing more than 40 shots during the party at an Airbnb property near Scottsdale and McKellips roads early Saturday morning.

No one was hurt, and no suspects have been identified, but detectives are chasing several leads. The city is also looking into whether the rental broke any local rules.

Police called the shooting “extremely concerning” and said it’s exactly why they’ve been ramping up pressure on short-term rental owners and renters.

A citywide crackdown

Scottsdale has put together a multi-agency “Short-Term Rental Team” — including police, the city attorney’s office, code enforcement, planning, water services and other departments — to hunt down “problem properties” and enforce the rules.

Police also launched a response unit last month, dedicated to short-term rental violations, nuisance properties and the kind of recurring complaints that wear neighborhoods down. The unit works with other city departments to coordinate enforcement.

The city says its approach centers on four things:

  • Enforcement — going after nuisance properties, criminal activity and ordinance violations
  • Education — teaching residents how to report problems and making sure owners and renters know their responsibilities
  • Partnerships — working with property owners, rental platforms and community groups on compliance
  • Data — using technology to flag violations and track enforcement

By the numbers

As of August, Scottsdale had nearly 3,000 active short-term rental licenses.

Police responded to 665 calls at short-term rentals from January through August, with more than a third occurring during the summer, according to city data.

There have been 460 charges filed at short-term rentals -- with the majority of them being for noncompliance. Noncompliance charges rose 46%, from 120 in spring to 175 in summer.

Police calls also dropped 23% from July to August. The city collected $9,000 in penalty fines in the month of August alone.

What’s next

According to the city, it is also working with rental platforms, pushing for legislative changes that would give cities more power to regulate short-term rentals and tightening licensing requirements.

As for the shooting itself, police say the investigation isn’t going anywhere.

“We have a proven track record of identifying and arresting those who come into Scottsdale and commit violent crimes. We intend to make this case no different,” Scottsdale Police Chief Joe LeDuc said in a statement. “When you bring violence into our community, we will commit the resources necessary to find you, arrest you, and hold you accountable.”

Residents who want to report short-term rental problems can email STR@ScottsdaleAZ.gov or call Scottsdale police’s non-emergency line at (480) 312-5000.
