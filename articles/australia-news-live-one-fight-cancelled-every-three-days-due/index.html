One flight cancelled every three days across Australia due to staff shortages

Worker shortages have cancelled one flight every three days on average in the last year, Airservices Australia has told a Senate hearing.

The government agency’s deputy CEO, Peter Curran, said Australia’s airports recorded 2,536 cancellations over the year to 31 August, of which 121 were attributed to Airservices. Sydney accounted for 103 of those.

Just this morning, the troubled airport recorded 14 inbound cancellations and more outbound cancellations, after some overnight staff were unavailable, Curran said. Airservices and airlines manage the shortage by delaying or cancelling flights.

Curran said Sydney had grown and now needed a team of 41 air traffic controllers, spread across the roster. It has 49 employed but eight are unavailable at the moment. Curran said:

When we have a bit of short-term sickness or unavailability, like we had last night, it makes it very difficult to recover.

When we have a bit of short-term sickness or unavailability, like we had last night, it makes it very difficult to recover.

Curran said retired staff had been asked to come back to work but some of them were “simply not interested”. He said there were about 1,100 air traffic controllers working across Australia and 91 would join in the coming year.

With more recruitment, Curran said Airservices would be able to enough on-call staff to cover two absences a day by March 2027.

It’s not an ironclad model, but it certainly reduces the likelihood of the sorts of issues that we’re having today.

Major fire breaks out at building in Sydney’s north-west

Authorities are investigating the cause of a major building fire in Sydney’s north-west.

Emergency services responded to reports of the blaze on Loftus Street in Riverstone at around 1.35pm.

Fire and Rescue NSW said about 40 firefighters and 12 trucks remained at the scene. The fire has since been contained to the building where it started.

Four people were assessed at the scene, but none were taken to hospital.

Fire and Rescue NSW said the cause of the fire was under investigation.

ATO employee charged after allegedly disclosing information to organised crime associates

An Australian Taxation Office employee has been charged with corruption-related offences by the AFP and other groups on allegations the man disclosed information obtained in his work to organised crime associates.

The AFP said today the Gold Coast man, 32, had been charged overnight with two counts of unlawfully disclosing information by a commonwealth officer. The offence carries a maximum penalty of two years in prison.

He is due in court later this month.

After a search warrant was carried out, the man was also charged with two counts of possession or control of child abuse material and 15 counts of using a carriage service for child abuse material. The maximum penalty for both offences is 15 years in prison.

Queensland’s credit rating downgraded for the first time in 17 years

Queensland has been dealt the first downgrade to its credit rating in 17 years, heaping more borrowing costs on to the state’s budget, AAP reports.

Credit agency S&P Global slapped a stable outlook on Queensland’s downgraded AA rating.

The loss of Queensland’s AA+ rating came on the same day NSW received an upgrade to its credit outlook. Queensland’s budget performance would remain “very weak” over the next two to three years compared to peers with a similar rating, S&P said on Friday.

The state’s historically large infrastructure program ahead of the 2032 Olympics and forecast deficits contrasted with previous expectations for sound surpluses from 2028, it said in a report.

The state could also expect lower stamp duty revenues after property tax changes in the federal budget exacerbated a deepening housing downturn, S&P said.

49ers rout Rams 27-7 in NFL clash at the MCG

Mike Evans, Demarcus Robinson and Deebo Samuel caught touchdown passes from quarterback Brock Purdy, and the San Francisco 49ers routed the Los Angeles Rams 27-7 Friday in the first NFL regular-season game in Australia.

Purdy passed for 205 yards against the Rams’ ineffective defense at the Melbourne Cricket Ground in front of 100,021 fans, the seventh-largest regular season crowd in NFL history.

Australia’s sporting capital reveled in its first real taste of American football – and in a dominant win by the 49ers, who had already won many Aussies’ affection by travelling to Melbourne a week earlier than the Rams, who only boomeranged in on Thursday morning.

Australian share market veering towards worst week since US-Iran war began

The Australian share market is barreling toward its worst week since the US-Iran war began, as surging oil prices pressure bond yields and squeeze central banks to hike borrowing costs.

The benchmark S&P/ASX200 index fell 112.2 points by midday on Friday, to be down 1.27% as the broader All Ordinaries tumbled 120 points, or 1.33%.

The top-200 is on track for a 3.2% slump since Monday and its worst week since early March’s 3.8% plunge, when US-led strikes killed Iran’s supreme leader and triggered a broader regional conflict.

The Australian dollar was buying 71.56 US cents, down from 72.16 US cents on Thursday at 5pm.

Asio chief reveals 16 terror plots have been foiled since Bondi attack

Australia’s domestic spy agency says it has disrupted 16 terrorist threats in the nine months since the Bondi attack, including an antisemitic attack, a plot to kill Muslims and a violent racist allegedly found with weapons and ammunition.

The Australian Security and Intelligence Organisation’s director general, Mike Burgess, outlined the “evolving threat” in a speech today on the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 terror attacks on the United States.

The Asio chief revealed his organisation had disrupted 16 terror plans since December where the “threat was considered serious but not necessarily imminent”. In June the figure was 14. Since 2014, it has foiled 33 major terrorism plots.

Burgess said Sunni violent extremism represented the majority of Asio’s counter-terrorism caseload but that it was “too simplistic to assume there is a single terrorism threat or a most likely terrorist threat as we did in the aftermath of 9/11”.

He said the threats included an individual allegedly inspired by extreme right-wing ideology, others inspired by extreme left-wing ideologies and others inspired by “mixed and seemingly incompatible beliefs”, including one person who allegedly wanted to blow up a school who was allegedly inspired by ideological and extreme Christian beliefs.

Burgess said:

These dynamics make the contemporary terrorism environment different – and in some ways, more difficult – than a quarter of a century ago ... Spikes in grievance, intolerance and polarisation are degrading our security environment and making politically motivated violence more likely.

Woolworths increased prices of at least four ‘family staples’ during ‘Price Freeze’ promotion

Woolworths raised prices for popular items – including a 33% mark up on chicken fillets – within days of promising to freeze prices on 300 “family staples” for three months to help customers with the cost of living.

Pricing data, compiled by comparison website CW Scanner and provided to Guardian Australia, shows the supermarket chain increasing the prices of at least four key products after starting the three-month “Price Freeze” promotion on 1 May.

The supermarket nearly doubled the price of a 420g can of Woolworths “Mexican Style 3 Bean Mix” during the promotional period – from 60c to $1 on 17 June – the data shows. Woolworths dropped the price back to 60c on 17 August

Woolworths has justified the increases because it regarded the previous prices as promotional prices, rather than the “standard shelf price” for each product.

A Woolworths spokesperson said that the end of a temporary promotion did not represent a shelf price increase.
