Uphold Sabah's rights, reaffirm spirit of MA63, TYT says in Malaysia Day message

TheTools · 15.09.2026 17:25 · source · permalink

KOTA KINABALU: Sabah's special rights must be addressed through sincere and constructive discussions, Yang di-Pertua Negeri Tun Musa Aman says in his Malaysia Day message.

"On this Malaysia Day, we must also reaffirm the spirit and principles underpinning the Malaysia Agreement 1963 (MA63).

"Matters concerning Sabah’s rights and interests under MA63, including the special grant equivalent to 40% of net revenue derived by the Federation from Sabah, must continue to be addressed through sincere and constructive engagement and the spirit in which Malaysia was formed," he said.

He added that there was much to be done as Sabah’s development gaps must be addressed with more attention to basic infrastructure, reliable water and electricity supply, roads and connectivity, healthcare, education and meaningful economic opportunities.

"Development must reach every district, town, village and rural community so that no one is left behind. A stronger and more prosperous Sabah ultimately means a stronger and more prosperous Malaysia," Musa said.

He said Malaysia’s greatest strength lies in its people, with an extraordinary diversity of races, cultures, religions and traditions.

"This diversity must never be allowed to divide us – rather, it should remain a source of strength and pride. Despite our differences, we share one country, one destiny and one future," he said.

He added that the roles of Sabah and Sarawak in the formation and continuing development of Malaysia are fundamental.

"Sabah and Sarawak are integral partners in the Federation and have contributed immensely to Malaysia’s political stability, economic development, natural wealth and rich cultural heritage.

"Their contributions and aspirations must continue to be recognised as we build our nation’s future together," he said, adding that Malaysia Day was a defining moment in history with Malaya, Sabah and Sarawak coming together to form the Federation of Malaysia founded on a shared aspiration for peace, progress and prosperity.

"The values of unity, mutual understanding and a common belief in a better future have guided us through more than six decades over of nation-building and must continue to guide us in the years ahead," he said.

Amid the global uncertainty posed by conflicts and geopolitical tensions as well as the growing impact of climate change and higher cost of living, he reminded the people that peace, stability and prosperity should never be taken for granted.

"In these uncertain times, Malaysia must remain resilient, united and forward-looking. We must continue to strengthen our economy, safeguard national stability and ensure that the well-being of our people remains at the heart of development.

"Economic resilience must translate into better livelihoods, meaningful employment, greater investment and opportunities that are shared fairly across our country," Musa said.

He said there is no place in the country for racial or religious extremism and that it is important for Sabahans to maintain their tradition of tolerance, mutual respect and acceptance, which must continue to be protected and strengthened for generations to come.

"I call upon all Malaysians, particularly those entrusted with leadership and those who aspire to public office, to place the interests of the people and nation above narrow personal or political considerations.

"Political advantage must never be pursued by exploiting racial or religious sentiments. Differences of opinion are part of a healthy democracy, but they must be expressed responsibly, respectfully and based on facts.

"Leadership should ultimately be measured by ideas, solutions and policies that improve people’s lives and move our nation forward," he said.
