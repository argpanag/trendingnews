Joanna Page says ‘inappropriate’ male actor whispered ‘awful stuff’ to her

TheTools · 18.11.2026 14:53 · source · permalink

The Gavin and Stacey actor, who made the comments while talking with female campmates on I’m a Celebrity, said: ‘You just can’t speak’

Joanna Page has spoken about an “awful” male actor who was “inappropriate” while they were working together. She explained that he would “whisper things in your ear” and sing chants about “what he was going to do to me”.

The Gavin and Stacey actor is taking part in I’m a Celebrity … Get Me Out of Here!, where she talked with some of her female campmates about working in the entertainment industry.

“I remember this one actor and he would come up to you and whisper things in your ear before you were going to do a take or something,” she said. “And it was just really inappropriate, awful stuff.”

Without naming the show she was referring to, Page continued: “One scene that I had to do with him, he spent the whole time just sort of making up this song and chanting about what he was going to do to me and nobody said anything. You just can’t speak.”

Presenter Mariella Frostrup also shared that DJs at radio stations would often grab and “honk honk” her breasts at work. “If you did speak, people would go: ‘Oh get over yourself,’” she said.

It’s not the first time Page has talked about such behaviour. Last year, she said she was “groped” by a male TV host after a female producer warned her that he was “very handsy”.

“Halfway through filming, he starts groping me,” she told the Times. “I hit his hands and said: ‘What do you think you’re bloody doing? Keep your fucking hands to yourself! Jesus, I feel like I’m in Bristol zoo being mauled by the lions.’”

She didn’t name him because “the legal people said: ‘Be careful.’”

Page’s comments come a week after Russell Brand’s rape conviction compelled an examination of men in the TV industry abusing their power. This was further fuelled by David Walliams losing his injunction to stop the publication of an investigation reporting that he preyed on young women for sex, which he denies.

Culture secretary Lisa Nandy has also addressed the issue, telling the BBC: “Time is up – it really is time for the media industry to take this seriously.”

She added: “Hand on heart, I can’t say to you as the culture secretary that this is not happening today. In fact, if I had to wager, I would say it is.”
