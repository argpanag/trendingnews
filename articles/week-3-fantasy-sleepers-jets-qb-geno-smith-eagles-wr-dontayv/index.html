Week 3 fantasy sleepers: Jets QB Geno Smith, Eagles WR Dontayvion Wicks offer upside

TheTools · 27.09.2026 21:54 · source · permalink

There’s no way to sugarcoat it. Injuries have sucked a lot of air out of the balloon that is the young season.

That’s not just from a fantasy perspective; every football fan wants to watch Caleb Williams, Jayden Daniels, Brock Bowers and Puka Nacua on Sundays. The sport is better when those guys are playing.

But in fantasy, we strategically picked guys like Nico Collins, Saquon Barkley and DJ Moore to help us win our leagues. Now we have to hear the friend who autodrafted boast about being 2-0? Not ideal.

This makes finding sneaky values that much more important. Not every addition has to be a league-winner -- my goal right now is to get you those week-winning stopgaps while your stars are on the mend.

With that being said, welcome to the infirmary of Week 3.

NOTE: All statistics come courtesy of Next Gen Stats, unless otherwise noted.

QUARTERBACKS

I did not expect to include Smith here at any point this season. But, as so often happens when we write him off, he doesn’t write back. Smith has been perfectly average in his second stint with the Jets, but my decision to mention him in this space has more to do with the team he’s playing on Sunday.

The Lions have given up the most completions (55), second-most passing yards (658) and second-most passing touchdowns (six) through two weeks. Put all that together, and you have the most fantasy points allowed to QBs this season at 64.02. Geno isn’t going to transform into Josh Allen on Sunday, but he can definitely put up some points, especially throwing to Garrett Wilson and the emerging Adonai Mitchell -- who leads the NFL with a 56.7% air yards share -- against a struggling secondary.

The Texans are quietly the most pass-happy team in football, posting the league’s highest pass-play rate at 66.9%. Through two weeks, Stroud leads the NFL in dropbacks (104) and pass attempts (93), though it hasn’t translated to the gaudiest numbers for the QB14 in fantasy.

Enter the Indianapolis defense, which has been the football embodiment of the viral video of the security guard who barely checks the people entering the stadium. The Colts rank dead last in passing yards allowed (706), explosive passes allowed (10) and yards per attempt allowed (9.8) -- a figure that would be the highest in NFL history. I’m expecting a massive day out of Stroud regardless of who suits up at receiver for Houston.

RUNNING BACKS

I told myself I wouldn’t be going near any more Titans this season. But I’ve also told myself that I wouldn’t go near the ice cream aisle at the grocery store. We all know how that story ends. In this analogy, Pollard is like Rocky Road: not everyone’s favorite, but can definitely get the job done in the right circumstance.

This matchup is that circumstance. Pollard leads all RBs (min. 12 carries) with a 66.7% rush yards over expected rate in 2026, while his 4.7 yards per carry and 1.1 yards over expected per carry each rank in the top 14. Meanwhile, the Giants have allowed the most rush yards over expected per carry (1.7) and highest rate of rushes over expected (57.9%) to running backs this season. I don’t know about you, but I can taste the chocolate ice cream, marshmallows and almonds already.

I laid out the Colts’ defensive deficiencies versus the pass earlier, but I assure you, the run defense ain’t much better. Yes, they played against Derrick Henry and Kenneth Walker III in the first two weeks, but Indianapolis has given up the second-most fantasy points to RBs this season (68.4). The Colts have allowed 1,029 total yards in two games, for crying out loud.

So, an avenue is open for Marks to have a productive day, even if he has had a quiet start to the year as the RB35. He has only played six fewer snaps and received eight fewer touches than starter David Montgomery, but Marks has posted the same 3.3 yards-per-touch mark. He even has seven targets to Montgomery’s five. It’s just that Knuckles has gotten all of the scoring opportunities. Marks’ PPR upside is high, and if he finds the end zone for the first time since Week 14 of last season, we all go home happy.

Gregg Rosenthal and Jourdan Rodrigue preview the Week 3 matchup between the Texans and the Colts.

WIDE RECEIVERS

Wicks has been a pleasant surprise for Philadelphia, trailing only DeVonta Smith in targets, catches and yards. Two of his seven highest-scoring games ever have been his first two games of 2026. His 2.8 yards per route leads all Eagles wide receivers and ranks seventh among WRs with at least 50 routes this season. His 14.7 yards per target leads all wide receivers with at least 10 targets.

More than anything, Wicks has been a big-play weapon with four explosive plays (20+ yards) this season. On Monday night, he will face a Bears defense allowing the league’s fourth-highest explosive pass rate (22.4%). Wicks should be heavily involved in the offense, especially if Saquon Barkley can’t go due to his stinger injury.

Speaking of big-play threats, Thornton might be the boom-or-bust play of the week. After putting up a goose egg in the opener, he had a solid outing last week, catching all four of his targets for 86 yards, including the third-longest grab of his career on a 45-yard deep ball that traveled a whopping 43 air yards. That’s kind of his thing -- Thornton’s 22.8 air yards per target is the highest among WRs with at least 30 routes this season.

Then there’s the Dolphins’ pass defense, which is allowing a league-high 0.5 EPA per dropback and +9.3% completion percentage over expected. The deep ball is squarely in play, considering Miami’s 70.6% success rate allowed on downfield (10+ air yards) pass attempts is third-highest in football. All it takes is one deep touchdown pass to Thornton to potentially win your matchup this week. Why not let it fly?

TIGHT ENDS

Is it true that Barner has fewer catches than Noah Fant, fewer yards than Tommy Tremble and hasn’t caught a touchdown pass in the regular season since Week 17 of last year? Yes, it is. But I think that all changes this week, and he doesn’t even need to catch the ball to have fantasy upside.

Barner’s going up against a Washington defense that has given up the third-most fantasy points to TEs this season (44.0), with each starter the Commanders have faced in 2026 (Dallas Goedert and Jake Ferguson) scoring two touchdowns against them. Then there’s the second part, which is "The Barnyard" -- Seattle’s version of "The Tush Push," where Barner’s tush is the one being pushed. He scored on that play in Washington last year, and did the same last week against Arizona. If it’s not one, it’ll be the other on Sunday.

Just like in 2025, it appears that all tight ends are viable in fantasy when going up against Cincinnati. Last season, the Bengals allowed the most completions (116), yards (1,444) and touchdowns (16) to the position; as such, they surrendered 357 fantasy points to TEs, the most in the league by a healthy amount. So far in 2026, they again lead the league in yards allowed to tight ends (200).

This is all great news for Freiermuth, who is quietly the TE11 this season and the only Steelers offensive player to find paydirt in 2026. Oh, and you’ll never guess who he has the most catches, yards and touchdowns against -- by comfortable margins, I might add -- in his career. I’ll give you a hint: It rhymes with angles!

DEFENSES

This is less about the prowess of Jacksonville’s defense and more about the Patriots’ propensity to put the pill in the palms of the opposition. New England’s five giveaways are tied for the second-most in the NFL -- four of them have been interceptions thrown by Drake Maye, all on his signature deep ball. Only Cooper Rush can say he’s thrown that many picks, and we might not see Rush play again this year.

Seattle was tied for the third-highest scoring D/ST performance against the Pats in Week 1 and then the Steelers were a top-10 unit in Foxborough in Week 2. Until I see Maye and company figure things out -- which I’m not expecting while A.J. Brown is out -- I’ll happily stream whoever plays them.

It has been a drab start to the 2026 campaign in Yinzer Land, but the Steelers can still hang their hat on the defense, which is one of only four to finish in the top 10 in fantasy scoring in each of the first two weeks. Pittsburgh is first in sack rate (13.2%), tied for fourth in sacks (seven) and tied for third in takeaways (four).

And man, do the Steelers love playing Joe Burrow. No team has picked him off more than Pittsburgh (10 times). Burrow's 92.4 passer rating against this AFC North foe is his second-lowest mark versus any team he’s faced multiple times in his career. And Cincy’s offensive line isn’t solved; the Bengals rank 24th in sack rate allowed (8.2%). A hard-nosed rivalry game equals low scoring, sacks and turnovers. Unless both starting QBs are 40 or older, apparently.
