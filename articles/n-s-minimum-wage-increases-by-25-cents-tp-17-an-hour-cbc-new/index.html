N.S. minimum wage increases by 25 cents tp $17 an hour | CBC News

TheTools · 01.10.2026 16:09 · source · permalink

It's the second increase to the rate this year

Minimum wage earners in Nova Scotia working 40 hours a week will begin taking in an extra $10 with the most recent increase to the rate that kicked in Thursday.

The rate is now $17 an hour after the second 25-cent increase this year following a recommendation from the province’s minimum wage review committee last December. The committee includes representatives for employers and employees.

The adjustment reflects the consumer price index plus one per cent, according to a release from the provincial government.

“This increase supports workers and recognizes the valuable work they do every day,” Labour Minister Nolan Young said in the release.

“We’re also helping employers manage costs by lowering taxes and investing in programs that help businesses attract and keep skilled workers.”

According to the province, about 6.5 per cent of workers in Nova Scotia — about 31,000 people — earn minimum wage.

Despite this and other recent increases, previous reports have said the minimum wage still lags far behind what's considered a living wage for workers in the province. A 2025 report by the Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives pegged the average living wage for Nova Scotia at $27.60 an hour.

The report defined a living wage as the hourly rate someone needs to earn in order to cover things such as rent, clothing, shelter, transportation, health care and basic household expenses.
