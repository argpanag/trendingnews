Person dies from severe form of West Nile virus, marking Yolo County's first death of the year

TheTools · 09.09.2026 19:54 · source · permalink

Yolo County announced its first human death from West Nile virus of the year, according to a Wednesday news release.

The person who died had neuroinvasive West Nile virus, which the Yolo County Public Health Department describes as the most severe form of the disease. Details on the age and gender of the victim were not released.

“Our thoughts are with those who are grieving the loss of this community member to West Nile virus,” Yolo County Health Officer Dr. Joanna Locke said in a release. “This is also a reminder that West Nile virus can cause serious illness. Older adults, people with weakened immune systems and those with certain underlying medical conditions are at greatest risk for severe illness.”

Yolo County Health said that about 1 in 150 people infected with the virus can develop severe neurological illness that could lead to hospitalizations or death. About 20% of cases show flu-like symptoms such as fever, headache, body aches, nausea or vomiting. The rest experience mild or no symptoms.

This death comes as West Nile cases for this year continue to increase. State data show significantly more cases this year so far compared to the same time of year in 2025. As of Sept. 4, there are 86 human cases compared to 22 at the same point in time last year.

In all of 2025, there were 128 human cases in California, 11 of them resulting in deaths. There have been at least six deaths so far this year.

Health officials recommend the following steps to reduce the risk of infection:

  • Drain standing water
  • Limit time outdoors during dawn and dusk hours
  • Wear long sleeves and pants when outdoors
  • Use an EPA-registered insect repellent, such as one containing DEET, picaridin or oil of lemon eucalyptus
  • Keep doors and window screens in good condition
  • Call 1-800-429-1022 to contact district personnel about any mosquito concerns
