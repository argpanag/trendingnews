ABC Is Abandoning The Rookie's Old TV Strategy With a Major Season 9 Reset

TheTools · 24.11.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

Big changes are coming to The Rookie on ABC ahead of its ninth season premiere in early 2027. The hit series became a Tuesday-night staple on the network, but it will switch to Wednesday nights in Season 9. It will air alongside its latest spinoff, The Rookie: North, which differs from how the network handled the last one, The Rookie: Feds.

When the show first debuted in 2018, it aired on Tuesday nights. From Season 2 through Season 5, it switched to Sunday nights. During that last season, The Rookie: Feds took over its Season 1 Tuesday timeslot. The show returned to Tuesdays after Feds was canceled, where it stayed until an early-season switch to Monday nights in 2026. When the show finally returns in January 2027, it will debut on Wednesday nights at 9 PM Eastern for the first time. The Rookie: North will follow at 10 PM. It's an interesting choice and not without risk.

The Rookie Has Become An Institution On ABC And Streaming

With Season 9 and the new spinoff still more than a month away, The Rookie earned two streaming victories. In early November, it jumped 11 spots on JustWatch's charts to become the world's 37th most-streamed show. Days later, FlixPatrol ranked it the world's second most-streamed show.

The Rookie has always been a streaming powerhouse, even more so since Disney+ and Hulu merged. It is arguably how most viewers experience the show. So, switching to Wednesdays for the first time may not be as drastic as such shifts have been for shows in the past. Of course, the show still puts up decent traditional ratings numbers whether it was on Sundays, Tuesdays, or, for last season, Mondays.

For all the convenience of streaming, some fans simply don't want to wait the extra day to catch it online when they can watch it as it airs or slightly delayed on their DVRs. Still, the series continues to perform well on streaming every year, even during the months-long hiatuses between new episodes.

As "appointment television" becomes a thing of the past, The Rookie is equipped to survive as a hybrid of traditional TV viewing and streaming. One need only look at all the various procedurals with numbers or letters in their titles to understand the importance of broadcast TV franchises.

From the suite of shows like NCIS, CSI, 911, and even Law & Order, it's clear that truly successful series aren't limited to one title. Yet many long-running shows, like Supernatural or Smallville, succeed without spawning spinoffs (though not for lack of trying). The Rookie itself is an institution, but The Rookie: North has a tough road ahead.

The Rookie: North Is In A Better Position Than The Rookie: Feds Ever Was

Fans know The Rookie: North isn't the first time series creator Alexi Hawley hastried to turn his hit show into a bona fide 'universe.' ABC's reasons for canceling The Rookie: Feds went beyond ratings and episode quality.

Perhaps the most questionable decision for the show led by Niecy Nash was its placement on the schedule. It aired on Tuesdays in the timeslot The Rookie proper would eventually occupy. However, the beat-cop procedural led by Nathan Fillion aired on Sundays during the 2022-2023 season. Even with streaming, Feds not having the original Rookie as a lead-in surely hurt it.

The other problem Feds faced was how different it was from the main show. The spinoff reinvents The Rookie, keeping the winning format while swapping the location. Feds featured a different kind of casework and environment for its characters. The Rookie: North will take place in a new setting, rural Washington, as compared to Los Angeles.

Yetthe weekly cases will more closely mirror The Rookie, because both shows center on street-level police work. This creates storytelling opportunities that more traditional cop procedurals, including Feds, can't embrace. As longtime fans know, John Nolan and his fellow officers never quite know what they are in for during any given shift.

Sure, it might be a sweeping case that involves everyone, from a potential terrorist attack to a crashed plane full of convicts. Yet, it's equally likely that an episode features multiple different investigations, from a missing person to undercover adventures or running down a serial killer. North will offer similar opportunities, with cases, characters, and even how policing works shaped by its rural setting.

In L.A., backup or emergency services are rarely far away. Whereas the characters in North will have a much larger area to cover, as well as new challenges like inclement weather.

ABC Is Wise To Move The Rookie To Wednesdays With Its New Spinoff

In today's entertainment landscape, any series that makes it to nine seasons is an impressive success. Part of the show's longevity comes from how The Rookie adapted after its first five seasons. Originally, the appeal was watching John Nolan find his place on the force as its oldest rookie.

Yet, he couldn't stay that way forever. Over the years, his experiences made him the perfect training officer, meaning the titular rookie is no longer Fillion's character but a rotating cast of new officers. The Rookie: North represents a return to form its predecessor had to abandon. Jay Ellis will play Alex Holland, the eldest rookie on his respective police force.

The unique challenges his character must overcome will feel familiar to viewers who watched Fillion's Nolan surmount similar obstacles early in his career. Unlike Nolan, Holland will not be a transplant to his city, so his personal history will play a bigger role in his story. This will also add interesting elements to the dynamic Holland will share with his fellow officers.

The first season of The Rookie: North will tell a brand-new story, but its familiar premise makes it as immediately accessible as The Rookie was when it debuted. The Rookie: North films in Vancouver instead of in Los Angeles like the main show. This means crossovers between The Rookie and North are limited by on- and off-screen geographic distance.

Feds featured more frequent narrative overlap, but even that wasn't enough to overcome the real-world separation of the shows on different nights. So, ironically, ABC pairing both The Rookie: North and the main series on Wednesday nights puts them "closer" in viewers' minds than the previous spinoff was.

This lets The Rookie: North define itself as a show. However, it will air at a time when fans of The Rookie can more easily follow the new characters.
