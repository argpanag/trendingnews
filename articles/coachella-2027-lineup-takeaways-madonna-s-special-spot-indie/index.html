Coachella 2027 Lineup Takeaways: Madonna's Special Spot, Indie Surprises, and... No Drake?

TheTools · 11.11.2026 18:41 · source · permalink

Who got top billing? They put that legendary artist below who!? What’s that name in the smallest font? And how exactly did Weezer negotiate the placement of their flying-W logo on the poster? There are always lots of questions when Coachella reveals its lineup. This year, the headlining trio of Dua Lipa, SZA, and Tame Impala — three of the most logical choices for a big festival set in the 2020s — might come as a little less of a surprise. But the rest of the lineup is full of exciting names, and a few notable omissions. Here are five of our big takeaways.

Madonna’s Dance-Tent Slot is an Amazing Nod to Coachella History

Back in 2006, not long after the release of her classic album Confessions on a Dance Floor, Madonna gave a performance that pointed towards Coachella’s future. At the time, the festival leaned hard towards rock, indie, and what some people still called alternative — that year’s headliners included Depeche Mode, Franz Ferdinand, and the Yeah Yeah Yeahs. So the news that Madonna would appear in the Sahara dance tent wasn’t greeted with universal excitement. A few fans wore “Madonna Killed Coachella” T-shirts, and Sleater-Kinney’s Carrie Brownstein even dissed Madonna from the stage.  But the six-song set was a total triumph, and Coachella would eventually become dominated by pop — perhaps to a fault. In any case, Madonna’s return to the dance tent, perhaps with a longer performance this time, couldn’t be a more appropriate celebration of her fantastic, equally dance-floor-oriented Confessions II album. —Brian Hiatt

But Hold On. Where’s Drake? 

When the lineup dropped overnight, many people wondered the same thing: Where the hell is Drake? It was a reaction shared by Drizzy himself, who commented on the festival’s Instagram post with a crying emoji. The rapper was heavily rumored to be headlining this year, which would have marked his first time performing at Coachella in over a decade (he played a full set in 2015, then made a surprise appearance during Future’s set two years later, for their collab “Jumpman”). More importantly, it would have been Drake’s first full set in the United States in two years, and a chance for us to hear material from his recent Iceman trilogy — as well as his recent take on a massive hit about Texas. Maybe he’ll hit the desert next year. In the meantime, we’ll be mourning in our brown fringe shawls. —Angie Martoccio

And No Veteran Rock Acts? Really?

It’s been a long time since Coachella booked a mainstage headliner like Roger Waters, Paul McCartney, or Guns N’ Roses to appeal to music fans who came of age prior to the year 2000. This makes a lot of sense, since that’s a demographic that does not include the vast majority of Coachella attendees. But in recent years, they’ve thrown a bone or two to the older set by booking cool acts like Devo, Kraftwerk, Iggy Pop, and the Go-Gos for older fans (or younger ones who respect their elders). Madonna arguably fills that role in the dance tent this year, and Weezer can bring in some Gen Xers, along with many, many young fans of their music. But there’s no real equivalent of Iggy or Devo further down the lineup. It’ll make it a rough weekend for the Coachella moms and dads, especially if they turn in before Madonna takes the stage in the dance tent. —Andy Greene

Daddy Yankee Will Make His Coachella Debut 

It’s been a long road to Coachella for Daddy Yankee. Back in 2021, the Latin icon revealed he’d been invited to the festival, but ultimately turned it down because, he said, “the pay was ridiculous.” Now, it seems Coachella has finally recognized Daddy Yankee’s worth. The reggaeton star will play the “In the Desert” portion of the festival, a top slot that typically features special production. While fans shouldn’t necessarily bank on hearing “Rompe” and “Gasolina,” given Daddy Yankee’s career pivot, his performance will mark a full-circle moment after his successors Bad Bunny and Karol G made history with their Coachella performances in recent years. —Maya Georgi

Give It Up For Independent Artists

If nothing else, indie fans can look forward to a strong crop of smaller acts getting their chance to shine. NOLA’s Twisted Teens will get to showcase the three records they dropped this year alone, while Underscores will get the crowd going wild for her maximalist pop sound. If the Coachella bookers choose chaos (and we certainly hope they do), they should have Québécois weirdos Angine de Poitrine play directly before a bigger act like Zara Larsson: Confuse the masses, then convert the curious few to polka-dot worshippers. 

Maybe the most eye-catching name is Steven Hall, better known as Nirosta Steel, appearing near the bottom of the bill on Friday. Hall was a close friend of Arthur Russell’s who rose to critical prominence just this year with his 40-years-in-the-making record My Skyscraper. The experimental pop record felt plucked directly out of Manhattan’s queer downtown art and music scene of the Eighties; now 69 years old, he’s found his moment in the spotlight, and who knows? He might go check out Madonna’s Confessions II set after he’s all done. “Simply fabulous,” he said of that album’s standout track “Danceteria.” —Jaeden Pinder
