Death Row Pastor Describes Horror of ‘Botched’ Texas Execution: ‘Torture’ - Newsweek

TheTools · 20.11.2026 11:08 · source · permalink

National Correspondent

A "botched" execution in Texas on Thursday left a man begging “please kill me, kill me” as he screamed for minutes following a lethal injection that should have left him unconscious in seconds, a death row spiritual adviser who witnessed it told Newsweek.

Seventy-year-old Reinaldo Dennes' execution took more than an hour at the penitentiary in Huntsville, as prison staffers struggled to find a suitable vein for the two intravenous needles carrying the lethal dose of the sedative pentobarbital.

Dennes was sentenced to death after being convicted of fatally shooting diamond dealer Janos Szucs during a robbery in January 1996.

“I can’t express what it was like to have my hand on someone, heaving and thrashing and doing all of these things and begging for them to kill him,” Reverend Jeff Hood said on a phone call early on Friday.

“'Please kill me, kill me.’ That's what he kept saying. You know, he wanted them to kill him… I think that that speaks to the fact that we're no longer talking about punishment, we’re talking about torture.”

Hood, , a longtime anti-death penalty activist who has served as a spiritual adviser to more than a dozen inmates on death row, added: “I have never seen someone of these lethal injections in which somebody is able to form complete sentences after 30 seconds, it just doesn't happen. And so long story short, Texas botched an execution tonight."

The procedure was completed at 7:07 p.m., 63 minutes after he was strapped to the gurney, the Associated Press reported. After being examined by a physician, he was pronounced dead at 7:08 p.m.

Prison officials said all procedures were followed appropriately. Amanda Hernandez, a spokesperson for the Texas Department of Criminal Justice (TDCJ) , acknowledged a number of unsuccessful attempts to find a suitable vein before needles were inserted in Dennes’ left foot and on the left side of his neck.

Hernandez also suggested Dennes may have exaggerated the pain, saying he began complaining before the drugs began to flow and also of pain in places where there was no IV.

Asked if he was faking, she replied, “I think, yeah, there were allegations. … There was nothing abnormal on our part.”

“This isn’t the first time we had to insert IVs into other locations. It’s also not the first time we’ve had an inmate make allegations of pain immediately,” Hernandez said.

Hood said: "In this situation, the clearest indication that they are full of s***—and you can quote me on that—is that they're supposed to go unconscious within 30 seconds and he's still screaming three four minutes into the execution. How is that possible?”

Newsweek contacted the Texas Department of Criminal Justice for comment via email outside regular office hours on Friday.

The execution comes amid renewed scrutiny of how the death penalty is carried out in the United States after Tennessee’s failed execution of Christa Pike on September 30. Pike survived two doses of pentobarbital that her attorneys say probably did not enter her bloodstream correctly and instead pooled in her arms, leaving them burned and blistered.

‘My Leg. It’s on Fire’: Inside the Execution Chamber

Hood told Newsweek that he began to suspect something was not right when he was not allowed into the execution chamber at the scheduled 6 p.m. start time.

“In those moments, it's pretty clear when they're struggling to find a vein…because that's the only thing that would be delaying a lethal injection,” he said.

When Hood was brought inside, he said there were signs that several attempts had been made.

“I could see that they had run a line in his neck, which is pretty unusual,” he said. “I'd also seen that they had tried to run lines in his arms, and then I could also see that there was a line run in his foot.”

Hood said he moved toward Dennes' head and placed his left hand on his shoulder.

“He said, ‘Jeff, is that you?’ And I said, ‘Yeah, I'm here. I'm not going anywhere. I'll be here the whole time,’” he said.

Hood said Dennes made a short statement where he said he knows “the love of the father,” before telling the warden to “go.”

Almost immediately after the drugs began flowing, Hood said Dennes started “to fight against the restraints in his arms.

“His chest starts like heaving upwards and he starts shaking the legs,” he added.

According to Hood, Dennes repeatedly cried out: “My leg, my leg, my leg. It's on fire, it's on fire, it's on fire.”

He said: “He shifts back and forth on the gurney. He keeps pulling at the straps.”

Hood, whose hand remained on Dennes' shoulder, said it felt like he was “riding a bucking bronco.”

“He kept saying that it's not working,” Hood said. “He's going ’s***, s***, f***, f***, f***. What's going on? It's not working.’ He kept saying that. ’It's not working.’ And then he said, ‘I don't think this is normal. What is going on? I don’t think this is normal.’

“And he then he starts saying, ‘take me father, please don't let me suffer like this, please, please, please.’”

Hood said it took more than four minutes before Dennes lost consciousness.

“I’ve seen three other lethal injections in Texas and this is the worst one by far,” he said. “He was supposed to immediately go unconscious, within 30 seconds, that's what I usually see.”

After Texas executed Jamaal Howard in October, the first execution after Tennessee failed to put Pike to death, Hood had expressed confidence in the state’ handling of executions. "They have a very set regimen about how they do this and you know, there didn't seem to be any problems at all,” he told Newsweek at the time.

But that confidence has disappeared after what happened in the execution chamber on Thursday night, he said.

“Going in, I thought Texas was the gold standard, and I left knowing very clearly that they had just botched an execution,” he said. “I think that what that says is that none of these methods are foolproof.”

Death Row Pastor Calls on Gina Hinojosa to Halt Executions

Hood has urged Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, a Democrat who ousted Republican incumbent Greg Abbott, to do everything in her power to halt executions in the state when she takes office in January. Texas does not have any more executions scheduled for the rest of 2026.

Hood argued that what he witnessed calls into question whether lethal injection executions can ever be carried out without prolonged suffering.

“This is a real moment and it's happening right as Governor-elect Hinojosa comes into office and it's asking a very important question and that is, can these things ever be done without risk of really botching, horrifying consequences?” Hood said.

He said what happened inside the execution chamber on Thursday shows that the Texas Department of Criminal Justice “needs to be cleaned up from top to bottom.”

He called for the removal of Bobby Lumpkin, the department’s executive director, noting that the head of Tennessee’s prison system had resigned in the wake of Pike’s failed execution.

“A botched execution should, at the very least, lead to the resignation of the director of the agency,” he said.

Hinojosa “needs to clean house and the Texas Department of Criminal Justice needs to get back in the business of rehabilitation instead of being a functionary of vengeance,” he said.

“The governor-elect can stop executions in Texas. And this is yet another reason why she must.”
