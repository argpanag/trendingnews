New grassroots group joins Lexington gun violence prevention efforts

TheTools · 24.09.2026 22:11 · source · permalink

LEXINGTON, Ky. — A new Lexington nonprofit is recruiting mothers to help monitor parks and other gathering places, adding a grassroots approach to local efforts aimed at reducing gun violence after four shootings in one night last month left two people dead and two others injured.

Moms Outside was started by Anonimuss Rose, a Lexington mother who said the group plans to organize neighborhood rounds from 6 to 10 p.m. Volunteers will check parks and frequently used areas where young people gather.

“The plan is to do 6 p.m. to 10 p.m. neighborhood rounds, just making sure to do parks and very frequently gathered places where kids are just riding through, making sure everything’s OK,” Rose said.

Rose said the idea came after her 16-year-old cousin, Di’zion Robinson, was shot and killed Aug. 15 during a gathering at Charles Young Park.

“And it just got me thinking. One of the mothers cried out. She said, ‘Well, I asked for help and I didn’t get that,’” Rose said.

Rose said Moms Outside is not trying to replace law enforcement or elected officials.

“We’re not the police. The police have a position. We’re not politicians. The politicians have a position. We are simply moms who care,” Rose said.

The effort comes as Lexington leaders point to progress through the city’s existing violence prevention work. Earlier this year, Mayor Linda Gorton has said community violence has dropped by 60% over the past five years, crediting the city’s Community Violence Intervention Program as a major factor.

One Lexington, the city’s program focused on reducing youth and young adult gun violence, also works with community partners.

“Our job is to leverage government resources and community partnerships to address youth and young adult gun violence,” said Devine Carama with One Lexington.

Carama said he welcomes grassroots groups such as Moms Outside, as long as they work with existing efforts.

“Oh, no question,” Carama said. “All we ask is when these organizations do come is that they come into the fold, come under the umbrella.”

Carama said progress does not lessen the effect of violence on families and communities.

“While we can acknowledge progress and see that things are getting better, that doesn’t mean anything to a mother who just lost a child to gun violence,” Carama said. “Or that doesn’t mean anything to those who were impacted at the Charles Young Park shooting.”

Moms Outside is continuing to recruit volunteers as it works to begin its neighborhood rounds.
