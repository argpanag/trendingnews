Senators Launch Inquiry Into Trump Administration's Withholding of Federal Funds and Reductions in Medicaid Fraud Oversight

TheTools · 18.11.2026 01:23 · source · permalink

WASHINGTON D.C. (KEYT) – California's Senators joined other members of the upper chamber in announcing an inquiry into the Trump Administration's public campaign against alleged fraud at state-managed Medicaid programs while the same Administration has dismantled federal fraud oversight.

"The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) is running the most aggressive public enforcement campaign against state Medicaid programs in the agency's history, while accelerating the systematic dismantling of the federal infrastructure Congress funded to prevent Medicaid fraud in the first place," argued last week's letter to Dr. Mehmet Oz, the Administrator of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services' Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. "That is not program integrity. That is political theater layered over a decade of neglect, with states across the country, and in particular, an overwhelming number of Democratic-led states that have become the Trump administration's targets, left to answer for both."

In April of this year, Administrator Oz sent letters demanding the revalidation of Medicaid service providers to all 50 governors within ten business days as part of the Trump Administration's stated efforts to dismantle allegedly fraudulent claims nationwide.

"Corrupt individuals and organizations masquerading as health care providers are defrauding Medicaid, and American taxpayers, of billions of dollars each year, placing valuable resources out of reach for those the program was intended to serve: low-income senior citizens, children, and disabled individuals," stated the letter from Administrator Oz in April. "Classes of providers with less rigorous enrollment and billing requirements (e.g. providers without National Provider Identifiers) are most vulnerable to fraud, presenting an opportunity to scope quick and decisive action towards areas of highest risk."

To enforce the change, Administrator Oz withheld billions in federal funding to states including more than $3.4 billion in Medicaid funds dedicated to California's In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) program noted Senators Padilla and Schiff in their letter earlier this month.

"IHSS [In-Home Supportive Services] is a federally approved Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) program and a cornerstone of California's long-standing efforts to keep people safe at home and out of costly institutional settings, an approach CMS [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services] has consistently encouraged for decades," stated the California Department of Health Care Services in a press release about the Administration's demands. California has built one of the nation's strongest HCBS systems, expanding access to in-home services in direct alignment with federal policy and the Americans with Disabilities Act's mandate to support individuals in the most integrated setting appropriate."

According to Senators Padilla and Schiff, the Trump Administration cited the growth of the program as the reason behind the withholding of Congressionally-approved funding "with no specific evidence of wrongdoing" despite public claims to the contrary.

"The IHSS [In-Home Supportive Services in California] caseload for individuals eligible for federal financial participation in Medi-Cal increased from 613,764 in State Fiscal Year (SFY) 2022-23 to 720,988 in SFY 2024-25, a 17.5 percent increase," explained the California Department of Health Care Services in response to the freezing of federal funding. "The average cost per hour went from $19.00 to $21.03 in the same period, representing an increase of 10.7 percent driven by statewide minimum wage increases and county-negotiated wage rates. Wage increases are a tool to recruit and retain qualified workers in a field experiencing a provider shortage. Finally, the average hours per case modestly increased over the same period due to the higher acuity needs of individuals receiving services in the program."

"Despite these longstanding federal policy goals and federal approvals for IHSS [In-Home Supportive Services] program growth, CMS' [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services] deferral notice states that CMS is deferring these funds simply because the growth rate in California's IHSS program is greater than other states," the state health agency noted.

To combat fraud within Medicaid, Congress established the Medicaid Integrity Program through the Deficit Reduction Act of 2005, which requires the federal government dedicate at least $75 million and no fewer than 100 full-time staff to work alongside states to fight Medicaid-based fraud and abuse.

Most state-based versions of the anti-fraud program are housed inside their state's respective Attorneys General offices and serve as the primary tool for conducting prosecutions of Medicaid provider-based fraud explained the Senators in their letter to Administrator Oz.

Despite that existing tool to combat Medicaid fraud, the Trump Administration placed all Medicaid Fraud Control Units nationwide under review and instead, "launched a Medicaid Fraud 'War Room' that your [Administrator Oz's] Deputy Administrator publicly calls 'what modern program integrity looks like.' Modern program integrity should start with meeting the mandates Congress already wrote into law" argued the Senators.

Additionally, Congress required that the fraud-prevention program provide federal technical assistance to states, on-site state reviews, provide analytical support to states during investigations and report its actions to Congress.

"Those obligations are not discretionary, and CMS [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services] cannot treat dedicated funding as optional funding or repurpose it at will," noted the Senators.

The Senators the explicitly detailed those obligations to combat medical funding fraud established under federal law including:

  • The replacement of comprehensive on-site reviews with remote "Focused Reviews" that issue non-binding "Observations" a change the Senators characterized as "performative enforcement"
  • That legally required minimum staffing requirements have not been met after Administration-directed reductions in the federal workforce
  • The elimination of state liasons, fraud alerts issued by the Office of Inspector General for the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services and an annual summary report of program integrity
  • A change to the Medicaid Integrity's residential training program alongside the U.S. Department of Justice for state-based integrity staff to a "handful of virtual webinars per year and a few regional in-person listening sessions"

In March, President Trump signed Executive Order 14395 creating a task force dedicated to eliminating fraud under the direction of the Vice President. The task force has halted Medicaid funds from reaching multiple states, including California.

"States administer these federally funded programs, and some States have embraced loopholes that avoid individual eligibility validation, allow self-certification of eligibility, and expand eligibility far beyond what the Congress intended. Worse, despite accepting Federal funds, some States have refused to institute basic fraud controls such as providing enrollee information to the Federal Government that would allow it to verify eligibility. As a result, illegal aliens, criminals, foreign gangs, bureaucrats, State and local officials, non-governmental organizations, and ineligible providers exploit these programs ‑- which are intended to provide a safety net to lawfully eligible Americans — with ease. This exploitation and lack of controls to prevent it have resulted in widespread fraud, waste, and abuse at the expense of the American taxpayers who pay for and utilize these programs, contributing substantially to the national debt," argued the Trump Administration in Executive Order 14395. "Self-dealing political actors use such public benefits programs to solidify control over their communities and our political systems. Due to lax immigration policy and immigration fraud, certain public officials admit into our country, and provide sanctuary from Federal immigration laws to, migrant populations who are likely to rely on means-tested, public assistance programs (welfare) and increase the political support and power of the public officials providing the benefits. This increased support incentivizes public officials to maximize the flow of welfare to these communities and makes public officials who do so more powerful. Many of these public officials then fail to police these programs — and in some cases, willfully turn a blind eye to fraud, waste, and abuse within them — to ensure that welfare flows to these migrants.

States subject to the change in funding and the existing oversight process found in federal law have noted that some of the claims from the Trump Administration regarding Medicaid fraud do not hold up to scrutiny.

In April of this year, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services admitted that Administrator Oz's claims that five million people were provided personal care services in New York last year, which would constitute almost three-fourths of the state's 6.8 million enrollees, was not true despite it being used to justify a probe into the state's Medicaid program and the withholding of Congressionally-approved money for the administration of the program.

Around 450,000 people were provided personal care services in New York last year, under seven percent of the state's total enrollees, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services later acknowledged.

"The administration publicly shared whatever figures served its political purposes, and the resulting mistake was then used to threaten a state," argued the Senators. "California has faced a separate but equally troubling pattern: CMS [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services] has imposed federal payment deferrals that have now accumulated to more than $3.4 billion, with California's Medicaid director publicly rejecting the validity of CMS's claims and stating that CMS has provided no evidence supporting the deferrals."

Minnesota filed suit against its deferral of $243 million in federal funds alleging that the Trump Administration unilaterally withheld the Congressionally-approved funds without sharing with the state why it was being denied the funds despite repeated requests for explanation shared the Senators.

"My office has a strong track record of success in fighting Medicaid fraud: we've won more than 300 convictions and $80 million in judgments and restitution since I've served as Attorney General, and I've asked the Legislature for more tools and more resources to hold more fraudsters accountable. Fighting fraud should be bipartisan but the Trump Administration not only hasn't helped the fight against fraud, they've actually harmed it," Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison stated in March of this year. "Trump's attempts to look like he's fighting fraud only punish the people and families who most need the high-quality, affordable healthcare that all Minnesotans deserve. As long as I am attorney general, I will do everything in my power to defend our tax dollars, both from fraudsters and from the Trump administration's cruelty."

Additionally, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services stated during an industry conference in October 2026, that it was going to use artificial intelligence to flag suspicious Medicaid billing patterns, "in near real-time before Medicaid payments are made", but those plans do not match Medicaid's existing billing process and ignore the changes made by the Administration to federal oversight highlighted the Senators.

"CMS [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services] does not, however, operate the claims systems that generate the underlying data," noted the Senators. "In fee-for-service Medicaid, state agencies adjudicate claims; in managed care, Medicaid Managed Care Organizations do. CMS receives Medicaid claims and encounter data on a lag, and the federal pre-payment analytical capacity that could plausibly support such a platform is precisely the capacity CMS dismantled."

The Senators ended their letter by requesting additional information and briefings from the Trump Administration to ensure compliance with federal law.

"Medicaid serves more than 73 million Americans," the Senators concluded their letter to Administrator Oz. "Program integrity in a program of that scale demands sustained investment, deep expertise, and genuine partnership with states. Congress created and funded the infrastructure to do this work, and the administration and CMS are required by law to carry out those mandates. CMS is not free to redirect the funding, redefine the mission, or replace true program integrity with a flashy, politicized rebrand."
