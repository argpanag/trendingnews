Hurricane Isaias live updates: Gulf Coast braces for dangerous storm | CNN

TheTools · 08.10.2026 07:23 · source · permalink

Here's the latest

  • Record-shattering Isaias continues to strengthen en route to landfall on the northern Gulf Coast by this weekend. The storm rapidly intensified into the Atlantic season’s first hurricane Wednesday night.
  • Isaias could near Category 3 strength, but will weaken some before making landfall as a hurricane by early Saturday morning. Locations on the coast could see up to 7 feet of storm surge and up to 10 inches of rain, with short-lived tornadoes popping up with little notice.
  • States of emergency have been issued in parts of Alabama and Florida, and mandatory evacuation orders have been issued in two counties in the Florida panhandle.
  • Some Florida airports are scheduled to close ahead of the storm. Amtrak services in parts of Louisiana and Alabama are also disrupted.

Hurricane Isaias is expected to strengthen significantly over Thursday and Friday. Even if it weakens slightly before landfall, the storm will remain dangerous along the Gulf Coast.

Hurricane Isaias is due to make landfall late Friday night into Saturday as a strong Category two storm. A state of emergency has been declared across Florida and Alabama.

All hurricanes are dangerous, but their impacts aren't always created equal. Meteorologist Chris Warren explains how storm size and storm strength influence the impacts communities experience before, during and after landfall.

To help people prepare for hurricanes, scientists studied 40 tropical cyclones and found storm surge hits in eight ways.

How Florida is preparing to respond to Hurricane Isaias

Florida is mobilizing equipment and personnel ahead of Hurricane Isaias, which is expected to make landfall as early as Friday night.

The Florida National Guard is also activating personnel and expects to have approximately 5,000 troops mobilized by tomorrow, DeSantis said. The governor declared a state of emergency for portions of the state earlier this week.

Hundreds of workers with the Florida Department of Transportation are making storm preparations now, including inspecting drainage systems and monitoring flood-prone areas.

More than 6,000 utility linemen and restoration personnel are ready to respond to outages during the storm. “If you’re in the Panhandle, assume you are going to lose power. Prepare that you are going to lose power,” DeSantis said.

Mobile, Alabama, prepares for storm surge, possible evacuations

Officials in Mobile are telling residents to prepare now, with the first impacts from Isaias expected to arrive along the Alabama coast as early as Friday morning.

“This is a serious storm coming,” Mayor Spiro Cheriogotis said in a briefing Thursday. “I don’t want people to take it lightly.”

Mobile residents living east of Interstate 65 and south of Interstate 10 — covering most of the city — should take extra caution with threats of storm surge between 5 and 7 feet, Cheriogotis said.

The mayor urged residents to have an emergency preparedness plan in place. So far, the southern portion of the city was under a voluntary evacuation zone, according to the Mobile County Emergency Management Agency.

At least three shelters are set to open beginning at 8 a.m. on Friday, according to a city update Thursday afternoon. The city’s Second Line Transit is offering transportation from a designated pickup location at the Bel Air Mall. After 9:30 a.m., service will stop running to the local shelters.

Lessons learned after deadly Hurricane Helene, Georgia governor says

As Hurricane Isaias gets closer to landfall, Georgia Gov. Brian Kemp said he learned valuable lessons from the devastating Hurricane Helene that hit the state in 2024.

“We just want to be overly prepared in case something changes, or if the storm moves, which we certainly saw that happen with Hurricane Helene, and we just want our fellow citizens to be prepared,” he said, announcing that he was declaring a state of emergency.

Helene made landfall in Florida as a monstrous Category 4 hurricane in September 2024 and ripped all the way up into the Appalachians. The storm’s wind and tornadoes killed at least 28 people in Georgia, according to the National Hurricane Center.

Many residents said they did not know they were in the storm’s direct path until the last minute.

“That’s a good lesson for all of us. These storms can move and turn and you just don’t really know,” he said. “If you’re not ready, it can be really devastating and especially from a loss of life standpoint.”

“If you don’t leave, you can’t leave.” Gulf Shores mayor urges residents to evacuate soon

Gulf Shores Mayor Robert Craft joined CNN’s Isabel Rosales on Thursday to discuss evacuations as the Alabama community prepares for Hurricane Isaias.

The area is under a voluntary evacuation request as of Thursday afternoon, but Craft is urging the governor’s office to issue a mandatory evacuation order.

Isaias' head-on impact could worsen storm surge

Isaias is expected to turn north and slam into the southern US nearly perpendicular to the Gulf Coast. That kind of direct approach drives ocean water straight toward land with little room to spread out, so water builds up as storm surge along the shore and in bays.

Winds on Isaias’ eastern side blow in the same direction the storm will be moving, and the combined motion will create stronger winds and push more water toward the coast.

The deepest flooding is expected near and east of where the storm makes landfall, with storm surge up to 6 feet possible as far east as Florida’s Big Bend, according to the National Hurricane Center.

Landfall is forecast near the Alabama and Florida border late Friday or early Saturday, but that spot could shift as Isaias nears the coast.

Isaias' forward speed will help carry damaging winds farther inland

Hurricane Isaias will pick up speed as it moves ashore, which will affect which parts of the Southeast are most impacted by this storm.

Surface winds typically weaken once a hurricane is over land, but a faster-moving storm can carry its powerful winds farther inland.

Damaging wind gusts up to 73 mph are possible in parts of southern Alabama and eastern Mississippi, more than 100 miles from shore.

This effect will likely be especially pronounced just east of where Isaias makes landfall. With the storm traveling north, the winds on its eastern side will blow in the same direction as the storm is moving — the combined speed will create stronger winds on that side.

More evacuations ordered in Florida

Mandatory evacuations have been issued for areas of Walton and Escambia counties as of noon Thursday. The Escambia County evacuation zones include Perdido Key and Pensacola Beach, but the order also applies to anyone living in a mobile home or low-lying area regardless of their official zone. Okaloosa County also ordered evacuations for certain areas, CNN previously reported.

Englin Air Force Base in Okaloosa County has issued a “limited evacuation order” for “service members, Department of War employees and their dependents” residing in certain areas of Okaloosa and Walton County.

Forecast rainfall totals tick up for Gulf Coast

Isaias is going to be moving quickly when it nears and moves through the Gulf Coast, but it could still drop a considerable amount of rain.

Projected totals increased today after forecast models picked up the possibility of Isaias dumping rain on parts of southern Alabama and the Florida Panhandle for much of Saturday morning.

Hard-hit areas could pick up 3 to 7 inches of rain, but up to 15 inches is possible in a handful of spots where storms move over the same location repeatedly.

For context, a place like Pensacola, Florida, typically gets about 4.7 inches in all of October. At least a month’s worth of rain could fall in some locations in a matter of hours.

Which states have declared a state of emergency

Florida and Alabama have declared a state of emergency as they brace for Hurricane Isaias to make landfall.

Florida

On Tuesday, Gov. Ron DeSantis urged residents of the state to make a plan and stock their emergency supply kits.

The state of emergency declaration, issued for 25 counties, “ensures communities have the time and resources necessary to prepare for potential severe weather impacts to the Panhandle and Big Bend region of Florida,” he said in a post on X.

Florida also has a State Assistance Information Line (SAIL) available for anyone needing assistance or information: 1-800-342-3557

Alabama

Gov. Kay Ivey declared a state of emergency for 40 counties. “While I hope for minimal impacts, we must take preparations seriously,” she said.

Ivey also announced that state offices and buildings will close at noon Friday in several counties, according to a statement.

Georgia

Gov. Brian Kemp declared a state of emergency Thursday. The order, which applies to the entire state of Georgia, allows for storm preparations and the mobilization of up to 500 National Guard troops if necessary, Kemp said at a news conference Thursday.

“We don’t believe we’re going to need this, but we want to have that option should something change with the storm and its movement over the next 24 hours or so, and certainly, as the storm hits,” he said.

What we’re seeing from space

Weather satellites show Hurricane Isaias swirling and strengthening in the Gulf as it pushes north. It also appears that the storm has a long tail-like cloud extending to the northeast.

This is something we’ve seen before — a similar feature formed on Hurricane Ian in September 2022. They develop as fast winds at high levels of the atmosphere stretch and pull a hurricane’s clouds to the northeast.

It’s also a clue to the atmospheric conditions the storm is approaching and how it could impact the storm. Once the center of Isaias gets closer to the coast, and closer to these winds, it will begin to get pulled apart and weaken.

Florida universities, public schools shutter

The Florida State University campus in Panama City and the university’s College of Medicine Regional Medical Campus in Pensacola are closed Thursday and Friday. The Tallahassee Campus will shift to remote classes Friday before closing completely at noon. Multiple university sporting events have been cancelled or postponed.

Additional state colleges and universities will also be closed, including Pensacola State College and University of West Florida

At least 14 Florida Panhandle counties are closing public schools Friday, and many have closed Thursday, according to the state’s Department of Education.

Nearly two-thirds of Gulf of Mexico oil production is shut down as Hurricane Isaias nears

Almost two-thirds of the current oil production in the Gulf of Mexico has been shut down as Hurricane Isaias moves towards landfall, federal regulators said Thursday.

As of 12 p.m. ET, personnel have been evacuated from 121 production platforms (33% of the total) in the Gulf of Mexico, according to the Marine Minerals Administration, an agency within the Interior Department.

Officials say personnel have also been evacuated from 5 of the 11 drilling rigs operating in the Gulf. BP said in a statement it removed all personnel from two of its platforms in the Gulf and evacuated non-essential workers from two other platforms.

The storm has caused operators in the Gulf of Mexico to shut in about 1.3 million barrels of daily oil production, accounting for 63% of the region’s output.

Another 57% of the natural gas production in the Gulf has been shut down, the Marine Minerals Administration said.

Oil and natural gas companies typically shut down production activities as a precaution when hurricanes threaten the region. This storm comes at a delicate time as the global energy system has already been pushed to the limit by the Iran war and Russia-Ukraine war.

Double red flags flying in Gulf Shores

CNN’s crew captured current beach conditions in Gulf Shores, Alabama, on Thursday. The double red flags mean going into the water is prohibited. The city’s mayor told CNN he’s asking state officials to order mandatory evacuations for everyone in low-lying, flood-prone areas.

The National Hurricane Center bet big on AI to forecast Hurricane Isaias

When Isaias first formed on Tuesday, computer models diverged sharply on where it would hit and how strong it would get.

While traditional models, based in atmospheric physics, generally showed a weaker storm moving toward the northern Gulf Coast, a prominent AI-driven model from Google DeepMind consistently showed a stronger storm, tracking farther northeast. NHC meteorologists tailored their forecasts to more closely match what DeepMind was projecting. So far, that bet has paid off.

That’s in keeping with past experience. The DeepMind AI model performed extremely well last season, according to NHC director Michael Brennan, and has been accurate in anticipating the intensity of many Pacific storms this season. “The AI models have shown more consistency” from one model run to the next, Brennan said, regarding the reliance on DeepMind for Hurricane Isaias.

Red Cross mobilizes supplies and personnel to Gulf Coast ahead of hurricane

The American Red Cross is surging resources and supplies to areas in the path of Hurricane Isaias, it said in a statement.

Ahead of the storm’s forecasted landfall Friday night, the organization said it is mobilizing more than 370 trained disaster volunteers across the Gulf Coast region. Many of the volunteers are local responders, it said.

It has also prepositioned blood supplies and relief items like blankets and shelf-stable food, the Red Cross said.

“Leave town,” Orange Beach, Alabama, administrator tells visitors ahead of Isaias

Visitors to Orange Beach, Alabama, are encouraged to “make that decision and leave town” ahead of Hurricane Isaias, Orange Beach City Administrator Ford Handley say.

The city, which has around 8,500 permanent residents, currently has almost 80,000 people within its city limits, according to Handley, with many in town for the neighboring Gulf Shores’ annual National Shrimp Festival, which has been canceled.

The City of Orange Beach is currently under a voluntary evacuation for all non-residents, but city officials have pushed the governor of Alabama to issue a mandatory evacuation for all non-residents.

“We’ll hopefully have that by the end of the day,” Hadley said.

Shelters opening in Mobile County, Alabama, tomorrow morning

Three shelters will open tomorrow morning in Mobile County, Alabama, ahead of Hurricane Isaias.

These are the locations of the shelters, according to the Mobile County Emergency Management Agency. They will open at 8 a.m. CT on October 9.

  • Theodore High School
  • Baker High School
  • Cranford Burns Middle School: Mobile County Health Department personnel will be on site for anyone with medical needs, officials said.

People should bring any medications, food, water and cleaning items with them, as well as blankets and pillows, the agency said.

Residents can also call 2-1-1 for additional disaster information and to arrange a ride to one of the shelters.

The Mobile and Gulf Shores area of the state are expected to see the peak impact of the storm late Friday afternoon into early Saturday morning.

Isaias' strength stays steady... for now

Isaias remained a Category 1 hurricane with 85 mph sustained winds as of the 1 p.m. CT update from the National Hurricane Center.

It seems the storm is taking a little breather from strengthening, but that’s not expected to last long. The hurricane center still calls for it to become a Category 2 hurricane (wind speeds of 96 to 110 mph) later today.

Isaias is currently located about 350 miles south of Louisiana, moving east-northeast at 10 mph. It’s expected to start to turn more to the northeast later today and then head to the north on Friday.

Isaias could landfall in the same area Hurricane Sally did

Hurricane Isaias is predicted to landfall very close to where Category 2 Hurricane Sally pushed ashore in September 2020, but the outcome won’t be identical.

Isaias is expected to accelerate toward the coast and weaken some before pushing inland as a Category 1 or 2. Sally, on the other hand, moved sluggishly and intensified as it pushed ashore. That means Sally’s strong winds and heavy rainfall battered the coast for longer than Isaias’ will.

Storm surge is where the two hurricanes could share a common trait.

Sally’s combination of surge and tides reached 5 to 7 feet above ground level in Baldwin County, Alabama, and Escambia and Santa Rosa counties in Florida, according to the National Hurricane Center. The predicted storm surge for Isaias along the Alabama coast and the western Florida Panhandle is the exact same.

What places take the brunt of Isaias’ most damaging surge could also differ from what happened during Sally depending on the hurricane’s precise track.
