Usher, Chris Brown Cancel Four Newly Added Stadium Shows, Citing Crew Health

TheTools · 12.10.2026 11:09 · source · permalink

Home »

Usher and Chris Brown have canceled four October stadium concerts on their joint “The R&B Tour,” with organizers citing concerns about the health and well-being of the touring party and crew. Notably, the cancellations eliminate all four new markets added during an eight-show expansion in August that promoters attributed to overwhelming fan demand.

The cancellations were announced Friday, October 9, affecting scheduled performances in Baltimore, Cincinnati, Columbia, South Carolina, and Indianapolis. Ticketmaster has confirmed the cancellations, with refunds being issued to affected ticketholders.

In an official statement published by Lucas Oil Stadium, the Indianapolis venue said the event promoter made the decision following an extensive run of sold-out stadium performances.

The cancellations were necessary “to prioritize the health and well-being of the touring party and crew,” according to the statement.

The promoter added that the demands of a tour of this length and scale were significant, and that the adjustments would allow the remaining performances to continue safely and sustainably. No specific illness, injury or other medical circumstances were disclosed.

The cancellations are particularly notable because all four affected cities were introduced to the itinerary during the tour’s August 17 expansion.

As TicketNews reported at the time, the Live Nation-produced tour announced eight additional stadium performances, with organizers citing extraordinary demand following the successful opening months of the co-headlining run.

Four of those additions represented entirely new markets: Baltimore, Cincinnati, Columbia and Indianapolis. The other four were additional performances in markets already on the itinerary, including East Rutherford, New Jersey; Atlanta; Los Angeles; and Miami.

All four newly introduced markets have now been canceled, while the additional performances in established markets remain scheduled.

The August expansion was promoted as extending the tour to 59 performances. At the time, organizers reported that the first 13 concerts had sold more than 566,000 tickets, while the tour generated more than $101 million in grosses from its first eight shows.

The selective cancellations have prompted speculation that ticket sales in the newly added markets may not have met expectations.

Entertainment commentator Roger Friedman, writing for Showbiz411, suggested disappointing sales were responsible for the canceled performances, pointing to substantial unsold inventory visible on Ticketmaster seating maps. Friedman also highlighted available inventory for upcoming concerts in other major markets.

Another detail raising questions about demand emerged from Indianapolis, where Lucas Oil Stadium had been promoting a giveaway for two free tickets to the October 31 concert just days before announcing its cancellation.

The stadium’s social media promotion was highlighted in weekend reporting by Complex and DancehallMag.

Ticket giveaways are a common concert promotion strategy and do not necessarily indicate weak demand. However, the venue’s effort to generate additional attention for the Indianapolis performance shortly before its cancellation adds another detail to the questions surrounding the commercial performance of the newly announced dates.

The fact that all four cancellations occurred in markets added during the same August expansion, rather than across the broader tour schedule, further distinguishes the situation from a typical reduction in touring dates for health or logistical reasons.

Still, neither Live Nation nor the affected venues have publicly attributed the cancellations to disappointing ticket sales. Verified sales figures for the four canceled concerts have not been released, and the volume of available inventory on a ticketing website does not necessarily establish total sales.

The tour has also demonstrated substantial commercial success elsewhere, making it possible that demand in some newly introduced markets differed significantly from the established markets that received additional performances.

The cancellations also come ahead of an important court appearance for Brown in the United Kingdom.

Brown is scheduled to be sentenced October 26 at London’s Southwark Crown Court after pleading guilty in July to affray in connection with a February 2023 nightclub attack.

According to the UK Crown Prosecution Service, Brown and his vocal coach, Omololu Akinlolu, entered guilty pleas on July 24.

Under his bail conditions, Brown was permitted to leave the United Kingdom to continue performing but was ordered to return by October 25, one day before sentencing.

That court appearance falls within the period affected by the canceled concerts, although organizers have not publicly linked the decision to Brown’s legal proceedings.

The October 26 sentencing date was already established when the additional concerts were announced in August. TicketNews noted the upcoming hearing in its original coverage of the tour expansion.

For fans holding tickets to the canceled shows, Ticketmaster says refunds will be issued automatically to the original payment method, generally within 14 to 21 days. If tickets were transferred to another person, the refund will be issued to the original purchaser.

The remaining tour dates are still scheduled to proceed, including an October 15 performance at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey. Following the canceled October dates, the tour is scheduled to resume November 5 with five performances at Atlanta’s Mercedes-Benz Stadium through November 11.

Additional performances remain scheduled in Los Angeles, New Orleans, Houston, Miami and Tampa through December.

Organizers have not announced replacement dates for the four canceled performances.
