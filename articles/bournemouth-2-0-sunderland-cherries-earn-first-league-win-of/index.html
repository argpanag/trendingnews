    FT: Brighton 5-0 Crystal Palace

    Crystal Palace

    Crystal Palace boss Pierre Sage told BBC Sport: "Only one feeling - shame. When you play this kind of game you have to show another face. It was our worst face.

    "We cannot accept that especially at the beginning of the game. We didn’t fight at this moment. Once it was 3-0 after nine minutes the game is really different.

    "Especially in the way we don’t understand in which context we were. In this kind of game you need different ingredients. It’s my fault because I am the coach, I gave the angle to the players. I gave the motivation. I prepare the situations. We played so bad it’s my fault.

    "I don’t want to speak really individually or thinking about details. I have to analyse what’s happened to take a decision.

    "At this moment once the score is [large difference] it’s easier to play. It’s about bravery. We have to deal with these things from the beginning of the game."

    On his message to the fans: "I’m really sorry because we didn’t give them what they deserved. I apologise because we are not Palace today."

  2. 'It's very painful'published at 18:56 BST
    18:56 BST

    FT: Bournemouth 2-0 Sunderland

    Sunderland

    Sunderland boss Regis le Bris, speaking to Sky Sports: "Tough game, disappointing game., really clear. Tough start, after six minutes 1-0 down then 15 minutes we played with 10 men for 75 [minutes] or even more. At half-time we decided to at least survive in the second half to make something happen. "We were below the level, it's really clear. It's hard to take, hard to digest. It's very painful because we were not Sunderland an it's not acceptable."

    On what he wants to see from his team: "Reaction. We will have ups and downs in thew season, this one is a big down. I'm very disappointed for the fans, they were with us until the last minute but they were not on the pitch which is a big problem in the Premier League because the opponent tis strong. They are relentless, if you make mistakes they will seize them and it was the case today."

    On whether he's worried: "To be fair, I'm always worried because it's my job too find the little problems and try to help the players to go higher. Today the job will be massive because it was a bad game. We won't find excuses. We'll keep working hard, stay humble. We know our position in this league. We have a busy schedule. We want to embrace the challenge but the position at the minute is not good."

    "We were clear in the dressing room. The players know that they were not at the level. I am with them 100 per cent. From that point we want to show the energy, we want to show belief, we want to show more from the team like Sunderland used to be and today wasn't the case."

  3. 'We have to be better than that'published at 18:55 BST
    18:55 BST

    FT: Leeds 1-1 Man Utd

    Manchester United

    Manchester United defender Harry Maguire speaking to Sky Sports: "You can't question the togetherness, spirit and fight. It's a tough place to come, Leeds have been flying in the league. But we have to be better than that, we have to show a bit more quality with the ball and when you come to places like this you can't get beat on set plays and that's what we've done and it's got them back in the game. We felt comfortable in our shape and they didn't really create too many chances so then to concede a set play just after half time it's disappointing. It changed the game and we had to reset. We had to dig in, there were 15 minutes where the crowd was up. We got our foot back in the game after 65-70 minutes and probably looked like the more likely to go on and win it but a draw is probably a fair result."

    On shape: "We worked on it this week. We wanted to match them up with the shape and be aggressive. We knew we could get joy playing through the middle with them only paying two and I feel like that's the area of the game where we could've done more, been a bit more brave and took the ball a bit more under pressure. We're in a situation where we're not as brave as we should be. We need to get that back because to win football matches for Manchester United you have to have an element of bravery."

    On Lisandro Martinez injury: "He went off with a knock, fingers crossed nothing serious. It's a game of moments and goals change games. The shape was good first half and we come out second half and concede a set play. When you come away to these type of places you can't get beat on a set play, we did it against Hull in the first game and here today so it's disappointing. It wasn't like they were cutting us open and creating loads of chances. We showed great spirit after we weathered the storm. We're disappointed and we know that this season we haven't done that enough."

    Team low on confidence? "The confidence in the team has maybe took a bit of a hit, you have to dig in. Spirit, togetherness and hard work will get you through games but you have to show a bit of quality and composure too. It grew in the game but their goal knocked our confidence. It will come. I have belief in this group we can do that.

    "It's [belief] tested all the time when you play for this club. We haven't played well enough to win football matches this season. The boys have to finds something within themselves to help the team and help the group."

    On Lisandro Martinez: "There has been a lot of noise around the club this week. Lisandro has apologised and we have moved on from it. I thought he was brilliant today so credit to him and let's hope it's not a serious injury."

Evanilson scored his second of the season in Bournemouth win

Bournemouth lifted themselves out of the relegation zone as goals from Marcus Tavernier and Evanilson secured their first Premier League win of the season while Sunderland's struggles continued.

Both teams began the day in the bottom three after poor starts this season on the domestic front despite winning their opening two Europa League games.

But Bournemouth boss Marco Rose got his first three points as a Premier League manager as Sunderland remain winless on the road this season and without a league victory since beating Fulham on 30 August.

Sunderland got off to a nightmare start of their own doing as defender Kevin Danso endured a disastrous17 minutes in which he helped Bournemouth to their opener and then was handed a red card for denying a clear goalscoring opportunity.

Cherries striker Evanilson bullied past the former Tottenham defender in the sixth minute before crossing for Tavernier to head home into an empty net for his fourth of the season.

Then just 11 minutes later, Evanilson's strength saw him get in front of Danso and as the defender fell, he pulled down the Bournemouth forward, who was on the edge of the area and in on goal, which saw referee Craig Pawson brandish the red card.

Black Cats goalkeeper Robin Roefs did keep them in the game with some good saves as he twice denied Brazilian winger Rayan.

Adam Smith should have scored on the rebound from one of Rayan's saved efforts but Sunderland gifted Bournemouth a second just three minutes after the restart.

Omar Alderete's back pass to Roefs was way too short which allowed Evanilson in and the Brazilian striker rounded the Sunderland keeper before sliding the ball home.

Bournemouth analysis: Timely win helped by in-form Tavernier

Marco Rose post-match interview - Bournemouth v Sunderland

Tavernier is a tone setter for this Bournemouth team this season.

The Englishman has now scored four goals this season – he is their top scorer – and three of them have been openers in games.

This time it helped lead to a first league victory which will help build confidence in Rose's team.

They have had some positive performances but were thrashed last week at Chelsea for their third defeat of the season which meant they needed to respond here.

They were obviously helped by Sunderland's calamity defending but with games against Manchester United, Aston Villa and Leeds United to come in the Premier League with a European date with Italian giants AC Milan before that run, it was a timely victory to lift them up the table.

If Tavernier and Evanilson can keep up this form then they will be a real challenge for those teams in the next few weeks.

Sunderland analysis: Black Cats yet to find last season's impressive form

Sunderland performance at Bournemouth was 'very painful' - Le Bris

Is this second season syndrome or are Sunderland struggling to deal with juggling European football and domestic duties?

They have now lost both their league games on the weekend after dates on the continent in the Europa League, albeit the previous one was an eight-goal thriller against league leaders Manchester City.

They have also had some tough fixtures so far including that trip to City as they have also faced champions Arsenal and in-form Brighton.

Yet after seven games last season as a newly-promoted side they had 11 points and at the same stage this term they have just four.

They had just three shots across the whole game and never tested Bournemouth goalkeeper Djordje Petrovic while the Cherries should have scored more from their 29 attempts.

This defeat may be put down to Danso's early errors and a gift of a second goal but head coach Regis Le Bris will hope last season's impressive form soon returns as they have now lost five games already and are struggling.
