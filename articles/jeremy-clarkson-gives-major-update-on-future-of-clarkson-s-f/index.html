Jeremy Clarkson gives major update on future of Clarkson’s Farm

TheTools · 23.09.2026 00:12 · source · permalink

Jeremy Clarkson has given a major update on the future of Clarkson's Farm.

Te former Top Gear presenter has revealed that filming for the sixth series of the hit Prime Video show is almost complete, with production at his Oxfordshire-based Diddly Squat Farm entering its final stages.

According to a report by Farmers Guide, Mr Clarkson said he and the production team are just a couple of weeks away from wrapping up filming for season six.

Speaking to Radio Times, the 66-year-old also opened up about the challenges he has faced during what he described as an "enormously challenging year" for farming.

The TV star explained how balancing the demands of his farming venture and his pub business has created a frustrating dilemma.

Mr Clarkson said: "When it's sunny, the pub does really well, but the farm does really badly.

"And when it rains, the farm does really well, and so I can never be completely happy."

He added: "Whatever happens with the weather, I'm going to say: 'Oh, it's disastrous at the pub' or 'Oh, it's disastrous at the farm'."

The presenter also painted a bleak picture of conditions faced by farmers during the latest growing season.

Speaking about agricultural production, Mr Clarkson said: "But for farmers, this has been a catastrophic year, and we've grown three ounces of hen food. You know, just an absolute waste of time."

The update will be welcomed by fans of Clarkson's Farm, which has become one of Prime Video's most successful factual entertainment programmes since launching in 2021.

The series follows Mr Clarkson's attempts to run Diddly Squat Farm near Chipping Norton, alongside farm manager Kaleb Cooper and a cast of local farming figures.

While the latest series has not yet been given a release date, Farmers Guide reported that it is expected to arrive in spring or summer 2027.

Mr Clarkson remained tight-lipped when asked about the show's longer-term future, offering only a brief response.

He said: "Nearly done six, and then who knows."
