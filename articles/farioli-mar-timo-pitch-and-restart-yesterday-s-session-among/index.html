Farioli, Marítimo pitch and restart: “Yesterday’s session, among the best…”

Francesco Farioli, FC Porto’s head coach, previewed the away trip to Marítimo in the Primeira Liga, mindful of the challenge posed by the opposition and the condition of the pitch, but leaving no room for excuses. Returning from a longer-than-usual international break, he highlighted the quality of the team’s work and the need to rediscover their rhythm. Speaking about the match, he warned: “It will be a challenging game for several reasons.”

The victory in the clássico left a positive feeling at the end of the first stretch of the season, but the schedule brought a break before a new run of games. Francesco Farioli framed this return as an exercise in continuity: restoring the connection between the players, maintaining the intensity of their work and preparing for an away trip that demands more than confidence in the team’s current form.

His assessment of the visit to Marítimo went beyond the condition of the pitch. Farioli recalled FC Porto’s historical difficulties at that ground and highlighted the opposition’s ability to fight for results right up to the final minutes.

“Historically, they are a team that has always caused FC Porto problems. Statistics show that it is the fourth-hardest ground to get a result at. That is something we have to keep in mind. They have been doing well since returning to the Primeira Liga, thanks to the coach’s work. They never give up and have managed to turn results around in the final minutes on three occasions. It will be a challenging game for several reasons,” he said. “The issue of the pitch has been raised several times. I hope they have done everything possible during this break to ensure the best conditions. They want that too. Hopefully, tomorrow the pitch will be in the best possible condition, but there will be no excuses.”

The pitch is therefore an acknowledged concern, rather than an excuse offered in advance. By recognising Marítimo’s persistence and rejecting excuses, the coach put his own team’s response at the heart of their preparations, regardless of the conditions they encounter.

Regarding the international break, Farioli distinguished between the interruption to their run of results and the continuity of their work. The friendly against Deportivo helped maintain their rhythm, while the players’ return brought the focus back to reconnecting with the team’s collective principles, with Santi [Giménez]’s situation still to be assessed.

“We finished the first stretch with very positive feelings. This break interrupted our momentum in terms of results, but not our desire to work and improve. The friendly against Deportivo helped us maintain our rhythm and work on collective aspects and small individual details. The players worked very well and, as of yesterday, the whole team is back, apart from Santi [Giménez], whose situation we will have to assess today. It is a challenge because the break normally lasted two weeks, but this time it was more than 20 days, with players scattered all over the world,” he explained. “Now it is time for them to reconnect with our patterns of play and our principles. I hope tomorrow they can reproduce yesterday’s session, which, for me, was one of the best ever. I often talk about the intensity of our work, but yesterday I had to tell them to stop. If it had been up to me, I would have carried on, because the session was fantastic. We need the right mentality to face this next run of games: nine matches, including a friendly, across several competitions.”

His praise for the training session reflects Farioli’s confidence in how the team has come back together, but it comes with a specific demand: taking that quality into the match. After a break of more than 20 days, the challenge is to turn their enthusiasm in training into performances that meet the demands of a nine-match run across several competitions, including a friendly.
