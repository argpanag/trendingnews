Maybe Nvidia (NVDA) doesn't need a big free-agent CEO signing to replace highly visible founder and former CEO Jensen Huang.

"If you don't bring Jensen back, I would not hire an outsider," Amazon (AMZN) founder and former CEO Jeff Bezos said at a Semafor conference on Tuesday. "Nvidia is too complicated. I think outside CEOs in complex companies have a vanishingly small chance of succeeding."

Bezos makes a good point.

A new CEO often comes in and runs the standard playbook from an MBA class: Do a listening tour with employees to better understand the business. Meet with the existing C-suite to see who they want to keep and who should get pushed out.

Six months into the job, the new CEO unveils a splashy growth plan that often includes profit-boosting layoffs.

And away they go.

The thing is, Nvidia doesn't need any of this playbook inserted into its unique culture Huang created. It also doesn't need its important operations disrupted by a meddling new CEO seeking praise from the world.

At best, Nvidia needs an internal CEO hire — someone with Huang's technical prowess who could sell the sizzle to a global audience.

At worst, it needs a big-name veteran CEO who promises the board no major overhauls, just small tweaks where needed. That person stays in the job for a few years and establishes a strong succession plan — one that Huang never whipped up.

For his part, Bezos and his board named then-AWS leader and longtime Amazon exec Andy Jassy to succeed him as CEO in 2021.

"I don't think it needs a big splashy hire. I think they have incredible bench strength in there," investor and "Shark Tank" star Kevin O'Leary said on Sozzi Unleashed. "I think they have a tremendous amount of engineers designing the next two generations of chips. I think it's a machine unto itself right now."

Who is Colette Kress, Nvidia's new interim CEO?

Interim CEO Colette Kress made a solid case late Tuesday for staying in the interim CEO role for perhaps longer than Wall Street expects. The company's third quarter was solid, the outlook strong, and Kress came across as a steady hand driving the Nvidia rocketship.

For the third quarter, Nvidia posted adjusted earnings of $2.68 a share on revenue of $115.8 billion. Wall Street had expected earnings of $2.47 a share on revenue of $109.15 billion.

Nvidia expects fourth quarter revenue of $127.4 billion to $132.6 billion, comfortably above the $125.25 billion analysts were looking for.

The company said it sees fiscal year 2028 revenue growth of 75%, up from a prior estimate of 70% offered just three months ago.
