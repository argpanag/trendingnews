Live updates from SpaceX SES satellite launch from Cape Canaveral, Florida

TheTools · 13.09.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

Launch recap: Scroll down to review live updates from the Sunday, Sept. 13, liftoff of a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral on SES's final O3b mPOWER mission.

Original story: A SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket will propel three SES O3b mPOWER satellites into medium-Earth orbit this afternoon — so long as sea-breeze-fueled storms and clouds don't force a scrub at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station.

Welcome to FLORIDA TODAY Space Team live coverage of today's SpaceX mission. Technicians continue targeting 2:49 p.m. for liftoff from Launch Complex 40.

The trio of satellites will complete SES's O3b mPOWER constellation, joining 10 others already in operation for the Luxembourg-based global satellite operator.

However, weather is a watch item. The Space Force's 45th Weather Squadron pegged the odds of favorable launch conditions at 60% early during the window, degrading to 40% by the end. Cumulus clouds, surface electric fields and anvil clouds pose threats as abundant low-level moisture supports scattered-to-numerous afternoon thunderstorms fueled by sea breeze.

Today's mission will mark the 29th liftoff for the Falcon 9 first-stage booster, which will soar due east and target landing on the SpaceX drone ship A Shortfall of Gravitas in the Atlantic Ocean. So, no Central Florida sonic booms should occur.

SpaceX launch weather: Gulf Coast storms should track toward Florida's Space Coast

Rick Neale

From the National Weather Service station at Melbourne Orlando International Airport: "Scattered storms on the Gulf Coast should propagate toward the interior this afternoon, with coverage peaking over east central Florida between 4 pm and 8 pm."

The 45th Weather Squadron's forecast narrative noted, "with the sea breeze over the Spaceport, the risk early in the window will be for showers and storms to quickly develop in the immediate vicinity."

"The threat transitions just inland later in the window as numerous collisions between ongoing activity likely drift back towards the Spaceport along with any lingering storms," the forecast said.
