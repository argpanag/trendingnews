BCP Council branded ‘zombie’ and 'asleep at the wheel' by Labour group leader

TheTools · 02.11.2026 07:35 · source · permalink

BCP Council has been branded a "zombie" administration that's "asleep at the wheel".

The attack by BCP Labour group leader Cllr Patrick Canavan follows an Echo investigation revealing that BCP Council leaders have held no meetings with government ministers about the authority's worsening financial crisis.

Cllr Canavan said: "BCP Council should not be putting out press releases in the hope the government may see them. This is a council leadership that's asleep at the wheel. It's a zombie council.

"The responsibility rests with BCP Council leaders to secure critically important meetings with the most important government ministers to make the case directly and press for the support our residents need.

"Issuing press releases is no substitute for engagement with Government. The council needs to be actively lobbying, building relationships, and getting around the table with decision-makers, including our MPs, to explain the scale of the pressures and the consequences for services.

"Our residents need action and results, not another council press release today outlining the problem they've created."

A Freedom of Information (FOI) request revealed neither council leader Millie Earl nor finance cabinet member Mike Cox arranged any meetings with Treasury or Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG) ministers between July 4, 2024, and September 9, 2026.

Furthermore, no formal requests for meetings were made by either leader between July 2024 and late January 2026.

Between January and September 2026, Cllr Earl submitted just one formal meeting request, while Cllr Cox submitted none, resulting in zero ministerial meetings being conducted.

The findings clash with claims that the council was lobbying Whitehall, including Cllr Cox's statement in September that it had been "shouting, screaming, lobbying" over funding shortfalls.

They also come at a time when council tax increases across BCP have risen far above the U.K. average, hitting struggling residents even harder in their finances.

For the 2026/27 financial year, BCP (Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole) Council increased its council tax by 6.74 per cent, which is above the standard 4.99 per cent general referendum cap and required special government permission.

BCP Council continues to face immense fiscal strain, including a forecast in-year overspend exceeding £32 million and a projected £38 million shortfall for the upcoming financial year.

The authority is burdened by a Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) deficit expected to reach £380 million by March 2028, incurring an annual interest bill of £10.5 million.

Bournemouth East MP Tom Hayes backed criticisms of the administration, arguing that council leaders had been "lobbing stones" at ministers rather than chasing direct engagement.

Mr Hayes highlighted that while the central government had reduced historic SEND debt by £165 million, local leaders must work constructively to tackle the remaining balance.

Cllr Mike Cox defended the council, saying its leaders had written to ministers several times and highlighted social-care pressures publicly.

He added that they would welcome an official meeting with ministers.
