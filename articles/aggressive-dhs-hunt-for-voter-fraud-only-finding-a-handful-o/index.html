Aggressive DHS hunt for voter fraud only finding a handful of possible noncitizen cases

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:09 · source · permalink

The Trump administration ramped up its hunt for noncitizens on voter rolls this week as the president continued to claim without evidence that such fraud is widespread and damaging America’s elections. 

But the rapidly expanding campaign by the Department of Homeland Security to track down voter fraud is failing to produce proof that backs up President Donald Trump’s claims that tens of thousands of noncitizens are illegally registered and voting. 

Across the nine states reportedly targeted in the surge, some said they had yet to hear from Homeland Security about any possible voter fraud investigations. Others were subpoenaed this week for voting history on what amounted to a small number of individuals compared to the states’ voting populations. Though more subpoenas could come, they haven’t come close to the tens of thousands of “potential” noncitizens the Trump administration claims to have identified on voter rolls.

On Tuesday, the secretary of state’s office in Georgia, one of the targeted states, received an “Immigration Enforcement Subpoena” requesting information about 13 individuals, according to a copy of the subpoena reviewed by MS NOW and a state election official. 

The subpoena requests an “Individual Voter Report including all voting history and participation and a copy of any available voting documents including identity and/or citizenship documents” for the 13 individuals, “pursuant to a criminal investigation being conducted by the US Immigration & Customs Enforcement, Homeland Security Investigations.” 

In Washington, Border Patrol officers requested similar information for two individuals this week, Washington Secretary of State Steve Hobbs told MS NOW. 

On Thursday the Wisconsin Elections Commission received subpoenas for roughly 15 individuals, requesting voting history, an election official in the state told MS NOW. The official said the information ultimately shared with DHS in such instances is all publicly available data.

“The small numbers of subpoenas that they’ve issued show that this is not the widespread phenomena they wanted the public to believe it could be,” said Andrea Flores, a former immigration policy adviser and DHS official during the Obama and Biden administrations. “It also should be a sign to Congress that they need to step in and demand information about why this entire investigatory agency is continuing to look for noncitizen voters and not continue their transnational criminal investigations.”

Flores — along with election officials, civil rights advocates and some former DHS officials — has warned that citizens have already been and could continue to be ensnared in the Trump administration’s noncitizen voter hunt, potentially resulting in eligible voters being purged from rolls or prosecutions against citizens helping with voter registration.

Asked about the latest “coordinated criminal voter fraud surge initiative” targeting specific states, which was first reported by CNN, an unnamed DHS spokesperson said “while ICE Homeland Security Investigations is not able to comment on any active investigations, HSI is actively rooting out and investigating election fraud wherever it can be found.” The states where investigations are reportedly ramping up include Washington, Wisconsin, Missouri, Georgia, California, Pennsylvania, Nevada, New York and Connecticut. 

“DHS is laser-focused on prosecuting and removing aliens who vote and preventing the dilution of the votes of U.S. citizens,” the DHS spokesperson told MS NOW.

For years, Trump has made outlandish and baseless claims that noncitizens are to blame for Republican electoral losses and that Democrats are “rigging” elections by registering noncitizens to vote.  

The search, first launched by DHS last year at the explicit direction of the White House, has steadily increased with more workers and resources diverted from across the department to work on the effort. 

Homeland Security Investigations agents, who usually investigate major crimes like human trafficking, terrorism and child exploitation, were tasked with launching the effort last year. And more HSI agents have since been assigned in field offices across the country to sift through voter data and “amazingly large” spreadsheets that were handed over to agents with no clear explanation on how the lists were produced or where they came from, according to two former HSI officials with knowledge of the initiative. 

In February the “nationwide program to investigate allegations of voter fraud” expanded as HSI agents were directed to investigate and prosecute naturalized citizens who may have improperly voted in past elections, MS NOW reported at the time.
