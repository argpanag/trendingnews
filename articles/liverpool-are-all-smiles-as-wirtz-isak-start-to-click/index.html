Liverpool are all smiles as Wirtz, Isak start to click

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:48 · source · permalink

BRUGES, Belgium -- Florian Wirtz was bemused by the question. When it was put to the Liverpool midfielder this week that he has seemingly rediscovered his smile of late, he was quick to set the record straight.

"I've heard a lot of people talking about my smiling or if I don't smile that much, maybe that's only because the people don't really know me," he said in a news conference ahead of Liverpool's UEFA Champions League clash with Club Brugge. "Normally I'm a very positive guy ... I'm just trying to play football -- that's my job and that's what I'm trying to do."

Certainly, after scoring in a fifth consecutive game for club and country in the Reds' 3-0 victory over Brugge on Wednesday, Wirtz can consider it a job well done. The 23-year-old has been under a microscope since his £116 million move from Bayer Leverkusen in the summer of 2025, with every facet of every underwhelming performance forensically dissected.

His record in Germany -- where he registered 53 goals and 59 assists in 186 appearances for Leverkusen -- suggested he would be an instant hit at Anfield. But, after a disappointing debut campaign, Wirtz's slow-burn start to his second season has now well and truly burst into life.

After providing an assist and a spectacular goal in Liverpool's 4-0 demolition of Manchester United on Sunday, Wirtz added another fine finish to his collection against Brugge when he beat Yann Sommer at his near post with a curling effort from the edge of the box. If he can carry his rich vein of form into this weekend's Merseyside derby against Everton, Liverpool fans might consider a chunk of his eye-watering transfer fee already repaid.

It was fitting, too, that it was Alexander Isak who bookended the scoring against Brugge. Like Wirtz, the Sweden international had been touted externally as an anticlimactic addition to the Liverpool squad during a debut campaign in which both form and fitness proved problematic.

After combining for just 19 goal contributions in 71 appearances last season, Isak and Wirtz have already managed 20 in 33 appearances this term.

With a goal in either half against Brugge, Isak now has 10 goals for the season in all competitions and has become just the eighth player in Liverpool's history to score in six consecutive away games. His first was a trademark finish from close range after an error from Sommer opened the door for Dominik Szoboszlai to lay the ball off in the 18-yard box.

His second -- a lovely dinked finish over the goalkeeper after a long ball from Alisson Becker -- was that of a striker brimming with confidence, while his work rate and link-up play proved instrumental in downing a Brugge side who were 11 games unbeaten going into Wednesday's match.

"It's always difficult to say exactly where you are and how good you are, but I can say I'm in a good place and the team is in a good place, most importantly," Isak told TNT Sports post-match. "Hopefully, it gets better from here.

"The attack in general is beginning to click. We have a lot of different combinations and whoever comes in, we have a dangerous attack. It feels good."

It was a sentiment shared by Iraola, who will know the trajectory of his team's season depends heavily on whether Wirtz and Isak can maintain their current form.

"Both are now in a good place," the Liverpool boss said in his post-match news conference. "When the team overall is playing well and we are getting good results, everyone looks a little better. When everything collectively is not there, everyone looks a little bit worse.

"The important part is they help the team to play well, to win games, to get results and if we play well then we have chances, we are solid, everyone will play their part and will look better. Alex and Flo right now are in a good moment."

Indeed, Liverpool as a collective find themselves in a good spot under Iraola. They are the first team in 19 games to keep a clean sheet against a free-scoring Brugge side and have moved up to fifth in the Champions League league phase table.

Having won four of their five games in the competition so far this term, they remain on course for a third consecutive finish in the top eight.

While Liverpool are still far from the finished article, there positive signs are beginning to emerge all over the pitch. Goalkeeper Alisson once again showed his class with a fine save from Felix Lemarechal, while teenager Rio Ngumoha sparkled after replacing the injured Víctor Muñoz in the first half.

The withdrawal of the Spain international after he sustained a heavy knock to his knee was the one blemish on an otherwise perfect night for Liverpool -- although Iraola said Muñoz was "optimistic" when he spoke with him after the game.

"I am very pleased with the result and the performance overall," Iraola said. "The result, 3-0, you can think it was an easy game but I don't think it was.

"We were facing a very good team that was tied with us [in the table] in the Champions League, having an incredible run of games and scoring loads of goals. I value a lot the clean sheet and the win."

As for Wirtz, his tally of eight goal contributions in his last five games for club and country suggests the tide may finally be starting to turn in his favour. Forget his smile, the midfielder now looks to have found his feet at Anfield and Liverpool are, at long last, reaping the rewards.
