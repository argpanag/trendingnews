DP World Tour chief fires shots at LIV Golf as hostilities renewed

TheTools · 18.09.2026 14:18 · source · permalink

A DP World Tour boss has dismissed LIV Golf's notion that it wants to "complete" the sport's ecosystem, rather than compete with it.

LIV CEO Scott O'Neil has fought to change the narrative surrounding the breakaway league since replacing Greg Norman last year. The league has since been plunged into a financial crisis, filing for Chapter 11 bankruptcy following the withdrawal of Saudi Arabian support.

LIV hopes to relaunch next year without the enormous debt burden it has accumulated, primarily from a lavish spending spree on star players in 2022. For "LIV 2.0," O'Neil has adopted the mantra of "complete, not compete," arguing that players should be free to play when and where they want.

But Ben Cowen, the DP World Tour’s chief tournament and operations officer, thinks LIV's messaging is disingenuous. He told Golf Digest: "Without a doubt, they were and are a competitor. I don’t think anyone could say otherwise. If they say they weren’t, that’s an interesting take on it.”

O'Neil stated his vision at LIV's season finale in Indianapolis last month, telling reporters: "Golf doesn’t need one tour to win and everyone else to lose. There are great tours around the world. They serve different purposes. We believe players should have the flexibility to compete across these platforms when they are good enough and enhance the fields.

“We shouldn’t live in a world where one group of golfers is uniquely restricted from playing elsewhere. Our goal isn’t to replace or restrict any tour. Our mission is to bring something distinctive to the ecosystem that elevates it and makes the whole game better."

But Cowen says it has been a "constant battle for the last five years," with the breakaway league "going after" events, venues and sponsors that were connected to the European circuit.

LIV lured Real Club Valderrama in Spain, formerly the host of the Andalucia Masters, away from the DP World Tour, and it went into business with the Asian Tour and hosted events in Singapore and Hong Kong, where the DP World Tour previously held co-sanctioned tournaments with the Asian Tour.

LIV was given four months to strategize ahead Saudi Arabia terminating its support, and national opens were a central part of the league's strategy to offer its players a full schedule.

Those plans went up in smoke when the Australian Open agreed a new co-sanction deal with the DP World Tour, which was soon followed by the Asian Tour walking away from its partnership with LIV to rekindle its relationship with the European circuit.

"It very much felt like a competition and a threat from outside, and we had to do what we could to try and protect our business," Cowen said.

"Everyone can say [LIV's strategy was] fine, that's business. We spent a lot of time talking to our partners and explaining to them why they should stay within our ecosystem as the global tour. The global TV distribution we have, the players that we have who have proven themselves when they get a card onto the PGA Tour.

"We were the ones that were providing the real value, and that value has really been there for over 50 years as the European Tour, now the DP World Tour. There were a lot of rumors that a lot of national opens, including Australia, were going to go to with LIV.

"It was great when we were able to announce [the Australian Open] continuing with us in partnership with the PGA Tour. That's taken a lot of hard work, a lot of time and effort. It's been a rocky road, but I feel we're in a really good place.”

Cowen's comments continue the DP World Tour's move to an increasingly hardline stance against LIV. Tensions had cooled by the start of this year, when nine LIV members were granted conditional releases to play across both tours without being fined. That arrangement is "highly unlikely" to continue in 2027, per DP World Tour sources.

It is a power play that makes the prospect of staying with LIV less appealing for top stars like Jon Rahm and Tyrrell Hatton.
