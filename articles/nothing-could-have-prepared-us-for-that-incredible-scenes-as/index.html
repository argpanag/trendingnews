Match report: Wallabies produce miracle win over Boks

On Saturday, heartbroken Fremantle fans left Optus Stadium after watching their team lose in the AFL Grand Final to Brisbane. On Sunday, Perth was desperate for a sporting miracle and the Wallabies delivered one emphatically against the world champion Springboks.

The Wallabies’ four-point victory came after a barely believable 12 minutes at the end of the game with four players off the field to yellow cards.

Harry Wilson, Ryan Lonergan, Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii and Hunter Paisami were all sin-binned, leaving the Wallabies with 11 players on the field with four minutes to go and a seemingly impossible task, holding the rampaging South African bomb squad out.

Until Wilson’s yellow card, the Wallabies had enjoyed a 25-point lead after a ferocious physical performance from their forwards and clinical running from their backline.

Lonergan and Suaalii followed to the sin bin, leading to two South Africa tries. The Wallabies then lost Paisami for a high tackle with five minutes left, leading to 11 Australians on the field and Springbok Ox Nche scoring a converted try to pull the score back to 42-38.

Battered, bruised, undermanned the Wallabies hung on and did the impossible for a famous victory in Perth.

Wallabies coach Les Kiss said that the Wallabies’ improbable victory was ultimately down to the players.

“The last 10 or 15 minutes, that’s very little to do with coaching; certainly we have a part to play, but these boys, it’s what they have built in the locker room, they are a tight group,” Kiss said.

“Their connection piece is strong, they care deeply about what they are doing in this jersey and we saw that tonight...the Springboks, the number one team in the world, they concentrate the mind in certain ways.

“So the boys really took the bit between the teeth and really challenged themselves to build a plan and to work hard to make that work for us.”

Kiss admitted that he had planned for many things in the build-up to facing the Springboks, but losing four players in quick succession was not one of them.

“I think you can plan for many things, we talked about our 6-2 [bench]...but nothing could have prepared us for that,” Kiss said.

“They [the Wallabies] knew that they had to find something. I keep talking about this backyard spirit, something came through there in that space where you just find a way, and you just know that it’s important...I’m bloody pleased for these boys.”

The Wallabies refused to follow the script that the Springboks had seemingly laid for them. The physical battle was ferocious in the forwards and South Africa were going to empty a replacement bench filled with World Cup winners. The Wallabies didn’t buckle, typified by their captain Harry Wilson. Wilson was a try scorer, joined by Len Ikitau (2), Ben Donaldson and Max Jorgensen.

Since Joe Schmidt’s appointment, the Wallabies have battled to create depth and after a victory against South Africa, it has arrived. Scrum half Lonergan who only debuted a year ago against New Zealand. Fullback Tom Wright who has struggled for form since an ACL injury against South Africa last year in Cape Town, was outstanding in attack in defence.

South Africa arrived in Perth from Baltimore after a gruelling four-Test series against New Zealand. The Springboks had won that series by three Tests to one, leaving coach Rassie Erasmus room to audition his supporting cast ahead of next year’s World Cup in Australia. The master coach will be holding further casting calls after this result.

Against South Africa, Kiss made a sizeable gamble, putting six forwards and two backs on the bench, with no noted replacement for five-eighth Ben Donaldson. Suaalii played his first Test on the wing, to help counteract the Springboks’ notable aerial threat. Ultimately, the gambles paid off. Just.

The Springboks were given a platform to deploy their powerful pack who mauled easily past the Wallabies’ defence from five metres out to score their first try through hooker Johan Grobbelaar.

The Wallabies levelled the scores with a try from centre Len Ikitau after a deft offload from second-rower Lukhan Salakaia-Loto. Ikitau then followed up with a barely believable try after a Lonergan box kick hit the soaring Suaalii’s shoulder and it fell fortuitously into the centre’s arms.

After half an hour, the Wallabies had two tries and had made 28 tackles, not missing a single one. The first one to be missed unfortunately was from Ikitau on South Africa fullback Aphelele Fassi after a wide cut out pass opened space for South Africa, leaving Edwill van der Merwe with a simple run-in to score his team’s second try.

The Wallabies knew that South Africa’s bench was going impact in the second half, so the converted try to Ben Donaldson just before half-time was perfectly timed. Patient work from the Wallabies forwards over multiple phases, before Suaalii’s no-look pass sent Donaldson across for a try.

The entire Springbok bench was emptied after 47 minutes, just after Lonergan had kicked a penalty to give the Wallabies a 10-point lead. The first Wallabies scrum four minutes later held firm under pressure.

The Wallabies extended their lead to 13-points with a Lonergan penalty, but Ikitau immediately left the field injured, depriving the home team of one of their best attacking weapons.

Wallabies captain Harry Wilson had spent the week listening to lessons from former Test cricketer and coach Justin Langer on the need for toughness under the most exacting pressure. Wilson had led his team bravely, and muscled over for a crucial try after eventually breaking down a brutal South Africa defence.

Even with a 25-point lead after a Max Jorgensen try after 63 minutes, the Wallabies knew there was going to be a South African response and it came dramatically.

After farcical scenes, Andrea Piardi spoke to his television match official on a mobile phone due to communication failures at Optus Stadium and Wilson received a yellow card for the final ten minutes after a high tackle.

The Wallabies’ scrum that had held up so well and so long was folded and from the resulting penalty South Africa thought they had scored a try. Piardi was back on the mobile and it was ruled out, before Lonergan was also yellow-carded for denying a certain try and a penalty try was awarded.

Suaalii was then yellow-carded for tackling from an offside position, leaving 12 people on the field.

Morne van den Berg scored a try with seven minutes to go and pulled the lead back to six points. The Wallabies thought they had put the game beyond doubt after the outstanding Wright scored an interception try, but it was pulled back after a high tackle from Paisami which lead to the fourth yellow card with five minutes to go.

Nche’s rumbling try set up what looked like a Springbok victory, but the Wallabies held on and defeated the world champions.

Wilson cut a relieved figure in the post-match press conference and said that the team drew on their care for the Wallabies jersey and each other in the final, desperate minutes of the game.

“Les is real big on that care: the care for the jersey, the care for the person beside you and the boys showed that,” Wilson said.

“Down to 11 men, still for every [South Africa] try the boys made them work, which took more time off the clock, which ultimately got us the victory in the end.

“And I’m so proud of those boys there...I just hope in the future we don’t put ourselves in those positions.”

Les Kiss: ‘They care deeply about each other … you can’t coach that’

Okay, now let’s hear from the unflappable Wallabies coach Les Kiss.

On the chaos of the last 10 minutes: “I’ve got to give credit to my coaching team. They were calm up in the box, just trying to get the right message in the right way. Apparently, all the boys behind the poles, when we’re under stress, they were very calm as well, which is very pleasing. In those moments, you’ve just got to hold your nerve and stay clear about what you want to do, and I think they did that.“

On having no plans to play with 11 men: “No, you can’t think of that and that’s where something else comes through. When you care deeply about something, and you care deeply about your connection to the team and what we’re about, you can’t coach that.

“They have to develop that, and that’s why I believe the locker room is the most important part of the club. It’s the heartbeat. What we saw come out there was what they developed amongst themselves. They cared deeply about each other, and that came through. Belief is something that we keep talking a lot about.“
