Heroic dinner lady who died saving schoolchildren at Aberfan honoured

TheTools · 21.10.2026 09:19 · source · permalink

The bravery of a dinner lady who saved the lives of five children during the Aberfan disaster is being recognised six decades on from the tragedy.

A special purple plaque, which recognise the actions of remarkable Welsh women, will be unveiled in Aberfan for Nansi Williams, whose heroic actions while she was collecting dinner money allowed five children to return home to their families, when over a hundred others weren't so lucky.

On the morning of October 21, 1966, Pantglas Junior School dinner lady Nansi was collecting dinner money from pupils when she heard a deep, rumbling noise. When the school started to shudder, she told the five children who had been queuing up to give her their dinner money to get on the ground and flung herself on top of them.

When 150,000 tonnes of waste from one of the huge spoil tips on the mountainside above the village engulfed the school, Nansi was killed instantly. She was 44 years old.

The five children she shielded all survived. One of the schoolchildren saved by Nansi previously said the dinner lady was the only reason she survived the tragedy.

Nansi's godson David Powell, has long campaigned for recognition of Nancy's bravery.

He said: "So many died that day but she was never really mentioned and she gave her life to save five children. Of course it was talked about in the family - my father was her first cousin - but not really outside that. That's why I'm so pleased she's getting some recognition in the community with the plaque."

The purple plaque, describing Nansi as a "woman of courage" will be unveiled on the cemetery wall in Aberfan on Friday, October 30, six decades after the tip disaster struck. Nansi was born and lived at Cemetery House until her untimely death in 1966.

Karen Thomas was one of those schoolchildren shielded by Nansi from the catastrophic collapse of a colliery spoil tip, which killed 116 children and 28 adults.

Karen Thomas was saved by Nansi as a schoolgirl. She spoke to the BBC in 2016 -Credit:BBC

Speaking to BBC Wales on the 50th anniversary of the disaster in 2016, she said she visits Nansi's grave every year to place flowers at her grave and to give thanks the woman who saved her life.

She recalled how she and four other children had gone to pay their dinner money to Nansi in a corridor. "At the other end of the hall glass started coming down the corridor from the head mistress's room and Nansi the dinner lady jumped on top of us," said Karen.

"The wall I think more or less pushed us all together and she took the full impact."

"We were shouting at the dinner lady and I was trying to pull her hair, to see if we could have a response from her because she wasn't saying anything to us," added Karen.

"I just thought to keep trying to pull her hair to wake her up, we just didn't know what was happening because we couldn't hear anyone else. It was just our voice and our screams that we could hear.

"It's only down to Nansi that I'm here today. I'll never be able to thank her enough for saving us."

The plaque will be unveiled on the cemetery wall in Aberfan on Friday, October 30 -Credit:Media Wales

Jane Hutt, of Purple Plaques Wales, said: "I am so pleased we are able to recognise a truly remarkable woman in Nansi Williams. We have celebrated women's remarkable achievements in areas such as sport, literature, the arts, politics and community activism - but we have never, until now, celebrated someone who made the ultimate sacrifice like Nansi did.

"Hers was a completely selfless act and her urge to protect the children must have been instinctive. We truly are so proud to help make sure this is marked with a fitting tribute in this Purple Plaque."
