Lunch Wrap: Pitchforks are out as shareholder unrest adds to the ASX's Monday blues | Stockhead

TheTools · 16.11.2026 02:10 · source · permalink

ASX slides into Monday with AI nerves and shareholder revolt brewing. Pic: Getty Images

  • ASX slips as Nvidia’s AI soap opera rolls on
  • BrainChip shareholders grab the pitchforks
  • Elders profit soars while Coles faces cyber scare

 

The S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.1% at lunchtime on Monday in Sydney, as investors returned to discover Wall Street had spent Friday having another nervous breakdown over AI.

And right in the middle of the circus, surprise, surprise, was Nvidia.

According to the Financial Times, outed CEO Jensen Huang is already discussing a comeback, with some Silicon Valley heavyweights apparently keen to put him back in the driver’s seat.

And the timing couldn’t be more awkward: Nvidia reports its quarterly earnings on Tuesday US time.

 

Back on the ASX this morning, miners were dragging the market into the red this morning, while tech was holding up best.

Meanwhile, former AI golden child BrainChip Holdings (ASX:BRN) has a shareholder rebellion on its hands.

Long-time investors Neil Rinaldi and Steve Liebeskind have rounded up support representing more than 5% of the company’s shares, enough to force the issue of a general meeting.

They want three directors – Antonio Viana, Pia Turcinov and Duy-Loan Le –  shown the door and replaced with two new faces.

Their complaint is pretty straightforward. Plenty of talk about commercialisation, not nearly enough commercial success, and shareholder communication that apparently leaves investors playing detective.

The pair have even launched a campaign called RenewBRN to rally the troops.

And you can see why some shareholders might be getting a little cranky.

Back in 2022, BrainChip was the belle of the ball, with shares at $2.15 and a market value north of $3.6 billion.

Today, those shares are worth around 10 cents. A fall from grace is an understatement of epic proportions.

 

In large-cap news…

Coles Group (ASX:COL) has had some unwanted visitors in its computer systems, with the supermarket giant investigating a cyber incident over the weekend.

No evidence so far of customer, employee or supplier data being compromised, and stores are running as usual.

So the only thing giving Coles shoppers a heart attack for now is still the checkout receipt.

Meanwhile, Elders (ASX:ELD) has rocked up with a rather tasty set of numbers.

Profit shot up 187% to $144.2 million, while revenue climbed 31% to $4 billion, thanks in large part to its $475 million Delta Ag acquisition.

The deal has already delivered $7.3 million in synergies, and the final dividend stays at 18 cents.

New CEO Rene Dedoncker only started in October. Talk about walking into a new job and finding someone’s already done the dishes.

Over in the coal pit, New Hope Corp (ASX:NHC) reported production down 9.8% and sales off 3.2% for the quarter.

The miner was deliberately shifting waste rock at its Bengalla operation, and production was actually 10.4% higher than a year ago.

Coal prices also rose 6%, helping quarterly underlying EBITDA hold at $168.1 million.

And with $588.4 million in available cash after paying a 30-cent dividend, New Hope can probably afford to take the occasional shovel break.

And finally… BlueScope Steel (ASX:BSL) is having a considerably better time than most.

The steelmaker is still expecting first-half underlying EBIT approaching $1.1 billion, up from its earlier $860 million–$960 million guidance.

Its American operations have been getting a nice little boost from Trump’s steel tariffs, which make imported steel less competitive.

 

ASX LEADERS

Today’s best performing stocks (including small caps) intraday:

Spenda (ASX:SPX) has brought fintech veteran Andy Taylor onto its board as an independent non-executive director to help steer its business transformation.

Taylor co-founded SocietyOne, which MoneyMe bought for around $132 million in 2022, and founded Douugh, which later became ASX-listed Stakk, so he’s no stranger to building and reshaping fintech businesses.

Red Mountain Mining (ASX:RMX) has hit some seriously high-grade antimony in its first drilling campaign at Oaky Creek in NSW, including 5 metres at 5.20% antimony and a standout 1 metre at 18.98%.

The mineralisation remains open at depth, meaning there’s more to chase, with a drone magnetic survey planned to help pinpoint the next drilling targets.

Black Rock Mining (ASX:BKT) has moved closer to funding its Mahenge graphite project in Tanzania, securing a revised, conditional US$40 million investment agreement with Korean giant POSCO and a non-binding proposal for another US$70 million from African Critical Minerals.

That would bring its planned funding package to US$258 million, leaving another US$40 million to find before the deals can proceed, with financial close targeted for the end of December.

LAST ORDERS

Neurizon Therapeutics (ASX:NUZ) has opened a share purchase plan targeting $2m at 6.8c a share, matching its recent placement price. Eligible shareholders can apply for up to $30,000, with one free option per share subject to shareholder approval and ASX quotation.

The offer is scheduled to close on December 4, and proceeds will help fund completion of Regimen I of the HEALEY ALS Platform Trial and further development of NUZ-001.

Pursuit Minerals (ASX:PUR) has sold 58.7% of its original holding in London-listed Kendrick Resources for estimated net proceeds of $2.08m, of which it has received $2.04m.

Pursuit holds $2.03m in cash and is prioritising an updated resource estimate for its Rio Grande Sur lithium project in Argentina and progressing its US$40m memorandum of understanding with EcoPro Innovation towards binding agreements.

Redcastle Resources (ASX:RC1) has advanced talks with four mill owners to process stockpiled ore and future production from Redcastle Reef and Queen Alexandra after a tank failure at the Wiluna mill.

The joint venture needs to secure suitable processing terms before it can restart mining, while Redcastle is also looking for a rig to drill priority targets in its TBone Belt portfolio.
