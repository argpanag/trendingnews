Inside 'death' of Kim Jong-un at 42 as daughter 'to be first female dictator'

TheTools · 19.10.2026 15:39 · source · permalink

Kim Jong-un may be dead(Image: KCNA VIA KNS/AFP via Getty Images)

Rumours of Kim Jong Un's death spread across the internet like wildfire this weekend. While the North Korean's condition is unknown, the fate of the poverty-stricken country will likely remain in the hands of the blood-thirsty Kim clan.

Speculation of the despot's downfall were sparked when a Chinese politician claimed online that a team of medical experts had been "dispatched" to treat the infamous dictator. China Democracy Party Chairman Wanjun Xie wrote on X: “It is rumoured that recently, Kim Jong-un, General Secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea and Supreme Leader of North Korea, suffered a severe cerebral hemorrhage and fell into a coma."

While others have added to the speculation, the Daily Star has not been able too find any conclusive evidence that Kim's health has taken a turn, although finding out the truth is not always easy when it comes to the notoriously secretive country, especially as rumours over the 42-year-old's health problems have long been circulated.

Unfounded rumours have been circulating about the North Korean leader's health

But were Kim Jong Un to pass away, how would we know? Who would replace him? And how would the secluded East Asian country react to the death of its all powerful leader?

How North Korea would announce it

When analysing how Pyongyang would announce the death of Kim Jong Un, the best bet is to look at what happened when his father passed away in 2011.

The death of Kim Jong-Il, who like his son ruled over North Korea as a controlling and feared dictator, was announced in North Korea by the country's state run media channel, with one of the show's anchors breaking the news two days after his passing on December 19.

Dressed all in black, presenter Ri Chun-hee told viewers that the ruler had died at 8.30am after suffering a heart attack, while travelling by train to an area outside Pyongyang, the country's capital.

The death of Kim's dad was announced on state TV

Kim Jong-il's funeral was held nine days after his death on December 28, followed by a period of mourning until the following day.

A huge funeral procession attended by thousands took place, featuring rows of sobbing mourners and hundreds of military officials in tow. His body was preserved and placed on public display at the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun, the mausoleum where his father, Kim Il-sung, also lies in state.

Flags were lowered at military units, factories, businesses, farms and public buildings as part of the official mourning arrangements.. while reports indicated that many forms of entertainment including music and theatre were banned during the official period of grief.

Mourners line the streets for Kim Jong-il's funeral

Who would replace him

It has long been rumoured that Kim's daughter Kim Ju Ae will go into the family dictator business, and take over the reins at the head of the world's most secretive country.

Experts suggest that The 12 to 14-year-old has been primed for the role for many years, with Kim including his youngest child in military exercises and other state matters in order to show her the ropes.

Not too much is known about the young daughter of Kim, with North Korea never even releasing her real name and instead calling her simply "respected child” - wording historically associated with Kim Jong Un himself before he took power - alongside grandiose phrases like “great person of guidance”, a title typically reserved for senior leaders.

Kim Ju-ae has been tipped for the top job(Image: KCNA VIA KNS/AFP via Getty Image)

However, despite the relative lack of knowledge on the young North Korean September this year, South Korea’s National Intelligence Service (NIS) briefed lawmakers that the evidence was quickly becoming “unmistakable” that Kim’s daughter has entered an official succession track.

However, despite the assurance from the South, it is far from confirmed that Kim Ju Ae will be handed the baton, with other names such as the current leader's sister Kim Yo-jong also being put forward. Others have suggested that Kim Jong Un may also have a son who may too be in the running, although this has never been confirmed.

It seems unlikely that anyone outside of the Kim family would be named leader, as the seat of power has remained with the Kims since the foundation of North Korea in 1948.

Kim Yo Jong, the sister of North Korea's leader Kim Jong Un, is another potential candidate (Image: KCNA VIA KNS/AFP via Getty Image)

Past rumours

This recent bout of rumours is not the first time speculation has run rife over Kim Jong Un's health.

In 2020, extraordinary (albeit totally unfounded) rumours emerged that the despot had been replaced with a body double, with internet sleuths pointing to apparent changes in his appearance including his teeth, ears and body shape.

And back 2014, Kim again was reported to have vanished from public view for more than a month.

Kim appeared to need a walking stick back in 2014(Image: Rodong Sinmun / Getty)

Interestingly, when he next appeared in photographs, he was shown walking with a clear limp and using a stick, suggesting he may have genuinely undergone some sort of medical treatment.
