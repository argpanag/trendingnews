PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — At least 40 charged in Oregon-led crackdown on SNAP fraud schemes that stole over millions of dollars in benefits using stolen EBT data, involving multi-state indictments.

Officials with the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Oregon made the announcement in a press conference on Wednesday. The suspects now face federal charges linked to more than $3 million in unauthorized Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) purchases. They say multiple groups carried out organized schemes by stealing Electronic Benefit Transfer card information, often using skimmers, then buying large amounts of SNAP-eligible items in Oregon, Washington and California.

“When SNAP benefits are stolen, real families go without food,” said Scott E. Bradford, U.S. Attorney for the District of Oregon. Bradford added, “That is unacceptable. My office will continue pursuing these cases to safeguard the integrity of the program and uphold the trust placed in us to protect Oregon’s communities.”

USDA Inspector General John Walk called the operation dangerous for vulnerable people.

“This organized criminal scheme not only resulted in the loss of food assistance benefits for needy families whose EBT card data was skimmed or otherwise stolen. Egregiously, the corrupt enterprise also placed public health and safety – including of babies – at risk by selling products like infant formula on the black market,” said Walk.

In U.S. v. Ramirez Dumitru and others, 17 people were charged with stealing more than $2.4 million in benefits from August 2023 to October 2024. Investigators say the group bought more than 120,000 pounds of SNAP goods—including infant formula and energy drinks—using stolen EBT accounts and shipped the items for illegal sale in California.

Ana Escalante, who helped move the stolen goods to California, was sentenced in September to 27 months in prison and ordered to pay $2.4 million in restitution. So far, 12 members have received a total of 157 months in prison, with two more set for sentencing in December.

A separate case, U.S. v. Alexandru Telescu and others, shows similar methods. Prosecutors say Telescu’s group stole EBT card data starting April 2025 through skimming devices and unauthorized purchases in all three states, causing about $700,000 in losses. Telescu admitted guilt to access-device fraud and aggravated identity theft and is due for sentencing in January 2027.

Two others, Aramis and Cristina Manolea, were sentenced in May to time served and three years of supervised release after pleading guilty to conspiracy.

Officials revealed 11 new indictments, including one against Bly Gnionsiekan Rodoophe Kehi, accused of installing skimmers in Washington County to collect card data.

Ten more defendants were recently charged in other cases for using stolen EBT cards and PINs in 2024 to get more than $1,000 in merchandise per case, totaling $143,000 in losses.

USDA Office of Inspector General, IRS-Criminal Investigation, Homeland Security Investigations, the U.S. Marshals Service and local police agencies are working together on these cases. Federal prosecutors say this joint operation remains focused on shutting down groups targeting public benefit programs.

Authorities say they are committed to finding and prosecuting those responsible for SNAP fraud as they work to protect recipients and taxpayer dollars.
