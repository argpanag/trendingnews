Nvidia (NVDA) interim CEO and longtime CFO Colette Kress gets an "A" for the financial performance and a "C" for the optics displayed on Tuesday's late-evening earnings call. New chair Harvey Jones — whom most Nvidia faithful have never heard of or from — gets a "C-" for his performance on the call.

The reality is Nvidia's leadership needs a better overall grade in the months ahead to sustainably win back market support.

Nvidia just put up another monster quarter, no doubt about it. Revenue and earnings easily topped expectations. Its outlook for the current quarter came in ahead of Wall Street estimates. Management pointed to approximately 75% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, up from the roughly 70% growth outlook then CEO Jensen Huang had previously discussed three months ago when he led the earnings call.

Let that sink in. Nvidia is talking about growing revenue at an extraordinary rate at a scale few companies in history have ever approached. Shares are up 2.5% on Wednesday in response.

There was more. The company cited a $2.5 trillion cloud backlog, expressed confidence that its next-generation chips will be released on schedule, and signaled that aggressive share repurchases will continue.

"FY28 underwritten as concern on near-term is largely addressed," Barclays analyst Tom O'Malley said.

(NVDA )

But then there was the earnings call itself. And this is where things went astray.

This was Colette Kress's moment. The veteran Nvidia CFO is now Nvidia's interim CEO, stepping into a role that suddenly carries enormous weight following the leadership upheaval surrounding the disgraced Jensen Huang.

Yet the call felt strangely detached from the magnitude of what is happening inside the company.

Eight analysts asked questions. Not one mentioned Jensen's name. That was in part because Nvidia stonewalled them at the start of the call by saying it wouldn't answer any Jensen questions. This was tone deaf by Jones and Kress, and the analyst community did a horrible job of adhering to such bullshit from the world's most important stock.

Kress didn't mention his name either. Not once.

I found that remarkable. This is the man who founded Nvidia in 1993, helped build the products driving today's results, and became the face of the global AI revolution. The quarter being discussed was built on years of work under his intense leadership. The products shipping over the next 18 to 24 months will largely reflect that legacy too.
