WEATHER BLOG: Severe thunderstorm watch issued for the Kansas City metro area

TheTools · 13.10.2026 15:59 · source · permalink

Tuesday is a First Alert Weather Alert Day, and the Kansas City area is under a Severe Thunderstorm Watch and Flood Watch ahead of anticipated heavy rain and thunderstorms starting Tuesday evening.

Tuesday starts warm and breezy. However, the risk for strong-to-severe thunderstorms along with heavy rain increases into the evening hours. Then the metro is in for a big shift thanks to the cold front pushing through our area.

Kansas City weather live updates:

7:59 p.m. - A severe thunderstorm warning has been issued for parts of the Kansas City area until 8:45 p.m. Tuesday. The warning includes Excelsior Springs, Kearney, Richmond, Lexington, Lawson and surrounding areas.

7:53 p.m. - Thousands of people across the Kansas City area are without power Tuesday evening as strong storms move through the metro. The latest outage numbers show 27,647 customers without power in Missouri, including some communities where more than half of customers have lost electricity. Gladstone is among the hardest-hit areas, with 7,905 customers without power. That's nearly 55% of the city.

7:30 p.m. - A tornado warning has been issued for central Clay County until 8 p.m. Liberty, Gladstone and nearby areas are included. No tornado has been confirmed. The warning is based on radar-indicated rotation.

7:14 p.m. - The National Weather Service has issued a new severe thunderstorm warning for parts of the Kansas City metro, with destructive winds possible. The warning is in effect until 7:45 p.m. Tuesday for Wyandotte County, northeastern Johnson County and eastern Leavenworth County in Kansas, along with parts of Jackson, Clay and Platte counties in Missouri.

6:52 p.m. - A severe thunderstorm warning remains in effect until 7:30 p.m. Tuesday for Johnson, Wyandotte and southern Leavenworth counties in Kansas, along with southeastern Platte County in Missouri. As of 6:50 p.m., a line of severe storms was moving southeast at 30 mph, stretching from Leavenworth to southwest of Linwood.

The storms could produce wind gusts up to 70 mph and hail the size of golf balls.

Severe thunderstorms are possible Tuesday starting around 6 p.m., the back end of the evening rush hour, and continuing into the overnight hours.

The biggest risks for these storms are damaging wind and large hail. The tornado risk for these storms is extremely low, but not zero.

The National Weather Service also warns of the potential for training storms Tuesday evening, meaning the storms could continue to form and flow along a defined line for a period of time. Some models show the potential for between 1 and 3 inches of rainfall overnight.

The entire metro area is under the Severe Thunderstorm Watch until 11 p.m.

A Flood Watch has been issued for most of the area, including the core Kansas City metro, ahead of these storms.

While the severe portion of this event should push out of the area late Tuesday night, rain will continue into Wednesday until around lunchtime. Early radar scans suggest a break in the rain until another round developing late Wednesday and into Thursday.

This storm is rolling through along a cold front, bringing a noticeable drop in temperature for our area. While we'll see temperatures near 90 degrees Tuesday, we'll drop into the 70s for Wednesday and Thursday.

We'll see blustery conditions on Friday with a high around 61 degrees.
