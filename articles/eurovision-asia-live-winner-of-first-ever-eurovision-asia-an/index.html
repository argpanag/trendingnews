  6. Vietnam's Tóc Tiên on entering bilingual song into the competitionpublished at 15:48 GMT
    15:48 GMT

    Thuong Le
    BBC World Service, Bangkok

    I caught up with Vietnam's entry Tóc Tiên before the show began.

    She told me that even though her song included English lyrics she was "determined" to "preserve" the song's Vietnamese verses.

    Tóc Tiên opened Eurovision Asia with EM TIÊN, having added English lines to the original version in an effort to appeal the competition's international audience.

    However, completely axing the song's Vietnamese lyrics was never an option, the artist says. This was "one element" she "definitely wanted to preserve".

    She's also proud that the song's melody has "a very traditional feel, something that is distinctly Vietnamese".

  7. Jury votes being counted - how do they work?published at 15:43 GMT
    15:43 GMT

    The jury votes are now being announced - what does this mean?

    Each of the eleven countries who performed tonight have selected a jury - this is a group of people from their country who choose to award points to their favourite acts of the night.

    They award eight, 10 and 12 points to three of the eleven acts.

    The outcome from this round of jury votes will account for 50% of the result. The other 50% will come from the public vote. Whoever has the most combined points will win.

  15. All eleven acts have performed - a recappublished at 15:01 GMT
    15:01 GMT

    That's a wrap on the eleven performances at the inaugural Eurovision Song Contest Asia.

    Here's a recap:

    • Vietnam's Tóc Tiên started off strong with slick choreogaphy, backing dancers and an elaborate fairy-like get-up
    • Monetrey from Cambodia then dialled things back, performing an ambitious ballad alone on stage under a single spotlight
    • Thinla from Laos followed up with the crowd-pleasing Bae, opting to perform in a vibrant, traditional Lao hilltribe outfit
    • Mongolian crooner Beck then brought the drama with a heartfelt performance of Chamguigeer (Without You)
    • Backed by a duo performing a romantic interpretive dance sequence, Nyendra from Bhutan showed off his impressive pipes with the folk-pop tune Lhayi Semo
    • Carmelle from the Phillipines then launched into Sobra-Sobra - blending vocals with a unexpectedly delightful rap verse
    • The beatboxing Bangladeshi group Beatmosphere eschewed the traditional backing track, instead using their voices to replicate the sounds of drums and basslines
    • Thailand's MANG x DAOU brought the harmonies, eliciting huge cheers from the home crowd for their emotive duet
    • JUNHEE of South Korea brought a slick K-pop number, and the crowd went wild when he took off his jacket
    • For Nepal, Pratik Himal Rai made good use of his staging, with a fiery backdrop for his performance
    • And finally, Malaysia's Ania Abdul closed the show with her powerful vocals - the other acts were on their feet as she ended
