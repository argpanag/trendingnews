Helen Haines: Attacked Australian MP had both legs amputated, says husband

TheTools · 21.10.2026 09:17 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Phil Haines, husband of MP Helen Haines, confirms she has had to have both legs amputated

  • He added that the "good news" was that she was "alive and stable"

  • Haines suffered severe lower body injuries on Monday when she was hit by a car

  • The man charged over the alleged attack, Brendan Nugent, has briefly faced court. Our correspondent Katy Watson was in the courtroom

  • Nugent, 43, did not apply for bail. He is due to return to court in February

  • He has been charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and causing harm to a Commonwealth public official

  • Police have not ruled out additional charges, including attempted murder

    08:48 BST

    Australian MP Helen Haines' husband, Phil Haines, has given an update on her condition following an alleged attack outside her office in Wangaratta, Victoria earlier this week that left her critically injured. Earlier today, her suspected attacker Brendan Nugent appeared in court for the first time where he was formally charged.

    Here is a recap of the day's events:

    • Phil Haines says his wife has had to have both legs amputated but that the "good news" is that she is "alive and stable"
    • Nugent appeared in court this morning, but he stayed silent and did not show any emotion
    • Nugent has been charged with multiple offences, including dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and causing harm to a Commonwealth public official
    • He also faces additional charges of cultivating and possessing cannabis, court documents show
    • His "full motivations" are still under investigation, said the police
    • The mood in Wangaratta is sombre, and many have left flowers outside Helen Haines's office
    • Local media report that in the seconds after she was hit by the car, Haines was thanking those who had rushed over to help her
    • The alleged attack has thrown the protection of MPs into the spotlight
    • It has also prompted the family of murdered UK MP Jo Cox to urge Australia to unite against political violence

  2. Helen Haines: Lawmaker, former nurse and new grandmotherpublished at 08:18 BST
    08:18 BST

    Helen Haines is a 65-year-old independent MP for the Indi division in Victoria, south-eastern Australia.

    She grew up in the south-west of Victoria on a dairy farm with four brothers before training as a nurse and a midwife.

    She became the matron of the Chiltern Bush Nursing Hospital in Victoria when she was 26, before completing a master's degree and PhD while raising her three children and working, according to her website.

    She was first elected to the Australian House of Representatives in 2019 and was re-elected in 2022 and 2025.

    As a lawmaker, she has championed climate action and better working conditions for healthcare professionals.

    Fellow independent MP Zali Steggall, who sits next to Haines in federal parliament, described her as "deeply loved and respected". Haines had recently become a grandmother and would share pictures of her baby grandchildren, Steggall said.

    Haines lives with her family on a small cattle farm outside Wangaratta.

  16. Alleged attack on Haines 'unfathomable' - colleaguepublished at 05:13 BST
    05:13 BST

    David Pocock, an independent senator for the Australian Capital Territory and a colleague of Haines, has said that working with her is "one of the great privileges of serving in our parliament".

    The two recently worked on a petition to support students in allied health fields.

    Addressing the National Press Club in Canberra a while ago, Pocock said the alleged attack on Haines was "unfathomable".

    "We must meet this attack on you by doubling down on the values you represent: democracy, transparency, accountability, care, and kindness," he said.

    "Now is the time to think about what brings us together, to defend our shared values, our democracy, and our freedom to live as Australians."

  19. Victoria Premier says Australia may be 'straying from the principles of our democracy'published at 03:49 BST
    03:49 BST

    Ben Carroll, the Premier of Victoria, has said in a statement that what happened to Haines raised fears that Australia was "straying" from its democratic principles.

    He said he visited Haines's campaign office a couple of years ago and was struck by "a warmth you rarely ever find in politics".

    "Why would you attack someone who has spent her life making things better?" he wrote. "If you care about Australia, and the place we might become, today calls for soul searching."

    "And I genuinely fear that we're straying from the principles of our democracy. Respect. Equality. A fair go."
