Nvidia (NVDA) stock tanked Thursday after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO. The company's CFO, Colette Kress, will serve as the interim head of the business until a permanent successor is named.

Nvidia stock dropped as much as 13% on the news before paring losses.

The leadership shakeup followed a series of allegations of Huang's extramarital affairs with a customer and two employees.

On Nov. 8, Huang admitted to an affair believed to be with Alice Huang, a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. The revelation embroiled the company in scandal this week, which only intensified after a report from The New York Times on Wednesday alleged that he also had a relationship with Nvidia executive Kimberly Powell. The company's board met on Wednesday night and decided to oust Huang.

On Thursday, Reuters reported that Huang had a third affair with a second Nvidia employee, who remained unnamed, as the fallout from the scandal spread.

    Scrutiny of AI chip giant Nvidia could widen amid multiple US Justice Department investigations that could ensnare the company, according to a report from Semafor.

    One of the probes is focused directly on Nvidia and examines potential antitrust violations, according to two administration officials cited by Semafor. Two other investigations involve Singapore-based tech company Megaspeed and AI server maker Super Micro Computer (SMCI).

    The latest scrutiny comes after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday following reports of two affairs.   • Nvidia has 3-4 months to find a new CEO: AI company founder

    Nvidia has only a few months to scout out a new CEO, if you ask Unconventional AI founder and CEO Naveen Rao.

    "I actually think it's pretty urgent, and I would say it needs to be closed probably in the next three or four months," Rao said about Nvidia's CEO search after Jensen Huang stepped down. CFO Colette Kress is serving as the company's head on an interim basis until a permanent successor to Huang is determined.

    Rao explained that the urgency is due to Nvidia's norms around its hardware roadmap, which has allowed the company to gain a competitive advantage.

    "Nvidia is famous for making quick changes to the roadmap," Rao said. "The V3 V100, where they got the Tensor cores, that was a very famous quick change. So I think someone has to be able to make those calls in real time, and this is not something you can you can leave out for six plus months. This needs to be closed pretty soon."

    He also pointed out that Huang helped attract Nvidia's strong talent base, and any delay in picking a new leader could erode employees' confidence in the company.

  • Nvidia CFO solid pick for interim CFO, but company outlook is clouded, Wall Street says

    Wall Street analysts reacted with temporary relief on Thursday as Nvidia (NVDA) announced that CFO Colette Kress would serve as interim CEO after Jensen Huang resigned following reports of affairs with a suspected Chinese spy and an Nvidia executive.

    Kress, who has served as Nvidia CFO, is seen as a steady hand amid the turmoil at the company. 

    Wall Street watchers say that, in the near future, Nvidia will be the revenue monster it's been since the start of the AI race, thanks to its existing product roadmap. 

    KeyBanc Capital Markets analyst John Vinh wrote in a note to investors that Kress's appointment as interim CEO is constructive "given her tenure as CFO since 2013 and deep institutional knowledge of Nvidia's business and strategy."

    But Huang, who led the company as a singular figure with dozens of direct reports, leaves a void that clouds Nvidia's long-term outlook.

    As a result, several analysts lowered their price targets and ratings on the company's stock.

    Bernstein analyst Stacy Rasgon, who reduced his price target from $400 to $200 and lowered his rating from Outperform to Market-Perform, wrote in an investor note that Kress's appointment is a positive for the company.

    "We aren't really worried about near-term impacts to the business as a result of the events of the past week, and of course the shares screen extremely cheap," he said.

    But Rasgon raised concerns about a new CEO's ability to maneuver Nvidia's product pipeline, customer relationships, and more.

    "We see considerable uncertainty around further newsflow that is probably going to be coming down the pipe, as well as the ability of any new CEO to truly fill Jensen's shoes given how tightly he managed the direction of the business and its relationships with customers, suppliers, employees, and the broader industry ecosystem," he wrote.   • The Bank of Nvidia just lost its banker

President Trump offered his first reaction Thursday to the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang by saying that former Apple CEO Tim Cook should take over, calling him "the only one man to take his place."

"A Legend replaces a Legend!," the president added on Truth Social, which also praised the ousted Huang as having "done a fantastic job."

The post did little to answer questions about whether Huang's affair with a suspected Chinese spy endangered US national security and came as Trump also appeared to stand by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, who has found himself under fire as part of the growing scandal.

White House officials told various outlets on Thursday morning that Trump maintains complete confidence in Lutnick.

The Bank of Nvidia just lost its banker

Jensen Huang didn't just sell the AI boom its chips. He bankrolled a good chunk of it too. Now that he's out as Nvidia's (NVDA) CEO, Wall Street is going through the tab line by line.

Nvidia owns pieces of Intel (INTC), SpaceX (SPCX), and CoreWeave (CRWV). It's pledged up to $10 billion to Anthropic and has a preliminary deal to put as much as $100 billion into OpenAI. Add it all up, and its latest filing lists about $530 billion in commitments and guarantees, by Yahoo Finance's count.

The selling this morning is following that money.

Neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nebius (NBIS), which rent out Nvidia chips and lean on Nvidia's backing, are getting hit hardest. Rivals Broadcom (AVGO) and AMD (AMD) are catching a bid, and most of Big Tech is shrugging it off.

Interim CEO Colette Kress — the CFO who signed off on many of these deals — now decides how many more checks to write. Watch whether she stands behind them when Nvidia reports Tuesday.

'First with a bang, then a whimper ...' — Bernstein analysts on Jensen Huang's fall from grace as Nvidia CEO

In a note to clients, Bernstein analysts reacted to the departure of longtime Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and the appointment of CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO.

"So what now? On the positive Colette should be able to keep things running for now and we aren't really worried about near-term impacts to the business as a result of the events of the past week, and of course the shares screen extremely cheap. That being said, we see considerable uncertainty around further newsflow that is probably going to be coming down the pipe, as well as the ability of any new CEO to truly fill Jensen's shoes given how tightly he managed the direction of the business and its relationships with customers, suppliers, employees, and the broader industry ecosystem.

The uncertainty proved too much for us; we moved to the sidelines on the stock last night as a result (more of our broader thoughts can be found in that note). Nothing would give us more pleasure than being able to revisit the story at some point, but we are having a hard time advocating stepping in here. We rate NVDA Market Perform with a $200 PT."
