But why quibble? Nobody can with Newlands' awe-inspiring vista, the 1,000-metre majesty of Table Mountain a towering symbol of nature's awesome scale and grace.

Test cricket has been played at Newlands since March 1889 - this is the 62nd time South Africa will play a red-ball match at the home of Western Province.

No man has scored more runs here than Jacques Kallis, whose 2,181 Test runs includes a 224 effort against Sri Lanka in 2012.

Graeme Pollock's 209 in 1966 is the highest score against Australia at the ground, with Neil Harvey's 178 in 1949 the best by an Aussie here.

Dale Steyn is the most prolific wicket taker at the ground with 74, but Kagiso Rabada has 52 in the nine matches he's played, with a best of 6/55. His average of 21.55 and a strike rate of 34.25.

Australia has played 14 times at Newlands since first visiting in 1902. They've won 10 of them.

Five of those victories came in the seven Tests held before 1970 and South Africa's international exclusion.

Since then, the ledger has flipped, with the Proteas winning five of seven.

Australia last won here in 2014, by 245 runs, but that is their only victory in their last four Tests here.

South Africa's victory in Gqeberha was made all the more remarkable given that Anrich Nortje barely bowled.

He won't be bowling in Cape Town either.

The hulking paceman suffered a groin injury in his first bowling spell, and has subsequently been ruled out of the final Test.

The Proteas have not named a replacement in the squad.

Nortje had not played a red-ball match in more than three-and-a-half years before the first Test in Durban.

He proceeded to bowl an energy-sapping 40 overs in that match, which took its toll on the 32-year-old.

South Africa's options include bringing Gerald Coetzee, Corbin Bosch or Dane Paterson into the team.

However, given the Newlands surface's propensity to take spin, left-arm spin-bowling allrounder Senuran Muthusamy could be an option as well.
