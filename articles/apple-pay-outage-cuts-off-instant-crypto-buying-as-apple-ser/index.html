Apple Pay Outage Cuts Off Instant Crypto Buying as Apple Services Fail — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 17.11.2026 20:05 · source · permalink

Apple Inc. (AAPL) suffered a broad services failure on Tuesday that disrupted iCloud sign-ins, the App Store and Apple Music, and also appeared to knock out Apple Pay, cutting off one of the most widely used instant payment routes for buying cryptocurrency in the United States.

The company's System Status page listed the App Store and Apple Music as down, along with issues affecting iCloud Account and Sign In, in an update posted at 1:39 p.m. ET. According to the page, the App Store and iCloud sign-in problems began at 11:45 a.m. ET, while Apple Music went down at about 11:15 a.m. ET. Apple Pay had not been added to the status page as of Tuesday afternoon, but complaints about the payment service on the outage tracker Downdetector jumped around 11:40 a.m. ET. Because the tracker counts only users who file reports, the actual number of people affected is probably larger.

The disruption matters beyond Apple's own ecosystem because Apple Pay is a common instant funding method for crypto on-ramps. Coinbase added Apple Pay to its Onramp tool in December 2024, citing more than 60 million Apple Pay users in the country at the time. Kraken allows customers to buy digital assets with Apple Pay inside its app, with a $10 minimum and weekly purchase limits of $1,000 to $5,000 depending on the account. MoonPay and other on-ramp providers also accept the service.

When Apple Pay fails, those instant purchases fail with it. Buyers can still enter a debit card directly or use a bank transfer, both of which run on separate payment rails. The App Store outage created a second problem for the industry: users could not download or update exchange and wallet apps, and developers reported trouble signing in to App Store Connect, the tool used to ship updates.

Apple has not publicly identified a cause. There is no confirmed evidence linking the disruption to a security incident. The Bitcoin network itself was unaffected and continued producing blocks normally.

Note: Apple Pay had not been posted to the status page; its timing is based on Downdetector complaint data.

The failure is the latest in a run of high-profile technology disruptions in the US. A banking outage that began at JPMorgan Chase (JPM) on Monday spread to Wells Fargo (WFC), Bank of America (BAC) and the payments platform Zelle, and the bank attributed the problem to a software bug. Last week, a communications outage disrupted air traffic while a Microsoft (MSFT) failure locked users out of Outlook and Microsoft 365 applications including Teams and Word; the software company later said its own network upgrade was responsible. Charles Schwab (SCHW) briefly had website problems on Tuesday, and the National Security Agency's classified internal network went down the same day, with no evidence of a cyberattack reported so far.

Crypto platforms have absorbed similar shocks before. An Amazon Web Services outage in October 2025 took Coinbase's mobile app and the trading platform Robinhood offline for hours, and Coinbase's own review described degraded performance lasting three hours and 17 minutes. A Cloudflare disruption also hit Coinbase, Kraken and other major exchanges.

For Apple, the operational hit came with a modest market response. The stock slipped less than 1% on Tuesday, a smaller decline than the S&P 500's roughly 1% drop as technology shares led a broad selloff. Bank stocks fared worse: the large lenders fell about 1% to 2% on Monday, the day customers reported login and money-movement problems, roughly in line with a market that fell about 2%, and they declined again on Tuesday, leaving JPMorgan down nearly 5% for the week.

Investors and crypto traders are likely to focus on how quickly Apple restores Apple Pay, since the payment method functions as a front-end gateway for exchanges that depend on card and wallet funding to convert new customers. The company's status page is the live reference for which services have returned, while Downdetector shows whether user complaints are still climbing or easing. Until service is restored, buyers seeking digital assets can complete purchases by entering a debit card directly or using a bank transfer, both of which bypass Apple Pay entirely.
