Hellebuyck makes 43 saves, Devils push past Capitals to stay hot | NHL.com

TheTools · 17.11.2026 03:32 · source · permalink

Goalie improves to 7-0-0 with New Jersey, which gets two goals from Rodrigues

Devils at Capitals | Recap

WASHINGTON -- Connor Hellebuyck made 43 saves, including 23 in the third period, and improved to 7-0-0 as a member of the New Jersey Devils in a 7-4 win against the Washington Capitals at Capital One Arena on Saturday.

Evan Rodrigues scored two goals, and Lenni Hameenaho, Jesper Boqvist and Connor Brown each had a goal and two assists for the Devils (15-6-0), who have won seven of their past eight, including a 5-2 victory against the Capitals on Thursday in the first half of the home-and-home. Luke Hughes had three assists.

“We have been talking here about nights when you don’t quite have it,” New Jersey coach Sheldon Keefe said. “Two power-play goals, a short-handed goal and great goaltending. All that stuff stacks up to help you find a way to win a game when you don’t have it.”

NJD@WSH: Rodrigues' goal expands the advantage

Pierre-Luc Dubois extended his goal streak to four games, and Ryan Leonard had a goal and an assist for the Capitals (10-8-0). Cole Hutson had three assists, and Charlie Lindgren made 26 saves.

“You can’t just outscore opponents every night, so we have to be held to a higher standard,” Dubois said. “Just go from here, but four big points against a (Metropolitan Division) team that we’re not going to get back, but move forward and find some positivity out of it.”

Dubois gave the Capitals a 1-0 lead at 5:13 of the first period when he collected a rebound from between Devils defenseman Anton Silaev’s skates and lifted the puck over Hellebuyck’s right shoulder.

Hameenaho tied it 1-1 at 8:15 when he scored on a one-timer from the left circle off a pass from Cody Glass.

“I think we’re just giving up too much,” Capitals forward Boone Jenner said. “We were giving them too many good looks.”

Leonard put the Capitals ahead 2-1. His slap shot from the top of the right circle went in between Hellebuyck’s body and left arm at 14:34.

The Devils tied it 2-2 on the power play at 17:08 when Jesper Bratt’s pass deflected in off Brown’s skate at the left post.

Anthony Beauvillier gave Washington a 3-2 lead at 18:40, backhanding the puck home during a scramble in front.

Fourth-line forward Boqvist then tied it 3-3 at 10:33 of the second period. Lindgren made a save against Hameenaho on the rush, but a charging Boqvist knocked in the rebound from the low slot.

“It’s different people each night,” Keefe said. “The bottom six has come through for us here in these last couple of games.”

Timo Meier gave the Devils their first lead at 4-3 with a power-play goal at 16:18. He controlled a pass from Arseny Gritsyuk with his skate and scored from the right post.

Nico Hischier put New Jersey up 5-3 with a short-handed goal 56 seconds into the third, roofing a pass across the crease from Brown.

“I mean they had some big pushes, but I feel like we didn’t break,” said Hischier, the Devils captain. “And some wins are ugly. I think that one wasn’t a nice one, but we found a way, and that’s all that matters.”

Ilya Protas pulled Washington within 5-4 at 1:22, scoring a power-play goal on a wrist shot from the bottom of the right circle.

Rodrigues increased the lead to 6-4 when his shot from along the left wall deflected in off Washington defenseman Timothy Liljegren’s skate at 3:18. 

“We had a tough stretch there (six games in 10 days), and to face these guys, a division opponent, at the end of it, home and away, is really big for us,” Rodrigues said. “It’s two big wins for us.”

Rodrigues added an empty-net goal at 16:45 for the 7-4 final.

“We’ve got to learn -- this has been going on throughout the year -- our whole group … we have to learn to play winning hockey for 60 minutes,” Capitals coach Spencer Carbery said. “You can’t make some of the mistakes we make in situations. … You’re going to make mistakes in a game, but they can’t be catastrophic.”

NOTES: Hellebuyck, who was traded from the Winnipeg Jets on Oct. 23, became the sixth goaltender in NHL history to win each of his first seven games with a franchise. The last to do it was Frederik Andersen (eight games in 2021-22 with the Carolina Hurricanes). … Bratt extended his season-high point streak to eight games (10 points; two goals, eight assists). … Silaev played 12:45 and had two shots on goal in his NHL debut. … Dubois tied his career-high goal streak (four goals), previously achieved twice.
