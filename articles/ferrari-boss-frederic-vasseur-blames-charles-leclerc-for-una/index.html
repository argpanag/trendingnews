Ferrari boss Frederic Vasseur blames Charles Leclerc for 'unacceptable' collision with Lewis Hamilton in Las Vegas GP

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:57 · source · permalink

The Ferrari team-mates clashed on track once again, just weeks after their costly collision in Mexico City derailed the team’s race; Charles Leclerc punished by stewards and blamed by team boss Frederic Vasseur after spinning Lewis Hamilton in battle over sixth place

Sunday 22 November 2026 09:39, UK

Ferrari team principal Frederic Vasseur blamed Charles Leclerc for the "completely unacceptable" collision with team-mate Lewis Hamilton in the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

The Ferrari drivers made contact when battling over sixth place with 12 laps remaining in a breathless, frenzied race in which the team were attempting to recover from a poor qualifying when both drivers were knocked out in Q2.

Hamilton was spun around in the Turn Seven-collision, dropping to 10th in the order. Leclerc was found to be at fault by race stewards, who imposed a 10-second penalty on him.

That was applied to Leclerc's final race time and dropped him to 10th place in the final classification, with Hamilton ending up seventh.

Two weeks after speaking to both drivers to remind them of their responsibilities to the team after a squabble on track in Mexico had cost them a likely win, Vasseur was left unimpressed.

"Clearly, it's unacceptable," Vasseur told Sky Italy.

"A little bit the same message as Mexico because you have to think about the team, about the people working in Maranello. About all the stakeholders, the sponsors and so on.

"We told Charles not to overtake, because in a race like this, you have to focus on your own race rather than risk being overtaken by others. Charles clearly made a mistake, and it has cost the team dearly. What happened today [Sunday] is completely unacceptable.

"Honestly, I am more than upset because that message was clear after Mexico.

"We had a chat all together in Brazil. Today we passed the message to think about the team when they were racing together and the outcome is this one. It's mega frustrating for all of us."

'I'm penalised so it has to be my mistake' - Leclerc frustrated but accepts decision

In their report confirming the decision to penalise Leclerc, the stewards said the Monegasque had started his move on Hamilton from "a considerable distance behind" in the sister Ferrari, Hamilton's car, and had "not earned the right to the corner" by the apex.

They therefore found him to be "wholly to blame".

On Leclerc's cool-down lap back to the pits at the end of the race, Vasseur told him on Ferrari team radio: "Charles, I want to see you just after the race, before everything"

While stopping short of any apology in his immediate post-race TV interview, instead saying he was "embarrassed for the team", a visibly-frustrated Leclerc said he accepted the stewards' decision.

"I have been penalised so, as a result, I guess the majority and the stewards thought that it was my mistake. I'll accept that," Leclerc told Sky Sports F1.

"I'm not going to say much more. At the end of the day, I'm penalised so it has to be my mistake."

Asked if Ferrari now needed to impose stricter rules, Leclerc said: "Well, I just feel quite embarrassed for the team in general. We'll review."

'I've done all I can' - will Hamilton's call for change be heeded?

Hamilton had called for rules of engagement between the drivers after an incident between them on the first lap of Ferrari's home race at Monza in September, when he was forced onto the gravel. Leclerc subsequently apologised.

Asked by Sky Sports F1 after Sunday's incident if it was not time for some proper rules, Hamilton shrugged his shoulders and said: "That's for Ferrari to decide, obviously.

"I've done all I can and I've said everything and more.

"It's been difficult. All I can do is just show up and give it everything I can and hopefully in the background they eventually figure it out."

Seemingly fully aware that they risked disadvantaging each other by fighting hard as they both attempted to make their way back up the order from the fringes of the top 10, Hamilton asked the team over the radio "can you tell him to back the f*** off" when they came up to the McLarens.

"I'm not going to get into the scenario of that," he said afterwards. "That's something for you to speak to Ferrari about.

"But we knew that were faced with a massive challenge this weekend. We didn't realise that there'd be such a limitation on these straights.

"Qualifying was obviously difficult but in the race I felt great and I was really progressing well. I really ultimately wanted to try and make sure we finish ahead of the McLarens, because that's who we're really fighting in the constructors'. Then avoiding a collision with one of the McLarens got me a 10-second penalty which was, I think, really harsh and which was frustrating.

"But I just got my head back down and kept chasing and managed to get back in the fight and then again got knocked back again.

"But I never gave up and I was just trying to get the best result. I really think we could have had a podium today with the pace we had."
