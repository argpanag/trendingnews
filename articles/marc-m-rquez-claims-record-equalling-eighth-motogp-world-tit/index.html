Marc Márquez claims record-equalling eighth MotoGP world title in Portugal

Spaniard matches Giacomo Agostini’s premier-class tally

Márquez defends his title despite double surgery in May

Marc Márquez sealed the 2026 MotoGP world title at the Portuguese Grand Prix on Sunday, after his eighth-placed finish proved enough to establish an unassailable lead in the overall standings.

The Ducati rider needed to finish in the top nine in Sunday’s race and despite starting second on the grid, Márquez rode with caution to finish eighth and seal the title with a round to spare. The Spaniard’s eighth premier-class title puts him alongside the great Giacomo Agostini at the top of motorcycle racing’s all-time standings.

The ⁠race in Portimão was won by Marco Bezzecchi, ⁠who held a 102-point advantage ​over Márquez at one stage this season, while his Aprilia teammate Jorge Martín and Trackhouse rider Raúl Fernández completed the podium.

All eyes were on Márquez, who marked the occasion by scaling a scaffold to address the cheering ⁠fans, before watching a big-screen montage of the defining moments of his career. As a final flourish, the Spaniard ascended a staircase accompanied by the silhouette of a goat on the screen, a nod to his growing ‘Greatest of All Time’ status, before receiving a golden ⁠commemorative helmet from Agostini himself.

“We need ​to be happy. I know that we are here celebrating a historic moment. ‌I know how I suffered,” said an ‌emotional Márquez. He underwent double surgery on his foot and shoulder midway through the season, following a crash at the French Grand Prix in May.

“I know that in the middle of the season, the fans believed more than I did. My people believed ‌more than I did. But I kept pushing, I kept doing my best,” he added. “I said last year that it was a championship where I was at peace with myself. This one is for all the fans that believed in me from 2020 until now. So thanks to all of you, this is for all of you.”

Márquez came agonisingly close to securing the title in Saturday’s sprint but his hopes were dashed when he crashed out on the penultimate lap while running in third place, a finish that would have been sufficient to crown him champion.

Unlike Saturday’s overcast conditions with spots of rain on the circuit, Sunday’s 25-lap race at the Algarve International Circuit was ‌held in bright sunshine.

Martín needed to outscore Marquez by 18 points while the defending champions’ equation was far more simple: secure a top-nine finish regardless of his rivals’ results to get his hands on the trophy again.

Márquez had been cautious in the ​sprint but this time he shot off the line to take the lead from polesitter Bezzecchi while several riders jostled for position behind him, with Martín moving up from seventh to fourth. Márquez was soon swallowed up by the faster Aprilia bikes around him, quickly falling to seventh as he exercised caution amid aggressive overtaking.

Pedro Acosta’s crash moved Márquez up to sixth but he had to concede two places to Fabio Di Giannantonio and Ai Ogura, with the 33-year-old rider happy to open the door ⁠to let them pass knowing eighth would be good enough for the title.

Up front, Bezzecchi finally moved past Fernández ​to take the lead on lap 15, while Martín also ​found a way past three laps later with an identical ​overtake to make it a factory Aprilia one-two.

On lap 19, Márquez fell to ninth as KTM’s Brad Binder nudged him aside, but a crash ​from Diogo Moreira on the last lap moved Márquez back up to eighth, and he held on to secure his 10th Grand Prix crown overall having previously won a title in both the Moto2 and Moto3 classes.

“Super happy about the season, obviously some highs and some lows, but my best season in MotoGP so far,” said Bezzecchi, who also missed races this season through injury.

“Still a lot of things to improve. But congrats to Marc, he really deserves everything. He’s one of the strongest riders in history, so to fight with him is an honour,” the Italian added. “Hopefully next year I will fight again and make his life even harder.“
