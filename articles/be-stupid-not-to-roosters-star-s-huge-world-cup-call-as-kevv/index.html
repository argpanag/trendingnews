‘Be stupid not to’: Roosters star’s huge World Cup call as Kevvie set to name Kangaroos squad

TheTools · 05.10.2026 01:46 · source · permalink

Rob Toia has made his World Cup call.

The Roosters centre is set to represent Tonga at this month’s tournament after being at the centre of a tug-of-war between Mate Ma’a and Australia.

Code Sports reported that both nations believed Toia has committed to play for them at the World Cup, and even the now premiership-winning centre admitted to being torn as recently as last week.

However, Toia confirmed to AAP after the grand final that he would be pledging his allegiance to Tonga in a big blow to the Kangaroos.

“I just thought of missing out on being a part of a special team, and (Tonga) has what it takes to win the World Cup,” Toia told AAP.

“I’d be stupid to not want to be a part of it.

“I back myself to be in contention to play for Australia in any other year.

“I’d still love to achieve that Australian dream but I feel like I’ve still got so much footy to play and I still back myself to put myself in contention in any other year.”

Bradman Best looks locked into one of the starting centre spots, with Latrell Mitchell, Kotoni Staggs, Jack Bostock and Tom Trbojevic among the other contenders.

Kevin Walters is expected to announce his squad on Monday, and while a few positions are up in the air Penrith fullback Dylan Edwards is one name that definitely won’t feature.

He has been ruled out of the upcoming World Cup due to a knee injury according to Code Sports.

The four-time premiership winner injured his PCL in last week’s preliminary final loss to the Knights.

Edwards injured the knee in the first half against the Knights and was clearly bothered by it and struggled to move laterally when carrying the ball.

Edwards will undergo surgery on the knee which means the battle for the Kangaroos No. 1 jersey looks to be between James Tedesco and Reece Walsh.

Walsh was the Australian fullback during last year’s Ashes Test series and was arguably the team’s best player while Tedesco is in sublime form heading into today’s Grand Final and looks to have the inside running for the No.1 jersey.

Edwards has played six Tests for Australia. His recovery from knee surgery won’t be a long one, but lengthy enough to miss the World Cup on home soil.

One injured Panther who will be available for the World Cup is Isaah Yeo.

The Daily Telegraph is reporting that the Australian captain will delay surgery on his ruptured pec in a bid to lead his country at a home World Cup.

Elsewhere, Panthers champion Nathan Cleary obviously won’t take any part in the World Cup and was spotted leaving hospital after undergoing another operation.

Cleary left Royal North Shore Hospital on Sunday morning after undergoing surgery on the broken right foot he took into last week’s game against the Knights.

It was the second time in seven days that Cleary has undergone surgery after his badly broken lower leg was operated on after he snapped it in the preliminary final loss to the Knights last Sunday.

Cleary was wheeled out of hospital with plaster covering the bottom half of both legs.

WORLD CUP SPOTS ON THE LINE IN THE GF

Fox League expert Luke Keary flagged that there could be added motivation for a few players in Sunday’s grand final, and it sounds like he is right with Walters tipped to watching the game very closely.

Code Sports reported that a standout game from Fletcher Sharpe could see him jump into contention for a bench spot with the Kangaroos, although the publication conceded the dynamic Knights playmaker is only an outside chance.

Walker is also in the frame for the vacant halfback jumper left by Cleary and could boost his chances of selection with a big performance against the Knights.

Keary named Sharpe as a “smokey” for his team, while he also said that Walker’s potential selection could hinge on how he performs in the grand final.

“If he goes and kills it against the Knights, I think he gets picked. If he doesn’t fire, well Mitch Moses went on the Ashes and his long-range kicking game is a nice complement to Munster,” Keary said.

MIXED NEWS FOR TONGA

Elsewhere, Isaiya Katoa is going to delay surgery on his injured ankle to suit up for Tonga but Mate Ma’a won’t have gun South Sydney forward Keaon Koloamatangi available for selection.

Instead, Code Sports reports that he is set on playing for Australia despite considering suiting up for Tonga.

Rabbitohs teammate David Fifita, on the other hand, is not expected to make the Tongan side but is a chance of making his Kangaroos debut.

ONE BIG POSITIONAL HEADACHE FOR KIWIS

Meanwhile, New Zealand is down to just one clear-cut option at hooker with Phoenix Crossland (suspended) and Brandon Smith (knee) both unavailable.

Jeremy Marshall-King is the easy pick to start for the Kiwis in the No.9 jersey but beyond that New Zealand is struggling to find a contingency plan,

Wayde Egan is the obvious choice and would likely start ahead of Marshall-King given he is coming off a stellar season which saw him claim Dally M Hooker of the Year.

The Warriors hooker is eligible to play for the Kiwis on residential grounds, having lived in New Zealand for more than five years.

However, he was not part of the Kiwis’ initial World Cup squad that was submitted to the International Rugby League earlier last month when Smith and Crossland were both available.

The Kiwis can apply for dispensation from the IRL but The New Zealand Herald reported that the New Zealand Rugby League has decided against it and are hopeful Crossland will be able to play later in the tournament.

Both Te Maire Martin and Kodi Nikorima are versatile enough to fill in at hooker if needed.
