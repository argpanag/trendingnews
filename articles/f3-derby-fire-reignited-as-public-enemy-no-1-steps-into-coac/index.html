F3 derby fire reignited as public enemy No.1 steps into coaching arena

TheTools · 16.10.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

Your digital subscription includes access to content from all our websites in your region. Access unlimited content and the digital versions of our print editions - Today's Paper.

Central Coast coach John Hutchinson still has coins that hostile Newcastle Jets fans had hurled at the then Mariners captain during his playing days.

Hutchinson was public enemy No.1 - and he loved it.

Jets fans would torment Hutchinson and hurl abuse and sometimes coins when he stood over a corner kick.

Rarely did an F3 derby go by without the Mariners' feisty No.7 shirt-fronting a Jets player. His duels - physically and verbally - with Jets legend Joel Griffiths were explosive to say the least.

Of course, the Jets - and Griffiths - had scored the ultimate derby triumph in the 2008 grand final.

The next chapter of the rivalry between the arch enemies will be written in the 2026-27 season opener at Gosford on Saturday.

This time, Hutchinson won't be on field poking the bear. He will have the coach's clipboard in hand.

"In terms of the rivalry, it has always been passionate. Fans from Newcastle and the Central Coast get into it. I think it is fantastic.

"I still have the coins at home that the Jets fans used to chuck at me when I played."

Hutchinson, who holds the Mariners appearance record with 271 games, returned to the club this season as head coach after after serving a coaching apprenticeship in Japan and the US.

"Myself and Mark Milligan played together back in the old NSL," Hutchinson said. "I considered him a close friend back then and now. I look forward to competing against him tactically and on the field between the players."

The Jets went down 3-2 to the Mariners in a thrilling derby in the opening round last season.

Jets substitute Lachie Rose scored in the 80th minute to level at 2-2. Then, seven minutes into stoppage time, Eli Adams had a penalty saved after Tom Aquilina was brought down in the box.

Mariners keeper Andrew Redmayne sent a Hail Mary down the other end. The bouncing ball was flicked on and then knocked high into the air out of reach of Jets keeper James Delianov. Nicholas Duarte pounced and slotted home in the 98th minute for a famous derby win.

It was Milligan's first league game in charge.

Of course, the Jets recovered from the derby despair and went on to record a historic season capped with the Premiers' Plate.

They iced the premiership with a resounding 4-0 victory at home over the Mariners in the final round.

Eli Adams, Clayton Taylor, Lachie Bayliss, Mark Nattam Lachie Rose, Ben Gibson and Kota Mizunuma have since departed.

The Jets have made a slow start to the new campaign. Their Australia Cup defence ended in the first game with a 3-2 loss to Preston.

The opening two games of the Asian Champions League Elite have also been defeats, going down 7-1 away to Kashima Antlers and 3-1 to Gamba Osako at home on Tuesday night.

The performance against Osaka was step up from the humbling in Japan.

Will Dobson was replaced after falling heavily on his shoulder against Osaka. He trained with the squad on Friday but remains in doubt.

Zach Clough has been ruled out after injuring his ankle.

Major signing Joe Lolley played off the bench on Tuesday and is likely to start.

The Mariners have undergone a major transformation in the off-season. They are under new owners as well as a new coach and have a very young squad.

"It is an important game for the club and our members and fans," Hutchinson said.

"That is what the players have to understand. They are not just playing for our team and themselves, they are playing for the people on the central coast.

"The Jets are a good team. Mark Milligan obviously had some really good teachers for him to play the football he is playing.

"You can see he is a very good coach. They are quick in all situations, they have multiple forward runs. We have to really be cautious of their run because they try to threaten in behind with the space."

Jets players and coaching staff were not available for comment.

Josh Callinan reports, Newcastle Jets veteran Cassidy Davis has called on community support as the A-League Women's squad prepare to play at their new home away from home this season.

Saturday will see the Jets line up for the first of five fixtures at Weston's recently redeveloped Hunter City Motor Group Stadium.

There are another three ALW matches scheduled at fellow Coalfields venue Maitland Sportsground during 2026-2027.

It comes after City of Newcastle confirmed recently that regular game headquarters, No.2 Sportsground, would be closed from this month until February due to "returfing and remediation work".

Davis, speaking on the eve of a Jets opener against Perth on October 17, expressed her disappointment in moving away from familiar territory but hoped fans would follow up the valley.

"It's good to be out at Weston and they've done a great job to get that facility as best it can be and apparently it's looking really good. So keen to get out there into a different community, " Davis said.

"But obviously No.2 is our main field and we've been at that field for a long, long time now. So it's disappointing we can't be there. That's our home and we used to train there as well.

"It's going to be different, definitely, and I wish we could play more games in and around Newcastle, but hopefully we can get the support up in the Hunter Valley.

"Obviously it's not our choice to play all our games up there so it's really important the community can just get behind us and make the trip from wherever they are and support us as much as possible."

Weston also later houses Jets encounters with Central Coast (February 7), Melbourne Victory (February 14), Canberra (February 20) and Western Sydney (March 28), all in the new year.

The Jets will welcome Wellington (January 1), Melbourne City (January 10) and Brisbane (January 24) to Maitland during the height of summer.

A club double header (women and men), versus Sydney FC, is slated for the region's biggest venue McDonald Jones Stadium on March 20.November 21 against Adelaide, the last hosting commitment of 20 overall appearances, remains listed as TBC.

Squad wise American quartet Claire Henninger, Alex Klos, Allie Thornton and Cameron Valladares could all debut for Newcastle this weekend, along with Akeisha Sandhu, Poppie Hooks and Tamar Levin.

Davis, Libby Copus-Brown and Natasha Prior are among the Jets familiar faces.

Emma Bates and Alexis Collins, who recently suffered a season-ending knee injury, are unavailable.
