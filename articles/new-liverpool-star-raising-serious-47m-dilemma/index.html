New Liverpool star raising serious £47m dilemma

Ronald Araujo Gives Liverpool a Different Edge as £47m Question Starts to Build

Liverpool have spent the opening weeks of the new season searching for certainty after a campaign in which too much felt improvised, especially in defence. According to ECHO, one of the more unexpected answers has come in the shape of Ronald Araujo, a player borrowed from Barcelona and asked to solve a problem that has tripped up plenty of others.

It is a fascinating subplot because it says plenty about where Liverpool are, what Andoni Iraola values, and why the club may soon have a serious decision to make over a reported £47m option. Araujo did not arrive as a glamorous headline signing. He arrived with a touch of uncertainty, some baggage from a difficult spell in Spain, and the sense that he might be useful across the back line. What has happened since is more interesting than that. He has quickly become an important part of Liverpool’s unbeaten start, and he has done so by playing a role that is not naturally his.

There is a certain irony in that. Liverpool have spent a long time grappling with the after-effects of life after Trent Alexander-Arnold, whose gifts were so singular that replacing him like-for-like was always going to be impossible. Since his departure to Real Madrid in 2025, the club have tried a variety of answers. Some were practical, some were desperate, and some simply exposed how awkward the question had become. Injuries, patchwork selections and short-term fixes all played their part in destabilising the side.

Araujo has not tried to imitate what came before. That may be why this has worked. He offers a defence-first interpretation of the role, one built on concentration, strength, timing and the kind of authority that eases anxiety in those around him. It changes the geometry of Liverpool’s back four. It gives Virgil van Dijk a trusted ally beside him and allows the line to defend space with greater conviction. Clean sheets have followed, and confidence tends to travel quickly once defenders start to believe the ground beneath them is stable again.

Right-back role reshaped under Andoni Iraola

That shift matters because Iraola is still imposing his ideas on this team. Early in a new era, a manager will take reliability over romance every time, and Araujo has provided that. He may see himself as a centre-back, and in the long term Liverpool may still imagine him there, but his current job has clear value. He has made the position more secure, more combative and more sensible. In a side still learning its new manager’s demands, that has real importance.

Photo IMAGO

The backdrop to all this is Conor Bradley’s absence. Liverpool still appear to view Bradley as the long-term fit for the position once he is fully recovered and back to full sharpness. His energy and aggression suit the role, and there is a feeling inside the club that his athletic profile can give Liverpool balance without asking him to mimic the passing repertoire of Alexander-Arnold. But for now that remains a plan for later, not a solution for today.

Today belongs to Araujo. His impact has been noticed by those around him and Van Dijk’s verdict carried weight. “He is a great player,” said the Liverpool captain. “I don’t think he was the captain of Barcelona for no reason.

“He is a fantastic player. He is not a natural right-back but he has filled in the best way possible and I am pleased for him. It has been an intense couple of weeks for him.

“So hopefully now in this international break, he can get a little bit of rest before the chaos will start when we come back. It is down to the whole team, but obviously he did very well so far.”

Those comments underline something else Liverpool have needed, leadership. The departures of senior figures in recent windows have left the squad a little lighter in personality and voice. Van Dijk remains the obvious standard-bearer and Alisson Becker continues to lead in his own measured style, but dressing rooms thrive on a spread of authority. Araujo arrived with that reputation already formed. He had captained Barcelona, he carries standing with Uruguay, and at 27 he has enough experience to command respect without saying too much.

£47m transfer decision growing on Merseyside

So the £47m question looms. It is far too soon to declare it an obvious bargain or an unavoidable priority. Liverpool will want a longer sample, Bradley’s recovery will influence the thinking, and Araujo’s own adaptability across the season will matter. Yet this much is already clear, the conversation has changed. What looked like cover now looks like possibility. What felt temporary now carries the first hints of permanence.

That is the mark of a successful start. Araujo has not arrived with noise or theatre. He has simply done the hard parts well, defended properly, brought calm to a troubled area and reminded Liverpool that solutions in football do not always come wrapped in glamour. Sometimes they arrive in a borrowed shirt, playing slightly out of position, and asking a club to think harder about the future than it expected.

Our View

From a Liverpool supporter’s point of view, this report is intriguing because Araujo seems to offer something the squad has badly needed, authority. Not every fix has to be spectacular. Sometimes you just want someone in that back line who looks like he enjoys defending, relishes duels and gives everyone else a lift.

The key thing here is that he appears to complement what Iraola is trying to build. Liverpool looked far too open at times last season and there were matches where the right-back slot felt like a warning light on a dashboard. If Araujo can steady that side while Bradley gets properly fit, that is already valuable.

The £47m figure still feels significant, and supporters will want to see more before calling for a permanent deal. There is a difference between a strong start and a full-season answer. Even so, there is enough here to believe this may become one of those smart pieces of business that grows on everyone very quickly.

What makes it especially interesting is the leadership element. Liverpool have lost important voices over the last year or two, and teams chasing major honours need more than talent. They need personality. If Araujo keeps defending like this and keeps adding that edge to the dressing room, plenty of fans will start wondering whether £47m is actually the right price for a problem solved.
