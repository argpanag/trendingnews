Sydney Marathon winner banned for three years, stripped of title

TheTools · 19.11.2026 07:41 · source · permalink
  • S

    By Simon Smale

  • Topic:Marathon

By Simon Smale

Topic:Marathon

Peres Jepchirchir won the Sydney marathon by more than three and a half minutes. (Getty Images: Jeremy Ng)

In short:

Peres Jepchirchir has been banned for three years and disqualified from the 2026 Sydney Marathon for doping.

Jepchirchir tested positive for EPO in an out-of-competition test.

What's next?

Second-place finisher Irene Cheptai of Kenya has been elevated to champion.

This year's Sydney Marathon winner Peres Jepchirchir has been handed a three-year doping ban after testing positive for EPO. 

The 33-year-old returned the adverse finding in an out-of-competition test in Kenya before travelling to Sydney in August.

One of the all-time greats of marathon running, Jepchirchir claimed victories in four of the World Marathon Majors; New York (2021), Boston (2022), London (2024) and Sydney (2026), the 2025 World Athletics Championships, as well as winning gold at the 2021 Olympic Games.

Her Sydney Marathon victory has now been stripped, and legacy trashed, following her doping result.

"In an interview on [September 17], the athlete denied using EPO and said she had no knowledge of how the AAF (adverse analytical finding) had occurred," the Athletics Integrity Unit said.

"Despite her explanations in the interview, the former world record-holder in the half-marathon subsequently signed an admission of anti-doping rule violations and acceptance of consequences form."

Peres Jepchirchir won in New York in 2021. (Getty images: Elsa)

Sydney Marathon race director Wayne Larden said he was disappointed the race had been won by a doper.

"We're disappointed for every athlete, partner and fan affected," Larden said in a statement.

"But this outcome shows the system works. Alongside our fellow [World Marathon Majors], the Sydney Marathon has voluntarily invested in additional out-of-competition testing, above and beyond what is required of us, and this case is exactly why that investment matters.

"There is no place for doping at the Sydney Marathon or in the sport at all, and anyone who cheats should know they will be caught."

Jepchirchir was provisionally suspended in October after an out-of-competition sample collected in Kenya ahead of the race returned an adverse analytical finding for EPO. 

She was also tested on race day in Sydney and returned a negative result, highlighting the importance of out-of-competition tests.

The Sydney Marathon announced that Kenyan runner Irine Cheptai, who finished second behind Jepchirchir on the day, will be elevated to champion.

Irine Cheptai came home second in Sydney, but is now a Marathon Major winner for the first time. (Getty Images: Jeremy Ng)

Cheptai endeared herself to the Sydney crowd, handing her drink to Australian Paralympian Michael Roeger during the race, earning her a nomination as a finalist for World Athletics' 2026 International Fair Play Award.

It is the 34-year-old's first win in one of the eight World Marathon Majors: London, New York, Boston, Chicago, Berlin, Tokyo, Sydney and Cape Town.

"Today we get to celebrate Irine Cheptai, who raced clean and with real sportsmanship, and we're proud that Sydney will be where she claims her first [World Marathon Majors] title," Larden said.

Sydney Marathon organisers said that all other tests from the event had now been cleared, meaning there would be no more disqualifications.

Cheptai finished three minutes and 40 seconds behind her Kenyan teammate, running 2:22:11.

Two Ethiopians will step up onto the podium as well; Shure Demise Ware will be credited with second after running 2:22:33, with Haven Hailu Desse up to third in 02:22:57.

Jepchirchir is yet another high-profile Kenyan athlete to test positive for doping, with more than 140 sanctioned for doping offences since 2017, when the AIU was established.

Last year, Kenya's ⁠women's marathon world record holder Ruth Chepngetich was banned for three years after admitting anti-doping rule violations involving hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ), a ‌prohibited diuretic.

Chepngetich's world record of 2:09:56 remains on the books, however, because it ​was set before her positive test.

"Out-of-competition testing is one of the most powerful tools we have in protecting clean sport," Athletics Integrity Unit chief executive Brett Clothier said.

"It reaches athletes when and where they least expect it. This case is a clear example of that, and it would not have been possible without the Sydney Marathon's voluntary investment in additional testing this year. 

"We're grateful to have such a committed partner in the fight for clean athletics."
