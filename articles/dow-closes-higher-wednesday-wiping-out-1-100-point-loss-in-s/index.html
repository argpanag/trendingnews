Dow closes higher Wednesday, wiping out 1,100-point loss in stunning rebound: Live updates

TheTools · 24.11.2026 23:03 · source · permalink

The Dow Jones Industrial Average erased an 1,100-point plunge on Wednesday as Wall Street tried to recover from steep declines led by artificial intelligence-linked stocks.

The blue-chip Dow advanced 164.25 points, or 0.33%, to end at 50,182.95. The Nasdaq Composite, however, fell 0.62% to 23,585.80, while the S&P 500 shed 0.25% to close at 7,182.78.

Stocks came well off their lows as the session went on. The blue-chip Dow lost more than 1,100 points, or 2.2% at its low of the day. The tech-heavy Nasdaq was down 4.6%, and the broad market S&P 500 shed 3.2% at their respective lows.

Traders chalked up part of the rebound to light trading ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday as well as the circulation of a blog post by Michael Burry, in which he said that his portfolio is "temporarily almost entirely long."

The Dow was boosted by gains in stocks such as International Business Machines, Salesforce and Sherwin-Williams. S&P 500 sectors such as materials, energy and real estate were all higher, bolstering the index.

However, Nvidia led AI-related stocks lower, dropping almost 4%. Micron Technology moved down 4.5%, while Oracle declined more than 5%, and Alphabet shed more than 2%. The State Street Technology Select Sector ETF (XLK) ended nearly 1% lower.

Meanwhile, semiconductor stock Ambarella plummeted more than 19% after its forecast for the current quarter came in light. The company's earnings and revenue for the third quarter also missed expectations.

"This market is very bifurcated" between AI and non-AI stocks, said David Wagner, head of equities at Aptus Capital Advisors. "The market is pulling back obviously due to a pullback in tech, pullback in semis, pullback in the AI narrative, but right now, I don't see that being a growth scare. I see it being a sentiment-based scare" based on factors such as the composition of Congress in the new year, he added.

To be sure, there's still a "big worry" among investors that the worries in AI could move from a sentiment-based scare to a growth-based scare "if the market starts pricing in too much Fed activity of hiking rates," Wagner also said.

October's personal consumption expenditures price index reading showed that inflation still isn't slowing down, which reiterated expectations for interest rate hikes from the Federal Reserve in December and January and put investors in a risk-off mood.

Headline PCE rose 0.3% last month, putting the annual inflation rate at 3.8%. While both figures were in line with expectations, the annual rate increased from 3.7% in September. Excluding food and energy prices, core PCE increased 3.3% year over year, coming in slightly above the Dow Jones forecast.

Investor sentiment was also weighed by the White House unveiling a barrage of new tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the European Union.

Wall Street is coming off a losing session for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, after tech stocks gave back some of their gains from the prior session as Nvidia sank and Anthropic fell below its IPO price.

Tuesday's choppy trading action was just the latest in a volatile month. Even before the ouster and reinstatement of Nvidia's Jensen Huang — which sent stocks soaring Monday — and Democrats' sweep in the midterm elections, investors were deliberating the future of the artificial intelligence build-out amid a worsening macroeconomic outlook.

The U.S. stock market will be closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving. The market will also close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended the session in positive territory.

The 30-stock Dow added 164.25 points, or 0.33%, to 50,182.95. On the flip side, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite fell 0.25% and 0.62% to 7,182.78 and 23,585.80, respectively.

U.S. consumers plan to spend more this holiday season, with higher-income households driving the increase while lower-income shoppers pulling back, according to a recent Citi survey.

The survey of about 2,500 consumers conducted with Citi's Innovation Lab from Nov. 5 to Nov. 16 showed that respondents plan to spend 12% more than 2025 holiday season, highlighting the K-shaped consumer theme.

Inflation remained a concern, with 72% of respondents at least somewhat worried about food and fuel prices. Among those cutting spending, 48% said higher food and fuel costs, up from 39% last year. Shoppers reported price increases across all 11 categories surveyed, led by groceries, clothing, electronics, and home improvement.

Higher-income households are driving the gain with households earning more than $100k planning to spend $1,202, up 34%, while those earning under $25,000 expect to spend $295, down 3%.

The divide reflects a reversal from last year, when the highest-income group planned the steepest spending cut (-15%) and the lowest-income group was the only one expecting to spend more (+6%), Citi wrote.

The U.S. proposed on Wednesday a slew of new tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the European Union and Japan.

A senior U.S. official said all of the trading partners have been informed of the new levies, and that neither country's tariffs will exceed rates announced as part of framework deals on reciprocal trade. The new tariffs will not stack on top of existing duties, except for China, which faces a 20% tariff on top of the 25% imposed in 2018 over forced technology transfers.

The Trump administration says the new measures are to combat "overcapacity," where a government subsidizes excess industrial production, allowing cheaper goods to enter the U.S. market and undermine domestic producers.

Tariffs announced on Wednesday are being implemented under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. In February, the U.S. Supreme Court struck down tariffs that the Trump administration had implemented under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.

A Swiss parliamentary committee has backed tougher capital rules for UBS, moving a proposal that the bank says would require it to hold about $16 billion in additional core capital one step closer to becoming law.

The lower house's economics committee endorsed a plan that would require UBS's Swiss parent bank to hold tier 1 capital equal to 90% of the book value of its stakes in foreign subsidiaries. It matches a plan backed by Switzerland's upper house in September — but falls short of the Swiss government's proposed 100% requirement.

The plans stem from UBS's emergency takeover of stricken rival Credit Suisse in 2023, with lawmakers aiming to make the bank more able to absorb potential losses abroad. UBS has resisted the move.

"If confirmed at the conclusion of the ongoing parliamentary process, today's recommendation by the committee would result in a further excessive tightening of Swiss capital requirements, which are already among the most stringent globally," UBS said in a statement.

The full lower house is expected to take up the bill in December. UBS shares were almost 1.2% higher in morning trade.

Data published by McKinsey on Wednesday showed 45% of Europe's industrial companies are showing "heightened levels of financial stress or even distress."

Analysts at the consultancy giant analyzed an index of around 1,200 public and private European companies with revenue of more than 100 million euros ($109.4 million).

"Industrial companies in Europe are a backbone of the region's economy, accounting for about 36 percent of value added, 29 percent of jobs, and more than 45 percent of R&D," the authors of a report on the findings said. "Yet this industrial engine is struggling to maintain its competitive position against other countries, particularly China."

But they noted that "decline is not inevitable" if companies enlist "a balanced set of actions taken in parallel." The report's authors pointed to strategies including raising labor productivity, addressing wasteful spending, using AI to reduce administrative burdens, and consolidating portfolios.

The UAE is "very optimistic" about achieving 7%-8% growth next year, the country's economy and tourism minister Abdulla bin Touq Al Marri told CNBC after the latest data from the International Monetary Fund showed the country would avoid a recession in 2026.

The IMF last week revised the UAE's real GDP forecast to 0.2% growth in 2026 from a 3.9% contraction predicted a month ago. "The UAE is coming back, and that adjustment within one month shows that there is a huge potential aspect when it comes towards the end of the year," the minister told CNBC's Dan Murphy.

He said the tourism industry was already picking up with occupancy rates returning to "almost near normal" and international carriers including British Airways and Air France rerouting flights to the UAE. The minister expects economic growth to be higher than 0.2% this year.

The Iran war has disrupted economic activity in the UAE, with missile and drone attacks from Tehran and maritime disruptions weighing on energy trade, tourism, aviation and private-sector confidence.

Earlier this month, U.S. President Donald Trump said that renewed attacks on Iran were "highly unlikely" if negotiations produce a "very strong agreement." However, on Monday, Trump threatened that Tehran could face fresh U.S. strikes next year if the two countries failed to strike a deal, as per Fox News.

"We can we cannot promise the world for any storms that will land on our shore, but we can always promise that the UAE's economy is ready for any storm," Al Marri said.

Chinese technology giant Huawei was convicted Tuesday in New York on multiple federal criminal charges, including fraud, U.S. sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets, following a three-month trial, according to the U.S. Department of Justice.

The verdict marks a major development in a long-running criminal case against Huawei, with charges first publicly disclosed in 2019.

A federal jury found Huawei and three of its subsidiaries guilty of multiple offenses, including bank and wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, the Justice Department said in a statement.

"No person or company is above the law," U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said, accusing Huawei and its subsidiaries of conducting business in the United States through deception and theft.

The Justice Department said Huawei misrepresented its activities in Iran to banks to secure billions of dollars and retain access to U.S. dollar-clearing services. The company also supplied surveillance cameras that enabled Iranian authorities to monitor protests, the department said.
