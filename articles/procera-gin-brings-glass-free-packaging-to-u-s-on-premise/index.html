Procera Gin brings glass-free packaging to U.S. on-premise

TheTools · 27.10.2026 19:57 · source · permalink

The African-distilled Procera Gin announced the introduction of its Eco-Solution packaging to bars and restaurants in the United States. The Eco-Solution replaces traditional glass bottles with a food-grade foil bag that holds 3.75 liters of Procera Blue Dot, or the equivalent of five 750-mL bottles. The innovation reduces waste and lowers the cost per ounce of the artisan gin, the brand says.

“Our initial vision is to help curtail single-use glass in the on-premise and then eventually lead the effort to dramatically reduce single-use glass in the retail sector through the release of an innovative refill concept,” said Guy Brennan, Procera co-founder, in a statement. “The idea came from our customers at luxury safari lodges in Kenya’s Maasai Mara. They were accumulating mountains of empty glass bottles with few recycling options and asked whether we could simply send them the gin.

“That request led to the Eco-Solution, which has also eliminated the problem of bottles breaking during transport over the rough roads of the African Savannah,” Brennan continued.

According to the Glass Recycling Foundation (GRF), single-use glass is creating similar challenges in the U.S., where more than nine million containers go to landfills annually, 80% of which comes from bars and restaurants.

“Procera has long been looking for a way to revolutionize how spirits are sold in the on-premise,” said Alan Murungi, Procera co-founder, in a statement. “Our experience has proven that this concept works and is a win-win-win for accounts, customers and Procera. Apart from reducing the amount of single-use glass, our Eco-Solution is ideal for those serving batched cocktails in terms of convenience and cost; the Eco-Solution reduces the price of Procera Gin to $2 per ounce or less.”

The Eco-Solution is initially available to accounts in New York, Massachusetts, Georgia and Tennessee. Procera also shared that it obtained B Corp certification, validating the brand’s commitment to economic, social and environmental objectives in its business model and corporate decision-making process, it says.

The Eco-Solution and B-Corp certification are milestones in the gin brand’s vision toward sustainability. Procera is proofed using 100% rainwater. The artisan bottles are hand-blown from 100% recycled glass. Earlier this year, Procera adopted a new shank comprised of 99% sugar cane waste, while the cartons used in packaging are made from 99% sugar cane by-product.

The necker is made from recycled water-hyacinth and the small botanical salt bottles are attached to the gin using Kenyan sisal agave fiber. The only synthetic element on the bottle at present is the shrink-wrapped seal around the neck, but plans are in place to use regenerated plant-based cellulose in the near future.

“Our vision is that, by 2027, Procera packaging will use 100% natural materials,” Brennan added.
