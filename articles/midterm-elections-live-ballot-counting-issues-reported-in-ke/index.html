Why is Trump nixing US military aid to Israel? Because of Abdul El-Sayed

Election Day is just getting started but one campaign issue is already having some policy consequences.

President Donald Trump caught even Fox News hosts off-guard when he declared during a Tuesday interview that, because of attacks from the political left, he would cut off US military aid to Israel at the end of the year.

“Why should we suffer the slings from lunatics like an (Abdul) El-Sayed when they (Israel) don’t need it?” Trump asked, referring to the Democratic Michigan Senate nominee.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has has told him that Israel doesn’t need the aid, Trump claimed. (CNN has reached out to the prime minister’s office and the White House.)

If elected, El-Sayed who would be the first Muslim senator. Aid to Israel has been a major issue in that race. Democrat Haley Stevens’ anger at an ad from an outside group supporting El-Sayed, which tied aid to Israel to Americans’ cost-of-living struggles, seemed to contribute to her shocking decision to switch her endorsement from the Democratic nominee to the GOP nominee on Sunday.

The US aid in question — a little less than $4 billion each year — also benefits the US defense industry since it is used to buy American military hardware. Trump said Israel can continue to buy the hardware themselves.

Asked to clarify if this move to cut off aid was specifically to remove a talking point from the left, Trump suggested it was.

“The left uses the $4 billion payment that everybody knows about, when it’s no secret, as a signal of unfairness, and we can’t allow that to happen,” Trump told Fox.

But it’s Congress, not the president, that allocates US taxpayer dollars. In July, more than 100 Democratic lawmakers voted for an ultimately unsuccessful amendment to block military aid to Israel.

In a preview of spending fights to come should Democrats take control of Congress, Trump said he’ll use the savings to try to balance the federal budget – something that hasn’t been accomplished in years, in part because of tax cuts enacted by Trump and Republicans.

"No one should be panicked" amid ballot issues, Westchester County executive says

Though ballot scanners cannot process paper ballots in New York’s Westchester County, there is no need at this time to extend voting hours, County Executive Ken Jenkins said this morning in a video message.

“Your vote will count, ” he told the county’s nearly 700,000 voters, explaining that completed ballots are being placed in secure bags or ballot boxes to be counted later.

Candidates and campaigns have asked for extended voting hours, Jenkins added without naming them. The county is just north of New York City.

21 Florida polling places moved due to weather emergency

Dangerous flooding across central Florida is forcing election officials to scramble for alternative polling locations.

As of 9:30 a.m. ET, the secretary of state’s office said 21 locations had been moved in Brevard, Broward, Highlands, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie counties. In many cases, precincts are being merged into open poll locations.

“For the safety and security of our election workers and the voters of Brevard, it is necessary to relocate a couple of precinct polling locations due to these unprecedented circumstances,” Brevard County Supervisor of Elections Tim Bobanic said in a statement.

The changes, which were announced with less than 24 hours notice, are permitted under state law “in cases of emergency and when time does not permit compliance” with the usual notice requirements.

Gov. Ron DeSantis declared an emergency on Monday covering seven counties, citing “a significant threat to the safety, security, and well-being of Floridians in portions of Florida.”

Gas and diesel prices shatter Election Day records

Gasoline — a critical indicator of the cost of living for many Americans — has never been as expensive on Election Day as it is this Election Day.

Even though prices at the pump have retreated in recent days and weeks, the AAA national average for regular gas still stands at $4.15 on Tuesday.

That is easily the most expensive price for gas on any Election Day on record, according to a CNN analysis of AAA data that goes back to 2000. This shatters the prior Election Day record of $3.80 a gallon set in 2022, the year inflation hit a four-decade high under then-President Joe Biden.

On the campaign trail in 2024, Donald Trump blasted Biden for record-high gas prices and promised to drive gas below $2 a gallon.

But the reality is, in large part because of President Trump’s decision to go to war with Iran, a gallon of gas is more than $1 more expensive than on Election Day in 2024, when the national average was just $3.10 a gallon.

Moreover, gasoline is more expensive during Trump’s second term ($3.481 per gallon so far) than during Biden’s term ($3.471 per gallon).

When adjusted for inflation, gas prices don’t look quite as high. Real (inflation-adjusted) gas averaged above $5 a gallon nationally on Election Day in 2012.

Prices for diesel, the workhorse fuel that powers the modern economy, stand at $6.29 a gallon on Tuesday nationally. That blows away the prior Election Day record of $5.35 per gallon in 2022.

Diesel is at an all-time high even on an inflation-adjusted basis.

Higher gas and diesel prices have cost American consumers about $151 billion since the Iran war started, translating to more than $1,100 per US household, according to Brown University.

DOJ sends election monitors to districts that largely went Democratic in 2024 election

The Justice Department has sent federal election monitors to 126 counties and cities in all 50 states.

A review of the list shows they are being deployed to some of the biggest — and bluest — cities and counties in battleground states and beyond. From Dallas and Detroit to Chicago and Cleveland, many of the locations chosen are Democratic strongholds.

Several of the jurisdictions are also key population centers that will be pivotal in the outcome of races that will help determine the Senate majority — with Johnson County in Kansas and Harris County in Texas only two of several examples on the list.

“The Department prioritizes monitoring in jurisdictions required to provide language assistance. Election monitoring may also occur in some overlooked areas where there is a shortage of election observation data,” a Justice Department official told CNN. “In addition, observers are sent to jurisdictions where the Department has entered into a consent decree based upon previous election issues.”

The department says that approximately 1,000 monitors — Justice Department lawyers, not law enforcement officers — will observe the polling places for federal voting law violations like racial discrimination, voter intimidation, and failures to provide required language access.

They are instructed not to interfere with voting.

Still, some Democrats and voting rights activists have raised concerns that this year’s monitors could be used to undermine the voting process or to collect evidence for long-shot lawsuits to interfere with the election outcomes.

While the mobilization to all 50 states is significantly broader than previous efforts, where monitors are dispatched can shift between election seasons and under different administrations. Republican administrations often send more to Democratic areas, and vice versa.

During the primary elections, the Justice Department sent more than 100 monitors to polling locations.

During the 2022 midterms, during the Biden administration, the department sent less than 400 monitors to 24 states.

Democrats could flip state legislative chambers in key battleground states

State legislative races may not get top billing in this year’s consequential midterm elections, but strategists in both parties see a political shift toward Democrats in several key statehouses around the country.

Democratic gains in battlegrounds such as Arizona, Michigan and Wisconsin — where Republicans currently have full or partial control of state legislatures — could have a seismic effect. Change in control could shape not just policies in those states but the future of congressional redistricting, along with the path to the presidency in 2028.

An independent political handicapper, Sabato’s Crystal Ball at the University of Virginia, recently shifted the ratings of 10 state legislative chambers in Democrats’ favor, citing this year’s difficult political environment for Republicans.

Currently, Republicans control both legislative chambers in 28 states and Democrats in 18, according to the National Conference of State Legislatures. (Power is divided between the parties in three, while Nebraska’s legislature is considered nonpartisan.)

Here are a few states to watch on Tuesday:

— Arizona: Both chambers are currently held by Republicans, but Crystal Ball rates them as toss-ups, raising the specter of a Democratic trifecta in the state if its Democratic governor, Katie Hobbs, wins a second term.

— Michigan: Democrats control the state Senate, while Republicans hold a narrow majority in the House. GOP strategists say their priority is to retain their House majority, but that could prove difficult. At the top of the ticket, Democratic gubernatorial nominee Jocelyn Benson leads Republican John James in polling, including CNN’s most recent survey. Crystal Ball rates both chambers as toss-ups.

— Pennsylvania: Another battleground state where legislative power is split between the parties, with Republicans holding the majority in the state Senate. Crystal Ball recently moved that chamber toward Democrats and now rates it as a toss-up.

Gov. Josh Shapiro, who is seeking a second term, has supported his fellow Democrats in competitive state legislative races and may have the coattails to help his party flip the Senate. He had a 23-point lead over Republican Stacy Garrity among likely voters in a recent Franklin and Marshall College poll.

— Wisconsin: Both legislative chambers are currently controlled by Republicans, but court-ordered redistricting has created more competitive seats. This election could offer Democrats their best opportunity of flipping a legislative chamber for the first time in roughly 15 years.

CNN’s Ariel Edwards-Levy contributed to this report.

A snapshot of one of the most vulnerable GOP seats in Congress, a few miles outside NYC

One of the most vulnerable Republican seats in Congress sits just a few miles north of New York City, in a district former Vice President Kamala Harris won in 2024.

Incumbent Republican Rep. Mike Lawler, who has held the seat since 2022, is in a tight race against Biden administration alum and combat veteran, Democrat Cait Conley. This seat could play a crucial role in Democrats’ attempt to flip control of Congress.

New York’s 17th district includes counties like Westchester, which has historically leaned Democrat, as well as counties like Rockland and Putnam that have recently leaned more in favor of the GOP.

Lawler, who flaunts his crossover appeal with his voting record in Congress and willingness to break with President Donald Trump, has also leaned into marketing himself as a family man from Rockland County, living with the same struggles as its voters. He secured several endorsements from across the aisle this election cycle, including from Maine Rep. Jared Golden and local Democratic politicians.

Conley, who served as Director for Counterterrorism on the National Security Council under President Biden, has highlighted her years of service as an Army combat veteran and her desire to lower costs for residents across the district. Conley struggled with name recognition in the early stages of the race.

This race has been one of the most expensive in this year’s election cycle, with millions flooding in from both sides of the political aisle. Over $40 million was spent cumulatively on ads tying Lawler to Trump and scrutinizing Conley’s connection to AI companies.

It was also a hot spot for prominent politicians from across the country to stop. Conley was joined on the trail by Democrats like House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, former Secretary Pete Buttigieg and Sen. Mark Kelly. Lawler hosted Republicans like Speaker of the House Mike Johnson and joined Trump at his rally in Rockland County earlier this year.

The cost of living was a top concern for voters in NY17 when CNN spoke to residents. Many expressed concern about high gas prices, expensive grocery bills, and the rising housing market across the counties.

Voting machines unable to scan paper ballots in Westchester County, NY but voting continues

Issues with how ballots can be cast throughout Westchester County, New York, stem from voting machines not being able to accept paper ballots, the county Board of Elections said this morning.

But the county’s nearly 700,000 voters can also cast ballots by hand, officials said. Moreover, the county is looking to use secondary machines that will accept the ballots, said Raquel Brown, executive assistant for Tajian M. Nelson, the county’s Elections Commissioner.

“The Westchester County Board of Elections (BOE) is aware of an issue involving paper ballots that is preventing voting machines from accepting ballots at polling locations throughout the County,” the elections board said in a statement.

Elections officials earlier today had pointed to “an issue involving the thickness of paper ballots” before issuing a corrected statement removing the word “thickness.”

Voters in the county just north of New York City are being asked to fill out ballots that will be placed in secure “orange emergency ballot bags, which will remain secure and monitored” before they are counted, the board said.

“We strongly encourage all voters to remain in line, exercise patience and, most importantly, cast their ballots,” the statement added.

“The BOE sincerely apologizes to voters and poll workers for the inconvenience this situation has caused, and the BOE recognizes the importance of a smooth and efficient voting process. They are taking every necessary step to address the issue as quickly as possible.”

“The Westchester Board of Elections wants to reassure every voter that your vote matters, your ballot will be secure and your vote will be counted,” it said.
