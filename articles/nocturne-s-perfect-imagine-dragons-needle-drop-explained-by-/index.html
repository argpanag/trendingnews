On rare occasions, a television show features a needle drop so perfect that you feel it in your bones — dare I say, enough to make your system blow.

In Apple TV's hit psychological thriller, Nocturne, one of those defining music moments comes in Episode 4, when Karl Anders (Rory Culkin) finally gets his Imagine Dragons on.

Spoilers for Nocturne Episode 4 ahead.

For those who need a refresher, when Jurek (Stephen Graham) got his hair cut by Karl in Episode 2, he asked the nurse to share his favorite song. Karl barely took a beat before answering, "Radioactive" by Imagine Dragons. Why? "They were big when I was in high school," he explained. "The song made me feel — I don't know. It doesn't matter…"

While the nugget of personal info seemed like a throwaway line to show Karl letting his guard down around Jurek, Nocturne brilliantly called back to his Imagine Dragons obsession in Episode 4, "The Courier," with a delightfully badass "Radioactive" needle drop. So naturally, Decider asked Culkin and Nocturne co-showrunner and EP John Hlavin how they settled on Karl's favorite song.

"My 13-year-old daughter was very into it for a long time," Hlavin told Decider over Zoom. "I probably heard that song 15 million times in my car. I heard a lot of Imagine Dragons with my daughter driving various places, and it's one of those songs that just really gets stuck."

After learning Jurek was being transferred to an out-of-state facility in Episode 4, Karl bucked the system again and agreed to deliver a note to Jurek's lawyer. He put on a lightly badass jacket with studs and statement pins, boldly carried the message out of the building, beelined to his 1992 Pontiac Firebird, and started the engine — which caused "Radioactive" by Imagine Dragons to unexpectedly blare.

Photo: Apple TV

Nocturne gave viewers a glimpse of the bumper stickers on his car, which read, "BOOKS NOT BOMBS," "TAX THE RICH," "EAT THE RICH," and "Vote Like Your Rights Depend On It." As Karl sped off on his mission with a cracked side-view mirror, fuzzy dice hanging, and windows down, he stared at the road ahead, turned the volume up, and cracked a small smile. He was in the zone, listening to the nostalgic track that made him feel… Alive? Fearless? Understood? All of those things and more, all of which Jurek assured him mattered. He mattered.

The experience was undoubtedly exhilarating for Karl, but because the needle drop was so layered and surprising, it also packed a powerful comedic punch — though Hlavin didn't choose the 2012 song to be funny; he chose it because it slaps.

"It wasn't meant in any way to be — we weren't having a laugh about it. It was like the kind of thing where we were like, 'Oh, it's very catchy.' But mostly from the writing point of view, it was that I had spent so much time with Imagine Dragons, I just wanted it to pay off somewhere," Nocturne's showrunner told Decider.

Culkin even revealed he listened to "Radioactive" when filming to get into character. "Imagine Dragons, they're great. When I read Imagine Dragons [in the script] I was like, 'Aw, this poor guy,'" he said with a chuckle. "I think it just goes along with the bumper stickers and just the kind of guy he is."

Photo: Apple TV

In a 2021 interview with Forbes, Imagine Dragons frontman Dan Reynolds said he used to say the song was "about depression in general," then over the years he realized it was "about not giving up hope after losing Mormonism." In terms of Nocturne, Culkin told Decider he'd "leave it to the audience" to decide what his character's favorite song said about him. It may simply be Karl's self-empowering anthem, or in the context of Jurek, it could symbolize Jurek infecting the formerly pure Karl, ot the awakening Karl has in seeing Jurek as a man instead of a monster; someone who deserves the same rights as anyone.

"I think with Karl, there's good in everyone, even the worst of us," Culkin told Decider when asked how Rory justifies helping Jurek. "There is the four-year-old boy beneath it all. Karl just doesn't give up on people."

While the song's meaning is up for interpretation, there's no doubt that Nocturne's "Radioactive" needle drop was a perfect music moment. And the real thanks goes to Hlavin's daughter for taking the aux cord in her dad's car.
