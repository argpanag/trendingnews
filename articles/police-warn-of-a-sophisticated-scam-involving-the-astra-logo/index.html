Police Warn of a Sophisticated Scam Involving the Astra Logo

TheTools · 07.10.2026 00:32 · source · permalink

Alleged public buses

The Innerhoden Cantonal Police is warning the public about a new phishing scam.

Fabian Sommer/dpa/dpa-tmn

Particularly brazen phishing emails claiming to be traffic fines from the Astra are currently circulating. Cybercriminals are behind them, as the Appenzell Innerhoden cantonal police warn.

Here's what it's all about

  • The police are warning about a phishing scam in which cybercriminals are sending emails on behalf of the Federal Roads Office (Astra).
  • These are fake traffic tickets or payment notices demanding that you pay a sum of money or disclose payment information.
  • However, Astra does not issue any fines. Furthermore, government agencies do not request payments via email or text message.

The Appenzell Innerhoden Cantonal Police is currently warning the public about particularly brazen phishing attempts using the logo of the Federal Roads Office (ASTRA). According to the police, the fraudulent emails claim that the recipient has an unpaid traffic fine or include a corresponding payment reminder. Recipients are instructed to pay a sum of money or disclose payment information within a short period of time.

However, anyone paying close attention is likely to become suspicious right away. This is because the Astra neither issues traffic tickets nor asks citizens via email or text message to pay money or enter credit card information.

It's best to delete it right away

If you receive such an email, the cantonal police recommend that you do not reply under any circumstances and do not click on any links or open any attachments. In addition, you should never disclose personal information or payment details, nor should you make any payments. The cantonal police also advise deleting suspicious emails immediately.

The police and other authorities repeatedly warn about sophisticated online scams, some of which can cause significant harm to those affected.

While phishing emails have been a staple of cybercriminals’ arsenal for years, these and other fraudulent methods are becoming increasingly sophisticated thanks to artificial intelligence. For example, online scammers are now cloning the voices of family members, authority figures, or business partners, and use them to gain trust.
