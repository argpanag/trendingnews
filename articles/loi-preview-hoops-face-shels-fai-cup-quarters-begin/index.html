LOI preview: Hoops face Shels, FAI Cup quarters begin

TheTools · 03.09.2026 09:27 · source · permalink

There's just one SSE Airtricity Men's Premier Division down for decision on Friday, with Shamrock Rovers hosting Shelbourne in Tallaght.

There's also a couple of Club Orange FAI Cup quarter-finals to look forward to: Waterford v Sligo Rovers and Drogheda United v Galway United.

SSE Airtricity Men's Premier Division

Shamrock Rovers v Shelbourne, Tallaght Stadium, 8pm

For all the talk of wobbles and shakes, Shamrock Rovers remain on solid ground in the title race.

Stephen Bradley's men have the opportunity to go eight points clear when they welcome Shelbourne to Tallaght. With just seven league games to be played after tonight, the chasing pack will desperately hope the visitors can throw a spanner in the works.

This is the Hoops' game in hand over St Pat's and Bohemians, and it's a big one. Next week they travel to Inchicore to take on the Saints. A slip-up here would give Stephen Kenny belief that the reigning champions can be reeled in, with Bohs - who come to Tallaght on Monday, 12 October - also waiting in the long grass.

Shels are fifth, bang in the middle of the table. They got sucker-punched by a late, late Jimmy Keohane equaliser in Galway last week, and John Russell will hope for a show of character from his men here.

They're far closer to ninth-placed Drogheda (seven points ahead of them) than third-placed Bohs (15 points adrift), but fourth place is within sight, and that could earn a European place if St Pat's or Bohs go on to win the FAI Cup. Shels are five points off Dundalk with two games in hand.

Team news

Shamrock Rovers: Dan Cleary and John McGovern returned in last Sunday's draw at Derry City and have come through the week's training unscathed. Jonathan Afolabi missed the Derry match after getting a knock in the KuPS game but he's also available. Danny Grant and Victor Ozhianvuna remain out.

Shelbourne: John Martin is still recovering from a calf issue and won't be available, with Sam Bone also working his way back to fitness. Rodrigo Freitas is back after serving a suspension.

Club Orange FAI Cup quarter-finals

Waterford v Sligo Rovers, the RSC, 7.45pm

Could Waterford end up being the story of the season?

Graham Coughlan has done a sensational job at the Blues, who look like they could beat anyone in the division right now.

They were excellent at Richmond Park last week, coming away with a fully deserved 2-0 victory over St Pat's. Now, attention turns to the cup.

It's 46 years since Waterford last lifted the trophy. They beat St Pat's 1-0 in the 1980 decider, Brian Gardner getting the goal.

With so much momentum behind them (unbeaten in 10 games), there's a feeling that Coughlan's charges could end this campaign with some silverware.

For Sligo Rovers, it's a different story. They haven't won in the league since the 2-0 defeat of Dundalk on 25 April. As Waterford have surged from the foot of the table up to eighth (and only three points off fifth), Sligo have dropped to the bottom, five points behind Drogheda.

Perhaps the FAI Cup will continue to offer Darren Purse some welcome relief from the grind of the league. They swept past Kerry 4-0 in the third round, and will be encouraged by the scoreless draw they played out with Waterford two weeks ago.

Victory is not beyond Sligo, but they'll certainly have to improve on last week's meek showing at Bohs.

Team news

Waterford: Will Johnson and Sam Glenfield remain the only players unavailable for selection.

Sligo Rovers: Striker Jamie McGonigle's loan spell from Coleraine was cut short at his request - he's moved on to Limavady United. Defender David Jonathan has also left the club.

Drogheda United v Galway United, Sullivan and Lambe Park, 7.45pm

The dust has settled on Drogheda's pulsating derby against Dundalk, which ended with Daryl Horgan bending home a brilliant winner for the Lilywhites. Tonight the Drogs will attempt to erase the pain of that defeat and energise belief they can win a second FAI Cup in three seasons.

Kevin Doherty led the Louth club to glory over Derry City in 2024 and a week later guided them past Bray Wanderers in the promotion/relegation play-off to secure their top-flight status. Could Drogheda be in for a similar climax to this campaign?

They're ninth, six points behind Galway United, whose sole FAI Cup triumph came in 1991 when Johnny Glynn's late goal slayed Shamrock Rovers.

Both teams will have been quietly happy when the draw for the quarters was made, which is why this one is so difficult to call. They've met three times this year: one win for Drogs, one win for Galway, and one scoreless draw.

It will be very tight at Sullivan and Lambe Park. Don't be surprised if it goes all the way to penalties.

Team news

Drogheda United: Owen Lambe is still out injured. Jason Bucknor is suspended.
