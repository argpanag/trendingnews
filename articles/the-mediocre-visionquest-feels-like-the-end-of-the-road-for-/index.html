The mediocre VisionQuest feels like the end of the road for Marvel’s TV universe

TheTools · 13.10.2026 00:20 · source · permalink

Paul Bettany reprises his role as the robotic Avenger in this serviceable but uninspiring sequel to WandaVision

3/5

All bad things must come to an end, or so Marvel has concluded, with its stonkingly OK-ish Disney+ caper, VisionQuest, marking the tailing off of a largely disastrous attempt to parlay the success of its superhero movies to the small screen.

The series, in which Paul Bettany reprises his role as the high-flying, perpetually angsty man-machine Vision, represents the emptying of the superhero cupboard by Marvel and Disney. With the exception of the third and final season of Daredevil: Born Again and the newly announced Zodiac, no new live-action shows are planned for the foreseeable. Viewer apathy has done something beyond the power of the galaxy’s mightiest villains. It has – for the meantime at least – banished the “MCU” from our televisions.

The small-screen Marvel universe hasn’t been uniformly terrible. But quality control went through the floor the more Disney tried to flog this exhausted nag for all it was worth. In that context, VisionQuest is an unexpected 90th-minute comeback. It isn’t uniformly atrocious, and is significantly better than the worst of the Marvel shows, most of which have vanished down a collective memory hole never to be thought of again.

Bettany is visibly up for reprising the role of Vision, a cross between Christopher Reeve’s Superman and an iPhone with a dodgy battery. Marvel fans will recall that the character was killed off several films ago. But he’s back thanks to some superhero jiggery-pokery, with Bettany playing a copy-and-paste recreation of the original.

He’s living in Edinburgh. Or at least a Hollywood picture-postcard idea of Edinburgh where everyone lives in beautifully lit single-storey houses and where the local culture is evoked by a random encounter with a can of Irn-Bru. As he mooches away in his bartender job, Vision is haunted by memories of his grand love affair with his fellow Avenger Wanda Maximoff. That romance ended badly when Vision died, and a distraught Wanda brought him back to life, a story told in the best of Marvel TV shows: the David Lynch-influenced WandaVision.

Awkwardly, Vision also has a son (Ruaridh Mollica). He was conjured from the interstellar gloop by the crazed Wanda but is now very much alive and wandering around London in a state of confusion. As if that isn’t enough to scramble his circuits, Vision is also being pursued by an assassin named Paladin (Todd Stashwick), who has somehow become an international killer for hire despite his obvious incompetence (every attempt to bump off Vision ends disastrously).

Paladin has a laconic assistant played by Diane Morgan, whose impregnable comedic powers shrivel away to nothing in the blinding radiation of this stodgy comic-book script. Every actor has their kryptonite. For the deadpan Morgan, it is overblown Marvel telly.

VisionQuest also shamelessly lifts from Pixar’s Inside Out by outfitting Bettany’s hero with a crew of electronic voices who live inside his head, in a simulated house, and represent different aspects of his personality. They include Orla Brady as Vision’s sociable side and James D’Arcy as his personal security chief, plus a wonderful James Spader, who returns as the digital ghost of Vision’s evil creator, Ultron. In the films, Ultron is a CGI robot. But for plot (and budget) reasons, he returns in human form, sporting a magnificent beard and a natty top hat.

Recommended

Bettany and Spader are quite clearly the only ones having fun here – Morgan certainly isn’t – and their scenes together crackle with bromantic chemistry. But these are also the parts where VisionQuest feels least like a Marvel show, which is obviously the problem confronting the streamer. You can put great actors on the screen. But if they’re saddled with a third-rate superhero script, they can soar only so high.

VisionQuest begins on Disney+ on Wednesday, October 14
