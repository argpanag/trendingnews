Pictures show crane attached to final SS Richard Montgomery mast before removal

TheTools · 30.09.2026 22:52 · source · permalink

PICTURES show a crane has been attached to the final mast of the SS Richard Montgomery wreck before it is dismantled in the dramatic last stage of a major Thames Estuary project.

The remaining mast of the US Liberty ship, which ran aground carrying munitions in 1944, is being prepared for removal in the Thames Estuary between Southend and the Isle of Sheppey.

Final - The last remaining SS Richard Montgomery mast looks set for removal (Image: unexploded.live)

The Department for Transport awarded Resolve Marine a £9.5 million contract to dismantle the masts, which have stood above the water for more than 80 years.

The operation is designed to ease pressure on the wreck without disturbing the estimated 1,400 tonnes of explosives still aboard.

Project - Two masts have already been removed (Image: Brownes Thames Estuary Boat Trips)

Work is under way and will continue over the coming days, but weather conditions will determine when the mast is removed.

The explosives-filled wreck of the SS Richard Montgomery remains one of the UK’s most closely monitored maritime hazards.

Iconic - The masts were a landmark in the Thames Estuary (Image: Supplied)

Campaigners want at least one mast preserved and displayed in Southend as a piece of maritime heritage.

The US government has granted permission for the masts to remain in the UK, allowing plans to preserve them for public display in Southend and on the Isle of Sheppey.
