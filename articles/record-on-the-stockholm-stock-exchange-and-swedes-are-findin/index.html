Record on the Stockholm Stock Exchange – and Swedes are finding their way back to shares

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:32 · source · permalink

The Stockholm Stock Exchange is trading at record levels, and never before have so many Swedes owned shares! After the major outflow in spring, small investors have started finding their way back – and Investor is strengthening its grip as Swedes' absolute favourite share.

Swedish share ownership continues to grow. According to new statistics from Euroclear Sweden, which keeps track of who owns shares in Swedish companies, there are now around 2.9 million unique shareholders in Sweden. This is the highest figure since measurements began in 2000.

And that is not the only record right now. On Thursday, the Stockholm stock exchange traded at record levels, with both the broad index OMXSPI and the large-cap index OMXS30 rising during the day, reports Dagens industri.

Small investors are back

Spring was tough for the stock market's small investors. During the second quarter of the year, around 18,000 private individuals left the stock market – the largest drop that Euroclear has ever recorded. But during the summer the trend reversed, and during the third quarter approximately 9,100 new private shareholders joined. That corresponds to roughly half of spring's outflow.

Euroclear's analyst Krister Modin notes that it is pleasing that private investors are on their way back, and that the record-high number of shareholders at the same time shows that interest in share saving in Sweden remains strong.

The value of private individuals' share holdings also increased. During the quarter, the market value rose by around five per cent, and the average portfolio came in at approximately 722,500 kronor – an increase of roughly 14,800 kronor.

The statistics cover 1,486 Swedish companies and are based on the closing prices on 30 September. The ownership figures include Swedish private individuals, companies and foreign owners alike – but it is precisely the return of small investors that makes this quarter's figures particularly interesting, as they are often seen as a gauge of how households feel about the stock market.

Investor – the Swedes' favourite

The investment company Investor was the quarter's big winner in terms of the number of new shareholders. Almost 23,700 people bought in during the period, meaning the company now has around 769,000 shareholders. This cements Investor's position as Swedes' absolute favourite share – with a lead of just over 336,000 shareholders over runner-up Volvo.

Among the 150 largest companies, it was Precise Biometrics that attracted the second most new owners, following the merger with Fingerprint Cards. Next came Axfood with around 9,200 and Atlas Copco with approximately 7,800 new shareholders. Atlas Copco also made it onto the list of the ten most widely held shares. In the other direction, Evolution lost the most owners – around 10,900 – since the share reached its yearly high in September.

Women and men choose differently

The statistics also show that women and men continue to choose different shares. Among women, H&M remains the most popular, while Cloetta made the quarter's biggest climb after the share rose by around 24 per cent. Apotea and Industrivärden are new on women's top list. Among men, mining company Viscaria tops the list.

During the quarter, men's portfolios grew slightly faster, at 5.6 per cent compared with 3.5 per cent for women. Looking at the whole year, however, women are still ahead by around 3.5 percentage points.

Reporting party on the stock exchange

Thursday's record trading was given extra momentum by several strong quarterly reports. Cybersecurity company Yubico and pharmaceutical company Camurus were two of the day's big winners after presenting their figures. Camurus increased its revenue by 28 per cent compared with the same period last year and beat analysts' expectations.

For those considering starting to invest in shares, it is also worth remembering that the stock market always fluctuates – and that long-term, well-diversified saving tends to be the wisest way to benefit from the upturns without taking unnecessary risks. The fact that more Swedes are once again daring to take the step into the stock market is, in any case, a clear sign that confidence in the future is beginning to turn around.
