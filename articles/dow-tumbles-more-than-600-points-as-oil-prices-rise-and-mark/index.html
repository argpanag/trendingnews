Dow tumbles more than 600 points as oil prices rise and market sell-off hits most sectors

TheTools · 22.10.2026 22:03 · source · permalink

The Dow slid on Friday as a sell-off in tech in the prior session shifted to other areas of the market and U.S. oil prices topped $100 a barrel amid rising fears that the U.S.-Iran conflict would flare up again.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 652.82 points, or 1.23%, to settle at 52,215.16, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.52% to 7,623.68. The Nasdaq Composite lost 0.21% to close at 25,955.34.

More defensive stocks came under pressure on Friday, with the consumer staples, utilities and healthcare sectors all lower. Dow component American Express fell more than 3% after announcing it was cutting about 5% of its global workforce, citing the need to "prepare for AI." Caterpillar also edged down about 2%, while Goldman Sachs lost more than 1%, adding pressure to the 30-stock index.

On Thursday, many technology stocks had pulled back as traders assessed the impact of outsized corporate borrowing and rising rates on AI and other tech names. Microsoft ticked higher on Friday. However, chip stocks such as Micron Technology and Advanced Micro Devices made a stronger move, with gains of nearly 3% and almost 2%, respectively.

"For the last two weeks, investors have been sprinting from tech stocks and data center beneficiary stocks to safe stuff, and eventually, you run out of people willing to do that sprint, and that's what today looks like and feels like," Jed Ellerbroek, portfolio manager at Argent Capital Management, told CNBC. 

Ellerbroek added that tech companies' earnings have proven strong this season, giving investors a reason to embrace tech stocks and focus less on emotion-based flights to safety.

"Emotion and fear play a really big role," the investor said. But, "we've gotten a few tech companies' [latest quarterly financial reports] and their earnings results have been good."

The 10-year Treasury yield was at around 4.999%, up more than 4 basis points on the day. The benchmark yield had fallen below the key 5% level on Thursday for the first time in a month as investors grew cautious about the massive borrowing that is underway to finance the building of AI data centers.

Oil prices also rose on Friday, further pressuring stocks. West Texas Intermediate futures were up 3.8% to settle at $103.27 per barrel. Brent crude prices rose 3.1% to end the session at $116.72 for the highest settle since April. The gains follow reports that the U.S. and Israel may be drawing up plans for renewed attacks on Iran as part of the regional war that started in late February. Iran has countered that it is prepared to strike back as well.

On the week, the S&P 500 lost 0.5%, and the Nasdaq shed 0.7%. The Dow gained 0.4% in the period.

The three major stock indexes ended Friday's session with losses.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 1.23%, or 652.82 points, to finish at 52,215.16. The S&P 500 ended down 0.52% at 7,623.68. The Nasdaq Composite closed down 0.21% at 25,955.34.

—Liz Napolitano

Shares of discount retailers are lower today after analysts at Wells Fargo warned in a note that rising diesel prices could pressure profits through the second half of the year and into 2027.

With third-quarter diesel prices up about 60% year-over-year, analysts estimate the average hit to operating margin will rise to about 27 basis points from 18 basis points in the second quarter. The drag on earnings per share is expected to more than double to 6.2%.

Dollar Tree and Dollar General are down around 5% in midday trading while Five Below and TJX Companies are both down more than 1% on the day. Wells Fargo says Dollar Tree is the most exposed, historically seeing a larger drag than peers when diesel spikes. They estimate the higher fuel costs will weigh on third-quarter profit by about $25 million, or 50 basis points of margin.

The increased costs could mean additional pressure on retail inflation.

--Nick Wells

Bank of America raised its price target on ServiceNow to $180, implying roughly 15% upside from Thursday's close.

BofA analyst Tal Liani reiterated his buy rating ahead of the company's third-quarter earnings on October 28, citing growing demand for its artificial intelligence offerings. 

"We believe ServiceNow's AI could see broader adoption and incremental spend, which should support durable revenue growth," Liani wrote in a Friday note.

The bank's October CIO survey showed that nearly 36% of respondents expect to allocate more than 10% of their software budgets to AI, while nearly 55% said they were likely or very likely to use ServiceNow for back-office AI applications.

The analyst expects third-quarter subscription revenue to grow nearly 21% year over year as IT, customer service, and security adopt new AI products and automation tools. 

— Deena Zaidi

Uranium, nuclear and clean energy exchange-traded funds all fell to 52-week lows on Friday, while the largest fossil fuel producers in the nation touched all-time highs.

Global X Uranium ETF and Sprott Uranium Miners ETF both dropped to their lowest levels since June 2025, while the VanEck Uranium and Nuclear ETF was at its lowest since May 2025. Of the stocks inside those ETFs, Centrus Energy, Oklo, NuScale Power, Energy Fuels and BWX Technologies were also at 52-week lows Friday.

Clean energy ETFs saw losses, too. Invesco Solar ETF and Invesco WilderHill Clean Energy ETF were at their lowest since September and August last year, respectively, while individual stocks in the group, such as NextPower, Solv Energy, Rivian Automotive and Fluence Energy, also dropped to 52-week lows.

Meanwhile, ExxonMobil, Chevron and ConocoPhillips all traded at all-time highs on Friday.

— Scott Schnipper, Gina Francolla

In a Friday note to clients, Goldman Sachs posed some key questions about hyperscaler Oracle's AI strategy as pressures mount on the AI trade and investors grow increasingly wary to part with capital.

Analyst Gabriela Borges at Goldman said the conversation around Oracle is centering on monetization in proportion to capital expenditures; the sustainability of demand amid customer concentration; and the company's ability to execute despite political pressures facing the data center buildout.

Borges has a buy rating on the stock and a price target of $206, implying upside of 64.1% compared to a closing price recorded as $125.54.

Among the key risks to the company listed by Goldman are "intense capex investment cycle and financing needs."

"Oracle has outlined a FY27 financing framework supported by roughly $20bn of equity issuance (ATM program, already executed), $20 – 25 bn of customer prepayments ($11 bn as of 1Q), ~$20bn of debt issuance (expected in 2HFY27), with the remainder funded through operating cash flow," Borges wrote.

– Tobias Burns

Stocks outside the AI trade are taking the lead.

The S&P 500 Excluding AI index closed Thursday up 12.4% this year, edging out the S&P 500's roughly 12% gain. The gap has converged largely in October.

The ex-AI index is up about 6.6% in October, while the S&P 500, weighed down by concerns around AI financing, is down roughly 0.4%.

The move suggests some leadership broadening beyond AI-linked mega cap names that have dominated in recent periods. By sector, Energy and Consumer Staples are each up double digits in October while Information Technology and Communication Services lead lower, each down more than 5%.

--Nick Wells

Morgan Stanley reduced its price target on CoreWeave to $70 from $99 Friday, citing mounting financial pressures the so-called neocloud company is facing. The new target still implies 26% upside from Thursday's close.

Shares of CoreWeave, down 2% on Friday, have sold off over the past week amid investor fears over its massive artificial intelligence-rated debt. Its bond yields have also jumped, while the spread on its credit default swaps — which are insurance against default — has widened to levels generally considered distressed.

Morgan Stanley believes CoreWeave's execution has been solid and said it "remains the speed and scale leader in a crowded neocloud ecosystem."

"However, growing financing uncertainty reduces visibility into the path to deploying existing contracted capacity and achieving 8 GW [gigawatts] (active) longer-term," said analyst Adam Wood, who maintained his equal-weight rating on the stock.

He sees a multitude of outcomes for CoreWeave's future financing, "though the most reasonable path forward would likely be through securing a portion of the financing via a hyperscaler agreement to obtain a more attractive rate."

— Michelle Fox

Consumer sentiment changed little in October from historically low levels as inflation weighed on the economic outlook, according to a University of Michigan survey released Friday.

The survey showed the headline consumer sentiment index at 48 for the end of the month, off just 0.1 point from September and better than the Dow Jones consensus for 46.3. Compared with a year ago, the gauge tumbled 10.4%.

Current conditions sentiment declined 8.3% monthly but the expectations measure rose 5.4% and was off just 3% from the same period in 2025.

On inflation, the one-year outlook rose to 4.7%, while the five-year expectation accelerated to 3.5%, both 0.1 percentage point higher than September.

—Jeff Cox

Shares of Gucci-owner Kering fell 12% in early trading after the luxury company posted disappointing third-quarter earnings.

The move puts the stock firmly at the bottom of the pan-European blue-chip Stoxx 600 index, and brings 12-month losses to 43%.

Same-store sales declined by 1% overall, driven by a 6% decline for Gucci, its biggest brand and main moneymaker.

Kering, whose brands also include Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, and Balenciaga, has struggled more than peers amid a luxury slump.

Reigniting brand momentum at Gucci has proven particularly challenging against a difficult macroeconomic backdrop where consumers grapple with higher costs of living, Bernstein analyst Luca Solca said.

"A high dependency on newness to drive traffic and sales points to an underlying lack of interest in Gucci's core product," Solca added. "Unsurprisingly, management has stepped back from its initial outlook of a return to growth by the end of the year."

European luxury peers were also in the red on Friday morning, with conglomerate LVMH down 1.5%, Cartier-owner Richemont down 2.4% and Hermes falling 1.2%.

U.K. retail sales fell 0.4% in September from the previous month, a steeper drop than the 0.1% decline forecast in a Reuters poll. The fall partly reversed August's 0.7% rise and puts consumer spending in focus ahead of the government's budget on Oct. 28.

Despite September's decline, retail sales volumes rose 0.7% in the third quarter from the previous three months, the Office for National Statistics said Friday.

Phil Monkhouse, U.K. country manager at Ebury, said September's retail sales slide indicates a still-fragile consumer backdrop as households continue to grapple with rising prices and the prospect of higher borrowing costs.

 "With the Autumn Budget less than a week away, uncertainty over potential tax changes adds another layer of caution for households and businesses alike," Monkhouse said in a note.

September's retail slide comes after U.K. GDP rose by 0.1% in August, which was driven mainly by services output.

— Hugh Leask

Saab's order intake included a 24.6 billion kronor contract for 16 Gripen E fighter jets for Ukraine, signed in June and booked in the third quarter. Deliveries are scheduled for 2029-2030.

Although Loh said that there was no fixed date for a handover, he noted that his contract runs till the middle of 2027, which would be an "ample time for us to plan thoughtfully and work through the arrangements together. We are not setting a specific date for the transition today."

Loh has been SGX's chief since 2015, and Koh had said that under the CEO, the share price of the SGX has grown 216% since the time that he took the helm.

The trade deal between India and the European Union is expected to be signed before the end of 2026 and become operational by March next year, India's Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said on Thursday.

The minister said it was the "fastest trade deal" the EU has entered into, "with zero dissent," adding that none of the bloc's 27 member states have opposed any part of the agreement.

India's free trade deal with the EU, which represents about 25% of global GDP and about a third of global trade, was announced in January. It had been in the works for over two decades.

Just days after India finalized the agreement with the EU, U.S. President Donald Trump also announced a trade deal with India.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Tuesday said Washington would execute a deal with India "shortly," and there were just a "small number of items left to wrap up."

Shares of South Korean chipmaker SK Hynix pared early losses to trade slightly lower on Friday after the company halted a review of a potential U.S. initial public offering for its subsidiary Solidigm.

Japan's core inflation in September hit its highest this year at 2.1%, overshooting analysts' expectations, pressured by steeper import costs due to a weak yen and the indirect impact of higher energy costs.

Core inflation, which strips out prices of fresh food, was in line with the 2.1% forecast by economists polled by Reuters, and accelerated from the 1.7% in the prior month.

Headline inflation came in at 2.1%, while the so-called "core-core" inflation, which excludes prices of fresh food and energy and is the Bank of Japan's preferred gauge, was at 2.4%.

"That acceleration was noteworthy, not least because it occurred despite a sharp slowdown in fresh food inflation and continued energy deflation," said Abhijit Surya, senior APAC economist at Capital Economics.

Rice prices notably declined 20.6% year on year, however, Surya said that this sharp drop could not prevent overall goods inflation from picking up to 2.8%, up from August's 2.6%.
