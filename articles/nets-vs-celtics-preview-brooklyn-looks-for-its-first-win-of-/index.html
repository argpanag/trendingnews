Nets vs. Celtics preview: Brooklyn looks for its first win of season

TheTools · 27.10.2026 15:24 · source · permalink
  • Nets Game Previews

It’s been rough for the Brooklyn Nets. Following a loss at Cleveland on Saturday night, they came home for a Sunday evening matchup against the Indiana Pacers and lost 136-109. Mikel Brown Jr. sat out (injury management) as things only got uglier.

The Boston Celtics look different this season after a busy offseason, but their early results have been encouraging. They enter Tuesday’s matchup coming off a 134-99 blowout against the Chicago Bulls.

Game Info & Broadcast Details

  • Matchup: Brooklyn Nets vs. Boston Celtics
  • Time: 7:30 PM EDT (6:30 PM CDT)
  • Venue: TD Garden (Boston, MA)
  • TV/Streaming: YES Network/DAZN

Nets vs. Celtics: Who’s Out?

  • Joshua Jefferson: OUT – G League Assignment
  • Drake Powell: OUT – G League Assignment
  • Ben Saraf: OUT – G League Assignment
  • Tyler Bilodeau: OUT – G League (Two-Way)
  • Grant Nelson: OUT - G League (Two-Way)

This is the second night of a back-to-back for Boston so we’ll find out a few hours before tip-off.

The Game

Jordi Fernandez’s message after Sunday was simple: “The performance that we put on after the first quarter was just unacceptable. 112 points in three quarters — you’re not winning basketball games.”

Indeed.

As we noted in our season preview roundtable, bad defense is a killer and things have gotten pretty ugly already. Erik Slater of ClutchPoints discussed the amount of open threes the Nets have been allowing through the first week of the season, and teams have punished those mistakes early and often. The Celtics have been top-four in three-pointers attempted per game dating back to 2022.

If Brooklyn’s defense continues to spring leaks, this game could get ugly quick.

One of Boston’s newest faces has been around a lot longer than you’d think. This is Year 17 for Paul George and he’s been a solid hand for Joe Mazzulla’s squad. George is a versatile vet with lower usage compared to what we remember — he doesn’t need to do the heavy lifting on most nights.

The downside to being in the game this long is you’re gonna get got every so often.

Mikel Brown Jr is back against Boston and that should be a welcome sight for Nets fans.

The 20-year-old will be one of several guards chasing Payton Pritchard around tonight. Pritchard hit for 28 points and seven three-pointers last night as he had the Bulls in a blender. PP has been one of the best reserve guards in recent years and is someone Mazzulla and the team can count on to deliver no matter what the role is on a given night. His ascension allows for more flexibility and makes things easier across the board for his teammates.

For Brown Jr, Pritchard offers a great test against on the defensive side of the ball, while playing against elite competition overall. It should be fun to see how he shapes out against one of the most prepared teams in the NBA.

Player to Watch: Jayson Tatum

“He should stay ready at all times,” said former teammate Jaylen Brown.

As the face of the franchise, Jayson Tatum has a lot to think about it. He’s the lead option on a championship contender and faces all pressure to keep the team afloat in a treacherous Eastern Conference. And these days, he’s awfully busy discussing his former All-Star teammate.

Either way, it’s a lot to handle for one superstar to handle.

On the court, Tatum continues to be one of the best around. After shooting 0-for-7 night from three on opening night, he’s starting to regain his rhythm. Tatum can hurt teams in more ways than scoring. Even when his outside shot isn’t falling, he contributes as a playmaker and rebounder while still applying pressure on the defense. Brooklyn needs to make him work for his looks and avoid giving him easy opportunities to get going.

As for the Nets: When a team is going through an early rut like this, it takes a lot to turn things around. Losses pile up if you aren’t careful and three losses can turn into five, five turn into eight — you get the rest. The schedule doesn’t help either. Neither does playing against someone like Jayson Tatum.
