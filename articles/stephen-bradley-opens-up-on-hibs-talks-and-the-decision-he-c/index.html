Stephen Bradley opens up on Hibs talks and the decision he could not make

TheTools · 07.10.2026 14:30 · source · permalink

Shamrock Rovers head coach Stephen Bradley has broken his silence on links with the Hibernian managerial vacancy last month, confirming the interest was at a 'serious point.'

The 41-year-old held talks with Easter Road chiefs about succeeding David Gray after the ex-captain was relieved of his duties, but Dutchman Marink Reedijk was eventually appointed to the role.

Now, Bradley has revealed that he backed out of talks with the Hibs hierarchy as things heated up, citing the fact that there were just five games of the League of Ireland Premier Division season remaining, with Rovers locked in a three-way title battle.

"There were talks and contact," Bradley said, as he previewed Thursday night's home game against Drogheda United at the Tallaght Stadium.

"But when it came down to it, I genuinely don't think I could look at the players, the fans, the club, and walk out the door with five games left. I don't think that could ever sit right with me.

"For what the fans and the club and these players have done for me personally – forget about what we’ve done as a collective – I don’t think I could do that.

“When I looked at everything, five games to go, regardless of what happens in these five games, if I’d done that, I couldn’t look at them in the eye.

“I couldn’t come back here and watch a game in the next, whatever, 10-20 years and be proud of myself or happy with myself. It just didn’t sit right with me," he added.

Bradley admitted Hibs' interest had got to a point where he was having 'pretty serious' conversations with his agent Graham Barrett, his family, and Glenn Cronin, his assistant.

'It was a critical point, so I needed to come away from it," he explained, adding that had Hibs' interest come at the end of the season, his reaction might have been different.

"That's a different conversation," he continued. "I’m not going to sit here and try to predict what’s going to happen next month or in two months' time. You know what my beliefs have always been and what my ambition is. 

“I do also understand that I’m a young manager. Although I’ve done it a long time, I’m a young manager, so what will be, will be. 

“I genuinely believe that. I need to keep trying to get better and keep that hunger. I think that’s number one.

"I could be managing Jobstown [Celtic] next year, I don’t know. I’m comfortable with wherever it takes me in terms of options and career, but I’m in no doubt that I am in the best job in the country and I’m privileged to be in it, especially for such a long time."
