Live updates: Thanksgiving storm leaves roads flooded and more than 900,000 without power in eastern US | CNN

TheTools · 24.11.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

Here's what to know

• Beast in the East: A massive Thanksgiving storm that spans from Missouri to Massachusetts knocked out power for more than 900,000 customers, flooded coastal communities and shut down some East Coast bridges and roadways. Water rose to record highs on the Mid-Atlantic coast, and snow blanketed parts of the Midwest before shifting south.

• Air travel snags: Hundreds more flights were canceled or delayed Thursday after weather disrupted the busy pre-Thanksgiving travel period. Here’s what to do if your flight is delayed.

• Parade paused: The Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed for the first time in its history. Americans who enjoy the tradition on TV will have to wait until Saturday.

Updates

Power slowly returning after outages topped nearly 1 million customers

Power is returning to some households, but around 805,000 customers across the United States remain in the dark after back-to-back storms.

At the height of the outages, more than 980,000 customers were without electricity, forcing some families to eat Thanksgiving dinner by candlelight on Thursday.

Here’s a look at the states that have been most affected, according to PowerOutage.us:

  • In Pennsylvania, more than 166,000 customers are still without power after wind and rain triggered flooding and damaged utility poles, according to Reuters.
  • Over 160,000 customers are without power in New Jersey, where significant coastal flooding was seen in Atlantic City. Some residents had to evacuate their homes while others were rescued from the floodwaters.
  • Maryland is also reporting significant outages with nearly 120,000 customers still in the dark. Earlier in the day, more than 1.9 million people in Montgomery and Prince George’s counties were asked to limit their use of water due to storm-related power disruptions at two water filtration plants.
  • Severe weather also struck Virginia, leaving almost 95,000 customers still without power. In Alexandria, strong winds downed trees and knocked out traffic signals, forcing road closures. Flooded streets left residents wading through high water.
  • Further west, more than 87,000 customers in Missouri do not have power after heavy snow. Widespread totals of 8 to 12 inches of snow fell across parts of eastern Missouri and neighboring western Illinois.

Other states impacted by outages include New York, Connecticut, Delaware and North Carolina.

Meteorologist on "monster storm": Brace for more this winter

The “monster storm” on the East Coast over Thanksgiving is the nation’s second major weather event tied to El Niño in just two months, according to meteorologist Nick Pittman, who warned of more extreme weather ahead.

“The storms are stronger. El Niño is only adding more moisture to the atmosphere.”

Pittman said that while major tidal flooding was still possible 50 years ago, lower tides and less moisture in the atmosphere would have made the impact less severe.

Looking at the effects in South Jersey, Pittman placed the storm in the top 10 over the past 50 years and said some areas experienced worse flooding than during Hurricane Sandy, which caused at least 72 deaths and an estimated $81.9 billion in damages in 2012.

“Cold air is going to be in limited supply this winter, but I think this year in particular, all the ingredients at one point will come together for perhaps one of those big blockbuster blizzards.”

Some families were forced to get creative with their Thanksgiving dinners after storms knocked out power for nearly a million customers across the US.

Residents wade through flooded streets in Old Town Alexandria

Flooding from a Thanksgiving storm forced police to close a stretch of Union Street to traffic in Old Town Alexandria, Virginia on Thursday.

Alexandria police also reported downed trees, traffic signal outages and flooding on other roads in the city.

Footage from CNN affiliate WJLA showed residents wading through the flood-ridden streets between rain showers.

A coastal flood warning is in effect for Alexandria’s shoreline until 3 a.m. ET Friday.

Moderate to major tidal flooding has hit several locations along the Potomac River and Chesapeake Bay, the National Weather Service said, warning that low-lying areas along the Alexandria shoreline could see 1 to 2 feet of water above ground level.

New York City's Central Park logs its wettest Thanksgiving on record

Thursday’s storm dumped 1.82 inches of rain on New York City’s Central Park, making it the wettest Thanksgiving on record at the site.

The previous record was set 20 years ago when 1.72 inches of rain was logged on November 23, 2006, according to National Weather Service data.

The storm also caused the city’s Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade to be postponed to Saturday.

In pictures: Thanksgiving storm hits East Coast

Heavy rain and strong winds hit the East Coast Thursday, putting a snag in some Americans’ Thanksgiving Day plans.

Trees came down onto houses and cars in Washington D.C., Maryland and elsewhere, prompting first responders to rescue motorists and evacuate residents.

Drivers tried to make their way through streets filled with floodwaters in New Jersey, and gusty winds battered pedestrians in New York City, where the iconic Macy’s parade was postponed due to the weather.

Ground stop for Delta flights heading to New York’s LaGuardia Airport

The Federal Aviation Administration has issued a ground stop for Delta Air Lines flights headed to New York’s LaGuardia Airport until 7:30 p.m. ET.

Carrier-requested ground stops are often used by airlines to pause incoming aircraft so they can avoid traffic jams and get caught up with the planes already on the ground.

An earlier ground stop for all flights heading to Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport was canceled after about an hour when the FAA reported its objectives had been met.

Meanwhile, an elevated monorail connecting the terminals at Newark Liberty International Airport was shut down due to concerns over high winds. Buses will replace the Air Train, the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey said.

And in Miami, a FedEx McDonnell Douglas MD-11 cargo plane that skidded off the runway has been removed, the FAA reported in an operations update. That runway has been out of service since the incident, causing some delays.It should reopen within 48 hours, the agency said.

Water begins receding in Atlantic City after major flooding and more than 14 rescues

Floodwaters in Atlantic City, New Jersey, are starting to recede after the storm brought major flooding Thursday, the city’s emergency management coordinator said.

“Things are winding down in the city, although we are still experiencing some water in the streets,” Scott Evans said in a video posted to the city government’s Facebook page. “But the city is in the de-watering process, and the city is draining.”

Some low-lying areas had close to 2 to 3 feet of water, Evans said. Emergency crews helped residents evacuate several homes and carried out more than 14 rescues involving vehicles stuck in deep water, he said.

“We were in major flooding today, so there is some damage out there,” Evans said, adding that officials will assess the damage soon. “We didn’t see the highest levels that we thought we might see, but the the potential was there.”

Some roads are still closed but are expected to reopen in the coming hours.

Where the storm is now and what to expect over the next few hours

The East Coast storm continues to take shape this evening, centered just off the Mid-Atlantic coast. Snow has blanketed parts of the Midwest and Ohio Valley while heavy rain, strong winds and coastal flooding pushed into the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast.

East Coast: Heavy rain and gusty winds are in full force across the Northeast but are beginning to taper across the Mid-Atlantic, though a few showers could linger into the evening. Conditions are deteriorating in New England and will reach their worst overnight.

Another round of coastal flooding will arrive as high tide approaches but, for most areas, won’t be as extreme.

Winter storm damages Missouri community ice arena

An ice arena was damaged during a winter storm in the city of Wentzville, Missouri, about 40 miles west of St. Louis, the city said.

The area received major snowfall Wednesday night into Thanksgiving Day.

The arena is closed until further notice while the damage is assessed, the mayor said.

Intense flooding prompts water rescues across parts of Virginia

As heavy rain and fierce wind continues to lash the Mid-Atlantic Coast, officials in several Virginia cities say emergency response teams have rescued people from high waters.

Norfolk Fire-Rescue crews performed 39 rescues as of 3:15 p.m. Thursday, a city spokesperson told CNN in a statement. Nearby Virginia Beach has also seen damage from the storm.

There were no injuries related to the water rescues, the spokesperson added. Two dogs were also rescued. The city previously said one person rescued was taken to the hospital, but later said the hospitalization was due to an unrelated incident.

Rising waters have also led to rescues in Poquoson, Virginia, a coastal city about 35 miles north of Norfolk. Around five people had been rescued from their homes as of about 3:15 p.m., according to the city’s fire chief, Mike Bryant.

No injuries were reported, Bryant said, and individuals were all taken to homes of family members less affected by the flooding.

Weather could spell trouble for Sunday travel

Though family and friends across the country are still feasting on Thanksgiving dinner (or in a post-meal food coma), a storm that could disrupt weekend travel plans across the country is already taking shape.

A weather system that’s set to bring even more rain and snow to the Pacific Northwest Friday night will dive south into the central US, spreading snow across the central Rockies.

The storm will then bring wet weather to the central Plains and Midwest Saturday afternoon and evening.

But the real trouble is likely to set in Sunday morning — typically the busiest travel day of the year — as the storm intensifies and brings the potential of heavy rain and gusty winds from the Louisiana Gulf Coast to southern Michigan.

The system will quickly scoot east and could complicate travel across the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic Sunday afternoon. Evening travel is going to cut it close in the Northeast as the storm swiftly approaches late in the day Sunday.

A second, stronger storm will also approach the West Coast Saturday night, likely impacting travel from eastern Washington to the Bay Area. Rain could spread as far south as Southern California.

Powerful waves halt Amtrak service along Southern California coastline

Washout from high tide conditions along Southern California’s coastline are impacting Amtrak’s Pacific Surfliner train service today with several services canceled or disrupted, according to the the railroad company’s X page.

Video from CNN affiliate KABC shows waves crashing on a seaside train track near Irvine, California.

A coastal flood advisory was in effect until noon local time today for the Malibu coast and Los Angeles County beaches. A high surf warning will also be in effect until noon on Friday, according to the National Weather Service.

Snow shifts south after burying parts of Missouri

Snow has pivoted into western and central Tennessee, where more than 1 inch has been reported so far. Flakes are also flying as far south as northern Alabama.

Snow showers should continue over parts of Tennessee overnight Thursday. A winter storm warning is in effect from 6 to 9 p.m. CT this evening for parts of central Tennessee, where 2 to 4 inches of wet snow is expected.

The snow is wrapping up over Missouri as the storm tracks east. Snowflakes switched to rain over St. Louis this morning after heavy snow overnight. Widespread totals of 8 to 12 inches of snow fell across parts of eastern Missouri and neighboring western Illinois.

The highest totals reported so far in Missouri are 14 inches 2 miles southwest of Kellogg and 2 miles northeast of French Village, according to the National Weather Service.

For Memphis, Tennessee, today was the first time in at least 70 years the area saw snow on Thanksgiving.

Snow will keep tapering off across Missouri through this afternoon.

More than 900,000 customers without power as storms slam the country

More than 900,000 customers across the US are currently without power, according to PowerOutage.us, as two separate storms bring strong winds and harsh weather conditions to the country.

Here’s a look at the states that have been most affected, according to the tracker:

  • In Pennsylvania, more than 213,000 customers are without power.
  • In Virginia, more than 150,000 customers are without power.
  • In Missouri, nearly 120,000 customers currently do not have power. Wet, heavy snow fell across parts of the state overnight, impacting utility lines.

Other states impacted by outages include New York, North Carolina and Illinois.

Widespread power outages in the St. Louis area could take days to fix

Widespread power outages in the St. Louis area could take days to fix, according to the power company Ameren.

Crews are working around the clock to try to restore power, the company said, adding it has requested mutual aid. Restoration efforts are expected to last multiple days, Ameren said.

The St. Louis area is contending with its largest November snowstorm in 46 years, causing significant damage in addition to the power outages.

Some vehicles banned from George Washington Bridge due to high winds

Empty tractor-trailers, empty tandem trailers, motorcycles and house trailers are now banned from the George Washington Bridge due to a high wind advisory, the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey said on X.

The agency said it will post updates when conditions change.

The National Weather Service has issued a high wind warning for New York City from 10 a.m. Thursday to 1 a.m. Friday.

Steady winds of 30 to 40 mph and gusts of 60 to 65 mph are possible anywhere in the city, according to Mayor Zohran Mamdani and emergency management officials. They warned winds will be strongest along the coast and on bridges and other high places.

The Metropolitan Transportation Authority has also banned empty tractor-trailers and tandem trucks from some of its bridges and tunnels, including the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge and the Throgs Neck Bridge, on Thursday.

Over 1.9 million Maryland residents asked to limit water use ahead of Thanksgiving dinner

More than 1.9 million people in Montgomery and Prince George’s counties in Maryland are being asked to use water for essential purposes only ahead of Thanksgiving dinner for many families in the area, according to a release from WSSC Water.

“At this time, water is safe and there is no need to boil before essential use,” the company said. The urgent request affects two of the state’s largest counties and is being issued “out of an abundance of caution” due to storm-related power disruptions at two Maryland water filtration plants that supply drinking water to customers.

Facilities at one plant are experiencing interruptions affecting its ability to maintain consistent water production, WSSC said, while another plant is out of service due to planned maintenance and storm-related power issues. Crews are working to bring the plant back online.

As many households welcome extra guests and prepare Thanksgiving dinners, WSSC is urging customers to shorten showers, quickly turn off faucets after use and avoid flushing toilets after every use. It is also asking people to limit dishwasher and washing machine use.
