Live updates: Thanksgiving storm leaves roads flooded and more than 900,000 without power in eastern US | CNN

TheTools · 24.11.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

Here's what to know

• Beast in the East: A massive Thanksgiving storm that spans from Missouri to Massachusetts has knocked out power for more than 900,000 customers, flooded coastal communities and shut down some East Coast bridges and roadways. Water rose to record highs on the Mid-Atlantic coast, and snow blanketed parts of the Midwest before shifting south.

• Air travel snags: Hundreds more flights have been canceled or delayed today after weather disrupted the busy pre-Thanksgiving travel period. Here’s what to do if your flight is delayed.

• Parade paused: The Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed for the first time in its history. Americans who enjoy the tradition on TV will have to wait until Saturday, while New Yorkers made the most of the unusually empty streets this morning.

Where the storm is now and what to expect over the next few hours

The East Coast storm continues to take shape this evening, centered just off the Mid-Atlantic coast. Snow has blanketed parts of the Midwest and Ohio Valley while heavy rain, strong winds and coastal flooding pushed into the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast.

East Coast: Heavy rain and gusty winds are in full force across the Northeast but are beginning to taper across the Mid-Atlantic, though a few showers could linger into the evening. Conditions are deteriorating in New England and will reach their worst overnight.

Another round of coastal flooding will arrive as high tide approaches but, for most areas, won’t be as extreme.

Midwest: Snow has almost entirely tapered off in Missouri and Illinois after significant snow fell overnight Wednesday, though rain still lingers. Parts of southeast Missouri, western Kentucky and western Tennessee will continue to see a mixed bag of rain and snow through this afternoon. A few inches of snow has already fallen in parts of eastern Tennessee. Wet conditions will begin to taper off this evening.

In parts of central and northern Tennessee, rain has transitioned to wet snow and a few inches will accumulate as temperatures drop. Winter storm damages Missouri community ice arena

An ice arena was damaged during a winter storm in the city of Wentzville, Missouri, about 40 miles west of St. Louis, the city said.

The area received major snowfall Wednesday night into Thanksgiving Day.

The arena is closed until further notice while the damage is assessed, the mayor said.

Intense flooding prompts water rescues across parts of Virginia

As heavy rain and fierce wind continues to lash the Mid-Atlantic Coast, officials in several Virginia cities say emergency response teams have rescued people from high waters.

Norfolk Fire-Rescue crews performed 39 rescues as of 3:15 p.m. Thursday, a city spokesperson told CNN in a statement. Nearby Virginia Beach has also seen damage from the storm.

There were no injuries related to the water rescues, the spokesperson added. Two dogs were also rescued. The city previously said one person rescued was taken to the hospital, but later said the hospitalization was due to an unrelated incident.

Rising waters have also led to rescues in Poquoson, Virginia, a coastal city about 35 miles north of Norfolk. Around five people had been rescued from their homes as of about 3:15 p.m., according to the city’s fire chief, Mike Bryant.

No injuries were reported, Bryant said, and individuals were all taken to homes of family members less affected by the flooding.

Weather could spell trouble for Sunday travel

Though family and friends across the country are still feasting on Thanksgiving dinner (or in a post-meal food coma), a storm that could disrupt weekend travel plans across the country is already taking shape.

A weather system that’s set to bring even more rain and snow to the Pacific Northwest Friday night will dive south into the central US, spreading snow across the central Rockies.

The storm will then bring wet weather to the central Plains and Midwest Saturday afternoon and evening.

But the real trouble is likely to set in Sunday morning — typically the busiest travel day of the year — as the storm intensifies and brings the potential of heavy rain and gusty winds from the Louisiana Gulf Coast to southern Michigan.

The system will quickly scoot east and could complicate travel across the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic Sunday afternoon. Evening travel is going to cut it close in the Northeast as the storm swiftly approaches late in the day Sunday.

A second, stronger storm will also approach the West Coast Saturday night, likely impacting travel from eastern Washington to the Bay Area. Rain could spread as far south as Southern California.

Snow shifts south after burying parts of Missouri

Snow has pivoted into western and central Tennessee, where more than 1 inch has been reported so far. Flakes are also flying as far south as northern Alabama.

Snow showers should continue over parts of Tennessee overnight Thursday. A winter storm warning is in effect from 6 to 9 p.m. CT this evening for parts of central Tennessee, where 2 to 4 inches of wet snow is expected.

The snow is wrapping up over Missouri as the storm tracks east. Snowflakes switched to rain over St. Louis this morning after heavy snow overnight. Widespread totals of 8 to 12 inches of snow fell across parts of eastern Missouri and neighboring western Illinois.

The highest totals reported so far in Missouri are 14 inches 2 miles southwest of Kellogg and 2 miles northeast of French Village, according to the National Weather Service.

For Memphis, Tennessee, today was the first time in at least 70 years the area saw snow on Thanksgiving.

Snow will keep tapering off across Missouri through this afternoon.

Widespread power outages in the St. Louis area could take days to fix

Widespread power outages in the St. Louis area could take days to fix, according to the power company Ameren.

Crews are working around the clock to try to restore power, the company said, adding it has requested mutual aid. Restoration efforts are expected to last multiple days, Ameren said.

The St. Louis area is contending with its largest November snowstorm in 46 years, causing significant damage in addition to the power outages.

Some vehicles banned from George Washington Bridge due to high winds

Empty tractor-trailers, empty tandem trailers, motorcycles and house trailers are now banned from the George Washington Bridge due to a high wind advisory, the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey said on X.

The agency said it will post updates when conditions change.

The National Weather Service has issued a high wind warning for New York City from 10 a.m. Thursday to 1 a.m. Friday.

Steady winds of 30 to 40 mph and gusts of 60 to 65 mph are possible anywhere in the city, according to Mayor Zohran Mamdani and emergency management officials. They warned winds will be strongest along the coast and on bridges and other high places.

The Metropolitan Transportation Authority has also banned empty tractor-trailers and tandem trucks from some of its bridges and tunnels, including the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge and the Throgs Neck Bridge, on Thursday.

Over 1.9 million Maryland residents asked to limit water use ahead of Thanksgiving dinner

More than 1.9 million people in Montgomery and Prince George’s counties in Maryland are being asked to use water for essential purposes only ahead of Thanksgiving dinner for many families in the area, according to a release from WSSC Water.

“At this time, water is safe and there is no need to boil before essential use,” the company said. The urgent request affects two of the state’s largest counties and is being issued “out of an abundance of caution” due to storm-related power disruptions at two Maryland water filtration plants that supply drinking water to customers.

Facilities at one plant are experiencing interruptions affecting its ability to maintain consistent water production, WSSC said, while another plant is out of service due to planned maintenance and storm-related power issues. Crews are working to bring the plant back online.

As many households welcome extra guests and prepare Thanksgiving dinners, WSSC is urging customers to shorten showers, quickly turn off faucets after use and avoid flushing toilets after every use. It is also asking people to limit dishwasher and washing machine use.

Hundreds of flights canceled and over 1,800 delayed today

At least 419 flights within, into or out of the US have been canceled today, according to tracking site FlightAware, as strong storms have slammed both sides of the country. Another 1,800 US flights have been delayed.

Earlier today, the Federal Aviation Administration warned that there could be delays at a number of major airports, as “significant” weather impacts were forecast.

Why the Lions and Cowboys always play on Thanksgiving

For decades, the Detroit Lions and Dallas Cowboys have been Thanksgiving mainstays.

The Lions have hosted a game on Thanksgiving every year since 1934, and the Cowboys joined the tradition as the afternoon game in 1966. Sometimes it’s been great theater, particularly in the late game as the Cowboys have been the more consistently competitive franchise over those decades, and sometimes it’s been … a tough watch, given the Lions’ historical ineptitude.

Of course, the Lions’ rise to prominence over the last four years has made the early game from Detroit much more watchable. Today’s contest against the Chicago Bears has lived up to expectations, with a tight, high-scoring game currently in the fourth quarter.

The NFL has been playing on Thanksgiving since the league’s inception but it took until the mid-1930s for the Lions to make it their own. According to the team’s media guide, owner George Richards instituted the game as a gambit to make the newly arrived Lions a part of Detroit’s sporting fabric; the team moved from Portsmouth, Ohio, to Detroit for the 1934 season. Richards convinced the NBC Radio Network to broadcast the game across the country and succeeded in driving big ticket sales.

From there, the game has become a part of the Motor City’s holiday fabric and the centerpiece of many family gatherings in the Great Lakes State.

In 1966, the Cowboys joined the annual party. Then-owner Tex Schramm agreed to host a game on Thanksgiving for the same reason Richards did three decades earlier: To drive ticket sales. It succeeded and the Cowboys became a fixture for the next several years, according to the team’s website.

The St. Louis Cardinals (the football version) took Dallas’ game in 1975 and 1977, but ticket sales and ratings were low so the NFL put the afternoon Thanksgiving game back in the Lone Star State in 1978 and it has been played ever since.

Tornadoes are rare in Washington – only a couple form in an average year.

Landspouts tend to be short-lived and weaker tornadoes but can still be dangerous.
