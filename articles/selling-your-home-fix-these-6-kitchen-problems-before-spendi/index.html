Fixing worn fixtures, dim lighting, and minor maintenance issues usually pays off: industry remodeling data shows a modest kitchen refresh typically recovers its full cost at resale, whereas a major midrange remodel recovers only around 50%.

Run the tap, check the faucet action, and inspect the cabinet underneath for leaks, worn sealant, or water damage. Find and resolve any plumbing issues first. “The sink area gets used constantly, so it needs to feel solid, clean, and well maintained,” explains Grambart.

If the sink needs replacing, select a durable model that drops into the existing cutout. Changing dimensions or mounting styles quickly inflates labor costs. Grambart notes, “If replacement is necessary, check the installation costs before choosing the product. Altering the countertop or cabinet can quickly change the budget.”

If the boxes are sound, focus on a deep clean, hinge adjustments, drawer repair, and fresh hardware. Worn doors can usually handle a well-prepared coat of paint, though Grambart suggests having peeling veneer, swollen panels, or structural damage evaluated before committing to refinish.

“Open every cabinet and drawer before the first showing,” he says. “Loose handles and sticking drawers are easy to overlook when you live with them every day. You do not need to replace every metal finish simply because they are not identical.”

Clear everyday appliances off the counters, clear out unnecessary furniture, and ensure all appliance doors swing open fully without obstruction. Removing an oversized table or rolling cart frees up immediate floor space without any construction.
