Man jailed for one month over alleged crime spree that halted freeway traffic

TheTools · 06.10.2026 18:42 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Courts

Topic:Courts

Five of the six males arrested on the Monash Freeway yesterday have been charged. (ABC News)

In short:

A 19-year-old man has been jailed for his role in an alleged crime spree that halted traffic on a major Melbourne road.

Ino Juol-Thor was among six people arrested in a dramatic police operation on Tuesday.

Four others are due to face the children's court today.

A man charged after a dramatic arrest on Melbourne's Monash Freeway has pleaded guilty and been sentenced to a month in jail.

The 19-year-old was among six males arrested after an alleged crime spree ended with a highly visible police operation at lunchtime yesterday.

Cars were rammed and freeway traffic was brought to a standstill as more than a dozen police officers, many dressed in tactical gear, detained the males near Chadstone at midday.

Less than 24 hours later, Ino Juol-Thor pleaded guilty to theft during a hearing in the Dandenong Magistrates' Court.

The four other teenagers arrested — three 16-year-olds and a 17-year-old — also had their cases called in the Children's Court.

The group was remanded in custody.

In court, the mother of one of the 16-year-olds accused police of brutality during the roadside arrest. 

"I believe he may have bleeding on the brain," she told the magistrate.

"It is brutal. The gun was smashed on his head, they used canines on him.

"It was extreme force, your honour. My son is severely traumatised. He's in an astounding amount of pain."

The magistrate said it was not her role to assess the police's use of force, and adjourned the teenager's case for a bail application later this week.

Three other teens did not apply for bail.

Two alleged home invasions preceded freeway arrests

The Dandenong Magistrates' Court heard Juol-Thor was a passenger in a stolen Nissan 4WD, which had been boxed in by heavily armed police.

Juol-Thor had only been out of custody for two months and was serving a community corrections order, the court heard.

In sentencing him to a month in jail, Magistrate Suzette Dootjes described the offending as "very serious" but conceded he played a limited role.

"You were with a group of young people in a car and driving erratically, and it was a real danger to the community, to other road users," she said.

More than a dozen police officers in tactical gear were involved in the operation. (ABC News)

Police have alleged a Brighton East home was broken into and the Nissan and an Audi were stolen while the residents were at home.

The two vehicles were then allegedly used in a Hampton Park home invasion. 

The Nissan was later seen being driven erratically on the Eastlink toll road.

Police followed the vehicle through Melbourne's eastern suburbs before intercepting it on the freeway in Chadstone.

The Audi was found in Endeavour Hills.

Teenager fell 'into the wrong crowd'

Juol-Thor's lawyer, Yossi Pinkus, said police had not accused his client of being involved in the alleged home break-ins.

Mr Pinkus said Juol-Thor's father was a locum doctor and his mother worked as an ambassador. He said he had completed high school but failed to enrol in university afterwards.

"He's drifted and certainly fallen into the wrong crowd," he said.

The arrests caused long traffic delays on the Monash Freeway. (ABC News)

Prosecutor Senior Constable Ryan Cunningham asked the magistrate to jail Juol-Thor, saying he had only been placed on a community corrections order last month and that his latest offending was in the company of children.

A Corrections Victoria worker also told the court Juol-Thor had failed to complete any of the mandated work hours of his existing community corrections order.

"He's said to have a poor attitude towards his order," she said.

Magistrate Dootjes said Juol-Thor had failed to take the opportunities given to him and needed to be deterred from further crimes.

"The courts have to protect the community as well," the magistrate said.

The underage teenagers have all been charged with various offences, including aggravated burglary, car theft and armed robbery.

One of the 16-year-olds has also been charged with intentional aggravated exposure of an emergency worker to risk by driving, reckless conduct endangering life, reckless conduct endangering serious injury and unlicensed driving.

Another 16-year-old has been charged with committing an offence while on bail.

Another man, 20, is under police guard in hospital.
