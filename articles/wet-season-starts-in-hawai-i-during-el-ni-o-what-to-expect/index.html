HONOLULU (KHON2) -- Wondering what October weather will be like in the islands this year?

While locals may be used to more rain and a bit less sun during the "winter" months, the extreme 2026 El Niño season could bring increased impacts to Hawaiʻi.

The U.S. Geological Survey described El Niño events as an irregular "warming of the ocean surface, or above-average sea surface temperatures, in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean."

As of Sept. 28, the equatorial sea surface temperatures were above average across the east-central and eastern Pacific Ocean, the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration reported.

"El Niño is strengthening, with a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event during the Northern Hemisphere fall and winter 2026-27," NOAA said.

The potential effects of an El Niño is a shift in easterly winds, which blow from east to west, weakening or becoming westerly winds blowing from west to east.

Typically, Hawaiʻi's climate is on the milder side with moderate humidity, continual trade winds and few severe storms, the National Weather Service said.

During October, which is the beginning of the wet season, weather experts said trade winds to the the Hawaiian Islands could drop from 90 percent to about 50 percent.

The reason for this change is storms will disrupt the usual trade winds Hawaiʻi receives, causing more rain.

NWS said the trade winds in winter "may be interrupted for days or weeks by the invasion of the fronts or migratory cyclones from the northern latitudes and by Kona storms forming near the islands."

Residents and visitors can expect more overcast days and rainy weather during the winter season. In fact, Hawaiʻi's heaviest rains come from winter storms in October and April, NWS said.

"Frequently, the heaviest storm rains do not occur in areas with the greatest average rainfall," the weather service said. "Relatively dry areas may receive, within a day or a few hours, totals exceeding half of their average annual rainfall."

But despite October being the start of Hawaiʻi's wet season, the months with the most rainfall are usually January and February.
