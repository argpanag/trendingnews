Game 1 of the World Series is underway as the Tampa Bay Rays and Milwaukee Brewers aim for history | CNN

TheTools · 23.10.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

What we're watching

  • The World Series is here: After nearly seven months, there are only two teams left competing for baseball’s biggest prize. The National League-champion Milwaukee Brewers host the American League-champion Tampa Bay Rays in the Fall Classic, beginning Friday night.
  • Game is underway: Game 1 is underway in Milwaukee. It’s 0-0 in the first inning.
  • Baseball’s best: The Brewers and Rays finished as the top teams in the National League and American League respectively this year and represent a throwback style of baseball.
  • History in the making: Neither franchise has won a World Series title before, the first World Series with a guaranteed first-time winner since 1980.

Updates

Misiorowski allows a couple hits but a double play allows a quick escape

Jacob Misiorowski is the presumptive Cy Young Award winner and he’s spent October setting radar gun records.

But his postseason starts haven’t been super sharp. He’s yet to get through five innings as hitters have been putting him to work. The hope was that a week and a half of rest would get him back to his whiff-inducing ways.

Early in Game 1 of the World Series, it looks like he’s not quite all the way back. His first pitch of the night of 103.7 mph – that’s classic Miz. But he got only two swinging strikes on his four-seam fastball and gave up two hard-hit singles before bailing himself out with a double play.

No damage done, but not the dominance the Brewers were hoping for.

Why baseball teams party so hard and so often in October

At the end of this series, the final party will begin. It will be the last in a long line of them.

Every major US sport has multi-round postseason schedules, but none of them party like MLB teams. Locker room celebrations in the NFL tend to be subdued until the Super Bowl, and since hockey players are famously superstitious, popping bottles early is practically verboten in the NHL; the Stanley Cup is all that really matters.

Boston Red Sox clubhouse manager Tom McLaughlin has spent over 40 years with the Red Sox, and he says he’s lost track of how many celebrations he’s been involved in, but he counts them in the dozens. He told CNN Sports it was “pretty tame” when he started in the ‘80s, when the postseason was much shorter, but the back end of the season now can feel like one enormous bender for the most successful teams.

Clinched a playoff spot? Party.

Successfully navigate the wildcard round? Let’s get wild.

Made it through the Divisional Round? Get another round in.

Won the Championship Series? We’re going on a spree.

By the time a baseball club wins the World Series, let’s just say that the celebration will not be their first rodeo. Everyone will have had plenty of practice.

Brewers put star pitcher Brandon Woodruff on World Series roster after long absence

Brandon Woodruff, the longest-tenured Brewer on the team, was named to Milwaukee’s World Series roster ahead of Game 1.

Woodruff last appeared for the Brewers on the Fourth of July, when he exited the team’s game against the Arizona Diamondbacks with shoulder inflammation.

The 33-year-old right hander has spend his entire nine-season MLB career with the Brewers, racking up 55-30 record with 136 starting appearances.

Woodruff spoke to the media earlier on Friday about his mindset as he waited to find out if he would make the Brewers’ World Series roster.

“It came down to I want one thing, whether I was on the roster or not, and that’s to win. So whatever was decided, I was good with,” he said.

The three-plus month absence also saw Woodruff and his wife Jonie welcome their third child back in July.

El Presidente takes the mound in Game 1

It was a bit of a surprise when the Rays didn’t name Drew Rasmussen – who has a 1.29 ERA so far this postseason – the starter to go against Jacob Misiorowski in Game 1 of the World Series.

Instead, they went with Nick Martinez – a 36-year-old journeyman who spent the middle part of his career pitching in Japan and joined Tampa just before spring training this year.

Part of the reason was that they wanted to give Rasmussen an extra day of rest. He had already thrown the most innings in a single season of his career before the Rays got to October.

But also, it’s an honor Martinez has earned by arriving in Tampa and quickly becoming the clubhouse leader.

“He’s the head of the snake. He’s the captain. He is El Presidente,” Rays pitching coach Kyle Snyder told CNN Sports. “He’s brought everybody together all year long.”

Martinez set up team dinners in every city the Rays played in this season. Ahead of the World Series in Milwaukee, it was Carnevor, a steakhouse.

“Getting the guys together is important. Creating that off-the-field chemistry translates to on the field,” Martinez told reporters.

“It’s easy to set up dinner, but we have a great turnout every time we set something up, and it speaks volumes of the guys in there.”

Murph’s tough love

Brewers manager Pat Murphy is a character but by no means a cartoon.

The two-time manager of the year can be something of a hard-ass as a coach. He’s not afraid to call it like he sees it, and he’s not afraid to bench a player who fails to hustle or commits inexcusable errors on the field.

“He did it to Contreras!” rookie Cooper Pratt marveled to CNN Sports about Murph’s willingness to bench even a multi-time All-Star like William Contreras, who’s making just under $10 million this year.

He also did it to Pratt himself, the Brewers shortstop of the future, who was signed to an extension before he ever debuted. Murphy publicly benched Pratt earlier this season in a high-profile incident that underscored an important Brewers philosophy.

“There’s nobody above the team,” Pratt said. “That’s how (Murphy) does it.”

“I’ve been benched plenty of times by Murph,” right fielder Sal Frelick told CNN Sports. “That’s good coaching, and I think most guys would agree. It’s Murph-style.”

It’s not, however, part of every team’s style. In a market that can afford big stars making hundreds of millions of dollars, a manager might not feel like they can reprimand a marquee guy in such an obvious way.

“There’s some teams you can’t really do that,” Pratt said. “I don’t think there’s anyone really on this team, other than maybe one or two, that you can’t. I think that’s part of the reason we are so good is because there really isn’t anyone above the team.”

“I think that’s why a lot of the guys that get traded here or come late fit in so easily, just because they can feel it the instant they walk in the clubhouse,” Frelick said. Like, ‘OK, I gotta play to this certain standard. I kind of understand how it goes here.’ But if you do that, you’ll be loved.

Is there anyone Murphy wouldn’t bench?

“He probably wouldn’t do it to (Brewers captain Christian) Yelich,” Pratt said, “but Yelich would never jog to first.”

Plenty of power and pop on display in this World Series

Something else you’re likely to see in this World Series? The fastest pitch and the slowest home run trot in Fall Classic history.

The former will undoubtedly be supplied by Jacob Misiorowski, the Brewers’ 24-year-old flame-throwing phenom and probable Cy Young Award winner. To set a new speed record, he needs to top the 103.2 mph reached by Aroldis Chapman in 2016.

And will he? Oh, absolutely. His first pitch of the Brewers’ postseason this year was 104.8 mph. The Miz is a miracle of mechanics and strength and human extremes.

“Arguably one of the best arms to ever touch a dirt circle in the big leagues,” Kyle Snyder, the opposing Rays pitching coach, told CNN Sports. “I mean, what a fantastic season and what an incredible talent.”

That blink-and-you’ll-miss-it stuff will be juxtaposed on the basepaths by the unhurried one-man parade put on by Rays’ third baseman Junior Caminero if he homers.

And will he? He’s got as good a chance as anyone else who steps to the plate this series.

Over the past two seasons combined, Caminero has the second-most home runs in baseball. He’s 23 years old, playing in his first postseason, and soaking up every second of it. When he homered in the clinch Game 6 of the American League Championship Series against the Cleveland Guardians, Caminero staged an elaborate celebration that took 38.66 seconds to round the base paths.

It was the slowest uninterrupted playoff home trot since MLB Statcast started measuring in 2015 – and the second slowest ever, behind only another one from Caminero in September of this season.

The Miz and Junior aren’t yet household names. But this World Series could change that.

These teams bring back batting average and balls in play and bunts

Talk to your parents about baseball and see how quickly it takes them to bring up that, back in their day, hitters used to put the ball in play. Now, everyone just swings for the fences and all too often winds up striking out.

Well, mom and dad, have I got good news for you. Tune in to this World Series for a throwback in style. The matchup features the two teams with the highest batting averages in the regular season – the Brewers were best in the sport, the Rays a close second. The Rays had the lowest strikeout rate in the game. The Brewers scored the second-most runs in the sport while hitting the second fewest home runs.

You will probably see someone bunt for a hit. The Rays’ 49 bunt hits in the regular season were not only a league-high this season, it’s the most any team has had since 2008. The Brewers were second in the sport this season in bunt hits. And if it’s Rays’ speed specialist Chandler Simpson who reaches first on a ball that fails to leave the infield, you will probably see a stolen base soon after. Simpson’s 46 steals led the American League in the regular season. He has never hit a home run in the majors.

This style tends to amount to close, low-scoring games. So maybe not the most fireworks, but plenty of drama.

Both teams are playing for the franchise’s first title

All that respect and regular season success has yet to translate to a ring for either franchise. There are five MLB teams that have never won a World Series. After this season, there will be four.

That alone imbues this series with compellingly straightforward stakes. For the Rays, it has been 29 years of existence and two failed trips to the Fall Classic, most recently in the neutral-site Covid-19 World Series in 2020. For the Brewers, it’s been 58 years of existence and they haven’t played in the final game of a season since 1982, before many of their current fans were even born.

The Rays, in particular, are often mocked on a national level for their fickle Florida fanbase that would rather enjoy the year-round sun than sit in the necessary traffic to get to games in St. Petersburg. But when the broadcast cuts to either crowd in a clinching scenario, keep in mind the people who are there are hoping to see history made.

A clash of greatness – and familiarity

Small payrolls may have imbued both teams with an underdog vibe as they made their way through the postseason bracket, but don’t be fooled: This matchup is total chalk, two one-seeds. The Tampa Bay Rays had the best record in the American League, the Brewers had the best record in the National League and all of Major League Baseball.

That success is not entirely new, either.

The Brewers had the best record in baseball last year, too, before they were swept out of the playoffs by the eventual champion Los Angeles Dodgers. In fact, if you go back to the start of 2024, the beginning of when that Dodger dominance started to feel inevitable, no team has won more games than the Milwaukee Brewers.

The Rays, known for punching above their weight in the big-spending American League East, missed the playoffs entirely the past two years. But a longer lens reveals how they’ve earned a reputation as perennial contenders. The Rays, MLB’s newest team, made the playoffs for the first time in 2008. Starting with that season through today – a 19-year span – only the New York Yankees and Dodgers have won more regular season games.

Within the sport, this sustained success has earned both organizations a reputation for being smart, savvy, and worth emulating. Front offices around the league are populated with people who honed their skills in Tampa or Milwaukee – and that actually includes Milwaukee.

Matt Arnold, now the Brewers president of baseball operations, spent nearly a decade in the Rays’ organization before coming to Milwaukee. Just about 24 hours before the World Series began, Arnold greeted the current Rays’ president of baseball operations Erik Neander, who started as an intern in Tampa while Arnold was a scout there, on the field. Neander lifted Arnold airborne and wrapped him in a bear hug.

“Still really good friends,” Arnold said of the relationships he still has in that front office. “Maybe not this week, but for sure for a lifetime. I consider them some of my closest friends.”

Ticket prices in Milwaukee are in the stratosphere

Milwaukee Brewers fans have waited 44 years for a chance to see their team in the World Series, and many are demonstrating that they’re willing to pay any price, and go any distance, to do so, setting a record for World Series ticket prices for this weekend’s games.

Ticket marketplace SeatGeek says that the average price for a Game 1 ticket stood at $2,900 as of Thursday evening, which would be a record if not for the $3,000 average for Saturday’s Game 2. The cheapest price available on SeatGeek as of Friday morning stood at $2,269 for standing room tickets, with the highest price at $9,000.

The previous World Series ticket price records were for Game 3 at Yankee Stadium in 2024, and Game 7 in Toronto last year, which both had average prices at about $2,300.

Many Brewers fans are apparently finding cheaper tickets by going to Tampa Bay, where the average prices paid for games 3 through 5 this coming week stand at between $1,300 to $1,400 on SeatGeek. About one quarter of all those Tampa Bay tickets purchased went to buyers in Wisconsin, according to both SeatGeek and rival site StubHub, which does not release ticket prices.

The prices don’t quite compare to what fans pay for Super Bowl tickets or for the games at Madison Square Garden in New York to see the Knicks try for their first NBA title in 53 years back in June.

New York is not only a larger market, but Madison Square Garden is a much smaller venue with far fewer available seats. But the Milwaukee tickets are 45% higher than the average price paid by fans in San Antonio, a similar smaller market to Milwaukee, to see Game 1 of the NBA finals.

The Fall Classic is finally here

Seven months, thousands of games and just two teams left: The World Series is finally here.

It’s a match-up between two teams that are looking to bring the Commissioner’s Trophy home for the first time in franchise history as the Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays face off.

It’s the first time the Brewers have made it to this stage since 1982 and the third time the Rays have gone this far. Tickets prices are off the charts and fans in Wisconsin and Florida are ready for history.
