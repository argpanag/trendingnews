BCP opposition leader demands answers over council's government lobbying claims

TheTools · 02.11.2026 07:35 · source · permalink

Conservative opposition leader Cllr John Beesley has demanded answers from BCP Council leader Millie Earl after an investigation revealed its leaders had not arranged a single meeting with the Government over its funding crisis, despite repeatedly claiming to have lobbied ministers for more financial support.

In an open letter to BCP Council leader Millie Earl, Conservative opposition leader Cllr John Beesley demands transparency after revelations that the leadership held no ministerial meetings, despite public claims of "shouting, screaming, lobbying".

As previously reported, a Freedom of Information investigation revealed "no formal meeting requests before January 2026" were submitted, raising serious concerns while residents face "much higher bills" and service pressures.

Calling out "claims they cannot verify," Cllr Beesley urges administration leaders to publish all correspondence and provide "clear answers and evidence that public money is being handled responsibly".

Below, we publish Cllr Beesley's open letter in full.

 Dear Councillor Earl,

The Bournemouth Echo’s FOI investigation, which was reported today, raises serious questions about the gap between what BCP residents have been told and what the Council’s records show.

You have said the Council had warned Government about its financial crisis for years. Cllr Mike Cox said you had been, “shouting, screaming, lobbying”.

Yet the Echo reports that neither of you held a meeting with ministers about additional funding or the council’s budget between July 2024 and September 2026.

It reports no formal meeting requests before January 2026, then one request from you and none from Cllr Cox. No meeting took place.

 You say the council has written to ministers. Then residents should be able to see the full record. They deserve more than repeated claims of lobbying whilst the most direct route to making the case appears not to have been taken by the leadership of the council.

 I am asking you to publicly answer the following questions:

  1. What letters were sent to ministers by you or Cllr Cox about BCP’s financial position? Please publish the dates, recipients, full text and any replies received.
  2. Why were no formal meeting requests made for so long, despite the council describing its warnings and lobbying as repeated and urgent?
  3. What did you mean when you said the council had been, “pointing this out to the Government since May 2024” and had been warning Government “for years”? Please explain what specific actions you were referring to when you make your emails public.
  4. What direct contact have you had with BCP’s MPs about the crisis? Tom Hayes MP says the council was not engaging with him politically, whilst at the same time its financial concerns were being aired in the press.  Why was his offer to help open the door to Government talks not taken up sooner?
  5. What is the current position concerning a future request for Exceptional Financial Support from the government, which this year (in addition to charging the new town council precept) led to a substantial increase in council tax above the 5% maximum without the need for a referendum? Given the unsustainability of the current year’s budget (which has been public for over 3 months), should you not be making provision for a referendum about any planned increase in council tax next year above the 5% maximum, if that is what you are planning to do?  

 These are not minor questions about diary arrangements.

The council is asking residents to accept difficult decisions about services and much higher bills. They need to know whether their leaders pursued every available opportunity to make BCP’s case.

Your administration promised openness and transparency when it took office. Those promises must mean more than words in a press release or consultation document.

With residents facing much higher bills, additional precepts being charged, and services under pressure or being cut, they deserve clear answers and evidence that public money is being handled responsibly. The evidence provided would appear to point the other way.

 Please publish your correspondence and respond to these questions urgently and in full. BCP residents have had enough of claims they cannot verify, costs they were not properly asked about and decisions that seem to put the administration’s agenda ahead of taxpayers’ pressing financial concerns.

 Yours sincerely,

 John Beesley
