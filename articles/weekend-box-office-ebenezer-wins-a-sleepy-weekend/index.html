Weekend Box Office: Ebenezer Wins a Sleepy Weekend

TheTools · 16.11.2026 09:16 · source · permalink

Weekend Box Office

Meanwhile, David Leitch's How to Rob a Bank stalls in seventh place, and Godzilla take s a tumble.

By Erik Childress | Published November 15, 2026

TAGGED AS: Box Office, movies, news

It was certainly a weekend of holds and drops. Some solid formers for films finding their audience and a big latter for the big lizard not easing the trauma of WWII. Audiences were not overly enthused with this week’s new offerings and while one modestly led the box office they may be saving their money for the next month worth of titles.

King of the Crop: Ebenezer Wins a Sleepy Weekend

On the list of stories that the movies have explored to death, the tale of Ebenezer Scrooge has to be up there with Robin Hood, King Arthur, Dracula, and the Predator. But here we are — with the addition of Johnny Depp, no less — from horror filmmaker Ti West. Ebenezer, released by Paramount, did lead the box office with $19 million. That’s lower than Robert Zemeckis’ animated A Christmas Carol back in 2009 ($30.01 million) and lower than the inflation-fueled opening of Paramount’s Scrooged with Bill Murray from 1988 (roughly $37 million today). That’s still not a disastrous number for the modestly budgeted ($38 million) adaptation. The film is Depp’s first notable headliner since the last Pirates of the Caribbean film back in 2017. His last role before that was as Whitey Bulger in Black Mass, which opened to $22.6 million and finished with over $62 million. Depp has never had a $100 million film that didn’t feature Jack Sparrow or wasn’t directed by Tim Burton. Ebenezer added another $23 million internationally.

Tales of the Top 10: The Cat in the Hat Takes Down Godzilla

The Cat in the Hat made $11.4 million in its second weekend, bringing its 10-day total to $38.2 million. The good news is that it will double its opening weekend. The bad is that it needs to do five times that to reach $100 million. In 2011, Warner Bros. had another animated disappointment with Happy Feet Two. George Miller’s film opened to $21.2 million and had $43.7 million after 10 days and a $13.3 million second weekend. If that is the pace the Seuss adaptation finds itself on, it could be headed for a domestic finish between $60-70 million. Maybe with Thanksgiving it can stretch itself a little further, but this is not The Polar Express and it’s looking like a big disappointment for WB.

After having one of the best openings for a non-English release ever last week, Godzilla Minus Zero fell out of the top spot with $8.3 million. That is one of the biggest drops of the year (73%), which brings its 10-day total to $48.8 million. We don’t have to look back too far to find a comparison here, as it is on a very similar path to Street Fighter from just a few weeks ago. It fell 72.1% in its second weekend after a $30.7 million opening (Godzilla Minus Zero opened with $30.6 million) and had a 10-day total of $46.9 million. The big difference is that Street Fighter cost $82 million and Minus Zero reportedly cost just $18 million. Globally Godzilla is over $80 million and a solid hit.

Over the years, Bollywood films have had solid domestic openings, but they tend to drop off a bit more sharply. This year’s Dhurandhar: The Revenge fell 54.1% in its second weekend, while films like Pathaan, Jawan, and Baahubali 2: The Conclusion dropped between 60.7% and 67.4%. Everyone’s favorite, RRR, fell 83%. This week, Ramayana: Rise of a Legend fell just 38%. The Sony release grossed $5.5 million and has made $24.1 million to date and is on tap to become the highest-grossing Indian film of all time in North America.

Let’s check in on the $100 million pace of You Can See Everything. After a fifth weekend of $5.4 million and a 31-day total of $89 million, Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s documentary is almost $10 million ahead of the pace of The Social Network ($79.5 million) and less than $2 million behind Captain Phillips ($90.8 million). In so many words, it’s right on pace for a finish between $100-105 million. It should hit the magic number during Thanksgiving week, helping to drive this year’s total of $100+ million films to over 20 before December. Take a moment to consider how a three-hour documentary did better than Mortal Kombat II, Supergirl, Practical Magic 2, Masters of the Universe, and Street Fighter this year.

The last time Jim Caviezel headlined a film, it launched the arrival of Angel Studios and also turned into one of the most controversial box office releases in recent memory. We still wonder how many people actually took advantage of those pay-it-forward tickets to Sound of Freedom. Numbers are still numbers and Caviezel has both that and The Passion of the Christ under his belt as major box office successes. While Ghost Soldier may seem like an Angel Studios release with the presence of both Caviezel and the not-so-subtle allusions to his character’s faith, the release actually comes from Sony. Too many bullets and explosions for Angel, even with a PG-13 rating. Nevertheless, the film opened to $5.2 million (and $8.3 million since its opening on Veteran’s Day), marking Sony’s third sub-$10 million opening this year for a film in over 2,500 theaters after The Breadwinner and The Social Reckoning. They had four in 2025 and five in 2024, but they also now own the highest-grossing domestic release of all time with Spider-Man: Brand New Day.

Amazon/MGM moved David Leitch’s How to Rob a Bank from its Labor Day opening into the holiday season. Leitch is coming off of Universal’s mega-bomb The Fall Guy, but that was after a trifecta of success that included Deadpool 2, Hobbs & Shaw, and Bullet Train. His latest with Nicholas Hoult and Zoë Kravitz — which has nothing to do with the 2007 IFC release of the same name with Nick Stahl and Erika Christensen nor the 2024 Netflix true crime documentary — opened with just $5.1 million. That is Amazon/MGM’s lowest opening of the year for a film in 2,000+ theaters. (Mercy with Chris Pratt in January was the previous low with $10.8 million.) How to Rob a Bank had a reported $60 Million budget and its studio is going to be putting the focus on I Play Rocky for the rest of the year. Peter Farrelly’s film moved into 29 theaters this weekend, grossing $504,000. The $17,373 per-theater average is the sixth best of the year for a film in its second weekend. It has made $954,000 in 10 days.

On the list of films finding or keeping their audiences, Martin McDonough’s Wild Horse Nine fell just 22.2% in its second weekend with $4.6 million, bringing its total to $13.3 million. The Searchlight Pictures release has already outgrossed McDonough’s In Bruges and The Banshees of Inisherin, and it should pass Seven Psychopaths’ $15 million this week. Globally the film has made $28 million to date. Also, in its fourth weekend, Laika’s Wildwood made another $4.5 million. After 24 days in theaters it has grossed $48.4 million. That is higher than ParaNorman, which had $45.4 million after a $4.19 million fourth go. Wildwood is now on pace to become Laika’s second-highest-grossing domestic release. The $100 million budgeted film has still made just $70 million worldwide to date.

Checking in on the glass half-full-or-empty numbers of WB and DC’s Clayface, especially in light of last week’s news, $3.7 million in weekend four brings its 24-day total to $63.6 million. That is $6.3 million behind Supergirl’s total at this point, but it had a weekend $1.9 million higher. Kill Bill Vol. 1 had a $4.5 million fourth weekend and Clayface is nearly $3 million ahead of its pace and should be looking at a finish in the $70 million region. Again, globally, the film is at $121 million, which is still behind Shazam: Fury of the Gods ($134 million), Blue Beetle ($130.7 million), and Supergirl ($126.4 million), as well as both pandemic day-and-date releases of Wonder Woman 1984 ($166.8 million) and The Suicide Squad ($168.7 million). On its own, Clayface should be a hit on the books for WB. But it is still the sixth sub-$200 million release from DC since 2020. Birds of Prey made $201.8 million worldwide, which was less than The Marvels, the lowest-grossing release in the other comic book universe, by nearly $175 million.

Beyond the Top 10: Drummer Boy Drops Out, Victorian Psycho Doesn’t Make the Cut

Angel Studios’ “action-musical” Drummer Boy fell 62.9% to $3.5 million in its second weekend, bringing its 10-day total to $16.1 million. Angel’s release of the Kevin James rom-com, Solo Mio, was at $16.9 million in 10 days earlier this year after a $6.3 million second weekend. Drummer Boy may be coming in for a softer landing in the vicinity of $20 million; that’s still their fourth-highest live-action domestic grosser. Black Bear’s release of Wicker grossed $1.51 million. It has made $23.3 million, the third best for a Sundance premiere this year after Buddy ($26.9 million) and The Invite ($26.1 million). Bleecker Street’s release of Zachary Wigon’s Victorian Psycho opened to just $1.08 million in 1,200 theaters.

Neon opened James Gray’s Paper Tiger with Adam Driver, Scarlett Johansson, and Miles Teller into six theaters this weekend. It made $215,000 for a per-theater average of $35,000, just the 19th best of 2026. By comparison, A24’s release of Jordan Firstman’s Club Kid opened in six theaters last week to $403,475 and had the fifth-best PTA of the year. This weekend, A24 upped Club Kid’s release to 27 theaters, where it made $756,000 and now stands with over $1.4 million. That $28,000 PTA is the third best this year for a film in its second week behind Spider-Man: Brand New Day ($32,149) and Netflix’s La bola Negra ($28,214), which made $500,000 this weekend in 341 theaters for a total of $3.5 million.

On The Vine: The Hunger Games Return

Next week, Lionsgate’s The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping will lead the way, hoping to outdo the first prequel, The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, in every way. Then in limited release, Focus begins the campaign for Andrew Scott at the Oscars with Elsinore, which is currently at 89% on the Tomatometer. Another Sundance favorite (Certified Fresh at 96%), Josephine with Channing Tatum, gets its release from Sumerian. Nicolas Cage stars in IFC’s Best Pancakes In the County, and his other face, John Travolta, headlines Ketchup Entertainment’s November 1963. See all the upcoming releases here.
