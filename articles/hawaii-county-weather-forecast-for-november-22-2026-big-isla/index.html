Breezy trades will focus clouds and showers along windward and mauka areas as a band of moisture sags south along the eastern end of the state through tonight. Then drier and more stable conditions are expected as the trades ease through midweek next week. A cold front is expected to move through on Wednesday night through Thanksgiving Day, bringing gusty northerly winds and increasing the chance of rain. Trades will briefly return for next weekend.

Satellite and radar imagery show a band of clouds and showers moving into windward and mauka areas over the eastern half of the state this afternoon on the moderate to breezy trades. Hi-res models indicate that this band will focus primarily over the Big Island overnight while drier air filters across the smaller islands. The shower activity along this band will remain slightly enhanced through Sunday as a weak upper level trough moves down the island chain.
The surface high northeast of the state will continue to gradually weaken while it shifts east-southeast through early next week as a broad low pressure system develops in the north central Pacific. The progression of these features will push the weak surface ridge near and eventually over the state by early to mid next week, causing the trade wind speeds to gradually decline. Aloft, ridging will build in from west early next week, helping to further stabilize the weakening trade wind flow. Increased stability combined with a drier airmass that will persist over the region will help to limit shower coverage into midweek.
By Tuesday night into Wednesday, models indicate that winds will become light and variable in advance of an approaching cold front. The cold front is currently expected to push down the island chain late Wednesday night through Thanksgiving Day. While both the GFS and ECMWF have been in decent agreement on the timing of the frontal passage, forecast confidence regarding the progression of this front remains on the lower side as the models are still struggling to resolve the synoptic features in the Pacific at that time range. However, based on the current forecast, the main threat is the potential for strong and gusty northerly winds. While the front is also expected to bring an uptick in shower coverage as it passes through, the greatest potential for heavy rain and thunderstorms looks to remain north of the state for now. Then much drier air will filter in behind the front, bringing drier and cooler conditions for Friday morning.
A new surface high behind the front will build north of the state Friday allowing moderate to locally breezy easterly trades to return next weekend.

A band of showers which brought spotty MVFR conditions to various airports today will move on and drier air is expected to fill in behind it. This means that scattered showers today should decrease more to isolated tonight, and the associated MVFR ceilings and visibilities will become fewer and father between. Breezy trade winds will continue through tonight and will begin to decrease gradually tomorrow evening.
