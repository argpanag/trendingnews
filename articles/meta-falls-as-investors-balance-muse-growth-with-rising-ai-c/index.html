Meta falls as investors balance Muse growth with rising AI costs

TheTools · 22.10.2026 20:17 · source · permalink

Oct 22 (Reuters) - Shares of Meta ⁠Platforms fell more than 5% in early trading on Thursday, as ⁠investors weighed the benefits of the company's fast-growing AI agent business against ‌the mounting costs of funding its ambitious AI expansion.

As of Wednesday's close, the stock had gained more than 5% this year. If current losses hold, Meta is set to shed about $85 billion ​in market value.

• The downbeat reaction comes despite ⁠Meta reporting stronger-than-expected quarterly revenue, ⁠forecasting fourth-quarter sales above Wall Street estimates and highlighting rapid adoption of its new ⁠AI ‌agent app, Muse

• The earnings report came hours after a New Mexico judge ordered the company to pay a $25 billion penalty in a ⁠data-privacy case linked to Cambridge Analytica. Meta said it ​plans to appeal the ‌ruling

• The Instagram parent is spending heavily to support its AI plans, ⁠with an ​expected annual capital expenditure forecast between $135 billion and $145 billion. It also reported a free cash flow deficit of $2.12 billion in the third quarter, compared with positive free cash flow ⁠a year earlier

• Deutsche Bank analyst Benjamin Black ​said Meta's AI investments were generating stronger advertising performance and engagement, creating a "positive thesis creep" around the stock as investors increasingly view computing infrastructure as a revenue-generating asset ⁠rather than a cost burden

• Meta also announced plans to create a new class of non-voting Class C shares, giving the company additional flexibility for acquisitions and employee compensation while preserving CEO Mark Zuckerberg's voting control

• At least 13 ​brokerages have raised their price targets on the stock ⁠after the results, according to data compiled by LSEG

• Concerns about the scale of ​Meta's AI investments have also spilled into credit ‌markets, with the cost of insuring the ​company's debt against default widening to a record high

(Reporting by Prathik Jayaprakash in Bengaluru, additional reporting by Rashika Singh; Editing by Devika Syamnath)
