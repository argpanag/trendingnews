  1. Chris Brown given suspended eight-month prison sentence over nightclub attackpublished at 14:06 GMT
    14:06 GMT

    US singer Chris Brown has been issued an eight-month custodial sentence, suspended for a year, after an attack at a London nightclub in 2023 which left music producer Abe Diaw needing hospital treatment for head and knee injuries.

    Brown, 37, and his co-defendant Omololu Akinlolu attended Southwark Crown Court after having each pleaded guilty to the offence of affray in July.

    Akinlolu, 40, was given the lesser sentence of four months which was also suspended - his role in the attack was deemed "significant" by Judge Tony Baumgartner but it was noted that in contrast to Brown he neither initiated it nor used a bottle.

    The court saw CCTV footage of the incident, and the judge explained how Brown hit Diaw with a tequila bottle - including using it to strike him on the head, describing "the repeated use" of a bottle as "plainly dangerous".

    The judge considered how it took place in a "crowded" and "confined" environment and that the two defendants pursued Diaw and continued the attack after he tried to move away.

    The brevity of the incident was also taken into account, lasting "no more" than 20 seconds, but the violence was "intense" although it wasn't planned, he said.

    Neither Brown nor Akinlolu will be going to prison today - but they were told their sentence should still be considered a "punishment". Both men must also each pay £1,500 towards the prosecution's costs.

  4. The defendants' custodial sentences were suspended - here's what that meanspublished at 13:00 GMT
    13:00 GMT

    As we've reported, each of defendants has been given a custodial sentence that has been suspended for 12 months.

    That means they won't go to prison immediately - but if they are convicted of another offence within the time period the sentence is suspended for (in this instance 12 months), then they are likely to have to serve that custodial sentence in addition to the sentence for the new offence.

    The judge today set out: "These sentences are the shortest custodial sentences which in my judgment are commensurate with the seriousness of your respective offences.

    "Each sentence is one of 12 months or less - by law I am required to suspend each custodial sentence unless you are an excluded offender or I am satisfied that exceptional circumstances relating to the offence or the offender justify an immediate custodial sentence."

    The Sentencing Act 2026 introduced a presumption to suspend short custodial sentences of 12 months or less.

    The judge explained the circumstances in this case "do not justify" displacing that statutory requirement.

    Nonetheless, he addressed the defendants: "Be in no doubt, however, a suspended sentence order is a custodial sentence, the imposition of such an order is a punishment with or without requirements."

    Sometimes, a defendant also will have to comply with requirements or conditions during that suspended period - but the judge said he did not consider it necessary or proportionate in this case.

    Addressing Akinlolu now, the judge says his culpability is lower than that of Brown's, with the prosecution accepting he neither possessed nor used a bottle.

  10. Brown not entitled to special treatment because of 'fame' - judgepublished at 12:09 GMT
    12:09 GMT

    The judge tells Brown that he played a "leading role in this joint offending" and that he will take that into account when sentencing.

    He notes that Brown has "two previous convictions involving violence", dating to 2009 and 2013. These are "relevant because of their nature" but they are "now old", he says.

    He adds that Brown has been under bail conditions for a "lengthy period", adding that he's considered his compliance with these.

    Other factors that the judge says he is taking into account are that Brown is a father of four, and the impact a custodial sentence may have on them. As well as his "stable employment" and "charitable activities".

    But he emphasises that Brown is not entitled to special treatment because of his "fame, commercial success, and professional standing".

    He adds Brown's pre-sentence report deemed his risk of further offending to be "low".

  12. Judge describes incident as 'short' but says violence was 'intense'published at 12:01 GMT
    12:01 GMT

    Continuing to run through the events, the judge addresses Akinlolu - saying he did not initiate the violence but nevertheless "became an active participant".

    He describes both defendants pursuing Diaw, and references a kick seen in the CCTV footage that was directed to Diaw while he was on the ground.

    The judge says he is "satisfied" the kicking foot was Akinlolu's and he will sentence him on that basis.

    The CCTV also shows Brown being "physically" pulled away and trying to get back to Diaw.

    The entire incident was "short", the judge continues, and adds that brevity is relevant, but the violence was "intense" although not planned.

    The judge then turns to the explanations from the two defendants - he says Brown's case is that he was uncomfortable with Diaw's presence and believed him a threat, while Akinlolu's case is that he thought Brown was being attacked and his initial motivation had been to "defend".

  13. Repeated use of a glass bottle 'was plainly dangerous' - judgepublished at 11:57 GMT
    11:57 GMT

    Beginning his remarks, Judge Tony Baumgartner reminds the two defendants that the maximum sentence is three years in prison, a fine, or both.

    He outlines the court proceedings which have led up to this point, with both defendants pleading guilty to affray.

    The judge notes that he has heard court submissions and viewed CCTV footage of the incident - which he has based his findings on - and starts outlining the events of the night.

    He says the incident occurred "in the early hours" of 19 February 2023, in the VIP area of Tape nightclub.

    Baumgartner says that Chris Brown "walked through the crowd towards" music producer Abe Diaw, and "struck him twice" with a tequila bottle. Brown then hit him on the shoulder and then attempted to strike him a fourth time.

    "The repeated use of a glass bottle in that way... was plainly dangerous," says the judge, adding that it "carried the risk of causing significantly more serious injury than was ultimately sustained".
