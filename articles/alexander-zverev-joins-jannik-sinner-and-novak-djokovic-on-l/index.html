Alexander Zverev joins Jannik Sinner and Novak Djokovic on legendary list as he reaches ATP Finals final

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:58 · source · permalink

Alexander Zverev defeated Daniil Medvedev, 7-6, 6-4, in the ATP Finals semi-finals.

The world number one edged a tight match in Turin to advance to his third ATP Finals final and his first since 2021. Zverev was better in the key moments of his semi-final match, particularly in the first-set tiebreak, which he won 7-0.

Zverev didn’t face a break point against Medvedev, and has yet to be broken in Turin. Reaching the final of the ATP Finals without being broken, the German wrote his name into the history books.

Alexander Zverev becomes third player since 2000 to reach ATP Finals final without being broken

Since 2000, only three men have qualified for the ATP Finals final without being broken: Zverev, Jannik Sinner, and Novak Djokovic. Sinner held serve until the final last year, and Djokovic did the same back in 2018. But who did Sinner and Djokovic beat on their way to the final, and did their serve hold up with the title on the line?

Players to reach ATP Finals final without dropping serve since 2000

  • Alexander Zverev (2026) – Six break points saved
  • Jannik Sinner (2025) – Eight break points saved
  • Novak Djokovic (2018) – Two break points saved

In 2018, Djokovic defeated John Isner, 6-4, 6-3, Zverev, 6-4, 6-1, Marin Cilic, 7-6, 6-2, and Kevin Anderson, 6-2, 6-2, setting up a final clash with Zverev without dropping serve in any of his first four matches. Unfortunately for Djokovic, he couldn’t keep the streak alive in the final. Djokovic lost to Zverev in straight sets, 6-4, 6-3, and was broken four times by the big-serving German.

Seven years later, Sinner beat Felix Auger-Aliassime, 7-5, 6-1, Zverev, 6-4, 6-3, Ben Shelton, 6-3, 7-6, and Alex de Minaur, 7-5, 6-2, reaching the ATP Finals final with his serve intact. Like Djokovic, Sinner was broken in the final against Carlos Alcaraz, but still emerged victorious in Turin. Sinner defeated Alcaraz, 7-6, 7-5, to become a two-time ATP Finals champion.

This means Zverev will have a chance to make history against Alcaraz in Sunday’s final. If he wins the match without being broken, he will become the first player this century to have held serve throughout an ATP Finals campaign. That’s easier said than done, though, especially against one of the most prolific returners we’ve ever seen. Alcaraz leads the ATP Tour in ‘Return Games won %’ in 2026 (30.1%).

The world number one would love to make history in Turin, but in reality, he won’t care how he wins the final, as long as he’s the man lifting the trophy. Zverev and Alcaraz will battle it out for the ATP Finals title on Sunday, November 21.

Alexander Zverev turns tide in head-to-head with Daniil Medvedev

Zverev walked onto the court as the favourite to beat Medvedev, although their head-to-head record told a different story. Before their latest encounter in Turin, Medvedev had won 14 matches to Zverev’s nine.

Securing the win and his place in the ATP Finals final, Zverev still trails, 9-14, but the momentum has swung in his favour. Zverev beat Medvedev in Paris last year and has now added another win to his resume.

Alexander Zverev and Daniil Medvedev’s head-to-head record (9-14)

Whenever they meet again on the ATP Tour, Zverev will be able to put the losing record to the back of his mind, knowing full well that the tide has turned in his rivalry with the Russian star.

While Zverev is expected back on court to face Alcaraz in the final, Medvedev can begin his off-season. As was said during Sky Sports’ post-match coverage, if there are any late flights from Turin to the Maldives tonight, Medvedev will be there.
