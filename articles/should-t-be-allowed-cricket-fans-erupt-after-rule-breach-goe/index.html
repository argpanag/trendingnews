'Should't be allowed': Cricket fans erupt after rule breach goes unnoticed

TheTools · 15.11.2026 01:36 · source · permalink

The rules were in the spotlight during the Sydney Sixers' win over the Melbourne Renegades.

It was a strange old night in the WBBL on Thursday as Ellyse Perry was run-out in bizarre circumstances and Deandra Dottin later appeared to get away with 'fake fielding'. New Zealand captain Melie Kerr starred with bat and ball to keep the Sydney Sixers unbeaten with an 11-run victory over the Melbourne Renegades.

But the result didn't come without some controversy and drama. Perry was run-out in highly unusual circumstances for 30 when the umpire originally ruled it not out and opted not use the third umpire.

It was only after replays were shown on the big screen that the on-field umpire asked for it to be reviewed upstairs. Replays showed that Perry's bat had bounced over the crease, and because she never had anything grounded behind the line she was eventually given out.

Cricket fans were left questioning whether it was fair on Perry that the third umpire was only called upon after replays were shown at the ground. However the general majority felt the correct decision was made in the end, regardless of how it was reached.

Under cricket rules, the third umpire can be used at any point up until the next delivery is bowled. Perry walked off as soon as she saw the replays and didn't seem to have an issue with the process.

Confusion erupts over 'fake fielding'

However it wasn't the only bizarre moment of the match. Later in the innings, Renegades star Dottin appeared to get away with breaking the rules around 'fake fielding'.

The West Indian missed the ball in the outfield while trying to prevent the Sixers taking a second run, but pretended she had the ball and faked a throw. It appeared a clear attempt to deceive the batters, which was outlawed by the ICC in 2017.

Deandra Dottin pretended the throw the ball in despite not having it. Image: Channel 7/WBBL

It should have resulted in the Sixers receiving five penalty runs, but the umpires seemed to miss it. Aaron Finch said in commentary for Channel 7: "The fact that Dottin pretends like she's throwing the ball to confuse the batters, my question if I'm out in the middle would be 'is that fake fielding?'"

Fans were divided on whether Dottin broke the rules, with some suggesting she was simply following through with her plan to throw the ball despite missing it. Others were adamant it should have been five extra runs on the Sixers' total, but thankfully it didn't cost them the victory.
