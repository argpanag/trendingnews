Belief all that’s left for Tigers

TheTools · 31.10.2026 06:49 · source · permalink

Instead of stamping their authority on the game, Bangladesh's batters were left in despair at the end of Day 3 of the first Test against West Indies at the Sher-e-Bangla National Cricket Stadium yesterday.

The dark clouds over Mirpur, which interrupted play several times and contributed to a reduced number of overs on each of the three days, brought back memories of other matches in recent years when batting collapses repeatedly saw Bangladesh surrender control.

With the Tigers reeling at 79 for six, leading by just 106 runs, the inevitable question at the post-day press conference was what had been going through the batters' minds.

A perplexed Nayeem Hasan, asked to explain Tawhid Hridoy's shot selection, said: "Actually, I don't know what was going on in his mind. It's better if I don't talk about it. At that time I was getting ready, so didn't see what the dressing room reaction was."

After an abysmal dismissal in the first innings on Day 2, Hridoy once again appeared to lose sight of the situation. Having just arrived at the crease, he launched into a wild heave against Roston Chase, offering a simple catch to mid-on off just the second delivery he faced. The previous day, he had been bowled on the second delivery he faced trying to do the same.

Batting collapses have long been part of Bangladesh cricket across formats, but in Tests, the team often seems to bring them upon itself. The decision to prepare a spin-friendly pitch may have backfired this time, but it was difficult to escape the feeling that the batters were doing little to help themselves.

When Mominul Haque, who had suffered a blow to the head while fielding, did not come out to bat following Tanzid Tamim's dismissal, concerns grew over the possibility of Bangladesh needing a concussion substitute. With Mushfiqur Rahim's services unavailable, the team had also turned to Hridoy in a crucial batting position, only to see him throw his wicket away with a shot that was so inexplicable that it can’t be termed a brain fade.

Before that, Tanzid had fallen at gully after attempting to flick a Shamar Joseph delivery and closing the face of his bat too early. Shadman Islam, who had scored a century in the first innings and looked settled again in the second, left a gap between bat and pad, playing for the turn against Chase and being bowled. Skipper Najmul Hossain Shanto suffered a similar fate, missing Chase's straighter delivery after playing for the turn.

Litton Das, meanwhile, fell prey to Jomel Warrican, who did what Bangladesh's spinners had struggled to do: flight the ball and extract turn from just in front of the batter. Litton, who did not play for the turn, was trapped leg-before.

It was left to Jaker, who came in as Mominul's concussion substitute, and specialist spinner Nayeem to offer some resistance. Their 26-run stand was hard-earned and proved valuable in the context of the match, taking Bangladesh's lead beyond 100.

Asked whether the poor shot selections stemmed from panic, Nayeem insisted there had been no such feeling in the dressing room.

"Everyone had belief that someone would carry the innings. Litton bhai had done it before, so there was that belief," he said.

But that belief has taken another hit. Litton is already back in the pavilion, while Jaker, who is playing his first Test in more than a year, was not even earmarked for the playing XI at the start of the match.

Bangladesh's batting plans now look increasingly convoluted, leaving the remaining batters and the spin attack with a monumental task if the Tigers are to keep their hopes of remaining in contention for the World Test Championship final alive.

"Believe that me and Jaker bhai can make our innings longer," Nayeem said in his parting assessment.

For now, belief and a miracle are all the Tigers have left to hold on to.
