Folding steering wheel enters mass production, Ti9 ventures into 300,000-yuan-class market

TheTools · 20.11.2026 15:19 · source · permalink

Gasgoo Munich- The large six-seat SUV market in 2026 is getting denser on supply and fiercer on competition.

Family travel demand continues to build, but product differentiation is being erased almost as quickly as it appears. Refrigerators, screens, plush seats, and a functional advanced driver-assistance system have all shifted from bonus features to standard equipment in the segment. Powertrain choices are also redrawing user expectations: the share of pure-electric large three-row SUVs keeps climbing, while plug-in hybrids hold their base through long-distance refueling convenience — though the very conditions underpinning that advantage are being steadily chipped away by expanding charging networks and high-voltage platforms.

When competitors can barely separate themselves on spec sheets, the center of gravity shifts to more fundamental product definition. For a large six-seat SUV that has to serve double duty — daily commuting and long-haul family trips — whether the powertrain can balance energy efficiency, refueling convenience, and driving dynamics remains a decision variable no family buyer can sidestep.

The plug-in hybrid route retains its user base precisely because it tries to cover two scenarios with one system. But as competition intensifies, PHEV six-seaters need to offer something more specific — and more road-tested — than simply "no range anxiety on long trips."

That's the question the Fangchengbao Ti9 arrives to answer. Priced from 279,900 yuan with the top-spec Folding Smart Cabin trim at 329,900 yuan, it debuts the DMS Super Hybrid All-Round Platform, deploying full-condition powertrain and chassis capabilities to address the trade-offs plug-in hybrids face in real-world use — while establishing a distinct technical identity at the brand level through a mass-produced folding steering wheel.

Fangchengbao General Manager Xiong Tianbo defined the Folding Smart Cabin as "the pinnacle of BYD's long-termist technological innovation — a forward-looking reserve for the L3 and L4 autonomous driving era." She also announced that Fangchengbao's brand positioning has been upgraded from a personalization brand to a personalization-luxury brand.

A technology reserve aimed at next-generation autonomous driving, placed inside a family flagship priced just over 300,000 yuan — that fact alone reveals more than the folding steering wheel's ten-second stowage cycle. The Ti9 is attempting to use a future-facing technical label to find a new narrative entry point for a present-day zero-sum contest. Can it pull that off?

What Can a Folding Steering Wheel Actually Deliver?

The folding steering wheel is undoubtedly the Ti9's most communication-efficient label.

Xiong traced the technology's timeline: the idea of a stowable steering wheel had been floated in the industry around 2016, but the mechanical column linking the wheel to the road wheels long stood as an unavoidable physical constraint.

What allows the Ti9 to push this concept into production is that the mechanical column has been replaced by a steer-by-wire system. The physical hard link gives way to electronic signal transmission, creating the spatial conditions for the wheel to fold away when parked. The Ti9's folding steering wheel achieves a wide-angle fold from 0° to 180°, stowing in under 10 seconds, with linked logic that allows simultaneous folding while parking. It's rated for 80,000 high-frequency deployment cycles.

From an engineering standpoint, this is a mass-production breakthrough of genuine substance — and BYD's first-mover advantage in standard-setting and production implementation is undeniable.

That said, whether an engineering breakthrough translates into user value depends on how well it matches real-world scenarios. The folding steering wheel currently operates only when parked; while driving, the wheel stays in place and the driver remains in control.

Looking at usage habits, potential buyers who regularly nap or camp in their vehicle may place more value on the driver's-side space freed up by the folded wheel — seeing it as a practical fix for the age-old "steering wheel digging into your legs" problem. Commuter-focused buyers who rarely rest in a parked car, however, may not weight it heavily in their purchase decision.

The official positioning as "a forward-looking reserve for L3 and L4" itself signals that the folding steering wheel's strategic significance points more toward the future. This deployment reads more like a preview of tomorrow's technology brought forward to today: until autonomous driving truly takes the wheel, what the folding steering wheel can actually deliver is largely the novelty and ceremony of a transformed cabin space while parked — plus the psychological suggestion that "this car is ready for the next era."

So its value lies not in the spec-sheet comparison column, but in brand mindshare.

In a highly homogenized category, an engineering label with strong recall value can accomplish the initial persuasion — "this car is different" — at remarkably low communication cost, buying attention for subsequent product-strength messaging.

Of course, once that attention is won, the Ti9 still needs something road-tested to hold it. And the key to holding this wave of attention is likely this: the DMS platform and chassis system.

Building a Real Foundation Through Systemic Capability

The Ti9's true product-strength anchor lies not just in the folding steering wheel, but in a system that's already working without requiring users to change a single habit.

The DMS hybrid all-round platform aggregates the capabilities of three technical routes — DM-i, DM-p, and DMO — pairing a 1.5T dedicated hybrid engine with dual motors delivering a combined 400 kW. It pushes a full-size SUV measuring 5,270 mm long with a 3,130 mm wheelbase from zero to 100 km/h in 4.8 seconds, while keeping depleted-battery fuel consumption at 6.4 L/100 km. The Ti9 carries BYD's second-generation Blade Battery, offering up to 400 km of CLTC pure-electric range and 1,630 km of combined range. Flash charging takes the battery from 10% to 70% in 5 minutes at room temperature, and from 10% to 97% in 9 minutes — even at minus 30°C, it needs just 3 minutes more.

The significance of these figures lies not in any single category leadership, but in their attempt to dissolve the trade-off structure that has long plagued large SUVs: strong performance usually means high consumption, generous space usually means clumsy handling, and long range usually means a compromised charging experience.

The DMS integrates four functions — differential, differential lock, electronic limited-slip, and wheel-end decoupling — using stepless left-to-right torque distribution from 0 to 100% to replace some roles of additional motors. The result: higher torque allocation capability without encroaching on cabin space.

This technical route choice itself reflects the Ti9's product-definition priorities: first ensure that a six-seat family flagship delivers simultaneously on power, efficiency, and space — then talk about technical labels.

The chassis and steering combination further reinforces this foundation.

Rear wheels offer a maximum steering angle of ±7°, giving a vehicle over 5.2 meters long a conventional turning radius of 4.95 meters. On suitable low-grip surfaces, the Leopard Turn mode compresses the turning radius to just 3.55 meters. The DiSus-A dual-chamber air suspension, paired with 15 meters of preview distance and damping response under 25 milliseconds, adjusts suspension state before the vehicle reaches surface changes. This capability addresses the real constraints a full-size family vehicle encounters nearly every day — tight urban spaces, underground parking, complex road conditions, and long-distance travel.

Space and cabin configuration serve the same foundation. The boxy design achieves a 90% space utilization rate, with up to 59 storage points throughout the vehicle. The trunk offers 655 liters normally, expanding to 1,245 liters with the third row fully folded. The third row gets independent ventilation, heating, and massage. The 5D cabin features a 30-inch panoramic screen, a 21.4-inch second-row ceiling screen, a 31-speaker Devialet audio system, a front-passenger zero-gravity recliner, and an 11.3-liter heated and cooled refrigerator. None of these constitute disruptive technology, but together they determine the experience floor of a six-seat flagship on family road trips.

The split tailgate is another element in the Ti9's space narrative that effectively showcases the advantages of the boxy category. A conventional top-hinged tailgate isn't friendly to a full-size SUV measuring 5,270 mm long — in a tight underground garage, a wall too close behind means it can't fully open, and loading large items becomes a struggle.

The Ti9's upper gate opens to a maximum of 72°, with stop-anywhere positioning between 0 and 72°. It can open with as little as 492 mm of clearance behind the vehicle — roughly 20% less rearward space than a conventional top-hinged tailgate. The lower gate supports up to 300 kg and, when opened, becomes a usable outdoor viewing platform — giving camping and rest stops a practical anchor. In effect, it transforms the tail from a mere cargo endpoint into an interface connecting daily family life with the outdoors.

Safety follows the same logic. The God's Eye B system is standard across the range, with AEB capable of bringing the vehicle to a complete stop for a stationary car inside a tunnel at speeds up to 135 km/h. The high-strength steel cage body uses 29.8% hot-stamped steel, with 13 airbags throughout and side curtain airbags extending to the third row. TSC high-speed blowout stability assist and six-piston front calipers deliver 100-to-zero braking in 34.5 meters. These items won't get the spotlight at launch events, but they're the non-negotiable baseline for a 300,000-yuan-class family flagship.

Positioning and the Upward Bet in a "Red Sea"

Place the Ti9 back in the competitive landscape of 300,000-yuan-class large six-seat SUVs, and its positioning choice is both clear and structurally pressured.

The most instructive lesson in the 300,000-yuan market doesn't come from products that never sold — it comes from those that sold well, then rapidly lost momentum.

A phenomenon worth heeding has surfaced repeatedly in the 300,000-yuan segment in recent years: certain large six-seat SUVs generate high attention at launch through category tailwinds, configuration leadership, or price positioning, achieving short-term sales breakthroughs. But as competing models flood in and core configuration gaps close rapidly, their sales often drop noticeably after a brief "glory period." Even introducing entry-level trims at more tempting prices fails to restore prior momentum.

The underlying reason isn't that these products lack mechanical quality or individual features — it's that the 300,000-yuan-plus price band demands far more brand mindshare than the 100,000- or 200,000-yuan segments. When spec sheets no longer provide a clear, repeatable purchase rationale, consumers facing homogenized options naturally gravitate toward top-tier products with more defined brand expectations. Price cuts can briefly slow the decline, but they can't substitute for a purchase reason.

For the Ti9, the reference point is this: product quality is merely the entry ticket to the 300,000-yuan market. Whether technical advantages can be translated into purchase reasons that users understand and articulate is what separates sustained sales from a flash in the pan.

The Ti9's strategy uses the plug-in hybrid route and the DMS platform's all-condition capability to establish an all-round positioning in the 270,000-to-330,000-yuan range that requires no trade-offs among performance, efficiency, space, and handling. The logic holds because it genuinely addresses an underserved need: families with real concerns about long-distance refueling convenience who simultaneously refuse to compromise on space and driving dynamics.

Returning to Fangchengbao itself, the Ti9's significance must be understood within an unfinished narrative.

The brand has surpassed 600,000 cumulative sales in 35 months since its founding, holding above 40,000 units for four consecutive months. Fangchengbao has already proven with the Titanium 7 that it can build a dominant product in the 200,000-yuan class. But the gap between selling in volume and selling at a premium while holding firm doesn't close automatically with cumulative scale. Fangchengbao still needs a model that can simultaneously drive sales and brand premium — especially now that the brand has been officially elevated from personalization to personalization-luxury.

The good news is that as the new Chinese luxury trend gains momentum over the past two years, users who've experienced traditional luxury brands are no longer satisfied with one-size-fits-all luxury paradigms. That's exactly the task the Ti9 needs to accomplish for Fangchengbao: building on the scale foundation established by the Titanium 7, providing a brand reason strong enough for 300,000-yuan-class buyers to take seriously.

Using a 300,000-yuan-class flagship that neither departs from the brand's established large-vehicle identity nor relies on a single technical label — but instead leverages the DMS platform and Folding Smart Cabin as a combined technical package to push the brand's price ceiling up a notch. Whether this path works depends on a question deeper than spec sheets: consumers above 300,000 yuan aren't just buying a set of features and parameters. They're buying a value proposition about family lifestyle — and a brand identity they're willing to defend in social settings.

Plenty of same-class products have offered clear warnings: in the 300,000-yuan market, product quality is merely the entry ticket; brand momentum and user mindshare are the variables that determine sales sustainability. The Ti9's technical reserves are substantial enough — the DMS's all-round capability, the flexibility of rear-wheel steering, the 5D cabin's coverage of third-row comfort are all assets that can translate into real experience. But whether these assets can be understood and trusted by target users at low enough cognitive cost depends on channel experience depth, long-term reputation accumulation, and brand communication consistency.

Bottom line: The Ti9 is a remarkably honest car in its product logic. It hasn't bet everything on the folding steering wheel as a single forward-looking label. Instead, it uses the DMS platform and a complete chassis, space, and safety configuration to build a foundation that supports daily use — then deploys the Folding Smart Cabin to open a technical narrative entry point oriented toward next-generation smart vehicles at the brand level.

At the juncture where large six-seat SUVs transition from incremental growth to zero-sum competition in 2026, this dual-layer strategy — stability at the foundation, novelty in the narrative — comes closer to the judgment a mature automaker should make than either spec-sheet stuffing or pure topic generation.

Of course, technological foresight can win attention, but brand elevation can only be earned kilometer by kilometer through real experience. Whether the Ti9 can help Fangchengbao cross from a scale brand to a value brand doesn't depend on how many times the folding steering wheel collapses under the spotlight — it depends on how many family users, in day-to-day use, genuinely feel the ease that the all-round platform delivers, and are willing to tell the next person about it.
