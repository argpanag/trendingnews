The Thunder Bay District Health Unit is urging residents at higher risk of severe illness to get vaccinated early as its annual fall respiratory illness vaccine campaign gets underway.

Appointments for flu and COVID-19 vaccines are now available for high-risk and priority groups, including people at greater risk of severe illness and those who work or live with them.

All other residents will become eligible on October 26.

“Staying up-to-date on seasonal vaccines is an effective action that each and every one of us can take,” said Dr. Greg Holzman, acting medical officer of health. “When more people are vaccinated, viruses have a harder time spreading from person to person. This protects the most vulnerable in our community, some of whom cannot be vaccinated, including infants under six months.”

Flu and COVID-19 vaccines are free for people who live, work or attend school in Ontario.

Health-care providers, long-term care and retirement homes, assisted living homes and hospitals began receiving supplies of the vaccines on Thursday, while pharmacies are expected to receive supplies in mid-October.

The health unit is also encouraging eligible residents to consider protection against respiratory syncytial virus, or RSV.

One dose of the RSV vaccine is recommended for adults 75 and older. It is also available to people between 60 and 74 who meet certain eligibility criteria.

The RSV vaccine can be administered at the same time as flu and COVID-19 vaccines, but is not currently available at pharmacies.

Ontario is also offering Beyfortus to help protect infants from RSV. The antibody medication is available to infants born after April 1, 2026 who are up to eight months old during their first RSV season.

Some children up to 24 months old who are at higher risk of severe illness may also be eligible during their second RSV season after consulting with a health-care provider.

Pregnant people can also receive the RSV vaccine Abrysvo through a health-care provider to help protect their infant after birth.

“RSV is a leading cause of infant hospitalization in Canada, but it is highly preventable,” said Dr. Jazmyn Balfour-Boehm. “The Ontario prevention program is free, and can lower the risk of hospitalization by about 80 per cent.”

TBDHU is also recommending residents speak with their health-care providers about the pneumococcal vaccine as cases of invasive pneumococcal disease have increased in Thunder Bay and the surrounding district in recent years.

The vaccine is available to people 65 and older, as well as those between 18 and 64 with certain medical conditions.

TBDHU vaccination clinics for high-risk residents will be held October 15 at the Oliver Road Community Centre and Oct. 22 at the Superior Inn.

Clinics will open to everyone eligible beginning in November, with dates scheduled for November 6 at the Da Vinci Centre, November 12 at the Oliver Road Community Centre, November 20 at the Da Vinci Centre and December 2 at the Oliver Road Community Centre.

All clinics are by appointment only.
