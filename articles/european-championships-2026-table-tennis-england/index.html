European Championships 2026 - Table Tennis England

TheTools · 13.10.2026 11:02 · source · permalink

European Championships 2026

See how the English athletes are getting on at the European Championships in Ljubljana, Slovenia.

Liam Pitchford and Connor Green topped their groups with two victories apiece to join Tom Jarvis in the Men’s Singles main draw, but Sam Walker is out and Ella Pashley and Tianer Yu were unable to join Tin-Tin Ho in the Women’s Singles main draw at the European Championships in Ljubljana, Slovenia.

Jarvis, seeded 22nd, has been drawn to meet Andrei Istrate of Romania in the Men’s Singles round of 64. Pitchford will have to battle the crowd as well as his opponent as he faces Slovenia’s Deni Kozul, the 28th seed, while Green meets another exciting young talent in Flavien Coton of France, who is seeded 32.

In the Women’s Singles, Ho is seeded 24th and will face Romania’s Elena Zaharia, the top-seeded player in qualifying in the round of 64. Wales and GB’s Anna Hursey, the 16th seed will play Alexandra Chiriacova of Moldova.

The singles main draw gets under way on Wednesday afternoon.

Sixth seeds Green & Ho fell at the first hurdle of the Mixed Doubles. Their opponents in the round of 32 were Elias Ranefur & Filippa Bergand of Sweden, who had defeated Sam Walker & Tianer Yu in the qualifying rounds.

It was a 3-1 (11-8, 12-10, 7-11, 11-7) defeat for Green & Ho, who have had a good year to date, reaching three WTT finals and winning one.

Hursey & Anders Lind of Denmark, seeded 10th, are into the last 16, having beaten Darius Movileanu & Andreea Dragoman of Romania 3-0 (7, 2, 6). They next face another Romania pair – Eduard Ionescu & Bernadette Szocs, the fourth seeds.

Ho & Hursey, seeded eighth, faced Nina Skerbinz & Karoline Mischek-Colucci of Austria in the last 32 of the Women’s Doubles.

Ho won the European Under-21 Women’s Doubles title with Mischek-Colucci in 2019, so knows her opponent well, and the British pair got the first game on the board, 11-6.

However, the Austrians hit back 11-5 in the second and moved ahead by taking the third 11-9. Ho & Hursey came out more assertively in the fourth, looking to get in on the second or third ball and opening up a 7-1 lead on their way to taking it 11-6.

They continued the momentum, winning the decider 11-4 with a late flurry of points and will next play Poland’s Zuzanna Wielgos & Anna Brzyska, the 10th seeds, in the last 16 on Wednesday.

In the Men’s Doubles, Jarvis & Green, seeded 13th, were knocked out in three straight by Vladislav Ursu & Andrei Putuntica of Moldova in the last 32.

Both opponents played against England at the London 2026 World Team Championships – a match England won – and took some measure of revenge as they did not let Jarvis & Green get into the match. It was 3-0 (11-8, 11-5, 11-8) to Ursu & Putuntica.

Liam Pitchford and Connor Green topped their groups with two victories apiece to join Tom Jarvis in the Men’s Singles main draw, but Ella Pashley and Tianer Yu were unable to join Tin-Tin Ho in the Women’s Singles main draw.

Green began with a 3-0 (11-8, 11-9, 11-5) win over Andrii Grebeniuk of Ukraine in Group 13, and then overcame Eitay Shushan of Israel 3-1 (11-8, 11-6, 8-11, 11-7).

Pitchford started with a 3-0 (9, 5, 5) win over Aleksi Rasanen of Finland in Group 19 and then defeated Italian youngster Danilo Faso 3-0 (11-8, 12-10, 11-6) to top the group.

Walker had to dig deep to get past Tugay Yilmaz of Turkiye in his first Men’s Singles match in Group 31, 14-12 in the fifth having led 2-0 and seen his opponent fight back to level.

His second group match resulted in a 3-1 (12-10, 4-11, 11-5, 11-6) defeat to Anders Eriksson of Sweden, meaning he had to beat neutral athlete Nikita Artemenko to have any chance of topping the group. However, it was a 3-1 (11-6, 6-11, 11-8, 11-5) defeat, ending Walker’s interest in the competition.

Tianer Yu started with victory in three straight (7, 5, 5) against Sabina Musajeva of Latvia in Group 4 of the Women’s Singles. She faced a tougher challenge against Katarzyna Wegrzyn of Poland and although she won the second game after losing a close first, Yu was ultimately defeated 3-1 (13-11, 6-11, 11-7, 11-8).

Ella Pashley, in Group 16, was defeated in three (2, 4, 5) by Hanka Kodet of Czechia and went out of the tournament with a 3-0 (6, 7, 4) by Sara Tokic of the host nation.

In the Women’s Doubles, Yu & Pashley had an agonising defeat in the first qualifying round, going down 12-10 in the fifth to Martine Toftaker & Christine Kalvatn of Norway, having twice fought back from a game down to level the match.

Walker & Yu got through their first qualifying match in the Mixed Doubles, beating Lithuanian pair Ignas Sisanovas & Lukrecija Juchnaite in four, but then lost out in four to Sweden’s Elias Ranefur & Filippa Bergand, the second highest-ranked pair in qualifying, in the second round. Ranefur & Bergand will face Green & Ho in the round of 32.

Ranefur is becoming a familiar opponent for English athletes – Pitchford & Walker faced him and world No 4 Truls Moregard in the Men’s Doubles and it ended in a 3-0 (11-6, 11-5, 11-8) defeat for the English pair.

Jarvis & Green are seeded into the main draw, where they will face Vladislav Ursu & Andrei Putuntica of Moldova in the last 32 – familiar opponents as England defeated Moldova at the London 2026 World Team Championships.

How did the other Home Nations players get on?

In the Women’s Singles, Charlotte Carey of Wales finished third on a three-way countback, having lost 3-0 (11-6, 11-7, 11-2) to Reka Bezeg of Serbia and beaten Georgieva Tsvetelina of Bulgaria 3-2 (9-11, 6-11, 11-8, 11-5, 11-5). It was Bezeg who progressed, having lost 3-1 to Georgieva.

Wales’ Danielle Kelly took a game off Viktoria Persova (BUL) and Maria Berzosa of Spain, but also did not progress.

Jersey’s Hannah Silcock picked up a 3-2 (11-8, 7-11, 11-13, 12-10, 11-8) win against Lara Opeka of the host nation but then lost out to legendary Luxembourg player Ni Xia Lian 3-0 (12-10, 11-5, 12-10).

Alisa Khalid of Scotland lost 3-0 to both neutral athlete Lizaveta Tsimashkova and Matilde Pinto of Portugal, while compatriot Faye Leggett lost out in three to both Dora Cosic of Croatia and Ozge Yilmaz of Turkiye.

Martin Johnson of Scotland and Alex Gillen of Ireland were both in the same group, along with Netherlands’ Gabrielius Camara and Italy’s Matteo Mutti. The British players both lost 3-0 to both Camara and Mutti.

They met in the final round of matches and it was Gillen who won 3-2 (6-11, 13-11, 11-8, 3-11, 11-4. The group was won by Mutti.

In the Mixed Doubles, Silcock played with Matthias Sandhold of Iceland and they won their first qualifying round match 3-2 (9-11, 11-6, 11-6, 3-11, 11-8) against Johnson & Khalid of Scotland. They were then defeated in four games by Madis Moos & Airi Avameri of Estonia.

Faye Leggett, alongside Poul Petersen of Greenland, lost to Niagol Stoyanov & Giorgia Piccolin of Italy 3-0 (3, 4, 5) in the first qualifying round.

In the Women’s Doubles, Silcock & Enisa Sadikovic (LUX) lost 3-2 (11-4, 4-11, 11-5, 10-12, 11-7) to Sofia-Xuan Zhang & Elvira Rad of Spain in the second qualifying round having had a bye through the first.

Kelly & Carey beat Diana Rusinova & Sabina Musajeva of Latvia 3-0 (7, 5, 6) in the first round but lost to Alexandra Chiriacova of Moldova & Maria Berzosa of Spain 3-0 (6, 9, 6)

Leggett & Khalid lost 3-0 (7, 9, 5) to Kornelija Riliskyte & Lukrecija Juchnaite (LTU) in the first qualifying round.

In the Men’s Doubles, Johnson & Alexandros Madesis of Greece lost 3-1 (5-11, 11-8, 13-11, 11-8) to Loic Stoll & Elias Hardmeier (SUI) in the first round. Gillen & Daniels Jogans of Latvia lost 3-0 (7, 4, 7) to Maciej Kolodziejczyk & Robert Gardos of Austria.
