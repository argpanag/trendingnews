Dell Stock Before Nov 24: Can A $95 Billion Backlog Still Make Money?

TheTools · 20.11.2026 08:42 · source · permalink

Today’s big story: Will 2027 Be A 2022 Repeat For Stocks?

Dell reports its quarterly results on Tuesday, Nov 24, after the market closes. Last quarter it booked $60.9 billion of AI server orders. It ended the quarter with a $95 billion backlog, meaning orders booked but not yet delivered. In May 2024, the backlog was $3.8 billion.

Can those orders still make money? Dell buys memory chips for these servers, and memory has become dearer. Shares of the memory maker Micron are up 206% in a year.

Dell’s stock has fallen 9.3% this week, to $312.54 at Thursday’s close. Other AI hardware stocks fell with it. HPE fell 5.8% and Micron 11.3%. Dell is now about 47% below its high.

Report days move Dell’s stock a lot. Over its last 12 reports, the median next-day move was 10.6%, up or down. The median is the middle result. Six of those moves were up and six were down. The worst was a 17.9% fall.

Dell sits about 47% below its high, and one report day can move it 10% or more. If you are waiting for a better price to buy, your cash does not have to sit idle. Is Dell Keeping Less Of Each Sales Dollar?

Dell’s sales have grown fast, but its gross margin has slipped. Gross margin is the share of sales left after the direct cost of making the products. Sales grew 93% from the quarter to Nov 2024 to the quarter to Jul 2026. Gross profit grew 85%.

Source: Trefis company data from Dell’s quarterly reports. Gross margin is gross profit divided by sales.

Why does the margin slip? An AI server is mostly parts made by other companies. These are Nvidia‘s chips and the memory that sits beside them. So Dell keeps fewer cents per dollar on AI servers than on the rest of its business.

AI servers are also a growing share of Dell’s sales. Dell expects $74.0 billion of AI server sales in fiscal 2027, the year to January 2027. It sold $32.5 billion in the first half. That leaves about $20.7 billion a quarter for the second half. Last quarter it sold $16.4 billion. More AI servers and dearer memory both push the margin down.

So far the market has not minded. Dell’s two best report days this year came after quarters in which its gross margin fell. The stock rose 21.9% in February and 32.8% in May.

The $95 billion backlog equals about 15 months of this fiscal year’s expected AI server sales. With that much already booked, what Dell keeps now matters more than what it sells, in our view. We look at how much of Dell depends on Nvidia in How Much Of Dell Stock Is A Bet On Nvidia?

What Could The Margin Show On Nov 24?

The signs are mixed. Dell’s gross margin was 20.9% last quarter. In the quarter to October 2025, it was 20.7%. On Nov 24, Dell reports the same quarter of this year. On Sep 1, Dell said its outlook for second-half margins had improved. It also said the cost of the parts it buys keeps rising.

Dell expects sales of $49.0 billion for the quarter to October. In our view, a margin below 20.0% would not be a surprise, even if sales meet that forecast. If the margin holds at 20.9% or better, Dell is passing higher memory costs on to its buyers. Then the backlog is worth more than we think.

The record also shows which stocks move with Dell. On its last 12 report days, HPE moved the same way as Dell all 12 times. Super Micro and Micron did so 10 times.

Source: Trefis, prices through Nov 19. Reports from Nov 30, 2023 to Sep 1, 2026. Dell reports after the close, so each move is the next day’s close against the report day’s close. The typical move is the median, up or down. The Nasdaq 100 moved the same way as Dell 9 of 12 times.

What Should You Do Before Nov 24?

Know the worst case first. Hold only as much Dell as lets you sit through a 17.9% fall in one day. Don’t count on HPE or Super Micro as a hedge, a holding that rises when another falls. On report days they have moved with Dell, not against it. If you want more Dell, set your buying price before the report. The risk to this view is that memory prices ease and the margin holds.

The bigger point. A backlog promises sales, not profit. Dell’s sales have fallen before, by 13.6% in fiscal 2024. The companies that buy these servers face their own squeeze.

What Should You Watch On Nov 24?

  • Did Dell’s gross margin hold?
  • Which company can raise its prices most in an AI server: Micron, Nvidia or Dell?
  • How much of the backlog was priced before memory costs jumped?

The order book will make the headline on Nov 24. In our view, the number that decides what it is worth is the cents Dell keeps from each dollar of sales.

No one can know in advance which way that number will go. Owning many companies, not just one, means a single report day matters less to your savings.
