Max Verstappen Wins Singapore GP As Lap 55 Reshapes The F1 Title Race

TheTools · 11.10.2026 15:07 · source · permalink

Saksham Singh

Share

Max Verstappen finally added the Singapore Grand Prix to his Formula 1 victory list on Sunday, delivering a commanding performance at the Marina Bay Circuit. The Red Bull driver led all 62 laps and finished 7.335 seconds ahead of Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc, completing a weekend in which he also claimed pole position and won Saturday’s sprint race.

Yet the biggest championship development unfolded several places behind him.

Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli finished third after taking advantage of a fierce battle between Lewis Hamilton and Lando Norris in the closing laps. The result extended his lead over teammate George Russell to 102 points, putting the 20-year-old Italian within reach of his first Formula 1 world championship.

For Verstappen, it was a long-awaited breakthrough at a circuit where he had never previously won. For Antonelli, it was another significant step towards a potentially historic title.

Max Verstappen Turns His 250th Grand Prix Start Into a Singapore Victory

Max Verstappen arrived in Singapore with renewed momentum after securing his first victory of the 2026 season in Malaysia the previous weekend.

He carried that form into Marina Bay, taking pole position for both the sprint and Sunday’s main race before converting each opportunity into victory.

Sunday’s race also marked his 250th Formula 1 Grand Prix start.

The four-time world champion faced an early challenge from Leclerc, who attacked on the approach to Turn 1. Verstappen defended his position and kept the Ferrari behind, establishing control of the race from the opening lap.

Although Leclerc maintained a competitive pace and recorded several fastest laps during the closing stages, he could not seriously threaten the lead.

By Lap 58, Verstappen had built an advantage of around nine seconds. He ultimately crossed the finish line in 1:42:00.785, securing his first Singapore Grand Prix victory and a second consecutive win.

After the race, Verstappen praised Red Bull’s performance, explaining that the car had been competitive on both tyre compounds when needed. He also acknowledged the difficulties drivers experienced on medium tyres and expressed optimism about the team’s progress in recent races.

The victory underlined Red Bull’s improved competitiveness, even though Verstappen’s championship deficit leaves him outside the title fight.

Antonelli Seizes His Chance as Hamilton and Norris Battle

While Verstappen controlled the front, the contest for the final podium position produced a far less predictable finish.

Hamilton had been running third for Ferrari, with Norris closing rapidly in his McLaren. The two experienced drivers fought over the position as Antonelli approached from behind.

On Lap 54, Norris attempted to attack Hamilton around the outside before switching to the inside, but the Ferrari driver defended both approaches.

Their battle created an opening for Antonelli, who moved past Norris into fourth.

One lap later, the Mercedes driver overtook Hamilton to claim third place. Norris subsequently passed the Ferrari as well, dropping Hamilton to fifth.

The situation worsened for Hamilton on the final lap when Oscar Piastri also found a way past, leaving the seven-time world champion sixth at the chequered flag.

Antonelli held off Norris to finish third, 34.573 seconds behind Verstappen.

What initially looked like a difficult afternoon for the championship leader ended with another podium and a stronger position in the title race.

Ferrari’s Virtual Safety Car Decision Costs Hamilton

Hamilton’s disappointing finish was shaped by a strategic decision earlier in the race.

The Ferrari driver was the only leading contender to start on medium tyres and made his first pit stop on Lap 19, switching to softs.

He later found himself behind teammate Leclerc and expressed frustration over the time he was losing, telling his race engineer, “We lost two seconds that last lap.”

Hamilton wanted to make use of his available pace, but Ferrari instructed him to manage his tyres.

The decisive moment came when Lance Stroll stopped on track, triggering a virtual safety car.

Leclerc and Antonelli took advantage of the opportunity to pit, while Hamilton and Norris remained on track.

Hamilton temporarily moved into second place, but his older tyres became an increasing disadvantage once racing resumed.

Leclerc reclaimed the position, and Hamilton eventually lost further places to Antonelli, Norris and Piastri.

Following the race, Hamilton acknowledged his part in the strategic outcome, telling his team, “So boys, I let you down today with that strategy.”

The sequence left Ferrari with a mixed result. Leclerc secured a valuable second place, while Hamilton slipped from a potential podium finish to sixth.

Russell’s Recovery Cannot Stop Antonelli Extending His Lead

George Russell entered the Singapore weekend as Antonelli’s nearest championship rival, but his difficult run continued.

A power-unit failure had disrupted his previous race in Malaysia, and a crash during Saturday’s Singapore sprint added to his problems.

Although Russell qualified sixth for Sunday’s Grand Prix, an engine-related penalty forced him to start from the back of the grid.

He recovered steadily, reaching 13th by Lap 10, but another setback arrived during the virtual safety car period.

Russell entered the pit lane just as the neutralisation ended, missing out on the full advantage available to drivers who had stopped earlier.

He eventually finished ninth, collecting two points.

Antonelli’s third-place finish earned him 15, increasing the gap between the Mercedes teammates to 102 points with six championship rounds remaining.

The Italian now has 340 points compared with Russell’s 238.

Could Antonelli Win the 2026 F1 Championship in Austin?

Antonelli’s Singapore podium has created the possibility of an early championship celebration at the United States Grand Prix.

The next round is scheduled for the Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas, in two weeks.

With six rounds remaining after Singapore, Antonelli would be able to secure the championship in Austin if he finishes that race at least 126 points ahead of his nearest challenger.

Doing so would make him the youngest world champion in Formula 1 history.

The Mercedes driver appeared surprised when informed of his opportunity following Sunday’s race. Rather than getting carried away by the possibility, he emphasised the importance of delivering another strong weekend.

His position is particularly notable because Mercedes also maintains a commanding lead in the constructors’ standings.

The team has accumulated 578 points, ahead of Ferrari on 444, McLaren on 339 and Red Bull on 332.

A Singapore Victory for Verstappen, but the Championship Spotlight Moves to Antonelli

Verstappen leaves Singapore having achieved something that had eluded him throughout his Formula 1 career. His first Marina Bay victory, combined with a sprint win and pole position, completed an exceptional weekend for Red Bull.

But the championship picture tells a different story.

Despite Verstappen’s return to winning form, Antonelli’s consistency has put the Mercedes driver in a position to settle the title race before the season reaches its final rounds.

The contrast was particularly clear in Singapore. Verstappen never surrendered the race lead, while Antonelli had to fight his way into a podium position through the closing stages.

Both drivers emerged with exactly what they needed.

Verstappen finally conquered Singapore. Antonelli left with the chance to become a world champion at the very next race.
