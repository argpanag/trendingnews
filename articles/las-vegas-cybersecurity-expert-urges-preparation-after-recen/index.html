Las Vegas cybersecurity expert urges preparation after recent tech disruptions

LAS VEGAS (KSNV) — After a week of disruptions affecting email, air travel and banking, a Las Vegas cybersecurity expert said the incidents show how quickly daily life can depend on technology and how important it is to have a backup plan.

Dr. Dasha Davies, chief information security officer for Stealth ISS Group, a cybersecurity and consulting company, told News 3 that outages are not always caused by hackers. Some, she said, can stem from misconfigurations or human error.

“Not everything makes the news,” Davies said.

The disruptions that are known unfolded over several days. Microsoft services, including Outlook, experienced a global outage after a technical issue during an upgrade on Thursday. The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop after a technical problem affected air traffic communications and radar systems on Friday. Customers also reported banking problems on Monday.

Davies said the Microsoft outage showed how the loss of one service can affect other parts of daily business. If email and communication tools stop working, she said, companies may struggle to coordinate work or complete transactions.

“I think the next step, at least in the cyber world or IT world, is to have a business continuity disaster recovery plan,” Davies said. “Which means, how do we communicate if this goes down? Do we have an alternate email like Gmail? Do we have our contact list printed out, so if we cannot reach them online, do we have those available?"

Davies has worked on cybersecurity operations at two Olympic Games: Rio in 2016 and PyeongChang, South Korea, in 2018.

"We actually had our team there to install and implement and bring in all the monitoring, the incident response, and that was actually for the Olympic and broadcast channel,” Davies said about her experience in South Korea.

She recommended businesses plan how they will communicate and continue operating if key services fail. For individuals, she advises keeping some cash on hand and making sure essential contact information is available offline.

“Everybody now is used to using your credit card, using your ATM, but if communication fails, that's it,” Davies said. “I don't want to say you have to be completely off the grid and go back to paper. That's impossible, at least for most of us. But make sure all your critical information that you have, contact information, paperwork - you don't have it electronically. Print it out. Have it somewhere. So, it's almost like stepping back one or two steps from the technology"

Davies identified power, water, and communications as concerns for Southern Nevada. She said water systems rely on electricity to run pumps but did not point to a specific threat to local infrastructure. However, she suspected Las Vegas may be a target because of the Las Vegas Strip.

“I don't see why not,” Davies said. "If you shut an entire casino down, entire power and everything, or this whole city, guess how much money the casinos and the city and the county are going to lose here? It's a money game as well. Casinos are always a target. They have a lot of personal information. They have a lot of financial information, and you've got a lot of people there."

Her advice is to think ahead about what would be needed if a phone, internet connection, or power supply became unavailable.

“Make sure you got cash on you,” Davies said.
