Plant native trees at the Boone County fall event

TheTools · 26.09.2026 19:47 · source · permalink

Residents can help plant native trees and shrubs while taking part in family-friendly activities during the Boone County Conservation District's Fall Celebration at LIB Conservation Area in Belvidere.

The event runs from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. Sept. 26 at 5601 Newburg Road, according to a community announcement.

Participation is free, but registration is required through bccdil.org.

The event, formerly known as Pioneers in Conservation, will include volunteer tree planting, nature-themed crafts and treats from Edwards Apple Orchard. Organizers say the event is designed to connect residents with local natural areas while contributing to habitat restoration efforts.

"LIB is a beautiful area along the Kishwaukee River. We are excited to get people outside to enjoy this natural space right in Belvidere," said Mark Freedlund, director of education for the Boone County Conservation District. "It is even more special that participants have the opportunity to take care of the land right in their own community!"

Restoration project tied to regional tree initiative

The tree and shrub planting is part of a larger effort funded through a subgrant awarded by the Morton Arboretum's Chicago Region Trees Initiative at the beginning of 2025. The project is intended to improve Belvidere's urban tree canopy and is scheduled to continue through the beginning of 2028.

According to the announcement, the work will create an urban woodland along the Kishwaukee River with habitat for wildlife, including waterfowl, while increasing plant diversity. District officials say the project also is expected to improve ecological health, reduce flooding impacts and support groundwater recharge in an area that already includes walking paths and picnic facilities.

"We want to provide the community with opportunities to recreate in a high-quality, diverse, and native riparian woodland and enjoy the natural areas of Boone County," said Frank Rabbit, director of natural resources for the Boone County Conservation District. "Transforming an overgrown riparian habitat with little ecological value to an ecologically healthy woodland with native shrubs and trees allows us to do just that."

Funding for the project was provided through the Inflation Reduction Act by the USDA Forest Service Eastern Region and is administered by the Morton Arboretum's Chicago Region Trees Initiative, according to the announcement.

About the conservation district

The Boone County Conservation District was established in 1964 and was the first conservation district in Illinois. The district now manages more than 4,400 acres of prairies, wetlands and woodlands that support a wide variety of plant and animal species.

The district's mission is to preserve and manage natural areas and open spaces for ecological, educational and recreational benefits for present and future generations.
