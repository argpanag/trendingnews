Woman faces multiple charges after driving through school soccer field in Grand Island

TheTools · 23.10.2026 17:22 · source · permalink

GRAND ISLAND, Neb. (KSNB) - A woman is facing eight criminal charges after Grand Island police say she drove drunk with a child in her car and drove through a soccer field at Grand Island Senior High.

Court records show Shoni Bussinger, 28, Grand Island, is charged with DUI-2nd offense, felony child abuse, reckless driving, failure to stop after a property damage accident-2nd offense, unlawful operation of a motor vehicle with a person under age 16, two counts of refusing to submit to a breath test, obstruction.

Officers were called just after 7 p.m. Thursday to Grand Island Senior High practice soccer field at Custer and State Street. Police found the driver, 28-year-old Shoni Bussinger, driving erratically in a silver Acura.

Police said witnesses saw her drive through a fence on the soccer field. She then drove a large loop through the field and parking lot before taking off eastbound on State Street, according to a graphic provided by Grand Island police.

Juveniles on field during incident

Police say roughly 15 juvenile teens were on the field playing soccer at the time. No one was hurt, but officers estimate about $2,000 in property damage.

An 8-year-old boy riding in Bussinger’s car was taken into safe custody.

Bussinger is in the Hall County jail on $150,000 bond. Her preliminary hearing in Hall County Court is Nov. 17

School district statement

Grand Island Public Schools released a statement on the incident:

“Last night an incident took place where a driver drove onto the GISH practice field and collided with one of the fences on the grounds. We are grateful to report the event took place outside of school hours, so no school activities were present on the field at the time, and all patrons on-site remained safe and unharmed. After the collision took place, GIPD arrived swiftly on the scene to handle the situation. We are thankful to GIPD for their continued partnership in keeping our community safe.”
