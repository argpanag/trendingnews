Robson Green, stunning Northumberland and murder – what more could you want?

New BBC daytime drama The Northumbria Mysteries is witty, diverting and brings bags of charm

4/5

The Northumbria Mysteries (BBC One) is a reminder of a better time. And that time was 1995, when Robson Green was the nation’s heartthrob. He won us over in Soldier Soldier, then hogged the charts with Unchained Melody as one half of Robson and Jerome. More than 30 years later, he’s livening up this daytime cosy-crime series with that same Jack-the-lad charm.

Of course, Green has done plenty of good work in between – Wire in the Blood, Grantchester, presenting shows about fishing. But The Northumbria Mysteries leans heavily into the persona that made him famous. And that, along with Green’s comic timing, makes this a cut above the usual daytime fare.

Each episode is a whodunnit, but the crimes are neither here nor there (there is no connection to the detective novels of LJ Ross, which are set in the same area). Sit back and enjoy the odd-couple dynamic at the heart of the show between Green and his partner in crime-solving.

Green plays Joe Ruby, a gambler and reformed ex-convict in a leather jacket, whose favoured lunch is three Greggs sausage rolls washed down with a can of fizzy pop. Ruby volunteers at a priory as part of a rehabilitation scheme under the wise and watchful eye of Father David (a lovely role for Simon Callow). They have serious chats about faith and forgiveness, because Ruby is a thoughtful soul with regrets about his past.

A murder in the grounds brings him into contact with DI Rose O’Connell (Liz White), who arrives in an evening gown straight from the opera. She’s Oxford-educated and very capable – gorgeous too – and instantly dismisses Ruby as a “Saturday night kebab shop brawler”. He replies that, while he’s in the kebab shop, O’Connell is probably spending her Saturday nights watching reality TV alone while nursing a glass of wine and wondering why no one appreciates her – and by the look on her face, you know he’s spot on.

And so they argue, banter and flirt their way through the series, solving crimes through a mix of her cool intelligence and his unorthodox methods – stealing a pushbike, punching someone in the face, that sort of thing. Green has said he took inspiration from the 1980s series Big Deal, in which Ray Brooks played a small-time gambler. O’Connell is less-than-ably assisted by DC Raj Singh (Gurjeet Singh), who thinks he’s far cleverer than he is, and is funny because he takes himself seriously.

The theme tune is reminiscent of Happy Valley, but there’s no darkness here, even if every episode begins with the discovery of a dead body. The Northumberland locations, rural and coastal, are stunning: Bamburgh, Rothbury, Alnwick, Brinkburn Priory, Dunstanburgh Castle, Budle Bay. We don’t actually see enough of them, which seems a bit of a wasted opportunity. Then again, it’s my favourite place in Britain. I’m off on my annual holiday there next week, and I’m more than happy for the place to remain under the radar.

The Northumbria Mysteries is on BBC iPlayer
