Qatar has proposed a seven-day peace plan to end the conflict.

TheTools · 28.10.2026 18:11 · source · permalink

A source familiar with the matter said Qatar has submitted a draft of a new peace agreement with a seven-day roadmap to end the conflict to Iran, and Tehran is expected to respond before the end of this week.

According to the Vietnam News Agency correspondent in the Middle East, Qatar has exchanged a new peace proposal with Iran , outlining a seven-day roadmap to end the conflict between Iran and the US and Israel, amidst intensified diplomatic efforts to prevent the risk of renewed hostilities in the Middle East.

A source familiar with the matter said Qatar has sent a draft agreement to Iran, and Tehran is expected to respond before the end of this week.

The proposal outlines a seven-day roadmap to address the concerns of Iran and the U.S., in which both sides would implement confidence-building measures daily. The process could begin early next week, with the participation of several countries acting as guarantors of the agreement's implementation.

The initiative was launched following a meeting on October 26 in Tehran between senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Türkiye, and Pakistan and Iranian leaders, including President Masoud Pezeshkian.

The meeting, initiated by Pakistan, aimed to prevent an escalation of the conflict but did not discuss Iran's nuclear program.

Tehran had previously stated that it would only resume nuclear negotiations after conditions for ending the conflict were met, particularly the requirement for the US to lift its blockade of Iranian seaports and sanctions on Iranian oil exports.

The new diplomatic effort comes as US President Donald Trump continued to warn on October 23 of the possibility of attacking Iran, while Tehran is believed to have prepared retaliatory options if the US and Israel resume military activity.

Qatar and Pakistan had previously acted as mediators in helping the parties reach a temporary peace agreement in June, but that agreement quickly collapsed. The prospects of the new initiative therefore remain dependent on Iran's response and the ability of the parties to reach a consensus on the conditions for ending the conflict.

Meanwhile, according to the New Arab newspaper, Qatar's Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani met with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian in Moscow on October 27 to discuss regional tensions, ongoing negotiations, and the Strait of Hormuz issue, as Doha reaffirmed its commitment to preventing a recurrence of conflict in the region.

The talks took place against the backdrop of the Qatari Foreign Ministry stating that mediation efforts to end regional tensions and reach a lasting agreement are continuing in coordination with Pakistan and other regional and international partners.

Ibrahim al-Hashimi, Director of the Communications and Press Department of the Qatari Ministry of Foreign Affairs, said at a regular press briefing in Doha that diplomatic efforts are progressing in a positive direction.

According to him, the reconciliation process is being conducted in coordination with Pakistan and other partners, with the goal of reaching a lasting agreement to definitively end the regional crisis.
