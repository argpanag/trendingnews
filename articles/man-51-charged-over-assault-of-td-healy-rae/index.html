Man, 51, charged over assault of TD Healy-Rae

TheTools · 18.09.2026 05:41 · source · permalink

A man has appeared in court charged with assaulting Kerry TD Michael Healy-Rae.

Anthony Foley, aged 51, of Rosary Road in Dublin 8, was charged with assault causing harm on Cork Street in Dublin on Wednesday.

Garda Paul Nolan of the Serious Crime Unit gave evidence of his arrest, charge, and caution, saying that Mr Foley made no reply when the charge was put to him.

He said that Mr Healy-Rae had been driving on Cork Street when he was approached by a person on his driver's side window before he was struck in the face.

He said that the Independent TD suffered a laceration to his upper lip which required seven stitches, and that he requires further assessment from a dentist.

He said that the incident was caught on CCTV.

Garda Nolan said that the accused was observed the next day walking his dog in the same area and gardaí arrested him.

He said that when arrested, Mr Foley possessed clothes similar to that of the man seen in the CCTV, and that the dog harness also matched that of the one in the footage.

He objected to bail citing the seriousness of the offence, and also said he believed there was a political motive to the incident.

He claimed that audio from the CCTV footage recorded the man shouting "'ah bucko, you’re in the wrong area, get the f**k out of here you f***ing snake".

Representing Mr Foley, solicitor Richard Young said his client denied to gardaí that it was him in the CCTV.

He said that when Mr Foley was arrested, he was asked if he had any political affiliation to which he said his client did not know why he was being asked that.

He said it was Mr Foley's contention that he did not own a jacket similar to that seen on the person in the footage, and that he contended that the dog seen could be a similar one from the area.

He said Mr Foley had bought the harness locally and others in the area could also have the same one.

He said he did not understand the gardaí's objection to bail, given that "at the height of it" the case was one of identification.

He said, although that is ultimately where the case is hinged, there had been no identification parade, and that his client was "engaged" in relation to the case.

He said there is an "incredible danger in relation to preliminary detention in such a weak case, which this case is".

He said Mr Foley is willing to sign on at his local garda station and pay a sum between €500-€1,000.

Solicitor Mr Young added: "What we have is CCTV, we’ve no formal identification, we’ve no identification in terms of a member of An Garda Síochána viewing the CCTV and saying ‘I recognise this person to be X'.

He said the arrest was only made on the basis he was seen walking his dog in the area the next day.

The court was shown CCTV of the incident, however there was no audio.

The hearing then took a break as Judge Karen Dowling considered what was put before her.

Richard Young said it is "quite clear" in the footage that it is not his client in the CCTV.

He argued that the court should not detain Mr Foley pre-trial given the correctness of the identification is being challenged.

He said that Mr Foley was willing to provide an address, sign on, and give an assurity.

Garda Nolan said the State’s case carries "far more weight" than what Mr Young had implied.

Judge Karen Dowling said that the facts of the case are for a later date and would not be dealt with today.

She said the court had "listened carefully" to submissions made on behalf of Mr Foley, as well as the objections of gardaí.

She refused bail, and Mr Foley was remanded in custody to Cloverhill Prison.

He is due to appear before the court again next Tuesday.
