LIHUE, Kauai (KHON2) -- Kauai businesses are still in recovery mode, and some said consumers may notice changes.

"We had to close the visitor center right off the bat," said Mark Bogner, Kauai Coffee general manager. "Thankfully it's open now, but it's only accessible from the Eleele side of the highway. No longer can folks come down from the Kalaheo side."

"I'll just say it, an easy like six months, it could be a good six months, just disturbance, irregularity, you know out there from a consumer's perspective," said Sierra-Lynn Stone of SBH Taro Farms LLC.

Stone said the farm, which is based in Hanalei, has been one of many across Kauai's North Shore without power since the storm hit, and that their structure saw significant damage.

"There's a lot of structural damage, buildings, whether they were damaged and/or gone, roofs missing, miscellaneous things all over the areas," said Stone. "Of course, the debris. So when the leaves, the trees, things of that nature, things were just everywhere. It was like just a mess everywhere."

Customers of the Koloa Rum Company may have also noticed a change.

Damage at the company stores and the tasting room in Lihue has kept both areas closed for a bit.

"Ideally we would like to target probably somewhere around Oct. 12 tentatively," said Robert Ramer, Koloa Rum Company COO. "We are not the only business there. There's obviously other factors outside of just us there."

Ramer explained they're working with other areas within their proximity to figure out an exact date they can reopen.

But as they recover, businesses across Kauai are looking at any bright side they can.

"The team at this point where we're at, we're as functional as what we can be for where we're at," said Bogner

"Overall, Kalo is resilient, and I think for most farmers we do find our way with fertilizing and our methods to implement where we can," said Stone. "Do what we can so that it ensures a better crop, or at least the best that we can in this circumstance."

"We are running a business, I'm not going to deny that," said Ramer. "But that comes secondary to this, right? We need the island to be in a good spot. We're trying to take care of our neighbors, do the right thing."
