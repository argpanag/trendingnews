Investing.com - Canada's main stock index closed higher on Friday and notched a weekly advance, recovering from an early decline as a rebound in global technology shares helped improve investor sentiment following a bruising selloff in the previous session.

The S&P/TSX Composite Index climbed 0.5% to end at 35,789.09 points, a day after the benchmark tumbled 2% for its steepest daily decline in five months. The gauge also gained 0.5% for the week.

The technology-led recovery helped Canadian equities reverse early losses, although domestic economic weakness and uncertainty surrounding AI spending continued to cloud the outlook.

Seperately, Statistics Canada reported that the unemployment rate rose to 6.7% in October from 6.5% in September, exceeding economists' expectations of 6.5%. The economy shed 4,700 jobs during the month, with private-sector and full-time employment bearing the brunt of the losses.

The latest figures brought Canada's cumulative employment losses this year to approximately 46,000 jobs, highlighting persistent weakness in the labour market amid economic uncertainty and trade tensions with the United States.

Thursday's selloff was particularly pronounced in technology shares, with the TSX technology sector tumbling 3.8%. Celestica was among the notable decliners as concerns surrounding AI-related spending and valuations intensified.

The energy sector also fell 2.5% as oil prices remained volatile amid uncertainty surrounding the Iran conflict and the security of tanker movements through the Strait of Hormuz.

Brent crude futures were last down 0.4% to $97.92 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures gained 0.5% to $90.98. Both benchmarks, however, remained on track for substantial weekly declines.

U.S. rebounds

The NASDAQ Composite surged on Friday, helping Wall Street largely rebound a day after the artificial intelligence trade suffered a historic rout sparked by worries over the implications of a Democratic midterms victory on the development of the nascent technology.

Market participants also received a blowout October nonfarm payrolls report, with job growth smashing expectations and levels for prior months being revised up. Stock futures had shown little reaction to the data, but U.S. Treasury yields had spiked.

The tech-heavy NASDAQ settled more than 1% higher. The advance came as a rout in U.S. stocks steadied, with dip buyers offering a respite to chip stocks following a broad-based equity-market rout sparked by worries over the implications of a Democratic midterms victory on the artificial intelligence boom.
