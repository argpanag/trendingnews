Two Merchant Ships Attacked by Sea and Aerial Drones in Bulgaria’s Exclusive Economic Zone, Prime Minister Says

TheTools · 06.10.2026 15:01 · source · permalink

Bulgaria is conducting a rescue operation some 70 nautical miles east of the coastal town of Byala, in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone in the Black Sea, Prime Minister Rumen Radev said here on Tuesday. Two merchant ships were attacked by sea and aerial drones at around 3:00 a.m., he said. Both ships sustained damage and one has sunk. 

“This is an absolutely unacceptable attack in Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone. It is an absolutely blatant violation of international law and maritime law,” Radev said. 

Approached by reporters later in the day, Radev said it is not clear where the drones came from and the Bulgarian authorities will try to establish that, "but they were clearly either Russian or Ukrainian".

The first ship, sailing under the Togolese flag, has sank. The second vessel caught fire after being struck, but its crew was evacuated and rescued by a Bulgarian vessel, Radev said.

According to the Defence Ministry Information Centre, it was Palau-flagged. 

According to the information available to the authorities, the second ship was carrying wheat and was sailing south, while the first ship, which sank, was sailing north. The cargo of the first vessel was not known, Radev said. 

The authorities are also monitoring for possible oil spills and environmental pollution. “As a state responsible within our exclusive economic zone for the health and lives of anyone navigating in this area, regardless of who is responsible, we are obliged to conduct emergency search and rescue operations,” Radev said.

The operation is still under way, with rescuers searching for survivors from the first vessel, the Prime Minister said. 

At the time of the attack, the Bulgarian Ro-Ro vessel DIOSCURIA was in the area. Its crew saw the explosion involving the first ship and headed to the site. They found an overturned boat, three overturned rafts and life jackets, but no people. The crew of the sunken vessel remains unaccounted for, Radev said. 

The DIOSCURIA also approached the second vessel and assisted in the rescue operation. A fast craft from the Bulgarian Navy was dispatched and managed to take two injured crew members aboard. 

One of the injured men had suffered burns and the other had a head injury. They were taken aboard the naval vessel and were expected to arrive at the port of Varna, where medical teams from the Naval Hospital in Varna had been mobilized to provide the necessary medical assistance. 

The remaining 16 crew members were being transported by a border police patrol boat. According to the information provided by Radev, 11 of the crew members are Turkish nationals and seven are Indian nationals.

The conditions for conducting search and rescue operations in the area are favourable, with waves at one to two degrees Douglas and visibility of more than 10 nautical miles, Radev said. He assured that every effort would be made to search the entire area and, if possible, rescue the crew of the first ship. 

According to preliminary information, released by the Bulgarian Defence Ministry's Armymedia.bg information centre, the targets of the attacks were two general cargo ships – the Togolese-flagged ALFA WATAN (IMO 7510884) and the Palau-flagged ABLE (IMO 9001136).

As a result of the attack, ALFA WATAN sustained critical structural damage and sank east of the Bulgarian coast. There is currently no official information on the fate of its crew or whether there are any survivors.

The second vessel, the motor ship ABLE, caught fire following the attack. The critical situation forced all 18 crew members to abandon the vessel.

A key role in the rescue operation was played by the Bulgarian-flagged RO-RO/passenger ferry DIOSCURIA (IMO 7931997). The vessel was urgently diverted to the scene of the incident to provide assistance. Its crew successfully evacuated all the distressed sailors from the burning ABLE and brought them aboard, Armymedia.bg said. 

Radev also commented on another vessel that was struck in August and has been drifting without a crew after its steering system failed. The vessel was overflown by the Bulgarian Air Force. “We are monitoring this vessel to make sure it does not enter Bulgaria’s territorial waters. If it continues in any way towards our coast, we are fully prepared to act,” Radev said.

He assured that the Bulgarian coast would under no circumstances be allowed to be endangered by drifting vessels. 

The Bulgarian Prime Minister warned that the war has been expanding geographically and increasing the risk of a global conflict, adding that the escalation has now reached attacks on civilian commercial vessels.

“What is happening is seriously disrupting shipping in the Black Sea, driving up insurance costs even further and making shipping extremely difficult,” Radev said.

Radev spoke at a meeting he convened with representatives of state institutions in connection with the attack on the two ships. The meeting was attended by representatives of the Defence Ministry, Interior Ministry, Transport Ministry, Ministry of Environment and Water, the President’s institution and the security services. 

As he spoke to the press later on Tuesday, he said that during a videoconference with members of the EU Council, which focused on competitiveness and energy, he briefed the session on the drone attack, which he described as "deeply concerning".

Asked by reporters if he believed the Consultative Council for National Security must meet over the drone attack - which is what the opposition are calling for - he said that it is for President Iliana Iotova to decide. His view, however, is that there is a risk for the Consultative Council to turn into a platform where the drone attack situation will be misused. "When incompetent people take advantage of such incidents, we hear all kinds of stupid theories," he said.
