Why Japan wants the new world map changed

TheTools · 10.09.2026 12:55 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Geography

Topic:Geography

The Island of Kunashir, is part of the disputed islands known as the Southern Kuriles in Russia and the Northern Territories in Japan. (Reuters: Yuri Maltsev)

In short:

Japan wants changes made to a new world map adopted by the United Nations last week.

The map shows the disputed Kuril islands, known in Japan as the Northern Territories, in the same colour as Russia.

What's next?

Japan has lodged a protest with the map site's operator to have the depiction 'corrected'.

Japan wants changes made to a new world map adopted by the United Nations because the disputed Kuril islands appear to belong to Russia.

The UN General Assembly voted last Friday to adopt the new map, which makes the African continent larger while reducing the size of North America and Europe.

Japan was among the 164 UN members that supported the change, with the United States being the only country to vote against it.

But the Asian nation has now taken issue with the map because it shows the Kuril islands in the same colour as Russia.

The disputed islands — known as the Northern Territories in Japan and as the Southern Kuriles in Russia — were seized in 1945 by the former Soviet Union, which then expelled the Japanese population.

While Russia controls the four islands land, both countries claim sovereignty over them.

Japan says the disputed Kuril islands are marked in the same colour as Russia. (Equal Earth/Tom Patterson)

Japanese government spokesman Minoru Kihara said the new map — known as the Equal Earth cartographic projection — "contained a depiction contradicting the Japanese government's position".

"We lodged a protest with the map site's operator through our diplomatic channel to demand the correction of this depiction," Mr Kihara told reporters.

He declined to say when the issue was brought to the government's attention but said Japan needed "to prevent misconceptions of our territories and geographical names from spreading".

Relations between Japan and Russia have also soured recently when President Vladimir Putin made a surprise visit to the disputed islands for the first time.

The disputed islands are located in an archipelago between Russia and Japan.  (ABC NEWS)

Japan then summoned Moscow's ambassador, with Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi saying Mr Putin's visit "offends the sentiments of the Japanese people".

She called the visit "absolutely unacceptable" with the islands being "an inherent part of Japan's territory, both historically and under international law".

Moscow hit back, saying it "categorically" rejected such statements, "which we consider politically inappropriate, insulting and, of course, legally unfounded".

The UN said the new map aimed to present "a more accurate representation" of continental landmasses than was portrayed by more traditional Mercator projections.
