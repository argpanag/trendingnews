QUICK TAKE | Jasprit Bumrah Sends Tom Latham Into Sehwag’s Unwanted Club. Seven Things to Know About India-New Zealand Test

TheTools · 19.11.2026 03:05 · source · permalink

New Zealand’s captain fell to Jasprit Bumrah without scoring on the opening morning of the first Test against India at Basin Reserve on November 19. It was his 15th duck while opening in Tests, putting him alongside Sehwag on the list.

West Indies’ Kraigg Brathwaite leads with 19, followed by England’s Mike Atherton with 17 and Sri Lanka’s Marvan Atapattu with 16. Latham and Sehwag share fourth place on 15. The tally counts innings played in the opening positions.

He faced two deliveries. A golden duck means being dismissed off the first ball faced. Both count as ducks in the record books because the batter’s score is zero, regardless of how many deliveries he survives.

It covers his innings as an opener. Ducks recorded while batting elsewhere are counted separately. That distinction matters when comparing players who moved up or down the order during their careers. Sehwag, for instance, began his Test career in the middle order.

Chris Gayle, Matthew Hayden and Andrew Strauss each finished with 14 ducks as openers, as did India’s Pankaj Roy. Their presence shows how frequently the new ball can catch even established opening batters before they get going.

The number needs context. Roy’s 14 came in 74 innings as an opener, while Hayden’s came in 184. Career length, scoring average and the proportion of innings ending in zero all help explain what a raw tally leaves out.

Shubman Gill expected more help from the Wellington surface early on. India picked Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj alongside Bumrah, with Washington Sundar as the spin-bowling all-rounder. Latham’s early departure gave India an immediate return on that decision.
