McFeely: 'Minnesota stinks' a losing message again for GOP

TheTools · 21.11.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

MOORHEAD — If Minnesota Republicans learned their lesson from the historic humiliation they suffered on election day, there’s no indication of it. News reports suggest they’re still missing the point, which explains why it’s been 20 years since they’ve won a statewide election.

It might be 20 more.

ADVERTISEMENT

The MNGOP seems dumbfounded that its nine-month messaging blitz of “fraud, fraud, fraud” didn’t land while masked federal thugs murdering Minnesota citizens on the streets of Minneapolis mattered far more.

Turns out "Minnesota sucks" is not good messaging.

This is the problem with a political party that dumps on the state’s two largest cities, hopes they fail, cheers a federal invasion and gears its election strategy toward appealing to some schmuck in Traverse County (population 3,000) flying an old Minnesota state flag to show the Dumbocrats who’s boss rather than trying to win votes in Hennepin County (population 1.3 million).

Republican math skills stink. Minnesota’s population is 5.8 million, 3.5 million of whom live in the Twin Cities and the suburbs. Yet in a tale as old as time, the GOP unapologetically does everything it can to alienate the 3.5 million. You can’t win in Minnesota without Hennepin and Ramsey counties, yet the GOP has decided it will try. Again and again and again.

They paid the price. Zero wins in statewide races, wipeouts in both houses of the legislature.

It’s almost as if Republicans would rather own the libs than actually win elections.

Democrats get hammered for not appealing to rural voters, yet Republicans shrug off being grossly unappealing to urban and suburban voters. They give a 40-year-old couple with two kids in Maple Grove no reason to vote Republican. And they don’t care.

ADVERTISEMENT

They fixate on guns over school safety, the state flag, phantom transgender surgeries, even supporting mining near the Boundary Waters (Twin Cities residents favor protecting the wilderness) – Republicans whiff on every issue that matters in the state’s most populous area.

The most stunning quote from a media report came from state Republican Party chairman Alex Plechash, who said the Iran war mattered more to voters than he anticipated. Really? The cost of everything is through the roof, in part because of President Trump’s wholly unpopular war, and the state party chairman was unaware?

Political malpractice.

Such is the cost of living in an online-centric fever swamp, where Facebook comments drive policy. It’s clear Republicans live online and can’t identify “real” issues voters care about anymore.

Yes, the state flag is kind of ugly. But the price of gasoline and beef is what voters of every political stripe and demographic are griping about. If you can’t identify that, you’re not living in the real world.
