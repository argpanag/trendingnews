Wales' highest-rated chef hits back after hygiene rating controversy

TheTools · 30.10.2026 11:39 · source · permalink

The head chef and co-owner of a top Welsh restaurant which earlier this year received a food hygiene rating score of one has defended his team and said his kitchen is spotless.

Gareth Ward, head chef at Ynyshir Restaurant and Rooms near Machynlleth, saw the restaurant receive a food hygiene rating of one in January, indicating "major improvement necessary". A re-inspection was then carried out in February and Ynyshir was given a five out of five rating.

Now, appearing on Apple TV's documentary series Knife Edge: Chasing Michelin Stars, he has said his team "go above and beyond everything to make what we do safe". "There's nothing in there that's amateurish. I run a spotless kitchen," he said.

"Whether you're a cafe, or a pub, or a two-Michelin-star restaurant. We've all got the same rules. But we go above and beyond everything to make what we do safe.

"I'm very proud of my whole team and my business and what we've achieved. It's obviously massive in the media, which anyone instantly jumps on and thinks it's about cleanliness. When something like this happens you're a bit blown away."

The restaurant made history in 2022 by becoming the first in Wales to earn two Michelin stars. Prized for its 30-course dinner experience which takes up to four or five hours to enjoy, and costs from £468 per head, the eatery was recently praised by Saturday Morning presenter James Martin who called it the best restaurant “anywhere in the world”.

However, following an inspection on November 5 last year the Food Standards Agency awarded the establishment a food hygiene score of just one out of five.

In January a spokesman for Ynyshir told WalesOnline the venue took the outcome "seriously" and explained some of its "specialised" cooking methods "differ from conventional kitchen operations". Shortly afterwards head chef Gareth Ward insisted he was "not embarrassed" and that the “place is immaculate”.
