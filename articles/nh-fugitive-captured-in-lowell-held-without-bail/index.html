NH fugitive captured in Lowell, held without bail

LOWELL — A New Hampshire fugitive considered “armed and dangerous” whose disappearance prompted a manhunt and temporary shelter-in-place order in Salem, New Hampshire, was captured in Lowell on Wednesday.

Daquhan Cromer, 29, of Lawrence, had been on the run for months after allegedly escaping from a New Hampshire transitional living facility. Hours after his capture on Bowden Street, he was arraigned in Lowell District Court on a fugitive-from-justice charge and two pending Lowell cases from August for which warrants had been issued.

Those cases stem from allegations that Cromer threw a brick through a Centralville apartment window while two people were lying down inside and later led police on a pursuit through Lowell and Dracut, according to Lowell Police incident report.

Cromer was taken into custody at 134 Bowden St. by members of the Lowell Police Department’s Warrant Unit, State Police, the U.S. Marshals Service, and the Methuen Police.

According to a press release from the Salem Police Department, Cromer had been sought on an escape warrant from New Hampshire in connection with multiple felonies in their town. His disappearance prompted an extensive search on Tuesday night that led authorities to issue a temporary shelter-in-place order before determining he was no longer in the area.

The Lowell Police charged Cromer as a fugitive from justice after confirming New Hampshire authorities were seeking his return. The Middlesex District Attorney’s Office said in Lowell District Court that New Hampshire intends to extradite Cromer, but prosecutors plan to resolve the pending Lowell cases first.

During Wednesday’s arraignment, the Middlesex District Attorney’s Office said Cromer has been wanted since December after escaping from a New Hampshire transitional living facility. His attorney, Daniel Murphy, stressed that Cromer allegedly escaped from a halfway house, not a correctional facility, as reported by a few news outlets.

Prosecutors also outlined Cromer’s criminal history, which they said includes periods of incarceration in Essex County as well as convictions for drug distribution and possession of a firearm without a license.

One of the Lowell cases stems from an Aug. 20 incident at a Willard Street apartment.

According to a Lowell Police incident report, a woman told investigators Cromer began sending her threatening text messages after she refused to purchase drugs from him. The woman, who told police she previously bought drugs from Cromer and knew him well, reported that he threatened to beat her up, shoot a friend and kick in her door.

The report states the woman “became increasingly frightened as she has seen Cromer with a gun on several occasions.”

Shortly after 4 a.m., the woman and her boyfriend were lying in bed when a brick crashed through a bedroom window, shattering glass throughout the room. Police said the brick landed beyond the bed after narrowly missing the couple.

The two victims claimed, police said, that they did not actually see Cromer commit the crime, but identified him by his voice and were “100% certain it was him.”

According to police, Cromer was also familiar with the apartment and knew the bedroom window was located directly beside the bed.

Based on the circumstances, police sought charges of two counts of assault with a dangerous weapon, threats to commit a crime and malicious destruction of property. A warrant was later issued for his arrest.

The second Lowell case arose the following night when police conducting surveillance related to the Willard Street incident spotted Cromer behind the wheel of a silver Toyota Camry near Bridge Street.

Police said Cromer was known to have active warrants and a suspended driver’s license. After officers activated their emergency lights and siren in an attempt to stop the vehicle, police allege Cromer accelerated away at speeds exceeding 60 mph.

Cromer allegedly drove into oncoming traffic, ran a red light in Dracut and nearly collided with several vehicles before officers terminated the pursuit out of concern for public safety.

Police later located the vehicle abandoned near a wooded area in Dracut. Police believe Cromer fled on foot before they arrived.

At the recommendation of the Middlesex District Attorney’s Office, Judge Mary Phillips ordered Cromer held without bail pending a dangerousness hearing scheduled for Monday in the assault case.

Cromer was also ordered held without bail on the fugitive-from-justice complaint and is scheduled to return to court on Monday for a status hearing. In the motor vehicle case, he was held on $2,500 cash bail and is scheduled to appear on Monday for a pretrial hearing.

According to a Lowell Police incident report, Cromer is also facing charges out of Lawrence District Court on charges of larceny, assault and battery with a dangerous weapon, assault and battery on a family or household member, and destruction of property.

The extradition warrant from Concord, New Hampshire, stems from charges of aggravated assault and escape.
