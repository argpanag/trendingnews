Your pre-checkout checklist for Black Friday sales

TheTools · 25.11.2026 18:55 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Scams and Fraud

Topic:Scams and Fraud

Black Friday sales promise shoppers big bargains, but experts say you shouldn't rush into a purchase.  (ABC News: Anna Chisholm)

Millions of Australians will be hunting for a bargain in the coming days as the Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales period reaches full swing.

But sneaky marketing tactics could lead shoppers to think they are getting bigger savings than they are.

"Consumers should be aware that a 'sale' sign doesn't always mean it's a good deal," ACCC deputy chair Catriona Lowe said.

Here's what to look out for.

When is Black Friday?

This year, Black Friday falls on November 27.

We'll also get Cyber Monday on November 30.

How can I make sure I'm getting the best price?

The best way to avoid getting duped by misleading sale ads is to focus on the end price of a product, rather than the discount, the ACCC says.

That means you should check for any exclusions or conditions and compare prices between retailers and over time where possible.

But if you start your shopping with a plan, you're more likely to ensure you're getting the best deal.

Financial comparison site Canstar recommends you:

  • Start with a list of what you actually need and a firm budget.
  • Compare prices with other known retailers.
  • Sleep on anything over $100.

Some shops offer storewide discounts, but you should check the fine print before being lured into a hasty purchase.  (AAP: Con Chronis)

Red flags to watch for from new sites

Throughout the year you might buy from trusted brands, but the bargain hunt might lead you to websites you haven't used before.

And some of those websites could be dodgy.

Asha Rao, a cybersecurity professor at RMIT, says to be wary if:

  • The offer is a much lower price than other stores
  • You have never heard of that shop before
  • The website is appearing as a sponsored result when doing a search

Four essential steps

Let's say you've been holding out on buying an appliance until the Black Friday sales hit.

You might be tempted to go with the first cheap offer you see, but Professor Rao says you should run through this checklist before checking out.

Compare with other sites

It's important to shop around.

"Make a note of their price — take a screenshot so you know exactly what the specifications of the product are," Professor Rao says.

"Check other stores, and see if you can find the same item, noting the specifications, as prices can vary when the specifications are not exactly the same."

Pay attention to specifications

Don't just go by looks, because sometimes two products can look the same but be slightly different.

"Often stores bring in products only for these sales, making it much harder to do comparisons," Professor Rao says.

"The product on sale may not have all the features you are looking for."

Look out for slight variations in the specific product name or the model number — an older iteration of the item might have a lower model number than the newer one you're looking for.

Check the reviews

Professor Rao says the next step is to check for independent reviews of that product.

"Google often has such reviews — check if the responses are mostly positive, and [ask] 'what do the negative reviews complain about?'" she says.

"Check other product review websites, and if available, forums, such as the Whirlpool forum, or Reddit.

"[On forums] take note of not only the query from the poster, but the answers and what they say about the product.

"These will probably not give you a full picture per se, but should help you make a decision."

Be smart with your payment method

Professor Rao suggests paying with a credit card with a smaller limit, just in case.

You could also use a debit card for an account you only transfer just enough money into.

There's also the option of using pay gateways instead of putting in your card details.

What misleading marketing tactics can I look out for?

The ACCC says it is cracking down on misleading practices and will be keeping a close eye on retailers during the sales period.

Below are some of the potentially misleading marketing practices on its radar.

While the ACCC says these practices aren't illegal, their basis needs to be genuine.

For example, retailers can't claim a sale will end at midnight and then extend it when the deadline ends.

Countdown timers

If you're an avid online shopper, chances are that at least once, a website home page has greeted you with a clock ticking down the seconds until a sale ends.

Canstar says the "age-old trick" is designed to rush you to make your purchase without stopping to compare prices.

Inaccurate countdown clocks may create a false sense of urgency in consumers, the ACCC says. (Australians Competition and Consumer Commission)

Scarcity

This tactic exploits the same psychology as a countdown timer.

A retailer might tell you there are only two items left in your size, or that dozens of other shoppers are currently viewing the same item.

'Sitewide' sale claims and the fine print

Stores often advertise "site-wide" or "storewide" discounts during sales periods.

But watch out for instances where a large portion of stock is actually excluded from the sale.

The same goes for the "fine print".

On store home pages, you're likely to find plenty of banners with phrasing along the lines of "up to 50 per cent off".

It sounds like a great deal, but Canstar says in reality, it's often that only a handful of items are discounted that heavily.

The ACCC says it's not OK for retailers to try to tuck away that wording in order to make their discounts seem broader.

If you look really closely, you can just the words "up to" written in tiny writing in the top of the number six in this website banner.  (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission)

Misleading reference prices

Some stores might compare their sale price to what's called a reference price to lure shoppers into thinking they're getting a bargain.

The ACCC warns some dodgy dealers might use reference prices to mislead shoppers when those items were never, or not recently, sold at that price.

How do you report a scammer?

People are encouraged to report scams to the National Anti-Scam Centre's Scamwatch website, regardless of whether they've lost money or not.

You can report cybercrimes to police through the Australian Cyber Security Centre's online reporting portal.

If you're concerned you're a victim of identity theft, you can contact IDCARE, a not-for-profit charity that describes itself as Australia's national identity and cyber support service.
