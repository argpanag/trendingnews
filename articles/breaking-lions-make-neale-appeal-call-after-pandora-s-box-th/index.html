BREAKING: Lions make Neale appeal call after ‘Pandora’s Box’ threat, nuclear option

TheTools · 23.09.2026 05:45 · source · permalink

Brisbane will challenge the tribunal’s decision to uphold Lachie Neale’s grand final suspension.

Neale’s bid to play in the 2026 AFL Grand Final is set to go down to the wire, with Brisbane set to launch a last-ditch appeal of the superstar’s striking ban at the AFL Appeal Board on Thursday.

The hearing will take place at 4pm.

After almost five hours Neale’s arguments that he didn’t strike Jack Ginnivan; that if he did strike Jack Ginnivan it wasn’t medium impact; and that if he did strike with medium impact he should be cleared due to his 320 games without suspension, were all shut down by the Tribunal.

Brisbane has four possible grounds at the AFL Appeals Board:

1. An error of law that has a material impact on the decision of the Tribunal;

2. That the decision was so unreasonable that no Tribunal acting reasonably could have come to that decision having regard to the evidence before it;

3. The classification of the offence was manifestly excessive or inadequate, or;

4. The sanction imposed was manifestly excessive or inadequate.

The arguments made in the case themselves cannot be re-litigated at the Appeals Board; the case must be won on one of the four grounds above.

The hearing is likely to be held on Thursday evening, and if that fails, the Lions could follow the path of Sydney who days before 1996 Grand Final obtained a Supreme Court injunction allowing Andrew Dunkley to play.

The Appeals Board was created in the late 90s in response to both that incident, and to Greg Williams fighting an umpire interference charge through the Supreme Court a year later.

The Herald Sun’s Jon Ralph believes Brisbane will appeal, claiming that Neale’s record should have had his one-game ban thrown out.

“I think that this is one that they would appeal on Thursday,” Ralph said on Fox Footy’s Midweek Tackle.

“I think by any measure, he has an exemplary record … This is the trial-by-video age where not one tackle has slipped a little bit too long, not one high bump has slipped from the shoulder to the head across 15 seasons.

“So, I would suggest that if Gary Ablett and Scott Pendlebury were able to access this discount — I understand it’s a strike, rather than a bump or a push — but I think he should have accessed that discount.

“For me, it’s the wrong decision … they would be able to go to that Appeals Board and say ‘it’s manifestly excessive’.”

Fox Footy commentator Corbin Middlemas added: “I’m sure the differentiator with those two to this one would be the fact that it was off the ball, as opposed to those two that were around the field of play where the football was.

“I wonder how much that has counted against Lachie tonight.”

Earlier, Ralph explained why the Tribunal took such a time to come to its decision.

“Because we’ve had the Jason Johnson situation — a Tribunal member who was in his car — that Zak Butters incident was quickly overturned on appeal,” he said.

“Then a couple of years back, Patrick Cripps effectively won the Brownlow Medal on appeal, after exceptional legal work.

“And so they are all the things that Jeff Gleeson (Tribunal chair) is trying to make sure that if this does go to appeal … (they) make sure it’s not thrown out on a technicality, it’s only thrown out on an error of law.”

Speaking on SEN on Wednesday morning, Bombers great Tim Watson thinks there should be a different criteria for acts that could cause players to miss a grand final in strong support of Neale.

“A grand final penalty for an action like that is extremely harsh,” Watson said.

“Does that penalty fit the crime? I say it doesn’t. Because I would put a higher level of importance upon a grand final and a threshold should be raised as a consequence for these actions.

“What Lachie Neale did does not justify him missing a grand final. You could downgrade that penalty to a suspended one-match penalty.

“Because I think the games that lead to grand finals need to be looked with a different lens than other games.”

However, Melbourne champion Garry Lyon believes that suggestion from Watson would “open Pandora’s Box”.

“I think this could've been downgraded to low (impact) and he would’ve got a fine and been able to play. But I have no sympathy for Lachie,” Lyon said.

“Lachie chased (Ginnivan) down off the ball, swung the fist. Then you put yourself in this position.”

To which Watson responded: “When this is revisited in 50 years time, people will shake their heads and they’ll point to that time in history when the game took a huge step towards non-contact.

“The same grainy vision will be shown to somebody in 50 years time ... someone will say: ‘That’s when the game took a turn for the worse and the first step towards non-contact AFL football we received’.”

Brisbane teammate Hugh McCluggage was “disappointed” with the outcome as the Lions weigh up challenging the ban.

“Lachie’s a teammate of ours and we really want him to be out there,” McCluggage said on SEN.

“I’m sure that the club will explore all the options they’ve got and we’ll see how it plays out.”

Similarly, fellow Lions star Dayne Zorko was “really disappointed” with the verdict.

“I haven’t spoken to anyone from the club about it but I imagine we’ll probably go another step forward (with an appeal) and see what can happen after that,” Zorko said on Nova 100.

Elsewhere, Brownlow medallist James Hird said it would “go down as one of the most remarkable suspensions” in a huge blow to the Lions.

But Hird noted it was consistent with the previous nine stomach punch instances this year.

“This is an absolute champion of the game and one of the most important players in last year’s grand final in the second half,” Hird said on Nine’s Footy Classified.

“He’s been in remarkable form throughout the year. This is an incredible result for Brisbane and going to make Fremantle’s job all the more easier.

“This is what (AFL footy boss) Greg Swann said at the start of the year he wanted out of the game. They’ve been consistent and what you’d have to say about the AFL is we admire your consistency because they’ve followed through in what was the biggest call of the year.”

Former Adelaide skipper Rory Sloane also suggested it was “harsh” on Neale and the Lions.

“When we watched the previous nine cases, I can see why he was found guilty. But it seems like such a harsh penalty missing a Grand Final, especially with that kind of footage,” Sloane said on Nine.

“With Ginnivan testifying to Lachie Neale, I think you’ve got to take his words into account here.”
