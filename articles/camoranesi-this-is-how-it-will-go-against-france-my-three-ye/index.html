Camoranesi: "This is how it will go against France. My three years in Cyprus, but now I want a chance in Italy"

The 2006 World champion: "An excellent choice to focus on youth, the team takes the pitch without the memory of three missed World Cups in a row"

  • Italiano
  • Deutsch

On 9 July 2006 in Berlin, he was also on the pitch against France. And at the end of 120 minutes of battle and a penalty shootout, Mauro Camoranesi lifted the World Cup alongside the other Azzurri. Twenty years have passed since then, but for the former Juventus winger the memory of that night, in which Oddo turned barber and cut his hair, remains indelible. “The best night of my career and a unique emotion,” recounted the Italian-Argentine, who is waiting for a new managerial venture, perhaps in our country. In the meantime, however, he studies football and keeps himself up to date. That is why tomorrow evening he will be in front of the TV to watch Italy v France. With his heart, naturally, beating for Mancini's Azzurri. 

Camoranesi, what kind of match do you expect between Italy and France? 

“I imagine it will be different from the first leg and I think Italy will try to build play a few metres higher up.” 

In Paris the Azzurri defended well until Olise's goal, but then had the strength to equalise. 

“Pushing the team higher up the pitch is a brave choice and means leaving space for counter-attacks. It will be necessary for the Azzurri to be careful because France have exceptional players on the break.” 

Although Mbappé was missing in the first leg, did you expect an Italy capable of stopping France? 

“Honestly no, and I didn't even believe the match could end in a draw. France were clear favourites, dictated the tempo of the match and confirmed that it isn't easy to face them because of the quality they can put on the pitch. Italy did well not to give away space and not to allow their opponents to build play in the final third. That way it was easier for the Azzurri to read the attacks of Zidane's men. Italy played a decent match and brought home an excellent draw.” 

Not many thought Mancini could revive the national team. Did you believe it? 

“We must not forget that during his previous spell as national team coach, Mancini restored Italy's identity and that, as a result, many victories followed, including the triumph at the 2021 European Championship. Such an achievement led the Azzurri to gain great international respect once again. When Mancini was chosen as the new coach last summer, I personally approved of the decision: he is a good manager and has clear ideas. He is not afraid to call up youngsters and then put them on the pitch.” 

Is it right that Italy are focusing on youth to rebuild? 

“For me it is entirely right. There is no other way than giving young players space, having faith in them and helping them grow. First of all because youngsters bring enthusiasm to the group; secondly because more new faces compared to the past can help the team take the pitch without the memory of three consecutive missed World Cup qualifications.” 

In September, in the opening match against Belgium, did we also lose because that "boulder" of a third missed World Cup was weighing on the Azzurri? 

“The disappointment of the play-off in Bosnia inevitably lingers for everyone involved, and I agree with Mancini's decision to call up different players. Both to bring a new tactical idea and because they have the desire to prove their worth. Doumbia, Kayode and the other lads called up by the coach can bring positive energy to Italy.” 

Who could be the key players at San Siro? 

“It's hard to say. It is not entirely clear in this national team who the protagonists might be, because over the first four matches of the Nations League group, the play was developed well and collectively. This allowed almost all the individuals to take centre stage, so much so that we have scored with defenders and midfielders too.” 

France, on the other hand, rely on individual brilliance. 

“France are more unpredictable and need less build-up play to score because they can rely on the immense quality of their individuals. Champions like Mbappé, Olise and Barcola can create a scoring opportunity with a single move, and that undoubtedly helps Zidane's work.” 

Camoranesi, what do you expect from your managerial career? 

“Since 2015 I have always worked abroad and never in Italy. I spent the last three years in the Cypriot league and until September I was on the bench at AEK Larnaca, whom I left after the penalty shootout defeat in the Conference League qualifiers (against Beitar Jerusalem, ed.).” 

Will we see you sooner or later on an Italian bench like your fellow World Cup winners Nesta, Gilardino, Cannavaro, Gattuso, De Rossi, Inzaghi, Grosso, Oddo, Barone, Amelia, Pirlo and Barzagli? 

“I will certainly return to work next year and honestly, for sporting and personal reasons, I would like to do so in Italy. I will definitely continue to pursue this profession as I always did as a player, meaning with passion and desire. I'm waiting for the right opportunity to come along and in the meantime I am keeping myself up to date.”
