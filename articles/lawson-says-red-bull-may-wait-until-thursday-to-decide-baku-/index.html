Lawson says Red Bull may wait until Thursday to decide Baku line-up | Grande Prêmio (Grand Prix)

TheTools · 16.09.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

Liam Lawson says he may have to wait until Wednesday or Thursday of Azerbaijan GP week to learn whether he will remain alongside Max Verstappen at Red Bull or return to Racing Bulls as Isack Hadjar continues his recovery.

News Summary

  • Red Bull could wait until Wednesday or Thursday of Baku week to decide whether Hadjar returns
  • Lawson is preparing for the possibility of driving either the Red Bull or Racing Bulls car
  • The New Zealander admits returning to Racing Bulls would now be difficult because of the differences between the two cars

Liam Lawson says Red Bull could wait until Wednesday or Thursday of Azerbaijan Grand Prix week before deciding whether Isack Hadjar is fit to return, leaving the New Zealander preparing for the possibility of racing for either Red Bull or Racing Bulls in Baku.

Hadjar has missed the last three Grands Prix with a wrist injury, allowing Lawson to return to Red Bull after his difficult two-race spell with the senior team at the beginning of 2025. He finished seventh at Zandvoort and sixth in Madrid, with Red Bull team principal Laurent Mekies describing his latest stint as “very strong”.

The situation could now remain unresolved until immediately before the Baku weekend.

“Probably all the way up to Wednesday or Thursday for Baku will be potentially when that’s decided,” Lawson said when asked how late Red Bull could make the call.

That uncertainty creates an unusual preparation problem. If Hadjar is cleared to return to the RB22, Lawson will move back to Racing Bulls and reclaim the seat currently occupied by Yuki Tsunoda. If Hadjar is not ready, Lawson is expected to continue alongside Max Verstappen for a fourth consecutive Grand Prix.

Lawson has already experienced the difficulty of preparing for both possibilities. Before Monza, he worked with both teams while Red Bull assessed Hadjar’s recovery, with his place in the senior squad only confirmed on the Wednesday before the race.

Returning to Racing Bulls after three consecutive weekends in the RB22 would bring another complication.

“I think it would be quite tough, honestly,” Lawson said. “The cars are completely different to drive.”

Hadjar’s return depends on his recovery rather than Lawson’s recent performances. Mekies said in Madrid that Red Bull would not put the Frenchman back in the car until he was fully ready, with further evaluation planned before Baku.

Lawson, meanwhile, has made considerably more of his second opportunity with Red Bull than his first. After being demoted just two races into the 2025 season, he has scored points in two of his three substitute appearances this year and produced his best result for the team with sixth place in Madrid.

The question for Baku is therefore not whether Lawson has done enough to continue, but whether Hadjar will be physically ready to reclaim his seat.
