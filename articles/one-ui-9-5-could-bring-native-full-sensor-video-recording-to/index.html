One UI 9.5 could bring native full-sensor video recording to Galaxy phones

TheTools · 19.11.2026 05:20 · source · permalink

Affiliate links on Android Authority may earn us a commission. Learn more.

Nov 19, 2026 — 12:20 AM ET

  • Samsung is working on a native Open Gate recording option for the Camera app in One UI 9.5.
  • It will let users record video using the entire imaging area of the sensor for more cropping and stabilization options in post-production.
  • The feature will support several frame rates from 24fps to 60fps.

Samsung has started rolling out the One UI 9.5 beta for the Galaxy S26 series, and it comes with several new features. However, Samsung is also working on plenty of features that aren’t yet enabled in the beta. This includes a new Camera app feature video enthusiasts will love: native Open Gate recording.

APK teardown

This reporting is based on work-in-progress code. These features may not ship.

While digging into the new Camera app in One UI 9.5, we found several strings suggesting that the company is preparing a new option for Open Gate video recording. Open Gate uses the entire imaging area of a camera sensor instead of cropping the footage to a standard widescreen format like 16:9. Since most camera sensors have a 4:3 aspect ratio, Open Gate captures a taller frame, giving users more options for cropping and stabilization in post-production.

According to the strings, Open Gate will be a dedicated option in the Camera app. Users will likely need to enable it in the Camera settings, then turn it on from the camera preview. We also spotted icons showing whether Open Gate is enabled or disabled. There could also be a setting that decides whether the option appears only in Pro video mode or in both Video and Pro video modes.

We also found several frame-rate options for Open Gate recording: 24, 25, 30, 48, 50, and 60fps. The strings further show that the app will display a toast with the full-sensor resolution and offer recommendations to help users capture the best possible footage. These recommendations include turning off video stabilization and using a tripod or gimbal to prevent digital cropping. The app could also show frame guides to preview how the footage will look in different aspect ratios.
