Livingston Fire Chief Urges Holiday Fire Safety

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:49 · source · permalink

Livingston Fire Chief Rocky Dial is calling on residents to prioritize home safety measures and emergency planning as the holiday season approaches.

Dial said the improper use of extension cords remains a frequent safety error in local households. Dial said residents often create fire hazards by running these cords under rugs or mats, which traps heat.

“Practice is very important,” Dial said. “It becomes like muscle memory then that you know what to do. You can practice it, have at least two ways out of your house or each room and everything and have a meeting spot outside where everybody can meet and make sure that everybody is safe.”

Dial said modern home construction utilizes synthetic materials that allow fires to spread with extreme speed. These materials create toxic smoke and intense heat that can make a residence uninhabitable within two to three minutes of ignition.

“Conditions can really progress very rapidly now with all the synthetic materials that are being used inside the house and for the construction part of it,” Dial said. “So the time from the beginning of the fire to the time it progresses to the point that it’s becomes really uninhabitable is, like they say, two to three minutes and then the house is not safe to be in due to the all the toxic smoke and the heat and stuff from the fire.”

Dial said families should install smoke alarms in every sleeping room, in every hallway, and on every level of the home. He said these devices require monthly testing to ensure they are functioning correctly.

“If you have battery-operated detectors that use the 9-volt batteries or AA’s, they need to be changed every six months or so,” Dial said. “You need to change the batteries out, and that can be found most times during the monthly testing. And with the time change, that’s always a good reminder to check your batteries and your smoke alarm and replace them if needed.”

Dial said local fire departments are available to help residents identify and practice secondary escape routes if a primary exit is blocked. He said having a predetermined second way out prevents residents from having to figure out an escape plan during an actual emergency.

“Make sure that when you start putting your Christmas decorations out they’ve been in storage make sure that there’s no frayed wires on them, use good UL listed extension cords, and be safe this holiday season,” Dial said.

Dial said the department typically sees an increase in calls related to heating equipment rather than holiday decorations. He said these incidents often involve heaters placed too close to furniture or the use of unsafe heating units.
