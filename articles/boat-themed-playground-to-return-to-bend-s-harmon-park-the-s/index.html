Boat-Themed Playground to Return to Bend's Harmon Park - The Source - Bend, Oregon

TheTools · 18.11.2026 20:28 · source · permalink

A new boat-themed playground structure will be coming to Bend’s Harmon Park next summer, replacing an old tugboat that was a fixture of the park near the Deschutes River for more than five decades.  

The Bend Park & Recreation District removed the tugboat play structure — the PS Happy Harmon — in September after deeming it a safety hazard due to chipping paint, rust and exposed metal. The tugboat was a repurposed lifeboat that a park district employee converted into a play structure in 1974. 

Some community members lamented the loss of the iconic structure without a chance for more public engagement and called on the district to refurbish and bring back the boat, or at least parts of it.  

The park district intends to continue that boat tradition, but not with a repurposed Happy Harmon, according to a Nov. 16 announcement.  

In 1974, park district employee Ken Lehto repurposed a lifeboat and converted it into the play tugboat structure, according to the park district. 

“BPRD recognizes the special meaning and cherished memories the boat held for so many members of the community and understands the importance in carrying forward Harmon Park’s tradition of boat-themed play by installing a new boat-themed play structure in its place,” said Ian Isaacson, project manager and landscape architect with the park district.  

What the new boat structure will look like is still in the works, and the public will have a say in that process. The district is working with three playground representatives and their design teams to come up with concepts for the structure. Renderings will be posted to the project’s webpage for community members to review and provide feedback through a survey.  

Feedback will help the district choose a concept and could inform potential tweaks to the design, the district said.  

Design concepts are expected to be available for community review and feedback by the end of 2026, with installation tentatively planned for summer 2027, according to a press release.
