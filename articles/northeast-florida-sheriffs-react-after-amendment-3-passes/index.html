Northeast Florida sheriffs react after Amendment 3 passes

TheTools · 04.11.2026 16:39 · source · permalink

Local News

JACKSONVILLE, Fla. – Northeast Florida sheriffs who raised concerns about Amendment 3 before the election are responding after Florida voters approved the property tax measure Tuesday.

Before the election, Nassau County Sheriff Bill Leeper and Clay County Sheriff Michelle Cook joined other public safety leaders in urging voters to reject the amendment, warning of potential revenue losses and effects on local services.

The Jacksonville Sheriff’s Office had said it typically does not comment on proposed legislation. Sheriff T.K. Waters also said he usually stays out of legislative proposals. After the election, Waters said the amendment’s passage means “much-needed relief is on the way for hard-working Floridians.” He said Jacksonville leaders have demonstrated a strong commitment to public safety and he expects that to continue.

“The Jacksonville Sheriff’s Office will remain focused on working hard, being responsible stewards of taxpayer dollars, and delivering the level of service our citizens expect and deserve,” Waters said.

Chris Hand, a government law attorney, said the amendment could have a significant effect on local governments. Jacksonville’s council auditors estimated the city could need to cut $200 million from its $2 billion general fund operating budget next year, Hand said Wednesday on The Morning Show. He said local governments across Florida may consider spending cuts and new revenue sources to address budget gaps.

The Nassau County Sheriff’s Office said it is unknown whether the amendment will directly affect the agency. The amendment requires local governments to use property tax revenue for public safety, among other things, and local governments will be responsible for evaluating and addressing budget implications, the office said.

“Our commitment to the citizens of Nassau County remains unchanged,” the statement said. “Residents can continue to rely on NCSO to provide the level of service, protection, and professionalism they have come to expect from our agency.”
