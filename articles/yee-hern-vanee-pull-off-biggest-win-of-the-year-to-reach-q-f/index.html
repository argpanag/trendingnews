Yee Hern-Vanee pull off biggest win of the year to reach q-finals

TheTools · 27.11.2026 00:00 · source · permalink
    bookmark

PETALING JAYA: Shuttlers Wee Yee Hern-Vanee Gobi may still have a long way to go, but their latest win suggests they are capable of challenging the world’s top mixed doubles pairs.

The world No. 61 Yee Hern-Vanee stunned top seeds and world No. 24 Dhruv Kapila-Tanisha Crasto of India 22-20, 21-15 in the second round of the Syed Modi International to claim their biggest scalp of the year at the Babu Banarasi Das Uttar Pradesh Badminton Academy in Lucknow yesterday.

The Malaysians will take on Indonesia’s Nawaf Khoiriyansyah-Indah Cahya in the quarter-finals today. National doubles coaching director Rexy Mainaky had earlier urged the pair to keep their feet on the ground following their Taiwan Masters triumph two weeks ago after beating South Koreans Ki Dong-ju-Jeong Na-eun.

“Step by step,” said Rexy briefly when contacted.

Their progress is particularly encouraging for the national mixed doubles squad, which has been struggling for results and is also without Toh Ee Wei, who is still recovering from an anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury.

However, Yee Hern-Vanee still have work to do in India, with a place in the semi-finals at stake against the Indonesian pair. Last year, they missed out on a semi-final spot after losing to Thailand’s Pakkapon Teerarat­sakul-Sapsiree Taerattanachai at the quarter-final stage.

Yee Hern-Vanee will be Malaysia’s only mixed doubles pair left in the competition after independent pair Lee Yi Bo-How Yin Xuan were knocked out 10-21, 10-21 by Indonesia’s fifth seeds Rehan Naufal-Melati Daeva.

Meanwhile in the men’s doubles, Wan Arif Wan Junaidi-Mohd Haikal Nazri lost 10-21, 10-21 to top seeds Raymond Indra-Nikolaus Joaquin of Indonesia.

Malaysia still have a representative in the women’s singles quarter-finals as Wong Ling Ching fought back from a game down to beat India’s Tanya Hemanth 16-21, 21-13, 21-17 and set up a last eight clash against Thailand’s Supanida Katethong.

The Malaysian faces a tough task against the world No. 26, having lost to Supanida in their last meeting at the China Masters in September.

In the other match, Low Zi Yu-Noraqilah Maisarah Ramdan will face England’s Abbygael Harris-Estelle Van Leeuwen in the quarter-finals after beating Indian pair Ananya Arun-Anjana Manikandan 21-16, 21-16 in the second round.
