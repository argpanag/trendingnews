Latest World Rugby rankings: Springboks surrender top spot in defeat to France

TheTools · 13.11.2026 23:08 · source · permalink

France captain Antoine Dupont celebrates beating the Springboks and the Latest World Rugby Rankings

South Africa have surrendered the top spot in the World Rugby rankings following their 21-19 defeat to France at the Stade de France in Paris.

New Zealand have benefitted from Rassie Erasmus’ side loss to Fabien Galthie’s Les Bleus with the All Blacks returning to the top of the rankings for the second time this year.

Springboks surrender top spot

Dave Rennie’s side went to the top of the standings following their win over South Africa in the Rugby’s Greatest Rivalry series but were knocked back down a week later after losing in Cape Town.

The Springboks‘ second defeat of the year has now seen New Zealand return to the summit with South Africa dropping down to second place before Saturday and Sunday’s Nations Championship action.

France received a 1.56-point boost with a two-point victory over the Boks, their first win over South Africa in Paris since 2005. That has seen them move ahead of Ireland into third position.

Andy Farrell’s men are unable to reclaim that ranking with a victory over Fiji in Dublin this weekend due to the rating point difference between the two nations before kick-off. However, the Springboks could return to the top of the standings before the end of the weekend, but they would need Wales to claim a first-ever win over the All Blacks in the professional era.

If Steve Tandy’s side do just that, New Zealand will drop back down to second place in the rankings and cannot fall lower than second regardless of the margin of defeat.

France dominate the first half, the Springboks the second

France led the encounter from the 14th minute as they punished the Springboks ‘ indiscipline, making the most of Lood de Jager’s absence after he was sin-binned for a high tackle on Maxime Lucu. Aaron Grandidier-Nkanang scored a minute of the sin-binning with Theo Attissogbe grabbing the hosts’ second eight minutes later.

Jan-Hendrik Wessels crashed over from the back of a powerful maul to get the Springboks back into the game but his try was cancelled out by Louis Bielle-Biarrey on the half-hour mark. The visitors looked to have scored through Kurt-Lee Arendse on the stroke of half-time, but his try was chalked off after it was deemed that he was offside from the lineout.

Jasper Wiese’s red card early in the second half was a major setback for Erasmus’ men and while Cobus Reinach scored in the 50th minute and Malcolm Marx in the 71st, the Boks fell short with Manie Libbok failing to convert the latter’s try to even the scoreline.
