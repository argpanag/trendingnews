Macklin Celebrini, Michael Misa credit Sharks’ ‘reset’ for bounce-back win over Kraken at home

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:15 · source · permalink

The San Jose Sharks bounced back from a blowout loss, using their three days off to "reset" and tackle the Seattle Kraken in a statement win.

The Sharks had three days to think about Tuesday's 5-2 loss to the Calgary Flames. On Saturday night, they needed 29 seconds to show they had.

The Seattle Kraken took a 1-0 lead on a power-play goal from Kaapo Kakko at 5:57 of the first period at SAP Center. Michael Misa tied it at 6:26, taking a pass from Dimitri Orlov, skating down the left wing and beating Philipp Grubauer, as the Sharks scored four straight goals on the way to a 4-3 win over the Kraken.

Orlov, who had three assists, set up Misa's goal, fed Macklin Celebrini for a first-period power-play goal with a cross-ice pass, and had the shot that led to Ivar Stenberg's power-play goal early in the second period. Celebrini said the Sharks used the days off well.

"I think that reset was really good for us, just a little break away from it and regroup, work on some things, and I thought we played a really solid game," Celebrini said.

Misa said the time off helped after San Jose played every other day, and he said the Sharks were ready from the start.

"It was definitely nice to have the days off. I think we kind of needed it after playing every other day, and yeah, we're coming out with juice tonight, and a lot of motivation and intensity," Misa told reporters. "I thought we kind of played a consistent 60 minutes."

The work in practice on Wednesday and Friday, he said, was about tightening up in their own end.

"I think we did a lot of things in those two practices, worked on a lot of things defensively, just tightening up on, you know, I think in particular the honey hole," Misa added. "I think teams are getting a lot of chances off us from that spot. I think we were pretty connected tonight in the o-zone, just playing three close and making plays."

The Sharks also changed their lineup, healthy-scratching forward Collin Graf and defenseman Michael Kesselring. Orlov said it does not matter who is on the ice with whom.

"We're one team, you know. I think it's all about, it doesn't matter who your line is with, we just need to talk, have conversations, help each other and work hard, and we're going to have success," Orlov said.

Celebrini said Orlov's game makes that easier, especially on the power play.

"There's just some magic in how simple he is and how well he moves that puck and his decision making," Celebrini said. "Especially on the power play, [it] makes our lives easier when he's getting those pucks through and knowing when to shoot it and when to pass."

Celebrini became the first Sharks player to reach 10 goals this season, and he has 13 points in his last nine games. The Sharks led 4-1 early in the second period and held on after Seattle scored twice, with a scoreless third period and 27 saves from Alex Nedeljkovic.

Orlov, speaking to Drew Remenda on "Sharks Postgame Live," said the Sharks still have things to clean up.

"We still have to manage that game better a little bit, but I think our first [period] today was great, so it's the right direction," Orlov said.

One win does not fix an 11-12-2 record, but four wins in the last six games is a trend worth watching, and the Minnesota Wild will put that reset to the test on Monday.
