Prince William has 'learned' to live without Prince Harry as royal feud reaches breaking point: expert

TheTools · 10.11.2026 08:05 · source · permalink

Sources say the Duke and Duchess of Sussex are not planning to apologize to William and Kate Middleton

Prince Harry must apologize to the royal family: author

Former Buckingham Palace spokesman Dickie Arbiter says Prince Harry must sincerely apologize to his family before he can hope to reconcile with King Charles. Arbiter has written a new book, "On Duty with the Royals."

Prince William has "learned" to live without Prince Harry as their bitter feud appears to have pushed the brothers past the point of no return.

"Harry has never believed he has anything to apologize for," Christopher Andersen, author of "Kate!," told Fox News Digital.

"William had never believed he had anything to apologize for. Reconciliation is not in the cards. It shouldn't come as any surprise that William views Harry as having betrayed not only the royal family but the institution of the monarchy itself. Harry returning to the U.K. and picking up where he left off before Megxit just rubbed salt into an already festering wound."

"These are two extraordinarily strong and stubborn men who have, sadly, learned over the past few years that they can live without each other," Andersen said. "If the death of their grandmother, Queen Elizabeth II, their father's coronation and the cancer battles of both the king and the Princess of Wales didn't bring them together, it's very hard to imagine what would."

Andersen's comments came after a source told People magazine that the Duke and Duchess of Sussex are not planning to apologize to William and his wife, Kate Middleton. The U.K.'s Express previously reported that Harry and Meghan hoped to apologize to the Prince and Princess of Wales over comments made during their 2021 interview with Oprah Winfrey as part of an effort to reconcile.

A source close to Harry and Meghan told People that the report was "nonsense."

British broadcaster Helena Chard told Fox News Digital that William is unlikely to forgive his brother. She noted that William once saw Harry as a supportive "wingman" as he prepared for his future role as king.

"Reportedly, Prince William finds it impossible to forgive his brother for leaving him to cope with the pressure of being future king on his own," Chard said. "The sheer stress and upset caused to his family over the past few years is impossible to forgive."

Harry has publicly said he would like to reconcile with his family. In May 2025, he told the BBC that he would "love reconciliation." In October 2026, he told GQ that "the most important thing is to reconcile. That's the key point."

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex stepped back from their roles as senior working royals in 2020, citing intrusive British press coverage and a lack of support from the palace. After moving to California that year, they aired their grievances in interviews and documentaries. Harry's 2023 memoir, "Spare," further strained relations with his family.

The couple returned to the U.K. with their children in August 2026 for an extended stay.

"On Duty with the Royals" by Dickie Arbiter is available now. (W. F. Howes Ltd.)

Dick Arbiter, a former press secretary to the queen and author of "On Duty with the Royals," told Fox News Digital that Harry should be the one seeking forgiveness, "not the other way around." He said the queen, who died in 2022, was disappointed when her grandson and his wife stepped back from royal duties.

Princess Diana visits the Peto Institute for Sick Children in Hungary. In the back is royal press secretary Dickie Arbiter. (Getty Images)

"He had his own reasons to go," Arbiter said. "I think we can all assume why. How is it affecting the rest of the family? Well, it upset his father for obvious reasons. What is more important to the king is his constitutional duty and the survivability of the monarchy as an institution. That comes first."

"He is not going to reconcile with his son as long as his son continues to rubbish the royal family and continues to talk about private meetings," Arbiter said. "This is one of the reasons why the king has had so few meetings with his son, because no sooner is the meeting over and the door has hit Harry in the back, it's all over the American media."

"He's got to be contrite. He's got to apologize and mean it, not the other way around, because he's asking for apologies from his family. And I'm not terribly sure what he wants apologies for."

Some royal commentators had previously hoped that Kate would bring the brothers together as the royal family's peacemaker. However, Andersen said his sources indicated that the Princess of Wales has no desire to get involved.

"Kate is very disappointed with her brother-in-law," Andersen said. "They were once so close, and I think when this all started, she felt she could bring them back together. But to be honest, Harry's attacks on William and the rest of the royal family have, for the most part, made that impossible. I don't think either side has the appetite to go over what has been said in the past."

Kate Middleton, Meghan Markle and Prince Harry on the balcony for Trooping the Colour in 2018. (Max Mumby/Indigo/Getty Images)

"It's only Harry who would 'love reconciliation,' not Prince William," British royal expert Hilary Fordwich said. "He remains furious regarding his brother's betrayal, insults and the damage he has wrought upon the family. The impasse is due to both the lack of trust any of the senior royals have in the Sussexes and the lack of apology from them. Without a formal mea culpa, there is no starting point."

Constant said William's next move could determine whether the brothers begin reconciliation talks. As the future king, his decision could send a message about leadership, forgiveness and the possibility of unity within the fractured royal family.
