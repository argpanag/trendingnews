    Leicester City

    Leicester City boss Russell Martin speaking to TNT Sports:

    "I'm annoyed we've conceded two goals but that's my job to be annoyed about that.

I think the change of shape helped us, the subs were fantastic, Harry [Howell] was brilliant when he came on, Tommy [Watson] the same and Olabade [Aluko].

    "So, I'm really pleased. It took a big group effort tonight and we score four goals, we maybe could have had a couple more and we're through which is the most important thing because it was a really tough tie tonight."

Leicester teenager Louis Page scored his first senior goal to set his team on their way to FA Cup victory at Oxford City.

Leicester City survived a difficult night in their first FA Cup first round tie for 18 years to progress at the expense of National League North side Oxford City.

The 2021 winners, who have since slumped into League One, went ahead in the first half through a first senior goal for promising 18-year-old Louis Page but were pegged back as Scott Pollock scored direct from a corner for the semi-professional outfit.

Goals from Foxes top scorer Emil Riis and Kamari Doyle seemed to have ended the brave resistance of the Hoops, but Josh Ashby's lobbed finish cut the deficit to one goal again.

Brighton loanee Tommy Watson came off the bench to secure Leicester's place in the second round with a sharp finish with eight minutes remaining.

There are 64 league places between League One promotion hopefuls Leiceter and their opponents, who lie second in National League North, and it showed in the opening phase as the home side were at full stretch to contain slicker, sharper opponents.

Leicester lacked a final product until teenager Page further enhanced his growing reputation by firing in from 25 yards, his shot taking a slight deflection off defender Ludwig Francilette.

The Hoops rallied and were level at half-time as midfielder Pollock claimed his 10th goal of the season by swinging a right-sided corner in with his left foot, the ball evading a host of players to nestle in the far corner.

Leicester boss Russell Martin sent on 18-year-old Brighton loanee Harry Howell at half-time, and he provided the quality in the final third which had been lacking.

His perfect cross to the far post left Riis with an unmissable header for his eighth goal of the season, and six minutes later Howell was again involved as Leicester seemed to take full control.

He again stretched the Hoops on their left side, but this time fed Page, who nudged the ball on for Doyle to finish expertly with the outside of his foot.

Oxford refused to lie down and three minutes later their strong pressing paid dividends as Leicester lost the ball and Ashby burst through to lob a clever volley over goalkeeper Franco Ravizzoli.

The effort began to tell on the part-timers however, and Leicester went for the kill, finally getting a decisive fourth goal through another Brighton loanee, as former Sunderland play-off hero Tommy Watson skipped past a defender and found the bottom corner.

Players were superb - Jenkins reaction

Oxford City manager Ross Jenkins told BBC Radio Oxford:

"The result was probably fair. We didn't do enough to win the game [although] we were definitely in the game.

"The players were superb. The defensive output the boys put in was through the roof, and we knew we had to respect Leicester - they're a top team with great players.

"It's been a week to get the game going, and the [temporary] stand up, and everything around the football club has been a big rush but the feedback from the fans has been superb and the boys should be super proud.

On Pollock goal: "100% [he meant to score]. He does it in training, and has done it a few times. Josh Ashby does it as well.

"We work our set-pieces quite well and have a variation - one of them is Scotty whipping it right underneath the crossbar. It's gone straight in and if it didn't one of our boys was going to head it in."

We played some great football - Martin

Leicester manager Russell Martin told BBC Radio Leicester:

"I'm really pleased just to get through. It was a really tough tie. They put it on TV on a Friday night because it was difficult - a different surface, a team that's really well-coached and really well-organised.

"We knew there would be moments when it was tough. We actually played some really good football at times but the last bit wasn't there in the first half.

"After we scored we didn't respond very well, and didn't respond very well to them scoring and they had a bit of a spell but the second half was great. We created a lot, played some really great, fast football and the subs had a huge impact.

"We changed shape and it helped us. The guys are used to playing a couple of shapes now so we can be adaptable, which is really good.

"There were some brilliant individual performances, we win and we score four goals."
