Erebus, PremiAir pledges after Gold Coast scrutiny

TheTools · 06.11.2026 01:36 · source · permalink

EREBUS Motorsport and PremiAir Racing have vowed to not repeat their Gold Coast 500 refuelling mistakes and pledged they will continue purely racing for themselves.

Late pitstops for Jarrod Hughes (Erebus) and Cooper Murray (PremiAir) ultimately helped fellow Chevrolet driver Anton De Pasquale (Team 18) advance to the Supercars semi-finals this weekend at Sandown.

That sparked conspiracy theories – not to mention insinuations by Ryan Walkinshaw of manufacturer orders, which are outlawed – that Supercars, General Motors and its teams swiftly sought to quash.

Asked by V8 Sleuth if changes had been made in the wake of the mishap, Erebus CEO Barry Ryan bristled.

“I’ve explained it so many damn times, I’m sick of hearing about it,” he said.

“We made a mistake, that’s it, it won’t happen again. If it does, it won’t be because of that drama, it will be because of something else.

“It was just a mistake.”

PremiAir team principal Roland Dane was a little less abrasive in sharing his answer: “Only the liars haven’t run out of fuel in Supercars over the years, if you have been in it for any length of time, whether it was driver-induced or team-induced, and this was very much a team screw up.

“I doubt it will happen again somehow.

“Of course we have made steps to try to ensure that it doesn’t happen again in our garage. It is what it is and I can’t undo it. All we can do is learn from it and do a better a job.”

Dane subsequently promised regarding the broader playoffs picture: “I can only tell you from our point of view as PremiAir, we’ll be racing for ourselves.

“We’ll be respectful for the people who are taking part in this finals thing – which I haven’t got any interest in myself – as much as you can be when you’re running your own program.”

Ryan echoed those sentiments from an Erebus perspective: “We’re going to take every result we can get.

“Like Anton, we’re aligned with GM but we’re not going to do anything that’s going to sacrifice our results, definitely not.”

De Pasquale is the last Chevrolet driver left standing in the title fight, and Chaz Mostert is in the same boat for Toyota.

Five Fords by contrast are in the Sandown semi-final seven.
