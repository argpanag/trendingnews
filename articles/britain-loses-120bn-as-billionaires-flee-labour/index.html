Britain loses £120bn as billionaires flee Labour

TheTools · 07.10.2026 14:26 · source · permalink

Money and assets moved out of UK as tax rises take toll on wealthy

Labour’s tax policies have driven more than $160bn (£120bn) out of the UK since the party came to power.

The collective value of the money and assets owned by people who have either loosened or cut ties with Britain in the past two years has rocketed, according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index.

It shows that £120bn of individual and family fortunes have left the country as tax rises under Labour have taken their toll on the wealthy.

The departure of Lakshmi Mittal, the steel magnate, is listed as the biggest blow to Britain’s economy, removing a net worth of more than $40bn (£30bn).

It emerged last year that he had moved his tax residence from the UK to Switzerland and would spend most of his time in Dubai.

Shravin Bharti Mittal, the son of Indian billionaire Sunil Bharti Mittal, is the next biggest departure, with a $23.3bn share of the family fortune. Their company Bharti Enterprises is the largest shareholder in BT with a stake of just under 25pc.

Tony Dalwood, chief executive of asset manager Gresham House, which looks after around £11bn of client money, said Britain’s taxes had become a challenge for people who want to grow businesses and create economic growth.

He said: “That virtuous circle is challenged if you start taxing and raising taxes on corporations and on private individuals.”

An increasing number of wealthy taxpayers have chosen to leave Britain’s shores in the wake of successive tax rises under Labour.

The Government also scrapped the UK’s “non-dom” regime in April last year, meaning income earned abroad is now liable for tax.

It emerged this weekend that David Reuben, the billionaire property mogul with a reported net worth of £9.9bn, moved to Monaco during the summer.

Monaco does not require residents to pay tax on income, capital gains or inheritances. Mr Reuben’s brother, Simon, also lives in the principality and has been based there for about 40 years.

Christopher Rokos, the hedge fund tycoon and philanthropist with fortune of $4bn, is also switching his residency to Greece.

Greece’s own non-dom regime allows people to pay a €100,000 (£86,000) flat tax on all foreign income for a period of up to 15 years.

Mr Rokos was listed as Britain’s third-biggest taxpayer last year, having paid £330m, according to The Sunday Times.

The taxes contributed by the 55-year-old last year were equivalent to the amount paid by 21,000 British households, based on the average family’s £15,700-a-year tax bill.

Luke Johnson, the co-owner of Gail’s Bakery, said Britain’s “very foolish” inheritance tax system was driving wealth abroad.

Speaking at a fringe event at the Conservative Party conference in Birmingham, Mr Johnson said: “They [billionaires] invest where they live. Now they’re in Monaco and Dubai, but they’re investing there. We’re no longer on their radar, but their families are still here.

“They want to come back, but they are not going to face the onerous inheritance tax we currently have – it’s very foolish the current system.”

John Caudwell, the billionaire founder of Phones4u, told Bloomberg: “What’s happening at the moment is disastrous.

“Instead of being a virtuous circle where we’re attracting millionaires, billionaires and inward investment, we are doing the reverse.”

A growing number of investors are concerned about the possibility of a raid on their savings. One in every eight investors surveyed by IG said they would consider moving their tax residence outside the country if the rate of Capital Gains Tax was harmonised with income tax, a policy that influential figures within Labour have advocated for.
