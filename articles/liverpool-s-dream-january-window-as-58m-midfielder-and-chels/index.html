Liverpool's dream January window as £58m midfielder and Chelsea star arrive

TheTools · 14.11.2026 07:00 · source · permalink

Liverpool are not a million miles away from the Premier League's summit after ten games played. But some Reds fans will think moves must be made come January if they are to challenge for the crown.

Andoni Iraola's reign at Anfield has begun satisfactorily. His side have lost just three of their 16 matches and currently sit fourth in the top flight with 18 points. They are three points behind Manchester City in third, four off Brighton in second, but nine off league leaders Arsenal.

When the Reds faced the Gunners at the beginning of the month, they slipped to a 1-0 defeat and many called into question their title credentials. A handful of positions could do with strengthening at Liverpool, with Milos Kerkez not convincing at left-back and Jeremie Frimpong perhaps seeking an exit at right-back.

A more defensive-minded midfielder is also missed, irrespective of Trey Nyoni's rise. So the ECHO is taking a look at what a dream January transfer window could look like for Liverpool and two moves they would do well to make.

Lamine Camara move hijacked

Nyoni has been nothing short of a revelation this term and the 19-year-old's strike in the Champions League against Fenerbahce epitomised just how confident he has grown.

However, he is still a teenager and cannot be relied upon to deliver silverware quite yet. The Reds therefore need a more disruptive, defensive midfielder who can operate in Iraola's pivot. As things stand, Alexis Mac Allister, Dominik Szoboszlai and Ryan Gravenberch are rotated together.

But if Mac Allister and Szoboszlai are paired, the midfield lacks the ball recovery and middle-third tackling in open games. Gravenberch, whilst he is an outstanding footballer, is not yet fully attuned to the art of aggressive ball-winning, either. That's where Lamine Camara thrives.

The Monaco midfielder ranks near the top of European football for ball recoveries, interceptions and middle-third tackling. If he's signed, he could be deployed when the Reds are facing an elite side or find themselves in a chaotic end-to-end clash. Tyler Adams played this role perfectly for Iraola with Bournemouth.

Camara, who is only 22, could stifle those teams that build up from the back with his aggression. He is also great when the game gets stretched and unlocks Szoboszlai higher up the pitch. Mac Allister's short passing and tempo control pair well with him and Gravenberch could also partner him for a frighteningly athletic, dynamic pivot.

Chelsea were on the verge of signing Camara in the summer for £47million before Monaco pulled the plug and he has only got better. Reports suggest £58m would be enough to secure his services and the Reds should try and cough up the cash and hijack the transfer if Chelsea turn their attention to Alex Scott instead.

Chelsea's Malo Gusto secured on loan

Speaking of Chelsea, they could be the solution to Liverpool's defensive frailties. As things stand, the left-back position is a concern. Kerkez hasn't adapted since his move from Bournemouth and at times, looks to be a liability.

There is, of course, time for him to adjust, but he has no true competitors pushing him, with Kostas Tsimikas not a long-term solution. There is also concern at right-back that Frimpong could depart, Conor Bradley is sidelined and Ronald Araujo is still only on loan and sometimes needed at centre-half.

Liverpool could do with a versatile, dynamic full-back who could play both positions and be scooped up on a risk-free loan. Malo Gusto can be that player.

The Frenchman's participation has cratered under Xabi Alonso and he has played fewer than 500 minutes so far this season. His profile is a mismatch for the Spaniard's system.

It has been reported that Gusto is seeking an exit and is valued at £67m. He is a great fit for Iraola's tactics - his recovery speed is excellent, he's good at inverting and is a forward-thinking defender - but that fee would be quite the gamble.

Liverpool have spent heavily on the position before and if Gusto doesn't work out, that could be a problem. Yet if the Reds could convince Chelsea to let him leave on loan with an option to buy, as they attempted to do on deadline day, that would suit both parties. Araujo was signed in this way and look how that has worked out.

Iraola's side would get defensive cover at both right-back and left-back as Gusto can play in both positions and Chelsea get his wages off their books and a potential cash influx come the summer if he impresses at Anfield - a win-win.
