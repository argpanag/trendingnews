Benchmark indices Sensex and Nifty significantly fell from day's high to trade in deep red on September 15 due to various reasons, including higher oil prices and global bond yields.

At 2:49 pm, the Sensex was down 665.26 points or 0.89% at 74,116.50, and the Nifty was down 236.20 points or 1.01% at 23,161.90. About 1,042 shares advanced, 3,022 shares declined, and 162 shares were unchanged. Sensex fell 1,300 points from day's high while Nifty was trading near the psychologically important 23,150-mark.

Shares of information technology, pharmaceutical, and select consumer goods companies were in gains while financial services and banking stocks dragged down the 50-stock index.

In the Nifty 50, banking and financial services stocks led the losses, with Shriram Finance, Bajaj Finserv, Bajaj Finance, and ICICI Bank shedding 2-3.5%. HDFC Bank bucked this sector-wide downturn, advancing 1.4%. The stock rose after the bank submitted two names to the Reserve Bank of India for the position of managing director and chief executive officer. Bharat Electronics fell nearly 4% and was the worst hit in the Nifty 50 index.

On the other hand, information technology stocks were the top gainer in the 50-stock index, with HCL Technologies up around 5%, and Tata Consultancy Service, Infosys and Tech Mahindra up 3–4%.

Among sectoral indices, only two were up. The Nifty IT index was the best performer, up nearly 3%, with most of its constituents trading with gains except Coforge, Oracle Financial Services Software, and Persistent Systems. Nifty FMCG was up 0.4% after losses in three consecutive sessions. Emami was up 4%, while shares of peer, Nestle India, were down 1%. ITC was up 0.4%, and Tata Consumer Products was up 0.3%. The Nifty India Defence index fell nearly 5%, extending losses for the fourth straight session. All index constituents fell except for Dynamatic Technologies, which rose nearly 1%.

In the broader market, mid-cap and small-cap indices fell 1.3-1.8%. The Nifty 200 and Nifty 500 indices fell around 1% each. In the Nifty 200, Tata Investment Corporation was among the top gainers, up nearly 12%. In the Nifty 500, Tata Chemical was up nearly 20% and Firstsource Solutions was up nearly 16%.

Key reasons behind markets turning red

1) Higher oil prices

Oil prices remain elevated on concerns that the Middle East conflict could spread further and threaten global energy supplies.

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis launched a new wave of attacks on Saudi Arabia and were digging into positions on the western coast of Yemen along the Red Sea, Yemeni officials said. Gulf Arab states also postponed planned talks with Iran, amplifying concerns the conflict could widen.

Brent crude prices were trading above $108 in Asian trading. Higher crude oil prices are a negative for India, the world's third-largest oil importer.

Accelerating inflationary pressures triggered by rising oil prices have also raised bets of a US rate hike later this week.

Meanwhile, India's retail inflation accelerated further in August as price pressures spread beyond food and transport, strengthening the case for a rate hike by the central bank next month.

2) Higher global bond yields

Benchmark 10-year Treasury yields hit the key psychological level of 5% on Monday for the first time since October 2023, a milestone that analysts say could ripple through the US economy.

The 10-year yield is seen as a global benchmark. In the US, a lot of borrowing is keyed off of the 10-year. Mortgage rates, for example, use the 10-year as a guide. And there's a lot of corporate borrowing that keys off of 10-year yields as well.

3) Technical reason

Analysts said Nifty has to cross 23,600-mark for bullish momentum to return to the markets.

"Going ahead, strength above 23,500 will signal pullback towards 23,650 levels in the coming sessions. Failure to do so will signal some consolidation in the range of 23,230-23,500.

"Immediate bias in the index remains down and a follow-through weakness below last week low 23,231 will open downside towards the short-term support placed around the June low of 23,070 levels in coming week. Only a formation of higher high and higher low on a sustained basis in the daily chart will signal a pause in the current down trend.

"Any pullback attempt will face immediate resistance at Wednesday's gap down area of 23,650. While stiff resistance is placed around 23,800-24,000 levels being the recent breakdown area and 20 days EMA," said Pabitro Mukherjee, Deputy Vice President-Research, Bajaj Broking.

4) India VIX rises

India VIX, the volatility gauge, rose 9% to 13.43, which indicates short-term selling pressure.
