“Imam has no right to premises”: Delhi HC refuses to restore power, water to century-old mosque near PM’s residence

TheTools · 23.10.2026 07:23 · source · permalink

The Delhi High Court on October 15 refused to restore electricity and water connections to the century-old Race Course Masjid, a Waqf-notified property and a Grade II heritage structure notified in 2009, after the utilities were disconnected on October 7.

The mosque is located within the Race Course Club, which spans around 53 acres near the Prime Minister’s residence at 7, Lok Kalyan Marg. The club premises and nearby slum settlements have been at the centre of multiple rounds of litigation over eviction, with the Centre citing “national security” to justify taking possession of the area.

The document shows the mosque and its adjoining graveyard, located behind Safdarjung Tomb in New Delhi, listed in the Delhi Administration’s Gazette notification dated September 8, 1977.

Listed under entry number 18, the property covers two bighas and includes both the mosque and the graveyard.

However, government sources said on Thursday that no eviction notice had been issued to the mosque.

The mosque’s imam, Mohd Haroon, who lives in a hujra on the premises, approached the High Court on October 12 seeking restoration of the utility connections.

On October 8, the Delhi Waqf Board had written to the executive engineer of the New Delhi Municipal Council (NDMC) seeking restoration of the services. The Board said the connections had been cut without “any prior notice or apparent valid reason”, even though all dues had been paid and the mosque had held a lawful electricity connection since 1976.

However, the NDMC refused to restore the utilities on October 15, telling the court that demolition work, including the removal of jhuggi structures, was still underway in the area and that restoring electricity could pose a risk to public safety.

The civic body also argued that the Land and Development Office (L&DO), which is overseeing the demolition and is set to take possession of the land, had not been made a party to Haroon’s petition.

Although the mosque was not a party to the litigation concerning the eviction of around 700 slum dwellers and the Race Course Club, the NDMC’s counsel told the court orally that the mosque premises were “covered” by a High Court judgment dated August 25.

That ruling had paved the way for the eviction of slum dwellers living near the Prime Minister’s residence. The residents have since been rehabilitated in Savda Ghevra, nearly 50 km away.

Justice Saurabh Banerjee dismissed Haroon’s petition, noting that the Waqf Board, which the court described as the “original occupant” of the premises, had not challenged the disconnection of electricity, The Indian Express reported.

The court also observed that Haroon, as an imam appointed by the Board, had no right to the premises and therefore could not claim a violation of his fundamental rights on account of the disconnection.

In its order, the court said the petition raised “serious disputed question of facts”, particularly concerning Haroon’s right, title or interest in the premises and whether the property fell outside the scope of the August 25 judgment.

“All these are way beyond the scope for this court to adjudicate in the present writ petition,” the court said while dismissing the plea.

Notably, the Delhi Waqf Board has not been reconstituted since the tenure of its elected body ended in August 2023. Bureaucrats appointed by the Lieutenant Governor took charge in January 2024 and have continued to run the Board in the absence of an elected body.
