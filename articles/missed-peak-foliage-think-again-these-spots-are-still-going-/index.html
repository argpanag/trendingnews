Missed peak foliage? Think again. These spots are still going autumn strong. - The Boston Globe

TheTools · 30.10.2026 01:45 · source · permalink

So many times people want to find that moment that’s the best. The peak, the time they can tell everybody else about. Not seeking that precise moment is one reason I’ve never liked the term “peak foliage.”

Officially, you might see a map or two showing the best foliage right now is over Cape Cod and the islands this weekend, or in southwest Connecticut, or even the Hudson Valley of New York. If you feel that you’ve missed the season this year, that’s not true. There is still plenty of autumn color to appreciate.

This year’s color started showing up in September, a sign the season would be good. Our nor’easter came early, before it could strip the trees that had already turned in the north. Dry days and crisp autumn nights brought on the color.

It takes a little more time to pause and see the beauty and the russets of an oak tree still fully in leaf, surrounded by the bare limbs of maples and ashes.

This week I’ve been at a retreat in New York, watching the hilly landscape outside the center. It’s not a big mountain, but trees cover it, most of them deciduous, and the palette of color is still vibrant and exciting and alive.

In the morning, the sun hits the browns and yellows and even tinges of green or burgundy. There’s a warmth to the colors this time of year. When a breeze comes, the last of the poplar or aspen leaves dance.

A warm November could keep more leaves on their branches through the month, so there will be plenty of opportunity to see the beauty of the season in one of my favorite times of the year. As more trees lose their leaves, the low sun slants deeper into the woods and lights up winterberry and rose hips drying on the branch.

The white bark of birches will stand out. An evergreen like a yew, lost in the busyness of summer, will push itself forward, shining green, and eventually wear snow on its branches.
