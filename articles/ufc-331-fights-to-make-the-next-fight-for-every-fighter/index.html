UFC 331 fights to make: the next fight for every fighter

TheTools · 21.09.2026 00:05 · source · permalink

Sean Sharaf spent fight week telling anyone with a microphone that he was going to sleep Gable Steveson, and then he did it in twelve seconds against a man priced at -2400. That knockout is the third fastest in Heavyweight history and it detonated the UFC’s most protected project. It is the loudest thing that happened in Los Angeles.

The best fight to make, though, sits at the top of the Flyweight division, and it has been sitting there since June.

What’s next for Joshua Van

Van didn’t just retain, he settled the argument. Five rounds against Alexandre Pantoja, double-digit takedown attempts defended, a knockdown in the fifth and nothing left for anyone to call lucky. The full result reads like a coronation.

The fight to make is Manel Kape. Our model lands on him and it isn’t close. Kape has knocked out four straight ranked opponents and then stepped aside to let this rematch happen, which means he’s owed and he’s fresh. Every other name at 125 has either been beaten by Joshua Van already or hasn’t done enough to be in the conversation. Stylistically it’s the first real test of Van’s chin since he took the belt, because Kape doesn’t need eight minutes of work to end a night, he needs one clean counter. Van’s volume and footwork against a one-shot finisher who has been sharpening that shot for a year.

What’s next for Alexandre Pantoja

He lost the belt and then lost the rematch, and at 36 the road back is longer than he’d like. It doesn’t mean a rebuild fight. The numbers back Tatsuro Taira, a grappler young enough and good enough that beating him restores Pantoja’s standing at the front of the division without the UFC pretending the last twelve months didn’t happen. Pantoja’s scrambling and top control against Taira’s length is a live fight on the mat, and it’s the best available way to find out whether the former champion still has one more run in him.

What’s next for Arman Tsarukyan

He jumped the cage, shook Dana White’s hand and asked for the belt, and he’d earned the right to. The elbow that finished Mauricio Ruffy with five seconds left in the first round was his sixth straight win.

Our model makes the only call available. Justin Gaethje for the title, and if the champion sits out into 2027 as he has indicated then the clean alternative is Ilia Topuria for an interim belt. Arman Tsarukyan has said he wants a December turnaround, and anything that isn’t championship gold at this point is a waste of a fighter in the form of his life.

What’s next for Mauricio Ruffy

One flush elbow after a round he was winning. Ruffy controlled the distance early, stuffed the takedowns and was never out of the fight. He doesn’t need a step down, he needs a name who’ll stand with him. Salahdine Parnasse is the pick. Two heavy-handed strikers who both come to the Octagon to entertain.

What’s next for Sean Sharaf

A man whose roster spot looked shaky won the biggest upset in company history and cleared a hundred grand in bonuses. Now the UFC finds out what they have.

The call is Gokhan Saricam. It’s a step up in level without asking a fighter with six pro bouts to swim in the deep end against a ranked contender, and it’s the sort of matchup that tells you inside one round whether Sharaf’s power travels.

What’s next for Gable Steveson

Four wins and a wrecked reputation, and he walked out of the cage before the decision was read, which won’t be forgotten quickly. Gable Steveson is 4-1 and still one of the best pure wrestlers on the planet, and the model does not send him to the wolves. Max Gimenis is the fight, a Heavyweight who’ll be in range of the takedown and who lets Steveson rebuild the only way that works, which is by dragging someone to the floor and holding them there in front of a crowd that has stopped believing.

What’s next for Patricio Pitbull

A first-round TKO of Dooho Choi and another finish bonus. At this stage of his career every fight needs to be worth something, so the model sends him at Mairon Santos, a young Brazilian on the way up who would take enormous credibility out of a win and who gives Patricio Pitbull a scalp that carries into the top fifteen.

What’s next for Dooho Choi

Fifteen years of war and another quick night. Yadier del Valle is the right call, a fight the Korean can win and one that isn’t a crossroads bout dressed up as a favour.

What’s next for Casey O’Neill

The slickest submission of the weekend and a hundred-thousand-dollar cheque for a first-round armbar on a genuine Women’s Flyweight prospect. O’Neill has been waiting for a night like this for years.

The fight to make is Rose Namajunas. Our model puts them together and the logic is obvious. Namajunas is the biggest name available at 125 who isn’t in the title mix, Casey O’Neill needs one marquee win to turn a bonus into a contendership and the styles fit, because Rose’s movement and counter boxing is exactly the puzzle that exposes whether O’Neill can get the fight where she wants it against elite footwork.

What’s next for Eduarda Moura

Caught cold in a fight she was expected to be competitive in. JJ Aldrich is the reset, a veteran who will make her earn every minute without ending her night early.

What’s next for Marlon Vera

Four losses became a third-round TKO and the Ecuadorian is relevant again. Chito has never been finished in the UFC and now he’s finishing people, and the model’s answer is Bryce Mitchell at Bantamweight. Mitchell is coming off a submission win and is a grappler in a division that punishes one-dimensional fighters. Marlon Vera has spent his entire career making grapplers pay for the privilege, elbows off his back, sneaky submissions, that grinding third-round cardio that breaks people. It’s a fight with a real stylistic collision at its heart and both men need it.

What’s next for Charles Jourdain

Three straight wins ended by a right hand. Jean Matsumoto keeps him in the fights he’s good in, which are the ones where somebody has to swing.

What’s next for the Light Heavyweights

Alonzo Menifield took a split decision off an unbeaten prospect and the model rewards it with Jan Błachowicz, a name fight against a former champion that turns a scrappy win into a career moment. Iwo Baraniewski loses the zero and gets Diyar Nurgozhay, a fellow finisher who’ll be there to hit.

What’s next for the Heavyweights

Robelis Despaigne got the split over Tai Tuivasa and now needs somebody who’ll test the gas tank rather than the chin, which is Kennedy Nzechukwu. Tuivasa is a different conversation. Eight straight losses ties the all-time UFC record and only he can decide whether there’s a ninth. If he fights on, the model’s answer is Elisha Ellison, a bout he can win and the softest landing on the roster.

What’s next for the Featherweights

Joanderson Brito iced Giga Chikadze in the first and the pick is Jamall Emmers, a durable gatekeeper who’ll drag Brito past the opening five minutes for once. Chikadze gets Muhammad Naimov, a kickboxing match he’s still favoured to win and one that doesn’t ask a 38-year-old to rebuild from the bottom.

What’s next for the Middleweights

Edmen Shahbazyan outpointed Brunno Ferreira over three and the model swings for the fences with Bo Nickal, the highest-profile fight available and one where Shahbazyan’s hands and takedown defence are the entire story.

Then there’s the neat one. Our model independently lands on Ryan Gandra against Brunno Ferreira from both sides, and it’s the fight to make. Gandra blew through Ozzy Diaz in the first, Ferreira lost a competitive decision and both are heavy-handed Brazilians with no interest in a judge’s opinion. Book it. Diaz gets Zachary Reese in a matchup that keeps him on television.

What’s next for the rest

Michael Aswell Jr took a split decision and the model sends him at Rodrigo Vera, a fight with enough danger to be worth winning. JooSang Yoo drops a close one and gets Alberto Montes, which is the right level for a man who deserved better on the scorecards.
