Andoni Iraola's pre-Manchester United press conference: 'You don't find bigger games than this - I feel lucky' - Liverpool FC

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:59 · source · permalink

Andoni Iraola understands the importance of a fixture involving Liverpool and Manchester United, with the Reds head coach looking forward to his first taste of the long-standing rivalry.

By Joe Urquhart and James Carroll

Anfield hosts the 219th meeting between the clubs on Sunday afternoon as the Premier League season recommences.

In the opposition dugout, Mauricio Pochettino will take charge of his first game for United, having been appointed during the international break.

Liverpool head coach Iraola sat down with reporters in a pre-match press conference at the AXA Training Centre on Friday to preview the occasion and more.

On the importance of the fixture...

I think it's true what you said: you don't find bigger games than this, honestly. I feel lucky to be here and privileged to be part of these games. I think we have to acknowledge the importance of the game. Also [there are] a lot of ingredients, they have a new manager, new challenges for us, but it's important for us in a lot of different ways. We want to improve our form at home. We've been very good away, but not good at home, and the first chance we have is against United on Sunday.

On the incident with a drone recording United's training session earlier this week and where he has seen the footage...

Yesterday, they were sending me the footage, but I think the important part of this is it shouldn't be legal. It happened to United, but it could happen today to me at today's training. Yesterday you had access to our training, the cameras recorded also 15 to 20 minutes of our training, [so] you know who trained with us, you know who didn't train, we know the same. But it's not the matter of the advantage, I think it's the fact that someone can do this. I think there is a reason we train behind closed doors, because there are some things that you have to respect, and I don't think it is nice for any club, honestly.

On whether it's difficult to know what to expect from United given they have a new manager...

I know their staff have been working with them last week and, for sure, they are going to arrive well prepared. But it's true they have this advantage that we don't exactly know, or at least we don't have the references like we have against other opposition. I think especially the beginning of the game will be important. We have to adapt to certain things and I hope we already have some time with the players and we can manage this situation at the beginning as quick as possible. We know the players very well, we know what they are, the strengths, the weaknesses that they've shown until today and that's not going to change from one day to the other.

On Trey Nyoni's development...

Probably he's used to playing a little bit more advanced with the academy, but it happens to most of the players. We all start being No.10s, wingers, No.9s, and then slowly, slowly you're normally going backwards. I finished as a full-back like this! And with the midfielders normally it's like this, everyone in the academy normally is more advanced and then they find their spot. I think, honestly, he can play everywhere. I think he has played as a six and as an eight for us. If one day we need him more advanced, he's going to be good and he's giving me not dilemmas, he's given me solutions. That is what we need from the players.

On his recent visit to Maghull FC and seeing grassroots football...

Yes, mostly it's because I have another job apart from managing and that is being a father. Sometimes when you can go and see your son play you have to [take the chance] and also see friends that play in front or behind his game. I think it was lovely for me because normally during the weekends I never can go to see my kids play. I also watched netball the day before and watched other things, but it is the things that we can do when this stops. It doesn't stop a lot, so you have to take the advantage of doing these things.

On the squad's recent visit to the LFC Museum and Dominik Szoboszlai's trip to a grassroots team and if it's an effort to connect with supporters...

I think it's something quite natural. I think it is something that Virgil [van Dijk] proposed because we have some new players from the academy and new signings, so it was to learn a little bit more. Actually, I was going to go but we had just two days to prepare for Crystal Palace, I said I cannot afford the luxury to go! And the staff had to stay here to prepare the game too, but it's good. Virgil comes a lot to see the academy. They are playing right now, the U21s started the game, and they were already talking and asking about things. They know all the players because sometimes they train with us and I think it's good that this connection, especially with the academy players, they know their names, they know these faces, they come to train and they are not someone strange. I think we are getting there.

On the approach for this game against United...

I think my players, the club, everyone knows United and everyone knows that the players that play there are very good. You are not facing a team that is 18th in the table, you're facing a much better team and we know this. Probably the precedent is that they've had good results against us lately, maybe it helps in terms of that we will [need to be at] our very best to beat them, especially in this moment with a new manager. For sure they want to start well. So, we know that we need our best game to beat them.
