PSG and Man City tell two very different stories - Taipei Times

TheTools · 13.10.2026 16:00 · source · permalink

Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain have reshaped soccer with the financial backing of Gulf state owners, but there is a stark contrast in the health of those projects ahead of their latest Champions League meeting on Wednesday.

City return to Champions League action for the first time since they were found guilty of breaking Premier League financial rules in a bombshell verdict which could threaten their place in the English top-flight.

PSG strived to follow City’s era of sustained success and are firmly established as Europe’s top dogs after successive Champions League titles to go along with their domination of French soccer.

Photo: Reuters

Abu Dhabi-backed City won the race against Qatar-owned PSG to become European champions first in 2023.

The triumph over Inter Milan in Istanbul that completed a treble of Champions League, Premier League and FA Cup was the pinnacle of City’s rise since a 2008 takeover fronted by Sheikh Mansour, the vice-president and deputy prime minister of the United Arab Emirates.

However, doubt hangs over City’s spoils from the past 15 years unless they can overturn the findings of the commission on appeal.

City were found to have used “sham” commercial deals with Emirati companies to inflate revenue and reduce costs to the tune of more than US$1.2 billion between 2009 and 2018.

No sanction has yet been meted out, but a severe points deduction or even expulsion from the Premier League are among the possible punishments.

The Premier League season has been thrust into turmoil while clubs await the appeal verdict, with City sitting top of the table after six successive wins, including a 3-0 rout of Liverpool on Sunday.

Winning the Champions League could be City’s only way back into the competition next season.

To do so they need to stop PSG’s quest to join a very select club of sides to win the Champions League or European Cup in three consecutive seasons.

Further UEFA action against City is unlikely as the club successfully fended off European soccer’s governing body when they attempted to act on the same leak of documents in 2018 that led to the Premier League’s case.

In 2020, City were found guilty of breaking UEFA’s Financial Fair Play rules, fined 30 million euros (US$33.6 million) and banned from playing in European competitions for two years.

City successfully overturned the ban at the Court of Arbitration for Sport, which ruled that most of the alleged breaches “were either not established or time barred”.

In the early days of Financial Fair Play rules, City and PSG entered into agreements with UEFA in 2014.

The French champions have managed to steer clear of such issues since, with president Nasser Al-Khelaifi becoming an influential figure, as a member of the UEFA Executive Committee and chairman of the European Football Clubs (EFC) organization.

At a meeting of the EFC in Copenhagen last month, Al-Khelaifi showed support for embattled City CEO Ferran Soriano.

“His career is fantastic and Manchester City did a lot of positive things also, but I am not in a position to judge,” said Al-Khelaifi. “I think that they have the right to appeal, so it is not the final decision.”

A crisis which could eliminate City as a Champions League rival for years to come would be another win for PSG on the field, giving Qatar the upper hand in the soft power battle with regional rivals the UAE.
