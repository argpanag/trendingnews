White House launches ‘catch the migrant’ arcade game

TheTools · 05.09.2026 05:23 · source · permalink

Tetris, Snake and others adapted to Trump’s Maga agenda, with one title letting players build border wall

The White House has launched a retro arcade games website in which players can capture migrants and build a border wall.

Five classic computer games have been adapted to incorporate the Make America Great Again (Maga) agenda and made available on the White House website.

A version of the game Snake has been changed to Rio Run, featuring a digital version of Tom Homan, the US border tsar, chasing migrants along the Rio Grande.

The migrants, who appear to be a range of ethnicities, wear orange jumpsuits once they have been caught, and the game counts how many have been “deported”.

Tetris has been adapted to create Build The Wall, where players are encouraged to “protect the border from the coming horde”.

Other games include Trump Savings Tycoon, where players collect money flying through the air to fill their children’s Trump Accounts, a savings plan that offers $1,000 to every child born during Donald Trump’s term.

In Supply Line, players sort healthy from unhealthy food for a school lunch to meet Make America Healthy Again food standards. A version of Flappy Bird sees a bald eagle fly down the National Mall.

The White House account on X posted a video with the Maga logo restyled to look similar to that of Sega, the games company. A caption reads: “Can’t stop winning. Build the wall. Deport. Fill a Trump Account.”

The games have attracted criticism from Democrats.

Matt Lesser, a Connecticut state senator, said: “Heating oil is near an all-time high but, hey, you can play border tsar Tom Homan in a video game.”

Jessica González, a Texas Democratic state representative, wrote: “Trump’s White House literally built video games on a government website before doing anything to solve America’s affordability crisis.”

Adriana Jasso, programme co-ordinator for a border volunteer group, said the arcade-style games showed a fundamental “lack of seriousness” by the White House.

Ms Jasso said: “The cruelty, the extremity of the administration ... is no longer surprising. This presents the issue as if it were a game, when in fact it is not.”

The White House said in a statement: “This administration is laser-focused on ways to innovate and tell the story of the president’s many accomplishments in a way that resonates with every American.

“This is an effort to further contrast between a culture of fun and winning and the dark socialist vision Democrats have for America.”

The Telegraph has approached Sega for comment.
