Ocean overwash continues on Pea Island ahead of Tuesday afternoon's high tide; N.C. 12 remains closed | Island Free Press

TheTools · 20.10.2026 19:39 · source · permalink

Share post:

Ocean overwash was continuing to impact N.C. Highway 12 near the Pea Island Visitor Center on Tuesday afternoon, Oct. 20, ahead of the afternoon high tide at approximately 3:45 p.m., as a prolonged nor’easter continued to affect Hatteras Island.

Farther north, no ocean overwash had been reported along N.C. 12 in the Canal Zone as of Tuesday afternoon, although the area remains vulnerable to erosion and flooding during upcoming high tide cycles.

N.C. 12 remains closed between the Marc Basnight Bridge and Rodanthe after ocean overwash breached protective dunes and flooded the highway near the Pea Island Visitor Center on Monday evening.

According to the North Carolina Department of Transportation (NCDOT), the highway closed at approximately 6 p.m. Monday, Oct. 19, after significant dune erosion and continued overwash made travel conditions unsafe.

In its latest update, Dare County reported that NCDOT crews were repairing and replacing protective sand along the roadway shoulder and rebuilding approximately 1,000 feet of dunes lost overnight.

The agency reported that the highway had not sustained pavement damage near the visitor center, although continued high tide cycles could affect repair efforts and delay the restoration of highway access.

No estimated reopening time has been announced, and motorists are prohibited from entering the closed section of N.C. 12 or driving around barricades.

NWS warns coastal flooding could continue through Thursday

The National Weather Service (NWS) Newport/Morehead City office issued its latest nor’easter briefing on Tuesday morning, warning that additional coastal flooding and ocean overwash remain possible through Thursday, Oct. 22.

According to the NWS, water levels during high tides Tuesday through Thursday could be similar to or higher than those already experienced during the storm, increasing the potential for additional overwash along vulnerable sections of Hatteras Island.

Moderate oceanside flooding is occurring in vulnerable areas, with major flooding possible in some locations. Water levels could reach 2 to 4 feet above ground level in the most exposed areas.

The NWS continues to forecast high coastal impacts across the Outer Banks through Wednesday and into Thursday morning, with conditions expected to gradually improve Thursday afternoon and Friday.

Although the latest forecast indicates that winds and seas should begin decreasing Thursday, coastal flooding could continue even as weather conditions improve.

The agency emphasized that residents should remain prepared for additional high tide cycles and the possibility of prolonged travel disruptions.

Strong winds and dangerous surf continue

Strong north to northeast winds are expected to continue through Wednesday, although the latest NWS briefing indicates that forecast wind speeds have decreased slightly.

Coastal wind gusts of 40 to 45 mph remain possible along the Outer Banks, with the strongest winds expected Tuesday evening and again Wednesday evening.

The persistent winds could result in isolated power outages, downed trees, blowing sand, and difficult travel conditions, particularly for high-profile vehicles.

Dangerous ocean conditions are also expected to continue, with elevated offshore waves and large breaking surf contributing to additional beach erosion and ocean overwash.

According to the NWS, wave heights will remain elevated through Wednesday before gradually subsiding Thursday and beyond.

Minor soundside flooding also remains a concern along southern Hatteras Island through Thursday, particularly in low-lying communities such as Hatteras Village.

Residents in flood-prone areas are encouraged to monitor water levels and move vehicles to higher ground if necessary. Updated water level forecasts are available at water.noaa.gov/wfo/mhx.

Hatteras Island visitor evacuation remains in effect

Dare County’s mandatory evacuation order for Hatteras Island visitors remains in effect, and visitors are prohibited from entering the island until conditions improve and the threat of ocean overwash along N.C. 12 has passed.

Dare County also announced that trash collection on Hatteras Island has been suspended until N.C. 12 is safe for travel. An updated collection schedule will be announced when service can resume.

Meanwhile, Cape Hatteras National Seashore campgrounds and lighthouse climbing operations remain closed. The National Park Service will announce the reopening of off-road vehicle ramps when conditions permit.

Several privately owned oceanfront homes in Rodanthe also remain structurally compromised and vulnerable to collapse due to ongoing erosion and high surf. The National Park Service continues to warn beachgoers to avoid these areas because of unstable structures and potential debris hazards
