Trump turns the GOP’s Ramaswamy ad headache into a migraine | CNN Politics

TheTools · 23.10.2026 08:00 · source · permalink

President Donald Trump has been undermining his own party for months, continuously causing more headaches for Republicans even long after most presidents would’ve taken the Election Day cue to rein themselves in.

So why would that stop on the eve of those elections?

Trump’s latest move is trying to bully Republicans into embracing a highly controversial political ad that seeks to weaponize a situation involving alleged child sexual abuse. The ad has clearly rubbed many GOP officeholders the wrong way, from both a moral and a political perspective.

The ad from Ohio gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy features the estranged mother of his Democratic opponent, Amy Acton, calling Acton a liar. Acton has previously said she hasn’t seen her mother since she was 12 years old, when her mother lost custody of her children and the man she married was indicted for allegedly sexually abusing Acton.

Trump not only defended the ad on Thursday, but he also urged his party to own it and put pressure on Republicans to fall in line. During an Iowa rally that night, he even told the crowd that Ramaswamy had called him asking for advice on whether to take it down, and the president told him he needed to leave the ad up.

“Look upstairs and say thank you, God, very much. I appreciate it,” Trump recalled saying to Ramaswamy.

In a social media post earlier in the day, Trump praised the mother for having the “courage” to speak out against her daughter. And he attacked one of the Republicans who has criticized the ad, Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine, as “disloyal to the Republican Party.”

Trump doubled down even when reporters noted his fellow Republicans’ concern about a situation involving alleged child sex abuse.

He said twice that he knew “nothing about” the backstory between Acton and her mother, but he argued that the mother had to be taken “seriously” regardless.

“Even if it’s a little bit true, you don’t want her as governor,” Trump said.

Looking beyond the fact that he’s clearly willing to dismiss credible abuse allegations, there are some real ironies here.

First, if a mother calling her child a liar is inherently disqualifying, that would be bad news for the man Trump made Defense secretary: Pete Hegseth. Hegseth’s mother wrote him an email in 2018 saying he “belittles, lies, cheats, sleeps around and uses women for his own power and ego,” according to a New York Times report.

(Hegseth’s mother disowned those sentiments and defended her son during his confirmation process.)

Also, Acton’s mother essentially accuses her of being an inveterate liar — including about her political opponent and her upbringing. For Trump, that is really casting stones from a glass house.

But even setting that aside, it’s a highly questionable political tactic. The Ohio governorship is important, but is it critical enough to risk tarnishing the party’s brand more broadly and causing an intra-party feud less than two weeks before an election?

Other Republicans aren’t just standing down here, and it’s permeating outside Ohio. The same afternoon that Trump made these comments, we learned the Iowa GOP Senate candidate Rep. Ashley Hinson became the latest Republican to call for the ad to be taken down. She told ABC News it was “absolutely terrible” for Acton to have “to relive that trauma.”

It’s one thing for Sen. Jon Husted and other Ohio Republicans to speak out against the ad and worry about what it could mean for them; it’s another for a key GOP candidate from outside Ohio’s borders to openly criticize it.

And even if the ad doesn’t blow back on the GOP, is there much to be gained here? Is it worth the risk?

The last time we saw an ad this brutal from family members of a midterm candidate was a 2018 ad featuring Arizona GOP Rep. Paul Gosar’s six siblings all denouncing him. He went from 71% of the vote in 2016 to 68% in 2018 — a small shift likely spurred by a worse environment for the GOP rather than the ad.

Even that kind of shift might not be enough for Ramaswamy, who trails by around five points in the average of recent quality polling. And it’s worth restating: the Gosar ad didn’t involve credible abuse allegations.

Trump is essentially asking his party to own something that it’s clearly very uncomfortable owning, just to help one candidate who might or might not benefit from it. His comments make no bones about this being who the Republican Party is these days, and he’s telling his allies to just grin and bear it.

Trump clearly doesn’t share the qualms about the morality of the ad. And maybe that shouldn’t be surprising from a guy who didn’t seem to get the fuss about the Epstein files and even repeatedly floated a pardon for convicted child sex-trafficker Ghislaine Maxwell. Praising the “courage” of Acton’s mother is very much in line with all that.

It’s too early to see credible polling on what Ohioans or Americans think of the ad yet, but Trump has repeatedly shown that he has no problem with forcing his party to deal with overwhelmingly unpopular things. That includes his tariffs, the Iran war, his taxpayer-funded political ads and his happy talk about inflation and the economy, which Trump has stuck with long after it clearly wasn’t working.

He goes with his gut and his impulse, even when those things have repeatedly failed his party politically of late. Now he’s asking his party to swallow something else unsavory that could drag down key candidates, less than two weeks before Election Day.
