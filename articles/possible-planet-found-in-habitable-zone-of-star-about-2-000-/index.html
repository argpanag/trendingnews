The habitable zone is a term astronomers use to describe the region around a star where conditions could theoretically support liquid water.

A new study published in The Astrophysical Journal identified a candidate planet designated HD 156295 b, which researchers estimate to be between 5.2 and 7.4 times as massive as Jupiter.

Meanwhile, the star itself, HD 156295, is an A-type star, about twice the sun's radius and about nine times as bright.

According to the paper, this sytem is roughly 140 million light years away — right in our cosmic neck of the woods. If the Milky Way Galaxy was the size of the Earth, this distance would be comparable to the distance between New York City and Newark, New Jersey.

The study analyzed observations from NASA’s TESS telescope, which was launched on April 18, 2018, aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from Cape Canaveral, Florida.
