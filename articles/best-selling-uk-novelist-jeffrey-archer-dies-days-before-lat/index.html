Best-selling UK novelist Jeffrey Archer dies days before latest book release

TheTools · 05.10.2026 21:00 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Author

Topic:Author

Jeffrey Archer sold more than 300 million books worldwide. (Reuters: Suzanne Plunkett)

In short:

Jeffrey Archer, the best-selling British novelist and former politician, has died aged 86.

Archer was a best-selling author and sold more than 300 million copies worldwide, with his final novel due to be published this month.

He was also a prominent Conservative politician, who was a senior party official under prime minister Margaret Thatcher in the mid-1980s.

British novelist and former politician Jeffrey Archer, Baron Archer of Weston-super-Mare, has died aged 86.

Archer died "suddenly and peacefully at his home between writing sessions" on Monday, HarperCollins said.

His editor, Kate Elton, called him a "master storyteller and an editor's dream".

The writer was known for pacy thrillers like his 1976 debut Not a Penny More, Not a Penny Less and Kane & Abel, with his final novel, Adam and Eve, due to be published on Tuesday.

The Sun tabloid reported that he had been signing books for fans at an event in London on Sunday.

Jeffrey Archer's wife, Mary, a scientist, stood behind her husband throughout his many scandals.

A life as colourful as the characters in his books

Archer had revealed in 2014 that he had been diagnosed with prostate cancer.

The author led a life as colourful as the characters in his novels, which sold more than 300 million copies, according to his website.

Talented and ambitious, he was a high-flyer who could never quite escape the whiff of scandal.

But in real life the combination led to the downfall of the man who once hoped to become mayor of London.

Jeffrey Archer used his experiences as material for his books. (AFP: Shantanu Das)

He was tried for perjury and perverting the course of justice after a friend admitted he had supplied a false alibi during a libel case the peer brought against the Daily Star tabloid in 1987.

Archer had sued the newspaper over a claim he had slept with a sex worker, winning 500,000 pounds ($948,000) in damages.

MP at 29

He joined the Tories while a student at Britain's prestigious Oxford University and in 1969 won a by-election victory to become a member of parliament at the age of 29.

His parliamentary career came to an abrupt end in 1974 when he was brought to the brink of bankruptcy by the collapse of a Canadian industrial cleaning company in which he had invested.

Archer during his time as a London Mayoral Candidate in 1999. (Reuters: Supplied)

He used his experiences as material for his first book, Not A Penny More, Not A Penny Less, published in 1975.

In 1985, his labours were recognised by then-prime minister Margaret Thatcher, who named him Conservative deputy chairman.

Scandal after scandal

But a year after his appointment, he was forced to resign by what he described as a "lack of judgement" after sending 2,000 pounds to a sex worker.

He maintained he only paid the money to help her escape hounding from reporters by going abroad and sued the Daily Star for suggesting otherwise.

In 1992, he was recommended by Thatcher's successor, John Major, for a peerage, entitling him to sit in the House of Lords.

Archer with his wife and son on the day he was released from prison after serving four years. (Reuters: Peter Macdiarmid)

In 1999, he launched a campaign to become the Tory party's candidate to become the first elected mayor of London.

But months later the claim emerged that he had asked a friend to provide him with a false alibi against the Daily Star.

This forced him to abandon his bid and he was expelled from the party.

In 2001 he went on trial and was found guilty of perjury and perverting the course of justice.

He was sentenced to four years in prison, but was granted parole in 2003.

Archer's wife, Mary, stood behind her husband in his many mishaps.

Famously praised for her "fragrance" by the judge in the 1987 libel trial, she once said: "We've explored the further reaches of 'for better and for worse' than most couples."

Archer retired from the House of Lords in 2024. Dame Mary Archer is a scientist and chancellor of the University of Buckingham.
