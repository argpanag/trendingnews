Manchester City’s draw with Fulham means Arsenal will go eight points clear if they win tonight. Second-placed Brighton play Hull tomorrow.

Mikel Arteta has said Bukayo Saka may not return before Christmas as the Arsenal forward continues his rehabilitation from a thigh injury.

Saka has been out since limping off against Bayern Munich at the end of last month, and it is understood he has yet to resume light training. Arteta revealed more tests during the international break confirmed the England forward did not need surgery and that he was expected to be out for another four weeks.

When asked whether Saka could be fit for the game against Manchester United on 20 December, Arteta was unsure. “I cannot be that specific,” he said. “In four weeks’ time a lot of things can happen. Maybe it is three and then it is five.

“At the end, there are a few elements. One is the healing of that tissue – and we cannot predict exactly when that tissue is going to be in the best condition to push it. Then there is the rehab plan that we have now, what he needs to do in the gym, what he needs to do in terms of treatment and on the field.”

With the temperature in Newcastle forecast to dip to 0C at kick-off, Matthias Jaissle is perhaps hoping that his team’s guests from north London (where a balmy 9C is predicted at 5.30pm) freeze in the Tyneside chill. Otherwise it is hard to envisage his young side overcoming a formidable looking Arsenal on a day when Newcastle’s head coach seeks what would be only his second win in seven games.

Home fans will not merely be hurt by the sight of their much-missed former captain Bruno Guimarães wearing Arsenal colours but decidedly nervous about a fixture that precedes a run of tricky engagements involving a trip to Brighton and home dates with Manchester United and Sunderland.

Jaissle’s cause could be helped should Mikel Arteta name Guimarães in his starting XI and the crowd respond to the once adored Brazilian’s defection by creating the sort of hostile atmosphere that might just galvanise a Newcastle team expected to contain the former Arsenal midfielder Joe Willock.

Meanwhile Jaissle’s defence must be mindful of the threat posed by another Newcastle old boy in Mikel Merino.

Preamble

Arsenal have a unique relationship with the north of England. They’ve won titles at Anfield and Old Trafford, lost them at the Reebok Stadium, and there have been periods in history when every Arsenal trip beyond Watford Gap felt like a referendum on whether they had the necessary minerals.

Mikel Arteta’s side aren’t much different. For years, St James’ Park was a symbol of their inability to take the final step to glory. That changed last September, when Gabriel’s 96th-minute winner set up a different neural pathway that ultimately led to Arsenal becoming champions for the first time since 2004.

Newcastle (A) was a more intimidating prospect 14 months ago than it is today. St James’ Park hasn’t been much of a fortress this season, with Newcastle taking only five points from five home games under Matthias Jaissle. But just as Arsenal changed the narrative last season, so Newcastle can do the same today.
