‘Lucky’ David McCague ‘loving the freshness’ of Scotstown’s new Ulster journey

TheTools · 08.11.2026 14:21 · source · permalink

It’s gone under the radar somewhat that David McCague has yet to lose a game within Monaghan, winning four titles on the spin as manager of Scotstown.

It’s rare you get a chance to reflect, the expectation building and the games coming thick and fast.

Even still, she 44-year-old has never been one to sit and lap up the plaudits, certainly not now, his focus shifting quickly again to the treacherous path beyond the Mick Duffy and toward the Seamus McFerran:

“I didn’t kick a ball, or hand pass a ball, or catch a ball in any of those games. I’m lucky I have a group of players at my disposal performing at a level - a consistent level I suppose - over the last three or four years, that means that they have been successful in Monaghan.

“That has come from their application but also our coaching team. Diarmuid Scullion has a big role to play in that. Barry McKenna (S&C) has got a big role to play in that, and this year Ronan O’Neill has come in. Donal Morgan as well, and Fergus Caulfield.

“Ronan has an encyclopaedic knowledge of the game. He has a lot to offer in all facets of our preparation. He’s been a really good addition, added a real freshness to our group over the last few months.

“Diarmuid has worked through development squads in Derry, minor level in Derry, he’s familiar with one or two of the Magherafelt players. Even over the last few years, we’ve been preparing for Glen, preparing for Newbridge, and in the course of that preparation, we’ve watched Magherafelt a couple of times and seen some of their players up close.

“Obviously it comes into a much sharper focus now, being the next opposition, and I love the freshness of that as well. That’s the beauty of the Ulster club competition.”

Scotstown had a similar period of dominance between 1977 and 1993, winning 11 county titles and four Ulsters in that spell.

From ‘93 until 2013, there was no championship silver to be had.

Undoubtedly, that fuels the fire in the bainisteoir, who came on as a sub 13 years ago, having lived and breathed the An Bhoth blues:

“My experience as a player was one of lean times for a long time. 2013, I was 31 years of age before I won a senior championship medal.

“I can recall winning a senior league back in ‘09, and the tears running down my face watching Nicholas Corrigan lift the Owen Ward Cup. They were tears of joy. I’d finally won something as a Scotstown senior player.

“I know how much that means to every Scotstown senior player. I was lucky that in 2013 Darren (Hughes), Kieran (Hughes), Shane (Carey), Rory (Beggan), Damien (McArdle), and then a young fella called Conor McCarthy came on the scene to supplement the likes of William Carroll, Frank Caulfield, Mark Duffy, Nicholas Corrigan, Brian McGinnity, men like that who soldiered for a long number of years.

“Fortunately we’ve been able to build on that success over the last 13 years.”

Whilst glowing in the praise of his management team, McCague references Enda Muldoon, Stephen Kernan and Gavin Devlin on the opposition line as ‘maybe even more of trump card than we have’.

There’s also an intriguing Derry link with 13 years ago:

“We didn’t beat Newbridge in normal time last year, we didn’t beat them after extra-time. It took penalty kicks, and that was just a level of composure and execution our players showed in those moments.

“Our previous experience was Glen, when they beat us in an Ulster club final. I go all the way back to 2013, when Ballinderry beat us in the first round of the Ulster in Clones, so there’ll be no sense that we’ll be underpreparing for a team like Magherafelt coming out of Derry.

“The standard of club football in Derry is traditionally and always very high.”
