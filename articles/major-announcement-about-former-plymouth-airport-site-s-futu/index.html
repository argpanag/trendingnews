Major announcement about former Plymouth airport site's future

TheTools · 27.11.2026 16:20 · source · permalink

Plymouth City Council is set to take back control of the former airport site at Derriford after 15 years, in a "significant moment in the history of the site".

The council is acquiring the long leasehold interest of the 95-acre site from Plymouth City Airport Ltd, a subsidiary of Sutton Harbour Holdings for £4.387 million. It is also acquiring a 15-acre adjacent parcel of freehold-owned land from the same company for £2.613 million.

Council leader Tudor Evans said: “For over 15 years the city has been in a holding pattern over this site and while there has been a lot of talk, the fact is there is little anyone could do while the land was leased by a third party.

“By owning the land outright, we are finally in control of decision making for the site that is of huge strategic importance to Plymouth. The council has been working hard to acquire this land for a long time and I’m pleased we are now in a position to move forward. It has been a complex undertaking, but this is a significant moment in the history of the site.”

The council already owns the freehold interest in the site. However, Plymouth Airport Ltd has 127 years left on the lease which gives them control of the site.

Holding both the freehold and the long leasehold interests will give the council complete control of future decisions relating to the site and future opportunities that benefit the city.

The council has powers to acquire land for statutory functions or wider purposes such as regeneration and economic development and has a strategic property acquisition fund already allocated in the capital budget to fund the deal.

As with any land acquisition, the council has to prove the purchase is value for money for the tax-payer. To do this the council is required to appoint an independent valuer as defined by the Treasury red-book. The valuer has described the purchase as ‘quite comfortably best value for the council’.

Mr Evans added: “We need to be crystal clear about what this decision is and what it isn’t.

“This is a strategic land purchase and is absolutely not a decision on future use. The land is being bought at market value and any future discussions or specific land deals will be subject to a separate decision that need to reflect the city’s policies. This is protecting the future of this site.”

The site is currently safeguarded and allocated in the adopted Plymouth and South West Devon Joint Local Plan under policy SPT8 Strategic Connectivity and PLY42 Plymouth Airport.

The Joint Local Plan runs until 2034. A safeguarding policy was put in place to give enough time for a private sector-led business plan to be finalised and that initial five years has been extended for another five years.

Work on a new Plymouth Local Plan is now in train, with a study examining future development opportunities in the north of Plymouth underway and its findings will be published as part of the consultation on the proposed Local Plan and supporting evidence base, currently planned for June 2027.

The site will be managed by Plymouth City Council and to offset holding costs such as security, grounds maintenance, insurance and inspections, the Council will actively seek short term ‘meanwhile’ uses.

These will be short term, non-permanent and will not impact any plans for possible future uses of the site. This could include temporary parking or storage, subject to the necessary permissions.
