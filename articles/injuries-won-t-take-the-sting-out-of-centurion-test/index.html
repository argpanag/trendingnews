Injuries won't take the sting out of Centurion Test

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:44 · source · permalink

Injuries have claimed South Africa’s captain and most solid batter along with Bangladesh’s highest capped player and their only threatening bowler at the Wanderers last week. But that shouldn’t take much gloss off the second Test between South Africa and Bangladesh in Centurion, which starts on Monday.

Bangladesh’s first innings of 400 in the first Test, replete with centuries by Towhid Hridoy and Litton Das, proved they can bat on the Highveld. South Africa showed how tough they could hang by taking a lead of 157 and then bowling the visitors out again for 168.

So the removal from the Centurion equation of Temba Bavuma, Mushfiqur Rahim and Hasan Mahmud, while regrettable, shouldn’t detract from the contest. Bavuma’s 68 not out and Mahmud’s 4/118, both in the first innings, were important elements of the Johannesburg match. But the major players in that contest are still in the mix.

Kagiso Rabada looms as the biggest factor of all. He was in furious form for his 5/70 and 3/48 at the Wanderers, and could be even better at a venue where he has taken 59 wickets at an average of 17.96 and a strike rate of 31.5. That makes Centurion Rabada’s most successful home venue on all three counts.

On top of that, Anrich Nortjé’s confirmed return to South Africa’s XI won’t make the Bangladeshi batters’ task any easier.

Aiden Markram will captain in Bavuma’s absence. Markram has led in four other Tests, all of them in Asia. South Africa’s only loss under him was to Pakistan in Lahore in October 2025. Two of his wins were achieved over Bangladesh in Dhaka and Chattogram in October 2024.

Notwithstanding Bangladesh’s competitive performance in Johannesburg, South Africa will be expected to win. In 30 Tests at this ground they have been victorious in 24 with three draws and three losses. And one of those defeats was the leatherjacket Test against England in January 2000, when the corrupt Hansie Cronjé took a cash bribe and a leather jacket — for his wife — from a gambler in exchange for engineering a result in a match that was destined to be drawn after three full days were lost to rain.

South Africa’s first legitimate defeat here was suffered against Australia in February 2014, when Shaun March made 148, Steven Smith 100 and David Warner 115, and Mitchell Johnson took 7/68 and 5/59. Then India won in Centurion in December 2021, when KL Rahul scored 123 and Mohammed Shami took 5/44 and 3/63.

In Hridoy and Litton, Bangladesh have batters who can hold their own in any company. But they have no bowlers of the class of Johnson and Shami. And this is more than anything a bowler’s ground. That makes runs here especially valuable, so it’s worth pointing out that of the 29 players who have scored a century here 11 have been foreigners — four of them from Asia.

But it’s the ball that wins in Centurion, and South Africa have the better bowlers.

When: November 23 to 27, 2026; 10am local/8am GMT/1.30pm IST

Where: Centurion

What to expect: A greentop presented itself the day before the match. But that might not mean much 24 hours later. In any event, this ground is every fast bowler’s friend. Thunderstorms have been predicted for each afternoon of the match, but that might also not mean much since they mostly pass quickly.

Team news:

South Africa: Temba Bavuma is out with a calf strain. He will be replaced by Tony de Zorzi. Anrich Nortjé comes in for Dane Paterson.

Confirmed XI: Aiden Markram (capt), Marques Ackerman, Tony de Zorzi, Zubayr Hamza, Matthew Breetzke, Ryan Rickelton, Wiaan Mulder, Marco Jansen, Simon Harmer, Kagiso Rabada, Anrich Nortjé.

Bangladesh: Mushfiqur Rahim and Hasan Mahmud will miss the match with groin and elbow injuries. Khaled Ahmed has been parachuted in and is expected to play. As is Shoriful Islam.

Possible XI: Tanzid Hasan, Mahfijul Islam, Mominul Haque, Najmul Hossain Shanto (capt), Litton Das, Jaker Ali, Towhid Hridoy, Mehidy Hasan, Khaled Ahmed, Shoriful Islam, Ebadot Hossain.

What they said:

“From a batting point of view in Centurion, you’re always going to be challenged up front. Then it can become a really nice place to bat. From a bowling point of view, you can take wickets in clusters.” — the lowdown on Centurion, courtesy of Aiden Markram.

“Whoever comes in for [Mushfiqur] will step up and do what we ask them to do. That’s why they’re here. Hassan is gone but we have four other fast bowlers, so somebody has to take his workload.” — Phil Simmons looks to make the best of an unfavourable situation.
