Lawson strong in Malaysian practice, logs two top 10 finishes

TheTools · 04.10.2026 16:44 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Liam Lawson has enjoyed a strong first day at Formula One’s Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, logging top 10 finishes in both Friday practice sessions at the Sepang International Circuit.

In his first taste of driving a Formula One car in Malaysia – after three wins from three races in the 2018 F3 Asian Championship – Lawson led Racing Bulls’ efforts on the first day, finishing eighth in the day’s first session (FP1) and ninth in the second (FP2).

After the Kiwi’s best time of the day, 1m 38.496s in FP2, he was 0.968s off the pace set by Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc in extreme track conditions that exceeded 60C at one point.

In the battle against Alpine to finish fifth in the constructors’ championship, Lawson also had a mixed day against Pierre Gasly – 0.391s behind in FP1, but 0.092s clear in FP2.

Lawson twice lapped faster than his Racing Bulls teammate, a week after he was taken out by Arvid Lindblad in the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. Lindblad will also start from the back of the grid regardless of where he qualifies, after taking on a new power unit for this weekend’s race.

Despite Leclerc’s best lap of 1m 37.528s, Red Bull’s Max Verstappen was arguably the driver who impressed the most. After topping FP1, Verstappen was only 0.257s back in FP2, despite only running the medium tyre, compared to Leclerc’s best time coming on the faster softs.

Verstappen’s Red Bull teammate Isack Hadjar finished 0.099s back from Leclerc as the next-best in FP2, before the day was brought to a premature end when Audi’s Gabriel Bortoleto stopped on track.

Previously scheduled to take place in April, the original Bahrain Grand Prix has been moved to Malaysia because of the conflict in the Middle East, which has also cancelled the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix and raised doubts over the Qatar and Abu Dhabi grands prix to come.

Regardless, with Sepang being one of the widest tracks in the world, multiple drivers made their excitement at returning to Malaysia clear, given the prospect of more overtaking over the course of the race weekend.

Yesterday’s practice sessions were raced in the dry, despite the threat of rain on the day, and a 90% chance for tomorrow’s grand prix.

Qualifying for the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia begins at 9pm today.

Earlier, Lawson logged the eighth-fastest time in FP1, putting in a best lap of 1m 39.106s to sit 1.586s off the pace set by Verstappen, but 0.091s clear of Lindblad in ninth.

Lawson recording the eighth-fastest time came after neither McLaren car placed inside the top 10, with Oscar Piastri 11th and Lando Norris 12th, both more than one second off the pace.

Track temperatures of more than 50C in FP1 disrupted drivers’ sessions because of excessive tyre wear, as well as repeated trips to the pit stop to cool their cars.
