Pope Leo to meet with former and current cycling champions at Vatican - Vatican News

TheTools · 19.11.2026 15:58 · source · permalink
  • Pope Leo XIV
  • sport
  • peace
  • Vatican

By Kielce Gussie

On Monday, November 23, Pope Leo XIV will meet with representatives of the world of cycling at the Vatican, as part of an event promoted by the Dicastery for Culture and Education in collaboration with the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI).

The group includes one hundred distinguished guests—from legendary figures and current champions of men’s and women’s cycling, including para cycling, to institutional figures from different Federations and organizers of the three men’s and women’s Grand Tours (Tour de France, Giro d’Italia and La Vuelta Ciclista a España) and other organizers from every continent.

Among the many former and current cyclists who are scheduled to attend are Eddy Merckx (BEL), winner of five Tour de France titles, five Giro d’Italia titles, one Vuelta a España title and three UCI Road World Championship titles; Greg LeMond (USA), two-time UCI Road World Champion and the first American to win the Tour de France, which he won three times; Christopher Froome (GBR), winner of all three Grand Tours; and Paul Seixas (FRA), a 20-year-old French cyclist considered one of the most promising talents in international cycling.

Cycling as a way to promote peace

Ahead of the event, the President of the Union Cycliste Internationale, David Lappartient, underlined the deep historical ties between cycling and the Catholic Church ranging from events organized by parish communities, to cycling teams inspired by Gospel values, to a number of papal audiences and, most recently, the 2025 Giro d’Italia’s passage through the Vatican.

The event this year, President Lappartient explained in a press release, is an opportunity to reinforce the commitment of cycling, as a sport, to promoting peace and intercultural dialogue between people.

Moreover, Cardinal José Tolentino de Mendonça, Prefect of the Dicastery for Culture and Education, recalled how this event comes after Pope Leo XIV’s message on sport in the letter “Life in Abundance.”

The Prefect stressed that today cycling forms part of our society’s cultural identity and represents an important source of values for humanity.

At the end of the audience, a short ceremony will take place, where the UCI will symbolically donate bicycles to Caritas Rome.

This initiative was organised by Athletica Vaticana, the Vatican's multi-sport association, which is also a member of the UCI.
