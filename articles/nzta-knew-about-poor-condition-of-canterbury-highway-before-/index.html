NZTA knew about poor condition of Canterbury highway before fatal crash - coroner

TheTools · 28.09.2026 00:29 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Crime and Justice

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._g.pKhUMkRnoOZwX4iq2fhLAqba5Rz0jb3mINKRqASmxf0/crime-and-justice/1620670%2Fnzta-knew-about-poor-condition-of-canterbury-highway-before-fatal-crash-coroner

The NZ Transport Agency failed to take adequate steps to improve the poor condition of a stretch of Canterbury highway with a high crash rate in the weeks before a woman died, a coroner has found.

Kellyanne Pattinson died when her car was struck by an oncoming truck at a bend on State Highway 1 near Pareora, south of Timaru, in January 2018.

The Bidfood truck had swerved across both northbound lanes and the centreline before colliding with the 31-year-old's hatchback.

In findings released on Monday, coroner Alexandra Cunninghame said there was no evidence that driver fault was a factor in the crash. Rather, the northbound lane where the crash happened was in a "poor state" because of a flushed and slippery surface.

"The northbound passing lane and southbound lane were in reasonable condition, but the northbound left lane was poor," she said.

"It showed signs of 'excessive tar bleed', meaning tar had risen to the top of the road and created a 'flushed' road surface. A flushed road surface results in slippery patches when the road is wet, and sticky patches when it is hot."

A four-kilometre stretch of State Highway 1 north and south of Pareora was under review by the Transport Agency (NZTA) at the time of the crash after locals had raised safety concerns about a nearby intersection.

According to NZTA data, there were five crashes within 250 metres of the crash site between 2012 and 2017.

One crash resulted in serious injuries, no-one was hurt in three crashes, and the other was a minor crash.

All of the them were described as "loss of control" crashes, and four out of the five involved a northbound vehicle, the report said.

NZTA arranged for the road to be resealed in early November 2017 before "chip loss and surface bleeding" was found in the southbound lane later that month.

"A contractor remedied this issue in December 2017 by applying dry chip to the southbound lane. At this time, the contractor noted that there was a loss of texture in the northbound left lane," the coroner said.

In the month before the crash, a contractor had informed NZTA about the deterioration of the northbound left lane, the report said.

"I am satisfied that NZTA was aware of the poor condition of the northbound left lane at the time of the crash," Cunninghame said.

Apart from a "slippery when wet" sign installed for northbound lanes about half-a-kilometre from the corner, no further action was taken by the transport agency.

"Given the documented concerns about the road, I am not satisfied that NZTA's earlier steps were adequate to ensure public safety," the coroner said.

Three days after the crash, the northbound left lane at the crash site was resealed as a temporary measure until a long-term fix for excess bitumen was found.

In submissions to the Coroners Court, NZTA said the site continued to be subject to regular crash monitoring, skid resistance testing and maintenance planning as per the agency's highway management responsibilities.

Planned safety upgrades between Timaru and St Andrews, including the installation of median barriers and intersection improvements, were originally due to be completed in February 2026 but did not progress because of changes in government funding priorities.

"Had the project proceeded, I would have been satisfied that adequate steps had been taken by NZTA to ensure public safety and to reduce the chance of further deaths occurring at the crash site," the coroner said.

The coroner recommended NZTA complete the work.

"While I accept that funding priorities may be a matter outside of NZTA's direct control, I make the above recommendation as a way of ensuring continued focus on the need to improve safety on a stretch of road which has claimed a life," Cunninghame said.

The coroner found no evidence that Bidfood's training procedures, or any failure of the truck driver to follow them, contributed to Pattinson's death.

Since the crash, Bidfood had continued to improve its training programme with a focus on improved safety, the report said.

Response from NZTA

In a statement, NZTA said it acknowledged the coroner's findings and recommendations and its inability to progress planned safety upgrades to the stretch of highway.

"The State Highway 1 Timaru to St Andrews safety improvements project, which the coroner recommends that NZTA implements, is a major project in size and scope, and we have so far been unable to advance it due to funding availability," a spokesperson said.

"The progression of the project in future will be subject to government prioritisation and funding availability."

The agency said the coroner had noted that NZTA did act on concerns at this location before the fatal crash, including plans to reseal the road, actions to remedy chip loss and surface bleeding at the crash site, as well as signage to address the risk of slippage on the road.

"We do acknowledge, however, the coroner's view that more could have been done prior to this crash.

"The coroner has also noted the work done by NZTA and its contractor since the crash, including resealing, testing of skid resistance at the crash site to ensure compliance, further signage and the installation of audio tactile profiled (ATP) road markings, or 'rumble' strips, on the centreline and the edge lines to help reduce the risk of run-off and head-on road crashes."

RNZ approached Transport Minister Chris Bishop's office for comment.

His office said specific projects such as the State Highway 1 Timaru to St Andrews safety improvements project were an operational decision for NZTA and its board, through the National Land Transport Fund.
