Damian Lillard and Portland thankful to be reunited | NBA.com

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:51 · source · permalink

Top Stories

As Thanksgiving nears, Lillard and the city of Portland are grateful to be together again.

Shaun Powell

Damian Lillard is averaging 20.6 points per game since returning to Portland.

There were 1,309 days and 256 games between the time Damian Lillard left the Portland Trail Blazers and when he returned, and in retrospect, the player and team should’ve counted every one of them.

His final game before departing was March 22, 2023, a win at Utah. Lillard scored 30 points with 12 assists. He sat the final 10 games that season with calf tightness. The Blazers lost nine of them, which frustrated him; the last by 56 points to the Warriors.

Until then, Lillard started every game in which he played in a Blazers uniform, 830 straight, including playoffs. That summer, the Blazers used the No. 3 overall draft pick on Scoot Henderson and projected him as their point guard of the future. That fall, Lillard was officially the point guard of the past when he requested a trade and was sent to the Milwaukee Bucks.

His return with the Blazers was last month, a breezy 41-point win over the Phoenix Suns. Lillard had 25 points. That wasn’t as meaningful, though, as the blissful homecoming embrace between the player and fans inside Moda Center that night in Portland.

The city, for various and obvious reasons, needed Dame. And Dame, for various and obvious reasons, needed Portland.

After two-plus years apart, it shows.

“I have a lot of joy,” he said.

The team, city and player were always meant for each other, from the time he was drafted in 2012, and this relationship is emphasized now, when all seems right again. Dame, at age 36 and coming off a year’s inactivity following left Achilles tendon surgery, isn’t as frisky as the shot-making supernova who’d casually pull up from 30 feet, though close enough. As for Portland, there’s a buzz once more about basketball, the only game in a quirky town defined by its infatuation with hoops. And the Blazers are winning, with generous help from Dame.

“It’s all a journey … that’s what we spend our entire lives working towards, getting to continue to push on this journey,” he said.

Nobody cares anymore why and how he left after 11 years together, because this reunion seems permanent and so far, perfect.

“The grass isn’t always greener but you have to learn that,” said former Blazers coach Terry Stotts, who coached Lillard longer than anyone. “He missed the comforts and continuity of his life in Portland. It was the realization that home is home.”

Lillard’s long-lasting impact on Portland

These relationships between a professional athlete and the city he reps can be tricky. There’s the facade of loyalty and love for one another, except those bonds are usually conditional. Loyalty and love are dictated by money and performance — if the player feels he isn’t compensated fairly or doesn’t like the direction of the team, he leaves; if his contributions to the team dip, he’s sent packing. Either way, the cord is cut along with the emotional tie, and in the end, there really wasn’t purity to the relationship after all.

Lillard and Portland are among the few duos to defy those rules. They’ve connected certainly due to his talent — he’s a nine-time All-Star — but also their personalities. Portland is folksy and treats the Blazers like neighbors. Lillard is grounded given his celebrity status, making it easy for locals to see him as one of them. Whereas his teammates mostly bail to bigger and bolder offseason destinations, Lillard, born and raised in Oakland, calls Portland “home.”

So his roots and investment run deep within the community. Molly Ouche discovered this early on, in 2017, when she approached and asked him to help rouse the students at Parkrose High School, where she’s the principal. He made an appearance and triggered a youth development program.

“This isn’t going to last, maybe just a year,” she remembers thinking about his cooperation. “He’ll come and go. It’s not sustainable.”

It’s still running, nine years later, and has since added two other Portland high schools and a middle school. The RESPECT program encourages, identifies and rewards respectful behavior from students. The core values are Show Up, Work Hard and Be Kind.

“He has made regular appearances in different capacities,” said Ouche. “He has done whole school assemblies and small groups with our leadership kids. Every spring we have senior commitment day. He came last year and just kind of hung out, took a million pictures. He gave a short speech but it wasn’t about his career. It was about celebrating them.”

During the pandemic, he made video appearances and never missed a beat; instead, he asked: “How do we make this work?”

In a more modern method of connecting with teens, he salutes deserving students with a social media shout-out, and the cache that comes with that — and Dame’s substantial following — is priceless.

“One student had just lost her grandmother and father and said she was so happy Dame took the time to do that,” Ouche said.

It’s not just his program that endears Lillard to the community; his fingerprints are around town, helping local businesses and others, often impromptu, asking nothing in return.

During his time with the Bucks, Lillard returned to Portland in the offseason to continue running his camp. There was a twist: He handed out Bucks gear, although that would change in the subsequent summers.

“I don’t know if he knows the depth of his impact or hears the stories about him that I get to hear,” Ouche said. “There’s just something in him that keeps him committed to this community. I’m sure it fills his cup or else he wouldn’t be giving as much.”

Evidently, Lillard needs the soothing of the people as much as the people need him. It cuts both ways.

“Everyone loves him,” Ouche said, “and he hasn’t given anyone any reason not to.”

As Trail Blazer icons go, Lillard differentiated himself from the only other two who compare, Bill Walton and Clyde Drexler, just by returning. Walton, a former MVP and centerpiece of the franchise’s only championship team, left in a huff after accusing the team medical staff of misdiagnosing his foot issues. Drexler maneuvered his way out after failing to win a championship and found a safety net in his hometown of Houston, next to Hakeem Olajuwon. Walton nor Drexler made Portland their home again.

“I believe he’s the greatest Blazer ever and it’s more than just the points, it’s what he does for the city,” said Stotts. “He’s ingrained in the fabric of the city. Portland appreciates that. Nobody begrudged him when he left to go to Milwaukee and they embraced him when he came back. He’s very open about his feelings for the city and what that means to him.”

And so the silver lining of the Achilles injury Lillard suffered in the 2024-25 season with the Bucks was this: Because he needed a full season to recover, Milwaukee needed to pivot. Therefore, by cutting him loose prior to last season, they essentially returned him to sender.

“I thought it was a necessary experience,” Lillard said about his Milwaukee detour. “It was an experience that I needed.”

Lillard never felt totally at ease in Milwaukee, though; he has indicated as much. His children stayed behind in Portland. The chemistry between him and Giannis Antetokounmpo, whom he respects, was clunky at times and the Bucks never advanced beyond the first round of the playoffs (though they did win the 2024 Emirates NBA Cup). By all indications, his most fulfilling moment happened when Milwaukee visited Portland the first year after the trade.

The volcanic reaction by the sellout crowd to his introduction and tribute … that’s when Lillard knew how badly he missed Portland and vice versa.

“It was probably the most moving experience you could have at an NBA game,” said Ouche. “The love for Dame, you could feel it. Incredible to be there, incredible to see.”

Iconic moments in Portland

Actually, that was matched by two previous fan eruptions, years earlier, each the result of twin Dame signature moments — dramatic walk-off shots to win first-round playoff series for the Blazers.

In 2014, the Blazers trailing the Rockets by two in Game 6 with eight-tenths of a second left, Lillard raced around screens, clapped for the inbounds pass and improbably swished a catch-and-shoot 3-pointer at the buzzer.

Lillard's game-winning 3-pointer in Game 6 pushes Portland past Houston in the first round of the 2014 playoffs.

In 2018, the final seconds clicking in Game 5 against Oklahoma City, Lillard lofted a 37-footer over Paul George and waved the Thunder bye-bye before being dogpiled by teammates.

Lillard on his game winner vs. OKC.

“What was cool about that shot against OKC was the clock was counting down and you didn’t know what would happen, but the anticipation of what was going to happen was building, because the ball was in his hands,” said Stotts. “To do that twice in your career is amazing.”

Lillard’s grasp of meeting the moment makes him appointment viewing. The stretchy shooting range, supreme confidence in himself and his trademark wrist-tap to reveal what time it is all help set him apart.

“I know what I bring to a team,” he said. “I’m a big shot-maker.”

New era with new stars

What’s ahead for Lillard, for the Blazers? The scent is delicious. The Blazers ended a six-year playoff dry spell last spring, and since added not only Lillard but Ja Morant, who arrived seeking rejuvenation.

What’s different the second time around is Lillard doesn’t always need to be the front man in order for the band to harmonize. Deni Avdija is an All-Star and Portland’s leading scorer, and with Jrue Holiday the Blazers have four players with All-Star pedigree: The three aforementioned … and Dame. This isn’t the 33-win, desolate team he left.

“It’s just a matter of playing for each other, doing the right things, playing the right way,” he said. “If our mentality is right, we can really be a tough time for everybody. We can be a dangerous team.”

He’s refreshed after a year recovering from injury and also giving his body a break. His shooting efficiency will require more time for sharpening, but opposing teams will leave the fifth all-time 3-point scoring leader open at their own risk — as the Knicks did in the closing seconds last Saturday and Dame drilled a game-tying 3.

Curiously, his first 30-point outburst since March of 2025 happened last week when he dropped 33 against his old team, the Bucks. It wasn’t personal; it was due from someone approaching 23,000 career points.

“I think the thing I’m most happy with or most excited about is I know that I’m not completely back all the way connected,” Lillard said. “I’ve been able to be as productive as I have without fully getting all the way back in form. I know there’s more to come.”

The bigger-picture view, though, says Damian Lillard is whole again. Here in his anticipated return, he’s rediscovering what is sentimentally acknowledged this time of year: The best place to be on Thanksgiving … is home.
