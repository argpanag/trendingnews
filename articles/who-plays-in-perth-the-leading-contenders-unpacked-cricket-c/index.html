Who plays in Perth? The leading contenders unpacked | cricket.com.au

TheTools · 27.11.2026 07:28 · source · permalink

We've broken down the selection picture for Australia's series opener against New Zealand

After several weeks of tumult, Australian cricket will return to more familiar territory this weekend when selectors pick an enormously consequential squad for the first Test against New Zealand, beginning December 9 at Perth Stadium.

It heralds the beginning of the most intense period of Australia's taxing 2026-27 run of matches. Pat Cummins' men will play 10 Tests in 13 weeks, facing the Kiwis four times across December and early-January, then immediately heading to India for five Tests, before returning for the 150th Anniversary game at the MCG in mid-March.

The stretch could hardly come at a more intriguing juncture. Putting aside the not insignificant toll of eight-and-a-half-year-old wounds being dredged up, Australia's selectors are grappling with the more immediate issue of defeats in four of their past seven Tests.

A kinder analysis of that run would exclude a coin-flip of an MCG contest against England, played on a dicey pitch after the Ashes were already won. Even so, there have been three losses from their past five starts – including one against Bangladesh.

Given Australia fielded XIs without a single player under the age of 30 in two of those five Tests, they are now under immense pressure to show their recent mis-steps were blips rather than early warning signs pointing to terminal decline.

With that in mind, we've broken down the selection picture for Perth.

Batters, Wicketkeepers and Allrounders

Batters, Wicketkeepers and Allrounders

From the top seven that featured in the Aussies' most recent match in Cape Town, there will be at least one change with the omission of Marnus Labuschagne already confirmed. Steve Smith will return after his neck issue ruled him out of that third Test against the Proteas, and him accepting a new role at No.3 would be a neat solution in the short term. That would allow Cameron Green, Josh Inglis and Alex Carey to remain at four, five and six respectively. In that formation, incumbent No.7 Cooper Connolly would likely have the edge to hold his spot over Beau Webster. That equation could change if Green, who is recovering from a concussion and has also battled abdominal and shoulder problems recently, is unable to bowl or needs to be eased back into that discipline given he has not sent down a ball in a match since August.

There are two ways of viewing a top three of Matthew Renshaw, Travis Head (the incumbent openers) and Smith. One is that it leaves a brittle batting unit that has been rolled for 99 and 111 in its two most recent Tests too top-heavy; Head and Smith have been Australia's two most productive batters over recent years, while Renshaw was their leading run-getter in South Africa.

A more positive outlook would see it as locking in a top three that could conceivably be deployed both in Australia and India. An expectation of less seam-friendly home pitches this summer – a trend that has played out through the first half of the Sheffield Shield season – may also make it less of a gamble.

If Smith were to return to No.4 instead, Australia would need to either shuffle the pack or call in a fresh face. Green was deployed at three when he wasn't bowling last year, while Inglis or Connolly could also conceivably make the jump up the order; both have experience at first drop in white-ball cricket. But moving Green or Inglis up risks jeopardising their recent successes through the middle-order. Such a move would also force Carey down to No.7, where he averages 30.66 versus 45.18 at No.6. Connolly has less bargaining power than Green or Inglis, but his deployment at No.4 in Western Australia's first innings against Tasmania this week marked only his third first-class dig batting in the top four.

Another avenue Australia could take to accommodate a Smith return to four is to blood a fresh top-order player, either opening or at three. A new opener could also allow one of Head or Renshaw to go down the order. On Sheffield Shield form alone, Sam Konstas and Jack Clayton are the two standout candidates having both averaged north of 68 this Shield season, and above 41 since the start of the 2024-25 season. Only Carey (59.70) has averaged more than Clayton (48.56) in that latter stretch, though there is a question of whether Clayton making the bulk of those runs at No.4 should qualify him to bat in the top three in Test cricket. Konstas on the other hand has predominantly opened in that time.

It seems strange to call Jake Weatherald a "left field" option given he holds a national contract but the manner of his axing after the Darwin defeat to Bangladesh meant few expected him to contend for a spot so soon again. The question for selectors will be whether they believe his technique, overhauled after his introduction to Test cricket against England last summer, will hold up as well against NZ as it has in bringing him back-to-back Shield tons.

None of the other options with Test experience have made strong cases. Kurtis Patterson scored a ton against a Jhye Richardson-led WA attack in Perth last month and batted valiantly for a draw on a turning fourth-day SCG pitch this week, but has not exactly barged the door down. Peter Handscomb is averaging 49.14 this season and has been a strong performer over a long period of time. His strong play against spin will surely have him in the frame for India, but he seems unlikely to be a candidate to bat in the top three on a bouncy Perth pitch. Nic Maddinson has made single-figure scores in six of his past 10 first-class innings, a span which includes his surprise Test recall, while Nathan McSweeney was averaging only 22.42 this Shield season after making 7 in SA’s first innings against Victoria this week.

Of the uncapped options, Jason Sangha was the best performed batted on the Australia A tour of India but he too has had a rotten run at Shield level; after scores of 32, 0, 12, 0, 1, 9, 22, he was out 91 to what appeared a poor umpiring decision against the Vics. Recent T20I debutant Sam Harper is a dark horse having had a phenomenal '25-26 Shield season with the bat last summer, adding a ton against India A before joining the Test squad as a back-up keeper.

Australia may well need one of these players in their squad for Perth even if they do not intend on picking them in the XI.

Fast Bowlers

Fast Bowlers

The cartel have been firmly in the spotlight for reasons other than fast bowling. On the field, the next few weeks shape as hugely important for the ageing trio to reassert their primacy.

There are of course no questions over Mitchell Starc, whose extraordinary career twilight has seen him take 78 wickets in his last 14 Tests. But January will bring his 37th birthday, a milestone after which no modern-day Australian Test quick (apart from Scott Boland) has excelled. Pat Cummins and Josh Hazlewood on the other hand could be considered to have points to prove going into this summer. Cummins averaged 55 with the ball in South Africa; as much as 328 career scalps at 22.51 gives him more than a few credits in the bank, the fact a bowler with Boland's record (87 wickets at 18.44) is missing out regularly means there is little room for underperformance. It's a similar story for Hazlewood. His two wickets in Gqeberha (where he was ill) and Cape Town cost 85 apiece, but that was after he snared 67 at 15 in his previous 14 Tests, including nine in Durban. Both need to show their so-so showings in consecutive defeats were outliers.

While all four are certain to travel to Perth, it seems far-fetched that any of them would expect to play in all 10 Tests given their advancing years (Cummins is the youngest at 33, Boland the oldest at 37). Recent history suggests Australia will carry at least one extra quick through the NZ Tests, while George Bailey and co. will be considering their reinforcements beyond that.

Michael Neser is the safest pair of hands, particularly with Queensland teammate Xavier Bartlett having recently undergone knee surgery, though Neser's brand of medium-fast nippers might not fit into a bowling attack without, say, Starc in it. Jhye Richardson and Brendan Doggett both played against England last summer and have the extra ball speed selectors have made no secret of wanting. Of the Test squad's likely contenders, those two been the best performed in the Shield so far this season with 15 wickets apiece. Sean Abbott, Henry Thornton and Liam Hatcher have all had useful performances too; Abbott was picked over Neser and Doggett in an initial squad for last summer's Perth Test, while Thornton and Hatcher have both featured for Australia A in recent times. As has Fergus O'Neill, though the Victorian has not shot the lights out so far this Shield season.

Then there is the enticing next generation of quicks. Mahli Beardman and Callum Vidler (both 21) are considered the most worthy longer-term successors to the Big Three but the former has never played first-class cricket and hurt his quad after playing two ODIs in a week, while the latter is recovering from a stress fracture in his back. Tom Straker (also 21) is believed to be highly rated by selectors and has proved more physically resilient, if not as explosive, as his fellow 2024 U19 World Cup winning quicks.

Spinners

Spinners

Nathan Lyon's place is of particular interest. After Cape Town, head coach Andrew McDonald reasserted the veteran remained firmly in their thinking for the series opener against New Zealand. Of the traditional Australian Test venues, Perth Stadium has been his most helpful with its extra bounce helping him capture 29 wickets at 21.31 from six Tests there.

But the recently turned 39-year-old has been left out of three of the past nine Tests he has been available for, facing stern competition from Boland in particular. Both played in last year's Perth Test; Boland took four second-innings England scalps, while Lyon bowled two overs for the match. The potential addition of spinning allrounder Connolly to the equation would give Australia some cover in the slow-bowling department, though the left-armer's career return of six first-class wickets at 66.33 underlines that he is not yet a frontline option.

The question for Perth, and the rest of the home summer, will be whether Lyon is the automatic choice when Australia do pick a spinner. In two of his past three first-class games an opposition spinner has taken match-defining 10-wicket hauls: Simon Harmer against Australia at Newlands, Queensland's Mitchell Swepson against NSW at the SCG. Indeed, Lyon has 11 wickets at 43.18 in those three games, while his spin counterparts have taken 30 at 16.17 in the same conditions.

Swepson is presently the Shield season's leading wicket taker with 24 at 16.91, while finger-spinners Todd Murphy, Matthew Kuhnemann and Corey Rocchiccioli have all made worthy cases to succeed Lyon. At a minimum, all four stand strong chances of touring India. It would be an exceptionally bold call to move on from a man with 577 Test wickets before then.
