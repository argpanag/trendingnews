Fashion retailer General Pants Co in voluntary administration

TheTools · 19.11.2026 03:04 · source · permalink

Australian-owned fashion retailer General Pants Co has entered voluntary administration amid financial strife.

The company's financial troubles had been forecasted and it had reduced its physical stores from 60 to 35.

There were currently four stores still open in New Zealand - three in Auckland's Newmarket, Mānawa Bay, Sylvia Park and one store in Wellington.

The company reportedly employed about 500 people across both countries.

Jason Tracy and Glen Kanevsky of Alvarez & Marsal, a global professional services firm known for its work in turnaround management, were appointed voluntary administrators by the group's Sydney-based owners Alquemie Group.

In a statement to local media, the company said it had been trying a range of options to address falling sales and less foot traffic through its physical stores.

"The recent Australian retail trading environment, and rapid structural changes in the sector, including the growing dominance of offshore online-only retailers, has seen General Pants progressively reduce its physical store footprint," Alquemie Group said in a statement.

"To address this, Alquemie Group actively focused on a range of options for General Pants including a sale process or significant capital injection to help it better compete against large scale competitors.

"None were ultimately viable."

1News has contacted General Pants Co for comment.

Its stores and website were continuing to operate.

According to Heart of the City, the company had evolved from "humble beginnings in 1972 as a specialty denim store on Sydney’s George Street" to be "recognised as a cutting-edge curator of fashion and style".

"General Pants Co. is influenced not only by global trends but by fashion, music, art, the street and urban subcultures," it said online.

Earlier this month, Australian department store giant David Jones collapsed into administration and receivership after years of mounting losses and following a breakdown of talks with its landlords.

Christopher Hill, David McGrath and Joanne Dunn of FTI Consulting confirmed to 1News that the Auckland department store would be included with 18 others in Australia to be shuttered.
