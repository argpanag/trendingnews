Global outage hits Microsoft's Outlook, 365 services

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:34 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Business

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._v0.KrRTFtoPsaA8nSDloPZ4KejQJr59ANQpD9ULgGwzaM0/business/2569444%2Fglobal-outage-hits-microsoft-s-outlook-365-services

Microsoft Outlook has suffered a major outage, with tens of thousands of users struggling to access their email as the company says it is working "aggressively" to reduce the impact.

Microsoft initially said it was investigating outage reports "in the Americas," but later said it had "confirmed the issue may affect Microsoft 365 service connectivity for users in any region."

Microsoft 365 is the company's cloud-based subscription service that includes popular applications such as Outlook and Teams.

In a statement on its website Microsoft said Teams, Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, Copilot 365 and Defender XDR may also be impacted.

According to outage tracker Downdetector, nearly 130,000 users reported issues with Outlook shortly after 1 pm Eastern Time (1800 GMT).

Reports of outages have since declined, with about 60,000 users reporting experiencing issues with Outlook as of 3 pm Eastern Time (2000 GMT).

"We've determined a portion of infrastructure is experiencing elevated load across multiple regions, which is likely contributing to the connectivity errors," the company wrote on X.

"We're aggressively pursuing all pathways to reduce impact," it said.

"We've paused all deployments, we're moving additional traffic off the affected infrastructure, and we're restarting affected servers to return them to service."

Thousands of users also reported issues accessing Microsoft Teams, according to Downdetector.

Health NZ told RNZ there had been "minimal disruption" to patient services so far.

"We are aware of the global issue affecting Microsoft's systems and are actively working with them to restore applications for our staff as soon as possible."

A spokesperson for the Public Service Commission said the Government Digital Delivery Agency was working with Microsoft and agencies to understand the impact to public services.

"Agencies experiencing issues are encouraged to contact their Microsoft representative."

Paul Spain on Microsoft outage

Gorilla Technology chief executive Paul Spain told Nine to Noon the issue seemed to be Microsoft attempting to implement some changes.

Paul Spain.

"They've made a change and there's been unexpected consequences that, you know, unfortunately they haven't been able to foresee, which usually there's a significant amount of testing that goes in before major changes are made."

He said Microsoft had made indications they were reverting systems back to a "prior state".

Spain said at times companies had to make changes urgently if there was a cyber risk.

"It used to be that these things would be spread out over much longer periods of time. Now those things are often being compressed and done as quickly as possible."

He said the global economic impact of the outage could be in the billions of dollars, and in the millions for New Zealand.

"This is probably one of the areas as a country where maybe we're not as well prepared as we should be for, you know, technology failings or for cyber security incidents.

"There'll be lots of businesses who have financial commitments to make, and some of those will involve, you know, email actually operating for it to work properly, or access to varying other, you know, Microsoft systems."

'Tsunami of issues'

Local software development firm Catalyst said the outage highlighted New Zealand's vulnerability to global technology glitches. Managing director Don Christie said the New Zealand economy was particularly reliant on a handful of global suppliers for digital services.

"There's a tsunami of issues hitting digital platforms right now, all driven by you know some of these big AI engines and companies, and we're all having to respond very, very quickly to that and to those issues.

"From a cyber security perspective, the government needs to be much more engaged. And the second point is that there are - across New Zealand - there are organisations that are hurting at the moment, and our main priority should be supporting those organisations and people."

Christie said other countries mandated open-source software for - which was free - as the default for government procurement, and New Zealand should do the same.

He said New Zealand often referred to Denmark, Singapore and South Korea as countries with exemplar government IT strategies.

"The thing about those countries is that they've been very deliberate about their IT strategy. They haven't just waited for you know, the next thing to come out of Amazon or the next thing to come out of Microsoft. They've actually gone out. They've stimulated their local digital sectors."

He said New Zealand was more focused on market-led solutions.

"But the market is two things. One, it's dominated effectively by monopolies that are not New Zealand-based. And number two, the government itself is effectively a monopoly buyer."

Christie said the Government's reliance on one or another dominant supplier drove market behaviour.

"So again, if the government were to be more strategic about how it adopts digital technologies, we would find a much richer base of our digital platforms, which potentially make us less prone to problems in the future."
