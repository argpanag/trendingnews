Irish Olympian and European champion Ciara Mageean dies

TheTools · 01.11.2026 10:09 · source · permalink

Irish Olympian and European champion runner Ciara Mageean has died aged 34.

Mageean revealed in July 2025 that she had been diagnosed with stage four bowel cancer.

A short statement on her Instagram account confirmed her passing. It read: "At 6:30am this morning, Ciara lost her battle with cancer surrounded by her loved ones.

"A beloved daughter, sister, wife, friend and inspiration to so many, she will be deeply missed.

"Ar dheis Dé go raibh a hanam."

A two-time Olympian, Mageean made history by winning European 1500m gold for Ireland in Rome in 2024.

The Portaferry woman represented Ireland at the Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games in the 1500m even, and was due to compete at the 2024 games before an Achilles injury ruled her out, just one day before her first heat at the Paris games.

Along with her European gold in 2024, she won European bronze in 2016, silver in 2022 and she also claimed silver at the 2022 Commonwealth Games.

Mageean holds Irish records in the 800m (1:58.51), 1000m (2:31.06), 1500m (3:55.87) and mile (4:14.58).

Speaking on RTÉ Radio in June, Mageean said she was determined to "fit as much living into the years I have left" as she spoke about her stage four cancer diagnosis.

"I am so grateful for the support of the Irish nation," she added. "It's been there for me throughout my entire career. But I feel supported and loved throughout this journey."

Mageean and her long-term partner Thomas Moran were married last month in a ceremony at Belfast City Hall.

Taoiseach Micheál Martin said he was deeply saddened by her loss: "As European champion and an Olympian, she inspired so many with her outstanding performances and brought immense pride to Ireland

"Ciara has left a remarkable sporting legacy. Ciara faced her recent health battles with great courage and dignity.

"My heartfelt sympathies go to her husband Thomas and all her family and friends."

Athletics Ireland released a statement in which they paid tribute to the County Down runner.

It read: "Ciara was a much-loved and inspirational figure who touched the lives of so many. Her courage, warmth and spirit will leave a lasting legacy.

"The thoughts and heartfelt condolences of everyone at Athletics Ireland are with Ciara's family, friends and loved ones at this incredibly difficult time.

"May she rest in peace."

In July Mageean was a guest of hounour at the Irish National Championships in which she hosted the 'Run with Ciara Mageean' event, where the Irish athletics community came together to pay tribute to her and her achievements.

Fellow athlete David Gillick said Mageean had secured a place in the heart of the nation.

He told RTÉ Radio 1: "I think we have to remember Ciara in the most positive of lights because she was just a beautiful person on the track and off the track. She resonated with so many Irish people.

"I had the luxury and the privilege to interview her post-race. And she was always so open and honest. She always just said how it was.

"When she won her first European senior medal in Amsterdam, it was eight years until she won that European gold in Rome a few years ago.

"It's the longevity and perseverance given the injuries she went through during that period of time. And I think people really, really respected that.

"She was just always a great person to be around. It's a bit surreal. It's hard to understand and believe."
