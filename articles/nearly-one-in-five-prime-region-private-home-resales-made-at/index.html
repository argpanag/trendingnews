      The collection, titled Pokémon: A Decade of Masters (1999-2009), included the Legendary Collection Complete Master Set. It had all 110 cards, the 110 Reverse Holo Firework cards, and four Box Toppers.

      The man began collecting when Pokémon cards first came out in England in 1999, to share his son's interest.       Sembawang North BTO flats with under 3-year wait to go on sale in November

      A total of 1,310 Build-To-Order (BTO) flats in the new Sembawang North housing estate will be offered in November, the Housing Board's last sales exercise of 2026. The waiting time for these flats is two years and 10 months.

      HDB said on 1 Nov that projects in Bayshore and Tengah will also be offered in the exercise. These flats have a waiting time of three years and two months.

      In all, 4,661 flats with waits of less than three years will be launched in 2026. This meets HDB's commitment to launch more than 4,000 such flats this year. HDB said it is on track to meet its target of launching 12,000 flats with shorter waits from 2025 to 2027, having offered 9,351 so far, including those in November. The exercise will also see the higher income ceilings of S$16,000 for families and S$8,000 for singles take effect. HDB did not reveal the date of the exercise.

      The Sembawang North project, Sembawang Anchor, is on a site in Admiralty Lane and Sembawang Drive. It will have two-room flexi, three-, four-, and five-room flats, and one of its six blocks will have rental flats. It will have a pre-school, an eating house, a supermarket, and shops. A new neighbourhood centre and a park are planned close to the project, HDB said.

      In Bedok, Bayshore Oasis is near Bayshore MRT station. It will offer 855 two-room flexi and four-room flats across four blocks, one of which will have rental flats. Another Bayshore project, whose name has not been disclosed, is next to it and will offer 1,640 units of two-room flexi, three-, and four-room flats.

      Plantation Dew in Tengah will be the first BTO project offered in the neighbourhood since October 2023. It will have 710 units of two-room flexi, three-, four-, and five-room flats, as well as three-generation flats. It is a five- to 10-minute walk from two stations on the upcoming Jurong Region Line, which is scheduled to open in stages between 2028 and 2029.

      Plantation Dew will offer open-concept white flats, with no internal partition walls between the living and bedroom spaces. It will be the third BTO project to offer such flats, and the first to offer them for five-room flats. Buyers can choose the white flat layout when they are invited to book a flat. Otherwise, they will receive the standard layout with walls.

      'We did it': Antonelli in tears after clinching F1 title as youngest world champion

      Kimi Antonelli became Formula One's youngest world champion, and Italy's first since 1953, after finishing third in a crash-hit Mexico City Grand Prix on Sunday (1 Nov). The 20-year-old Mercedes driver finished behind multiple champions Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton.

      Verstappen won, 0.799 seconds ahead of Hamilton's Ferrari, with Antonelli 3.207 seconds behind in third. It was Verstappen's record-extending sixth win in Mexico, his third this year, and the 74th of his career. The race was split in two by a 45-minute pause after Valtteri Bottas crashed heavily in his Cadillac, and included two safety cars.

      Ferrari's Charles Leclerc was fourth, and Lando Norris of McLaren was fifth. Antonelli's Mercedes teammate George Russell, who needed to win to have any chance of stalling his title, was sixth.

      Hamilton started on pole, a record 105th, but Leclerc attacked on the opening lap and took the lead. Hamilton radioed Ferrari that he was "pushed out by my own teammate". Leclerc was given a five-second penalty and Russell a 10-second penalty for causing a collision. Hamilton said he thought Ferrari had the pace to win, and that the team must "hold some serious talks behind the scenes".

      Antonelli sat tight in third as Hamilton pressed Verstappen. He crossed the line in tears, led in by two drivers with 11 championships between them.

      Singapore exploring build-to-rent homes as more young Singaporeans seek independence

      The Government is exploring build-to-rent (BTR) housing models as part of its efforts to give Singaporeans more housing options. The move comes as more young Singaporeans want to live independently before they buy a home. A Ministry of National Development (MND) spokesman told The Straits Times (ST) that home ownership "remains a key tenet" of housing policy. However, the Government recognises demand for flexible, longer-stay rental housing, he said. This includes younger Singaporeans who want to live independently or rent before buying, as well as those who come to Singapore to work or study.

      National Development Minister Chee Hong Tat said in August that the authorities were looking at BTR, co-living, and long-stay serviced apartments.

      Henry Tay, 36, a freelance photographer, and Sarah Tong, 32, a civil servant, wanted to live on their own after marrying in May 2024. They decided to rent first as their Build-To-Order (BTO) flat in Tanah Merah was about three years from completion. They moved from a one-bedroom unit in a Bright Hill condominium to a three-room flat in Ang Mo Kio, paying S$2,200 to S$2,500 a month. This took about 25 per cent of their combined take-home pay.

      Renting could cost them up to S$100,000 over three years, on top of the S$570,000 price of their BTO flat, they told ST. They still see it as cheaper than a resale flat in a good location, which could cost as much as S$1 million in some parts of Singapore.

      Observers who spoke to the newspaper said BTR could serve as an interim pathway for those who want private space and independence. Sociologist Tan Ern Ser of the Institute of Policy Studies said affordability would still matter, as living with parents is cheaper. Sing Tien Foo, a real estate professor at NUS Business School, said BTR would supplement ownership by meeting the short-term needs of families waiting for their flats, or singles under 35.

      The observers agreed that BTR would not make renting more attractive than home ownership, which the Government has long pursued as a key national policy.

      ERA Singapore's Eugene Lim said BTR needs a clear value proposition compared with traditional rentals, and a discount on the median condominium rent could help. The median monthly rent for non-landed homes in the first eight months of 2026 was S$5.24 per sq ft. He pointed to a recent co-living pilot under the SG Youth Plan, where over 600 Singaporeans aged 21 to 35 showed interest in more than 100 units priced about 30 per cent below market.

      Others have rented before buying. Asher Chua, 31, rented a room in a flat with two friends before marrying, then bought a resale flat in Clementi with his wife using their CPF savings.

      OCBC's head of wealth advisory, Chez Anbu, noted that S$1,500 in monthly rent adds up to S$54,000 over three years and S$90,000 over five. Living independently can bring benefits such as privacy, autonomy, and household budgeting skills, he said.

      Dozens of flights delayed as presidential plane flies over White House for Halloween party

      A White House flyover by the US presidential plane during a children's Halloween party on Thursday evening (29 Oct) delayed dozens of flights in Washington and raised concerns among some in the capital. The plane made two lower-than-normal passes around the White House, which prompted the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to temporarily halt traffic at nearby Reagan Washington National Airport.

      The plane is designated Air Force One when President Donald Trump is on board. He was hosting the party when the flyover occurred. The White House posted a video of the flyover on one of its social media accounts, calling it "epic".

      The Washington DC city council said in a social media post that only 15 minutes' notice was provided before the flyover to anyone in downtown DC, at the Pentagon, or at National Airport. "It startled many, including us," it said. Airline officials also said they had less than an hour's notice.

      Some people flying to Washington reported that their planes were forced to circle or delayed because of the flyover. The plane also flew over nearby Washington neighbourhoods. FlightAware said 26 per cent of departing flights and 17 per cent of arriving flights were delayed on Thursday, or 202 in total. Most of the delays were tied to the airport disruption.

      SIA flight SQ213 lands safely in Perth after go-around in strong winds

      Singapore Airlines (SIA) pilots have won praise online after safely landing a plane in strong winds in Perth, following a go-around caught on video. Flight SQ213, which flew from Singapore, landed at Perth International Airport on 31 Oct.

      The Straits Times reported that a video posted on Facebook by user Chris Perry shows the SIA jet approaching the runway before tilting noticeably to its right, bringing the right wing close to the ground. The pilots then abandoned the landing and flew back into the sky. A second video from Perry, who claimed his sister was on the flight, shows the aircraft later landing without incident.

      Perry's first video had more than 7,200 likes and over 1,800 comments as at 11pm on 1 Nov. Many commenters praised the pilots. One said the experience must have been "pretty frightening" for many on board, but that SIA is known for choosing and training the best pilots. Another called the pilot "very calm and skilled".

      SIA said in response to queries on 1 Nov that the plane met strong winds on approach, and that the pilots decided to conduct a go-around as a result. It did not identify the pilots. The Airbus A350-900 landed safely at 12.30pm local time and taxied to the gate, the airline said.

      The US Federal Aviation Administration describes a go-around as a safe, routine manoeuvre that a pilot can carry out at their own discretion or at an air traffic controller's request.

      Data from the Australian Bureau of Meteorology showed the wind speed at Perth Airport at 12.30pm was 32km/h, with gusts of 43km/h.

      Nearly one in five prime-region private home resales made at a loss in Q3

      Nearly one in five private homes resold in Singapore's prime location market ended in a loss in the third quarter, even as the vast majority of sellers islandwide stayed in the black. In the Core Central Region (CCR), 19.5 per cent of deals ended in the red, up from 17.7 per cent in the previous quarter.

      Data compiled for The Business Times by property consultancy Cushman & Wakefield (C&W) showed the islandwide share of loss-making deals crept up to 4.6 per cent. This was up from 3.7 per cent in the previous quarter and 3.3 per cent a year earlier. It is the highest share since the second quarter of 2022, when 6.9 per cent of resale deals were made at a loss.

      C&W studied 2,021 private home deals in the quarter that had been bought within the previous 10 years. Of these, 93 were sold at a loss, or about one in 22. The CCR accounted for 49 per cent of the loss-making deals. In the Rest of Central Region, 4.5 per cent of deals made a loss, up from 2.7 per cent, while in the suburban Outside Central Region the figure edged up to 1.6 per cent from 1.3 per cent.

      The five biggest losses among non-landed homes, by both percentage and dollar amount, were all for 99-year leasehold properties in the CCR. The steepest percentage loss was at Marina One Residences, where a 710 sq ft unit sold for S$1.25 million in August, about 32 per cent less than the S$1.83 million paid in July 2018. The biggest loss in dollar terms was an OUE Twin Peaks unit, sold for S$3.88 million in August after being bought for S$4.9 million in March 2017. That is a loss of about S$1 million, or 20 per cent.

      Profit margins also continued to shrink. The median gain for profitable resale and sub-sale deals fell to 24.8 per cent in the third quarter, from a peak of 30.4 per cent in the second quarter of 2025. C&W's head of research for Singapore and Southeast Asia, Wong Xian Yang, said private home prices are at historical highs and global uncertainty remains.

      Buyers are expected to be "more selective", he said, and future resale performance is likely to vary more widely across developments. Three of the five most profitable deals by dollar amount were still in the CCR, and all five were in freehold developments, which he said tend to command a premium.

      Executive condominiums (ECs) again delivered the biggest percentage gains. All five top deals were at Hundred Palms Residences in the Outside Central Region, with gains of between 138.9 per cent and 163.9 per cent over about nine years. The biggest was a 1,055 sq ft unit sold for S$2.26 million in September, after being bought for S$860,000 in July 2017.

      C&W's analysis excludes transaction costs and taxes, such as stamp duties.

    SIA flight SQ213, an Airbus A350-900, landed safely in Perth on 31 Oct after its pilots conducted a go-around. (PHOTO: Nicolas Economou/NurPhoto via Getty Images)

    Singapore Airlines (SIA) pilots have won praise online after safely landing a plane in strong winds in Perth, following a go-around caught on video. Flight SQ213, which flew from Singapore, landed at Perth International Airport on 31 Oct.

    The Straits Times reported that a video posted on Facebook by user Chris Perry shows the SIA jet approaching the runway before tilting noticeably to its right, bringing the right wing close to the ground. The pilots then abandoned the landing and flew back into the sky. A second video from Perry, who claimed his sister was on the flight, shows the aircraft later landing without incident.

    Perry's first video had more than 7,200 likes and over 1,800 comments as at 11pm on 1 Nov. Many commenters praised the pilots. One said the experience must have been "pretty frightening" for many on board, but that SIA is known for choosing and training the best pilots. Another called the pilot "very calm and skilled".

    SIA said in response to queries on 1 Nov that the plane met strong winds on approach, and that the pilots decided to conduct a go-around as a result. It did not identify the pilots. The Airbus A350-900 landed safely at 12.30pm local time and taxied to the gate, the airline said.

    The US Federal Aviation Administration describes a go-around as a safe, routine manoeuvre that a pilot can carry out at their own discretion or at an air traffic controller's request.

    Data from the Australian Bureau of Meteorology showed the wind speed at Perth Airport at 12.30pm was 32km/h, with gusts of 43km/h.

    More private home resales in Singapore's prime districts ended in a loss in the third quarter. (PHOTO: Getty Images)

    Nearly one in five private homes resold in Singapore's prime location market ended in a loss in the third quarter, even as the vast majority of sellers islandwide stayed in the black. In the Core Central Region (CCR), 19.5 per cent of deals ended in the red, up from 17.7 per cent in the previous quarter.

    Data compiled for The Business Times by property consultancy Cushman & Wakefield (C&W) showed the islandwide share of loss-making deals crept up to 4.6 per cent. This was up from 3.7 per cent in the previous quarter and 3.3 per cent a year earlier. It is the highest share since the second quarter of 2022, when 6.9 per cent of resale deals were made at a loss.

    C&W studied 2,021 private home deals in the quarter that had been bought within the previous 10 years. Of these, 93 were sold at a loss, or about one in 22. The CCR accounted for 49 per cent of the loss-making deals. In the Rest of Central Region, 4.5 per cent of deals made a loss, up from 2.7 per cent, while in the suburban Outside Central Region the figure edged up to 1.6 per cent from 1.3 per cent.

    The five biggest losses among non-landed homes, by both percentage and dollar amount, were all for 99-year leasehold properties in the CCR. The steepest percentage loss was at Marina One Residences, where a 710 sq ft unit sold for S$1.25 million in August, about 32 per cent less than the S$1.83 million paid in July 2018. The biggest loss in dollar terms was an OUE Twin Peaks unit, sold for S$3.88 million in August after being bought for S$4.9 million in March 2017. That is a loss of about S$1 million, or 20 per cent.

    Profit margins also continued to shrink. The median gain for profitable resale and sub-sale deals fell to 24.8 per cent in the third quarter, from a peak of 30.4 per cent in the second quarter of 2025. C&W's head of research for Singapore and Southeast Asia, Wong Xian Yang, said private home prices are at historical highs and global uncertainty remains.

    Buyers are expected to be "more selective", he said, and future resale performance is likely to vary more widely across developments. Three of the five most profitable deals by dollar amount were still in the CCR, and all five were in freehold developments, which he said tend to command a premium.

    Executive condominiums (ECs) again delivered the biggest percentage gains. All five top deals were at Hundred Palms Residences in the Outside Central Region, with gains of between 138.9 per cent and 163.9 per cent over about nine years. The biggest was a 1,055 sq ft unit sold for S$2.26 million in September, after being bought for S$860,000 in July 2017.

    C&W's analysis excludes transaction costs and taxes, such as stamp duties.
