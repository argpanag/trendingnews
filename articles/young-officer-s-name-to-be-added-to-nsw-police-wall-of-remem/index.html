Young officer's name to be added to NSW Police Wall of Remembrance

TheTools · 28.09.2026 20:00 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Police

Topic:Police

Constable Mia Lay, aged 20, was killed while on duty last Friday. (Supplied: NSW Police)

In short:

Constable Mia Lay, 20, who died in a car crash while on duty, will be added to the NSW Police Wall of Remembrance in Sydney.

A service at the Domain for National Police Remembrance Day will observe a minute's silence for officers who have lost their lives.

The Wall of Remembrance was unveiled at the location in 1999.

Constable Mia Lay's name will be added to the NSW Police Wall of Remembrance in the Sydney CBD, where police officers killed in the line of duty are honoured. 

The 20-year-old died in the early hours of last Friday morning when the police car she was travelling in crashed into a tree in Redfern while responding to reports of a car theft. 

Mia Lay's family lay wreaths in her honour. (ABC News: Ethan Rix)

Constable Lay's partner, Daniel Vasquez. (ABC News: Ethan Rix)

NSW Premier Chris Minns said the "tragic loss" of someone so young made today's National Police Remembrance Day particularly poignant.

"Being a police officer is one of the hardest jobs in this state," he said in a statement.

"You start a shift not knowing what you are going to face, and we ask these young men and women to put themselves in harm's way to keep the rest of us safe.

"Today is about remembering those officers we have lost, but it’s also about their families, their colleagues and everyone who continues to feel that loss."

The National Police Remembrance Day service will take place at the Domain. (ABC News)

Remembered by loved ones, colleagues and the community as a "competent", "confident", and "promising" officer who loved her job, Constable Lay's name will become the 286th addition to the wall.

The commemoration at the Domain will observe a minute's silence, with Mr Minns, NSW Police Commissioner Mal Lanyon and Police Minister Yasmin Catley all expected to place wreaths at the site.

Tributes were also paid to Sergeant Mark Anthony Bransgrove, who died in July last year, and whose name was the 285th addition to the wall.

Sergeant Bransgrove's career spanned 38 years, with most of those spent in the Forensics Services Group.

"I'm incredibly grateful to every man and woman who puts on that uniform and serves our state. We owe them an enormous debt of gratitude,"

Ms Catley said police officers "put on the blue uniform every day not knowing the dangers" a shift could bring.

"They are called upon to deal with situations that most of us could never, and will never, fully imagine," she said.

"The risks that our officers face are significant and the sacrifices they make are even greater, yet they show up day after day, putting duty above personal safety.

"It's because of our police that our community is a safer place."

The first National Police Remembrance Day was held on September 29, 1989.

Ten years later in December, then-NSW premier Bob Carr unveiled the wall of remembrance at the southern side of the NSW Art Gallery.
