Severe Storms Possible Into Tonight, Then A Big Cooldown Arrives

TheTools · 19.09.2026 13:20 · source · permalink

TOPEKA, Kan. (WIBW) - Scattered storms develop after 5 PM today along a cold front over eastern and northeast Kansas, some severe with wind gusts around 65 mph, large hail, and heavy rain possible. Storms continue overnight into Sunday with lower severe chances. Cooler air arrives Sunday with highs in the 60s and 70s!

First Alerts:

  • Storms Develop Late This Afternoon, Severe Threat Fades After Dark: Scattered strong to severe storms are expected to develop along a front late this afternoon and early this evening. Shower and storm chances linger through the night, though the severe threat should largely diminish after dark. Stay weather-aware and have multiple ways to receive warnings this evening.
  • Dramatic Cooldown Arrives, Sticking Around All Week: A dramatic temperature swing occurs between today and early next week, with below-average temperatures sticking around through the workweek. A refreshing change after the recent heat and humidity.

First Alert Forecast:

Storm coverage increases after 5-6 PM, with initial development likely along a line from Sabetha to Manhattan to Herington. A Slight Risk (level 2 of 5) for severe storms is in place, with storms likely to form along a cold front from Minneapolis to Marysville and points southeast, mainly between 5 and 10 PM. Damaging winds of 60-70 mph, hail up to an inch, and heavy rain are the main concerns, with the strongest storms expected between 6 and 10 PM. Storms could grow into clusters as the system moves eastward, with the severe threat diminishing after sunset in far eastern Kansas. Another round of storms moves into central Kansas overnight with gusty winds possible before weakening farther east, and activity should be winding down by midnight.

Lingering showers are possible into the early morning before decreasing after sunrise. The cold front clears south of the area by midday, ushering in noticeably cooler temperatures following a six-day stretch of 90-degree heat, with a slight chance for rain remaining across east-central areas into the afternoon. A cooler air mass fully settles in as high pressure builds early next week. Highs range from the mid 60s in far northern Kansas to the mid 70s near I-35 Monday, with Tuesday potentially the coolest day of the stretch, with highs in the 60s area-wide, even cooler than recent overnight lows. Temperatures slowly climb back up through the week but stay below average overall, with rain chances returning later in the week as another disturbance approaches, though confidence remains low.

Advisories, Watches, & Warnings:

Severe T-Storm Watch 670 in effect until 11 PM CDT for south-central, central, and northeast Kansas, including Wichita, Salina, Manhattan, Topeka, and Emporia. Stay weather-aware and have multiple ways to receive warnings tonight.

Looking Ahead:

Tonight: Thunderstorms Early. Thunderstorms, some locally heavy early will become more isolated after midnight. Potential for severe thunderstorms. Low 67F. Winds N at 10 to 20 mph. Chance of rain 70%.

Tomorrow: Isolated Thunderstorms. Cloudy in the morning with scattered thunderstorms developing later in the day. High 72F. Winds NNE at 5 to 10 mph. Chance of rain 40%.
