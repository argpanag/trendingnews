Super League saved? NRL set to lock in $57m lifeline … despite key holdouts

TheTools · 09.09.2026 23:04 · source · permalink

The Super League looks set to be saved, with an NRL financial package to bail out the cash-strapped English competition in a landmark deal.

Dwindling broadcast rights money and league-wide operating losses have left the code in dire straits in the UK, while rugby league in Australia goes from strength to strength.

And the NRL is prepared to lend a helping hand, with a $57 million rescue fund to result in the Australian league eventually owning a 50 per cent stake.

The Daily Telegraph revealed on Wednesday that Australian Rugby League Commission chairman Peter V’landys will conduct a phone hook-up with his English Rugby League Commercial counterparts on Thursday night to ratify the deal.

On the table for weeks, the offer will see the NRL pump $11.5 million per year into the Super League over a five-year period.

In return, they would take 10 per cent ownership of the league, increasing yearly by the same amount until becoming equal partners.

The offer was initially rejected by Super League clubs after the league initially confirmed an offer had been made in August, who believed the NRL’s offer was too low.

Leeds Rhinos chairman Paul Caddick has voiced concerns about being “swallowed up” by the bigger, wealthier NRL.

But with negotiations with English broadcaster Sky Sports dragging on and the current rights deal expiring at the end of the 2027 season and clubs’ losses mounting, the Australian offer has emerged as a crucial lifeline.

Sources have told the Telegraph that at least 10 of the league’s 14 clubs are now in favour.

The deal comes amid growing concerns for the health of rugby league worldwide ahead of the 2026 World Cup, to be held later this year in Australia and Papua New Guinea.

Speaking to Sky Sports on a fact-finding mission to the UK earlier this year, then-NRL CEO Andrew Abdo advocated strongly for the “globalisation of rugby league”.

“This is a great game, and we want to take it to as many people as possible,” he said.

“We want to win as many fans as possible – so while the game is growing really well in the southern hemisphere, we also want the game to be strong here in the north.

“This is about a partnership where we can collaborate on how we can grow the game, how we can grow revenues, and how those revenues can be pumped back into the game.

“That’s pretty nuanced, and that’s something we have to develop together.”

Despite its financial concerns, the quality of the Super League’s top teams has remained on par with the NRL.

Super League teams have won the last three World Club Challenge matches pitting the two leagues’ premiers against one another, with Hull Kingston Rovers defeating the Brisbane Broncos 30-24 in Hull earlier this year.
