Johnny "Mad Dog" Adair and ex-boxer Alex Arthur pictured at Paul Ferris' funeral

TheTools · 19.10.2026 13:13 · source · permalink

Johnny "Mad Dog" Adair and former world boxing champ Alex Arthur were amongst mourners who turned out for Paul Ferris' funeral.

The notorious enforcer, 62, was laid to rest at Daldowie Crematorium in Uddingston near Glasgow on Monday. He died on October 6 at University Hospital Ayr, after having been admitted two days earlier.

Medical documents revealed his cause of death was multiple organ failure and alcohol-related cirrhosis of the liver.

Hundreds of mourners gathered at the Glasgow crematorium alongside Ferris' children.

Johnny "Mad Dog" Adair, who was once one of Northern Ireland's most feared loyalist figures as the leader of the Ulster Defence Association's C company, was seen wearing black sunglasses as he stood outside to greet the hearse.

He now lives in Troon, Ayrshire, after a loyalist feud forced him to flee Belfast.

Scottish champion boxer Alex Arthur was also in attendance. The 48-year-old won a gold medal for Scotland at the 1998 Commonwealth games and was crowned WBO super featherweight world champion in 2008.

He also previously stood as a candidate for Alex Salmond’s Alba Party in 2021.

Actor Paul Brannigan was also in attendance. The BBC star was set to play Ferris in a movie, and appeared alongside the former gangster in the last public photograph taken of him in June this year.

The star, best known for his roles in Angels' Share and River City, was spotted at Molly Malone's pub in Glasgow city centre over the summer as he, Ferris, and Wee Man director Ray Burdis discussed the up-and-coming project.

The planned movie entitled Ferris, begins in 1993 and tells the tale of Ferris' arrest and imprisonment on gun running charges before he eventually gave up crime to become a successful author.

Brannigan was lined up for the role of Ferris 13 years after Martin Compston's portrayal of him in The Wee Man.

All three of the men had their heads bowed alongside hundreds of other mourners as the family exited the cars.

Ferris' leaves behind his former wife Carolyn and five children, Paul, Dean, Carolyn, William and John-Paul.

His partner Amanda was also in attendance.

Floral arrangements spelling out "legend" and "dad" were seen inside the funeral car, while Ferris' sons Paul and Dean were pallbearers who carried the coffin inside to the song I Won't Back Down by Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers.

The celebrant, Tony Welsh, led the hour-long service and did not attempt to whitewash his criminal past - only pausing while snaps of Ferris and family were beamed onto a screen to the tune of Brothers in Arms by Dire Straits.

The packed service continued as the celebrant spoke of Ferris' relationship with his children and friends. The humanist pondered whether Ferris had indeed left his criminal past behind in the last 25 years as he promised.

The celebrant quipped: "As Paul would say, no comment."

Ferris, from Blackhill in Glasgow, was originally a feared enforcer for Glasgow crime boss and godfather Arthur Thompson Snr, collecting debts on his behalf.

He was put on trial accused of the murder of Arthur Thompson Jnr in 1992 but was cleared after a 54-day case. Thompson Jnr had been shot dead outside his home in Provanmill, Glasgow while on weekend leave from prison.

Ferris' associates Joe Hanlon and Bobby Glover were murdered following Thompson Jnr's death and Ferris blamed gangland boss Tam McGraw but before he could take revenge he was jailed in England in 1998 for gun-running.

Upon his release from jail he said he was going straight in his book, Ferris Conspiracy. Shortly after he became embroiled in a violent knife fight with bitter enemy Tam McGraw in the east end of Glasgow in 2002.

Both men sustained injuries. Ferris was sent back to prison after having his parole revoked. McGraw died in 2007. In a twist of fate he was also laid to rest at Daldowie Crematorium.

Ferris was released from jail and continued to insist he had left his life of crime behind and worked as a consultant for various private security firms.

His book was later turned into Bafta-winning movie The Wee Man, starring Line of Duty star Martin Compston as Ferris.

He penned another four books documenting his criminal life and had been planning a sequel to The Wee Man and also a third movie before his death.
