Spain train crash kills at least 68 as witnesses describe devastating scenes | CNN

TheTools · 28.10.2026 09:45 · source · permalink

Here's the latest

• Deadly rail crash: Scores of people have been killed and about 100 injured after a high-speed train collided with a stationary train. Spain’s transport minister called it “inexplicable” that a train was allowed to continue its journey despite a red signal being in place.

• ‘People were hurled into the air’: Several witnesses told CNN about the chaotic moment when passengers were thrown airborne during the collision. Rescue operations are still ongoing and the high-speed line linking Madrid with Andalusia remains suspended.

• Third train crash this year: This is the third rail disaster in Spain in 2026. In January, two high-speed trains collided near Adamuz, in southern Spain’s Córdoba province, killing more than 40 people. A commuter train near Barcelona derailed days later.

Updates

At least 24 killed on high-speed train, 44 on stationary train, regional government says

Of the at least 68 people who died in Wednesday’s horrific crash, at least 24 were traveling on the high-speed AVE train and 44 people were found on the stationary Iryo train, the regional government of Castilla-La Mancha said.

The AVE train, operated by the state-owned Renfe railway company, had 393 passengers on board when it collided with the Iryo train, which had 151 passengers.

There are currently more than 100 people with injuries of “varying severity,” the regional government added.

Authorities say they have deployed “all the means and resources” at their disposal to deal with the emergency.

Several US citizens injured in crash, State Department spokesperson says

“Several” US citizens were injured in the train crash in Spain, a US State Department spokesperson told CNN.

They did not provide specific details on the number of Americans hurt in Wednesday’s deadly train crash, which has killed at least 68 people.

“The Department of State and the US Embassy in Madrid are working closely with local authorities to assist Americans following the train collision,” the spokesperson said. “We extend our deepest condolences to the families and loved ones of all those affected.”

CNN saw US embassy officials at the scene of the collision in Spain’s Toledo province.

The spokesperson noted that Americans in need of consular assistance, or with concerns about American loved ones in Spain, can contact the State Department 24/7 at +1-202-501-4444 from abroad or +1-888-407-4747 from the US and Canada.

“We encourage US citizens in the area to notify loved ones of their safety and monitor local media for updates,” they said.

“Don't let them fall asleep – they'll die,” passenger recalls being told

A surviving passenger from Spain’s deadly train crash has described scenes of chaos as she tried to assist fellow travelers to safety.

Three train cars were in “terrible shape,” Eduarda Contreras told CNN. “One was on fire, in another there were people trapped and crying … And then in the main one (impacted) – there were people buried under debris, people who are probably dead.”

Other passengers were trying to break the train’s windows to escape, she said.

Contreras recalls being told not to let injured passengers fall asleep while assisting in rescue efforts before help arrived. “They told me, ‘Stay with them, don’t let them fall asleep – they’ll die, they’re slipping away.’”

Spain’s King says he shares in "sorrow" of grieving families

Spain’s King Felipe VI has conveyed his condolences over today’s deadly train crash in the Spanish province of Toledo, saying he shares in the “sorrow of the families and loved ones” of the deceased.

The social media post said the royal family’s support goes out to the emergency teams involved in the recovery effort and “all those working in the area to help as much as possible under these circumstances.”

CNN’s Duarte Mendonca contributed reporting.

Transport minister says "inexplicable" erroneous control center order caused crash

An erroneous order from a train controller, following a signaling system failure, resulted in Wednesday’s deadly train crash, according to Spain’s transport minister.

Speaking during a press conference on Wednesday afternoon, Óscar Puente said that a computer failure with the main LZB signalling system triggered an automatic stop for all trains in that area, including the Renfe high-speed train and Iryo train involved in the collision.

The control system for Adif, Spain’s railway infrastructure manager, then took command of the system and a decision was made for the Renfe train to continue its journey and the train was authorized to proceed despite a red signal prohibiting movement remaining in place.

The Iryo train remained stationary on the track around 25 kilometers (16 miles) ahead.

The Renfe train was authorized to proceed without any speed restrictions, meaning that between 8:36 a.m. and 8:52 a.m. local time, it traveled approximately 25 kilometers (16 miles) at a speed of about 200 kilometers per hour (124 miles per hour) before colliding with the Iryo train, Puente said.

The transport minister described the crash as “truly implausible” and said that the control center’s decision to allow the Renfe train to resume its journey while the Iryo train was stopped on the track was “inexplicable.”

Sánchez promises accountability for deadly crash if faults are identified

Spain’s Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said authorities are working hard to identify the cause of today’s deadly train crash in Toledo province, and vowed that any negligence, if identified, will be addressed with “utmost firmness.”

Sánchez added that if Spain must “strengthen safety even further in order to rebuild confidence in our high-speed rail system, we will do so. Because what is unacceptable for a country like Spain is to suffer two high-speed rail accidents in the same year, as we have.”

“Therefore, every measure considered appropriate will be taken,” he said.

He also said the cause of the crash was “not for me to say,” but rather for the train network operators and the country’s Independent Commission on Rail Accidents to investigate.

Passengers describe "very chaotic" moment of train crash, as people were thrown from seats

Train passengers have told CNN that the moment of the crash was “very chaotic” and people were immediately thrown out of their seats and on top of one another inside the train carriages.

Authorities have moved at least one bus full of passengers who do not have serious injuries away from the site. Several passengers being bussed away had minor injuries, including cuts on their faces and around their eyes, and what appeared to be bloody noses. Many people also had blood on their hands and arms.

“There was terrible communication. No one could leave the trains for 10 minutes after the crash, we didn’t know what was happening,” said one passenger, Rey, who declined to give his last name.

He said he and his family, who were on the way to a vacation in Seville, are OK but also “desperate to go.” More busses have arrived on the scene now to move additional passengers away from the area.

Another passenger, Yoander, 25, told CNN: “I was sleeping, and when I woke up I was already crashing into the seats in front of me.”

“I hit my face and my leg,” he said. “I’m in pain, but there are people that need more help than I do.”

Train crash in Toledo bears resemblance to Adamuz train disaster earlier this year

The deadly train crash in Spain’s Toledo province has some similarities with a train disaster in Córdoba province that killed more than 40 people earlier this year.

Both train collisions happened along the same high-speed rail route that runs between the capital, Madrid, and Seville, about 300 miles south.

The Toledo crash happened about 130 miles from the site of January’s train disaster near Adamuz, in Córdoba province. Following Wednesday’s crash, authorities have suspended that high-speed line linking the Madrid region with Andalusia.

Both train accidents also involved the collision of two high-speed trains, although one of the trains on Wednesday was not travelling at high speed at the time of the crash.

Spanish emergency authorities have not yet announced a precise cause of Wednesday’s crash, but they have disclosed some details. The local Castilla-La Mancha 112 emergency services told CNN they received a report of a collision at 8.45 a.m. local time. According to the reports, a train owned by the company Iryo was stationary or traveling at low speed when it was struck by a high-speed AVE train operated by Spain’s national railway company Renfe.

It appears the crash on Wednesday was a rear-end collision, authorities at the scene told CNN. The train crash earlier this year involved one train derailing and crossing onto an adjacent track, according to Spain’s public broadcaster RTVE.

For context: Spain’s train drivers’ union, Semaf, has previously raised concerns about high speeds causing vibrations and subsequent wear and tear along high-speed railway lines, a source from the union told CNN in January, in the aftermath of the crash near Adamuz.

In August 2025, the union sent a letter to the national railway operator, Adif, asking for a reduction in the maximum speed on all high-speed train lines from 300 kmph to 250 kmph, after some stretches were vibrating and placing stress on the infrastructure, the source said.

Death toll in train crash rises to 64, regional government says

At least 64 people have now been confirmed killed in a deadly train collision in Spain this morning, a spokesperson for the regional Castilla-La Mancha government said.

The spokesperson told CNN that this number is expected to rise further as emergency workers continue to work at the scene.

Death toll from train crash rises to at least 32, local official says

The death toll from today’s train crash in the Spanish province of Toledo has now risen to at least 32, according to Emiliano García-Page, the president of the regional Castilla-La Mancha government.

García-Page shared the latest update when speaking to Spanish national broadcaster Radio Nacional de España.

Previously, authorities had said that at least 30 people had been killed and around 100 injured in the collision.

Civil Guard urges victims' relatives to come forward to accelerate identification

The Spanish Civil Guard announced that they have opened four offices in different cities of Spain in order to swiftly identify the victims of the crash.

A spokesperson of institution has said they will also take DNA samples of the relatives to bring a quick response. “We are calling all close relatives to get to this offices and speed up the identification process,” the spokesperson said.

The offices are in the Civil Guard zones of Madrid, Andalusia, Cordoba and Toledo.

CNN reports from town nearest to the crash site

CNN’s Pau Mosquera has been reporting from Urda, a town roughly five miles from the site of today’s deadly train crash.

Dozens of ambulances and emergency service vehicles have been streaming into the town’s sports centre, where Red Cross workers are treating the injured.

One of the survivors recounted how she was thrown from her seat as the crash’s impact sent her flying into the air. She was grateful for the care provided to her by the emergency staff in Urda.

Massive emergency response at crash site as passengers evacuated, says CNN team on scene

The scene at the site of the crash is frantic as a serious rescue operation is underway, according to CNN’s team on the ground in Toledo province.

Dozens of fire trucks, ambulances and police cars are here. At least three helicopters are also at the crash site to assist with operations. Multiple ambulances have come and gone from the cordoned-off area.

Meanwhile, emergency responders are combing through the rubble of some of the heavily damaged carriages of the train operated by Iryo. Several carriages of the state-owned Renfe high-speed train are tilted on their side after coming off the rails.

The Spanish civil guard has set up multiple tents for what amounts to a huge emergency response.

Authorities have moved a bus full of passengers who do not have serious injuries away from the site, and some those passengers told CNN they are now being taken to their original destination of Sevilla.

Passengers described a chaotic scene when their train hit another train on the tracks.

Those being bussed away from the crash site included citizens of multiple nationalities, including Spain, Brazil and elsewhere. Staff from the US Embassy in Spain are also at the site.
