For the Vancouver Canucks, Tuesday’s surprising 6-5 overtime win will have felt special all day, even without the triumph. Every player will tell you that the feeling of a clean slate is unique: everything is fresh, hope truly springs eternal, everyone is tied for first place. It’s finally your time again, a chance to make something happen.

But there is something extra special about launching your home game. Your fans are there, they’ve got anticipation in their blood, just as you, the player, does. The roar that erupts when you first step on the ice before the game is equalled only by the roar at the opening of the playoffs.

The playoffs here, of course, are nothing but a dream. They are not expected. But in the player’s heart maybe, just maybe, they can get off to a hot start and then all of a sudden it’s November and you’re surprising people and then you know the odds of making the playoffs from the end of November and then…

The dream is there. This is the beginning of it.

The buzz: Remember Tuesday? That was fun. That was silly. That was everything Canucks fans want: they just want to see a team with heart. And Paul Cotter certainly has that in spades.

Playing at home, feeling the vibes of a hopeful home crowd? You know the Canucks will want to go an improbable two for two.

The history: Tuesday saw Cotter score twice and singles from Marco Rossi, Max Sasson, Jonathan Lekkerimaki and Elias Pettersson (the forward). The Canucks went 1-3-0 against the Oilers last season.

The hope: The Oilers looked brutal defensively and goalie Tristan Jarry was just as bad. Head coach Mike Babcock was greatly displeased post-game; can he find any solutions in the 48 hours between games? The Canucks hope not.

The fear: The Oilers remain a far better outfit than the Canucks. Connor McDavid’s line scored three times on Tuesday so it wasn’t like the loss was on the stars. And Evan Bouchard had the first season-opening hat trick by a defenceman since Mark Howe in 1980. They are going to show up guns blazing.

The top guns: Likely the only time all year we’ll mention Cotter alongside McDavid, but here we are. Cotter had as good a debut for the Canucks as you could dream of. But just as important was Filip Hronek’s pair of assists and the big minutes he munched. His second assist was on a deft deflection by Pettersson, which put the visitors into a 5-2 lead with just five minutes played in the third. McDavid had just two assists despite his line’s dominance. Bouchard added two assists to his three goals, while former Canuck Vasily Podkolzin scored a pair of quality goals.

The wounded: Canucks: Demko, hip surgery recovery (IR); and Filip Chytil, shoulder (IR). Oilers: Mattias Janmark (undisclosed) day-to-day; Alec Regula (undisclosed) day-to-day; Jason Dickinson (foot) week-to-week; Frederik Andersen (undisclosed) week-to-week; and Matthew Savoie (undisclosed) week-to-week.

The quote: “Is that Rocket Richard?” — Drew O’Connor, reportedly, post-game on Cotter.
