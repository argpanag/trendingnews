Messi's Penultimate Match in Chaos: Burkina Faso Refuse to Travel, U23s Called Up

TheTools · 03.10.2026 23:37 · source · permalink

national teams

The 'Stallions' rebelled, refusing to fly to Buenos Aires. The federation has scrambled to respond by calling up their youth squad. The protest is rooted in a lack of compensation and significant logistical challenges.

During the lengthy international break, Argentina, with no official fixtures, had scheduled three friendlies: Bolivia (which they won 4-0), Burkina Faso, and Benin. The match against Benin was intended to mark Leo Messi's farewell to the national team. However, these third-tier opponents have sparked endless controversy at home, with difficulties in convincing players to participate.

THE GREAT REFUSAL

An international scandal has now erupted. The Burkina Faso national team was scheduled to depart for Buenos Aires yesterday, but as they prepared to board, the 'Burkinabé Stallions' refused to travel, with only one player stepping onto the gangway. The African Federation swiftly intervened, 'convincing' their Under-23 squad to make the journey and cancelling a friendly against Mali planned for just hours later. The plane carrying the young Stallions eventually departed from Casablanca, Morocco, significantly delayed. It was expected to land in Buenos Aires at 3:30 PM local time, with the match against Argentina scheduled for 7 PM at the Monumental stadium.

NO FEE

The Argentine Football Association has recently shown a keen interest in African opposition, having invited Mauritania and Zambia in the spring, and now Burkina Faso and Benin. Notably, these are not major footballing nations like Morocco, Senegal, Nigeria, or Ivory Coast. The reason is straightforward: the appearance fee for these smaller African teams is precisely zero. All revenue generated goes to the local football federation and the Colombian agency responsible for organising the matches. For the African opponents, there's nothing. Furthermore, travel is arranged via low-cost scheduled flights. This involves long-haul journeys of 12-13 hours each way, with just one night in a hotel and a match sandwiched in between. It was for these reasons that the Burkina Faso players refused to participate, forcing their federation to call up the Under-23s and even arrange a charter flight. It remains to be seen what will happen with the Benin fixture.
