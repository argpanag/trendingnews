Scentre Group (ASX:SCG) Real Estate Scentre Group (ASX:SCG) 3.39 AUD -0.010 ↓ 0.294% Last Updated at: 2026-10-14T05:20:00Z , the owner and operator of the Westfield shopping centre portfolio in Australia and New Zealand, is in the spotlight after Lush Cosmetics said it will not renew leases at its stores in Westfield centres. The retailer said it wants to serve local communities in the right locations, while the landlord had not responded to requests for comment when the news was reported on Tuesday. Scentre Group features among the ASX Bluechip Stocks on investors' radar this week.

What was reported

Lush Cosmetics confirmed it will leave its Westfield locations when current leases end. The company said it wants to be where local communities are best served, without detailing the reasons or the timing. It remains unclear whether the retailer chose to leave or whether the landlord did not extend the tenancies.

Closure dates and the effect on staff have not been confirmed, and the retailer has not said whether it intends to open elsewhere in the same suburbs through street-front or standalone locations, which would indicate a change of format rather than a retreat from the market. The ABC reported that Scentre Group had not responded to requests for comment, so the landlord's perspective is unknown at this stage. Until more is disclosed, the story is best read as a single tenant decision rather than a trend.

Why tenant moves matter to a landlord

Shopping centre owners earn rent from a mix of anchors, specialty retailers and services. Specialty stores typically pay higher rent per square metre than department stores or supermarkets, so even small tenants can matter to income. A departure creates a vacancy that must be re-let, often to a different category.

The landlord's task is to keep centres full, relevant and busy. Strong centres are able to replace tenants quickly and sometimes at better terms. Weak ones may be forced to give incentives, which reduces net income. That is why market watchers follow each prominent tenant announcement.

The scale of the portfolio

Scentre Group owns and manages some of the country's largest and most productive shopping centres. The centres are concentrated in major cities and benefit from high foot traffic, which helps with leasing. This gives the landlord leverage and means individual exits are normally absorbed.

The company also earns income from development, from advertising and from management fees. Those streams add diversity, although rent from retailers remains the dominant source. Management has stressed that it curates its tenant mix and regularly changes it to keep centres fresh.

Market reaction in the property sector

Listed property was one of the weaker sectors on Monday after a strong week, with real estate falling more than the broader market. Goodman Group fell, while Lendlease and Centuria were flat. Scentre was not singled out as a major mover that day.

The previous week, listed property led the market as bond yields eased, helped by speculation about interest rates. That context suggests Monday's softness was a pause rather than a response to retail news, and the Lush announcement came later.

Retail conditions underneath

The retail backdrop is mixed. Consumers face high living costs, yet spending has stayed resilient in many categories. Retailers have been consolidating store networks, shifting towards online sales and demanding more flexible lease terms from landlords.

Beauty and personal care is a competitive field with strong online presence. Lush has a distinctive product and brand, but retailers of all types are re-examining physical footprints. A decision to reduce presence in one landlord's centres could reflect rent levels, store profitability or strategic changes in the tenant's network.

Rent negotiations and incentives

Leasing is a negotiation. Tenants often seek lower rents, shorter terms or fit-out contributions, while landlords aim to maintain occupancy and protect income. Where the balance falls depends on foot traffic, centre quality and the tenant's bargaining power.

Reports of a single tenant leaving say little about the average outcome. Landlords generally disclose occupancy and leasing spreads in their results, and these show whether pressure is building. Market watchers tend to look at those numbers rather than isolated news stories.

Index placement and positioning

Scentre Group is a member of the ASX 50, and it is typical in portfolios built for yield from property. Its distributions and balance sheet strength matter to income-focused funds, and it is sensitive to interest rate expectations because property values are linked to discount rates.

The company also sits among the names tracked under ASX Bluechip Stocks this week for infrastructure and real estate. Moves in bond yields often matter more to the share price than tenant announcements, which is why the sector responded strongly to falling yields in recent days.

Spring trading and consumer demand

Spring is a seasonal turning point for shopping centres, with events, fashion launches and the approach to the Christmas period. Retailers use this time to plan stock and staffing, and landlords adjust marketing campaigns to lift traffic.

Auction volumes in the housing market have been softer this spring, according to Cotality data reported on Monday, which hints at hesitant consumers. Weaker property markets can influence confidence in big purchases, though the link to everyday shopping is less direct.

Risks and offsets

The main risks for the landlord are weaker retail spending, rising incomes of competitors such as online platforms, and any move in interest rates that pressures valuations. A clutch of tenant departures would be more concerning than one.

Offsetting factors include the quality of the centres, a strong development pipeline and the ability to re-let space to growing categories such as dining, health and services. Landlords with premium assets have historically been resilient through cycles.

Lease structures and income stability

Retail leases usually run for several years and include fixed annual increases, which gives landlords a predictable income stream. Specialty tenants often sign shorter agreements than anchors, which makes turnover more frequent but also allows rents to reset toward market levels when conditions are strong.

Because renewals are spread over time, the effect of any single decision is smoothed. Landlords also keep bank guarantees and security deposits that cover some of the risks around vacancy, so the income effect of a departure is typically limited to a gap before a replacement tenant begins paying rent.

Redevelopment and the changing mix

Large centres have been adding dining, entertainment, health clinics and medical services to complement traditional retail. Those uses draw regular visits and are less exposed to online competition, so landlords increasingly prefer them when space becomes available.

A departing specialty retailer can therefore create room for a category that suits the changing mix of the centre. The landlord's development pipeline and apartments or office additions around centres also support long-run income, which means the outlook does not rest only on rent from fashion and beauty stores.

Retail sales and shopper behaviour

Shopping centre income ultimately depends on how much shoppers spend. Retail sales have been resilient in nominal terms, helped by population growth, though real spending per person has been subdued as families prioritise essentials and discount offers over discretionary purchases.

Beauty, cosmetics and gifting categories can be sensitive to sentiment, but they are also supported by regular repeat purchases. A retailer that chooses to concentrate on its best performing locations may be reacting to this pattern rather than signalling a collapse in demand, and landlords are well placed to fill the space with other uses.

What to watch next

Watch for a statement from the landlord, for details on closure dates, and for further tenant announcements. The next occupancy and leasing update will show whether the Lush decision is isolated or part of a wider reshaping of the tenant mix.
