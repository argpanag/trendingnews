Porsche’s Mission S Gets New Wheels and Tires for More Virtual Street Cred

TheTools · 17.10.2026 03:00 · source · permalink

That it will probably be delayed a few more times before it finally heads into production. After all, the automaker decided to put a pin on it several times so far. Let’s not forget that it was supposed to hit the roads in 2020 originally, and that was three years after it debuted as a study. And speaking of that, it looks different from the concept, and some have called it the offspring of the Tesla Cybertruck and a previous-generation supercar.

Now, we are heading into a different realm, so we’d better end it here and move on to the real reason behind this story, which is all about the Porsche Mission S. Uncovered at about the same time as the new Tesla Roadster’s demonstrations, the is not a full production car. Far from it, actually, because what we have here is a concept, albeit a very nice one that proves that the Zuffenhausen brand continues to do what it does best, as instead of chasing after crossovers and SUVs, it has just dropped another super sports car.

Even if it is nothing more than a study, it is a very important one for the automaker, because it represents a first official glimpse into a future super sports model that will clearly sit above the 911. Not only that, but it also previews the company’s future design identity, and to be honest, it is not bad at all, is it?

The styling at the rear may be quite dramatic, but at the front, it looks quite familiar. If anything, one can easily mistake it for a track-focused 911 that costs a lot of money, and that’s not necessarily a bad thing, this writer believes. It has a long nose with a large air intake in the lower part of the bumper, a sharp apron with blades on the sides, a vented front hood, familiar-looking headlights, and a roof scoop.

Porsche’s Mission S Concept therefore remains easily identifiable as something that comes from the Zuffenhausen company, and even if the paint on its body has barely dried out, it has already fallen into the hands of the rendering world. Actually, it is the wheel-making rendering world this time, because several wheelmakers have dropped CGIs of this machine that imagine it sitting on various alloys.

We do not know the size of the wheels on which this imagined Porsche Mission S sits, but if we were to guess, we’d say that they are probably the 22-inch proposal. The automaker says that the wheels of the real Mission S “draw on an iconic design from the Porsche history and develop it further,” mentioning “the dial-style wheels of the 928,” and adding that it is “more than just a reinterpretation of a design motif,” as it is “a link between the sports cars of yesterday and the driver’s cars of tomorrow.”

Despite being uncovered, we do not know much about the oily bits of the Porsche Mission S, though we can tell you that it features a read mid-engine setup, and that’s great news for those who do not necessarily fancy electric vehicles. Recent reports indicate that the Zuffenhausen auto marque is working on a punchy new eight-cylinder boxer engine for its upcoming flagship model, and this is expected to be the production version of the Mission S, so Porsche looks like it has a winner in its future super sports car stable.
