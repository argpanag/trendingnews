70-year-old Ottawa woman sets new marathon world record

When Jennifer MacDonell laced up her shoes at the TCS Toronto Waterfront Marathon earlier this month, she was determined to make history.

A little over three hours later, the 70-year-old Ottawa woman did just that.

MacDonell set a new marathon world record for women aged 70-74, crossing the finish line on Oct. 18 with a time of 3:23:59.

That's more than three minutes faster than the previous record of 3:27:00 set by France's Laurence Dupont Alnet in 2024. 

"I was ecstatic and relieved at the same time — and exhausted," MacDonell said with a laugh. 

She's had her eyes on taking the record since the spring, tweaking her physical and mental training to improve her time by about six minutes with the help of her coach, Steve Boyd. 

"I wouldn't say I knew I could do this. I knew I had a chance of doing it, because you never know until that day," she said. 

Jennifer MacDonell said the race became a mental game during the final two kilometres as she suffered a calf cramp. (Marathon Photos Live)

Race day pressures

On race day, as the finish line inched closer, MacDonell said the pressure only got more intense.

"I was thinking the whole time, is this going to happen? Is this going to happen?" she recalled.

With two kilometres left in the race, she said it turned into a mental game.

"I had started to get a little cramp in my calf muscle and I thought, no, you're not going to do this right now. You are not. I'm just going to keep going. I'm going to stay positive."

As she climbed the final hill, with about 200 metres to go, she began pumping her fists in the air, knowing she was moments away from reaching her goal. 

Afterwards, she and her husband Norman, who was also running in the race, treated themselves to a glass of wine — and then some doughnuts when they returned to Ottawa. 

MacDonell's time has now been submitted to the World Masters Athletics for ratification. 

Jennifer MacDonell, centre, crossed the finish line in 3:23:59, about three minutes faster than the previous record set in 2024. (Sarah Holman/Canada Running Series)

A late start

When she first started running decades ago, MacDonell never thought she'd one day break a world record.

She had always been an athletic kid, but she didn't get serious about running until one day in her mid-30s when she decided to get in better shape by going on regular 15-minute jogs along the Rideau Canal. 

"Seven half-minutes out and seven half-minutes back. That was my goal and I was elated just doing that," MacDonell recalled. "Pretty soon I was running around the canal," she added. 

MacDonell has since gone on to compete in races and marathons across North America, including the Boston Marathon in 1997 and the 2021 Chicago Marathon.

For her, age isn't a factor, and she wants others to feel the same. 

"Why not try it? I mean, so many people say, 'Oh, I can't do that,' or 'I can't even run around the block.' Blah, blah … I'm like, you'd be surprised," MacDonell said. 

"It's never easy. You just gotta stick to it. Believe in yourself."
