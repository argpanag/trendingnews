Miliband signs off on Falklands drilling despite Argentina row

TheTools · 15.10.2026 09:05 · source · permalink

Foreign Secretary’s approval means oil production could begin next year

Ed Miliband has approved a massive new oil field development in waters around the Falkland Islands in defiance of Argentina.

The Foreign Secretary said the UK would support the Falklands’ plans to become a major oil exporter and introduce secondary legislation to protect businesses investing there.

The Sea Lion field, about 150 miles to the north of the islands, is thought to contain up to 1.7 billion barrels of oil and could now be in production as early as next year.

The decision triggered an immediate reaction from Argentina, which claims the Falklands as its own. Javier Milei, the president of Argentina, filed legal action on Tuesday seeking to halt the drilling authorised by the UK.

In a filing lodged at the Hamburg-based International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea, Argentina said it would seek to preserve “sovereign rights over ‌its ‌continental shelf ⁠and prevent activities authorised unilaterally and illegally by the United Kingdom”.

Mr Milei has threatened to arrest executives from Navitas and Rockhopper, and on Sept 29 gave the UK a two-week deadline to withdraw its backing or face legal action.

The tribunal may take up to eight weeks to approve or reject Argentina’s application for so-called “provisional measures” to halt the drilling. If the measures are approved, the case could then move to a full arbitration over what rights Argentina and the UK have over the Falklands’ continental shelf.

Mr Miliband condemned Argentina’s “attempts to intimidate businesses and individuals engaged in legitimate commercial activity”.

He said: “Companies should be free to make lawful commercial decisions without the threat of political coercion.

“We will always stand up for the Falkland Islanders, their right to determine their own future, and their right to pursue legitimate economic development.”

The Guardian reported that the Government had written to Interpol, the agency for international police cooperation, asking it to treat with caution any Falklands-related Argentinian arrest warrants.

Jonathan Reynolds, the Business Secretary, said: “This Government will act where Argentina seeks to impede lawful UK commercial activity. We will introduce measures under the Protection of Trading Interests (PTI) legislation to protect UK interests.”

Mr Miliband’s decision will also infuriate many of Labour’s backbench MPs along with environmental groups.

The acceleration of the Falklands fossil fuel industry contrasts Mr Miliband’s decision as energy secretary to halt all new oil and gas exploration in the North Sea. There are separate delays in the approval of the UK’s Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea.

Andrew Bowie, the shadow energy secretary, said: “Approval of Falkland oil development is welcome. But it begs the question of why Labour keeps delaying its decisions on the Rosebank and Jackdaw fields.

“Why is Ed Miliband, the man who blocked North Sea drilling, happy for it go ahead in the South Atlantic but not off the coast of Scotland?”

A decision on the North Sea projects was delayed until after the Holborn and St Pancras by-election, which Labour won ahead of the Green Party last week.

Mr Bowie said: “Labour have kicked the can down the road for fear of upsetting activists in north London. But the by-election in Holborn is over. Burnham and his Cabinet have no excuse for continued dithering and delay.”

Under the Falklands Offshore Minerals Ordinance 1994 – the core statute governing oil and gas activity in Falkland waters – the Falklands governor may grant exploration or production licences only with the consent of the UK Foreign Secretary.

However, the UK Government’s position is that the Falklands are independent, meaning Mr Miliband had little option other than to give his approval.

The Falklands lie in the South Atlantic off Argentina, made up of two large islands – East Falkland and West Falkland – plus nearly 800 smaller islands.

The Sea Lion field lies in water depths of 500 metres, making it relatively easy to access using modern drilling techniques.

Navitas started drilling earlier this year with plans for 23 wells in the northern part of the Sea Lion field. It has contracted two oil production ships to transport the fuel, targeting eventual production of 180,000 barrels a day.

Ian Ramsay, chief executive of Navitas Petroleum Development and Production, welcomed Mr Miliband’s backing for the project.

He said: “In the face of Argentina’s increasingly aggressive efforts to pressure companies associated with the development, we are grateful that the UK Government has made its position clear; that it will robustly defend the Falkland Islands’ right to pursue lawful economic development”.

Richard Tice, Reform UK’s energy spokesman, said: “It is a matter of national energy security that the exploration around the Falkland Islands is maximised and that as much benefit as possible comes back to the UK.

“This should strengthen our position against Argentina, not weaken it.”
