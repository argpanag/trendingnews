The Trinidad and To­ba­go Po­lice Ser­vice (TTPS) has failed in its ap­peal chal­leng­ing a judge’s de­ci­sion to scrap the pro­mo­tion process for po­lice in­spec­tors seek­ing el­e­va­tion to the rank of as­sis­tant su­per­in­ten­dent of po­lice (ASP). 

De­liv­er­ing a judg­ment yes­ter­day, Ap­pel­late Judges Prakash Moo­sai, Mi­ra Dean-Ar­mor­er and Eleanor Don­ald­son-Hon­ey­well re­ject­ed a chal­lenge to Jus­tice Frank Seep­er­sad, up­hold­ing a law­suit from In­spec­tor Mark Her­nan­dez in Feb­ru­ary last year. 

In re­ject­ing all the grounds of ap­peal raised by the Of­fice of the Po­lice Com­mis­sion­er, the ap­peal pan­el found that it could not fault Jus­tice Seep­er­sad’s rea­son­ing. 

“Ul­ti­mate­ly, we were un­able to hold that the judge was plain­ly wrong,” Jus­tice Dean-Ar­mor­er, who wrote the pan­el’s judg­ment, said. 

The out­come means that the process will now have to be restart­ed afresh un­less the TTPS mounts a fi­nal ap­peal be­fore the Unit­ed King­dom-based Privy Coun­cil. 

The ap­peal pan­el first­ly dis­missed al­le­ga­tions of ap­par­ent bias lev­elled against Jus­tice Seep­er­sad, which were based on him writ­ing to for­mer po­lice com­mis­sion­er Er­la Hare­wood-Christo­pher re­lat­ed to per­son­al se­cu­ri­ty be­fore he de­ter­mined the case. 

Not­ing that the let­ter was sent af­ter at­tor­ney Ran­dall Hec­tor, who served as a State pros­e­cu­tor, was as­sas­si­nat­ed last year, Jus­tice Dean-Ar­mor­er found that a fair-mind­ed and in­formed ob­serv­er would have un­der­stood Jus­tice Seep­er­sad’s gen­uine con­cerns and the gen­tle pro­fes­sion­al tone of the let­ter. 

She not­ed that Deputy Com­mis­sion­er of Po­lice (DCP) Natasha George, who was di­rect­ly in­volved in the pro­mo­tion case, was mis­tak­en­ly copied on the email by the judge’s sec­re­tary. 

Jus­tice Dean-Ar­mor­er ques­tioned the tim­ing of the bias al­le­ga­tion as she point­ed out that it was on­ly raised af­ter Jus­tice Seep­er­sad de­liv­ered his rul­ing. 

Deal­ing with the sub­stance of the ap­peal, the pan­el found that Jus­tice Seep­er­sad was not wrong to up­hold Her­nan­dez’s claim that he and his col­leagues, who par­tic­i­pat­ed in the pro­mo­tion ex­er­cise, all wrong­ly re­ceived “out­stand­ing” grades in their an­nu­al per­for­mance ap­praisals. 

Her­nan­dez’s lawyers Ger­ald Ramdeen and Dayadai Har­ri­paul had con­tend­ed that the of­fi­cers should have been fair­ly as­sessed based on their in­di­vid­ual per­for­mance in­stead of au­to­mat­i­cal­ly re­ceiv­ing the same pos­i­tive grad­ing. 

Jus­tice Seep­er­sad agreed as he took is­sue with the fact that DCP George de­clined to dis­close even a redact­ed list of ap­praisal grades to prove her claim that there was not a uni­ver­sal grade pol­i­cy for all of­fi­cers. 

Stat­ing that his rul­ing was based on her with­hold­ing ev­i­dence, Jus­tice Dean-Ar­mor­er said: “It was there­fore jus­ti­fi­able for the judge to draw an ad­verse in­fer­ence against DCP George that there were no non-out­stand­ing re­ports.” 

She did not over­rule Jus­tice Seep­er­sad’s de­ci­sion to find fault with the lack of polic­ing ex­per­tise on the pan­el se­lect­ed by Odyssey Con­sult­Inc Ltd, the con­sul­tan­cy firm hired to as­sist with the process, to in­ter­view Her­nan­dez. 

She al­so re­ject­ed com­plaints over Jus­tice Seep­er­sad’s de­ci­sion to in­val­i­date the en­tire pro­mo­tion process and or­der that it be re­done based on find­ing breach­es at every stage of the three-tiered process. 

Jus­tice Dean-Ar­mor­er dis­missed con­cerns raised over Her­nan­dez’s col­leagues, who were af­fect­ed by him pur­su­ing the lit­i­ga­tion. 

Stat­ing that they would on­ly suf­fer de­lay in be­ing pos­si­bly pro­mot­ed, she said: “This detri­ment is out­weighed by the detri­ment to the pub­lic in­ter­est in fail­ing to en­sure that of­fices of such sig­nif­i­cance are filled ac­cord­ing to mer­it.” 

The TTPS was rep­re­sent­ed by Rishi Dass, SC, Tama­ra Toolsie, Akee­nie Mur­ray, Ka­dine Mat­tew, ASP Ram­dath Phillip (Head of the TTPS Le­gal De­part­ment) and Anya Mo­han.
