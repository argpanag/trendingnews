Stock Market Today Highlights: BSE Sensex plunges over 400 points, NSE Nifty50 closes below 24,000 - The Times of India

TheTools · 04.09.2026 03:41 · source · permalink


The BSE Sensex was up 18.21 points, or 0.02 per cent, at 76,588.56 at 2.38pm.

Adani Ports rose 1.88 per cent to Rs 1,704.30, while Axis Bank gained 1.52 per cent to Rs 1,274.10. HDFC Bank advanced 1.24 per cent to Rs 709.65, followed by BEL at 0.72 per cent, ICICI Bank at 0.59 per cent and Asian Paints at 0.45 per cent.

Asian markets traded largely higher, with most major regional indices in positive territory.

Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Index gained 126.93 points, or 0.5%, to 25,438.14. South Korea’s KOSPI was the strongest performer among the key markets, rising 1.33%, or 86.96 points, to 6,649.68. Singapore’s Straits Times Index advanced 0.61% to 5,778.92, while Shanghai Composite climbed 0.42% to 3,958.026 and Shenzhen added 0.18% to 13,635.321.

Japan’s Nikkei 225 rose 117.94 points, or 0.18%, to 64,443.58. Taiwan’s index gained 0.13% to 46,225.82, while Malaysia’s benchmark edged up 0.27% to 1,713.43. Australia’s ASX 200 advanced 0.38% to 9,012.7. New Zealand’s NZX 50 slipped 0.05% to 13,923.97, while Thailand’s SET Index was unchanged at 1,575.07.

Asian equities staged a recovery, with MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan gaining 0.5%. The move followed modest gains on Wall Street and came as investors awaited fresh US economic data and comments from central bankers for clues on monetary policy. Japanese government bond yields declined from historic peaks, tracking an easing in US Treasury yields.

The benchmark US 10-year Treasury yield fell 0.99 basis point to 4.784%, while the 30-year Japanese government bond yield declined 10 basis points to 4.065%. Currency markets also saw movement, with the dollar index slipping 0.05% to 99.54. The yen strengthened 0.07% to 158.59 per dollar after gaining 0.9% in the preceding session, while the euro edged up to $1.1589.

US equities recovered after a three-session decline, with gains in large technology companies helping the major indices close higher. The S&P 500 advanced 0.5%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.6% and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.5%. Investors had been watching rising oil prices and higher bond yields, which had contributed to market pressure earlier in the week.

Technology stocks linked to artificial intelligence provided additional support. Dell Technologies jumped 15.8% after reporting stronger second-quarter profits and raising its fiscal-year revenue outlook, citing accelerating demand for AI computing. Nvidia gained 3.2%, while Micron Technology rose 2.4%. Palo Alto Networks reported quarterly results above Wall Street expectations, although its shares fell 9.3%.

Crude prices remained elevated amid concerns over possible disruptions to supplies through the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude was around $95.07 a barrel in futures trade, while the WTI was around $90.37. In a subsequent session, Brent stood at $95.33 a barrel, down 30 cents, or 0.31%, while WTI traded at $90.88, down 13 cents, or 0.14%.

Both benchmarks had seen sharp swings during the preceding session, moving as much as $2 higher before giving up some gains. Brent eventually settled 1% higher. Shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz also remained below normal levels, with four commodity vessels transiting the waterway compared with a 10-day average of about 13.

Rupee closed Wednesday's session at 94.73 against the US dollar, gaining 22 paise despite weakness in domestic equities and higher crude prices. The currency opened at 94.97 and moved between an intraday high of 94.73 and a low of 94.99. The gain followed a 27-paise rise in the previous session, while the rupee had also strengthened by 21 paise and 2 paise in the two sessions before that.

Forex traders attributed the latest movement to robust foreign capital inflows. Foreign institutional investors purchased equities worth Rs 6,688.37 crore on a net basis. The currency's performance came even as the dollar index stood at 99.82 and Brent crude remained around the $95-a-barrel level.

Benchmark indices remained under pressure, with the Sensex declining 373.93 points, or 0.49%, to close at 76,570.35, while the Nifty fell 141.35 points, or 0.59%, to settle at 23,914.45. The decline marked the third consecutive losing session of the month and pushed the Nifty below the 24,000 level. Selling was spread across the market, with all broad-market indices ending in negative territory.

Among sectors, Nifty Auto was the worst performer, falling more than 1%, followed by IT. On the BSE, Adani Ports, Power Grid, NTPC, Titan, ITC, Reliance, Trent, Bajaj Finance and L&T were among the gainers, while Infosys, M&M, Tech Mahindra, SBI and ICICI Bank were among the major drags.
