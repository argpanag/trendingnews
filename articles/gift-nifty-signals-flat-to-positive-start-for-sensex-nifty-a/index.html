Indian benchmark indices Sensex and Nifty are likely to open on a flat to mildly positive note on Friday, with GIFT Nifty indicating marginal gains, as a rally across Asian and US equities and a further decline in crude oil prices provide support. Easing US Treasury yields are another positive cue. However, oil still remaining above $100 a barrel and persistent Middle East tensions could keep investors cautious at higher levels.

GIFT Nifty was trading at 23,345 around 7:45 am, up 12 points, or 0.05 percent. Indian markets ended mixed in the previous session. The Nifty rose for a second consecutive day, gaining 53 points, or 0.23 percent, to 23,271, while the Sensex slipped 22 points, or 0.03 percent, to 74,315.

Asian equities advanced on Friday as investors assessed the global shift towards tighter monetary policy, while the pullback in crude oil and some stability in bond markets improved risk appetite. Investors are also awaiting an expected interest-rate increase from the Bank of Japan.

MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan gained 0.55 percent. Japan's Nikkei rose 0.9 percent, while South Korea's Kospi surged 2 percent. Bond markets stabilised after another sharp selloff this week. The benchmark 10-year US Treasury yield was at 4.936 percent, easing back below the 5 percent level after climbing to its highest since 2007.

US stocks rebounded sharply on Thursday, supported by lower oil prices, easing Treasury yields and solid labour-market data, as investors moved past the Federal Reserve's first interest-rate increase in more than three years. Technology stocks led the rally, pushing the Nasdaq Composite up 1.69 percent to 26,418.30. The S&P 500 climbed 1.14 percent to 7,637.74, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.62 percent to 51,779.85.

The Fed had unanimously raised its benchmark interest rate on Wednesday for the first time since July 2023. Investors continue to expect further tightening.

Crude oil prices extended their decline for a third consecutive session on Friday, offering some relief to Indian markets after the recent surge. Brent crude futures fell 1 percent to $103.77 a barrel, while West Texas Intermediate declined 1 percent to $100.88. Both benchmarks had also dropped around 1 percent on Thursday. Prices eased as hopes that alternative routes could help Middle Eastern crude reach global markets outweighed concerns over the continuing conflict between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis.

Ponmudi R, CEO of Enrich Money, said the retreat in WTI from its recent peak provides some macroeconomic relief, but oil around $100 remains high enough to keep investors guarded. He cautioned that continuing geopolitical uncertainty remains a major overhang, with any renewed escalation capable of quickly reversing the recent decline in crude prices.

Ponmudi sees 23,300-23,400 as the immediate resistance zone for the Nifty. A sustained move above 23,400 could extend the recovery towards 23,500-23,600, while 23,100-23,070 remains the crucial support area.

Foreign institutional investors continued to pull money out of Indian equities on Thursday, selling a net Rs 3,208.76 crore in the cash market. Domestic institutional investors remained buyers, purchasing equities worth Rs 3,617.75 crore and providing a counterweight to persistent foreign selling.
