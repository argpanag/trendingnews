Macy Grimshaw’s Custom Pieces Have Already Charmed Harry Styles and Emma Corrin, Now She’s Won H&M’s Design Award

TheTools · 19.11.2026 16:44 · source · permalink

Macy Grimshaw was announced this year’s winner of H&M’s Design Award in London yesterday—the Swedish company’s prize for fashion graduates is back after a six-year hiatus. The Central Saint Martin’s MA student, who made her paper-and-leather graduation collection about watching her grandmother’s memory fade with dementia, has already blown up across Instagram since she graduated in February.

Looking a bit stunned as she received a giant bouquet and €150,000 from H&M’s Ann-Sophie Johansson, she blinked, “I need to switch on my phone and tell my parents!”

“The whole purpose of the award is to celebrate creativity and to encourage and support young designers,” said Johansson. The competition was organized through a call-out to 70 fashion schools around the globe. The finalists, whittled down to a shortlist of eight, who get €10,000 each, turned out to be an assembly of highly successful grads from Polimoda in Florence, the Bunka school in Tokyo, IFM in Paris, La Cambre in Brussels, the Antwerp Academy, and Central Saint Martins. Three have already landed jobs in Paris houses.

Grimshaw’s prize-winning work explores how her grandmother’s memory faded with dementia.

Grimshaw’s custom pieces for Harry Styles, Emma Corrin, Raye, Alex Consani (Grimshaw has had the stylist Harry Lambert as an enthusiastic supporter since she was a BA student) might have had something to do with swaying the jury in her favor. Lambert testifies to her prolific creativity. “On everything we’ve collaborated on, Macy’s always come up with a dozen fully-formed ideas. She’s incredibly hard-working and I’m thrilled for her.”

Grimshaw’s also one of the few amongst the cohort who’ve already struck out into business on their own. Next up, she’s hoping to stage a presentation in London Fashion Week in February. The cash prize will boost her chances of affording that, but (before or after her show ) she also has the second part of the H&M Prize to work on: a separate contract for a collaboration collection. “I’m just excited for what it means to have help to produce my collection,” she said. “But also, I think it’s going to be a fantastic opportunity for myself to learn how that’s done on a larger scale. I’ve done it myself once on a very small scale, so I think it would be really interesting to learn from people how to do it. And what this means for my brand, I don’t really know. But I know I need to hire a financial advisor!”

Johansson illuminated: the winner goes to Stockholm to meet the collection team, product developers, and pattern makers. “Then we go on a production trip, most probably somewhere like Shanghai, to see the products actually taking shape.”

Grimshaw’s presentation.

Zooming out, the comeback of the H&M graduate prize fits the bigger picture of the fierce competition to upgrade fashion credibility that’s gripping the high street. With Zara collaborating with John Galliano, Steven Meisel, and a stable of high-flown creative talent, and the likes of Gap hosting Victoria Beckham, H&M is presumably looking to defend its home turf. Some of its past designer collaborations have been legend, particularly the ones with Martin Margiela and Versace. Lately, they’ve tapped Elie Saab and Stella McCartney, who was one of the Swedish retailers earliest collaborators.

Talking to the gathering of the finalists also gave a good overview of the aspirations and ideals of the class of 2026. Though they’re navigating a very difficult time for young designers, it’s their fearlessness and pragmatism, as well as their highly specific dedication to making both materials and meaning, that unites them. Ennis Finnerty Mackay (also from CSM MA) combined his outstanding tailoring skills and the poignant backstory about the generational impact of alcoholism in his collection. It won him an internship with Daniel Roseberry at Schiaparelli, and now a full-time role at Balenciaga couture, where he’ll be working with Martine Rose. “I love what I’m doing”, he said. “I want to learn as much as I can now. Only then, maybe a long time in the future, would I do my own thing.”

Jessica Lin, a BA graduate of IFM also made a collection with a strong personal message. “A tribute to my mother and also a reflection of migrant women in Hong Kong back in the days. She’s had several migration experiences, to the Philippines and coming to Hong Kong and then back to China.” Lin’s clothes incorporate boiler suits, textiles evoking tiles, plastic slip-on shoes, and frontal harnesses suggesting baby-carriers. “I was inspired by the migrant woman who has dual identities as a mother and also at the same time as a worker.” Her ambition, eventually, is to start a lifestyle brand, between China and Europe.

The H&M Design Award finalists.

The finesse and sexuality of Evelina Kryvopust’s collection was exceptional in close-up. “I think I’m the only Ukrainian that’s ever been in the H&M finals,” she smiled. The first step, literally, for this Polimoda graduate is into footwear. She’s producing her lingerie lace kitten heels in Italy. “I found a great factory in Naples.” Now she’s living in Milan, hoping to set up a studio with her designer boyfriend. One glance through her Instagram @evikrv proves this is someone who lives her brand, and who we’re likely to see a lot more about.

Custom orders are generally a way of life and a financial support-system for this generation. That’s how Macy Grimshaw’s done it so far. For many it makes complete sense, and is far more profitable than the risks involved in self-funding production for wholesale upfront, and then waiting months to get paid, if at all. On the other hand, this gathering of talent did go to prove that interesting, skilled, and intelligent young designers still are getting hired in top places. Fantin Delattre Pirenne, from La Cambre, is designing denim at Saint Laurent, while the exquisitely light textiles hand-made by the Antwerp Academy’s Yvonne Schichtel have landed her an internship at Dior haute couture. It may be a precarious time, but there was a lot of hope in that room.
