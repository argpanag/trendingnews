Tammy Abraham’s double powers Aston Villa to Carabao Cup win at Coventry

TheTools · 16.09.2026 21:02 · source · permalink

For Aston Villa, an overdue drama-free outing as Unai Emery’s side cruised into the Carabao Cup fourth round courtesy of a Tammy Abraham double and a Ross Barkley header.

The satisfaction was detectable in Emery’s voice as he reflected on a night of positives: Abraham’s first goals of the season, Ibrahim Mbaye’s encouraging full debut and another half-hour for the £52m club-record signing, Johan Manzambi. Are things clicking into gear after a summer of changes?

Unfortunately for Coventry, it was a sorry case of more of the same. Frank Lampard’s side have lost five of their six matches in all competitions, across which they have leaked 15 goals. It was hard to know whether Abraham’s tap-in or Barkley’s free header, both from corners, was worse from a Coventry perspective. The sight of Lampard hunched in the dugout, hands clasped, has become a familiar sight. “If you give two set pieces away as simply as we did, it’s quite hard to win against Premier League opposition,” the Coventry head coach said.

Lampard’s side, who have lost their opening league four matches by an aggregate of 10-0 and on Saturday visit Nottingham Forest hoping for lift-off, are yet to score outside this competition. Victor Torp’s sweet free-kick from the edge of Villa’s 18-yard box, to cancel out Abraham’s opener, represented Coventry’s first goal since Torp capped the scoring at Plymouth in the previous round. This victory earned Villa a fourth-round trip to Bournemouth at the end of October.

This was the first competitive meeting between these sides since 5 May 2001, when Coventry’s initial 34-year stay in the top flight came to an end with a 3-2 defeat at Villa Park. When Aurèle Amenda limped off with a calf injury after 10 minutes, Bobby Thomas, wearing the captain’s armband, was the only player left of the starting lineup from Sunday’s 5-0 home defeat against Brighton.

Emery made seven changes himself and George Hemmings, one of those to keep their place, was the architect for Villa’s opener. Matteo Ruggeri fed Hemmings down the left and the 19-year-old sent a sumptuous pass into Abraham with the outside of his right boot.

Coventry struggled to retain possession and Villa were enjoying themselves, exchanging crisp, clean passes. But then Barkley was penalised for fouling Caleb Yirenkyi on the edge of the Villa box and Torp stepped up to lift the subsequent free-kick over the Villa wall. John McGinn, the Villa captain reduced to the role of draught excluder, picked himself up from the turf.

A 57th-minute Rhys Norrington-Davies own goal sent Fleetwood into the last 16 of the Carabao Cup for the first time as the League Two club stunned their Championship opponents, Sheffield United, 1-0 at Highbury, before pulling a home tie against Arsenal out in the draw.

Matt Lawlor’s side, unbeaten now in nine games in all competitions, had another Danny Andrew effort ruled out for offside and twice hit the woodwork in a dominant second-half display. It was a searching left-wing cross from Andrew that Norrington-Davies nodded beyond Michael Cooper to give the fourth-tier club a historic victory.

Tom Cannon did get the ball in the net for Chris Wilder’s visitors who lined up with only four starting survivors from last Friday’s 1-0 league defeat by Wolves. But a flag for offside was raised before the striker slid the ball into the net.

United started positively and Ollie Arblaster had a goalbound effort blocked after only 36 seconds. But Mark Helm forced Cooper into a seventh-minute save before the former United forward Ched Evans put his follow-up header narrowly wide.

Jay Lynch denied Joe Rothwell as the visitors attacked again but Cooper was the busier, saving from Lewis McCann and Crispin McLean.

Helm’s dipping right-foot effort from 18 yards grazed the top netting as Fleetwood finished the first half in the ascendancy and they deserved their break from Norrington-Davies’ own goal.

United finally came to life in the last 10 minutes with Lynch brilliantly denied Sydie Peck and Ben Brereton Díaz, while Cannon saw a goalbound strike superbly blocked.

Everton’s forgotten man Charly Alcaraz stepped up when his side needed some inspiration to score the only goal in the win over Wolves three minutes after coming on. The Argentinian had played just 14 minutes all season but belied his lack of match sharpness with a quick switch of the ball from one foot to the other to guide a left-footed shot past the former Everton goalkeeper João Virgínia in the 80th minute.

It also represented a minor success for Tyler Dibling, whose career has not really taken off since his £35m move from Southampton just over a year ago, as he claimed the assist at the end his most encouraging performance for the club. Everton's reward is a home tie against Newcastle. Press Association

Suddenly Coventry were full of confidence, a little giddy. Thomas moonlighted as a striker, forcing an error from Tyrone Mings, but then declined a golden opportunity to shoot at goal and the chance fizzled out. Gustavo Hamer then whistled a left-foot shot just past a Villa upright.

Coventry sprung to life but Villa responded. McGinn smacked his hands together after spurning a decent chance and a few minutes later Barkley sent a low drive at goal that Dan Bentley did well to push wide for a corner, from which Villa prospered. Mbaye’s inswinging delivery was on the money and Barkley, totally unmarked in the six-yard box, headed in at the front post.

Coventry had the meanest defence in the Championship last season but have been porous since promotion. More slack marking enabled Villa to extend their lead. McGinn whipped a corner into the six-yard box and Abraham prodded in after walking off his marker, the 18-year-old Coventry debutant Stephen Mfuni, who this month joined on loan from Manchester City.

Abraham’s second gave Villa breathing space. Emery, whose side visit Tottenham on Saturday, felt comfortable enough to withdraw McGinn and Mbaye just after the hour. Zion Suzuki replaced the injured Marco Bizot at half-time, the goalkeeper reporting a back problem. Nicolas Jackson later came on in place of Abraham. Emery cycled through the changes but Alejandro Garnacho, who has played just 31 minutes since signing, remained on the bench.

“He is progressing but we have players in front of him now,” Emery said. “George is playing fantastic, Alysson is understanding a lot of things very good; he needs time but he is giving us things I want. Mbaye responded well. We want a competitive team and the players ready to compete on the standard we have. Of course, he [Garnacho] is a little bit behind because of some circumstances he had in pre-season. It is time to be patient for the time he needs.”
