Meet the Girl Who Made a Netflix for John Galliano Runway Shows - CR Fashion Book

TheTools · 15.09.2026 13:53 · source · permalink

Meet the Girl Who Made a Netflix for John Galliano Runway Shows 09/15/2026 ‎ ‎‎ — Umar Niaz Courtesy of Getty ImagesNidhi Srinivasan, professionally known as Aira, is a 23-year-old fashion writer and content creator based in India. She created the website to do something seemingly simple: make John Galliano’s entire runway archive feel as easy to explore as Netflix. But what started as a personal study project quickly became something much bigger. It began after the cancellation of the Metropolitan Museum of Art's planned John Galliano exhibition. Disappointed, Aira decided to conduct her own chronological deep dive into Galliano’s work, studying his career collection by collection and exploring the references, techniques, and ideas behind it.The result? A “Netflix with a syllabus” for studying John Galliano. GallianoFlix currently brings together footage from 132 runway shows, spanning his career across his label, Givenchy, Dior, and Maison Margiela, alongside external books, resources, and study materials. For Aira, the project is rooted in a belief that runway shows shouldn’t be an intimidating or exclusive part of fashion reserved for those with years of knowledge or an invitation to fashion week. Instead, she sees the runway as performance art—and performance art should be accessible to everyone.Below, CR spoke to Aira about creating the archive, discovering Galliano’s work through the process, and why she believes fashion’s most spectacular shows should be studied, appreciated, and enjoyed.Courtesy of Getty ImagesCR: OK, so explain to me, what is GallianoFlix?Aira: It's a website I designed to mimic Netflix, but for John Galliano’s runway shows. I have about 132 shows on there that I’ve sourced from publicly available footage, combined with study material from certain books and online resources. The goal was to be able to watch a runway show and read about it and understand it on a level where you could analyze and appreciate it on a deeper level.CR: What inspired you to make GallianoFlix?Aira: Most of my work comes down to wanting to democratize runway knowledge. I always felt runway was this intimidating side of fashion, and you needed vast knowledge, credentials, or an invitation to attend fashion week to appreciate it for the performance art that it is. I wanted to change that. I genuinely believe that runway as performance art can also be entertainment. I don’t think the scattered nature of runway research should be a barrier to entry. I think someone who doesn’t necessarily have as much knowledge about fashion should still be able to appreciate it as entertainment.CR: Why was John Galliano the perfect subject to center this around?Aira: When I heard about the Met exhibition, I was really excited about the conversations around his work. A lot of my favorite creators would be revisiting his work and talking about archival pieces that I probably had never seen before. I was excited about the influx of knowledge that was about to come in. When they cancelled it, I was so disappointed. I felt like a kid who got her candy stolen. That’s when I decided I was going to study his work chronologically myself so I could deep-dive into his mind in a way that I thought I would have if they had continued with the Met.Courtesy of Getty ImagesCR: How long did it take to create all of it? So, when did you start this?Aira: A couple of years ago, I started Vibe Coding to analyze data and trends for a project. I was really inspired by @databutmakeitfashion; she’s such an inspiration. I think she inspired a whole generation of people to combine fashion and tech. I made the first functioning website in a day with Claude Code. The archive itself is an entirely different conversation because it’s still ongoing research. I started it with the most popular resources I could find, books and footage. But now it’s become more of a community-driven phenomenon because people keep coming to me with better-quality resource suggestions, better footage, or anecdotes about John Galliano’s work.CR: Why do you find yourself so drawn to John Galliano’s work?Aira: Some girls find God and some girls find Dior by John Galliano. It’s a formative experience finding Dior by John Galliano at the age that I did. I believe it changed the way that I approach fashion.CR: What’s your favorite show?Aira: Dior Couture 2007, Madame Butterfly. I love that show. I think John Galliano’s storytelling is unmatched. There is no greater storyteller in fashion. Like, if you catch me blank-faced at any point during the day, I’m probably thinking of that Shalom Harlow origami wedding dress. Insane.Courtesy of Getty ImagesCR: What was the most challenging part of making GallianoFlix?Aira: I think the hardest part was working out the most reliable sources, especially when you have no map starting out. I’m leaning on my community a lot more than I planned to. Also, finding a video is one thing. Identifying it and dating it correctly, and cross-referencing the footage against collection imagery and established publications, is another. It’s a task. There are also restrictions. I’m not allowed to host any footage on my platform, so all I can do is curate and embed different sources on my site. So, if it’s not publicly available, I can’t put it up.CR: Was there a specific show or era of his career that was quite difficult to track down or find?Aira: I still haven’t found about 11 shows from his label. Most of them are from the ’80s, you know, before the greatest invention of our time, YouTube, was a thing.CR: Did you know it would go viral?Aira: Absolutely not. I genuinely did not build it for the sake of content. I built it from my own personal study of his work, and the reaction has been overwhelming. I’ve been a relatively small content creator for a while now, so this much online attention has been a whole new experience.CR: So, which Flix are you working on next? I heard McQueen?Aira: McQueen. Yes. I think I have not personally explored his work in the way that I have explored Galliano’s work. I think to understand a designer’s work, it’s really important to learn chronologically about their evolution. This one will be a group project. I’m truly grateful that I can lean on my community for good-quality resources. Courtesy of Getty Images
