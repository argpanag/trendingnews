Flu and discharge waits cause hospital bed shortage - AOL

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:01 · source · permalink

More patients are waiting in emergency departments without a bed due to rising flu cases and delays to discharges, NHS bosses have said.

Gloucestershire Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust said last week about 150 patients were medically fit to leave its hospitals but remained on wards, while about 60 beds were taken up by flu patients.

Emergency department staff are having to care for patients in corridors at peak times as about 30 people a day are waiting for a bed, the trust added.

Gloucestershire Health and Care NHS Foundation Trust, which runs the county's community care - often the next step after hospital care - said it was working to reduce delays.

Inpatients at Gloucestershire Royal Hospital in Gloucester and Cheltenham General Hospital are currently waiting about 13 days before being discharged compared with a national average of 6.3 days.

Kevin McNamara, chief executive of the hospital trust, said the availability of beds was a bigger issue this year compared to last.

"Part of that is a flu issue - we're seeing sicker and frailer people coming into hospitals," he said.

"There's also a structural issue that is the number of patients that are delayed with their discharges.

"Once you add that with the flu issue, it means it's a very challenging space for staff and patients," he added.

McNamara said the number of beds taken up by patients waiting to be moved somewhere else was as high as 220 per day in recent years.

The hospital trust had managed to get this down to about 100 a day, but this has now risen to 150 a day, he said.

This was echoed by Gloucestershire Health and Care NHS Foundation Trust, whose chief executive Douglas Blair told the BBC: "Delayed discharges are an issue across the health and care system and this includes discharges from our community hospitals.

"We and our partners, including Gloucestershire Hospitals Trust and Gloucestershire County Council, are firmly focused on reducing delays across the county and improving patient flow," he added.

Gloucestershire vaccination lead Sarah Gallagher urged those eligible for a flu vaccine to get it as soon as possible in order to ease pressures on local hospitals.

Gallagher said there had been more flu cases this year, which would rise further over the winter.

"The flu vaccine actually takes 14 days to start to work, so we really want anybody out there who's eligible and hasn't come forward yet to be vaccinated because then by mid-December they will be covered," she said.

She added that almost 210,000 people so far have been vaccinated, which equates to a 53% uptake.
