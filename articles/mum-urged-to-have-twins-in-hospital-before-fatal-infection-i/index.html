Mum urged to have twins in hospital before fatal infection, inquest told

The inquest into the deaths of four babies is being held at Lismore Coroners Court. (ABC)

In short:

An inquest is looking into the neonatal deaths of four babies born by freebirth or homebirth in NSW.

Deputy State Coroner Joan Baptie will hear evidence about the advice and treatment given to the babies' mothers.

Today the inquest heard about the death of Baby B in the Hunter Valley with the inquest told her mum was urged against a homebirth.

A midwife has sobbed telling an inquest into baby deaths after homebirths and freebirths she warned a Hunter Valley mother about the risk of giving birth to twins at home, before one infant died.

Deputy State Coroner Joan Baptie is presiding over the two week inquest examining neonatal deaths of three babies born by freebirth in northern New South Wales, and a fourth born by homebirth in the Hunter Valley.

The NSW Coroners Court in Lismore heard the infants died between 2022 and 2024.

Today a midwife, who can't be named, choked back tears when talking about the Hunter baby's death after a homebirth she assisted with.

"I advised her it was recommended she birth in hospital in case there was any emergency that arose,"

The midwife said the infant known as Baby B was a twin and that carried extra risks.

The court heard the closest specialised maternity hospital was two hours away.

"I encouraged her to stay in Maitland and in close proximity to the hospital," the midwife said,

The Hunter woman was expecting twins and was urged by a midwife to give birth in Maitland hospital, two hours away. (ABC Newcastle: Liz Farquhar)

"For the majority of pregnancy I didn't think she would end up birthing at home."

Home prepared for birth

The midwife said the expectant mum was adamant she wanted to birth at home.

"She had a very strong view about her body's ability to birth her babies," she said.

"She has built her home and prepared her home for her children and had done immense research to do with birth and labour."

The inquest heard doctors told the woman during her pregnancy, birthing twins carried risks. (Unsplash: Anastasiia Chepinska)

The midwife was among a team of four who attended to the woman, with a twin boy arriving first and Baby B second.

Ambulance called

The midwife told the court the woman phoned her at night about 12 hours after the babies arrived and all was well, but the next morning Baby B was not responding.

At that point the mum called an ambulance, with a snippet of her call referred to in court.

"Our little baby, it is our little baby she has just stopped breathing," Baby B's mum told the Triple Zero (000) operator.

The infant was rushed to a local rural Hunter hospital where she died.

Counsel Assisting the Coroner, Ragni Mathur, told the inquest evidence would show the cause of death was Group B Streptococcus (GBS).

The World Health Organisation said it was a common bacterium that was usually harmless in adults but can contribute to life-threatening infections in infants, including sepsis, pneumonia and meningitis.

An ambulance rushed the baby to a rural Hunter hospital where she died. (ABC News: Timothy Ailwood)

The court heard 40 per cent of women have the bacteria in their vagina.

Death not linked to homebirth, expert says

Midwifery expert Professor Hannah Dahlen agreed the homebirth was not the cause.

"This baby sadly died from early onset from GBS that can also happen in hospital and this was not directly related to a homebirth,"

Professor Dahlen did not fault the team of midwives who had a plan and had advised she birth in hospital.

Homebirths were deliveries supported by a registered midwife outside a clinical setting.

A freebirth means a baby was born without medical assistance, often supported by family, friends, a doula or "birth keeper".

Doula questioned

Yesterday, the inquest heard about the death of Baby DH who was born by freebirth in northern New South Wales in 2024.

Baby DH's mother did not access clinical antenatal care throughout the pregnancy, instead choosing a freebirth and engaging the services of birth doula Amy Lou Smith.

In her evidence,  Ms Smith said she would have supported the mother to seek medical assistance if she asked for it.

She said she did not have the medical knowledge to recognise the baby was at risk, and thought the baby was simply cold.

Ms Mathur pointed to text messages tendered to the court that indicated Ms Smith did not suggest the parents seek medical help when they raised growing concerns about their baby.

The court heard a post-mortem revealed Baby DH died from severe and complex congenital cardiac abnormalities.

Expert reports tendered to court said while the baby could not have survived without specialised care, the death "was not inevitable".

On Tuesday the inquest was told twins delivered via freebirth in northern NSW would probably have survived if their mother had sought antenatal care.

The inquest continues.
