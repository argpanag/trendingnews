Battle of LA: Missing Ingram and Beal Cost the Clippers Against the Lakers

TheTools · 24.10.2026 17:30 · source · permalink

Battle of LA: BI Mathews looks at how the absences of Brandon Ingram and Bradley Beal exposed the Clippers‘ shot creation problem against the Lakers and what it means for the Clippers. In short, the Clippers are desperately missing these two players.

The Battle of LA is back. Last night, the Los Angeles Lakers took down crosstown rival LA Clippers, and it was clear the game came down to two missing players.

Brandon Ingram and Bradley Beal.

The Clippers’ first two games have provided something valuable: a scrappy defensive style. In their home opener, versus the Sacramento Kings, they were able to hold lottery pick Darius Acuff Jr. to 12 points and 5/24 from the field, an inefficient shooting night. They followed that up against the Lakers, holding their star Luka Doncic to 22 points and 8/21 from the field, well below his average. Those things are valuable, but the questions are on offense.

That’s when Ingram and Beal come in.

What do they both provide?

Both Ingram and Beal are on the Clippers’ injury report. Ingram is dealing with a partially torn Achilles he suffered while with the Toronto Raptors in the playoffs last year. Beal has been dealing with right knee inflammation as he ramps up.

However, when they get back on the court, they can significantly help the Clippers in one area: shot creation.

The Clippers’ All-Star point guard Darius Garland scored 30 points in their home opener versus the Kings. That is a team which seemed to run much of the offense through Acuff Jr. and not their big man Domantas Sabonis, or their off-ball scorer Zach LaVine. Part of that was the Clippers’ stifling defense; the other part was the Kings not exploiting the mismatch.

The Lakers had a much different game plan: keep the ball out of Garland’s hands as much as possible, and when he does get it, trap him. That is a game where you need another shot creator, not just for scoring, but to free up Garland. The Clippers provided the same problem for Doncic, who struggled. However, Doncic had his backcourt mate, Austin Reaves, to lean on, who scored a game-high 28 points on 76% shooting.

The Clippers got balanced scoring from Brook Lopez (22), Rui Hachimura and Derrick Jones Jr. (15 each), and Keaton Wagler (13). While they provided much-needed defense, the game was decided in the fourth quarter, and the Clippers needed another consistent 20+ scorer like Ingram/Beal to take them home.

The Clippers will have another contest on Sunday, Oct. 26, on the road versus the powerhouse Oklahoma City Thunder. Another game where they’ll need shot creation.
