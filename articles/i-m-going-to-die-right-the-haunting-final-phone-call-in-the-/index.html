"I'm going to die, right?": The haunting final phone call in the 9/11 tragedy.

TheTools · 10.09.2026 23:04 · source · permalink

(Dan Tri Newspaper) - 25 years after the 9/11 terrorist attacks in the US, archival documents reveal that innocent victims attempted to make final, haunting phone calls before their deaths.

On September 11, 2001, nearly 3,000 people died in the deadliest terrorist attack in American history. The story of that horrific day is largely told through shocking videos and heartbreaking images of the most devastating terrorist tragedy in American history.

The final phone calls and text messages left by the victims also paint an equally somber picture of the despair and suffering of innocent people trapped in the burning twin towers or on planes hijacked by terrorists.

It's the bewilderment and heartbreak when disaster suddenly strikes, the last words sent to loved ones, or the desperate plea for help.

"I think we're being hijacked."

Looking back at the horrific moments of the 9/11 terrorist attacks (Video: USA Today).

The first signs that something unusual had happened on the morning of September 11th appeared shortly after 8:14 a.m., when air traffic controllers lost radio contact with American Airlines Flight 11.

Five minutes later, flight attendant Betty Ong began calling American Airlines staff to report an attack on a flight from Boston to Los Angeles.

“The cockpit wasn’t responding. Someone was being stabbed in the business class cabin. And I think there was pepper spray; we couldn’t breathe. I don’t know anymore. I think we were being hijacked,” Ong said.

A few minutes later, air traffic controllers in Boston received two messages from Mohamed Atta, the hijacker piloting Flight 11, which he had inadvertently broadcast over the communications system.

“We have several planes. Just be quiet and you’ll be fine. We’re returning to the airport. No one is to move. Everything will be alright. If you try to do anything, you’ll put yourselves and the planes in danger. Just be quiet,” Atta said, thinking he was speaking to passengers on Flight 11.

Flight 11 carried 76 passengers, 11 crew members, 5 hijackers, and approximately 37,854 liters of jet fuel on its transcontinental journey.

At 8:46 a.m., Flight 11 crashed into the North Tower of the World Trade Center (WTC), between floors 93 and 99, and erupted into a massive fireball.

The burning wreckage of the plane that fell blocked access to all three emergency stairwells from the 92nd floor upwards, trapping hundreds of people on the upper floors.

Just minutes after the crash, New York City's 911 system was flooded with calls. Around 8:50 a.m., one of the first recorded 911 calls from inside the North Tower came from Christopher Hanley, 35, who was attending a conference at the Windows on the World restaurant on the 106th floor.

"There's smoke and things are pretty bad. We can't get down the stairs. There are about 100 people here," Hanley calmly told the operator.

Neither Hanley nor the 911 operators knew that a plane had crashed into the tower; they only knew that some kind of explosion had occurred. Even some witnesses to the crash believed it was an accident.

The 911 operator connected Hanley to a fire department dispatcher. The dispatcher told him to stay put and “just be patient.” Hanley’s last words on the call were: “Okay, please hurry.”

Nearly 3,000 people died in the tragedy (Photo: AFP).

Jim Gartenberg, a real estate executive, had just started a new job and went to the World Trade Center on September 11th to clear out his desk. Trapped on the 86th floor of the North Tower, Gartenberg made numerous phone calls seeking help.

Gartenberg never escaped the North Tower, but he managed to speak to Jill one last time and tell her how much he loved her and their two-year-old daughter, Nicole. Jill was three months pregnant with their second child at the time.

"If that happens, it will happen very quickly."

The New York Twin Towers are shrouded in smog as seen from above. (Photo: AFP)

At 8:15 a.m., United Airlines Flight 175, carrying 51 passengers, took off from Boston, also bound for Los Angeles. Peter and Sue Hanson, along with their two-and-a-half-year-old daughter Christine, were on their way to Disneyland.

There were five hijackers on Flight 175. Similar to Flight 11, they attacked the crew and took control of the cockpit.

Peter Hanson called his father, C. Lee Hanson, to tell him that the plane had been hijacked. Neither of them knew that a plane had already crashed into the World Trade Center, but Peter had a terrible premonition that it would happen.

"Don't worry, Dad. If it happens, it will happen very quickly," he said.

While Peter and his father were talking, Mr. Lee was watching a television news report about the North Tower being engulfed in smoke. At that moment, Peter began muttering, "Oh my God, oh my God, oh my God!" Mr. Lee watched in horror as his son's plane crashed straight into the South Tower.

The two WTC towers were engulfed in flames before collapsing (Photo: AFP).

Julie Sweeney Roth didn't get a chance to speak to her husband, Bryan Sweeney, a former U.S. Navy pilot who was also on Flight 175. But when she got home, she found a voicemail he had left that morning on his phone mounted on the back of his seat.

“Jules, it’s Bryan. Listen, I’m on a hijacked plane. If things don’t go well, I just want you to know that I love you very much. I want you to be well, to enjoy your time. My parents and everyone else too. I truly love you, and I’ll see you when you get there,” Sweeney wrote in his final message.

"I'm going to die, right?"

The scene inside the South Tower was horrific. Flight 175 was carrying approximately 34,448 liters of jet fuel, and the crash and explosion ignited massive fires across multiple floors, severing elevator cables and rendering two of the three emergency stairwells unusable. One stairwell remained passable, allowing some people in the upper section where the plane crashed to escape.

Melissa Doi was on the 83rd floor of the South Tower when the second plane crashed into the WTC. The 32-year-old manager of IQ Financial Systems called 911 at 9:17 a.m., about 15 minutes after the crash.

Doi's final call was one of the most agonizing and was played during the life sentence trial of Zacarias Moussaoui, an accomplice in the 9/11 attacks.

“Nobody’s here yet, and this whole floor is engulfed in flames. We’re up here and we can’t breathe, it’s incredibly hot,” Doi told the operator.

The operator tried to keep Doi calm while waiting for rescue forces to arrive, but her situation was extremely critical.

"I'm going to die, aren't I?" Doi asked, tears streaming down her face.

"No, no, no, no, no, no, ma'am, please pray," the operator replied.

“I’m going to die. I know I’m going to die,” Doi said, bursting into tears.

Once again, the operator advised Doi to “think positively,” but the last words he heard before the line was cut off were: “Oh my God, it’s so hot. I’m being burned alive.”

"We'll regain control of the plane."

The anguish of the women witnessing the tower burning in New York (Photo: AFP).

Twenty-five minutes behind schedule, United Airlines Flight 93 was still in the air when three other hijacked planes crashed into the World Trade Center and the Pentagon, respectively.

Flight 93 was the only flight where passengers and crew were able to fight back against the hijackers, preventing the plane from reaching its intended target. Much of their motivation to resist stemmed from information received via phone calls with family and others on the ground.

Alice Hoagland, mother of Flight 93 passenger Mark Bingham, spoke to her son shortly after 9:37 a.m. After the call ended, she left him a voicemail warning that other hijacked planes had been used as weapons and urging him to try to regain control of the aircraft.

"The news reports say the plane has been hijacked by terrorists. They might intend to use the plane as a target to crash into something on the ground, so if you can, try to overpower them. They might use the plane as a weapon," Hoagland told his son.

Deena Burnett spoke to her husband, Tom Burnett, another passenger on Flight 93, and told him that the Pentagon had been attacked. She later recalled hearing her husband relay that information to other passengers.

"I'm making a plan. We're going to get back control of the plane," Tom told his wife.

CeeCee Lyles, a flight attendant on Flight 93 and former police officer, left a voicemail for her husband, Lorne.

“The plane was hijacked. I’m trying to stay calm. They turned around and I heard some planes crashed into the World Trade Center. I hope to see you again. I love you, goodbye,” Lyles said.

Lyles called back and was able to speak with Lorne. They prayed together, and Lyles ended the call with the message: “Tell the children that I love them. We’re about to do it now. It’s happening!”

In another call with a telephone operator, passenger Todd Beamer shouted, "Is everyone ready? Charge!"

By fighting off the hijackers and attempting to enter the cockpit, the passengers and crew of Flight 93 prevented the plane from reaching its target in Washington, D.C., where investigators believe the intended target could have been the Capitol building or the White House.
