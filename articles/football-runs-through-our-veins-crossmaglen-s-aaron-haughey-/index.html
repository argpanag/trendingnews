‘Football runs through our veins...’ Crossmaglen’s Aaron Haughey looking forward to first taste of Ulster action

TheTools · 07.11.2026 20:48 · source · permalink

CROSSMAGLEN were getting battered. Clann Eireann ran riot in the first half of their Armagh championship semi-final and as score followed score it seemed there would be no way back for the Rangers.

Cross were sinking but one young player kept his head above water.

Aaron Haughey carried the fight to the Lurgan outfit with a couple of defiant scores that just about kept his club in the game, although there were 14 points between the teams at half-time.

In the second half Haughey was a dynamo in the middle third as Crossmaglen got their hands on the ball, clicked into gear and produced a famous comeback to win the game with an epic Oisin O’Neill two-pointer.

The Rangers progressed to the final and won it too of course with several more of their emerging young players, including Pearse Blessing and substitute Keelan McEntee, standing tall and sweeping them to victory over Killeavy.

It was the club’s 48th county title, it was Haughey’s first title.

“It’s very special,” he said afterwards.

“It’s special for everyone whether it’s your first, or for some of the boys it’s their seventh or maybe eighth.”

His uncles John and Tony McEntee played in the era when Crossmaglen ruled the land. All-conquering Cross sides won 19 county titles in 20 years as well as 11 Ulster championships and half-a-dozen All-Ireland titles between 1997 and 2012.

That level of dominance wasn’t going to last forever and the balance of power had shifted by the time Haughey came along. In his first two seasons of senior football Cross failed to reach the county final.

“They won the championship the year before I came onto the team (2024) and I suppose I thought: ‘This is what it’s going to be like every year’ but it didn’t work out like that,” he said.

“We had a few harsh words at half-time that day against Clann Eireann and our main men showed what they can do, Oisin was outrageous

“I lost a couple of minor finals and I lost a couple of finals at school (with St Colman’s, Newry) so it’s nice to win one.”

Haughey was last year’s Armagh U20 skipper and the experience of that campaign under Barry O’Hagan and three seasons of senior football should have prepared him well for his first taste of Ulster championship action.

Crossmaglen begin the quest for a 12th provincial title against Vinny Corey’s Cavan champions Gowna at Kingspan Breffni on Saturday (7.15pm).

“I’m doing alright,” says Haughey modestly.

“I got a bit of momentum from the U20s and I took it into the club. We were down a few men earlier on in the year, we were missing Rian and Oisin (O’Neill) so I suppose I had to stand up a wee bit.

“Somebody had to step up and thankfully I did that.”

He was one of a talented new wave of Crossmaglen players who got their first taste of a championship final a fortnight ago. After the victory against Killeavy manager Shane Lennon applauded the efforts of the youngsters who did so much to help the club regain their Orchard county crown.

“It’s well-documented the stars we have but we had at least six lads started their first final,” said Lennon.

“That’s not including Keelan McEntee and James Teague who came on. The amount of youth in the dressingroom… There’s eight or nine of the panel who are U21s, which is unbelievable to have.

“People look at all the superstars but it’s hard to believe Pearse Blessing, Aaron Haughey and Eugene McEntee and these boys are still U21 and they are chomping at the bit.”

Haughey and his cousins Eugene and Keelan McEntee added three more medals to the family treasure trove well stocked by the previous generation.

“The club means everything,” he said.

“Football just runs through our veins, my granny is Mona McEntee (mother of John and Tony) so it’s good to add another medal to her collection.”
