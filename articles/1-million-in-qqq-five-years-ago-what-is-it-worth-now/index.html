QQQ is the fund that holds the AI leaders. Say you put $1 million into it on Nov 18, 2021. You reinvested every dividend and never sold. At Wednesday's close, it was worth about $1.69 million.

That is the ending. The path to it was much harder.

How Far Did $1 Million In QQQ Fall?

It sat below $1 million for almost two years.

Six weeks after you bought, it started falling. By Nov 3, 2022, your million was worth $652,000. From January 2022, it stayed below what you paid for 487 trading days in a row. That is about 23 months. It did not get back above $1 million for good until Dec 12, 2023.

To get from $652,000 back to $1 million, QQQ needed a 53% gain. It got one, rising 54.9% in 2023. But nobody knew that in November 2022.

The swings have not stopped. On Oct 6, the same million was worth $1.95 million. Six weeks later, at the Nov 18 close, it was $1.69 million. That is $257,000 less. Across the whole five years, it was below $1 million on 41% of all trading days.

QQQ's million fell to $652,000 before it grew to $1.69 million. Growth is easier to hold on to when the rest of your money falls less. The Trefis Income Solution pays a dividend yield about 3x the S&P 500's, and it lost less than the S&P 500 in all 9 big selloffs since 2018. See the metrics.

What If A Fund Never Gets Back?

It happens.

ARKK is an innovation fund that rose 153% in 2020. It then turned $1 million into $263,000 by December 2022. It rose 69% in 2023, 8% in 2024 and 35% in 2025. That is three good years in a row. Yet at Wednesday's close, the million was still only $635,000. In five years, it never once closed back above $1 million. From $263,000, it needed to almost quadruple. From here, it still needs a 58% gain.
