Dow dives 1,000 points as Anthropic IPO milestone causes investors to take profits: Live updates

TheTools · 18.11.2026 23:02 · source · permalink

Stocks tumbled on Thursday, falling to their lows of the session the very moment the widely anticipated artificial intelligence IPO Anthropic began trading.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq dropped 2.2% after gaining as much as 2% earlier in the session. The S&P 500 shed 2%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 1,084 points, or 2.2%.

Anthropic, trading at the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, opened shortly before noon at $111.50 per share, a 34% pop. The stock was trading more than 10% off the first trading price and was last up 17%.

The major averages fell to session lows immediately following the start of trading for the much-hyped IPO, as investors appeared to take profits at the culmination of this moment for the AI trade.

The artificial intelligence company priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above the expected range. That had chip stocks and other AI plays jumping in morning trading.

Chipmakers Nvidia and Micron Technology gave up their gains, along with tech giants Microsoft and Meta Platforms. Advanced Micro Devices lost more than 7% after Hunterbrook Capital disclosed a short position in the name, pointing to alleged U.S. export control breaches that involve China.

"This was always going to be a critical test for the tech sector. Anthropic could be the new poster child for AI trade, especially after Nvidia headlines," said Aleks Spencer, Bogart Wealth CIO. "There's a lot here too that needs to be processed and digested, which translates to this period of uncertainty and volatility."

The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX), or Wall Street's so-called fear gauge, reached a day high of close to 29 — its highest level since March — shortly after Anthropic began trading and investors moved out of tech stocks. The index was last above 28.

"The enthusiasm comes in, and it's a great moment to sell the rally and take some profits," Spencer added.

The market has become more cautious on AI as of late, with investors focusing on whether heavy spending on the buildout will translate into meaningful profits. The iShares AI Innovation and Tech Active ETF (BAI) has fallen 10% this month, and embattled AI bellwether Nvidia has dropped 27% while the company grapples with the resignation of its CEO Jensen Huang. Shares of CoreWeave, which had seen its bond yields trade above 16% earlier this month, is down 16% in November.

To be sure, some on Wall Street aren't too alarmed about the rollercoaster ride sparked by Anthropic's debut.

"Today doesn't matter," said Baird tech strategist Ted Mortonson, who deemed the day's action in Anthropic akin to that of a casino. "It's a blink of an eye of their journey. It's an important event, but how they navigate the next three years that will determine valuation."

Zoom Communications is set to be a follow-on winner from the Anthropic IPO due to the relationship between the two companies and its ability to monetize AI in-house, according to StoneX.

StoneX maintained its buy rating on Zoom in a Nov. 12 note, with a $125 price target. The stock was trading around $88 per share around noon on Thursday.

"The way capital markets received Anthropic shows that, for premium assets, the market is still very interested in the AI trade," he added. "This is very positive for the rest of the AI industry, from data center builders and the people building turbines for their power to chip providers from Nvidia and AMD to TSMC."

The head of France's central bank told CNBC he is confident there will be a budget put in place for next year, despite political divisions creating an impasse in talks among lawmakers.  

A draft budget proposed by Prime Minister Emmanuel Moulin's government, which aimed to drastically cut spending, was unanimously rejected by lawmakers in France's National Assembly last month.

French government bonds, known as OATs, have come under intense pressure this year, with borrowing costs surging to multi-decade highs as the Iran war pulled global bond yields higher and investors became increasingly concerned about France's fiscal picture. The spread over German debt has touched on levels not seen since the euro zone debt crisis.

Speaking to CNBC's Charlotte Reed in Paris, Banque de France Governor Emmanuel Moulin said he thought there had been an "overreaction" within financial markets.

"Spreads are high, very high, and too high — they are too high compared with the situation of the French economy, but also its fiscal situation, which is not good, which needs to be dealt with, but which has not changed materially from June to September," he explained. "But I think it's important for the government to give signs that they are serious about reducing the deficit."

Moulin said the government had put forward a budget aimed at decreasing the French budget deficit from 5.4% of GDP to 5% of GDP.

"I am quite confident that the budget will be implemented," he said. "I am not sure it will be voted [through] by Parliament because there is no majority and it's quite polarized, but there are other ways to have the budget adopted."

Since 2024′s snap election delivered a hung parliament, successive governments have used Article 49.3 of the constitution to force budgets through without a vote.

But threats of motions of no confidence have previously forced concessions. If a government is ousted, lawmakers must go back to the drawing board with the budget.

Another option is to pass the budget by ordinance, which has never been done in the French Fifth Republic, created in 1958. If a government is ousted after using this power, the budget it forces through remains in place.

Lecornu secured the 2026 budget via Article 49.3, after replacing ousted Prime Minister François Bayrou.

"This is, I think, the two routes that the governments is looking at," Moulin told CNBC.

— Chloe Taylor

The International Energy Agency said Thursday that a resolution to this year's energy crisis "could usher in a buyers' market for oil and natural gas, with significant downward pressures on prices."

"The prospects and timing of any resolution are highly uncertain but, if constraints on flows through the Strait of Hormuz are decisively eased, market balances could switch," the industry body said in its flagship World Energy Outlook report.

Oil has cooled off from its wartime highs of well above $100 a barrel, but still trades at elevated prices.

The IEA also noted in its report that confidence in the Strait of Hormuz "has been shattered."

"Additional bypass projects are moving ahead, but it will not be easy or quick to manage without oil shipments through this strategic waterway," it said.

New pipeline projects are under construction or consideration to enhance Gulf producers' ability to reach international markets without transiting the strait, the IEA said.

"Building them would require an estimated USD 40 billion, adding around USD 2 to the cost of a barrel of oil exported from the Middle East," it said in the report. "If all these projects are completed, non-Gulf producers step up output, and importers bring down demand as in the ARC, then our Low Hormuz Case reduces the need for transit through the Strait to 2 mb/d by 2035."
