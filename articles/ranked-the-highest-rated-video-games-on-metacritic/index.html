Which Video Games Have Earned the Highest Ratings?

Key Takeaways

  • Grand Theft Auto VI and the original The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time share the highest Metascore of 99 out of 100, despite being released 28 years apart.
  • Nintendo accounts for 12 of the 22 platform exclusives in the graphic, including five Zelda releases.

From GoldenEye 007 and Halo to Grand Theft Auto and Zelda, some of gaming’s most recognizable franchises have earned near-perfect reviews from critics.

This graphic highlights 33 releases scoring at least 96 out of 100 on Metacritic’s all-time games list, using a November 18, 2026, snapshot of critic scores. Metacritic converts critics’ ratings to a 100-point scale and weights some publications more heavily when calculating each game’s Metascore.

GTA VI Matches a Score Zelda Set in 1998

Grand Theft Auto VI and the original Ocarina of Time share a Metascore of 99 out of 100, making them the only two games in the graphic to reach that mark.

Released in 1998, Ocarina of Time brought Zelda into 3D on the Nintendo 64 and introduced Z-targeting, a feature that influenced many subsequent action games. Its 2026 remake for the Nintendo Switch 2 is among the few recent releases featured in the ranking.

The table shows each featured game’s release year, platform, and Metascore.

Only five of the 33 releases scored 98 or higher. The three games at 98 are SoulCalibur, Super Mario Galaxy 2, and Grand Theft Auto IV, representing fighting, platforming, and open-world crime games across different generations.

GoldenEye 007, the earliest release on the timeline, scored 96 in 1997. The table uses the scores displayed on Metacritic’s all-platform list, which can differ from individual platform scores. For example, Baldur’s Gate 3 is listed at 96 for PC, while its Xbox Series X edition earned 99 on its platform-specific page.

Nintendo Has More Exclusives Than All Other Platforms Combined

Nintendo accounts for 12 of the 22 platform exclusives, more than all other platforms combined. PlayStation contributes five, while PC, Sega, and Xbox account for the remaining five.

The Legend of Zelda franchise is particularly prominent, with five top-scoring releases across four Nintendo systems, more appearances than any other series.

The graphic classifies games as multiplatform if they reached systems from more than one company within three months of their debut. Eleven releases meet that definition.

Four Grand Theft Auto games made the cut, all scoring 97 or higher. GTA III debuted on PlayStation 2 in October 2001, but its PC version arrived the following May, outside the three-month window. The three later entries qualify as multiplatform, including GTA V, which also ranks among the best-selling games of all time.

Anthropic vs. OpenAI: Who’s Winning the AI Race?

Anthropic vs. OpenAI: Who’s Winning the AI Race?

Anthropic and OpenAI have traded the title of top AI model eight times since 2024. See who leads now, and how long they’ve held it.

Who’s Ahead in the Race Between Anthropic and OpenAI?

Key Takeaways

  • Anthropic’s Fable 5.2 leads the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 64.6 as of Nov. 17, 2026, ahead of OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra at 62.1.
  • Anthropic has held the lead for seven consecutive months since April 2026, its longest stretch in front, after leadership changed hands eight times between September 2024 and April 2026.
  • The two companies released 13 frontier models in 2026 through mid-November, more than the 11 released in 2024 and 2025 combined.

The rivalry between Anthropic and OpenAI has become one of the defining competitions in artificial intelligence. Each new flagship model offers another chance to claim the top spot on AI intelligence benchmarks.

This graphic tracks the highest scores achieved by each company on the Artificial Analysis (AA) Intelligence Index over time. It includes Anthropic’s Opus and Fable models and OpenAI’s leading GPT and o-series models, showing only releases that set a new high for their company.

The index currently combines nine tests covering knowledge work, coding, science, and general knowledge. AA updated its methodology on Oct. 27, so scores before and after that date are not directly comparable.

Anthropic Has Had the Smartest AI Models Since April 2026

For much of the AI race, neither Anthropic nor OpenAI could hold onto the top spot for long. Between May 2025 and April 2026 alone, leadership changed seven times, with some advantages lasting only weeks.

That pattern changed when Anthropic’s Opus 4.7 overtook OpenAI in April 2026. Despite a steady stream of new releases from both companies, Anthropic has remained ahead on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index ever since.

The table below lists every model on the chart in order of release, along with its score on each version of the index:

Scores for models released before Opus 4.8 (May 2026) are Artificial Analysis estimates. Version 5.0.1 scores are shown for models plotted after the Oct. 27 methodology change.

Some victories in the AI race have been remarkably short-lived. Anthropic’s Opus 4.5 held the lead for just 17 days before OpenAI’s GPT-5.2 overtook it in December 2025.

The longer-term picture is more balanced than the current streak suggests. Since March 2024, Anthropic has spent 529 days in front, compared with 459 days for OpenAI. That makes its current run especially significant in a competition where leadership has historically been difficult to maintain.

Anthropic’s lead also survived AA’s Oct. 27 update, which added two tests, upgraded three, and removed three.

However, different benchmarks can produce different winners. On Epoch AI’s Capabilities Index, OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra leads with a score of 174.2, narrowly ahead of Fable 5.2 at 173.5. The difference falls within both models’ margins of error.

Frontier AI Model Releases Have Doubled in Pace This Year

The competition has also accelerated. In 2025, Anthropic and OpenAI released a new frontier model roughly every 52 days. In 2026, that interval has shortened to about 25 days, with 13 releases through mid-November.

Anthropic has driven much of that pace, accounting for eight of the 13 releases this year. OpenAI has often responded within days. GPT-6 Astra arrived two days after Fable 5.1 and scored 0.7 points lower. In November, GPT-6.2 Astra followed Fable 5.2 by eight days and finished 2.4 points behind.

But benchmark leadership is only one measure of success. OpenAI also faces growing competition in the broader AI chatbot market, where user adoption tells a different story.

ChatGPT accounted for 88% of global AI chatbot website traffic at the end of 2024, but its share fell to 56% by September 2026 as Anthropic’s Claude and Google Gemini gained ground.

Mapped: Internet Censorship Around the World

Which countries censor the internet most? Explore government censorship worldwide, from North Korea and China to the U.S. and Europe.

How Internet Censorship Varies Around the World

Key Takeaways:

  • North Korea (-3.74) has the most restrictive government filtering of political information online, followed by Ethiopia (-3.26) and Algeria (-3.23).
  • Saudi Arabia and the UAE rank among the five most restrictive countries, despite having near-universal internet access.
  • European countries dominate the least restrictive end of the rankings, led by Belgium, Lithuania, and Luxembourg.

Governments around the world vary dramatically in how much they restrict political information online, with some countries imposing extensive controls over what citizens can access.

This visualization uses the Digital Society Project’s Digital Society Survey, Version 8 (2026) to compare government filtering of political information across 179 countries in 2025.

Which Countries Censor the Internet Most?

The following table shows government filtering scores from the Digital Society Project, with lower scores indicating more censorship. Scores are expert estimates, not percentages.

One striking result is that several countries rank as more restrictive than China (-2.82) and Russia (-2.00). Ethiopia, Algeria, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE all score lower on government filtering of political information, highlighting how the rankings differ from common perceptions.

At the opposite end, Belgium (2.70) and Lithuania (2.56) are among the countries with the least restrictive filtering.

Why Is Internet Censorship So High in the Middle East?

Saudi Arabia and the UAE illustrate the difference between connectivity and freedom. Both report 100% internet usage, as do Bahrain and Kuwait, while Qatar exceeds 98%.

High internet penetration, however, does not guarantee unrestricted political expression. Governments can maintain widespread connectivity while blocking sensitive political content, restricting websites, or penalizing online criticism.

Freedom House has documented restrictions on political content and arrests related to online expression across the region, illustrating how widespread internet access can coexist with extensive censorship.

Asia’s Divided Internet

Asia shows striking differences. China (-2.82) and Vietnam (-2.71) impose substantial filtering, while Japan (1.76) and South Korea (1.48) are comparatively open.

Across Southeast Asia, governments have increasingly pressured platforms to remove content, according to Deutsche Welle.

Why Europe and North America Score Better

Nordic countries perform strongly, including Norway (2.50), Denmark (2.37), Finland (2.35), and Sweden (2.19). European privacy protections generally regulate how personal data is handled rather than restrict political criticism, allowing strong privacy laws to coexist with relatively open political discourse.

The United States (1.53) scores above Canada (1.07), France (1.25), and Germany (0.52). Constitutional protections for political speech may help explain the U.S. score, although this measure does not capture every form of platform moderation or surveillance.

The U.S. also illustrates an important distinction between censorship and surveillance. While it scores relatively well on government filtering of political information (1.53), its government social media monitoring score is considerably lower (-0.11).

This suggests that relatively open access to political information does not necessarily mean limited government monitoring of online activity. The two measures capture different dimensions of internet freedom.
