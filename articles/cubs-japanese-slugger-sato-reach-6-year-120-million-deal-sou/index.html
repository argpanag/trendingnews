Cubs, Japanese slugger Sato reach 6-year, $120 million deal (source)

TheTools · 24.11.2026 17:17 · source · permalink

CHICAGO – The Cubs have invested years and resources into creating an environment and organizational culture that makes the ballclub an attractive destination for top Japanese players looking to make the jump to the Major Leagues. Chicago has once again come through in that arena.

On Tuesday, the Cubs reached an agreement with Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato – one of the top bats available this offseason – on a six-year contract valued at $120 million, sources told MLB.com’s Mark Feinsand. The deal is pending a physical and was not confirmed by the team.

The agreement with Sato comes the morning after Chicago also reached a deal with free-agent lefty Trevor Rogers on a five-year, $120 million deal, according to sources. The two major additions come as the Cubs had multiple holes to fill in the rotation and two outfield vacancies, following a substantial list of free-agent departures.

Among the players reaching free agency this offseason for the North Siders were lefty Shota Imanaga and right fielder Seiya Suzuki, Japanese stars who joined the Cubs after standout careers overseas. Yu Darvish, who previously had a stint in Chicago, also played a role in convincing Suzuki that the Cubs offered a great environment for Japanese players.

During his time as the Cubs’ top front-office boss, president of baseball operations Jed Hoyer has strived to maintain a strong scouting network in Japan, while cultivating relationships on that front. With the signing of Sato – posted by the Hanshin Tigers after six seasons with the club – the Cubs have again put their years of groundwork on display.

The 27-year-old Sato is coming off a standout campaign for the Tigers in which he won a Triple Crown by leading Nippon Professional Baseball’s Central league in average (.306), home runs (39) and RBIs (106). The lefty-swinging slugger also had 40 doubles with a .383 on-base percentage and .609 slugging (.991 OPS).

Prior to that showing, the two-time Central League MVP hit .277 with 40 homers, 34 doubles, 102 RBIs and a .924 OPS in 139 games in 2025. He has been prone to swing-and-miss, but cut his strikeout rate to 26.7% in ‘26 from 27.3% in ‘25, and similarly saw an increase in walk rate to 11.1% in ‘26 from 9.5% in ‘25.

Sato – a five-time All-Star – has played third base in recent seasons, even picking up a Gold Glove for his work at the hot corner in ‘25. The Cubs, however, have star third baseman Alex Bregman locked into that spot for four more seasons. With both Suzuki (right field) and Ian Happ (left field) hitting free agency, Sato fits Chicago’s roster as a corner outfielder, plus at-bats at designated hitter.

Sato has drawn comparisons to Munetaka Murakami, who was the most recent NPB player to win a Triple Crown before him and is a fellow elite left-handed power bat.

Murakami, of course, became an instant sensation in his rookie season with the White Sox, mashing 35 home runs, making the AL All-Star team and helping Chicago make the playoffs after three straight 100-loss seasons.

The two of them aren't exactly the same type of hitter. Murakami is more selective at the plate and draws more walks, but he also swings and misses more. Sato is more aggressive, and he also makes a little more contact, especially in the strike zone and against breaking and offspeed pitches.

And if Sato can make a Murakami-sized impact in the Majors, that will be a big win for a Cubs team that is looking to replace the offensive hole left by Suzuki, Happ and catcher Carson Kelly reaching free agency this winter.

Prior to the 2022 season, the Cubs signed Suzuki to a five-year deal that was worth roughly $100 million between his contract with Chicago and the posting fee sent to his former NPB club, Hiroshima. In five seasons, Suzuki grew into one of the game’s premier right-handed power threats, positioning himself for a long-term deal in free agency this winter.

That is the kind of successful path the North Siders hope to repeat with Sato, who will be stepping into an organization that has learned from its recent experiences with both Suzuki and Imanaga.
