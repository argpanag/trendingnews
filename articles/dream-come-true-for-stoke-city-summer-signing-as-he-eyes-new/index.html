'Dream come true' for Stoke City summer signing as he eyes new chapter

TheTools · 16.11.2026 05:00 · source · permalink

Luke Graham says it was a dream come true to make his first Scotland start at Hampden Park.

The 22-year-old Stoke City centre-back won his first camp last month when he came off the bench against North Macedonia. His excellent form at club level meant he was ready to step up and make his first start when a gap opened up on the left of a back three, also against North Macedonia, on Friday night.

He helped secured a 2-1 Nations League win which Scotland now hope to build on away against Switzerland this evening (7.45pm).

Graham had narrowly missed out on a place in the World Cup squad and is determined to be a part of a new chapter under Sebastian Pocognoli which will involve a chance to test himself and represent the country in major tournaments.

“I was like everyone else [during the World Cup], just in the pub with my mates watching it," he told The National. "It was pretty amazing and hopefully I can be part of that someday.

“I wasn’t too far away. Steve Clarke, the old manager, said that to me; I wasn’t too far away. It’s just about trusting in myself and hopefully getting there one day.

“[To start my first game at Hampden was] amazing. Dream come true. I’ve said it in a lot of interviews before; the one thing I wanted to do in my career was play for Scotland, so to get that over the line was pretty special.

“There’s not much that is going wrong for me at the minute. It’s just about maintaining that and keeping that going every week.

“[The manager] just said to just go and enjoy it. Just go and do what you’ve been doing every week at Stoke, and I done alright anyway. I’ve got to be ready for it if it’s something that I’ve wanted to do ever since growing up.

“I’ve got to be ready for the moment and try and take it when it comes. I feel I did that on Friday night.”

It is believed the Scotland start will have triggered an extra payment from Stoke to Dundee, on top of the £2 million up front fee.

Graham knows the next challenge will be to try to cement himself as a regular pick.

He said: “There is lots of great competition. We’ve got a lot of really good centre-halves. It was unfortunate for [Stephen] Welsh and [Luis] Binks to pull out with injury, so that opened up a space for me to try and come in and try to cement a place.

“It’s just about when you’re on that pitch, just doing everything you can to try and stay in the team.

“I gave my all against North Macedonia and I just need to see what happens going forward.”
