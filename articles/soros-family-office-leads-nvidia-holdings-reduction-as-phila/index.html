Soros Family Office Leads Nvidia Holdings Reduction as Philadelphia Semiconductor Index Plunges More Than 5% in a Single Session — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 17.11.2026 01:05 · source · permalink

All three major U.S. stock indexes closed lower on Nov. 16. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1.41% to 51,608.17, the S&P 500 dropped 1.92% to 7,416.59, and the Nasdaq Composite declined 2.49% to 24,509.76. The semiconductor sector bore the brunt of the selling, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index falling more than 5% in a single session. Leaders including TSMC, ASML, and AMD generally fell more than 5%, while Micron Technology, Marvell Technology, and Intel dropped more than 7%, and ARM fell more than 8%.

One of the direct triggers for the market swing came from quarterly holdings disclosures. According to aggregated data from U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) 13F filings, George Soros's namesake family office reduced its Nvidia stake by nearly one-fifth in the quarter ended Sept. 30—the largest disclosed reduction this year—after having previously added to the position. At least 12 other family offices also reduced their Nvidia holdings during the same period, but all retained partial positions, with their combined Nvidia holdings still worth more than $1 billion at the end of the third quarter.

Expectations for returns on artificial intelligence capital spending are being marked down, and combined with risk-off sentiment ahead of Nvidia's earnings, this constitutes the main pressure behind the current tech-stock correction. Nvidia is scheduled to report third-quarter results after the close on Nov. 18, but market attention has shifted from the results themselves to broader questions about the industry's direction.

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh disclosed in remarks published on the Fed's website on Nov. 16 that central bank staff have begun drafting multiple scenario plans to assess the potential impact of accelerated AI adoption on the U.S. economy. The three paths he outlined are: AI drives growth and productivity gains and brings prosperity; economic activity grows but the positive effects are offset by large-scale unemployment; and the technology fails to meet market expectations, triggering a stock market decline and an economic slowdown.

Warsh wrote in the remarks: "After a period of large-scale capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns may decline and equity valuations may pull back, leading to slower economic growth and a more pronounced rise in unemployment." He added that the economy would eventually recover over time but would not achieve the expected productivity gains. The Fed chair made clear that the purpose of assessing AI's impact is neither to support nor oppose the technology, but to try to understand one of the biggest unknowns currently facing the U.S. economy.

Rate expectations also intensified. The October consumer price index (CPI) recently released by the U.S. Labor Department came in above expectations for both headline and core readings. After the data, interest rate futures priced the probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike in December at more than 80% to 90%, while the probability of a 50-basis-point hike rose from nearly zero to more than 10%. A December hike is largely priced in by the market, making the pace of subsequent increases and the peak rate the new focus.

Other asset classes also came under pressure. International precious metals futures broadly closed lower, with COMEX gold futures down 0.70% at $3,859.00 an ounce and COMEX silver futures down 4.50% at $46.91 an ounce. International oil prices fell sharply, with the front-month U.S. crude contract down 3.95% at $85.06 a barrel and the front-month Brent contract down 4.27% at $91.75 a barrel. The geopolitical risk premium that had supported oil prices has clearly faded, and market expectations of oversupply continue to strengthen.

Goldman Sachs Group's commodities research team led by Daan Struyven said in its latest research note that despite lingering supply disruption risks, the global oil market will still face a "large surplus" in 2027. The report estimates that even after factoring in supply disruption risk of about 1 million barrels per day, the surplus—global crude production minus end-user demand—is still expected to reach 2 million barrels per day in 2027. Goldman Sachs expects Brent crude futures to average $88 a barrel next year and fall to $78 a barrel by year-end.

The International Energy Agency's latest Oil Market Report noted that with the gradual recovery of oil exports from the Gulf region, global oil supply has exceeded demand for the first time since the outbreak of the Iran war. The report showed that global oil supply averaged 105.4 million barrels per day in October, up 2.3 million barrels per day from September; North Sea crude prices fell from a high of $136 a barrel in early October to about $93, a decline of more than 30%.

Most Chinese concept stocks closed lower, with the Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index down 1.00%. H World Group rose more than 8%, KE Holdings gained more than 4%, and XPeng rose more than 3%; NIO and Baidu fell more than 5%, Futu Holdings and Bilibili dropped more than 4%, and Alibaba declined more than 3%. Major European stock indexes also edged lower in tandem, with Germany's DAX 30 down 0.57% at 26,598.64, France's CAC 40 down 0.2% at 8,352, and the UK's FTSE 100 down 0.09% at 10,892.93.

Judging from the holdings data, the overall moves by family offices look more like position rebalancing than a full-scale exit. At least 12 institutions retained exposure after reducing holdings, and their combined position of more than $1 billion shows that long-term capital has not abandoned the stock, though scrutiny of valuations and return cycles has clearly tightened.

For investors, three threads need to be digested simultaneously: first, whether the leading company's earnings can deliver on previously high expectations; second, whether the Fed's scenario analysis of AI's employment impact will affect the policy path; and third, the discount-rate pressure on high-valuation growth stocks from rising December rate-hike odds. Volatility in the semiconductor sector is likely to remain elevated in the near term, and the reallocation of capital between tech stocks and defensive assets may continue.

Note: The index, commodity, and individual stock price-change data in this article all come from publicly available market quotes during the Nov. 16 trading session.
