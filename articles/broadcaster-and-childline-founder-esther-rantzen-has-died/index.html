Broadcaster and Childline founder Esther Rantzen has died

TheTools · 30.09.2026 18:02 · source · permalink

Following a long battle with lung cancer, respected journalist Esther Rantzen, who also founded the Childline service, has died.

The 86-year-old revealed in 2023 that her condition had progressed to stage four cancer, and she had signed up to the Swiss assisted dying organisation Dignitas just to keep her options open.

Then, in 2025, she was diagnosed with a second form of cancer for which she received chemotherapy and radiotherapy, before doctors advised her to stop treatment as the side effects were outweighing any benefits. 

Speaking previously, Esther revealed her heartbreaking final wish was to be reunited with her late husband Desmond Wilcox, whom she lost to a heart attack in 2000 when he was just 69 years of age.

In an interview with The Times, Esther said: 'If there is a heaven, it would be a very happy place. 

'It's a lovely idea to meet Desmond again and all those I have loved and lost - my parents and grandparents, my close friends and family.'

Confirming her passing on Wednesday, Esther's loved ones paid tribute to the 'most wonderful mother' and 'cherished grandmother'.

They added that she will be remembered for her 'endless capacity for joy'.

Just weeks before her death, Esther said it was too late for her to 'buzz off' to Dignitas, despite having 'spent the money and made the plans', as she was 'too physically fragile to make the journey.'

Esther was always an outspoken advocate for social issues across society in the UK.

She was best known for presenting the show That Life! for 21 years from 1973 until 1994, which reached an audience of 18 million viewers at its peak.

It was a consumer programme that investigated a vast range of issues, from exposing faulty washing machines and dodgy salesmen to life and death issues like organ donation.

In 1986, she founded Childline, following a particularly harrowing episode of That's Life!, which was inspired by the death of a young child who had died of starvation.

She set up a helpline for children to report incidents of abuse after the show aired - it was only meant to be open for 48 hours, but was utterly swamped with more than 50,000 attempted calls.

Her concept was taken under the umbrella of the National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children to be able to expand the service, which has been copied in more than 150 countries around the world.

She also set up another charity in 2012 called The Silver Line, which supports elderly people suffering from loneliness.

Esther also fronted another TV show in the late 1980s called Hearts of Gold, celebrating people who carried out random acts of kindness.

All her work to help others in both a personal and professional capacity earned her an OBE and a CBE before she was named on the New Year's Honours list in 2015, becoming Dame Esther Rantzen.

She did have a stint in politics too, trying to make an impact on society when she ran for parliament in 2009 as an independent candidate.

There were 11 people in the running, and she came fourth behind the three main political parties with 4.4 per cent of the vote.

Even though her work was heavily loaded, she showed in 2008 that she still knew how to have a laugh when she entered the camp on I'm A Celebrity Get Me Out Of Here!, becoming the fifth campmate to be evicted.

In 2016, she even took part in the first episode of Celebrity First Dates when she was 76 years old.

Esther endured a fairly turbulent love life before she married her husband, Desmond Wilcox, in 1977. She was married to him until he died in 2000.

In her autobiography, she admitted falling in love with a married man, Scottish politician Nicholas Fairbairn, when she was in her 20s.

'He wore idiosyncratic suits and waistcoats, and I fell in love with him... I didn't realise he was married until far, far too late,' she wrote.

Her relationship with her eventual husband also had a scandalous start, as he was married to her friend Patsy when they began their affair. All three worked for the BBC at the time. Patsy Wilcox refused to divorce Desmond until Esther became pregnant.

After announcing her cancer diagnosis in January 2023, Esther made another statement in May of the same year, revealing her condition was terminal.

She then began campaigning for a free vote in the UK on assisted dying, saying it was her belief that the UK is lagging behind many others.

Speaking of her own decision to join Dignitas, she told the BBC's The Today Podcast: 'I have joined Dignitas. I have in my brain thought, well, if the next scan says nothing's working, I might buzz off to Zurich – but it puts my family and friends in a difficult position because they would want to go with me.

'And that means that the police might prosecute them. So we've got to do something. At the moment, it’s not really working, is it?'

Dame Esther Rantzen died in September 2026. She is survived by her children Miriam (formerly Emily), Rebecca and Joshua.
