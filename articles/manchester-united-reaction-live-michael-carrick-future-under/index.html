  7. Man Utd fans think blame needs to be sharedpublished at 13:51 GMT 26 October
    13:51 GMT 26 October

    Man Utd 0-3 Bournemouth

    Jack Gray
    BBC Newsbeat

    BBC Newsbeat has been out in Manchester catching up with a few fans - and it's fair to say the wind and rain is matching their mood.

    "We’ve just been going down and down and down, and Manchester’s weather sums up how Manchester United have been playing for the last few years," says Chenai Masunungure.

    Chenai, 20, thinks the club hasn't brought in enough "world-class or top-quality players" and thinks the blame needs to be split between the board and the players.

    "You can tell from who we’ve brought in, there’s not enough thinking put into it," she says.

    "But then as well as that when you’re watching them [the players] you can just see there’s not enough intensity."

    Rafael Millin Fialho agrees and says fans are "fed up constantly".

    "It seems like the same cycle, constantly sacking managers and getting behind [them] and then sacking," the 18-year-old adds.

    The first season Rafael remembers is when David Moyes took over from Sir Alex Ferguson back in 2013, saying the squad he inherited "wasn't good enough".

    "From then onwards it’s been a mix of not the right signings, wrong managers, I think the club’s been going downhill and there’s a toxic environment around the club at the minute," he says.

    Rafael was at the game yesterday and admits he feels bad for Michael Carrick - but Chenai thinks he does need to be sacked.

    "As much as I love him as a person, he does need to go because I don’t think he’s up to the role enough," she says.

    "But not in a toxic way, in a nice pleasant way."

  9. It just feels like the end - Nevillepublished at 13:40 GMT 26 October
    13:40 GMT 26 October

    Man Utd 0-3 Bournemouth

    After Sunday's defeat to Bournemouth, Gary Neville suggested that Michael Carrick's time as Manchester United manager could already be done and dusted, saying the feeling is that he was already a "dead man walking":

    "They're probably going to sack Carrick in the next two, 12, 24 hours. There were rumours on Thursday, Friday about Jason Willcox being torn over whether the replacement will be Eddie Howe or Julian Naglesmann and Carrick is a dead man walking already.

    "When he comes into his presser on Friday and starts speaking about the club's history, it just feels like the end."

    Neville also questioned why Carrick felt the need to remind players of the club's history and the standards expected of them.

    He said: "Don't sign a player for this club if you have to explain to them how big it is. They should be privileged to be out there."

    The former United defender also argued that the supporters' anger is not simply about the results, but also the decisions being made behind the scenes.

    "The history is why the fans are here," Neville added. "That's why they're angry because they can't accept the decision making, from season tickets to lack of recruitment.

    "Tottenham have taken their two big targets [Mateus Fernandes and Sandro Tonali], they should be out there wearing red shirts."

    Neville also raised the question of whether a change of manager would actually address the deeper issues embedded at Manchester United.

  12. 'If you don't run you aren't going to win' - why Carrick dropped Fernandespublished at 13:25 GMT 26 October
    13:25 GMT 26 October

    Man Utd 0-3 Bournemouth

    Michael Carrick's decision to drop captain Bruno Fernandes for Sunday's defeat to Bournemouth followed the Portugal midfielder's criticism after Wednesday's 4-1 loss at Como in the Champions League.

    Fernandes was seen shaking his head as he walked around the pitch in Como after being substituted in the second half, with United trailing 3-0 at the time.

    Following the defeat, Fernandes aired his views in a post-match interview, in which criticised the choice of tactics.

    He said that it was "a decision from the staff to not press man to man...but sometimes when you give too much the ball to these teams they get confidence".

    Asked directly whether Carrick got his tactics wrong, Fernandes added: "That's on the manager and staff to see it, not me. Sometimes you have to be more brave under pressure, take the risk of pressing high and making them uncomfortable."

    Carrick delivered a curt response in his post-match news conference, when asked about the comments: "Whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, if you don't run, and you don't run forward, and you don't run back, you aren't going to win."

    On Sunday, Carrick was asked why Fernandes was left on the bench and the manager confirmed that his captain was fit and available, but he chose not to put him on.

  15. Carrick speaks from the heart and drops captain but players fail to respondpublished at 13:03 GMT 26 October
    13:03 GMT 26 October

    Man Utd 0-3 Bournemouth

    Michael Carrick kicked off his pre-match news conference before Sunday's defeat at home to Bournemouth with an emotional five-minute address, after which he said he was "100%" the right person to take Manchester United forward.

    He refused to discuss his plans for captain Bruno Fernandes after the Portugal international criticised the tactics he employed in a damaging midweek Champions League defeat at Como.

    But as soon as the team sheet was revealed, it became clear that Fernandes was being made an example of.

    Lisandro Martinez, who had previously criticised the club's hierarchy for failing to back Carrick in the transfer market over the summer, also started the match on the bench, but as a precaution as he returned from an injury.

    Despite Carrick's best efforts, United's starting XI failed to respond, putting in another lacklustre display as goals from Rayan, Marcus Tavernier and Evanilson handed the Cherries a deserved victory at Old Trafford.

  16. Can you imagine what they're saying in that dressing room? - Nevillepublished at 12:55 GMT 26 October
    12:55 GMT 26 October

    Man Utd 0-3 Bournemouth

    Speaking on his podcast after Manchester United's 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth at Old Trafford, former United defender Gary Neville shared his fears that Michael Carrick has lost support, with tensions between players and the manager threatening to worsen.

    Neville said he has expected problems to emerge this season, but was surprised by how quickly the situation had deteriorated.

    "It was my fear this was going to happen this season. I didn't think it'd happen this quickly, but the players were booed off at the end with real anger by the supporters in the Stretford End. Usually the fans are supportive of the players and manager."

    Neville also highlighted the reaction to Bruno Fernandes and Lisandro Martinez, following their recent comments about Carrick and the club's leadership.

    "So the fans are telling those players you've turned on your own in the last week."

    Neville believes the public criticism could represent only a fraction of what is being said behind closed doors.

    "I always say if a footballer speaks out like those two have, you're only getting the one or five per cent of what they're actually thinking. Can you imagine what they're saying in that dressing room? What the toxicity between them all must be like?"
