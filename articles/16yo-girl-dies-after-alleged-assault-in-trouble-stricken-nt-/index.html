16yo girl dies after alleged assault in trouble-stricken NT community

NT Police are investigating a suspicious death in the remote community of Alpurrurulam. (ABC News: Victoria Pengilley)

In short:

Northern Territory Police are investigating a suspicious death in the isolated community of Alpurrurulam, also known as Lake Nash.

The 16-year-old girl who died was allegedly physically assaulted prior to her death.

A 26-year-old man police believe was in a relationship with the girl is in police custody and "assisting with inquiries".

Northern Territory Police say a 16-year-old girl in the remote community of Alpurrurulam, west of Mount Isa on the Queensland border, has died after she was allegedly physically assaulted early this morning.

Police have said they are treating the death as suspicious, with specialist police units currently travelling to the location, about 1,500 kilometres from Darwin, and less than 20km from the Queensland border.

Police previously described the victim was a woman but have since clarified that she was aged 16.

"Around 5:30am this morning, police received a report from the local clinic that a [girl] had died after allegedly being physically assaulted," police said in a statement.

Alpurrurulam is about 360 kilometres from the nearest city, Mount Isa. (ABC News: Victoria Pengilley)

"Local police, with assistance from Avon Downs police, are currently responding to the incident with detectives from the Crime Command and Forensics travelling to the community."

Speaking to the media this afternoon, NT Police Acting Commander Mark Grieve said upon arrival at the local clinic, the 16-year-old had suffered a cardiac arrest and later died.

"It was at that time that the clinic staff also noticed that she had various number of injuries to her body,"

"Cause of death will be pursuant to autopsy."

Acting Commander Grieve said police believed the teenage girl primarily resided in Queensland near Mount Isa, but that she was "quite transient between here and there".

A 26-year-old man police believe was in a relationship with the girl is in police custody and "assisting with inquiries" over the incident.

"We believe at this point in time there was some form of domestic relationship between the two," Acting Commander Grieve said.

"How long the parties have been together, we're unable to confirm at this point in time."

He said there were a range of police teams, including Queensland Police, involved in the investigation.

"We've got two detectives that flew up from Alice Springs along with two forensics personnel. We've got an investigator from Tennant Creek as well, along with two additional uniform members from Tennant Creek," he said. 

"We've got Camooweal police on the ground. We've also got Avon Downs members on the ground, and we're flying two more Major Crime members into community as we speak.

The community's police station has been closed since 2018. (ABC News: Lucy Murray)

"We wanted to make sure we had ample resources from not only an investigative point of view, but also a uniform point of view, just in case the community unrest might bubble up again."

Alpurrurulam, also known as Lake Nash, has been struggling with internal community unrest, which caused the recent evacuation of its clinic staff from the community.

Acting Commander Grieve said at this stage police believed the girl's death was unrelated to the recent unrest.

He also said the clinic had been staffed when the girl arrived, with health personnel having returned to the community.

NT Health has been contacted for comment.

The NT has the highest rates of family and domestic violence in Australia, with a rate of intimate partner homicide seven times the national average.

No permanent police presence

There has not been a permanent police station in Alpurrurulam since 2018, with the closest officers stationed 170 kilometres away at remote Avon Downs.

Alpurrurulam is a dry community, but the Barkly Regional Council has reported large quantities of black market supply are being transported over the border from Queensland.

The electorate's NT MP, Chansey Paech, said in a statement this morning he was "deeply saddened to hear reports this morning of a tragic loss of life in Alpurrurulam".

Chansey Paech has questioned why additional police resources have not been directed to Alpurrurulam. (ABC News: Dane Hirst)

"[This is] a community that has been desperately calling out for more help, resources and support in recent months," he said.

"My thoughts are with the family, loved ones and the entire community at this difficult time.

"But serious questions need to be asked about what is being done to support this community and respond to the concerns residents have been raising."

Mr Paech accused the NT government of "ignoring the bush" and urged it to take action on extra police resourcing for the community and efforts to better combat illegal alcohol supply.

Acting Commander Grieve said he understood Alpurrurulam community members would like to have a police station, but the "viability around doing that" was an issue.

The NT government has been contacted for comment.
