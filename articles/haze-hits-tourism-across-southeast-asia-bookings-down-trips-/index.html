Haze hits tourism across Southeast Asia, bookings down, trips canceled - VnExpress International

TheTools · 27.10.2026 20:03 · source · permalink

Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, and Jakarta were listed among the world's 10 most-polluted major cities on Oct. 20 by air-quality monitoring company IQAir.

In Singapore, known as one of world's cleanest countries, some tourists have adjusted their itineraries to limit exposure to polluted air, while others have continued sightseeing wearing N95 masks, The Straits Times reported.

The city-state experienced a 19-day stretch of unhealthy air between Oct. 5 and 23.

Its Six Stars Tours and Services reported a noticeable decline in inbound bookings and an increase in cancellations since air quality began deteriorating in September, FTN News reported.

VTL Travel, which offers walking, cycling, and corporate tours in Singapore under the Rediscover Singapore brand, reported a 20-30% drop in ticket sales since September.

The company operated just two tours a month in September and October, down from its usual four to eight. It is considering shifting outdoor activities indoors and offering customers free date changes.

Singapore received 1.23 million international visitors in September, down 2.2% from the same month in 2025, according to the Singapore Tourism Board.

A man cycles with Kuala Lumpur’s skyline in the background shrouded in haze from regional wildfires, Oct. 15, 2026. Photo by Reuters

Malaysia is also experiencing weaker tourism demand due to the haze.

Inbound tour operators have reported cancellations for November and fewer group bookings, while overseas travel agents have sought updates on air quality, Bloomberg reported, citing Mint Leong, president of the Malaysian Inbound Tourism Association.

Malaysia targets 47 million visitors in 2026 under its extended Visit Malaysia 2026 campaign.

It recorded 28.8 million in the first eight months of the year.

In Johor State, hoteliers have reported weaker bookings in recent weeks, The Star newspaper reported.

Ivan Teo, chairman of the Malaysian Association of Hotels' Johor chapter, said bookings had fallen by about 20% since the haze began affecting the state.

Johor attracts visitors also to its outdoor activities, including cycling, making air quality a concern for people seeking fresh air and nature-based experiences.

Jarod Chia, chairman of the Malaysia Budget Hotels Association's Johor chapter, said members had recorded occupancy declines of 15-20%.

In Indonesia, forest fires and haze have disrupted domestic and international flights, forcing tour operators to cancel trips and prompting travel companies to redirect visitors to alternative destinations.

The disruption has hit nature-based tourism in Kalimantan, where visitors travel to see orangutans in Tanjung Puting National Park.

Tanjung Puting is a major attraction for wildlife enthusiasts, particularly Europeans, who travel specifically to see orangutans.

Ricky Setiawanto, director of Indonesia-based leading travel agency Panorama Destination, said worsening haze had prompted two groups to cancel their trips to the park in one week.

The impact in Singapore, Malaysia, and Indonesia underscores how prolonged haze can undermine tourism, particularly in destinations reliant on outdoor attractions, wildlife experiences and air travel.

The countries are racing to contain the haze as the peak tourism season gets underway.

In Indonesia, authorities have deployed ground crews to combat fires and target hotspots in Sumatra and Kalimantan in addition to aerial firefighting operations.

They also dropped 3.2 tonnes of dry ice over Palembang as part of cloud-seeding efforts to induce rainfall.

Singapore has intensified enforcement through its National Environment Agency issuing preventive notices and warnings to local and Indonesian-linked companies suspected of connections to fire-prone land concessions.

It has also offered firefighting personnel and cloud-seeding support to help tackle the haze.
