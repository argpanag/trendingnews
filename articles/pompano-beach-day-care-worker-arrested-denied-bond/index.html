Pompano Beach Day Care Worker Arrested, Denied Bond

TheTools · 24.11.2026 21:10 · source · permalink

A 20-year-old former day care worker was denied bond in court on Tuesday after a 7-year-old girl told investigators he sexually abused her twice at the Pompano Beach child care center where she attended after-school care. Brenard Branch was arrested in Fort Lauderdale on Monday and booked on two counts of lewd or lascivious molestation of a child younger than 12 by an adult.

According to the Broward Sheriff's Office, as reported by WSVN, the girl told investigators the incidents happened over the summer before July 4 at All My Children Academy on North Dixie Highway in Pompano Beach, where she attended after-school care. A detective responded to the academy as part of the investigation, per the arrest affidavit.

Girl Says She Was Told to Sit Beside Him

Per the affidavit, Branch told other children to leave the room, then told the girl to sit beside him before touching her inappropriately over her underwear. The report also describes another child present in the room during one incident, whom Branch instructed to close her eyes before he touched that child as well, according to the same account.

The girl told investigators Branch warned her that she would be in trouble and that he would make her clean the whole classroom, the outlet's report states. She later identified Branch in a photo lineup, per the affidavit.

A Disclosure That Went Unheeded

The girl's mother told investigators her daughter disclosed the abuse on Friday. Separately, the academy's director told a detective that the girl had reported the abuse to her about a month earlier, but the director reportedly said she did not believe it because Branch no longer worked there, according to the affidavit as described in the report.

A separate Brevard County case shows how alleged failures to report abuse can lead to charges against day care staff. In 2022, three employees—including a center director—were arrested on allegations they failed to report suspected abuse of a 3-year-old boy who later died. Investigators said staff knew about his injuries; the allegations and circumstances were distinct from the Pompano Beach case. The Brevard arrests drew attention to the reporting duties imposed on child-care workers.

State law also sets screening and training standards for child care personnel. They include Level 2 background screening, employment-history checks and searches of criminal records and relevant registries, as well as introductory training that covers identifying and reporting child abuse. Personnel generally must begin the required training within 90 days of employment and complete it within a year, though the law provides some training exemptions. Those statewide requirements provide regulatory context; they do not establish whether Branch met them or what screening or training he received. Florida’s child-care licensing statute spells out the standards.

Florida law requires anyone with knowledge or reasonable cause to suspect child sexual abuse to report it immediately to the state’s central abuse hotline. The statute specifically lists day care center workers and other professional child care workers among those who must identify themselves when making a report; it does not make an internal report to a supervisor a substitute for contacting the hotline. The affidavit account described in this case does not establish whether a hotline report was made. Florida’s child-abuse reporting law sets out those requirements.

Detectives said Branch initially denied the allegations when questioned but later admitted to touching the girl over her clothing. He was booked on the two molestation counts and, according to booking records, held pending his first court appearance. In court Tuesday, he was denied bond.
