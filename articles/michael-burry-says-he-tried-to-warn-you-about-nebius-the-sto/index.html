Michael Burry Says He Tried to Warn You About Nebius. The Stock Just Crashed 16% and Is 72% Below Its Record

TheTools · 21.11.2026 01:44 · source · permalink

This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.

Nebius Group (NASDAQ: NBIS) closed at $81.31 on Friday, down 16% from $96.92. It was the stock’s fourth-worst day since it started trading under the Nebius name in October 2024, and it came a day after a 9% drop on Thursday.

The shares are now 72% below their record close of $286.69 on June 18. A $10,000 stake bought at that record is worth about $2,836 today. Friday’s close is also below the $83.70 where Nebius ended 2025, so the stock is down 3% for the year. It’s the lowest close since Feb. 5, 2026.

What Michael Burry posted

On Thursday afternoon, as AI stocks slid, Michael Burry posted on X: “Coreweave, Nebius, NVIDIA, semis, I tried to warn you all. I didn’t say short. But I provided the information one would need to sell.”

He followed up before Friday’s open: “One similarity today to 1929 and 2000 is the lack of a proximate cause in earnings.”

Burry, the investor known for betting against housing before the 2008 crash, has written for months about the risks in AI spending, including how tech companies account for the chips they buy. Not everyone gave him credit. Futurum Equities strategist Shay Boloor answered that he must have missed the part where Burry told people to sell.

Down 47% in seven trading days

Most of the damage came after Nebius reported third-quarter results on Nov. 12. The stock closed at $154.69 the night before. Seven trading days later it’s down 47%. It lost 30% in the last week alone.

The quarter itself was strong. Revenue rose 571% to $980.8 million. The problem was the cost of growing that fast. Nebius said 2026 revenue would land at the low end of its $3 billion to $3.4 billion range and raised its spending plan to about $27 billion, as data centers built by outside landlords ran late. For 2027 it expects $12 billion to $13 billion of revenue on $40 billion to $45 billion of spending.

The rest of the AI trade

Nebius rents out computing power built on Nvidia chips, so it trades with the rest of the AI buildout. Nvidia had its worst week since 2008 this week, and the companies that rent out its chips fell with it.

Long-term holders are still ahead. Nebius closed at $20 on its first day of trading under the new name in October 2024, so Friday’s price is about four times that.
