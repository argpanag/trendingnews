Amateur prospector finds $150,000 gold nugget in outback

TheTools · 26.10.2026 03:58 · source · permalink

  • By Patrick Reincke

  • Topic:Gold

By Kristina Rosengren

By Patrick Reincke

Topic:Gold

While the nugget weighs 1,218 grams, testing shows it contains about 940 grams of gold.  (ABC Broken Hill: Kristina Rosengren)

In short:

A Broken Hill prospector has unearthed a gold nugget weighing more than a kilogram in outback NSW.

The nugget, nicknamed the "Tibooburra Spud" by the lucky prospector, is said to be worth about $150,000.

What's next?

A business owner selling prospecting supplies says more people are becoming interested in the hobby.

Sounds of excitement fill the air as an amateur prospector digs his hands into red, rocky dirt.

What he comes up with is something he hopes isn't "a painted rock", but instead a gold nugget worth about $150,000.

He had been digging for several days on Mount Stuart station near Tibooburra, in the north-western corner of New South Wales.

But he said the discovery was one of persistence, after having nearly walked past the nugget.

'The machine was squealing'

The lucky prospector, who wishes to remain anonymous, has been fossicking for years and is familiar with coming up empty.

"You dig lots of rubbish … lots of lead bullets, old timer's nails, horseshoes, everything," he said.

Metal scraps are the most common find for prospectors. (ABC Broken Hill: Kristina Rosengren)

He was finishing up a day of searching in mid-October, when he stumbled across a signal with his metal detector, the Gold Monster 2000.

"It wasn't a loud signal for the size of the piece that it is [but] once I got down to 100 [millimetres] or something like that, the machine was squealing," the prospector said.

"It might have been on the first or second scrape I just seen this bright yellow dot in the centre of the hole."

A lucky Broken Hill prospector has dug up a gold nugget worth about $150,000.  (ABC Broken Hill: Kristina Rosengren)

The gold nugget specked with quartz weighs more than 1,200 grams, with testing showing about 940 grams is pure gold.

On spot price, it is worth between $150,000 to $160,000.

But with plans to sell the nugget to a collector, the price could be pushed as high as $200,000, according to the prospector, who has nicknamed his precious find the "Tibooburra Spud".

Prospecting a popular pastime

In his 20-odd years selling prospecting supplies in Broken Hill, Greg Spangler has only seen one other person find a gold nugget that weighs more than a kilogram.

It is more typical to find gold flakes or smaller nuggets weighing only a few ounces.

"We have a fictitious club [called] the Ounce Club … but not many people [are] in the 'Kilo Club'," Mr Spangler said.

Greg Spangler says more people are taking up prospecting as a hobby. (ABC Broken Hill: Kristina Rosengren)

Despite the rarity of discovering a nugget worth six figures, Mr Spangler believes a surge in gold prices has drawn more people to the hobby.

"It's got a lot of people interested," Mr Spangler said.

Gold prices soared to new heights this year, peaking in January at almost $8,000 per troy ounce (or 31.1 grams). While the price has since dropped, it is still sitting favourably at $5,700 per troy ounce.

The nugget has been affectionately named the "Tibooburra Spud" by the prospector who found it. (ABC Broken Hill: Kristina Rosengren)

Beyond the chance of an instant boost to your retirement fund, Mr Spangler said there were other benefits to the hobby, including exercise, fresh air and a bit of quiet.

"You get up in the morning, you're the first one to see the sunrise … it's very peaceful," he said.

"Just another pastime you can involve the whole family in."

More gold in Tibooburra

Gold prospecting around Tibooburra, which dates back to the late 1880s, is a major drawcard for many tourists.

Today, fossicking on the Tibooburra Common, an area surrounding the remote township, is currently banned while Native Title permissions are being pursued.

But at least two stations in the region, Mount Stuart and Gumvale, allow fossicking for a fee.

The lucky prospector found his $150,000 nugget with the Gold Monster 2000.  (ABC Broken Hill: Kristina Rosengren)

And Mr Spangler said for anyone looking to have a go, there could be plenty more gold to be found.

"[It could] keep going forever and ever, because every time it rains, other people find gold where they didn't before," he said.

As for the prospector, he has just one tip for would-be fossickers: "Just keep walking, keep swinging [and] keep digging every target."
