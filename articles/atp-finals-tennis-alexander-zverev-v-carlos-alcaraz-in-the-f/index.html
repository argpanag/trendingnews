Zverev 2-6 Alcaraz* At change of ends, Jamie Murray notes Zverev is winning points on the Alcaraz serve so, if he can just take a few more on his own, he can do something in the match. But he also knows Alcaraz is better than him, playing without conviction and belief, the ace swerving down the T at 30-15 raising two set points. And Alcaraz only needs one, a netted return ending a 50-minute 6-2 set that could scarcely have gone worse for Zverev. Can he sort himself to make a match of it?

*Zverev 2-5 Alcaraz Gosh, at 15-0 Zverev chases down a volley and plays to the corner, so Alcaraz triers an unnecessary lobbed tweener ... and the putaway is missed! Again, that’s so poor from Zverev, then a gorgeous backhand slice, sent zoning cross-court, dips below the tape and he just can’t get it back … then down 15-30, again runs down the shot-clock and this time the time violation means he loses a first serve. He’s as delighted by this as you imagine, letting the umpire know that he was waiting for the crowd, then plays a good point … only to overhit a drive volley. He just can’t sustain any quality, and at 15-40 goes log from the back under little pressure and, after going through the tournament unbroken, he’s now lost his serve for the third time in this set. Alcaraz is full of confidence, bouncing about the court having a timer; Zverev looks like he’d rather be anywhere else.

Zverev 2-4 Alcaraz* Alcaraz isn’t even playing that well. Of course, his decent level is stratospheric, but that’s not what’s beating Zverev here; rather, it’s the opponent, occasion and circumstances. He’ll be absolutely desperate to prove himself a legit No 1 in the world, but he knows the truth: he’d be nowhere near without circumstances conspiring in his favour, and that he can’t match Alcaraz’s mix of power, touch and imagination; that’s a lot to carry around in your kitbag. He does, though, make 30-all in the next game and a double hands him break point … and this time, a return sent close to the baseline incites the mishit. Six games, three breaks, and Zverev is still in the set.

Not for the first time against a top player, Zverev has no-showed, and he’s surely en route to losing this set, spooked by the genius on the other side of the net.

Preamble

It’s not been a vintage year for men’s tennis. Jannik Sinner has missed almost half of it, including the US Open; Carlos Alcaraz was absent at Wimbledon and feeling his way back in New York; and there’ve been a litany of other injures to top players, meaning a succession of underpowered events.

Alexander Zverev has taken full advantage of the situation, snaffling two majors just as it looked like he might never win one and finishing the year top of the rankings. The asterisks in people’s minds, whether moral or competitive, are conspicuous by their absence from the record books, trophy engravings and cheques.

But they remain present in Zverev’s mind. He enjoys neither having the value of his triumphs challenged, nor being asked about the domestic abuse allegations he settled out of court – his claims, earlier this year, that “it has been proven that the accusations were false” and “my innocence has been proven” led to his being served with a lawsuit just a few days ago, accused of violating the terms of the agreement. He can, though, begin addressing the first aspect of that displeasure this evening.

No one, not even Zverev, could possibly argue he is a superior player to Alcaraz. However, arriving at this match in spectacular form, having reached the final without dropping a service-game never mind a set, he will be certain he can win – not something that has always been so. The problem has has it that once a rally is under way, he can’t match the creativity and versatility on the other side of the net and, though his forehand and volleying have improved, at this level they remain weaknesses.

In a race to two, though, there is less scope for class to make the difference and, if Zverev can play well enough to win here, he’ll feel differently about things the next time the two meet in a best-of-five. And, with the Australian Open beginning in less than two months, it’s not too much to say that, though we’re about to enjoy the finale of this season, we’re also watching the start of next.
