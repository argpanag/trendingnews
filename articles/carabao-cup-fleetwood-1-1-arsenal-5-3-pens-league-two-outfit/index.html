  7. 'I cannot fault at all the attitude of the players'published at 22:59 GMT 27 October
    22:59 GMT 27 October

    FT: Fleetwood 1-1 Arsenal (5-3 pens)

    Arsenal

    Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta, speaking to Sky Sports: "First of all, congratulations to Fleetwood for going through in the next round, in the manner they've done it, the atmosphere they have created, and, yeah, a really sad day for us.

    "I cannot fault at all the attitude of the players. I haven't seen anyone not doing what is required at this level. But, obviously, we insisted so much, we have so much of the ball, so many chances, so many shots, and if you want the margins to be bigger than going to the penalties, obviously, you need to capitalize on that. And we didn't. And football is about that.

    "It is, and especially when they go ahead and, and they create that momentum. We gave them almost nothing in the whole game and we created so many chances, and didn't capitalise on that. And then the margins become so small, that's it. This football, that's the beauty as well of this competition, and, especially in this country, it's a very sad day."

    On Max Dowman's red card: "It's difficult to see on the cameras like that, so I prefer to watch it back properly and then I can assess the situation."

    On if Dowman will learn from this: "Yeah, we'll learn from everything."

    On if he saw enough: "Yeah, the attitude-wise, I have zero. Otherwise, I would be here absolutely fuming. I'm sad, I think we are all sad. We have a massive responsibility to carry this badge in the best possible way. It had nothing to do with effort or application or determination to win it, but we lack other things to, to create a much bigger margin than today, with what we produce, they really should have been shown in the scoreline."

Fleetwood keeper Jay Lynch saved Bruno Guimaraes' penalty, Arsenal's fourth, to set his side on their way to an improbable victory

Fleetwood Town pulled off one of the biggest upsets in Carabao Cup history as they beat Premier League champions Arsenal in an astounding penalty shootout to reach the quarter-finals.

Mikel Arteta's side were left shell-shocked when Aaron Loupalo-Bi gave the hosts - ranked 84 places below the Gunners in the football pyramid - an unexpected lead in the 21st minute.

Viktor Gyokeres pulled the visitors level after the break, but a wasteful Arsenal side could not put the game to bed and they were reduced to 10 men in the 86th minute when teenager Max Dowman kicked out after a hefty tackle by Harrison Neal and was sent off.

Jay Lynch made two sublime saves to stop Ben White and Martin Odegaard right at the death to take the hosts to penalty shootout.

And the Fleetwood goalkeeper pulled off more heroics as he stopped Bruno Guimaraes' spot-kick before Conor Haughey sparked scenes of ecstasy at Highbury Stadium by converting the winning penalty.

Fans flooded on to the pitch, players swore in their post-match interviews, while manager Matt Lawlor was reduced to tears during the emphatic celebrations.

Fleetwood will find out their quarter-final opponents when the draw takes place following Chelsea's game against Liverpool on Wednesday.

Arsenal analysis: Wasteful Premier League champions left dumbstruck

Arsenal were welcomed by flames and fireworks, but the game had barely got going before referee Michael Salisbury stopped play after spotting a drone flying above Highbury Stadium.

The match re-started eight minutes later - after the players had briefly been instructed to leave the pitch - and Arsenal were soon caught off guard when Loupalo-Bi rolled Marli Salmon and slotted past Illan Meslier.

Despite having countless chances and dominating possession, Arteta's side trailed at the break - much to the delight of the raucous home crowd.

But the Fleetwood's elation did not last long as Gyokeres scrambled in the six-yard box to poke home an equaliser four minutes into the second half.

Introduced at half-time, Arsenal captain Odegaard had injected a sense of urgency into the team, but they still lacked a clinical edge - with only one goal to show for 26 shots and 59 touches inside the Fleetwood box.

Their evening in the north west went from bad to worse for them when 16-year-old Dowman saw red, kicking out at Neal before facing the wrath of Arteta as he headed down the tunnel.

Meslier, making his debut for the Gunners, failed to get close to any of the Cod Army's penalty kicks, while it was a lacklustre strike from Guimaraes that effectively sealed the Gunners' fate.

Fleetwood analysis: League Two minnows defy the odds

With Fleetwood languishing 18th in the English fourth division, Arteta made nine changes from Saturday's win over Everton - opting for a blend of youthful talent and Premier League non-starters.

Making just his second senior appearance for Arsenal, Salmon lacked the physicality needed to hold off Loupalo-Bi before the Fulham loanee jabbed the ball through Meslier's legs - and the 17-year-old defender was hauled off at half-time.

Fleetwood's defenders, meanwhile, gave everything to keep Arsenal at bay, with Rhys Bennett, Haughey and Shaun Rooney all throwing themselves in front of every shot as they weathered wave upon wave of relentless pressure.

The home fans - perhaps expecting Arsenal to open the floodgates at some stage - took delight in their side's ability to hold on for so long, and chants of "champions of England, you're having a laugh" echoed around Highbury Stadium.

Ultimately, it came down to conviction. Ronan Coughlan, Neal, Will Davies, Andy Cannon and Haughey each slammed their penalties emphatically past Meslier, showing a fearlessness that stood in stark contrast to Arsenal's timid overall performance.
