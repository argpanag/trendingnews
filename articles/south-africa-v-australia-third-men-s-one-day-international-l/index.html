WICKET! Connolly b Coetzee 23, Australia 118-3

Keep it simple. Coetzee returns after being pumped early on, he comes around the wicket to the lefties, and he arrows a yorker in at the base of middle and off. Scrambled seam, perfect length, Connolly can’t jam down.

17th over: Australia 118-2 (Connolly 23, Peake 11) Mokoena isn’t doing anything that stands out, just running in and hitting the deck, hard length mostly, and nothing much happens until the end of the over, when Peake tries to slam a full ball from outside leg stump through midwicket, but misses. It crashes into his boot.

16th over: Australia 113-2 (Connolly 21, Peake 9) Australia scored 79 in nine overs off the top, then 28 in six overs since Head’s wicket. Fortuin continues in that vein, singles coming for the two left-handers from the left-armer.

15th over: Australia 107-2 (Connolly 17, Peake 7) First boundary conceded by Mokoena, as Connolly gets on top of a pull shot and picks his spot around the corner.

14th over: Australia 102-2 (Connolly 12, Peake 7) So it’s time for Don’t Tell Mom the Babysitter’s Dead. Peake and Connolly have been left at home while the folks are out of town. Peake chunks a couple of runs over the leg side, then nicks Fortuin past slip for four.

WICKET! Marsh lbw Fortuin 56, Australia 94-2

The formula holds true. Marsh, big early shots, race to 50, Australia flying, South Africa introduce spin, sand in the gears, Marsh gets out without converting. Flat ball skidding past a sweep shot. DRS review doesn’t change the umpire’s decision.

13th over: Australia 94-1 (Marsh 56, Connolly 11) The South Africans have held back Mokoena until the relatively gentle environs of the 13th over, and Marsh is kind to him as well, just taking a couple of singles to cover.

12th over: Australia 91-1 (Marsh 54, Connolly 10) What is with these Australians and spin? Fortuin works away at Connolly, who apparently has no fracking idea how to do literally anything with a spin bowler. Almost chips back a catch, finds the field repeatedly, and takes five balls to get off strike with a tuck to square. Marsh’s early charge has slowed right down, partly because he’s barely had strike. He hits Fortuin hard off the back foot to deep cover, but there’s now a fielder back.

11th over: Australia 89-1 (Marsh 53, Connolly 9) A nice cover drive from Connolly against Bosch does the job. That’s his best shot.

The Head-Marsh partnership ends today at 1379 runs, one behind Watson and Haddin. Could we compound them as the Headmarshter?

10th over: Australia 82-1 (Marsh 52, Connolly 3) Fortuin into the attack, and a catch goes down! Spin is immediately the danger again for Australia, Connolly drives in the air to cover, and how has Matthew Breetzke dropped that? He’s taken some stunners this series, then shells the one that loops to him at head height to hit the meat of both palms.

9th over: Australia 79-1 (Marsh 51, Connolly 1) Plenty of time for Cooper Connolly today to build an innings, and no run chase to worry about.

WICKET! Head b Bosch 24, Australia 78-1

He’s chopped on again! Just keeps on doing that lately, does Head. Bosch buys him out, really. A friendly bit of width from Bosch earlier in the over, not once but twice, and both disappear over cover. No fielder in the deep there during the fielding restrictions. But then Bosch gets tighter to the stumps, still with some angle across, and Head tries to flash it away with an angled blade. Bottom edge, stumps.

Half century! Mitchell Marsh 51 from 33 balls

8th over: Australia 70-0 (Marsh 51, Head 16) Marsh is just so effortless in the way he hits these. Leans back a little and plants a huge pull shot over the leg side, as so often coming from a ball that isn’t too short. Then aims through midwicket and gets an edge down through fine leg for four, again standing tall and swinging hard.

Marsh and Head, the combo has quickly become something very impressive. They’re currently 29th on the list of Australian one-day partnerships, which doesn’t sound too exciting, but at around 1350 runs and climbing, they’re within a couple of hundred runs of overtaking a bunch of other entries – and they’ve had fewer innings than any pairing ahead of them, having only batted together 26 times.

Marsh adds a brace to end the over, and raises another 50. He’s had one in every match this series.

7th over: Australia 57-0 (Marsh 39, Head 16) As in the previous two games, Marsh is striking clean as a whistle, while Head is more a rusty gate. But his runs are coming, as they have done recently. Bosch the bowler, and Head almost chops onto his stumps, before slashing a ball that’s almost caught at deep third, but goes just square of the fielder for four.

6th over: Australia 52-0 (Marsh 38, Head 12) Marsh just keeps hitting straight! These two shots tend towards the on side rather than the off, but they find the fence regardless, Marsh just swinging through the line of Lizaad Williams’ bowling

5th over: Australia 42-0 (Marsh 30, Head 11) Lovely straight drive from Head, very simple back past Coetzee along the floor. Then Marsh tucks in! Drives him square for four, huge powerful swing of the hands there, then lifts him off a length – that ball isn’t too full, it’s swinging away slightly as well, and Marsh doesn’t care. He hits it anyway, over long off for a one-bouncer. He’s in touch…

4th over: Australia 29-0 (Marsh 22, Head 7) The topsy-turvy game continues, with Williams now bowling an over that goes for just one.

3rd over: Australia 28-0 (Marsh 22, Head 6) Coetzee’s second over is a contrast to his first. The first boundary isn’t middled, just tucked away off the hip. The second boundary isn’t ever, toed over mid on. But the third big hit is crunched, pull shot over midwicket for six.

2nd over: Australia 10-0 (Marsh 5, Head 5) And the quiet times lasted one over! First Head, then Marsh, play pull shots from Williams for four.

1st over: Australia 0-0 (Marsh 0, Head 0) A maiden over to begin the match. That’s quite something from Coetzee, who has got the ball swerving around the place like a remote-controlled plane. There has been early swing in this series, before the belting of the ball begins. Marsh is happy to wait, defending stoutly.

Elsewhere, in Guwahati, West Indies are making sure to give Virat Kohli plenty of runs to score in the chase. They’ve got 330 from 40 overs!

So. Another game for Peake, one of the young brigade the Aussies are investing in. That pushes Jack Edwards out, after taking five wickets, and Cam Green down to No7. What is going on with the Aussie selectors and Green? The guy who made 17 off 34 to tank the chase last time around is now the finisher? He’ll want to have completely renovated his approach during three days in the nets.

SA get de Zorzi and Rickelton in for Miller and Bavuma, Bosch and Coetzee in for the Jansens, and inexperienced seamer Mokoena in for the spinner Maharaj. That will please the Australians.

South Africa win the toss and bowl

Hooray, coin time. Temba Bavuma is rested, with Aiden Markram taking his place as captain.

This would be much more interesting if they at least had the toss, so we would have the teams. Keen to see who’s playing today. Is Pat Cummins going to have a warm-up before the Test series? It would be more like a cooldown out there. He kind of has to play really, Ellis can’t play after that side strain that took him out of the last match early.

It’s not raining, they just had a lot of rain earlier, so even though the covers are off, they have a lot of soaking-up work to do. Potch is at reasonably high altitude, so apparently it’s cold today as well as wet. Not much fun for cricketers.

Toss delayed

Okay, we spoke too soon. There is a wet outfield at the ground in Potchefstroom, so the toss has been delayed.

You know what they say: better to have delayed and tossed than never to have tossed at all.

Preamble

Hello! Game 3 and final today, with South Africa already taking the series in the first two. It has been a battle of the middle overs, with the home spinners thoroughly doing the job over the visiting batting. Australia, defending champions, need a solution to getting tied down, with just a year to go until the next World Cup, also in South Africa. So, still a lot to play for today.
