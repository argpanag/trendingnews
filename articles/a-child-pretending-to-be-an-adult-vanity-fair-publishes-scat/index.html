'A child pretending to be an adult': Vanity Fair publishes scathing Taylor Swift exposé from over 100 sources

TheTools · 23.11.2026 17:30 · source · permalink
"It's truly Regina George."

Taylor Swift is the biggest pop star on the planet, and she has spent much of her career being celebrated as a generous friend, a champion for artists’ rights, and an underdog who fought her way to the top. But a new magazine profile is telling a very different story.

The lengthy profile, published on Monday, is built on interviews with more than 100 sources. It describes Swift as controlling and “punitive,” and it questions nearly every part of the image she has built for herself.

Taylor Swift’s Image

Few artists have shaped their own public narrative as carefully as Swift. For years, she has described herself as an outsider growing up, saying in interviews that her friends abandoned her as a kid. Her songs have turned her breakups, feuds, and business battles into chapters of a story with her as the protagonist, and often as the one who was wronged.

The biggest of those battles came in 2019, when music executive Scooter Braun acquired her former label, Big Machine, and with it the masters to her first six albums. Swift responded with a scathing post on Tumblr, accusing Braun of “incessant, manipulative bullying,” and she went on to re-record those albums as “Taylor’s Version.” Her fans rallied behind her, and in 2025, she bought back the original recordings, which was widely celebrated as a win for artists’ rights.

Swift has also been involved in plenty of public feuds over the years, including with Kim Kardashian and Kanye West, Katy Perry, and more recently Charli XCX. Charli’s 2024 song “Sympathy is a knife” was widely believed to be about Swift, and Swift’s “Actually Romantic” from The Life of a Showgirl was seen as a pointed response. Earlier this month, Swift appeared to put an end to feud rumors with former friend Karlie Kloss at a Kansas City Chiefs game.

Through it all, her popularity has only grown. Her Eras Tour became the highest-grossing tour of all time, she became a billionaire in 2023, and she married Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce in a high-profile wedding at Madison Square Garden this summer.

But the last few weeks have been rocky. Swift was reportedly urged to “take back control” after details from the wedding leaked, and she has found herself caught in a legal battle over The Life of a Showgirl. Even one self-described superfan recently declared that “the blind love era is over.”

Now, Swift faces a rather scathing hit piece.

‘She Is Punitive’

Vanity Fair’s piece, written by May Jeong, never actually uses Swift’s name, referring to her only as “The Star,” and it gives nicknames to most of the other people involved as well. It is structured like a record, with “A side” sections telling the familiar version of Swift’s story and “B side” sections offering a much less flattering one.

Many of the harshest claims come from anonymous sources. A music industry executive, referred to as “The Exec,” described Swift bluntly.

“She is punitive,” the executive said.

The same executive compared Swift’s social circle to the villain of Mean Girls, saying, “It’s truly Regina George.”

Another source, described as a person who used to be close with Swift, said that she “loves to smack people in the head” in a figurative sense, as a way of disciplining people. That source also said Swift has a pattern of cutting people out of her life.

“Nobody is beyond reproach with her. If you don’t see it her way, she comes after you,” the source said.

The piece also runs through many of Swift’s feuds over the years, including her rift with Katy Perry, who is referred to as “The California Girl.” Jeong described Perry as “an adult pretending to be a child,” and then turned the line around on Swift, writing that she “might be a child pretending to be an adult.”

According to Jeong, many people refused to speak on the record, and one former collaborator even advised Jeong to turn down the assignment. Swift declined to comment for the story.
