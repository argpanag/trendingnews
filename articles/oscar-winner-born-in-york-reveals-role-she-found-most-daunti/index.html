Oscar winner born in York reveals role she found most daunting over glittering career

TheTools · 28.10.2026 18:09 · source · permalink

Legend of the stage and screen Dame Judi Dench has revealed the role she found most daunting in her seven-decade career.

Speaking on BBC's The One Show, the actress said she was most "terrified" by the part of Sally Bowles, which she played in the original West End production of Cabaret at London's Palace Theatre in 1968.

The role came just two years after the musical's Broadway debut.

She told presenter Alex Jones she was particularly worried about her singing abilities and the sound of her voice on stage.

The show ran for 336 performances and earned her critical acclaim, despite her reservations.

At the time, Dame Judi was best known for her work with the Old Vic and the Royal Shakespeare Company.

She has since gone on to win eight Olivier Awards and a Tony Award.

Her film career has been equally successful, with eight Academy Award nominations and a win for Best Supporting Actress in 1999 for her role as Queen Elizabeth II in Shakespeare in Love.

Dame Judi is currently promoting her new memoir, Is It Too Late to Make a Run for It?, written with her friend Brendan O'Hea and released on October 22.

The memoir reflects on her life and career but comes at a time when she has stepped back from acting due to health issues.

She was diagnosed with age-related macular degeneration 14 years ago.

In a recent interview on BBC Radio 4's Today programme, Dame Judi spoke openly about how the condition has affected her work.

She said: "I can't be on stage. I can't do a play any more. I can't do a film any more. I can't read any more.

"You've got to adjust to all that, make allowance for it, and just get on, and don't think about it too much."
