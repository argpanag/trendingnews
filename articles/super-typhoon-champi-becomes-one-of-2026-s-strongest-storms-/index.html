Champi peaked at Category 5-equivalent strength with 185 mph winds earlier this week. It is now lashing Taiwan with heavy rain and damaging winds.

One person has died and 171 others have been injured as Typhoon Champi lashes Taiwan.

The person died on Thursday after being swept by waves in Liuqiu, a small island off the southeastern coast of Taiwan's main island, according to the fire department, the country's Central Emergency Operations Center reported.

The casualty figures, also from the fire department, were as of Friday morning.

All of Taiwan's 262 domestic flights were canceled along with 669 international flights, according to the transport ministry. Schools and offices across Taiwan's main island were closed Friday.

Authorities in Taiwan issued a land and sea warning Thursday as Typhoon Champi, which has weakened from a super typhoon, is forecast to make a close approach to the northeast of the island before curving toward Japan.

Close to 1,300 people have been evacuated from vulnerable, mountainous areas, Taiwanese news agency Focus Taiwan reported.

Taiwan's Central Weather Administration has also urged residents in coastal areas to avoid outdoor activities and driving and be cautious of falling objects.

Champi tied April's Super Typhoon Sinlaku for the strongest 1-minute sustained winds of 2026 at 185 mph, equivalent to a Category 5 hurricane.

The Joint Typhoon Warning Center classifies a storm as a super typhoon when its 1-minute sustained winds reach at least 150 mph.

The typhoon is now packing gusts of 53 mph near its center, according to Taiwan's Central Weather Administration.

"A tropical cyclone is a generic term used by meteorologists to describe a rotating, organized system of clouds and thunderstorms that originates over tropical or subtropical waters and has closed, low-level circulation," according to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration.

The main difference between hurricanes and typhoons is geographic: Storms in the Northwest Pacific are called typhoons, while those in the North Atlantic and central and eastern North Pacific are called hurricanes.

What is Champi's path?

As of 4 a.m. ET Friday, Champi was about 210 miles northeast of Taiwan's southern tip, moving slowly north-northeast at about 4 mph, according to Taiwan's Central Weather Administration. It had sustained winds near 96 mph and gusts up to 119 mph. Forecasters said its effects could last nearly two days.

The storm, which is barreling across the western Pacific Ocean, is expected to brush past the island's northeastern coast and gradually weaken as it approaches, passing northeastern Taiwan, turning north toward the Chinese coast and eventually moving into southern Japan, Reuters reported.

What are the expected impacts?

Champi is expected to bring heavy rain and strong winds to parts of Taiwan and eastern China through the weekend, according to AccuWeather senior meteorologist Jason Nicholls.

Heavy rain is expected to affect Taiwan into Saturday (Oct. 24) and east-central China from Friday night (Oct. 23) through Sunday (Oct. 25). Rainfall is forecast to total up to 18 inches, with the heaviest rain expected in northeastern Taiwan.

Experts expect the typhoon to impact the island for two days and have issued mobile phone alerts warning of waves of more than 6 meters (20 feet) in coastal towns, while total rainfall of nearly one meter (3 feet) was expected in northern mountain areas, including the hills near the capital Taipei, Reuters reported.

The heavy rain could cause flooding, mudslides, structural damage and transportation delays, according to AccuWeather.

Strong winds from Champi, with wind gusts up to 120 mph, are expected to affect Taiwan and eastern China from Thursday night into Sunday. The winds could cause structural damage, prolonged power outages and transportation delays.
