Mississippi Senate Race Too Close To Call With Most Votes Counted

TheTools · 04.11.2026 07:00 · source · permalink

Mississippi’s U.S. Senate race was too close to call early Wednesday morning, with just 28 votes separating Democratic candidate Scott Colom and GOP incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith.

With the overwhelming majority of the vote counted, Colom had 462,779 votes to Hyde-Smith’s 462,751 shortly before 2 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday. If Colom pulls out a victory, it would be a tremendous upset and mark the eighth Senate seat Democrats have flipped from GOP control during the midterms.

President Donald Trump won Mississippi by more than 20 percentage points two years ago.

The state does not have an automatic recount law, though it is possible to request a recount from a judge.

The race had received attention in the closing days of the midterms as a long-shot flip opportunity for Democrats, who saw Colom ― a local prosecutor ― as a strong candidate against Hyde-Smith, who both parties agree is a weak campaigner.
