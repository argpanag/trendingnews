'Sloppy work': Judge reprimands ICE in Oregon warrantless arrest lawsuit

PORTLAND, Ore. (KATU) — Federal immigration agents repeatedly violated a court order prohibiting warrantless arrests, and one agent admitted to writing incorrect information to justify an arrest, a federal judge wrote in an order Monday reprimanding federal immigration agencies.

In February, Judge Mustafa Kasubhai in the U.S. District Court of Oregon issued a preliminary injunction prohibiting Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents from making warrantless arrests without evidence of an escape risk.

Immigration advocates behind the lawsuit, who criticize the arresting policy as "arrest first, justify later," claimed recently that federal agents were violating the order, and on Monday Kasubhai agreed.

Kasubhai wrote that ICE and the Department of Homeland Security, which is also a defendant, "routinely" violated the preliminary injunction.

"ICE has continued to conduct warrantless arrests without individualized, pre-arrest probable cause determinations," Kasubhai wrote. "In at least one incident established in this record, an ICE agent admitted to falsifying agency records."

As a result, Kasubhai ordered ICE and DHS to release more arrest data and reports to the plaintiffs, who are individuals represented by the Innovation Law Lab.

The federal agencies must routinely communicate to their officers, employees, and contractors the requirements of the preliminary injunction every 30 days. The agencies must also share all documents related to recent arrests, including body camera footage.

DHS and ICE did not immediately respond to KATU's multiple requests for comment on Monday.

Jordan Cunnings, an attorney with the Innovation Law Lab, called Monday's order from Kasubhai "validating."

Monday's order "is hopefully going to bring to light any future non-compliance (by ICE)," Cunnings told KATU, adding that it "also gives evidence for us to again seek compliance in the future, if they continue to not follow the order."

In July, federal agents arrested a Medford man at Emigrant Lake, where he and a friend went to fish. The man, identified in court documents as HDGH, bought fishing supplies with his friend at Wal-Mart before driving to the lake. A vehicle carrying ICE agents followed their car from the store parking lot, Kasubhai wrote.

An officer involved in that arrest, identified as JG in court documents, wrote in a report that HDGH was the subject of an arrest warrant. In court, however, JG testified that was incorrect, and there was a warrant only for the other man in the car with HDGH.

Kasubhai described the reporting error as an intentional act of falsifying information.

"It defies logic that such consequential errors ... are merely a 'mistake,'" he wrote. "He lied in an official record to facilitate the impression he was conducting a lawful warrant-based arrest."

"There is no telling how many other agents in Oregon engaged in similar misconduct," Kasubhai wrote.

The judge also criticized ICE agents' use of "boilerplate" language in reports to justify an arrest, like citing a person being in a car as a possible escape risk.

"This is sloppy work," Kasubhai wrote.

Expert witnesses for the defense testified that data shows federal immigration agencies arrested 574 people in Oregon between Feb. 5 and July 31. During that period, federal agencies reported only 13 warrantless arrests.

Kasubhai wrote that the true number of warrantless arrests by ICE agents is "significantly more than thirteen."

He added that the reporting errors and continued violations of the injunction "prove that, without external accountability or supervision, ICE is not capable or competent in conducting its investigative and enforcement activities with integrity."

A trial in the case has not yet been scheduled.
