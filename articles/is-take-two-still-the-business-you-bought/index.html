If you own Take-Two Interactive Software (TTWO), you bought a games publisher for hits such as Grand Theft Auto, NBA 2K and Zynga's mobile games. Released on November 9, the company's report for the quarter that ended September 30, 2026, offers the final look at its accounts before Grand Theft Auto VI comes out on November 19. With that launch so close, does Take-Two still work the way it did when you bought in?

Partly: Take-Two's Capital Spending Is Now Above Depreciation

A pattern in Take-Two's accounts that held for eleven quarter ends in a row broke in its fiscal second quarter of 2027, which ended September 30, 2026. From December 2023 to June 2026, the company's capital spending over each twelve months stayed below its depreciation and amortization over the same twelve months.

In the twelve months to September 30, 2026, Take-Two's capital spending came to $269 million, against $196 million of depreciation and amortization in its filing data. Management separately guides $623 million of amortization of acquired intangibles for fiscal 2027, a charge the filing figure above leaves out, so capital spending is above only that narrower measure. About half of that spending came in the September quarter alone: $138 million, against $31.9 million in the same quarter a year earlier. The excess is small, though, at 1.1% of Take-Two's revenue over the same twelve months.

Management Planned More Capital Spending At Take-Two

For fiscal 2027, management said on the November 9 call that its expectation to spend about $290 million is unchanged. In fiscal 2026, the year that ended March 31, 2026, Take-Two reported capital spending of $163 million.

Take-Two's games had a soft quarter, but the outlook for the year held. Net bookings, the company's own sales measure, were $1.6 billion in the September quarter, slightly below management's guidance, with NBA 2K27 below its forecast. Management reiterated its fiscal 2027 net bookings outlook of $8 billion to $8.2 billion. So the change in Take-Two's accounts comes from spending that management planned, not from the games it sells, whose yearly outlook still stands.

Take-Two's Free Cash Flow Fell By About Half

Over the twelve months to September 30, 2026, Take-Two's free cash flow, the cash left after capital spending, was $98.0 million, against $203 million a year earlier. Cash from operations barely moved over the same period, so the drop came from the higher spending, which used 73.3% of the cash from operations.
