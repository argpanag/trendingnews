  • Andrew Scott expressed concerns about being typecast as a queer actor and missing out on straight roles, believing it underestimates the audience to think it's a big deal for gay actors to play straight characters.
  • Apart from his role in Fleabag, Andrew Scott is known for playing Jim Moriarty in BBC's Sherlock and has starred in various films and theatre productions, including 1917 and Hamlet.
  • Scott pushed back against the idea that queer talent should only be cast in queer roles, stating that it is simply prejudice and he refuses to cater to those beliefs.

Andrew Scott revealed that there was one aspect of being an openly gay actor that worried him. With a little help from a Batman analogy, he explained his concerns and what he appreciated in an audience.

Scott revealed that he was afraid that he would miss out on straight roles because he was a queer actor. He stated, "I guess there was a fear that I wouldn't be able to get cast in those roles. That's what everybody said."

However, he believes that allowing gay actors to play straight roles should not be a big deal and that doing so is underestimating the audience. He shared his opinion by saying, "I feel very proud that that was quite a big moment because people were like, 'The actor is gay. Whoa!' And it is like this idea that the audience is stupid. We go to see 'Batman,' and it's like, 'Oh my god! He is not a bat?' "

Scott also pushed back against the idea that queer talent should only be cast in queer roles. He said, "That's just prejudice, and we won't cater to those people, or I'm certainly not going to."

What is Andrew Scott known for starring in apart from Fleabag?

Apart from Fleabag, several roles have defined Andrew Scott's career. One of the major roles he is known for comes from the BBC's Sherlock, in which Benedict Cumberbatch plays Sherlock Holmes, and Scott plays Jim Moriarty. For his portrayal of Sherlock's sworn nemesis, Scott earned considerable acclaim.

His role in Fleabag as 'The Hot Priest' was also widely appreciated and helped relieve his aforementioned fears of never being cast as a straight character again. Other roles that Scott has made a name for himself in include his lead role in All of Us Strangers, alongside Gladiator II's Paul Mescal. The film generated significant critical buzz and positively boosted Scott's career as well.

He has also played roles in films such as 1917, Spectre, Pride, and Netflix's Ripley, and is active in theatre, having played iconic stage roles, including Hamlet.
