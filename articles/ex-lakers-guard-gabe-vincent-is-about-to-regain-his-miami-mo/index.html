Ex-Lakers guard Gabe Vincent is about to regain his Miami mojo with Kings

TheTools · 23.11.2026 20:00 · source · permalink

Gabe Vincent is headed back to where it all began. The former Los Angeles Lakers guard is "finalizing a deal" with the Sacramento Kings, as reported by NBA insider Shams Charania on Sunday.

Vincent got his NBA start with the Kings franchise when Sacramento offered the undrafted Vincent an Exhibit 10 deal back in 2018. He played well for Sacramento's G League affiliate, the Stockton Kings, which led to the Miami Heat signing Vincent to a two-way deal in 2020, and a career was born.

Gabe Vincent's new opportunity with the Kings will lead to a career resurgence

Vincent ultimately didn't fit with where the Lakers were headed in their new Luka Doncic era. Last season, Vincent, 30, didn't help erase the narrative that the Lakers needed to get younger. And it didn't appear like many other teams were interested in Vincent's expiring contract at the deadline, either ... until Lakers general manager Rob Pelinka worked his magic and managed to send Vincent to the Atlanta Hawks in the Luke Kennard deal.

30 years old doesn't need to mean the end of the road for an NBA player, especially when it comes to one as gritty and skilled as Vincent. Let's also not forget that Vincent has delivered in the postseason at the highest level. With Miami, he dropped 29 points in an Eastern Conference Finals win over the Boston Celtics. He also scored 23 in an NBA Finals victory over the Denver Nuggets. He's not afraid of the big moment. Never has been.

Gabe Vincent is a valuable NBA guard who can really shoot

That's the player the Lakers gave a three-year, $33 million deal to in 2023. Unfortunately, knee issues torpedoed his 2023-24 season in LA. He rebounded to an extent in 2024-25 (6.4 points per game in a rotation role), but Vincent never really had a stretch of excellence with the Lakers as he did with the Heat (multiple times).

Now, Sacramento needs a veteran ball-handler to back up Darius Acuff Jr., and Vincent is the perfect guy for the job. With the Kings operating in a low-pressure situation from a competitive standpoint, Vincent will be free to cook and hoist shots to his heart's content, without having to worry about his role being adjusted.

It's the ideal opportunity for Vincent to give his career some momentum again -- the sort of momentum his Lakers stint never afforded him. I fully expect Vincent to play his best basketball since Miami, and good for him.
