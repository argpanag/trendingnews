Depression Charles leaves Madeira en-route for Portuguese mainland – Portugal Resident

TheTools · 11.11.2026 08:28 · source · permalink

Rain and wind to intensify into afternoon

Depression Charles has left Madeira, mopping up and calculating the costs of damage, but mercifully with no loss to life (the lifeguard missing at sea earlier this week was a casualty of storm Bruna). Winds reached 130 km/ hour and are expected to increase on their way to the Portuguese mainland, where the full force of this major storm is expected to hit mid-afternoon.

Until then, rainfall and winds will increase; the sea will get rougher (predictions are for waves reaching 13 metres) – and the message to all citizens is ‘if you can stay home, please do so’. There is no need for roads to be cluttered up with vehicles unless they are transporting essentials. 

Supermarkets in general are remaining open, but the Pingo Doce chain has just announced that it is closing all its branches today, ensuring that staff do not have to put themselves at risk travelling to work. 

In Lisbon, where mayor Carlos Moedas had announced the closure of all tunnels and underpasses, some tunnels have remained open. Traffic is much reduced on normal flows, but there are still some people having to go to work, and they do appear to be using the tunnels that are open. Reporters on the ground (under umbrellas) say that the main problem in Lisbon are ‘sheets of water’ that have built up due to the relentless rain.

For now, the centre and southern areas of mainland Portugal remain under red alert for rain and high winds.

In the last hours there was a ‘change in trajectory’ of Charles, which is now expected to enter the country between Cabo Carvoeiro (Peniche area) and Cabo de São Vicente (Sagres/ Costa Vicentina region of the Algarve). According to SIC, “the coastal Alentejo and the Algarve are expected to be the most affected places”. The north of the country looks like being ‘spared’ the worst, but will still suffer intense rainfall.

Warnings today – with schools and all ‘non-essential public services’ closed – are that power outages are likely.

source: SIC Notícias/ Lusa
