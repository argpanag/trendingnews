Is the Government throwing Jennifer Carroll MacNeill to the wolves? It certainly looks like it

TheTools · 25.11.2026 17:31 · source · permalink

Health minister Jennifer Carroll MacNeill addressing the Oireachtas Committee on Health on Wednesday.

Health minister Jennifer Carroll MacNeill cut a solitary figure as she descended the blue-carpeted stairs that leads from the Dáil Chamber, where the Taoiseach had just contradicted her multiple times.

Micheál Martin, in refusing to express confidence in the board of the HSE, was putting clear water between himself and the health minister, who just a matter of minutes earlier had publicly backed the board when she was questioned about the shock sacking of HSE chief executive Anne O'Connor by members of the health committee.

Tánaiste Simon Harris had not shown up in the Dáil to sit in solidarity with his party colleague in her most difficult hour. When asked about his whereabouts, a spokesman said the Fine Gael leader was not normally in the Chamber for leaders' questions, but added he would be present later as the Finance Bill progressed through the House.

Walking down those stairs, Ms Carroll MacNeill must have felt she had been thrown to the wolves.

"They will eventually catch you," was a warning issued to one senior Government figure several years ago, who spoke up and questioned some of the practices within the HSE.

Ms Carroll MacNeill, during her less than two-year tenure in the Department of Health, has taken an aggressively proactive approach to addressing cost overruns and perceived inefficiencies in the health system.

On entering office, she quickly moved to address emergency department congestion by publishing performance reviews after each bank holiday.

In this vein, she actively pushed for the rollout of rostering changes to bring hospital consultants and senior clinicians on-site to treat patients over weekends and evenings.

In June, she was involved in a standoff, in which she eventually prevailed, with Dublin’s Rotunda Hospital over consultants on public-only contracts carrying out private maternity work at the publicly funded hospital.

In another prolonged disagreement with pharmacists over the rollout of free HRT, Ms Carroll MacNeill argued while a system of paying dispensing fees had previously been applied, she believed the taxpayer should not have to carry this financial burden. The issue was ultimately resolved when both sides compromised.

She also became embroiled in a nasty spat with the board of Dublin's Mater Hospital over productivity levels, and during a meeting produced spreadsheets to prove the number of new consultants recruited was not resulting in the expected increase in patients treated.

"You may be the health minister, but you are not God," David Begg had told her at the meeting, a row which led to him stepping down as chair of the hospital's board.

Far from having God-like status, Ms Carroll MacNeill is quickly realising she may not even be afforded the usual protections given to previous ministers in distress.

In the recent past, when ministers came under fire and were engulfed in controversy over everything from water charges to Garda whistleblower scandals and indeed health system failings, they were backed to the hilt.

In early 2020, as Mr Harris, then in the Department of Health, faced the threat of a no-confidence motion, the Government took the decision to go to the country, deemed to be a self-preservation measure at the time.

Far from circling the wagons around her and showing up in the Dáil in a strong signal of support, Ms Carroll MacNeill has been left exposed.

The Tánaiste is scheduled to take leaders' questions in the Dáil every Thursday; his demeanour, body language and explanation of what is a spiralling disaster in the HSE and the Department of Health will be telling.

What an absolute mess.
