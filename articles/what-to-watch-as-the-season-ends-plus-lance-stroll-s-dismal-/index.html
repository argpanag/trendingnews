What to watch as the season ends. Plus: Lance Stroll’s dismal DNF streak

Welcome back to Prime Tire, where we’re just three weeks away from the end of the 2026 Formula 1 season. (Find the schedule here.)

Right now, the season is still set to end in the Middle East. Let’s review other season storylines that took a back seat while Kimi Antonelli was charmingly winning a record-breaking first title.

I’m Alex. It’s just me today. Let’s go.

The Finale: What to watch as the season ends

So, yes, F1 has now said that the scheduled races in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, following next week’s Las Vegas GP, are still a go. This is despite the ongoing Iran War in the region.

F1’s official position is that it will act if the conflict flares up significantly again. It has stood Imola down from its role as back-up season-finale venue.

If those races are called off at the very last minute, Vegas, presumably, becomes the denouement.

On a much less serious topic, the F1 teams are arguing about the cost cap, which is five years old in 2026. Madeline Coleman will be bringing you an investigation on this next week. It delves into an idea from Williams boss James Vowles about how teams further down the constructors’ championship might be afforded more cost cap money to invest in tools at their factories that would make their cars faster.

Funnily enough, the big teams that have these tools already aren’t in favor (at least judging from Mercedes team boss Toto Wolff’s comments in Singapore last month).

But Williams can say for certain who is going to be driving its cars in Las Vegas next week, plus in 2027. Two of its rivals down the order — Haas and Cadillac — cannot.

At Haas, the team is yet to confirm if Rafael Câmara will race in Vegas, or if Ryō Hirakawa will return to the seat that was Esteban Ocon’s until Mexico, now Hirakawa has wrapped up. Ocon was released after falling out with the team in Mexico.

Haas and Ollie Bearman also still need to do a deal over the Briton’s 2027 contract. And, as Luke Smith covered with Valtteri Bottas, it remains possible Cadillac could drop the likable Finn next year and replace him with IndyCar star/Formula 2 disappointment Colton Herta.

Remember yo-yo racing? Me neither, because when it isn’t raining in Brazil and the Ferrari drivers aren’t tripping each other up in Mexico, the 2026 on-track action has been pretty dull of late.

By my count, Antonelli’s sensational Italian GP win was the last race where I didn’t suggest to my colleagues mid-race that the action would be spiced up by the appearance of the safety car.

  • Madrid was awful.
  • Baku got two safety car periods, which very much made its finale worth watching after George Russell brilliantly controlled the opening stages.
  • Software glitches took the headlines at Sepang, where Verstappen crushed Mercedes before doing likewise to everyone (twice) in Singapore a week later.
  • And Austin was a strategically interesting contest, but Antonelli rather had it in the bag from the start.

What has happened is the teams have converged on the best energy deployment strategies, and so overtaking has become much harder. In Mexico, there was even a de facto speed limit on the main straight.

So, the one thing that had merit about the 2026 rules set, to me at least, is gone. It was baffling while it lasted.

Onto one storyline refresher that deserves special focus.

Stroll’s Season: A potential unwanted record

“Lance has had to retire due to a battery issue.”

I can’t say I was surprised to see those words from an Aston Martin spokesperson appear on my phone midway through last Sunday’s race in Brazil.

After all, Lance Stroll has officially retired 13 times this season and was non-classified in Australia because he finished 15 laps down. Even though he was on-track at the finish in Melbourne, he’d been in the pits four times as Aston and Honda grappled with their severe early-season engine battery issue.

The difference between non-classified and a race retirement might matter for one very rare F1 record in 2026. Because if Stroll retires from the three remaining races (and he’s only finished once in the last four races, in Austin) he’ll either match or break the record for most races in a season without reaching the finish.

The record (16) is currently held by Andrea de Cesaris for the Brabham team in 1987.

For a recap of how Aston got here, Madeline summed it up perfectly, explaining why the green team was bringing a massively updated car to the Hungarian GP just before the summer break. Things have barely improved since then.

TL;DR? Car, bad. Engine, even worse.

In Austin last month, Aston team boss Adrian Newey said, “We’re making huge amounts of progress. It really feels now like the team’s coming together.”

Which is where Aston was at the end of last season …

Longevity: Red Bull avoids another driver mistake

The other big news this week was Red Bull/Racing Bulls announcing Liam Lawson and Arvid Lindblad will again race for Red Bull’s junior team in 2027.

Sense prevails. Because, for a moment there, it was a real possibility Lawson could lose his seat to up-and-coming Red Bull junior star Nikola Tsolov. Instead, the Bulgarian racer will be a reserve driver for Red Bull and Racing Bulls.

As I’ve said in depth already, dropping another driver in brutal fashion — the classic style of Helmut Marko, the former Red Bull adviser/Red Bull junior team supremo — would’ve been a big mistake.

Well done to Red Bull’s new senior F1 management for taking a different approach.
