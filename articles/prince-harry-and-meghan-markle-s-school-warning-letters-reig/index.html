Prince Harry and Meghan Markle’s school warning letters reignite privacy battle they ‘cannot control’: experts

TheTools · 07.09.2026 17:56 · source · permalink

Parents at Cotswolds schools reportedly received warning letters about photographing and sharing details of pupils

Prince Harry and Meghan Markle plan return to UK after six years in US

Fox News senior foreign affairs correspondent Greg Palkot reports on Prince Harry and Meghan Markle relocating to the United Kingdom after residing in California for six years, noting that the couple plans to establish a residence outside London and enroll their children in a British school while King Charles undergoes cancer treatment.

Prince Harry and Meghan Markle’s return to the United Kingdom could become their biggest privacy test yet as their children Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet begin school life in the English countryside.

Last week, Markle, 45, and Harry, 41, along with their son Archie, 7, and daughter Princess Lilibet, 5, left their home in Montecito, California, for an extended stay in Britain.

According to reports, the Duke and Duchess of Sussex plan to settle in the Cotswolds, an affluent countryside region in south-central England, located about two hours outside London. Archie and Lilibet are reportedly set to begin classes at a private school in the U.K., though the school has not been publicly named.

In a report published Monday, royal commentator Kinsey Schofield told Page Six that parents in the Cotswolds area received warning letters from school officials after Harry and Markle "approached multiple schools" for Archie and Lilibet ahead of their move.

The letters reportedly reminded families of privacy rules surrounding students and high-profile families.

"The message sent to families essentially said that, from time to time, some families joining the school may attract a greater level of public or media interest," Schofield told the outlet.

The message also reportedly noted that the school wanted to preserve a "safe, happy, and private environment" for all students.

"It then reminded parents and carers not to photograph or film other children without permission, or share photographs, information or details about pupils or their families on social media," Schofield said.

A school in the Cotswolds sent warning letters to parents as the couple's children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet, are set to begin classes at a private school in England. (Meghan Markle/Instagram)

Page Six did not identify the school that sent the letters, citing privacy concerns, but reported that it is not the school Archie and Lilibet are attending.

Harry and Markle have long tried to shield their children from uncontrolled public exposure. The couple had previously skipped the traditional royal baby photo call, limited public images of their children and raised security concerns around U.K. visits.

However, royal experts told Fox News Digital that school life presents a different challenge because it places Archie and Lilibet’s privacy partly in the hands of other parents, classmates and the broader community.

Schofield, who also spoke to Fox News Digital about the couple’s return to Britain, said the school warnings underscore how difficult it may be for Harry and Markle to manage their children’s privacy in everyday settings.

Harry and Markle have been fiercely protective of their children's privacy. (Meghan Markle/Instagram)

"School introduces an enormous number of variables Harry and Meghan cannot control," the host of YouTube’s "Kinsey Schofield Unfiltered" said.

"Other parents have phones. Children have friends. There are school events, sporting fixtures, birthday parties, drop-offs, and pick-ups," Schofield added. "You cannot manage a school community like a communications operation."

Schofield noted that the letter she had seen was "deliberately vague" but explained that the reminders were likely not unusual on their own. However, she argued that the attention surrounding Harry and Markle will change the dynamic for other families.

"None of those requests are unreasonable," Schofield continued. "Archie and Lili deserve exactly the same opportunity as every other child to go to school without feeling watched. But inevitably, when one family attracts this level of global attention, the entire school community becomes more conscious of what it photographs, posts, and discusses."

"Other families are effectively being asked to adjust their behavior because Harry and Meghan have arrived," she added.

Markle shares photos of their children on Instagram but typically with their faces obscured. (Meghan Markle/Instagram)

While speaking with Fox News Digital, royal expert Hilary Fordwich pointed out that Markle posted photos of Archie and Lilibet on her own social media, sharing her view that it was "beyond hypocritical to on the one hand want ‘privacy’ yet, on the other hand, monetize their family."

While Markle has shared photos of her children on Instagram, they are often pictured from behind, with their faces hidden or in carefully chosen images.

The former "Suits" star’s representative previously defended Markle’s approach in a statement to Newsweek.

"The Duchess has always been clear that there is a distinction between sharing moments from her life and exposing her children to public scrutiny," her spokesperson said in June. "By obscuring their faces, she is demonstrating the very principle she advocates for: giving children privacy, agency, and protection in an increasingly digital world."

Fordwich shared her view that the Sussexes’ decision to keep Archie and Lilibet largely out of public view may have had the unintended effect of making candid images more sought-after.

She contrasted the Sussexes’ approach with that of Prince William and Kate Middleton, who typically release official images of Prince George, 13, Princess Charlotte, 11, and Prince Louis, 8, on birthdays and other milestones.

"The school has a legal and moral obligation to protect their students, so it puts them likewise in a precarious position," she said.

"There will be a huge bounty regarding any resulting photos, so the incentive is there, as unlike Prince William and Catherine, who carefully release photos of their children on special occasions, the Sussexes have created this bounty situation themselves," she argued.

"Now, ordinary families have such constraints put upon them. So now they are being made subordinate to the Sussex brand."

Schofield noted that the Cotswolds has long attracted royals, celebrities and wealthy public figures seeking privacy.

Highgrove House, the country home of Harry’s father, King Charles, and Queen Camilla, is located nearby, while Princess Anne is based at Gatcombe Park.

David and Victoria Beckham, Kate Moss, Ellen DeGeneres and Portia de Rossi, Jeremy Clarkson, Hugh Grant and Elizabeth Hurley are also among the famous names who either live there full-time, keep country homes there or are frequently linked to the region.

"The Cotswolds are full of extremely wealthy and famous people precisely because discretion is part of the culture," Schofield said. "These are people who are accustomed to seeing celebrities and royals without turning it into a spectacle."

"I suspect the novelty of Harry and Meghan will wear off very quickly if their presence brings photographers, security concerns, or increased media attention," she added.

Schofield argued that the school controversy reflects a broader tension that has followed the Sussexes since they stepped back from royal duties and moved away from the U.K. in 2020.

"Privacy was one of the central themes surrounding their departure, and six years later they have returned to Britain and almost immediately we are talking about privacy again," she said.

"But there is an important distinction between reasonable privacy and control," Schofield continued. "Harry and Meghan are entitled to protect their children, their home and genuinely private moments. Where they have struggled historically is accepting that privacy does not necessarily mean controlling every headline, photograph, or public conversation about them."

"If they want this move to work, they may have to develop a much higher tolerance for things they cannot control," Schofield added.

Schofield suggested that Harry and Markle consider emulating the example set by William and Middleton.

"I do think Harry should look at how William and Catherine have navigated this," she said. "Their children have grown up under extraordinary scrutiny, yet the Waleses have established boundaries with the media while accepting that public interest comes with their children’s unique position."

The Sussexes’ desire to reconnect with Charles and other members of the royal family has been reported as one of the reasons behind their move back to the U.K.
