Duterte makes ICC debut

TheTools · 17.09.2026 00:00 · source · permalink

Former president Rodrigo Duterte appeared in person at the International Criminal Court for the first time, as he faces charges over his “war on drugs” that killed thousands.

The 81-year-old had appeared by video link when he was first brought to the court in The Hague, but had since skipped hearings, with his defence team saying he was too ill.

Dressed in a dark suit and white shirt with no tie, the former presi­dent yesterday sat with his chin resting on his hands as proceedings opened.

Judge Joanna Korner noted his appearance in court for the first time, and said he could leave the court at any time without seeking permission from the bench.

But he stayed throughout the hearing, which lasted one hour and 15 minutes, showing no emotion and seeming to follow proceedings through headphones.

Duterte did not speak during the hearing but waved at the public gallery when the judge had left the courtroom at the end.

He is the first Asian former head of state to face trial at the ICC, which prosecutes individuals for the world’s worst crimes such as war crimes and crimes against humanity.

Duterte faces three counts of crimes against humanity, with prosecutors alleging his involvement in at least 76 murders between 2013 and 2018 during his offensive against drug users and sellers.

Korner has set Nov 30 as the start date of the trial, which could take months or even years.

The only time Duterte had been seen since his arrest in March 2025 was an initial appearance via video, where he seemed confused and tired, his speech barely audible.

Yesterday’s hearing was a “status hearing” for the parties and the judge to fix logistical details.

Lawyers clashed over evidence filed by the prosecution, which they said was around 15,000 pieces but the defence put at more than 62,000 documents.

“It’s quite enormous. It’s impossible,” said defence lawyer Peter Haynes.

“We haven’t got a prayer of reading” such a mountain of docu­ments before the trial starts, said Haynes.

“The current state of the case is a mess. If I had a younger, heal­thier client, I would invite you to put back the trial by six months ... but I don’t,” said Duterte’s lawyer.

Korner chided the prosecution for “wholesale dumping” of documents and urged the parties to work together to reduce the size of the case file.

Relatives of some of those killed in the anti-drugs campaign welcomed Duterte’s appearance in court.

Randy Delos Santos, 48, an uncle of Kian Delos Santos, a teenager killed by police in August 2017, when Duterte was president, said: “Of course, we are very happy that he is now able to attend in person.

“We hope that his being physically fit will help speed up the hearings,” Santos said in Manila.

In a submission published Tuesday, Duterte’s lawyers said he suffered from “significant memory impairment that prevents him from retaining recent information and reliably accessing memories”.

Judges are weighing whether he is mentally fit to be tried and have ordered a medical assessment by three experts.

That assessment has not been made public but Duterte’s defence cited the results of examinations contained in the report.

According to the defence, these examination showed Duterte thinks he is living in 2007 or 2008, that he is 84 or 85 years old, and could not recall the name of the current US president. — AFP
