Lee Reeves: Tributes pour in for Limerick boxer and suicide prevention volunteer

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:34 · source · permalink

Gary Culley (left) in action against Limerick's Lee Reeves. The Roxboro native had been seriously ill since being stretchered from the ring after a bout at Dublin’s 3Arena on August 1. Picture: Damien Eagers/Press Association.

Tributes have been pouring in for Limerick boxer Lee Reeves, widely known as El Champo, who died aged 31 on Monday night.

The Roxboro native had been seriously ill since being stretchered from the ring after a bout at Dublin’s 3Arena on August 1.

After major surgery at Beaumont Hospital in Dublin, he was placed in an induced coma before being transferred to the Mater Hospital.

He was recently transferred to University Hospital Limerick (UHL) to be closer to his family, where he had been receiving physiotherapy as part of his treatment.

He died peacefully at UHL shortly after 10pm, surrounded by family and friends.

Family pay tribute

His sister Jessie led the tributes, sharing a series of family photographs.

“I’ll see you on the other side my brother. I’ve no words for this. I love you endlessly El Champo,” she wrote.

“Now mam gets to wait patiently for her boy this life is so unfair my brother. Your words, your wisdom, your positivity, your patience. Everything will stay for life, I couldn’t have got a better brother in this life, my greatest gift was a big brother. You were the glue to our little family we had left,” Jessie added.

Alongside a series of photographs of Lee and his nephews, his sister Shannon Devin said she would “love that face forever”.

“You’ve me broken brother. I will mind them boys til the very end, they are your legacy. I will make sure of that, I’ll do you proud to the very end,” she wrote.

“You were my whole life, I idolised you and always will. We got so close over the last few years, so you says I got wiser!” Ms Devin added.

A life dedicated to helping others

An event organised by Limerick Treaty Suicide Prevention (LTSP) in August drew hundreds of messages of support for Reeves. Attendees heard how he had “helped save lives” and made a difference in the community.

Reeves was known not only for his achievements in boxing but also for his extensive work helping others.

Having lost his mother, Cathy, to suicide, he dedicated time to patrolling with the LTSP group and served as an ambassador for the organisation. He was praised locally for jumping into the River Shannon to save a woman’s life while on patrol.

Volunteer David O’Mahony, who was at Reeves’ bedside on Monday night, previously told the Irish Examiner that the boxer had been chosen as an ambassador because he “embodies everything” the group stands for.

Away from boxing and his voluntary work, Reeves regularly shared heartwarming videos on social media featuring his nana, whom he would pick up in his car.

Fundraising for young Limerick boy

When Alex Heaney, 11, from Patrickswell, was diagnosed with Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), Reeves launched a fundraising campaign to help him and his family.

Lee became the young boy’s “first true hero”.

“He was captivated by Lee the first time he met him (we all were), in awe of the presence he had. He sat himself down on our couch for a chat and within a minute it was like we had known him forever. Like a big brother to Alex who'd whisper funny things to him so we couldn't hear, like a friend to Dean who would compete with him, and like a friend to us who cared about you and wanted to help in any way they could,” Alex’s family said in tribute.

They described the boxer as a “magnet” who “attracted people to him” and was “truly 1 in a million”.

“I feel sorry for those who never got to spend time around Lee, and I feel very lucky and blessed that we did and got to call him our friend,” they said.

As tributes continue to pour in, many have shared memories of Reeves, describing how Limerick had lost its brightest light.
