BOYZ ON A MISSION - Jamaica Observer

TheTools · 17.11.2026 05:12 · source · permalink

Jamaica chase history vs Mexico in Concacaf Nations League clash

INSPIRED by their famous victory over Mexico on Saturday, Jamaica’s Reggae Boyz will be looking to complete the job today and book their place in the Concacaf Nations League semi-finals.

Jamaica take on host Mexico at the BBVA Stadium in Monterrey in their second-leg quarter-final starting at 9:30 pm. The national team needs only a draw to advance following their 3-2 first-leg victory at the National Stadium.

However, the Rudolph Speid-led Boyz will be hoping the long journey to Mexico doesn’t take a toll on their performance after spending much of Sunday travelling to Monterrey on commercial flights.

The team left the island early Sunday morning but didn’t arrive in Monterrey until late Sunday evening. Players and technical staff were split in two groups, with one travelling through Miami and the other via Panama.

The team conducted their sole training session at the BBVA Stadium on Monday evening.

Jamaica Reggae Boyz player Renaldo Cephas reacts during the Concacaf Nations League first-leg, quarter-final football match against Mexico at the National Stadium on November 14, 2026. (Photo: JFF Media)

Speid will also be one player short after Tottenham Hotspur midfielder Tyrese Hall, who was recently named Premier League 2 Player of the Month, returned to London for personal reasons. The 21-year-old, who only has two caps including his debut last March, was left on the bench in Saturday’s win.

Mexico’s playing conditions are often a concern for visiting teams, although the Boyz will avoid the high-altitude conditions associated with the Azteca.

Speid says he isn’t concerned about the external factors as they seek to topple the hosts.

“We played in Mexico this year so some of the players are used to [the conditions]. I am not one of those coaches that worry about where we play because if you let that weigh heavily on what you do, it [causes] an adverse reaction to how the team performs,” he said.

“It is what it is. Wherever they put us to play, we’ll do the best we can. It’s the same thing when it comes to teams; whoever we get to play, we’re going to try our best. So we’re really not worried, we’re focused on Mexico. They’re a top-ranked team; they didn’t get there by fluke, but that’s where we want to be so we have to prepare for them.”

Barring any late issues, Jamaica could field a similar line-up which led the world number 10 ranked Mexicans 3-0 after 69 minutes.

They could also stick to the same approach that proved successful against Rafa Marquez’s side.

“If we sit back and just watch the Mexico team play, they’ll just take us apart eventually,” he said. “So part of the plan was not to let that happen and we picked our moments, when we press forward. So it wasn’t just a total press, it was always calculated ones; press forward when it was necessary to ensure that we kept them in their half for long periods of time.”

Members of Jamaica’s Reggae Boyz squad train at Stadium East on November 13, 2026. (Photo: Karl Mclarty)

Jamaica have never beaten Mexico on Mexican soil in a senior men’s international match from 10 attempts. However, they did earn a 2-2 draw in their last game back in March 2023 in the Nations League group stage.

Mexico, though, have a slight advantage with the away-goals rule in effect, meaning a 1-0 or 2-1 win would be enough to send them through despite the aggregate score being level.

Head Coach Marquez is currently under immense pressure from the Mexican media having gone winless in his five games since taking over in July.

He’s expecting a strong response from his team who have made the semifinals of every Nations League tournament since its inception in 2019.

“It’s almost the same defensive line that played in the World Cup and they were extraordinary back then,” he said. “While the contexts are obviously different, I believe we must demand more of ourselves to ensure we don’t repeat these kinds of errors.”

“I think we should [also] focus on the positives, the way the team reacted, and how the substitutions really helped spark that reaction,” he said. “Now we need to take advantage of playing at home. Monterrey is a city where the atmosphere and the fan support can really give us a boost and that’s what we’re hoping for in the next match.”

Jamaica’s sole appearance in the Nations League semi-finals was in the 2023/24 season where they went on to finish third.

 

Jamaica Squad:

Goalkeepers – Jahmali Waite, Amal Knight, Joshua Grant

Defenders: Joel Latibeaudiere (captain), Miguel Freckleton, Richard King, Ronaldo Webster, Kyle Ming, Denzel Hall, Christopher Ainsworth, Damion Lowe, Jeovanni Laing

Midfielders: Courtney Clarke, Karoy Anderson, Dwight Merrick, Ramone Howell

Forwards: Kaheim Dixon, Renaldo Cephas, Javon East, Ricky Jade-Jones, Khori Bennett, Daniel Green, Warner Brown
