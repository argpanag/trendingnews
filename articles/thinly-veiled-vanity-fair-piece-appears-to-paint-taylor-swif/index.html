Thinly veiled Vanity Fair piece appears to paint Taylor Swift as vindictive

TheTools · 24.11.2026 17:21 · source · permalink

A magazine article about an unnamed "mastermind" appears to describe Taylor Swift as a pop star with a mean streak.

The Vanity Fair story in question begins by describing "a generational talent" born in Reading, Pa., in 1989 who signs with a record label in Nashville.

"Her name foretells her future fate: the Latin root of celebrity is celere, meaning "rapid," "fast," "swift," the article seemingly hints. It further notes that the subject is recently married, using the phrase "your English and your gym teachers wed."

That's how Swift announced her engagement to NFL star Travis Kelce on social media.

What follows is a critical essay based on conversations with more than 100 people linked to the story's subject. One music executive refers to her as "punitive."

Several people Vanity Fair contacted refused to speak about "The Star" without permission or asked that their conversations be kept confidential. The implication is that they fear retribution.

"If you don't see it her way, she comes after you," an acquaintance of the shrewd operator said.

Chanice Skinner, who went to school with the unidentified celebrity, described her as a privileged snob who's reinvented herself as an underdog.

"If anything, you were one of the bullies," Skinner said.

The Vanity Fair's story alleges "The Star" is largely viewed as a "combination of victim and victimizer."

It also paints its subject as being generous to fellow musicians who hitch their rising stars to her rocket-fueled superstardom. Many of those people are said to remain in her orbit.

As if the allusions to Swift weren't clear enough, Vanity Fair's piece details an incident in which masses of people gathered at Madison Square Garden, where she Swift and Kelce married in July.

Not everyone was looking to commit a hard foul on Swift Monday. Knicks guard Jalen Brunson told US Weekly that he considers the "Bad Blood" singer a true fan weeks after his teammate Tyler Kolek questioned her sincerity when she's sitting courtside at the Garden cheering on the championship winning team.

"I can't believe I have to say this," Brunson told US Weekly. "Yes, she's a member of the Knicks fandom. Yes, she is."

Swift's representatives haven't responded to a request for comment nor did the subject of the Vanity Fair article respond to inquiries.
