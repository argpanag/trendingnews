RLCS 2027 Esports Reportedly A Break Year Ahead Of UE6

TheTools · 18.09.2026 16:55 · source · permalink

Jack Marsh

Share:

Jack Marsh

Share:

The RLCS 2027 season is set for a revamped short circuit ahead of a temporary ‘break’ from action in the Spring. The Rocket League Unreal Engine 6 transition was announced back at the Paris Major. Coming as a long-overdue overhaul to the core game.

Fans of the car-football title have waited over five years for the upgrade, which was previously planned to jump from UE3 to UE5, and will now skip to become the first game in the world to use the next-generation platform.

That will mean great things for the future of Rocket League, it will come at the expense of the current RLCS 2027 structure. Which is reportedly planning to shorten the 2027 season to make time for an esport hiatus amid the new engine port.

A report from Sheep Esports claims the RLCS 2027 season will run from December to March, starting with a 24-team Kick-off weekend in London and a second global event in North America in March.

The London Kick-Off event will be held at the Copper Box Arena, which has hosted three RLCS events already. In an invitation-only event of 24 partnered organisations will be invited. we’ll move away from the current open era.

The 2026 season also had an invitational Kick-Off, but with just eight teams. It’s proposed that the 24 organisations will include those 21 teams that currently feature in Esports Shop within Rocket League, with three more expected to be added.

A change to a two-tournament structure is supposed to give Epic Games the chance to ship Unreal Engine 6 and iron out any initial wrinkles before competitive play can resume.

However, the ‘break’ will conclude by August 2027 as Epic Games signed an ongoing commitment with the Esports World Cup last year to feature RL in EWC through 2028.

The change in format is also supposed to be joined by an altered partnership scheme which tweaks the distribution of the esports decal sales agreement. Putting teams back in control of their own profits, rewarding the teams with bigger communities while putting the onus on design and marketing.
