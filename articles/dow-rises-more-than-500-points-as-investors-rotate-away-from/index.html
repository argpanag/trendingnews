Dow rises more than 500 points as investors rotate away from AI trade: Live updates

TheTools · 14.10.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose on Thursday as concerns around tech companies overspending on artificial intelligence led investors to rotate out of the trade and seek value elsewhere. Traders also digested U.S. data that pointed to persistent inflation.

The blue-chip index added 556.21 points, or 1.08%, to end at 52,177.69. The S&P 500 gained 0.33% to close at 7,744.06. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite closed up 0.08% at 26,713.03, rebounding from session lows. At one point, the index was down 1.4% on the day.

Seagate Technology and Western Digital added to their declines this week, along with shares of SK Hynix, which drove a rout in South Korea's Kospi index overnight. Traders were becoming concerned about a potential memory chip glut as South Korea faces increasing competition from China. Tesla CEO Elon Musk discussed the matter in a post on X, saying that "China will be making a LOT of RAM in 2028."

Compounding worries in the AI space, sources told CNBC that Anthropic's revenue grew to $16.8 billion in the third quarter, with some traders concerned the AI giant's top line might not be growing fast enough to justify the lofty gains in chipmakers.

However, other stocks in the AI trade recovered. CoreWeave shares gained almost 6%, reversing course from the prior day's losses. The stock tumbled more than 15% in the previous session as investors grew fearful that the company was borrowing too much for its AI build-out. CoreWeave corporate bond yields also surged. Additionally, Micron Technology rebounded from earlier losses to gain nearly 2%.

"When Treasurys are yielding 5% and that's the highest since the early 2000s, investors are going to start weighing that versus taking a huge bet on on AI," said Alex Shahidi, co-CIO of MAI Capital Management's Evoke. "It's not are you in or out of AI; it's how concentrated you want to be."

The market was supported by gains in areas outside of tech. Shares of retailer Walmart climbed 1.5%, while Nike shares jumped more than 5%.

Investors also reacted to more insights on the inflation front. The September reading on producer prices recorded an increase of 1.3% on the month and 6.3% year over year — the biggest monthly and yearly gains since 2022.

Concerns that the Federal Reserve could tighten monetary policy more aggressively before year-end weighed on equities Wednesday after new data showed consumer prices in September rose faster than expected on a year-over-year basis.

"Today's PPI report, after Wednesday's CPI, confirms inflation is getting worse as energy prices stay high," said David Russell, global head of market strategy at TradeStation. "Election or no election, prices are rising and the Fed might be forced to act."

The three major averages finished Thursday's session with gains.

The S&P 500 rose 0.33% to end at 7,744.06, while the Nasdaq Composite gained just 0.08% to 26,713.03. The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 556.21 points, or 1.08%, to 52,177.69.

— Sean Conlon

Analysts at Barclays say they're seeing signs that a SpaceX-Tesla merger is something that executives are thinking about, analysts with Barclays said Wednesday.

"We believe recent management comments indicate they see key benefits of a combination," analyst Dan Levy wrote to clients in a Wednesday note, though he didn't get into the details.

Vertical integration of production pipelines for two the companies, in addition to the elimination of overhead redundancies, would boost value added, Levy said. He put the Texas-based Terafab data center, which is already structured as a joint venture, at the center of his estimation.

"We see Terafab as a key driver of the potential combination as CEO Elon Musk strongly believes that Terafab will be required to support TSLA/SPCX compute needs," he wrote.

— Tobias Burns

Performance is broadening out within the S&P 500.

More than half of S&P 500 stocks are trading above their 200-day moving averages, an improvement from less than 40% at the end of September when gains were more concentrated, likely within tech stocks.

Some of the names moving above their 200-day moving averages for the first time in more than a month were Walmart (WMT), Kroger (KR), HCA Healthcare (HCA) and United Health Services (UHS).

— Nick Wells and Tobias Burns

CoreWeave stock broke into black in Thursday afternoon, trading up 3.5% just as of 2:53 p.m., after being down as much as 9.5% in morning trading.

The uptick proved a Wednesday Deutsche Bank note to be prescient. Analyst Brad Zelnick said that Wednesday's dislocation was "presenting buying opportunity."

"If this is purely about broader AI financing concerns, we'd argue CoreWeave continues to scale rather responsibly," he wrote to clients. "Near-term liquidity is in decent shape."

The stock got hit with a combination blow on Wednesday, with higher-than-expected inflation data initially pushing up bond yields, industrial titans fretting about the stock online, and some warnings from analysts about its sector.

— Tobias Burns

The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) is lower for an eighth straight trading day, its first eight-day slide since September 2015; it fell for nine sessions in May 2006. The ETF has fallen more than 15% over that stretch. The fund was last down around 2%.

The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) is also down an eighth straight day, making it the longest slide since May 2006 when it fell for nine straight sessions.

— Sarah Min and Nick Wells

More than half of S&P 500 constituents are trading above their 200-day moving averages, a notable improvement from late September when that figure was around 40%.

The shift suggests that participation in the market's advance is broadening beyond recent high flyers of the AI trade. The 200-day moving average is a widely watched indicator of a stocks' long-term trend and a shift above that level can show improving momentum.

Among the names moving above their 200-day moving averages for the first time in a month are Walmart, Kroger, HCA Healthcare and Universal Health Services.

— Nick Wells

Wall Street firm Baird upgraded Bank of America to outperform from neutral Thursday, while maintaining its $62 price target, implying a 21% upside from its Wednesday close of $51.

The higher rerating on the bank comes despite it reporting a soft quarter before the open Thursday.

Bank of America delivered an earnings per share miss and weak noninterest income along with higher expenses on Wednesday, sending shares lower.

Baird called the sell-off "overdone" and sees the stock's weakness as a buying opportunity. That stems from the firm's view that the bank's 2027 net interest income outlook is an "incremental positive" coupled with the stock's attractive valuation.

— Paulina Likos

Gil Luria, analyst at DA Davidson, said Anthropic's revenue for the third quarter was "not what anyone wanted to see."

Sources told CNBC that the AI giant's revenue grew to $16.8 billion in the third quarter, up from its second-quarter revenue of $11.6 billion. But, that growth in the third quarter was slower than its rise in the previous one, as first-quarter revenue for the company was just $4.7 billion. 

Luria said, though, that there was good news as the Financial Times reported that Antrhopic had positive cash flow in the third quarter, pushing back on the narrative that the AI labs can't get to profitability. But he added that investors shouldn't be looking at a single company to judge the performance of the sector, and rather focus on corporate and consumer spending on the technology. 

"We expect the overall market for AI spend to continue on a steep path as it diversifies away from Anthropic and OpenAI," he wrote in a Thursday note. "These two will see their market get cut from providing all-encompassing AI services to only providing frontier tokens for mission critical cost-insensitive applications… This overall demand should support more AI compute demand and more data center construction, even if it doesn't meet the most outlandish extrapolations."

— Davis Giangiulio

Crocs shares spiked almost 6% on Thursday after Barclays upgraded the stock to overweight from equal weight. Analyst Adrienne Yih also lifted the price target 18% to $139 from $118, saying that there is now greater visibility into the company's multiyear growth story.

CNBC's Justin Zacks recently called out the opportunity in Crocs stock, saying that its product innovation has led to more stable sales growth and it has a huge opportunity to grow in international markets like China. He also expects a turnaround at its HeyDude brand could be a catalyst for the stock.

Yih cited these reasons and added that the company was showing direct-to-consumer strength, promotional discipline, and an inflection in its inventory management.

"Importantly, many of the company's strongest-performing products are newer franchises outside of the traditional Classic Clog, giving us greater confidence that growth is becoming broader and more durable," Yih wrote.

— Christina Cheddar Berk

The producer price index, a gauge of wholesale inflation, rose 6.3% in September on a year-over-year basis. That's well above a Dow Jones consensus of 5.5% and marked the largest 12-month advance for PPI since December 2022. Month over month, PPI rose 1.3% last month, topping a 0.6% estimate.

The report comes a day after the BLS released hotter-than-expected U.S. consumer price index data.

— Fred Imbert

Investors on Thursday morning will get another look at the inflation picture with the release of the September reading for the producer price index.

On a monthly basis, economists polled by Dow Jones are expecting the index to show a 0.6% increase, while the year-over-year figure is expected to come in at 5.5%.

Excluding food and energy, core PPI is anticipated to rise 0.3% month over month.

The data comes a day after September's consumer price index reading, which showed slightly hotter-than-expected figures for the annual rate of headline inflation as well as core CPI on a monthly and yearly basis.

— Sean Conlon

Industrial production in the euro zone fell 1.1% year-on-year during August, outweighing the 0.1% dip expected by analysts in a Reuters poll.

Industrial output was down 0.5% month-by-month, according to Eurostat, the EU's statistical office, lagging analyst forecasts of a flat print.

"For the third month in a row, industrial production declined in the eurozone," said Bert Colijn, Chief Economist, Netherlands at ING.

"While manufacturers remain upbeat about output, production data paints a less rosy picture. Industry may not be immune to higher energy prices and global uncertainty after all."

— Hugh Leask

Shares in Ericsson surged in morning trade after the Swedish multinational telecoms mainstay reported better-than-expected third-quarter earnings.

Net sales for the three months ended Sept. 30 reached 59.5 billion Swedish krona ($5.91 billion), a 6% rise year-on-year and a 13% increase from the previous quarter.

Ericsson's adjusted earnings before interest, taxes and amortization were 10.1 billion Swedish krona, beating consensus forecasts of 7.87 billion.

The company moved to the top of Europe's benchmark Stoxx 600, with its shares up about 7.5% by 8:55 a.m. in London (3:55 a.m. E.T.).

In a statement, Per Narvinger, president and CEO, said the results come amid a challenging environment, adding that an overhaul of the company's operating structure "will strengthen our customer focus and drive further efficiencies over time."

— Hugh Leask

Oil fell Thursday as traders assessed potential supply disruptions following attacks on ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, while digesting the International Energy Agency's latest monthly report on the outlook of the oil market.

Futures for international benchmark Brent crude for December delivery dropped 1.66% to $104.31 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures for November fell 1.09% to $94.05 per barrel.

A Singapore registered ship was sunk in the critical Strait of Hormuz, which raised worries of a prolonged Mideast conflict. "Such attacks are unprovoked, unjustifiable and a breach of international law," the Singapore maritime authority said in a statement Wednesday.

Iran, meanwhile, has said it is "counting the moments" until it can start striking U.S. warships.

The International Energy Agency in its oil report for October out Wednesday cautioned that constraints on diesel markets could continue well into the next year, with fresh attacks on Saudi Arabia threatening to derail a recovery in oil supply.
