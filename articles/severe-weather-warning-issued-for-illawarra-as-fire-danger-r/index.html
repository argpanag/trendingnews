Severe weather warning issued for Illawarra as fire danger rating rises early in spring

TheTools · 04.09.2026 08:30 · source · permalink

Your digital subscription includes access to content from all our websites in your region. Access unlimited content and the digital versions of our print editions - Today's Paper.

The first weekend of spring has brought with it higher fire danger ratings and a severe weather warning.

The Illawarra's fire danger rating has been forecast as high on Saturday, September 5, while Sydney's rating will be extreme with a total fire ban in the city in place.

The Bureau of Meteorology (BOM) has issued a severe weather warning for parts of the Illawarra, with strong winds averaging between 50 and 60 kmh and damaging wind gusts about 90 kmh.

It comes the day after a bushfire in Jamberoo served as a timely reminder for residents to prepare their properties as temperatures warm ahead of summer.

The one-hectare blaze began near Clover Hill Road in Jamberoo at 11.52am on Thursday, September 3.

Twenty-five Rural Fire Service (RFS) firefighters and five trucks responded to the scene and quickly brought the fire under control.

Later at 1.09pm, crews were called to investigate reports of smoke near Stanwell Tops, but the RFS found no active fire in the area.

On the same day, firefighters on the Far South Coast worked to bring a 22-hectare bushfire at Rocky Hall, near Bombala, under control.

RFS spokesperson Greg Allan said the fires were a reminder "to do what you can to prepare your property before fire comes".

"There are simple things you can do around the home, like cleaning your gutters, trimming any overhanging trees and branches, mowing your lawn, removing any combustible materials around your home, whether that's furniture or other materials like wood and mulch and things like that," he said.

"Most importantly, is having a bushfire survival plan. So whether it's you and your flatmates or your family, having that discussion about what you'll do ahead of a fire if it were to come.

"If you're going to stay and defend your property, are you prepared to do so, or are you going to leave, and at what point will you leave, what are you taking, where are you going.

"If you can have a discussion about that before an actual fire, you're going to be in a much better position."

Mr Allan said the public could expect fires this season due to the amount of rain and flooding the region has seen in the last few years.

"There has been a lot of vegetation growth, which when it does dry out or cure off, you know, it is going to be quite flammable," he said.

"So many areas of the state need to be prepared.

"No matter where you are, conditions can change at any time, so it's really important to prepare your property."
