  2. Market 'volatility' or 'fundamental rethink' on AI? Experts weigh in after US stock market reopenspublished at 15:30 GMT
    15:30 GMT

    It is not unusual to see "a lot of volatility" in these types of events, says strategist at investment management firm AlphaSimplex Katy Kaminski after tech stocks edged back up when trading reopened in the US.

    It came after Thursday's major sell-off of shares following concerns of an AI slowdown after the Democrats' midterms win.

    "People tend to sway [on] the extremes until they kind of figure out what this really means," she tells BBC News.

    On the AI industry more generally, Kaminski says it's has been "under some investigation" over the past few months, and rapid shifts in politics can also cause "an even larger shifts in terms of where the valuations are going to be".

    "Investors aren't just on the AI train now. They're really trying to determine which of these companies are actually going to be profitable, which are priced at a value that can actually deliver in the future," she says.

    But Russ Shaw, founder of Tech London Advocates, tells BBC News that he thinks there is a fundamental rethink around AI that "surfaced in a very extreme way" after the US midterm elections.

    "The big tech players have to do a much better job to make the case for AI because it's become a significant political liability," he says.

    With tech stocks dropping down yesterday, big tech’s big bet on data centres is under the spotlight.

    Many governments have agreed, some taking action to speed up planning and construction of data centres dedicated to AI workloads.

    It shows just how much work tech giants may need to do to - and money they'll need to spend - to convince those daunted by the prospect of a data centre on their doorstep of its benefits.

    Virginia has the most existing and proposed data centres with 710, followed by Texas with 612.

  13. US government borrowing costs fall amid stock market sell-offpublished at 13:42 GMT
    13:42 GMT

    Anuk Weerawardana

    US government borrowing costs have fallen since Wednesday’s midterm results, with analysts suggesting investors are looking to put their money in lower risk assets such as bonds.

    The yield on a 10-year US Treasury bond – effectively the interest rate charged on a 10-year loan to the US government – has fallen from 4.95% to about 4.85%.

    High levels of government debt are worrying for bond investors, but the midterm results suggest the US's deficit "won't spiral beyond what was already expected", according to Coatsworth.

    Other countries have also seen borrowing costs fall.

    The yield on 10-year UK government bonds has slipped from around 5.26% on Wednesday to 5.16% on Friday - which means it’s slightly cheaper for the UK government to borrow money too.

  14. This sell-off 'does not cancel Christmas', says tech investorpublished at 13:34 GMT
    13:34 GMT

    Jonathan Josephs
    Business reporter

    If this is a major sell-off, why are US futures pointing up?

    They are suggesting that when the New York stock markets open at 1430 GMT the major indices will have recovered some of Thursday’s losses.

    Well, for some investors the dip in prices - especially for tech stocks - is a golden buying opportunity.

    They see the building of AI data centres as something of a runaway train that can be slowed but not stopped.

    The prominent Wall Street tech investor Dan Ives says the reality is that the amount of US data centre capacity is set to double by the end of next year.

    Roughly a third of that is in Texas, and any data centres that already have permits and are under construction won’t be stopped.

    “Demand is three to four times the capacity at this rate of the build over the next 3 years”, he explains.

    He also points out that even if 10% of the 800 – 1,000 data centres that are currently planned in the US no longer get built, those that do exist will be able to charge more for their use, increasing their profitability.

    Overall he says this setback “does not cancel Christmas and the AI revolution will continue to happen, just at a slower rate”.
