Typically, when chit-chatting with your parents in the kitchen, a variety of topics are on the menu. You could be seeking sage advice about the problems in your life, gossiping about the drama among your family friends, and everything in between. What you're probably not doing is putting your heads together to solve a double homicide case.

But that's exactly why Line of Fire is far from your typical family drama. The new NBC series, premiering tonight at 10:00 p.m. ET/PT, centers on the Hollingsworths, a clan of agents from various branches of law enforcement who find themselves tangled up in a case that escalates into a full-blown conspiracy. Patriarch Mike (Peter Krause) and son Micah (Tommy O'Brien) both work for the Secret Service, while mother Jane (Hope Davis) and daughter Claire (Kat Cunning) put in time with the U.S. Marshals and FBI, respectively. And, in an exclusive sneak peek of tonight's pilot, we see the Hollingsworth women doing their best multitasking, cracking a murder mystery while also whipping up dinner.

"Did I tell you a neighbor's nest cam caught an Uber delivering an envelope to Carlos's house the day he died?" Claire asks her mother as we dive right into the action. "Driver said he didn't look, but it felt like cash. My hunch is that the killer paid Carlos to lure Libby to that blind spot on the cliff, promised they'd get rid of her to keep his hiding place a secret, then killed them both."

"You still haven't offered why a deputy marshal would be targeted for a hit," Jane simply replies.

"Sandoval knows Libby's looking for him. Maybe she's getting close."

"But why would Libby be looking for Sandoval in the first place? We have a special task force on him. She could have asked to be on it. She didn't."

"Maybe she wanted brownie points for finding him herself," Claire reasons.

"Or maybe she wanted to find him for another reason," Jane responds.

The kitchen collusion is interrupted when Claire gets a call from the medical examiner. But it cannot be stated enough how incongruous this dialogue is with the actual visuals of the scene. Two characters are talking through possible motives for a government agent's murder, something we've seen hundreds of times across the history of procedurals. But the talk is going down around the kitchen island, as an apron-clad Jane is nonchalantly preparing a meal while reasoning through what could have happened. Even just through this 45-second clip, we get a sense of what Line of Fire can provide in mashing up the concept of a work/life balance: A brutal murder as literal dinner conversation.

Check out the sneak peek of the pilot episode above. Line of Fire premieres tonight, Sept. 21, at 10:00 p.m. ET/PT on NBC.
