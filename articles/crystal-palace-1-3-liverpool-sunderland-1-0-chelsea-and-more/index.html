John Brewin was at the Amex Stadium, where Brighton cantered to victory over 10-man Brentford:

Who can stop Brighton’s ascension in English and European football? They continue to set standards, practices that leave almost everybody else trailing in their wake. Their second-place position in the Premier League would once have read like a glitch in a Football Manager session.

Come November 2026, they appear the only team capable of throwing down a challenge to Arsenal. Under low winter sun, Brentford, the club Brighton are often compared to, though one with a different outlook, were readily swatted aside. If Keith Andrews’ team, and their fans at the Amex, might level blame at a couple of refereeing decisions, it was a contest they were given little chance of rescuing anything from.

Andy Brassell saw Sunderland inflict a fourth defeat of the league season on Chelsea, the most of any team in the top nine:

A lot can change in five-and-half months, but as an early winter chill descended on Wearside this week – both meteorologically and metaphorically – Sunderland were determined that some things should remain the same.

Just as they had beaten Chelsea on the final day of last season in May to ensure European qualification for the first time in 53 years, they did so again on Sunday to lift themselves off the bottom of the Premier League. The real triumph was that a patched-up defence, without the injured Dan Ballard and the banned Omar Alderete, kept Chelsea at enough distance to allow Robin Roefs a tranquil afternoon. Chelsea may be developing into a chaotic must-watch under Xabi Alonso but they were subdued here, and fall out of the top four.

David Hytner was at Selhurst Park to see Liverpool snaffle three goals and as many points:

It was the moment when Liverpool knew they would win here in south London and so move on from the Premier League loss to Arsenal at Anfield last Sunday and the one in midweek at Fenerbahce in the Champions League.

Florian Wirtz had timed his run as Liverpool broke up the right and Bradley Barcola’s low pull back was made to measure. Wirtz had to take it on first time and it was a test of his technique under pressure. He never looked like failing it, not least because smoothness in this area is his calling card. Liverpool were 2-0 up, Alexis Mac Allister having headed them in front towards the end of the first-half, and all you could hear was the eruption of delight in the away supporters’ enclosure.

GOAL! Crystal Palace 0-3 Liverpool (Isak, 89 mins)

Now Isak has his goal! Another break in which he combines with Munoz: an excellent pass plays the Spaniard down the right, a low ball infield finds Wirtz and he touches to Isak, who takes his chance expertly!

Chance for Isak, but Henderson saves! Liverpool break from their own penalty area: Wirtz hoofs the ball clear, Isak and Munoz exchange passes and the Swede carries it to the edge of the area before shooting low, hard, and too close to the keeper.

Munoz tees up Wirtz for another first-time shot, but this one’s too close to Henderson. It hasn’t been an overwhelming performance from Liverpool by any means, but Palace look a beaten side now (cue inevitable comeback).

Munoz throws himself over Lerma’s foot and is rightly booked for diving. Mac Allister confronts Lerma in the aftermath, and both players also end up with yellow cards.

Rio Ngumoha, a 76th-minute substitute, takes an extraordinarily optimistic shot, from maybe three yards short of the byline and 15 yards wide of goal. It had almost no chance of going in even if it was on target, which it very emphatically was not.

Brilliant chance for Crystal Palace! A defensive slip allows Richards to run through on the right and square to Pino, but it’s a bit behind him and he’s forced to take a touch, and by the time he shoots Alisson has raced off his line and the shot hits the keeper!

GOAL! Crystal Palace 0-2 Liverpool (Wirtz, 54 mins)

Liverpool double their lead! Barcola is played down the right and sends in a nice low cross, possibly aiming for Isak, but it runs to Wirtz and he strokes it into the corner!

Two players run clear for Crystal Palace, and Wharton just has to slide the ball across for Strand Larsen to turn it in! But his pass is abysmal, Strand Larsen can’t reach it, and he’ll be grateful in the circumstances that he was about 10 yards offside when the ball was played through.
