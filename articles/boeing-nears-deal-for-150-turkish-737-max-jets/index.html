Boeing nears deal for 150 Turkish 737 MAX jets

TheTools · 16.09.2026 04:12 · source · permalink

Boeing is close to finalizing a long-delayed order for 150 737 MAX jets with Turkish Airlines, after engine maintenance disputes stalled progress. The agreement, potentially signed next week, supports Istanbul-based expansion plans. CFM International’s LEAP-1B engine terms remain under negotiation.

By InsideFlyer

Sep 16 · 6:12 AM · 1 min

The 150-aircraft commitment, part of a broader 2025 deal including up to 75 787 Dreamliners, faced delays due to disagreements over CFM engine repair costs and maintenance access. Turkish Airlines sought premier-level facility status with CFM to manage future engine servicing in-house, reducing long-term financial exposure. Boeing has not confirmed final terms but is working toward a firm contract.

This resolution would preserve one of Boeing’s largest recent narrowbody orders and align with Turkish Airlines’ goal to reach 800 aircraft by 2033. The carrier already operates the 737 MAX 9 and is evaluating other models like the A220 and 777X. Finalizing engine support terms remains critical to locking in the order and securing fleet economics for decades.

Travelers may see expanded routes from Istanbul as Turkish Airlines grows its MAX fleet. Delays risked shifting the order to Airbus, which could alter regional capacity and pricing dynamics for years to come.
