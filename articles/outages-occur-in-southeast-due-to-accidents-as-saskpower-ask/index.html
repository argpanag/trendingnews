Outages occur in southeast due to accidents as SaskPower asks for safety

TheTools · 21.10.2026 19:13 · source · permalink

Southeast Saskatchewan has been dealing with a series of outages as SaskPower has been sending crews out to deal with ongoing issues.

Outages reported

While wind has been picking up from some systems over the past week, Heather Johnson, a spokesperson from SaskPower, explains that the causes were more human caused recently.

"We had a look at sort of what was going on in the southeast over the last few days. We had a couple that were caused by accidents, so that could be accidents running into power infrastructure, lines coming down, that sort of thing. We did have a broken cross arm, which is a piece of equipment on a pole that caused another outage. So there really isn't one particular cause, just sort of a scattering of our usual causes for outages."

While we've got an upcoming dangerous season with winter around the corner, Johnson says that fall doesn't pose any unique challenges for power lines.

"Generally, in fall and throughout all the seasons, the majority of outages, there's not really seasonally specific, except when snow is involved. The majority of our outages are weather related, so things like high winds, fallen trees, branches or debris coming onto lines or equipment, that sort of thing."

Responding to a knocked-down line

If anyone comes into contact with a power pole or sees a line on the ground, Johnson details what people should do.

"The one thing and probably the most important thing to remember is always assume that the line is energized, even if it doesn't look like it, even if you're not seeing sparks or hearing any sounds, always assume that that line is live. We would ask you to stay back at least 10 meters, that's about the length of a bus essentially, so stay well back and keep others away obviously as well."

Johnson says people should either call SaskPower's outage centre at 310-2220 or 911 in the case of a power line emergency.
