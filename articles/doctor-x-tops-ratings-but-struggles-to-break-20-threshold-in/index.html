Doctor X tops ratings but struggles to break 20% threshold in Korea

TheTools · 07.11.2026 00:50 · source · permalink

5 weeks of dominant No. 1 remained intact, but the so-called dream threshold of 20% is still high.

Kim Ji-won and Lee Jung-eun attracted attention with the surprising teacher-student cooperative ending of SBS Friday-Saturday drama 'Doctor X: Age of the White Mafia' (hereafter 'Doctor X'), and amid various controversies the show's rising viewership trend has hit the brakes.

In episode 9 of 'Doctor X,' which aired on the 6th, the desperate cooperation between Gye Su-jeong (played by Kim Ji-won), who plunged into an extremely difficult surgery to save 8-year-old pediatric cancer patient Lee Hyun-ah (played by Lee Chae-yu), who was at the brink of life and death due to a rare tumor, and Jang Hee-sook (played by Lee Jung-eun), who made a surprise appearance in the operating room and revealed her identity as a mentor, was portrayed.

The viewership numbers themselves are superficially strong. According to Nielsen Korea, that broadcast recorded a peak of 16.6% and 15.3% nationwide and in the metropolitan area respectively, securing first place in the same time slot as well as the No. 1 position across all channels and programs for five consecutive weeks. The 2049 rating also held the throne at a peak of 5.86%.

However, looking beneath the surface the situation is somewhat different. The sharp box-office momentum that had surged to 19.5% in episode 6, bringing it close to the '20% breakthrough,' appears to have stagnated. In particular, contrary to the typical weekend-drama pattern where Saturday viewership usually rises significantly compared with Friday, a rare reverse trend has been detected recently in which Saturday broadcasts fell compared with the previous day.

Analysts say the background to this failed rebound lies in the continuous external pressures and noise that persisted from the beginning of the broadcast. After current medical doctors requested a review by the Korea Communications Standards Commission, saying "it simplifies medical sites into incompetent villains and ignores the uncertainty of medical practices," strong backlash from the medical community continued, and the show experienced unusually large pains both inside and outside the work, such as viewers' divided reactions over an episode dealing with the May 18 Democratic Uprising and the appearance of a "petition to stop broadcasting" on the National Assembly's national petition board.

Still, lead actress Kim Ji-won is carrying the work with such outstanding acting that she is even being mentioned as a strong candidate for year-end acting awards, and the ultimate card Lee Jung-eun is also active, so it is worth watching whether it will rebound.
