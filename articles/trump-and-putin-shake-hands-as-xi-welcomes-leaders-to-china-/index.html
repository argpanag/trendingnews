    Anthony Zurcher
    North America correspondent

    This is the kind of high-profile diplomatic event Donald Trump just loves. The American president, standing alongside the leaders of China and Russia, to tackle issues of global importance.

    Although Thursday's trilateral meeting was reportedly the idea of Russian President Vladimir Putin, Trump - whose political worldview was formed during the high-stakes drama of the Cold War – has seemed to be an enthusiastic participant.

    The growing power of artificial intelligence, which could be the more pressing, perhaps even existential, threat to global security and economic prosperity isn’t likely to come up here or in separate bilateral talks with Chinese leader Xi Jinping in any substantive way.

  5. Trump accompanied by key allies with 'different views' on Chinapublished at 12:17 GMT
    12:17 GMT

    James FitzGerald
    North America reporter

    This visit to China by US President Donald Trump comes as his top team wrestle with how to handle China in the wake of a recent alleged spying scandal that has engulfed American tech firm Nvidia.

    It has unfolded against the backdrop of a fierce AI race - in which the US and China are the world's top two competitors. Nvidia is central to Trump's mission to dominate in the field.

    The US administration has taken relatively tough steps over the issue in recent days, indicating that the figures who take a hawkish stance to China are proving influential.

    For one, the US has confirmed it is investigating a Singapore-based company which is suspected of breaking US restrictions by helping Nvidia's chips to China.

    The Trump administration also renewed its efforts to further curb China's access to advanced semiconductors - which are also critical for the AI industry.

  7. Criticism over US sanctions unites China and Russiapublished at 12:09 GMT
    12:09 GMT

    Jack Lau
    Global China Unit, reporting from Hong Kong

    President Xi Jinping may have given President Donald Trump a rare personal welcome at Shenzhen airport, but he joins President Vladimir Putin in sharply criticising US economic sanctions and restrictions.

    At the Apec CEO summit this morning, Xi called for "free and open trade", while Putin touted Russia's resilience under sanctions.

    Russia's military, technology and industrial sectors have been targeted over Moscow's war in Ukraine. China, meanwhile, faces sanctions and restrictions linked to human rights, advanced technology and military development.

    Xi has repeatedly criticised sanctions from Washington DC, here at Apec and elsewhere. Beijing says it does not recognise unilateral sanctions imposed outside the UN Security Council, where China and Russia both hold veto powers.

    Dr Wan Qingsong, a Russia expert in Shanghai, says both countries are facing growing pressures as supply chains fragment and governments move to protect domestic industries, particularly hi-tech sectors.

    "For China and Russia, working more closely together is not only about mutual economic benefit - it is also a way to guard against global economic risks," says Wan, also the deputy director of East China Normal University's Russia Studies Centre.

  16. Historic Xi-Trump-Putin meeting reportedly to take place at a villapublished at 10:12 GMT
    10:12 GMT

    BBC Chinese

    The trilateral meeting between China, the US and Russia is reportedly set to take place at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Thursday, with security significantly tightened ahead of the talks.

    The state-owned compound, which opened in 1997, has long hosted Chinese leaders and high-profile foreign visitors to the city.

    Beijing’s decision to opt for a more private setting rather than a formal conference venue suggests the meeting is “more of a sideline rather than a separate, significant summit on its own”, says Minseon Ku of DePaul University, a specialist in summit diplomacy.

    “Xi would want that, since, as the host of Apec, China would want the Apec summit to be the primary event and would not want the trilateral meeting to overshadow it,” she tells the BBC.

    "The setting carries less diplomatic formality than an official venue in Beijing, framing the roughly one-hour meeting as a working-level engagement. The lower the ceremonial profile, the lower the political cost if the meeting produces no tangible outcomes," he says.

  18. Taiwan demands participation on 'equal basis'published at 09:40 GMT
    09:40 GMT

    Benny Lu
    BBC Chinese

    Apec is one of the few international bodies of which Taiwan is a formal member – the self-governed island is in fact a founding member – and its attendance at the leaders' summit usually goes fairly smoothly.

    This year's gathering in China, however, has introduced complications because of tensions with Beijing.

    China says it welcomes Taiwan's participation under the "one-China principle", which asserts that Taiwan is not an independent territory but a part of China. But Taipei has rejected this, with President William Lai saying last week that Taiwan's participation should be regarded on an "equal basis".

    "Apec is different because Taiwan already enjoys formal membership," Felix Brender, a postdoctoral fellow in Taiwan studies at Harvard, says, arguing that it'll be hard for Beijing to challenge Taipei's participation – unlike in the cases of the UN and the World Health Organisation, where Taiwan is not officially recognised.

Quoted by Chinese state-run news agency Xinhua, President Xi has voiced his desire for a "constructive, strategically stable relationship" with Washington.

Several hours before Trump's arrival, Russian leader President Putin landed in Shenzhen, with Putin and Xi holding talks, and the Russian leader saying the foreign policy strategy of the two countries was working.

Trump in Manila

Trump's Shenzhen arrival follows a 21-hour stay in Manila for the Asean Summit, where he met India's Narendra Modi, Japan's Sanae Takaichi and Australia's Anthony Albanese.

While in the Philippines, Trump signed trade agreements with Thailand and the Philippines - but both are light on details and specific commitments.

At a dinner with South East Asia leaders, the US president said: "This is one of the world's most vibrant and productive regions filled with the tremendous potential."

Filipino President Ferdinand Marcos Jr hosted Trump at the Malacañang presidential palace and sent Jollibee fried chicken to the US president's party as a midnight snack.

What's to come?

Trilateral talks between Moscow, Beijing and Washington will be Thursday's focus. Issues relating to global peace will be high on the agenda, according to China's foreign ministry.

BBC Chinese

The trilateral meeting between China, the US and Russia is reportedly set to take place at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen on Thursday, with security significantly tightened ahead of the talks.

The state-owned compound, which opened in 1997, has long hosted Chinese leaders and high-profile foreign visitors to the city.

Beijing’s decision to opt for a more private setting rather than a formal conference venue suggests the meeting is “more of a sideline rather than a separate, significant summit on its own”, says Minseon Ku of DePaul University, a specialist in summit diplomacy.

“Xi would want that, since, as the host of Apec, China would want the Apec summit to be the primary event and would not want the trilateral meeting to overshadow it,” she tells the BBC.

Professor Shaoyu Yuan of New York University, who studies US-China diplomacy, notes that Kylin Villa is relatively easy to secure. Its proximity to the airport would also allow Trump to depart directly after the meeting.

"The setting carries less diplomatic formality than an official venue in Beijing, framing the roughly one-hour meeting as a working-level engagement. The lower the ceremonial profile, the lower the political cost if the meeting produces no tangible outcomes," he says.

Taiwan demands participation on 'equal basis'published at 09:40 GMT
09:40 GMT

Benny Lu
BBC Chinese

Apec is one of the few international bodies of which Taiwan is a formal member – the self-governed island is in fact a founding member – and its attendance at the leaders' summit usually goes fairly smoothly.

This year's gathering in China, however, has introduced complications because of tensions with Beijing.

China says it welcomes Taiwan's participation under the "one-China principle", which asserts that Taiwan is not an independent territory but a part of China. But Taipei has rejected this, with President William Lai saying last week that Taiwan's participation should be regarded on an "equal basis".

"Apec is different because Taiwan already enjoys formal membership," Felix Brender, a postdoctoral fellow in Taiwan studies at Harvard, says, arguing that it'll be hard for Beijing to challenge Taipei's participation – unlike in the cases of the UN and the World Health Organisation, where Taiwan is not officially recognised.

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi also arrived in Shenzhen a little earlier.

This is Takaichi's first official visit to China since taking office in October 2025 - and comes as relations between the two countries have fallen to the lowest point in over a decade.

A row erupted between Tokyo and Beijing in November last year, when Takaichi appeared to suggest that Japan could activate its self-defence force in the event of an attack on Taiwan, a self-governed island which China claims as its own.

This set off a war of words, then led to trade and export restrictions.

Analysts believe formal talks between Takaichi and Xi on the sidelines of Apec are unlikely, although they may have a brief exchange such as a handshake.

This is no ordinary welcomepublished at 09:24 GMT
09:24 GMT

Laura Bicker
Reporting from Shenzhen

This isn’t simply an airport greeting by Chinese President Xi Jinping. This is a carefully choreographed diplomatic moment.

Xi understands the value of this gesture, as it is thought to be the first time that he has greeted a foreign leader at the airport as President.

Firstly, he is mirroring Trump’s own actions.

The US president was there to meet Xi at the airport in Washington earlier this year - and now the Chinese leader is returning the compliment.

But Xi will also be aware that Trump places considerable importance on personal recognition, ceremony and status.

By not delegating this task, the Chinese president is flattering his US counterpart and making him feel important by singling him out for the honour of a personal greeting.

Xi may hope that this sign of good will smooth the path for negotiations over trade, Taiwan and technology.

He can also afford to be gracious here without losing face. China is already controlling the setting and arrangement for the unprecedented summit between Trump, Xi and Putin.
