Ade Adepitan's parents threw out his wheelchair after being left furious

TheTools · 16.11.2026 17:30 · source · permalink

I'm A Celebrity contestant Ade Adepitan once saw his wheelchair thrown out by his parents before defying his disability by enjoying sporting glory and TV fame. The Nigerian-born star, 53, contracted polio at 15 months old, losing the use of his left leg.

At three, he moved to London with his parents, relying on callipers to walk, which were painful and slow. Therefore, he was delighted to be gifted a £700 wheelchair by Frank Bruno's boxing academy as a teenager.

While Adepitan saw the wheels as a way to enhance his life and gain more independence, his parents were against it, with his furious father even throwing the chair out of the house. He later sneaked the wheelchair back in, hiding it under a tarpaulin in the garden.

Adepitan told the Guardian in 2013: "My parents thought me getting into a wheelchair was like me giving up on walking. And they were worried about the stigma. People in wheelchairs out and about was a completely new thing to most people."

But the wheelchair did change his life for the better. After joining the Newham Rollers wheelchair basketball team, he never looked back, playing professionally in Spain and representing Great Britain.

Despite being cut from the national squad seven times, Adepitan persevered to win Paralympic bronze at Athens 2004 and gold at the 2005 Paralympic World Cup, earning an MBE for services to disability sport.

Amid his sporting success, he successfully transitioned into media work, becoming one of the UK's first physically disabled mainstream presenters. He has anchored major Paralympic broadcasts alongside Clare Balding and fronted various BBC and Channel 4 documentaries.

He has now become the first wheelchair user to enter the I'm A Celebrity jungle. Producers have specially adapted the Australian camp - including providing an electric wheelchair for rugged terrain - for him.

Adepitan intends to embrace the challenge of shifting public perceptions of disability, declaring that living outside his comfort zone every day has prepared him for the jungle.

He said: "Words like honour and privilege are ones I feel, but I don’t think they sum it up enough. For me, this is a moment in time. Even if I go out in the first week, I will always be remembered as the person in a wheelchair. I am slightly overwhelmed.

"It's never been done before, and it never crossed my mind I would be the first. It's the biggest show on TV and this is a huge opportunity. How often do you get to spend three weeks in the jungle doing this crazy stuff?"

The cast line-up includes former footballer Wayne Rooney, actress Joanna Page, model Leomie Anderson, Kaiser Chiefs frontman Ricky Wilson, YouTuber and content creator Sharky, TV presenter AJ Odudu, comedian Jack Skipper and broadcaster Mariella Frostrup.
