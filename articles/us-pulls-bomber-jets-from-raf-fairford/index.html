US pulls bomber jets from RAF Fairford

TheTools · 04.10.2026 22:56 · source · permalink

Pentagon confirms all B-1 aircraft have returned to home stations after fresh intelligence about an Iran-backed plot

The US has withdrawn all of its bomber jets from RAF Fairford after air force commanders received new reports of an Iran-backed plot against the base.

The Pentagon said all B-1 bombers deployed to the base, which US forces have used to strike Iran, have returned to the US.

Counter-terror police are investigating whether Iran was behind the plot. Five British nationals and a UK-Iranian national have been arrested on suspicion of suspected terrorism and explosive offences over the incident. All of those who were arrested have since been bailed.

The unusual and quick withdrawal followed new threats received by the US military regarding an Iran-backed plot to target the facility.

The need to move the bombers was so urgent that some of the planes were ordered into the air while refuelling aircraft were still being scrambled, the New York Times reported, citing two US officials.

A Pentagon official told The Telegraph: “While operational security precluded us from confirming the movement of our assets and forces in real-time, we can acknowledge now that all US bombers that were deployed to RAF Fairford have re-deployed to their home stations in the United States.”

They added that the bombers remain ready “to deliver precision global strike capabilities” from the continental United States.

US officials told the Wall Street Journal the decision was in response to security concerns over suspected terror plots against the facility.

Last week police said they found a quantity of petrol in three vans linked to the five suspects, but no explosives.

Police received a call about three suspicious vehicles that appeared to be travelling towards RAF Fairford in the early hours of Sept 27.

They described the plot as complex and said they were considering whether a foreign state had been involved.

The Ministry of Defence has been approached for comment.

On Wednesday, Andy Burnham, the Prime Minister, said there were “strong indications that Iran played a part” in the suspected plot.

Donald Trump has said the suspects were looking to do “big damage” at the air base and that they had been under investigation by British and US authorities. Marco Rubio, the US secretary of state, ⁠also ⁠pointed to the “hands of a foreign actor” in the incident.

The US president has been critical of the decision to release the suspects. In response, Mr Burnham insisted that the UK was working closely with the US and that the situation was under “the strongest grip”.

Iran has said accusations of its involvement were baseless. On Thursday, Tehran summoned the British ambassador in protest over the allegations.

RAF Fairford, located on the border of Gloucestershire and Wiltshire, regularly hosts deployments of long-range US aircraft, including B-1s, B-52 bombers and U-2 reconnaissance aircraft. It also houses hundreds of US military staff.

Since March 1, US heavy bombers have taken off from the base “for defensive missions to destroy Iran’s missile capabilities”, according to the US military.

In July, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps warned that any British base used to strike Tehran was a “legitimate target”.

The Telegraph revealed last week that Israeli intelligence had warned MI5 about an imminent Iranian-backed attack on the air base shortly before Ed Miliband, the Foreign Secretary, announced plans to ban imports of goods from Israeli settlements in the West Bank.
