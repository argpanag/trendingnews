What’s on TV tonight: The Great British Bake Off: The Final, and more

TheTools · 24.11.2026 17:13 · source · permalink

Your complete TV guide to the week’s best shows, across terrestrial and digital platforms

The Great British Bake Off: The Final
Channel 4, 8pm
Even the most delicious of cakes can go stale. After 17 series, The Great British Bake Off does not taste quite as fresh as it used to, with many of its tropes and formats beginning to turn. The appointment of vivacious new judge Nigella Lawson, however, replacing the retiring Prue Leith, has given the show a few much-needed sprinkles. Indeed, tonight’s finale tops off one of Bake Off’s best series in years. The three finalists – Mo, Moyin, and Tom – must tackle a citrus celebration cake for their signature challenge and crème brûlée doughnuts for the technical round. The final showstopper, meanwhile, tasks the bakers with crafting spectacular tributes to iconic British landscapes.

Prue Leith may have departed the tent, but she has not left our screens. Following tonight’s finale, she joins fellow Bake Off alumna Sandi Toksvig for Prue & Sandi’s South African Adventure (9.15pm). The spirited six-part travelogue follows the pair as they tour Leith’s homeland, taking in its natural beauty and learning about its ugly apartheid past. In Cape Town tonight, Leith reflects on how her “privileged liberal family” were against the country’s racist policies but ultimately “accepted it”.

Rob Lowe: The Outsider
Netflix
Just for the record, clarifies Gwyneth Paltrow, “I never slept with Rob Lowe… because he has slept with everyone.” Along with Hollywood stars like Demi Moore, Paltrow contributes to this reflective profile of The West Wing actor, who rose to fame as a teen idol during the 1980s.

Stacey & Joe: In Too Deep
BBC One, 8pm
Fame makes life easier in some ways and harder in others. Consider a bemused Stacey Solomon and a furious Joe Swash reading made-up newspaper stories about them going through “a tough patch”. To her credit, Solomon stops Swash from hitting back and feeding the fire.

Inside the Music
Sky Arts, 8pm
Mahler’s Fifth Symphony is “a life journey through despair, darkness to utter joy”, says Edward Gardner, conductor of the London Philharmonic Orchestra. Tonight, its musicians wax lyrical on the work of the Austro-Bohemian composer, whose pieces, according to one, “shower [the stage] with glitter”.

MasterChef: The Professionals
BBC One, 9pm
The fourth and final week of heats begins with six new chefs taking on the dreaded skills test. Tonight, they must fillet a sea bream and use it to create a shellfish risotto. Sharp knife skills are essential, along with the finesse necessary to balance the delicate seafood flavours.

Accidentally Famous: The Story of 883
Sky Atlantic, 9pm & 10.05pm
Pop duo 883 are a household name in Italy, thanks to a string of popular hits in the early 1990s. The first series of this playful Italian-language biopic charted their unlikely rise. It returns tonight with a two-part premiere in which newfound fame begins to test their friendship. SK

Wednesday 25 November

Blade Runner 2099
Amazon Prime Video
A closer cousin to Ridley Scott’s original than Denis Villeneuve’s 2049-set sequel in tone and style, Silka Luisa’s eight-part sci-fi epic is awash with synths and saxes, emotional chill, narrative opacity and a production design that packs quite a punch. Eighty years after Harrison Ford’s Rick Deckard (of whom there is no mention here) was a “blade runner” hunting and “retiring” rogue replicants created by the all-powerful Tyrell Corporation, the tables have turned: following an uprising in Los Angeles, the replicants reign while the humans are the underclass.

Following a brief preamble in which Freddie Fox’s slick Tyrell exec and Michael Smiley’s sceptical factory owner (just two of many faces familiar from British television) set the scene, we join Hunter Schafer’s Cora, a human dockworker and member of the human resistance who, running out of luck, is sent undercover as a replicant blade runner, partnering Michelle Yeoh’s serpentine, near-obsolete replicant Olwen. Their target is a human runaway harbouring a big secret and the journey is a familiar, pleasing blend of the existential and the earthy, with plenty of pulsating chase sequences and fights. Newcomers may be baffled, but for fans this is an aesthetic and cerebral treat.

Rivals
Disney+
It’s all looking rather like Tony Baddingham (David Tennant) has won the day, having secured the franchise bid for Corinium. But of course nothing is certain in this hair-sprayed world of one-upmanship and extra-marital affairs. Meanwhile, Lizzie’s (Katherine Parkinson) terrible husband continues to try and win her back, and Rupert (Alex Hassell) attempts to fight his feelings for Taggie (Bella Maclean) by escaping to Argentina.

The Buffalo Miracle
HBO Max
Two-time Oscar winner Ben Proudfoot’s startling documentary tells the true story of a McDonald’s that stayed open to offer shelter and provisions to locals caught out in a devastating blizzard that swept into Buffalo, New York over Christmas in 2022.

Celebrity Race Across the World
BBC One, 8pm
At the crucial moment, Sam and Kaz slip up, Princess and Junior’s progress is halted by dwindling funds, Jeff and Matt get stuck in the back of beyond, and Ella has a moment of introspection with Nana. One episode remains and everything is up for grabs.

Murder Case: Fire in the Suburbs
BBC Two, 9pm
The climax of this two-part series is in effect a tribute to one woman’s perseverance and courage: Bex Williams overcomes serious and emotional injury and investigation dead ends to bring the killers of her boyfriend to justice when an essential piece of evidence surfaces.

Hunting Alice Bell
Channel 4, 9pm
The Not Alice Bell women are off to the Best of Britain awards when disaster strikes and their uneasy coalition fractures under the weight of mutual suspicion and intense external pressure in the wake of the attack on Simon Pegg’s revolting Jason Nash. The tricky balance of thrills and social comment looks equally uncertain, but this twisty mystery is a compelling yarn nonetheless. Concluding tomorrow. GT

Thursday 26 November

Sweetpea
Sky Atlantic, 9pm
A welcome return for the darkly funny comedy drama starring Ella Purnell as Rhiannon, an overlooked young woman who finds a voice through acting out her most violent thoughts. It’s been two long years since the shocking, edge-of-seat cliffhanger left us slack-jawed at the close of series one in November 2024, eager to know the fate of our murderous anti-heroine. And the good news is, the wait was worth it.

Without giving too much away, let’s just say that while things really do pick up exactly where they left off, Rhiannon quickly finds a way to return to work at the Gazette. And she’s soon enjoying a new lease of life in her dream job as a reporter when a sensation-loving new broom, Freya (Taj Atwell), arrives to shake things up in the male-dominated newsroom. Meanwhile, Rhiannon’s nemesis, Detective Marina Farrar (Leah Harvey), remains tied to her desk and forced to undertake an anger management course, but just as delightfully obsessed as ever. It’s a brilliant comeback, with as much wit, energy and even more plot twists than before, plus a great line-up of fresh talent – including Atwell, Jenny Walser and Tamsin Greig – boosting the cast.

The Slightest Touch
HBO Max
This feature-length documentary tells the affecting story of Emma Fogarty, born with a rare, severely life-limiting condition that causes her skin to tear at the slightest touch, and how she persuaded her friend, film star Colin Farrell, to celebrate her 40th birthday by running the Dublin marathon – with him pushing her wheelchair the final four kilometres.

Tulisa: the Reckoning
Paramount+ 
The N-Dubz singer, former X Factor judge and I’m a Celebrity contestant presents a three-part series on her own bruising experience of fame. There’s a particular focus on the notorious “Fake Sheikh” tabloid sting that derailed her TV career, and her subsequent campaign for press reform and media regulation.

Recommended

Shetland
BBC One, 9pm; all episodes are already on iPlayer
With the mystery surrounding Linden’s death deepening, Calder (Ashley Jensen) and Tosh (Alison O’Donnell) get wind of a new suspect. And things take a dangerous turn for Emily (Christine Bottomley) when she warns her cousin to stay away.

Why Sinkholes Collapse
BBC Two, 9pm
What do residents of Bangkok, Paris, Turkey, Ripon in North Yorkshire and Godstone, Surrey have in common? They’ve all experienced the terror of the ground opening suddenly beneath their streets and swallowing everything above. This deep-dive, well-illustrated documentary reveals all you could ever want to know about the phenomenon.

Cunk on Cinema
BBC Two, 10pm
Too many of her interviewees look like they’re in on the joke these days for it to work as well as it used to. Even so, the concluding episode of Cunk’s (Diane Morgan) boneheaded history of film has plenty of laughs as she heads to Hollywood to survey the modern era and share her thoughts on the future of cinema as we know it… or as we don’t. Friday 27 November

Alan Carr’s Great Big Bucket List
Amazon Prime Video
Alan Carr is going to die one day, and before he does, he wants to tick a few things off his bucket list. Regrets, he may have a few. In the first episode of this charming four-part romp, Carr teams up with pal Mel Giedroyc (call sign ‘Cougar’) to achieve his apparent lifelong dream of flying a plane. An experience that leaves him looking like he’s been put through a tumble dryer. Elsewhere, he is so terrified by the idea of doing a tandem bungee jump with Irish comedian Joanne McNally that he chickens out completely. “I’ve got a medical condition,” he says, defending his traitorous behaviour, “I’m gay!”

Recommended

The best bucket list ticks are more modest. Mel Giedroyc has always wanted to walk into a pub and declare, “the drinks are on me!” £168 later, it is ticked. Top of the list for bone-dry comic Joe Wilkinson is a photo with a Highland cow. Moo. Tick. While Carr has always wanted to object at a wedding. “This marriage is a sham,” he shouts in the middle of a ceremony, disguised as a bearded man named Reg. “Claire, I love you! Do you remember our night of passion behind the bins?”

Gardeners’ World: Winter Specials
BBC Two, 8pm
As winter settles in, the appropriately named Adam Frost shares practical tips to keep gardens looking their best through the colder months, from planting shrubs to laying a brick path. Elsewhere, Rachel de Thame shares her secrets for spectacular dahlias. Soothing wildlife check-up Naturewatch precedes at 7.30pm.

The Dog House at Christmas
Channel 4, 8pm
It is Christmas at Woodgreen Pets Charity – where dogs are matched with new owners – and Santa Paws is coming to town. Tonight he delivers Freddy, an 11-year-old Staffy, to couple Tina and Charli, while poorly lurcher Butterscotch is still seeking a new home. Time for a Christmas miracle?

Bill’s Included
BBC One, 9.35pm
The Uncle Bryn-esque Bill (Rob Brydon) spends much of this week’s knockabout episode going to extreme lengths to earn himself a nickname from his cool student lodgers: including putting too much jam on his toast and deliberately falling through a table full of champagne glasses.

Country Music Association Awards 2026
BBC Four, 10.30pm
Country music’s biggest night returns for its 60th edition, with Lainey Wilson hosting the glittering ceremony from Nashville. Country star Ella Langley, who leads the nominations, performs on a bill including Miranda Lambert and Cody Johnson. Plus there is a special tribute to Dolly Parton.

The Graham Norton Show
BBC One, 10.40pm
Slow Horses star Jack Lowden is on the sofa tonight to promote his turn as Mr Darcy in Netflix’s Pride and Prejudice. Will Graham Norton ask him about reportedly losing out on James Bond to Robert Pattinson? Joining Lowden are comedian Jack Whitehall and singer-songwriter Sienna Spiro. SK
