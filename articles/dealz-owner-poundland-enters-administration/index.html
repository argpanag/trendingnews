Dealz owner Poundland enters administration

TheTools · 25.11.2026 15:04 · source · permalink

Dealz parent company Poundland has entered administration, its American owner has announced, putting around 11,000 jobs and 600 stores at risk.

Poundland uses the brand name Dealz in Ireland, and employs 1,150 people across around 40 stores.

"Efforts to preserve as many jobs and stores as possible will continue, led by the administrators," Poundland Limited's owner Gordon Brothers said in a statement after the US investment firm appointed outside administrators, AlixPartners, to try and save parts of the business.

"This appointment follows an extensive three-month M&A process, which explored every avenue to secure a sale of the whole company.

"Commercial negotiations with interested parties for both the whole of the company and with established retailers interested in acquiring substantial portions of the business will now continue, led by the administrators, and conclude through the administration process."

The retailer will continue to trade from its stores as the administrators seek a sale of the retailer and its assets, such as stores.

A statement on the Dealz website said: "The company, including Dealz in the Republic of Ireland, will continue to trade in the near term and most stores will remain up until further notice."

The company said it would "no longer be providing refunds or exchanges in-store under our previous 28-day returns policy for items purchased prior to 26 November 2026.

"If you bought something before Poundland entered administration, you can make a claim for a refund under our previous policy, but because Poundland is in administration, your claim will be dealt with as an unsecured claim in the administration rather than being paid directly by Dealz."

The company added that from tomorrow it would "no longer offer refunds or exchanges for items purchased in-store if you simply change your mind" but said customers' statutory rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2022 remain unaffected.

"If your goods are faulty, not as described or otherwise do not meet the statutory requirements, you may still be entitled to a refund, repair or replacement. However, where your purchase was before the start of the administration, Dealz may not be able to honour such entitlement as a result of the administration and any loss you suffer as a result will be dealt with as an unsecured claim in the administration."

Meanwhile, the Department of Enterprise has said it has not received a collective redundancy notification from Dealz.

Such notifications are required in cases of thirty or more planned redundancies.

"The Department has not received a notification of proposed collective redundancies from Dealz," a spokesperson said.

"Any further queries should be directed to the company," they added.

Earlier this month, the group issued a second notice of intention (NOI) to appoint administrators, allowing it 10 working days extra to hold talks over a rescue deal without the risk of creditors forcing the business into administration.

However, the retailer has not been able to strike a deal over the period and is now at a heightened risk of collapse.

Poundland's ownership had received a number of offers, including from real estate firm Martin Property Group, investment firm Xyra Holdings and German investment firm Zachert Private Equity.

Gordon Brothers have faced mounting political pressure to protect the chain's future and MPs have accused it of deliberately cutting off funds and pushing the retailer to the brink of insolvency.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham had called for executives to work with the UK government to find a solution that saves shops and jobs.

Speaking today, he said that the retailer was "part of the fabric" of high streets across the country and "the news that it has gone into administration just weeks before Christmas will be deeply concerning" for workers and their families.
