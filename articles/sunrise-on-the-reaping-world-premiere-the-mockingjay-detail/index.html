Sunrise on the Reaping World Premiere: The Mockingjay Detail

TheTools · 17.11.2026 08:38 · source · permalink

Mckenna Grace’s Mockingjay wings stole The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping world premiere in NYC, attended by Jennifer Lawrence.

Did you catch it? Because every Hunger Games fan with a pulse did — and no, it wasn’t Jennifer Lawrence (though, yes, she was there). Jennifer Lawrence, Josh Hutcherson, Woody Harrelson and Elizabeth Banks reunited in New York City last night at the world premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping at Alice Tully Hall, joining the prequel’s new stars — Joseph Zada, Mckenna Grace, Elle Fanning, Ralph Fiennes, Jesse Plemons, Glenn Close and Whitney Peak — plus The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes alums Tom Blyth and Rachel Zegler. Directed by Francis Lawrence and adapted from Suzanne Collins’ best-selling novel, the Lionsgate film opens in theaters November 20, 2026.

Three generations of Panem. One (purple) carpet. I’ve covered more premieres than I’d care to count, and this is the first one where I actually needed a family tree.

Photo Credit: Dimitrios Kambouris/Getty Images for Lionsgate

The Detail Every Fan Clocked

Mckenna Grace wore wings. Violet, Mockingjay-inspired wings, over a gem-encrusted gown by Pamela Roland — and if you think that was just a pretty fashion moment, you haven’t been paying attention. Grace plays Maysilee Donner, Haymitch’s District 12 ally and (for the book people among us) the original owner of the Mockingjay pin, the very one that finds its way, decades later, to Katniss Everdeen. The girl who owned the Mockingjay showed up dressed as one. Somewhere, a stylist deserves a raise.

Photo Credit: Slaven Vlasic/Getty Images for Lionsgate

Two Haymitches Walk Into Lincoln Center

Let’s do the math, because the carpet certainly made me. Woody Harrelson — who spent four films giving us Haymitch Abernathy as a gloriously pickled, secretly heartbroken mentor — shared the evening with Joseph Zada, who plays Haymitch at sixteen, on the morning his name gets pulled for the 50th Hunger Games. Elizabeth Banks, the original Effie Trinket (and, frankly, the original Capitol fashion week), was there alongside Elle Fanning, who inherits the role. And President Snow? Ralph Fiennes plays him now; Tom Blyth played him young. Donald Sutherland would have loved this.

It is a little dizzying. It is also exactly the kind of franchise flex that only works when the fandom has been loyal for well over a decade — and this one has been, to the point of tattoos.

Photo Credit: Kevin Mazur/Getty Images for Lionsgate

The Quell That Started It All

For the uninitiated (hello, I see you, and no judgment): Sunrise on the Reaping is set 24 years before The Hunger Games, during the Second Quarter Quell — the 50th Games, with twice the usual number of tributes, because the Capitol has never met a cruelty it couldn’t double. Zada’s Haymitch forms an unlikely alliance with fellow District 12 tribute Maysilee Donner (Mckenna Grace), falls in with a coalition determined to break the Capitol’s grip, and decides to take the Games down from inside the arena. If you know where Haymitch ends up by the time Katniss meets him, you already know this is not a story with a picnic at the end.

Rounding out a cast that reads like a very expensive dinner party: Jesse Plemons, Glenn Close, Kieran Culkin, Maya Hawke and Kelvin Harrison Jr. Billy Ray and Michael Lesslie wrote the screenplay; Nina Jacobson, Brad Simpson and Francis Lawrence produced.

Photo Credit: Slaven Vlasic/Getty Images for Lionsgate

The Purple Carpet Came to Play

Now, the part I actually came for. The cast went full avant-garde, and every look was dressed in a little bit of Panem.

Zada arrived in a militant maroon suit by Louis Vuitton — tribute energy, but make it tailored. Fanning, who has never once phoned in a carpet, blushed in a shimmering palette dress with matching headpiece from Givenchy; Effie would have wept. Whitney Peak wore the film’s emblem — a white bird stitched onto royal-blue velvet — by Robert Wun, which is the kind of thematic dressing I wish more stylists had the nerve for.

Jennifer Lawrence, meanwhile, did what Jennifer Lawrence does: walked in looking effortlessly, almost unfairly elegant in a coat dress by Dior under Jonathan Anderson. No theatrics required. The girl on fire has nothing left to prove.

And then, of course, there were those wings. (We’ve been over the wings. I am still not over the wings.)

Photo Credit: Slaven Vlasic/Getty Images for Lionsgate

The Woman Behind the Wheel

Leading the procession was the woman responsible for all of it, Suzanne Collins, alongside producer Nina Jacobson and director Francis Lawrence, who has steered Panem since Catching Fire and clearly has no intention of handing over the keys. Watching the three of them walk in ahead of a cast spanning fourteen years of films was, I’ll admit, a little moving. (Don’t tell anyone.)

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opens only in theaters November 20, 2026. Bring tissues. Bring a friend who hasn’t read the book. Watch their face.
