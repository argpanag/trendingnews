Princess of Wales nearly hits bystander while playing shinty

TheTools · 18.09.2026 00:08 · source · permalink

Catherine transfers her hockey skills to the Gaelic sport on visit to Bute

The Princess of Wales nearly left an innocent bystander with an unwelcome souvenir of her royal visit when she was invited to try the Gaelic sport of shinty.

The Princess and her husband joined a rain-soaked penalty shoot-out at Bute Shinty Club on their trip to the Scottish island.

She needed a couple of attempts to master her swing, but on her third, the ball whistled past the goal post, and just inches from kilt-wearing bystander Andy McAlindon, an influencer known as Andy The Highlander.

The Princess put her hand to her mouth and later told him: “I was a bit worried I was going to take someone’s teeth out.”

The royal couple looked relaxed on their visit to the island despite the fall-out from Earl Spencer’s new book in which he claimed that during a phone call before the funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales, the King remarked: “Rest assured, we’ll forget her soon enough.”

They were introduced to the traditional Gaelic game, played with a long curved wooden stick, known as a caman, and a small leather ball, as they watched an under-17s match at the island’s shinty club.

As the game went to a penalty shoot-out, the couple agreed to step up when officials suggested they try their hand.

The Prince of Wales took command, missing his first shot but connecting with his second.

The Princess was then cheered on to the pitch by the crowd, and raised her fist in acknowledgement of their support.

Her husband shouted “long run-up” as she stepped up to take her first attempt which produced only an air shot.

The Prince suggested she take a single step up to the ball, and Catherine struck her second attempt cleanly.

By her third attempt, she had found her stride and the ball flew swiftly past the watching Mr McAlindon.

He said afterwards: “I was standing by the goal as Her Royal Highness took her third shot, and she narrowly missed me. It was a great shot and she said sorry.”

The club was founded in 1946 and is run by volunteers, many of whom are former players.

As the couple met players in the clubhouse, Prince William said: “We both played hockey at St Andrews and had seen shinty, but not played. It looks a bit bruising.”

The Princess, studying a ball and stick, remarked: “It’s quite like a rounders ball. And is the ball normally in the air or on the ground? So the idea is that you are hitting it in the air... how do you control..?”

She added: “I want to give it a go,” adding: “It’s fantastic for community to have sport, so good to get people together at all ages.”

Barry Martin, chairman of Bute Shinty Club, said: “The shinty club is very much part of the community on the island.

“It’s used by lots of groups from the Boy’s Brigade, Highland Games Committee and keep fit classes.

“[The royal visit] is right up there, it’s an honour, and they’ve taken on titles of Duke and Duchess of Rothesay.

He added: “It’s a tremendous honour as it’s their first visit to island with new titles.”

Later the royal couple posed for photos with Jenny Sallie, 94, from Bute, who said: “I followed them for years. It was lovely.”
