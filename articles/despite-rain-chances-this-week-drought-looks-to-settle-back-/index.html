While the region may have a chance of getting some rain on Wednesday, drought looks like its settling back into the region following very dry, hot weather recently. 

Meteorologist Derrick Snyder with the National Weather Service in Paducah says the break in the extended heatwave was a welcome one, but heat index values are set to spike right back up into the triple digits this week, before a cold front attempts to push through the region, bringing rain and storms.

The severe threat doesn’t appear too worrying at this time, with the main threat being possible damaging winds.  Any rain that does fall would be appreciated, says Snyder, as the Pennyrile area of western Kentucky begins to see drought conditions creep back in—but he doesn’t think it will improve our dry conditions all that much.

The heat continues into next week—sorry fall lovers—and Snyder says a lot of folks could even see rapid onset drought conditions, also known as a ‘flash drought’, with no appreciable rain in the data.

Our best chance for stormy weather will be Wednesday into Thursday, though smaller rain chances will stick around into the weekend. Stay tuned to the WHOP Family of Stations for any watches or warnings that may pop up, including heat related advisories.
