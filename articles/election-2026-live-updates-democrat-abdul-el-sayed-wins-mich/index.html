It’s been a good night for Democrats, who will control both chambers of Congress beginning in January after flipping seven Senate seats.

What to know on Election Day

  • DEMOCRATS WIN CONTROL OF CONGRESS: Democrats will take control of the House and the Senate in January, NBC News projects. The party has flipped seven Senate seats: Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, South Carolina and Texas.
  • HOUSE MARGIN: An NBC News analysis shows that Democrats will win at least 258 House seats — an estimate that could still tick up or down by a few seats but will be well beyond the 218 needed to secure a majority.
  • GOVERNOR RACES: Democrats have also flipped governorships in Texas, Georgia, Ohio and Iowa.
  • NBC NEWS EXIT POLLS: NBC News Exit Polls showed a dissatisfied electorate, with President Donald Trump’s disapproval rating at a dismal 62% and more than three-quarters of voters saying they disapprove of the job Congress is doing, as well. Nearly half of voters said they view the election as an opportunity to rebuke Trump.
  • WHERE TO WATCH: Follow along this evening for results, detailed election data, analysis and on-the-ground reporting from correspondents across the country. Steve Kornacki is live at the big board and NBC News primetime coverage will be going all night.

NBC News Exit Poll: LGBTQ voters remain among the most loyal Democratic blocs

LGBTQ voters have again emerged as one of the country’s strongest groups of Democratic supporters, according to the NBC News Exit Poll.

Eighty-six percent of LGBTQ voters supported Democratic candidates for the House this year; 12% supported Republicans. That’s on trend with recent election cycles: In 2024, LGBTQ voters supported Kamala Harris over Trump by the same margin.

LGBTQ voters are firmly in the progressive camp, the poll found. Nearly two-thirds described themselves liberal, while just 8% identified as conservative. Most LGBTQ voters (66%) had a favorable opinion of socialism, and 45% said they considered themselves democratic socialists.

NBC News Exit Poll: MAHA voters are divided over Trump's impact on health

The Make America Healthy Again movement made waves in the 2024 presidential election — but its supporters are divided over whether Trump has improved the health and well-being of people in the U.S.

According to the NBC News Exit Poll, 30% of voters consider themselves supporters of the MAHA movement, while 39% do not and 31% don’t know enough about it to say.

Many MAHA supporters are older (42% are 65 or older), and most identify as Republicans (65%).

Most MAHA supporters say the Republican Party would be better able to handle healthcare (66%) than the Democratic Party (14%). That is in stark contrast to voters who don’t support the MAHA movement — most of them say the Democratic Party would better handle healthcare.

But MAHA supporters do not necessarily think the Trump administration’s policies have improved the health and well-being of people in the U.S.: 52% say Trump's policies have made things better, while 48% say they've made things worse or not made much difference.

NBC News Exit Poll: Jewish voters buck the shift toward Democrats

Half of Jewish voters identified as Democrats this year, down from 64% in 2018, according to the NBC News Exit Poll. Over the same period, the share identifying as Republicans has more than doubled, from 10% to 22%.

That makes Jewish voters an exception in an election in which several key groups, including Latino voters and voters under 30, moved toward Democrats.

U.S. support for Israel has become a sharp point of debate in American politics. Jewish voters are divided over the issue: 38% say U.S. support for Israel is about right, 33% say it is not strong enough, and 29% say it is too strong.

That puts Jewish voters at odds with the electorate as a whole. Half of all voters say U.S. support for Israel is too strong, while 37% say it is about right, and 12% say it is not strong enough.

NBC News Exit Poll: In Alaska’s Senate race, Democrat Mary Peltola sees strong support from independents

To be competitive in deeply Republican Alaska, Democratic candidates have to rack up big margins with the state’s large chunk of independent voters. According to the NBC News Exit Poll, that’s exactly what Democrat Mary Peltola appears to be achieving in her effort to unseat GOP Sen. Dan Sullivan.

NBC News characterized Alaska’s Senate race as too close to call when polls closed at 1 a.m. ET.

Both of the major candidates have amassed big leads among their own parties’ voters: Peltola is winning 98% of support among Democrats, while Sullivan is getting 90% of support among Republicans. But Republican voters outnumber Democrats by more than 2 to 1 in Alaska, making independents (47% of the state’s electorate this year) key to any Democratic victory.

Peltola, who served a single term as Alaska’s lone member in the House, which ended in 2025, is beating Sullivan handily among independents, 63% to 31%.

NBC News Exit Poll in Michigan: Support for Democratic Senate candidate ticks down among Black voters

Abdul El-Sayed’s support among Black voters (86%) is about 4 percentage points lower than is typical for a Democratic Senate candidate in Michigan, according to the NBC News Exit Poll. His support among white voters (47%) is more typical and even slightly higher than Democrat Gary Peters' support among white voters in 2020 (44%).
