Blanche defends Hegseth amid GOP congressman's push to impeach him over Iran war

Attorney General Todd Blanche on Tuesday defended Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth after a Republican congressman brought articles of impeachment against him, arguing that “follows the law.”

Republican Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky made a surprise push earlier Tuesday to impeach Hegseth for allegedly violating the War Powers Resolution by conducting a war against Iran without congressional authorization.

Blanche also pushed back on a reporter’s question characterizing the United States’ military action toward Iran as a war, despite several administration officials and the president having, at times, referred to the conflict as a war.

Investigation into Trump foes to continue without diGenova, Blanche says

Attorney General Todd Blanche said that the so-called “grand conspiracy” investigation into former government officials who previously pursued cases around President Donald Trump is going to continue, despite the departure of its lead prosecutor Joe diGenova last week.

“We’re not going to slow down just because someone leaves,” Blanche said in the Rose Garden press conference.

Other Justice Department lawyers had a similar message just days ago for attorneys for former CIA Director John Brennan, according to a court filing yesterday. Brennan is under investigation for making an alleged false statement to Congress related to his work on intelligence about Russian influence in the 2016 election. He is also being pursued as a witness in a broader conspiracy case, his attorneys were told the day after diGenova resigned.

No indictments have been filed. But court proceedings and reporting have revealed the conspiracy idea to be an effort to potentially prosecute former officials who worked in the FBI, CIA and potentially other agencies from 2016, when Trump’s campaign was investigated for its connections to Russia, through 2022, when Trump’s Florida resort was searched for unsecured classified records.

When asked on Tuesday to comment on CNN’s reporting on Monday that he had fought with diGenova last week, Blanche said, “We’re very appreciative of the work that he did.”

White House press briefing with Todd Blanche has ended

Attorney General Todd Blanche just wrapped up a news briefing at the White House.

“I will tell you, the fact that Karoline Leavitt did this for as long as she did is incredible,” he told reporters before leaving the podium.

Blanche says administration could continue pursuing mail voting reforms even after ruling

Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Supreme Court’s ruling blocking President Donald Trump’s efforts to restrict mail-in voting would not dissuade the administration from pursuing investigations and policy proposals to deal with alleged voter fraud.

He said the Justice Department would comply with the ruling, but wouldn’t stop looking to make reforms.

He left open the possibility the administration could continue pursuing changes to mail voting ahead of the 2028 presidential election.

“President Trump’s efforts to make sure that we have free and fair elections are not going to stop just in November or after or thereafter. So I expect you’re going to see that work continue,” he said.

Blanche described Trump as “very frustrated” at the court and defended the president’s earlier statement that the three justices he appointed had changed since he interviewed them during his first term.

“He has every right, and indeed he should communicate his concerns with the Supreme Court whenever he has them. This isn’t the first time that he’s done it, and it won’t be the last,” Blanche said.

Blanche opens briefing by touting new arrest in alleged terror case

Attorney General Todd Blanche on Tuesday highlighted a new federal case against a 21-year-old Pennsylvania man accused of plotting an attack on behalf of ISIS.

The man, Jonathan Hunter Kramer, was arrested yesterday on allegations that he intended to use firearms and ammunitions to commit a crime on behalf of a terrorist organization.

Blanche highlighted the case as he began holding a White House press briefing in the Rose Garden.

According to court documents, Kramer, who was found responsible as a minor of planning a mass casualty event, offered to share explosive manuals with people online. He also allegedly expressed his intent to prepare for his “mission” and that he was just missing a “weapon,” according to prosecutors.

FBI agents surveilled Kramer as he allegedly purchased a disposable phone, met with an individual, and paid for a hotel room with cash. When they later executed search warrants, agents allegedly found a semiautomatic rifle, five magazines, a rifle bipod and scope, a prayer rug, pepper spray and approximately 30 knives. They also found a handwritten note stating “DoNt look For me[.] IDK what I did buT I cant live In constant Fear[.].”

The Senate hearing with Kash Patel has ended

Lawmakers on the Senate Judiciary Committee questioned FBI Director Kash Patel for several hours today.

The hearing has now ended, but the agency head fended off criticism about his use of FBI resources, the threat of bad actors using AI and the Epstein files, among a range of other topics.

Patel claims there was no purge of agents who worked on Trump January 6 election case

FBI Director Kash Patel claimed during Tuesday’s congressional testimony that a majority of the agents who worked on the classified documents case against Donald Trump had retired and were not fired.

“My understanding is the overwhelming majority of those agents and FBI personnel retired,” Patel said when asked about past comments from now Attorney General Todd Blanche in March.

During a conservative conference, Blanche boasted that “there is not a single man or woman at the Department of Justice who had anything to do with those prosecutions.”

Blanche said all FBI or DOJ employees on the cases had either been fired, resigned, or took early retirement.

Patel was pressed by Democrat Sen. Adam Schiff as to whether agents who worked on the investigation into Trump’s efforts to overturn the 2020 election results had been purged from the department.

Many agents and federal employees across law enforcement have taken retirement options in the second Trump administration, as his rise has changed the face of the storied department.

Many agents involved in those cases and earlier investigations into Trump were fired by Patel and dozens have sued the FBI and Patel, alleging he violated their First Amendment rights, punished them for a case randomly assigned, and fired them without cause.

Patel says there are extensive AI safeguards at the FBI

FBI Director Kash Patel said the bureau has a “ton” of safeguards in place to ensure artificial intelligence models do not “break free” from their intended purposes.

“We don’t just bring in AI and let it run loose,” the director said in response to a question from Democratic Sen. Alex Padilla.

Patel said any AI models used within the FBI are subjected to feedback and reviews to see whether they abide by FBI and Justice Department policies, and the law.

“We’re going to continue that effort every time an AI system comes in,” Patel said. “That’s the exact pattern that we execute at the FBI to make sure there’s no breaking free, for lack of a better phrase, of AI to run rampant.”

Patel later described the AI threat environment as a “new frontier” but said the bureau would need an “intake of a lot of money” to confront any potential issues.

“What we need is the ability to buy tech,” Patel said. “We don’t have the money to buy tech.”

FBI director is asked about deploying agents to polling stations during midterm elections

FBI Director Kash Patel said the FBI would be monitoring elections in the upcoming midterms but wavered about where agents would be stationed.

Earlier in Tuesday’s hearing, Patel told Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar that he would follow federal guidelines that prohibit FBI agents from conducting investigations or being stationed inside open polling places on Election Day.

Shortly thereafter, Patel said that the FBI was going to “house our agents and intel analysts in our field offices for election security,” but expressed some skepticism that the bureau can’t deploy agents to the polls.

In an exchange hours later with Democratic Sen. Peter Welch, Patel grew angry over claims that he promised not to send agents to the polls.

“When did you hear that?” Patel asked, and Welch pointed to his comments earlier in the hearing about agents at the polls.

“Do you pledge that you will not, in any way, interfere in the will of the American people when they go to the polls in November?” Welch asked.

Patel said, “I pledge unequivocally that I will not participate in your charade of lies.”

Sen. Booker calls Patel a "thug," alleging the FBI investigated journalists

FBI Director Kash Patel grew heated after New Jersey Democratic Sen. Cory Booker asked about his agency allegedly investigating journalists, including a New York Times reporter who earlier this year wrote about Patel’s girlfriend being “escorted by an FBI SWAT team.”

Asked if FBI agents interviewed Patel’s girlfriend, Alexis Wilkins, or if they ran reporter Elizabeth Williamson’s name through federal databases to gather information about her, Patel did not answer.

“You can say whatever lies you want. We’re not going to participate,” Patel said.

Booker accused Patel of “using resources from the FBI in a way that he’s not willing to be transparent and open about,” also accusing the FBI director of attacking the press.

The senator also referenced the Trump administration’s subpoenaing of New York Times journalists who reported on security concerns around a Qatari-gifted jet serving as the new Air Force One, highlighting that this is something that Patel does “often.”

Patel said that his agency was not targeting journalists, accusing Booker of mischaracterizing.

In response, Booker said that “senators on both sides of the aisle should have to know why are you using FBI resources in ways that benefit yourself and your family and your girlfriend. But you won’t even answer questions.”

Patel again says there's no evidence of Butler assassination conspiracy

Following a social media post from President Donald Trump calling into question the FBI’s investigation into the attempt on his life in Butler, Pennsylvania, FBI Director Kash Patel again reiterated there was no evidence the shooter was working with others.

“I think we looked at some 27 devices, exfiltrated all the data off of them, looked for connections to individuals in America and overseas,” Patel said of the investigation into the shooter’s devices. “We found no evidence that that individual was literally even speaking to any other human being other than his parents, who he resided with.”

But Patel’s attempt to squash conspiracy theories comes amid a continued release over the past several months of documents, using the Freedom of Information Act, related to the investigation.

“Everybody knows the Crooked Joe Biden FBI didn’t do what they should have with respect to the Lunatic who shot me in Butler, PA,” Trump posted on Tuesday, alleging that information around the shooting was “missing, altered, corrupted, or gone” and had newly been uncovered.

Trump oddly suggested the documents were part of a “Democrat Plot, to get me out of the Election.”

Congressional investigations, as well as those led by the Secret Service, FBI, and an independent panel, have all come to the same conclusion: The shooter, Thomas Matthew Crooks, acted alone and was able to bypass security through clear human error on the part of federal and local law enforcement.

The FBI has also had to push back on the conspiracies born from the newly released documents in the past.

Sen. Welch presses Patel on trip to Italy during Olympics

Following a heated response from FBI Director Kash Patel to a question about media reports alleging that he drinks to excess, Vermont Democratic Sen. Peter Welch asked the agency head about his trip to Italy during the Olympics earlier this year.

Back in February, Patel garnered criticism after he was seen celebrating with the US men’s hockey team in Milan after their historic gold medal win over Canada, having traveled to Italy in a government plane.

Welch noted a post-9/11 government regulation that requires the FBI director to use government aircraft for all travel, including personal trips, but asked why Patel was in Italy in the first place.

Patel had “a dinner with the ambassador, an hour with Italy counterpart operatives, three 20-minute meetings, a security briefing and a 30-minute tour over four days, and then you barged into the celebration of our winning Olympic team,” Welch said.

Patel said that the US men’s hockey team, who he called his “friends,” invited him into their locker room to celebrate with them.

The FBI director also reiterated that while he was in Italy, his agency arranged to have a “top cybercriminal” from the Chinese Communist Party deported to the US instead of going back to China.

Patel declines to discuss Georgia election probe, citing ongoing investigation

FBI Director Kash Patel declined to discuss his bureau’s ongoing probe into the 2020 election results in Georgia, citing the ongoing investigation.

CNN previously reported that the FBI had assigned 260 staffers to investigate the 2020 results in Georgia, where President Donald Trump has repeatedly and falsely claimed election victory in 2020. The elections office in Fulton County, Georgia, was raided by the FBI earlier this year as part of that probe.

Questioned by Vermont Democratic Sen. Peter Welch during today’s Senate Judiciary Committee hearing, Patel declined to comment on the progress of that investigation because “the matter is ongoing.”

But Patel in the past has frequently commented on ongoing investigations.

He was confronted earlier in the hearing for his premature comments following shootings involving federal immigration officers this year. He has also been previously criticized for early, and ultimately false, initial statements he made after the shooting of Charlie Kirk and a shooting on the Brown University campus.

FBI director says firing of experts on Iran threats was over their past "weaponization"

FBI Director Kash Patel reinforced to senators Tuesday that a dozen agents and staff members who were experts on Iranian threats were fired earlier this year because of their alleged “weaponization” efforts.

The officials were fired in late February, sources told CNN, just days before the United States launched a major military operation in Iran. They were ousted because of their involvement in the investigation of President Donald Trump’s alleged retention of classified documents at his Mar-a-Lago estate, CNN previously reported.

“Individuals that have been removed were removed for reasons that they basically failed to meet the standard of the FBI,” Patel said of the dismissals. In some previous public testimony, he has denied knowing that the fired employees were focused on Iran threats.

“The reports were that they were fired because they worked on the classified documents investigation involving President Trump,” Democratic Sen. Richard Blumenthal asked. “Do you think firing the president’s political opponents is more important than retaining the decades of experience and expertise monitoring intelligence threats from Iran?”

Patel responded, “I think what’s most important is not have a weaponized FBI, and I will endeavor every single day to make sure we have no one there that is doing that.”

Patel dodges questions on reporting that oligarch funded Trump Jr.'s wedding celebration

FBI Director Kash Patel dodged questions from Sen. Richard Blumenthal over reporting that a Russian oligarch funded part of Donald Trump Jr.’s wedding celebrations.

ProPublica reported on Monday that the oligarch, Umar Kremlev, spent hundreds of thousands of dollars to fund the lavish celebrations. Kremlev, the president of the International Boxing Association, is a close ally of Vladimir Putin.

Trump Jr.’s wife, Bettina Anderson, confirmed the reporting in a post to Instagram where she described Kremlev as their “dear friend” who “very generously hosted two incredible nights of celebrations” for the couple after their May wedding.

“It was an extraordinarily generous wedding gift from a friend, and something for which we were and remain incredibly grateful,” Anderson wrote.

Blumenthal asked Patel what steps his agency is taking to investigate Kremlev’s funding of the celebrations. Patel responded that “public reporting is generally inaccurate” and instead touted the bureau’s counterintelligence missions.

Whitehouse confronts Patel about withholding of some Epstein files

Nine months after the first batch of the Epstein files were publicly released, FBI Director Kash Patel continues to face questions about what was, and was not, contained in those files.

Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse questioned Patel about 37 pages that were withheld from the public release. Whitehouse specifically asked about “302” forms that the agency uses to memorialize interviews with witnesses.

CNN previously reported that dozens of FBI records were missing from the initial public release of the documents, including three interviews that were related to a woman who accused President Donald Trump of sexually assaulting her decades ago.

The Justice Department later released some of those documents, but Whitehouse, during today’s Senate Judiciary Committee hearing, said not all have been made public.

Trump has long denied any wrongdoing related to Epstein or any allegation of sexual misconduct.

Patel said he wasn’t aware of the 37 pages that Whitehouse was referencing.

“We have complied with the statute and the court orders and produced three and a half million pages of documents for the first time ever,” the director said.

Senators push Patel to explain FBI hiring standards for those accused of bestiality

FBI Director Kash Patel was challenged Tuesday about why the bureau no longer automatically disqualifies some job applicants who allegedly committed crimes like soliciting prostitution and engaging in bestiality.

Democratic Sen. Dick Durban began those challenges, asking Patel whether he believes those individuals are “qualified candidates that the FBI is losing?”

But his answer only invited additional questions, including from Republicans.

Under questioning from Republican Sen. John Kennedy, Patel said that the change only applies if you were “forced” into participating in those crimes.

Bestiality is sexual activity between a human and an animal.

Patel responded: “In a lot of these cases, when human traffickers — who are some of the worst individuals on planet Earth — they don’t just traffic you for prostitution. They traffic you for degrading acts of all kind, and they force you into that type of conduct.”

Durbin grills Patel on violence during immigration operations

Sen. Dick Durbin, the top Democrat on the Senate Judiciary Committee, asked FBI Director Kash Patel about violence during immigration operations that took place last year.

“One year ago, my home state of Illinois was experiencing the beginning of a ruthless, reckless, deadly campaign orchestrated by this administration, known as Midway Blitz,” Durbin said.

Durbin referenced the shooting of US citizen Marimar Martinez by a Customs and Border Protection agent in October, as well as the fatal shooting of Mexican national Silverio Villegas-Gonzalez by an Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer in September 2025.

The Department of Homeland Security, Durbin said, “branded (Martinez) as a domestic terrorist,” while an FBI agent “falsely claimed” that she had chased a border patrol agent before she was shot.

After the shooting of Martinez, Patel also reposted a video of a separate collision that was not related to Martinez, though the initial poster claimed that it was.

In his repost of the video, Patel wrote: “Attack our law enforcement, and this FBI will find you and bring you to justice.”

Durbin asked Patel if he stood by those “false statements.” Patel denied that they were false.

The senator also asked why the FBI had not investigated these shootings, to which the agency director said that “investigations are currently ongoing.”
