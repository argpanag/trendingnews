ACL Two: Sailors eye Round of 16 berth in crucial BG Pathum clash - Lion City Sailors FC

  • News
  • 25 Nov 2026
  • News
  • 25 Nov 2026

Buoyed by back-to-back victories over Australian side Adelaide United, the Lion City Sailors will be looking to secure their place in the Asian Football Confederation Champions League (ACL) Two Round of 16 when they face Thai League 1 outfit BG Pathum United at the True BG Stadium on Thursday (26 November) night.

The Sailors head into the penultimate Group F fixture in pole position, knowing that a draw against their second-placed opponents will be enough to secure progression to the knockout stages, while a victory will guarantee top spot in the group.

Speaking at the pre-match press conference, Sailors Head Coach Mark Jackson outlined his side’s determination to get the job done in Pathum Thani.

“It’s good to be back here. We’re excited for the game, and we know that if we play our game, we’ll get the reward of qualifying for the next round,” said the 49-year-old, who returns to Thailand for the first time since guiding Buriram United to a historic treble last season.

“We played them in my first game in charge, and we know they’re a very good team who are well organised and play a certain way. But we’re a completely different team now. We’re creating chances, scoring goals, and are also very solid defensively. There’s been a big evolution of the team in a short space of time. 

“The deciding factor is whether we can maintain our momentum. Can we maintain the standards we’ve shown in recent weeks and keep this run going? The signs point towards us doing that, as the players have been very focused. 

“We don’t underestimate our opponents. It’s going to be very tough, and we have to be on top of our game in every position and in every phase of play. If we do that, we know we’ve got a good chance.”

New faces bolster BG Pathum’s threat

Standing in the Sailors’ way, however, is a BG Pathum side boasting considerable quality and depth, with a squad of 30 players, including 15 foreigners.

The Rabbits remain the only side to have breached the Sailors’ defence in this season’s ACL Two, having held the reigning Singapore Premier League (SPL) champions to a 1-1 draw in their opening Group F encounter on 17 September.

Since then, the Thai outfit have further strengthened their ranks with two new signings in October.

(Photo: 2026 Asian Football Confederation (AFC))

Bosnian forward Marin Jurina has made an immediate impact, scoring four goals in just three appearances, including a hat-trick in a 4-1 home victory over Tai Po FC earlier this month.

His exploits have propelled him to the top of the ACL Two scoring charts alongside Al Jazira’s former Barcelona attacker Malcom.

Providing further firepower is Spanish playmaker Hugo Rama, who previously plied his trade in Spain’s second division with the likes of Real Oviedo and Deportivo La Coruña.

Rama has also made his presence felt on the continental stage, scoring a superb direct free-kick in BG Pathum’s 3-0 away victory over Tai Po on Matchday Three.

There has also been a change in the dugout, with Peerapong Pichitchotirat appointed permanent Head Coach over the weekend following an impressive spell in interim charge.

The 42-year-old has overseen four wins, one draw and two defeats in seven matches since replacing Vladimir Vujović in mid-October, earning himself the role until at least the end of the season.

(Photo:BG Pathum United)

Having masterminded successive victories over Tai Po to put BG Pathum firmly in contention for a knockout-stage berth, Peerapong is now looking to steer his side past the Sailors.

“During the recent international break, we had time on the training pitch to make the necessary adjustments, so we’re optimistic about our chances of winning tomorrow’s match,” said Peerapong.

“The first time we played the Sailors (in September), it was Mark Jackson’s first game in charge, so it was understandable that he was trying different things. From his time at Buriram, we know how good he is in terms of his game plan and style of play. 

“Since that match, the Sailors have built up a lot of momentum. They have a very good squad, so we cannot underestimate them. But we believe in the work we’ve done in training, and we’ve watched enough videos of them to be ready for tomorrow.”

That ambition is shared by long-serving captain Sarach Yooyen, who is hoping to help the Rabbits reach the knockout stages of an AFC club competition for the first time since their 2022 AFC Champions League campaign.

(Photo:BG Pathum United)

“Of course, the Sailors are a good team with many talented local players and top foreign stars, but we’ve prepared well and it’s all about whether we can show that on the pitch tomorrow,” said the 34-year-old midfielder. 

“We have some players (like Marin and Hugo) who are not yet registered for the domestic league, but they’ve been taking good care of themselves. They’re adapting well, learning about our style of play, and sharing their ideas and knowledge with the rest of the squad. They bring character to the team and contribute to a positive atmosphere. 

“We’re playing in front of our home fans tomorrow, and we know their support can help us put pressure on the Sailors and overcome them.”

Buoyant Sailors determined to keep winning

The Sailors, meanwhile, arrive in Thailand full of confidence, riding an eight-match winning streak across all competitions, during which they have also kept seven consecutive clean sheets.

Their attacking options have also been boosted by the growing influence of Ilhan Fandi, who has steadily found his feet since joining the Sailors.

The 24-year-old has registered five goals and two assists in 11 appearances, including strikes in consecutive home victories over Adelaide and Geylang International in the ACL Two and SPL respectively. 

Thursday’s encounter will hold added significance for Ilhan, who returns to familiar surroundings after spending the 2024/25 season with BG Pathum, where he scored seven goals in 30 appearances.

“It’s nice to be back here, and I’m going to see a lot of familiar faces tomorrow,” said Ilhan. “That being said, we have a job to do, which is to get the three points and bring them back to Singapore. At the end of the day, we’re not just representing the Lion City Sailors, but also Singapore.  

“This is a team of fighters. Coach Jacko’s message to us is never to take our foot off the gas because that’s when things start to go wrong. I think the boys have shown time and again that we’re in control of what we do. 

“We know how big this match is and what’s at stake, and that we’ll qualify if we win. But that’s not at the front of my mind. What’s most important is to put in a good performance, and hopefully we’ll get the three points.”
