6-0 or 5-1? Here’s what a win Monday would mean for the 49ers

TheTools · 19.10.2026 17:00 · source · permalink

Of the 31 teams to start 6-0 in the past 25 seasons, 15 have reached the Super Bowl. The teams with the best quarterbacks have won it all.

In a division where every game usually carries weight, the 49ers’ Monday-night matchup with the Commanders … doesn’t seem so important.

At 5-0, the 49ers have survived a wave of injuries at wide receiver and defensive back to grab the early-season lead in the NFC West. They’ve defeated all three division opponents, including the reigning champion Seahawks, who are in second place despite a 3-3 record. Quarterback Brock Purdy has thrived with a rotating cast of receivers, the rookie class is making valuable contributions, and the offense and defense have consistently worn down opponents in the second half of games.

The Commanders, meanwhile, are 1-3-1 and have a bottom-three scoring defense in the NFL.

With several stars expected to miss Monday’s game and wide receiver Mike Evans uncertain to play, the 49ers could lose. But the difference between 5-1 and 6-0 is meaningful, especially considering how teams that have started 6-0 have fared in the past 25 seasons.

Since 2001, 31 teams have started 6-0, and 29 made the playoffs, according to Champs or Chumps (opens in new tab). Only the 2003 Vikings and 2009 Broncos missed the postseason. Kyle Shanahan’s 2019 49ers, who started 8-0 before losing to the Seahawks, are one of the 15 teams to start 6-0 and also reach the Super Bowl.

By comparison, 17 teams since 2001 have started 5-0 and lost their sixth game. Fourteen made the playoffs, two reached the Super Bowl — including the 2023 49ers — and none won it.

The 2019 49ers, who featured George Kittle, Nick Bosa, Fred Warner, and Kyle Juszczyk, had the NFL’s No. 3 regular-season point differential and No. 3 yardage differential. How does this year’s team compare? Entering Monday’s game, it has the No. 1 point differential and No. 6 yardage differential. 

Nearly half of the 31 teams to start 6-0 since 2001 have reached the Super Bowl, but only four have raised the Lombardi Trophy. 

The 2004 Patriots (Tom Brady), the 2006 Colts (Peyton Manning), the 2009 Saints (Drew Brees), and the 2015 Broncos (Manning) were all led by elite quarterbacks. Purdy, the final pick in the 2022 NFL Draft, doesn’t stack up to those three, but he’s off to the best start of his career and will qualify as the highest-rated passer in league history — yes, ahead of Brady, Manning, and Brees — after five attempts in Monday’s game against the Commanders.

Brady, a sixth-round draft pick, was already a two-time Super Bowl champion and two-time Super Bowl MVP when New England started 6-0 in 2004, but the best years of his career were still ahead of him.

Manning was in the prime of a Hall of Fame career when he led the 2006 Colts to a title while Brees was already a three-time Pro Bowler and a former first-team All-Pro before the Saints started 6-0 and won it all in 2009. 

The 2015 Broncos are an outlier, because Manning missed several starts with an injury and posted subpar numbers, but Denver’s top-ranked defense carried the team to a title.

Starting 6-0 wouldn’t guarantee the 49ers anything, but a win over the Commanders would give San Francisco a three-game lead in the NFC West and Purdy his 36th win in 56 regular-season starts. It might be too soon to consider the 49ers to be Super Bowl favorites, but as long as Purdy continues playing at a high level, history suggests Shanahan’s team would be a legitimate contender.
