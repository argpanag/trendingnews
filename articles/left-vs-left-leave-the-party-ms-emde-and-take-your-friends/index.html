Left vs. Left: “Leave the Party, Ms Emde, and Take Your Friends”

The expected expulsion of Vanessa Emde from the Left Party’s parliamentary group is unlikely to put an immediate end to the debate over antisemitism surrounding the election-winning party. After all, the CDU is not alone in stressing that the real problem runs deeper. Warnings are also coming from within the Left Party itself. Thomas Hennig, 62, chief conductor of the Hauptstadt-Sinfonieorchester and, according to his own account, a member of the Left Party in Berlin for around ten years, told the Morgenpost: “Vanessa Emde is not an isolated case.”

Hennig is an ordinary party member and has written an open letter. Its title is: “Enough Is Enough! Neukölln Is Berlin, but Berlin Is Not Neukölln.” Anyone who regards Islamist terrorism – referring to Hamas, which attacked Israel on 7 October 2023 – as “historically grounded political resistance” fails to recognise “political realities”, he writes. Excusing or ignoring “religiously motivated violence” is incompatible with left-wing principles.

His letter is addressed to Elif Eralp and the party leaders. But he has no criticism of them. His real targets are others: Vanessa Emde and Martha Kleedörfer.

Open Letter Within the Left Party: “Berlin Is Not Neukölln”

On election night, Emde posed for a photograph with Palestinian activist Ibrahim Ibrahim. He described the Hamas leader and mastermind behind the 7 October attacks as a “martyr hero”. At an event in May, Emde also said that calling for a one-state solution was politically legitimate and should not be criminalised. “States have no right to exist; people have a right to exist.” Taken on its own, that statement is correct. In this particular context, however, Emde is accused of allowing her criticism of Israel to cross into antisemitism by denying Israel, a state that protects Jews, the right to exist.

Next week, the parliamentary group will decide on her expulsion. The party leadership is not justifying the move on the grounds of allegations of antisemitism, but rather on a lack of solidarity. According to the leadership, Emde has failed to honour joint agreements and is not acting in the party’s interests. The leadership no longer trusts her.

After the parliamentary group’s executive committee submitted the motion for her expulsion on Tuesday, Kleedörfer – the second target of Hennig’s criticism – recorded a video message. Kleedörfer comes from Mitte, has recently taken her seat in the Berlin House of Representatives and sits on the executive committees of both the party and its parliamentary group. In her video, she says it is legitimate to call for a democratic one-state solution with equal rights for everyone, as Emde had done.

According to its own official policy, the Left Party supports a two-state solution. Kleedörfer acknowledges that calling for a one-state solution is currently “utopian” – but not antisemitic. Defending the right to advocate such a solution is important in the struggle for freedom of expression. The fact that the Left Party has failed to dispel the allegations of antisemitism against Emde is, she says, a “major failure”.

Antisemitism Without Any Ifs or Buts?

The author of the open letter disagrees. “When Ms Kleedörfer says that Ms Emde can advocate a one-state solution and that this is protected by freedom of expression, the logical implication is that denying Israel’s right to exist would also be protected by freedom of expression,” Hennig emphasises in his conversation with the Morgenpost. “This approach is antisemitic. For me, there are no ifs or buts about it.” He has not yet spoken to Kleedörfer personally.

In his letter, he calls on Emde to give up her parliamentary seat and leave the party. “All those who want to steal our political home to pursue the interests of their own supporters” should follow her. Asked for clarification, Hennig says this also includes Kleedörfer.

He also has Trotskyist groups such as Marx21 in mind. He accuses them of using the issue of solidarity with Palestine to gain a foothold in Left Party district branches such as those in Neukölln and Mitte, and of exploiting the party for their own purposes. “It is up to us as members to say that we do not want this.” He believes that the vast majority of Left Party members feel the same way.

Affordability – not solidarity with Palestine – is the decisive issue for the Left Party in Berlin, Hennig argues. He wants his party to take on the responsibility of governing, which he believes voters elected it to do. “Anyone who does not want to govern and shape policy has no reason to stand for election in the first place.” He calls on those who share this view to challenge the vocal minority within their own ranks who oppose entering government. His letter is intended as a starting point.

Martha Kleedörfer: No Room for Antisemites in the Pro-Palestinian Movement

“There are antisemites who are trying to exploit and misuse solidarity with Palestine for their own purposes,” Kleedörfer says when the Morgenpost confronts her with the allegations on Thursday. “As members of the Left Party, we are working to ensure that these forces are given no space within the pro-Palestinian movement.” The pro-Palestinian movement is a human rights movement, she says. Antisemitism has no place in it.

She stands by her statement that it is legitimate to call for a one-state solution. “Saying that it is legitimate to advocate a particular position does not mean that you personally support that position.” From conversations they have had, she knows that Emde also believes Israelis and Palestinians must decide for themselves how they want to live together. However, Emde should have made this clearer publicly. Kleedörfer says she tried unsuccessfully to persuade her to do so.

She does not see affordability and solidarity with Palestine as competing priorities. The Left Party stands for a life of dignity for everyone. “Making life affordable cannot be separated from the commitment to human rights, including in Palestine.”
