Watch live: SpaceX to end Starship mission early after rocket orbits Earth

TheTools · 28.09.2026 15:13 · source · permalink

Summary

  • SpaceX's Starship rocket is now orbiting Earth following lift-off from Texas - watch live footage above

  • The SpaceX team have decided to end the mission early, without Starship making the planned six orbits of Earth. The craft is set to splash down in the Pacific Ocean after a three-hour mission

  • Earlier, as the rocket made its way to orbit, its booster successfully detached from the upper stage and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico

  • There was drama as engineers had to decide if they would send the rocket to orbit after an engine issue. Our science reporter explains sending it to orbit makes it more dangerous

  • SpaceX describes Starship as "the world's most powerful launch vehicle ever developed" and says it will eventually carry crew and cargo "to Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars and beyond"

  • The aim of the spacecraft - which will undergo its 14th test flight today - is to be able to carry up to 100 people on "long-duration, inter-planetary flights"

SpaceX's Starship rocket is now orbiting Earth following lift-off from Texas - watch live footage above

The SpaceX team have decided to end the mission early, without Starship making the planned six orbits of Earth. The craft is set to splash down in the Pacific Ocean after a three-hour mission

Earlier, as the rocket made its way to orbit, its booster successfully detached from the upper stage and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico

There was drama as engineers had to decide if they would send the rocket to orbit after an engine issue. Our science reporter explains sending it to orbit makes it more dangerous

SpaceX describes Starship as "the world's most powerful launch vehicle ever developed" and says it will eventually carry crew and cargo "to Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars and beyond"

The aim of the spacecraft - which will undergo its 14th test flight today - is to be able to carry up to 100 people on "long-duration, inter-planetary flights"

    15:58 BST

    Nasa administrator Jared Isaacman has congratulated SpaceX on its Starship mission.

    Writing on X he said it was a, external "gorgeous launch, getting Ship to orbit and managing every step in a safe, responsible, and especially inspirational way".

    He added that Nasa is "excited to help where we can and for Starship missions to become routine!"

    In 2024, Isaacman became the first non-professional astronaut to walk in space.

    15:52 BST

    Starlink satellites delivered into orbit will 'greatly' increase user speeds – SpaceX

    One of the objectives of the mission, which has already been completed, was the delivery of 26 "next generation" Starlink V3 satellites into orbit.

    Those join the existing Starlink constellation in low Earth orbit which deliver broadband internet on Earth.

    The new satellites added today will "greatly expand the network's capacity and user speeds", SpaceX says.

    As we reported earlier, the satellites were carried into orbit by the Starship. After exiting the Starship they unfold their antennas and make initial contact with the ground and the rest of the satellite constellation via radio and laser links, SpaceX says.

  12. The longer the flight time, the more data SpaceX can collectpublished at 14:30 BST
    14:30 BST

    Georgina Rannard
    Climate and science reporter

    The test flight today could be as long as 10 hours as the Starship capsule attempts to loop around Earth six times.

    Until now, all Starship flights have been suborbital, meaning the capsules were high enough to reach space, but did not travel fast enough or long enough to complete a loop around the Earth. Most of the previous flights were about one hour.

    Today, Starship is travelling at about 275km above Earth in orbit, and the company want to push the craft to its limits. That will involve collecting data throughout the flight and deploying new generation Starlink satellites.

    The longer Starship can spend in orbit, the more SpaceX can test the craft and collect valuable information.

    The long flight should also allow the company to line the capsule up for a re-entry point near the west coast of Chile, but if required, it could end the flight earlier.
