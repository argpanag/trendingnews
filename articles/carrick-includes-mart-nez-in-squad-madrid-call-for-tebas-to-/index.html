Real Madrid demand 'immediate resignation' of La Liga president over Goebbels comparisons

Real Madrid has again demanded the resignation of Javier Tebas, the president of La Liga, after he compared the club’s efforts to discredit referees with the propaganda techniques of the German Nazi regime, writes AP.

Javier Tebas lashed out at Madrid on Friday on social media with a long post that criticized its club television channel’s habit of running reels of what it considers glaring errors by match officials who are set to work their upcoming games. Tebas compared the tactic with those of Joseph Goebbels, the minister of propaganda in World War II Nazi Germany.

“The executive of Real Madrid has an orchestra of mouthpieces and Real Madrid leads the way,” Tebas wrote. “Its script is always the same: select plays that favour it, repeat the alleged affront, increase suspicion and name enemies. It reminds one of the propaganda techniques that Goebbels used.”

Tebas added “the comparison is with the techniques, not with the ideology or, of course, with Madrid fans.”

Madrid responded by saying in a statement that Tebas, who has clashed several times over the years with club president Florentino Pérez, was unfit to hold his job representing Spain’s top clubs, demanding his “immediate resignation.”

“Javier Tebas’s repeated conduct demonstrates a lack of neutrality, respect, and impartiality incompatible with his institutional responsibilities,” it said. “His constant attacks on Real Madrid and those demanding transparency and regeneration represent, in our club’s view, a genuine scourge for Spanish football.”

Coach Jose Mourinho has also voiced complaints that Madrid, which holds a record 36 Spanish league titles, is regularly hurt by referees.

In September, Madrid demanded Tebas step down after he criticized the club for attacking the refereeing crew in the 2-1 loss at Atletico Madrid .

In Friday’s statement, Madrid repeated its complaint that Tebas hasn’t done enough over the ongoing judicial probe into Barcelona’s alleged payments to an official on the refereeing committee of the Spanish soccer federation for several years.

Spain have been fined and told to partially close the stadium hosting their next international friendly after Islamophobic chanting was heard during a match against Egypt earlier this year, writes the Press Association.

A section of Spain fans chanted “whoever doesn’t jump is a Muslim” during the friendly in Barcelona on March 31, with anti-discrimination messages displayed on the big screens and announcements made at half-time urging fans to stop the chants.

The Spanish federation (RFEF) sought to have the disciplinary process thrown out, in part on the grounds that the word Muslim is the accepted term for a follower of Islam.

However, Fifa’s disciplinary committee concluded the chants “clearly use religious identity as a means of distinguishing and discriminating against the opposing side” and said a reasonable and objective observer would see the word “Muslim” used in this context “as a marker of exclusion and ridicule”.

The RFEF has been fined $20,000 (£15,000) and ordered to close 15% of the stadium for their next men’s senior team friendly, or fill that percentage of seats with community or special interest groups, such as families, students and anti-discrimination organisations, subject to Fifa’s approval.

Spain star Lamine Yamal said at the time he found the chants “intolerable” as a Muslim himself, adding: “I understand not all fans are like this, but to those who sing these things, using a religion as a mockery on the field makes you ignorant and racist people. Football is to be enjoyed and encouraged, not to disrespect people for who they are or what they believe in.”
