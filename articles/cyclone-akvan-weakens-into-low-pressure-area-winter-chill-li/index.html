Cyclone Akvan weakens into low-pressure area, winter chill likely to increase from Tuesday

TheTools · 21.11.2026 11:50 · source · permalink

Bangladesh

Rain may continue in parts of the country over the next two days, while night temperatures are forecast to fall gradually.

 

The impact of Cyclone Akvan on Bangladesh is beginning to ease, with rainfall expected to decrease from Tuesday (24 November) and mostly dry weather forecast across the country, according to the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD).

The department also expects night temperatures to fall gradually, bringing a further chill to the weather.

The outlook was detailed in the BMD's five-day forecast issued today (21 November) and signed by meteorologist Md Shahinul Islam.

According to the forecast, the cyclone weakened into a deep depression, then a depression, before further weakening into a well-marked low-pressure area. By 6am, it had weakened further into a low-pressure area over Odisha and adjoining areas.

From 9am, light to moderate rain or thundershowers, accompanied by temporary gusty winds, are likely in many places across Rajshahi, Dhaka, Khulna, Barishal and Chattogram divisions, and in a few places in Rangpur, Mymensingh and Sylhet divisions over the following 24 hours.

Moderate to heavy rainfall is also likely at isolated places in Rajshahi, Dhaka, Khulna, Barishal and Chattogram divisions during this period.

Day and night temperatures across the country are expected to remain nearly unchanged.

During the next 24 hours, rain or thundershowers may occur at one or two places across all eight divisions. Night temperatures are likely to remain nearly unchanged, while daytime temperatures may rise by 1–2 degrees Celsius.

Rainfall is expected to decrease further over the following 24 hours. Light rain may occur at one or two places in Rangpur, Rajshahi, Mymensingh, Dhaka and Sylhet divisions, while the weather is likely to remain mostly dry elsewhere, with partly cloudy skies.

Night temperatures may fall slightly during this period, while daytime temperatures may rise slightly.

From Tuesday (24 November), the weather is likely to remain mostly dry across the country for the following 48 hours, with partly cloudy skies at times. Night temperatures may fall slightly, while daytime temperatures are expected to remain nearly unchanged.

No significant change in day or night temperatures is expected on Wednesday (25 November).

The BMD said no significant change in the country's overall weather conditions is likely over the five days following the forecast period.
