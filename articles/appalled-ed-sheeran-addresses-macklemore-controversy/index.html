'Appalled': Ed Sheeran addresses Macklemore controversy

TheTools · 15.09.2026 22:03 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Ed Sheeran has broken his silence after dropping American rapper Macklemore from his US tour over “free Palestine” comments that pro-Israel advocates – including billionaire and stadium owner Robert Kraft – deemed “hate speech”.

Macklemore was removed from the remainder of Sheeran’s Loop Tour in the US this week after opening for two of his 10 scheduled shows.

The rapper drew controversy for pro-Palestinian comments during a brief speech on stage at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, where he expressed solidarity with people in Gaza and the occupied West Bank.

The performance, which also included a protest song and images of the destruction in Gaza onscreen, was condemned by pro-Israel groups for promoting “anti-Israel propaganda” and prompted a petition calling for his removal from the rest of the tour.

One day after Macklemore addressed the fallout on social media, Sheeran clarified his stance to fans, claiming he tried to find a resolution for everyone involved but was unsuccessful.

“I am appalled by the conflict between Israel and Palestine,” Sheeran wrote in a bullet-pointed statement.

“The suffering and pain caused on both sides is a human tragedy. There are too many wars across the world causing immeasurable pain, none of which should be tolerated.”

Sheeran said venues on the tour “communicated that they would pull the shows if he was still supporting”.

“I am not complicit. I have my personal views on this devastating conflict.

“Just because I choose not to speak publicly, doesn’t mean I don’t have them, and it doesn’t mean I don’t care. Nor does it mean I don’t support causes in my own, personal way.”

Sheeran said he refrains from using his platform to promote his political views, given his audience skews young and comes from “all backgrounds”.

“Those who come to my shows do not expect a political forum. I respect Macklemore’s strength of purpose to stand up and shout for what he believes.

“However, there is room for multiple approaches to the same end: peace. I choose to use my fame and platform to be a place of safety and sanctuary, to maintain diplomacy and keep conversations open, not closed.

“If we only focus on shouting the loudest, nothing will ever change.”

Sheeran said he’d spent all week speaking to the venues and other stakeholders in search of a middle ground, but stressed that the final decision was made by the venues and the promoter, who Macklemore’s tour contract was signed with.

“I would never let down the fans who had made plans, nor would I abandon the touring crew and other support acts and musicians who rely on me for work and to make a living,” he said.

“I know who I am, both as a person and as an artist, and those who know my heart know my values too.”

In an Instagram post yesterday, Macklemore said Sheeran had dropped him from the tour after an ultimatum from Kraft, the billionaire owner of the New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium in Massachusetts. The stadium is the next stop for Sheeran’s tour.

Macklemore has been an outspoken supporter of Palestine for years, long before joining Sheeran’s Loop concerts, with the Grammy-winning rapper debuting a pro-Palestinian protest song, Hind’s Hall, during a 2024 performance in Wellington.

He told fans the announcement came after a week of intense discussions with Sheeran, who he has been friends with for 13 years and considers a “brother”.

“As I told Ed multiple times on the phone, our friendship can’t change my responsibility to tell the truth about what happened,” Macklemore wrote.

According to the rapper, Sheeran received a call from Kraft telling him he would not allow Macklemore to perform at Gillette Stadium.

“Ed was in a f***ed-up position,” he wrote. “He knew it. His typical apolitical stance was being challenged in a way it had never been before.

“What I said at MetLife I have been saying on stages around the world for almost three years. So why is it an issue now? Because now I’m sharing the stage with one of the biggest artists in the world, in some of the biggest stages in America.”

Macklemore said he understood speaking up on Palestine would cost artists money and he has lost a lot for doing so – but he added that did not stop him from wanting to do what he believed was right.

“And I get why people don’t. Taking a side can cost you. Money, brand deals, sponsorships, festivals, private shows, relationships and access. I’ve lost all of those things. But there is no neutral position between the oppressor and the oppressed. When one side has the bombs and bottomless financial and military support from America, refusing to take a side doesn’t leave you in the middle,” he wrote.

“If those words cost me the stage, while turning the conversation back to Palestine, then it was the most successful tour I’ve ever been on.”

According to Rolling Stone magazine, the rapper was meant to open for Sheeran on eight of the remaining 10 shows of the tour.

Multiple venues objected to Macklemore performing after he called for a free Palestine and spoke up on the situation in Gaza.

In a speech on stage on September 4, while opening for Sheeran, Macklemore said: “A big part of the reason why I wanted to do this tour in the first place is so I could stand up in stadiums like this and say two words that are very near and dear to my heart: ‘Free Palestine’.”

He told the audience he wanted people in Gaza to know they were not forgotten. “I said: ‘Free Palestine!’ I want those words to be loud and clear, so that people all the way in Gaza to the occupied West Bank know that we have not forgotten about them.”

Addressing the controversy, Macklemore said neither he nor Sheeran were victims.

“We have careers, money, opportunities, safety and audiences around the world,” he said. “Whatever consequences any of us face for what we say, we get to go home to our families.

“The victims are the Palestinian people. The men, women and children who have been killed, starved, displaced and forced to live through unimaginable violence.”
