Prediction market giant Kalshi is hiring someone to play GTA VI—no resume or cover letter is required, and it pays up to $135,000 | Fortune

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:15 · source · permalink

Ahead of Grand Theft Auto VI’s release on Thursday, companies across the country could face millions of dollars in lost productivity. But Kalshi is turning that workplace distraction into a high-paying new job. The $40 billion prediction market giant is hiring a “GTA VI Researcher” to play through the entirety of the highly anticipated game.

“We’re looking for someone whose parents said video games would never pay the bills. They were wrong, and you can tell them we said so,” the job listing stated.

The gig comes with a hefty paycheck: a $100,000 to $120,000 fixed project fee, plus an additional $15,000 completion bonus. The successful applicant will begin playing at midnight on November 19—livestreaming with camera via YouTube or Twitch—and continue until beating the game’s story mode. 

Exactly how long that will take will vary by player. Rob Nelson, who oversaw the game’s development at Rockstar Games, told The New York Times that his own playthrough in Leonida—the game’s fictional U.S. state name—took about 80 hours, including the main story and some optional side quests.

“I hope that there will be people that want to get up, have a cup of coffee, shower, get dressed, go out for a little exercise,” Nelson said.

Kalshi has explicitly noted that “sleep and touching grass are both allowed,” meaning the researcher won’t have to power through the game in one sitting. But playing through the story is only part of the assignment.

Kalshi’s $135,000 “GTA VI” job has attracted more than 25,000 applicants

If the Kalshi gig sounds too good to be true, the company has specifically said it’s a “real job.”

Ryan Conlon, who leads talent at Kalshi, said in a LinkedIn post over the weekend that the listing attracted 25,515 applications in less than 48 hours, making it the most-applied-to job in the company’s history.

Not just any gamer will qualify. Kalshi is looking for someone at least 18 years old with a decade of “sweaty” gaming experience, including at least three years of livestreaming. Applicants must also have their own equipment, internet connection, and streaming software. When applying, no resume or cover letter is necessary—instead, just a few multiple-choice and short-answer questions, including, “What’s the first thing you’re doing in Leonida?”

The researcher will also be expected to investigate the in-game economy, produce weekly research reports, and analyze the real-world fallout of the game’s release. They’ll also be tasked with “min-maxing everything”—meaning finding the most efficient strategies and exploiting every possible advantage.

“Find what’s broken, what’s mid, and what’s actually goated, and bring receipts,” the listing added. 

Fortune reached out to Kalshi for further comment.

GTA VI could generate billions—but its release could cause headaches for employers

GTA VI is expected to generate billions of dollars in revenue for parent company Take-Two Interactive. But for employers around the world, the game’s release could come with a very different price tag: lost productivity as millions of workers take the day off, call out sick, or sneak away during work hours to play.

But some companies are getting ahead of the inevitable distraction—and even embracing it.

The U.S. Army, for example, used the game’s hype as a recruiting tool, offering active-duty soldiers a four-day pass to play the game as an incentive to reenlist.

Meanwhile, $13 billion tech giant Logitech announced last week that it would give its 7,000 global employees a day off to play video games. CEO Hanneke Faber said the dedicated gaming time is expected to actually be a company productivity boost—and she plans to take advantage of the perk herself and spend part of the release day playing GTA VI.

“It’s time to retire the stereotype that gaming is a waste of time,” Faber said at the time. “As a company designing products for work and play, we see every day how engagement in one sphere supports performance in the other. ”

Preston Fore is a reporter on Fortune's Success team.
