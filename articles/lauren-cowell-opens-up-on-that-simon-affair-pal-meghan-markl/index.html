Lauren Cowell opens up on THAT Simon affair, pal Meghan Markle & wedding plans

TheTools · 03.10.2026 11:20 · source · permalink

LAUREN Cowell may be physically present in our east London studio, but her mind is 5,462 miles away.

She and her music mogul other half, Simon, spent the summer in Malibu with their son Eric, 12, and Lauren’s 20-year-old son Adam, while Simon filmed America’s Got Talent.

When Lauren, 49, returned to Britain with Eric for the start of the school year, Simon stayed behind in California after undergoing surgery linked to the serious back injury he suffered in 2020.

But the distance is proving a struggle for his partner of 12 years.

“He’s back home now but, in terms of recovery, I don’t know when he’s going to get better or when he’s going to be able to fly, so I don’t know when he’s coming back,” says Lauren.

“It’s hard. Eric and I are really missing Simon. I’m feeling a bit overwhelmed. I’m a nurturer and we’re so close, so being this far away feels very odd. I feel helpless.”

It’s a raw, exposing admission from a woman who has deliberately stayed out of the spotlight but is now taking a very different path.

Viewers of last year’s Netflix series Simon Cowell: The Next Act got their first proper introduction to Lauren – who is also a guest on our brilliant No Parental Guidance parenting podcast in an episode out today.

Cameras spent two years filming inside the Cowell family residences in south-west London, Oxfordshire and Malibu as Simon put together his latest boy band, December 10. But they also captured the woman behind years of tabloid headlines.

In 2013, Lauren became pregnant with Simon’s child while still married to businessman Andrew Silverman, Adam’s dad. The fallout was huge.

Today, she admits that for years she carried deep shame about how their relationship began.

“I felt ashamed and embarrassed, like I had a scarlet letter on my chest,” explains Lauren. “For a long time I struggled a lot with it. I get why people had that opinion of me. I would feel exactly the same way if I was looking in as an outsider.”

Now, in the final months of her 40s, after therapy, plenty of self-reflection and ditching alcohol, Lauren has made peace with her past.

“I’m not trying to evade accountability or say that what I did was correct, but I’m human, and it happened. What am I supposed to do for the rest of my life? F**king beat myself up over it? I don’t think that’s fair.”

So, how does she feel now when she looks back at the Lauren of 13 years ago?

“I feel a lot of compassion for that girl,” she says, her voice breaking. “It makes me want to hug my former self. I beat myself up so much and allowed what other people were saying about me to define me.”

Another high-profile American woman who has endured intense public judgement springs to mind – Meghan Markle. Lauren – chatty, friendly and unguarded – reveals that she and the Duchess have already connected over Zoom.

“I definitely feel a lot of, I don’t want to say similaritie­s, but I can relate to her in a lot of ways,” says Lauren, explaining they have discussed “some charity work stuff”, including their shared interest in children’s online safety.

It’s predominantly the reason why Lauren is here today. An active supporter of Raise The Age, a UK campaign lobbying for a social media ban for under-16s unless platforms can prove they are safe, she is now determined to use her position in the spotlight for good.

“I’m so privileged and grateful to be able to be a mother. But I always knew that I had more to say and more to give. I just didn’t know what it was.”

All became clear when Lauren heard the story of British mum Ellen Roome, whose 14-year-old son Jools died in April 2022, in circumstances that may have been linked to a dangerous online challenge.

Lauren reached out to Ellen – who has spent years campaigning for bereaved parents to be allowed access to their children’s digital data – and the pair forged a bond.

Now, Lauren is executive producing a drama she has sold to ITV, working alongside acclaimed screenwriter Jeff Pope, telling Ellen and Jools’ story. Remarkably, it is Lauren’s first proper job.

“Literally, at 50 years old, I’m starting work,” she laughs. “I never worked. I was always a stay-at-home mum, so this is totally new for me.”

She insists she has never felt any frustration living in the shadow of one of entertainment’s most powerful men.

“I was quite happy being the supportive partner and full-time mum. But this is an opportunity to make a difference and make a legacy,” she explains.

Earning her own money appeals, too, of course.

“I want to get to the point where I can put away money for my children and for myself,” she says. “Having that freedom and independence, but also knowing what I’m doing is going to help other people.”

For years, Lauren stayed a Silverman for the sake of Adam, who studies business in Miami, the city where she grew up. But when her son reached adulthood, she legally became a Cowell in 2024 – just without organising a wedding.

“Having to quantify your relationship by a piece of paper seems a bit antiquated to me,” she says, before conceding that the nuptials will eventually happen.

“Will we get married? Yes, when we’re ready and when the time is right. We’ve been through everything together – death, divorce, childbirth, business. In terms of all the ingredients for a marriage, of course we’re married. We just don’t have the piece of paper yet.”

After a rocky first few years in the spotlight, public opinion shifted as the world saw Lauren’s genuine connection with Simon.

She was credited with taming telly’s so-called Mr Nasty – softening the famously work-obsessed bachelor and introducing him to a family life he always insisted was never for him.

“You can’t quantify what having a child is going to do to you,” says Lauren.

“Having Eric and having me has really opened up another side of his personality and emotions.”

She and Simon once considered “maybe having another child”, but life intervened. Lauren was regularly travelling to New York to see Adam under her shared custody arrangement, while Simon’s beloved mum Julie died following a stroke, eight months after Eric was born.

“That was a really rough time,” says Lauren. “He always says: ‘Thank god I have you and Eric,’ because he was so low.”

It’s a glimpse of Simon few beyond his inner circle see, and Lauren offers plenty more insight.

“He’s really shy, almost to the point where it’s debilitating. Walking into a cocktail party is his idea of hell,” she says. “He hates small talk, whereas I can talk to a brick wall. I have to get him pepped up to go anywhere.”

At home, the couple love old-school telly – shows like Columbo and Marple – while Simon’s drinks order is equally intriguing. He imports Corona Premier because he can’t get it in Britain, pours half the contents away, then tops up the bottom with orange-flavoured sparkling water.

As for Simon’s wardrobe, it remains famously monochrome.

“Sometimes, I’ll wear a pair of trousers and he’s like: ‘Wait, I want those.’ We’re becoming the same person!” Lauren laughs.

Well, not exactly. Not when it comes to parenting.

“We both care about kindness, respect and our kids being polite and having manners, but our parenting styles are so opposite. I’m more regimented and strict. He’s the yes guy.”

Describing Simon as “the eternal naughty child”, Lauren recalls how she caught him committing a big parenting faux pas.

“One of us always lies with Eric before bed and I used to wonder: ‘Why don’t you ever pick me?’ One night I realised why. I thought they were reading, but Eric was watching YouTube with him!’

The anecdote exposes something understood by many couples – the division of parental labour. It’s a niggle that was briefly exposed in The Next Act, when Lauren took a moment away from the cameras during a disagreement.

“Simon’s very involved and hands-on, but in terms of the day-to-day schedule and routine, it’s me in the trenches. I’m doing most of the logistics, so I need order and control,” she says.

When Lauren returns to Miami later this month for Adam’s parents’ weekend at the University of Miami, the wider blended family will gather, including ex-husband Andrew, his wife Samantha and their kids.

“They’re all coming over to my mom’s house and she’s cooking a brisket,” says Lauren, whose own parents divorced amicably when she was five. And it says everything about how far she has travelled since 2013.

“I always knew we would get there, or I hoped we would. Honestly, if we can get through what we got through, anyone can.”

After Simon’s 67th birthday next week, the next big celebration will be Lauren’s 50th – which is hard to believe. She could easily pass for 10, even 20, years younger.

Simon has been open about overdoing injectables in the past and giving up filler after Eric was “in hysterics” over his dad’s altered appearance.

As for Lauren?

“I’m open to trying things, let’s put it that way,” she says, preferring not to “be specific” about any work she may have had done. “I feel that people should do what they want to do and what feels right for them.”

What was the last. . .

Time you cried?

Just five minutes ago. Well, almost!

Argument you had?

With my mom. We argue all the time. I love her, but she treats me like I’m 12!

TV show you watched?

Currently, it’s The Five-Star Weekend. It’s based on the book by Elin Hilderbrand.

Kiss you had?

With Simon, before I left him in Malibu.

Message you sent?

To my personal shopper at Selfridges. I said: “I’m back. Can you send me some stuff?”

Thing you splurged on?

I bought a bag this morning from Khaite, which is really nice, but I might take it back because it was a lot.

Lauren has followed US fitness coach Tracy Anderson’s workouts for two decades and also enjoys Pilates, cycling, swimming, padel and paddleboarding.

“My mental health is directly linked to my activity level. Some people enjoy knitting or cooking but for me, movement has a really positive effect.”

There are, refreshingly, no strict diet rules.

“I love bread, pasta, sweets and pastries,” she says, although she leans towards “clean” food and gave up drinking a couple of years ago.

“For a long time, alcohol was a way for me to maybe not deal with stuff, or to escape or suppress it,” she says. “But the irony is it does the opposite. It amplified things. I used to cry a lot.”

She also says being teetotal has helped ease her perimenopausal symptoms. “Drinking when you’re going through perimenopause – the combination of those two things is lethal.”

Lauren stopped drinking after a Christmas party hosted by socialite Tamara Ecclestone, who was a fellow mum at Eric’s school at the time.

“She’s the reason I stopped drinking. I asked her: ‘Do you not want a glass of champagne?’ and she said: ‘No, I don’t drink. I don’t like the way it makes me feel.’

“I woke up the following day and thought: ‘I don’t like the way it makes me feel either.’ And that was it.”

For her big birthday next July, Lauren plans to whisk a small group of her friends – she’s close to Ayda Field, Robbie Williams, Bruno Tonioli and Nicole Scherzinger – away somewhere nice and hot for an intimate celebration.

“I feel so excited. It sounds corny, but everyone says 50 is the new 30, and I really feel like I am coming into my own,” she smiles. “Getting older is a privilege. I want to embrace it.”
