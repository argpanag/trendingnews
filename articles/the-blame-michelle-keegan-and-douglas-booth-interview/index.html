The Blame: Michelle Keegan and Douglas Booth interview

TheTools · 20.09.2026 10:55 · source · permalink

Entertainment

  • Abby Allen

Credit: ITV/David Hindley

Credit: ITV/David Hindley

Michelle Keegan and Douglas Booth sit down with Stylist to talk about bringing detectives Emma and Tom to life in ITV’s upcoming crime drama The Blame. 

There’s nothing like a truly gripping British crime drama to tempt you indoors as the evenings draw in. Following in the footsteps of titles like Broadchurch, Line Of Duty and Happy Valley, ITV’s The Blame combines a twisty murder investigation with the treacherous politics of a fictional police force. Based on Charlotte Langley’s novel and written by Megan Gallagher (All Her Fault), it ventures into uncomfortably familiar territory, exploring institutional failings and the people who enable them.

Set in the fictional world of Wakestead, the series begins when the body of a teenage figure skater is discovered, sending shockwaves through the town. Detective duo DI Emma Crane (Michelle Keegan) and DT Tom Radley (Douglas Booth) are tasked with investigating, but the case soon draws them into a tangled web of lies, institutional cover-ups and moral compromise. As the investigation unfolds, Emma is forced to turn her attention to her own team and question the people she thought she could trust.

Credit: ITV/David Hindley

Speaking to Stylist ahead of the launch date, Keegan and Booth explain it was the script that initially hooked them both – as well as working with each other and the creatives at Quay Street Productions. “I got to the end and I was so shocked,” says Keegan. “That’s [always] a really good sign if I want to do a drama – I have to want to tell the story.”

Both actors also drew on the expertise of real-life detective Lisa Farrand, who previously advised on Happy Valley. “I felt really confident when she was on set, especially when we did interrogation scenes,” says Keegan. “If she read something that wasn’t 100% correct, she’d always ask to pull it out or add something to the script. So when she was on set, I knew we were going to be safe hands.”

At the heart of the show is an exploration of systemic sexism – and the difference between not participating in that behaviour and actively challenging it. For Tom, speaking up is far from straightforward, and Booth says the BBC’s Panorama investigation into misconduct at Charing Cross police station helped inform his preparation for the role.

Credit: ITV/David Hindley

“Firstly, we hope people will be entertained, but I hope that it uncovers some of the ugly truths,” says Booth. “From a male point of view, I hope that men who watch it will also question some of their own behaviour – even if they recognise micro details of behaviour that impact other people. Or if they themselves have been made to feel uncomfortable and aren’t able to speak out and challenge that authority. Because that’s what’s always playing within it – people are keeping their head below the parapet, but at what point do you become complicit?”

For Emma, challenging that culture means confronting corruption within her own male-dominated police force. “I love playing strong female roles, and I feel like Emma was exactly that. She holds her own; she’s opinionated when it matters. She’s got a good moral compass and she goes above and beyond for justice,” says Keegan.

Alongside its darker themes, the series promises the twists and turns of a classic ITV crime drama, with plenty of intrigue to keep viewers hooked across all six episodes. So what are its stars most excited for audiences to see? “I’ve just finished them all, and I would say [episodes] five and six,” says Booth. “Just because you start to get some really juicy answers. My wife was trying to talk to me while I was watching it, and I was like, ‘Do not talk to me!’ It gets really intense.”

Twists, tension and a quest for justice? Consider our Sunday evenings booked and busy for the foreseeable.

The Blame lands on ITV on Sunday 20 September.
