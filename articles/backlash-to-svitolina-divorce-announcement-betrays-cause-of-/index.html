Backlash to Svitolina divorce announcement betrays cause of split with Monfils

TheTools · 24.10.2026 00:27 · source · permalink

Elina Svitolina has been unfairly painted as the villain after her separation from Gael Monfils sent shockwaves through the sport

Elina Svitolina has had to put up with a shower of online vitriol since she announced on Monday that she intends to divorce her fellow tennis player husband, Gaël Monfils.

Her Instagram post was met with countless replies, many of them hostile and misogynistic, complaining that she had ruined Monfils’s planned home farewell from tennis at the upcoming Paris Masters.

Yet there is an irony here, in that Svitolina – the world No 6 and former Olympic bronze medallist – had only made their private situation public to defend Monfils from rumours about integrity issues.

When Monfils suddenly pulled out of his remaining tennis events – including not only Paris on November 2 but also the ATP 250 tournament in Lyon this week – some commentators in his native France started querying whether he might have breached anti-doping or anti-gambling rules.

Svitolina responded by announcing her decision to end the relationship on Monday, in a move which was meant to explain the real reason why Monfils – who is 40 – had suddenly put his name on the “retired players” list on October 5.

The move was at least partially successful in its own terms, at the expense of a venomous outcry from tennis fans that painted Svitolina as the villain and Monfils as the victim.

Then, on Tuesday, Svitolina clarified the situation further in a second post that called for kindness and understanding, while making the very specific point that “unfortunately he [Monfils] had many accusations thrown at him and he didn’t deserve that, so we jointly released a statement”.

As friends of the couple have suggested to Telegraph Sport, none of this sounds like the behaviour of a heartless and selfish woman – even if that is how Svitolina has been portrayed by many uninformed observers.

Rather, she comes across as a dutiful and sympathetic partner. It is also understood that the couple are still cohabiting in Dubai, still co-parenting their four-year-old daughter Skai, and still have a high regard for each other.

No third party is said to have been involved, which has led some of those close to the couple to query whether they might have been overly hasty in placing this sad situation into the public domain. If nothing else, this sort of announcement makes it much more difficult to reconcile.

The timing has been highly unfortunate. If Monfils had not been approaching the end of his farewell tour – which was to involve not only those final two ATP events in France but also an exhibition in which he was to be joined by fellow “Four Musketeers” Richard Gasquet, Gilles Simon and Jo-Wilfried Tsonga – then the original rumours would never have started circulating in the first place.

By suddenly aborting his participation, Monfils – whose own ranking peaked at No 6 in 2016 – initiated a chain of events which has only added stress to an already challenging domestic situation.

Some tennis insiders have questioned whether he was genuinely ready to compete in any case, after a summer in which his ability to train has been constrained by both his parenting responsibilities and his new job with wealth-management firm AlphaSwiss. Telegraph Sport understands that Monfils has already begun seeking clients, starting with his peers in the ATP locker-room.

In her latest post, Svitolina effectively acknowledged being the instigator of the split by saying: “I didn’t want to keep living a life where I wasn’t truly happy.”

Her first post had also described the effect of living with constant stress from the war in her native Ukraine – which left her with “sleepless nights … and the feeling of slowly losing herself”. She also credited Monfils with “never letting go of my hand” before concluding that “some battles happen deep inside you, and no amount of love from anyone else can win them for you”.

On Tuesday, a report in the French sports newspaper L’Équipe put the story very much from Monfils’s perspective, describing him sitting at home in Dubai with Skai after Svitolina’s departure for a series of tournaments in China at the end of September when she notified him of her intentions by telephone.

As the report continued: “When Monfils learned the news … tennis, which he had often experienced as a kind of gentle madness, suddenly lost all meaning. He was no longer able to play. Hence his abrupt decision to remove himself from the anti-doping lists on October 5, a gesture of no return that ultimately reflects his personality, as he struggles to manage stressful situations.”

If Monfils responded to the call from Svitolina by bringing up the possibility of a sudden retirement, then perhaps it was intended as a way of saying “don’t make me do this”. If so, she was not to be moved.

But the ultimate effect has been to bring the situation into the public town square, and to place Svitolina under enormous pressure and scrutiny as she heads towards the biggest tournament on the WTA Tour: the upcoming finals event in Indian Wells, which she qualified for at the weekend. She may well have to ask for press conferences to be restricted to the action within the white lines of the court, otherwise questions about her marital situation will be hard to escape.

Another irony is that, towards the beginning of their relationship more than seven years ago, Monfils and Svitolina created an Instagram account called g.e.m.s.life – a title which combined their initials – to catalogue their lives together.

The account carried photographs from their wedding in July 2021, but has not been updated since their third anniversary in 2024.

As a couple, they embraced social media as part of their relationship. That same social media has taken a nuanced private situation and created a polarised narrative of goodies and baddies. It is very much a story of our times.

Representatives of Svitolina and Monfils have declined to comment.
