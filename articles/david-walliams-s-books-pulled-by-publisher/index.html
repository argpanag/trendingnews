David Walliams’s books pulled by publisher

TheTools · 18.09.2026 21:34 · source · permalink

Author’s titles will not be reprinted by HarperCollins after Telegraph investigation into reports of inappropriate behaviour

David Walliams’s books will no longer be printed by HarperCollins.

The publisher dropped the children’s author last year after a Telegraph investigation revealed allegations of inappropriate behaviour towards young women.

HarperCollins had maintained his catalogue of previous titles, but has now dropped his entire backlist.

He was one of Britain’s most successful authors, with more than 40 books and short story collections, and has sold more than 60 million copies. It was estimated in 2019 that he and HarperCollins had sold £100m worth of books.

The publisher will no longer sell or reprint his titles, which include Gangsta Granny and The Boy in the Dress.

It will also phase out its bookshop stock under the plans, first revealed by The Bookseller.

E-book and audiobook versions remain available online, but will be removed.

The Telegraph revealed in December that Walliams had been accused of harassing junior, female employees at HarperCollins, which is owned by Rupert Murdoch.

The author was at the centre of an investigation at the publisher last year after an employee raised a complaint. It is understood that staff were interviewed as part of the inquiry.

One woman who raised concerns was given a five-figure payoff and left the business.

Former staff also said employees were told to work in “pairs” when meeting Walliams and were advised not to visit his home.

He has strongly denied the allegations.

At the time, a HarperCollins spokesman told The Telegraph: “After careful consideration and under the leadership of its new CEO, HarperCollins UK has decided not to publish any new titles by David Walliams. The author is aware of this decision.”

It is understood that Walliams did not know about the HarperCollins investigation and that its conclusions were not put to him.

In December, his agent said: “David has never been informed of any allegations raised against him by HarperCollins.

“He was not party to any investigation or given any opportunity to answer questions. David strongly denies that he has behaved inappropriately and is taking legal advice.”

Claire Allfree

Walliams, who rose to fame more than 20 years ago through the comedy series Little Britain, was already a television star when he started writing children’s books in 2008.

He often produced two novels a year and was responsible for 44 per cent of HarperCollins’s sales of children’s books in the UK in 2018.

It is not known whether book rights have reverted to Walliams, which would mean that he could publish with another company in the future.

Walliams has been contacted for comment.
