UOB's 3QFY2026 net profit 10% below expectations due to 41% decline in non-II

TheTools · 06.11.2026 02:10 · source · permalink
  • U11.SI
    -2.01%

UOB's 3Q net profit was 10% below expectations due to a 41% q-o-q decline in non-II and is likely to cause a sell-off

UOB announced net profit of $1.25 billion for the 3QFY2026 ended Sept 30, down 15% q-o-q and 10% below the Street's forecast of $1.376 billion as other non-interest income fell by 41% q-o-q to $370 million. This was due to the absence of non-recurring divestment gains recorded in the previous quarter, as well as lower trading and investment income. This brings the bank's net profit for the 9MFY2026 to $4.17 billion, up 27% y-o-y.

In 3Q2026, net interest income (NII) fell by 1% q-o-q to $2.3 billion due to lower net interest margin (NIM) of 1.68% which was also below expectations. The lower NIM, according to UOB, was due to higher deposit costs.

Net fee income grew 10% q-o-q while customer-related treasury income also reached a new high. Total expenses fell 4% q-o-q to $1.6 billion, in line with income moderation. Credit costs for 3QFY2026 stayed within expectations at 24 basis points, with non-performing loan (NPL) ratio lower at 1.5%. Non-performing assets (NPA) coverage stood at 86%, while unsecured NPA coverage stood at 269%, down from 88% and 309% in the previous quarter.

In its CFO slides, the bank says its real estate and hospitality exposures are "manageable" and are "under close watch" in the key markets of greater China and the US. Portfolio repositioning is also "underway".

During the quarter, customer loans from greater China stood at $49 billion and comprised 0.5% of NPL, while loans from the US stood at $17 billion, comprising 0.1% of NPL.

The bank's NPL ratio for greater China alone stood at 5% as at end September, with NPA coverage of 40% and unsecured NPA coverage of 196%, compared to 4.8%, 42% and 323% in the quarter before. NPL ratio for the US stood at 1.3%, with NPA coverage of 214% and unsecured NPA coverage of 1,186%, compared to 1.2%, 269% and 1,243% in the quarter before.

The bank's common equity tier 1 (CET-1) ratio fell by 80 basis points (bps) q-o-q to 14.6% while fully-loaded tier 1 ratio fell by 100 bps q-o-q to 14%. This too was below expectations.

In 2027, the group is guiding for mid single-digit growth for loans and overall NII to come above FY2026's figures. Fee income is expected to grow by mid to high single-digits, while operating costs are also estimated to grow by did to high single-digits. Total credit costs are guided to be between 25 to 30 bps.

Deputy chairman and CEO Wee Ee Cheong says UOB continued to see healthy customer activity across its businesses this quarter, with strong growth in wealth management, healthy loan growth and record fees and customer-related treasury income.
