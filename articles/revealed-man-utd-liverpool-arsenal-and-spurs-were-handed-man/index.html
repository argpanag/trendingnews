Revealed: Man Utd, Liverpool, Arsenal and Spurs were handed Man City guilty verdict early

TheTools · 10.10.2026 00:39 · source · permalink

Exclusive: Telegraph Sport understands the four clubs who gave City legal notice of right to seek compensation were informed in advance

The four Premier League clubs who reserved their right to seek compensation from Manchester City were notified of the guilty verdict last month before the rest of the league.

Telegraph Sport understands that Manchester United, Liverpool, Arsenal and Tottenham Hotspur were all contacted in advance of the email to the other 15 clubs on September 25 – which required executives to sign a non-disclosure agreement to view. 

None of the four clubs would comment on the early notification when contacted. The Premier League declined to comment. 

In September 2024, shortly before the six-year limit on giving notice to seek damages in the event of a guilty verdict for City, the four clubs had instructed legal counsel individually to give notice to City that they reserved the right to seek compensation.

At the time there was no means by which any Premier League club could contact the independent commission to be advised on whether, in the event of one or more guilty verdicts against City, the issue of compensation would play a role in the judgment, or potential sanctioning.

The rest of the Premier League shareholders at the time, including Chelsea, who subsequently faced Premier League and Football Association charges over historic wrongdoing, declined to give notice. That is not thought to preclude those clubs from compensation claims, although it may complicate the procedure.

City were found guilty of all but one of the Premier League charges in what amounted to a deeply critical verdict from the commission – the members of which have not yet been named – that was published eight days ago. 

The core decision document, as it was known, ran to 40 pages and was heavily redacted in parts. There are also extensive appendices to the decision which are yet to be published and would play a role in specific compensation claims.

The question of compensation hangs over the outcome of the process, with Burnley having already been given an award of £35m against Everton relating to the 2021-22 season. Everton, who are appealing, were subsequently found to have breached financial controls in the 2021-22 season, at the end of which Burnley were relegated in 18th place.

City have given notice that they will appeal the verdict, which will be heard by a newly assembled three-person commission. The appeal commission has to return a decision, under Premier League rules, within 12 weeks – giving a deadline of January 21. The club say they have “a comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence”. Upon announcing their appeal, City said that the verdict comprised “on multiple grounds, the opinion that contains clear material errors, of law, principle and fact, and is unsafe”.

A successful appeal would have to demonstrate that the three people on the commission made material errors on points of law or procedural mistakes. The commission found the club had benefited from more than £920m to its bottom line between 2009 and 2018 with £830m from what was described as “sham contracts” – sponsorship deals inflated by back-channelled funds from City’s owner, the Abu Dhabi United Group.

Kyle Walker, the former Manchester City right-back now at Burnley, who won six Premier League titles with City, told Talksport radio: “Obviously it doesn’t look great. I know it’s in an appeal stage so, for me to start commenting on the legal action and everything like that, probably it’s not going to do myself justice or anyone involved in it.

“But, at a personal level, I can say the lads, we spent many and many a great year celebrating many trophies and everything like that. That’s something that, whatever happens, they can’t take away from us because we’ve experienced those nights. So, whatever will be, will be, and it’s just down to the appeal now and what goes from there.”
