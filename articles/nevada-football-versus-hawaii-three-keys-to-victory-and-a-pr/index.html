The Nevada football team plays Hawaii at Mackay Stadium on Saturday. Nevada Sports Net’s Chris Murray breaks down the game against the Rainbow Warriors with his three keys to victory and prediction. This feature is presented in partnership with Bradley, Drendel & Jeanney.

Hawaii (3-7, 2-5 MW) at Nevada (4-6, 3-3 MW)

When: Saturday, 7:30 p.m.

Where: Mackay Stadium (capacity 27,000)

Surface: FieldTurf

Weather: High of 38; low of 20 (10 percent chance of rain)

TV/Radio: FS1/105.7 FM

Online: None

Betting line: Nevada by 4.5 points; total of 40.5

All-time series: Nevada leads series, 15-14

Last matchup: Hawaii won, 34-13, on Oct. 26, 2024 (in Honolulu)

Three keys to the game

1. Red zone defense: Hawaii’s offense has been a major issue, scoring just 19.6 ppg, which ranks 125th out of 138 FBS teams. That is down from last year’s 29 ppg. Part of the issue has been a less explosive set of wide receivers after losing Jackson Harris to LSU, Brandon White to Kansas State and Pofele Ashlock to eligibility issues. Hawaii, however, has been able to move the ball better than its scoring total indicates, averaging 360.9 yards per game, higher than Nevada’s average of 318.9. The Rainbow Warriors just haven’t been good in the red zone, scoring on 72.9 percent of its red zone possessions (127th in the nation) while converting those opportunities into touchdowns 45.9 percent of the time (you’d prefer that to be in the 60s). Nevada’s defense must continue Hawaii’s red zone woes.

2. Edge explosiveness: After being held to 56 rushing yards against UCLA and New Mexico, the Wolf Pack got its ground game on track last week at Northern Illinois. Nevada rushed for 308 yards, its second-highest total this season. The Wolf Pack attempted just 13 passes, its third fewest since moving to the FBS in 1992. On a frigid night at Mackay Stadium, Nevada will try and do the same against Hawaii, which is allowing 162.5 run yards per game and 4.6 yards per carry. It has allowed 18 rushing scores. Hawaii is coming off a strong run defense contest last week when it held North Dakota State to 86 yards on 36 carries, only the Bison’s second game this year below 125. Nevada’s ground game will be a huge indictor in who wins this one, with Herschel Turner Jr. attacking the edges of the line with his speed a major deciding factor. He rushed for 153 yards on 13 carries last week, averaging 11.8 yards per touch.

3. Punish Alejado: Hawaii will take the opposite offensive approach, with quarterback Micah Alejado leading the nation in pass attempts with 423. He’s completed 61.2 percent of those for a Mountain West-best 2,720 yards. He’s also been sacked 30 times; Hawaii has allowed the 10th most sacks nationally. The Rainbow Warriors have started six offensive line groups, so there’s been little continuity up front. Hawaii did start its original starting five up front last week against North Dakota State but allowed five sacks, including a big hit late in the game. The 5-foot-10, 180-pound Alejado has taken a number of big hits this year. Given how often Hawaii is going to throw the ball, the Wolf Pack needs a strong pass rush from its front four, which it should be able to produce behind defensive ends Dylan LaBarbera and Myles Williams plus interior lineman David Payne Jr., who had two sacks last week.

Prediction

Nevada 20, Hawaii 17: Nevada needs a win to keep alive its bowl aspirations while Hawaii was eliminated from bowl contention last week. It’s also going to be in the low 30s for the 7:30 p.m. kickoff at Mackay Stadium. Both of those things favor Nevada. But Timmy Change, the former Wolf Pack assistant, has owned this series, going 3-0 against Nevada with each win by a minimum of 13 points, and Hawaii was a betting-line underdog in two of those games. The Rainbow Warriors should come out with a strong effort, but they have scored more than 17 points against a MW opponent just once this season. Nevada’s defense should limit Hawaii, and the offense should do just enough. Season record: 7-3 (straight up); 4-6 (against the spread)
