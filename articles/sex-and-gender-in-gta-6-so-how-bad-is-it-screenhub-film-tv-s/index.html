Sex and gender in GTA 6 – so how bad is it? | ScreenHub: Film, TV, Streaming and Games

TheTools · 18.11.2026 23:11 · source · permalink

Games

GTA 6. Image: Rockstar Games.

One of the most interesting things for me about my playthrough of Grand Theft Auto VI was observing how sex and gender are portrayed. Obviously, I wasn’t expecting it to be one of the great feminist texts. I went in expecting to see a celebration the manosphere’s nonsense turned up to 10, but instead I got something far more nuanced.

It’s still infused with the background radiation of misogyny and dehumanisation of female NPCs that you’d expect from GTA, just in an a self-aware, more satirical way, which I had forgotten in the 12 (or so) years since I last played GTA 5.

So, let’s dive in and look at a few different ways sex and gender are represented in GTA 6, and why it matters.

Sex and gender in GTA 6 – quick links

Dehumanisation of unnamed female NPCs

The thing that really struck me in the first half of the game, and which took me the most adjustment, is that until Chapter Three, any women who weren’t Lucia were mostly spoken about or treated like pre-warmed fleshlights who are out to grift the money of hardworking, manly men. They are about as two-dimensional as you can be while still having absolutely massive boobs.

Most of the named men are shown as being bosses, gangsters or regulars aspiring to those careers, while the women are mostly shown as strippers, arm candy or pregnant. A lot of the talk about women on the radio and from NPCs was mostly talking about pursuing women for sex, or how women were just trying to scam them. Some of it came across as clear (and somewhat funny) satire of the manosphere’s victim mentality, while some of it just sounded like things I hear people say genuinely.

I understand that manosphere devotees, incels and other misogynist chuds do make up a large part of the player base for GTA. So, presumably the writers had to walk a fine line of mocking how ridiculous that mindset is, while also not offending that quite delicate and easily upset population – like the jokes in a Pixar movie playing to both the children and adults in the audience. It just means that sometimes the satire didn’t land, which made playing the game exhausting at times.

It’s also just not a particularly clever way to satirise it, when instead of being edgy, it just follows in a millennia-old tradition of dehumanising and fetishising women. Remember kids: it’s not edgy if it upholds or attempts to reinstate a dominant political structure.

(Mostly) better writing for the named female characters

In contrast, while most of the named female characters likely experience significant back pain due to their enormous chests, from Chapter Three onwards they’re mostly written as complex and interesting people with highly specialised skills.

I don’t want to call out names or details, because this is going to be as spoiler-free as I can make it. But there is a specialised character in Chapter Three that I wish we got to spend more time with before the story moved on, and a character in Chapter Five who I desperately wanted more backstory on.

I also really love the writing of Lucia. She’s not just some textbook ‘strong female character’. She has a lot more depth and nuance than that, is hands down the most interesting character in the game, and is the one who feels the most real.

Part of that is down to the acting – the lines are delivered naturally and with wit (when appropriate) – but her writing is so well-rounded, and her character is so well fleshed out that she is one of my favourite female video game characters of all time. I did not expect that from GTA.

The game even passes the Bechdel test multiple times, despite the unbalanced ratio of named female and male characters. Though, I struggle to imagine much femslash fan fiction coming out of it.

Master(baitors) of the single entendre

I am truly in awe of how many sex, masturbation and (particularly) dick jokes the writing team managed to fit in the game. It was rare to see a surface that didn’t have a reference to at least one thing that involved a penis. You have the obvious things, like the car and animal identification app Waink It, banking app BuckMe and Lyft equivalent RydeMe, but there are hundreds, if not thousands throughout the game, on almost every billboard and collectable, and I’m honestly just impressed.

Porn and strippers are everywhere in Leonida, from low budget strip joints where you definitely wouldn’t want to come into contact with any of the furniture, to the most premium houses of sin you can imagine. You can pay for lap dances for either of the main characters, and throw $1 bills at pole dancers of varying skill.

The main characters also spend a surprising amount of time in mega mansions owned by various porn magnates (all men, of course). Sex work is clearly one one of the main industries in Leonida, despite the actual workers apparently not being a particularly well respected, even in that universe.

In the background, NPC interest in sex and intimacy is portrayed with the same enthusiasm as a dog chasing a car. I’m also pretty certain most of these characters would be just as confused if they actually got what they were chasing. There’s no intimacy or humanity to it. It’s almost violent in its excess.

Early on there is a scene where you stumble past a series of orgies. It’s the first portrayal of sex in GTA 6, and instead of being sexy it comes across as the kind of thing someone who has only seen porn might imagine sex would be like.

There’s a radio station that at one point has two women talking about the time they had sex with each other. It’s the only reference to lesbianism or queer women I remember coming across in the game, and it was played for laughs, going so far over the top to mock male enjoyers of the ‘lesbian’ porn category.

Part of it is satire. But a lot of it also seems to be presented specifically for an audience of people somehow simultaneously unaware and far too aware that porn exists on the internet.

I will note, there is equal opportunity nudity. This is possibly the most penises I have seen in my life. The difference is, though, that the majority of the half and fully naked women are presented for titillation, and the dicks and half-naked men are mostly there for laughs. 

Lessons on intimacy

That’s such a huge contrast to how Lucia and Jason’s sex is portrayed. I unexpectedly actually really love how intimacy is shown between the two of them. Of course, you don’t have to play it as a ‘Good Boyfriend Simulator’, and can instead treat Lucia poorly or neutrally. But the only way to unlock the many, many sex scenes between Jason and Lucia is to have a strong romantic relationship.

Where the other depictions of sex and horniness in GTA 6 are rough, transactional, and extremely male-centric – aimed at the ‘fellas, is it gay to have sex with women’ crowd – Jason and Lucia’s sex scenes are intimate and equally wanted by both parties. They’re gentle and loving, in the way sex is in most healthy relationships. Whereas other scenes might have you sticking dildos into holes in the wall, back in Jason and Lucia’s shower it tastefully fades to black to respect their privacy.

When I asked Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative on GTA VI about the contrasting ways sex is presented in the game, he said:

‘That came about organically, just by thinking about public sex and private sex and treating them differently. Sex is very in your face in Miami – it’s a beach town so no one is wearing very much anyway.

‘And then you’ve got strip clubs existing in a much more mainstream way than most other cities. And then this tension between it being where a lot of porn is produced while having some of the strictest anti-pornography laws of any US state.

‘It would be hard to tell a story about the city and its underworld without having sex be a big part of it. And then at the same time, we wanted to have the possibility of playing out this love story and having it feel romantic and sexy and not be gratuitous.’

NPC masculinity

So many of the named male characters in the story threads are just gross. Even the ones we’re supposed to like feel as though they’re ‘good guys’. A lot of the NPCs seem to abide by manosphere ideals: not expressing emotions, taking a lot of hormones to become swole, dehumanising women, and other harmful nonsense that helps no one but the bank accounts of the grifters promoting it.

The hyper-masculine guys in particular just come across as so mentally weak. Sure, physically they could probably snap me like a twig, but they have the energy of a guy who’s too afraid to wipe his own bum in case touching his own arse makes him gay.

Then there are the radio stations discussing manosphere themes and conspiracy theories, which veer between sharp satire, failed attempts at satire, and just the regular manosphere talking points.

Humphries says the exaggerated depiction of the manosphere was in response to the real world.

‘We try to represent a version of the world we are living in at this exact moment as best as we can. It should almost feel like nostalgia for the present moment in time. So if something like the manosphere is represented it’s because that’s what we are seeing when we log onto YouTube or Instagram. Stupidity, hubris, hypocrisy are all great material for us, so we point that out wherever we see it.’

I fully admit that I am not an expert in men, which is what made it so interesting (and mildly horrifying) for me to see a work so firmly rooted in the straight male gaze. Even the scenes with male strippers are more focused on being a male power fantasy than being something that appeals to straight women.

Jason’s masculinity

I’ve read a few online threads where Online Men are super angry that Jason is woke. I would argue that he’s the most normal and grounded man in the game. Aside from the murder, he comes across as just a regular guy.

Some of those same triggered Online Men are also going around calling Jason a feminist, like that’s a bad thing. Yet this is a guy who sits around while his friends make some truly gross comments about women. He doesn’t join in, but never once calls his friends out.

I only noted down one time when he actually called out a character for their increasingly aggressive misogynistic comments, but these comments were about Lucia (‘his’ woman) and this man was not a friend. Not being gross is the baseline, being a good man involves calling in their friends.

That said, one of Jason’s best traits is that he comes across as comfortable and confident in his own skin. He’s not trying to prove that he’s the manliest man to ever live, because he’s not insecure about that. He is clearly extremely masculine, he’s just also charismatic and respectful of others. Of all the male characters in the game, he seems like the one you would be most likely to see in the real world and actually talk to. It’s not a coincidence he’s also one of the very few characters in the game in a healthy relationship.

One of the things I really love about GTA 6 is that it strongly encourages the player to have a strong relationship with Lucia, and then provides a checklist on how to woo a girl you like and keep the relationship strong:

  • Make your home nice for her
  • Make sure there’s fresh food and flowers when she gets out of prison
  • Periodically check in to see how the other is going when on separate missions.
  • Ensure you spend time together
  • Chat about each other’s lives while driving around
  • Try new things together, like car racing, meditation and murder – not just focusing on the interests of one partner, but trying each other’s hobbies with an open mind

The little relationship-building detail I enjoyed the most was that you have the option to hold hands when you walk around, including walking into a bank to rob it or escaping the police. It’s such a little thing, but it really builds their relationship, and it’s kind of sweet. If these horny teenage players want to see Lucia naked, first they have to learn how to be a good boyfriend, hand-holding included.

It would be amazing if GTA 6 somehow taught a generation of young men how to be a good partner. What a gift that would be – though it seems unlikely, given a lot of the rest of this list.

How will this be framed outside video game circles?

I can’t see the future, but here are my predictions.

Some conservative commentator is going to walk past the TV at the wrong time and we’re going to have to endure an entire news cycle about how there’s interactive porn in the game (there isn’t, not in the traditional way) and they’re going to call for the game to be banned. No one tell these people about Steam.

Someone will try to have a valid discussion about the misogyny in the game, and how this kind of casual, background radiation form of misogyny (whether it’s played for satire or not) can be harmful for younger or more impressionable players if not paired with conversations about respect, consent and what the game is trying to satirise. This will turn into some kind of insult-slinging match, ignoring the nuance and the actual valid, core issue will be ignored in the quest for more clickable headlines and social media likes.

And there are going to be multiple PhDs written about sex and gender in GTA 6, and the cultural impact of the game. I can’t wait to read those.
