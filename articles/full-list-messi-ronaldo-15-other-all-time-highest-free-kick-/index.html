Lionel Messi has climbed to second on the all-time list of free-kick scorers following another productive year from set pieces, moving ahead of several renowned Brazilian specialists and extending his advantage over Cristiano Ronaldo.

The Argentina captain has now scored 75 direct free-kicks throughout his career, compared with Ronaldo’s 63. Messi’s six goals from free-kicks in 2026 have seen him overtake Juninho Pernambucano, Roberto Dinamite and Jair, who have 72, 73 and 74 respectively. He is now only three goals away from Brazilian great Marcelinho Carioca, whose tally of 78 remains the record.

The latest figures highlight how dramatically the picture has changed since the early stages of Messi and Ronaldo’s careers. In 2017, Messi had converted 34 direct free-kicks, while Ronaldo had 53. A Yahoo Sports article from that period even carried the headline “One of the records that Messi will never dare to break of Ronaldo,” reflecting the size of Ronaldo’s advantage at the time. However, Messi’s improvement from dead-ball situations during his thirties has completely altered the standings.

Ronaldo, 41, currently occupies ninth position with 63 free-kick goals. The Portugal forward could still move above Jorge Aravena, Siniša Mihajlović and Marcos Assunção, who have 65, 67 and 68 respectively, although breaking into the top five would require a significant increase in his tally.

According to Planet Football on Wednesday, the leading free-kick scorers in football history are:

1. Marcelinho Carioca – 78
2. Lionel Messi – 75
3. Jair – 74
4. Roberto Dinamite – 73
5. Juninho Pernambucano – 72
6. Marcos Assunção – 68
7. Siniša Mihajlović – 67
8. Jorge Aravena – 65
9. Cristiano Ronaldo – 63
10. Zico – 62
11. Diego Maradona – 61
12. Rogério Ceni – 59
13. Ronaldinho – 57
14. Shunsuke Nakamura – 56
15. David Beckham – 53
16. Alessandro Del Piero – 53
17. Franz Binder – 50

With Messi still playing and continuing to add to his free-kick tally, the eight-time Ballon d’Or winner has moved within three goals of Marcelinho Carioca’s record.
