    Preamble: Europe 8-8 USA

Team Europe are finally summoned to the tee. They’re bouncing around in the manner you’d imagine. Carlota Ciganda busting some fine moves to her song, Seven Nation Army. Then they race off again, in a long snake, high-fiving everyone standing at the front of the gallery, plus all of the volunteers who have gathered around the tee. Everyone killing time as they wait interminably for the closing ceremony. Nobody minds.

It all started at the 1st tee on Friday morning … and that’s where it’ll end tonight. Everyone packed into the stands surrounding the tee box, clapping and bopping to the music pumping, the presentation ceremony about to begin. All of Europe’s players, some draped in the flags of England, Ireland, Spain, Denmark and Sweden, wait to be announced. They’ll be lifting the cup in a minute or two.

The losing captain Angela Stanford, bitterly disappointed but still wearing a sporting smile, speaks to Sky Sports. “You hear people say how difficult it is … and it really is … we fought hard … the Europeans played great today and that’s how it goes … good for them … I try not to have regrets … I believe in all 12 … there are a lot of captains in line … I think we have a lot lined up ready to go.”

That was yet another magnificent Solheim Cup. Nip and tuck all the way to the end. Carlota Ciganda’s channelling the walkabout spirit of her compatriot Severiano Ballesteros on Friday evening; Jennifer Kupcho making those putts heard around the world in the gloaming on Saturday; Charley Hull and Lottie Woad finally sprinkling their stardust on Sunday, before Ciganda saw off a Kupcho comeback to seal the deal. Marvellous entertainment. Europe the thoroughly deserved winners, but Team USA will be pleased with their performances too, once the pain of defeat eases off a bit. Every last player a hero.

FINAL SCORE: Europe 15-13 USA

The entire European team and their caddies advance up the 18th fairway linked arm in arm. Celine Boutier nearly drains a monster across the green for a birdie and that half she’d quite like … but it doesn’t drop. A wry smile as she concedes the hole to Rose Zhang … then wanders off to celebrate with her pals!
Rose Zhang beats Celine Boutier 2UP

There are still Is to dot and Ts to cross. Celine Boutier and Rose Zhang halve the 17th, so they’ll head up the last.

The winning captain Anna Nordqvist – who heads into retirement as a Solheim Cup winning captain – speaks to Sky Sports. “This feels so good … so much hard work … all the background team … we had a really good vibe all week … everyone has been a really good team-mate … we talked a lot about fast starts … I’m really proud for them starting strong and finishing strong … it’s been such a long road … I’ve been living my dream … it doesn’t feel like I’ve worked a day in my life … what makes the Solheim Cup special is you get to share it with others … thank you everyone!”

There’s just one match left out on the course now. Rose Zhang is 1UP on Celine Boutier, having just won the 16th. Boutier will be desperate to win one of the last two holes, as she’s the only player on the European team not to have contributed any points. I’m pretty sure her team-mates will say she was an integral part of a collective effort nonetheless. They’ll have her back.

Europe 15-12 USA

Maja Stark picks up Lindy Duncan’s coin on the 17th green. The concession seals a fine win for the late-blooming rookie against one of Europe’s grittiest competitors. Stark doesn’t care. She just wants to race off and join the party with her team-mates. They all get in a huddle and bounce around singing her song. All together now: ♫ Maja Stark, doo doo doo doo doo doooo, doo doo doo doo doo doooo, doo doo doo doo doo doooo, MA-JA STARK! ♪♫

Carlota Ciganda, who officially sealed the deal for Europe, talks to Sky. “I am so happy … we had a great team all week … very happy for Anna … you need a team, that’s what Europe is about … we had a great time … Kupcho is a fighter … I know I celebrated yesterday and probably pissed off all the Americans! … I was really motivated to beat her!”

Europe 15-11 USA: Europe win the Solheim Cup!

What a difference a day makes. Yesterday evening, Jennifer Kupcho drained putts of 92 feet and 30 feet on 17 and 18. Now she underhits a putt from the swale to the right of 16, the ball only just getting to the green. Carlota Ciganda nearly drains her eagle putt, but she doesn’t need to make the one coming back, because Kupcho’s long birdie effort races past the hole, and the American concedes. Europe have officially won the 2026 Solheim Cup!

Europe 14-11 USA

… but Europe haven’t officially won, because Carlota Ciganda has to complete her match to register the half-point she’s certain of posting. She finds the heart of 16 in two, while her opponent Jennifer Kupcho misses to the right. No ear cupping today from Kupcho. It’s not been the smartest of moves by either Kupcho or Auston Kim in retrospect … but then if you can’t celebrate wildly after making 122-feet’s worth of putts, when can you? So having said all of the above: the celebration police can do one. Kupcho will always have Saturday night.

Europe win the Solheim Cup (effectively, if not officially)!

Lottie Woad’s win sparks huge celebrations on the 17th green! Anna Nordqvist comes over to give the hero of the hour a huge hug, before Sky have a word with the brilliant 22-year-old from Farnham in Surrey! “I didn’t know it was to win … I thought Carlota’s match was to win … I was playing good golf but not getting the points … I felt I was kinda letting everyone down … then the captain spoke to me … said I was third on birdies … I had to trust that … that’s golf sometimes and I get the winning putt!”

Europe 14-10 USA

Auston Kim’s putt shaves the left-hand edge of the cup. She hangs her head. She knows the jig is up, though she makes Lottie Woad tap in to seal the deal. With Carlota Ciganda assured of half a point, Woad has effectively won the Solheim Cup for Europe!

Lauren Coughlin with a huge display of grit on 16, getting up and down to scramble par having found the drink. That secures a half, and puts her dormie two. Kim will have a chance to extend if she makes her birdie effort from 15 feet or so. She’ll have got a read off Woad too. But she needs to make it, because if not, the cup is effectively Europe’s (although not officially, because matches have to be completed before counting).

Jennifer Kupcho doesn’t put a good stroke on her birdie putt. The ball jumps a little as it rolls wide right. That gives Carlota Ciganda the chance to put this match to bed … but her putt is always staying up on the left. The hole halved, and Ciganda is dormie three. Europe are now guaranteed to reach 14 points, which means they can’t lose the 2026 Solheim Cup … but they need to get to 14-and-a-half, because the USA as holders have the tie.

Lauren Coughlin’s second into 16 finds the drink. So nothing’s signed and sealed just yet. Leona Maguire whistles her second long and right of the green. Much will depend on her lie. Meanwhile both Carlota Ciganda and Jennifer Kupcho are on the 15th green, with similar looks at birdie. Kupcho perhaps a bit further out, but as we saw last night on 17 and 18, distance is not exactly an issue for her.

Auston Kim bundles her chip up to 12 feet, but she’s outside Lottie Woad … and can’t make the par putt. That leaves Woad with two putts to win the hole from ten feet … and she only needs one. She tickles it in, and goes dormie two. Europe are guaranteed half a point now, so if Carlota Ciganda can hold onto her lead over Jennifer Kupcho … and having just halved 14, she’s 3UP with four to play … the Solheim Cup will be heading back to Europe! But last night’s shenanigans proved there’s many a slip twixt cup and lip …

Maja Stark makes a 20-footer on 14 to keep her slim hopes alive against Lindy Duncan. And on 16, Yealimi Noh, having laid up, doesn’t get particularly close with her wedge … and then Julia Lopez Ramirez races her eagle putt six past! Both of these players have really suffered with the old greenside nerves in the last 30 minutes or so.

Lottie Woad gives her birdie putt an aggressive run, and with her ball ending up three feet past, it’s not conceded. Woad takes her time and eventually tickles it in for the half. She remains 1UP. Meanwhile Leona Maguire isn’t giving up. Two putts for the hole on 14, and she takes them carefully, reducing Lauren Coughlin’s lead to two holes. And Julia Lopez Ramierz fires a fairway wood over the creek at 16 and off the bank to the right of the green, using its contours to gather the ball back onto the putting surface. She’ll have a look at eagle from about 20 feet.

Auston Kim’s tee shot at the par-three 15th lands just short of the green. Lottie Woad goes over the flag. They’re similar distances away, but Kim will go first. Her putt never looks like dropping and rolls a couple of feet past, but that should be enough for the par. Over to Woad …

Auston Kim continues her comeback against Lottie Woad! She makes a 25-footer on 14 for her third birdie in a row … and that’s her third hole in a row! Just the one-hole advantage for Woad now. Kim walks off the green cupping her ear, mouthing “I can’t hear you!” at the gallery. Gloriously provocative. Great fun. What moxie! Meanwhile Yealimi Noh misses a short one on 15 to gift a precious hole to Julia Lopez Ramirez. This could still go either way!
