Taylor Auerbach filmed in troubling meltdown in streets of Potts Point

TheTools · 24.11.2026 03:01 · source · permalink

Fallen journalist Taylor Auerbach has been caught on camera in a bizarre street meltdown, running at full pelt into a removalist truck.

In a clip shared online, Auerbach - who has since been charged - appears barefoot and dishevelled as he runs towards a branded removalist truck, spectacularly colliding with the vehicle as an onlooker films from a distance.

The meme account that first posted the video, Brown Cardigan, did not identify Auerbach, though he was soon recognised by former industry colleagues.

The troubling footage was filmed in Potts Point, just behind Kings Cross Police Station at about 10.20am on Monday.

Officers attached to Kings Cross Police Area Command attended Ward Avenue and found a car had collided with the rear of a garbage truck 

According to a police media release, officers were told the driver, a 35-year-old man, had allegedly also run into the front of a truck, damaging the front of the vehicle and cracking its windscreen.

Auerbach underwent a roadside breath test, which returned a negative result. A roadside drug test allegedly returned a positive indication for cannabis.

He was arrested before police searched the vehicle and allegedly located a small quantity of drugs, which were seized for forensic examination.

Fallen journalist Taylor Auerbach (left) lapped up the media spotlight during the Lehrmann trial. Now, he has been caught on camera in a bizarre street meltdown in Potts Point

The former television producer and paralegal was taken to Kings Cross Police Station where he underwent a secondary oral fluid test.

He was assessed by NSW Ambulance paramedics before being taken to hospital for further treatment.

Upon his release, he was taken to Kings Cross Police Station and charged with drive recklessly/furiously or speed/manner dangerous, destroy or damage property less than and equal to $2000, not give particulars to other driver and drive vehicle under influence of drugs.

He was given conditional bail to appear before the Downing Centre Local Court on Tuesday 12 January 2027. 

The vision offers a rare glimpse of the now-reclusive man who became a central figure in the Bruce Lehrmann saga, later earning the label of Channel Seven's 'fixer' as the network courted the former Liberal staffer for a high-profile interview.

More than two years have passed since Auerbach found himself thrust into the spotlight during Bruce Lehrmann's failed defamation case against Channel Ten and journalist Lisa Wilkinson, after Mr Lehrmann sued over a Project broadcast featuring allegations by Brittany Higgins that she had been raped at Parliament House in 2019. 

But in recent months, he has seemed deeply troubled, sharing bizarre ramblings on social media. Once a promising journalist for The Daily Telegraph and other outlets, he is now believed to be unemployed following a brief stint working as a paralegal for high-profile civil lawyer Rebekah Giles.

Auerbach is said to own a mortgage-free waterfront apartment in Elizabeth Bay, which he inherited from his late father, Greg Auerbach. Greg had inherited the property from his parents, medico Egon Auerbach and his wife Judith, who bought it in 1975 for just $27,000 before leaving it to their sons, Marsden and Greg.

The former Spotlight producer's arrival as a last-minute witness in Mr Lehrmann's defamation trial in March 2024 became a cause célèbre as he spilled about the allegedly underhanded methods of Seven's news‑gathering operation.

He told the Federal Court that he accompanied Mr Lehrmann during a January 2023 trip to Sydney while Seven was pursuing an exclusive Spotlight interview, effectively acting as the former Canberra party boy's 'babysitter' as the network tried to lock down its scoop.

He claimed Mr Lehrmann purchased cocaine during the trip and attempted to arrange sex workers at a Meriton hotel, later seeking reimbursement for money spent. Auerbach alleged this was reimbursed by Seven as 'per diems'.

Auerbach also claimed he used a corporate credit card to pay for Thai massages for Mr Lehrmann, and later offered his resignation because he felt uncomfortable and ashamed about aspects of the arrangement.

Seven denied any wrongdoing and vigorously challenged the allegations and Auerbach's credibility.

Mr Lehrmann's barrister also told Auerbach that he only came forward to cause as much damage to Seven and his former colleagues as he could.

Under cross-examination, Auerbach accepted he was drinking heavily during parts of the relevant period – more than 20 standard drinks a day – but maintained his account was accurate and that his drinking had not affected his memory.

Auerbach's evidence famously prompted Justice Michael Lee to reopen the case after the trial had effectively finished. The testimony was relevant because Ten argued it went to Mr Lehrmann's credibility and to whether he had improperly shared confidential material while pursuing media opportunities with Seven.

Auerbach also claimed he used a corporate credit card to pay for Thai massages for Bruce Lehrmann (pictured) as Seven tried to land an exclusive interview with him for Spotlight

Ultimately, Justice Michael Lee accepted substantial parts of Auerbach's evidence, although he noted some aspects required caution.

At the end of the trial, Justice Lee ruled that, on the balance of probabilities, Mr Lehrmann had raped his former colleague Ms Higgins at Parliament House and was willing to lie when it suited his interests.

Auerbach's evidence formed part of the broader credibility assessment.

Justice Lee's ruling meant Mr Lehrmann lost his defamation case.

Auerbach could not be reached for comment.
