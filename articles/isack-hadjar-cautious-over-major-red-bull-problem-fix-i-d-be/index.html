Isack Hadjar cautious over major Red Bull problem fix: 'I'd be lying if I said yes'

TheTools · 06.11.2026 11:45 · source · permalink

Red Bull finally aced its starts in Mexico City, but Isack Hadjar is not getting too excited about it just yet.

Isack Hadjar has sounded a note of caution over whether Red Bull can replicate the strong race starts it finally produced in Mexico City, as the team heads to Interlagos still searching for consistency off the line.

Red Bull's starts had been a persistent issue throughout the season, with unpredictable clutch delivery repeatedly costing both Hadjar and Max Verstappen positions at the first corner. 

The problem was at its most costly in Austin, where Hadjar dropped away from third on the grid after what was described as an uncontrollable clutch overdelivery at the moment of getaway. 

No hardware fix was introduced before Mexico, yet the Milton Keynes-based team's launches improved markedly there, with Verstappen converted fifth on the grid into a strong win.

Despite the exceptional starts for both cars in Mexico, Hadjar is not ready to call the issue solved.

Asked if Red Bull's strong starts in Mexico can be repeated, the Frenchman told select media, including RacingNews365: "I'd be lying if I said yes, because, I mean, I also don't want to say it was a fluke, because we worked hard on it from Austin, and then we achieved it in Mexico. So, lots of hard work.

"Now the goal is to replicate it, find out why it worked that well, and keep it that way until the end of the year. And next year, take an even further step."

Isack Hadjar

Isack Hadjar confident form is 'going to click'

That hedge reflects a wider theme in Hadjar's recent weekends, as Red Bull's RB22 has been capable of winning races in the hands of his team-mate. 

But Hadjar's own results have not matched the car's potential, with mechanical and electronic gremlins, from a gearbox failure in Singapore to a late battery issue that cost him a likely podium in Malaysia, repeatedly disrupting his Sundays.

Discussing now having a race-winning car at his disposal, Hadjar explained: "That's what makes it more frustrating for me right now. 

"The last couple of races, I couldn't even be in the top five. I mean, except for Malaysia, it's always been something happening on Sunday.

"But, yeah, it's going to click. It's just a matter of time, and on my side, I think the pace has been decent. So I just want to keep pushing, and here anything can happen with the conditions, so stay very, very sharp."
