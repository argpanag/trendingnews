Record warmth possible in Chicago, but the forecast is about to change dramatically

TheTools · 21.11.2026 17:12 · source · permalink

Soaring temperatures could threaten records in the Chicago area this week, but a cold snap of reality is expected in the coming days

Iisha Scott has the latest forecast.

Soaring temperatures could threaten records in the Chicago area this week, but a reality check is in store for next week.

According to the NBC 5 Storm Team, the city of Chicago could threaten a nearly 100-year-old record on Friday thanks to the above-average temperatures, but next week things will feel decidedly more like late fall, along with a chance of some snow just ahead of Thanksgiving.

Until then, Chicago-area residents will get to enjoy plenty of warm temperatures and sunshine over the next few days. Thursday is expected to be downright pleasant, with highs in the 60s and partly-to-mostly sunny skies in the city and suburbs.

Temperatures could be slightly warmer on Friday, and that’s when a long-standing weather record could fall. According to the National Weather Service, the warmest November 20 on record occurred in 1930 when the temperature hit 68 degrees, and while the NBC 5 Storm Team has the current forecasted high in the mid-60s, it’s entirely possible we could see that record fall.

For comparison, average temperatures for late November in the Chicago area are only in the mid-40s, so residents should plan to enjoy it as much as they can.

Saturday could again see above average temperatures, with highs in the mid-to-upper 60s, but a big change will arrive late Saturday and then into the day Sunday with the arrival of a cold front across the area.

Sunday will see readings in the 50s, but the big story could be moderate-to-heavy rain that could fall in the late morning and then into the afternoon hours, including during the Chicago Bears’ game at Soldier Field.

Once that front passes, temperatures will be way closer to normal, with highs in the low-to-mid 40s throughout the week leading up to Thanksgiving according to the NBC 5 Storm Team.

Those cooler temperatures will also be accompanied by a chance of precipitation on Wednesday and then into Thursday, and it’s possible that precipitation could fall in the form of snow, which could give Chicago its first recorded snowfall of the season just ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

Things will likely clear out by Friday, but temperatures will remain around their seasonal averages in the 40s, with overnight lows in the mid-to-upper 20s.
