Grand Theft Auto VI has the franchise's the first non-optional female lead character

Grand Theft Auto VI has the franchise's the first non-optional female lead character

The blockbuster video game Grand Theft Auto VI has two protagonists, a man and a woman. NPR's Ayesha Rascoe talks with gaming expert Adrienne Shaw about why this is a significant departure.

AYESHA RASCOE, HOST:

The blockbuster video game franchise Grand Theft Auto is a bacchanalia of bad behavior. But there's something new in the latest edition. Players rampage through Vice City as Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos.

(SOUNDBITE OF VIDEO GAME, "GRAND THEFT AUTO VI")

MALLORY WU: (As Andy) Lucia, there's police everywhere. Just text me where you are. I'll come to you.

RASCOE: Lucia is the first non-optional female lead character in Grand Theft Auto.

Adrienne Shaw is a media studies professor at Temple University, where she studies and lectures on video games, gender and sexuality. Thank you so much for being with us.

ADRIENNE SHAW: Thank you for having me.

RASCOE: This is the first time that you have a character, Lucia, who is central to Grand Theft Auto's story. That seems like a big deal.

SHAW: Like, it is a big deal for this franchise. I think a lot of it is realizing that there is a bigger audience out there than who had been playing Grand Theft Auto games.

RASCOE: Which was men. Like - or boys and men were the main targets of the game before, you think.

SHAW: Both the target for the games and also the fan base of the games. Like, GTA games are largely known as games that - that's what boys play, especially, like, teenage boys and 20-something men.

RASCOE: Well, and so in GTA VI, while playing as Jason, you have to do domestic chores and show affection to Lucia in a number of ways. People are talking about this online.

SHAW: Video games historically have not been known for doing romance, especially realistic romance, well. You know, a lot of games historically, if there were romances in it, it's - you show enough of a present to a person, and suddenly they fall in love with you. That's not real romance. And so I think, actually, there's something about how they decided to tell the story of these characters that's really leaning into the everyday. And that's always been a little bit of the franchise's goal, is representing the real world in, like - in all of its interesting minutiae.

RASCOE: Have you seen any backlash to this? I mean, are you seeing people, you know, complaining - this isn't the GTA I remember. I want to be going around with the prostitutes - and all that stuff?

SHAW: Absolutely. There are people posting about being mad about the domestic labor of it. There are people who think that the team who developed it is too woke. I also believe it is lucrative to be angry on the Internet these days. And so some of it is - how much of that is real outrage versus how much of that is outrage to drive views and clicks and likes and the sort? And also, one of the things that I've sort of been casually noticing is all the people who are posting videos mad about the game bought the game and are playing it.

RASCOE: It's also worth pointing out that, you know, plenty of gamers are women. So do you think all of this shows that Rockstar, the company behind the Grand Theft Auto franchise, is just trying to sell more video games?

SHAW: A hundred percent. Like, I do not believe that the choices of representation were out of just a place of goodness and hope. There are millions of indie games that are trying to tell nuanced stories of different identities. I think Rockstar realizes how diverse the game industry is. And so I do think that part of the choices GTA made in this game of what you can and can't do demonstrate that they are trying to toe a very careful line of not turning off huge portions of the population because they want them to play this game.

RASCOE: Much of your work as an academic is focused on queer studies and the LGBTQ+ communities. What ways does the Grand Theft Auto franchise address those identities?

SHAW: Yeah. I've been documenting queer representation in video games for 20 years now. And Grand Theft Auto as a franchise has by far the most LGBTQ representation of any other game series, and it's built into every layer of the game. So there's a lot of background media in these games, like television shows you can watch, radio programs you can listen to. There are lots of background characters or phone apps that have characters, and embedded in all of those have been queer characters.

And interestingly, unlike a lot of other game series, those queer characters are a variety of genders. There are a variety of races. There are a variety of class positions. Grand Theft Auto has always pulled from the real world. And I think that they're going to do what they've always done, which is have things that are homophobic and transphobic in the game, and they'll represent pro-LGBTQ opinions as well.

RASCOE: That's Professor Adrienne Shaw of Temple University. Thank you so much for speaking with us today.

SHAW: Thank you for having me.
