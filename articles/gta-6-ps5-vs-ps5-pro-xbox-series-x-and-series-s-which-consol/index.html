GTA 6: PS5 vs PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Series S: Which Console Plays Best?

TheTools · 18.11.2026 13:38 · source · permalink

Feature

Resolution, performance and visual settings compared.

Grand Theft Auto 6 is here - and that means we can finally share the full details of how the game performs on each console platform: base PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Series S.

We've already covered GTA 6's visuals in detail, focusing on the top-end experience on PS5 Pro, but the game's performance and fidelity can vary significantly depending on your platform of choice. The PlayStation and Xbox releases use different upscalers, for example, while the PS5 Pro release adds extra ray tracing effects. The Series S version is perhaps the most interesting of all, as it has to cram an extraordinary visual burden onto the weakest current-gen hardware - and more or less pulls it off, despite some noticeable cutbacks.

This article compares the game on every platform based on over 100 hours of testing across all four versions by John and Oliver, focusing on image quality, visual features and performance.

Resolution and image quality

First, let's touch on resolution. The game runs at 2560x1440 (1440p) resolution on PS5, PS5 Pro and Xbox Series X, with no immediate signs of dynamic resolution scaling.

On PS5 Pro, this is upscaled to 4K using PSSR, the Pro's bespoke AI temporal upscaler. PS5 and Xbox Series X instead get a spatial upscale to 4K using Rockstar's in-house TAA. The game therefore doesn't use temporal upscaling - such as QSSR on PS5 or FSR on Series X - and appears visually softer as a result. However, the decision does make sense.

The Series S is the odd one out here, with an internal resolution of 720p, temporally upscaled to approximately 1080p using FSR 4. From what we understand, this is the first shipping game on Series consoles to use the new upscaler.

Performance, frame-rate and VRR

All platforms target a 30fps frame-rate, a rarity for a current-gen game, but given the huge scale and intense simulation elements, the design makes sense. There's no option for a 40fps or 60fps mode on any platform.

As an aside, Xbox consoles can run the game in a 120Hz VRR container as an OS-level feature, which allows for a slightly smoother experience. PlayStation users don't have a 120Hz option, and while you can force VRR on, the system-level VRR only operates above 48fps and doesn't support low frame-rate compensation, so this does nothing in a 30fps title.

In terms of performance, the PS5 Pro offers the smoothest experience, with a consistent 30fps experience through around 75 hours of standard play in our testing. However, it is possible to experience short drops to a minimum of 24fps when destroying vehicles with the grenade launcher, but the frame-rate tends to recover quickly.

The base PS5 and Series X use the same 30fps target, but hold it a little more loosely, often flicking between 30fps and the high 20s during traversal, combat and other more challenging scenes. Returning to the same vehicle destruction hotspot, the frame-rate remains in the mid-30s while the carnage is unfolding, and only recovers when we leave the area. Again, it's possible for frame-rates to dip into the low 20s at the worst case.

Series S offers the loosest 30fps lock, with large portions of the game playing out at frame-rates from the mid to high 20s. Again, it's possible to depress the frame-rate to around 20fps with vehicular destruction. The other consoles do a credible job of hitting 30fps, but Series S often lies in a 25-30fps no-man's-land.

Note that, distinct from frame-rate, the game featured some animation update rate unsteadiness in pre-release footage (and our early testing on Xbox Series consoles). Thankfully, this long-standing RAGE engine issue seems to have been fixed in patches prior to launch, and animations now play out with consistent frame pacing.

Finally, input lag is similar to other Rockstar 30fps titles, like Red Dead Redemption 2. We measured around 180ms of end-to-end camera movement latency on Series X, including a wireless controller and around 10ms of display latency. This didn't seem to change substantially across 60Hz, 120Hz and 120Hz VRR. This is similar to RDR 2 and GTA 5 Enhanced, though input response is perceptually a bit better in GTA 6.

Visual features and settings

While all versions of the game - PS5, PS5 Pro, Series X, Series S - use ray-traced global illumination (RTGI) and ray-traced reflections, only the PS5 Pro version additionally supports ray-traced shadows. On other platforms, less consistent shadow maps are used, though these do support variable penumbra and have a high-resolution appearance.

Note that the RT reflections on all consoles are part of a hybrid approach, with screen-space reflections (SSR) layered in.

The Series S version is surprisingly feature-complete compared to the PS5 and Series X versions, supporting strand-based hair and the two RT effects, but it does come with some cutbacks. Most notably, lighting is lower precision overall and RT reflections are softer, lower-res and sometimes miss details. Shadows also appear visually degraded, with lower resolution shadowmaps and some absent cutscene self-shadows.

Additionally, driving at high speed through the game world results in more noticeable pop-in. Other minor downgrades include muddier textures at oblique angles, lower-res volumetric clouds, flatter water, lower-res hair and facial hair, and lower resolution depth of field effects.

Despite these necessary cutbacks, due to the Series S console's more limited memory and cut-down GPU, the overall visual feature set is still similar to Xbox Series X and PS5. Thankfully, the full-fat CPU and SSD on the Series S mean that the scale and scope of Leonida isn't affected.

It's worth bearing in mind that the nature of the game make it hard to nail down absolutely every change, especially as John played only on PS5 and PS5 Pro, while Oliver played only on Xbox Series X and Series S. We'll have more direct cross-platform comparisons in future content.

To sum up: the best console to play GTA 6

In terms of visual features and performance, the PS5 Pro version is the best option available, with solid frame-rates, 4K-like image quality and a noticeable visual upgrade thanks to ray-traced shadows. However, the game looks and plays well on base PS5 and Xbox Series X as well, despite occasional minor frame-rate drops during regular play and a softer 1440p image. The Series S version of the game is the hardest to recommend, with consistent frame-rate drops to the mid-20s, a 1080p output resolution and a long list of visual cutbacks, though the overall character of the game's visuals still shines through.
