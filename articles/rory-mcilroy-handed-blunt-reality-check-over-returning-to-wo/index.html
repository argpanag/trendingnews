Rory McIlroy handed blunt reality check over returning to world No. 1 spot

TheTools · 14.10.2026 17:19 · source · permalink

Since May 2023, Scottie Scheffler has held the No. 1 spot in the world golf ranking, maintaining a lead that Rory McIlroy, his closest rival, may struggle to bridge.

On the OWGR, the point gap separating Scheffler from McIlroy is roughly 348 points, which is a margin nearly equal to the gap between McIlroy and the remainder of the rankings.

Scheffler has been dominant at the top of the list for his 178th consecutive week, and he finished off the 2026 season strong by breaking Tiger Woods' career earnings record and earning a win at the Tour Championship at East Lake.

He gained more world ranking points with the win, as points are distributed according to tournament field strength and final position on the leaderboard, calculated across a rolling two-year ranking cycle.

Having dedicated most of his life to golf, the 30-year-old world No. 1 maintains a sharp focus on the sport. Meanwhile, McIlroy, who has achieved a career grand slam, has spoken openly about his work-life balance, which means he might not reclaim the top ranking for the rest of his playing career.

Analysts say there has yet to be a weakness in Scheffler's game.

“He's the best player in the world by a country mile," said Sky Sports' Andrew Coltart, referring to Scheffler. "It's his distance control and his ability to get scoring irons pin-high basically all the time, along with an incredible level of consistency.”

His 2026 season saw five runner-up finishes and three wins, while McIlroy earned the green jacket once again and finished in second place just one time.

"Also, very importantly, I think it's his golfing IQ and his understanding of how his game is and where his game is at - that's why he's the dominant force in the game,” continued Coltart, referring to Scheffler.

Scheffler has earned $130,390,661 throughout his time on the PGA Tour, surpassing Woods with his win at East Lake and the PGA Tour’s season finale.

Woods, who has yet to confirm if he will return to the game of golf since last playing a competitive round at the 2024 Open Championship, made $120,999,166. Keep in mind purse increases and inflation.

Despite the record-breaking move, Scheffler didn’t seem to care.

“You're kind of comparing apples and oranges,” said Scheffler, referring to the 2026 broken record. “But I think a lot of success we've had out here, especially when you look at it from a financial perspective, Tiger has a lot to do with that. Like I said, it's apples and oranges. The reason our purses are so large today is because of the influence and impact that Tiger had on the game.

“I wouldn't really consider that a Tiger record being broken. I think it's more... I think it just shows one of the many effects he had on the game of golf in a positive way. The Tour is only getting more competitive as it goes forward, and I think that's part of how he played the game.”

Scheffler will most likely not play the rest of 2026 until the Hero World Challenge.
