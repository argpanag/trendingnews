Verstappen blasts "Mickey Mouse racing" after chaotic start to Spanish GP | Grande Prêmio (Grand Prix)

TheTools · 13.09.2026 13:27 · source · permalink

Max Verstappen vented his frustration with the Spanish GP and the nature of racing at Madring after cutting a corner while fighting Kimi Antonelli on the opening lap and becoming caught in a confusing position handback

News Summary

  • Max Verstappen cut a corner while battling Kimi Antonelli at the start of the Spanish GP and initially emerged in second place.
  • The opening-lap sequence became confused as Red Bull instructed Verstappen to give a position back, with Lewis Hamilton also involved immediately behind.
  • Frustrated by the situation and the racing at Madring, Verstappen complained over the radio: “Fucking Mickey Mouse racing, this is.”

Max Verstappen needed only the opening lap of the Spanish Grand Prix to make his feelings about the racing at Madring unmistakably clear.

After an aggressive start from third, the Red Bull driver became embroiled in a confusing fight involving Kimi Antonelli and Lewis Hamilton. Verstappen cut a corner while battling Antonelli, emerged in second place and was subsequently instructed to surrender a position as Red Bull sought to avoid the risk of a penalty for gaining an advantage off the track.

It was the sequence itself — and, more broadly, the way the race was unfolding at Madrid’s new circuit — that triggered Verstappen’s irritated radio message, rather than anger directed specifically at Red Bull.

“Fucking Mickey Mouse racing, this is,” Verstappen said.

The remark captured the four-time world champion’s frustration with an opening lap in which the circuit limits and the difficulty of sorting out the running order became as important as the wheel-to-wheel battle itself.

Verstappen had started third after Lando Norris beat Kimi Antonelli by only 0.011s to take the first Formula 1 pole position at Madring.

When the lights went out on Sunday, Verstappen immediately attacked the Mercedes driver ahead. During the fight, however, the Red Bull went beyond the conventional route through the corner and rejoined ahead of Antonelli, effectively putting Verstappen second behind Norris.

That created the problem.

Rather than leave the incident for the stewards to assess and risk a sporting penalty, Red Bull instructed Verstappen to return the position. But the order on track had already changed, with Hamilton — who had started fourth — now involved in the same battle.

The resulting reshuffle meant Verstappen had to surrender track position to Hamilton, intensifying his irritation with how the opening phase of the Grand Prix had developed.

Crucially, however, Verstappen’s radio message should not be interpreted as an attack on Red Bull for issuing the instruction. His frustration was directed at the circumstances surrounding the incident and the racing situation created by Madring’s layout and track limits.

Max Verstappen criticised the racing at Madring after a chaotic opening lap (Photo: Red Bull Content Pool)

The episode was particularly costly because Verstappen had put himself in an excellent position with his launch.

Starting immediately behind Norris and Antonelli, he had the opportunity to convert third on the grid into second before the field had properly settled into the race. Instead, the shortcut and subsequent position handback prevented him from consolidating that gain.

The moment also reinforced some of the concerns surrounding Madring before Formula 1’s first race at the venue.

Verstappen had already described the new Madrid circuit as challenging during the build-up to the weekend. With no previous F1 race data available, drivers and teams entered Sunday still learning how the layout would behave in genuine wheel-to-wheel competition.

The Dutchman had also compared parts of Madring with Jeddah and warned that the margins for error were extremely small. Its combination of narrow sections, walls, unconventional corner sequences and limited passing opportunities had already generated discussion throughout the weekend.

Even Verstappen’s first experience of the venue had been eventful. He crashed twice while learning Madring in Red Bull’s simulator, joking at the time about how difficult the unfamiliar layout was to master.

Sunday’s opening lap provided a much less amusing demonstration.

Verstappen had the pace to attack Antonelli, but his attempt ended with the Red Bull outside the limits of the circuit and produced exactly the type of awkward racing situation that drivers had feared could become a feature of the inaugural Spanish GP at Madring.

His verdict over the radio was short and emphatic.

“Fucking Mickey Mouse racing, this is.”
