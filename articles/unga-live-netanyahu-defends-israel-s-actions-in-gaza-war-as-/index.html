Climate change "does not recognize borders," says Nepal's PM

Nepal’s Prime Minister Balendra Shah decried the impact the climate crisis has had on his country in an impassioned speech Thursday night, weeks after catastrophic flooding that killed at least 1,400 people in his country and dozens more across the border in China’s Tibetan region, with thousands more still missing.

Late last month, a 2,000-foot-wide mass of rock and glacier ice broke off Langtang Lirung, plunging 7,000 feet into the river below, releasing the same amount of energy as a magnitude 5.2 earthquake. The impact instantly melted ice and unleashed a flood wave that roared more than 20 miles downstream at devastating speed.

More the 5,500 remain missing in Nepal, and at least 500 more on the Chinese side, according to official reports.

Shah underscored that his country “contributes less than 0.1% of global greenhouse emission,” yet is paying a “colossal price” for the severity of the crisis.

Shah went on to highlight that climate change “destroys roads, bridges, schools, homes, farms, and livelihoods.” He added: “It destroys clean energy infrastructure that we need to fuel our future, and and it forces a poor country to borrow money to repair damage it did not cause,” referring to the many hydroelectric plants that were damaged by the flooding.

As the country begins to reckon with the mammoth task of rebuilding, Shah appealed for help from other countries.

He proposed to establish a “Himalay Climate Resilience Mechanism” led by Nepal, India and China, which would share satellite data and expertise, monitor dangerous glacial lakes and strengthen early warning systems.

“When disaster strikes, let us coordinate assistance across borders,” Shah said.

Iran says it presented new proposal to restart talks with US and re-open Strait of Hormuz

Iran has presented a proposal to the United States that it says would re-open the Strait of Hormuz and restart negotiations toward a “final deal,” Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Thursday.

The proposal, presented to the US through mediators this week, calls for the US to meet certain conditions within seven days to restart the talks and re-open the strait, Araghchi said. The foreign minister declined to elaborate on the conditions but said that they were “nothing more” than what was included in the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding.

That agreement, which was reached by the two countries in June, included provisions on sanctions relief; the release of frozen assets; and a cessation of military operations, including in Lebanon.

In response to Araghchi’s comments, a White House official told CNN they are having “positive and constructive discussions through the mediators.”

“The United States is in a very strong position with control of the Strait of Hormuz, so we are not in a rush. Nearly 22 million barrels of oil left the strait last night,” the official said.

The seven-day period for the US to meet the conditions would begin the day after the US agreed to the proposal, Araghchi told a group of journalists and experts on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly.

The foreign minister said they are awaiting a response from the US. He claimed that Tehran is not in a hurry, but said Iran believes it would be better if the US accepted and talks restarted before the US midterms in early November.

France to send defense assets including troops to protect Saudi oil site

As world leaders gather at the UN General Assembly amid growing concern over conflict in the Middle East and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, French President Emmanuel Macron said France will deploy defense assets including military personnel to help protect a major Saudi oil export facility damaged in a recent Houthi attack.

Speaking in a televised interview with TF1 and France 2 on Thursday, Macron said France had finalized discussions with Saudi Arabia to help secure the Yanbu energy export complex on the Red Sea coast.

Macron said the facility previously handled more than 5 million barrels of oil per day and described protecting regional energy infrastructure as part of a broader effort to prevent disruptions to global fuel supplies.

Macron said he had recently hosted Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Iraq’s prime minister and leaders from Jordan and Lebanon as part of efforts to secure alternative energy routes and maintain supplies.

France has already increased its military presence in the region, sending Rafale fighter jets to the United Arab Emirates and deploying the nuclear-powered aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle to the Mediterranean as the conflict expanded across the Middle East.

Hungarian president speaks of new era of governance and relationships

Guyana’s president urges Venezuela to comply with upcoming ruling on territorial dispute

While addressing the UN General Assembly on Thursday, Guyana’s President Mohamed Irfaan Ali urged Venezuela to comply with the upcoming ruling by the International Court of Justice (ICJ) regarding the long-standing territorial dispute between the two countries over the Essequibo region.

The dispute reached the ICJ in May of this year, when two hearings were held in which Guyana and Venezuela presented their arguments concerning the territorial conflict.

“With full respect for international law, Guyana participated in — and encouraged Venezuela to participate in — the ICJ proceedings; now that we have both taken part in the hearings that concluded last May, we urge Venezuela to comply with the upcoming ruling in accordance with its obligations under the UN Charter,” said Ali.

During the May hearings, Venezuela stated that it would not renounce its “historic rights” to the Essequibo, while Guyana indicated that the Venezuelan stance “reflects a strategy to avoid a judicial ruling that might not favor its claims.”

The Essequibo — a territory of approximately 160,000 square kilometers — is the subject of the controversy between the two nations, a tension dating back to the 19th century and the 1899 Arbitral Award, which granted the United Kingdom sovereignty over the entire area currently in dispute.

As a former British colony, Guyana has controlled the area claimed by Venezuela since gaining independence from the United Kingdom in 1966. This territory accounts for roughly two-thirds of the country’s total land area.

ICJ deliberations typically span several months, so a ruling on the dispute is expected by the end of the year.

Abbas calls on international community to ensure viability of two-state solution

Palestinian Authority President Mahmoud Abbas called on the international community to ensure the viability of the two-state solution in the face of the ongoing Israeli expansion of settlements in the occupied West Bank and the war in Gaza.

In a pre-recorded speech, Abbas said there was a need to halt Israel’s decision to proceed with E1, an internationally condemned settlement that risks separating dividing the West Bank.

“This requires urgent and responsible intervention by the United Nations Security Council,” Abbas said. Earlier this month, the UK led a group of more than a dozen countries who said they would ban products from Israeli settlements in the West Bank or planned on doing so.

“We have long warned the international community, through the United Nations General Assembly and through the Security Council, that these Israeli policies, in violation of international law, would destroy the two-state solution, and that is what is happening now,” Abbas said. He also warned that Israel was withholding more than $6 billion that should have been transferred to the Palestinian Authority and demanded the release of the money — stopped by Israel’s far-right finance minister — to alleviate financial hardship in the West Bank.

Abbas called for the implementation of the stalled 20-point ceasefire plan brokered under President Donald Trump. He said the Palestinian Authority had undergone the reform measures required by the plan, but the Trump administration has hardly engaged publicly with the Palestinian Authority.

“The Palestinian people will remain on its land. The Palestinian people will not accept displacement and will not disappear. Neither military occupation nor settlement expansion and annexation, nor settler terrorism can be a solution or create lasting security,” Abbas said.
