FRA pulls funding from new Amtrak Chicago maintenance facility (updated) - Trains

WASHINGTON — Amtrak’s controversial plan to build a Midwest Maintenance Facility at Canal Street in Chicago is dead.

The Federal Railroad Administration has pulled funding that would have allowed Amtrak to move its shop operations from 14th Street to a new facility that was to be built at Union Pacific’s idle Canal Street Yard, Trains has learned.

In a letter to interim Amtrak President Byl Herrmann on Monday, Nov. 23, FRA Administrator David Fink said the agency is withdrawing a $572 million grant for the project, as well as an $87 million grant for related bridge and viaduct work. FRA will consider letting Amtrak use the $572 million grant for more limited work at 14th Street.

FRA awarded the funds on Aug. 14, relying on Amtrak’s representations about its public-private partnership with Canal Edge LLC. Now FRA says it’s pulling funding to avoid the potential for massive cost overruns.

“Subsequent due diligence by Amtrak and Canal Edge LLC has exposed major, previously unquantified infrastructure costs that render the Canal Street relocation fiscally untenable. Most notably, an independent lifecycle analysis estimates approximately $400 million in cumulative costs over 50 years, above and beyond the work to be funded by the grant, to stabilize 17 viaducts and bridges at the site,” Fink wrote. “Alongside these projected costs is a deficit in Amtrak’s required non-federal cash match, resulting from its updated determination to retain the A5 main line segment and forgo the associated land-sale proceeds. Although earlier pre-award assessments indicated the segment was not needed, subsequent operational evaluations and the completion of due diligence identified significant additional scope that Amtrak considers essential for a viable relocation from 14th Street Yard to Canal Street Yard. Delivering that essential additional scope would require a further capital investment of $700 million or more.”

FRA will prohibit Amtrak from selling the 14th Street Yard, which was to become a mixed-use development including homes, businesses, and possibly a new stadium for the Chicago White Sox.

Amtrak, in a statement issued Tuesday, Nov. 24, said that the company, “in consultation with USDOT, is shifting its focus to redevelopment of our existing 14th Street Yard as a better long-term approach to modernizing our Chicago maintenance facilities. FRA has agreed to rescope the grant to reflect the new direction of the project. The redevelopment of Amtrak’s existing Chicago Coach Yard at 14th Street, which offers better operational access to Chicago Union Station, will provide substantial benefits to the traveling public. Amtrak will focus on addressing critical infrastructure needs, improved support for new trains and helping to advance future Midwest service growth.”

The $900 million project also would have relied on more than $125 million from developer Justin Ishbia, whose firm Canal Edge would build the Canal Street maintenance facility and lead the 14th Street redevelopment. Ishbia also holds an option to purchase the White Sox following the 2034 season.

FRA also will bar Amtrak from acquiring the Canal Street Yard. And the railroad must now provide FRA with “a reasonable, well-justified scope of work to modernize the 14th Street Yard,” Fink wrote. “Failure to do so may result in FRA’s withdrawal of all grant funding associated with the Midwest Heavy Maintenance Facility project.”

Neighborhood residents opposed the project, saying they were concerned about noise and environmental impacts of the maintenance facility. A City Council committee, Cook County, and the Rail Passengers Association also called on the federal government to require a full environmental review.
