Gold gains on weaker oil, greenback; US inflation data in focus

    [BENGALURU] Gold rose on Monday (Oct 12), supported by a softer US dollar and lower oil prices, as investors awaited key US inflation data this week for clues on the US Federal Reserve’s next policy move.

    Spot gold was up 0.3 per cent at US$4,206.01 per ounce, as at 0253 GMT, after hitting its highest since Oct 2 earlier in the day. US gold futures for December delivery gained 0.3 per cent to US$4,230.40.

    “Softer oil prices on the back of Trump’s reported energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine, combined with a more subdued (US) dollar, have given gold a window of opportunity to the upside to start the week,” said Tim Waterer, chief market analyst at KCM Trade.

    “As oil eases, it takes some of the immediate inflation and interest rate anxiety out of the market, which helps gold from a yield perspective.”

    US President Donald Trump made a surprise announcement of an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine on Sunday that he said was “effective immediately”, but Ukrainian leader Volodymyr Zelenskiy said he was still waiting to see details of the deal.

    Oil prices fell on expectations of more Russian supplies.

    The US dollar was muted, making greenback-priced bullion more affordable for buyers holding other currencies.

    While gold is typically seen as a hedge against inflation, higher interest rates tend to diminish non-yielding bullion’s appeal.

    Markets are now awaiting the US Consumer Price Index report on Wednesday, followed by the Producer Price Index (PPI) and retail sales data on Thursday.
