Langley City and Township both have new mayors - Langley Advance Times

TheTools · 18.10.2026 03:15 · source · permalink

Published 8:15 pm Saturday, October 17, 2026

By Langley Advance Times Staff

Early results show a dead heat between Eric Woodward and Jay Lundgren in the contest to become the mayor of Langley Township.

With eight polling places of 28 counted as of 8:13 p.m., Lundgren had 5,050 votes to Woodward’s 5,018.

The leading council candidates were, in order: Margaret Kunst, Barb Martens, Blair Whitmarsh, Kim Richter, Teresa Townsley, Michael Pratt, Lauren Chalus, and Steve Ferguson.

More results are expected soon as polling places have closed and votes are being counted.

The election campaign saw two mayoral candidates, 20 people contesting the eight council seats, and 15 challengers for the five school board seats.

Two slates fielded the majority of candidates: incumbent mayor Woodward led the Progress For Langley team, while Lundgren was running with the Langley Strong group. Both teams included incumbent councillors, with four on each slate.

There were also five independent council candidates and five independent trustee candidates.

At the council level, the debate has centred on the Township’s debt levels, with Progress’s candidates arguing they had a sustainable plan to build infrastructure that will be paid for by ongoing development, while Strong was critical of the spending.

UPDATE:

An 8:30 p.m. update still shows Lundgren ahead of Woodward by three per cent, with 8,565 votes for the Langley Strong lead over 8,068 for the Progress for Langley leader.

Right now, still in order of votes, Kunst, Martens, Whitmarsh, Richter, Townsley, Chalus, Pratt, and Ferguson are leading for council positions.

And with Langley Township selecting five trustees for the school board, so far Langley Strong has all those positions, based on current counts, with Charlie Fox, Holly Dickinson, Neil Turner, Candy Ashdown, and Kathie Dalziel leading thus far.

FURTHER UPDATE:

An 8:45 p.m. check in still has Lundgren out ahead with 13,829 votes to Woodward’s 11,809. The margin between the two mayoral candidates is growing to almost eight per cent.

In the meantime, numbers are hard to find for the City, but at an event featuring incumbent mayor Nathan Pachal and his Langley City First team in downtown Langley tonight, he has just stood up and said the results were not what he and the team were looking for, with the exception of newbie Heather Jenkins getting in.

Numbers will be shared as soon as possible.

KEEPING YOU UP TO DATE THE BEST WE CAN:

We are unable to verify any City numbers as of yet, and will keep you apprised.

In the meantime, at 9 p.m. there were 23 of 28 polling stations tabulated, for a total of 28,225 votes – so far a 26.17 per cent voter turnout.

Lundgren and his Langley Strong team are still ahead with 53 per cent of the vote (14,789) compared to Woodward who has almost 47 per cent or 13,687 votes.

As for councillors, Langley Strong is again leading in this order: Kunst, Martens, Whitmarsh, Richter, Townsley, Chalus, Pratt, and Ferguson in the top eight spots.

WELL, ALL THE RESULTS SEEM TO BE TALLIED:

At the 9:15 p.m. point, Langley City has declared former councillor Delaney Mack the new mayor, as well as many in the Achieving for Langley City slate.

She’ll be joined by Andre Flitton, Michael Hylands, Scott Thompson, Rick Orlando, Simi Rajput, and Heather Jenkins (Jenkins being the only Langley City First candidate elected).

As for the community opinion vote on new city facilities, voters said yes to a possible pool expansion, no to the new performing arts and cultural centre.

Meanwhile, across town in Langley Township, all 28 polling stations have been tallied and there’s a new mayor there, as well.

Woodward has been dethroned by Lundgren, who took 54 per cent of the votes, with a 31.55 per cent voter turnout.

Lundgren will be joined on council by Kunst, Martens, Whitmarsh, Richter, Townsley, Chalus (all Langley Strong slate members), as well as two Progress for Langley candidates and incumbent councillors Steve Ferguson and Michael Pratt (who just joined Woodward’s slate in the last year).
