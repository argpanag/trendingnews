US stocks recovered on Thursday as Treasury yields tumbled and chip stocks pared losses following an AI-driven sell-off fueled by debt-financing concerns.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) rose 1.1%, while the S&P 500 (^GSPC) erased losses to gain 0.3%. The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) edged slightly higher after declining more than 1% earlier in the session.

A sell-off in tech over the past two days sparked a flight to treasuries, with the 10-year bond yield (^TNX) sinking to 5.09%

(^IXIC )

Semiconductor stocks trimmed earlier losses after the KOSPI index (^KS11) declined 7% overnight. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSM) stock fell 2% on Thursday despite posting a record profit and beating estimates amid global demand for AI chips.

CoreWeave (CRWV) reversed losses after Nvidia (NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang expressed optimism about the data center operator following a 15% rout on Wednesday.

The stock's sell-off underscored concerns about the neo-cloud provider's debt-heavy balance sheet and whether it can scale its business quickly enough to cover rising interest expenses.

Meanwhile, new data showed September's wholesale inflation index rose at its fastest pace since 2022, sparking fresh concerns about how rising prices may affect the Federal Reserve's next move.

    As my colleague Jake Conley wrote on this blog earlier this afternoon, the cost of insuring CoreWeave against default reached a record today. Pricing implies roughly 60% odds of default within five years.

    The rebound follows yesterday's 15% plunge, its worst day since February. My other colleague Brian Sozzi reported earlier on the financing concerns dogging the heavily indebted AI infrastructure provider.

    Rocket Lab (RKLB) is also recovering from its lows but remains down double digits after pushing its Neutron rocket debut into 2027. Meanwhile, quantum-computing names IonQ (IONQ) and Rigetti (RGTI) have turned substantially higher after early declines.

    These businesses face different hurdles. But funding expensive infrastructure and getting ambitious technology into service can both delay the profits investors are counting on.

    For CoreWeave, watch whether its debt starts recovering alongside its shares. That would offer stronger evidence that financing concerns are easing.

  • Homebuilders rally as Treasury yields tumble

    Yields on US government debt are plummeting. Homebuilder stocks are heading the other way.

    The 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields (^TNX, ^TYX) are falling more than 10 basis points, putting both on track for their biggest daily declines since February. That's giving housing investors something they've been missing — hope for relief on mortgage rates.

    Lennar (LEN) is gaining over 5%. D.R. Horton (DHI), PulteGroup (PHM), Toll Brothers (TOL) and KB Home (KBH) are advancing a bit less. Mortgage lender Rocket Companies (RKT) is also moving higher.

    There's still ground to recover. Lennar remains down roughly 25% this year, and today's rally has yet to erase its October losses.

    Lower Treasury yields can take pressure off mortgage rates, though the two don't move in lockstep. The next test is whether yields stay lower and mortgage rates follow. That would give prospective buyers some of the relief housing stocks are anticipating today.

  • Apple is avoiding the worst of the tech selloff

    Apple stock is becoming a safe haven for investors looking for a place to park their dollars that isn't chips.

    Yahoo Finance's Dan Howley reports:

    "In the midst of the AI sell-off, the iPhone maker's market capitalization crossed the $5 trillion mark. It's the second time Apple has reached the milestone. It initially hit $5 trillion in July, before falling back down into the $4 trillion range.

    The move stands in stark contrast to its AI-centric peers. It doesn't hurt that, unlike others in the space, Apple hasn't had to shell out billions building new AI data centers, which has helped insulate it from broader market shocks."

    Read more here.

  • Nike, Lululemon lead an apparel rebound

    Nike (NKE) and Lululemon (LULU) are having a brutal year. Today, they're helping lead the retail rally.

    Both are gaining around 5%, alongside Boot Barn (BOOT) and Macy's (M). Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS) is joining the advance, with buying seen across footwear, clothing and the stores selling both.

    There's plenty of damage to repair. Nike remains down roughly 42% this year. Lululemon has lost more than half its value. Measured from their record closing highs, both stocks are down roughly 80%. A good day barely dents those losses.

    Still, retail is getting its moment while tech struggles. The SPDR S&P Retail ETF (XRT) is gaining over 3% as the S&P 500 hovers near the flatline.

    If tech finally manages to rebound, watch whether apparel hangs on to today's gains. That would suggest buyers are finding reasons to own these stocks beyond simply ducking the tech selloff.

  • CoreWeave credit risk reaches all-time high

    CoreWeave's (CRWV) credit risk reached an all-time high on Thursday, per data from the Intercontinental Exchange, reflecting investors' rising concerns over the feasibility of the debt profile at the leading "neocloud" provider.

    The price of CoreWeave credit default swaps, essentially a form of insurance against default risk, rose by 74.5 basis points to price in 60% odds that the company will default on its debt within the next five years, per Bloomberg.

    At the same time, some of CoreWeave's bonds continued to slide further, as 9% notes fell to trade at 80.75 cents on the dollar with a yield of 15.3% as investors demand a premium to hold the debt.

    That said, shares in the stock began to reverse course through Thursday trading after a sharp move downward in the previous session, in the red by roughly 2% near 12:30 p.m. ET on Thursday.
