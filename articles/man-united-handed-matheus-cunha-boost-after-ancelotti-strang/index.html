Man United handed Matheus Cunha boost after Ancelotti strangely snubs striker

TheTools · 10.09.2026 10:52 · source · permalink

Carlo Ancelotti's decision to omit Matheus Cunha from his Brazil squad for the upcoming international break will be music to the ears of Manchester United fans. The 27-year-old has been in fine form at Old Trafford since his £62.5million move from Wolves a little over 12 months ago.

In 2025/26, the versatile forward managed 10 goals and four assists in the Premier League alone, and was instrumental to the Reds' third-place finish to clinch Champions League football. And Cunha has shown more of the same this season, managing two assists from United's opening three matches.

However, Ancelotti has not seen enough from the marksman to select him for Brazil's upcoming trip to Australia for the Queensland Football Series during the international break at the end of the month. While the likes of Joao Pedro, Estevao and Rayan will spend the week away from the English top flight representing their nation, Cunha will remain in Greater Manchester.

Speaking about the competition, which will see Brazil make a round-the-world trip, Ancelotti said: "We are excited to be there because there are a lot of fans in Australia of the national team of Brazil. We are going to play against a tough national team. Australia did really well in the World Cup and so we are excited to be there to show our qualities to the supporters."

While Cunha was part and parcel of Ancelotti's first-team at the World Cup in North America this summer, scoring three goals in five games, it appears as though the Italian has had a change of heart over the United talent.

However, Reds fans - while disheartened by being denied the opportunity to see their talent shine on the world stage later this month - may be content with regard to Cunha's lack of participation.

It means that they will not fret at the prospect of the forward, who can also operate as a No.10, risking injury or fatigue during one of his parent club's most important campaigns in recent history.
