Coinbase Is Buying Back COIN Again as Shares Slide

TheTools · 25.11.2026 22:14 · source · permalink
  • COIN +1.88%

Trading disclosure

The above button links to Coinbase. Yahoo Finance is not a broker-dealer or investment adviser and does not offer securities or cryptocurrencies for sale or facilitate trading. Coinbase pays us for certain activity generated through this link. Prices displayed are informational.

Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong said Tuesday evening that the company has been buying back its stock this quarter. He called it a way to "take advantage of market dislocations" as crypto and AI shake up financial services.

  • No size yet: Armstrong didn't say how many shares Coinbase has bought or for how much, and no filing gives a number. In a reply, he said the buying falls within the existing $4B authorization, which had about $1.96B left as of Sept. 30th, per Coinbase's Q3 10-Q. Last year, Coinbase disclosed its Q4 buybacks in mid-February.

  • After a quiet Q3: Buying had nearly stopped last quarter, at roughly 38,000 shares (~$5.3M), all in August. That followed heavy purchases from Nov. 2025 through March. All told, Coinbase has retired about 10.2M shares at an average of roughly $200.

  • The stock: COIN closed Tuesday at $102.35, down 5.9%. On Wednesday morning it touched $94.88, a new 52-week low, before bouncing to around $104 by midday. It's down about 75% from its July 2025 closing high of $419.78.

  • The 10b5-1 wrinkle: The same 10-Q shows Armstrong adopted a trading plan in August to sell up to about 8.17M shares from option exercises. Its estimated start was Nov. 16th, but sales only happen above undisclosed price thresholds. No sales had been filed as of Wednesday morning.

  • The backdrop: BTC briefly fell below $58K on Wednesday morning, and spot BTC ETFs have shed about $4.9B in November through Nov. 24th, a monthly record per Farside data.
