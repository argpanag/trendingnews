Match report

Thank you for joining me this evening. Australia are still not firing on all cylinders but they’re now just 160 minutes away from yet another World Cup triumph. I’ll see you back here next weekend for a couple of mouthwatering semi-finals. Here is Jack Snape’s report from the Kangaroos’ 66-6 win over Cook Islands in Townsville …

Reece Walsh showed if there was anything James Tedesco could do, he can do better with a starring role for the Kangaroos in a partly clunky but otherwise entertaining victory over the Cook Islands in Townsville.

Coach Kevin Walters faces a choice between the two brilliant No 1s for the much-anticipated semi-final against either Samoa or Tonga next week, but this only sometimes convincing display showed there is still work to do in camp Kangaroo.

Although we’re technically still in the group phase we are now effectively into the knockouts.

Lebanon and France will complete their duties from 5:40pm tomorrow, after which it’s blockbusters all the way, starting with Tonga v Samoa to establish which of the Pacific Island nations will finish top of the group and play New Zealand, and which will finish second and take on Australia.

Then it’s onto those massive semi-finals next weekend, before the decider in Brisbane on Sunday 15th November.

What did we learn?

  • The Kangaroos are still well short of their best. We have yet to see Origin-level intensity and discipline.

  • Player of the match Reece Walsh is in the box seat to wear the No 1 jersey next weekend. His ability to drift into the line on either edge and open the game as a second playmaker is so fun to watch.

  • Tom Dearden should start in the No 7 jersey after a decent outing, but he could yet be overtaken by Sam Walker purely for the Roosters’ goal-kicking ability. Walsh started the night as the designated kicker but was hampered by a foot injury that forced Munster then Strange to take over conversion duties.

  • The forward pack remains a work in progress. Yeo, Carrigan, Martin, and Grant all made uncharacteristic errors in possession, but there was also plenty of dynamic running, from Preston and Fa’asuamaleaui in particular.

  • Ethan Strange has forced his way into contention for a spot on the bench. His versatility and effervescence make him an ideal man to have among the six reserves and he added plenty of impetus in the second half.

Full-time: Australia 66-6 Cook Islands

The hosts and defending champions win Pool A and make it safely through to the semi-finals.

CONVERTED TRY! Australia 66-6 Cook Islands (Grant, 79)

Chaos in Townsville. Australia should score in the right corner after a brilliant pass from Walsh. Then Cook Islands should run the ball clear after an interception but spill the ball on the ground. The Kangaroos come back at speed and Walsh sends the whole of the stadium to the left before passing to Grant on his right hip to send the hooker behind the posts.

Strange opens his international account from under the crossbar.

76 mins: Cook Islands continue to give their all in the dying stages. Takairangi in particular has left it all out there.

74 mins: Now Takairangi drops the pass at first receiver on the last. Someone put a fork in this one, it is done.

72 mins: Carrigan with the loose carry just after returning to the fray off the bench.

70 mins: Marsters is enjoying a golden spell on the ball. Takairangi kicks early in the set – there’s room behind, the sprint is on – but the ball beats Rennie and Bostock and bobbles out of play.

68 mins: Cook Islands have a second wind! They regather the dropout then Teague almost hustles his way through. Ngatikaura tries to repeat his snipe from dummy-half that worked so well early on. The on the last, under the crossbar, Fa’asuamaleaui concedes a brainless penalty, and the siege continues.

67 mins: And they force a dropout! Marsters gets busy, Takairangi dabs a kick over the top, and Bostock is forced to take the conservative option.

66 mins: A rare spell of Cook Islands possession after Staggs is scragged and dragged into touch.

64 mins: Strange has made a difference coming off the bench. He’s brought urgency and intensity into the game and his effervescent has rubbed off on Munster who has played with more characteristic freedom since half-time.

62 mins: Australia think they’ve scored again but Walsh is instead pinged for a forward pass, tipping on what looked to the naked eye like a majestic try-assist to Staggs. To add injury to insult the fullback reels away in pain after taking a heavy hit from Holmes. Walsh’s kicking foot, his left, is far from 100%.

TRY! Australia 60-6 Cook Islands (Bostock, 60)

Aaaaaand another. Walsh again too quick and strong carving through the line on the left edge. He could assist on his inside to Best but chooses Bostock on his outside instead and it’s hat-tricks all round for Australia’s wingers.

Munster can’t extend his left touchline conversion streak.

CONVERTED TRY! Australia 56-6 Cook Islands (Trbojevic, 58)

Trbojevic has another Temu hat-trick. The Cooks Islands’ defence is done for the night and Walsh on the burst is way too quick and powerful to stop. He hares towards the left corner but before attempting to score himself feeds an inside pass to Trbojevic who almost fluffs his moment just inches from the line with KL Iro executing a fierce tackle but the big winger manages to plant the ball down.

Munster kicks another.

CONVERTED TRY! Australia 50-6 Cook Islands (Fa’asuamaleaui, 55)

Two six-agains in the restart set as the Kangaroos cruise deep inside Cook Islands territory. Then Tino Fa’asuamaleaui spots a gap, executes a sidestep, pins his ears back and tears towards the line for a fourth try in quick succession.

Munster continues to rack up the kicking stats.

CONVERTED TRY! Australia 44-6 Cook Islands (Preston, 52)

Australia are cutting loose now. Best makes an incursion on the left edge, Munster picks the ball speedily from the ruck and continues the momentum, fending-off one tackler then offloading to Preston on his left who does magnificently to remain in touch and dive over like vintage Xavier Coates.

Munster slots over his second touchline conversion from the left wing!

50 mins: Despite Dearden’s performance, does the absence of goal-kicking options mean Sam Walker has to start by default? There’s no way Australia can head into the knockouts with Cameron Munster as first-choice kicker, surely.

TRY! Australia 38-6 Cook Islands (Trbojevic, 48)

Strange is on to inject some energy and unpredictability. And his industry leads Australia to the 20m line, from where Australia show the cohesion that’s been lacking all world cup. Dearden marshals his right edge, Walsh enters the line, holds the ball up way longer than you can believe, then fizzes a pass out to Trbojevic who finishes in the right corner.

Munster makes a hash of his latest conversion attempt. He clearly prefers the left touchline to the right.

CONVERTED TRY! Australia 34-6 Cook Islands (Bostock, 46)

Order restored. Australia assert themselves through the middle, engineer some space on the outside, and Bostock finishes expertly, stepping in off the left touchline and busting two tackles in the process.

Munster still has kicking duties – and the part-timer nails the touchline conversion!

45 mins: Cook Islands reach halfway in reply. Takairangi runs on the last, creates some space on his right edge, but Wigmore drops a simple pass. This is not an elite start to the half from either side.

44 mins: Disjointed and sloppy from Australia inside 20m with no cohesion whatsoever in attack, highlighted by Trbojevic absolutely ironed out by Takairangi in the process. A set restart on the last spares the blushes and a couple of tackles later an overwhelmed Cook Islands defence is powerless to deny Yeo one-out from a couple of metres. But even that is found to have contained an error! The skipper didn’t maintain possession with a couple of defenders hanging off him and the Bunker removes the score.

42 mins: Simple from the Kangaroos first-up with a set punctuated by a Grant snipe that allows Dearden to gain metres on the right edge. Brown then continues his horror night by losing the ball in contact and Australia have a full set inside the red zone.

41 mins: Cook Islands get us back under way in Townsville.

Australia selection battles:

  • Reece Walsh has done well at fullback but he looks to be carrying a knock.

  • Tom Dearden has shone at halfback.

  • Jacob Preston has made two eye-catching try-assist linebreaks on the left edge.

Reece Walsh has done well at fullback but he looks to be carrying a knock.

Tom Dearden has shone at halfback.

Jacob Preston has made two eye-catching try-assist linebreaks on the left edge.

There are still plenty of issues to solve for Kevin Walters though. Against modest opposition the Kangaroos have completed at just 66%, conceded seven errors, and executed only one offload. All world cup so far Australia have played with a lack of intensity and focus. It’s understandable tonight in a virtual dead rubber but the lack of leadership and attitude in green is concerning.

Feel free to spend your half-time break following the cricket. It has been South Africa’s morning so far as the third Test and the series goes down to the wire.

Half-time: Australia 28-6 Cook Islands

Australia are comfortably the superior of the two sides but there are still serious issues with execution for the defending champions.

39 mins: And Cook Islands almost make them pay! The bomb on the last isn’t dealt with and the scraps land in white hands. There are numbers on the right but a combinatino of inaccurate passing and Australia’s desperation denies the score. Cook Islands come again and Takairangi grubbers through on tackle two but the Kangaroos are well set. The underdogs get the ball back again and this time Holmes tries to grubber on the left, but to no avail.

37 mins: Another sloppy couple of phases from the Kangaroos. First up there’s a poor connection on the left edge with a try in the offing following the penalty to Munster. Then Yeo loses his bearings on halfway and instead of accepting contact throws a pass that’s both forward and off target.

35 mins: Ooof! A massive sharp intake of breath as Munster yelps in pain as Holmes near-hip-drop tackles the stand-off as he took bodies with him towards the posts. He seems ok, thankfully. Meanwhile replays show Trbojevic wincing after rolling his ankle. Kevin Walters will be keeping Ethan Strange on his toes.

34 mins: Australia with a solid restart set, Cook Islands not so much. Not only does Brown carry loosely coming out of defence he burns a captain’s challenge in the process.

CONVERTED TRY! Australia 28-6 Cook Islands (Walsh, 32)

Too easy for the Kangaroos. On defensive 30 Australia shift from the right edge through hands to Munster who holds the ball up and drops it inside to Preston who accelerates through a non-existent defensive line. For the second time tonight the Bulldog has only one man to beat and this time he draws in Holmes then offloads to Walsh on his shoulder. A training ground 70m score.

Interestingly, Munster takes over kicking duties. There has been a suggestion Walsh has picked up a foot injury. The Storm five-eighth makes no mistake with the easy conversion.

30 mins: Couple of issues on Australia’s right side with a forward pass denying Trbojevic a line break, then the Manly flyer getting beaten in the air and knocking on. Or maybe not. A captain’s challenge successfully proves the first touch in the duel was from Brown.
