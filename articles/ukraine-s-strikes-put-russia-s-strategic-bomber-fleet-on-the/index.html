Ukraine’s Strikes Put Russia’s Strategic Bomber Fleet on the Run

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:12 · source · permalink

Russia is relocating its strategic bomber fleet in an effort to protect the aircraft from further Ukrainian strikes on military airfields, Ukrainian Air Force spokesperson Yurii Ihnat said during a televised interview, according to Ukrinform on November 16.

The movements come after Ukrainian operations targeting Russian military airfields, including a Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) Operation Spiderweb that damaged Russian military aircraft.

Ihnat explained that relocating strategic bombers could help Russia reduce potential aircraft losses, but stressed that Moscow has no intention of abandoning their use as long as it retains long-range weapons capable of striking Ukrainian targets.

“As long as there are bombs, as long as there are missiles that can fly long distances—I mean the Kh-101 missiles they use—of course, no one is going to retire them,” Ihnat said.

According to the spokesperson, Russia also moved its strategic aviation assets following the SBU operation targeting military aircraft, seeking to keep its bombers beyond the reach of additional Ukrainian attacks.

However, these redeployments do not necessarily reduce Russia’s ability to carry out future missile strikes. Ihnat noted that Russian strategic bombers can fly to the European part of Russia within half a day, where they can begin preparations for another attack.

While moving aircraft between airfields may offer Russia greater protection against Ukrainian strikes, Ihnat emphasized that such measures should not be interpreted as a sign that Moscow intends to stop using its strategic aviation fleet.

Earlier, reports emerged that Russia’s Kazan Aviation Plant, a major production facility for nuclear-capable Tu-160 bombers and Tu-214 aircraft, has delivered just three Tu-214 jets since 2022 while failing to establish sustained production of new strategic bombers, despite ambitious government plans to expand aircraft manufacturing.
