MobLand Officially Delivers Tom Hardy’s Best Acting Ahead of Season 2 Finale

For two seasons, Harry Da Souza has been the Harrigans’ stone-faced fixer, a man who cleans up other people’s bloodshed without changing his expression. That changed in Episode 9, when Tom Hardy let the character smile for the first time in the series.

It was a small moment, and it was the best acting of Hardy’s run on MobLand. It came in Nervous Breakdown, which aired November 13, and it left the audience with one big question going into the finale: What happens when the man who never shows emotion finally lets some out?

Tom Hardy’s Restrained Menace Defines His Best MobLand Performance

The smile comes during a tense sit-down in an abandoned pub. Frankie Cooper has kidnapped Harry’s teenage daughter, Gina (Teddie Allen), and Harry walks in knowing he may not walk out. Asked whether he has just sacrificed himself for her, he shrugs it off and says the answer is about to become clear.

Hardy keeps the scene tightly controlled. Harry says he timed the walk from the car to the pub and figured out that Frankie’s men need seven seconds to get to him. In his view, that’s plenty of time to do real damage with the pint glass sitting in front of him. While he spells it out, a grin flickers across his face. It’s quick, and it’s creepy, and it hits so hard because Hardy has held this back from us all season.

Most of what makes this work is how little Hardy actually does. He barely raises his voice. When Harry threatens someone, he says it the way you would tell a friend what’s for dinner. So when the smile finally shows up, you feel it. Harry has always been about duty, and he says so himself when he tells Frankie, “It’s not loyalty, it’s my job.”

But Gina is the one person who gets past all that. The grin tells you he’s just a dad who is scared and has given up hiding it. You would expect that to soften him, but it really doesn’t. If anything, you trust him even less.

The episode has some funnier bits too. Harry runs off to find Gina in a bathrobe, with coffee, toast and a submachine gun in his hands, and Hardy plays the panic as plain annoyance. Later he shares a scene with Helen Mirren’s Maeve Harrigan, and he seems to be holding back another smile.

Then there’s his speech about wearing Frankie’s face to Notting Hill Carnival if anything happens to Gina. He says it calmly, almost like he’s chatting, and the joke buried in it makes it feel even worse.

Johnny Flynn’s Frankie Cooper Gives Hardy a Worthy Rival

Johnny Flynn deserves a lot of credit for how well the scene works. He plays Frankie, and he was born in Johannesburg in 1983. He also fronts the folk band Johnny Flynn & the Sussex Wit. On screen, he’s been around since the 2013 film Song One and has played some pretty creepy characters on TV since then.

Frankie is a London drug lord who turned up at the very end of the Season 2 premiere, and he treats every threat like an inside joke.

Flynn has said his first day on the job was a scary one, because it was the scene where Frankie attacks Bella in Episode 2. He also based the character on the Gallagher brothers in the mid-90s, which explains the swagger. He says Frankie is usually the smartest person in the room until Harry walks in, and that’s exactly what you see in the pub. Both men talk quietly and swap amused threats like two guys who respect each other. Hardy gives Flynn plenty of room to hit every beat.

Flynn has called pushing Harry “poking the biggest bear in the woods.” Kat McAllister (Janet McTeer) is making her move on the Harrigan empire as the episode closes, so the finale should show whether that bear has finally been pushed too far.
