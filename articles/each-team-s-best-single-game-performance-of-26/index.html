Each team's best single-game performance of '26

TheTools · 28.11.2026 10:05 · source · permalink

A 10-RBI game? Three home runs in a historic MLB debut? A one-hit shutout with 15 strikeouts? 

All of those single-game feats happened in 2026, a memorable season in which MLB hitters combined for four cycles and 16 three-homer games, while MLB pitchers totaled 29 games of 12 or more K’s. 

Those are just a few of the performances that stand out from another rollicking season of baseball. Here is a look back at each team’s best individual single-game performance of 2026, with an honorable mention listed for each club.

American League East

Blue Jays: Dylan Cease
July 8 at SF (8 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 11 K)

Dave Stieb stands alone as the only pitcher in Blue Jays history to throw a no-hitter, but Cease knocked on the doorstep on a Wednesday afternoon in San Francisco. Although Cease needed 115 pitches to hold the Giants hitless through eight innings, he bounded out of the dugout to take the mound for the ninth anyway. The effort was short-lived, as Heliot Ramos banged a 1-1 fastball into center field to end Cease’s bid at history. But Cease – who was not yet born when Stieb threw his no-hitter – still provided one of the best pitching performances by anyone to wear a Blue Jays uniform (and, it should be noted, the honorable mention belongs in that category, too). 

Honorable mention: Cease, July 25 at BOS (9 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 12 K)

Orioles: Gunnar Henderson
Aug. 30 at ATH (4-for-4, 2 HR, 5 RBIs) 

It may have been a down year for Henderson, but the uber-talented shortstop put his full talent on display on this particular day against the A’s. Fueled by two home runs and a triple, Henderson recorded 12 total bases, the most by an Orioles player since Ryan Mountcastle notched 13 total bases on June 19, 2021. Henderson also helped the O’s to a win, making three sublime defensive plays at shortstop. 

Honorable mention: Pete Alonso, Aug. 9 at TEX (3-for-3, 2 HR, 4 RBIs, 2 BBs)

Rays: Drew Rasmussen
AL Division Series Game 1 vs. NYY (8 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 10 K)

Rasmussen kickstarted a thrilling October for the Rays with one of the greatest pitching performances in franchise history, blanking the Yankees across eight innings of one-hit ball in a narrow 1-0 victory. He became just the seventh pitcher in postseason history to take a no-hit bid at least 7 2/3 innings and the first since Aníbal Sánchez in Game 1 of the 2019 NLCS. Rasmussen’s 10 strikeouts were tied for the most in a postseason game by a Rays pitcher, joining Tyler Glasnow in Game 2 of the 2020 ALDS. 

Honorable mention: Junior Caminero, June 25 vs. KC (3-for-5, 3 HR, 6 RBIs)

Red Sox: Ranger Suarez
April 27 at TOR (8 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 10 K)

Suarez did not waste much time showing why the Red Sox signed him to a five-year, $130 million deal last offseason. In his sixth start with Boston, the steely left-hander delivered eight scoreless innings against the defending AL champions, notching 10 strikeouts. Suarez did not allow a base hit until Jesús Sánchez snuck a single inside the third-base bag leading off the sixth inning. 

Honorable mention: Payton Tolle, Aug. 7 vs. ATH (6 IP, 2 H, 1 R, 14 K)

Yankees: Cam Schlittler
AL Wild Card Series Game 1 vs. BOS (6 1/3 IP, 2 H, 0 R, 10 K) 

In one of the best seasons by a pitcher in the Yankees’ storied history, Schlittler once again saved his best for the postseason -- and, specifically, for the Red Sox. The AL Cy Young Award winner shut down Boston with a 10-strikeout gem over a career-high 117 pitches in Game 1 of the AL Wild Card Series, energizing the Bronx much like he did in his playoff debut a year earlier. Schlittler joined the legendary Bob Gibson as the only pitchers to record two 10-strikeout outings in the postseason against the Red Sox. 

Honorable mention: Ben Rice, AL Wild Card Series Game 1 vs. BOS (4-for-5, 2 HR, 6 RBIs)

AL Central

Guardians: Gavin Williams
Sept. 1 vs. TOR (7 IP, 2 H, 0 ER, 13 K)

Williams led the AL with 248 strikeouts in 2026 thanks to performances such as the one he had against the Blue Jays to open September at Progressive Field. He didn’t allow a hit until the fourth inning, cruised through seven frames with only an unearned run allowed and finished with 13 K’s, the most by a Guardians starter in a game since Triston McKenzie in 2022.

Honorable mention: Travis Bazzana, June 20 at HOU (4-for-4, 2 HR, 5 RBIs)

Royals: Noah Cameron
Aug. 16 at LAA (9 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 8 K)

Cameron was excellent for the Royals in 2025, finishing fourth in AL Rookie of the Year voting with a 2.99 ERA in 24 starts. He struggled early in ’26 but closed the year strong with a great second half, including a one-hit road shutout of the Angels in mid-August. The only hit the lefty allowed was a bloop single in the seventh, and he finished with K.C.’s first one-hitter since Zack Greinke in 2009.

Honorable mention: Jac Caglianone, Aug. 8 vs. CHC (4-for-4, 2 2B, 2 HR, 4 RBIs)

Tigers: Colt Keith
June 15 at HOU (3-for-4, 3 HR, 6 RBIs)

Keith’s lone multihomer game in 2026 was quite the effort: He launched three dingers in a 9-3 rout of the Astros. After being hit by a pitch with the bases loaded his first time up, Keith smacked a two-run tater in the third inning, another two-run shot in the seventh and a solo shot in the ninth. He finished with six RBIs, beating his previous career high (set in 2024) by two.

Honorable mention: Casey Mize, June 29 at NYY (7 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 10 K)

Twins: Bailey Ober
May 12 vs. MIA (9 IP, 2 H, 0 R, 7 K)

Ober pitched the Twins’ first shutout since Pablo López in July 2023, blanking the Marlins at Target Field. He allowed a bunt single in the second inning and another base hit in the fourth, but that was all Miami could muster against the 6-foot-9 right-hander, who struck out seven without issuing a walk.

Honorable mention: Byron Buxton, April 14 vs. BOS (4-for-5, 2 HR)

White Sox: Tristan Peters
July 10 vs. ATH (4-for-4, 2B, 3B, HR, 4 RBIs)

The 2026 season was a breakout year for Peters, who went from a onetime member of the Savannah Bananas to a key contributor for the White Sox. He etched his name into club history with a cycle against the Athletics, slugging a homer and a triple in the same seventh inning to complete the feat. One day later, Peters was named an All-Star for the first time in his career.

Honorable mention: Miguel Vargas, Aug. 14 at DET (4-for-5, 2B, 2 HR)

AL West

Angels: Zach Neto
Aug. 20 at HOU (5-for-6, 2B, HR, 7 RBIs)

Neto has blossomed into a dynamic shortstop for the Angels, and we saw that in full force on this day. Neto set a career high with five hits and seven RBIs, becoming the first player in Angels franchise history to achieve both of those feats in the same game. 

Honorable mention: Reid Detmers, May 24 vs. TEX (8 IP, 1 H, 1 R, 14 K)

Astros: Lucas Spence
Sept. 24 at ATH (3-for-4, 3 HR, 5 RBIs)

Entering this game, Spence hadn’t homered in his first 13 career MLB games in which he recorded a plate appearance. He proceeded to hit not one, not two, but three home runs in his game -- the first three homers of Spence’s career. Spence joined Yordan Alvarez as the only Astros rookies to homer three times in a game. 

Honorable mention: Yordan Alvarez, July 4 vs. TB (3-for-4, 2 HR, 6 RBIs)

Athletics: J.T. Ginn
May 18 at LAA (8 IP, 2 H, 2 R, 10 K)

Ginn carried a no-hitter into the ninth inning in Anaheim, setting himself up for history before an unfortunate ending. The A’s righty lost his no-hitter to begin the ninth inning, and Zach Neto followed with a walk-off two-run homer. Nonetheless, Ginn tossed eight innings of two-run ball and struck out 10 batters. 

Honorable mention: Nick Kurtz, June 15 vs. PIT (3-for-4, 2 HR, 5 RBIs)

Mariners: Cal Raleigh
Aug. 24 vs. PHI (3-for-4, 3 HR, 4 RBIs)

Raleigh might have struggled to live up to the lofty heights of his 60-homer season in 2025, but we saw his epic power in this game. Raleigh slugged three home runs against the Phillies, which was remarkably the first time in his career that he homered three times in a single game. 

Honorable mention: Bryan Woo, Sept. 6 vs. ATH (8 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 9 K)

Rangers: Brandon Nimmo
April 11 at LAD (3-for-4, 2B, 2 HR)

Nimmo’s best game in his first season with the Rangers came early in the year against the Dodgers in L.A., as the veteran outfielder recorded three hits and two home runs. It was Nimmo’s only multihomer effort this season, and for good measure, he also doubled. 

Honorable mention: Jacob deGrom, Aug. 31 vs. ATH (7 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 8 K)

National League East

Braves: Chris Sale
Aug. 27 vs. LAD (9 IP, 5 H, 0 R, 11 K)

Sale is an ageless wonder, and the 37-year-old was at his best against the defending World Series champs. He tossed his first shutout since June 5, 2019 -- when he was still with the Red Sox -- and became the first Braves pitcher to throw a shutout since Max Fried on April 23, 2024. The 10-time All-Star also unleashed a 100.1 mph fastball to Shohei Ohtani, cracking triple digits on the radar gun for the first time since 2018. 

Honorable mention: Ronald Acuña Jr., May 30 at CIN (2-for-4, 2 HR, 2 SB)

Marlins: Eury Pérez
Sept. 25 vs. ATL (9 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 8 K) 

Pérez waded into new territory for his first career complete game, a one-hit shutout against the NL East champion Braves. He had never pitched into the eighth inning in any of his previous 67 career starts before firing a career-high 105 pitches. Pérez, just 23 years old, became the seventh Marlins pitcher to toss a one-hit shutout and the first since Aníbal Sánchez on Sept. 10, 2011. 

Honorable mention: Pérez, July 5 at ATH (7 IP, 0 H, 0 R, 8 K)

Mets: Francisco Lindor
Sept. 12 at NYY (3-for-5, 2 HR, 5 RBIs)

Lindor tends to be at his best when the Mets play the Yankees, and that was certainly the case here. The five-time All-Star slugged two home runs -- a leadoff blast off Gerrit Cole and a grand slam -- on the fifth anniversary of his three-homer game against the Yankees, nonetheless. Lindor became just the eighth player in MLB history to hit a leadoff home run and a grand slam in the same game, per the Elias Sports Bureau. 

Honorable mention: Bo Bichette, June 12 vs. ATL (2-for-3, 2 HR, 6 RBIs)

Nationals: Andrés Chaparro
July 17 at ATH (4-for-5, 2 HR, 8 RBIs) 

Only one player -- the Pirates’ Ryan O’Hearn -- had more RBIs in a game this season than Chaparro, who tallied a career-high eight RBIs in a 23-4 Nationals romp. Chaparro became the first Nationals player to have at least eight RBIs in a game since Mark Reynolds notched 10 RBIs on July 7, 2018. The 27-year-old, who also belted two home runs, went into the night with just one home run and seven RBIs through his first 31 games of 2026. 

Honorable mention: Cade Cavalli, Aug. 13 vs. CHC (8 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 10 K)

Phillies: Bryce Harper
June 20 vs. NYM (4-for-5, 2B, 3B, HR, 3 RBIs) 

Is it possible to overshadow a three-homer game? That’s what Harper did to his Phillies teammate, Kyle Schwarber, in a 15-3 rout of the Mets. Harper became the 10th Phillies player to hit for the cycle when he barreled into third base on an eighth-inning triple. The two-time MVP showed plenty of emotion as he completed the feat, and for good reason -- not only are cycles incredibly rare, but Harper entered the game mired in a 1-for-22 slump over his past seven games. 

Honorable mention: Jesús Luzardo, Sept. 7 vs. ATL (9 IP, 2 H, 0 R, 12 K)

NL Central

Brewers: Jacob Misiorowski
June 12 vs. PHI (9 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 15 K)

Misiorowski turned in not only one of the best pitching performances of the season but one of the best in recent memory. The NL Cy Young Award winner gave up just one hit -- a Kyle Schwarber leadoff single in the fourth inning -- and induced a double play to end up facing the minimum 27 batters. Most impressively, Misiorowski racked up 15 strikeouts, the most by any pitcher in a game in 2026, on just 95 pitches.

Honorable mention: Cooper Pratt, World Series Game 4 at TB (4-for-5, 2B, HR)

Cardinals: Joshua Báez
Aug. 15 at CHC (3-for-4, 3 HR, 5 RBIs)

No player had homered three times in their Major League debut before Báez, who did it in his first three career AT-BATS, a remarkable achievement for the young St. Louis outfielder. Báez ripped a 449-foot two-run dinger in the first inning, slugged a solo shot in the fourth and added another two-run homer in the sixth.

Honorable mention: Alec Burleson, Aug. 3 at NYY (3-for-5, 3 HR, 6 RBIs)

Cubs: Dansby Swanson
July 1 vs. SD (3-for-5, 3 HR, 8 RBIs)

Swanson had quite the series against the Mets in late June at Citi Field, driving in 15 runs in three games, and he carried that production into a home set with the Padres. After two homers and three RBIs on June 30, Swanson launched three dingers the next day -- a solo shot, a three-run homer and then a grand slam -- in a 23-3 Cubs rout.

Honorable mention: Pete Crow-Armstrong, June 15 vs. COL (4-for-4, 2B, 3B, HR)

Pirates: Ryan O'Hearn
July 7 vs. ATL (4-for-5, 3 HR, 10 RBIs)

A first-inning grand slam kickstarted a historic game for O’Hearn, whose 10 RBIs were the most in a game in franchise history and the most in MLB since Shohei Ohtani in 2024. O’Hearn slugged a pair of three-run homers to reach double-digit RBIs, then added a single for his fourth hit of the day.

Honorable mention: Paul Skenes, May 12 vs. COL (8 IP, 2 H, 0 R, 10 K)

Reds: JJ Bleday
Aug. 22 at AZ (4-for-6, 2B, 3B, HR, 3 RBIs)

Bleday led off the sixth inning at Chase Field with a home run, giving him his first career cycle and the fourth and final cycle across MLB in 2026. Picked up from the Athletics after being non-tendered, he achieved the feat for Cincinnati in just four at-bats, hitting a double, a triple and a single before his solo dinger.

Honorable mention: Hunter Greene, July 10 vs. CHC (7 IP, 3 H, 0 R, 12 K)

NL West

D-backs: Jose Fernandez
March 31 vs. DET (3-for-4, 2 HR, 4 RBIs)

There have only been nine cases of players homering multiple times in their regular-season debut and, remarkably, three of them came in 2026. The players to achieve the feat were the Cardinals’ Joshua Báez (3 HR), the Guardians’ Chase DeLauter and Fernandez. Fernandez homered twice and had three hits against the Tigers, including the game-winning three-run homer in the eighth.

Honorable mention: Ildemaro Vargas, April 26 vs. SD (3-for-5, 2B, 3B, HR, 4 RBIs)

Dodgers: Andy Pages
May 6 at HOU (3-for-5, 3 HR, 6 RBIs)

Pages’ career-best season was exemplified in this performance against the Astros, as he recorded the first three-homer game of his career and drove in six runs. Pages and Max Muncy were the only Dodgers to hit three home runs in a game in 2026. 

Honorable mention: Muncy, April 10 vs. TEX (4-for-5, 3 HR, 3 RBIs)

Giants: Heliot Ramos
July 6 vs. TOR (3-for-5, 3B, 2 HR, 5 RBIs)

Ramos was traded to the Yankees a month later, but he had one final monster performance in store for the Giants. Ramos had two homers, a triple and five RBIs against the Blue Jays. 

Honorable mention: Matt Chapman, June 5 at CHC (2-for-3, 2 HR, 8 RBIs)

Padres: Jackson Merrill
Sept. 9 vs. WSH (4-for-5, 3B, 2 HR, 6 RBIs)

Merrill’s season might have started slowly, but he sure turned his fortunes around as the year progressed. In a torrid second half that saw many notable performances, it was this one against the Nationals that stood out, as he recorded four hits, homered twice and drove in six runs. 

Honorable mention: Michael King, NL WC G1 vs. CHC (7 IP, 1 H, 0 R, 8 K)

Rockies: Hunter Goodman
June 14 at ATH (5-for-6, 2B, 2 HR, 4 RBIs)

It was a breakout season for the Rockies catcher, as he crushed 41 home runs and was one of eight players with a 40-homer season. His best performance came in a rout of the A’s in Las Vegas, when Goodman homered twice and set a career high with five hits. 

Honorable mention: Goodman, July 19 vs. CIN (3-for-4, 3 HR, 3 RBIs)
