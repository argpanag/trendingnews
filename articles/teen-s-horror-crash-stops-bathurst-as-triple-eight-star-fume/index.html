Teen’s horror crash stops Bathurst as Triple Eight star fumes over track ‘disgrace’

TheTools · 08.10.2026 21:10 · source · permalink

The journey to the Great Race has begun, with Thursday’s uneventful Supercars practice hampered by complaints over the Bathurst track surface.

Watch every race of the 2026 Repco Supercars Championship LIVE & ad-break free during racing onKayo Sports.

But the biggest moment of the day would come late as Victorian teen Jake Santalucia was lucky to walk away from a horror smash that saw Porsche Carerra Cup qualifying red flagged.

Santalucia had already secured pole but was still flying down the mountain when he lost control, hit the wall and became airborne before slamming into the concrete on the opposite wall.

The crash then delayed the final event of the day as clean up crews got to work.

Earlier it was youngsters Kai Allen and Lochie Dalton who were able to top the Supercars timesheets in the two sessions - the second a practice only for co-drivers, as their more experienced rivals raised concerns over the Mount Panorama surface.

Triple Eight star Scott Pye even going as far as labelling it a “disgrace” during his time behind the wheel on Thursday afternoon.

“Just a note for race control, that’s probably some of the worst track conditions I’ve ever seen here for marbles off line,” Pye could be heard telling his Triple Eight team.

“I don’t know what they need to do but they’ve got to get out here and clean it over the weekend because it’s a disgrace.”

Mark Winterbottom, pairing with Cam Waters, was then heard asking: “Is there oil at turn one?”

“Just see what’s going on there mate,” he added.

In commentary Garth Tander labelled it “tricky conditions” while Kiwi great Greg Murphy was puzzled as to why the track hadn’t improved as Thursday’s racing rolled on.

“There is so much dirt and dust out there on the racetrack as well which is clearly causing some issues,” Murphy said.

“Get off line ever so slightly, brake problem or not, you’re going to run wide and potentially go up the escape road as we saw a couple of cars do.

“The track is filthy out there at the moment… it’s really weird – I’m not really sure where all that dirt and dust has come from!

“We’ve got a lot of debris on the racetrack ... It should be getting better now and I’m not sure it’s got that much better this afternoon.”

But despite the tricky conditions, all drivers managed to avoid any carnage on day one of practice.

Reuben Goodall went within a kitten’s whisker of putting Thomas Randle’s Tickford Racing entry into the wall at Reid Park while Tim Slade had to bail out at turn one to avoid disaster. Tony D’Alberto gave his team a scare when he ran off at The Chase while Lee Holdsworth went the escape route at Murray’s Corner.

But the afternoon belonged to Dalton, who also topped the opening DUNLOP Super2 Series practice session on Thursday morning, as he upstaged several big name co-drivers for two-time Bathurst winners Erebus.

Earlier in the day it was brake issues hampering a number of teams including both Triple Eight Racing and Walkinshaw TWG Racing entries.

Racing returns on Friday from 10am with a third practice.
