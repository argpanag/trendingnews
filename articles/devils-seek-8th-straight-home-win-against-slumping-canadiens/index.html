Devils seek 8th straight home win against slumping Canadiens

TheTools · 18.11.2026 03:26 · source · permalink

Connor Hellebuyck is 7-0-0 since joining New Jersey, as Montreal looks to snap an 0-for-12 power-play rut and avoid a third consecutive defeat.

The New Jersey Devils have been on a roll. Especially at home, and especially since the addition of Connor Hellebuyck.

The Devils aim for an eighth consecutive home victory Wednesday night in Newark, N.J., against the Montreal Canadiens, who hope to avoid a third straight loss.

New Jersey is 13-3-0 since opening the season with two wins and three losses. The Devils have outscored opponents 32-15 during their current seven-game winning streak at home, where they're 9-2-0 overall.

"There's 75% of the season remaining," Devils coach Sheldon Keefe said. "But I have a tremendous level of optimism because of our group and how we've come together and found ways to win.

"All we're gonna do is push ahead. We're not going to get too excited about anything we've done to this point."

The Devils return to Newark after sweeping a home-and-home with Washington. On Saturday, the Devils overcame a 3-2 deficit after one period to win 7-4 at Washington.

It wasn't the prettiest victory for New Jersey, but a triumph, nonetheless.

"We have been talking here about nights when you don't quite have it," said Keefe, whose team allowed four goals for the fourth time in the last six games.

"You find a way to win a game when you don't have it."

The Devils are 7-0-0 in games started by star goaltender Hellebuyck, who has a 2.25 goals-against average since being traded by Winnipeg to New Jersey on Oct. 23.

Backup Jake Allen -- traded to the Devils from Montreal during the 2023-24 campaign -- is 4-1-0 with a 2.39 goals-against average at home, where he last saw action Oct. 24.

New Jersey is 6-0-1 versus Montreal since last losing to the Canadiens in regulation, 3-2 on Jan. 17, 2024. And these Devils might be catching the Canadiens at the right time.

Montreal averaged 3.67 goals during a 9-0-3 stretch, but has been outscored 7-2 in a pair of losses since. The Canadiens opened the scoring Monday at the New York Rangers, but fell 3-1.

It was the third time in four games that Montreal managed just one goal. The Canadiens also rank second-to-last in the league in converting on 12.5% of their power-play chances this season, and are 0-for-12 in that department in the last four games.

"The more games you go without producing on the 'PP,' I think the pressure rises, for sure," Montreal coach Martin St. Louis said. "Probably, the stress level goes up.

"It's uncomfortable right now for those guys on the 'PP,' and we've got to find a way to get comfortable through conversations, through teaching, through some reps."

Montreal star Cole Caufield has recorded just one of his team-leading 12 goals in the last five games, and only two power-play scores this season. Caufield has just two assists and no goals in his last four games against the Devils -- and no power-play goals in 13 career games versus New Jersey.

The Canadiens' Jacob Fowler has allowed 18 goals in his last six starts. Teammate Jakub Dobes has yielded seven in his last two.

New Jersey's Jesper Bratt has two goals and eight assists during his eight-game point streak. He's recorded 10 goals with 13 assists in 23 career games versus Montreal.
