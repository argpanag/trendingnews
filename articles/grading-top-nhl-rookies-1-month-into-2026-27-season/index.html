Grading Top NHL Rookies 1 Month Into 2026-27 Season

TheTools · 03.11.2026 00:56 · source · permalink

It's not exam season at school yet, but we're pulling out the red pen for the top rookies in the NHL. One month into the season, we're seeing how their starts stack up against one another and where they might go from here.

Sometimes a rookie takes a while to find his feet, whether this plays out as a hot start that cools off, a slow start that trends upward, or an inconsistent run of games as he adjusts to playing in the big leagues. 

You could lump players like Anton Frondell and Roman Kantserov of the Chicago Blackhawks, as well as Ilya Protas of the Washington Capitals, under this label. Frondell had an impressive start, though his pure scoring has not continued; Kantserov didn't score in his first five games, but has started to pick it up. As a group, they're a pretty good example of the range of rookie experiences.

We'll likely be folding these three into our grading in future updates, so keep an eye out. For now, let's turn our grading scale first to Boston and a former projected first-overall pick who went seventh.

James Hagens, C, Boston Bruins

Grade: A

Bruins forward James Hagens is currently tied with two others for second in rookie scoring, with nine points in 13 games (4 goals, 5 assists). His points-per-game pace is also good for second in rookie scoring, at 0.69 points per game.

More impressive is how he's producing: Eight of those points, including all of his goals, have come at even strength. He isn't relying on special-teams opportunities to generate offense—an encouraging sign as he adjusts to a full-time NHL role.

Those who have followed his game for years may not be surprised, but this is a stronger start than many likely expected. At 5'11" and 180 pounds, he isn't imposing, yet he consistently gets into the dirty areas. He's shown he can drive play, score highlight-reel goals and process the game quickly.

It's that processing that helped him realize that he can't rely on those fancy goals or sticking to the perimeter, and once he clocked that, he started driving to the net more and getting inside more. That adaptability is going to serve him very well, and because of that, the rest will come.

Michael Brandsegg-Nygård, RW, Detroit Red Wings

Grade: A-

Winger Michael Brandsegg-Nygård has had a solid start on a team that is, to put it mildly, in turmoil. He currently sits fifth in rookie scoring, with eight points (2 goals, 6 assists) in 12 games; his points-per-game pace at 0.67 is better than that of one of the three players tied for second.

Brandsegg-Nygård is also averaging about 15:33 per game; the only players on this list with higher average playing times are Cole Hutson and Ivar Stenberg. He's really being relied on, and that seems like it's not going to change any time soon. 

If he continues on his current trajectory, he could end up a dark horse for one of the Calder nominee spots. Obviously, we want to see how his play bears out over more games before we make that call, but it's never too early to note his potential. He's one of the few Red Wings players consistently bringing physicality and pairing it with offensive production, something the team seems to value.

He's got a bit of a leg up on other rookies who didn't have the time in the league he did last season; between that familiarity and being trusted on the second power-play unit, it's not a stretch to think he could stay on his current path.

Alberts Šmits, D, New York Rangers

Grade: C+

This isn't the start we wanted to see from Alberts Šmits, but some of that comes down to the team around him. The Rangers haven't exactly been a welcoming environment for developing players lately.

Still, head coach Mike Sullivan's praise—even as Šmits searches for his first NHL point—suggests the team sees promise in him. He's also the first 18-year-old defenseman to play for the Rangers since Dave Maloney in 1974.

Šmits is an incredibly intelligent, detail-oriented player. He processes the game at a high level, which should help him continue to improve. He's still making mistakes, but his decisiveness helps limit their impact: He commits to his reads rather than compounding an error with hesitation.

With all of that in mind, the Rangers could put him in a better position to succeed. He's struggling alongside Braden Schneider, and an extended run with a different defense partner would give us a better sense of what he can do. He doesn't look overwhelmed, which is encouraging as he adjusts to the NHL. But the Rangers can't simply count on him to figure it out; they need to help him along rather than say, "Oh, he'll be fine," and carry on.

Porter Martone, RW, Philadelphia Flyers

Grade: C+

What is going on in Philadelphia? Porter Martone has just six points in 15 games and is averaging about 14 minutes of ice time.

Last season, he averaged 17:02 across nine regular-season appearances, and his overall play looked better. That doesn't necessarily mean more minutes would solve his struggles, but it's fair to question his usage.

With the Flyers struggling, why not give such a skilled rookie more opportunity? He's ostensibly on the second line, but his ice time hardly suggests a prominent role.

To be clear, Martone also needs to make the most of the minutes he gets. This isn't all on the Flyers. He's been a bit too passive along the boards and has lacked urgency at times—issues we flagged during his draft season and hoped he was moving past. His skating limitations become more apparent when he isn't playing with pace.

Even so, the Flyers need to give him more ice time. The only way out is through, and Martone may need a longer leash to find consistency. His upside makes that a chance worth taking.

Ivar Stenberg, LW, San Jose Sharks

Grade: A-

The only reason this is not a solid A-grade is that we'd really like to see more production from Ivar Stenberg at even strength. Right now he's feasting on the power play—and you know what, good for him. But he's a highly skilled player, and he absolutely has the talent to score at even strength.

Stenberg is one of three players tied for second in rookie scoring with 9 points; unlike the other two, his split is three goals and six assists. His points-per-game pace is tied with Hagens at 0.69, good for second among rookies. He's a great example of why we don't use plus-minus as an overall judgment metric, because he's a minus-9 right now, and that's not really reflective of how he's played this season.

So far, seven of his points have come on the power play, as noted above. It's great that he's being trusted with that top power-play unit time, and it's something that we both hope and expect to see continue. He has been particularly good at identifying and taking high-danger chances, which is not surprising to anyone who has watched him prior to this season. 

Hopefully, next time we grade these guys, more of Stenberg's points will be 5-on-5.

Gavin McKenna, LW, Toronto Maple Leafs

Grade: A

To no one's surprise, No. 1 overall pick Gavin McKenna is leading all rookies in points with 11 in 15 games at the time of writing. His points-per-game pace is also the highest, at 0.73, and his points are almost evenly split between special teams and even strength, with five power-play points and six at even strength.

Although McKenna's 6:36 outing against the Buffalo Sabres brings down his average ice time, 13:46 per game still feels low for a first-overall pick. It's unclear why Toronto Maple Leafs head coach Jim Hiller has limited his minutes. If it's a deliberate effort to manage a rookie's workload, that would be easier to understand.

However, that figure should be going up pretty soon. McKenna just started playing with Auston Matthews on the top line following Kirill Marchenko's recent injury. 

McKenna has had some struggles through the neutral zone and with some of his decision-making in general, but that was the kind of adjustment issue we expected.

We're just looking for him to continue to grow that offensive contribution and find consistency. Since we're only a month into the season, no alarm bells are ringing for anyone.

Trevor Connelly, LW, Vegas Golden Knights

Grade: B+

Vegas Golden Knights winger Trevor Connelly is tied for second place in rookie scoring with Stenberg and Hagens at nine points, but with a slightly lower points-per-game pace of 0.64, as he's played 14 games.

His four-goal, five-assist split is the same as Hagens, and if he continues to play the way he has been this season, we fully anticipate him breaking the 20-goal mark.

Regardless of his goal totals, Connelly has shown he has the ability to enter the Calder conversation. He's a highly skilled player whose on-ice discipline and decision-making have sometimes limited his offensive impact.

While a month isn't a long time, this start suggests he is letting his skill lead the way. Another encouraging sign: He has no penalty minutes so far. For a player whose on-ice discipline has drawn criticism, that's a positive start.

If Connelly continues on his current trajectory, he could become another homegrown scoring success for Vegas—a player the organization may want to retain rather than trade.

Cole Hutson, D, Washington Capitals

Grade: B+

Hutson gets a B+ rather than a slightly lower grade in part because of the instability around him. In just 11 games, he's already played with seven defensive partners, the latest being Jack St. Ivany on Wednesday night.

Yes, you read that right: His seventh in 11 games.

That's the kind of chaos even the Edmonton Oilers' coaching carousel of the early 2010s couldn't have dreamed up. Against that backdrop, Hutson's five points—most of them at even strength—and roughly 19 minutes of ice time per game are impressive. He's already topped 20 minutes four times.

Ideally, the Capitals will find Hutson a regular defensive partner soon. The constant changes make it difficult to assess the finer points of his game. He's an exceptionally skilled player, aggressive on offense and perceptive all over the ice. A steady partner who can anticipate his decisions and complement his style should help him make better use of those strengths.
