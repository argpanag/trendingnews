Near-Record Heat Fueling Strong Storms and Flooding Threat

TheTools · 12.10.2026 10:12 · source · permalink

We continue to monitor a volatile combination of near-record heat and high humidity Tuesday. This sets the table for strong to severe storms and possible flooding across the area Tuesday evening into Wednesday morning.

Latest data shows storms popping up as early as 4 p.m. but more likely after 6pm. Most of these will be strong with a few severe storms possible. Our severe risk level is 2/5 for gusty winds 60+ mph and hail up to quarter size. Tornadoes are not likely but also not a 0% chance.

50% chance your evening plans could be interrupted. Latest models show these storms tracking across Kansas City.

Localized ponding of water is possible from scattered stronger storms during the evening. Storms become more widespread, losing their severe capability but spreading the flood risk to more communities.

WEDNESDAY & THURSDAY: The cold front sparking these storms loses momentum and stalls just of Kansas City. The front hangs out here all day Wednesday, generating more storms. Newest data suggests dry times with rounds of showers and some heavier downpours expected most likely south of Kansas City

A new system of rain and storms heads north in the late afternoon. This last round of heavy showers and storms arrive Wednesday night and spills over into most of the day Thursday.

Current data produces 1″ - 3″ rain totals with isolated areas receiving 3″ - 5″. We begin to dry out and cool as a secondary punch of truly fall Canadian air works in from north to south.

LATE WEEK: Get ready for a dramatic switch-up in temperatures. From a high of 90 Tuesday to a high of 60 on Friday. Saturday morning’s low temperature to 39 degrees is our coolest since May 9th. Time to exchange our summer clothes for fall gear. Change the filter in your hvac system because your heater will kick on if you want to stay comfy in your home.
