Head to head: Porto have played Manchester City four times in Europe, and are yet to beat them. In the 2020-21 Champions League, City won 3-1 at home and drew 0-0 away in the group stage – before going on to lose the final to Chelsea in this very stadium.

The two sides also met in the 2011-12 Europa League knockouts, with City winning 6-1 on aggregate, before getting bundled out by Sporting in the next round.

Over in Bruges, Aston Villa are holding on to a 3-2 lead with 10 minutes to go. Follow that one with Rob Smyth …

Maresca has apparently introduced music to training sessions – Gabby Logan pushes him on the genre, and he says: “house music – I like house music, you know, boom-boom-boom.” Italo-house, perchance?

Enzo Maresca chats to Amazon Prime before the match: “It’s a hard place to come, but we’re going to try our best … it’s the first game, it’s going to be a long run. We’ll see where we are in December and January.”

He’s asked about the pressure of following Pep Guardiola, but brushes it off. “I’m trying to do what I did at Leicester, at Chelsea, it’s nothing different.” On summer signings, he adds: “We have quality players, that means service for Erling [Haaland], movement between the lines.”

Preamble

Manchester City’s Enzo Maresca era has got off to a decent start, with three wins from three in the Premier League. The Champions League will offer a sterner test than their domestic opposition so far, with PSG, Barcelona, Napoli and plenty more top-tier teams dropping into their autumn fixture list.

First up, though, is a September trip to Porto – nice for the fans, not so much for the City players. Also managed by a tactically cautious Italian in Francesco Farioli, the Portuguese champions also boast a 100% league record this season. With the Manchester derby up next, the hard work starts here for Maresca and his new-look City side.

Kick-off is 8pm (BST).
