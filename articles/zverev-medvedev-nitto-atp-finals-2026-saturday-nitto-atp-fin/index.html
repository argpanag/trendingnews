Zverev Medvedev Nitto ATP Finals 2026 Saturday | Nitto ATP Finals | Tennis

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:58 · source · permalink

Alexander Zverev set a fitting finale at the Nitto ATP Finals Saturday, when he ousted Daniil Medvedev to set up a championship-match clash with second seed Carlos Alcaraz at the prestigious season-ending event.

The top-seeded German is yet to drop a set this week in Turin and continued that trend to defeat familiar foe Medvedev 7-6(0), 6-4 in the semi-finals, notching his Tour-leading 69th win of the season. Zverev is now 9-14 in the pair’s Lexus ATP Head2Head series, having also defeated Medvedev in their most recent meeting, which came in the Rolex Paris Masters quarter-finals in 2025.

"I was finding ways even when I was not playing my best from the baseline," Zverev said following his triumph. "I was still finding ways. I am very happy with the way I served today. I felt like there were not a lot of [break points] because both of us were serving unbelievably. Credit to him [Medvedev]. He played a fantastic tournament and a fantastic season."

Zverev, who won the Nitto ATP Finals crown in 2018 and 2021, will now aim to add to his stellar 2026 Sunday, when he faces Alcaraz in the final. The pair last squared off in the Australian Open semi-finals, in which the Spaniard edged Zverev in a five-set battle, eventually clinching the crown to become the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam. Zverev trails 6-7 in the pair’s rivalry.

Since that meeting, Zverev has enjoyed standout success of his own. The German clinched his first two major trophies at Roland Garros and the US Open, then ascended to World No. 1 for the first time in his career early in November.

Zverev celebrated another landmark achievement earlier this week in Turin, where he was honoured for his ATP Year-End No. 1 presented by PIF finish. The 29-year-old is now eyeing his third Nitto ATP Finals crown to put the finishing touch on a career-defining season.

In Saturday's semi-final clash, both players dominated behind their serves throughout the opening set, maintaining a high first-serve percentage and offering little opportunity on return. Neither player managed to earn or face a break point, as the set inevitably headed into a tie-break.

Zverev continued maintaining his serving precision to produce a flawless tie-break in which he did not drop a single point. Medvedev, meanwhile, struggled with a series of uncharacteristic errors, allowing Zverev to take complete control. The German ultimately capped off a commanding tie-break display with a clutch running forehand pass to draw first blood.

The second set followed a similar pattern, with both players holding serve comfortably. However, swinging freely off both wings, Zverev turned up the pressure at 3-3 and capitalised on a sloppy service game from Medvedev, who surrendered the break with a costly double fault. Taking a 4-3 lead, Zverev maintained his composure and eventually closed out the match in one hour and 26 minutes.

Zverev did not drop his serve throughout the round-robin stage and once again dominated behind his deliveries inside the Inalpi Arena. The German finished having won 81 per cent of his first-serve points (39/48) and fired 16 aces to Medvedev's 12, according to Infosys ATP Stats.

Medvedev, the 2020 champion, was seeking a return to the Nitto ATP Finals championship match for the first time since 2021, when he fell to Zverev. The former World No. 1 ended his season with a 54-22 record, according to the Infosys ATP Win/Loss Index, highlighted by title triumphs in Brisbane, Dubai, Hangzhou and Almaty. Medvedev also reached the final at the ATP Masters 1000 event in Indian Wells, where he fell to Jannik Sinner.
