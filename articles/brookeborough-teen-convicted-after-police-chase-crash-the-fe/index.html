Brookeborough teen convicted after police chase crash - The Fermanagh Herald

TheTools · 25.11.2026 21:00 · source · permalink

A BROOKEBOROUGH teen who claimed he wasn’t the driver of a car that crashed following a police chase said he would take the conviction for his friend.

On Wednesday 19-year-old Kristers Kimenis contested multiple driving charges at Enniskillen Magisterates Court.

He was accused of dangerous driving, not having insurance not displaying learner (L) plates and driving unaccompanied as a learner driver.

The case relates to November 1, 2025, where police were in pursuit of a Volkswagen Passat between the Clabby Road and Drumcor Road in Tempo.

The Passat crashed into a field and the occupants fled and hid in long grass. Police apprehended two men shortly after.

Kimenis told police at the scene that he was the driver and admitted having no insurance, stating that he had bought the car earlier that day.

However at a subsequent interview he gave ‘no comment’ to numerous questions and told police he was not the driver.

‘Taking the wrap’

In court defence counsel Alan Blackburn questioned the police officer who attended the scene, who said that viability on the night was ‘poor’ and he could not see who was driving the Passat.

Kimenis took to the stand and said that he was not driving on the night.

When asked by a prosecutor why he said he did, the teen said he was ‘taking the wrap’ for his friend, who he claimed was driving.

He said that fingerprints taken from the car would prove he was not driving, and when asked by the prosecutor if he had driven the car at all that day he said no.

When the prosecutor said he could be facing a conviction Kimenis said he would take the conviction, adding that he would not say who the driver was.

District judge Alana McSorley, after taking time to consider the case, said that Kimenis was not ‘credible’ in his account, citing his change of story after being caught by police.

Kimenis, who has no previous criminal record, was convicted on all counts and given a 12-month driving ban and £450 in fines.

Mr Blackburn sought bail to appeal the conviction without leave to drive and judge McSorley released the teenager on his own bail of £250.
