Thinking About Switching To An Electric Car? Here's One To Put On Your Radar

TheTools · 20.10.2026 05:25 · source · permalink

With petrol prices putting increasing pressure on household budgets, more Australian drivers may be reconsidering what they want from their next car. While switching to an electric vehicle is a significant financial decision, the prospect of spending less at the bowser is becoming an increasingly compelling reason to explore the alternatives.

Enter the Volvo EX60, the Swedish marque’s newest all-electric SUV, which is set to arrive in Australia in late 2026.

Combining Scandinavian design with long-range capability and the latest in-car technology, the five-seat SUV is designed to make electric driving a practical proposition for everyday life, without compromising on the comfort and refinement expected of a luxury vehicle.

For drivers weighing up the cost of making the switch, running expenses are an important consideration. Charging an electric vehicle at home can cost considerably less than refuelling a petrol-powered car, depending on electricity tariffs, driving habits and vehicle efficiency. The savings will vary, but it’s one of the reasons EVs are attracting attention as fuel costs fluctuate.


Inside, Volvo has focused on the details that make a car feel like a considered investment. The cabin features a panoramic electrochromic roof, integrated child booster cushions and a premium 28-speaker Bowers & Wilkins sound system, complete with headrest speakers in all four main seats. Apple Music with Dolby Atmos will also be pre-installed, while Google’s Gemini AI assistant will allow drivers to interact with the car using natural conversation.

Of course, buying a new luxury SUV won’t automatically translate into savings, and the purchase price needs to be weighed against charging costs, insurance, depreciation and individual driving needs. But for anyone already considering their next vehicle, the EX60 offers another reason to look beyond the petrol pump.

The Volvo EX60 is expected to arrive in Australia in late 2026, with prices starting at $86,990.
