Wales boss Craig Bellamy urges fans to 'just celebrate' Cristiano Ronaldo after Portugal exit

TheTools · 17.11.2026 21:43 · source · permalink

Craig Bellamy has said football will miss Cristiano Ronaldo on the international stage but believes Portugal fans should celebrate his greatness and not focus on his controversial departure from the national team.

Wales take on a Ronaldo-less Portugal in their final Nations League A4 group game on Tuesday, with the 41-year-old having ended his international career earlier this month after 234 games and 146 goals -- both of which are records in the men's game.

Portugal began the official post-Ronaldo era with a 4-1 home defeat by Denmark on Saturday, fuelling further debate about Ronaldo's retirement from the national team earlier this month.

Ronaldo's mother Dolores Aveiro has made headlines in Portugal by expressing satisfaction that Jorge Jesus' side had lost to Denmark, and said her son had been deceived by national team selection.

The former Manchester United and Real Madrid great, now playing for Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia, announced his retirement from international football on Nov 5 after he was suspended for 30 days and fined €255 (£210) by the Portuguese Football Federation after walking out on the national team.

Ronaldo left the camp shortly before a Nations League game against Denmark following a row with head coach Jesus, making his final appearance for Portugal in the 1-0 win over Wales in Lisbon on Sept. 24.

Wales boss Bellamy said: "You were all having a go at him for playing, and then as soon as he retires, suddenly it's all hell breaking loose! He is one of the best players that has ever played the game. We're all going to miss him.

"Just be grateful you got to see an incredible talent that you could call yours -- and this is me speaking to Portuguese people.

"He's left a mark on football that will live forever. I feel it's time to stop the talking now. Just celebrate him."

Tuesday's clash at Cardiff City Stadium pitches top against bottom in Group A4, with Portugal having won their first four games before being upset by Denmark.

Wales will finish bottom of the group after losing 3-1 in Norway on Saturday, and face a March play-off against a League B runner-up to determine if they will remain in the top tier for the next edition of the Nations League.

Bellamy said: "Does it [Ronaldo's departure] change how we would adapt our game? It doesn't, because it's just the level of quality you're up against.

"They still have about 15 Champions League winners in their squad. Portugal are going to be fine. They were fine before him, they had a brilliant moment with him [winning Euro 2016].

"You're able to leave £80 to £100m players, who have been bought for that transfer market value, out of your squad. You can pick 30 or 40 players and be ridiculous."

Leeds United midfielder Ethan Ampadu misses out for Wales through suspension after picking up a second yellow card in the competition on Saturday.

Nottingham Forest full-back Neco Williams is absent after being injured in Oslo, and Manchester United goalkeeper Karl Darlow is doubtful with a knock.

"I can sit here feeling quite positive [about the group]. I feel it's just those tight moments, certain moments in games," Bellamy said.

"It makes me even more determined to try to stay in this group. If we go into a play-off, that's going to improve us."
