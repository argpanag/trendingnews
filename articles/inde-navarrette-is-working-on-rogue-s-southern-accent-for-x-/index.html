Inde Navarrette Is Working on Rogue’s Southern Accent For 'X-Men'

Chris Tremblay READ TIME: 3 MIN.

Actor Inde Navarrette says she is finding her way into Rogue’s Southern voice as she prepares to play the Marvel character in an upcoming X-Men film. The actor discussed the role with E! News at the Governors Awards in Hollywood on November 15; the interview was published on November 17.

“I’m really excited to really do it justice,” Navarrette told E! News, adding that she was still figuring out the accent. She then offered a playful attempt at Rogue’s drawl: “I’m figuring it out, darling, sugah.” Navarrette said she wants to “bake” the character’s Southern accent into her performance, and hopes to visit Mississippi as part of her connection to the role.

“I wanna go to Mississippi. Rogue means a lot to me,” she said. She added: “I want to give her as much as I have.” E! News reports that Rogue’s backstory places her upbringing in Mississippi, where she was raised on a commune before her mutant powers emerged and she joined the X-Men.

PinkNews also reported on the interview, noting Navarrette’s comments about the accent and her wish to travel to Mississippi. The outlet characterized Rogue as a much-loved character and repeated Navarrette’s remarks that she wants to do the part justice. E! News and PinkNews’ accounts both trace the comments to the same November 15 interview, rather than to separate statements by the actor.

A role announced at D23

Marvel Studios announced Navarrette as Rogue at Disney’s D23 fan event in August, Reuters reported. Reuters reported that the film is scheduled for release in May 2028. The announcement placed Navarrette in an ensemble that includes Sadie Sink as Jean Grey, Kit Connor as Cyclops, Christopher Abbott as Professor X, Maya Boyd as Storm and Adam Driver as Nathaniel Milbury, also known as Mister Sinister.

The upcoming film gives Navarrette a prominent role in a franchise with an established screen history: Anna Paquin previously played Rogue in the Fox X-Men films, Variety reported. Variety also identified Jake Schreier as the new film’s director. Marvel’s casting announcement and Navarrette’s recent interview establish her as the actor taking on the character in the new film, but neither source provides details about how the screenplay will portray Rogue.

Preparation beyond the accent

A separate interview published by Variety on November 16 touched on Navarrette’s preparation for the part. Asked about exercise and reading comics, Navarrette said she loves reading and stretching, but is “definitely not a working-out kind of girl.” She said she wants to build her endurance so she can keep up with the physical demands of the role.

The accent and endurance remarks describe different parts of her preparation: one focused on how Rogue sounds, the other on physical stamina. The reports do not indicate that Navarrette has set a date for a Mississippi visit, finalized her accent, or begun a specific training program.

The interview coverage offers an early account of how Navarrette is approaching a role announced only months earlier: she has spoken publicly about the character’s significance to her, the accent she is working through and the stamina she hopes to develop. Details about the film’s story, her performance, and the production timetable have not been established in these interviews. For now, Navarrette’s stated goal is straightforward: to do justice to a character she says means a great deal to her.
