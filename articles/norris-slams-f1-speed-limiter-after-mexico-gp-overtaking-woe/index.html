Norris Slams F1 Speed Limiter After Mexico GP Overtaking Woes

TheTools · 05.11.2026 07:51 · source · permalink

Main Story: Lando Norris says Formula 1’s 2026 battery-deployment speed limit is undermining overtaking by making cars with similar pace hit the same top-speed ceiling, even when one has a slipstream. Lewis Hamilton has also criticized the rule, which Norris says—not power-unit hardware—is the problem.

  • Maximum electrical deployment is capped at 350 kW.
  • In standard mode, deployment tapers above about 290 km/h and stops at 345 km/h; Overtake mode raises the limit to 355 km/h.
  • Mexico City’s front straight is 1.2 km long.
  • The speed-linked deployment limits are removed from the 2027 regulations.

Norris said the rules make passing harder, but acknowledged McLaren’s poor Saturday—not just the regulations—contributed to his result. He also argued that a major pace advantage can still make a pass possible.

  • The 2026 rules combine active aerodynamics with tightly controlled energy deployment, creating a speed ceiling on long straights.
  • Drivers say the limiter can erase the advantage of a draft when cars are closely matched.
  • Hamilton has separately criticized the engine profile that causes the speed clipping.

The limits are set to disappear in 2027, alongside planned changes intended to ease energy shortages, including increased fuel flow, turbo boost and energy harvesting. Until then, drivers will race under the current restrictions.

The debate is about more than outright speed: when software limits neutralize a slipstream, they can take away one of racing’s simplest and most reliable passing tools.
