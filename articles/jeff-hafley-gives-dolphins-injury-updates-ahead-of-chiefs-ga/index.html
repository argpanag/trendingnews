Jeff Hafley Gives Dolphins Injury Updates Ahead of Chiefs Game

Miami Dolphins head coach Jeff Hafley provided several important injury updates Friday while also discussing the challenge presented by the Kansas City Chiefs, Miami’s ongoing development, and his approach to building the Dolphins’ defense.

The biggest news involved wide receiver Caleb Douglas, who Hafley confirmed will not play Sunday against Kansas City.

Douglas’ absence adds another challenge for a Dolphins receiving corps already dealing with injuries.

There was better news regarding Ryan Miller. Hafley said Miller would practice Friday after dealing with foot and back issues that had been lingering rather than stemming from one particular play. Hafley said the Dolphins rested Miller to let the soreness calm down and believes he should be OK for Sunday.

Chris Bell also appears on track to play. Bell had discussed dealing with fluid and soreness in his knee, but Hafley said the Dolphins feel good about his chances of playing after consulting with the team’s medical staff.

The wide receiver injuries will force Miami to make some offensive adjustments.

Hafley acknowledged that the game plan can’t remain exactly the same with different players available. While he expects Miami’s overall approach to remain similar, there will be tweaks to roles and opportunities for other players to step up.

Jaylen Wright Doubtful, Ollie Gordon Ready

Miami could also be without running back Jaylen Wright.

Hafley described Wright as doubtful for Sunday’s game. When asked about Ollie Gordon’s readiness to take on a larger role if Wright can’t play, Hafley’s response was simple: He’s prepared, and Hafley feels confident in him.

That could create another opportunity for Gordon as Miami looks to build on the improvement it showed in the running game last week.

Hafley also confirmed Beal will be out, while JuJu Brents was expected to practice Friday.

Hafley Emphasizes Development

One of Hafley’s biggest themes throughout the press conference was development.

Miami has a young roster with players learning to work together, and Hafley said the Dolphins need to find additional opportunities to get those players repetitions.

The Dolphins held a developmental 7-on-7 period after practice for players who weren’t receiving enough regular practice reps. Hafley also revealed that players who are inactive Sunday will essentially have a practice that morning, including individual work, special teams, and group periods.

For Hafley, those extra repetitions are essential.

“We need to develop because we’re going to need these new guys entering different positions as we go,” Hafley said.

That development also applies to Miami’s communication problems.

Hafley acknowledged the frustration caused by communication mistakes but pointed out that they aren’t unusual for a young group playing together for the first time. He said the players have worked hard throughout the week to improve in that area.

Chop Robinson Set to Return

Miami should also benefit from the return of Chop Robinson after he missed last week’s game.

Hafley said Robinson practiced hard this week and expressed excitement about getting him back on the field.

“I’m excited for him to get a chance to go back out and play,” Hafley said.

Robinson’s return could be especially important for a Dolphins pass rush that needs to generate more pressure.

Hafley said his defensive philosophy isn’t built around pressure necessarily coming from the edge. Instead, the goal is to find the best pass rushers and determine how to create pressure with the personnel available.

He has coached defenses where interior linemen generated sacks, others where defensive ends were the primary pass rushers, and still others where blitzing and stunts were required.

His philosophy is essentially to do whatever is necessary to generate pressure.

Hafley Has High Praise for Steve Spagnuolo

Sunday’s game will also put Hafley opposite one of the defensive coaches he respects most: Chiefs defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo.

Hafley spoke at length about his relationship with Spagnuolo and praised his ability to continually evolve defensively throughout his career.

Hafley pointed to Spagnuolo’s willingness to study trends, adjust his pressure packages, and avoid allowing his defense to become stagnant.

Hafley wants to incorporate that philosophy in Miami.

“You have to evolve,” Hafley said.

Hafley believes a defense needs core fundamentals and principles, but coaches must also adapt to their personnel and continue adding concepts. Otherwise, NFL offenses will eventually figure them out.

Miami Preparing for Everything Kansas City Offers

Hafley also provided some insight into how Miami constructs a defensive game plan.

The process begins with studying the opposing coordinator’s tendencies, including favorite concepts on early downs, third downs and in the red zone. From there, Miami studies personnel and determines which players must be stopped, which matchups can be attacked and which Dolphins defenders may need protection.

The challenge is putting all of that together while ensuring the players aren’t overloaded with a plan they can’t execute at full speed.

One Kansas City player who has Hafley’s attention is center Creed Humphrey.

Hafley praised Humphrey’s intelligence and believes he plays a major role in keeping the Chiefs’ offensive line organized in both the running and passing games.

Hafley said Humphrey probably doesn’t get as much attention as he deserves because of the other stars on Kansas City’s roster, but called him one of the biggest reasons the Chiefs’ offense works.

Dolphins Looking to Take Another Step

Miami enters Sunday’s matchup needing improvement in several areas, but Hafley’s comments made one theme particularly clear: The Dolphins aren’t treating development as something that happens only during games.

Extra practice repetitions, developmental periods and additional work for inactive players are all part of Hafley’s attempt to accelerate the growth of a young roster.

Meanwhile, the potential return of Robinson and the likely availability of Bell and Miller would provide some much-needed help against Kansas City.

Douglas is out and Wright is doubtful, meaning other players will have opportunities to step into larger roles.

For a Dolphins team still searching for its first win of the season, those players may need to grow up quickly against the Chiefs.
