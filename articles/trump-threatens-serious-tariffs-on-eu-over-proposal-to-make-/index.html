Edited by Alex Smith

  1. What is an EU 'associate member'?published at 08:21 BST
    08:21 BST

    As we reported in our previous post, Ursula von der Leyen told the European Parliament on Wednesday that she wanted to work with Mark Carney on "opening the door for Canada to be the first associate member of the EU".

    But the status of "associate member" of the EU doesn't currently exist - and, if agreed, the process could take years.

    It's not clear exactly what form this membership would take, but von der Leyen laid out a number of different sectors where they could form closer ties.

    The idea has been floated in the past - for example, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz suggested earlier this year that Ukraine could be granted the same status. But the idea was met with pushback from some member states.

    The bloc is currently comprised of 27 countries - while nine others are official candidates pursuing full EU membership, but it's a years-long process from start to finish.

    A free-trade agreement was struck between the EU and Canada nearly a decade ago, but 10 members are yet to ratify the deal. Some would also prefer to build on existing defence and trade agreements rather than create a new form of membership for Canada.

  2. 'This is not a partnership against anyone else', says von der Leyenpublished at 07:58 BST
    07:58 BST

    In her wide-ranging State of the Union address yesterday, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said “this is not a partnership against anyone else, but for our common strength".

    Her comments did not mention the US by name - the country is currently embroiled in a trade dispute with Canada.

    With Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney watching on, von der Leyen said she wanted to open the door for his country to become the EU's "first associate member".

    "We must urgently re-imagine our partnerships," she said, citing tech, defence and energy critical minerals as areas where they could "join forces".

    Separately, von der Leyen also expressed a desire to build a European Security Council - including the EU and its partners, such as the UK, Ukraine and Canada.

  3. How US and Canada trade talks derailedpublished at 07:47 BST
    07:47 BST

    Canada and the US have been locked in an escalating trade war, following the breakdown of trade negotiations in late August.

    Negotiators had been engaged in intense talks since July - but broke down at the last minute. Both sides blamed each other for torpedoing the tentative deal.

    Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said "last-minute changes in the US proposed terms were unfair, uneconomic, and called into question the reliability of any deal".

    US trade representative Jamieson Greer said Canada had "declined to finalise" the agreed terms, despite the "US offer to Canada to receive the best treatment of any major exporter to our market".

    The US then imposed tariffs on key Canadian sectors including steel, aluminium and lumber, and introduced an additional 50% levy on about C$28bn ($20bn; £15bn) of Canadian goods.

    In response, Ottawa slapped levies of up to 50% on C$28bn ($20bn; £15bn) of American products, from steel to furniture to cotton T-shirts.

    Carney said the US "asked too much and they offered too little", adding that Canada was retaliating with tariffs "reluctantly" to protect its interests.

    Meanwhile, Trump said Canada wants "the benefits of being a state, without being one" - echoing his repeated references to turning Canada into the 51st US state.

  4. Carney's new love-in with the EU has everything to do with Trumppublished at 07:38 BST
    07:38 BST

    Katya Adler
    Europe editor

    "Wouldn't it be lovely if Canada was the 28th state of the European Union rather than the 51st state of the United States?" asked Finland's President Alexander Stubb this summer.

    The question was rather tongue-in-cheek.

    US President Donald Trump, who is currently locked in an escalating tariff tussle with Ottawa, has, on a number of occasions, declared his ambition to make Canada the 51st American state - causing deep offence amongst many Canadians.

    But Stubb put his finger squarely on the pulse of deepening EU-Canadian relations.

    And in a speech on Wednesday, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said she wanted to work "on opening the door" for Canada to be the EU's "associate member" - which would involve closer ties on various sectors including manufacturing and energy.

    The catalyst for this very public, ardent new love-in is Donald Trump.

    Just the other day, the US president posted a map of North America on his Truth Social site, including Canada and Greenland - a territory that's part of EU country Denmark - all overlaid with the US flag.

    Trade negotiations between the US and Canada also broke down late last month, and European countries, which have been hit by their own Trump tariffs, have privately watched the spat play out with a mixture of interest and, for many, admiration.

  5. Prospect of Canada becoming an EU associate member 'laughable', Trump sayspublished at 07:37 BST
    07:37 BST

    Speaking to reporters last night, US President Donald Trump called the prospect of Canada becoming an associate member of the EU "laughable".

    He said: "Look, the whole thing with Canada, Canada's been a terrible trade partner."

    Describing the move, if it happens, as a "hostile act", Trump said he would put "very serious tariffs or stop trading with Europe on many things".

    "If it's a good intention, that's fine. If it's a bad intention, we'll put very heavy tariffs on Europe," he said.

    US President Donald Trump has threatened to take action against the European Union (EU) if it moves forward with a proposal to make Canada its first "associate member".

    Canada's relationship with the US - long considered its closest partner - has soured in recent months, and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has been developing closer relations with the EU.

    Last week, he said he's seeking a "unique alliance" with Brussels. And, in her State of the Union address yesterday, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen’s said she wanted to work on “opening the door” for Canada, which would involve closer ties in various key sectors.

    Speaking to reporters last night, Trump said: "If they do that, if I think it's at all a hostile act, I will put very serious tariffs or stop trading with Europe on many things.

    "And so if they do that, if Europe does that with a bad intention, if it's a good intention, that's fine. If it's a bad intention, we'll put very heavy tariffs on Europe."

    Carney is due to address the European Parliament in Strasbourg, France later this morning - he's attending the parliament's plenary meeting as a guest of honour.
