Analysis: Marco Rubio uses a dramatic backdrop to sketch a post-MAGA foreign policy | CNN Politics

TheTools · 07.10.2026 21:51 · source · permalink

In the golden light of an Athens evening, with the Acropolis over his shoulder, Marco Rubio hardly looked like a man planning to ride off into a political sunset.

The secretary of state’s speech in Greece on Wednesday will surely reignite speculation around his future presidential intentions. It implicitly positioned him as both the heir to and the modernizer of the “America First” movement — just weeks before the 2028 race roars into life after the midterm elections.

Rubio depicted the West as a continuous inheritance dating from Ancient Greece to modern America, epitomizing “individuality and relentless ambition, daring exploration and invention” as well as duty and heroism. But he warned that the civilization, ashamed of its achievements and embarrassed by its past, risked “atrophy, servitude and decline.”

“Under President Trump, the United States has made its choice. We choose the unapologetic pursuit of national power,” Rubio said, giving himself some political cover for the high-profile appearance.

One interpretation is that this was yet another bid to retroactively impose doctrinal coherence on Trump’s erratic foreign policy of Iran war-style gut calls, power grabs and a transactional approach to resources.

And Rubio is just the latest administration official to bemoan Europe’s supposed decline; failure to tackle mass immigration; and prioritization of the “global community” over national interest. This canon was hallmarked by Vice President JD Vance’s stemwinder in Munich last year.

Yet he was seeking something deeper, at times, sketching the framework of a post-MAGA foreign policy in which US sovereignty and action remain paramount, while​ reinvigorating friendships shattered by Trump’s hostility.

“We must rebuild our institutions and alliances” to face new great power rivalries and respond to a “new universe” of threats and attacks, Rubio said.

His comments also reinforced a message to decayed European militaries that the US now expects alliances to offer tangible military multipliers to Washington. It was a hint that while nothing will be the same after Trump, a future Republican president might still value updated transatlantic ties.

Rubio seeks to shape a new American foreign policy — and maybe something more?

Rubio also seemed to be fusing three strands of traditional GOP foreign policy — the muscular internationalism of Ronald Reagan, the realism of Richard Nixon and Trump’s coercive nationalism. He might also have been trying to make sense of his own political evolution from aspirational conservatism to his current, darker worldview. (Rubio’s critics portray his conversion to MAGAism as the cynical act of a political chameleon desperate for power.)

The speech was also jarring and contradictory.

He was the latest Trump acolyte to travel to Europe to explain Europe’s history to Europeans and to adopt an idealized view of Western civilization that fits the needs of America’s Christian nationalist right.

When Rubio laments eroded national sovereignty, like Vance, he seems oblivious to enduring national and cultural differences distinguishing French, German, Italian, and other European citizens. He ignored gruesome history that led late 20th-century leaders to constrain raging nationalism within shared political and economic structures.

And while Trump’s team believes something is rotten in Europe, many Europeans perceive a corrupt and increasingly authoritarian American is most to blame for obliterating Western values. Noting that “passions” have rocked the alliance, Rubio used a deceptive euphemism for Trump’s bullying.

Rubio’s lionizing of the ancient Greeks as foundational to the US also overlooked the founders’ concern that their direct democracy encouraged factionalism and promoted demagogues. Their answer was the Electoral College.

Some Europeans will be offended by Rubio’s speech. But he’s seen by allies as the most conventional and sympathetic Trump foreign policy official, so his comments may be less provocative than rebukes by Vance or Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Inevitably, such an ambitious speech using the optics of a major campaign address will stir presidential speculation. Visually, Rubio’s setting recalled then-Sen. Barack Obama’s hilltop speech at the Citadel in Amman, Jordan, in 2008.

A Rubio run in 2028 seems problematic with Vance in pole position as the vice president. But whether he next takes aim at the White House in two years, in 2032, or later, Rubio showed the world Wednesday his ambition is unquenched.
