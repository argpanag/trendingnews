CR7 in 2008

Ronaldo's 2008 season is often viewed as one of the greatest individual campaigns in modern football because he combined elite individual numbers with the biggest team trophies.

For Manchester United, Cristiano Ronaldo scored 42 goals in all competitions, an extraordinary return for a winger in that era. He finished as the Premier League's top scorer with 31 league goals and helped United win both the Premier League and the UEFA Champions League. He then went on to win the 2008 Ballon d'Or.

Ousmane Dembélé 2025

Dembélé won the 2025 Ballon d'Or after a standout season with Paris Saint-Germain, helping the French club secure its first UEFA Champions League title. The French forward finished ahead of Lamine Yamal and Vitinha in the voting, claiming football's most prestigious individual award for the first time. The ceremony took place in Paris on 22 September 2025

Rodri 2024

The 2024 Ballon d'Or was won by Rodri, who became the first Manchester City player to claim football's most prestigious individual award. The Spanish midfielder was recognised for his outstanding 2023-24 season, helping City win a fourth consecutive Premier League title and playing a key role in Spain's triumph at UEFA Euro 2024, where he was named Player of the Tournament. He finished ahead of Vinícius Júnior and Jude Bellingham in the final voting.

Lionel Messi 2023

Messi won the Ballon d’Or for a record-extending eighth time in 2023, despite leaving elite European soccer for MLS and Inter Miami that summer. By this point, the award was based on the previous season rather than a calendar year, encompassing his second and final season with PSG and, crucially, the 2022 World Cup, which he won for the only time in his career with Argentina. That sealed the deal without any doubt, along with his 38 goals and 25 assists in 54 appearances across all competitions.

Lionel Messi 2021

Messi began a new chapter of his career in 2021, leaving Barcelona, which had previously seemed unthinkable, for Paris Saint-Germain in the summer. The Copa del Rey was the only team trophy he won, while he made a slow start to life in France, totaling only 11 goals in the 2021-22 season. Despite that, he still managed 43 goals and 18 assists in 61 appearances for club and country and won his first major trophy with Argentina, the Copa América.

Lionel Messi 2019

In 2018, Luka Modrić became the first player not called Lionel Messi or Cristiano Ronaldo to win the Ballon d’Or since Kaká in 2007. The interruption, however, was short-lived as Messi won his sixth award, edging him back ahead of Ronaldo. The Argentine “only” won LaLiga with Barcelona, but his 50 goals and 18 assists in 58 games across the year were enough to earn him the prize.

Lionel Messi 2015

Messi’s next Ballon d’Or came three years later in 2015, a year in which he scored 52 goals (and provided 26 assists) in 61 games. Ronaldo’s goalscoring record was superior, but the prize went to the Argentine thanks to Barcelona’s four-trophy haul, including the Champions League, LaLiga, the UEFA Super Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup.

Lionel Messi 2011

Messi’s 59 goals and 37 assists (yes, thirty-seven) fired Barcelona back to the top of the European tree, with their second Champions League victory over Manchester United in three finals the highlight of 2011. However, it was non-stop success for the Catalans, who won LaLiga for a third straight year, as well as the Spanish Super Cup, UEFA Super Cup and FIFA Club World Cup.

We’d almost certainly be talking about Andrés Iniesta and/or Xavi as multiple Ballon d’Or winners if it weren’t for Messi.

Lionel Messi 2010

Messi upped the scoring ante significantly in 2010, which saw him score 60 goals and provide 17 assists in 64 games in all. That year, Barcelona ‘only’ won two trophies, defending their LaLiga and Spanish Super Cup titles. But there was plenty more to come, and guess who was at the heart of it all.

Lionel Messi 2009

Messi, who had come second to Ronaldo in 2008, won his first Ballon d’Or the following year, in which he managed 41 goals and 15 assists in 64 appearances for club and country. A modest return for what was to come, if we’re being honest, although that didn’t take away from his undoubted brilliance.

That year, Barcelona won an unprecedented sextuple under Pep Guardiola: LaLiga, the Champions League, the Copa del Rey, the Spanish Super Cup, the UEFA Super Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup. The only question was which Barça player would win it.

Cristiano Ronaldo 2017

Ronaldo’s fifth and most recent victory was his second back-to-back award and came in the midst of another hugely successful period in the Spanish capital. The Portugal captain, who scored 53 goals and provided 12 assists in 60 games across the year, fired Los Blancos to their first LaLiga title in five years in 2016-17, while also winning the Champions League, the UEFA Super Cup, the Spanish Super Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup.

On top of that, Ronaldo and Madrid would retain their Champions League title once more the following season, his last in Spain.

Cristiano Ronaldo 2016

After a year off, Ronaldo won his fourth Ballon d’Or in 2016, the year in which he lifted his first major trophy with Portugal at the European Championship. With Real Madrid, there were also repeat victories in the Champions League and FIFA Club World Cup, with his 55 goals and 16 assists in 57 games playing a huge role in successes for club and country.

Cristiano Ronaldo 2014

Ronaldo also won the Ballon d’Or in 2014, which proved to be the year Real Madrid resumed their European dominance after more than a decade of frustration. The Portugal star recorded 61 goals and 20 assists in 60 appearances over the calendar year, in which Madrid won their first Champions League since 2002, the Copa del Rey, the UEFA Super Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup.

Cristiano Ronaldo 2013

Having moved to Real Madrid in the summer of 2009, Ronaldo banged in a whopping 69 goals (with 15 assists) in just 59 games in 2013. Somehow, Los Blancos still ended up trophy-less in 2012-13, although bigger things were about to come the following year…

Cristiano Ronaldo 2008

As we mentioned a bit earlier, it won’t just be Player X who wins. It will be Player X in Year Y. Fans get the chance to vote for their favorite Ballon d’Or winner in a particular year, rather than overall. With that in mind, let’s take a look at the two big favorites’ respective Ballon d’Or victories, starting with Cristiano Ronaldo in 2008, the first year of the duopoly.

Up until 2022, the award was based on what a player had achieved in a calendar year, rather than in a season. That awkwardly means there were two half-seasons to consider.

According to the Messi vs. Ronaldo app, the Portuguese scored 35 goals (and added 12 assists) in 58 appearances for club and country in 2008, which helped Manchester United win the Premier League in both 2007-08 and 2008-09, as well as the Champions League and FIFA Club World Cup in 2008.

The Red Devils also reached the Champions League final in 2009, which they lost to Barcelona in Rome. That was Ronaldo’s last game for United until he returned in 2021.

BOAT vs Super Ballon d’Or

Now, you may have noticed a mention of the Super Ballon d'Or among all that, which was won by Alfredo Di Stéfano in 1989. That one-off award was handed out by France Football to the former Real Madrid star as the best player of the previous three decades.

The organizers have stressed, in pretty strong terms, that the BOAT is not designed to emulate or replace the Super Ballon d’Or. Here’s what France Football itself had to say about the whole thing:

"The B.O.A.T. is exclusively a public vote designed to celebrate the passion of our supporters. It is neither a physical trophy nor an official award presented at our ceremony. For this special occasion, fans around the world can vote for their all-time favorite Ballon d'Or winner.

“Unlike the B.O.A.T., the Super Ballon d'Or was an official, physical, and historic trophy awarded by France Football only once in history. It took place in December 1989 to elect the best multiple Ballon d’Or winner of the previous three decades.

“That singular trophy, determined by an expert jury, former winners, and readers, was presented to Real Madrid legend Alfredo Di Stéfano. On that occasion, the ‘Saeta Rubia’ topped the voting ahead of icons of the caliber of Johan Cruyff and Michel Platini, among others.

“Since then, the Super Ballon d'Or has never been awarded again, and there is currently no official date set for a second edition."

That’s us told. Never, ever think of confusing the BOAT and the Super Ballon d’Or again.

The BOAT contenders: Karl-Heinz Rummenigge

The Bayern Munich legend’s Ballon d’Or victories came in 1980 and 1981, with the first coming in the year he captained West Germany to European Championship glory. The striker also won the European Cup with Bayern twice in the 1970s and led his country to two World Cup finals, both of which ended in defeat.

The BOAT contenders: Kevin Keegan

Keegan played for Liverpool longer than any other team in his career, winning three English titles, a European Cup and two UEFA Cups during his time at Anfield. But it wasn’t until he moved to German club Hamburg in 1977 that he achieved individual success, with his Ballon d’Or victories coming in 1978 and 1979.

“King Kev” sadly died from stomach cancer on July 20 this year, the day after the 2026 World Cup final.

The BOAT contenders: Alfredo Di Stéfano

Di Stéfano was the main man in Real Madrid’s five successive European Cup victories in the 1950s, which laid the foundation for the club’s dominance of the modern-day Champions League. Born and raised in Argentina, Di Stéfano won the Ballon d’Or twice (1957 and 1959), having become a naturalized Spanish citizen in 1956.

The former striker was also the recipient of the Super Ballon d’Or, a one-off award handed out by France Football in 1989 to recognize the best player of the previous three decades.

The BOAT contenders: Ronaldo Nazário

Regularly cited by current players as the striker they looked to emulate when they were growing up, Ronaldo won the Ballon d’Or in 1997 and 2002, either side of serious injury problems that prevented him from achieving even more success. The Brazilian excelled for both Barcelona and Real Madrid and won the World Cup twice, scoring both goals in the 2002 final.

The BOAT contenders: Franz Beckenbauer

Nicknamed “der Kaiser” (“the Emperor”), Beckenbauer won the Ballon d’Or twice, in 1972, the year he won the European Championship with West Germany, and in 1976. He is often credited with inventing the sweeper role, which is rarely used in soccer nowadays but played a prominent role in the sport in the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s.

Beckenbauer, who also had two spells with New York Cosmos, is one of three men to have won the World Cup as both a player (1974) and a coach (1990). He died of natural causes in January 2024.

The BOAT contenders: Marco van Basten

Van Basten won the Ballon d’Or in 1988, 1989 and 1992, all after moving from Ajax to AC Milan. His finest year was undoubtedly 1988, when he inspired the Netherlands to European Championship glory, scoring a spectacular goal against the Soviet Union in the final. The Dutchman was forced to retire at just 31 due to ankle problems.

The BOAT contenders: Johan Cruyff

Cruyff claimed the Ballon d'Or award in 1971, 1973 and 1974, with the first two victories coming while he was playing for Ajax and the third with Barcelona, whom he later coached. In the final of those years, he was the star of the Netherlands’ legendary Total Football team at the World Cup in West Germany.

The BOAT contenders: Michel Platini

Platini, a huge star for Saint-Étienne, Juventus and France in the 1980s, was the first player to win three Ballon d'Or awards in a row (1983, 1984 and 1985). His most memorable year was 1984, when he led France to European Championship glory, scoring nine goals in the tournament, a record that remains unmatched.

The BOAT contenders: Cristiano Ronaldo

Ronaldo, meanwhile, has won the Ballon d’Or five times (2008, 2013, 2014, 2016 and 2017). His strongest years were 2016, when he won the Champions League with Real Madrid and the European Championship with Portugal, and 2017, when he won LaLiga and the Champions League with Madrid, finishing as the leading scorer in both competitions. And he likely has a LOT of voting fans.

No player in history has played more games or scored more goals for his country, while Ronaldo also holds the record for goals scored in the UEFA Champions League. 

The BOAT contenders: Lionel Messi

But in the interest of neutrality, let’s run through the credentials of all 10 players involved. We’ll start with the two favorites, simply because they have won the most Ballon d’Or awards.

Messi is the favorite, having won the Ballon d’Or a record eight times (2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2015, 2019, 2021 and 2023). His standout years include 2009 and 2015, when he won the treble with Barcelona, and 2022, when he led Argentina to World Cup glory. Those seasons are among the strongest candidates for the greatest in history.

On top of all that, Messi has won the European Golden Boot award a record six times and is the most decorated player in the history of soccer, winning 47 team trophies.

Will the BOAT really be who France Football says it is?

In theory, France Football is expected to announce one winner, whether it be Lionel Messi for his 2023 victory, Cristiano Ronaldo for his 2017 win or Freddy Adu in 2004 (that dream shone briefly, but brightly).

However, we also expect to be given the number of votes each annual winner receives. So even if Messi is officially voted the BOAT for his 2023 Ballon d’Or victory, Ronaldo could still earn more votes overall if his votes are spread out across his five awards. Or vice versa, of course.

So there could be plenty of contention among fans (we’re looking at followers of two players in particular, by the way…) for who the actual BOAT is.

How does voting for the BOAT work?

Unlike the Ballon d'Or itself, which is voted for by journalists from the top 100 countries in the FIFA World Ranking, the general public decides who the BOAT is.

Voting opened on October 1 and continued until yesterday, October 27. Sadly, it’s too late to vote for your favorite if you were still hoping to do so.

Voting, however, was not free. Everyone who took part in the vote, which cost around $1.20 (€1), was entered into a draw to win two tickets to next year’s 2027 Ballon d’Or ceremony and receive a photograph of the first-ever Ballon d’Or.

Now, an important point.

Participants didn’t simply vote for their favorite of the 10 players mentioned above (Messi, Cristiano Ronaldo, Platini, Cruyff, van Basten, Beckenbauer, Ronaldo Nazário, Di Stéfano, Keegan and Rummenigge, in case you’d forgotten). Instead, they were asked to vote for the best, or their favorite, Ballon d’Or win in a particular year.

That means that instead of voting for Messi, they voted for Messi’s 2012 victory. They didn’t simply vote for Cristiano Ronaldo; they picked the Portuguese star’s 2008 Ballon d’Or win instead. This opens up some interesting additional possibilities…

What is the BOAT?

France Football launched the BOAT on October 1, stating its desire to give “football fans, enthusiasts and connoisseurs” the power to choose the greatest Ballon d'Or winner of all time. It is, though, not an ‘official’ award. 

Why now? Well, this marks the 70th anniversary of the very first Ballon d’Or award, won by legendary English winger Sir Stanley Matthews in 1956.

Some have also speculated that it may have something to do with Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo approaching the ends of their careers and being highly, highly unlikely to win any more Ballon d’Or awards (although Messi gave it a right good go this year).
