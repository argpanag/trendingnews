Lawson hails career-best start after Sepang charge to seventh

TheTools · 04.10.2026 12:29 · source · permalink

The Racing Bulls driver started from 11th but immediately launched himself into the leading group, climbing to fourth when the race finally got underway following a delay of nearly two hours.

By the first corner he was already alongside Max Verstappen in a fight for third, although the eventual winner retained the place.

Lawson remained in the fight for points throughout a race shaped by changing conditions, Safety Cars and constant movement through the order, eventually taking seventh after late drama reshuffled the points positions once more.

“Yeah happy with seventh,” he said after the race.

“It’s funny because I was thinking towards the end, like we’ve had such a good race, all of this just for P9. Because the gap to the front guys, we can’t keep up with them.

“So I was thinking we deserve more. And then we got lucky at the end with a couple of those guys not finishing. So yeah, very happy.

“Our car’s been very good. It was strong in the race. So yeah, happy.”

The New Zealander gradually slipped back as the quicker cars came through, with Isack Hadjar taking fourth from him early before Lewis Hamilton, Oscar Piastri, Charles Leclerc and Lando Norris also moved ahead prior to Lawson’s final stop.

That visit to the pits briefly dropped him outside the points, but Lawson worked his way back into contention before Piastri and Norris again found their way ahead as the strategies played out.

A late Safety Car brought another opportunity, with George Russell retiring from third while Norris was forced off the circuit at the opening corner as the field bunched up.

At the restart, Lawson managed to edge ahead of Piastri through Turns 2 and 3, only for the McLaren driver to reclaim sixth at Turn 4, with the Kiwi eventually crossing the line behind Piastri in seventh.

The points were part of a strong overall haul for Racing Bulls, with Arvid Lindblad recovering from last on the grid to claim 10th and the final point.

Lawson felt the team had made the most of its opportunities on a weekend where rivals Alpine again showed strong pace, with the double points finish further strengthening Racing Bulls’ advantage in the constructors’ championship.

“It’s good. It’s two weekends where Alpine’s been a bit quicker than us and we’ve managed to outscore them,” he said when asked about the growing gap to their rivals.

“I think that’s really been on good decision making today on the strat for the start.

“I had one of the best starts I’ve ever had in my career. And that put us in a great position for the rest of the race.”

It marked the team’s seventh double points finish of 2026, while Alpine failed to score, leaving Racing Bulls 22 points clear in fifth in the constructors’ standings.

Lindblad admitted the race had been one of the busiest of his F1 career, with the rookie delighted by the outcome despite feeling there was still plenty to improve in his own performance.

“It’s been a pretty nuts day. It’s been an interesting one,” the Brit said of his race.

“To be in the points from starting last is more than we could ever imagine. I think from that side it’s really good.

“Amazing day for the team. Amazing day for the whole of the Red Bull family.”

The result completed a strong day across the wider Red Bull operation, with Verstappen taking victory and RBPT Ford celebrating its maiden Formula 1 win.

Lindblad, however, was not completely satisfied with his own race after struggling for pace through part of the middle stint.

“On my side, I’m not that happy with my own performance,” he added.

“Obviously there was some good stuff, but there was also a good part in the middle where I was just too slow.

“So I need to understand why and why I’ve been a little bit weaker in long runs than short runs this year.”

F1 returns next weekend for the Singapore Grand Prix.
