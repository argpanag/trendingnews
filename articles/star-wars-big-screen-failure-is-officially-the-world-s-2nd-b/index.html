Star Wars’ Big Screen Failure Is Officially the World's 2nd Biggest Movie

TheTools · 19.09.2026 16:45 · source · permalink

In his time at Collider, he has also helped drive diversity by writing stories about the multiple Indian film industries, with a goal of introducing audiences to a whole new world of cinema. 

If you go with the theory that general audiences treated the latest Star Wars movie not as the franchise's first theatrical installment in seven years, but as an extension of a streaming series meant for home viewing, it wasn't the legendary box-office failure it was labeled as. For instance, the movie grossed three times as much at the global box office compared to the DC Universe's second installment, Supergirl, and Amazon MGM's would-be franchise-starter Masters of the Universe. The Star Wars film recently debuted on Disney+, ostensibly its home turf, and delivered a solid performance in its first five days. In fact, the viewership figures posted by the film were good enough for it to claim the number-two spot on the Nielsen chart.

Nielsen made a major change in its latest viewership report. The analyst firm would previously update viewership for streaming titles with a four-week delay, but this has now been reduced to a two-week delay thanks to advancements in audience measurement. The latest report, which tracks data for the week of August 31 to September 6, was topped for the fourth time in a row by Prime Video's Reacher, which accumulated 1.2 billion minutes watched. The new Star Wars movie, on the other hand, took the 10th spot on the overall list, which included just two movies alongside eight shows.

Here's Where the New Star Wars Movie Placed on the Nielsen Chart

We're talking, of course, about The Mandalorian and Grogu, director Jon Favreau's spin-off to the platform-defining Disney+ series that launched a small-screen franchise. The movie adaptation ended its theatrical run as the lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars installment, generating around $345 million worldwide against a reported budget of $165 million. It received mixed reviews, including from Collider's Aidan Kelley, and is now sitting at a 61% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes. The aggregator website's consensus reads, "Bountiful in action but threadbare in narrative thrust with its episodic structure, this Star Wars is more of a skirmish that coasts on the charm of its central dynamic duo." However, the movie's 86% audience score on the aggregator seems to be working in its favor. According to Nielsen, The Mandalorian and Grogu scored 727 million minutes watched in its debut week on Disney+, finishing second on the movies list behind the Netflix thriller The Whisper Man, which led the pack with 863 million minutes watched. The Star Wars franchise will hope to bounce back next year with Star Wars: Starfighter, a new standalone adventure starring Ryan Gosling and directed by Shawn Levy.
