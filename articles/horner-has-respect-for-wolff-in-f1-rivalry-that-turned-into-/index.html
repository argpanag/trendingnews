Horner Has Respect for Wolff in F1 Rivalry That Turned Into a Hero-Villain Situation

TheTools · 14.10.2026 18:55 · source · permalink

Christian Horner has praise and respect for Toto Wolff, as he stresses on authentic F1 rivalry and not made up for TV or Netflix.

The rivalry between former Red Bull team boss Horner and Mercedes’ Wolff was at an all-time high during the 2021 F1 season when young Max Verstappen was going all guns blazing against the veteran Lewis Hamilton, for the drivers’ title.

While Mercedes managed to secure the constructors’ title, Verstappen got the better of Hamilton to not only secure a career-first F1 title but also deny the Brit from winning the record-breaking eighth world championship to go past Michael Schumacher.

It was a controversial championship win, nevertheless, which in some ways was a fitting end to a year full of drama, which was all authentic and not even created for Netflix’s Drive to Survive. The Horner-Wolff saga is now a well-known topic in the world of F1.

Usually, it is the drivers who take the center stage, but Horner and Wolff were at it all through. Despite the drama and some not so kind words, the Brit has praise and respect for the Austrian and what he has done and achieved in his career.

Horner Has Respect for What Wolff Has Achieved

But he certainly enjoyed the rivalry, especially as it was an energy drinks company up against a big car manufacturer. “Well, Toto’s a formidable competitor and Mercedes are a massive team and, of course, the success that they’ve enjoyed and had,” said Horner in a video interview to Autosport.

“That’s what made 2021, even more important to us. But he’s emotional, he’s competitive, he’s driven and we enjoyed a rivalry. I mean I have huge respect for what he is, for what he has done and achieved. But I think in all sports you need to have rivalry. You can’t all just be best friends all of the time.

“It’s not true to the reality. And certainly, that was a way of galvanizing the team that Mercedes are the opposition. We have to try and, as a subsidiary of an energy drinks company, take them on and take the fight to them,” summed up Horner, who reiterated that it was authentic from either side.

Horner Says TV/Netflix Could Edit Someone as Hero Villain

But being the “villain”, he felt it was all at the hands of TV and Netflix, who had hours of footage with them and could edit and show whatever they wanted to show. He admits that it did become a bit of a theatre as the seasons progressed – a bit more dramatic.

“I think that when the cameras first turned up, it was very authentic, and then you could feel as the seasons went on that there was considerably more male grooming going on, and it became a little more theatre, shall we say,” said Horner. “But I felt authentic to myself. And of course, these guys are making a TV show.

“So, they take hours and hours and hours of footage. And of course, it’s all about how you edit something. You can either make somebody look like the villain or the hero or vice versa,” summed up Horner. Despite some of the downsides, the Brit has praise for the work done by Netflix and generate a new crop of F1 fans and lure them into the sport.

Horner Credits Netflix for Bringing New Fans to F1

The success of Drive to Survive has helped in F1 capturing the American market, with three races in Austin, Miami and Las Vegas. “That show has been incredibly successful because it suddenly lifted the lid on some of the rivalries, the personalities, the driver’s personalities, some of the characters,” continued Horner, with a sarcastic dig at former Haas boss Guenther Steiner.

“I mean, Guenther Steiner has now made a career off the back of it. I don’t think he ever achieved a podium in Formula One. But good for him. And he is a character, quite a scary character when he worked for us. But yeah, it’s brought a whole new fan base into the sport which has been which has been fantastic,” summed up Horner.
