EUR/USD might be bottoming out amid peak Fed hawkishness and dovish repricing risks

TheTools · 26.11.2026 13:49 · source · permalink
    Tags
  • USD
  • EUR
  • EUR/USD Technical Analysis

Summary

  • EUR/USD could find further upside support if weaker US data or less hawkish Fed commentary triggers a dovish repricing in interest rate expectations. The dollar has been supported by safe-haven flows and the hawkish repricing, while the euro still faces headwinds from France’s unresolved budget issues and uneven growth. However, with the US dollar heavily overbought and EUR/USD largely driven by USD dynamics, a shift in Fed expectations could outweigh euro-related concerns.

USD:

The US dollar has found support recently from a hawkish repricing in interest rate expectations following Governor Waller’s remarks, in which he even floated the possibility of a larger 50 bps hike at the December meeting. Higher-than-expected US inflation data added to the hawkish sentiment.

Moreover, the greenback benefited from safe-haven flows as global growth concerns intensified amid a more hawkish Fed, the prolonged US-Iran stalemate and a global stock market selloff.

Those same growth concerns, though, triggered a dovish repricing of Fed rate hike expectations further out the curve, as markets anticipated that the stock market rout could significantly weigh on the broader economy. This has caused the yield differential to favour the upside for the EUR/USD pair, although the greenback remained resilient thanks to safe-haven demand.

Looking ahead, we might see the US dollar coming under more pressure in case the Fed sounds less hawkish than expected or the economic data triggers a dovish repricing in interest rates expectations.

EUR:

On the EUR side, the ECB is widely expected to raise interest rates by 25 bps at the December meeting and deliver one final hike in June 2027. Growth is holding up well, but the recovery remains uneven, with France being a drag. Inflation and energy-related risks are still complicating the ECB’s job of getting inflation sustainably back to target.

France's 2027 budget battle is still ongoing, and the government is struggling to get its deficit-reduction plans through parliament. So, the French fiscal story remains unresolved. The key risk for French bonds is that parliament waters down the government's consolidation measures, leaving France with a higher deficit for longer. This could keep the French-German 10-year yield spread and the euro under pressure.

At the end of the day, the EUR/USD pair is 80% driven by what happens with the US dollar, so a dovish repricing in Fed interest rate expectations will likely outweigh euro-related woes, especially given the overbought US dollar positioning.
