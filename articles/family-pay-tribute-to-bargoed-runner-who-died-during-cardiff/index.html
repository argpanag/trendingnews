Family pay tribute to Bargoed runner who died during Cardiff Half Marathon

The family of a 24-year-old runner from Bargoed have paid tribute following his death during the Cardiff Half Marathon.

Ross Williams-Griffiths suffered a cardiac arrest while taking part in the race on Sunday October 4.

He had been running alongside his partner, Katie Bowen, to raise funds for Tenovus Cancer Care under the name “Team Griff-Bow”. He had also recently joined a local running club alongside his brother, Rhys, and Rhys’ fiancée, Jayde.

In a tribute, released through Run 4 Wales, his family described him as a dedicated athlete, close to his family, who lived at home with his parents, Mandy and Shane.

With a love of travelling, camping and attending festivals with family and friends, Mr Williams-Griffiths was said to often be the person entrusted with organising adventures, from booking accommodation to planning itineraries.

He had built a promising career at GE Aerospace and had a bright future ahead of him, having earned the respect and admiration of colleagues through his dedication and work ethic.

The family said: “Ross was a young, fit and healthy man who loved running and being part of the community that surrounded it. He was kind, caring and full of life, and brought laughter, loyalty and warmth wherever he went.

“The support we have received since losing Ross has been overwhelming and we are deeply grateful for the kindness shown to our family. We know that many people have wished to honour Ross and show their support following his death, and we wanted them to know who Ross was and how much he meant to us.

“While we are yet to learn the exact cause of Ross’ death, we hope that sharing his story may raise awareness of sudden cardiac arrest. If raising awareness in Ross’ memory helps even one family avoid the heartbreak we are experiencing, then that will be part of his lasting legacy.”

Matt Newman, chief executive of race organiser Run 4 Wales, said the organisation’s focus remained on supporting the family, adding: “We are grateful to the event and hospital medical teams who responded with extraordinary speed and professionalism.

“We thank Ross’ family for sharing his story during such a difficult time. We hope that the awareness they wish to raise in his memory will honour a young man who meant so much to so many people.”

Run 4 Wales confirmed it is liaising with bereavement charity 2wish to provide ongoing support to the family.

Two fundraising appeals have been set up in Mr Williams-Griffiths’ memory. Donations to fund cardiac screening can be made at rosswilliams-griffiths.muchloved.com, and donations to 2wish can be made at 2wish.org.uk/donate.
