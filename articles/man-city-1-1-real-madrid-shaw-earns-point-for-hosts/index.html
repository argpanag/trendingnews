    Manchester City's Khadija Shaw speaking to Disney+ after the game: "I think when you look at the game, we tried the second half much better than we started the first half and I think that's one thing we're going to look at as a team. Manchester City 1-1 Real Madrid

Man City hold on to claim draw against Real Madrid

Manchester City drew for the second week running in the Women's Champions League as Real Madrid tested them at the Joie Stadium.

City manager Andree Jeglertz said it was "business as usual" before the game as the women's team looked to separate themselves from the saga involving the club's breaches of Premier League financial rules.

Their preparation was inevitably overshadowed, but there was no sign of that being a distraction as City looked sharp in attack.

However, the hints of defensive frailty that they have shown since the start of the season were exploited by a talented Real Madrid frontline.

Linda Caicedo put the visitors in front midway through the first half with a clever finish, though goalkeeper Ayaka Yamashita perhaps could have done better.

City responded early in the second half, as Khadija Shaw spun in the box and finished powerfully into the far corner to draw the teams level.

Colombia international Caicedo threatened throughout for Real, linking up nicely with Swedish forward Felicia Schroder and troubling City's defence.

She went close to doubling her tally when Real pounced on a mistake by substitute Grace Clinton in the second half but her effort flew wide.

City had created chances in a fairly open first half as Shaw's looping header was tipped over, after Mary Fowler earlier nodded on to the crossbar.

Both teams continued to attack freely and there were big opportunities for City's Lauren Hemp, Yui Hasegawa and Beth Mead that were all squandered.

There was a scare for City in the final minutes too when Mead collided with the referee during a dangerous Real Madrid attack.

Before being accidentally blocked off by the official, Mead had appeared usefully placed to intercept the ball on the edge of the penalty area.

The whistle was blown just before Real's Silvia Cristobal fired into the back of the net and there were protests in the dugout as a result.

Real Madrid manager Pau Quesada said afterwards: "I've got a lot of experience in football, and everything has to be top level in my profession.

"When you get to this top level of women's club football in Europe, you need to have refereeing worthy of that level. You cannot have a referee standing in a place where she could block or be involved in attacking plays.

"The biggest source of my frustration is the positioning of the referee and at this level, they shouldn't be there."

City return to Women's Super League action this Sunday against Arsenal (16:30 BST) and will hope to maintain their 100% record in the domestic competition.

Analysis: Entertainers show attacking quality

City began their Champions League campaign with a solid draw at Bayern Munich last week, coming from two goals down to snatch a 2-2 result.

They were keen to get off to a winning start in their home games but knew it would be a challenge against an exciting Real Madrid, who will fancy their chances of going far in the competition.

The visitors' star names in Schroder and Caicedo made their mark early on, but City were able to play through Real Madrid's press with relative ease and caused plenty of problems of their own.

It promised to be an attacking affair and did not disappoint with both teams prepared to take risks, even if they did leave gaps at the back.

City had options they could turn to on the bench, including USA midfielder Sam Coffey, who added intensity when she came on in the second half.

Some of Manchester United's players were watching from the stands at the Joie Stadium, knowing they will face City later this month, and were treated to rich entertainment.

But there was sloppiness from City too as they continued to be put under pressure by the Spanish side, with Clinton giving the ball away and almost allowing Caicedo to make it 2-1.

Caicedo was in on goal again later on when she forced a smart save out of City goalkeeper Yamashita, only for the offside flag to go up.

Jeglertz will have been frustrated by his team struggling to impose control on the game, but they showed resilience to come back from a goal down and secure a point against strong opponents.

Two points from City's opening two matches in the league phase is not a bad tally considering these opponents are likely to be their toughest, even with Paris St-Germain still to come.

"We have played those two teams that are definitely at a very high level. If you want to go far in the tournament, you also need to win these games," said Jeglertz.

"We have two points, haven't lost and we had two good halves. But we're still looking for a 90 minutes where we feel we can do a great game.

"It's an OK start. It's not ideal but it's a good start. We know what is needed from us for the rest of the tournament."
