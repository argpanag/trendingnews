Microsoft makes big cuts at Halo Studios, shifts franchise to Activision [Update]

TheTools · 24.09.2026 22:07 · source · permalink

Deal to sell Ninja Theory falls through, dimming studio’s prospects

The radical transformation of Microsoft’s gaming business is continuing today with another wave of layoffs, a rearrangement of its studios, an increased risk of one closing, and a massive change to its Halo franchise, all announced by Microsoft in a post today.

The cuts: 268 workers, mostly from Microsoft’s Halo Studios (former 343 Industries), which recently released Halo: Campaign Evolved. Those cuts also include people from Xbox Games Studio management. The cuts are part of the 3,200 announced in July, half of which had been implemented then.

The potential studio closure: In July, Microsoft said it had a deal in the works to sell Ninja Theory, the UK-based studio behind the Hellblade games, to a buyer it didn’t identify. That deal has fallen through, Microsoft said, as has a subsequent one, leaving the studio at risk of shutting down. Microsoft is beginning a consultation process that could lead to such a move (Microsoft also said it is selling Undead Labs, makers of State of Decay, back to its management; there’s no update around Arkane Lyon, another studio Microsoft will either sell or close)

Major Halo changes: The greatly reduced Halo Studios will no longer lead development of arguably Microsoft’s most iconic franchise, instead taking on a caretaker/support role for existing projects. Development of the next Halo game will occur at Microsoft-owned Activision, by an as-yet-unidentified team (it’s unclear if Activision will tap an existing team among its roster of first-person-shooter-making Call of Duty studios or is assembling a new team). Activision will publish the game as well.

Update - September 22, 10:20am: A statement from Activision president Rob Kostich regarding Halo: “For Halo, our goal is clear: make the greatest Halo game ever, worthy of its universe and legacy, while staying true to what made players love it in the first place. It is a bold ambition, one that this franchise deserves. We have already begun assembling a purpose-built team, unique in capability and talent, ready and excited to deliver this next chapter with the community.”

Studio rearrangement: Several studios that had been part of Microsoft’s central Xbox Game Studios group are moving into other parts of the company’s gaming group.

  • Rare (Sea of Thieves, once upon a time Banjo-Kazooie) will report in to Activision

  • World’s Edge (Age of Empires) also will report in to Activision

  • Obsidian (Grounded, upcoming Fallout game) will report to Bethesda

  • Microsoft Casual Games (Minesweeper, Solitaire Collection) will report in to King (Candy Crush)

  • Turn 10 (Forza) is merging with Playground Games (Forza Horizon, Fable)
