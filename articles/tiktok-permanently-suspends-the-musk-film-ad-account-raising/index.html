TikTok “Permanently Suspends” the ‘Musk’ Film Ad Account, Raising Suppression Fears (Exclusive)

TheTools · 17.10.2026 15:02 · source · permalink

Move comes just as Alex Gibney doc film expands to nearly 500 screens. Distributor Bleecker Street says it is appealing.

TikTok has banned the Musk film advertising account, citing a repeated pattern of political content that violates its policies. The move, which comes just as the Alex Gibney’s investigation into Elon Musk expands to more than 470 screens across North America, raises concerns about Big Tech suppression that the very film seeks to expose.

Distributor Bleecker Street was informed Friday afternoon a “permanent suspension” was coming regarding any ads it tried to buy, and the company was unable to do so shortly after. The native unpaid TikTok account, which generally allows more leeway on so-called political content, remains active, though Bleecker Street is not allowed to pay to boost its content, as many companies traditionally do.

A spokesperson for the distributor confirmed that TikTok reps had informed them of the suspension and noted “disappointment” in the timing. After grossing $70,000 in four theaters in New York and Los Angeles in its first weekend of release, Musk is now playing in dozens of cities this weekend. The spokesperson said the company is in the process of appealing the move; even a reversal, however, could still prove costly if it doesn’t come during a weekend window when many people are likely to see the film.

A TikTok representative did not comment. Email correspondence between TikTok and Bleecker Street representatives shows a cycle of rejection and appeals on various paid posts, including the trailer, which an official said will “continue to get pulled from the platform” due to political content. Another spot, a shot of a quote from The New Yorker saying that “Americans should probably watch this film like their life depends on it,” was also stopped, due to a “static image.”

Musk goes deep on a number of its subject’s actions and companies, which Gibney spent more than three years investigating. The film devotes a large portion of its second half focusing on Musk’s political activities with DOGE and beyond, though does not take a political position on, well, politicians, nor advocate for one candidate or cause over another. It also centers on his acquisition of Twitter, which Gibney suggests was done to control the digital public square, adding an element of irony to the TikTok move.

An official message from the company to Bleecker Street noted a “level of severity with political-policy violations” and said that “some violations trigger immediate suspension.”

It was unclear, however, what those violations were. Bleecker Street was unable to pay for placement of about a half-dozen pieces of content in recent days. In one, a cheeky ad about what to do during intermission of the nearly four-hour doc, on-screen text suggested deleting your X account, “chat about a fascist hellscape” and “”switch your seats if they are too far to the right.”

A second post suggested seeing the movie “before it gets shut down,” adding a further element of irony to the ban. The former was piece of material was visible on the unpaid account, and had garnered 270,000 views. But the inability a company to buy ads or pay to boost unpaid posts hampers a campaign’s visibility and removes a key tool that studios use to spread the word about a movie currently in theaters.

TikTok has no official affiliation with Elon Musk, though it is partly owned by Larry Ellison, who gets a rough moment in the doc when a text from him offering to pay billions to the mogul to help finance his Twitter acquisition is shown. TikTok has been more resistant to Musk content than some of its competitors, refusing to reverse its position against accepting an ad for the trailer even after Meta and Google/YouTube did just that. X, unsurprisingly, has been unwilling to accept any advertising for the film.

Larry’s son David is set to release Gibney’s doc when his newly acquired HBO, which financed the film, puts in on the platform in 2027.
