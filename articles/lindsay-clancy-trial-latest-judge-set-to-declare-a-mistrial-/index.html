Lindsay Clancy trial latest: Judge set to declare a mistrial - follow live

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:28 · source · permalink

Summary

  • The judge in the trial of Lindsay Clancy - the US mother accused of murdering her three children - says he intends to declare a mistrial, prompting an audible gasp in the courtroom

  • It comes after the jury said "with a heavy heart" they were "unable to come to a unanimous decision"

  • Clancy's legal team want to appeal the decision - the judge says they have one hour. Here's more on what a mistrial means

  • Clancy, 36, is charged with three counts of first-degree murder in the deaths of her three children - Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and eight-month-old Callan - at their Massachusetts home in 2023

  • She does not deny killing them, but her lawyer argues she was suffering from postpartum psychosis, and could not distinguish right from wrong

  2. A timeline of twists and turnspublished at 18:15 BST
    18:15 BST

    After six and a half days trying to reach a verdict and several notes to the judge, Lindsay Clancy's jury is still deadlocked.

    The judge has now stated his intention to declare a mistrial.

    Here's a timeline of the key moments in deliberations so far:

    • 27 August: Attorneys for both sides make closing arguments and jury begins first day of deliberations
    • 1 September: Jurors notify the judge they are unable to reach a unanimous decision - the judge instructs them to continue deliberating, but they end the day with no verdict. State police arrest a woman outside court and charge her with intimidation of a witness or jurors
    • 2 September: Jurors tell the judge they still cannot reach a unanimous verdict - the atmosphere in court is incredibly tense, according to our reporter at the scene
    • 3 September: On day six, Clancy's attorney asks the judge to remove one juror who he says isn't following the judge's instructions regarding reasonable doubt. The judge rejects the request and says he does not want to interfere. Discussions between the two are animated. The judge sends the jury home and orders them to continue deliberations
    • 4 September: Judge Sullivan once again denies Clancy's attorney's request to remove one of the jurors. After the jury resumed private deliberations, it sends their third note this week saying they do not have a unanimous decision. Sullivan says he plans to declare the case a mistrial. Prosecutors support this, but the defence opposes. Clancy's team has been given an hour to file an emergency appeal with the state's apex court

  9. This situation is unusual, expert sayspublished at 17:21 BST
    17:21 BST

    Grace Eliza Goodwin
    US reporter

    Clancy's lawyer Kevin Reddington has asked the judge to delay declaring a mistrial for an hour while he files an appeal to the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court - a request the judge has granted.

    Such a move is "very, very rare and unusual", particularly if it means the jury has to come back to court tomorrow, on a Saturday, says William Kickham, a Massachusetts criminal defence attorney.

    Reddington is "trying to make sure that the preservation of rights of his client is not disturbed. In other words, if there's going to be a mistrial, he wants it taken care of today in terms of the ability to have a mistrial," Kickham says.

    "He wants to avoid another trial, and doing this may increase the chances that he can avoid that trial," he adds.

  11. US maternal mental healthcare isn't sufficient - expertpublished at 17:09 BST
    17:09 BST

    Pratiksha Ghildial
    Reporting from court in Massachusetts

    Maternal mental health experts say this case has thrown the spotlight on the gap in postpartum mental health healthcare in the US.

    Postpartum mental health expert Nicole Kumi tells the BBC that just one appointment six weeks after delivery - the standard practice - isn't enough for mothers to get the help they need.

    "We can’t keep expecting them to ask for help when they don't understand what's happening. We need to educate moms and workplaces in maternal mental health so her village is more prepared to step in. We wait for moms to be in crisis and then expect them to navigate the system which is poorly designed," Kumi says.

    She says that support for Lindsay Clancy's mental health struggle doesn't mean agreeing with the deaths of her children.

    "The best outcome from such a tragedy would be perinatal healthcare reform across the US Rather than allowing this to be another tragic case, it could be the turning point for maternal mental health."

  12. Criminal convictions must have a unanimous verdictpublished at 16:56 BST
    16:56 BST

    In US criminal trials, the jury must reach a unanimous decision in order to convict a defendant.

    The 12 member panel in the Clancy trial has been unable to meet this high level of agreement, telling the judge just now that, after several attempts at deliberation, they are still unable to come to a decision.

    This differs from in the UK where if the jury cannot reach a unanimous verdict then the judge can allow them to reach a majority verdict.

  14. What just happened?published at 16:46 BST
    16:46 BST

    We are in limbo once again after a last-minute request from Clancy's attorney.

    Here's what happened:

    The judge called everyone back into the courtroom after he received a note from the jury who said they still could not come to a unanimous decision. In US criminal trials, jury unanimity is required for a conviction.

    Prosecutors were in favour of a mistrial, while Clancy's attorney Kevin Reddington asked the judge to tell jurors to keep deliberating.

    The judge said he had no choice but to declare a mistrial, leading Reddington to say he wanted to file an emergency appeal before a mistrial is declared.

    It's not clear on what grounds Reddington will fill the appeal, but Judge Wiliam Sullivan said he would give Reddington one hour to handle it.

  15. What happens if there's a mistrial?published at 16:38 BST
    16:38 BST

    Madeline Halpert
    US reporter

    Judge William Sullivan has just stated his intention to declare this case a mistrial after jurors said they could not come to a unanimous verdict.

    If he declares it, multiple things could unfold.

    The prosecution can either refile and restart the trial with a new jury or negotiate a plea agreement. Alternatively, prosecutors could dismiss the charges altogether.

    In serious felony cases like these, prosecutors almost always ask for a retrial. If they do, the jury selection process will take place once again and a new trial will be scheduled.

    If a new trial is planned, Clancy will remain in the same forensic mental health facility she has been in - though her defence attorneys could ask for a review of her bail situation, said Heather Cucolo, a New York Law School professor and expert in mental health law.
