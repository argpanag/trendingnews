“Two years ahead of Samsung”: China’s DRAM provocation as CXMT plans to launch next-generation memory this year

“Two years ahead of Samsung”: China’s DRAM provocation as CXMT plans to launch next-generation memory this year

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China’s largest memory chipmaker, said it would launch server chips this year using 4F2 (four-F-square) technology, which South Korea’s leading semiconductor companies are still researching. In effect, China has declared war in its bid to overtake Samsung Electronics and SK hynix in the next-generation DRAM market. While some view CXMT’s claims as excessive technology marketing, concerns are growing that they are a “warning signal” showing China’s R&D capabilities rapidly catching up with South Korea.

According to the semiconductor industry and local media on the 12th, CXMT President Cao Kanyu said on the 10th at the 4th Integrated Circuit and Chiplet Conference in Shanghai, China: “We will launch next-generation DDR5 RDIMM server memory using a 4F2 DRAM structure by the end of the year.”

Products based on the 6F2 (six-F-square) structure are currently the norm in the DRAM market. But further performance gains will require shrinking the DRAM circuit structure to 4F2. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are both developing 4F2 DRAM, but neither has announced plans for mass production. The industry expects Samsung Electronics to begin mass production around 2028. If CXMT’s claim proves true, it would start mass production two years ahead of Samsung Electronics.

The prevailing assessment is that China’s semiconductor technology lags South Korea’s by two to three years in DRAM and by at least three years in high-bandwidth memory (HBM). Recently, however, China has announced a series of innovative technology developments, putting South Korea’s semiconductor industry on edge. Huawei previously unveiled logic-folding technology, describing it as a new technology that would overturn “Moore’s law.” In packaging, Samsung Electronics is also paying royalties to use Xtacking, a bonding technology developed by Yangtze Memory Technologies Corp (YMTC).

South Korea’s semiconductor industry considers Chinese companies’ announcements of new technology developments exaggerated in some respects, but says they are not to be dismissed. “Developing a technology and bringing it into mass production are two different things,” an industry insider said, adding, “But we shouldn’t dismiss Chinese technology, as YMTC developed Xtacking and applied it in mass production.”

South Korea’s semiconductor industry is particularly focused on the fact that CXMT is targeting the “server DRAM” market. Unlike DRAM for PCs and smartphones, where Chinese DRAM is widely used, server DRAM for data centers remains a market dominated globally by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

If CXMT completes development of the new 4F2 technology, it could shake up the server DRAM market. In a presentation that day, Cao said CXMT had been researching 4F2 DRAM since its founding in 2016 and had disclosed more than 300 related patent families, a portfolio that surpasses those of all domestic and international competitors.
