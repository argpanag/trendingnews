Report: Raiders planned to start Mendoza soon even if no Cousins trade

TheTools · 15.11.2026 14:05 · source · permalink

The Fernando Mendoza era likely would have begun soon, with or without the Las Vegas Raiders trading away Kirk Cousins.

Sources told NFL Network that the Raiders planned to unleash the No. 1 overall draft selection in the next few weeks.

If Las Vegas had lost Sunday to the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks and the Denver Broncos on Nov. 22, Mendoza would have started Nov. 29 against the Cleveland Browns.

The Raiders (4-5) always said publicly there was a plan for Mendoza, though they kept it private. This was the plan, especially if two consecutive losses meant the playoffs were an uphill battle.

It's all moot now, with Las Vegas trading Cousins to the Minnesota Vikings on Tuesday for a 2027 third-round draft pick.

Raiders coach Klint Kubiak said Mendoza will have the entire playbook at his disposal, with the organization believing the reigning Heisman Trophy winner works even more than they thought.

"His teammates know how prepared he is," Kubiak said. "It's not just lip service, but he has shown it on the practice field."

While there undoubtedly will be growing pains, the Raiders are overwhelmingly excited to see their future. They feel like Mendoza is ready, thanks in part to the work he has done with veteran quarterbacks coach Mike Sullivan.

Prior to each play in practice, Mendoza calls the play for Sullivan away from the group then goes through his footwork as if he is the quarterback, while Cousins took the first-team reps. It was mental reps-plus for Mendoza.

During games, Sullivan and Mendoza stand 30 yards away from the play, with Mendoza practicing calling it, making the checks and watching how Cousins executed. What the Raiders smartly have is a personal tutor for their prized draft pick, and this has gone on since the spring.

"His command of the offense, his ability to articulate the huddle calls [and] communicate at the line of scrimmage, has improved consistently," Sullivan said. "It's certainly credit to him."

After each practice, Sullivan and Mendoza break down film of his performance on the scout team, and when there are miscues, he repeats it. Mendoza then studies the opponent's offense that he has been running just to learn.

While Cousins had taken nearly all of the first-team reps, the Raiders had sneaked in some reps with the starters for Mendoza. For instance, if the first team was working on the rushing offense on a Wednesday, Mendoza would get a snap or two just to mix him in. It rarely happened on passing plays, with Cousins taking all of those.

Now it's Mendoza's turn, as the future is now for Las Vegas.
