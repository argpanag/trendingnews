FTSE 100 slumps in AI rout – latest updates

TheTools · 06.11.2026 17:52 · source · permalink

Key moments

The FTSE 100 fell sharply on Friday as a tech sell-off on Wall Street spread across the world.

The UK’s flagship stock index slumped as much as 2.4pc, putting it on track for its worst day since March, while Japan’s Nikkei plunged 6pc overnight after turmoil in US stock markets spread to Asia and Europe.

Democrats’ victory in the US midterm elections appeared to be the trigger for the global sell-off, with many newly elected Democrat governors and senators opposed to the rapid rollout of data centres, which are packed with semiconductor chips used to power AI.

America’s tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 index plunged as much as 6.4pc on Thursday in its worst decline since March 2020, with shares in semiconductor makers the hardest hit. It is now 11.9pc below an all-time high set last month, putting it into technical “correction” territory.

Tech shares rebounded on Wall Street on Friday, with the Nasdaq 100 up more than 2pc. However, it was not enough to recover losses made in the previous session.

Vivek Arya, an analyst at Bank of America, said the “indiscriminate selling of chip stocks on AI capex fears” could run its course by the early months of next year.

The FTSE 100 has a low exposure to the tech sector but was still swept up in the sell-off as fears grow about the knock on effects of an AI slowdown.

BT was the worst performer, dropping more than 6pc, over concerns its cost saving programme could be undermined.

Pharmaceutical giants including AstraZeneca and GSK suffered some of the largest losses on Friday.

In South Korea, Samsung and SK Hynix sank 6.4pc and 10.4pc respectively. In Japan, Kioxia plunged 8.7pc while Taiwan’s TSMC fell 4.7pc.

AI investor SoftBank, which is one of Arm’s biggest backers, fell 10.8pc.

On Thursday, the S&P 500 in the US fell 4.4pc on its worst day since Donald Trump announced his “liberation day” tariffs in April 2025. The wipeout more than erased the US stock market’s gain from the day before, which was its best in three months.

Musk companies begin to rebound

Elon Musk’s Tesla and SpaceX have began to rebound, after the pair were hit heavily by the sell-off on Wall Street.

Telsa is currently up 4.9pc for the day, with SpaceX rallying 7.3pc, after each dropping by around 10pc on Thursday.

The tech billionaire shed $80bn from his net worth during the market turmoil for tech shares.

He does remain the richest person in the world, however, with his total wealth estimated to be around $784.9bn.

Mr Musk spent a record-breaking $445m to $474m backing Republican candidates during the midterm elections.

FTSE closes lower as US sell-off extends to London

The FTSE 100 has closed the day 1.1pc lower, after falling as much as 2.4pc, following yesterday’s tech turmoil in the US.

The index managed to stage somewhat of a recovery in the late afternoon session, propped up by rallying Scottish Mortgage Investment Trust shares, up 2.3pc, and Coca-Cola’s bottle supplier up 2.2pc.

The biggest fallers included Airtel Africa, which closed 4.2pc lower, and Admiral Group, dropping 3.1pc, as overall sentiment weakened.

It’s an indication that a loss of market confidence is extending across to the UK, after Democrat officials were elected to Congress, which has prompted fears of an AI slowdown.

UK retail investors moving to ‘buy the dip’ on Wall Street

British retail investors are moving to ‘buy the dip’ on cheaper technology stocks, after $3.2tn (£2.39tn) was wiped off Wall Street yesterday.

The tech-focused Nasdaq Composite suffered its steepest daily drop in more than eighteen months, with build-out firms like CoreWeave falling even further.

However, UK retail buyers have viewed the plunge as a buying opportunity, rushing to pick up beaten-down shares.

This has helped Wall Street rally in early trading, with the Nasdaq Composite rising 1.54pc in Friday’s session.

As I've mentioned before, I think a large portion of this selloff is being driven by deleveraging rather than a fundamental deterioration in the AI investment cycle. Hedge funds are reducing their exposure, cutting leverage, and becoming increasingly cautious about rebuilding positions after what has been a very difficult October. And this is exactly why retail buying matters so much right now. Retail investors have been one of the biggest sources of support for this market over the past few years, consistently stepping in to buy dips even as institutional investors reduced exposure. If that buying pressure continues, it could help absorb some of the selling from hedge funds and create the stability we desperately need. Because ultimately, we need hedge funds to start coming back into these trades. But that's probably not going to happen until they see some sustained stability in AI and semiconductor stocks. They're unlikely to aggressively rebuild positions while volatility remains elevated and other funds are still liquidating their exposure. Obviously, one late session reversal doesn't mean the selloff is over, and retail investors still finished yesterday as net sellers. But the fact that buyers were willing to step back in after such an aggressive decline is encouraging. If retail investors can continue absorbing the selling pressure over the coming sessions, we could eventually see hedge funds begin rebuilding their positions, which would provide another source of buying pressure for the market. Burnham: AI remains ‘on the top of the agenda’

Andy Burnham has said that artificial intelligence (AI) will remain “on the top of the agenda” in Britain.

This is despite concerns of a possible slowdown in development of the technology in the US.

The Prime Minister told the BBC that “there is a case for AI”, but cautioned that the Government must be “clear eyed about the potential risks.”

He added: “We need a global code to capture the benefits while safeguarding people against the risks, and that’s what we’ll see to do.”

John Healey, the chancellor, announced more than £600m of investment in projects related to AI in the Budget.

Asian economies under threat from AI slowdown

Asian economies are under growing threat of a sharp economic slowdown, as a possible pullback in artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure spending in the US could damage chip demand.

South Korea and Taiwan in particular, which are heavily reliant on chip exports, depend overwhelmingly on whether global tech giants continue investing heavily in the data centre buildout.

This has come under threat since the Democrats won control of both Houses of Congress, with markets uneasy over the future of AI regulatory intervention.

Mark Williams, Chief Asia Economist at Capital Economics, warns that growth in Korea and Taiwan “is likely to slow sharply in 2027 and 2028” as a result.

He wrote: “Exports from Korea and Taiwan will decelerate and chipmakers in both places will probably scale back their own investment plans.”

America’s AI backlash will change the world

The city of Harwood in North Dakota does not have much to put it on the map. With a population of 794, the town is best known for a heroic operation in 1997 when locals built a five-mile wall to keep out floods, as well as its wonderfully relaxed local motto: “It’s all good.”

Harwood’s easygoing nature became more apparent this week when it became the only place in America to vote in favour of data centres. 

Around 325 voters, three quarters of turnout, voted against a proposal to slow large industrial developments including data centres. The measure, if passed, may have affected plans for a $3bn (£2.2bn) development by the tech company Applied Digital near the town.

The vote was conspicuous by being unique. There were 47 local referendums on data centres this week; 46 voted to make it harder to build the giant facilities that are crucial to artificial intelligence.

Retail investors sell at fastest pace since Covid

American retail investors dumped stocks at the fastest pace since the first Covid lockdowns over fears an AI bubble was bursting.

Vanda Research said private individuals with their own stock accounts sold a net $379mn of single stocks between the start of trading in the US on Thursday and 2pm eastern time. That was the biggest drop since March 2020.

Analyst Viraj Patel said: “That selling hit at the same time as the sharp intra-day sell-off. That is highly unusual: on big down days, retail is normally the dip buyer, so something clearly made them hit the sell button. 

“They reverted to type later in the session, led by $1.2bn of ETF buying.”

Bank of America described the sharp sell-off in semiconductor companies as the “storm before the calm”.

He said: “Congressional policy clarity and buybacks could help restore confidence.”

He suggested that opposition to data centres in one state could also “boost demand elsewhere, with AI capex now a critical, outsized driver of US GDP growth”.
