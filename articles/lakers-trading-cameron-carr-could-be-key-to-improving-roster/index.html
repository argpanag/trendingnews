Lakers Trading Cameron Carr Could Be Key to Improving Roster Around Luka Doncic amid New NBA Rumors

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:22 · source · permalink

The Los Angeles Lakers have a few obvious holes on their roster and no obvious ways to patch them up.

Well, one obvious way, actually, but Lakers fans won't want to hear it.

With L.A. working the NBA trade market in search of, presumably, a backup center and maybe rebounding wings, it has so far found that teams "are not willing to bite" on the future second-round picks at its disposal, per Heavy.com's Sean Deveney.

First-round rookie swingman Cameron Carr, though, "would have value" if the Lakers dangled him for frontcourt reinforcements, one executive told Deveney.

For some Lakers fans, the idea of dealing Carr will be a non-starter. Or a non-starter in terms of targeting a second-teamer, at least.

Carr had lottery buzz before slipping to the 24th pick. His scouting report points to serious support-player potential: a disruptive three-and-D perimeter player with positional versatility and explosive athleticism. Bullish projections even point toward some show-and-go creation in his future.

It's almost all excitement in front of the 22-year-old. But how far into the future are the Purple and Gold planning right now? Because it certainly feels like the franchise's present should trump all else with this team tied atop the league standings with an .824 winning percentage.

The Lakers, for their part, sound ready and willing to pump resources into putting the best possible roster around Luka Dončić.

"We'll be active at the trade deadline to shore up things that we see," general manager Rob Pelinka said. "We think this team has a real chance to make a run, so we want to give the group as much talent as we can."

Pelinka touched on the fact that it's early and how L.A. needs to be careful about not reading too much into this start. That said, proceeding with caution shouldn't mean standing pat.

Because a lot of what we're seeing now can sustain.

Maybe Dončić doesn't flirt with a 40-point scoring average all season, but he is obviously capable of posting MVP numbers. A healthy Austin Reaves is a legitimate co-star. Walker Kessler has stabilized the starting center spot. Investments in the supporting cast should provide more dividends than they have.

And yet, backup center is a problem that isn't going away. The same almost certainly rings true for the defensive rebounding woes (29th in defensive rebounding percentage). Since Carr can't directly help those issues—he's not even a rotation regular yet—the Lakers might have to sacrifice him for someone who can.

It would be painful, sure, since no one else on the roster can match his blend of youth and upside. It would be shortsighted, too, since L.A. would likely be dealing him before he logged enough minutes to drive up his trade value.

It could also be absolutely justified if it proves the most viable path toward the center depth and rebounding presence this group so clearly needs.
