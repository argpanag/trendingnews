  4. 'Clear red card'published at 22:49 BST 23 October
    22:49 BST 23 October

    FT: Ipswich 1-1 Nottm Forest

    Nottingham Forest

    Nottingham Forest manager Oliver Glasner speaking to Sky Sports:

    "Mixed feelings. More positive, playing so well and we had a great start and after 15-20 minutes we were the better side and started to control the game. Then we made the mistake and it's 1-1. The momentum can change but the reaction second half was amazing. We had many situations where we could score. I loved the reaction, the way we played and the spirit. We are moving up on performances and staying patient."

    On not putting chances away: "We don't need to address it. Sometimes you try a huge effort and things don't work. You are encouraging them to keep going because you have these periods in sport and life. When I see the way we play, the spirit and the chances, the only way now is to stay patient and not make the problem bigger. It's a question of time and maybe in a few weeks you will be asking why we are scoring so many goals."

    Was it a red card? "Yes, I felt during the game it was a clear red card. I think he [McAtee] was preparing his finish by going a bit right. I think it was the wrong decison but we couldn't have played a more dominant second half. We created enough chances to decide the game and that's why I will never blame the referee. Many decisions not on our side this season but again, we stay patient and keep going.

    "Referee's decison is always subjective. I liked that he went to the screen, this is the right procedure. It was wrong [decision] but he didn't do it on purpose and I have to be honest - I remember the situation at Palace in the FA Cup final when Dean Henderson touched the ball outside the box and it was the same explanation and he didn't get a red card but today I feel it was wrong."

  5. 'Results not what we want'published at 22:45 BST 23 October
    22:45 BST 23 October

    FT: Ipswich 1-1 Nottm Forest

    Nottingham Forest

    Nottingham Forest manager Oliver Glasner: "Very good performance at the end and a great start to the game. For 15-20 minutes Ipswich were in control and then we made a mistake just before half-time to get a 1-1 draw.

    "The reaction in the second-half was amazing, really dominating and it isn't easy playing here at Ipswich, in their home stadium and all the effort keeping them in a 1-1 finish overall, but it is important to stay calm because it feels everything is going into the right direction, we have to be patient and then we will get the reward."

    On the mistakes his defenders made: "It is football and it is a game of mistakes and no one does it on purpose but it is how we react and the reaction was great, we kept playing forward and we created these moments.

    "It's the only thing we can do is to support the players, everyone sees and feels the way we are playing the game is right, but the results aren't what we want. We have to respect that Ipswich are a good team, so lets take the point and respect the point and we will rebuild on our performance.

    "There is no other way than to support the players and encourage them because they work so hard to get into the situation do so many things in training we are showing them videos and speaking to them well.

    "It is a lot of positives to take, if we did the opposite and blaming the players, they will loose their confidence. We have to support them and keep believing."

    On VAR: "It doesn't make sense [to speak to the referee] because it is what he decided and we have to accept it, it looks like the VAR was not from the East Midlands today it felt like it was a red card but we couldn't have played a more dominant game than we did in the second half.

    "Not to blame the referee, we will keep analysing, and practising. Maybe I am wrong or I don't know if the referee was a former football player. He won't change it anymore, so we accept it."

    Ipswich Town

    Ipswich manager Gary O'Neil speaking to Sky Sports:

Highlights: Nottingham Forest end run of three defeats with 1-1 draw at Ipswich

Nottingham Forest moved out of the Premier League relegation zone but were again left to rue missed chances as they were held to a draw by Ipswich at Portman Road.

Oliver Glasner's side dominated large periods of the game and took a deserved lead in the 13th minute when Neco Williams cushioned home Daniel Munoz's right-wing cross at the back post for his first Forest goal since April.

The returning Morgan Gibbs-White also had an effort saved by the toe of Town keeper Christian Walton before Leif Davis hacked the ball off the line.

Ipswich made Forest pay in the 41st minute when Nikola Milenkovic dwelt on the ball and his eventual backpass was too short and allowed Julio Enciso to collect and slot past Matz Sels for his first goal for the club since his summer move.

There was a controversial moment three minutes into the second half when Forest midfielder James McAtee was brought down by home defender Issa Diop when attempting to break through on goal.

Referee Thomas Bramall initially waved play on but the video assistant referee (VAR) recommended an on-field review for a red card for the denial of a goalscoring opportunity. But, after reviewing the footage, Bramall opted to award a yellow card and a free-kick, explaining that McAtee "had knocked the ball to the right side of the goal".

Forest boss Glasner told Sky Sports it was a "clear red card", but he "liked" the referee going to the screen and following the "right procedure".

That seemed to light a spark in his side, who went on to miss numerous chances to seal a second victory of the season after a disappointing start under Glasner.

An unmarked Murillo headed a corner wide, Dan Ndoye had an effort cleared off the line by the outstretched leg of Davis and striker Liam Delap headed over from six yards and whipped a low effort just wide as he returned to his former club.

Callum Hudson-Odoi also dragged an effort off target in late pressure from the visitors, while Jack Clarke curled wide at the other end for Gary O'Neil's men.

After three straight defeats, the point moved Forest above Coventry City and Tottenham up to 16th place on six points, although they could end the weekend back in the bottom three, while Ipswich are ninth.

Analysis: Stalemate sums up Forest's season

Glasner urges Forest to 'stay patient' after 1-1 draw at Ipswich

If ever a 90 minutes summed up a season, this was it for Nottingham Forest.

After their opening seven league games, their expected goals tally stood at -6.2. That figure only continues to rise after an xG of 2.31 tonight.

The under-performing leaves more for questions for Glasner to answer, especially with the previous disdain Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis has shown for a poor run of form.

Last season alone they had four managers, and it is just over a year since Ange Postecoglou's 39-day reign ended following six defeats and no wins in eight games.

Glasner also had a slow start at former club Crystal Palace, failing to taste victory in his opening six games in charge and then their first eight in his first full season, and the Austrian and Marinakis are said to be in constant contact and have a good relationship.

Their lack of goals - now a joint-league low of six in seven games - is a concern and it was confusing why Glasner left Chris Wood, who he has asked to lead a group discussion around goalscoring in training this season, on the bench until the 94th minute.

Delap worked hard again but failed to hit the target with any of his three shots and, with only one goal since his £50m summer move from Chelsea, his place in the starting XI is likely to come under question sooner rather than later.

On the subject of missed chances, Glasner added on Sky Sports: "We don't need to address it. Sometimes you try a huge effort and things don't work.

"You are encouraging them to keep going because you have these periods in sport and life.

"When I see the way we play, the spirit and the chances, the only way now is to stay patient and not make the problem bigger.

"It's a question of time and maybe in a few weeks you will be asking why we are scoring so many goals."

After his 150th Premier League appearance for the club, Gibbs-White added Forest have been asking themselves about their struggles in front of goal and accepted it was a "mentality thing", but also a period of "bad luck".

Ipswich will be disappointed with their own display - they only had one shot in the second half - but a seventh home point of the season matches their tally from the 2024-25 campaign when they were relegated.

Boss O'Neil told Sky Sports: "There's no aim. Of course we want a second year in the Premier League but we will try and finish as high as we can.

"Newly promoted sides can find it difficult but we have bridged that gap well. We have found a way most weeks to find a way to bridge the gap."
