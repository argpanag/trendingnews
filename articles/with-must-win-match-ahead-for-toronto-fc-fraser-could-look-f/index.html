With must-win match ahead for Toronto FC, Fraser could look for help from Gilman

TheTools · 30.10.2026 11:54 · source · permalink

Toronto FC successfully kept alive its MLS playoff hopes earlier this week with a come-from-behind win at home. 

But TFC still has plenty of work to do, starting with Sunday’s road game vs. Atlanta United FC, if it hopes to clinch its first post-season berth since 2020. 

Toronto (9-11-12) is tied for 10th in the Eastern Conference, four points behind a playoff spot. The Reds will be eliminated from playoff contention if they don't win Sunday.

Realistically, TFC must win both remaining road games to close out the season to have a legitimate shot at making the playoffs. As a result, the team faces a pressure-filled situation where coach Robin Fraser will most likely rely on his veteran players who have the most experience. 

But Fraser might also look for help from defender Jackson Gilman, who has impressed when given opportunities during his rookie season. Gilman, 22, was a second-round pick in the 2026 MLS SuperDraft out of the University of Pittsburgh and, in February, signed with Toronto FC’s second team, TFC 2, and competed in the MLS NEXT Pro reserve league. 

With Toronto FC mired in an injury crisis, Gilman was signed to a short-term loan deal and made his senior team debut in a loss to Atlético Ottawa in the first round of the Canadian Championship in May. Despite his lack of experience, Fraser trusted Gilman enough to start him the following week in an MLS game against Charlotte FC, slotting him into the middle of the defence next to veteran Walker Zimmerman.   

Gilman responded with a man-of-the-match display for the Reds with his solid two-way play on the night — he was a dynamo on the defensive side of things with three blocked shots, two tackles, two interceptions and two clearances. He also had a perfectly good goal in the first half ruled out following a VAR review. Six days after the trip to North Carolina, Gilman was signed to an MLS contract, making him a full-fledged member of Toronto FC. 

“When I got the news that they had reached out to (my agent to) negotiate terms for a first-team contract, it was definitely a shock of a morning. I wasn't expecting to wake up to that news, and it was just another one of those blessings that has come with the season,” Gilman recently told Sportsnet. 

Since the Charlotte match, Gilman has gone from strength to strength when called upon by Fraser. 

He’s registered one assist in his 12 subsequent MLS appearances, displaying great positional versatility by playing at centre back, both fullback spots and in central midfield while barely putting a foot wrong. 

“It can be difficult to change constantly, but I think that's one of my strong suits, and it's why I've been able to break into the first team — being versatile and playing multiple positions. It's probably one of the reasons that I'm here with the first team,” Gilman offered.

Did the youngster envision being called into the first team so soon after he was drafted by Toronto?

“It's been pretty surreal. If you were to ask me that in January or in December last year, I would have probably laughed a little bit and said, ‘Hey, that's above and beyond what I'm striving for.’ Obviously, I have goals and aspirations as a player, but to be honest, this is a lot more than I could have ever hoped for or imagined coming into my rookie year,” Gilman said. 

“I’ve just been trying to take everything for what it is and trying to build on performances and training sessions. And I've been blessed with the opportunity to play and start in a lot of these matches in the second half of the year, and I’m hoping to just continue to build on that into the post-season and into next year.

Gilman’s most recent appearance came on Oct. 17 in a 2-0 road win over the New York Red Bulls in difficult circumstances. Right fullback Kobe Franklin suffered a shoulder injury while making a crucial tackle and had to be subbed out after only 28 minutes.

Fraser immediately slotted Gilman into the right side of the back four, and the MLS rookie responded with a composed performance that belied his age and lack of experience, playing a critical role in helping the Reds lock things down defensively and record just their third road win of the campaign. 

“You never want to come on the pitch due to an injury, so it was tough in that sense to see a close friend and teammate go down the way he did. It's a difficult task to come 25 minutes into a game when all your teammates have had the rhythm and flow of a match,” Gilman said, 

“Thankfully, I was able to help the team get three points and a clean sheet.”  

In some ways, Gilman is reminiscent of current assistant coach Steven Beitashour, a key player for Toronto FC during its treble-winning season in 2017, with his defence-first attitude as a fullback who can also offer offensive flourishes.   

Gilman sees adding more attacking bite to his game as the next major step in his evolution with Beitashour’s help. 

“He was one of the first people that came over and put his arm around me when I came into pre-season. We were training before we left for (Spain), and he seemed to appreciate my values and my work rate as a defender and as a player,” Gilman said of Beitashour. 

“Ever since then, he's been an important staff member in my first season. We regularly meet and discuss certain training things or watch game film and clips, and he's done a lot more individual work with me about trying to improve and sharpen my game this year,” Gilman said.
