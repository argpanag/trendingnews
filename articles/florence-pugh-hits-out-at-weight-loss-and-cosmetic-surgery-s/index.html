Florence Pugh hits out at weight loss and cosmetic surgery speculation

TheTools · 21.11.2026 01:15 · source · permalink

Florence Pugh has said she was "disturbed" to see "thousands of people" discussing her recent appearance at the Governors Awards, and denied speculation about weight-loss drugs and facial surgery.

The actress wrote on Instagram how "stressful" it was waking up to people "talking about you and your body in ways that you can't even comprehend".

"I have never been on Ozempic. I have never done any face surgery," she wrote. "And don't ever say that I didn't make my own nose, I've always had that beautiful ski slope. Even as a baby!"

She added: "For the first time in my entire career, I have had the opportunity to be rested, to sleep, to wake up and work out and to eat regularly and healthily."

In her eight-page statement, the Oppenheimer, Dune and East of Eden star told followers: "I'm looking after myself, and that's literally it."

Pugh highlighted that losing weight had not been her goal, and she had lost "two pounds".

"The thing that's most important to me and my trainer is muscle," she said. "I think it's the most important thing a woman can do. Build muscle and be strong. I can now do six pull-ups and lift 110kg.

"Which I'm sure to many of you isn't a lot... but to me?! It's everything."

The actress said that from the moment she entered the acting industry she was "subjected to fat shaming".

"I have loved every time I get to tell someone they are being inappropriate about my weight, and I'm not changing that now just because you've all said so."

She also said she was "disturbed" that "women who now think that I'm someone I'm not, for having my body dissected and pointed at again".

"I have spent my whole career defending and protecting bodies that just grow the way they grow."

Professor Heather Widdows from Everyday Lookism, which campaigns to end body shaming, told BBC News: "Pugh is right that we are in a place where 'beauty standards are changing, getting harder and harder to keep up with'.

"A global beauty ideal makes impossible demands and normalises what were once extreme," added Widdows, who is Professor of Philosophy at the University of Warwick.

"We know that body-shaming individuals is harmful. Given this, we should not say negative things about other people's bodies... in a world where our bodies are increasingly ourselves, when you shame bodies you shame people."

Pugh, who was Oscar-nominated for Little Women in 2020 and played Black Widow assassin Yelena Belova in this year's Spider-Man: Brand New Day, has previously spoken out against judgement about women's looks.

In 2024, she told the Times she aimed to "challenge how women were perceived".

"Look, not everybody has legs that go on for days. I remember watching this industry and feeling that I wasn't represented," she said. "I've loved challenging ideas I don't like."

She also spoke about why she thinks she receives criticism about her appearance.

"I've always been aware that seeing a confident woman be happy in her skin is triggering to a lot of people and for a long time just accepted that I will receive commentary like that," she said.

"But now this...? I'm asking us all to just be mindful. We are real people, living real lives."

Pugh did not have comments turned on for her post, but it was liked by celebrities including Ashley James, who is known for speaking out about body positivity, plus Carol Vorderman, Zoe Ball, writer and actress Katy Brand and doctor and broadcaster Amir Khan.

'Please be gentle'

Pugh also said she had sued several publications earlier this year, claiming they published AI-generated images appearing to show her receiving cosmetic injections that were presented as real photographs.

"I was... horrified," she wrote, claiming the articles had damaged her reputation.

The actress concluded by urging followers to think about their attitudes towards physical appearance.

"I understand that we are in a warped part of image history right now, with beauty standards changing, getting harder and harder to keep up with," she said.

"But please be gentle to yourselves and others when you go in on people's physical appearance.

"I'm not angry at you, I'm angry at the situation that's being projected onto us.

"It feels inescapable, but part of changing the tide is on you as much as us in the public eye."
