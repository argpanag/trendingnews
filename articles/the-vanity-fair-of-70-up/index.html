The vanity fair of 70 Up

TheTools · 16.09.2026 05:00 · source · permalink

The series presents a Britain riven by the gulleys of class

By Nicholas Harris

The wise travelling vagrant, the adulterous East End cockney bounder and the prize posh shit – can the mess of life truly produce such clear, predictable, Thackerayan archetypes? Or do we project them on to real people, squinting the three-dimensional down to two? This was the question that I found myself asking after watching 70 Up, the final instalment of the celebrated television chronicle of everyday British life.

The original series, Seven Up!, covered the lives of 14 seven-year-olds in 1964, long before “reality TV”. It was a novelty to film normal life, and to what I now think of as my shame, I had never seen the programme before. I was grateful therefore that 70 Up, which is being marketed as the last in the series, has a retrospective, summative quality, scrapbooking through the previous films – each made seven years apart – and forming a series of microbiographies, with the whole sequence then forming a masterpiece of microhistory.
