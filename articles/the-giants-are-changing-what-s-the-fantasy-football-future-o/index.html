The Giants are changing. What’s the fantasy football future of Jaxson Dart?

TheTools · 13.09.2026 23:56 · source · permalink

Head coach John Harbaugh is loading Jaxson Dart's plate with additional responsibilities. Rich Storry / Getty Images

Jaxson Dart contains multitudes. He offers immense upside and hilarious downside. Dart was certainly responsible for several of the wildest individual plays of the 2025 season. And yes, the previous sentence is intended as both complimentary and derisive.

For fantasy purposes, of course, Dart was a party last year. His bouts of inaccuracy and carelessness hardly mattered. He was mostly terrible in the Denver loss, for example — 15-for-33, one interception, four sacks — yet still delivered 28.4 fantasy points. It wasn’t always a clinic with Dart as a rookie, but he paid the fantasy bills when healthy.

This season, however, he’s expected to be more than a frenetic wind-up toy. Dart is now the face of a century-old franchise coached by a Harbaugh. Things have gotten serious.

New York has assembled a Traveling Wilburys-style offensive coaching staff loaded with familiar names — Matt Nagy, Greg Roman and Brian Callahan among them. As The Athletic’s Dan Duggan explained this week, the new staff is challenging Dart to operate in unfamiliar ways and do so with great precision.

“The Giants, who made signing 300-pound fullback Pat Ricard from Baltimore a priority in free agency, are planning to deploy a power rushing attack. The quarterback needs to operate from under center to facilitate that. In turn, the play-action passing game should be opened up,” wrote Duggan.

“Dart’s rookie season rarely featured under center play action. Instead, Dart ran the offense while surveying the defense from the shotgun. Under center play action requires the quarterback to turn his back to the defense, which makes reading the defense post-snap more challenging. There’s also a greater emphasis on Dart’s footwork in this system, which must be synced up with the timing of the receivers’ routes,” he added.

If this all seems like a radical change for a guy who was directing a Lane Kiffin offense just two years ago … well, yeah. It’s a big ask.

Additionally, the Giants are doing all they can to help Dart cultivate a sense of self-preservation, which may or may not stick. As a runner, Dart seems hardwired to seek contact. Hopefully that’s more bug than feature. Running back Cam Skattebo’s job is to run like a proton in a particle accelerator, but Dart’s certainly is not. We welcome the rushing stats in fantasy, although not at the risk of violent contact and injury.

New York opens the season at home on Sunday night in primetime against the Cowboys, a huge stage for the debut of Dart 2.0. Several of the most pressing questions in fantasy football should be answered in that game. New York’s star wide receiver Malik Nabers is expected (though not guaranteed) to return to face Dallas following last season’s ACL and meniscus injuries. He and Dart only shared the field for one quarter last year, connecting on two receptions. When Nabers was sidelined, the Giants had a conspicuous lack of offensive game-wreckers, and Dart was routinely out-gunned.

This year, things should be different. Let’s hope all those new coaches have begun to address Dart’s flaws while preserving the best parts of his game. He’s an extreme range-of-outcomes player, but the upside is clear. If Dart can level up and Nabers is anything like the pre-injury version of himself, New York’s offense might just be a fantasy carnival.

Kaelon Black was curiously involved in the 49ers run game

This offseason, like every previous offseason, Christian McCaffrey’s head coach Kyle Shanahan spoke openly about finding ways to reduce the lead back’s rushing workload and preserve him for the weeks that matter most. It’s possible that 2026 will be the year a team actually achieves this goal.

Indiana rookie Kaelon Black handled 14 carries in the Niners’ season-opening win over the Rams, rushing for 65 yards. He was involved on the first series for San Francisco and started the second, so this was not a case of a backup piling up stats late in a game. McCaffrey and Black were used in tandem, splitting the snaps 36 to 28. CMC still ran more routes, as you’d expect, and he dominated the backfield targets, catching five balls on eight chances. McCaffrey was on the field in goal-to-go situations, too, so it’s not as if his usage was deeply problematic. We’ll accept 88 total yards on 15 touches in a game during which he looked awesome.

McCaffrey seemed pleased with the backfield arrangement in postgame comments, as he should be. If the Niners want to launch a rookie back into an opposing defensive front a few times each game, sparing CMC the wear and tear, that’s probably a good thing. Black absorbed a few huge collisions on Thursday night. He’s a good player, but obviously not in McCaffrey’s class as a multi-purpose threat.

If you only drafted CMC this year for his 400-touch potential, then you’re probably a little panicky right now. Let’s just please remember that he controls the highest-value touches; he’s coming off a season in which he caught 102 passes. If McCaffrey remains healthy over 17 games and he’s somehow still your biggest fantasy problem, it’s gonna be a great year.

But also: We’re adding Black everywhere we can.

Jadarian Price is both a revelation and a problem

Reactions to Jadarian Price’s regular season NFL debut ranged from elation to outright panic within the fantasy community. On the one hand, it seems inarguably true that Price is the most explosive ball-carrier on Seattle’s roster. His first touch against New England was an eye-opener:

Vision, change of direction, after-contact ability — the rookie showed us everything we like to see.

What Price did not show us, however, is any role whatsoever on third-down or near the goal-line. He played 24 offensive snaps against the Patriots on Wednesday and carried the ball 10 times, working exclusively on first and second downs. George Holani essentially filled the Zach Charbonnet role in the opener while playing 23 snaps.

If you’re a pessimistic manager with Price on your roster, you probably regard the present situation as settled, permanent and nightmarish. Generally speaking, a running back needs to have either a passing-down or goal-line role to remain consistently fantasy relevant. But a more optimistic manager might reasonably be pleased with the fact that Price passed the eye-test in every possible way. He’s a serious talent who is absolutely capable of earning additional touches in a thin backfield.

Over the next two weeks, Seattle faces Arizona and Washington, a pair of matchups in which game flow will likely favor the role currently occupied by Price. We should feel bullish about him in the near and long-term. Let’s not eliminate him from the flex plans just yet. No one expects the Seahawks to pivot away from the ground game while Drew Lock is directing the offense.

Brock Bowers, the consensus TE1, will miss opening week

In the rich and cherished tradition of NFL coaches lying to us in utterly pointless ways, Klint Kubiak may have just set a new standard.

Early on Wednesday afternoon, when asked specifically about the status of Brock Bowers, the Raiders head coach declared, “We’re expecting to have all our guys ready for Sunday.”

Approximately two hours later, Bowers was a surprising no-show at practice. Within minutes, the world learned that he had actually undergone “a meniscus trim” on Tuesday and is expected to miss perhaps two games. He will not, in fact, be ready for Sunday.

Just an all-time instance of meaningless coach misdirection by Kubiak — among the most senseless and silly we have yet seen. If it wasn’t so flagrantly disrespectful to everyone who follows the team, I’d admire it. We need to file away the fact that Kubiak cannot be trusted, not ever. Practice reports contain a hint of truth, but Kubiak does not.

As for replacing Bowers … well, you can’t. Not fully. Sorry. He’s one of just two NFL tight ends who’s likely to see double-digit targets in any given week. Guys like that aren’t sitting out on the waiver wire. Michael Mayer is the direct replacement for Las Vegas, and he’s certainly an acceptable fantasy option. Mayer caught 18 passes on 24 targets last season in the four games in which he was active and Bowers was sidelined. As a one- or two-week placeholder, you (and Vegas) can do worse.

Justin Jefferson gets a subtle value bump

It may not have seemed like a meaningful fantasy event when we learned that Carson Wentz, not J.J. McCarthy, would serve as Kyler Murray’s backup in opening week. It’s not actionable, certainly. No one is gonna tell you to go add Wentz.

But it’s worth noting that Minnesota’s featured receivers were thriving last season with Wentz at the controls, even if the team’s offense was somewhat weird and un-fun. Justin Jefferson averaged 6.8 receptions and 95.4 yards per game in Wentz’s five starts while Jordan Addison averaged 5.3 and 77.3. Neither player was considered a fantasy problem until the calendar flipped to November, at which point McCarthy fully faceplanted.

So if you were fretting about having selected Jefferson — arguably the best wide receiver in the game — in the first round of your fantasy draft, today you can feel just a bit better about his floor. He will not be sabotaged again, at least not in the same manner as last year.

Chris Bell, Caleb Douglas to start for Miami

Dolphins head coach Jeff Hafley confirmed Wednesday that a pair of third-round rookies, Chris Bell and Caleb Douglas, will start in Miami’s opening week matchup against the Raiders. Douglas was a buzzy name throughout the summer, and he made his first “wow” play on his first preseason series:

He pairs well with Douglas, at least in theory. We love to see a rebuilding team actually toss young skill players into the fray. The Dolphins have a chance to be sneaky-fun in fantasy terms, particularly if the defense is a sieve.
