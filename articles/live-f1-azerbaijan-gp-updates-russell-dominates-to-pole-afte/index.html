Qualifying report

F1 Azerbaijan GP: Russell dominates to pole after Antonelli crashes out in qualifying

George Russell takes his fifth pole of 2026 at Formula 1's Azerbaijan Grand Prix while championship leader Kimi Antonelli won't start higher than 16th after hitting the wall

Oscar Piastri, third: "Yeah it was good, in qualifying we found a bit of pace. It's not the first time it's happened to us this year, we seem to be on the back foot and then come out in qualifying and find that bit extra. I was surprised to see us 1-2 after the first lap of Q3, but we had no hope against George.

"It's never easy here, especially when it's so gusty, but happy to be back in the top three. Trying to keep it between the lines isn't easy, it always sounds dumb talking about wind with an F1 car but when it's a bit more open and you get more of a gust, it's not easy at all."

Charles Leclerc, second: "Honestly it was quite a good lap, Lewis was a few seconds in front and managed to do a good lap at the end. But when I see the gap to George maybe the lap wasn't good enough! Overall we've been on the back foot all weekend, we expected to be very far off by the end of the Friday, but we had more hope this morning.

"We'll see tomorrow, our straightline speed is not our strength but the pace has been quite strong in FP1 for the high fuel so hopefully that's the case again tomorrow."

George Russell, pole for the Azerbaijan GP: "Yeah, it's been a while, so it definitely feels good. That's probably the biggest margin of my whole career, even in the F2/F3 days. So the lap felt really sweet, on a track like this when you can hug it up [to the wall] like that, it's amazing.

"I was feeling good. I think Max's been very fast this weekend, I don't know what happened to him, maybe didn't get that last chance. But it was the perfect lap at the end, got a little slipstream from Hadjar which got me another tenth or two. "

"Just over 0.8s to Leclerc," Russell is told. "Woah!" is the response. 

Russell, Leclerc, Piastri, and Hadjar make up the top four, all outqualifying their team-mates. 

Antonelli, meanwhile, has quite a bit of work to do...

Hamilton moves up to sixth, with Leclerc moving into second - but 0.837s behind Russell. Piastri then goes third, behind Leclerc by just 0.001s.

Verstappen can't get any higher than eighth as he seemed to encounter the yellow flag, while Norris stays fifth despite going deep at Turn 3. 

Pole is Russell's, by 0.837s.

Hadjar puts together a good first sector, but Russell's just absolutely on it at this stage and is finding even more time. 

Norris has stopped at Turn 3, which means he's not going to improve.

Hadjar goes up to third, while Russell finds a 1m42.526s! 0.9s up on everyone else as it stands! What do the rest have?

Hadjar posts a 1m43.703s to go third, but Russell vaults to the top with a 1m43.037s to find over half a second over the McLarens. Big lap. 

Colapinto has an off at Turn 15, while Leclerc moves up to second. Gasly gets up to sixth, ahead of Hamilton. 

Hadjar's starting his lap now, and we'll have the Ferraris and Alpines follow suit in due course. 

The Frenchman was up in the first sector, but Russell's finding time too. 

Russell kicks us off with a 1m43.713s. There's the benchmark, but Norris is going well.

Verstappen posts a 1m44.487s, as Norris does a 1m43.672s to go top...but Piastri then finds a 1m43.571s!

So Russell, Verstappen, Piastri, Norris, Sainz, and Hadjar have gone out, while the Ferraris and Alpines have stayed behind for now. 

"Out, warm-up, push, and then quick turnaround?" Norris asks. Will Joseph, is engineer, says "Yes. Or maybe that's what we want people to believe..."

Incredible 4D chess here from McLaren. I don't know what to think...
