Mary Trump just explained why her uncle actually enjoys Natalie Harp's constant presence, and her theory is brutal. Donald Trump's niece broke down the psychology behind the president's relationship with his 35-year-old personal aide in a new podcast interview.

Mary Trump says Harp's unwavering attention helps explain her constant presence around Donald Trump

Mary Trump told The Daily Beast Podcast that Natalie Harp's unwavering devotion explains why Donald Trump keeps her close. She described the 35-year-old personal aide as someone with "the qualities of a stalker." However, she noted her 80-year-old uncle welcomes the attention rather than pushing it away.

Mary argued that all of Trump's relationships are purely transactional. "It should become obvious to him that, you know, his children don't care about him any more than he cares about them," she said. She added that "those relationships are determined purely by who's getting what of whom."

Mary also pointed to cracks within Trump's inner circle, citing former Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt's departure. She called Leavitt "one of the most loyal, sycophantic, and disgraceful propagandists in the administration." Yet even Leavitt had limits after the Air Force One incident, Mary explained. Leavitt resigned in August and later joined Fox News as a contributor.

Mary distinguished Harp from other Trump loyalists by saying something is "psychologically wrong with her." She referenced personal letters Harp wrote to Trump in 2023. In those letters, Harp called Trump her "Guardian and Protector" and expressed fear of letting him down. Mary called it "the only case I've ever seen in which the stalking victim has welcomed being stalked."

She attributed Trump's acceptance of Harp to a deeper emotional void. "Donald is not getting the attention he needs, because nobody cares about him," she said. Mary added that Harp "is willing to fill that role." Harp has rarely left Trump's side in recent months. She was among three aides on the president's secret military jet leaving Turkey in July. A September Daily Mail/J.L. Partners poll found 42 percent of respondents wanted more answers about Harp's White House role.
