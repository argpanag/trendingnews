UP Weather Tomorrow: Will Rain Ease Across Uttar Pradesh on September 29? Check IMD’s District-Wise Forecast & Latest Update

TheTools · 28.09.2026 15:26 · source · permalink

UP Weather Tomorrow: It is anticipated that Uttar Pradesh will experience an easing of the recent rainy spell on Tuesday, September 29, as there is projected to be relatively less rainfall over the state. The latest weather forecast of the India Meteorological Department (IMD) does not have warnings for both the regions of East Uttar Pradesh and West Uttar Pradesh. Below is what every student and parent must know about the weather and school status tomorrow.

UP Weather Tomorrow: Will there be heavy rainfall on September 29?

The latest IMD warning bulletin does not indicate a heavy-rain warning for either East or West Uttar Pradesh on September 29. This marks a change from the previous few days, when parts of the state were affected by heavy to very heavy rainfall and thunderstorms.

Does no warning mean that Uttar Pradesh will remain completely dry?

No, a “no warning” classification does not necessarily mean that every part of the state will remain dry. City-level forecasts can still indicate cloudy conditions or isolated light rain, even when no severe-weather warning is in force. The latest forecasts for several major UP cities, however, point towards generally partly cloudy conditions on September 29.

What weather is expected in Lucknow tomorrow?

Lucknow is expected to experience partly cloudy skies, with temperatures around 23°C to 32°C on September 29. The IMD’s city forecast lists no warning for the day, suggesting that the intense weather conditions seen earlier are not expected to continue at warning-level intensity.

What will the weather be like in Agra on September 29?

Agra is forecast to remain partly cloudy, with the minimum temperature around 24°C and the maximum around 34°C. The IMD forecast also carries no warning for the city on September 29.

Will Bareilly receive rain tomorrow?

Bareilly is forecast to see mainly clear skies on September 29, with a minimum temperature of about 22°C and a maximum of around 32.6°C. The IMD forecast shows no warning for the day.

What weather can people in Varanasi expect tomorrow?

Varanasi is likely to see partly cloudy skies, with temperatures expected to remain around 25°C to 32°C. The IMD’s latest city forecast also shows no warning for September 29.

What about Prayagraj and nearby areas?

Prayagraj is forecast to have partly cloudy skies, with a minimum temperature of about 25°C and a maximum of around 33°C on September 29. The IMD forecast lists no warning for the city.

Which parts of Uttar Pradesh experienced heavy rainfall in the last few days?

Several parts of Uttar Pradesh recorded significant rainfall during the recent spell. IMD observations for September 27, for example, recorded 164.7 mm at Bareilly, 60.2 mm at Azamgarh, 68 mm at Bijnor-Najibabad, 46.4 mm at Aligarh, 45.4 mm at Bahraich and 29 mm at Ayodhya.

Why did Uttar Pradesh receive heavy rain during the previous spell?

According to the IMD, a depression over northeast Madhya Pradesh and adjoining north Chhattisgarh contributed to heavy to very heavy rainfall over parts of the region. The IMD had forecast heavy to very heavy rainfall over parts of Uttar Pradesh during the September 26–27 period.

Is the weather expected to improve further after September 29?

The latest IMD subdivision-wise outlook indicates no warning for East or West Uttar Pradesh from September 30 through October 4. City forecasts for Lucknow, Agra and Varanasi also show partly cloudy or increasingly clear conditions during the following days, indicating a generally quieter weather pattern compared with the previous spell.

What should people in Uttar Pradesh expect tomorrow?

People across Uttar Pradesh can expect a comparatively calmer weather day on September 29, with partly cloudy or clearer conditions in several major cities and no IMD warning currently issued for either East or West Uttar Pradesh. However, local weather can change, so residents should continue checking the latest IMD updates, particularly if travelling or living in areas that recently experienced waterlogging or heavy rainfall.
