Blackhawks name Connor Bedard captain; 21-year-old is 37th ‘C’ in Chicago history

TheTools · 15.09.2026 14:42 · source · permalink

NHL Offseason

Connor Bedard took on a larger leadership role last season in a room full of young players. Michael Reaves / Getty Images

Connor Bedard has meant so much to the Chicago Blackhawks in such a short time.

From on the ice to off it, the 21-year-old Bedard has become the face and voice of the Blackhawks in a lot of ways over his first three seasons.

Now, he’s also become their team captain.

The Blackhawks named Bedard their 37th captain in franchise history on Tuesday.

“Connor perfectly embodies what it means to be a Blackhawk,” Blackhawks general manager Kyle Davidson said in a news release. “From the moment he entered the league, Connor has led by example and has set a remarkable standard for himself while demonstrating his ability to make the players around him better. He has earned the respect of his teammates and the entire organization through the way he prepares, competes and conducts himself every single day, and there is no doubt that he is ready to take on this tremendous responsibility. We’re proud to have him lead this storied franchise and excited for him to shape the next chapter of Blackhawks hockey.”

The idea that Bedard would eventually become team captain has been around since the Blackhawks drafted him No. 1 in 2023. It became more realistic heading into this coming season after Chicago dealt Nick Foligno, the franchise’s last captain, to the Minnesota Wild at last season’s trade deadline. Over the summer, Bedard solidified his future with the organization by signing a five-year, $75 million contract extension on July 20.

“Being named captain of the Blackhawks is an unbelievable honor and something I’m truly humbled by,” Bedard said in a release. “The captains who have come before me have left an incredible legacy. To now have the opportunity to carry that tradition forward is something I don’t take lightly. I’m grateful to the organization and my teammates for putting their trust in me to lead this team. We’re building something really special here, and I’m committed to making the entire Blackhawks organization and our fans proud.”

Bedard had been asked multiple times since the end of last season what being named team captain would mean to him.

“Just getting to play for the Blackhawks organization in general without any of that is the best thing in the world,” Bedard said in July. “If I was able to become a captain, or anyone to become a captain, if it’s someone else, I’d be just as happy for them. If I was able to get that honor, it would be so special and something I know I wouldn’t take for granted for a second. But we have so many guys in the room who can lead. If I was chosen for that, it would be unbelievable, for sure.”

Bedard took on a larger leadership role last season in a room full of young players. Blackhawks coach Jeff Blashill recognized Bedard’s growing role and made him an alternate captain late in the season.

The Blackhawks do have a somewhat recent history of naming young captains. They made Jonathan Toews their youngest captain ever in 2008. He was 20 years old and had played just one NHL season.

Toews was the Blackhawks captain from the 2008-2009 season through the 2022-23 season. The Blackhawks decided to keep the captaincy vacant for the 2023-24 season out of respect for Toews, then made Foligno captain for the 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons. Foligno was traded to the Minnesota Wild in March.

Bedard will likely have to wait to don his new letter. After an injury in training in British Columbia, he had surgery to repair his left shoulder in early July and was expected to miss about four months. That timetable would delay his season debut until closer to November. He has been training on the ice in recent weeks at the Blackhawks practice facility as he continues to rehab his shoulder.
