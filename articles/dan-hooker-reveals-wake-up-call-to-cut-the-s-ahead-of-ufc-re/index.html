Dan Hooker reveals 'wake-up call to cut the s***' ahead of UFC return in Paris

TheTools · 05.09.2026 13:51 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Dan Hooker has learnt that with success come distractions.

As the Kiwi UFC lightweight returns to action in Paris this weekend against rising French star Salahdine Parnasse, he does so feeling as though he has rediscovered the competitor who became a mainstay in the division’s top 10.

The past couple of years have been eventful for the 36-year-old, with injuries limiting his opportunities to compete inside the octagon, and a busy life outside.

And after some honest reflections following his loss to France’s Benoit Saint-Denis in Sydney in February, Hooker was made to see the error of his ways.

“With success, you get busy, you know what I mean? And I had a lot of things going on. I was doing the One Minute Scraps at the same time – I think I ran one like a week or two weeks out from that fight – never really taking a rest and giving my UFC career and my opponents the respect they deserve,” he told the Herald.

“The guys that I’m fighting at this calibre deserve my whole focus and deserve my complete concentration. So that’s what Salahdine’s got, and that’s going to be the difference in this fight.

“I’ve been locked in for the last five or six months, and I’ve been training, and I’ve been putting in the work because we identified that I was, outside of fight camp, not putting in the level of commitment required to succeed at this level of competition.

“I’ve gone away and, man, you just have to humble yourself and get in the gym and listen to your coaches. I feel like that’s the big change here, that I’ve ticked the boxes that I needed to tick.”

Hooker’s last UFC win was in 2024, returning from an injury layoff to claim a split-decision victory over Mateusz Gamrot.

It was more than a year before he made his next appearance, but two losses in three months forced him and his team to evaluate things.

“It is easy to get distracted, and I did get distracted,” he said.

“Having four surgeries and a lot of time off my normal routine, which for the previous 10 years was just eat, sleep, train, eat, sleep, train. But having that time off, [I] let some bad habits slip into place.

“But nothing’s more of a wake-up call in this game than getting your ass kicked, and that BSD [Saint-Denis] fight especially was a big wake-up call to cut the s*** and to refocus.”

In Paris, he gets a chance to show an improved focus against Parnasse, who joins the UFC as a highly touted prospect after his success on the European scene.

Parnasse, 28, joins the promotion with a 23-2 professional record, with eight wins by knockout, seven by submission and eight by decision. A long-time featherweight (65.7kg) who has also fought at welterweight (77kg), his two losses came in those divisions, and he is yet to be bested in the 70kg division.

Hooker is also a former featherweight now fighting at lightweight. He has a 24-14 professional MMA record with 11 wins by knockout, seven by submission and six by decision. He has a 14-10 UFC record since joining the promotion in 2014.

This isn’t the first time Hooker has been the man called upon by the UFC to test the level of its new signing, and he is happy to be that measuring stick.

“There’s obviously some risk involved that a lot of other lightweights in the division don’t want to take. It’s a safer bet fighting other guys that are already in the rankings because then, even if you lose, you only move down a couple of spots.

“But where’s the fun in that? There’s no fun. We didn’t get this far by playing it safe. So, yeah, we’re taking a bit of risk. The UFC’s obviously fronted up with a bit of danger money, but man, it’s a big fight, a main event. I’ve been looking forward to going five rounds again, so I’m more than happy to oblige.

“This is something that I’ve always done, and the UFC, they take care of me quite well because I am a guy that steps up, and they do like to reward that.

“You’ll take the fights that people don’t want to fight. I’ve taken guys coming into the UFC in the past, taken fights on short notice, and that’s how you get rewarded by the UFC. I feel like I’ve probably made a lot more money than a lot of people that have held UFC titles because I do take those risks and the short-notice [fights] and step up to the plate.

“You can’t play it safe forever. We don’t play the game on safe mode.”
