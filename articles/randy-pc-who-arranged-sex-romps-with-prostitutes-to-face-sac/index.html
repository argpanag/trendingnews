Randy PC who 'arranged sex romps with prostitutes' to face sack hearing - AOL

TheTools · 08.09.2026 10:53 · source · permalink

A police officer accused of romping with a prostitute is set to face a misconduct hearing this week that could result in him being sacked from his force. Police Constable Oliver Hawley is alleged to have "used the services of a sex worker in exchange for payment" on two separate dates over a 10-day period in October 2025. During the same period, PC Hawley is also accused of contacting a sex worker to "make arrangements to meet and have sexual relations in exchange for payment" on three dates.

A document published by South Yorkshire Police, announcing the hearing, states that the alleged conduct - should it be proven - would amount to a "breach of the standards of professional behaviour in respect of discreditable conduct and authority, respect and courtesy".

"The matters set out above are breaches of the standards which are so serious as to justify dismissal and therefore constitute gross misconduct," the document adds.

Earlier this year a fellow South Yorkshire officer was sacked after she admitted being attracted to a convicted criminal's "bad boy image" - despite being handed a direct order to stay away from him.

PC Sarah Bromfield, who worked within South Yorkshire Police's own Professional Standards Department, was dismissed without notice after a misconduct hearing found she had breached a lawful order not to associate with the man, who was not named for legal reasons.

The hearing, held in Sheffield on July 23, was told the officer had been in a relationship with the man - who has criminal convictions - and was seen with him at Manchester Crown Court on March 9, 2026, more than a year after she was formally ordered to cut contact.

Chief constable Poultney, who chaired the hearing, ruled the force had no choice but to dismiss her, adding she would also be added to the College of Policing barred list.

The relationship first came to light in December 2023, when PC Bromfield submitted a report saying she was on "nodding terms" with the man. By October 2024, she had updated bosses to say the relationship had turned intimate following his release from prison.

At a risk mitigation meeting that same month, she was given a domestic violence disclosure about the man and indicated she needed to consider her "exit plan". Days later, at a further meeting, she told bosses the relationship must end - and admitted she was attracted to his "bad boy image", while he was drawn to the fact she was a police officer.

She was warned her vetting was at risk and told to have no further contact with him.

In November 2024, PC Bromfield was arrested, with one of her bail conditions barring contact with the man. She stuck to that while the condition was in place - but resumed the relationship once it lapsed.

On February 27, 2025, the chief constable of South Yorkshire Police authorised a lawful order banning any association with the man. She was notified by email on March 3 and acknowledged it on March 10.

Despite this, she continued the relationship in secret, becoming engaged to him in August 2025 - something she never disclosed to the force.

She was then spotted with him at Manchester Crown Court in March this year, in direct breach of the order.

In her defence, PC Bromfield argued the relationship had by then become long-term and committed, and that the order breached her human rights. But the hearing was told she herself had previously described it as "an autumn fling" at the time the warnings were first issued.

Chief constable Poultney rejected her arguments, ruling the order was "a necessary and proportionate interference" given the risks to force integrity, confidentiality and public confidence.

He said her culpability was increased "by virtue of her long-service, and in particular her role within the professional standards department", adding she would have been "acutely aware of the risks of her actions" but continued regardless.
