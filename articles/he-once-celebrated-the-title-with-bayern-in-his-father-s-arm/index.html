He once celebrated the title with Bayern in his father's arms, now he can debut against Germany: The extraordinary story of Ruben van Bommel

TheTools · 24.09.2026 07:02 · source · permalink

He may have inherited his father's curls and temperament, but Ruben van Bommel is a very different player to Bayern Munich legend Mark van Bommel. After a few tough knocks throughout his career, the 22-year-old is on the verge of making his debut for the Netherlands national team.

In May 2010, Bayern Munich had won the German championship title again and the players went out to the balcony of Munich's city hall, in Marienplatz, to celebrate the success.

It was a celebration that is still remembered today. Arjen Robben wore his signature hat, while coach Louis van Gaal gave his famous speech, during which he asked the fans: "Who has the best defense?", not forgetting to send a kiss to all the mothers.

But who would have thought that one of the people on the balcony that day would be part of Thursday's match between the Netherlands and Germany in the Nations League?

This is not about a sudden return of Thomas Müller to the German national team.

The answer is Ruben van Bommel, the son of then-Bayern captain Mark van Bommel.

Photos from that party show the former Dutch midfielder holding Ruben, then just five years old, and his two other children, Thomas and René, on the balcony of the town hall.

Today, Ruben is 22 years old and plays as a left winger for PSV Eindhoven. The new Netherlands coach, Xavi Hernández, has called him up to the national team for the first time for upcoming international matches.

This constitutes the greatest achievement so far in his young career, which has not been easy at all.

When Mark van Bommel ended his successful career as a professional footballer at PSV in 2013, Ruben, then eight years old, joined the club's academy.

In 2018, his father took charge of the first team, but was fired at the end of 2019.

Just a few months later, Ruben, now 15 years old, was also forced to leave the club.

Although he was considered a promising talent, academy directors had doubts about his physical development. He was too short and frail for the demands of professional football.

The next step was to move to the academy of MVV Maastricht, a club that competed in the Dutch second division and where his brother, Thomas, had also played.

Their grandfather, Bert van Marwijk, the former coach of the Dutch national team, also played for this club during the 1970s and 1980s.

"The move from PSV to MVV is a big step backwards in every respect. Some talents are completely destroyed after such disappointments," his former PSV academy coach, Edwin de Wijs, later declared.

However, he emphasized that Ruben had reacted differently.

"He turned that blow into strength and motivation."

The footballer himself admitted that leaving Eindhoven had done him good.

"When I look back now, leaving the PSV academy was a good thing for me," he declared.

In 2022, Ruben made his professional debut at Maastricht, playing alongside his brother, Thomas.

By then, the length problem had completely disappeared.

From the 1.66 meters he had been when he left PSV, he had reached 1.92 meters.

While his father had built his career as a defensive midfielder, Ruben had chosen a completely different role: the left wing of attack.

In his first season as a professional, Van Bommel directly contributed to 20 goals for Maastricht.

His dribbling skills and intelligence to exploit space led former footballer and television analyst Hans Kraay Jr. to compare him to Argentine star Ángel Di María.

In 2023, he moved to AZ Alkmaar, and two years later he realized his childhood dream.

PSV Eindhoven brought him back for 16 million euros.

"I've always dreamed of this," Van Bommel declared after his return.

The return to PSV started promisingly. In the first eight games, Ruben registered four goal contributions.

But in the big match against Ajax, he suffered a serious injury, tearing the cruciate ligament in his knee.

The injury kept him away from the field for a long time, and his return came only during summer preparations.

However, the striker did not lose his place. He immediately regained his starting role on the left wing and has convinced with his performances at the start of the new season.

But the curly-haired footballer also caused a major controversy with an intervention that reminded many of his father's temperament.

Mark van Bommel was known throughout his career with the nickname "Aggressive Leader", while Ruben seems to have inherited part of this character.

In early September, during the match against Ajax, he clashed violently with opponent Aaron Bouwman in an aerial duel, causing clashes and tensions between players from both teams.

The scene was even compared to German goalkeeper Toni Schumacher's infamous tackle on Frenchman Patrick Battiston in the 1982 World Cup semi-final.

To the surprise of many, Van Bommel was only punished with a yellow card.

Bouwman suffered chest and lung injuries, and the incident sparked widespread criticism of the PSV striker and the referee's decision.

Also present at that match was Ajax's German goalkeeper, Marc-André ter Stegen, who will face Van Bommel again in Thursday's duel between the Netherlands and Germany.

Three generations of the Van Bommel family in the Dutch national team

If Ruben makes his debut for the Netherlands, he will become the third family member to reach the national team, after his father and grandfather.

Mark van Bommel played 79 games for the Netherlands and was part of the team that reached the 2010 World Cup final, while Bert van Marwijk was the coach who led that team to the final.

However, Ruben's father will not be able to be present to closely follow this special moment. The reason is related to his commitments as a coach.

After leaving PSV, Mark van Bommel managed Wolfsburg and Royal Antwerp, before taking charge of the Belgium national team this summer.

He will make his debut on Friday, September 25, on the Belgian bench in the away match against Italy.

So, while Ruben can write the first chapter of his career with the Dutch national team, his father will start a new chapter in his career as a coach.

From the five-year-old child who celebrated Bayern's title on the balcony of Munich's city hall, Ruben van Bommel now has the opportunity to take the field against Germany in the orange jersey of the Netherlands. /GazetaExpress/
