'Trust your gut': parent glad she removed toddler from now-closed childcare

TheTools · 25.09.2026 07:00 · source · permalink

Your digital subscription includes access to content from all our websites in your region. Access unlimited content and the digital versions of our print editions - Today's Paper.

A parent who previously used a now-shut childcare centre said she was glad she recently removed her son from the service.

Kidscape Early Learning Centre Albury was immediately closed and suspended for six months by the NSW Early Learning Commission on Wednesday, September 23.

The commission confirmed the immediate closure was a result of multiple visits and compliance actions related to child safety.

Kidscape Early Learning Albury has been contacted for comment.

While it has left families in the lurch to find alternative childcare, a working mum said she was relieved to have "followed her gut" and removed her toddler from the centre just a few weeks ago.

"I'm so glad I pulled him out when I did," she told The Border Mail.

Her two-year-old son began attending the East Albury centre in February 2026 and she said she trusted her "mum instinct" and made the decision to find a new childcare centre.

"I was following my gut but also listening to my son's behaviour, he was never really settling when I would leave him, he would really cry and cling to me," she said.

"But also coming home with bruises or bitemarks and the educators not telling me about it."

The mum said she "wasn't surprised" to hear the service had been closed by the regulator.

"My son is actually anaphylactic and has an EpiPen and on his last day I went in and asked for his medication and the educators just looked at each other and said 'where's his EpiPen?'," she said.

"Still to this day it makes me super angry that if my son were to have a reaction and need his EpiPen what was going to happen?"

NSW Early Learning Commissioner Daryl Currie confirmed inadequate medication management was a key concern that led to the centre's immediate closure.

"Medication for young children is really important and they of course cannot manage that themselves so parents are entrusting the professionals in those services to ensure that their children are protected from harm," he said.

"The safe storage and access to an EpiPen, in terms of risk, that is life-threatening."

The service's approval was suspended by the commission for six months after an unannounced visit on Wednesday, September 23.

If Kidscape Early Learning Centre Albury works to meet the expectations of the commission the suspension could be shortened, but it could also be lengthened or approval cancelled altogether if it cannot meet the standards expected.

"An unsafe service is not one we want operating in NSW," Mr Currie said.

Mr Currie said it was important for parents to trust their judgement and raise concerns about their child's care if needed.

"If parents do have an unsettling feeling about what's happening they should feel comfortable raising questions with the service if something doesn't feel right," he said.

Kidscape Early Learning Centre Albury had garnered the attention of the commission by having received nine compliance notices in the past two years and consistently ranking as "working towards" national standards.

"It is frustrating when there is a service that is working towards [the national quality standards] and despite supports, guidance and interventions they continue not to meet the standards that's expected not only by the commission but by the general public and families that use the service," Mr Currie said.
