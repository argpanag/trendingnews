Knicks Prepare Defensive Plan to Contain Giannis Antetokounmpo

TheTools · 27.11.2026 23:14 · source · permalink

New York needs a coordinated transition defense to contain Giannis Antetokounmpo when the Knicks face the Miami Heat, with the challenge centered on preventing mismatches before Miami establishes its preferred offensive formation. The defensive approach is particularly important given uncertainty surrounding several players' availability.

According to the NBA's 4:30 a.m. injury report, Antetokounmpo was listed as probable with right groin tendinitis. OG Anunoby was doubtful for New York, while Andre Drummond was questionable. Those designations did not represent final lineup decisions.

Antetokounmpo scored 32 points in Miami's 111-104 loss to the Philadelphia 76ers. The Heat's breakdown of that game highlighted how quickly the team advanced the ball and exploited defensive cross-matches involving Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo.

That approach creates problems before defenders can settle into their assigned matchups. New York therefore needs to address Miami's transition opportunities rather than relying exclusively on individual defensive assignments.

Josh Hart could take responsibility for meeting the ball early if he is the first wing defender returning. Mikal Bridges would track the initial pass, while the center would protect the basket. This approach would add an obstacle before Antetokounmpo could attack open space.

The proposed adjustment would apply primarily during transition rather than permanently changing the Knicks' defensive assignments. Once Miami moves the ball, New York's players could organize their matchups without automatically sending two defenders toward the ball handler and leaving Adebayo unguarded as he arrives behind them.

The adjustment would also require New York to accept a potential offensive cost. Against Cleveland, the Knicks recorded 20 fast-break points of their own, demonstrating the value of having players available to run after securing possession.

Sending an additional wing back on defense would leave one fewer player near the offensive basket for rebound opportunities. The suggested approach is to accept that trade-off early against Miami and relax the restriction when Antetokounmpo rests or cannot play.

Anunoby's availability could provide head coach Mike Brown with another established defensive matchup. However, his potential return would not eliminate the need for the Knicks to respond quickly when Miami pushes the ball up the court.
