David Jones creditors owed $490m told 'too early' to know payout

TheTools · 20.11.2026 19:51 · source · permalink

Hundreds of creditors owed money by collapsed department store chain David Jones have been told it's too early to say how much they will get back as administrators try to sell the business.

Some 500 parties looking to recoup a total of more than A$400 million (NZ$490 million) gathered online on Tuesday for the first creditors' meeting after the loss-making business filed for voluntary administration on November 5.

McGrathNicol representatives, in tandem with receivers FTI Consulting, set out the legal process involved, revealing David Jones had been running mounting pre-interest and tax losses since 2024/25.

Administrator Shaun Fraser set out the company's history, including its acquisition by Anchorage Capital in 2023, and the continuous pressure on its revenue and margins.

Its balance sheet was in decline with net assets falling to A$19 million (NZ$23 million) as of October 26, while total liabilities rose to A$608 million (NZ$746 million).

Asked how many cents in the dollar creditors could get, administrator Matthew Hutton from McGrathNicol told a concerned supplier there were too many variables involved ahead of a possible sale or liquidation of the business.

"We haven't got to a point where we've engaged enough with bidders to have a sense of what value there might be," he said.

"So sorry ... it's too early."

Asked if it could be as little as between three and 15 cents in the dollar, Hutton said creditors could expect more clarity at the second meeting, possibly to be held in mid-late January.

David Jones in Auckland's Westfield Newmarket. (Source: 1News)

David Jones' recent accounts show that in the first four months of 2026/27 its gross margins fell two percentage points to 33% as rent and outgoings as a per centage of revenue rose four per centage points to 15%.

That translated to a net loss of A$69 million (NZ$84 million) for the year to date.

David Jones made a net loss of A$228 million (NZ$279 million) in 2025/26, according to a slide provided by the administrator to creditors.

That's on top of losses of A$16 million (NZ$19.6 million) and A$39 million (NZ$47.86 million) in 2024/25 and 2023/24, respectively.

The deadline for potential buyers of the 188-year-old David Jones to submit expressions of interest closed on Friday and Hutton said the administrators were engaging with about 150 local and international parties.

"We are pursuing a going concern sale transaction to maximise value for the benefit of creditors and provide as much certainty as possible for employees, suppliers, landlords, and other stakeholders," Hutton said.

Non-binding indicative offers are expected to be received by the end of November ahead of potential buyers moving into the due diligence phase before final offers are submitted.

The receivers plans to close 18 of its 37 Australian stores, and one in Auckland, by year-end.

Anchorage Capital bought David Jones for around A$100 million (NZ$122.7 million) from South Africa's Woolworths Holdings, which paid A$2.1 billion (NZ$2.58 billion) in 2014 to take over the listed retailer from its shareholders.
