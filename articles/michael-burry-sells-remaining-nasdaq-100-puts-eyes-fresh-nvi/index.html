The "Big Short" investor Michael Burry has closed his remaining Nasdaq-100 put options, signaling a shift toward bullish bets even as market volatility rises. The investor is holding roughly 25% cash, favoring undervalued stocks while considering a fresh short position in Nvidia (NVDA) near $200.

Michael Burry's Bearish Bets Shrink As Cash Position Grows

In his latest Substack post, Michael Burry said his investment now leans more toward undervalued stocks as he scales back bearish bets and builds a sizable cash reserve.

"Today, as tech stocks were weak, I sold the rest (final half) of the Nasdaq 100 (NDX) put position," said Burry.

His only outstanding bearish options positions are tied to Palantir Technologies (PLTR) and MetLife (MET). Burry added that he is still evaluating potential short opportunities but currently prefers owning stocks. Around 25% of his portfolio is held in cash.

Overseas Stocks Lead Burry's Biggest Positions

MercadoLibre (MELI), Temple & Webster Group and JD.com Inc. (JD) currently rank among his largest holdings. His JD.com exposure also includes call options with expiration dates extending to 2029.

"This international focus at the top is not uncommon in my portfolio strategy, and is a byproduct of continued high valuations in the United States."

Burry's other investments include Adobe (ADBE), Birkenstock (BIRK), Deckers Outdoor (DECK), Lululemon Athletica (LULU), and PayPal Holdings Inc. (PYPL). His smaller investments include Hong Kong-listed businesses such as BYD, Tencent, and Meituan. Together, these positions now represent slightly more than one-fifth of his portfolio.

Nvidia Short Could Return Near $200

Burry said he continues to hold call options on Nvidia, which have already doubled in value. "My plan is to short Nvidia again nearer $200/share during a period of market optimism. That time is not now."

"So for now, I am long undervalued common stocks, LEAP calls and preferred stock, with a modest cash pile."

On Monday, after Jensen Huang returned as Nvidia's CEO, Burry suggested that if Huang helps revive the stock market, regulators might lose interest in investigating the company.
