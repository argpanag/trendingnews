Singapore’s top badminton player Loh Kean Yew one win away from first Asiad medal

TheTools · 26.09.2026 01:38 · source · permalink

Loh Kean Yew of Singapore mimicking anime character Monkey D. Luffy pose after defeating Sen Lakshya of India during the Aichi-Nagoya 2026 Asian Games badminton men’s singles on Sept 26.

Kimberly Kwek

NAGOYA – On a day when he registered his first two match wins at the Asian Games to make it to the men’s singles quarter-finals, national badminton player Loh Kean Yew celebrated by bending down and pressing his right fist to the ground.

With the Asiad held in Japan, there was hardly anywhere more fitting to bust out this celebration, an homage to Monkey D. Luffy’s iconic Gear Second pose from the anime One Piece.

In the manga series, Luffy uses Gear Second to accelerate his blood flow using his rubber organs and vessels to drastically increase his agility, speed and strength.

After a first-round bye, the unseeded world No. 13 Loh displayed all these qualities to take out India’s eighth seed and 14th-ranked Lakshya Sen 21-12, 15-21, 21-14 in their round-of-32 match in the morning of Sept 26.

He then returned to the Ichinomiya City Municipal Gymnasium in the evening to see off Malaysia’s world No. 48 Justin Hoh 21-14, 21-14 to book his place in the quarter-finals where he is set to meet Indonesia’s world No. 1 Jonatan Christie.

After his win against Sen, the 29-year-old said: “For normal tournaments, winning is definitely a happy one and at major Games, you are carrying the Singapore flag.

“It’s an honour and privilege to be here representing Singapore to participate in a major Games, whether it’s the Olympics, Asian Games, Commonwealth Games or the SEA Games. To be able to win here, it feels even more special.”

While his Asian Games debut in China three years ago ended in no wins and two defeats in the men’s singles and team events, Loh is now one win away from his first Asiad medal.

The only time Team Singapore stood on an Asian Games badminton podium was when the women’s team won bronze in 2006.

Standing in Loh’s way is 2018 men’s singles champion Christie, who has a perfect 10-0 head-to-head record against the Singaporean but hasn’t been at his best since he became the first Indonesian to ascend the summit of the men’s singles world ranking in August.

At these Games, the 29-year-old remarkably lost all of his men’s team matches to Malaysia’s Leong Jun Hao in the quarter-finals, Thailand’s Kunlavut Vitidsarn in the semis and China’s Shi Yuqi but still ended up with a gold medal.

It also means he has played three more matches than Loh, who will be encouraged by how open the competition is with China’s defending champion, seventh seed and world No. 12 Li Shifeng crashing out after losing to South Korea’s 89th-ranked Choi Ji-hoon.

Christie’s last-16 singles match against Sri Lanka’s 124th-ranked Viren Nettasinghe started at only 12.55am on Sept 27, and he was taken the distance before winning 15-21, 21-13, 21-15.

Team Singapore are also represented by world No. 45 women’s singles player Yeo Jia Min and men’s doubles pair Junsuke Kubo and Wesley Koh, who are ranked 42nd.

Competing in her first event in three months, Yeo got off to a winning start, beating Fathimath 21-9, 21-13.

She advanced to the last 16, where she will face former world champion Ratchanok Intanon from Thailand.

The Asian Games marked Yeo Jia Min’s return to competition after a left knee injury kept her out of action for three months.

The 27-year-old was glad to be back, after a left knee injury kept her out of action for the past few months. 

The last competition she took part in was the Australian Badminton Open in June, where she retired in her round-of-16 match against Chinese Taipei’s Chen Su-Yu.

Yeo said: “It’s definitely been a tougher period this time, having been through injuries again and again.

“Nevertheless, I’m very thankful to have people in Team Singapore cheering me on and believing that I can come back again, putting in the work together with me.

“I just can’t wait to give them something to support and make them proud.”

Kubo and Koh, who beat Nepal’s Kshitiz Khanal and Praful Maharjan 21-13, 21-16 in the round-of-32 on Sept 25, face South Korea’s world No. 14 duo Ki Dong-ju and Kang Min-hyuk in the last 16.
