With more access to information, have we learned anything? - Philadelphia Gay News

TheTools · 22.09.2026 16:56 · source · permalink

This past summer, actor Richard Gere spoke out against President Donald Trump for cuts to the U.S. Agency for International Development. “He has idiots around him,” Gere remarked.

In response, White House Communications Director Steven Cheung posted a low blow to social media: “Not going to listen to a guy who may have put a gerbil up his ass.”

This is a low blow because it is untrue.

While that urban legend still continues to haunt Gere, it did not necessarily harm the actor’s career in the long term. However, it did harm the career of local KYW newscaster Jerry Penacoli when he was accused of doing the same in 1985, with the rumor seeming to run him out of town.

PGN tried to discover the truth through journalistic practices. “The emergency room directors of eight area hospitals confirm that they have neither treated nor heard of a case in which a man was hospitalized for injuries resulting from the insertion of a gerbil or other live animal into his anus,” reads a PGN article published at the time.

Journalist Allen Salkin continues to unpack these spinning gerbil tales through The Hollywood Reporter podcast, “Gerbil” — with the second episode discussing the Penacoli rumor specifically. But it’s wild that we still need to clarify these unsubstantiated claims all these years later.

Those PGN articles about Penacoli may be lost from the digital archives at this time but there are still many online sources refuting both that story and Gere’s. Before making that post about Gere, Cheung could’ve easily did a quick Google search. If you searched “Are the Richard Gere gerbil rumors true?” right now, there are dozens of articles for your research consumption. The Google AI result even states, “No, the rumor about Richard Gere and a gerbil is completely false and is an unproven urban legend.”

While we should always look at AI results with a critical eye, it’s hard to believe that anyone in a White House run by a money-hungry oligarch is anti-AI by any means. Given that presumption, why isn’t the White House using these AI tools at their disposal? There are so many ways to access information. And there are also ways to access misinformation. The problem is that so many people are willing to hear a rumor and run with it.

In the past decade, there have also been claims that the LGBTQ+ community wanting to add a “P” to the acronym, standing for “pedosexual” — which is exactly what it sounds like it’s supposed to mean. I remember seeing well-meaning Facebook friends post about how this was “wrong” and even some LGBTQ+ friends who felt they needed to clarify that they were “not like other gays” and that they would always stand against pedophilia. But they were getting those untrue rumors (in case it needs to be said, the only people who are defending pedophilia are pedophiles) from internet trolls. My tin-foil hat theory is that these rumors began a domino effect toward the conservative propaganda that the LGBTQ+ community is full of groomers.

Cheung and those well-meaning friends on Facebook could’ve easily done a quick internet search. We have more access to information than ever before. You might even be reading this editorial from your phone. You should use that phone to Google — even if it’s about the claims made in this editorial. It seems that we need a constant reminder to practice critical thinking. And with so much access to information, that’s a damn shame.
