Descendant of David Jones caught in collapse of retail empire

November 10, 2026 - 8.40am

The collapse of David Jones has left smaller brands out of pocket, but for one business owner, the fallout is personal - with the empire his family built now threatening to tear his own business apart.

Ben Nothling started his shoe brand, Holster, 25 years ago in Noosa on Queensland’s Sunshine Coast, and it’s stocked in more than 30 countries worldwide.

In a cruel twist of fate, the great-great-great-grandson of David Jones is among the creditors fighting to recoup unpaid stock. A Current Affair

You could say retail is in Ben Nothling’s blood - after all, his great-great-great-grandfather happens to be the David Jones.

“If you’re an Aussie brand, that’s like the pinnacle of retail. You’re like, I want my brand in David Jones. So we were just focused on trying to get in there, and yeah, we eventually did in 2011,” Nothling told A Current Affair.

Until recently, Holster was proudly displayed on the shelves of Australia’s largest and most trusted department store, founded by his own ancestors.

Welsh immigrant David Jones opened the first department store on the corner of George Street and Barrack Lane in Sydney in 1838.

Jones and his wife Jane Mander went on to have nine children.

Ben’s father, Martin Nothling, was born four generations later, and his son followed in the family’s footsteps by going into fashion.

Welsh immigrant David Jones opened the first department store on the corner of George Street and Barrack Lane in Sydney in 1838. Supplied

The department store remained in the family’s hands for 140 years until they sold their control in the 80s.

In a cruel twist of fate, Nothling has now joined the growing list of creditors hoping to claw their money back.

Until recently, Holster was proudly displayed on the shelves of Australia’s largest and most trusted department store. A Current Affair

“$130,000 of the first summer shipments, and then we’ve got about $118,000 of stock sitting in the warehouse at wholesale value,” Nothling told A Current Affair.

Nothling isn’t the only supplier left in the lurch as retailers head into the busiest time of the year.

Jacob Leung started supplying David Jones with handwoven blankets from his small business, Slowdown Studio.

Descendant of David Jones caught in collapse of retail empire. Supplied

He delivered his last order to the department store only a month ago, and payment is now overdue.

Leung told A Current Affair he heard about the collapse in the news, with no correspondence from David Jones in the lead-up.

“We are currently owed $75,000 from David Jones, and that puts a massive strain on the business,” Leung said.

“We have staff, manufacturing, and printing costs that have actually been paid for, but we have no money to pay it yet.“

The iconic department store collapsed last week. A Current Affair

David Jones owes $320 million, with $100 million of that outstanding to suppliers and trade creditors.

Despite the financial distress, receivers say there’s been a record surge in foot traffic and sales, with replenishment supply orders taking place.

“The best thing that Australian consumers can do at this stage, given this David Jones situation, is just to buy direct from small businesses,” Leung said.

“That way, that business is getting all of the profits that they don’t have to share.”
