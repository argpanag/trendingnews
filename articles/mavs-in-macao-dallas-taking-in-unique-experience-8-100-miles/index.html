Mavs in Macao: Dallas taking in unique experience 8,100 miles from home

TheTools · 09.10.2026 01:50 · source · permalink

The Mavericks and No. 9 overall pick Morez Johnson Jr. are on the other side of the globe preparing for their first preseason game.

The Mavericks and No. 9 overall pick Morez Johnson Jr. are on the other side of the globe preparing for their first preseason game.

The sound of basketballs thumping all around him was no different than the thousands of basketball practices he’s had in his 20-year life, but little else about Wednesday’s scene and circumstances was normal for Mavericks rookie Morez Johnson Jr.

Let’s start with the obvious: The Mavericks are 8,100 miles from home, in Macao, a special administrative region of China on its southern coast.

And specifically, this morning the Mavericks were in The Venetian Arena, practicing for about 90 minutes as they prepared for preseason games here on Friday and Sunday against Houston.

The Mavericks' first 48 hours in the region that bills itself “the Las Vegas of Asia” have been vivid and eventful. They've included jet lag from the 17-hour flight; two practices; excursions around Macao; and for a few Mavericks, a Wednesday afternoon ferry ride to Hong Kong. All in all, it’s quite a way for the No. 9 overall pick, Johnson, to make his NBA debut.

"I was just looking forward to playing basketball, but there's so much behind-the-scenes stuff that I'm enjoying, stuff they don't talk about on TV, but I'm enjoying it here with my teammates," Johnson said. "I didn't know anything about us going to China, and I'm just grateful to be here."

New head coach Dusty May was equally excited.

"It's been a really cool experience to see how they live. It feels very similar to home in some ways, but yet feels different in others. Most importantly, our guys have had an opportunity to see something new and different, but also they've spent quality time together. I think that's one thing that's so important on these trips. You get away from home a little bit, and you do get the chance to learn a little more about each other, spend time, know each other's rhythms and things of that nature," May said. "We're holding each other to a very high standard, and the way we play, the way we're interacting, is important to everyone. And so as we establish new standards and a new style of play, I think our guys are in the infancy stages of doing it. And they've been very adaptable up to this point."

May said that while they're experimenting this preseason, they still hope to win.
