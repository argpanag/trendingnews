Amidst electric start, Ullmark has ‘couple of things still to clean up’

TheTools · 17.11.2026 14:20 · source · permalink

The Ottawa Senators have been one of the surprising stories a quarter of the way through the NHL season, sitting tied atop the Atlantic Division despite a rocky off-season.

Star forward and captain Brady Tkachuk was shipped off to Florida after a trade request, leaving a sour taste in the mouth of Sens fans and forcing the team that made the playoffs as the eighth seed to replace a point-a-game player.

On top of that, the team has been ravaged by injuries in the early going, with top defenceman Jake Sanderson and forwards Warren Foegele, Andre Burakovsky and William Eklund all missing significant time early on.

Despite it all, Ottawa is off to a scorching start, sitting 13-6-2 with wins in five of their past seven games, and a large part of that is owed to the play of goaltender Linus Ullmark - who also missed nine games due to a lower-body injury.

Ullmark has picked up two wins in two games since returning from the injury on Thursday, but still thinks there’s more room for improvements.

“I was really good before the injury,” Ullmark told The Athletic’s Julian McKenzie on Monday. “Now, (my game is) slowly coming back. A couple of things still to clean up. I’ve been lucky and fortunate that we’ve managed to pull two wins out of the bag in different ways now, where I haven’t really felt at my best.”

Ullmark has a 9-1-0 record in 10 starts this year, with a .939 save percentage and a 1.47 goals-against average - both best in the league.

In his return to the lineup last Thursday, Ullmark allowed three goals in a win against the Colorado Avalanche, and followed that with a win on the road against the Pittsburgh Penguins, where he allowed just one goal on 17 shots.

The netminder is quick to credit the play of the team in front of him for his season to date, which has put him in the thick of the Vezina Trophy race.

“I’ve always said that when you have a shutout or whatever it is, it’s never a one-man show,” Ullmark said. “People might think so. But at the end of the day, I can’t do it alone. I never will. I will, for sure, lose every single game that I play out there by myself.”

Ullmark started last season with an .862 save percentage through 10 games, and never really got going from there. He dealt with injuries, and spent 35 days out of action after he took a mental health leave and consulted a health professional from the NHL/NHLPA’s player assistance program.

“It’s been, I think, an improvement from last season,” Ullmark said Monday. “There [were] a lot of things going on last season, obviously.

“I didn’t start at a minus and had to work my way back to a normal level. This [year], I could say I was starting at a plus.”

Ullmark spoke about how some changes around the goaltending room this season have helped, including the addition of Swedish goaltending guru Maciej Szwoch, who worked with Ullmark when he was young, and backup goaltender Samuel Ersson, who was acquired in a deal with the Toronto Maple Leafs.

“He knows my tendencies,” Ullmark said of Szwoch. “He knows me from way back when I was a young buck and all the things that I did at that point, and what worked ... it’s been a good addition to the organization. I really hope that the front office and those guys, like [GM Steve Staios], feel that way.”

Ullmark says the dialogue between goaltending coaches and players have been “so valuable,” and he also highlighted how reading has helped him decompress on road trips.

Those changes have amounted to a stark jump in team save percentage, due in large part to Ullmark’s fantastic performance. The Senators finished 29th last season in that category (.878), but rank 12th through 21 games this year (.890).
