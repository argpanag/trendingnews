'We're ahead of schedule' - Croke Park ready for Taylor

TheTools · 04.09.2026 08:16 · source · permalink

Having just dismantled the extravagant stage used for Bon Jovi's sellout concert last Sunday, Croke Park officials have been busy preparing for arguably the biggest night in Irish boxing history, as Katie Taylor gets ready to take on Flora Pili at GAA HQ.

The stadium is well used to putting on huge shows, from Garth Brooks to Oasis. However it's a long time since it staged a card of boxing.

It's 54 years since the last professional boxing event took place at Croke Park, when the great Muhammad Ali fought Al 'Blue' Lewis in July 1972.

Speaking to RTÉ Radio 1's Morning Ireland, Croke Park stadium and commercial director Peter McKenna outlined the logistics of tailoring the venue for tomorrow night's historic occasion.

"It's very similar to what we do at concerts," he said. "It's in the middle of the park this time. I think the issues really were getting time to take down the whole Bon Jovi setup, putting in the boxing setup and then putting 12,000 seats onto the stadium ground.

"We have two crews working really hard on it. Having seen it this morning, it looks like we're in great shape and ahead of schedule."

It promises to be a unique atmosphere, with lots of families and children in attendance. Organisers have booked entertainment to cater for all tastes: Westlife are the headline act, with plenty of other spectacles planned.

"This is where Matchroom Boxing and Peter Aiken (of Aiken Promotions) have worked really well together," added McKenna.

"They both know what they do very, very well. So they're going to intersperse the boxing card with musicians like The Mary Wallopers and Westlife. And then there'll be pyrotechnics and various other displays during the course of the evening.

"The whole thing is to keep the crowd entertained up until the big moment, which is the ring walk.

"It's a big feature of international boxing and world title fights, where the challenger gets exposed first and then the champion is given the adulation of the crowd.

"I think it's an event that's captured the imagination of everybody."

"I think it's an event that's captured the imagination of everybody."

"There's a 10-minute interlude there where people can really get to celebrate Katie and cheer her on before she gets into the ring and then prepares for the fight. That itself brings its own logistics because boxing bouts don't have a defined time like we have in our own games.

"As soon as she gets into the ring, we will have the stadium prepared to exit, if you like, because the fight can end very quickly and then people want to go home. So it's logistically slightly different than we would for a match or for a concert."

The weather forecast looks decent; as a spectacle, Taylor's final fight is set to be monumental.

"It's hugely exciting," McKenna said. "I think it's an event that's captured the imagination of everybody.

"Nobody, apart from maybe Katie and those close to her - Peter Aiken and Eddie Hearn - foreseen that this would sell out. I certainly didn't see it selling out, but it has.

"There's people coming from all different perspectives on this, die-hard boxing fans, Katie supporters and people who just don't want to miss out on what's going to be a very unique international event, celebrating one of the greatest Irish boxers of all time."
