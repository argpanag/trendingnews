Monaco’s Anti-Aging Show Moves to Cannes as Hotel Capacity Reaches Its Limits

TheTools · 19.09.2026 18:35 · source · permalink

After 25 years at the Grimaldi Forum, Monaco’s Anti-Aging Show is moving to Cannes in 2027. The event drew 17,000 visitors this year and accounted for around 10% of the venue’s revenue, but limited hotel capacity and rising room rates have made further growth increasingly difficult.

The Anti-Aging Show (Salon anti-âge) had become a fixture of the Grimaldi Forum’s spring calendar.

Every year, thousands of exhibitors, doctors, and visitors interested in healthy aging and wellness treatments gathered in Monaco for an event that also represented a significant source of revenue for the venue.

But in 2027, the show will move to Cannes.

The main reason is straightforward: accommodation. With Monaco offering around 2,200 hotel rooms, the event had reached a point where its continued growth was becoming increasingly difficult.

For Grimaldi Forum General Director Sylvie Biancheri, the departure highlights one of the structural limits facing Monaco when it comes to hosting very large business events.

The Anti-Aging Show has been a long-standing fixture on your calendar. Why is it moving to Cannes in 2027?

"It’s primarily a question of hotel availability and room rates. Scarcity drives prices up, and that became a significant burden for exhibitors bringing their own clients. When an event reaches the point where its growth is being held back, as happened with the Anti-Aging Show, it’s understandable that the organizer looks elsewhere. We had very little room to maneuver given Monaco’s hotel capacity, which tops out at around 2,200 rooms. When you have 17,000 visitors over three days, as we did with the Anti-Aging Show this year, the options become extremely limited. Its departure will have an impact. The show accounted for around 10% of our revenue, and that is not something you replace easily."

Have you already found events to fill those dates?

"Yes. We have already reassigned the dates to other events, including a new show dedicated to yacht and boat renovation, which has been entrusted to us by Informa, the organizer of the Anti-Aging Show. We’re optimistic about its potential. Of course, you don’t replace a 25-year-old event with a new one overnight. It takes time to grow. In terms of scale, it won’t immediately generate the same revenue, either for us or for other businesses in the Principality. But there is demand, there are opportunities, and Monaco remains attractive."

Does the departure illustrate a broader limit for Monaco when it comes to major events?

« =This isn’t the first time it has happened. In the past 25 years, this is probably the fourth or fifth event we’ve lost because we’ve reached maximum capacity. What can we do about it? We can’t simply expand Monaco’s hotel capacity overnight. At some point, we have to accept that this can happen. It’s always a little painful, especially when an event relocates somewhere so close. There is a sense of helplessness because we simply cannot offer more than we have."

Does that change the way you select future events?

"Our approach is to manage the calendar strategically and choose events that make the best use of the available space, work well for Monaco’s hotels, and attract visitors with the kind of spending power we’re looking for."
