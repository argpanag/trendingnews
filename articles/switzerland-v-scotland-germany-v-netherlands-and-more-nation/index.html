Craig Bellamy admits football will miss Cristiano Ronaldo on the international stage but believes Portugal fans should celebrate his greatness and not focus on his controversial departure from the national team.

Wales take on a Ronaldo-less Portugal in their final Nations League A4 group game on Tuesday, with the 41-year-old having ended his international career earlier this month after 234 games and 146 goals – both of which are records in the men’s game.

Portugal began the official post-Ronaldo era with a 4-1 home defeat by Denmark on Saturday, fuelling further talk about Ronaldo’s exit from the national team earlier this month.

Ronaldo’s mother Dolores Aveiro has made headlines in Portugal by expressing satisfaction that Jorge Jesus’ side had lost to Denmark, and said her son had been deceived by national team selection.

The former Manchester United and Real Madrid great, now playing for Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia, announced his retirement from international football on November 5 after he was suspended for 30 days and fined 255 euros (approximately £210) by the Portuguese Football Federation after walking out on the national team.

Ronaldo left the camp shortly before a Nations League game against Denmark following a row with head coach Jesus, making his final appearance for Portugal in the 1-0 win over Wales in Lisbon on September 24.

Wales boss Bellamy said: “You were all having a go at him for playing, and then as soon as he retires, suddenly it’s all hell breaking loose! He is one of the best players that has ever played the game. We’re all going to miss him. Just be grateful you got to see an incredible talent that you could call yours – and this is me speaking to Portuguese people. He’s left a mark on football that will live forever. I feel it’s time to stop the talking now. Just celebrate him.” PA
