Grand Theft Auto 6 delivers major boost to physical games market

GTA 6 is the biggest physical game launch since 2019, reveals UK data tracker Nielsen IQ.

It generated £32 million in revenue in just three days. The last time a game sold this many copies in the UK during its first week was FIFA 20 in September 2019. GTA 6 is the No.17 biggest physical game launch in the UK since records began in 1995.

The Game Business understands that the game’s physical launch was even bigger in France, with over one million boxed copies sold in the country in its first three days on sale. It’s not dissimilar to the number of copies GTA 5 sold in 2013. Meanwhile, in Spain, GTA 6 is the biggest physical retail launch in the chart’s history.

In terms of UK console sales, PS5 sales rose 46% during the week GTA 6 came out, while Xbox Series S and X sales jumped 75%. It’s the biggest jump for Xbox since 2024. Overall, UK console sales last week were up 38%, which comes on the back of a 29% increase the week prior.

Over in the US, Circana revealed that the week before GTA 6 (the week ending November 14), PS5 sales jumped by more than 2.4 times the previous week, whereas Xbox Series S and X sales jumped by over 2.3 times.

GTA 5 enjoys player bump despite GTA 6 launch

The number of people playing GTA 5 last week increased by 4.5% during the launch period of GTA 6, reveals Ampere Analysis.

The data firm revealed that the day before GTA 6 launched, GTA 5 players leapt by 73.6% compared with the same day a week earlier. On the launch day of GTA 6, GTA 5 user numbers rose 19.4% week-on-week. It suggests that the buzz around the franchise positively benefitted the older game, which is available on more platforms.

Indeed, mobile downloads for older GTA titles also saw a sharp increase week-on-week.

However, Grand Theft Auto is the outlier, as most other games saw a sharp drop in player numbers during the GTA 6 launch window.

The Call of Duty series suffered the steepest fall. Over a five-day period, Call of Duty player numbers dropped by over 37% compared with the same period in the previous week. Fortnite saw a 24% player drop, while EA Sports FC 27 saw player numbers fall by nearly 22%. You can see the full impact below, with declines particularly noticeable over the weekend.

These declines are likely temporary and some companies, including Activision, speculate it might see an increase in new and returning players once people finish playing GTA 6.

Ubisoft wants Rayman to become the next global transmedia game character

The release of Rayman Legends Retold next week is the first stage in an ambitious relaunch of the Rayman IP.

Retold is a reimagining of the 2013 classic Rayman Legends, and the first new Rayman title since that year.

The new release features all new levels, gameplay, story, and characters. And the reason Ubisoft has taken this approach as opposed to launching a new game, is because there wasn’t a dedicated Rayman team anymore.

“The people working on Rayman 13 years ago have moved onto other projects, or left the company,” said brand producer Loïc Gounon.

Gounon believes going back to Legends is a good way to re-introduce Rayman as it’s where the franchise left off, but it’s also a means to teach new developers what Rayman is all about.

“It’s a way to have senior people that know Rayman internally onboard more junior people that did not know Rayman,” he said. “And it’s a transition from what we did in the past to what we want to do next, aligning on what [Rayman] is, what’s in the Rayman DNA, and getting used to that DNA. For all of us [it was] a very beautiful training, because we ended up doing a bit more than we planned… but we want to be even more ambitious for the future.”

Retold is intended as the first step in a Rayman relaunch, Gounon said. He hints at plans for a 3D Rayman game, and even the prospect of Rayman appearing in other mediums.

“I would like to see Rayman as a must-have IP for video games across the world,” he said. “Rayman was generally stronger in France and the UK, and in some cases in the US. Enlarging the reach of the IP, also towards Asia and performing well in the US, is the first ambition… making sure that we are a must-have platforming adventure title across those markets in the video game world.

“And I guess beyond the video game world. So, comics, TV series... actually, there’s a French comic book that came out with Rabbids and Rayman being reconnected.”

We suggested a move to 3D might make Rayman appeal more widely, but Gounon argued it comes down to character and story.

“3D is not the answer to selling more. It could even be the opposite,” he argued.

“Rayman is iconic because of its gameplay. But going back to the adventure roots of Rayman, having people dive into those characters, exploring deeper into that world and mythology, that’s what we believe in.

“It’s the world-building part, the consistency of that world-building, and the empathy for those characters, that will make the franchise last and maybe become bigger.”

“3D is not the answer to selling more. It could even be the opposite.”

Gounon added that the new Creative House structure within Ubisoft – where the company has split its franchises into various groups with its own leadership – should prove beneficial to the Rayman IP in the future.

“The Creative House model should bring more autonomy to the team, so that we can lead our IP and have the means adapted to the size of the project,” he told us.

“Obviously, Rayman is not an Assassin’s Creed. It doesn’t need the same team, or the same effort necessarily, but you need people that are focused and dedicated. Being more focused and sharp in terms of our effort on every type of IP, every way of selling games, and every type of audience, to me, is a smart move. We should be more flexible, more agile, and more organized that way.”

Meanwhile…

  • EA is bringing back the voice of the late football commentator John Motson in EA Sports FC 27. His commentary will return using a combination of archived recordings and generative AI.

  • Ubisoft beat its Q2 guidance after stronger-than-expected sales of Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced. Ubisoft’s net bookings hit €389 million, above the expected €370 million. But its cost cutting continues, with 166 job losses in its Montreal and Quebec teams. It is also merging Creative House 3, which focuses on shooter games like Ghost Recon, with Creative House 5, which is focused on casual games like Uno and Just Dance.

  • In the US, $4.2 billion was spent on games in October, down 12% from a year ago (Circana data). This is despite Call of Duty launching in October this year, whereas it arrived in November in 2025. Modern Warfare 4 was the best-selling game of the month, and there were six new releases in the charts, including Gears of War: E-Day at No.2 and Star Wars: Galactic Racer at No.3. Last year’s big October games including Pokémon Legends: Z-A and Battlefield 6.

  • Build A Rocket Boy is pursuing litigation against unnamed parties who apparently ‘sabotaged’ its game MindsEye. The company says this will allow it to repay its £61.7 million debt, most of which is owed to NetEase (£36.4 million).

  • UK developers who released games on Google Play between August 2018 and July 2026 can now claim for their share of a £260 million settlement agreed in a class action suit. The case accused Google of abusing its dominant position to prevent developers from using alternative storefronts, and charging an unfair commission.

  • The European Commission is looking for public feedback on its proposed Digital Fairness Act (DFA), which would introduce new rules on virtual currencies and in-game purchases by children. The act has been criticised by various European trade bodies, alongside mobile game giants Supercell and King. The criticism is around how the act could undermine parental choice, increase costs for developers, and weaken the industry.

  • Ambitious game studio Gravity Well has entered hibernation after failing to secure funding. The studio was formed by Infinity Ward and Respawn veterans Drew McCoy and Jon Shiring.

  • Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president following a unanimous vote by its Board of Directors.
