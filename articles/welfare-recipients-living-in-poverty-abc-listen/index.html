Welfare recipients living in poverty - ABC listen

TheTools · 11.10.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

A new report has found three out of four Australians receiving social security payments continue to live in poverty.

So advocates are renewing calls to raise jobseeker and other income support.

It comes as the Government allocates an additional 4.7 million dollars for relief services including food charities.

A new report has found three out of four Australians receiving social security payments continue to live in poverty

Rachel Mealey: A new report has found three out of four Australians receiving social security payments continue to live in poverty. Advocates are renewing calls to raise JobSeeker and other income support. Annie Guest reports.


Annie Guest: When she was in her 30s, then marketing manager Marina Berger felt confident her life was on a good path. But since then, she's taken time out of the workforce as a single parent and also had to combat cancer, leaving the now 63-year-old on the poverty line.


Marina Berger: Extremely tough, waiting in line with 50 to 100 other people, all desperate to put food on their table.


Annie Guest: After moving 10 times during her daughter's childhood, Marina Berger was grateful to move into social housing in 2022. She spends about half her JobSeeker payment on her subsidised rent of about $200 a week and finds she can't cover all the basics like food and health care.


Marina Berger: So I get JobSeeker, it's about 400-odd dollars, a little bit more each week.


Annie Guest: And how have you found searching for work?


Marina Berger: It's very difficult. I send off about, I don't know, 10 applications a day and get very little, if any, response at all. Mainly, I think it's because of my age. I mean, I want to work.


Annie Guest: A report out today, co-authored by the Australian Council for Social Service, finds 3.7 million Australians are living in poverty. CEO, Cassandra Goldie, says that includes 750,000 children, 1 million people in work and 2 million people on welfare payments.


Cassandra Goldie: For key working age payments like JobSeeker, youth allowance and parenting payment, about three in four people are experiencing poverty.


Annie Guest: Co-author, UNSW Scientia Professor, Carla Treloar, drew the data from the Melbourne Institute's HILDA survey for 2022-23.


Carla Treloar: Payments for JobSeeker, youth allowance and parenting payments are so low that they're forcing people to live in poverty.


Annie Guest: And what's the trend in people living in poverty in Australia? Is it getting any better or worse or staying the same?


Carla Treloar: So that group of long-term unemployed has increased by about 20% since 2019 and in fact doubled since the global financial crisis.


Annie Guest: So the Council for Social Service is renewing calls for an increase in income support, including for JobSeeker to rise from 41% to 60% of the minimum wage. A spokesperson for the Minister for Social Services, Tanya Plibersek, says the government has increased pensions by $6,500 a year, boosted JobSeeker by $4,700 annually and delivered the largest ever increases to Commonwealth rent assistance, with maximum rates rising by more than 50% since 2022. And they pointed to Ms Plibersek's announcement yesterday of an additional $4.7 million for relief services.


Tanya Plibersek: Well, it's always a priority for our government to assist Australians who need a helping hand. This is part of a $460 million program that supports food relief and financial capability and wellbeing.


Rachel Mealey: Social Services Minister Tanya Plibersek ending Annie Guest's report.
