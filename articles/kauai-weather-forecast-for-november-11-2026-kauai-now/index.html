Prev discussion

Issued at 403 PM HST Tue Nov 10 2026
A 1030 mb high building far north of the state tonight into Wednesday will strengthen the trade winds and will bring windy conditions across the state. A Wind Advisory has been issued for the typical windy areas around Maui County and the Big Island starting at midnight tonight and continuing through Wednesday night. Winds will be strongest through gaps, and over and downslope of ridges Wednesday through Wednesday evening. Wind gusts of 50 mph is expected during this time. For Oahu and Kauai, strong trade winds are expected during this time, but confidence was not quite high enough to see advisory level winds. Gusty conditions should still be expected with wind gusts of 40 to 45 mph for areas downwind of terrain on Wednesday. The combination of strong trade winds and mostly dry conditions will elevate fire weather concerns on Wednesday. Please see Fire Weather section for more details.
An old frontal boundary is expected to move north through south of the island chain Wednesday night into Thursday, which should bring a brief increase of scattered showers, especially over windward and mauka areas. The strong trade winds should begin to ease some by Friday afternoon down to breezy to locally strong speeds. From Friday through the weekend, we should see our typical trade wind shower coverage with showers most frequent during the overnight and early morning hours over windward and mauka areas. Trade winds should restrengthen back to strong levels Saturday night into Sunday as another high passes far north of the state.

Aviation

Issued at 403 PM HST Tue Nov 10 2026
Trade winds are expected to strengthen tonight to locally strong wind speeds. Showers embedded within the trade winds will continue to primarily impact windward and mountain areas. Brief MVFR conditions are expected within showers while VFR prevails elsewhere.
AIRMET Tango will remain in effect for moderate turbulence downwind of terrain due to the breezy trade winds. Upper level turbulence is also expected to be present over the state through tonight, roughly between FL300 and FL370.
AIRMET Sierra is in effect for mountain obscurations for windward areas of Molokai, Maui, and the Big Island. Overnight showers could warrant this AIRMET being expanded to include other windward and mountain areas.

Marine

High pressure will build north of Hawaii resulting in trade winds becoming strong tonight and continuing through Thursday. A Gale Warning has been issued for the typical windy waters around Maui County and Big Island, while the Small Craft Advisory (SCA) for all other coastal waters remains in effect through Wednesday night, with the SCA likely to continue for most areas Thursday.
Seas are also forecast to exceed 10 feet beginning Wednesday with the arrival of a large north northwest swell. Meanwhile, a front will drop south into the northern offshore waters tonight and weaken to a trough. The trough reaches the central waters by Wednesday night and will lead to increased shower activity into Thursday. Winds will slightly taper off this weekend as a front approaches from the north.
The large north-northwest (340 degree) swell will arrive on Wednesday and will bring advisory to near-warning level surf to exposed north and west facing shores through Thursday. A High Surf Advisory has been issued for north and west facing shores of Niihau, Kauai, Oahu, Molokai and for north facing shores of Maui from Wednesday morning through Thursday afternoon. Surf will decrease Friday followed by a reinforcement this weekend which could push surf back to advisory levels.
Surf along east facing shores will increase for Wednesday as trade winds strengthen. East shore surf will remain elevated and choppy through Friday, then may diminish slightly this weekend. A small, long-period south swell may bring a small bump to south shore surf on Wednesday.

Fire weather

Issued at 403 PM HST Tue Nov 10 2026
Fire weather conditions will remain near critical levels over the next several days, mainly do the uptick in winds tonight that will persist through Thursday. With the trades strengthening under a drier air mass, winds and humidity will likely reach critical thresholds on Wednesday, while fuel conditions remain below critical thresholds due to the above average rainfall over the past few months. Due to the fuels, Red Flag Warnings are not expected at this time, but near critical fire weather conditions are expected Wednesday and Thursday. Winds should ease slightly with a modest increase of humidities on Friday.
Temperature inversion heights will remain between 5,000 and 7,000 feet across the state. The strong low level inversion will make winds quite gusty especially during the afternoons and evenings downwind of terrain over the next several days.
