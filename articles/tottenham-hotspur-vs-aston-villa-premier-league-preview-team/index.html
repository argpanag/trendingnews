  1. Top-scoring Seagullspublished at 14:42 BST
    14:42 BST

    Brighton v Arsenal (15:00 BST)

    Brighton's 5-0 victory at Coventry City equalled their biggest Premier League away win and took their tally to 13 goals from their opening four matches, a club record at this stage of a top-flight season.

    The result also made them the league's top scorers after four matchrounds. That is no fluke as the Seagulls are the only team to have accumulated an expected goals total of 10 or more.

    But they have also made the most of their chances, scoring almost three goals more than Opta's xG model would predict.

  2. 'Arsenal could expose Brighton'published at 14:41 BST
    14:41 BST

    Brighton v Arsenal (15:00 BST)

    Chris Sutton
    BBC Sport columnist

    There might be a bit of an edge to this one after Brighton boss Fabian Hurzeler had a pop at Arsenal over their time-wasting when they beat the Seagulls near the end of last season.

    I love the way Brighton play. It doesn't seem to matter who they are missing from their team, they are just bold as brass with the way they press and get men forward.

    Arsenal are the real deal and I don't see any weaknesses in their team, as their impressive win at Sunderland underlined.

    They are brave going forward, but they are also streetwise defensively and can see out a game when they have to, time-wasting or not.

    They have that steel about them but I also think they could expose Brighton if Hurzeler's side go toe-to-toe with them, which is what I am expecting.

    Sutton's prediction: 1-2

  3. Evasive Encisopublished at 14:40 BST
    14:40 BST

    Everton v Ipswich (15:00 BST)

    Ipswich Town have six points after four matches - it took them 16 matches to reach that tally in their previous Premier League campaign in 2024-25.

    But they are only the third team in the competition's history to achieve six points at this stage despite conceding as many as 10 goals.

    A key part of their willingness to play open football is the threat posed by summer signing Julio Enciso. The 22-year-old tops most of Ipswich's attacking metrics, including touches in the opposition box (14), shots (14) and dribbles (20).

    14:39 BST

    Everton v Ipswich (15:00 BST)

    James Garner's all-action performance in the goalless draw at Tottenham was pivotal in earning a hard-earned point and a return to his superlative form of last season following groin surgery before this campaign.

    Garner's physicality, creativity and versatility are all likely to be crucial assets if the Toffees are to push back towards the European places this season, but also in matches against unpredictable opposition.

  5. 'Decision to make on Botman' but 'Miley criticism harsh'published at 14:38 BST
    14:38 BST

    Newcastle v Hull (15:00 BST)

    John Anderson
    Former Newcastle defender on BBC Radio Newcastle

    Reacting to criticism of Sven Botman and Lewis Miley after the defeat to Leeds

    There was a concern about Botman coming into the season because he had a really poor pre-season. Things didn't go well for him. He did well against Liverpool and Spurs but the thing about Botman is he wants to play out from the back all the time and yet again [against Leeds] he is playing out and gives it away.

    I thought him and Thiaw got bullied by [Dominic] Calvert-Lewin. He pushed and shoved them and they didn't push and shove back. I think Jaissle has got a decision to make. He doesn't have Dan Burn available but he could play [Fabian] Schar there. I think he will have a decision to make at the weekend."

    I think the criticism of Miley is a bit harsh. Miley will develop into a very good player. He is a good player as it is. He is definitely not the quickest but his awareness of people around him is good and he is still growing and filling out into his body.

  6. 'We are optimistic, even with some injury challenges'published at 14:37 BST
    14:37 BST

    Newcastle v Hull (15:00 BST)

    Newcastle boss Matthias Jaissle on bouncing back from the defeat by Leeds: "We are fully committed to showing a clear reaction in front of our fans. It would be nice to finish this block of fixtures, before the international break, with a good result. We are optimistic, even with some injury challenges."

    When asked about Sven Botman's form: "I'm fully convinced about his performances. If a player has a down-spell, that is totally normal in a season because we are all human beings. He is still a brilliant player with huge potential. He is a warrior, that's what I appreciate about him."

  • None of the last 22 Premier League meetings between Spurs (15 wins) and Aston Villa (7) have been drawn. Spurs have lost five of the last eight, more than they had in their previous 29 against the Villans (W18 D7 L4).

  • Aston Villa have won four of their last six Premier League away games against Spurs (L2), more than they had in their previous 21 visits (W3 D5 L13).

  • Tottenham Hotspur have drawn 0-0 in each of their last two Premier League games, as many times as in their previous 181 matches in the competition; the last side to record three successive goalless draws in the competition was West Ham United in December 2015, with Spurs last doing so in January/February 2001 under George Graham (run of 4).

  • Only in 1919-20 (first 7), 1964-65 (7) and 2025-26 (5) have Aston Villa failed to win any of their opening five matches of a top-flight league season (D1 L3 so far).

  • After winning each of his first three encounters with Spurs’ Roberto De Zerbi, Aston Villa boss Unai Emery has lost each of the last two meetings, including a 2-1 reverse at Villa Park in May.

  • Alysson’s goal against Nottingham Forest was Aston Villa’s 34th Premier League goal via a substitute since the start of 2023-24. Only Brighton (40) and Arsenal (36) have scored more in that time, with two of Villa’s three goals against Spurs last season coming via subs.
