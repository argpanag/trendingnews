Andreeva vs. Svitolina: Everything to know about the 2026 FAGE WTA Finals championships

TheTools · 14.11.2026 00:20 · source · permalink

INDIAN WELLS, Calif -- It all comes down to the this. Sunday's championships at the FAGE WTA Finals will wrap up the 2026 season on the WTA Tour Driven by Mercedes-Benz.

FAGE WTA Finals championship matches will simulcast for free on Tennis Channel 2

At Indian Wells, No. 3 seed Mirra Andreeva brings her unbeaten and straight-set wins streaks to the table against Elina Svitolina in a rematch of the Group 2 opener. Second seeds Katerina Siniakova and Taylor Townsend will face No. 8 seeds Sara Errani and Jasmine Paolini in the doubles final. 

Below is a breakdown of everything to know ahead of Sunday's finals:

What is the order of play?

The singles final between Andreeva and Svitolina begins play at 12 p.m. at Indian Wells Tennis Garden's Stadium 2, followed by the presentation of the Billie Jean King trophy for the singles champion.

The doubles final between Siniakova and Townsend & Errani and Paolini will take place upon conclusion of the singles trophy ceremony, and festivities conclude with the presentation of the Martina Navratilova trophy for the doubles champions.

What are the ranking points and prize money at stake?

At Indian Wells, a total of $10 million was available in prize money, distributed among match wins and participant fees. 

In singles, Andreeva could earn the maximum $3.37 million in prize money and 1500 ranking points for undefeated campaign. If she loses, she leaves with $1.73 million and 1000 ranking points. 

Svitolina, with one loss in the group stage, can earn at most $3.14 million in prize money, and 1300 ranking points. If she loses, she departs with $1.5 million and 800 ranking points.

The singles champion will also receive a one-of-a-kind MANUFAKTUR Made to Measure Mercedes-AMG SL 55 4MATIC+ in the WTA's iconic purple, presented during the trophy ceremony. 

Siniakova and Townsend, with one loss in the group stage, can earn a maximum of $675,000 and 1300 points. In a defeat, they'll split $345,000 and earn 800 points. 

Errani and Paolini, who are unbeaten this week, can earn a maximum of 1500 points and $725,000. 

The doubles champions will also enjoy a 12-month complimentary lease of a Mercedes-Benz vehicle. 

At the WTA Finals, 1500 ranking points is the maximum amount of points possible for a player or team's standing in the PIF WTA Rankings. Each round-robin win earned 200 points, a semifinal win earned 400 and the championship is worth 500. 

Any change in rank is based on how the player performs in the tournament compared to the points earned in the same week last year. 

By the numbers: Andreeva vs. Svitolina

Andreeva and Svitolina have played three times previously, with all three meetings coming on outdoor hard courts -- and two in Indian Wells. Andreeva won both of those, triumphing 7-5, 6-3 in the 2025 quarterfinals en route to her second WTA 1000 title, then again 6-4, 6-0 in the group stage of the WTA Finals this week. Svitolina notched a 6-2, 6-4 victory in the Australian Open fourth round this year.

If you’re wondering how much to read into Mirra Andreeva’s win over Elina Svitolina in group play, here’s something to chew on. Since round-robin play returned in 2003, seven matchups have been replayed in the final, and four went the other way the second time.

2003: Kim Clijsters beat Amelie Mauresmo in both group play and the final.
2005: Mary Pierce won in group play, then Amelie Mauresmo beat her for the title.
2006: Amelie Mauresmo won in group play, then Justine Henin beat her for the title.
2009: Serena Williams beat Venus Williams in both group play and the final.
2014: Simona Halep won in group play, then Serena Williams beat her for the title.
2016: Angelique Kerber won in group play, then Dominika Cibulkova beat her for the title.
2021: Garbine Muguruza beat Anett Kontaveit in both group play and the final.

Andreeva

2: Mirra Andreeva is the second-youngest player in the history of the FAGE WTA Finals to win all her first four matches, older only than Chris Evert, then 17, in 1972.

55: With a victory, Andreeva could finish the season with 55 wins this season to lead all players on the WTA Tour. She has made six finals this season, joining Elena Rybakina for most.

Svitolina

11: Svitolina has now claimed outright the most top-10 wins this season with 11, surpassing Rybakina, and tying her career-best (2017). By defeating Andreeva Sunday, Svitolina could surpass her most in a season

14: She is the 14th player to reach three or more championships at the WTA Finals since the event's beginning in 1972. Among the 14, she is only the second to never hold the World No. 1 ranking, along with Gabriela Sabatini.
