Australia go down fighting to Brazil in fiery 4-2 friendly defeat

TheTools · 29.09.2026 12:31 · source · permalink

  • Socceroos led 2-1 with goals from Metcalfe and Circati

  • Two late goals sealed win for five-time world champions

Socceroos led 2-1 with goals from Metcalfe and Circati

Two late goals sealed win for five-time world champions

His first touch taking the ball goalward, Vanderson felt the pressure from Australian defenders closing in and laced a shot goalward, burying it in the top corner of Patrick Beach’s net and putting Brazil ahead. All of 132 seconds had transpired at Lang Park, and, after being forced to salvage a 1-1 draw with effectively the last touch of the game four days prior, it not only represented a perfect start for the Seleção, but also felt like it was sending a message. We’re Brazil, you’re Australia, and we’re going to put you in your place. And 90 minutes later, a level of pride had been restored when it finished 4-2 to the South Americans.

As that effort from the Monaco defender nestled in the back of the net, memories of Nestory Irankunda’s free-kick blast at Queensland Country Bank Stadium were already fading. And as Raphinha twice thundered efforts into the body of Beach a minute later, it started to feel almost like it was 2017 again, when Diego Souza scored after just 11 seconds at the MCG to set up a comprehensive 4-0 win or 2013, when Jô scored after seven minutes in Brasília to set up a 6-0 win.

The Socceroos looked shellshocked. After being savaged back home for being held to a draw against a perceived lesser footballing nation, the tourists had come out of their corner and socked them straight in the mouth.

Yet the collapse that so easily could have occurred did not eventuate. And after taking stock and getting their foot on the ball, the hosts began to work their way back into the contest. More than that, they began to show some of the same attacking intent and bravery in possession as they had flashed in Townsville four days ago.

There were some nervy exchanges as they attempted this – a 19th-minute turnover almost giving Vinícius Júnior scope to shoot, only to be closed down at the last – but they didn’t simply bunker down and try to survive to half-time. They were trying to play their way out of trouble.

“We’ve played a lot of big nations over the last few years – top opposition, top players – and we were always competitive in these games,” Jackson Irvine said. “But I think we showed tonight we can be competitive in a different kind of way. And I think that’s really exciting to see – that we’ve added that to our game.

“We still run and defend and have that part of our game, which is a non-negotiable. It has to be there. But the ability [and] desire to run, to get on the ball, to break pressure, to get yourself free. Fantastic. A lot to be pleased with. Obviously disappointing that we couldn’t [win].”

In the greater context of this side’s progression, with an Asian Cup looming in January, the result almost felt immaterial as the darting runs and slick pieces of interplay were demonstrated. It was near unrecognisable from the side that couldn’t find a way to break down Egypt just three months ago at the World Cup – the evolution that had been demanded from Tony Popovic playing out before our eyes.

“They played differently [to the World Cup] today,” Brazil boss Carlo Ancelotti said. “Honestly, I have to say, they press really well with high intensity.

“They are really well organised, and they surprise us in this sense. Considering the first game and this game … they put much more pressure in front, and we had more building up from the back. It’s a really good team, in my opinion, a really good team.

“Maybe you don’t have the quality that we have, but they are really powerful players, well organised, strong in the duels. A really good national team.”

Then in the 28th minute, it was 1-1. A Connor Metcalfe corner found its way through a mass of bodies – taking a touch off Gabriel, allowing VAR intervention to overrule the subsequent offside flag – and all the way to Alessandro Circati to flick home at the back post. Five minutes later, the Socceroos were ahead, Metcalfe receiving a lay-off from Circati and thundering a pearl of a strike beyond debutant keeper Otávio.

Forget the pyro and light show that preceded kickoff, the real fireworks were on the pitch, with more action delivered on the stroke of half-time: Harry Souttar – who didn’t have his best game – brought down Danilo and conceded a VAR-adjudged penalty converted by Raphinha. Irankunda blasted over the bar just minutes later instead of making it 3-2.

The Japanese referee, Yusuke Araki, was far busier than he probably expected for a friendly, as further fireworks broke out as the second half progressed, this time between the two teams. A 64th-minute clash between Souttar and half-time substitute Endrick sparked an all-in by the touchline that at one point saw the young Brazilian go to ground and almost clean up Socceroo assistant coach Paul Okon.

But that proved something of a turning point. After coming on for Raphinha, Endrick ultimately proved to be the spark that turned the game. He provided the cutback that allowed Bruno Guimarães to make it 3-2 minutes after the fracas. Brought down by a faint touch by Irankunda in the 79th minute, Vinícius then sealed the game from the spot.
