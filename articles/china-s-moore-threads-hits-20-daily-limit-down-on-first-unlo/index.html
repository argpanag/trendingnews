China's Moore Threads hits 20% daily limit down on first unlock day; market cap falls below 200 billion yuan — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 07.09.2026 05:25 · source · permalink

China's domestic GPU leader Moore Threads (688795.SS) opened at its 20% daily limit down on September 7, with shares at 415.48 yuan, a record low since listing, and total market capitalization falling below 200 billion yuan (approximately $29.8 billion). The immediate trigger was the unlocking of 25.7745 million restricted shares from the company's IPO offline placement, which took effect that day, expanding the free float from 30.2255 million to 56 million shares — nearly doubling it.

The stock opened at 498 yuan, down 4.11%, and was slammed to the limit-down price within roughly four minutes of continuous auction trading. This marked Moore Threads' first limit down since its debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on December 5, 2025.

Responding to the share-price move, a Moore Threads securities affairs representative said the day marked a large unlock of offline placement restricted shares, urged investors to view the resulting price change rationally, and emphasized that company fundamentals remain normal.

Unlock scale and profit cushion

The 25.7745 million shares unlocked represent 5.48% of the company's total share capital, with a lockup period of nine months from the IPO listing date. Since September 5, 2026 fell on a non-trading day, the unlock was deferred to the next trading day, September 7.

Even if sold at the limit-down price of 415.48 yuan per share, offline IPO subscribers would still book a gain of 301.2 yuan per share versus the 114.28 yuan offering price — a return of 263%. This means unlocking shareholders retain substantial unrealized gains even under extreme market conditions, giving them ample incentive to sell.

After the unlock, Moore Threads' free float as a proportion of total share capital jumped from 6.43% to 11.91%. A bigger test lies ahead: on December 7, 2026, 185.9064 million shares will unlock, representing 39.55% of total share capital. Further unlock waves of over 70 million shares and approximately 145.4 million shares are scheduled for December 2027 and December 2028, respectively.

Fundamentals versus valuation tension

On the financial front, Moore Threads' operations are on an improving trajectory. Its 2026 interim report showed first-half revenue of 1.736 billion yuan (approximately $258.7 million), up 147.42% year-on-year. Net loss attributable to the parent narrowed sharply by 95.73% to -11.56 million yuan (approximately $1.7 million), putting the company within a hair's breadth of breakeven. Excluding share-based compensation, adjusted net profit was 83.53 million yuan (approximately $12.4 million), the company's first half-year profit since inception.

However, on a non-GAAP basis excluding non-recurring items, the company still lost approximately 150 million yuan (approximately $22.3 million), of which 88.23 million yuan (approximately $13.1 million) came from government subsidies and 59.52 million yuan (approximately $8.9 million) from fair-value changes in financial assets and disposal gains. Revenue is heavily concentrated in cloud AI computing products, with growth deeply tied to China's domestic computing-power buildout. Profit quality still awaits market scrutiny.

Sector contagion and industry shifts

Moore Threads' limit down quickly spread across China's domestic GPU sector. MetaX (688802.SS) fell as much as 13% intraday, Hygon Information Technology dropped about 2%, and Cambricon weakened in tandem. This contrasted sharply with strength in the broader semiconductor market: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 3.37% the prior Friday, South Korean-listed Samsung and SK Hynix rose notably the same day, and China's STAR 50 Index gained 1.4%.

The industry landscape is undergoing profound change. China's four major domestic GPU vendors — Moore Threads, Biren Technology (06082.HK), Iluvatar CoreX (09903.HK), and MetaX — all posted rapid first-half revenue growth, but their overall scale remains small and R&D spending is enormous. Achieving large-scale profitability in core operations will take time.

More notably, with Enflame Technology's new share issuance completed and listing pending, all of China's "GPU Four Dragons" have now gone public. Combined with already-listed Cambricon, Hygon Information Technology, Iluvatar CoreX, and Jingjia Micro, plus Kunlunxin, T-Head, Vastai Technologies,瀚博半导体, and 砺算科技 in the pipeline, the scarcity of GPU concepts in the capital market is rapidly evaporating. These dozen-plus companies together account for only half of China's domestic GPU market share; the other half is held by Huawei HiSilicon (Ascend). China's domestic GPU industry is about to enter a period of intense competition, and the capital market's valuation logic and aesthetic standards will both face a reset.

Lessons from the "unlock curse"

The pattern of a small free float being hotly speculated upon after listing, followed by a sharp pullback under unlock pressure, has precedents among similar listings. AI model company MiniMax saw its stock driven to HK$1,330 (approximately $170) shortly after listing, then fall to HK$186.2 (approximately $24) after its first sizable unlock. Zhipu peaked at HK$2,980 (approximately $380) when its free float was just 11.739 million shares, or 2.67% of total share capital; after the July unlock the stock tumbled, most recently quoted at HK$1,050 (approximately $130), with an even larger unlock representing 40% of total share capital still pending next January.

CXMT faces similar pressure. Its current free float is only 6.63% of total share capital, with unlocks scheduled for January and July 2027 and January 2028. The biggest pressure point arrives on July 27, 2029, when 36.497 billion shares will unlock.

For investors, pricing in China's domestic GPU sector is shifting from a "scarcity premium" to "fundamentals-based pricing," and the unlock calendar has become a critical variable that cannot be ignored when assessing position risk.
