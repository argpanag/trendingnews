Woman tried to pull back tiger victim before he jumped into enclosure

TheTools · 14.10.2026 01:03 · source · permalink

Witness was overpowered by man before fatal attack at Yorkshire Wildlife Park

A woman tried to stop the man killed by a tiger at a zoo in Yorkshire from jumping into its enclosure, witnesses have said.

The mother “desperately tried to pull back” the victim as he attempted to jump off a bridge into the animal’s pen, a young couple who visited Yorkshire Wildlife Park on Saturday said.

But she was overpowered by the young man and was unable to prevent him jumping in full view of her two children.

Another witness said the man posed for a photograph with the animal before entering the enclosure.

Emergency services were called to the park in Doncaster at around 1.35pm after receiving reports of a man being attacked by a tiger. He was pronounced dead at the scene.

Anthony Hunt and his girlfriend Scarlet Hogan told The Telegraph that they were taking pictures of the animal, named Altai, when they saw it become alert to something nearby.

Ms Hogan, a 21-year-old pharmacy worker, said she was telling her partner that they had taken some “good shots” of the tiger looking directly at them before they realised that a man had entered the enclosure.

“We tracked the tiger’s eyes and we saw him in the corner just walking across the enclosure,” she said. “The tiger got alerted. He went into stalk mode. The tiger came flying off the ledge and approached him.

“The man started praying to the tiger, bowing down to it – the tiger looked a bit confused. Up until he got attacked, it was like he was in a trance, not in reality at all.”

Ms Hogan said the man was dancing in the pen with his phone out, adding: “It looked like he was taking selfies. Then when he got on all fours he started praying. It was the strangest thing ever.

“He had arms wide open ready for the tiger to attack him. It was quite bad. We were just stood there, frozen... the last thing we saw was the tiger’s jaw on his neck.”

The couple said they had seen the man dancing “erratically” earlier in the day when they parked their car but had thought nothing of it.

After visiting the penguin and cheetah enclosures, they arrived at the tiger pen and recognised the man, believed to be in his early to mid 20s.

A mother in the area with her two children then tried to pull him back as he attempted to jump.

“He overpowered her and jumped off. She seemed really frightened,” Mr Hunt said. “We spoke to her after and she was like, ‘I tried to pull him back’ but I guess she didn’t want to risk her own life as well.

“We did hear some gunshots but that was when they shot the ground, then the police told us [the man] was pronounced dead.”

Mr Hunt, who recently graduated with a physiotherapy degree, said: “The woman said the man was saying something religious.”

The wildlife park remained closed on Sunday as staff affected by the incident received specialist support. The park also confirmed that the tiger would not be euthanised.

Footage posted to social media showed the man crying “no, no” during the fatal attack and screaming “stop” as he pointed his finger at the animal.

The tiger could be seen approaching the man from behind and grabbing the back of his top near the neckline, lifting him off the ground.

Moments later, the tiger lurches towards his right shoulder as the man clenches both fists with his little fingers sticking out. Some have suggested that this could have been the sign for “bad” in Makaton, a form of communication used by people with learning disabilities.

As the animal swipes its paw at the man’s right side, he falls over and appears to shout “no, no, no” and later “stop” repeatedly.

Throughout the footage, witnesses can be heard shouting in distress in the background, with one man screaming “hey” twice in an effort to distract the animal.

In a separate video, said to have been filmed moments before the incident, the tiger is seen in a relaxed state, lying on the grass in its enclosure.

A man is heard asking another visitor to take a photograph of him with the animal. The guest replies “yeah” and the man then thanks them before saying “one more, one more, one more” excitedly.

It has been suggested that the man asking for the photo was the victim.

Del Taylor, a visitor, said the man had approached her to ask if she would take a picture of him with the animal.

“He was completely alone. At first he was dancing on the bridge overlooking the enclosure. He ended up standing next to me,” she told The Sun.

“My partner was recording the tiger and you can hear me having this interaction.”

Marie Miller, a visitor at the park, said she had seen the young man entering the enclosure shortly before the attack.

She said he had been “happily enjoying seeing the tiger” and had asked another visitor to take a photo of him.

“The man who I saw on the bridge with his AirPods [headphones] in, dancing, was happily enjoying seeing the tiger,” she said.

“He obviously wasn’t with anyone and he asked a total stranger to take a picture of him with the tiger in the background.”

Charlotte Pywell, an animal ranger at the park, said: “I don’t know who the guy was or what made him jump. It was not the tiger’s fault at all. It was just something else to play with. The keepers are all distraught.”
