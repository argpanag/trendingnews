Jaissle must show Newcastle are addressing Howe's final complaint at Fulham

TheTools · 06.11.2026 12:00 · source · permalink

Newcastle United head to Fulham this weekend, the scene of Eddie Howe's last Premier League game in charge of the club

Newcastle United make the trip to Fulham on Saturday afternoon, five-and-a-half months on from their last visit to Craven Cottage, which ultimately proved to be Eddie Howe's final competitive match in charge.

The Magpies put in a sorry display that day in the blistering West London heat as they were beaten 2-0 by the Cottagers and surrendered to a bottom-half finish in the Premier League table.

Sandro Tonali pulled out with injury in the warm-up which caused a late tactical reshuffle, Anthony Gordon and Kieran Trippier were unused substitutes, while Bruno Guimaraes was replaced with injury in the second half after one of the flattest performances of his four-and-a-half year stay on Tyneside.

This summer all four players left Tyneside, as well as Howe, and looking back on that Fulham game it felt at the time that it was the end of brilliant era for the club.

Change was coming in the summer and it felt like a necessary change after watching an all too familiar Newcastle defeat on the road.

"It was a disappointing performance from us," Howe said in the aftermath of that defeat in May. "For a whole host of reasons we weren't quite at the level we needed to be. There were some promising bits but by and large I think Fulham deserved to win.

"We weren't a big enough goal threat in the game, I don't think we defended with enough solidity, we weren't quite right. We tried to change things numerous times in the game but I think it was more of a mental problem than anything else."

Howe had been set to lead the club through that period of change until a change of heart over the summer saw him leave Newcastle via mutual agreement.

Matthias Jaissle came in and after a decent start to the season, riding on the wave of a new manager bounce, the 38-year-old has now hit similar turbulence in recent weeks.

Howe's complaints that Newcastle weren't a big enough goal threat and they didn't defend with enough solidity could quite easily be describing what we have seen from Jaissle's side this term.

But with four defeats in five matches in this block of fixtures since the last international break has accentuated the problems. Newcastle have been too easy to play through this season and the suitability of Jaissle's 'vertical football' system has been heavily questioned in recent weeks.

His side were booed off at half-time and full-time in Monday's 1-0 home defeat to Aston Villa and the mood around the club has quickly turned from fresh excitement and belief to apathy and frustration.

They head to Fulham on Saturday looking to arrest those issues. Jaissle spoke well in Thursday's press conference about how united things remain behind the scenes and the alignment in his vision at all levels of the club.

But there has to be evidence of that on the pitch at Craven Cottage and the meek surrender of Newcastle at the same venue under Howe just a matter of months ago is a decent yardstick to compare whether Jaissle's Newcastle is better, worse, or more of the same.

Fulham themselves have a different man in the dugout to that game in May with Marco Silva leaving to join Benfica and former Real Madrid boss Alvaro Arbeloa replacing him.

The Spaniard came under pressure after a difficult start but the Cottagers have taken seven points from the last nine available and are moving forwards rather than backwards. Another defeat for Jaissle on Saturday, and Newcastle will be irrefutably moving in the opposite direction.
