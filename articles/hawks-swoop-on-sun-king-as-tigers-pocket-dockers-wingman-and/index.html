Hawks swoop on King, but Tigers nab only recruit of day two

Richmond have secured ex-Fremantle winger Nathan O’Driscoll in a low-cost move to bolster Adem Yze’s midfield division.

The Tigers handed over pick 61 for O’Driscoll in the only completed deal on the second day of the trade period on Tuesday.

However, Hawthorn did lodge paperwork for sharpshooter Ben King to join as a free agent from Gold Coast, while Zane Duursma doubled down on his request to move to Melbourne, and will not consider staying with the Kangaroos.

O’Driscoll, who played a career-most 17 matches this season, informed the Dockers in the past week that he would explore his opportunities elsewhere after rejecting a three-year offer.

This masthead has confirmed that O’Driscoll signed a three-year contract.

The Western Bulldogs had also expressed interest in the 24-year-old, who was axed after Fremantle’s shock qualifying final loss in Perth to Hawthorn and never regained his spot.

Richmond list boss Blair Hartley said O’Driscoll would bring experience and depth.

“At 24, he will complement what we have on the list and bring a skill set that suits the way we want to play, with his running power and ability to cover the ground at a high speed,” Hartley said.

“He is a quality young man, and we are excited to see him playing on the MCG in the yellow and black next year.”

Meanwhile, the Hawks lodged paperwork for King, but there is no firm guarantee from the Suns that they will not match a bid. With Hawthorn’s Aidan Schubert and his family touring Gold Coast’s facilities on Tuesday, the hope and expectation is the young forward agrees to seek a trade.

If that happens, the Suns will be predisposed to not matching King’s free agency contract offer and accepting pick seven as compensation for losing him.

If, however, Schubert decided against a move north, there’s a risk for Hawthorn that the Suns will match the offer and force the Hawks into a trade. Hawthorn have already traded picks to Essendon for star midfielder Zach Merrett, whose move was made official on the opening day of trade period.

The strong likelihood is Schubert, taken with pick 23 in last year’s draft, will agree to a trade.

Elsewhere, out-of-contract forward Duursma won’t consider reversing his trade request and returning to North Melbourne despite new coach Jaymie Graham meeting with him in another attempt to change his mind.

Graham was officially unveiled as Alastair Clarkson’s coaching successor on Tuesday, and wasted no time getting started. But Duursma’s stance follows Kangaroos list boss Brady Rawlings saying on Monday that he still hoped they could talk him into staying if negotiations with Melbourne broke down.

Duursma wants to join the Demons, and his decision is based on wanting a fresh start rather than a cash grab, given Melbourne’s two-year offer is believed to be less than what he would earn for staying at Arden Street.

The Demons have informally presented North with three different options – pick 47 in this year’s draft, or a future second-round selection in 2027 or 2028, according to a source familiar with negotiations.

But the Demons feel their offer for Duursma, the No.4 pick in the 2023 draft, is comparable to North’s own for ex-Swan Dylan Stephens in 2023. Stephens was a No.5 pick and was traded along with pick 25 for a future end-of-first-round pick and pick 44 in that year’s draft.

But the Roos remain unmoved, another source said, and none of those picks is expected to be enough to tempt them to trade Duursma, but there is more than a week left before the trade deadline for Melbourne to potentially improve on their offer.

Duursma has kicked 28 goals in 38 matches across his first three seasons, averaging seven disposals and three marks.

There is an acceptance that Duursma has not realised his potential at North Melbourne, but Rawlings said on Monday they still envisioned him being a “major part” of their future under new coach Jaymie Graham.

“He’s requested a trade to Melbourne and being out of contract we need to look at it. But I still think we could talk him around if it wasn’t of value to us,” Rawlings said.

“There’s no bad blood here. He’s indicated he wanted a fresh start, but I’ve got no doubt that he and his manager understand what we’ve invested in him, both from a time and resources point of view [and] what we gave up three years ago at the draft.”

The Demons are also pursuing fringe Brisbane Lions forward Will McLachlan, who met with Melbourne and Essendon on Monday.

The Lions are keen to retain McLachlan, a 2024 mid-season draftee, but he wants to play regularly at AFL level after making only seven senior appearances, including just one game this year.

The 21-year-old kicked 35 goals in 11 VFL matches in 2026.

AFLPA flags free agency as key to next pay agreement

Free agency is under-used in the AFL and has not affected the equality of the competition, according to the players’ union – and could face a major overhaul as part of the league’s next pay deal.

The AFL Players’ Association’s review into free agency, seen by this masthead, concluded that the player movement mechanism had not “materially” impacted competitive balance and was not being used to its potential.

AFLPA chief executive James Gallagher, who officially replaced Paul Marsh in October last year, said free agency should create a more open player movement market and enhance competitive balance.

“Our review suggests free agency is underutilised and hasn’t materially impacted competitive balance either for better or worse,” Gallagher said.

“The gap between the bottom- and top-placed teams in the competition is no different now than what it was before the introduction of free agency.”

Why Jaymie Graham is ready to lead Kangaroos

New North Melbourne coach Jaymie Graham was told “to just be himself” by club officials before facing the media for the first time in his new role on Tuesday.

Truth be told, a relaxed Graham, 43, was already doing that for the previous 10 minutes, sipping on a coffee and enjoying a relaxed chat with midfield coach Michael Barlow at the back of the common room at Arden Street, as the bright lights awaited.

After the tumult of the Alastair Clarkson sacking last month, amid claims players were confused about the game plan and mixed messaging, the Kangaroos were after a strong but caring people manager, who could deliver a modern game plan understood by a younger generation of player, and their sometimes short attention spans.

In Graham, a former West Coast Eagles player, and a man just pipped at the post for the recent senior coaching roles at Carlton and Essendon, the Kangaroos believe they have the right man – their seventh coach since the start of the 2019 season, including Brad Scott.
