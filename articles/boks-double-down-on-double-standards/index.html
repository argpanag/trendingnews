Boks double down on double standards

TheTools · 20.11.2026 16:19 · source · permalink

Mzwandile Stick says if Ireland flyhalf Sam Prendergast were Fijian, he’d be back home drinking kava and taking tackling lessons.

The Springbok assistant coach on Friday questioned the consistency of rugby’s disciplinary process.

Prendergast was cleared by an independent disciplinary panel after being cited for an alleged hip-drop tackle that injured Fiji No 8 Peceli Yato last week.

The 23-year-old, who became the first Test player cited under World Rugby’s new focus on hip-drop tackles, successfully contested the charge and is free to face South Africa.

Bok head coach Rassie Erasmus had predicted on Monday that Prendergast would be cleared on a “technicality”, during a press conference in which he also highlighted 17 knee-drop tackles he claimed match officials had either missed or ignored in their clash against France.

Erasmus was subsequently handed a six-match suspension by World Rugby.

He then took aim at what he believes is inconsistent treatment of players from different countries.

“You know what they call a traditional drink in Fiji? Is it kava? If that was a Fijian player, [Prendergast] would be sitting in Fiji now doing some extra lessons of how to tackle. But it’s an Irish player, it’s free to play.”

Stick said coaches needed greater clarity on how the revised tackle laws should be interpreted.

“When there are laws and new rules, people need to explain,” he said. “The players look up to us as coaches. They also don’t know what’s right or wrong.

“We want to play the game clean and make sure players know how to tackle. But other parties aren’t coming to the party, and then we’re getting punished.”

He finished with a passionate appeal for equal treatment.

“I’m a proud South African. I come from Africa. I’m black from New Brighton. I’m a Xhosa. And I will never apologise to anyone about that,” he said.

“The new rules and laws need to apply to everyone. If there was a Fijian player, he would be in Fiji learning how to tackle. Things need to get better.”
