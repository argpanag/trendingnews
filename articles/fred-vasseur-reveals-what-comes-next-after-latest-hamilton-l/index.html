Fred Vasseur reveals 'what comes next' after latest Hamilton-Leclerc meltdown

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:40 · source · permalink

Ferrari boss Fred Vasseur has reflected on a Las Vegas Grand Prix where his drivers have collided once again.

Ferrari boss Fred Vasseur is planning further talks with his feuding drivers Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc after their latest collision.

On Lap 38 in Las Vegas, Leclerc attempted to pass the sister car of Hamilton at Turn 8, but misjudged and clattered into the Briton - spinning him around and earning himself a 10-second time penalty.

The collision comes after growing tensions between the drivers after their argument in Mexico City when Leclerc attacked Hamilton on the opening lap, with both drivers going off, which allowed Max Verstappen to sneak through.

The pair also made contact at Turn 2 of the Italian GP - with a clear-the-air meeting taking place in Sao Paulo, but at the next race, contact was made again as Vasseur reflected on "what comes next," as Hamilton finished seventh and Leclerc in 10th after his penalty was applied.

"What comes next is tomorrow morning - and I want to digest tonight," he told media, including RacingNews365.

"I want to have a discussion tomorrow and to also have Charles digest the situation so it is not the right place to discuss with them two minutes after the race. 

"I saw Charles, and I told him what I had to say."

Speaking further about the discussion with Leclerc immediately after the race, Vasseur indicated that it was a rather one-way discussion. 

"It was not a discussion. I spoke, and I think he apologised, that he understands blah blah blah, but the result is there: that he made a mistake."

"It is not an issue between Lewis and Fred or Charles and Fred. It is an issue between a team and two drivers.

"Honestly, it is not a matter of rules; it is not a matter of what you can do differently, except that you have to respect your team-mate.

"In this kind of race, you have to overtake the cars in front of you, and you can't stay behind; it is completely unmanageable, but you have to do it with some respect when it is your team-mate to take less risk - to perhaps come side-by-side and to come back behind. 

"But you can't put it on paper, as I think the race, nobody will write a scenario like this - and nobody can imagine a race like this.

"It means that they just have to act in a professional way."

Then, when asked why the professionalism wasn't there, Vasseur indicated that "tension" was a factor, and that, after hearing Leclerc and Hamilton's comments after the meeting in Sao Paulo that appeared to clear the air, that he "hears the same story with my kids."

Vasseur on latest Hamilton-Leclerc collision

Reflecting on the yo-yo nature of the race with the slipstream, Vasseur pointed to the fact that no other team-mates had collided. 

"I don't know what could have been the outcome of the race because it is quite difficult to imagine, but that is not the issue," he said.

"The issue is that once again, it is unacceptable, and it is a lack of respect for the team and the sponsors. [It is] the same speech as after Mexico.

"I think we had a clear discussion in Sao Paulo, and we passed the message to Charles during the race to think about the team when he was fighting - but it is not the right word, but you can't freeze the positions in this kind of race.

"Because when you have the slipstream, if you slow down, the guy who is behind you is all over you and all the other teams were able to overtake each other 20 times during the race - Antonelli, Russell, Piastri on Norris and even Hadjar on Verstappen. 

"Honestly, you cannot imagine the frustration for me, but more importantly, for all the guys at home."
