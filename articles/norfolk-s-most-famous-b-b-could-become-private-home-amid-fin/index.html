Norfolk's most famous B&B could soon become a private house.

Owners Varian and Natalie Bush have applied to change the use of parts of the Grade II listed Cley Windmill, near Wells, which they bought for £3m in 2021 and run as a B&B and wedding venue.

A planning statement said the couple have struggled to make the business financially sustainable.

"The applicants have been trying to sell the business and premises for some time with no success," it added.

"This has been through a specialist agent with a bespoke marketing campaign which is designed to limit damage to the business and bookings.

"Weddings in particular due to the long lead time need confidence in the venue.

"If publicly advertised this may deter couples from choosing the venue."

Natalie and Varian Bush pictured shortly after buying the mill five years ago with their three children Theo, Otis and Bella. (Image: Supplied)

The windmill has operated as a bed and breakfast and wedding venue for decades, with previous owners also running a shop from the site.

But Mr and Mrs Bush say the current business model is not working, and attempts to diversify have not yielded the results they need.

A heritage statement adds: "It is therefore proposed to change of use of the main windmill building to a residential dwelling only leaving the rest of the site as a wedding venue and continue the use of outbuildings as letting rooms.

"The letting rooms would be available to general guests when not in use for weddings."

Mr and Mrs Bush said the wedding and events venue which has the potential to grow will become the prominent business on site.

"The applicants will look to improve this aspect with owners accommodation in the mill allowing full management of the day, to meet overnight requirements or emergencies and provide the hands-on approach," their planning statement added.

Cley Windmill on the Norfolk coast (Image: Mark Bullimore)

"A residential dwelling within the mill will also improve borrowing capacity and would allow the owners to obtain more cost-effective funding whilst avoiding staff costs on non-earning days."

The windmill was built in the early 19th century and first appeared in an advert in the Norfolk Chronicle in 1819.

It remained in the Farthing family until 1875 and last ground corn in the early 20th century.

In 1921, it was sold to Mrs Sarah Maria Wilson, who converted it into a holiday home.

The building later passed through several owners before being turned into a guesthouse in 1983, following planning permission from North Norfolk District Council.

The windmill has since been used as a bed and breakfast, wedding venue, and restaurant.
