Ferries cancelled and disrupted as strong gales and rain hit Scotland

TheTools · 15.10.2026 03:51 · source · permalink

More than a dozen ferry routes were cancelled as strong gales and heavy rain hit Scotland.

A Met Office yellow warning covered parts of the Highlands and Western Isles until 19:00 with the risk of gusts touching 70mph (113km/h) in places. Gusts of 109mph (175km/h) were recorded near Applecross.

Heavy rain is also expected across Scotland as the remnants of Hurricane Isais hit parts of the UK.

All remaining routes on CalMac's west coast ferry network were warned to expect disruption or cancellation at short notice.

Sailings on 18 services were cancelled, with normal operating hours expected to resume on Thursday.

Anyone travelling is urged to check if their service is running before heading out.

Barra Airport and Benbecula Airport were closed due to strong winds, with all flights cancelled.

Some roads near the north-west coast were blocked by fallen trees, including the B8056 near Shieldaig and the B863 near Kinlochleven.

What has been happening with Hurricane Isaias?

Hurricane Isaias was the first Atlantic hurricane of the season and made landfall in the southern US last Friday.

It weakened rapidly and ceased to be a hurricane but its remnants threw heavy rain across the eastern side of North America.

A powerful jet stream scooped up the leftovers over the Atlantic, which developed into an area of low pressure undergoing what meteorologists describe as explosive cyclogenesis - also referred to as a weather bomb.

However, the centre of the storm will hit hundreds of miles away, close to Iceland.

In the UK, the far north-west of Scotland has had the worst of the winds.

A Met Office yellow weather warning was valid until 19:00 BST, and warned of a high risk of moderate impacts including disruption to rail, air and ferry transport, delays on roads and bridges - especially for high-sided vehicles - and possible short-term power cuts.

Widespread gusts of 50-60mph (80.5km/h-96.6km/h) were forecast with 70mph (113km/h) possible for exposed coasts and hills around the middle of the day.

Outside the warning area, Shetland saw 50mph (80.5km/h) gusts with less disruption expected.

Gusts of about 40mph (64.4km/h) were forecast for other parts of Scotland, Northern Ireland, northern England and north-west Wales.

The wind and rain is expected to leave overnight, with colder and drier conditions forecast for later in the week.

September marked the start of storm season and the Met Office recently released storm names for 2026/27.
