Davis Cup Finals: GB's Henry Patten on ending Harri Heliovaara partnership

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:52 · source · permalink

Henry Patten (right) will pair up with Neal Skupski for the 2027 season

  • Published
    25 November 2026

Britain's Henry Patten agrees he was "ruthless" in splitting with his doubles partner and close friend Harri Heliovaara, just days after they received the year-end world number one trophy.

Heliovaara and Patten joined forces in 2024 and won Wimbledon together for the second time in July.

In a post on his blog, the Finnish player detailed the disappointment, shock and anger he felt when Patten phoned him with the news.

"I got a lot of stick for calling Harri to tell him," Patten told BBC Sport before Great Britain's Davis Cup quarter-final against Germany.

"I was crumpled over the toilet after the match and Harri, as he often does, had left very quickly to catch a flight back to his family.

"It was ruthless - it was a really difficult decision for me to make for two reasons.

"The first one is we are year-end number ones and we have a great track record together.

"Secondly, me and Harri grew really close during that time. I still consider Harri one of, if not my best friend.

"He's invited to my wedding in the summer. It was probably the first time in my career that I have had to make a decision like that, and I didn't enjoy it."

Patten's new partner will be compatriot Neal Skupski, who says he decided in the space of a "very stressful few hours" to end his pairing with American Christian Harrison.

Davis Cup Finals: Great Britain v Germany

Thursday, 26 November from 09:00 GMT

Patten and Skupski could play their first match as a regular pair against Kevin Krawietz and Tim Putz to decide Britain's quarter-final against Germany on Thursday.

There is little doubt the dust is still settling on a bruising weekend.

"It was a very uncomfortable few hours, but we did speak over the phone for a long time,” Patten continued.

"Obviously I wasn't expecting him to be over the moon about it, and he absolutely has every right to be angry.

"But in that discussion I said I feel like this decision is based on the tennis. I feel that for both of us [it] has stagnated a bit.

"With some time, we'll still remain good friends. That's certainly my hope."

Patten says he has looked up to Skupski for a long time, and enjoys the energy he brings to the court.

He made contact with Ken Skupski, who coaches his brother, last Friday, and did not speak to Neal until he and Harrison had lost in the last four of the ATP Finals the following day.

"It was a very stressful few hours," Skupski recalled.

"After coming off the court, I went to lunch, spoke to [British doubles coach] Louis Cayer, who couldn't really tell me too much as other [British] players are involved.

"It was down to me and my brother to decide what I wanted to do and, at the end of the day, there was only one decision.

"That was the most difficult part - speaking to Christian. We spoke at the hotel, I told him that this opportunity doesn't come along very often to play with another Briton who is a world number one and be able to play Davis Cup and the Olympics in the future.

"So, I think he understood. I don't know if he quite believed me that we hadn't spoken beforehand, but it was two or three hours after the match in which I decided.

"I'm very happy and comfortable with my decision."

Fery working on returning Zverev serve

The singles in Bologna are likely to feature Arthur Fery and Cameron Norrie and, as British number one, Fery can expect one of the toughest tasks in men's tennis.

The 24-year-old will be up against world number one and two-time Grand Slam champion Alexander Zverev in the second singles match.

Zverev beat Fery in the Wimbledon semi-finals and again at the Vienna Open, with the Briton only winning two games the second time around.

"I've played him a few times already this year, so it's not going to be a completely new thing, which will help me," Fery told BBC Sport earlier in November.

"The serve for me is a challenge because he's close to 2m and I'm on the shorter side of the players out there.

"I struggled to return him, the ball just gets up a lot on me, but I have been working in practice to fight that.

"That's going to serve me not only against Zverev but in general."

I'm going to use the lessons from Wimbledon - Fery

Norrie is expected to face Jan-Lennard Struff in the first singles.

The German reached the quarter-finals of Wimbledon and, at 39 in the world, is currently 13 places above Norrie.

Norrie needs little motivation, but the fact Leon Smith is in his last week as captain after over 16 years in the role may help a little.

“I'm so grateful to be here and I think it adds a little bit of motivation to have Leon in our corner. I want to leave it all out there for him,” Norrie added.

“I really like playing with him on the bench there, I feel very relaxed, very chill, he knows when is the right moment to say something.

"He's a great leader and it's a great honour to be in his team."
