Local surfer identified after Geraldton shark attack

A surfer who was attacked by a shark at a Geraldton beach on Monday morning has been identified as local man Mel Ismail.

The Department of Primary Industries and Regional Development reported the incident occurred at 9.45am at Glenfield Beach.

It is understood Ismail, aged in his 50s, had been surfing alone when he was attacked.

Shortly after, another local surfer arrived at the beach and spotted Ismail waving about 40 metres from the shore, surrounded by blood.

He paddled to the site of the attack on his own board and dragged Ismail back to shore.

They were then assisted by a group of teenagers who helped administer emergency first aid and tied him a makeshift tourniquet on his leg to stem the bleeding.

Authorities have yet to identify the size or species of the shark, but it is understood to have bitten a section of Ismail’s board.

St John Ambulance said Ismail was rushed to Geraldton Hospital from the beach as a priority one emergency. He arrived about 10.30am.

It is understood he has suffered leg injuries, and he is in a serious but stable condition. People with knowledge of the incident said it looked possible Ismail could lose part of his leg.

Ismail is a prolific hobby surfer, a keen sportsman and works for the region’s emergency services.

A Department of Fire and Emergency Services spokesperson said they were aware of Ismail’s injuries, and he had been surfing while off-duty.

“Our thoughts are with him, his family, friends and DFES colleagues,” they said.

“I would like to thank the first responders who went to his aid.

“The DFES wellness team and regional staff are providing support to the injured staff member, his family and his work colleagues.”

City of Greater Geraldton Mayor Jerry Clune said the attack had come as a shock to everyone in the community.

“We are anxiously waiting to hear about what has occurred here, and we are certainly hoping for the best, but we are still gathering information about what has actually occurred,” he said.

“A lot of people use the area for early morning swims, and it is not known for having a lot of shark activity – at least not in recent years.”

Clune urged the community to adhere to warnings from the DPIRD and local police in the area as the city seeks further information on the attack.

“We urge the community to stay safe and at the moment our thoughts are with the person who has been attacked,” he said.

“Once we know the extent of the incident we will be providing further comment.

“This has come as a real shock to community.”

The City of Greater Geraldton has closed parts of the beach, which is a popular surfing spot.

“A portion of Glenfield Beach south of the intersection of Glenfield Beach Drive and Surfside Terrace in the vicinity of BEN sign CGG22 has been temporarily closed following a shark incident,” a spokesperson said.

“To ensure community safety all recreational water users are advised to avoid the area as the shark may still be in the vicinity.”

Opposition fisheries spokeswoman and local member Kirrilee Warr said the latest attack prompted questions about how the state government managed sharks in WA.

“Our thoughts are with the victim, their family and the first responders involved in this incident. At this stage, little is known and we are seeking further updates from authorities and the minister in relation to the incident,” she said.

“What we do know is that fewer than 300 sharks are currently tagged under the state’s monitoring program. We have previously called on the government to undertake a review of the shark monitoring system to ensure it is providing the best possible information and protection for coastal communities.”

Ocean users are being advised to be cautious as authorities investigate the incident.
