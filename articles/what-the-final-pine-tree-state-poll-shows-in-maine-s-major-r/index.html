What the final Pine Tree State Poll shows in Maine's major races

TheTools · 02.11.2026 19:11 · source · permalink

The University of New Hampshire Survey Center has released its final Pine Tree State Poll before Election Day, which is Tuesday, Nov. 3.

The poll shows that in Maine's high-profile U.S. Senate race, 54% of likely voters said they will vote or already have voted for Democratic challenger Troy Jackson, while 45% said they will vote or already have voted for Republican incumbent Sen. Susan Collins. Less than 1% of likely voters support another candidate and less than 1% are undecided. In the previous Pine Tree State Poll released in September, 51% of those surveyed supported Jackson while 47% supported Collins.

According to the latest poll, 60% of Jackson supporters said their vote is more against Collins, 38% said their vote is more for Jackson and 2% said neither of those things is true or they were unsure.

While the poll shows that Jackson is leading in the race, 56% of likely voters in Maine believe that Collins will win the election while only 26% think that Jackson will be victorious. The remaining 18% of voters are unsure about the outcome.

The poll also shows that 48% of likely voters said their vote is in opposition to President Donald Trump while 14% said their vote is in support. The remaining 37% said their vote is unrelated to Trump.

Polling on Maine governor's race

Democratic candidate Hannah Pingree continues to lead in the gubernatorial poll, as 51% of likely voters said they will vote or have voted for the former Maine House speaker. This marks an increase from the 48% she received in the September poll.

The latest poll shows that 40% of likely voters said they will vote or have voted for Republican candidate Bobby Charles, who also received 40% in the previous poll.

Independent candidate Rick Bennett received 7% of the vote, an increase of 2% from the September poll.

The remaining 2% of likely voters surveyed were split between supporting another candidate and being undecided about their vote.

In addition, the poll found that 61% of likely voters believe that Pingree will win the race for governor, while 26% believe that Charles will be victorious. Only 1% of likely voters think that Bennett will win the gubernatorial election, while the remaining 12% of those surveyed are unsure about the outcome.

Polling for congressional races

The race for Maine's 2nd Congressional District seat will help determine the balance of power in the U.S. House, as incumbent Rep. Jared Golden, a Blue Dog Democrat, decided not to run for reelection.

The final Pine Tree State Poll shows that former Gov. Paul LePage, a Republican, holds a lead over Democratic State Auditor Matt Dunlap in the race. According to the poll, 52% of likely CD2 voters said they will or have voted for LePage, while 47% said they will or have voted for Dunlap. The remaining 1% either support another candidate or are undecided. In the September poll, Dunlap (51%) had a lead over LePage (45%) with a larger margin (approximately 5%) of undecided voters.

The poll also found that in the race for Maine's 1st Congressional District seat, Democratic incumbent Rep. Chellie Pingree holds a sizeable lead (65%) over Republican challenger Ron Russell (32%) with 3% of likely CD1 voters supporting another candidate and less than 1% of likely voters were undecided. This marks an increase in support for Pingree from the previous poll, where she had 57% to Russell's 35%.

More about the Pine Tree State Poll

The UNH Survey Center said that 1,722 Maine residents completed the survey online between Tuesday, Oct. 27, and Saturday, Oct. 31. The margin of sampling error for the survey is +/- 2.4%.

"We try to be pretty careful when we characterize something as a lead or as tied," said Sean McKinley, project director at the UNH Survey Center. "We could do this survey again in a week and nothing might have changed in the fundamentals of the race, but we might have a different result. When the races are close, that could mean that the other person is now ahead."

McKinley said that while all of the polls strive to be as accurate as possible, none of the results should be taken as gospel.
