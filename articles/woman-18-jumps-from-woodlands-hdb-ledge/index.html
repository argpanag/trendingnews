Woman, 18, jumps from Woodlands HDB ledge

TheTools · 17.11.2026 05:01 · source · permalink

An 18-year-old woman was apprehended under the Mental Health (Care and Treatment) Act after jumping from the 16th-floor corridor ledge of an HDB block in Woodlands on Sunday (Nov 15).

She landed on a safety life air pack deployed at the foot of Block 688E Woodlands Drive 75 and was conveyed unconscious to Khoo Teck Puat Hospital.

In response to AsiaOne's queries, the police said they received a call for assistance at about 8pm.

Upon arrival, officers spotted the woman sitting on a corridor ledge.

As it was assessed that she might pose a danger to herself, officers from the Crisis Negotiation Unit and the Singapore Civil Defence Force (SCDF) responded to the incident.

SCDF told AsiaOne it was alerted to the incident at about 8.05pm and found the woman sitting on a corridor ledge on the 16th floor.

A safety life air pack was then deployed and the woman subsequently fell onto it.

Rescue operation lasted about an hour

According to Stomp, a witness known as Shazzy arrived at the scene at around 8.30pm and saw police officers cordoning off the area.

She said officers from SCDF's Disaster Assistance and Rescue Team (DART) arrived about half an hour later and made their way to the roof of the building.

However, before rescuers could take instructions from the police and bring the woman to safety, she appeared to make a phone call before jumping from the ledge, Shazzy recounted.

The witness, who shared a video of the incident with Stomp, said she heard a loud thump followed by screams from onlookers.

According to the police, the woman jumped from the ledge at about 9.05pm and landed on the safety life air pack deployed below.

Shazzy also claimed to have seen a paramedic performing cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) on the woman before she was taken away on a stretcher.

SCDF confirmed that the woman was conveyed to Khoo Teck Puat Hospital.

The police said she was taken to the hospital unconscious and was subsequently apprehended under Section 7 of the Mental Health (Care and Treatment) Act.
