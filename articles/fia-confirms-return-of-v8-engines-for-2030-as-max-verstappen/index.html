FIA confirms return of V8 engines for 2030 as Max Verstappen takes Las Vegas pole

TheTools · 21.11.2026 08:40 · source · permalink

Formula One

Formula One is preparing for a return to its traditional engine philosophy, with the FIA confirming plans to introduce V8 turbo hybrid power units in 2030, as Red Bull's Max Verstappen claimed pole position for Sunday's Las Vegas Grand Prix following a penalty for newly crowned world champion Kimi Antonelli.

The FIA announced on Friday that an agreement had been reached to replace the current controversial power units, which split power between internal combustion and electric components, with simpler V8 turbo hybrid engines featuring reduced electric power.

The decision was unanimously agreed with Formula One Management, all 11 teams and the six engine manufacturers, with every power-unit manufacturer committing to the proposal. The new 2.1-litre V8 engines will run on sustainable fuels and are expected to be lighter, cheaper and less complex than the current units.

"The overriding objective is to deliver a power unit which is a lot less complex, lighter and cheaper than the current power units," the FIA said in a statement.

The next step will be to present a definitive 2030 regulatory framework to the FIA World Motor Sport Council meeting in Shanghai next month.

FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem described the agreement as a significant milestone, believing the return of V8 engines would reconnect the sport with its traditional identity.

"The return of V8 engines represents an opportunity to reconnect with the DNA of this championship, bringing back the sound, emotion and pure racing that is loved worldwide," he said. "Our vision is clear: lighter, simpler and more affordable cars that put driver skill at the heart of the competition."

Meanwhile, Verstappen secured pole position for Sunday's Las Vegas race after Antonelli was handed a five-place grid penalty for an internal combustion engine change. The Red Bull driver was actually second quickest in qualifying, finishing 0.258 seconds behind Mercedes' Antonelli, who topped the timesheets with a fastest lap of 1:33.893.

However, the penalty means Verstappen will start from the front of the grid alongside Mercedes' George Russell, who was third quickest with a time of 1:34.224.

"I think we had a solid qualifying. We executed well," Verstappen said. "My first lap in Q3 was pretty much as good as it can be, so I think to be on the front row is very good for us."

Russell was encouraged by Mercedes' performance, while Antonelli admitted he had struggled to get comfortable in his car despite setting the fastest time.

"It was a very weird qualifying, I couldn't get the tyres working, zero feeling with the car and the brake pedal was very long," the 20-year-old said.

McLaren's Lando Norris was fourth quickest, with Red Bull's Isack Hadjar completing the top five.

It was a frustrating session for Ferrari, with both Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton failing to advance into the final qualifying round. Leclerc's hopes were dented after he locked up at Turn Seven and could not complete another flying lap in Q2.

"We didn't do a good job. I did a mistake, the only lap I was given. I've been very poor," he admitted.

The Monegasque driver was also pessimistic about Ferrari's chances of making up ground during Sunday's race, pointing to a lack of straight-line speed.

"We are losing a second down the straights – so there's literally nowhere we'll be able to overtake," he said.

With Formula One already laying the groundwork for a new engine era, attention now turns to Las Vegas, where Verstappen will look to convert pole position into victory.  |  IOL Sport
