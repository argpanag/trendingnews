Hit movie filmed in Suffolk to be adapted into West End musical

TheTools · 19.10.2026 23:27 · source · permalink

A hit movie set and filmed in Suffolk is set be adapted for the West End stage.

Yesterday, the 2019 romantic comedy directed by Danny Boyle and written by Richard Curtis, is being reimagined for the theatre by Curtis and his wife, broadcaster and producer Emma Freud, with Boyle returning to direct the stage version.

Ms Freud said: "Putting Yesterday onto the West End stage feels like the biggest project we have ever done.

"It's very exciting, and a completely different way of working to film and television – it feels like creating a family rather than a piece of art."

The film follows struggling Lowestoft musician Jack Malik, played by Himesh Patel, who wakes up after a global blackout to discover he is the only person who remembers The Beatles.

He begins performing their songs as his own, quickly finding fame while navigating the impact on his relationships, particularly with his long time friend and manager Ellie, played by Lily James.

Ed Sheeran also appears in the film, helping Jack’s rapid rise in the music industry.

Much of the movie was shot in Suffolk, with locations including Halesworth, Dunwich, Lowestoft, Ramsholt, and the Henham Estate, as filming got underway for Danny Boyle's new movie.

Emma Freud, pictured with husband Richard Curtis, recently received an honorary doctorate from the University of Suffolk (Image: University of Suffolk/Gregg Brown)

Freud and Curtis chose to set the story in the area because of their personal connection to the county.

Ms Freud said: "We set Yesterday in Suffolk because you have to write about what you know, and what I know is Suffolk.

"That Christmas was also set within the county as being somewhere that has magic inside it, and I do believe that Suffolk has magic inside it – it's made me who I am, it's given my children all their best qualities, it's a really remarkable, rare, beautiful, creative, independent, interesting county."

Production work on the stage adaptation is ongoing, with plans to involve Suffolk-based suppliers in the project.
