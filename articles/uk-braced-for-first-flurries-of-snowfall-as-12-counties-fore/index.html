UK braced for first flurries of snowfall as 12 counties forecast within weeks

TheTools · 03.10.2026 07:42 · source · permalink

The UK is set to experience its first autumn snow within the coming 10 days, according to forecasts. Weather charts from WX Charts indicate flurries of snow across certain regions of the UK before mid-October arrives.

The projections, utilising the GFS system, have been generated by WX Charts, which serves as a reliable resource for communities nationwide monitoring upcoming weather conditions.

Charts on the forecasting platform show the white stuff arriving on October 12, with accumulations anticipated across northern Scotland, where as much as 4cm could settle. Meteorologists suggest the flurries may make a return two days afterwards on October 14.

Twelve counties could witness the white stuff during the 48 to 72-hour period, encompassing Orkney and Caithness, Sutherland, Ross-shire and Cromartyshire and Inverness-shire. Additional counties likely to be affected include Nairnshire.

Morayshire is also expected to receive a covering, while Banffshire, Aberdeenshire and Argyllshire are unlikely to escape either. Kincardineshire and Angus round off the list - bringing the total number of counties preparing for flurries of the white stuff to 12, reports Birmingham Live.

A BBC update for October 12 to October 25 states: "Low pressure systems may become more common across the UK around the middle of October, so a wetter week may be in store between about the 12th and 19th of October.

"There should be higher chances of periodic rain alongside possibly stronger winds at times, with rainfall amounts potentially above normal or at least near average. The western and northern UK may be wetter than southern and eastern areas.

"Despite the rain, it is more probable than not that temperatures will mostly stay above the October average.

"The second half of the month has some signs of high pressure reasserting itself. So another drier than average period could be in store, although that does not preclude a little rain here and there.

"When nights are clear and still there could again be chances of fog patches and chillier conditions, but daytime temperatures should mostly be near or higher than the long-term average.

"There is no sign yet of any notably cold outbreaks."

Netweather TV adds of October 12 to October 19: "This week looks set to see more unsettled weather develop, with high pressure centres to the west and east of Britain and a trough settling into the gap in between.

"The early part of the week is expected to continue mostly dry and settled, but with potential for a northerly blast which may bring some showers into northern Scotland and the eastern side of England.

"The trough looks set to establish later in the week when there is a greater likelihood of rain belts interspersed with brighter showery weather establishing across the country.

"Temperatures have potential to drop below the seasonal average early in the week, but recovering to near or slightly above normal towards the end of the week.

"Mean temperatures are forecast to be very close to normal overall. Rainfall totals are forecast to be much closer to normal than during Week 2, but with some uncertainty over the extent of the wetter weather later in the week.

"Drier weather early in the week should prevent the week as a whole from being unusually wet.

"Sunshine totals will probably end up above normal for most regions due to sunny weather early in the week associated with polar air masses and high pressure."
