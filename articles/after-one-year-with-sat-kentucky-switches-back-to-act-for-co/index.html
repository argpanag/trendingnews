After one year with SAT, Kentucky switches back to ACT for college exam

TheTools · 19.11.2026 18:20 · source · permalink

After just one year using the SAT, Kentucky juniors will go back to the ACT for its state-funded college admissions exam.

The Kentucky Department of Education made the announcement Thursday, saying a 2026 state law required it to run a new competitive procurement, and now, the ACT is cheaper.

The ACT contract is for four years beginning in spring 2027, with an option for up to five one-year extensions.

KDE said back in 2025, the ACT cost around $38 a student. The SAT cost $30 a student when it made the switch.

Now, with the ACT, it will cost even less, $28.50 a student.

KDE says that will save the state $500,000 a year compared to the cost of it in 2025.

All high school juniors in the state are required to take the test offered by the state. Students can still choose to take the SAT, but it would not be covered or required by Kentucky.

The new contract also includes more free support for students, families and educators, including free online practice tests, KDE said.
