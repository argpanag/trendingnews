School closures loom on separate days next month as teacher pay dispute escalates

TheTools · 26.09.2026 01:22 · source · permalink

Hundreds of secondary schools are set to close on October 14th after members of the Association of Secondary Teachers Ireland (ASTI) voted overwhelmingly for industrial action.

A work-to-rule will commence on October 5th but both the ASTI and Irish National Teachers’ Organisation (INTO) have confirmed their members will stage a one-day strike nine days later.

The INTO, which represents a substantial majority in the primary school sector, has said it was planning for a second one-day strike a week later, on October 21st.

The Teachers Union of Ireland (TUI), which like the ASTI represents about 20,000 secondary schoolteachers, is due to conclude its ballot next week and if, as expected, its members also vote to take action in pursuit of a new public-sector pay deal virtually all of the country’s schools would be likely to close.

Ninety-five per cent of ASTI members who participated in the ballot voted in favour of taking action, with a turnout of 56 per cent.

.

In a statement issued on Friday, recently appointed ASTI general secretary Linda Kelly said the ballot outcome “gives a clear message to the Government about the depth of feeling amongst teachers”.

“Teachers’ pay has fallen significantly behind inflation,” she said. “Meanwhile, the cost of living continues to climb. The Government’s approach to date has shown scant regard for the day-to-day realities faced by teachers and other public-service workers.”

In a briefing to members, INTO general secretary John Boyle said the union had recruited 1,000 new members in recent weeks and that a second one-day strike, on October 21st, was already being planned.

“There is no equivocation on the part of our union – we are in this to win this,” he said. “We will secure a fair pay deal for all our members and all public servants, come what may.”

In addition to education, health and local government services are set to be hit by the action after similar votes by Fórsa, Siptu, Unite and the Irish Nurses and Midwives Organisation. Many of their members will begin their action next Wednesday before escalating to the one-day strike.

Members of the Garda Representative Association have also announced several days of action while the Association of Garda Sergeants and Inspectors has scheduled a delegate meeting to consider the issue for October 5th.

The dispute has been prompted by the breakdown in preliminary talks between the Government and union representatives who sought guarantees that cost-of-living issues would be addressed in the early part of any negotiation.

The Government has said it is willing to engage in talks without preconditions at any point.
