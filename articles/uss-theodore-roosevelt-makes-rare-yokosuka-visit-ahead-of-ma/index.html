USS Theodore Roosevelt makes rare Yokosuka visit ahead of major US-Japan exercise

TheTools · 16.10.2026 01:21 · source · permalink

YOKOSUKA NAVAL BASE, Japan — The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt steamed into Tokyo Bay on Friday morning, becoming the first visiting U.S. carrier to dock at 7th Fleet’s homeport in more than four years as its strike group prepares for a major U.S.-Japan military exercise.

The San Diego-based carrier entered the bay with the guided-missile cruiser USS Chosin and guided-missile destroyer USS Decatur, arriving around 9 a.m. and taking the berth used by the USS George Washington, which is deployed to the Middle East.

The ships will resupply and undergo maintenance before participating in Keen Sword, scheduled for Oct. 19-29, according to a Navy news release Friday.

“Port visits like this are vital for crew morale and mission readiness, allowing our Sailors to rest, recharge, and prepare to operate seamlessly alongside our allies during exercise Keen Sword,” Capt. Will Mathis, the Roosevelt’s skipper, said in the release.

Carrier Strike Group 9 includes approximately 5,000 sailors and Marines, 7th Fleet spokesman Cmdr. Matthew Comer said by email Friday.

The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt steams toward Yokosuka Naval Base, Japan, on Oct. 16, 2026. (Akifumi Ishikawa/Stars and Stripes)

The Theodore Roosevelt is the first U.S. carrier not homeported at Yokosuka to visit the base since the USS Abraham Lincoln stopped here in May 2022.

The visit drew protests from three local organizations concerned about the risks of hosting nuclear-powered warships and the possibility of more frequent carrier visits.

In a statement provided by attorney Masahiko Goto, a group representative, the organizations warned that a natural disaster could cause a nuclear accident at the base.

“Given that a major earthquake or tsunami could strike Yokosuka Naval Base at any time, the frequent port calls by nuclear-powered aircraft carriers only increase the risk that such an event could trigger a nuclear accident,” the groups said.

The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt arrives at Yokosuka Naval Base, Japan, on Oct. 16, 2026. (Jonathan Baez/Stars and Stripes)

Tugboats assist the aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt as it pulls into Yokosuka Naval Base, Japan, Oct. 16, 2026. (Alex Wilson/Stars and Stripes)

They also expressed “profound concern” about potential incidents involving visiting personnel, citing the recent arrest of a U.S. Marine on Okinawa and unspecified reports of incidents during previous port calls.

Lance Cpl. Devin Jacob Ballard, 20, was arrested Oct. 4 on suspicion of killing and robbing a Japanese woman at a hotel in Naha. Ballard has denied the allegations, according to police.

U.S. military personnel on Okinawa are under monthlong restrictions that include a midnight-to-5 a.m. curfew and increased supervision at installation gates.

Those restrictions do not extend to the visiting strike group at Yokosuka. Its personnel will follow standard U.S. Forces Japan liberty guidelines and regulations for visiting U.S. forces, Comer said.

Commanders reminded their crews that they are “expected to uphold good order and discipline, comply with the liberty policy, and treat their Japanese hosts with respect,” according to the Navy release.

Following Keen Sword, the Theodore Roosevelt is expected to deploy to the Middle East, where it could potentially relieve the Yokosuka-based George Washington.
