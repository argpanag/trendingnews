NatWest slashes 1,000 jobs as it rolls out AI

TheTools · 30.10.2026 20:33 · source · permalink

NatWest has cut 1,000 jobs in the last three months as it pushes forward with AI advances.

The bank said on Friday that it had cut a total of 1,400 jobs so far this year, with the majority coming in the last quarter. The figures did not include the impact of the acquisition of wealth manager Evelyn Partners.

A quarter of the role reductions were down to a recent sale of Mentor, the bank’s business consultancy service.

Paul Thwaite, the chief executive of NatWest, said: “We’ve acquired some businesses and we’ve also sold some businesses. But there’s no single change that’s influencing the numbers.”

The job cuts come as NatWest rolls out AI across the bank, including for customers.

Its fraud triage agent, called Cora, has dealt with 52,000 credit card customer issues since it was launched at the end of August, the bank said.

“Roles are changing. We’ve got new roles that are very much dedicated to AI,” Mr Thwaite added.

“We’ve got other roles that are being changed because of AI. My job is to make sure we get the right people with the right skills doing the roles.”

NatWest is the latest bank to announce significant job cuts. Earlier this week, Barclays said it was planning to cut 5,000 jobs this year in an AI-related cull.

HSBC is planning to use AI to reduce the ranks of its UK financial advisers by almost 70pc, it emerged last month.

Bill Winters, the boss of Standard Chartered, was criticised earlier this year for referring to AI replacing “lower-value human capital” as he announced 8,000 redundancies.

Mr Thwaite said: “The way that we’re using AI is for the benefit of our customers and for the benefit of our colleagues. It’s helping manage our risk better and protect our customers better.”

Job cuts across banking also come ahead of new laws that will make it harder for companies to let staff go from next year.

Rules on unfair dismissal have been strengthened under the Employment Rights Act 2025. Several surveys show that companies are expected to be more cautious about hiring as a result.

Some evidence also suggests companies are considering workforce reductions before the new rules take effect. A study commissioned by Acas, the public body that advises on workplace relations and dispute resolution, has found that almost half of large businesses are likely to make staff redundancies by January 2027.

NatWest’s job numbers came as the bank reported pre-tax profits of £2.2bn in the third quarter, flat year-on-year. The results were slightly above City estimates.

‘We need strong banks’

From next year, banks will face higher taxes as John Healey plans to double the bank surcharge from 3pc to 6pc for 15 months from January.

While Mr Thwaite refrained from criticising the increase directly, he said it was important that it remained time-limited.

“If we genuinely want a strong economy, we need strong banks, and that means banks using the capital they’ve got to support customers whether they’re households or whether they’re businesses because ultimately, that’s what drives the underlying growth picture,” Mr Thwaite said.

“The Chancellor laid out, obviously, an increase in the surcharge but also that it was a temporary increase. I think that’s very important because the time limit provides welcome clarity both to banks and businesses so we can plan.”

Other banks have criticised the tax increase, with JP Morgan saying it “lacks principle”. The Wall Street giant said it was unfair for the Chancellor to “single out” banks.

Shares in NatWest dropped by as much as 9.4pc, with trading briefly halted, after the bank said that it expected a slowdown in interest income next year.

Katie Murray, the chief financial officer, said on a call with analysts that the bank expected its net interest margin, the difference between what it earns from lending and what it pays on deposits, to be “flatter” in 2027.

Since Mr Thwaite took over as chief executive in 2023, he has made developing the bank’s wealth management capabilities a major part of its plans to diversify away from income tied to interest-rate changes.

This year, the bank completed the £2.7bn acquisition of Evelyn Partners, Britain’s third-largest wealth manager, its largest acquisition since the disastrous takeover of ABN Amro just before the financial crisis.
