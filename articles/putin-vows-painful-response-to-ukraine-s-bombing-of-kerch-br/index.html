Putin vows ‘painful’ response to Ukraine’s bombing of Kerch bridge

TheTools · 19.11.2026 19:07 · source · permalink

Russian president makes remarks after Ukraine strikes strategic bridge to Crimea for third time in a week

Vladimir Putin has said Russia’s response to the bombing of the Kerch bridge to Crimea will be “painful”, after Ukraine attacked the strategic connection for the third time this week.

The warning was delivered at a press conference after Putin met Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in China. The Russian president claimed he had been considering agreeing to local ceasefires until the Kerch road bridge was destroyed.

“After the strikes on the Crimean Bridge, we will not negotiate,” Putin said, insisting the bridge was used only by civilians. “We will respond to these attacks. Our response will be far more painful than Kyiv’s attack on the bridge.”

A series of explosions were heard in the Ukrainian capital in the mid-afternoon, and there were reports that a Russian drone had struck near the Dutch embassy in Kyiv, though it was unclear if it was deliberately targeted.

Shortly after midnight on Tuesday, Ukraine’s military blew up the central span of the Kerch road bridge in a complex air raid that involved Ruta cruise missiles made by a Dutch company as well as several Ukrainian cruise missiles and drones.

Russia threatened the Netherlands afterwards, with the Russian embassy in the country asking: “Is the Dutch government openly taking part in the conflict?” after the company, Destinus, had said it was a key element in the attack.

Tom Berendsen, the Dutch foreign minister, confirmed that “a projectile struck behind” the embassy in Kyiv. “Fortunately, our colleagues are unharmed, though there is some minor damage to the building,” he said, adding that officials were now investigating the incident.

A 26-year-old man had been killed and five others injured in the afternoon attacks, city authorities said.

Ukrainian military sources said the destruction of the Kerch bridge had been deliberately timed to embarrass Putin before the extraordinary summit, in which the Russian president sat down with Trump and Xi to discuss the war and other issues.

After his talks with Putin, Trump dialled into a meeting of Ukraine’s European allies in Berlin, during which he reportedly asked why European countries continued to spend billions on military support for Kyiv and said Putin was ready to negotiate a peace deal.

The British prime minister, Andy Burnham, when asked at a press conference whether Trump was painting too optimistic a picture of Russia’s desire for peace, said: “Too rosy a picture? No, certainly not. That was not the phone call that we heard from the president this afternoon. I would say quite the opposite, to be honest.”

Ukraine followed up Tuesday’s bombing of the Kerch bridge with a further attack on the remaining rail bridge to Crimea on Wednesday night. Train services were halted as a result of the strikes, which Ukraine’s military said were conducted with Neptune missiles made in Ukraine.

Sergei Aksyonov, the Kremlin-appointed governor of Crimea, said on Wednesday night that there had been another attack on the bridge, then added on Thursday that trains would start crossing from “approximately 4pm”.

Emergency repair workers had made “heroic efforts” to restore the track, the governor said. Nobody was reported wounded as a result of the incident, Russia’s transport ministry added.

Ukraine’s military said the Kerch bridge was “the main land artery the Russian army uses to supply its occupation groupings in the south with fuel, ammunition and equipment” – in contrast to the comments made by Putin.

The $3.6bn (£2.7bn) bridge, built by Russia after its occupation of Crimea, has been struck three times in a week. Initial attacks on Monday had shown that Tuesday’s full-scale assault would be possible.

A 227-metre section of the road spanning the Kerch strait collapsed into the Sea of Azov after a complex air raid that had been prepared and planned over several weeks.

A source in Ukraine’s armed forces said this week’s attacks were designed to embarrass Putin on the world stage. “The bridge is a symbol of Putin’s rule. Destroying it sends a message to the other two great leaders that Putin is not as strong as he pretends,” they said.

For Ukraine, the bridge across the Kerch strait is a hated symbol of Russian arrogance and colonial thinking. Ukraine has previously declared that the bridge, built eight years ago, is illegal. Kyiv had tried to blow it up twice in the past – with a truck bomb in 2022, and a sea drone the following year.

Zelesnkyy described the earlier attack this week as the culmination of a seven-stage campaign to demolish the bridge, largely involving domestically produced drones and ballistic missiles, clearing the radar and air defences of Russian-occupied Ukraine.

Flamingo FP-5 cruise missiles and Palianytsia drones struck the central section of the bridge, bringing down its supporting arch.
