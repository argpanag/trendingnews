Is Ben Shelton Tennis’s Next Big Celebrity?

TheTools · 31.08.2026 21:14 · source · permalink

Ben Shelton, the 23-year-old American tennis player, is playing it pretty cool. It’s a hot-as-hell day in Mason, a suburban city in Southern Ohio that hosts the Cincinnati Open, and, as Shelton shakes hands on an outdoor terrace wearing a plain black tee and spiral-seamed track pants, one can’t help but think he’s on the brink of becoming tennis’ next big celebrity. A galvanizing—and sometimes polarizing—player, he’s expressive, loud, and brash on the court, but he has also matured of late; where once he cockily mimicked the gesture of hanging up a phone (to signal he was “dialed in”) in victory, such antics have mostly dissipated. When you see his thundering serve, which can reach speeds of over 150 miles per hour, or his increasingly impressive mobility for being 6’4”, you understand where he gets that confidence.

A few hours earlier, Shelton took a private jet to Cincinnati from Montreal, where he’d just won the National Bank Open for the second year in a row (though in 2025, it was held in Toronto—the tournament alternates back and forth). The National Bank Open is notable: It’s one of nine annual Masters 1000 tournaments, which are just below the majors in terms of size and repute. Shelton has clinched four titles alone in 2026, and seven in total across his burgeoning career, having gone pro in 2022. On top of these, he’s reached the semifinals of two Slams—the US Open in 2023 and the Australian Open in 2025—and, pre-tour, he also won the NCAA singles championship when he was a student at the University of Florida (in fact, Shelton was raised in Gainesville, as his father Bryan was the head tennis coach of the Gators before joining his son’s team full time).

Ben Shelton at the 2026 US Open.

And there’s that It factor: Shelton’s intrinsic star quality has started to carry his persona beyond the baseline. He’s dating Trinity Rodman, the US Women’s National Team soccer star and Olympic gold medalist (and daughter of Dennis Rodman). He sits courtside at NBA games. He counts a personal Instagram audience of 1.3 million people (only about a dozen professional men’s tennis players have a following of 1 million or more).

“Having a big audience is a blessing and a curse,” he says. “It does kind of feel that eyes are on you all the time, and in all aspects of your life. But it is a privilege to have such a large audience; you just…sometimes, the things you post or share, they can go kind of crazy. I’ve definitely stopped posting as much as I did when I was younger. And I haven’t had Twitter [X] since I’ve been on tour. That’s one of the best decisions I’ve ever made. I think that’s the most negative form of social media there is.”

Shelton’s relationship with Rodman—which the duo hard launched in March 2025 on Instagram—has drawn attention and, like all public courtships, scrutiny. The two are relaxed about it, though; Rodman recently appeared on Kylie Kelce’s Not Gonna Lie podcast to talk about the relationship. Shelton brought Rodman up of his own accord.

“There are a few people that keep me honest,” he says. “My girlfriend is one of them. I think, with us both being professional athletes, we’re able to relate on certain things and keep things in a certain perspective. She’s a very positive person, which helps me. If I get down on myself, or feeling like I am behind in my progression, these are definitely areas that she helps a lot in. She helps me to not get ahead of myself.”

Ben Shelton and Trinity Rodman.

What I was surprised to learn about Shelton was that he’s fairly introverted away from the lights. He’s aware that there’s a performative element to tennis, and few current players wrap the crowd into the emotions of the game like he does (Carlos Alcaraz is comparable in this regard). But Shelton says he’s not as outgoing in his personal life.

“I think, when I’m not playing tennis, I’m somewhere in between. I can still be the fun and energetic person. But I’m a homebody. I stay at home all the time. My social battery drains pretty fast, and I kind of just do my own thing. I don’t mind being alone at all. So I think from that perspective, I’m different to how I am on court, or how people view me on court. Really, you’re not going to see me outside very much.”

Ben Shelton at the 2026 US Open.

This kind of low-key verve extends to his personal style too. At this year’s US Open, Shelton is wearing a custom kit by his sponsor On called “Lights Out.” It features day and night colorways, with a kind of circular, speckled ombré motif, in the aesthetic vein of what the company has been doing in recent years. “I love the day and night,” he says. “Roger [Federer] did a great job of this throughout his career, with his darker-colored night session fits.” In a pre-tournament appearance, Shelton was also seen wearing a snakeskin-print anorak: an unreleased piece from Zendaya’s forthcoming collaboration with On, which was acknowledged by the actor’s image architect Law Roach, who thanked Shelton for wearing the jacket in an Instagram post.

Ben Shelton, ON, Lights Out Collection.

Shelton’s on-court uniforms are easily distinguished, but not overdone, which mirrors the player’s own sensibility. “My off-court style is based around streetwear,” he says. “For me, the guys who have the coolest outfits are European footballers. They have crazy style. But I’m more quiet about my own. I’m more of a minimalist. I think it’s pretty easy to put on an outfit that looks good without trying too hard.”

Shelton enters this US Open ranked number nine in the world, but is slotted as the eighth seed, thanks to Jannik Sinner’s withdrawal. There are high hopes and momentum, despite an early exit in Cincinnati (which, I think, can be attributed to fatigue from winning in Montreal).

Prakash Amritraj, a host for the Tennis Channel, tells me Shelton will be one to watch at the US Open. “There’s been a little bit of a crack in the armor at the top of men’s tennis, which Sinner and Alcaraz have dominated for the last few years. Sinner won’t be there. Alcaraz is back, but we don’t know how healthy or sharp he’ll be after being injured this summer. All of it is bubbling up to a really interesting time. I put a guy like Shelton [or Taylor Fritz] as a favorite at the US Open,” he says. Patrick McEnroe, the retired pro player and now commentator, went so far as to tell the hosts of TODAY: “It’s Ben Shelton who is going to win it. I love his moxie, I love his attitude.”

Ben Shelton at the 2026 US Open.

Shelton says: “I’d love to win the US Open one day, especially as an American. But at the end of the day in general, I want to know that I didn’t leave anything on the table—that I became the best version of myself. That’s what makes me most excited. Not the wins or the results.”

And as for the next few weeks? As he says, humbly, “It’s a world full of unknowns.”
