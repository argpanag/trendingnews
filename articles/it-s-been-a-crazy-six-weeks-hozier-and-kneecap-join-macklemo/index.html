‘It’s been a crazy six weeks’: Hozier and Kneecap join Macklemore for 'Free Palestine' Dublin gig

TheTools · 27.10.2026 01:14 · source · permalink

MACKLEMORE BROUGHT HOZIER, Kneecap and Beoga to Dublin’s 3Arena for the opening night of his Free Palestine concert series on Monday. Kneecap thanked Ed Sheeran for helping to make it happen.

The Belfast trio’s pointed thank-you was one of the lighter moments in an evening of fundraising, political appeals and a line-up that felt closer to a festival than a conventional headline gig.

Hozier used his surprise appearance to urge the Irish government to approve visas for students in Gaza who have secured scholarships here, directing the audience towards a petition displayed behind him.

The concert came six weeks after Macklemore was dropped from Sheeran’s US tour following pro-Palestinian remarks during performances in New Jersey.

The tour’s promoter said venues had indicated they would not host concerts with Macklemore on the bill. Sheeran subsequently said the decision to remove him had been the promoter’s, rather than his.

Beoga were among the acts who withdrew in solidarity. On Monday, they were among those sharing the stage for a concert raising money for organisations supporting Palestinians.

By the time Macklemore appeared after 10pm, the audience had already seen plenty of performances, including a video performance by a children’s choir in Gaza.

Macklemore pictured holding a Palestinian flag on stage at the 3Arena. Andrew Walsh / The Journal Andrew Walsh / The Journal / The Journal

“I have been blown away this entire evening. It has been a crazy six weeks,” Macklemore told the crowd.

“And we took, you know, my shortest tour of all time, which was two nights at MetLife Stadium, and we turned that shit into this moment right here.

“And how fitting that the first show would be in Ireland.”

‘Tide is changing’

The event was campaign first, concert second.

All proceeds from the event are going to organisations supporting Palestinians, including Gaza Soup Kitchen, Medical Aid for Palestinians and the Palestine Children’s Relief Fund.

Outside, merchandise sellers offered Palestinian flags and keffiyehs. 

A merchandise stand pictured outside the 3Arena on Monday. Andrew Walsh / The Journal Andrew Walsh / The Journal / The Journal

Inside, a Medical Aid for Palestinians and UNRWA stand sat opposite the concert merchandise, while appeals from organisations supporting Palestinians played on screen before the music started.

Keffiyeh-patterned fabric hung on both sides of the stage, with olive trees arranged around the instruments.

Actor Liam Cunningham welcomed the audience before Palestinian singer Lina Makoul opened the musical performances with a wordless a cappella passage.

“I come all the way from Occupied Palestine,” she told the arena.

A video performance by the Gaza Children’s Choir was shown during the evening, alongside messages from actors including Mark Ruffalo, Cynthia Nixon and Ilana Glazer.

Those who attended the event also heard from Ukrainian activist Nadia Greenspan, Palestinian-Irish artist Samir Eskanda and Palestinian-American activist Maher Joudi.

After a short performance, Beoga’s Eamon Murray spoke about the band’s involvement in Irish Artists for Palestine, recalling instructions at an earlier gig against flags and chants.

“The tide is changing,” Murray said.

“We have never been prouder to be from the country we’re from.”

Murray also referred to criticism from Alan Shatter and Graham Linehan following the band’s recent appearance on The Late Late Show.

Hozier’s appeal

Hozier received one of the night’s biggest welcomes, performing Too Sweet during a set that also included an appeal on behalf of students in Gaza.

“55 students in Gaza who are waiting for their visas to be approved for scholarship places they earned and are waiting for them here in Ireland, and the government has not approved their visa applications,” he said.

“It’s baffling why it hasn’t been solved yet, but if you want to sign a petition, there it is behind me on the screen.”

A QR code directed the audience towards an open letter to the government.

Hozier and Kneecap were among artists who signed a letter last week urging Taoiseach Micheál Martin to intervene on behalf of the 55 students refused study visas despite securing fully funded scholarships at Irish universities.

DJ Habibeats and US hip-hop duo Dead Prez also performed before Kneecap opened their set with H.O.O.D., sending the standing crowd into motion and prompting a mosh pit.

‘More controversial than the suffering’

Macklemore’s set included water guns during White Walls, performances of Same Love and Hind’s Hall, and a change into a Bohemians jersey towards the end.

Between songs, he spoke at length about Palestinian solidarity and the attempts to silence it.

“Because freedom that requires another person’s oppression is not freedom at all,” he said, repeating the line.

He described footage of parents pulling children from rubble and families being erased, returning to the reaction provoked by the words “Free Palestine”.

“And somehow, those words have become more controversial than the suffering that made them necessary,” he said.

“That’s why we’re here tonight.”

Macklemore also praised Irish support for Palestinians, naming Hozier and Kneecap among those who immediately agreed to take part in the ‘Free Palestine tour’ when he called.

“I called a lot of people in the last six weeks, and you know who picked up that phone right away and said yes? Hozier and Kneecap.

“I have been so proud, to watch overseas how you guys have stepped up” he said.

An encore included a song he said he had written a week earlier.

Having gone beyond the scheduled finish, he closed with Can’t Hold Us.
