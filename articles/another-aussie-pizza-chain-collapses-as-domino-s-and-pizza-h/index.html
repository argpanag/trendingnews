Another Aussie pizza chain collapses as Domino’s and Pizza Hut feel the squeeze

TheTools · 24.11.2026 23:41 · source · permalink
  • Lifestyle
  • Food
  • Fast food

November 24, 2026 - 11.41pm

A South Australian pizza chain has collapsed, marking the latest casualty in a rapidly shifting local fast-food market.

For decades, a Friday night takeaway meant picking up a $5 pizza from Domino’s or Pizza Hut, while competitors such as Pizza Haven and Eagle Boys Pizza came and went. Today, the fast food pizza sector is facing unprecedented pressure, and Adelaide-based Dough Balls is the latest brand to fold under the strain.

Another popular pizza chain has gone under. Facebook/DoughBalls

The business expanded from its original Moseley Square venue in Glenelg to open six restaurants and a pop-up site, including locations at Mawson Lakes, Dudley Park, Fort Largs Eatery & Markets, a pop-up at Paxton Wines in McLaren Vale, and a city venue in Rundle Mall, which launched in 2024.

Four of those venues had already closed, and liquidators have been called in to wind up the company’s two remaining eateries at Glenelg and Rundle Mall.

Dough Balls joins a growing list of pizza operators that have shut doors over the past five years.

Pizza Capers officially ceased operations in July 2026 after a prolonged decline under owner Retail Food Group. It once operated more than 100 outlets across Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and the ACT.

Dough Balls built a loyal local following for its thin, crispy bases. Facebook/Dough Balls

Sydney-based casual dining chain Bondi Pizza entered voluntary administration in March 2024, but survived the process and remains in operation in two locations: Macquarie Park and Parramatta.

Melbourne gourmet institution Mojo’s Weird Pizza closed its remaining suburban outlets during the early 2020s.

Australia’s fast-food heavyweights Domino’s and Pizza Hut are battling it out against each other amid a growing trend towards high-protein fast food options like chicken, and smaller, shareable meals.

Domino’s is Australia’s most successful pizza brand based on sales figures. iStock Editorial

Australian consumers are also pivoting towards more supermarket ‘fake away’ options such as frozen pizzas and pre-prepared meal as cost of living pressures continue.

In its FY26 results, Domino’s reported a 4.7 per cent drop in same-store sales across Australia and New Zealand after deliberately pulling back on heavy $5 to $7 value discounting to protect store profitability.

Even Australia’s fast-food heavyweights Domino’s and Pizza Hut are battling it out. Pizza Hut Australia

Domino’s has shifted away from rapid store rollouts toward consolidating weaker sites and preparing a restructured pricing and operating model for FY27, alongside major leadership changes that saw former McDonald’s executive Andrew Gregory take over as group chief executive and former Guzman y Gomez marketing boss Lara Thom appointed group chief marketing officer.

In mid-2026, Domino’s announced it would close 29 underperforming outlets across Australia and New Zealand as part of a $259 million asset write-down, trimming its local network from a 2022 peak of around 754 stores down to roughly 700.

Retail expert Gary Mortimer told nine.com.au that slimming down the store network was a smart play for the pizza giant and “isn’t necessarily a bad thing.”

“I often say that sometimes, in order to grow, you have to shrink. And this is simply a strategy of rightsizing ... quality over quantity,” he previously told nine.com.au.

“It’s better to run a smaller fleet of businesses, but they’re all profitable, and the share market has responded.”

Pizza Hut posted a $1 million financial loss in its most recent financial year. It currently operates roughly 260 to 300 stores, down from its peak of 400 outlets in the mid-to-late ’90s.

Pizza Hut posted a $1 million financial loss in its most recent financial year. Pizza Hut Australia

Pizza Hut lost its crown during the 2010s when Domino’s moved faster on proprietary ordering apps, GPS driver tracking and high-volume delivery logistics.

Without Domino’s scale or delivery volume, Pizza Hut has had to lean heavily on third-party aggregators like Uber Eats and DoorDash, sacrificing steep commissions while competing directly against local independents.

In September 2026, Pizza Hut Australia launched an ambitious $10 million turnaround program aimed at overhauling store formats, revamping its menu and rebuilding brand relevance.

Experts believe there is still plenty of life left in Australia’s two most nostalgic pizza brands after half a century of serving hungry households.

Macquarie University consumer behaviour expert Jana Bowden previously told nine.com.au, “We’ve gone through this evolution where we’ve been seeking more sophisticated eating experiences, but that has all also been tempered by this significant price pressure that consumers are feeling.

“You’ve got consumers who can afford to engage and consume from restaurants and pay more for their product when they eat out,” Bowden said.

Fast food lovers are feeling significant price pressure. Getty Images/iStockphoto

“But you have a significant swathe of consumers across multiple demographic segments who just can’t afford to do that.

“Fast food places like Domino’s and Pizza Hut remain options if they are viable for that consumer in terms of pricing.”
