Prince of Wales title a 'put-down to Welsh people,' says Hollywood actor

TheTools · 03.09.2026 07:50 · source · permalink

The Prince of Wales title is "essentially a put-down to Wales," a Welsh actor has claimed.

Michael Sheen, a three-time Bafta Cymru winner, has insisted that Welsh nationals should be "asked" whether they want to take the title back when it next becomes vacant.

The 57-year-old actor is set to play Owain Glyndŵr, the last Welsh-born Prince of Wales, in his later theatre production, Owain & Henry, in Cardiff.

His character marks an important chapter in Welsh history.

In 1282, Llywelyn ap Gruffydd, who was the last prince of an independent Wales, was killed in battle by the English during their conquest of the nation.

His head was ultimately displayed on the Tower of London by King Edward I.

The King then installed his son, Edward II, as Prince of Wales in 1301 - the first Englishman to hold the title.

It is a move that Mr Sheen has branded as an act designed to keep Wales "in your place," during an interview with the BBC.

A century later, Mr Sheen's character successfully staged a 15-year revolt to reclaim the title as Welsh, before Wales was ultimately reconquered by the British.

He has since become a symbol of Welsh nationalism.

Mr Sheen conceded that, while this was all "a long, long time ago," it is still important for a Welsh voice on the issue because "these things have power still".

He acknowledged, however, that Welsh people might ultimately prefer the status quo.

"The Welsh people might decide that they don't want the title Prince of Wales to come back to Wales, and they're happy with it the way it is," he added.

"But I think it would be nice to be asked."

The Royal Family has kept the title historically for the heir to the throne, with Prince William inheriting it from his father, now King Charles III, in September 2022, following the death of Queen Elizabeth II.

When accepting the title in 2022, Kensington Palace vowed that William would "support the aspirations of the Welsh people."

"The prince and princess look forward to celebrating Wales's proud history and traditions as well as a future that is full of promise," the Palace added.

"They will seek to live up to the proud contribution that members of the Royal family have made in years past."

Upon taking his latest role, Mr Sheen remarked that the production had deepened his engagement with Welsh history and hoped it would "start a conversation."

Mr Sheen sought to re-educate himself on the history of his native country as his schooling had been "from an English point of view," which had left "a massive hole in my knowledge about my own country" - something he said was the case for much of the Welsh population.

The 2009 GQ Magazine Actor of the Year handed back his OBE in 2017, having been awarded the prestigious honour in 2009 for his services to drama, while learning about the history of Wales.
