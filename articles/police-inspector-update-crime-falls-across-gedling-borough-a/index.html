POLICE INSPECTOR UPDATE: Crime Falls Across Gedling borough as Police Target Burglars, Drug Dealers and Anti-Social Behaviour - Gedling Eye

TheTools · 09.09.2026 06:25 · source · permalink

Crime figures across Gedling borough are moving in the right direction, according to the borough’s neighbourhood policing inspector, with significant reductions in burglary, shoplifting and other theft-related offences recorded over the summer.

In his September update, Gedling Neighbourhood Policing Inspector Steve King said officers had continued working closely with residents, businesses and partner agencies to tackle the issues that matter most to local communities.

The force’s current policing priorities remain acquisitive crime, anti-social behaviour and drugs.

Burglary and shoplifting down

Inspector King revealed that August saw a 16.9% reduction in acquisitive crime across the borough.

Figures showed:

  • Shoplifting offences fell by 38%
  • Overall burglaries dropped by 26.7%
  • Business burglaries were down by 65%

Police say the reductions are the result of coordinated work between neighbourhood officers, response teams, investigators, local businesses and members of the public who continue to report suspicious activity and provide information.

Among recent successes, officers arrested a suspect in connection with a burglary in Calverton on 13 August after responding to an incident at around 5.30am. Property believed to have been stolen was recovered and enquiries remain ongoing.

Several arrests have also been made as part of investigations into a series of business burglaries across the Gedling area.

Inspector King said officers remain committed to identifying and prosecuting those responsible.

Warning over vehicle crime

While overall vehicle crime has remained relatively stable, police have noted a rise in thefts from vehicles despite a reduction in the theft of motor vehicles.

Residents are being reminded to take simple precautions, including locking vehicles, removing valuables and ensuring items are not left on display.

In one recent incident, officers arrested a 49-year-old man in Arnold on suspicion of theft from a motor vehicle and possession of a bladed article in a public place after he was seen acting suspiciously during the early hours.

Inspector King praised the role of local residents in helping police respond quickly to incidents.

Crackdown on nuisance bikes

Anti-social behaviour remains one of the most common concerns raised by residents, particularly the illegal use of off-road motorcycles, e-bikes and e-scooters.

Acting on intelligence from the community, officers have continued targeted enforcement activity across the borough, leading to a number of vehicle seizures and action against offenders.

Inspector King stressed that the operations are aimed at tackling dangerous and disruptive behaviour rather than preventing people from enjoying their hobbies responsibly.

He also urged parents and guardians to ensure children understand the law surrounding the use of such vehicles.

Police have additionally received reports of youth-related anti-social behaviour in parts of Gedling, including incidents involving young people climbing onto roofs and intimidating residents.

Work is ongoing to identify those involved, with police warning that civil enforcement powers and tenancy-related sanctions could be used where appropriate.

Major cannabis grow discovered

The supply of illegal drugs remains a key focus for neighbourhood officers and specialist policing teams.

Inspector King said intelligence-led operations continue to disrupt organised criminal activity linked to drug supply networks operating in local communities.

One of the most significant recent discoveries was a large-scale cannabis cultivation in Ravenshead.

Officers uncovered more than 500 cannabis plants at the property and investigations remain ongoing.

Police say they remain committed to pursuing those involved in the production and supply of illegal drugs, which are often linked to wider criminality and the exploitation of vulnerable people.

Inspector King thanked residents for their continued support and encouraged anyone with information about crime or anti-social behaviour in their area to continue reporting concerns to help keep Gedling’s communities safe.
