Jessica Pegula calls Mirra Andreeva 'diva' after Indian Wells practice session

TheTools · 05.11.2026 21:30 · source · permalink

Jessica Pegula and Mirra Andreeva have only met competitively in doubles and mixed doubles

  • Published
    5 November 2026, 14:12 GMT

The Women's Tennis Association says it takes "full responsibility" for not telling players there were microphones on practice courts after private comments Jessica Pegula made to her coach about fellow player Mirra Andreeva were live-streamed.

The session at Indian Wells, California, was streamed by the WTA before its Tour Finals start on Sunday.

In a private conversation with her coach Mark Knowles, world number five Pegula described Andreeva as "a diva".

The American could also be heard saying the French Open champion "needs to be humbled" and her coach Conchita Martinez encourages her behaviour.

A representative for Pegula's agent said she had been "completely unaware" the conversation could be heard by courtside microphones, "let alone live-streamed to the public".

"Jess has since been in direct contact with those involved, which she felt was important to do personally rather than through the media," they added.

YouTube streams of Wednesday's practice sessions at Indian Wells have since been removed by the WTA, but the footage had already been shared on social media.

It is understood the microphones were only intended to capture the sounds of rallies and not any conversations on court, and there will be no audio on future videos.

"The WTA takes full responsibility for failing to inform our players and their teams that microphones, in place to give fans a livestream of the players' practice, were active on the courts at the WTA Finals," a statement read.

"We sincerely apologise for failing to meet our responsibility to ensure players and coaches were properly informed."

Pegula, who is a member of the WTA players' council, also praised Andreeva, saying the world number three is "wonderful" and "smart".

The season-ending WTA Finals, being held at Indian Wells for the first time, run until 15 November.

Pegula's representative and the WTA have been approached for comment.

In a separate incident, world number one Elena Rybakina appeared to have an angry exchange with her coach before a practice session with Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova.

What did Pegula say?

Some of the audio is unclear, but Pegula can be heard describing how Andreeva and Martinez complained throughout the session, even though Andreeva won every game.

Knowles: "She treats Conchita so bad."

Pegula: "Conchita's always been very nice to me, but I've lost a little respect for her because I'm like, this girl - I think she's a nice girl - she's just a diva. She needs to be humbled. She needs to be [inaudible] putting her in her place; [inaudible] stand up to her."

Spaniard Martinez - the 1994 Wimbledon champion - began working with Andreeva in April 2024. Andreeva reached the Wimbledon quarter-finals last year and claimed her first Grand Slam title at Roland Garros in June.

Pegula: "Conchita pokes her and eggs on her behaviour."

Knowles: "She needs somebody to put her in her place. Once she gets that, I think she'll be really good. I mean, she's already really good. Look how she moves."

Pegula: "She moves like a cat. She's wonderful. She's got the perfect serve. She's smart. She anticipates well."

Pegula, who has not won a Grand Slam, then compared Andreeva to world number four Coco Gauff. The American was also 19 when she won her first Grand Slam. Now 22, she has won two majors.

Pegula: "It's so funny how you see her and then you see Coco's mentality. If she had Coco's mentality, she'd be better than her."

Knowles: "Oh, definitely."

Pegula: "The serve, everything, all-around, and she moves just as good. But like mentally, she's..."

Pegula and Andreeva are in different groups at the WTA Finals but could meet in the semi-finals or final.

What happened with Rybakina?

Pegula's group opponents are Gauff, Rybakina and Noskova, and the latter pair's practice session at Indian Wells was also live-streamed.

Australian and US Open champion Rybakina appears to remonstrate with coach Stefano Vukov as they walk to the net, saying: "Stay here. I'm talking to you. Can you stand here?"

They shared a few more words, which are inaudible, before going to the other side of the court together to greet Noskova and her team.

Vukov was suspended by the WTA in February 2025 following an independent investigation into his behaviour towards Kazakhstan's Rybakina. He returned to her team that August.

Pegula was among the players to complain about additional behind-the-scenes cameras at the Australian Open in January.

The semi-finalist described the filming as an "invasion of privacy" that needs to be "cut back".

"You just feel like you're under a microscope constantly," she said.
