Lap 18/56: At the moment, Antonelli would emerge behind Verstappen if he pitted now and everything proceeded smoothly. But it looks like he’ll pit just once this race, with Verstappen going twice.

Lap 17/56: Antonelli is 11.6sec ahead of Hadjar in second but has yet to pit. Max is gaining around a second a lap on the Italian. Here’s Beau Dure: “Is Verstappen’s radio broken? We haven’t heard him complain yet.”

Lap 16/56: Verstappen is already up two places after his pit stop, using his new tyres nicely. Leclerc pits and emerges sixth, one place ahead of Hamilton again.

Lap 15/56: Hamilton goes on to mediums when he pits and comes back in ninth. He needs to finish fourth or better to keep his (very, very, very) faint hopes of winning the title alive.

Lap 14/56: No further action for Bortoleto after he was investigated for leaving the track. Max goes into pit and reemerges in eighth place. He’s on soft tyres still. He’ll be happy with that smooth pit.

Lap 13/56: Leclerc, in third, is putting distance between himself and Ferrari teammate Hamilton in fourth. Kimi’s lead over Verstappen continues to hover around 2.4sec.

Lap 12/56: Alonso slips past Albon and into 15th. There’s a big gap to Hulkenberg in 14th though – around six seconds.

Lap 11/56: Bortoleto now under investigation for leaving the track and gaining an advantage. He’s down in 17th at the moment. Moral of the story: drive on the track.

Lap 10/56: Hamilton is fourth, which means that even if Russell’s five-second penalty puts him outside the top eight, Antonelli would not wrap up the title today if he wins the race (as things stand).

Lap 9/56: Kimi is estimated to pit with his medium tyres between laps 21 and 27. His lead is up close to three seconds now.

Lap 8/56: Russell is given a five-second penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage. He doesn’t seem impressed on team radio. “No need to send anyone to El Paso for a penalty start,” emails David C Hunter. “All Austin drivers are suffering as I-35 is entirely rebuilt!”

Lap 7/56: Antonelli and Verstappen are evenly match. Kimi will pull off one good lap and Max will hit back with another. That means we’re at a status quo – Antonelli’s lead is still just over two seconds at the end the lap.

Lap 6/56: Russell is under investigation for leaving the track and gaining an advantage on the first lap. He was battling with Piastri at the time.

Lap 5/56: Verstappen was starting to catch Antonelli but a quick few laps and the Italian’s lead is at 2.5sec.

Lap 4/56: Hadjar, who started the day third on the grid, is down to fifth after the Ferraris passed him on the opening lap.

Lap 3/56: George Russell is currently sixth – he needs to finish outside the top eight for Antonelli to clinch the title today. Max has the fastest lap of the race so far, but will be disappointed he didn’t get a better start with the grippy softs.

Lap 2/56: There’s some debris on the track and the virtual safety car is out. Antonelli has a lead of 2.1 seconds and is looking comfortable. Looks like Max may have driven over some of the debris but it doesn’t seem to have caused any damage.

Lap 1/56: Antonelli gets a very good start, Verstappen didn’t seem to get any power and the Italian increases the control he already had by sitting on pole. The Ferraris also had a good start – Leclerc is up to third and Hamilton up to fourth.

“Instead of penalizing Bortoledo 80 places, they should just have him start in El Paso,” chuckles Beau Dure.

Tyre update: Kimi will start on medium tyres, while No 2 and No 3 on the grid – Verstappen and Hadjar – are on softs, as are the Ferraris.

Grid change. Lance Stroll will now start from the pit after setup changes.

What Antonelli needs to clinch the title today

Just a reminder what Kimi needs to do to become the youngest world champion of all time today. He’ll clinch the title if he wins and George Russell finishes outside the top eight, with Lewis Hamilton outside the top four.

Max Verstappen may hold off attacking right at the start of the race. He’s asked how important he thinks turn one is for this race. “Not as important as in Singapore,” he replies quickly.

The grid

Lewis Hamilton sounds sort of positive ahead of today’s race, which he’ll start from fifth on the grid.

“I’m hoping we can challenge for a podium. That would be awesome but it’s going to be tough. It’s definitely going to be really tough. We just have to see where we go,” said Hamilton.

Conditions are pretty good for racing today. Temperatures will be around 28C/82F and no rain is broadcast. It’s not as blustery as it was yesterday either.

Max Verstappen starts second today behind Antonelli but don’t be surprised if he ends up winning today’s race. He’s also worked himself up a little before the race, having criticized the track in Austin as too bumpy. When circuit boss Boby Epstein brushed off those concerns, Max was having none of it: “I invite him to drive my car,” Verstappen told Dutch journalists on Saturday. “I think he’d have to get out after five laps and wouldn’t be able to walk anymore.”

If I drove Max’s car for five laps I wouldn’t be able to walk either, but for very different reasons.

Preamble

So here we are. Nine-year-old Kimi Antonelli* has a chance to become the youngest champion in F1 history today – and he’s sitting on pole too. He leads the championship by 102 points over his Mercedes teammate George Russell. He’ll clinch the championship if he wins and Russell finishes outside the top eight, with Lewis Hamilton outside the top four. While the two Britons will push Antonelli, the man who appears to be most on his mind is Max Verstappen, who has been in brilliant form – and not just against amateur karters – and sits second on the grid in Austin.

“Max has been very quick and did an incredible job in qualifying so it’s not going to be easy,” Antonelli said on Saturday. “Hopefully we’ll be leading into turn one and we’ll try to set the pace, even though it’s going to be very tight.”
