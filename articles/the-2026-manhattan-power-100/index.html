The 2026 Manhattan Power 100

TheTools · 09.09.2026 00:03 · source · permalink

Power Lists

The borough’s evolving hierarchy in a time of disruption

City & State presents the 2026 Manhattan Power 100. Ayman Siam; William Alatriste, NYC Council Media Unit; The Campaign

  • Power Lists
  • New York City

Manhattan’s status quo is under threat, and not just because of a far-left mayor from Queens who’s making himself at home in Gracie Mansion. New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin, an Upper East Sider, hasn’t pulled any punches since assuming the leadership post at the start of the year. Three members of Congress representing parts of Manhattan – Reps. Jerry Nadler, Adriano Espaillat and Dan Goldman – are heading for the exits, with a trio of politicians lined up to succeed them. Meanwhile, there’s a new U.S attorney for the Southern District of New York, a new leader of the Partnership for New York City and a new president of Columbia University – and it’s an open question what each one will make of their new job.

But that’s a question for 2027. As for now, we have an updated ranking of the Manhattanites who are true power brokers in New York City and state politics and government. City & State’s latest Manhattan Power 100, written and researched in partnership with journalist Aaron Short, sheds light on the biggest players across the borough.

1. Julie Menin

New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin has positioned herself as Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s leading foil in government. After securing support for the speakership in November, the Upper East Side politician passed legislation to curb emergency contracts, scrutinized questionable education spending and secured funding to expand the city’s Fair Fares and housing voucher programs in the city budget. She continues to advocate for her district, advancing measures to revive year-round outdoor dining and ban horse carriages in Central Park. Now, Menin is demanding answers from City Hall over a Legionnaires’ outbreak on the Upper East Side.

2. Mark Levine

Mark Levine gained trust from Manhattanites by posting coronavirus updates during the pandemic and proposing solutions to the housing shortage as their borough president. Now, he has much broader responsibilities as New York City comptroller. He revealed in January that the city faced a $12.6 billion budget gap before revising that figure to $7.3 billion in March. He also called on a charter revision commission to clarify the rules around the city’s rainy day fund. Levine has also investigated the city’s approval process for outdoor dining setups and audited no-bid migrant shelter contracts.

3. Brad Hoylman-Sigal

Brad Hoylman-Sigal’s legislative record in Albany championing LGBTQ+ rights and strengthening tenant protections helped him comfortably win last summer’s Democratic borough president primary. Now, Hoylman-Sigal is focused on addressing the housing crisis, approving more than 1,400 new housing units and creating a Manhattan-wide tenants union. This summer, he announced he would direct his $50 million discretionary budget to museums and other cultural initiatives in response to the Trump administration’s threats to cut arts funding. And he joined Rep. Jerry Nadler to demand more transparency in the president’s Penn Station reconstruction plans.

4. Alvin Bragg

Alvin Bragg did not make his historic fraud prosecution of Donald Trump a centerpiece of his district attorney reelection campaign last year, but voters certainly remembered. The Manhattan prosecutor easily dispatched his primary challenger before overwhelming two opponents with three-quarters of the vote in November. Bragg’s allocation of resources toward reducing gun violence and recidivism has contributed to a steep drop in murders and shootings in the borough since 2021. He also scored several legislative wins in Albany, securing new restrictions for ghost guns and bolstering protections against tenant harassment.

5. Liz Krueger

State Sen. Liz Krueger’s institutional memory in the state Senate Democratic conference is newly important as the deputy majority leader announced his departure. The Senate Finance Committee chair, who represents Manhattan’s East Side, steered the state’s protracted budget process despite suffering a mild stroke in April (don’t worry, she’s bouncing back) before the budget was finally adopted in late May. In the midst of negotiations, Krueger got lawmakers in both houses to pass her SUNNY Act, which would legalize small plug-in solar panels to help households lower their energy bills.

6. Micah Lasher

Assembly Member Micah Lasher’s congressional campaign didn’t score an endorsement from New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, but Lasher relied on Morris Katz, one of the mayor’s key strategists, to win the country’s second-most expensive House primary ever. The West Side lawmaker did snag endorsements from his former bosses, Rep. Jerry Nadler, Gov. Kathy Hochul and former Mayor Mike Bloomberg. Lasher’s unassuming nature, long track record and slew of establishment endorsements won over enough voters to defeat Assembly Member Alex Bores and several other opponents. Now, Lasher hopes to bring a little of his magic to Congress.

7. Erik Bottcher, Cordell Cleare, Kristen Gonzalez, Robert Jackson & José M. Serrano

State Sen. Robert Jackson first made a name for himself as an advocate for equitable public school funding, and the Upper Manhattan lawmaker has continued to fight for education investments in Albany, recently securing an $860 million increase for New York City schools. He now chairs the Civil Service and Pensions Committee, which played a role in passing major pension reforms this year. He endorsed Zohran Mamdani, a fellow Muslim, in the New York City Democratic mayoral primary last year.

State Sen. Cordell Cleare of Harlem chairs the Committee on Aging. Cleare was ranked as a top 10 Senate Democrat in Albany in 2025 by The Center for Effective Lawmaking, based on the substance of her bills and how far they advanced. This year, she sponsored Karilyn’s Law, which would make it easier for friends and relatives to visit individuals under guardianship, if it’s signed by the governor.

State Sen. Erik Bottcher made a savvy move late last year when he exited a crowded congressional primary to seek the state legislative seat being vacated by Brad Hoylman-Sigal. Bottcher won easily, jumping from the New York City Council to a state Senate district covering the Upper West Side, Hell’s Kitchen, Chelsea and parts of Greenwich Village. The state Senate Cities 1 Committee chair helped pass Francesco’s Law, a safety measure that would strengthen firearm storage requirements.

State Sen. Kristen Gonzalez is straddling several divides. Geographically, her district covers parts of Manhattan, Queens and Brooklyn. Ideologically, she is a proud member of the Democratic Socialists of America but has also earned the trust of the Democratic establishment in Albany. And she is the rare state lawmaker in Albany to chair two full committees, Elections as well as Internet and Technology. She has passed legislation targeting artificial intelligence and recently helped build support for a statewide data center moratorium.

State Sen. José M. Serrano, who represents the South Bronx and East Harlem, chairs the Senate Democrats’ Majority Conference and the Committee on Cultural Affairs, Tourism, Parks and Recreation. Serrano, who was first elected over two decades ago, is a champion of green spaces, affordable housing, and arts and culture.

8. Jessica Tisch & Merryl Tisch

The Tisches enjoy an outsized presence in Manhattan, with their family-run Loews Corp., an insurance, energy, hospitality and packaging conglomerate, headquartered on West 57th Street. Last year, Ben Tisch became the company’s third-generation president and CEO, and his father, James Tisch, chairs the board. But it’s the women in the family who have fully embraced public service. Jessica Tisch, Ben Tisch’s sister, is one of the most powerful public officials in New York City in her role as police commissioner. An Adams administration holdover, Jessica Tisch enjoys the backing of the business community and has helped Mayor Zohran Mamdani reassure some critics wary of his socialist ideology. Jessica Tisch’s mother is Merryl Tisch, who has chaired the SUNY board since 2019. The statewide higher education system has a presence in Manhattan, with the Fashion Institute of Technology and the Harry Van Arsdale Jr. School of Labor Studies based in the borough. James and Merryl Tisch also helped fund the Mount Sinai Tisch Cancer Center and a director of branch libraries and education position at the New York Public Library.

9. Darializa Avila Chevalier & Brad Lander

Two years ago, Darializa Avila Chevalier helped organize a pro-Palestinian encampment at Columbia University. The CUNY doctoral student and DSA member was motivated to run for Congress after seeing how little effort Rep. Adriano Espaillat’s office put toward freeing pro-Palestinian activist Mahmoud Khalil from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention. After gaining traction in the polls, Avila Chevalier received Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s endorsement in May before toppling Espaillat by nearly 4 points in a major primary upset. Starting next year, the Afro-Latina organizer will represent the Ivy League school where she spearheaded Gaza solidarity protests.

A third-place finish in the New York City Democratic mayoral primary would have disappointed most politicians, but Brad Lander embraced his role defeating Andrew Cuomo by cross-endorsing Zohran Mamdani and consolidating the progressive vote. The former city comptroller eyed a role in the Mamdani administration, but instead mounted a challenge to Rep. Dan Goldman, who represents a district straddling Brooklyn and Manhattan. Lander declared his candidacy in December, immediately earning Mamdani’s endorsement. Lander’s pointed criticisms of the Israeli government, in contrast to Goldman’s defense of the country, proved to be a key issue for voters who supported Lander by a 2-to-1 margin in the June primary.

10. Al Sharpton

The Rev. Al Sharpton has enjoyed his position as a Democratic Party kingmaker, welcoming nine prospective presidential candidates to his National Action Network conference in April. But the civil rights leader regularly meets with local candidates too. Last summer, he hosted an event with Zohran Mamdani after the mayoral primary, called on Andrew Cuomo to drop out, then facilitated a Hakeem Jeffries endorsement of Mamdani. This year, Sharpton had breakfast with Jack Schlossberg at The Regency and invited Darializa Avila Chevalier and Rep. Adriano Espaillat to address voters at a Harlem church in June.

11. Jerry Nadler

Last fall, Rep. Jerry Nadler made the difficult decision to retire after 34 years in Congress, citing a need for generational change in the Democratic Party. The Upper West Side institution endorsed Assembly Member Micah Lasher in February and then helped ensure his former aide would edge out several strong rivals in the pivotal Democratic primary. In the past few months, Nadler has sought to stand up to President Donald Trump, demanding more scrutiny over Amtrak’s Penn Station renovation plans and introducing a bill to block the president from putting his name on federal buildings.

12. Shaun Abreu

New York City Council Majority Leader Shaun Abreu built a reputation as an innovative public figure, initiating pilot programs on trash containerization and rat contraceptives to improve constituents’ quality of life. So it was no surprise that after he decisively won reelection, Council Speaker Julie Menin selected the West Harlem lawmaker to lead the Transportation and Infrastructure Committee in January. Abreu, who’s also in a key leadership role as majority leader, has continued an aggressive approach to making streets cleaner and safer by opposing criminal summonses for e-bikes, expanding Fair Fares and supporting legislation to ban horse carriages.

13. Félix V. Matos Rodríguez & William C. Thompson Jr.

Félix V. Matos Rodríguez and William C. Thompson Jr. have made the City University of New York one of the nation’s best deals in higher education at a time when college costs are spiraling out of control. Matos Rodríguez has helped revitalize CUNY, increasing its enrollment over the previous year for the third year in a row. Last fall, the chancellor unveiled CUNY Beyond, a universitywide career preparation program that will help 180,000 students receive a job offer or acceptance letter upon graduation. He is also overseeing CUNY’s redevelopment project turning Hunter College’s Kips Bay campus into SPARC Kips Bay, an academic, healthcare and commercial life sciences space.

For the past decade, Thompson has shepherded the city’s public university system, hired key leaders and shaped its strategic vision. He has presided over the school’s steadily rising enrollment in recent years, crediting the governor’s Reconnect program at community colleges for driving growth. Thompson helped secure a $6.7 billion investment in the CUNY system in the latest state budget. Thompson, a former New York City comptroller, also weighs in on the city’s finances as a member of the state Financial Control Board.

14. Keith Wright

Manhattan Democratic Party Chair Keith Wright knew this year would be challenging for incumbents during a period of anti-incumbent fervor. Wright went so far as to signal a thawing of his cold war with his longtime foe, Rep. Adriano Espaillat, with Wright’s son, Assembly Member Jordan Wright, attending an event with Espaillat. Espaillat couldn’t stop his left-wing challenger, but Wright’s son was one of few sitting legislators to defeat a DSA opponent this cycle. Keith Wright also runs the consulting firm Wright Strategies.

15. James McDonald

The Southern District of New York may no longer be known as the “Sovereign District” in the Offices of the U.S. Attorneys, but being appointed the top prosecutor of the U.S. Department of Justice outpost still confers plenty of power and prestige. In July, James McDonald rejoined the office as deputy U.S. attorney under Jay Clayton, and shortly afterward was appointed U.S. attorney by federal judges when Clayton was confirmed as President Donald Trump’s director of national intelligence. McDonald, a former partner at Sullivan & Cromwell, represented Trump during his appeal of his criminal conviction.

16. Linda Rosenthal & Rebecca Seawright

Assembly Member Linda Rosenthal opted not to run for the seats opened up by Rep. Jerry Nadler, her former boss who’s retiring, or former state Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal, an ally who’s now the Manhattan borough president. Instead, the Upper West Side representative is staying put in the Assembly, where she has driven affordable housing policy as chair of the Housing Committee. The prolific lawmaker authored several major bills this session, including a ban on 3D-printed guns, a prohibition on shackling pregnant and postpartum women in the custody of law enforcement and a health data privacy measure.

Upper East Side Assembly Member Rebecca Seawright was a lead sponsor of New York’s Equal Rights Amendment, which was approved in 2024. This year, the Committee on Aging chair has taken aim at elder abuse and fraud, and she has championed legislation raising the income eligibility threshold for Senior Citizen Rent Increase Exemption and Disability Rent Increase Exemption programs.

17. Grace Lee

Assembly Member Grace Lee is poised to move to the state Senate after trouncing former Assembly Member Yuh-Line Niou in the June Democratic primary for outgoing state Sen. Brian Kavanagh’s seat. In 2023, Lee made history when she became the first Korean American woman to serve in the state Legislature. The lower Manhattan lawmaker led the Asian Pacific American Task Force, launched the first Asian American and Pacific Islander Summit and served as co-chair of the Democratic Assembly Campaign Committee. Lee’s bill to improve how schools teach AAPI history was signed into law last year.

18. Gale Brewer, Carmen De La Rosa & Yusef Salaam

New York City Council Member Gale Brewer has a good sense of how government works, thanks to her two stints serving in the city’s legislative body and two terms as Manhattan borough president. The Upper West Side legislator, who also has ample experience as a government staffer, now chairs the Committee on Governmental Operations, State and Federal Legislation. She has plenty on her plate, from composting and cannabis enforcement to affordable housing and micromobility safety. She also secured funding for investigating what city government knew about toxins from the 9/11 attacks.

New York City Council Member Carmen De La Rosa, a Dominican American politician representing Inwood and Washington Heights, has held her seat since 2022, when she moved over from the Assembly. The former Civil Service and Labor Committee chair recently passed a measure providing bonuses of up to $10,000 to school paraprofessionals, although the Mamdani administration has sued to block it on the grounds that it circumvents collective bargaining. She also sponsored the union-backed Construction Justice Act, which passed last year. Her political patron, Rep. Adriano Espaillat, recently lost his congressional primary.

New York City Council Member Yusef Salaam won a seat representing Central Harlem in 2023, completing a remarkable story arc for one of the “Central Park Five” who was wrongly convicted of raping and beating a jogger as a teenager and later exonerated. Salaam, a protégé of Manhattan Democratic Party Chair Keith Wright, ran unopposed for reelection last year, and now chairs the Committee to Combat Hate.

19. Andy Byford

When U.S. Transportation Department Secretary Sean Duffy announced in May 2025 that Andy Byford would oversee Penn Station’s renovation, expectations were high that the former New York City Transit leader would deliver the long-awaited overhaul. A year later, Byford named Halmar and Skanska as master developers and unveiled plans for the $8 billion redesign that will move the station’s main entrance to Eighth Avenue and create a single-level concourse while keeping Madison Square Garden in place. Byford also signaled he was open to through-running trains between New Jersey and Long Island, unifying the region’s railroad systems.

20. Jeff Blau

Few people have created an entirely new neighborhood, but Jeff Blau is one of them. The Related Companies executive spearheaded the Hudson Yards megadevelopment on Manhattan’s West Side. Blau also expanded Related’s luxury office portfolio with 70 Hudson Yards, anchored by Deloitte, and 625 Madison Ave. Elsewhere in the borough, Related is partnering on a major redevelopment of the New York City Housing Authority’s Fulton and Elliott-Chelsea Houses. Last year, Related reached a deal with City Hall to finance a $2 billion platform over the Far West Side rail yard, but the current administration may not prioritize it.

21. James Whelan

James Whelan pledged to work with Zohran Mamdani to address the housing shortage last September after he won the Democratic mayoral primary. The president of the 14,000-member Real Estate Board of New York found common ground with Mamdani over upzoning neighborhoods to allow for more housing density, expanding low-income rental vouchers and staffing up city agencies. But Whelan could not convince the mayor that a rent freeze could make housing less affordable or that a pied-à-terre tax could weaken the economy. Whelan previously helped drive lower Manhattan’s recovery post-9/11 as a City Hall aide and ran the Hudson Yards Coalition.

22. Anthony Marx

Anthony Marx was thrilled when Zohran Mamdani pledged last year to end the annual library budget dance after the Adams administration repeatedly sought steep spending reductions. In February, the mayor’s preliminary budget proposed a $29 million cut to the city’s three systems, but Marx reminded the mayor to keep his campaign promise to set aside 0.5% of the city’s budget for libraries. By May, Mamdani had restored funding to libraries, although it accounted for only 0.42% of the budget. Marx meanwhile is exploring ways to add affordable housing above branches needing renovations.

23. Suri Kasirer

Suri Kasirer has been recognized as New York City’s top lobbyist during the past decade thanks to her relentless advocacy on behalf of her clients. Over the past year, her firm helped more than 50 nonprofits secure nearly $100 million, and it also worked with Northwell Health to win New York City Council approval of its $2 billion plan to modernize Lenox Hill Hospital. Kasirer also helped Louis Vuitton to obtain approvals to renovate its Fifth Avenue flagship store and represented the New York Historical in getting city approval for its $175 million Tang Wing expansion.

24. Bradley Tusk

Bradley Tusk is a man of many talents, including venture capitalist, political operative, philanthropist, podcaster and independent bookstore owner. The Tusk Strategies founder led a campaign called Solving Hunger to enact universal school meals statewide, helped pass the FARE Act to ban broker fees and worked with the New York City Council to reduce scaffolding. Tusk also invests in tech startups as well as technology that would enable smartphone voting. And he still finds time to write columns for the Daily News and offer advice to investors on CNBC and Fox Business.

25. Adriano Espaillat & Dan Goldman

Reps. Adriano Espaillat and Dan Goldman were toppled in June amid an anti-incumbent wave driven by voter dissatisfaction with the status quo. Espaillat had endorsed Zohran Mamdani for mayor once Mamdani won the Democratic primary, then sought Mamdani’s support for his reelection campaign. But the mayor backed Darializa Avila Chevalier, a political neophyte, instead of the five-term lawmaker after observing her gain momentum. Espaillat secured endorsements from much of the political establishment, visited a Newark immigration detention facility and campaigned extensively in Harlem. But it wasn’t enough to prevent the state’s biggest congressional primary upset since Joe Crowley lost to Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez in 2018.

Goldman was a prominent Donald Trump critic and a leading liberal Jewish voice in Congress. But his refusal to endorse Mamdani after he won the primary and his pro-Israel stance made him a top target among the mayor’s allies, who consolidated support behind Brad Lander. Goldman touted his lawsuit forcing U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to grant access to its facilities, but acknowledged his views on Israel did not match the electorate. Goldman lost by more than 30 points, and he has continued speaking out against antisemitism.

26. Christine Quinn

Christine Quinn has been fighting for more resources to help New York’s most vulnerable residents. Last year, she urged Gov. Kathy Hochul to backfill funding for food assistance for 3 million New Yorkers after the Trump administration cut SNAP benefits. Quinn also called on Mayor Zohran Mamdani to expand CityFHEPS, New York City’s housing voucher program, and defend New Yorkers’ right to shelter. When Mamdani reversed his stance on CityFHEPS, Quinn called the move a “betrayal” – then kept up pressure for more funding before praising a final budget deal that added $175 million for rental assistance.

27. Marc Holliday

Marc Holliday had dreams of building a $5.4 billion casino in Times Square, but a key advisory committee voted down the project last September. Meanwhile, Holliday still presides over New York City’s largest portfolio of commercial properties. He’s also behind the 750 Third Ave. office-to-residential conversion, with more than 650 units, and a new office tower at 346 Madison Ave. The rest of his Manhattan commercial portfolio looks great too, thanks to a rebounding office market that’s the strongest Holliday has seen in his career.

28. Elsie Encarnacion, Harvey Epstein, Virginia Maloney, Christopher Marte & Carl Wilson

New York City Council Member Christopher Marte is having an eventful summer. In July, his legislation to ban horse carriages got a hearing after gaining crucial support. In August, the news broke that the Mamdani administration scrapped a long-simmering plan to turn the Elizabeth Street Garden into affordable housing, siding with Marte and others. Marte, who has been in office since 2022, is reportedly eyeing a run for public advocate.

New York City Council Members Harvey Epstein, Virginia Maloney and Elsie Encarnacion were all victorious in 2025. Epstein brings the most experience to the job, having previously served in the Assembly. Epstein, who's known nationally thanks to a “Saturday Night Live” skit poking fun at how his name evokes both Harvey Weinstein and Jeffrey Epstein, represents a district that includes Greenwich Village, the Lower East Side and the East Village. He formed the Animal Welfare Caucus and chairs the Consumer and Worker Protection Committee.

Encarnacion brings ample government experience to her job as well, as she was chief of staff to former New York City Council Deputy Speaker Diana Ayala and director of youth services for former Speaker Melissa Mark-Viverito. The East Harlem native now chairs the Immigration Committee, pushing for protections for immigrants in legislation.

Maloney literally grew up in government circles, thanks to the long congressional tenure of her mother, former Rep. Carolyn Maloney. The younger Maloney, who represents a district that includes the Upper East Side, chairs the Committee on Economic Development. She has also joined her mother in calling on Congress to pass the Equal Rights Amendment.

New York City Council Member Carl Wilson was elected earlier this year in a spring special election for the seat vacated by Erik Bottcher, his old boss who moved up to the state Senate. Wilson won handily, winning support from Council Speaker Julie Menin and overcoming a foe backed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani. The lawmaker also kept the seat representing the Stonewall Inn as a “gay seat.”

29. Steven Rubenstein

Since joining Rubenstein in 1992, Steven Rubenstein has enhanced his family’s legacy as a premier strategic reputation management firm and PR adviser to New York City’s iconic institutions, including “Saturday Night Live” and The Museum of Modern Art, among hundreds of clients. He also chairs the Association for a Better New York, an influential civic group, interviewing Zohran Mamdani about democratic socialism before a live audience in October. Rubenstein recently added the Manhattan Jewish Foundation to its client list to promote Rabbi Mark Wildes’ global book tour.

30. Steve Fulop

Steve Fulop once lured employers from New York to New Jersey, but now he’s working to keep them in Manhattan. As Jersey City’s mayor, he ran one of the fastest-growing cities in the Northeast, adding nearly as many apartments as Manhattan did in 2024. After finishing third in New Jersey’s Democratic primary for governor last year, Fulop moved to New York in January to run the Partnership for New York City following Kathy Wylde’s retirement. He has the business group fighting the Mamdani administration on tax hikes and economic development policies.

31. Brian Kavanagh

State Sen. Brian Kavanagh announced in February he would not seek reelection after representing lower Manhattan for two decades, igniting a scramble for his seat. During his tenure, the state Senate Housing, Construction and Community Development Committee chair helped craft laws to protect tenants, maintain affordable housing and create thousands of homes for homeless and working-class New Yorkers. He recently passed an extension of the J-51 tax credit for co-ops and condos, increased income thresholds for the Senior Citizen Rent Increase Exemption and other programs, and launched a four-year pilot of the Housing Access Voucher Program.

32. Jessica Lappin

Since joining the Downtown Alliance in 2014, Jessica Lappin has overseen the dramatic transformation of Lower Manhattan. Its population rose above 70,000 for the first time last year while demand for office space was at its highest since 2019, with 4.75 million square feet leased in 2025. This year, the business improvement district leader teamed up with property owners to launch the RE:Store program, offering small businesses three months of free rent and build-out grants as retail vacancies lag. The former New York City Council member also called for a crackdown on placard abuse to reduce gridlock.

33. Allen Roskoff

Allen Roskoff vowed that his political club would ensure that Eric Adams and Andrew Cuomo didn’t get elected as mayor last year, and he was right. The Jim Owles Liberal Democratic Club president got excited about the Mamdani administration, but tempered his views when Zohran Mamdani endorsed Lindsey Boylan for a vacant City Council seat. Roskoff believed the West Side needed LGBTQ+ representation and endorsed Carl Wilson, who won an April special election in one of the mayor’s few political miscalculations. Roskoff’s support for David Siffert’s Assembly bid helped make the difference in a race decided by 15 votes.

34. Manny De Los Santos, Charles Fall, Keith Powers, Tony Simone, Al Taylor & Jordan Wright

Assembly Member Jordan Wright upheld the family legacy this year when he held on to his Harlem seat despite a primary challenge from a candidate backed by the Democratic Socialists of America in an otherwise strong cycle for the DSA. Wright’s father is Keith Wright, a former Assembly member who now leads the Manhattan Democratic Party. 

Assembly Member Tony Simone was floated as a potential candidate for Rep. Jerry Nadler’s seat or former state Sen. Brad Hoylman-Sigal’s seat, but he opted to stay put in his Assembly district that includes Chelsea, Hell’s Kitchen and the heart of Midtown. Simone is making the most of his office, introducing measures that would prohibit U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents from wearing masks and repeal a ban on adult saunas and bathhouses.

Assembly Member Keith Powers is back in elected office after winning a special election earlier this year for the East Side seat vacated by Harvey Epstein, who was elected to the New York City Council. Powers previously served in the New York City Council, where he was majority leader, then mounted an unsuccessful bid for Manhattan borough president last year.

Assembly Member Charles Fall represents a district covering a sliver of lower Manhattan as well as Staten Island’s North Shore and parts of Brooklyn. The lawmaker, who’s also the Democratic Party chair for Staten Island, hasn’t ignored his Manhattan constituents, helping secure long-term rent protections for Gateway Plaza residents, in addition to advancing resiliency and quality-of-life improvements in Battery Park City. He also chairs the Subcommittee on Consumer Fraud Protection.

Assembly Member Al Taylor has represented Harlem, Washington Heights and Inwood since 2017. He has pushed for housing affordability and passed legislation protecting children with food allergies.

Assembly Member Manny De Los Santos, an acolyte of outgoing Rep. Adriano Espaillat, was first elected in a 2022 special election. He represents Upper Manhattan and sponsored a 2025 lithium-ion battery safety measure.

35. Jon Silvan

Jon Silvan’s Global Strategy Group just keeps growing. The public relations executive runs one of the most trusted public affairs, communications and polling operations in the business, building a national profile while counting the Metropolitan Transportation Authority, Google, National Grid and Seaport Entertainment among the clients he has assisted in New York. This election cycle, the firm has been working with Gov. Kathy Hochul as well as congressional candidates Micah Lasher and Brad Lander. GSG has also helped Vanderbilt University in its expansion into New York City and has supported the Gateway Program.

36. Emily Giske

For more than three decades, Emily Giske has served as one of New York’s most effective lobbyists and a transformational force behind the state’s landmark civil rights laws. Giske, who joined Bolton-St. Johns in 1999, helped pass the landmark New York Marriage Equality Act as a top lobbyist for LGBTQ+ coalitions. She also has served as a Democratic superdelegate and has mentored public affairs professionals as well as Democratic women candidates through Eleanor’s Legacy and Women Lead. She currently leads a team of 30 government relations staffers.

37. James Dolan

The New York Knicks’ unlikely title run has made some New Yorkers reconsider James Dolan’s legacy. The long-vilified Madison Square Garden head honcho fired the team’s previous coach last year while vowing to win a title. Dolan invited President Donald Trump to attend Game 3, then sparred with the mayor and police commissioner over a watch party’s cancellation. He even insulted New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s speech during the Knicks victory party. Meanwhile, Dolan has continued to throw money around in electoral campaigns, recently getting behind Republican gubernatorial candidate Bruce Blakeman, and he has become the GOP’s biggest donor in New York.

38. Rob Speyer

Tishman Speyer’s $3.5 billion Rockefeller Center refinancing two years ago made investors wonder whether New York City’s commercial market was undervalued, but Rob Speyer has always been a believer in midtown Manhattan. After refinancing The Spiral in Hudson Yards for $2.8 billion in January 2025, Speyer opened JPMorgan Chase & Co.’s new headquarters at 270 Park Ave. Now, Tishman Speyer is in advanced talks to repurchase the Chrysler Building. Speyer, a co-chair of the pro-business Partnership for New York City, has reportedly been in favor of a more adversarial approach against Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

39. Lisa Silverstein

For more than a quarter century, Lisa Silverstein has worked with her father, Larry Silverstein, to rebuild Lower Manhattan and develop housing in some of the nation’s hottest markets. Now 25 years after 9/11, Silverstein Properties and American Express broke ground on 2 World Trade Center, a 55-story skyscraper that will be the last tower in the WTC footprint. Since becoming CEO in 2023, Silverstein has led the development of several projects, including 55 Broad St. and the Brooklyn Tower. Her firm’s Opportunity Zone funds have also financed the development of nearly 5,000 homes across the country.

40. Jonathan Bing, John Mascialino & Ed Wallace

Ed Wallace has been involved with several New York firsts throughout his career. He shaped the city’s skyline by negotiating land use review approvals for the Columbus Circle towers, Riverside Center, as well as the city’s three most prominent private universities and served as counsel to the city’s 2012 Summer Olympics bid. At Greenberg Traurig, where he serves as co-chair of the firm’s New York office, Wallace represented Hornblower in the city’s first ferry contract and JCDecaux in the citywide bus shelter agreement. He is chair of the French-American Foundation and a vice chair of the Citizens Budget Commission.

In February, Jonathan Bing was named chair of Greenberg Traurig’s Liquor Licensing Group, a role in which he coordinates with a dozen GT attorneys across multiple states. Bing, a former Assembly member who represented Manhattan’s East Side, is part of the firm’s New York Government Law & Policy Practice. In Albany this session, he helped the domestic violence services provider Sanctuary for Families build support for Kyra’s Law, which if signed by the governor will require taking a child’s health and safety into account when determining child custody and visitation.

John Mascialino is the chair of the New York City Government Law & Policy Practice at Greenberg Traurig, where he deals with government contracting as well as regulatory and administrative law. Earlier in his career, he was counsel to the New York City deputy mayor for operations. Marc Landis has long advocated for social and economic justice and stood up for New York City’s Jewish community. Landis is also a former Democratic district leader on the Upper West Side and is the treasurer to Westside Progress, a super PAC that spent $144,500 to support Carl Wilson for New York City Council. Last year, Landis was sworn in as a Democratic New York City elections commissioner.

95. Milton Tingling

Milton Tingling is the first Black county clerk in Manhattan’s history. He has modernized the office over 100 employees and 11 divisions by helping them switch to electronic filing and changed protocols to make processing cases and wrangling jurors easier. Tingling also shortened the period of eligibility for jurors from every six years to two to make the pool larger. He was appointed to New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s Advisory Committee on the Judiciary and also chairs the West Harlem Development Corp., which has come under scrutiny lately amid allegations of mismanagement.

96. Julie Stein

Julie Stein has made Union Square one of New York City’s most attractive destinations thanks to her commitment to safe streets and livability. Over the past three years, the former New York City Economic Development Corp. official has brought new sanitation, public safety and beautification improvements to the area. Thanks to her efforts, Union Square welcomed 51 new businesses, increased foot traffic by 16% and saw retail occupancy improve. Now, Stein is working with city agencies to launch a $3 million design study to modernize Union Square Park and reconfigure the 14th Street corridor.

97. Miesha Smith

Weeks after Brad Hoylman-Sigal took office as the new Manhattan borough president, he hired Miesha Smith to serve as his No. 2. Smith was appointed deputy borough president in February, bringing a representative from Harlem into the office’s leadership team. Smith was previously assistant dean for student life at Hunter College, where she launched the CUNY school’s food pantry and fresh food program and worked on improving student retention and graduation rates. Her current work involves promoting public education, small businesses as well as arts and cultural institutions.

98. Larry Scott Blackmon

Larry Scott Blackmon has always felt like a bridge-builder for his Harlem community. The Manhattan state committee member and former New York City Council candidate founded his own political consulting firm in 2023 to connect uptown organizations, entrepreneurs and businesses with the city’s political establishment. At the end of February, Blackmon convened the Harlem Black Men’s Brunch after seeing the lack of resources and opportunities that brought Black men together to network professionally. He has also sought to increase awareness about the need for prostate screening among Black men.

Editor’s note: Larry Scott Blackmon is a member of City & State’s advisory board.

99. Reggie Thomas

A year ago, Reggie Thomas took on a new challenge handling government affairs in New York for one of the biggest companies in the world. On behalf of Google, Thomas engages with policymakers and business and community leaders on issues such as artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, workforce development and tech literacy. Google has a major campus on Manhattan’s West Side, where it employs thousands of New Yorkers. Thomas served in the Bloomberg, de Blasio and Adams administrations and had a stint at the Real Estate Board of New York.

100. Laura Ann Rosenbury

Laura Ann Rosenbury’s three-year tenure atop Barnard College has been far from smooth. She faced opposition from the campus community for disciplining students involved in pro-Palestinian demonstrations, received a no-confidence faculty vote in 2024 and laid off 77 staff members last year to reduce its $252 million debt. Yet Rosenbury has also boosted the school’s endowment, completed the renovation of the Francine A. LeFrak Foundation Center for Well-Being and issued $63 million in need-based scholarships last year. This fall, she plans to open the Roy and Diana Vagelos Science Center, the city’s first net-zero carbon, all-electric academic science building.
