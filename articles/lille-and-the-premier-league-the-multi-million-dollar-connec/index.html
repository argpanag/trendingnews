Lille have sold 22 players to English clubs since 2006, generating nearly $870 million. Now, Arsenal and LOSC meet again in the UEFA Champions League


Lille OSC from France's Ligue 1 has found one of European football’s most lucrative markets in the Premier League. From established stars like Eden Hazard to young talents such as Ayyoub Bouaddi, the French club has turned player development into a multi-million-dollar business. This Tuesday, LOSC faces Arsenal in the UEFA Champions League, bringing the close ties between the French side and English football back into focus.

LILLE, A TALENT FACTORY FOR ENGLISH FOOTBALL

Les Dogues have sold 22 players to Premier League clubs since 2006, generating approximately $870 million in total revenue. That is a record among Ligue 1 clubs.

One of the earliest major examples was Eden Hazard, who left Lille to join Chelsea in 2012. The Belgian forward became one of the Premier League’s biggest stars, helping establish LOSC’s reputation as a club capable of developing elite talent.

In 2019, Nicolas Pépé’s move to Arsenal, for approximately $93 million, highlighted Lille’s ability to negotiate high-value transfers. He is currently the second-most expensive African signing of all time, behind only Yan Diomandé ($150 million) of Real Madrid.

The Ivory Coast international recorded 22 goals and 11 assists in Ligue 1 during the 2018–19 season, statistics that attracted interest from several European clubs.

At the time, Pépé became the most expensive signing in Arsenal’s history. He now ranks third on the club’s all-time transfer list, behind Declan Rice ($130 million) and Bruno Guimarães ($98 million).

Lille’s formula combines scouting, player development, and strategic negotiations. The club does not rely solely on its on-field results; it also generates revenue by selling players whose market value rises significantly on the international stage.


GABRIEL, ONANA AND YORO: MILLIONS HEADED TO ENGLAND

Lille’s profitability is not limited to its forwards. Defenders and midfielders have also found the Premier League to be a destination where clubs are willing to invest significant sums.

In 2020, Gabriel Magalhães joined Arsenal for around $30 million. The Brazilian defender subsequently established himself as a key part of the London club’s back line and will face his former team again in the UEFA Champions League.

In 2022, Amadou Onana joined Everton for approximately $47 million, while Dutch defender Sven Botman moved to Newcastle United for around $43 million.

Another notable case was Leny Yoro, who signed for Manchester United in 2024 in a deal reportedly worth around $72 million, excluding potential additional bonuses. Several European giants were pursuing the young center-back, but the English club ultimately won the race for his signature.

These transfers highlight the appeal of the British market: Premier League clubs have significant financial resources and are willing to invest in players with potential and experience in European competitions.


AYYOUB BOUADDI AND THE CHAMPIONS LEAGUE CLASH AGAINST ARSENAL

The latest chapter in this strategy features Ayyoub Bouaddi. In August 2026, Manchester City confirmed the signing of the 18-year-old midfielder from Lille. Although the club did not disclose the transfer fee, various reports valued the deal at approximately $117 million, including bonuses.

The transfer reaffirmed LOSC’s ability to develop young talent and negotiate extraordinary fees for its players. Bouaddi, a product of Lille’s academy, became one of the most significant sales in the club’s history and an example of the value English clubs place on promising players with international potential.

This Tuesday, Lille faces Arsenal in the UEFA Champions League league phase, a matchup that links the French club’s current European campaign with one of the teams that has shaped its transfer history through the signings of Nicolas Pépé and Gabriel Magalhães.

With approximately $870 million in sales to English clubs since 2006, Lille has demonstrated that its model goes beyond identifying talent. It involves developing players, competing with them, and finding the right moment to sell.

The challenge is maintaining a balance between transfer revenue and on-field competitiveness. As long as Premier League clubs continue to seek talent to strengthen their squads, LOSC will have the resources to remain one of France’s leading sellers. The clash against Arsenal serves as a timely reminder that this financial relationship is also part of both clubs’ footballing history.
