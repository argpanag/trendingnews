  13. Suspect's family say they are long-time friends of Helen Hainespublished at 07:30 BST
    07:30 BSTBreaking

    The family of the man accused of attacking Helen Haines have released a statement, describing the alleged attack as "devastating and unfathomable".

    The suspect's father, Rod Nugent, and father's partner, Christine Cunningham, say they've been friends with Haines "for more than 20 years".

    The suspect has been named by local media as 43-year-old Brendan Nugent, an environmental campaigner in the city of Wangaratta.

    The family statement adds they will be "assisting Victoria Police in any way we can".

  14. 'I don't think I'm a hero,' barber who detained suspect sayspublished at 07:05 BST
    07:05 BST

    The local barber who grabbed the suspect out of his car told reporters earlier he doesn’t think he is a "hero".

    "I just think I was a person who had the opportunity to intervene in a safe-ish sort of way," Ricky Jefferies says.

    Jefferies, whose barber shop is near the scene of the attack, says he searched the suspect and found a knife.

    "He didn’t pull it [the knife] out. I just wasn't comfortable with his movements and so I took it out of his pocket and threw it away," Jefferies says.

    Other bystanders also got involved too, he adds.

    "They didn’t even check for danger, they just ran to help," he says. "The driver was still in the car, the car was still on and they sat in front of a rolling car, that's not an exaggeration.

    "There is a man who sat in front of a rolling car to help someone, that really happened."

  16. Who is Helen Haines?published at 06:24 BST
    06:24 BST

    Helen Haines, the independent MP for the Indi division in Victoria, is well regarded by her colleagues and seen by many as a unifying force in an age of increasingly divisive politics.

    Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has called her a "beacon of light" while fellow MP Allegra Spender says she's the person she turns to "when I have an ethical dilemma".

    The independent MP has only been a federal parliamentarian for a relatively short time - having been elected in 2019. However, in that time she has become an influential advocate for integrity in politics.

    Haines proposed legislation for a national anti-corruption watchdog in 2021, and has been credited for driving the creation of the National Anti-Corruption Commission under Labor in the previous parliamentary term.

    Before entering politics, she worked as a nurse, midwife, and public health researcher and she has published several research papers on maternal health.

  17. MPs say the apparent attack is their 'worst nightmare' come truepublished at 06:04 BST
    06:04 BST

    Tiffanie Turnbull
    BBC News, Sydney

    As increasingly frequent acts of violence against politicians have shocked the globe in recent years, Australia has watched on with a contradictory feeling: that attacks like that could not happen here, and that it was only a matter of time until they did.

    But MPs and public figures – backed by experts – have been warning of a volcano of threats against them as social cohesion cracks, polarisation intensifies and the tone of debate grows hotter.

    Coalition Senator James Paterson told News24 on Tuesday: "I'd like to say that I was surprised… but I'm not at all surprised."

    MPs from across the spectrum have been describing a political climate in which they've found themselves wearing disguises in public, locking their office doors, and scanning every crowd for potential threats.

    Women in politics are particularly scared, said Victorian state MP Georgie Purcell, who shares part of her electorate with Haines: "For many of us, the news... is our worst nightmare."

    There have been frustrated rumblings in Canberra from some MPs who say security arrangements have not changed to match the threat and – if a political motivation for this incident is confirmed – one of the greatest tenets of the nation's political culture could now be at risk.

    05:35 BST

    Helen Haines, the independent federal MP for Indi, is in a "critical but stable condition", Australia's prime minister has confirmed.
