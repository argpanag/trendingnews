​New Estimates Suggest russia Can Produce Up to 15 Su-34s, 12 Su-35Ss Annually | Defense Express

TheTools · 30.10.2026 01:51 · source · permalink

The russian military industry is currently capable of delivering up to 10–15 Su-34 and up to 10–12 Su-35S fighter jets annually. These figures are approximate, as specific figures for 2026 have not yet been disclosed.

The Defense Intelligence of Ukraine provided this information in response to a request from UNIAN. The report also provides estimates of the number of aircraft of these types in service with the russian Aerospace Forces as of the end of 2025.

As of the end of 2025, russia reportedly had approximately 20 Su-57s, 135–140 Su-35Ss, 125 Su-34s and Su-34Ms, and an estimated 140–145 multirole Su-30M2, Su-30SM, and Su-30SM2 aircraft at its disposal.

There is no serial production of new Su-24M, Tu-22M3, and Tu-95MS aircraft. The russian defense industry is reportedly focused exclusively on repairing existing aircraft of these types, although their exact numbers are not specified in the aforementioned response from the Defense Intelligence of Ukraine to UNIAN.

From Defense Express we would like to note that although all the figures listed above are marked as "approximate," they are based on reliable data provided by official sources.

Previously, assessments of the russian military industry's capabilities had to rely either on very rough estimates by Polish authors, which suggested that by 2026 russia would have produced 14 Su-34s, Su-35s, Su-57s, and Su-30SM2s while also having lost at least 10 aircraft.

Alternatively, assessments could be based on russian statements, which may be significantly inflated. For example, these statements reportedly indicated a requirement for the delivery of as many as 20 Su-35S aircraft to the russian Aerospace Forces in 2025–2026, or even 34 Su-34 aircraft in 2024–2025.

Any estimates of the russian federation's combat aviation capabilities are clearly necessary to demonstrate to Western partners the current need for modern combat aircraft for the Ukrainian Air Force, particularly the JAS 39 Gripen and the Rafale.

Ivan Kyrychevskyi, serviceman of the 413th Raid Regiment of the Unmanned Systems Forces of Ukraine, weapons expert at Defense Express.
