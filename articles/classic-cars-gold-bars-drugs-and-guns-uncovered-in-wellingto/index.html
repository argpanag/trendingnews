Classic cars, gold bars, drugs and guns uncovered in Wellington raids

TheTools · 14.11.2026 02:06 · source · permalink

Two people have been arrested after a haul of guns, drugs, gold bars and classic cars were uncovered while police raided properties in Wellington.

The haul was discovered during a two-day search of two properties and included GHB (fantasy), cannabis, a kilo of methamphetamine in distinctive wrapping worth approximately $105,000, and space vape liquid and packaging.

Officers also found cases of cash containing an estimated $100,000 in cash, gold bars and $50,000 in gold coins. A large recycling bin was also filled with copper.

A 3D-printed gun was among the items seized by police. (Source: Supplied)

A rifle, a 3D-printed firearm and nine classic cars, including a limousine, a Chevrolet Bel Air and a Plymouth Savoy, were also seized.

Police said the raids and subsequent arrests followed years of investigations.

"One of the men has been supplying a wide range of illicit drugs from his property for many years, benefitting off the addiction of others,” Detective Senior Sargeant Lee Underhill said.

A case full of cash seized by police. (Source: Supplied)

“It may have been a long time coming, but we are pleased to have taken these drugs and firearms out of the community, as well as seizing millions of dollars’ worth of assets that I believe are the profits from his illegal enterprise."

A 53-year-old Wellington man was charged with possession for supply of fantasy, methamphetamine and cannabis, cultivating cannabis and money laundering.

He was remanded in custody and is due to reappear in the Wellington District Court on December 3.

One of the nine classic cars seized by police. (Source: Supplied)

A 32-year-old Johnsonville man was charged with unlawful possession of a restricted weapon and possession of methamphetamine.

He was remanded on bail and is due to reappear in the Wellington District Court on December 3.

"Further charges are likely," police said.

Detective Sergeant Sam Buckley said police had noticed an increase in criminals buying virtual assets, shares and precious metals to hide the proceeds of their offending.

“This is in addition to the more common methods of investing, through the purchase of vehicles and property. Asset Recovery Units will continue to cast their net wide, with a view to locating and seizing all types of criminal proceeds, no matter what form they take.”

A haul of drugs was also found during the raids. (Source: Supplied)

Underhill said police had seen an increase in possession of 3D printed guns which "present a danger in our communities as well as to those who use them".

"This is just one indicator of the danger that these criminals pose and we will continue to focus our efforts on cracking down on their trade.

“The view from the top of the hill might have been worth a million dollars, but the cost of alleged criminal activity could prove considerably higher.”
