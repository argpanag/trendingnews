Inside the Largest Arby’s in the World: It Has More Than Meats the Eye

TheTools · 10.11.2026 18:08 · source · permalink

Joseph Lamour

I recently spent an afternoon in the world’s biggest Arby’s in Colonial Heights, Virginia.

A short drive down Interstate 95 from Richmond, Virginia, on Temple Lake Drive, it’s a rustic, gigantic reddish stone building that stands out next to the smaller, gray ones beside it.

Without the drive-thru lane and the cowboy hat-shaped sign, you might miss that it’s a fast-food spot. From the outside, it looks more like a gallery.

I park and walk down a path lined with lanterns, past an outdoor area with several patio tables and to the front door, where I note another parking lot. Once inside, I saunter through the spacious interior to a register where a cashier takes my order.

Despite the size of the building, I see only three employees dealing with customers directly, but there are many more working in the back. Packages of turnovers and zucchini bread tower on the wide counters and a rotisserie oven roasts whole chickens.

I stare at the vaulted ceilings. There’s a canoe hanging up there. You could easily seat a wedding in the space between the soda machines and the cash registers.

Arby’s says this location is one of a kind but reflects what fans love about Arby’s in general: the meats ... and the hospitality.

The chain says the Colonial Heights Arby’s sells “unique offerings” like rotisserie chicken, loaded baked potatoes, zucchini muffins and a seasonal Brunswick stew.

“The restaurant has become a beloved stop for locals and travelers alike, thanks to the dedicated team that brings it to life every day,” says the Arby’s representative.

With my tray of Brunswick stew, curly fries, an apple turnover and a chicken cheesesteak, I go to sit. There are two large seating areas: one has booths, a long family-style table with grandiose chairs and a working fireplace, and the other contains more booths, a totem pole, animal sculptures and a sunny indoor terrace.

The Colonial Heights Arby’s is 7,125 square feet, and seats 166 diners inside and 40 more outdoors. It’s three times the size of an average location. Foot traffic is quiet this afternoon, however, with about 20 diners total, including me.

Couples banter across from me, and a little boy downs curly fries in a chair four times taller than he is.

Two diners sitting nearby, grandmother and granddaughter Bobbi and Ashley Johnston, chat with me. They’ve been coming here for a long time.

“I love coming here. We come at least once a month,” Bobbi says. “Our Sunday school class comes and eats lunch after church, between 35, 40 people, and we come once a month and eat after church.”

Ashley says it’s also a popular spot for students to gather before school dances.

“I’ve just considered it a restaurant. I’ve never really considered it a landmark,” she says, adding that she’s made memories with her kids here over the years.

“My daughter’s now 15,” Ashley continues, gesturing toward the terrace outside. “Out there, seeing her in her little tutus before dance recitals, dancing around the patio. It’d be sad if something ever happened to it because it is a good place for people to get together.”

The largest Arby’s is owned by Richmond-based The Restaurant Company, which owns 15 other Arby’s locations. Another Arby’s location it owns north of Richmond is bigger than most but doesn’t hold the crown.

In 2024, Matt Williamson, marketing coordinator with Arby’s Richmond, told WTVR that the restaurant’s decor was “loosely based on Disney’s Wilderness Lodge,” a hotel designed in 1994 to evoke American landmarks like Glacier National Park and Yellowstone.

I happily finish my meal, and an older employee cheerfully greets me and sweeps. I smile and pack up to go.

As I drive away from this giant Arby’s, I feel refreshed, despite all the meat I’m digesting on the way home.
