Arsenal hold off energetic Sunderland to continue perfect title defence

Arsenal maintained their perfect start to the season after two moments of brilliance from David Raya and Bruno Guimaraes set them on course for victory at Sunderland.

On 55 minutes, Gunners defender Ezri Konsa was penalised for pulling down Dan Ballard in the box, but goalkeeper Raya read Enzo le Fee's penalty with a superb save low to his left.

And its true significance was not revealed until 121 seconds later, when midfielder Guimaraes - brought on as a half-time substitute - brilliantly curled home the opener off the bar from 20 yards out.

It was the summer signing's first Arsenal goal and had added meaning as it came against Sunderland - the arch-rivals of his former club Newcastle United.

The win was sealed in the final seconds as Bukayo Saka slotted home a penalty after being fouled by Reinildo Mandava, who was given a second yellow card and sent off.

By finding a way to succeed once again, Mikel Arteta's men extended their 100% start to the season with six wins from six in all competitions, including four in the Premier League to put them top of the table.

Despite dominating possession with 66% of the ball in the first half, Arsenal had, until that point, their chances severely limited by Regis le Bris' disciplined Sunderland side.

Their best chances before the break included a Kai Havertz effort that flew over from six yards, before the German striker had a volley tipped over by home keeper Robin Roefs soon after.

Arsenal came close to a second before the late penalty when Ballard brilliantly cleared off the line to deny Havertz, while Jurrien Timber found the net but his header was ruled offside.

Sunderland have four points from as many league games to leave them 14th in the standings.

Arsenal analysis: Raya proves worth with crucial contribution

Arsenal's first-half frustration was encapsulated in manager Arteta being booked shortly before the break for leaving his technical area too many times.

They had been nullified by Sunderland at that point, with attacking metronome Martin Odegaard kept quiet by their former player Granit Xhaka.

Greece winger Christos Tzolis continued his inconsistent start to the season, more reminiscent here of his toothless showing against Aston Villa rather than the productive display at home to Chelsea.

Konsa, even before giving away the penalty, struggled to deal with Sunderland's direct attacking approach and the physicality of striker Brian Brobbey. And there was further concern at the back when Ben White went off injured at half-time.

But in a game where their attacking talent largely failed to sparkle, Arsenal could rely on their quality at the other end of the pitch as Raya showed his value.

The Spaniard kept his fifth clean sheet in six games this term, and marshalled a defence that has only conceded nine shots on target in the league so far - the lowest in the division.

Raya was also called into action late on to palm away a Xhaka cross that caused danger as Sunderland packed the box looking for a leveller.

As a result Arsenal have won each of their first four games in a top-flight season for the sixth time in their history, last doing so in 2022-23.

Although they lost, Sunderland should take positives from a performance where they went toe-to-toe with the English champions.

As he has so often since joining Sunderland last summer, Xhaka was at the centre of many of the best things the Black Cats did against the midfielder's former club.

His brilliant free-kick was headed narrowly wide by Ballard in their best first-half chance, while the final action of the opening period had Xhaka flicking the ball away with his heel as it looked to be heading to Havertz to finish from close range.

The Swiss leads all Sunderland players in the league this season for touches, chances created, passes played into the box, fouls won and successful passes.

It was not enough, however, to improve his side's dire record against Arsenal - they have one victory in 30 Premier League meetings with the Gunners and are winless in 18.

Sunderland will instead look ahead to hosting AZ Alkmaar in the Europa League on Wednesday, their first European match for 53 years.

  • Sunderland have won just one of their last 29 Premier League meetings with Arsenal (D10 L18), and are winless in 17 (D6 L11) since a 1-0 victory in November 2009 after Darren Bent’s goal.

  • Arsenal have only lost one of their last 14 league visits to Sunderland (W8 D5), winning five of their last seven (D2), although did draw this exact fixture last season 2-2.

  • This is just the second Premier League campaign Arsenal have faced Sunderland as reigning champions, previously beating them 3-1 at home and 4-0 away in 2002-03. All four of the Black Cats’ home wins against reigning Premier League champions have been against different teams (Man Utd 1996-97, Man City 2012-13, Chelsea 2015-16, Leicester 2016-17).

  • Sunderland have won their last two Premier League home games, both against London sides (Chelsea and Fulham). They last won three in a row at the Stadium of Light in January/February 2012, all against City sides (Manchester, Swansea, Norwich).

  • Arsenal are looking to win each of their first four games in a top-flight season for the sixth time in their history. They went on to win the league the first three times they did so (1930-31, 1947-48, 2003-04) and have finished second the last two times (2004-05, 2022-23).
