Forget David Fincher, New 109-Minute Thriller Officially Topples His Near-Perfect Masterpiece

TheTools · 06.10.2026 01:00 · source · permalink

David Fincher has spent most of his career making movies that audiences catch up with later rather than on opening weekend. Zodiac is the one cinephiles tend to name when asked for his best work, and it is also the one the industry treated as a problem from the start. Fincher and Fischer approached Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM) to finance the film, but talks fell through because MGM wanted the running time fixed at two hours and fifteen minutes.

Fincher held out for nearly two hours and forty, and in March 2007 the movie opened to roughly $13 million domestically, losing its weekend to a Tim Allen motorcycle comedy. It grossed over $84.7 million worldwide on a production budget of $65 million. That is not a flop, exactly, but it is not what a studio hopes for from a thriller with Gyllenhaal, Ruffalo, and Downey Jr. on the poster either. In the years since its release, the film has garnered a large cult following, with many claiming it to be David Fincher's magnum opus. The lesson a lot of us took from that trajectory was that the patient journalism procedural about people who stare at a monster for too long is simply not a theatrical genre anymore. Nineteen years later, a much shorter film working in roughly the same territory has quietly undercut that assumption, and it did so in about ten days.

Robert Pattinson's 'Primetime' Has Already Passed 'Zodiac' Domestically

The film is Primetime, A24's 109-minute dramatization of the To Catch a Predator years, with Robert Pattinson playing Chris Hansen. It opened to 19.2 million from 2,863 theaters, giving it A24’s third-biggest opening weekend, behind Backrooms (81.4 million) and Civil War ($25.5 million). That debut alone put it comfortably ahead of Zodiac’s opening weekend in raw dollars.

In its sophomore weekend, Primetime dropped 58% to $8 million, bringing its 10-day total to $34 million. Zodiac finished its whole North American run at about $33 million, so Primetime has now, officially if not quite spiritually, passed the Fincher film on home turf before most people have had time to argue about whether it is any good.

'Primetime' Connected Faster Than David Fincher's 'Zodiac'

Both are American true-crime films that refuse to be about the criminals and insist on being about the institutions that chase them. Zodiac funnels an unsolved case through the San Francisco Chronicle newsroom and a pair of exhausted detectives. In 2006, the journalist Chris Hansen hosts To Catch a Predator, a Dateline NBC series in which he conducts sting operations with the anti-pedophile vigilante group Perverted-Justice.

Primetime funnels its predators through an NBC news division and a host who starts to believe his own promos. In both cases, the real subject is what the pursuit does to the pursuer, and in both cases the killer or the suspect is almost beside the point. The two films also differ sharply in how they approached their real-life subjects, and that difference is arguably part of why one opened to a bigger audience.
