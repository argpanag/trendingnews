K League 1 defending champion Jeonbuk Hyundai has returned to the Asian stage. As the first opponent is a Japanese J-League team, we plan not to let our guard down.

Jeonbuk will play the first leg of the AFC Champions League Elite (ACLE) League Stage in 2026-27 season against Kashiwa Reysol at Jeonju World Cup Stadium on the 16th.

Jeonbuk, which will play in the ACLE stage as a K League 1 winner last season, returned to the top stage in Asia for the first time in two seasons since the 2024-25 Champions League Two (ACLT).

After the ACLE was newly reorganized last season, the number of participating countries this season has increased from 24 teams to 32. The top eight teams will advance to the round of 16 after six league stages are divided into 16 teams from each east and west, and from the quarterfinals, they will gather in neutral areas to play a single match until the final.

Starting with the match against Kashiwa, Jeonbuk will meet Vissel Kobe (Japan), Beijing Guoan (China), Johor Darul Takjim (Malaysia), Kashima Antlers (Japan), and Gamba Osaka (Japan). He has a tough schedule to face four J-League teams.

In response, Jeonbuk coach Chung Jung-yong expressed his intention to bring results as it will be held at home from the first game. "I'm honored to participate in the ACLE stage as a coach," he said at a press conference on the 15th, a day before the game, adding, "As it's a game at home, I'll definitely win and get off to a good start."

Regarding the ACLE goal, he said, "It's a tight schedule, but it's a competition to determine the best in Asia. I don't want to miss one percent. I'll go as high as I can. I will go up step by step and try to reach the semifinals," he said.

Kashiwa, the opponent of this game, is the runner-up team in the J1 League in the 2025 season. In the 2026-27 season, which was converted to the spring season, he is ranked third with 5 wins and 2 losses.

Coach Chung said about Kashiwa, "The opponent maintains more than 60% of the market share and strong pressure is the strength. It also has excellent connection with the midfielders' passes. In particular, we should be wary of the creative play of No. 9 (Hosaya Mao) who leads the attack."

Until now, K-League teams have often struggled with foreign strikers on the ACL stage. However, there are no foreign attackers in Kashiwa. It is a team composed of only pure Japanese players.

"It is unusual that there are no foreign players under the Japanese coach system," coach Chung said. "However, they have their own strengths, such as organizational power and organization." We will take advantage of our own strengths and deal with them," he said.

Captain Kim Tae-hwan, who was present with coach Chung, emphasized that it was a match between Korea and Japan and said, "We should not lose anything trivial. All of the players strengthened their mental armament not to lose even a small part. I'll show you a good game."

He said, "I won't let Kashiwa do what he's good at. I prepared to dig into the part where my opponent was struggling. It will be a different atmosphere from the K-League. We will communicate with the players during the game and work together," he added.

[Kim Young Hoon, MK Sports Reporter]
