Canada’s economy ‘much weaker’ than many expected, says expert after sluggish retail sales report

TheTools · 20.11.2026 18:06 · source · permalink

Canada’s economic woes were on full display Friday, according to one financial expert who says the country’s latest retail sales figures were disappointing, and they’re expected to get worse.

“The Canadian economy is sort of, not on life support, but it has gotten much weaker than I think people were expecting,” Paul Harris, portfolio manager at Harris Douglas Asset Management, told BNN Bloomberg on Friday.

“I think it’s a reflection of many of the issues that we’re facing from a trade basis, but also kind of a global slump that’s going through the world right now.”

Harris’ comments came after Statistics Canada (StatCan) released retail sales data for September, which showed a modest 0.8 per cent increase, boosted mainly by higher prices at gas stations and other fuel vendors.

Core retail sales, which exclude those segments of the economy as well as motor vehicle and parts dealers, rose just 0.1 per cent in September.

“A lot of it was gasoline, so from a regional basis there were some differences, but I think overall, it was not a good number, and October doesn’t look like it’s going to be a good number,” Harris said.

StatCan’s preliminary flash estimate for retail sales last month shows them dropping 1.5 per cent.

“I think that you’re seeing this kind of slowdown in the Canadian economy and all the issues with tariffs and stuff like that catching up to us in the last little while,” argued Harris.

‘Better than expected’

Sal Guatieri, director and senior economist at BMO Capital Markets, says retail sales growth has slowed in the second half of 2026 after a solid start to the year, but he also noted that September’s final retail sales figure came in slightly higher than StatCan’s initial estimate of 0.7 per cent.

“It was a little better than expected, and a little better than StatCan initially estimated for September,” he told BNN Bloomberg on Friday.

“(It was) nice to see some upward revision to the August figure as well, with a more than one per cent gain there, but there were a couple of disappointments though.”

Firstly, Guatieri said, much of September’s retail sales increase was price related, driven by higher costs for gasoline and fuel but also for other consumer goods, he noted.

“So, the volume increase is probably not all that great,” Guatieri said, “and the more worrisome thing is the flash estimate for October… that sets a pretty weak stage and setup for fourth quarter spending.”

Still, Canadian consumer spending has “been holding pretty tough” so far this year, he argued, growing nearly two per cent in real terms in 2026 through the second quarter.

“It does seem like consumers at least are hanging in there,” said Guatieri.

Though still elevated, gasoline prices have started to fall in Canada due to changing global supply and demand dynamics.

Lower fuel prices will improve Canadians’ purchasing power heading into the important holiday retail season, but many shoppers remain wary of other affordability issues such as inflation and rising interest rates.
