Northwest Notes: Thunder, Dort, Wiggins, Towns, Wolves, Jazz

TheTools · 21.11.2026 01:13 · source · permalink

The Thunder have gone 5-5 since they lost Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace and Chet Holmgren to long-term injuries. Williams and Wallace might be out for the season, while Holmgren likely won’t return until at least January.

While their ceiling and floor have both dropped considerably in the wake of the devastating injuries, the Thunder still have some adjustments they could try to make the most of the situation they was dealt, according to Rylan Stiles of SI.com, who writes that running opposing shooters off the three-point line and funneling them into the paint would be one option.

Stiles acknowledges it would be difficult for Oklahoma City to change its defense mid-season, but he views the available personnel as incapable of playing the team’s preferred style. Three-point differential has been a common theme throughout the club’s recent 1-4 stretch, Stiles notes.

On the other end of the court, Stiles thinks Ajay Mitchell needs to increase his offensive aggression and be a consistent No. 2 option alongside Shai Gilgeous-Alexander for the Thunder to maximize the current group’s ceiling.

Here’s more from around the Northwest:

  • The Thunder’s injury woes — including another knee surgery for Thomas Sorber — have obviously cast a heavy cloud over their season. But Wednesday’s return of Luguentz Dort and Aaron Wiggins — longtime key role players who were traded in separate cost-cutting moves over the summer — was a feel-good moment for fans despite dropping the game to Atlanta, writes Joe Mussatto of The Oklahoman (subscriber link). Both players received tribute videos. “It was weird,” Wiggins said. “But obviously felt the love. Yeah, emotional. And also trying to stay composed to play the game.” The veteran wings combined for 30 points on 50% shooting from three-point range and would certainly be nice to have around right now, Mussatto observes. “I didn’t expect them to cheer ‘Lu’ when I was shooting threes today, but the fact that they did, I felt the love,” Dort said. “… It was something I got used to for so many years.”
  • While Karl-Anthony Towns spoke glowingly of his time with the Timberwolves ahead of Thursday’s game in Minnesota, he also said it was “bittersweet” that he was unable to win a championship with his longtime former team, writes Stefan Bondy of The New York Post. “I wanted to win one there. I didn’t accomplish that,” said Towns, who helped the Wolves reach the Western Conference finals in the 2024 playoffs before he was traded to New York in October 2024. “But it was great to have those moments, to bring Minnesota back to the playoffs and be able to do a lot of things.” Towns famously helped the Knicks win the title last season and is off to an excellent start to 2026/27 — he had 22 points, 12 rebounds, four assists, three blocks and was plus-12 in 33 minutes in Thursday’s three-point win.
  • Law Murray of The Athletic (Twitter video link) recently asked head coach Will Hardy about why the Jazz have struggled with both committing fouls and being unable to force turnovers defensively. Hardy explained that the team has been undisciplined with too many reaching fouls, especially early in quarters. He also said a stretched rotation amid injuries has contributed to the lack of forced turnovers.
