Royal Mail confirms correct spot to put stamps on Christmas card envelopes

TheTools · 22.11.2026 13:01 · source · permalink

The placement of stamps matters on an envelope.

Christmas is now just over a month away so there’s not long left to get the endless list of festive tasks done in time for the big day, including sending Christmas cards.

If you are planning to send Christmas cards to family and friends this year, Royal Mail has warned that you “allow plenty of time for posting” this year to ensure items will arrive in time. The postal service has set out its last posting dates for Christmas this year and is urging people who have items to send to make sure stamps are placed in the correct spot. While it may not seem that important, the placement of stamps on envelopes does actually matter and getting it wrong can lead to delays.

Royal Mail has confirmed that the correct spot to place a stamp is in the top-right hand corner of an envelope. Stamps need to be placed here to ensure that envelopes can be properly processed by automated sorting machines, otherwise it may need to be sorted manually which can cause delays.

In its Christmas posting advice, Royal Mail said: “It matters where you place a stamp on an envelope; it should go in the top right-hand corner to ensure your Christmas card is processed correctly and efficiently by our automated sorting machines. Placing it elsewhere can cause delays as it may require manual handling.

“Prices vary by weight and size so sending large letters (up to 100g) by 1st and 2nd class will cost slightly more.”

If you’re posting cards to addresses in the UK, Royal Mail recommends using your local postbox or Post Office branch, and to ensure you do this well in advance of the last Christmas posting dates to make sure they arrive before December 25.
