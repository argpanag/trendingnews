Currently (3AM HST), radar and satellite imagery shows a few scattered showers and clouds across the state. Expect northeasterly trades to gradually decrease today. As we head into this evening and for the next few days the weather pattern gets rather complicated.
A large vertically stacked low sits about 800 miles to our northeast. A front extends to the east and then south of the low, and a trough extends southwest from the decaying end of the front toward Hawaii. A separate upper level trough is our northwest and Tropical Storm Olana sits to our southwest. The northwestern trough will sag southeast helping pull Olana toward the islands. Tropical Storm Olana is expected to weaken into a tropical depression and stay to the west of the islands Tuesday. The trough extending from the low to our northeast will eventually extend all the way to the remnants of Olana, and then drift north over the state. This trough will bring abundant tropical moisture with PWATs close to 2.5 inches and remain nearly stationary over the area through the end of the week. A series of small lows will form with one or more potentially moving over the state. Models still show disagreement on timing and locations of these features. This makes the uncertainty higher than normal.
