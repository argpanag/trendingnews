    Tom Bateman
    Reporting from Paris

    I’m travelling with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, at a G7 foreign ministers meeting in Paris, as he studiously avoids talking about the Jensen Huang story.

    News of Huang’s statement and Nvidia’s investigation broke just before Rubio was motorcaded into a US airbase in Florida last night as we were about to get on the government plane.

    The New York Times reports that Rubio oversaw the drafting of a highly classified memo in the spring warning other top US officials that Huang was in a close relationship with a Chinese spy.

    The BBC has not independently verified the report and the State Department and the White House have not responded to our questions.

    As we’ve reported, Huang accompanied Trump on his trip to Beijing in May, after the Rubio memo was circulated, according to the New York Times. I was part of the White House press pool on the trip, and saw Huang among other tech leaders including Elon Musk in the president’s close entourage.

    The executives were brought into the heart of the world’s consequential bilateral relationship when Trump unexpectedly invited them into the room during one of the highly choreographed meetings with President Xi.

    Nvidia, opening its investigation, says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.

    There's a great deal of news at the White House this morning, including a new incoming chief of staff and a visit by Qatar's prime minister later today.

    The BBC has asked the White House for clarification on the timeline of Trump's knowledge of the affair, whether any action was taken, and whether the initial exclusion of Jensen Huang from a trip to China earlier this year was related to it.

  12. Before AI took off, Nvidia was well known in the gaming industrypublished at 12:09 GMT
    12:09 GMT

    Tinshui Yeung
    Live reporter

    Nvidia has come to the fore in recent years, but it was established well before the generative AI trend, and its early business had little to do with it.

    Established in 1993, Nvidia’s early business was mainly in computer graphics cards - hardware responsible for processing graphics. As graphics are especially important for gaming, the company became well known in the gaming community for producing high-quality cards.

    One big difference between a computer’s graphics processing unit (GPU) and central processing unit (CPU) is that the GPU has more cores, as parallel processing is more important in graphics computing.

    That is precisely why GPUs are widely used in AI computing, which also requires the ability to perform calculations simultaneously.

    As a result, Nvidia has soared in the recent AI boom and become one of the most valuable companies in the world.
