Democrats Appear to Flip 1st Midterm Seat as Guam Drops Unofficial Results

TheTools · 03.11.2026 21:43 · source · permalink

Associate News Editor

Democrats appeared poised to score the first congressional flip of the 2026 midterms after unofficial results from Guam showed Alicia Anne Garrido Limtiaco leading Republican incumbent James Moylan for the territory’s non-voting U.S. House delegate seat.

The result offers Democrats an early symbolic gain as they seek control of the House, but the Guam seat does not count toward the 218 seats needed for a majority.

The outcome is not yet final, with outstanding ballots still to be processed before the Guam Election Commission certifies the election.

The latest unofficial Guam Election Commission figures put Limtiaco, a former Guam attorney general, at 15,645 votes, or 51 percent. Moylan had 14,983 votes, or 48.84 percent, leaving the Democrat ahead by 662 votes.

The commission's latest release did not specify how many of Guam's 59 precincts were included, according to Guam News Network, which publishes the commission's figures.

The early numbers also showed Guam delivered a divided result rather than an across-the-board swing toward either party. Republican Vicente "Tony" Ada led the governor's race with 15,945 votes, or 51.45 percent, against 14,925 votes, or 48.15 percent, for Democrat Therese Terlaje.

Alicia Limtiaco Leads James Moylan in Guam House Race

Limtiaco's advantage, if it holds up through the remaining count, would return Guam's congressional delegation to Democratic hands. Moylan first won the territory's delegate seat in 2022 and entered Tuesday seeking another term.

He carried the 2024 election with 52.7 percent of the vote.

The latest tally reflects a much tighter, politically reversed contest. More than 30,600 votes had been recorded in the delegate race, including write-ins, while the territory reported overall turnout of 31,414 ballots from 59,146 registered voters, or 53.11 percent.

Why the Guam Delegate Flip Does Not Change the House Majority

Guam's early count carries political significance for Democrats, but it does not give the party another voting seat in its effort to control the House. As a U.S. territory, Guam sends a delegate, not a full voting representative, to Congress.

The delegate can participate in congressional work but cannot vote on final passage of legislation on the House floor.

A Limtiaco victory would constitute a partisan change in congressional representation without changing the arithmetic needed to control the 435-member House.

Still, Guam attracts outsized attention early on Election Day because its location in the western Pacific puts it hours ahead of the continental United States.

Its results can provide an early political data point before most mainland races have produced meaningful returns, though the territory's electorate and nonvoting congressional status sharply limit how much can be extrapolated nationally.

Republicans Lead Guam Governor's Race

While Democrats were ahead in the delegate contest, the gubernatorial results pointed in the opposite direction. Ada and his Republican running mate, E.J. Calvo, had 15,945 votes, compared with 14,925 for Terlaje and Democratic running mate Sabina Perez, which put the Republican ticket ahead by 1,020 votes in the latest unofficial count.

A Republican victory would represent another significant political change for Guam.

Democratic Governor Lou Leon Guerrero, elected in 2018 and reelected in 2022, cannot seek another term because of term limits. Terlaje emerged from a competitive Democratic primary in August, while Ada won the Republican nomination.

Earlier returns had actually placed Terlaje narrowly ahead. With 21 of 59 precincts counted, she led Ada by just 80 votes, 5,243 to 5,163, before the Republican ticket moved ahead as more ballots were counted.

Donald Trump Celebrates Republican Showing in Guam

President Donald Trump seized on the Republican performance in Guam's governor's race, portraying it as an encouraging early sign for the GOP as Election Day unfolded across the United States.

"Guam, which has always been considered strongly Democrat, just had an early call for a REPUBLICAN Victory in the top spot, Governor — Congratulations to Tony Ada!" Trump wrote on Truth Social.

"Wow, hopefully that's an indication for things to come!" he added while also touting Republican legislative gains.

When Guam's 2026 Election Results Become Final

The Guam Election Commission has not certified the contests, meaning the margins could still change.

The latest figures by the time of publication list 31,414 ballots cast from 59,146 registered voters, but absentee and provisional ballots remain part of the post-election process.

Under the commission's timetable, voters with qualifying provisional ballots have until November 13 to address certain deficiencies, while absentee ballots must arrive by 5 p.m. on November 18.

The commission is tentatively scheduled to count outstanding ballots and certify the results on November 19.
