Three ex-Oilers forwards off to strong starts, but not yet departed Edmonton folk hero

TheTools · 04.11.2026 02:01 · source · permalink

Three ex-Edmonton Oilers forwards are off to great starts this year in their new NHL homes, but local folk hero Connor Brown, now of the New Jersey Devils, isn’t one of them, not yet anyway.

Brown, now 32, has struggled with a lower body injury this year, going on injured reserve in mid-October, and having played only six games so far.

Last year Brown equalled the best scoring season of his career, with 43 points in 75 games. It tied his highest point total since 2019-20. It came on the first year of a four-year contract that pays him $3 million a year.

But his spot in the line-up is not yet secure this season, as he played just 10:14 in his last game on Friday against Vegas.

Brown earned folk hero status in Edmonton for playing in dogged and enthusiastic fashion as he was coming back from knee surgery in the 2023-24 season. He went without scoring a goal in his first 54 games but his work ethic never relented. He ended up with only four goals in 71 games, but he was a beast on a wildly-successful Oilers penalty kill in the 2024 playoffs. That unit was a key reason the Oilers made it to the Stanley Cup Final, losing to Florida in seven games.

Brown returned to Edmonton for one more year. He scored 30 points in 82 games and again ramped up his play in Edmonton’s run to the 2025 Stanley Cup Final.

Following the season — with the Oilers in a terrible cap crunch and unable to pay Brown what he was worth — he signed with New Jersey.

The Devils are a team on the rise, full of young talent. Here’s hoping Brown grabs hold of a third-line spot and plays out his career there in good health. I find it impossible not to root for the guy.

Hot -ex Oilers

The three ex-Oilers forwards off to strong starts this year include Jack Roslovic in Toronto, Viktor Arvidsson in Detroit and Dylan Holloway. Each of them is close to averaging one point per game.

Kailer Yamamoto in Utah is also off to a decent start with seven points in nine games.

But most other ex-Oilers forwards are struggling this year, including Ryan McLeod in Buffalo, just one goal in 14 games, Andrew Mangiapane in Chicago, zero goals in three games, and Carolina’s Taylor Hall, also dealing with injury.

It’s been even tougher for a number of former Oilers who have not yet been able to find an NHL contract or retired for other reasons, including Evander Kane, Jujhar Khaira, Adam Erne, Noah Philp and Adam Henrique.
