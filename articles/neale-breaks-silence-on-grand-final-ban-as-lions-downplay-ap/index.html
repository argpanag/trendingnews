Lachie Neale failed to have his one-match ban overturned at the AFL tribunal. (Getty Images/AFL Photos: Michael Willson)

In short:

The Lions will appeal Lachie Neale's one-match ban in the hope of having the two-time premiership co-captain cleared for the grand final.

Neale says he will remain in Melbourne to support Brisbane regardless of whether he is allowed to play or not.

What's next?

An appeals board hearing will be held tomorrow, beginning at 4pm, AEST.

Lachie Neale says he will remain in Melbourne to support Brisbane in Saturday's AFL grand final even if he is not successful with his appeals board hearing. 

An appeals board hearing is the final avenue available for Neale, who unsuccessfully challenged the ban at a tribunal hearing last night.

The Lions announced this afternoon they would appeal the tribunal's decision.

Neale has travelled to Melbourne with the Lions squad. The appeals board hearing will be held tomorrow, beginning at 4pm, AEST.

"Obviously, as you guys will be aware, the club and myself have decided to appeal the decision," Neale told reporters at Melbourne Airport.

"Obviously very hopeful that that's successful, but regardless of the result, I will be here to support the boys and the club this week. It's a big week for the club."

Neale did not take any questions from reporters and departed after giving a brief statement. 

Brisbane veteran Dayne Zorko said the lead-up to this year's grand final was "not too dissimilar" to last year', when Neale was under an injury cloud.

There were doubts over Neale's availability in the lead-up to last year's grand final against Geelong due to a calf injury. (Getty Images: Darrian Traynor)

"There was a lot of scrutiny last year about whether he'd play or he wouldn't [and] in '24 he had a broken foot, so we're used to it," he said.

"He [Neale] seems to be fine. He wants to play and he's just taking it in his stride.

"You work so hard to get to this position, so he is disappointed, but we'll appeal it and go from there.

"We've had this stuff in the past. It's water under a bridge for us. We just keep going forward with our process and worry about what we can control.

"[Neale has an] impeccable record, you'd like to think that would carry a bit of weight [at the tribunal]. Maybe it doesn't.

"We can't get too carried away about it just being a Lachie Neale team. We're the Brisbane Lions, we've got a lot of players on our list.

"We played a few finals last year without him, as well. We'll just let that run its course. We're not going to get distracted by that, it's not our business to get distracted by it.

"We'd love for him to be out there, we hope he's out there, but if he's not, the next man up mentality is going to come into play."

Brisbane has brought an extended squad down to Melbourne ahead of the grand final. 

No decision has been made yet on who will replace Neale if his suspension is upheld.

To succeed at the appeals board hearing, the Lions will have to prove the tribunal finding was either an error of law, an unreasonable decision, a manifestly excessive classification, or a manifestly excessive sanction.

They face Fremantle in Saturday's grand final at the MCG, chasing a third successive premiership.

Neale was suspended after the tribunal rejected his "inconsistent" evidence in a landmark case.

He was found guilty of striking Hawthorn's Jack Ginnivan in Saturday night's preliminary final.

The two-time premiership captain argued his action was not a strike, but a body-check.

After a dramatic four-and-a-half-hour hearing, the tribunal upheld the charge and dismissed pleas for a downgrade due to exceptional and compelling circumstances.

The off-the-ball blow on Ginnivan was graded as intentional conduct, medium impact and body contact by match review officer Michael Christian.

Neale argued contact was made with his shoulder and chest and was not a strike.

He said Ginnivan "might have taken a bit of a dive to get a free kick".

"My shoulder made contact with his body and shoulder first and my arm wrapped around Jack," he said.

"But I didn't feel like I was striking him at all. It was more a bump or body-check."

The Lions will roll the dice on Lachie Neale's ban at an appeals board hearing. (Getty Images/AFL Photos: Robert Cianflone)

But tribunal chair Jeff Gleeson KC dismissed Neale's version of events.

"We find that this was a forceful blow delivered with a swinging arm and an open hand," Gleeson said.

"When played at normal speed, it appears clear to us that this was, to use the common vernacular, a strike to the guts.

"We are comfortably satisfied that this is what occurred. We find that this is what Neale intended.

"We have listened carefully to Mr Neale's evidence but we find it was inconsistent in key respects with what we saw on the video and, to that extent, we do not accept it."
