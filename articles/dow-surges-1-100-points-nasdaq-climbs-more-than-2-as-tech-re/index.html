Dow surges 1,100 points, Nasdaq climbs more than 2% as tech rebounds from last week’s tumult: Live updates

TheTools · 08.11.2026 23:15 · source · permalink

Stocks surged on Monday, boosted by a decline in oil prices as well as gains in technology shares after last week's volatility.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average popped 1,104.02 points, or 2.15%, to close at 52,389.75. The blue-chip index was supported by gains in stocks such as Goldman Sachs and Caterpillar. The S&P 500 rose 2.35% to end at 7,549.94 for its best day since April 8, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.74% to 25,253.14.

With Monday's rally, the Dow is now above the level it closed on Election Day. The S&P 500 and tech-heavy Nasdaq still remain slightly below, however.

Oil prices fell on Monday, lifting investor sentiment. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures dropped 4.38% to settle at $86.85 per barrel. International benchmark Brent slid 5.85% to $92.57 per barrel.

Friday's market partially rebounded from Thursday's brutal artificial intelligence-driven sell-off, as chip stocks recovered on renewed buying. However, concerns linger in the wake of the Democrats' strong midterm results, with investors fearing that tighter regulations on data center construction, energy use, and development costs could slow the AI infrastructure boom.

The tech sector also focused on the bombshell announcement Sunday that Nvidia's board of directors is opening an investigation into co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a businesswoman who U.S. intelligence agencies say is a Chinese spy. Nvidia shares closed down 4%.

"Magnificent Seven" names were higher contrary to Nvidia. Amazon gained about 3%, while Tesla and Alphabet climbed 5.5% and 1%, respectively. Microsoft also added almost 3%.

Other chip stocks advanced as well, with Broadcom popping nearly 9% and Advanced Micro Devices jumping 13%. The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) — which dropped around 13% last week — rose more than 7%.

The recent chip sell-off was "overdone," said Jay Hatfield, CEO at Infrastructure Capital Advisors. The turmoil was still "partially correct," he added, given that there "definitely is a policy overhang from the Democrats in both the short run, but, more importantly, in the long run from '28." To be sure, he believes data centers are ultimately "going to get built" in the U.S.

For the broader market, Hatfield said that "it's not like the coast is clear" with Monday's bounce. In fact, he anticipates the S&P 500 will be rangebound through the end of the year.

"There are still a lot of overhangs on the market," the CEO said, pointing to elevated oil prices as a result of the Middle East conflict as well as the Federal Reserve's interest rate outlook.

This week, investors are looking ahead to key AI-related earnings — CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials are all on deck — as they assess the outlook for tech and whether the recent sell-off has further to run.

They'll also watch for the releases of both the consumer price index on Tuesday and the producer price index on Friday, looking for clues on the Fed's next move.

The three U.S. leading indexes popped on Monday.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 1,104.02, or 2.15%, to settle at 52,389.75. The S&P 500 rose 2.35% to close at 7,549.94, while the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.74% to 25,253.14.

General Motors' move to the Nasdaq on Nov. 17, and its possible inclusion in the Nasdaq-100 index as early as December, could bring increased investor visibility and focus to the overlooked automaker, according to Citi analyst Michael Ward.

If GM joins the Nasdaq-100 index it "would have a 0.2% weighting in the Index which represents about $4.6 billion of buying, or 10% of the non-indexed market cap," he wrote.

Additionally, he expects GM to buy back $10 billion in shares by the end of 2027, about 20% of the non-indexed market cap, which will support shares. If it joins, GM will become the cheapest stock in the index with the highest buyback yield, Ward noted.

CNBC recently said GM's switch to the Nasdaq could help it reframe its brand identity and boost its depressed valuation with the exposure to a new set of potential investors. Citi agreed.

"In our view, the structural improvements at GM and the North American industry have been missed, and if GM becomes part of the Nasdaq 100 Index, new investors will need to take a look which could bring added value to the stock," Ward wrote. The move helps reposition GM as a broad technology-first manufacturer, not just a combustion engine maker. "Consumer connection and complex manufacturing expertise position the auto sector to benefit from developing trends in energy storage, autonomous technologies, and connected platforms," wrote Ward.

Citi rates GM a buy with a $131 a share price target, which is more than 50% above Friday's close.

— Justin Zacks

Stocks, primarily those exposed to the AI buildout, were hit last week following a bigger-than-expected victory by Democrats in the midterm elections. But Oppenheimer chief investment strategist John Stoltzfus thinks that the market can rebound, as it started on Monday.

"So long as the underlying fundamentals that support economic growth and nurture revenue

and profit growth have not dramatically deteriorated… the market can find opportunity to not just regain lost ground but even rise well above where it had been," Stoltzfus wrote in a Monday note. 

This week, he said that all eyes will be on the inflation report set to be delivered Tuesday, particularly off a hot jobs report on Friday, as investors look to predict what the Federal Reserve will do with interest rates at its December meeting. And while some investors were nervous about the AI trade following Democratic victories last week, Stoltzfus doesn't share those concerns.

"It would seem to us that it is much too early to predict the failure of AI to gain traction or achieve profitability," he said.

The New York Stock Exchange has chosen Avalanche as the primary blockchain network for processing clearing and settlement on its upcoming affiliated tokenized securities alternative trading system.

The price of the Avalanche token (AVAX) jumped 8%, according to CoinGecko. Shares of Avalanche Treasury Corp, which was created to be the proxy trade for the blockchain network, jumped 12% in light trading.

Avalanche is a six-year-old blockchain built for business. Unlike many networks aimed primarily at retail traders, Ava Labs, the company that helped develop Avalanche, has for years pursued financial institutions interested in tokenizing real-world assets and moving traditional financial infrastructure onto blockchain rails. Users include BlackRock, Franklin Templeton, Apollo, FIFA and the state of Wyoming.

"At the NYSE, we're looking at how blockchain technology can expand what we offer in equities, and we're picking the right technology for each job," said Dennis Lee, head of digital issuance at NYSE, said in a statement. "The upcoming digital trading platform will support equities listed on a range of blockchains, though the ledger that records and settles trades needs to be fast and reliable, let us control who can take part, keep costs predictable, and have a large community of developers behind it."

— Tanaya Macheel

Crédit Agricole has proposed merging its Italian operation with Banco BPM in a deal that would create Italy's third-largest banking group.

The French lender said in a statement Monday that the "friendly proposal" to engage its Italian peer in merger discussions is a "natural extension" of the long-term partnership between the pair.

Under the deal, Crédit Agricole would increase its current 29.6% stake in Banco BPM to approximately 45% of the new combined entity.

The proposal offers Banco BPM shareholders a 20% premium to the relative valuation of the businesses, based on estimated 2027 price-to-earnings multiples, and a 1 billion euro ($1.12 billion) special dividend, according to Crédit Agricole's announcement.

The Paris-headquartered bank said the deal — which has been submitted to Banco BPM's board of directors for evaluation — could deliver 800 million in annual synergies, with about 24% 2027 earnings-per-share accretion expected.

"This strategic project would create Italy's third-largest banking group, serving 6.5 million customers through more than 2,300 branches, by combining a strong local presence, financial strength, and business expertise," Crédit Agricole added.

— Hugh Leask

UniCredit's third-quarter net profit rose by a record 24% year-on-year to 3.3 billion euros ($3.68 billion), beating consensus estimates of 2.78 billion euros, as revenues reached 6.9 billion euros, up 12% year-on-year.

The Italian bank, which is set to take control of German lender Commerzbank, also hiked its full-year profit guidance to full-year profit guidance to "well above" 11.5 billion euros, from more than 11 billion euros.

Net interest income was up 5% over the quarter, and 8% year-on-year, while common equity tier (CET) 1 capital ratio — a measure of a bank's solvency — reached 15% during the period, up from 14.3% the previous quarter.

"UniCredit has delivered its best quarter ever, and the best in the sector, beating across all KPIs: highest net profit growth, best RoTE and cost/income ratio and organic capital generation supporting best-in- class shareholder returns while increasing capital," CEO Andrea Orcel said in a statement.

The Milan-headquartered lender said the Commerzbank acquisition would strengthen its pan-European footprint and boost earnings and shareholder distributions. It expects the remaining approvals to be greenlighted by European regulators later this month, with the deal's integration plan set to begin in the first quarter of 2027.

Producer prices in China grew at the fastest rate since June 2022, accelerating for the third month in a row.

The PPI for October came in at 5.7%, compared to the 5.3% expected by economists polled by Reuters. The increase was driven by a sharp rise in prices in the mining and raw materials industries.

Consumer inflation in China slowed to 0.7% in October, its first deceleration after two straight months of increases.

Core inflation, which strips out food and energy prices, came in at 0.8%, down from the 1% seen in the month before.
