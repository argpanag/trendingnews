‘The Fantasticks’ Review: A Gender Swap for a Still-Tender Story

TheTools · 17.11.2026 03:00 · source · permalink

Advertisement

Supported by

Now two men play the young lovers in this long-running musical from 1960. Its quaint nostalgia remains intact for this Broadway production.

Debuting on Broadway 66 years after its premiere, the “Fantasticks” revival that just opened at the Helen Hayes Theater throws a defiant challenge.

Luisa and Matt, the wide-eyed young lovers in Tom Jones and Harvey Schmidt’s endearing, enduring musical, are now both male: Lewis (Nathan Levy) and Matt (Maxim Chlumecky).

Yet, beside the trend of gender-swapping in classic musicals (a lesbian pairing in “Oklahoma!” here, a female Bobbie in “Company” there), it’s hard to find a place more tolerant of queerness than Broadway. The gleefully pansexual “Rocky Horror Show” is running just a few blocks away. A gay couple onstage is not going to make anyone in a New York City audience clutch his pearls.

Even within the world of the show — in which the conspiring fathers have been replaced by meddling mothers — nobody bats an eyelash.

“Two boys?” the narrator and trickster El Gallo (Zane Phillips) asks. “And you’re not worried?”

Lewis’s mom, Bessie (Ruthie Ann Miles), is unbothered: “They love each other. Why should we be worried?”
