NZ Breakers owners pay up to keep team on the road

TheTools · 10.10.2026 13:29 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Sport

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._Q.VKhexyL5oal5uCV3AUQmI1EOdryHi3hh73RdibV20lY/sport/1784292%2Fnz-breakers-owners-pay-up-to-keep-team-on-the-road

In the space of a fortnight the Breakers will travel to just about every corner of Australia that has an NBL team - and one that doesn't.

The Breakers are 24 seasons deep into the trans-Tasman competition and like all 10 teams, can still "poke holes" in the schedule.

At least Singapore is not a stop off for the Auckland-based club like it was briefly a decade ago.

Extended stretches on the road are expected for the Breakers each season but the tradeoff of a span of four home games this December will be one the players will have circled on the calendar.

The upcoming schedule has the Breakers playing four road games in a row. Their last game was also an away win against the Adelaide 36ers on Sunday.

On Saturday the team will play the Perth Wildcats, four days later they play the Sydney Kings in Canberra, and four days after that they play the Tasmania JackJumpers.

A week after that it is a trip to the Cairns Taipans.

Breakers captain Parker Jackson-Cartwright has played Cairns on his home floor, now the Auckland team travels to Far North Queensland.

Photosport

The first three of those road games will be the club's longest away stint of the season so far.

The decision to not return to Auckland between those games, and spend 11 days on the road, was done with player welfare in mind according to the club's president of basketball operations Dillon Boucher.

"The priority is the wellbeing of the players and ensuring their bodies are recovering, so staying over was the obvious choice."

It can be a challenge logistically when the club decides to stay on in Australia.

"It is training courts, even things like having to travel with your basketballs on the road, whereas if you're normally just going for one game, it's all supplied.

"Each team has to supply the accommodation when you go there for two nights, so they supply that, anything outside of that then we pay for.

"So, it's finding a place to do weights, recovery, all those sorts of things, massage, you name it, it's a lot of logistics when you stay somewhere."

Boucher said the club would travel with an extra player this month.

"We're taking an extra body on this road trip because of the training requirements and we've got a few banged up bodies that we want to make sure we're managing.

"That's where cost goes up because you're adding these players to it but it's important because the welfare, again, of the players is number one and we want to make sure we've got healthy bodies come game time and you can only do that by having training bodies there to make sure they're carrying some of the load."

For years Breakers coaches have been questioning the schedule and current coach Gordie Herbert did wonder how his team ended up in this position.

"I'm not sure who makes up the schedule, but it is what it is," Herbert said.

While Herbert was perplexed by the travel schedule, Boucher was playing in the league when the now defunct Singapore Slingers added an extra travel element for teams for two seasons.

Breakers' Ben Pepper against the Singapore Slingers in 2007.

Photosport

The Slingers were a move by the NBL to expand the reach of the competition, now they are doing that within Australia with the Kings hosting the Breakers in the capital on Wednesday.

Canberra do not currently have an NBL team but the city is in the mix for an expansion side.

New Breakers assistant coach Sebastian Gleim praised the "really good conditions" the team does have when they travel.

"I was never in better conditions on the road so far in my career."

The Breakers' road form turned around against the 36ers. After two away losses, the win against last season's runners-up is a good launch pad into what they face next.

Breakers Reuben Te Rangi talks to Tukaha Cooper.

Blake Armstrong/Photosport

Tall Black Reuben Te Rangi is in his 15th NBL season and he has played for four different teams. Now in his second stint with the Breakers he is accustomed to what being on the road is like and how it can galvanise a team.

"I feel like this period here is going to be crucial for our season," Te Rangi said.

"It's awesome getting these road games out of the way, but we've got to come out of these games with a good record.

"I think we're doing it for the right reasons, staying on the road, but it's going to be good for us."

Tipping off the road trip in Perth is fitting.

"I think us and Perth have some of the toughest travel schedules out of all of the teams, but we don't make that one of our excuses, we deal with it," Te Rangi said.

"We all know that the travel's going to be tough, but we take it in our stride."
