Auckland FC v Central Coast Mariners: Champs seek first win in a month

TheTools · 28.11.2026 10:02 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

Auckland FC's Luis Felipe Gallegos. Photo / Photosport

Auckland FC face the Central Coast Mariners in Gosford.

Auckland FC are heading in the right direction – but the results need to change.

That’s the blunt message from coach Pedro Moreira, ahead of Saturday’s trip to face the Central Coast Mariners. It’s an understandable premise.

Moreira can see signs of progress and is satisfied with how certain aspects, including their attacking game, have evolved. But he is also a realist.

The Portuguese knows that the defending champions have to start banking wins, for confidence, belief and to stay in touch with the leading pack.

Saturday’s 1-1 draw with Melbourne Victory was their third successive stalemate, after the derby (0-0) and the 1-1 tie in Brisbane, where the Roar equalised with the last kick of the match.

It’s left Auckland FC in eighth place, though only one point off the top six in a congested ladder. Moreira was honest on Wednesday when asked if the team were on the right trajectory.

“In the quality of the game, yes, in the points, no,” Moreira said.

“For me as a coach, for us as a team, we need the points. So sometimes, even if we don’t play with quality, we must fight for the points.”

The collective spirit seems to be there, shown by the way they persevered for an 89th-minute equaliser on Saturday, against a Victory side who parked the bus for most of the second half.

It was also illustrated in the Wellington Phoenix clash, as Auckland FC markedly lifted in the second half, creating a number of chances. But the goals aren’t yet flowing; Lachie Bayliss’ strike ended a period more than four hours without one, since Hiroki Sakai’s early goal in Brisbane. But there are signs and Moreira is adamant it will turn soon.

“We played with more quality in the last games,” the coach said. “We improved a few issues that we need to improve. We consolidated the process ... especially in the offensive moment, but also in the defensive moment. We are working hard. We must join the process with the results.”

There have been incremental gains. Defensively, the team have looked more solid and after struggling for decent possession early in the season, that has flipped in the last two matches. But everything hinges on their product in the final third, the most difficult aspect of the game.

Moreira hopes for small improvements; faster transitions, more accurate deliveries, greater numbers in the penalty area, which add up to the required output. The players, including Bayliss, back Moreira’s sentiments.

“From the first few rounds, excluding round one, we’ve improved dramatically, especially with the ball,” the All Whites midfielder said. “We’re playing a lot better football and it’s just [about] converting chances at the end of the day.”

It was important to be “more ruthless” in front of goal but Bayliss felt that a turnaround wasn’t far away.

“We’re building really nicely and everything will start to click into place.”

Young defender Ronan Wynne had a similar view.

“We’ve shown flashes in each game,” Wynne said. “It’s just little moments that have let us down.”

Still, despite the general optimism, Moreira doesn’t hide the fact that he is feeling some heat. Auckland FC are used to being pacesetters – they were first (15 points) and second (11 points) respectively in the previous two seasons at the same stage. The overall context can’t be ignored – as Auckland FC have lost many of their most important players and overhauled the coaching staff, with Moreira trying to bed in new ideas – but football is unforgiving.

“I feel the pressure every week, especially on the next game,“ Moreira said. ”But it’s our work, we do what we love, it’s the best profession in the world. It’s the best job and I’ve very happy to be in Auckland. Of course, we have the pressure and we must feel the pressure because we know what we must do on the pitch.“

Midfielder Cam Howieson, who has been missed after a high-grade hamstring strain suffered in pre-season, trained well on Wednesday. He is a chance to feature on Saturday, though Moreira is also wary of bringing him back too early.

“He’s close,” he said. “If we put [Howieson in] already, it could be a risky situation for him. We are analysing everything ... he was a few weeks out.”
