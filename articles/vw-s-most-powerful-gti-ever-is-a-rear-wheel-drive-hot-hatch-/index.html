VW’s Most Powerful GTI Ever Is a Rear-Wheel Drive Hot Hatch, But There’s a Catch or Two

TheTools · 16.09.2026 16:33 · source · permalink

Its 322 horsepower mean this forbidden fruit EV is the most powerful car to ever wear the GTI badge.

The Volkswagen ID.3 may not have a Golf badge on the back, but it is the modern-day electric Golf in everything but name. And fortunately for enthusiasts in the countries that offer the electric VW, that means it now is getting a GTI variant.

Much like the ID.Polo GTI announced earlier this year, the ID.3 GTI applies the basic GTI formula to an electric economy car just as Volkswagen typically would with an internal combustion car. Unlike that spritely sub-compact, however, the ID.3 GTI sends its power solely to the rear wheels. That’s a major departure for a GTI model, breaking from a long run of the badge signifying VW’s best in front-wheel drive performance.

The power itself is a departure, too: with 322 horsepower under the hood, so to speak, the single-motor ID.3 is the most powerful VW to ever wear the GTI badge. It’s a jump of just short of 100 hp over the smaller ID.Polo GTI, putting the ID.3 in line with internal combustion hot hatches like the Honda Civic Type R and Toyota GR Yaris and Corolla.

The compact ID.3’s output is still short of more dedicated performance models like the 510 hp Tesla Model 3 Performance, the 601 hp Hyundai Ioniq 5 N, or the ridiculous 680 hp Mercedes-AMG CLA 45, but a future dual-motor model could theoretically line up against those cars if one were built. Since the internal combustion Golf GTI gets an all-wheel drive relative in the Golf R, such a model could be possible with that badge if VW thinks the demand exists.

As the ID.3 is an electric hatchback from Volkswagen, its bona fides as an economy car remain important. An estimated range of 373 miles on a 79-kWh battery pack gives it serious practicality, although that number is a brand estimate likely based on Europe’s relatively generous WLTP cycle. Peak charging speeds of 189 kW pushes the battery from 10 percent state-of-charge to 80 percent in a manufacturer-claimed 29 minutes on a DC fast charger, too.

While the ID.3 GTI sounds like a pretty compelling package, American buyers are unlikely to get a chance to buy their own any time soon. VW only offers the basic ID.3 in Europe, and the GTI seems destined to follow the same path. American buyers looking for a performance EV may be left wanting for a while longer, but at least the U.S. still gets the internal combustion GTI and Golf R models. Buyers looking for an electric Volkswagen here, meanwhile, will have to settle for the ID.4 crossover or the ID.Buzz van.
