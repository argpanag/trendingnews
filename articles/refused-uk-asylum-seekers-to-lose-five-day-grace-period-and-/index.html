Refused UK asylum seekers to lose five-day grace period and face immediate removal

TheTools · 16.11.2026 20:06 · source · permalink

Home Office plans will cause unnecessary distress and anxiety, say refugee charities

Asylum seekers who have exhausted the formal appeals process will be stripped of a five-day grace period and become eligible for immediate removal under Home Office plans condemned as “distressing” by refugee charities.

Shabana Mahmood, the home secretary, will amend a bill currently before parliament to allow immigration enforcement officers to detain and remove people who have unsuccessfully appealed against their asylum or immigration claim being rejected without further notice.

Currently, enforced returns for rejected claimants can only take place after a minimum notice period of five working days, which can allow people to launch other legal interventions or prepare themselves for removal.

Imran Hussain, the director of external affairs at the Refugee Council, condemned the change.

“We must treat people with dignity. Removing people at such short notice will cause unnecessary distress and anxiety,” he said.

“We also need to look at the evidence of what works. The reality is that voluntary removals are far more effective at removing people than enforced returns. If you assist people to return with dignity, many will take that option, at far lower cost to the taxpayer.”

Louise Calvey, the executive director at Asylum Matters, said: “People deserve a fair chance to make their claim for help and sanctuary, but forcibly removing people from the country so they don’t get a chance to make their claims won’t fix that.

“Right now, 63% of the population don’t have access to an asylum legal aid provider. What that means is people are attempting to navigate a horrendously complex and hostile system in a new country, often in a second language. In that environment, many people may not have had the proper advice in initial claims, so many who make further claims are found to have a genuine need for support.

“Expelling people from the country to ensure we never get to hear their legitimate claims is nothing to be proud of – it will lead to real injustices.”

Mahmood has tabled an amendment to the immigration and asylum bill that will scrap the five-day timeframe.

Officials would instead be given a six-month period to remove refused asylum seekers and rejected migrants before further notice is required, the Home Office said.

The changes follow a similar approach to the system that Denmark successfully operates, the Home Office has said.

The number of migrants awaiting removal in Denmark was cut from more than 2,185 cases at the end of 2020 to fewer than 1,000 at the end of 2025.

The UK has increased the number of removals since Labour came to power, but faces huge backlogs in asylum applications. As of 30 June 2026, 40,168 people were waiting for an initial asylum decision in the UK. The number of refused applications hit more than 90,000 in the same month, breaking previous records.

There are currently exceptions to the five-day notice period for removals, such as where the removal was disrupted by the individual or was cancelled due to bad weather. In these scenarios, removal can be attempted again without further notice for 21 days.

This timeframe will now be increased to six months under reforms in the bill, which returns to the House of Commons for report stage and third reading on 17 November.

The bill will also introduce a new immigration appeals authority with the aim of speeding up removals and changes to how human rights and modern slavery laws are applied to asylum applications to root out what the government described as “vexatious” claims.

Ministers hope the bill will bring into force proposals that will create a firm but fair asylum system, and which will reduce the pull factors driving illegal immigration.

Under other reforms, the responsibility to make modern slavery decisions for children will be devolved to local safeguarding partners.

This will ensure professionals who already support the child, and are closest to the child, are making the decisions for their future, the Home Office said.

Anna Turley, the minister for border security and asylum, said: “This government is ramping up removals of those with no right to be here, with over 80,000 illegal migrants and foreign nationals returned since the general election.

“Now we are ensuring those migrants who have exhausted their right to appeal can be removed promptly, rather than being given an opportunity to frustrate their legitimate removal from this country.”
