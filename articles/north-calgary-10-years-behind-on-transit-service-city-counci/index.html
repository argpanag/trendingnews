North Calgary '10 years behind' on transit service: city councillor

TheTools · 04.11.2026 15:11 · source · permalink

By Jillian Code

Posted Nov 4, 2026 9:11 am.

A Calgary city councillor is urging change with transit service on Centre Street, saying residents in the northern portion of the city aren’t seeing sufficient access and can’t keep waiting for it.

“We’re behind. We’re 10 years behind on everything,” Ward 3 councillor Andrew Yule says.

City council was presented with four plans at the city’s executive committee Nov 3. on how to bolster the Max Green bus line, with plans ranging from $500 million for curbside rapid transit to more than $2 billion for a standalone bus transit-way that could eventually be replaced by the Green Line LRT.

But after years of delays and Green Line cost overruns, Yule says his residents can’t wait for a plan that may or may not work. Rather, he says they need access now.

“Service over studies. We have studied the Green Line to death. It’s time to actually take action and get transit to Ward 3 that isn’t there, isn’t working.”

Executive committee gave the go-ahead to look into the $500 million plan that would see a dedicated curbside lane for busses.

Upwards of 30,000 Calgarians take transit along the Centre Street corridor daily, higher than the ridership of the western segment of the Blue Line LRT, and just shy of the expected ridership of the current Green Line LRT plan from downtown to Shepard.

Mayor Jeromy Farkas says he would have preferred to see the city build out a standalone route, but adds he just wants to see people moving whether on the bus or by train.

“We need to build out our Max Green bus line as close as possible to an LRT spec including LRT-quality stations, and once we do have the money for laying down track, installing the wires, it will be a lot more cost effective rather than to start from scratch to build a brand-new LRT system.”

Funding has yet to be approved. City Council will hear more details on the plan by fall of 2027.
