We drove across the country twice, and kept the receipts. Here’s what it cost.

TheTools · 07.09.2026 13:50 · source · permalink

When my sister and I drove her car from the San Francisco Bay Area to her college in upstate New York in 2023, we kept track of our mileage and our gas costs: $578.99. We figured it would be fun to compare when we drove back across the country in 2026.

We knew the soaring gas prices from the war in the Middle East would push our fuel bill up this time. We just didn’t know how much.

This Friday, as holiday weekend travelers loaded up for their own road trips, the national average price for a gallon of regular was $4.14. That’s the highest ever at this time of year, according to AAA.

And spare a thought for the truckers out there: The price of diesel hit a record $5.85 on Friday.

For me and my newly graduated sister, Zoe, the trip out west back in May was mostly an adventure. But along the way, we spoke to people at gas stations across the country who told us how fuel costs are affecting their lives.

Zoe completed the first leg of the journey herself. She topped off in Lenox, Massachusetts, where we had stopped back in September of 2023. There, she paid almost a full dollar more for almost a full gallon less than we had three years earlier. Off to an expensive start.

After she picked me up in New York City, we made our way down south. At a pit stop at Carroll Motor Fuels in Elkton, Maryland, we spoke with Riley Norris, a high school student who said she was expecting to pay over $50 for a full tank. 

“I’m literally wiping out probably my whole shift of work today on my gas,” Norris said. “But in a way I do believe Trump when he says that, you know, he’s gonna lower [gas prices], that they’re gonna lower after this whole war with Iran. And I do believe that.”

Though our route different somewhat in 2026, we did manage to stop at the exact same gas station in Richmond, Virginia, where we’d paid $3.73 in 2023. Zoe and I placed bets on what it would cost this time. She predicted $4.35 a gallon. I went with $4.50, which turned out to be pretty darn close: $4.49.

Driving around Richmond, we spotted a Citgo with what ended up being the cheapest gas we’d see all trip, by far: $2.49 per gallon.

By the time we left Charleston, South Carolina, we had driven about 1,100 miles. On our way out of town, we spoke with Rose Durant and Julia Schultz at an Exxon station. They were expecting gas to cost around $3.90 a gallon. It was $4.19.

At the same station, we spoke with Monique Middleton, who said she was only putting $15 worth of gas in her tank — a little over 3.5 gallons — because of the extreme price. 

“Four dollars and nineteen cents for one gallon is ridiculous,” she said. “Now I gotta head straight home because, with gas prices, you can’t go too far.”

Though the gas in Texas was, on average, cheaper than any of the other states we drove through, the drive through the state was long and hot — did I mention that we were driving in 95-degree heat and our A/C was busted? — and and gas stations were few and far between.

At The Crossing Travel truck stop in Winters, Texas, Malakye Kelly, a senior at Texas Tech, said he expected to pay $30 for half a tank. 

“I had to come down [to Amarillo from Lubbock] for the weekend, but I just try to stay home as much as I can,” he said.
