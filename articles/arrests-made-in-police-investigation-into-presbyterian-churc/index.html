Arrests made in police investigation into Presbyterian Church in Ireland

TheTools · 05.09.2026 10:21 · source · permalink

Two people have been arrested as part of an investigation into safeguarding concerns at the Presbyterian Church in Ireland.

There have been 127 contacts made with police from potential victims or witnesses, the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) said.

Their investigation into the church was announced in November last year.

It came after the church said there were "serious and significant failings in the central safeguarding functions of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland from the period 2009 to 2022".

The PSNI said their investigation is continuing and they are working "closely with partner agencies to ensure a safeguarding-led approach throughout the investigation".

The Moderator of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland (PCI), Rev Richard Kerr said the church remains "saddened by the circumstances which gave rise to the PSNI investigation".

He added that they are "continuing to assist the police in every way we can".

"We also recognise the bravery and strength of those who have already come forward and continue to encourage all victims, survivors and those with concerns to do so."

The church ordered an independent external review of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland's governance and safeguarding arrangements in March.

Kerr said the church "was continuing to improve the safeguarding capabilities" and that about 2800 people received training in May and June on its safeguarding policies and procedures.

He added that the church recognises that this "continues to be a difficult time for many who placed their trust in the Presbyterian Church and we remain mindful of any who have been hurt by our actions or inactions".

"We are committed to delivering the progress and change that is required to put things right."
