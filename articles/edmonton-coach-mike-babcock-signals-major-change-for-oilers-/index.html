Edmonton coach Mike Babcock signals major change for Oilers power forward

TheTools · 27.10.2026 19:57 · source · permalink

Edmonton Oilers coach Mike Babcock gave a strong signal about the shape of things to come when he promoted young power forward Colton Dach from fourth line centre to second line winger after veteran winger Zach Hyman again left a game with injury against Detroit.

Dach won several puck battles and scored a goal going to the net and deflecting in an outside shot on his new line with Ryan Nugent-Hopkins and Kasperi Kapanen.

After the game, Babcock said of Dach: “I thought Dacher had a real good night. He played heavy. He’s a way better winger than he is a centre. And soon as we moved him to the wing, that helped.”

My take

1. Babcock was keen to try Dach at centre this year, partly because it’s a tantalizing idea to have such a big, powerful centre in a third or fourth line role, and partly because the coach was keen to find more centres who could help out the team in a pinch. That need ratcheted up when veteran centres like Ryan Nugent-Hopkins and Jason Dickinson went out with injury and, of course, with the decision to play long-time centre Leon Draisaitl on the wing with Connor McDavid.

2. The experiment with Draisaitl and McDavid on the same line is arguably the most significant reason the Oilers are off to a strong start this year, so it’s hard to argue with Babcock’s move there, though many do so.

The experiment with a checking line of three newcomers, Owen Michaels, Mathieu Joseph and Alex Formenton, has not worked out, with the line out for just five goals for and nine against at even strength. The trio is unable to consistently play heavy hockey and cycle the puck in the offensive zone.

The experiment with Dach at centre? It was a good idea to try. Dach has done better than I thought he would. He’s done a decent job working with his d-men down low in the Oilers zone to both stop opposing attacks and advance the puck.

But the move to centre has given Dach far less opportunity to do what he does best, forecheck like a massive demon, pounding opposing players into the boards and out-battling them in front of the net.

3. Of course, as Babcock made clear, he knows what Dach can bring on the wing. The coach simply wanted and arguably needed to find out what he could bring at centre. Now Babcock knows.

At the same time, the coach now needs a big, tough and somewhat skilled winger who can play with Ryan Nugent-Hopkins.

4. RNH is a fast, slick, smart and skilled centre, but if you team him up on a line with other smaller players like him, that line is in danger of getting pushed out of the game.

RNH last thrived at centre when he was on a line with big, skilled Hyman and ferocious, skilled Evander Kane in the 2025 playoffs. In a short run of games, until Hyman got injured, that line was magic for the Oilers, four goals for and just one goal against in 66 even strength minutes.  If not for Hyman’s injury against Dallas, that line might have helped Edmonton win the 2025 Stanley Cup.

Dach seems a natural to me to play on a line with RNH and either Hyman, Vasily Podkolzin or Kasperi Kapanen (who has struggled with his risk/reward decision-making this year).

5. Of course, when need arises we may still see Dach at centre this year. But he’s such a strong forechecking threat on the wing, it’s hard to imagine he won’t land there, just as he did against Detroit.
