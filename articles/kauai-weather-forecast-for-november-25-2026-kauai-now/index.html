Synopsis

Dry and stable weather conditions will continue into Wednesday as the subtropical upper level ridge builds in over the island chain. Expect light and variable winds for most areas through Wednesday morning with increasing southwesterly winds by Wednesday afternoon. A cold front will swiftly march from west to east down the island chain producing a brief period of clouds and showers from Wednesday evening through Thanksgiving Day morning. Breezy, dry and cool northerly winds will develop after the front passes each island. Dry trade wind weather remains in the rest of the forecast into early next week.

Discussion

Mostly sunny skies have become partly cloudy across a large portion of the state this afternoon as low clouds developed across leeward and interior areas due to daytime heating and as some fair weather cumulus filtered in on the gentle trades. The upper level ridge, coupled with the dry, stable airmass over the area will maintain mostly dry conditions into Wednesday. Winds speeds will become light and variable overnight into early Wednesday as a weak surface ridge sinks south over the island chain. This setup will allow for efficient radiational cooling overnight, with low temperatures dipping into the upper 60s for many locations at lower elevations.
As a cold front approaches from the northwest tomorrow, winds will briefly strengthen out of the west before breezy to strong northerly winds fill in behind the frontal boundary. The front will reach Kauai Wednesday evening, then sweep down the rest of the island chain overnight into Thanksgiving Day. With limited moisture and better forcing for heavier showers and thunderstorms north of the area, expecting brief moderate showers just ahead of and along the frontal boundary before noticeably cooler, drier air quickly fills in. Winds may briefly reach advisory levels at lower elevations as the front passes through, but they aren't expected to remain above criteria for long.
Aloft, as an upper level trough sweeps across the region, a strong jet streak will move over the state and bring wind advisory threats to Haleakala summit on Maui and high wind warning impacts for the highest summits of Mauna Kea and Mauna Loa on the Big Island from Wednesday evening through Friday morning. A High Wind Watch is in effect and will likely be upgraded to a warning tomorrow. Stronger west-northwesterly winds will be limited to the summit areas of our highest mountain peaks, as breezy northerly winds are forecast for lower elevation areas.
A surface high will build in from the northwest behind the front and winds will gradually veer northeasterly on Friday, then moderate easterly trades will return for the weekend. Trade wind speeds will briefly weaken on Sunday as a the high weakens, then strengthen into early next week as another stronger high builds in from the northwest.

Aviation

Gentle trades of a dry and stable atmosphere will bring prevailing VFR conditions across the state into Wednesday. No AIRMETs are currently in effect.
A cold front approaching Kauai Wednesday afternoon raises possibilities for issuing AIRMET Sierra for brief periods of mountain obscuration and decreased visibility due to enhanced showers over the western half of the state going into Thanksgiving morning.

Marine

No significant changes to the forecast with the afternoon package.
The Coastal Flood Statement remains in place starting tonight and persisting into Thursday for the higher than predicted water levels. Water levels at Kahului and Hilo Harbor during the high tide around 4am this morning just grazed 1 ft above the MHHW (Mean Higher High Mark), which is where we have typically seen coastal impacts. High tide is expected to be larger over the next two mornings, which will increase the possibility of coastal impacts, particularly with the arrival of the incoming northwest swell. High tide is expected around 4 or 5 am each morning.
A cold front moving towards the islands will cause winds to shift out of the southwest tomorrow as the front enters the northwest offshore waters. As the front sweeps down the island chain Wednesday night through Thanksgiving Day, north to north-northeast winds will strengthen in its wake, bringing Small Craft Advisory level winds across most, if not all, the coastal waters.
The cold front will also bring the chance for thunderstorms to the north and east offshore waters Wednesday through Thursday.
The current north-northwest swell will bring seas in excess of 10 feet to most coastal zones. Seas are forecast to drop below 10 feet Wednesday morning, at which point there will be a brief pause in small craft conditions due to seas.
Additional Small Craft Advisories will likely be needed through the holiday due to a combination of the arrival of an even larger north-northwest swell and the strong winds mentioned above behind the front. The new swell is expected to bring seas in excess of 10 feet by Wednesday night. Winds will gradually weaken and veer to the northeast Friday and Saturday.
Surf along north and west-facing shores near or at warning levels tonight are due to the above referenced large, long- period north- northwest swell. The swell appears to have peaked at buoy 51001 midday, meaning the surf is expected to peak overnight. Surf will be on the decline tomorrow morning, however a new extra large, long- period north- northwest swell is expected to arrive Wednesday night into Thursday that is expected to bring another round of warning level surf to north- and west- facing shores before gradually fading into the weekend.
Surf heights along east-facing shores got a bump as the current north swell wraps into east shores. Due to the swell angle (350 degrees), eastern shores of Oahu have been included in the current High Surf Warning. Another round of wrapping swell is possible with the extra large north-northwest swell on Thursday into Friday. This swell will shift more northerly with time, so more swell energy will make it into east-facing shores by Friday even as the swell begins fading.
Surf along south-facing shores will remain small through the period, with minor bumps in long-period energy expected late Wednesday and over the weekend.

Fire weather

Light and variable winds will develop into Wednesday as weak high pressure moves over the state, maintaining low fire weather concerns. A cold front will swiftly move from west to east down the island chain from Wednesday evening through Thursday morning producing a brief period of showers over each island. Breezy, dry and cool northerly winds will mark the frontal passage for each island, bringing moderate fire weather conditions across the state. Continued drying trends will follow for the foreseeable future. Subsidence inversion temperature heights will range from 4,000 to 5,000 feet elevation near Maui and the Big Island tonight.
