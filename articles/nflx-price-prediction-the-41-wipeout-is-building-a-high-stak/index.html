NFLX Price Prediction: The 41% Wipeout Is Building a High-Stakes Earnings Trade Setup

A Stock in Freefall Hunting for a Floor Ahead of October 20

Netflix is set to report Q3 earnings after the close on October 20, 2026, with the stock locked in a firmly bearish trend despite what increasingly looks like a reasonable valuation. The tokenized NFLX on Binance is reflecting that exactly — $67.68, up a forgettable 0.56% over the last 24 hours, bouncing in a microscopic range of $67.29 to $67.89 that tells you precisely nothing is resolved. The stock has dropped 41.64% over the past year despite fundamentals that remain solid, including FY2025 revenue of $45.18B and levered free cash flow of $11.15B as of June 30, 2026.

That's the central contradiction traders need to get right before October 20. Netflix isn't a broken business. It's a broken chart on a stock that the market has re-rated aggressively lower while the income statement keeps compounding. That gap either closes on earnings, or it doesn't — and positioning into that binary defines whether you make money here. Q4 2025 revenue grew 18% year-over-year, driven by membership growth, higher pricing, and ad revenue; despite unfavorable FX, revenue still came in 1% above guidance on stronger membership and ad sales. Q2 2026 revenue then grew 13% year-over-year to $12.6B with an operating margin of 33%. This is not a company missing numbers. The problem is multiple compression, not operational failure.

The Technical Picture: Oversold Doesn't Mean Over

Every moving average on this chart is stacked above the current price — SMA 7 at $68.67, SMA 20 at $71.52, SMA 50 at $76.27, with MACD momentum flat-lining at zero after a prolonged bearish slide. There is no bullish momentum crossover here. The trend is down, period. But momentum exhaustion is real, and the indicator stack is starting to tell a different story about what happens next.

The Stochastic at 11.26/%D at 9.01 is in deeply oversold territory, and with Bollinger %B at just 0.1642, price is hugging the lower band at $65.80. Mean reversion trades off the lower Bollinger band require a catalyst — and October 20 earnings is that catalyst. The pivot point sits at $67.62, with immediate resistance at $67.95 and strong resistance clustered at $68.22. Breaking and holding above $68.22 would be the first constructive signal worth trusting.

What's notable is the derivatives positioning. Smart money on Binance Futures is running a top-trader long/short ratio of 4.49 — 81.8% long against just 18.2% short. Retail is also net long at 75.4%. The taker buy/sell ratio of 1.23 shows live buying pressure despite the down-trend. These aren't guarantees, but they signal that informed participants are already leaning into a bounce. Open interest declined 4.80% over 24 hours though, meaning some of that positioning is being unwound — the smart money isn't loading up recklessly, they're being selective. Wall Street expects the September quarter to deliver EPS of $0.82 and revenue of $12.87B, but recent earnings beats have still been met with share price declines — so even a clean beat won't automatically translate to a gap-up unless guidance impresses.

Fundamentals That Don't Match a $67 Handle — And Wall Street Knows It

The fundamental disconnect here is glaring. Netflix's trailing P/E has compressed to 22.44x and forward P/E sits at 18.69x, while the operating margin (ttm) runs at 33.38% and return on equity is 49.54% — with revenue (ttm) of $48.37B and quarterly revenue growth of 13.40% year-over-year. For a company compounding revenue at double-digit rates with a profit margin north of 28%, a forward multiple below 19x is historically cheap. The trailing P/E of ~24x is close to its three-year low of 23.7, suggesting the stock may be undervalued compared to its historical performance.

Netflix narrowed its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $51.0–$51.4B in its July 16 quarterly report, while maintaining its forecast for an operating margin of 31.5%. Ad commitments for the 2026–27 season nearly doubled compared with the prior year, in line with internal expectations. That advertising flywheel matters — ad revenue grew more than 2.5x versus 2024 in 2025, surpassing $1.5 billion, and the trajectory points materially higher into 2027.

Netflix carries a consensus "Moderate Buy" rating from 55 analysts, with 33 buys, four strong buys, 16 holds, and two sells — and the average 12-month price target is $95.51. More specifically, 45 analysts carry an average price target of $93.66 against the price at time of the data pull, with targets running from $70.00 to $135.00 and a rating split of 35 buy, 16 hold, 0 sell. The October 1 confirmation from Guggenheim's Michael Morris — raising the price target to $80 from $75 while maintaining a Buy rating — shows that even the more cautious revised calls still see 18% upside from current levels. That's not a consensus that has abandoned this stock. That's a consensus waiting for the chart to catch up to the business.

The Trade: Two Paths, One Hard Decision Before October 20

Here's where conviction has to replace nuance. NFLX at $67.68 is trading at an approximately 29% discount to the analyst mean target of ~$95. The Bollinger lower band at $65.80 and strong support at $67.02 define the near-term floor. The bull case and the bear case are structurally distinct.

Bull scenario (60% probability over 30 days): Q3 earnings deliver $12.87B+ revenue and EPS at or above $0.82, with management providing constructive guidance on ad revenue trajectory into Q4. The stock fills back toward the $71.52 SMA 20, then targets $76–$80 over the 30-day horizon. Entry at current levels with a stop below $65.80 (lower Bollinger / strong support zone) risks approximately 3% to target a 12–18% move. Based on 55 analyst ratings carrying a consensus "Moderate Buy," the high price target for NFLX sits at $135.00 while the low is $57.00 — meaning even the bull camp is capped around $80–$95 on a near-term realistic basis.

Bear scenario (40% probability): Given that recent earnings beats have still been met with share price declines, a guidance miss or softer-than-expected ad revenue commentary could break $67.02 support, opening a flush toward $65.80 and potentially the $62–$63 range where no meaningful technical support exists. This is the kill zone if you're oversized and wrong on October 20.

The strategy: small position sizing with a defined stop below $65.80 makes sense for long exposure here. This is not a conviction-size trade — it's a risk-managed bet on oversold conditions and a fundamentally cheap multiple resolving into an earnings catalyst. Anyone swinging full size into NFLX ahead of October 20 without accounting for the gap-down risk on a guidance miss is fighting the recent pattern. Netflix's scale, recurring revenue, and strong profitability support premium valuation and potential for double-digit top-line growth, with management guiding for 12% revenue growth and 33.2% operating margin next quarter — but Wall Street needs to see it delivered, not projected.
