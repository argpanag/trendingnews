"A Delicious Revenge Thriller": The 6 Best Jason Statham Films to Stream Right Now

TheTools · 07.10.2026 09:54 · source · permalink

We earn a commission for products purchased through some links in this article.

His line-up of thrillers with insane premises is endless

Jason Statham is a twin-turbo charisma machine. He’s a double barrelled, sawn-off hot-gun of a geezer.

Yes, his American accent has a habit of wandering off into the Atlantic. So what? Give him a schlocky B-movie to polish and he'll shine the shit out of it until it gleams. Give him a line that would crash and crumple in the hands of a lesser actor, and he'll drive it straight through the wall. When Statham says something like "I now pronounce you man and knife" (The Expendables, 2010), you know he's about to put his foot down and make it roar. Ask him to play a medieval farmer called Farmer in a Lord of the Rings-style epic fantasy (In the Name of the King, 2007) and he’ll roundhouse extras across a cold, muddy forest until he runs out of road. He’ll do it because he’s Jason Statham: channeller of heroes, drop-kicker of men.

He’s also surely one of Hollywood’s hardest working actors – in just the last ten years, he’s starred in no less than 15 movies. Before acting found him, he was a diver on the British National Diving Team and finished twelfth in the World Championships in 1992. He has also been a fashion model and a salesman.

Let's just cut to the chase: Jason Statham is a British national treasure; the kind who'll still be kicking men through windows at 80.

So, to celebrate this British icon, here are six of Jason Statham's best movies to stream right now.

1 | Snatch (2000)

One of the most quotable – and quoted – movies of the cenury. It’s the second barrel of the gun that fired Statham into the limelight, and brought Guy Ritchie into Madonna’s orbit. Snatch and Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels set the tone for a new wave of British gangster movies – brash, comic, violent with a breakneck script that you could probably now call Ritchian. On the surface, it’s an hour and a half of lairy geezers maiming or killing each other in creative ways – if not with guns or knives, then with gold clubs, car doors or sex toys. But it blew the bloody doors off cinema at the time. And it stands up, not least thanks to Statham, Vinnie Jones, Brad Pitt and the ever-wonderful Stephen Graham. It’s priceless cultural artefact, really, a bump of the best early-noughties nostalgia on the market; a line of pure, uncut Britpop-era gold.

WATCH

2 | Crank (2006)

So, the premise is simple: a hitman (Statham) wakes up to find he's been injected with a Chinese synthetic poison that will kill him if his heart rate drops. With about an hour to live, he races around Los Angeles keeping his adrenaline pumping, fighting, driving and generally causing chaos, while hunting down the man who poisoned him. At one point, realising nothing exciting is happening to him, he persuades a woman to have sex with him to save his life. This is a fun movie – fast and rollicking with little to say about life, except to distract you from it. That said, in our hero’s words: “It's like all my life I've just been going, going, going. Wish I'd taken more time to stop and smell the roses so to speak. Guess it's too late for that now.” Yeah, but, y’know, it’s never too late to watch Crank.

WATCH

3 | The Meg (2018)

As mentioned above, Statham was a member of the British National Diving Team before acting. So, he knew what was required of him when asked to play deep-sea rescue diver Jonas Taylor, parachuted in when a submersible gets trapped on the ocean floor by a giant prehistoric shark, a megalodon thought to be extinct. After he helps save the crew, the beast follows them to the surface and heads for a crowded Chinese beach resort, so Jonas has to hunt it down before it feeds.

WATCH

4 | The Bank Job (2008)

Based on a real 1971 robbery, a gang of London small-timers (led by Statham's Terry) tunnel into a Lloyds Bank vault on Baker Street. They think it's a straightforward heist, but the safety deposit boxes also hold compromising secrets about politicians and royals, and now MI5, the police, a porn kingpin, and London's criminal underworld all want what they stole. It’s an intelligent, engaging film, and one that allows Statham to stretch his acting wings a little. He may not fly far, just out of the nest, but it’s a sweet dodgy geezer crime caper-that-comes-a-cropper film that does its job far better than the protagonists do theirs.

WATCH

5 | Spy (2015)

Susan Cooper (Melissa McCarthy) is a desk-bound CIA analyst who guides top agent Bradley Fine (Jude Law) through his missions from a basement office. When Fine is killed and the identities of the other agents are exposed, she volunteers to go undercover in Europe to track down a stolen nuclear device. Statham plays Rick Ford, a reckless fellow agent who keeps trying to take over her mission. It’s a very knowing role from Statham, a sort of self-parody. Or, at least, a hilarious construct of his character type.

WATCH

6 | The Beekeeper (2025)

It's a deliciously daft, stripped-back revenge thriller that knows exactly what it is. Statham plays a former member of a secret vigilante organisation called the Beekeepers, who goes after a phishing-scam operation after it ruins a kind old neighbour. The plot is ridiculous, the dialogue is full of bee puns delivered with a straight face, and the action is brutal and efficient. But you know what? Statham, as always, holds fast throughout. The worker bee that he is, he knows exactly what his job is, now. And here is a movie that proves – even after all these years – he's lost none of his sting.
