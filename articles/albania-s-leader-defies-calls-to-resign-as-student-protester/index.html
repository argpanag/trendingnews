Albania’s leader defies calls to resign as student protesters rampage in capital

TheTools · 15.10.2026 12:13 · source · permalink

Albanian Prime Minister Edi Rama on Thursday rejected calls to resign as thousands of student-led protesters smashed windows and fought running battles with riot police firing water cannon and tear gas in the capital.

Protesters voiced anger about perceived corruption, the cost and quality of education and a deal to sell off parts of its pristine southern coastline to a luxury resort development backed by U.S. President Donald Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner.

They demanded Rama’s resignation, saying no one had listened to their concerns during a summer of largely peaceful protests over the development.

In a written statement to Reuters, Rama rejected calls to quit.

“My responsibility is to listen, address legitimate concerns and keep Albania moving forward. The alternative would be a betrayal of the Albanians who gave me a strong mandate to complete negotiations on European Union membership,” he said.

A student who gave her name as Kiara said the cost of living, poor job prospects and alleged corruption were the main issues.

“Our prime minister is the meaning of the word corruption. You can only find work in your field if you know a guy who knows a guy,” she said, her face covered by a mask, like many on the streets on Thursday.

The protests, now in their third day, mark the biggest challenge yet to Rama, who won a fourth term last year, with E.U. membership at the center of his campaign. Parliament canceled its session as the violence erupted again nearby.

Rama, whose government denies corruption, said he would be ready to discuss the rising cost of education, but added: “Attacking institutions, destroying public property and threatening public safety have nothing to do with the legitimate demands of students.”

Tirana’s streets were littered with burned-out garbage bins, shattered bus stops and broken traffic lights.

Wafts of tear gas drifted across the center. Protesters hurled stones at police, who used water cannon to push protesters back. In the central Skanderbeg Square, police hit fleeing protesters with batons as tourists looked on.

“This government is corrupted, led and governed by a corrupt prime minister,” protester Lulzim Tanushi, a lawyer, said. “Since Rama came to power he has abused and ruined people’s lives and that is why people are showing their anger — when they were not heard before.”

The protests follow months of mostly peaceful demonstrations sparked by the plans to build the resort, which has faced legal challenges.

Villagers told Reuters in July that their land was wrongfully sold for development. Reuters later reported that the man who sold the land was wanted by prosecutors for drug trafficking, charges his lawyer denied.

Reuters found no evidence of wrongdoing by Kushner, who is not directly involved in the villagers’ dispute.

Albania’s image has been tarnished over the years by corruption scandals, and political analysts say the country is a hub for gangs seeking to launder money from drugs and weapons trafficking, complicating its efforts to join the E.U.

Along with Montenegro, Albania is currently leading the accession process among Balkan countries and is aiming for entry as early as 2028.

“Corruption must be confronted and, as the E.U. itself has recently reaffirmed, Albania is tackling it as never before,” Rama said to Reuters.

“Perhaps some forces, at home and abroad, do not want Albania to succeed as a forerunner for E.U. membership. But I will not allow violence to derail Albania’s European future.”

A European Union mission to Albania in a statement on X said: “Constructive dialogue is the only way forward.”

The unrest is part of a wave of student activism in Europe. In France, high school students have staged weeks of protests and school blockades demanding more teachers and better facilities, arguing that deteriorating school buildings and overcrowded classrooms are undermining education.
