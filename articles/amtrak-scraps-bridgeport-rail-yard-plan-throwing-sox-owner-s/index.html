Amtrak Scraps Bridgeport Rail Yard Plan, Throwing Sox Owner’s Plan For New Stadium Into Doubt

TheTools · 24.11.2026 14:48 · source · permalink

Credibility:

  • Sources Cited

CHICAGO — Amtrak is reversing course on its controversial plan to move its maintenance operations from the South Loop to a new facility in Bridgeport, instead shifting its focus to redeveloping its existing rail yard near 14th Street, the railroad announced Tuesday.

The decision could have major implications for developer and White Sox owner-in-waiting Justin Ishbia’s proposed Railyards development, which envisions redeveloping Amtrak’s current 14th Street property and building a new White Sox stadium on the land. It’s unclear what Amtrak’s announcement means for those plans.

Amtrak spokesman Marc Magliari said the railroad, in consultation with the U.S. Department of Transportation, is changing direction and that the Federal Railroad Administration has agreed to rescope its grant to reflect the new approach.

“Amtrak will focus on addressing critical infrastructure needs, improved support for new trains and helping to advance future Midwest service growth,” Magliari said in a statement.

Trains.com first reported that Amtrak will no longer sell its 14th Street property for the proposed White Sox stadium development.

The announcement marks a major reversal for a roughly $900 million project that would have moved Amtrak’s maintenance operations to the existing Canal Street rail yard in Bridgeport, which drew harsh backlash from nearby residents. 

Amtrak had been scheduled to hold a series of public feedback meetings in early December about the Bridgeport project.

In a statement, Ald. Nicole Lee (11th) applauded the reversal of plans, saying the Bridgeport site, “situated squarely amid a residential area, was not a feasible location for this maintenance facility.”

“We have advocated relentlessly to ensure this project did not destroy the neighborhood that thousands of Chicagoans call home, and we are proud to have succeeded,” Lee said.

The alderwoman also said she looks forward to continuing conversations about “the future of the White Sox and a path to reimagining 35th Street to be a more vibrant economic environment surrounding our team. That work must start today.”

Representatives with Canal Edge, Ishbia’s company that planned to develop the 14th Street property, and Shore Capital Partners, his private equity firm, could not immediately be reached for comment.
