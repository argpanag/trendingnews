Morkel Urges India Pacers to Attack on Christchurch Pitch

TheTools · 26.11.2026 22:13 · source · permalink

India's fast bowlers face a demanding challenge on Christchurch's green surface, with bowling coach Morne Morkel urging them to attack early with the new Kookaburra ball ahead of the series finale. The advice comes as India attempt to strengthen their position in the ICC World Test Championship (WTC) race.

Morkel, drawing on 16 years of international experience with the Kookaburra ball, stressed that India's quicks must make the most of the early overs before the ball loses its hardness and effectiveness.

"It does make life a lot harder, especially when the surface also loses a little bit of pace with the softer ball. That is my experience, playing for 16 years with the Kookaburra ball, that you need to make that new ball count. You need to get the ball in the right area, sometimes be a bit braver in terms of your lengths and your lines, even going for a few runs, because when the ball gets soft, you can always put a ring field in and bring the run rate back." Morkel said.

He highlighted the differences between bowling conditions in India and those encountered with the Kookaburra ball overseas.

"We play with the SG ball in India, where we bring spin into the game. But playing with the Kookaburra ball in these conditions, there is a different pattern that you need to be comfortable with, and you need to sometimes be brave." Morkel said.

India currently occupy fourth place in the WTC standings with 50 percent points. South Africa, Australia and New Zealand are ahead, leaving the Indian side needing an almost perfect run to reach the final.

Morkel acknowledged the difficulty of that target and urged the players to concentrate on their performances rather than become distracted by the standings.

"All these guys want to play in that [WTC] final, and they will give everything possible to make it. But realistically, if we are honest about it, it is going to be a challenge," Morkel said.

He emphasized the importance of recovering quickly from difficult periods during matches.

"So after every bad session, we can't get caught up in things that we need to do. We just need to focus on playing our best cricket, coming up with solutions, and from there take it forward." Morkel said.

His approach also involves breaking matches into smaller periods to help maintain concentration.

"That will be one thing we will speak about a lot, breaking it up session by session, focusing on how we can play our best brand of cricket." Morkel said.

The Christchurch pitch has attracted attention because of its substantial grass covering. However, Morkel cautioned against assuming that the surface would automatically produce fast and bouncy conditions.

"The pitch from what I have seen looks again very green. I think it is a longer sort of grass cover they have got there. We will have to see how it plays," Morkel said.

The previous Test in Wellington played slower than expected, despite pre-match expectations of a quicker surface.

"Everybody expected the wicket in the last game to be fast and bouncy, and it wasn't like that. We can't control that. We just need to, with bat and ball in the first session, play the conditions we are going to face as best as possible." Morkel said.

India also have several fast-bowling options available, with all their quicks fit for selection. Uncapped Gurnoor Brar has strengthened his case after collecting nine wickets at an average of 15.89 in the recent ODI series against New Zealand, including best figures of 4/30.

Brar was named Player of the Series, and Morkel believes his pace and bounce could suit the Christchurch conditions.

"There is a lot of excitement about him since he has joined the Indian team, with his pace and bounce. I think he is a guy that will be well suited to the conditions here. We will see how it goes." Morkel said.

The bowling coach said Brar must remain prepared while waiting for his opportunity to make his Test debut.

"Gurnoor has been knocking on the door. He's doing all the right things. The selection as a player is something that he can't control.

He needs to just make sure he's ready when he gets the opportunity." Morkel said.

Morkel also explained that maintaining focus can become difficult for players approaching their first international opportunity in a new format.

"A lot of times, when we're close to that opportunity, as a bowler you can get distracted. When he gets his opportunity, my job is to make sure he's 100 percent ready." Morkel said.

The coach also defended Anshul Kamboj following his performance in the first Test in Wellington. Kamboj recorded match figures of 3/114 across 45 overs in his second Test appearance.

Morkel pointed to the difficulties created by the changing pitch conditions and questioned whether the criticism directed at the young seamer was justified.

"Anshul (Kamboj) did a great job. It is quite hard to judge from the outside, sitting in the dressing room, after the second day, if the wicketkeeper is standing up to a bowler who is two metres tall, that is worrying." Morkel said.

He urged the team to recognize Kamboj's contribution rather than focus solely on his individual figures.

"Unfair to pick on Anshul as he gave us control. Let's support the guy as he bowls hard overs for the team." Morkel said.

Morkel added that the Wellington surface lost pace early, affecting bowlers from both teams rather than Kamboj alone.

"The wicket lost a lot of pace early and hence the drop in pace of the bowlers. Wickets can get flat but you don't want them to lose pace. It was not just him; look at the New Zealand side, the speeds on that surface were down." Morkel said.

Mohammed Siraj also received strong backing from the bowling coach, who described the senior fast bowler as a world-class player despite the challenges surrounding selection.

"For me, I think he is bowling well. In terms of the phase he is going through, he is a world-class bowler for me. Sometimes decisions and selection are tough ones to take, and it is not easy." Morkel said.

Morkel revealed that his conversation with Siraj focused on individual responsibility and identifying areas for improvement rather than worrying about selection decisions.

"The conversation I had with him was to say, ‘listen, let's just step it back a little bit. Where are we at the moment, what do we need to improve, and where can we get better? Selection we can't really control.’

And it is all about self-responsibility, of taking that on board." Morkel said.
