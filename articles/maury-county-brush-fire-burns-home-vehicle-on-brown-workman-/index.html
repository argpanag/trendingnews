Maury County Brush Fire Burns Home, Vehicle on Brown Workman Lane

TheTools · 24.10.2026 12:49 · source · permalink

A brush fire chewed through roughly 7 acres on Brown Workman Lane in Maury County Friday afternoon, threatening multiple homes before spreading to set one house and a vehicle ablaze. Fire crews knocked down both of those fires, and the overall blaze was reported under control by 4:30 p.m.

According to NewsChannel 5, fire units operated at the scene as the fire threatened multiple homes along Brown Workman Lane. The Maury County Fire Department said in its initial alert that crews were responding to a large brush fire with structures in danger, and the department later confirmed that one home and a vehicle caught fire before crews extinguished both. Mount Pleasant Fire Department and Summertown Fire Department both responded to assist through mutual aid.

The department noted that this was the second brush fire Friday involving threatened structures, underscoring how active the day had already been for Maury County crews. The department's Facebook post also urged people to refrain from burning as conditions remained hazardous.

A Parched Middle Tennessee Sets the Stage

Friday's fire landed in the middle of one of the driest stretches Middle Tennessee has seen in generations. Extreme drought has been expanding across the Midstate, per WSMV, and Nashville recently tied its longest dry stretch in recorded history at 36 consecutive days. Through October 21, the station reported, 2026 ranked as the ninth-driest year in Middle Tennessee's records, which stretch back to the early 1870s.

The outlet's forecast offered little relief: no rain was expected through the end of the month, aside from the possibility of a few sprinkles on Wednesday, October 28. That dryness has made brush fires far more common — and far more dangerous when they start near homes, as Friday's did.

Firefighters Already Stretched Before the Weekend

The Brown Workman Lane fire wasn't an isolated event. In the weeks before it, Maury County Fire Department units had already responded to 10 brush fires that consumed more than 30 acres, trees, and resulted in two outbuildings catching fire, with one completely destroyed, the same WSMV report noted. Those earlier fires hit areas spread across the county, including Mooresville Pike, Rally Hill Road, Southpoint Ridge Road, Scott Hollow Road, Campbellsville Pike, Joe Brown Road, South Cross Bridges, New Lewisburg Highway and Bell Road.

The causes behind that string of fires varied widely, the station reported, with culprits ranging from fallen power lines and a mower that caught fire to illegal trash burning and controlled burns gone wrong. The fire department said firefighters were stretched very thin, compounded by the fact that every surrounding county was dealing with similar call volumes and strained resources of its own.

Burn Permits Still Required Statewide

The dry conditions come as Tennessee's seasonal burn-permit rules are already in effect. The Maury County Fire Department confirms that burn permits are required from October 15 through May 15, though the department itself does not issue them. The Tennessee Division of Forestry likewise requires permits during that same window every year, a rule meant to curb exactly the kind of uncontrolled burns that have kept Maury County crews busy in recent weeks.
