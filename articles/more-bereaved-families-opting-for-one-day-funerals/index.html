More bereaved families opting for one-day funerals

TheTools · 28.11.2026 10:02 · source · permalink

AI-translated article with transliterated names. Read original Chinese article here.

More bereaved families in Singapore are opting for one-day funerals, giving their loved ones a simple but dignified send-off.

One funeral director interviewed said such arrangements account for nearly 10% of cases. Reasons vary: some families are concerned about costs, while others are honouring the wishes of the deceased, who did not want to burden their relatives.

Shin Min Daily News (《新明日报》) previously reported that some members of the public felt a lengthy wake could take a mental and physical toll on bereaved families, who would also have to take more days of compassionate leave. Cost was another major consideration.

It is understood that more families have recently been choosing one-day funerals, with the entire process, from collection of the body to cremation, taking no more than 24 hours.

The simplified arrangements follow a tight schedule: after being notified, the funeral director collects the body, applies make-up and dresses the deceased in funeral attire before taking the body straight to the crematorium.

Given the short notice, relatives have to attend at whatever cremation slot is available. A farewell ceremony lasting no more than 20 minutes is then held in the service hall before the body is cremated.

Ang Ziqian, vice-president of the Association of Funeral Directors Singapore and executive director of Ang Chin Moh Funeral Directors, said he had observed an increase in such cases since the pandemic. They account for nearly 10% of all cases this year.

Jenny Tay, managing director of Direct Funeral Services, said the company had handled more than 300 such cases a year since 2023. “We handled 399 cases in 2023, 419 in 2024 and 385 last year. As of October this year, we had handled 361.”

Guo Xuze, owner of Wufu Casket, also said one-day funerals had become more common over the past two years. His company previously handled fewer than one such case a month, but now handles four or five, accounting for about 10% of its total.

Asked why families chose one-day funerals rather than wakes lasting three to five days, funeral directors said circumstances and reasons varied from case to case.

Guo Xuze said financial considerations could be one reason. “A traditional wake and funeral can cost upwards of S$10,000, while a one-day funeral costs S$1,000-plus. The price difference is substantial. Some families feel there is no need for an elaborate funeral after their loved one has died.”

He added that choosing a one-day funeral did not mean relatives lacked filial piety, but reflected changing attitudes. “Some may already have spent large sums on medical treatment or devoted considerable time and effort to caring for the deceased. Besides one-day funerals, sea burials and garden ash scattering have also become increasingly popular in recent years.”

Ang said some families felt a simple funeral would suffice because the deceased had been single or childless, with few close relatives or friends. In other cases, the deceased had explicitly expressed a wish for a quiet departure. “We have encountered patients who did not want their families to be burdened with organising a funeral after their death.”

Tay said some small families with limited means also made the decision because of costs. “Generally, wakes lasting three to five days are still more common. Seven-day wakes are less common, and are usually held to allow relatives and friends overseas time to return and pay their respects.”
