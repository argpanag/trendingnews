Katie Zacharia appeared to confirm her departure as White House press secretary Friday night, praising President Donald Trump hours after he publicly disclosed that he encouraged her to reconsider remaining in the position.

"President Trump is a kind, wonderful, gracious, thoughtful, enthusiastic, and hardworking man," Zacharia wrote on X. "I wish more Americans appreciated him. No one will ever make America great the way he has and continues to.

"It was an honor to work for him."

Zacharia included in the post a photograph of herself standing behind Trump, who was seated at the Resolute Desk in the Oval Office.

The post appeared to be her first public statement since reports emerged earlier Friday that she was leaving the position after just five days.

Zacharia did not say whether she resigned or was fired.

Trump's comments earlier Friday, however, suggested he had encouraged her to step down, telling reporters he advised her to reconsider staying in the position amid intense media scrutiny.

Trump publicly characterized the decision as Zacharia's, although The New York Times subsequently reported she had been fired, citing three people familiar with the matter. The Washington Post reported that she was out of the position but did not specify whether she resigned or was dismissed.

"And I said to her, honestly, if I were you, I'd reconsider. You think about it, but I would reconsider," Trump told reporters outside the White House before departing for a campaign rally in Georgia.

His comments aired live on Newsmax and the free Newsmax2 streaming platform.

Asked whether he intended to keep Zacharia, Trump said, "I'm going to let her make that decision."

He said he had spoken with Zacharia minutes earlier and asked her whether she wanted to endure the scrutiny accompanying her new position.

"I said, 'Katie, is this for you?' Because, you know, she's been treated very rough and she's a nice person, very nice person," Trump said. "I've watched her for years on television."

He said Zacharia told him about the difficulties of the position.

"She said this is a lot tougher," Trump said. "I said, welcome to the world of Trump."

Zacharia's brief tenure came under intense scrutiny after her first press briefing Tuesday, which lasted about 14 minutes and included questions from only three news organizations.

She also faced questions about her legal credentials after repeatedly identifying herself as a lawyer despite not being licensed to practice law. She did graduate from Pepperdine University's law school.

In an interview Thursday on Fox News, Zacharia admitted to failing the California bar examination and never retaking it.

She said she had planned to move to China after graduating from law school. The Post reported that Zacharia worked at a law firm in China without formally practicing there as an attorney.

Trump, who initially praised her debut, defended Zacharia again Friday while acknowledging the toll of the controversy.

"And you know Karoline did a very good job. I think Katie maybe would too," Trump said, referring to former press secretary Karoline Leavitt.

The president also cited Zacharia's family as a reason she might want to reconsider remaining in the position.

"You've got a beautiful husband and family. I met the family yesterday. Beautiful kids. It's like from a picture postcard," Trump said.
