How Eagles found out they didn't have Lane Johnson vs. Rams

TheTools · 06.10.2026 20:04 · source · permalink

The Eagles had to scramble on Sunday when they found out they didn't have Lane Johnson.

Jordan Mailata and his teammates didn’t find out the news until they arrived to Lincoln Financial Field on Sunday morning.

Lane Johnson was out.

The Eagles’ future Hall of Fame right tackle missed the team’s 24-20 loss to the Rams on Sunday for what the team called personal reasons. The Eagles had to start rookie third-round pick Markel Bell in his place on extremely short notice.

“It’s tough. Real tough,” Mailata said. “But this is why we train the way we do and we practice those rotations during the week so that the next man up isn’t a new thing for us, that we’re able to get things going.”

The Eagles were understandably tight-lipped about Johnson’s situation. And when head coach Nick Sirianni was asked if he expects to get the 36-year-old back, he simply answered, “We’ll see where we are.”

Sirianni declined to say anything further. But we do know Johnson missed some time early in training camp for personal reasons before returning at declaring that the 2026 season would likely be his last in the NFL.

“It’s tough because that’s one of our brothers,” Cam Jurgens said. “We miss him out there.”

But there wasn’t much time for the Eagles on Sunday to worry about Johnson. They had a football game to play. And because Fred Johnson was already out this week with a knee injury, there really wasn’t a decision to make.

The Eagles had to start Bell.

“The game’s coming up pretty soon after that, so you’ve just got to be ready to go,” right guard Tyler Steen said. “It’s not really something you can be too emotional about. You just gotta get prepared. We have all the confidence in the world in Markel to go out there and execute.

“I think Markel took it like a professional and I think he’s ready to go. He’s been trained up all of camp, playing right tackle. I’m really proud of how he performed. I had all the confidence in the world and execute.”

The Eagles this spring drafted Bell in the third round (No. 68 overall) out of Miami. Even though Bell played only right tackle for the Hurricanes, the Eagles got him a ton of snaps at right tackle this summer. And when Johnson missed that time early in training camp, it was Bell who got most of those first-team snaps.

Sunday’s game wasn’t perfect for Bell. But given the circumstances, he seemed to hold up fine.

“Sometimes it's hard to see exactly what's happening with an offensive lineman unless it's going really bad,” Sirianni said. “I didn't feel like, 'Hey, that one was on Markel.' We'll see. We've got to watch the tape. But I thought he battled and went in there and played his butt off. I know he was coming off the field every time, just having given it his all. He was ready to play and you love that about him. We'll have to look at the tape, but I think he battled and played his butt off.”

Bell had a very impressive training camp and Johnson actually raved about him this summer. Based on the way Bell performed in his first training camp, many think he’s the long-term replacement for Johnson.

The Eagles just hope that day won’t come until at least next season.

With Johnson: 98-42-1

Without Johnson: 15-29

“Very unfortunate for his unavailability today,” Jalen Hurts said. “In the end, it just was a next man up mentality. Kept the focus on the team, kept the focus on us trying to go out there and be at our best. We had an opportunity to do that. A lot of respect for everyone that stepped in unexpectedly and even throughout the week, different roles, new opportunities, taking advantage of those in the end. This is one that you can learn from, obviously.”
