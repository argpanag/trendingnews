Dentist pleads guilty to professional misconduct in 23-year-old's death | CBC News

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:16 · source · permalink

Virginia Blatz died after going to have her wisdom teeth removed by Dr. Ronald Janz in Morden, Man., in 2024

A Manitoba dentist was found guilty of professional misconduct Monday in connection with a woman's 2024 death, admitting he gave Virginia Blatz much more local anesthetic than the recommended dose.

Dr. Ronald Janz pleaded guilty to charges under the provincial Dental Association Act inside a brightly lit room flanked by windows at a downtown Winnipeg law firm during a morning hearing. 

By that afternoon, a Manitoba Dental Association inquiry panel had found him guilty of several breaches of the association's code of ethics, including going against the principles to do no harm, to improve the patient's condition and to provide competent care.

The panel found him guilty of professional misconduct, and of displaying a lack of knowledge, skill or judgment in his dental practice with respect to the care he provided to Blatz. 

Blatz, 23, was pronounced dead after she went to the Boundary Trails Dental Centre in Morden, Man., to get her wisdom teeth removed on Nov. 27, 2024.

According to an agreed statement of facts, Janz far exceeded the recommended dose of Articane — a local anesthetic drug typically given to manage pain — when providing care to Blatz.

At the time of her death, Blatz weighed about 100 pounds, and the dose given to her exceeded the maximum dosage she should have been given by up to about 200 per cent, an expert report included in the agreed statement of facts said.

Blatz's family is suing the dentist. (Submitted by Dulaney and Victoria Blatz)

A final report by the regulator's complaints committee referenced in the statement of facts concluded that Blatz was given 10 carpules of the local anesthetic. 

Janz told an investigator with the dental regulator he might have given Blatz seven carpules. Still, he said his intention was to give 10, as that's the amount he said he usually uses for wisdom teeth procedures.

Lawyers for the dental association's complaints committee said that regardless of whether she was given seven or 10 carpules, the dose given to Blatz "well exceeded" what would be considered safe for a person of her size. The expert report suggested she should have been given about four carpules instead.

Janz told the investigator he did not know what the maximum dosage was for articaine, and that he did not know he had given Blatz too much based on her weight, according to the facts read out in the hearing room.

As a result of the articaine overdose, Blatz experienced local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST), in which levels of the pain-blocking medication become too high in a person's blood.

Janz didn't recognize Blatz was experiencing LAST and failed to appropriately treat it, said the agreed statement of facts.

Blatz's mother, Victoria Blatz, said in a victim impact statement read before Monday's inquiry panel that her daughter was a friendly and smart young woman who was considered an anchor within the family. 

For two years, Victoria has been replaying what she saw in the clinic over and over in her mind, thinking about the ways she could have possibly saved her daughter. She said she's struggled with nightmares and brain fog since her daughter's death. 

Her father, Dulaney Blatz, said he was so proud of his daughter, who had planned to follow in his footsteps and build her own farm one day. Now he's trapped under an "overwhelming fog of grief" that makes it hard to concentrate on life both on and off the farm, he said.

Blatz's sister says she was her best friend and 'safe space.' (Submitted by Dulaney and Victoria Blatz)

Miranda Blatz said her sister Virginia was her best friend and "safe space," who always fostered a sense of calm and peace. Without her best friend, it has been hard to dream of the future, she said with a wavering voice.  

The inquiry panel hearing has yet to make a decision about the penalty Janz will face.

Lawyers for the dental association's complaints committee argued his licence should be suspended. However, the counsel didn't specific how long the suspension should be for, saying that's up to the inquiry panel to decide. 

They also argued Janz should have to repay about $135,000 related to the costs of the investigation.

Janz's lawyer argued the dentist shouldn't be suspended and that he should instead repay a portion of the costs, suggesting a payment of $30,000 instead.

Both sides agreed the penalties should include a $10,000 fine, a reprimand and the publication of the decision.

Janz had previously agreed to be restricted in his ability to administer local anesthesia after the incident. The hearing was told Janz was not allowed to administer local anesthetic until January and the dental association's registry currently shows he can now only provide it under supervision. 

In a prepared statement read by his lawyer, Janz shared his sympathies with Blatz's family, saying he has thought about her every day since she died. He said his dental practice has been affected and that he has done training in the wake of Blatz's death. 

Janz has taken several courses since the incident, including one about local anesthesia and one on medical emergencies, his lawyer said.

Blatz's family is suing Janz, his dental practice and the corporation that owns and operates the Boundary Trails Dental Centre, alleging the care provided to Blatz was negligent.

CBC News did not initially name Janz or the clinic involved in this article because the Manitoba Dental Association Act prohibits reporting the identity of a dentist until the inquiry panel makes its findings.
