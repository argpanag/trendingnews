Lawmakers look to Lexington gun violence prevention program for guidance

TheTools · 24.09.2026 21:09 · source · permalink

FRANKFORT, Ky. (WKYT) - Leaders from Lexington’s violence prevention initiative, ONE Lexington, met with state leaders in Frankfort to share strategies used locally as lawmakers consider policies for the Department of Juvenile Justice.

Lexington is on track to record its fifth consecutive year of declining gun violence. ONE Lexington is a factor in that decline, according to the initiative’s leaders.

Program built on community partnerships

ONE Lexington leaders attribute their success to multiple programs and resources, and most of all, community partnerships.

“Our approach is built around connecting the right people at the right time, responding to the immediate crisis, engaging the community, interrupting potential conflicts and supporting families through the aftermath,” a ONE Lexington representative said.

Director Devine Carama said gun violence primarily impacts people ages 10 to 29, which is why the organization’s outreach extends directly into schools.

Outreach recommendations for schools

When asked by a Kentucky Department of Education official how to reduce barriers statewide, Carama recommended creating a pathway for reformed citizens to volunteer.

“Something that drives them and keeps them on a straight path, is actually the opportunity to prevent other young people from following into their footsteps, from making the same mistakes that they make,” Carama said.

Smaller communities look to adapt approach

Carama said the approach can work in smaller communities as well. Henderson County Attorney Steve Gold said his community is no stranger to youth gun violence, and he came to Frankfort to see how Lexington’s methods could be adapted with fewer resources.

“I know you all said, you know, you look at Chicago, San Francisco, places like that, and you’re jealous of the resources they have; the rest of the state is jealous of the resources. Lexington has to be honest with you,” Gold said.

ONE Lexington leaders said they hope state lawmakers will continue financial support, including funding to help establish their own permanent municipal department.
