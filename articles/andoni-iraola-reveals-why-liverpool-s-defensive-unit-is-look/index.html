Andoni Iraola reveals why Liverpool's defensive unit is looking increasingly "solid"

TheTools · 20.09.2026 16:39 · source · permalink

Sunday afternoon at the Vitality Stadium offered a familiar narrative for anyone tracking Liverpool’s autumn trajectory.

A hard-fought, occasionally turbulent Bournemouth 0-1 Liverpool victory secured a third consecutive Premier League clean sheet, pushing the Reds further along a path of defensive stability.

It is now nearly a month since the Merseysiders last conceded a domestic goal that blemish coming via Morgan Gibbs-White’s penalty for Nottingham Forest back on August 29th in a 2-2 draw at Anfield.

Since then, shutouts against Ipswich Town, Fulham, and now the Cherries have quietly laid down a marker. Yet, this latest triumph was far from a comfortable afternoon by the seaside.

The visitors had to weather periods of sustained pressure, relying heavily on a world-class, miraculous first-half save from their veteran German goalkeeper and an authoritative, all-action masterclass from their young French centre-back to keep the hosts at bay.

For a club accustomed to chaotic encounters, this new-found ability to shut the door and grind out a victory away from home points to a subtle, important shift in mindset.

The anatomy of a new back four

Speaking candidly during his post-match press conference, the Liverpool manager offered a window into how this defensive solidity has been forged. Change at the back is rarely seamless, and the arrival of fresh faces during the window meant the technical staff faced a bedding-in period.

The integration of a 21-year-old French centre-back alongside a towering 35-year-old Dutch captain has formed a formidable, if unfamiliar, central partnership.

Factor in the addition of a 27-year-old Uruguayan defender arriving as a high-profile loan reinforcement from Barcelona to anchor the right side and a dynamic 22-year-old Hungarian full-back raiding down the left flank, and the scale of the transition becomes clear.

"I think the more time especially the defenders play together, I think it becomes more solid as a unit in the defensive line," the Liverpool boss reflected pitchside after the match.

"I think the French central defender and the Dutch captain, with the help now ofilles the Uruguayan right-back and the Hungarian full-back, are getting the connections. I think we are becoming more solid and in moments that you need to defend or they are putting the pressure [on] I think we are looking solid."

The numbers speak for themselves. While the attacking structure continues to evolve under rigorous tactical demands, establishing an unbreachable foundation is what ultimately wins championships.

Old heads are learning the tendencies of the new guard, and the communication barriers across a multi-national back four are rapidly dissolving into instinct. If the connections at the back continue to tighten at this pace, the clean-sheet streak won't be stopping on the south coast anytime soon.

For supporters making the long journey back to Merseyside, the takeaway was simple: Liverpool may have rode their luck at times, but they are building something durable where it matters most.
