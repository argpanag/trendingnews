Ewan McGregor on selling his ’65 Ford Mustang: I forgot I had it

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:27 · source · permalink

The actor bought the car on impulse while filming Moulin Rouge! — after 26 years he’s selling it at auction

When the hammer falls on his 1965 Ford Mustang 289 Convertible at the Goodwood Revival Bonhams sale in a couple of weeks’ time, Ewan McGregor will of course feel some sadness. It’s a car he has owned since filming Moulin Rouge! in early 2000.

He has driven it from London to the south of France several times, and had one especially cherished road trip with two of his daughters up the west coast of Scotland.

But in a way he processed the separation years ago. Because, McGregor freely admits, for a number of years he had forgotten all about it.

“That’s a terrible [admission],” he confesses. “Nobody wants to read that, do they? That this actor forgot he had a Ford Mustang. What a nightmare. It sounds like the most privileged thing I’ve ever heard.”

The last time McGregor, 55, drove his pony car was seven or eight years ago. At the time he lived in Los Angeles, but the Mustang, which was a rare right-hand-drive conversion, was stored and looked after by a hire car company owner in Southwark, south London, for use in the UK. 

“Whenever I came over it was ready for me and he was a great guy. He really looked after it. But when I last drove it the brakes were a bit spongy, so it went off to this other guy to have them fixed, and then I went back to the States. And, just through one reason or another, I just forgot all about it.

“Then, about four years ago, I suddenly went, ‘Where’s my f***ing Mustang?!’”

McGregor realised that his car was still with the mechanic who’d been commissioned to fix his brakes. Not only that, but when he tracked it down, it had been stripped down as part of undergoing a full restoration — without his knowledge.

“He’d totally taken it all apart,” McGregor says. “There was rust under where the convertible hood sits, and I’m sure there is often a bit of rust in there, but he had pulled the car to a million pieces and was just completely restoring all of it. Uninstructed by me, I have to say.”

It took some time to sort out that mess — and McGregor admits that he will have to pay the restorer for his rather presumptuous efforts — but the car is now back in his hands, rust-free and looking brand new.

So why is he selling it?

“The trouble is I’ve moved to Scotland and I’ve got a nice shed here, but it’s full, and I don’t really need a convertible up here in Perthshire. Not really. I’ve got a Morgan, so that sort of ticks that box if I want [to drive a convertible sports car]. So I just decided to sell it.”

The Mustang was an impulse buy after he saw it for sale in Australia while filming Moulin Rouge!, the acclaimed musical directed by Baz Luhrmann. It was love at first sight.

“There was a guy in the crew who had a [Mustang] Mach 1 — 1972 or something — and he was trying to sell it so he parked it outside where we had our dressing rooms. I know that he was trying to sell it to me. He just knew what I was like. But I must have started looking for other classic Mustangs for sale near me.

“I found this one for sale outside Sydney. It was a 1965 convertible, ivory white, light-blue interior, light-blue roof, and that was much more my bag.

“It had really good power steering and it was very easy to drive. It’s got that nice thin steering wheel. I love that old thin steering wheel feel and it had the V8 rumble in it. That’s a great sound. It wasn’t gonna break any speed records, this car, but it was such a classic look.”

McGregor shipped it back to London, garaging it near Abbey Road and using it when he could. It was mainly used for getting out of town, “Because driving around London in a convertible when you’re me — it was just a bit of a pain in the arse, you know? Everybody would say to me, ‘Hey, what year is your car?’ But often it was just a way to get to have a chat, you know?”

He had similar experiences in LA when he owned a replica of a Porsche 550 Spyder — the car in which the actor James Dean died — and says he quickly tired of that, but on holiday with the Mustang in the south of France it could be less irksome. “People loved it and would come up and chat about it, and it was sort of nice to have interactions about something other than just me and my work.”

McGregor would drive down from London to Cassis, where he would stay with his children and first wife, Eve Mavrakis, a French-Greek production designer.

“I’d always just drive it down alone, usually with my dog Sid, and they’d fly ahead and we’d meet them there because it’s quite a long way.”

Some of these journeys were his favourite times with the car, he says. “Cassis is a bit further along the coast from Marseille, and it’s quite rugged and beautiful. And it was a nice car to drive with the roof down in that hot French air, getting near the coast and you start smelling the ocean. It was beautiful. I loved it for that.”

On one occasion McGregor stopped in Paris and then, when following the sat nav out of town, found himself crawling through the city’s back streets. “I put in ‘no highways’, but what I should have done is do that when I was outside Paris. It didn’t take me on the Périphérique, or any of that. But it was awesome; there was so many different areas of Paris that I’d never seen.”

But it was a road trip with two of his daughters that McGregor says is his favourite memory of his time with the Mustang.

“I took my two little girls, Esther and Jamyan, when they were pretty young — probably seven or eight — up the west coast of Scotland in it, from Crieff up through Glencoe and then Fort William, and then you turn left and you go along to this amazing little town called Arisaig. It’s pretty beautiful — tiny but it’s not that touristic. My uncle Denis [Lawson, the actor] shot the movie Local Hero on a beach there, called Camusdarach beach.

“When we got to Glencoe, every five minutes we’d stop and put the roof down, and five minutes later we’d have to stop and put the roof up [due to rain], and then we’d stop and put the roof down. I mean it was just a joke. But if it wasn’t raining I wanted the roof down, and so did they, you know, they were only little. And we had such a great time.”

That trip included a magical moment for the three of them on the way back.

“We bought a bunch of picnicky stuff and stopped at a great spot south of Arisaig. We pulled off the road and found a place right next to the sea. It was absolutely glorious — a beautiful day — and then having that picnic, and then getting back in the Mustang and carrying on south was great. It was a brilliant car for that kind of thing.”

Despite all the long journeys, his faithful Mustang only once let him down, says McGregor, when the starter motor failed while picking up his children from a holiday camp in Besançon, France, near the Swiss border. He was rescued by a member of staff who also happened to own a classic Mustang.

“I was thinking, shit, what am I gonna do? I’m in the east of France. This guy came out and went, ‘Hang on,’ and he jumped in his car, drove home, took the starter motor off his car and came back in about 20 minutes. I changed it and it started straight up, and he said, ‘Just send it back to me when you get back to London.’ So I drove back to London, put it in a cardboard box and sent it back to him. I thought it was a nice old car moment.”

McGregor says he always treated his Mustang with respect due to its age. “I’m a bit like this anyway, but I drove it quite delicately because I thought it was so old. But now I’ve got a car that is 1937, so I know what a really old car is. I wish that I’d tuned the knockers off the Mustang, you know!”

McGregor’s Mustang is being auctioned by Bonhams at the forthcoming Goodwood Revival sale, which is part of the famous historic motoring festival. He can’t be there himself, he tells me, as he’ll be sailing from Oban in Scotland to Rio de Janeiro for a documentary, but that’s probably for the best, he reckons. “I would probably just end up buying it off myself. I know what I’m like! Best keep me out of the way.”

And besides, his Mustang deserves to find a home where it’s looked after and driven, McGregor tells me. “I’ve sort of had to let it go because I just don’t have room for it. I’ve got a nice wee collection of things up here in Scotland and I just feel like it’ll be better used by somebody else. 

“I’m quite good at that. I don’t mind. I think we’re just the purveyors of cars, you know? They don’t really belong to us, beautiful old cars. We just look after them for the time we have them, and then we sell them on and they’re looked after by someone else, hopefully.

“But Moulin Rouge! was one of the most important films for me — in my career and just the experience of it — so as a marker of that, the Mustang is very special to me.”

Ewan McGregor’s 1965 Ford Mustang 289 Convertible is up for sale at Bonhams’ Goodwood Revival: Collectors’ Cars and Automobilia Sale on September 19, with an estimate of £30,000-£40,000. View the lot at the Bonhams website
