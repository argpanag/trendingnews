  5. Should second Palace goal have stood?published at 16:37 BST 11 October
    16:37 BST 11 October

    FT: Crystal Palace 3-1 Nottingham Forest

    Dale Johnson
    Football issues correspondent

    Nottingham Forest boss Oliver Glasner might have something to say about Crystal Palace's crucial second goal.

    While it has been credited to Jefferson Lerma, replays seem to suggest that he may not have been the goal scorer.

    There appears to be evidence that Lerma headed the ball onto the elbow of teammate Anan Khalaili, and that is what took the ball towards goal before it found a way past keeper Matz Sels.

    A goal cannot be scored if the ball touches the arm, so Forest may believe the goal should have been ruled out. The VAR determined that there was no conclusive evidence to get involved.

    This will sting for Forest after they had a Neco Williams goal ruled out for handball against Spurs, after he bundled the ball into the net on the goal-line.

    There is a case that the evidence was clearer for handball by Khalaili than for Williams, and Forest have been on the wrong end of both.

Glasner suffers loss on Selhurst return as Palace beat Forest

Oliver Glasner endured a frustrating return to Crystal Palace as his Nottingham Forest side let slip an early lead to lose to the Eagles.

After guiding Palace to FA Cup and Conference League success, Glasner left the club at the end of last season and took on the Forest job in the summer.

Initially it looked like it would be a triumphant return to Selhurst Park as Forest took a third-minute lead through Dan Ndoye's neat low finish. They then dominated much of the first half, with Liam Delap somehow denied a second from point-blank range by Walter Benitez.

Palace were not in the game at all but incredibly turned the tables with two goals just before half time.

Daichi Kamada finished off a nice team move to level four minutes before the break for his first Premier League goal, and Jefferson Lerma headed in a second deep in stoppage time.

It got even better for Palace in the second half when Yeremy Pino slotted into the bottom corner, prompting Eagles fans to tease Glasner with chants aimed at their former manager.

Benitez made another fine save late on to prevent substitute Chris Wood from reducing the deficit and giving Forest some hope of snatching a point.

Palace's second win of the season moves them up to 12th in the Premier League, while Forest are 14th.

Sage post-match interview - Crystal Palace v Nottingham Forest

Palace's unexpected turnaround

Glasner brought Crystal Palace unprecedented success during his time at the Eagles, leading them to their first major trophy with the FA Cup before adding European glory.

As a result, it will have been tough for their fans to watch him take a seat in the opposition dugout.

For a good 40 minutes of Sunday's match Palace supporters will have been fearing the worst as Forest - aided by Glasner's knowledge of their side - appeared to have the measure of the home side.

Forest were in complete control while Palace were defensively a mess and were lucky to survive when Morgan Gibbs-White struck the crossbar and Delap's shot on the follow up was somehow denied by Walter Benitez.

That moment would prove pivotal as five minutes later Palace were level, and the momentum then swung significantly in their favour.

Having looked like they could have been two or three goals down after 40 minutes, they could have actually won by a bigger margin, with Lerma also seeing a header well saved by Mats Selz.

After losing three of their first five games, another defeat will have increased the pressure on Pierre Sage but instead their display from 41 minutes onwards will provide Palace fans with optimism.

Glasner post-match interview - Crystal Palace v Nottingham Forest

Frustration and controversy for Forest

Forest fans will have left Selhurst Park wondering how it all unravelled for their side, given just how in control they were for the majority of the first half.

They looked sharp and hungry for the ball, as well as capable of scoring with every attack but that almost all disappeared from the moment Palace equalised.

Forest may also feel aggrieved that Palace's crucial second goal - scored moments before half-time - was allowed to stand.

Initially it looked like a good header by Lerma through the legs of Sels, but replays showed that his effort did graze the elbow of Anan Khalaili.
