Slow-Moving Nor'easter Off the Carolinas Has a 40% Chance of Becoming Josephine as NC 12 Stays Closed

TheTools · 22.10.2026 13:01 · source · permalink

The storm pounding North Carolina's Outer Banks is not a hurricane, and as of early Wednesday, it was not even tropical. Still, the National Hurricane Center gives the storm-force, non-tropical low spinning a few hundred miles off the Mid-Atlantic coast a 40% chance of becoming a subtropical or tropical cyclone within 48 hours. If that happens, it would take the next name on the Atlantic list, Josephine.

For people on Hatteras Island, the label matters far less than the water. A mandatory evacuation for visitors has been in effect since 8 p.m. EDT on Oct. 16, and N.C. Highway 12, the island's only road to the mainland, has been closed between the Marc Basnight Bridge and Rodanthe since about 6 p.m. Monday after ocean overwash breached dunes near the Pea Island Visitor Center, Dare County officials told ABC News.

Forecasters were blunt that a name would not change the danger. "Regardless of the status of the system, high winds, coastal flooding, and dangerous surf are expected along portions of the U.S. East Coast during the next few days," the hurricane center wrote in its 2 a.m. EDT outlook.

A Hybrid Storm Making a Run at a Name

The system, tagged Invest 93L, was still attached to weather fronts early Wednesday, according to NHC. Over the next day or two it could shed those frontal features while drifting north-northeast over the warm Gulf Stream, allowing it to "briefly develop subtropical characteristics." By the end of the week, NHC expects it to accelerate northeast away from the U.S. and into conditions that are unfavorable for further development.

FOX Weather hurricane specialist Bryan Norcross placed the storm about 350 miles off Virginia Beach on Wednesday morning and noted it would still be sitting over "unusually warm water" as it turned out to sea, according to FOX Weather. That warm water is the reason a classic cold-season storm can flirt with tropical status.

The difference comes down to fuel. A nor'easter is a coastal low powered mainly by the clash between cold air over land and warm air over the ocean, with fronts attached. A tropical storm runs on heat released by thunderstorms around a warm center, with no fronts. A subtropical storm is a hybrid that draws on both — and its strongest winds often sit farther from the center. NHC names both types from the same list, and an October 2021 nor'easter later became Subtropical Storm Wanda.

Overwash, Lost Dunes and a Fragile Lifeline on Pea Island

On Tuesday, Dare County said the highway had not yet suffered pavement damage at the visitor center and that crews were rebuilding roughly 1,000 feet of protective dunes lost overnight. By Wednesday morning, WNCT reported that continuous waves had caused about 800 feet of pavement damage there, and NCDOT urged drivers to avoid NC 12 on Pea Island entirely.

The National Weather Service office in Newport/Morehead City has posted coastal flood warnings for Hatteras and Ocracoke islands, with 2 to 4 feet of inundation possible in vulnerable areas, and a high surf advisory for Hatteras Island through 8 p.m. EDT Thursday with breaking waves of 9 to 12 feet, according to NWS products summarized Wednesday by The Watchers. Farther north, ABC News reported sustained northeast winds of 25 to 35 mph with gusts near 50 mph and water levels 1 to 3 feet above normal at high tide, with minor to moderate flooding likely in Virginia Beach and Atlantic City.

This is the second time in a month the road has failed. A late-September nor'easter damaged about 1,000 feet of NC 12 and forced NCDOT to activate an emergency ferry between Stumpy Point and Rodanthe for the first time since 2013, before the road reopened to all traffic on Oct. 1. Nature World News previously reported that North Carolina has spent nearly $51 million in 15 years keeping NC 12 open in Dare County, a figure likely to climb once repairs from both storms are tallied.

Visitors, Residents and Renters Face Different Rules

The Dare County evacuation order applies only to visitors in Rodanthe, Waves, Salvo, Avon, Buxton, Frisco and Hatteras Village. Residents have not been ordered to leave, and no evacuation is in place north of Oregon Inlet. Visitors with upcoming reservations should not travel toward the island and should contact their rental company directly, since the county cannot change lodging bookings.

Residents who stay face practical limits: no road access north, suspended trash collection and sudden closures at each high tide. FOX Weather noted that 32 homes have collapsed into the ocean near Cape Hatteras since 2020, so oceanfront owners in Rodanthe and Buxton should keep watching the surf line.

Warning signs that call for immediate action include water or sand moving across a road, waves reaching dune lines, and sudden flooding around homes. Never drive into water covering NC 12 — call 911 in an emergency. Residents can get county alerts by texting DareEmergencyAlerts to 77295 and check DriveNC.gov before any trip. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood damage, so photograph property now in case of a flood claim.

Thursday's Shift North and the Weekend Exit

The Weather Channel expects the low to sit off Massachusetts by Thursday before a cold front sweeps it out to sea Friday, though models still disagree on exact timing. That means Cape Cod and southern New England could see rough surf late in the week while conditions slowly ease on the Outer Banks.

Key questions remain open. NHC has not determined whether the system will become Josephine, NCDOT has not announced a reopening timeline for NC 12, and damage is still being assessed. NHC issues Atlantic outlooks at 2 a.m., 8 a.m., 2 p.m. and 8 p.m. EDT and would begin formal advisories if a storm forms, while Dare County will announce when visitor restrictions lift.

The bottom line: whatever name it gets, this storm has cut Hatteras Island's road link for the second time in four weeks. Visitors should stay away, residents should plan around high tides, and everyone should follow Dare County and NWS instructions rather than social media posts.

Developing Story Timeline

Oct. 21, 2 a.m. EDT: NHC outlook keeps 48-hour and seven-day formation chances at 40% (medium) and says the low could briefly become subtropical.

Oct. 19, about 6 p.m. EDT: NC 12 closes between the Marc Basnight Bridge and Rodanthe after overwash breaches dunes near the Pea Island Visitor Center, according to Dare County.

Oct. 16, 6 p.m. and 8 p.m. EDT: Dare County declares a state of emergency for Hatteras Island, followed by a mandatory evacuation order for visitors.

What Readers Want to Know

Is the nor'easter going to become Tropical Storm Josephine?

It might. As of 2 a.m. EDT Wednesday, NHC gave it a 40% chance of becoming a subtropical or tropical cyclone within 48 hours. That is a probability, not a forecast that it will be named.

Would a name make the storm more dangerous?

No. NHC says high winds, coastal flooding, and dangerous surf are expected along parts of the East Coast regardless of the system's status.

Who is under evacuation on Hatteras Island?

Only visitors. The order covers all seven Hatteras Island villages. Residents and areas north of Oregon Inlet are not under an evacuation order.

When will NC 12 reopen?

No timeline has been announced. The road remains closed between the Marc Basnight Bridge and Rodanthe while NCDOT assesses damage and rebuilds dunes near the Pea Island Visitor Center.

What is the difference between a nor'easter and a subtropical storm?

A nor'easter is fueled mainly by temperature contrasts between air masses and has fronts attached. A subtropical storm is a hybrid that draws energy from both warm ocean water and those contrasts.

When will conditions improve?

Forecasters expect the low to move off Massachusetts Thursday and be swept out to sea by a cold front Friday, with improving conditions late in the week, though timing remains uncertain.
