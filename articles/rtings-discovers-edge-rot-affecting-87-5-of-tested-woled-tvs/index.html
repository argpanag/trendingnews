RTINGS discovers "edge rot" affecting 87.5% of tested WOLED TVs - VideoCardz.com

TheTools · 08.11.2026 10:23 · source · permalink

An RTINGS investigation reveals dead pixels around the edges of older LG Display WOLED panels.

RTINGS finds “edge rot” in 14 of 16 tested WOLED TVs, QD-OLED panels remain unaffected

OLED panels show dead pixels along their edges after prolonged use.

RTINGS has published findings from its 18,000-hour accelerated longevity test, revealing a problem affecting older WOLED televisions. Of the 16 LG Display WOLED panels tested, 14 developed dead pixels or visible deterioration around their edges. None of the three Samsung Display QD-OLED televisions showed the same problem.

RTINGS calls this problem “edge rot.” It usually appears as dead pixels or small clusters of dead pixels around the edges of the screen. Unlike burn-in, it has nothing to do with displaying static images or how the TV is used. RTINGS suspects manufacturing defects or damage to the protective layers that keep moisture away from the OLED panel.

Older WOLED panels were affected more severely

The problem appeared in nine of ten televisions equipped with LG Display’s older WBC panels. Five experienced severe deterioration, while another three had substantial defects. Newer WBE panels performed better, although five of six still developed some form of edge deterioration. These included models from LG and Sony.

The three unaffected QD-OLED televisions were the Samsung S95B, Samsung S95C and Sony A95K. RTINGS also examined three OLED monitors, including one WOLED model, and found no edge deterioration. However, the publication cautions that its sample sizes are too small to establish whether QD-OLED technology is immune to the problem.

LG and Samsung Display respond to findings

LG Electronics and LG Display acknowledged the phenomenon in a statement to RTINGS. The companies said it may occur in a small number of OLED products exposed to prolonged high temperatures, humidity or other harsh environmental conditions. They added that such deterioration is not expected under normal viewing conditions.

Samsung Display says its QD-OLED panels avoid this problem thanks to Thin Film Encapsulation technology, which helps keep moisture out of the panel edges. RTINGS has not confirmed exactly what caused the defects in its tests, but moisture getting inside the panels or contamination during manufacturing are among the possible explanations.

There is some good news for owners of newer OLED TVs. Most of the affected models were released between 2020 and 2022, and RTINGS has yet to determine whether panels made from 2024 onward have the same problem.
