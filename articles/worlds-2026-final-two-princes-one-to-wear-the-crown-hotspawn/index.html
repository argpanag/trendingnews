It’s LPL vs LCK in the Worlds 2026 final: both Bilibili Gaming and Gen.G have the chance to crown a dream they have been chasing for years.

On one hand, there is the Chinese team looking to bring glory to the LPL; on the other hand, Gen.G wants to establish a new legacy after T1’s historic three-peat.

With all eyes at Barclays Center in New York on Saturday, November 14, which team has a higher chance of becoming world champions? Here’s our breakdown ahead of the big match.

Both teams come into the grand final with great momentum and the common element between them is none other than Canyon-Chovy and Xun-knight.

The two teams have heavily relied on their respective jungle-mid duos to take control over the pace of the game. On Gen.G’s side, the agency was given a lot more to Canyon, while BLG often relied on knight. That said, BLG has also started putting Xun in a carry role, as shown by his Naafiri game against T1.

In the first two games of the final, we will likely see both teams shape their drafts and matchups around these four players. Champions like Qiyana and Lee Sin/Jarvan IV/Nocturne will be either traded or contested, with mid laners playing a champion that synergizes well.

Based on what we’ve seen so far, Gen.G seems to be the more consistent duo, although BLG can easily match them on the day in good form. These four players will likely decide the outcome in the first rounds, with other roles gaining importance later in the series.

Speaking of other roles, Viper and ON had a major ramp-up throughout the tournament, peaking against T1 in the semifinals.

Compared to Ruler and Duro, BLG’s bot lane seems to be more comfortable playing a wider variety of champions and Viper also had the chance to play Cassiopeia as a flex pick in the bottom lane. The two bot lanes also share plenty of picks (Ezreal, Jhin, Ashe), so it will become a matter of who has more cards to play.

The question mark is on Gen.G: the team hasn’t played any game 4 at this Worlds so far (two clean sweeps in knockouts), so they could have some ace up their sleeves they haven’t shown yet.

If there is something to praise about Bin at Worlds 2026, it must be his ability to play for the team. No longer fully focused on just min-maxing the laning phase and being aggressive, the Chinese top laner has seemingly learned when to back off for the greater benefit of the team.

In one way or the other, he’s become more and more like Kiin in terms of consistency. The Korean top laner has often been tasked with playing strong blind picks, but he also shined on counter matchups. With both teams being relatively close in firepower, the two top laners could swing the series in their favor depending on how they show up.

It will ultimately come down to the draft, but both players proved they have enough discipline. In terms of laning phase, however, I still favor Bin slightly, as he can often do little details better and turn them into a greater lead.

Prediction: BLG 3–2 GEN

While GEN have looked cleaner in Knockouts, I believe the team had arguably easier opponents compared to BLG. We should have close games throughout the series, with mid-game skirmishes likely dictating the outcome.

The longer the series goes, however, the bigger advantage I think BLG will have. They have more flexibility in the bot lane and likely in the top lane as well (Bin can play carries better than Kiin), two factors that can be crucial in game 4 and game 5.

If the LCK team is able to get an early lead in the series, it will be much harder for BLG to reverse sweep. A lot will come down to how well Xun and knight can hold the jungle-mid matchup. And judging by their performance in previous series, there is confidence they can pull that off.
