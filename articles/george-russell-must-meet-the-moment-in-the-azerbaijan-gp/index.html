George Russell must meet the moment in the Azerbaijan GP

TheTools · 25.09.2026 18:27 · source · permalink

George Russell has been the dominant force this week at the Formula 1 Azerbaijan Grand Prix.

But he must finish the job on Saturday in the main event.

Russell took pole position in dominant fashion on the Baku streets Friday, capturing P1 by more than eight-tenths over Charles Leclerc. Just how impressive has Russell been this week? Not only did he top the timing sheets in both FP1 and FP2, but during Friday's qualifying hour he was fastest in Q1, fastest in Q2, had the fastest first sector, the fastest second sector, the fastest third sector, and was fastest when it mattered most, in the closing minutes of Q3.

That added up to the fifth pole position of the season for the Mercedes driver, and his first since the Austrian Grand Prix.

"Yeah, it's been a while," said Russell trackside after the qualifying session. "It's been a while, so it definitely feels good. It's probably the biggest margin in my whole career, even including F2, F3 days. So, the lap felt really sweet. The car's been feeling great this weekend and, on a track like this, when you can hook it up like that, it feels really amazing."

Speaking moments later in the FIA Press Conference, Russell outlined that he has been in a "great spot" the entire weekend.

"Yeah, I mean, I've been feeling in a great spot this whole weekend. From FP1 I felt confident, I felt hooked up. You know, as I've said recently, I'm just getting more and more on top of this car and what it requires, and it's just all feeling more intuitive," said Russell. "And, you know, I felt very confident we were in the running for pole, but when I crossed the line and I saw the gap, I was, yeah, I was shocked. I knew the lap was great. I had a nice slipstream at the end, which gave me an extra probably tenth and a half, two tenths, and it just all perfectly came together."

That great spot looks even better when you zoom out and look at the big picture. Russell entered the week trailing teammate Kimi Antonelli by 81 points in the Drivers' Championship standings, and he conceded after the Spanish Grand Prix — won by Antonelli — that he was "realistically" not in the fight in that title chase.

But Russell was singing a different tune when he arrived in Baku this week, vowing to keep fighting until it was "mathematically impossible" for him to catch Antonelli. And with the way the Azerbaijan Grand Prix has set up, this might be his best chance to cut into Antonelli's lead.

Not only is Russell starting in P1, but Antonelli will start at the back of the field after a "very stupid" mistake in Q1. The young Mercedes driver clipped the inside wall at the apex of Turn 1, the left-hander after the long straight that runs from the left-hand Turn 16, through the start/finish line, and into the opening of the lap:

Antonelli called it a "good way to come back to Earth" when he met with the media after the session.

As a result of that incident, Antonelli was classified down in P16, and will be at the back of the grid when the lights go out. This gives Russell a very good chance to cut into Antonelli's lead if he continues his form in Baku.

However, we have been here before, and recently.

Rewind to a few weeks ago, and the Italian Grand Prix. Russell qualified on the front row alongside pole-sitter Pierre Gasly, while Antonelli started at the back of the grid due to a power unit change.

Yet, Antonelli wound his way through the field, storming to a stunning win ahead of Russell by nearly four seconds.

There are differences between that week, and these past few days in Azerbaijan. While Russell qualified in P2 at Monza, he has been dominant this week at the Baku City Circuit. taking pole position by over eight-tenths ahead of Leclerc is a statement, in contrast to qualifying second behind Gasly as he did at the Italian Grand Prix.

And this weekend, Antonelli will not get the benefit of a brand new power unit, as he did in Monza.

Russell was asked if a championship comeback begins Saturday in Azerbaijan.

"You know, I think the performance outside today was great, but, you know, [Antonelli's] been flawless this whole season. I think when you look at every championship campaign, even for the winners, there are mistakes along the way. So, yeah, I still keep on believing and keep on fighting until it isn't possible anymore," began Russell.

"But I don't feel any pressure. I feel excitement every time I jump in the car now, purely because I feel more confident in myself. And we spoke about it on Wednesday here: when that confidence starts to build, it's just like this upward cycle. On the flip side, when you're on the back foot, you start losing that confidence and then you start pushing the limits more, and that's when mistakes happen. But, as I said, he's been flawless this season. It's the first mistake he's made in a qualifying or race this whole year. So, yeah, let's see tomorrow."

Tomorrow is his best chance yet to begin a championship comeback. Russell must meet the moment.

Otherwise the math might get away from him.
