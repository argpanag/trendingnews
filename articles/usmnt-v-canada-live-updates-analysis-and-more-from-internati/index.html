18 min: More Canadian pressure, and Schwake has to boot it away. Freeman almost ends up with it but can’t control it.

I’ve made it 18 minutes without mentioning I’ll be seeing Canada’s most legendary band this month.

16 min: Some lunging but fair tackles each way, and Shaffelburg unleashs a shot from 25 yards that has some power but is straight at Schwake.

Then it’s Shaffelburg again from an acute angle – probably more of a cross than a shot – but Schwake handles well. The US keeper, making his first start, has been up to the task thus far, though he hasn’t been asked to do anything spectacular.

15 min: David with a nonviolent but completely unnecessary foul in his own half. That’s eight fouls, and I wouldn’t say they’ve been bad calls.

14 min: Belated call to give the USA a free kick. Canada had started to go off to the races.

That’s Canada’s seventh foul. On pace for 45!

13 min: Eustáquio surprises everyone with a short pass to Liam Millar, who scuffs his shot well wide.

12 min: Corner to Canada. Not a great delivery from Eustáquio, but Shaffelburg fights for possession and draws a foul. Free kick a few yards away from the upper corner of the box.

11 min: This is the second time St. Clair has been forced to play the ball with his feet and wound up scrambling to keep the ball from rolling out for a corner. Awkward.

10 min: A bit more direct from the USA here, seeking Albert on the left wing, but the young US player fouls Sigur.

Canada play quickly upfield and end up with a wayward shot.

The USA are choosing to do nothing aggressive with free kicks at midfield, like they’re resuming a possession drill.

Preamble

Ah, the friendly. It’s a can’t-lose game. Win, and it’s proof that the team is on the upswing. Lose, and it was all just an experiment.

That said, when a bunch of youngsters are romping over Mexico, it’s hard to suppress the optimism entirely.

And this one has all the added subplots of two countries sharing the world’s longest border – 5,525 miles or 8,891 kilometers – who aren’t really on speaking terms right now.

Did we mention that the Canadian coach, Jesse Marsch, is from the USA and has a tendency to take a few jabs at his native land?

So this one might be worth more popcorn than the typical friendly. Enjoy.

Jeff Rueter on the kids, after Cavan Sullivan’s long-range chip helped the US to a 3-0 win over Mexico:

The more incredible thing about Sullivan’s moment, aside from its historic nature and its semi-dreamy origins, is how normal it is for this window for the US. All but one of the 13 potential debutants called in by Mauricio Pochettino have seen time on the field, most of them impressing. Julian Hall, 18, has scored in all three of his appearances. Mathis Albert broke Christian Pulisic’s decade-old benchmark to become the program’s youngest goal scorer and held the record for a whole four days before Sullivan’s chip.

Given the opponents so far, it’s fair to say these players aren’t inching into the shallow end of the pool. They’ve been thrown in at the deep end, and they are swimming.
