GdS: 'The trophy they've never won' - Amorim to rely on youth to begin European mission

TheTools · 16.09.2026 08:30 · source · permalink

AC Milan begin their Europa League campaign against Benfica tonight, a trophy they must look to target for a few reasons.

As La Gazzetta dello Sport (seen below) write this morning, Milan have won everything there is to win in club football, with 18 ‘international’ trophies in the modern era. Actually, the Rossoneri have won almost everything.

The trophy cabinet is missing the Europa League (formerly the UEFA Cup and the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup). The closest the Diavolo have come is the semi-finals: eliminated by Tottenham in 1972 and beaten by Borussia Dortmund in 2002.

Seven European Cup/Champions League titles are more than enough to make up for that gap, yet the 2026–27 Europa League looks like a realistic target. Why not seize the opportunity? Especially since winning the competition secures Champions League qualification, without worrying about top four.

A long absence

Tonight, Milan return to the European stage after a 575-day absence, following that now-infamous Champions League play-off against Feyenoord. It was on 18 February 2025: the match ended 1–1, and Sergio Conceiçao’s side were eliminated before finishing 8th and missing Europe entirely.

One might expect Milan to field their strongest team tonight against Benfica, especially given that over 65,000 fans are expected at San Siro. Yet, according to the latest whispers from Milanello, Ruben Amorim plans to field a sort of ‘second-string’ side, leaving several key players out of the starting XI.

Leaving out Luka Modric – who is struggling to adapt to the change in style – is understandable. But why bench Mario Gila, Adrien Rabiot, Diego Moreira and Christian Pulisic? Is it because Lecce are visiting San Siro on Sunday?

Milan vs. Benfica is never an ordinary match. It carries the rich history of being worthy of two European Cup finals, both won by the Diavolo (a 2–1 victory at Wembley in 1963 and a 1–0 win in Vienna in 1990).

Then there is Rui Costa, a unifying figure for both clubs. A formidable midfielder, he won the 2003 Champions League with Milan in the final against Juventus. Now, he serves as Benfica’s president. It is a game with a story of emotional ties.

Red-hot form

This decision to field a ‘Milan B’ seems questionable, especially against Benfica. In the Primeira Liga, the Lisbon giants sit in second place, two points behind Francesco Farioli’s Porto. However, they have outscored everyone else, netting 21 goals in six rounds for an average of 3.5 per game.

They are an attack-minded team featuring Vangelis Pavlidis as their centre-forward. The Greek striker has already bagged 14 goals this season: six in the Europa League qualifiers and eight in the league.

These are impressive figures, particularly when compared to the solitary, meager goal Gonçalo Ramos has managed in the first four rounds of Serie A. Ramos came up through the ranks at Benfica, and the hope is that the ‘law of the ex’ might snap him out of his late-summer slump.

He has already expressed his affection and respect for the club, stating: “I spent more time at Benfica than with my own family. If I score, I won’t celebrate.” Among Milan fans, a fear is beginning to creep in that the striker – who cost €70m – might end up a flop like his predecessor Santiago Gimenez.

Ironically, Gimenez was the scorer of the Rossoneri’s last European goal, in the aforementioned match against Feyenoord in February of last year. On the bright side, Benfica might opt ​​for heavy squad rotation, as they have a clash against league leaders Porto this coming Sunday.

Pavlidis himself could start on the bench as a result of this, replaced by the Colombian Jhon Duran who arrived on loan from Saudi Arabia in the summer and is still a top striker to have as a back-up.

Faith in youth

Amorim seems willing to lean on the youngsters, specifically the two attacking midfielders playing behind Ramos: 23-year-old Omari Hutchinson and 20-year-old Alphadjo Cisse.

We caught a glimpse of Hutchinson against Lazio: during a 15-minute cameo, he stood out with a cross and a shot. He displayed both personality and quality, and tonight at San Siro, he faces his first real test.

Cisse has shown great ability, scoring two fine goals against Torino and Juventus, and has even earned a spot on head coach Roberto Mancini’s list of potential national team call-ups. The hope is that Hutchinson and Cisse will create chances and supply Ramos.

The time for experiments and settling-in periods is nearly over. It is time for Amorim’s brand of football to fully take shape. Performances so far have been inconsistent, a mix of the good and the bad.

Milan, the Italian club with the most success abroad, cannot afford to treat European competitions lightly. Especially with that gap in the trophy cabinet waiting to be filled.
