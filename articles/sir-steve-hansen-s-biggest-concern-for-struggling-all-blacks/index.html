Sir Steve Hansen's biggest 'concern' for struggling All Blacks

TheTools · 24.11.2026 05:19 · source · permalink

Legendary coach Sir Steve Hansen has weighed in on the All Blacks’ struggles in Europe, which he believes are rooted in the unique demands of their tour to South Africa.

The All Blacks’ regression on their northern tour has been well documented, with their upset to Wales immediately followed by an historic defeat to England at Twickenham on Sunday.

The Springboks have also been on the back foot, losing three of their four Test matches since hosting the All Blacks.

The two-time World Cup-winning coach, who’s highly experienced with the challenges of the annual year-end tour, believes both teams are feeling the effects of the intensity of their four-Test battle two months ago.

The All Blacks were comprehensively out-muscled by England in their biggest loss to a European opponent, and it’s the lethargy in the visitors which stood out to Hansen.

“All the contests were won by England and the energy levels of the All Blacks were a concern to me. It’s not so much that they lose, it’s why did they lose?” Hansen said on the Rugby Unity podcast.

Steve Hansen consoles Dane Coles after the All Blacks lost their 2019 World Cup semifinal to England. (Source: Photosport)

“If you look at South Africa, you say the same thing - both those teams have lost two in a row. Now, you’ve got to ask yourself why because they haven’t become bad rugby teams overnight, so something’s going on.

“When you look at it, they’ve done a lot of travel, they’ve played big Test matches, the top two teams battered themselves earlier in the year.

“They’ve had to dig into their reserves and get replacement players and so forth. One of the things that has happened in modern times is in the northern hemisphere tour – and I know this from my own experience – the boys are buggered.”

Hansen’s comments echo the sentiments of his predecessor Sir Graham Henry, who highlighted the same concerns on Monday.

Including the non-Test match fixtures in South Africa, the All Blacks will play 17 games this year compared to 13 in 2025 and 14 in 2024.

“You’ve just got to adjust your training programme, you’ve got to change your training week to try and freshen them up and it just looked like they had no energy, and England took full advantage of that,” Hansen continued.

“When you get the possession source that they got and they just keep coming at you, coming at you, they’re going to score tries because they’re too good a side to pass up that many opportunities inside the 22 like we did.

“The energy levels are a concern.”

All Blacks (Source: Photosport)

Although coach Dave Rennie has been quick to dismiss such suggestions, the impact of the death of head of security Logan Cadwallader is another factor which Hansen said could not be overstated.

“I don’t think you can underestimate the fact of the passing of Logan, that’s emotionally something they’re having to deal with,” Hansen said.

“You don’t do that in a week. He was like a family member, like a brother, so I think that’s taken an emotional toll as well as a mental toll.”

The knives have been out for Rennie and his players after their miserable fortnight, to the extent NZ Rugby CEO Steve Lancaster publicly spoke out in support of the first-term head coach.

But in their return to Twickenham this weekend for a rematch against England, Hansen said the All Blacks have the ideal chance to make a resounding statement heading into the summer.

“All the naysayers are out and they’ve got plenty to say, and you want them to because that’s what makes New Zealand rugby expectations so high, you’ve got to front up,” Hansen added.
