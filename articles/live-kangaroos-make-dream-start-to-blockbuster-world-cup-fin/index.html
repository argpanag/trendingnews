Men's World Cup winners history

J

By Jon Healy

By Jon Healy

Let's have a look back at some Rugby League World Cup history.

The Kangaroos have won 12 men's Rugby League World Cups, including nine of the past 10, which doesn't leave much room for anyone else to have broken through over the years.

Despite being the de facto number two in the world for the past few decades, New Zealand has only one crown, in 2008; while Great Britain won three, including the inaugural 1954 final against France, and the 1960 and 1972 deciders.

So that's:

  • Australia – 12: 1957, 1968, 1970, 1975, 1977, 1988, 1992, 1995, 2000, 2013, 2017, 2022
  • Great Britain – 3: 1954, 1960, 1972
  • New Zealand – 1: 2008

How did the Jillaroos and Kiwi Ferns get here?

J

By Jon Healy

By Jon Healy

There was nothing resembling a speed bump for the Aussie women through the group stages as they won their three games against Samoa, England and Wales by a combined 242-6.

Then came the semifinal against Fiji, which was supposed to be a similar story but the Bulikula staged an early ambush for an early 8-0 lead. It may not sound like much, but it wasn't just the score, it was the way Fiji went about it, dominating with physicality and skill.

The Jillaroos eventually resettled for a 30-14 win, and more than a few of them said it was just the sort of hit-out they needed before the final.

Fiji was also right in the game against the Kiwi Ferns to kick off their tournament, before a last-minute Leianne Tufuga try gave New Zealand an 18-12 win.

Then they went 92-0 over France, 40-12 over Papua New Guinea, and 32-0 over Samoa.

Pretty similar journeys, albeit in reverse order, for both teams. So hopefully it leads to a very even contest this afternoon.

Who has won the women's World Cup finals?

J

By Jon Healy

By Jon Healy

The Australians and the New Zealand have split all six women's world titles since they started playing World Cups back in 2000.

The Kiwi Ferns won the first three, before the Jillaroos won in 2013, 2017 and 2022.

  • 2000: New Zealand 26-4 Great Britain
  • 2003: New Zealand 58-0 New Zealand Māori
  • 2008: New Zealand 34-0 Australia
  • 2013: Australia 22-12 New Zealand
  • 2017: Australia 23-16 New Zealand
  • 2022: Australia 54-4 New Zealand
  • 2026: Australia vs New Zealand

So this afternoon will be the tie-breaker.
