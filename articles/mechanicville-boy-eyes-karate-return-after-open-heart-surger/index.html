Mechanicville boy eyes karate return after open-heart surgery

TheTools · 11.11.2026 22:58 · source · permalink

MECHANICVILLE, N.Y. (WNYT)- A 12-year-old boy is recovering after his heart stopped at a cross country meet and emergency responders helped save his life.

Six weeks ago, Vincent Farnell went into cardiac arrest as he crossed the finish line at a cross country meet in Malta’s Luther Forest. The seventh grader from Mechanicville later learned he had a congenital heart defect.

At 12, Farnell said he loves anime and Pokemon. He also has trained in karate for about five years and joined the cross country team at the start of the school year.

“I’m a big plushy fan,” Farnell said.

“Whenever I was running I would get very exhausted, I would stop, I would breath very hard, and my eyes would go full static so I could barely see,” Farnell said.

On Oct. 6, a Tuesday, Farnell ran a 2K race in Malta. He said he does not remember starting the race or reaching the finish line before he collapsed.

What happened next?

Two nurses at the finish line started CPR and paramedics with Malta EMS used an AED after Farnell’s heart stopped. At the time, his mother, Carolanne, was driving to the meet when she got a call that her son was going to Saratoga Hospital.

“We actually were right behind the ambulance, we had just missed them coming out of Luther Forest when we were right there,” Carolanne Farnell said.

Carolanne Farnell said she walked into the ER and saw her youngest son receiving emergency care. From there, he was taken to Albany Med, where doctors found the 12-year-old had a congenital heart defect.

“It’s hard as a parent to not lose it, to see something like that and to stay together,” Carolanne said.

Farnell was diagnosed with anomalous left coronary which means the left valve is on the right side of the heart instead of the left.

Days later, Farnell was at New York Presbyterian Hospital for open-heart surgery to reposition the valve. He is expected to make a full recovery and said he feels mostly normal now.

He still feels discomfort when he coughs or sneezes as his incision heals. Farnell hopes to return to school after Thanksgiving and get back to sports by March.

“I want to go straight to karate,” Farnell said when asked what he was most looking forward to doing again.

In the meantime, Farnell and his family are grateful for support from friends, classmates and the community. That includes cards from classmates, money raised at a fundraiser at The End Zone in Mechanicville and cross country teammates dyeing their hair red like his.

“This is from my friends in karate, everyone even from the adult class signed it, even the senseis and staff,” Farnell said showing off a framed certificate.

“It feels nice to know people have my back and care about me,” Farnell said. “I’m just thankful for everyone being so supportive.”
