Heat Notes: Thompson, Starting Lineup, Giannis, Adebayo

TheTools · 05.11.2026 05:50 · source · permalink

The Heat finally got the shooting performance they’ve been expecting from Klay Thompson in Friday’s win over Indiana, writes Eric Nehm of The Athletic. After connecting at 32.4% from behind the arc in Miami’s first five games, Thompson drilled seven three-pointers and finished with 25 points. He fulfilled a prediction by coach Erik Spoelstra, who told reporters before the game that Thompson could help transform a team that was struggling to score.

“I think once he really hits his stride, I think that’s gonna have an exponential effect on our offense,” Spoelstra said. “He’s already gotten some great looks. I mean, if two or three of those in transition (go down), that just changes the entire complexion of how our transition game looks and feels.”

The Heat counted on signing Thompson throughout the summer, refusing to spend money on other shooters while hoping he would reach a buyout agreement in Dallas. They targeted him as the perfect outside threat to help stretch defenses and create more room for Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo.

“A huge reason why I’m here is to play with a talent like Giannis and help him, hopefully, do what he’s done before and that’s win a championship and an MVP trophy,” Thompson said. “Spo defined my role very clearly when the season started. It’s to play hard-nosed defense and space the floor to give our best players room to operate. I thought I did a good job of that tonight and it’s something I’m definitely gonna build on, and I’m just really happy with the patience I showed tonight.”

There’s more from Miami:

  • Thompson’s flurry gave a much-needed boost to the starting lineup, which has been one of the worst groups in the league this season, Anthony Chiang of The Miami Herald observes in a subscriber-only piece. Thompson, Antetokounmpo, Adebayo, Davion Mitchell and Andrew Wiggins had been outscored by more than 30 points in 53 minutes over the first five games. On Friday, they outscored Indiana by 20 points in their 13 minutes together, including an 18-4 run to begin the third quarter. “We’ve really been working at it,” Spoelstra said. “We understand what can be our kryptonite and what can be in our strength zones. I wanted to see the guys have a game like this tonight where they’re having fun and having a great feel for where the ball can go. It didn’t feel like guys we were taking turns. There was a flow to it. The guys were celebrating each other. Those were pretty good steps in the right direction for a good offense.”
  • Antetokounmpo said he and Adebayo are figuring out how to create their own spaces on the court, which wasn’t happening during the team’s 0-4 start, per Ira Winderman of The Sun-Sentinel (subscription required). “We’ve got to be like, you know how the magnets are? They stick together, but if you flip them, they kind of go away from one another,” Antetokounmpo said. “That’s how me and Bam got to be out there. When he’s on the top, I’ve got to work the baseline.  When I’m on the top, he’s got to work the baseline. We’ve just got to keep on being away from one another, away from one another’s space and vicinity.”
  • Now that the statue of Dwyane Wade outside Kaseya Center has been updated with a new face, Winderman considers in a mailbag column who might be next in line to receive a statue.
