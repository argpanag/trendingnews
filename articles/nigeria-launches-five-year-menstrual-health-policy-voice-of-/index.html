Nigeria Launches Five-Year Menstrual Health Policy - Voice of Nigeria

TheTools · 27.11.2026 16:59 · source · permalink

The Nigerian Government has launched a National Policy on Menstrual Health and Hygiene Management 2026–2030, with stakeholders calling for adequate funding, state adoption and effective implementation to improve the health and dignity of women and girls.

The policy was unveiled at the Second National Summit on Menstrual Health and Hygiene Management 2026 in Abuja.

It was approved by the Federal Executive Council on October 28, 2026, following consultations and validation involving stakeholders across the six geopolitical zones.

The Minister of Women Affairs and Social Development, Hajiya Imaan Sulaiman-Ibrahim, represented by the Chairman of the Governing Council of the Maryam Babangida National Centre for Women Development, Princess Jumai Idonije, urged state governments to adopt and domesticate the policy and provide dedicated budgetary allocations for its implementation.

She also called on the 774 Local Government Authorities to direct resources to public schools, primary healthcare centres and displacement settlements to ensure access to affordable menstrual products, clean water and hygienic sanitation facilities.

The Minister stressed the need to move beyond policy approval to funded implementation and measurable results, noting that the success of the policy would be determined by improvements in the daily lives of women and girls, particularly those in rural, underserved and conflict-affected communities.

She announced plans by the ministry to introduce an Annual National Menstrual Dignity and Hygiene Excellence Competition and Awards to recognise states, local governments, development partners, traditional and religious institutions, community champions and corporate organisations supporting menstrual health.

In her remarks, the Country Director of WaterAid Nigeria, Evelyn Mere, identified government leadership, partnerships, institutional commitment and broad stakeholder consultations as critical to the successful development and implementation of the policy.

Mere, represented by WaterAid Nigeria’s Head of Strategy, Programme Effectiveness and Learning, Abdulmalik Abubakar, called for a costed implementation strategy with clear targets, timelines and continuous monitoring to ensure tangible results.

She commended the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development for providing institutional leadership in developing the policy, while acknowledging the contributions of development partners, civil society organisations, academia, the private sector and the National Technical Working Group.

She noted that policy approval was only the beginning, adding that its success would depend on adequate funding and budgetary allocations to guarantee access to affordable menstrual products, safe sanitation facilities, accurate information and quality menstrual health services.

Mere also emphasised the need to address stigma and discrimination associated with menstruation.

Similarly, the representative of Population Services International (PSI), Plangnan Dishil, identified dedicated financing, state adoption and effective accountability mechanisms as essential to translating the policy into action.

She said the provisions must translate into affordable menstrual products, safe sanitation facilities and improved access to menstrual health information at state and community levels.

Dishil reaffirmed PSI Nigeria’s commitment to working with national and subnational authorities to strengthen existing structures and support states in taking ownership of the menstrual health agenda.

Also speaking, the representative of the Nigerian Supreme Council for Islamic Affairs, Nike Abdulrahman, described the policy as more than a government document, saying it represented a promise to every Nigerian girl.

She said Islam did not regard menstruation as a source of shame but as a natural condition ordained by Allah.

Abdulrahman also called for greater attention to endometriosis and other menstrual-related health conditions affecting women.

She pledged the Council’s support in promoting menstrual dignity through mosques and Islamic schools, using the platforms to raise awareness and encourage parents and husbands to support girls and women during menstruation.

Similarly, the Gara of Masaka, Chief John Ahmadu, called for women’s issues to be taken to the grassroots to ensure that awareness campaigns and interventions reach communities where they are most needed.
