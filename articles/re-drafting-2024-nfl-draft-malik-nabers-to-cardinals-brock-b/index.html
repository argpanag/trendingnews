Re-drafting 2024 NFL Draft: Malik Nabers to Cardinals, Brock Bowers to Giants, Cooper DeJean to Rams & more | Sporting News

TheTools · 16.10.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

Vinnie Iyer

Mark J. Rebilas-Imagn Images

The 2024 NFL Draft was loaded with offensive prospects going off the board early. The first round tied a record with six quarterbacks being selected, all within the top 12 overall. There were 23 offensive picks in the top 32, including all of the top 14.

Even though eight first-rounders have already made the Pro Bowl in at least one of their first two seasons, there has been more disappointment. Many of the third-year players haven't delivered yet for their teams through the first quarter of the 2026 NFL season.

Although several teams would stick with the selections they made only two-plus years ago, you can bet there would be plenty of different picks based what's known now about the most productive players from the class.

What would those changes be and what players would have new homes? Here's a retro mocking of the first round:

1. Chicago Bears: QB Caleb Williams
2. Washington Commanders: QB Jayden Daniels
3. New England Patriots: QB Drake Maye

Although Williams is the only one of these three quarterbacks who hasn't played in a conference championship game, when healthy, all of these teams have gotten top results when operating the right offenses for them. None of them would be succeeding in the same way had they traded places.

4. Arizona Cardinals: WR Malik Nabers

There's no doubt the Cards wouldn't have picked Marvin Harrison Jr. ahead of Nabers, even knowing about the latter's major knee injury from 2025. The pre-draft debate landed toward Harrison, but Nabers would have been a real game-changer for Arizona's pass-happy offense.

5. Los Angeles Chargers: OT Joe Alt

The Chargers know how important Alt's blocking has been to their team when healthy. It's unfortunate he hasn't been able to stay healthy to dominate in front of Jusin Herbert and the running game.

6. New York Giants: TE Brock Bowers

The Giants, with Nabers off the board and not wanting to take Harrison, would be jumping on Bowers, who they had rated high two years ago. Where they are now in a tight end-friendly offense under Matt Nagy with free-agent star Isaiah Likely, Bowers would have smashed it for a healthy Jaxson Dart.

7. Tennessee Titans: QB Michael Penix Jr.

This is tough. J.C. Latham has provided mixed results as their right tackle as the first-round follow up to star young guard Peter Skoronski. But knowing 2025 No. 1 overall pick Cam Ward is on the early bust track, it would have been smart to not trust '23 second-rounder Will Levis and take as shot at Penix, who has played very well when healthy during his third season.

8. Atlanta Falcons: EDGE Jared Verse

Had the Titans taken Penix, the Falcons wouldn't have been put in the awkward position of picking him while already signing Kirk Cousins to a four-year, $180 million deal in March '24. Verse should have gone here to be the much-needed productive pass rusher for them after they had great interest in his talent.

9. Chicago Bears: WR Rome Odunze

Odunze hasn't been up there with Nabers, but he also hasn't been Harrison. He was a good immediate pick pair with Williams and that's still the case. The returns will be there with Ben Johnson now that Williams is back healthy.

10. New York Jets: OT Olu Fashanu

The Jets were set to pick No. 11 before the Vikings traded up to take J.J. McCarthy one spot higher. Fashanu keeps showing upside as a budding left tackle. That's a position difficult to address through any other means.

11. Minnesota Vikings: EDGE Dallas Turner

Turner was taken at No. 17 overall by the Vikings, who traded up with the Jaguars from No. 23. Knowing McCarthy was a bust, they would have been fine taking Turner, a blossoming pass rusher in Brian Flores' defense.

12. Denver Broncos: QB Bo Nix

The Broncos have been pleased with Nix's clutch play and natural execution of Sean Payton's offense with plenty of early winning results to show for it.

13. Las Vegas Raiders: CB Quinyon Mitchell

The Raiders hit on a couple of key secondary players as star rookies from the '26 draft, cornerback Hezekiah Masses and safety Treydan Stukes. But former Packer Eric Stokes, signed in '25 free agency, has been a major liabilty. With Bowers gone, Mitchell would have be a great pick.

14. New Orleans Saints: OT Taliese Fuaga
15. Indianapolis Colts: EDGE Laiatu Latu
16. Seattle Seahawks: DT Byron Murphy II

Here are three more unchanged picks in the middle of the first round. The Saints are seeing Fuaga tap more into his blocking potential in '26. Latu and Murphy have been very effective up front for their respective teams.

17. Jacksonville Jaguars: WR Ladd McConkey

The Jaguars had one big season from Brian Thomas Jr. but now their top receivers are '23 sixth-rounder Parker Washington and Jakobi Meyers, acquired in a trade in the middle of last season. Adding McConkey for frequent play in the slot instead of taking Thomas and dealing for Meyers would have been the smart original move.

18. Cincinnati Bengals: OT Amarius Mims

The Bengals got their starting right tackle in the draft and he's been rather good in pass protection for Joe Burrow opposite left tackle Orlando Brown Jr.

19. Los Angeles Rams: CB Cooper DeJean

The Rams were tied to taking DeJean until Verse slipped to them. Verse ended up being a big part of the trade to require Myles Garrett on the edge. But Los Angeles was so heavily tied to taking DeJean for ace inside coverage it was a near-lock. He should have been a first-round pick instead of an early second-round pick by the Eagles. He immediately helped his actual team win the Super Bowl with his big-play ability.

20. Pittsburgh Steelers: OT Troy Fautanu

The Steelers are getting stronger returns from their rebuilt offensive line including Fautanu as their steady left tackle in front of Aaron Rodgers.

21. Miami Dolphins: WR Xavier Worthy

The Dolphins took too long to move on from Jaylen Waddle and Tyreek Hill to accelerate their rebuilding process. The speed of Worthy could have fit well to rejuvenate Mike McDaniel's offense with Tua Tagovailoa before the whole offense had to rebooted. This definitely would have been a better upside offensive pick than edge rusher Chop Robinson for the defense.

22. Philadelphia Eagles: CB Kamari Lassiter

Although the Eagles would have scored neither Mitchell or DeJean in this re-draft, they could have rebounded fine in the secondary with Lassiter, who has emerged as the top corner in the Texans' pass defense.

23. Minnesota Vikings: RB Bucky Irving

Irving should have been the one running back taken in the first round of this draft with his explosive versatility as a potential workhorse as the Vikings continue to piecemeal the running game now well removed from the Dalvin Cook era. Irving should not have fallen all the way to the fourth round as a great value pick for the Buccaneers.

Bob Donnan-Imagn Images

24. Dallas Cowboys: OT Tyler Guyton
25. Green Bay Packers: OT Jordan Morgan

These two picks also shouldn't have changed, including Dallas staying put and not trading down to No. 29 with Detroit. There were plenty of starting offensive tackles taken in this class. Guyton remains strong for the Cowboys for Dak Prescott and the running game. Morgan is finding his groove at the right time with the Packers needing his protection more in a pass-happier attack with Jordan Love.

26. Tampa Bay Buccaneers: C Zach Frazier

Graham Barton was picked here as their starting center, but he has had a rough go on their offensive line. Frazier, meanwhile, has been dominant as the Steelers' starter after being taken No. 51 overall in the second round.

27. Arizona Cardinals: EDGE Austin Booker

The Cardinals whiffed here with Darius Robinson, who toggles between end and tackle off the bench in their 4-3. They should have instead taken Booker, who has become a productive young star for the Bears after lasting all the way until the fifth round.

28. Buffalo Bills: WR Brian Thomas Jr.

Thomas did have a spectacular rookie season, catching passes plenty from Mac Jones. The chemistry as a big-play field-stretcher has been off with Trevor Lawrence. There's a good chance he could have fit a lot better with Joe Brady's offense as a go-to outside target for Josh Allen. The Bills made an initial mistake by trading this pick to the Chiefs so their AFC rivals could land Worthy, with the Bills settling for Keon Coleman four picks later. 

29. Detroit Lions: CB Renardo Green

The Lions made a disastrous off-field whiff on Terrion Arnold when they moved up to No. 24 and the Cowboys took Guyton here. Green is starting to become a top cover man for the 49ers, who drafted him at the end of the second round at No. 64 overall.

30. Baltimore Ravens: CB Nate Wiggins

The Ravens round out the list of 16 picks (half of the first round) that should not have changed. After Mitchell, DeJean, Lassiter and Green, Wiggins is right there as a solid cover man, now for Jesse Minter's scheme.

31. San Francisco 49ers: OT DJ Glaze

The 49ers were seen reaching for then buzzy wide receiver Ricky Pearsall, who has not been able to stay healthy with a lot of uncontrolled circumstances. Glaze has been outstanding for the Raiders as their third-rounder at No. 77 overall in '24. San Francisco was in the right tackle market then minus Mike McGllinchey and Glaze would have been the right pick over Latham.

32. Kansas City Chiefs: WR Keon Coleman

Some might say the fates of both Worthy and Coleman would have much improved had the latter been a Bill and former was drafted by the Chiefs. Although there are some signs both picks can still pan out, Coleman was perhaps better built to thrive with Patrick Mahomes in a more prominent role.
