COLUMBUS, Ohio (WSYX) — Ohio Secretary of State Frank LaRose says his office is reviewing complaints about a recent political ad targeting Dr. Amy Acton.

LaRose told ABC 6 the complaints are related to the ad featuring Dr. Acton’s mother.

"We've had complaints from various people about those ads. And so I'm not going to comment on the specifics of them because, you know, our team, once we receive a complaint, goes through and just does a simple evaluation, has the law been violated or not? And then if it has the then, you know, we issue a fine and that kind of thing. So I'm not going to go into that," LaRose said.

He added, "I will say that, you know, candidates will answer for their own ads, that, you know, I probably wouldn't run an ad like that, but every candidate gets to make their own decision."

LaRose explained that his office checks whether ads follow legal requirements, including having the proper disclaimer and being funded from permissible sources. "The test that that we ask is, does it have the proper disclaimer on it? You know, is it being, funded by a permissible source, those, those kinds of things," he said.

The Ohio Election Integrity Commission, managed by the secretary of state’s office, handles these complaints. LaRose described the enforcement process as straightforward and transparent, comparing it to receiving a speeding ticket. "The Ohio Election Integrity Commission that's housed within our office is still a bipartisan body that runs it, but administratively, it's housed within our office. And what I'm, proud of is how quickly that's working. Now, we get a complaint and within a matter of a month or two, we will have issued a disposition on that. And it kind of works like a speeding ticket," he said.

LaRose detailed what typically happens after a violation is found. "We send them a fine, they pay it, everybody moves on. And then we publish that because transparency matters. So if you look at those case dispositions every two weeks, we're publishing those and and letting people know that, hey, this candidate didn't file a campaign finance report or this candidate accepted funds that were above the limit or, this candidate didn't have a disclaimer on their yard signs, whatever," he said.

LaRose said he could not estimate how long the review might take in this current case. He said that in past situations, investigations usually result in a fine, but said that because this is an active investigation, he would not go into further detail.
