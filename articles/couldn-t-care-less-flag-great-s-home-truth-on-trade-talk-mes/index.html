‘Couldn’t care less’: Flag great’s home truth on trade talk messing with Lions’ title defence

TheTools · 12.09.2026 09:07 · source · permalink

You need so much to go your way to win a premiership. To win three in a row, you need nearly everything to go your way.

Brisbane has been through its fair share of trials and tribulations on its quest for a three-peat.

They were widely written off just a month ago due to defensive concerns and again face a longer road to a grand final – and a shot at AFL immortality — after a shock qualifying final loss to an undermanned, yet inspired, Swans.

It’d be brave to write off Chris Fagan’s side, who’s responded time and time again when faced with adversity.

But this season is a particularly intriguing litmus test for a Lions side with those defensive deficiencies and trade speculation surrounding stars.

DOES SPECULATION ON STARS’ FUTURES IMPACT CONTENDERS?

Lachie Neale and Zac Bailey’s season-long trade links have presented unique circumstances for Fagan’s group in 2026.

While Bailey recommitted to Brisbane this week on a mega eight-year extension after he, ironically, rejected massive interest from semi-final opponent Adelaide, it’s certainly uncommon for a contender to have two of its best players the subject of such intense noise and Fagan constantly publicly questioned on their futures.

The Neale discussion has gone a step further than just noise, with the two-time Brownlow Medal winner widely expected to be at Collingwood next year. Those free agency papers seem stamped.

Not that it’s affected the individual form of either player.

If anything, Neale has thrived throughout the speculation around his future and challenging personal circumstances, including a particularly strong first half of 2026 as arguably Brisbane’s best player.

Even despite a quiet showing last week when he was nullified by Sydney’s James Rowbottom.

The same goes for Bailey, who backed up a career-best 2025 campaign to claim a second-straight All-Australian blazer.

Unlike Neale, perhaps Bailey always knew deep down he was going to stay a Lion.

It’s simply rare for teams that win premierships to lose bona fide star players to other clubs.

Sure, Brisbane lived this to some degree 12 months ago with Callum Ah Chee and Brandon Starcevich’s departures. But they’re not exactly on the same level as dual Brownlow winner Neale, one of the best players of the modern era.

The likes of Brandon Ellis (2019 Tigers), Scott Lycett (2018 Eagles) and Matt Suckling (2015 Hawks), who also left after premiership wins, also don’t quite fit the star category, nor held the same importance to their teams as Neale.

Lance Franklin was an outlier, with Hawthorn’s 2013 Grand Final win over Fremantle the legendary forward’s final game in brown and gold before he left for Sydney. Even if that Hawks team, and having Franklin on grand final day in general, was a different beast that could overcome just about anything.

Franklin’s close friend and premiership teammate Jordan Lewis says “not once did it cross my mind” that murmurings around Franklin’s future would have “any negative impact on the group.”

Nor does Lewis believe players’ futures should bear any weight on teammates.

“I think it’s really for the player themselves to compartmentalise and be able to perform and put those distractions aside. If you were to ask ‘Bud’, it’s certainly a different story,” Lewis recalled to foxfooty.com.au.

“But in terms of the Hawthorn playing group, we actually couldn’t care less. Yes, we were sad he was leaving and would rather have him in our team.

“Certainly, from my point of view, I don’t think any player spent any more time than they should thinking about a person that potentially won’t be there the following year.

“I think players have an unbelievable ability to park it and be in the moment. What you also understand as a player, and it happens a lot differently in rugby league, but if a player is out on the field and in that moment a teammate, you have full trust that they’re giving 100 per cent for that period of time.

“Certainly, with Lachie Neale, I don’t think you can question the way he’s gone about it on field.”

Of course, the Neale and Franklin situations are very different. It’s hard to know exactly what impact Neale’s off-field issues have had internally at Brisbane, while Franklin was iconic at Hawthorn.

That said, Lewis suggested there was no element of the Hawks wanting to send off Franklin in style in 2013.

“Nope,” Lewis said. “I think if you’re using those things as motivation, it’s fabricated, in a way.

“We wanted to win because we wanted to win. If it just so happened to be when ‘Bud’ was there, great. If not, that’s fine.

“The environment is bigger than the individual. If you get the environment right, it doesn’t matter what individuals are there.”

Sure, there’s probably been a component of Neale and Bailey being in ‘contract years’ — when they’d be fighting to maximise the value of their next deal. But at times, it can go the other way, so you have to respect their professionalism and ability to get on with the job and perform at an elite level.

In Neale’s case, perhaps we’ve matured enough as an industry to accept a star player that’s bound for another club is still able to play a key role on a legit contender and it have no impact, like in the NRL.

If it doesn’t affect the performance of those teams, it shouldn’t affect anyone else; certainly not those in the cheap seats.

THE LONGER FINALS ROAD… AGAIN

Despite last week’s shocker against Sydney, Brisbane has still been Brisbane throughout 2026.

Even if the Lions have taken their foot off the pedal at times, like reigning premiers can, they finished top four again and their best is probably still the best.

Though they say your warts come out in finals; the ultimate pressure cooker. And Brisbane looked a shell of itself last week.

Possibly the most important element for any premiership team is to have everyone pulling in the same direction towards a common goal. To be all in and building momentum and belief throughout the finals series.

We saw that complete buy-in from Sydney last week, even if it was far from the Swans’ best 23, to shoot the Lions down a peg or two.

But, again, the harder road is nothing new for Fagan’s side.

They won a flag from outside the top four in 2024 and lost a qualifying final to Geelong last year, to on both occasions take the long route to the decider. They were still dominant in both grand finals.

Jordan Lewis’ Hawks experienced this same longer road to the grand final while competing for a historic three-peat.

Hawthorn lost a road qualifying final to West Coast three weeks prior to reversing the result against the Eagles and pulling off its flag trifecta in 2015.

It was the only qualifying final the Hawks lost across their four premierships under Alastair Clarkson.

Looking back, Lewis thinks players “subconsciously” held back in that 2015 qualifying final.

“Certainly, my mindset going over to the west was: ‘If we win tonight, great. If we don’t, it doesn’t matter’. I think in the back of our minds, we knew if we lost and had to go the long way round, we were supremely confident we could overcome those next two games and go back to Perth and win,” Lewis added.

“When you get to the stage where you’re been successful and you play a game with a double chance, I don’t think you’re as sharp as what you are when you’re playing the next game — when if you lose, you’re out.

“You become more desperate. I think that was true with ‘15. You knew there was that safety blanket of the double chance.

“The mentality from week one to week two is totally flipped. It’s just that underlying confidence of an ability to perform when it absolutely matters. There was definitely a mentality change.

“Because we knew when there was a game that had serious consequences, we’d back ourselves against anyone.”

Brisbane might be the ‘best backs to the wall’ team in the competition that personifies having that innate ability to respond to adversity when it needs to most.

Fagan, as scathing as he was of his side’s performance against Sydney, has always stuck fat with his core, instead of making bold selection statements.

The Brisbane coach has maintained that faith in his troops yet again and backed them to respond. Ty Gallop replaces the injured Noah Answerth for Saturday’s Gabba semi-final against Adelaide as the Lions’ only change.

Lewis said players don’t want to see “panic” from a coach in a finals series and that “it’s important you keep everything really stable, as boring and as repetitive as it has been in the past.”

The four-time premiership Hawk expects Brisbane would have added motivation to bounce back from last week’s loss to a Sydney outfit missing serious starpower in the fallout from the Pullman Hotel scandal — a result some have called the Swans’ best win outside of their premierships, given the circumstances.

“You come up against a side missing five senior players and three of their best. You’re confident you can roll over to Sydney and just turn up and win, but it becomes a reality check,” Lewis said.

“These weeks are relatively easy in a sense that the motivation will be there from the embarrassment of the previous week. I think that’s enough to drive a playing group.

“When things get tough with successful teams, they absolutely have trust with their teammates. You buy back into the system and the selfless nature, because you know they’re the ingredients that work.

“They’ve still got an unbelievable challenge; there’s no doubt about that. As a player, what you’re thinking about entering it is 80 per cent confidence, 20 per cent hope.”

The carrot of a premiership three-peat and writing themselves in legendary footy folklore remains for Brisbane.

They have a chance to be the third team this century to follow in the footsteps of Brisbane (2001-2003) and Hawthorn’s (2013-2015) champion hat-tricks and entrench themselves as one of the greatest sides of the modern era.

So then, what’s the hardest part of winning three flags in a row?

“I think the hardest part is everyone is waiting for you to fall,” Lewis said.

“Everyone is writing you off and in a funny way there’s a lot of negativity around your team and you’ve got to be able to deal with that.

“As well as you’re going, there is a lot of people that want to see you fail. That’s hard to deal with and be able to perform. You’re constantly the hunted. Everyone wants to bring you down.

“You’re cognisant of the fact that you are on the precipice of achieving something that only two teams in the modern era have achieved. That’s rare air.

“For us, it was achieving something Hawthorn had never achieved in their history. You’ve got to understand the position you’re in right now and how close it is to becoming part of history.”
