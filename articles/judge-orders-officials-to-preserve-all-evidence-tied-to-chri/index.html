Judge orders officials to preserve all evidence tied to Christa Pike’s botched execution

TheTools · 07.10.2026 19:22 · source · permalink

Judge tells Tennessee officials all items in execution room must be saved as Pike remains restrained in hospital bed

A judge ordered Tennessee officials on Wednesday to preserve all evidence related to the botched execution attempt on convicted murderer Christa Pike, as it emerged that the state has refused pleas from medical staff to allow her to be unshackled from her hospital bed.

At a hearing in Nashville on Wednesday, judge I’Ashea Myles said: “Anything that was in the actual room” during the attempted execution a week ago must be preserved by the state, including all drugs, intravenous lines and tubing used in the death chamber.

The order came as Pike’s lawyers said she has pneumonia, a blood clot, and is unable to move either of her badly injured arms, after she regained consciousness on Tuesday.

“The first thing she said was: ‘Where am I and who are you?’,” one of Pike’s lawyers said on Wednesday afternoon.

Pike, 50, was taken to hospital unconscious but alive from Tennessee’s execution chamber last Wednesday after two supposedly lethal doses of the barbiturate pentobarbital. She had been on a ventilator, with an unknown prognosis, before she woke and spoke on Tuesday.

“There’s still a lot of cognitive confusion, and so I will say that she has wanted to know what’s happened, and she’s asked about several people. She’s asked about her team. She’s asked about the victim’s family … I think she is angry and confused about everything right now,” one of Pike’s lawyers, Randy Spivey, said.

The updates came after a contentious court hearing where Pike’s legal team accused Tennessee officials of “secreting away” evidence. Attorney Luke Ihnen said items from the attempted execution could be key to future legal challenges by Pike, should the state attempt to kill her again.

“The state has made no promises that Ms Pike will not be subject to a future execution, and the evidence that we are seeking to preserve now is relevant for any future challenge that she makes, because as we pled in January, the same thing is likely to happen,” Ihnen said.

Pike survived a lengthy procedure and the large doses of the sedative as Tennessee officials had attempted to put her to death, in a case that shocked many in the US and created headlines around the world.

At a press conference after Wednesday’s hearing, Spivey said it was unclear “what if any function” Pike would regain in her arms. “Her hands look like if you were to blow up a surgical glove,” Spivey said.

Despite her condition, Pike was still being restrained, her team said, shackled to her hospital bed by her feet and right arm.

“Based on our understanding, medical staff have asked that the shackles be removed and have been denied that request [by the Tennessee correction department],” he told Myles earlier. He added: “We would also amend our request and ask for that relief.”

Witnesses to the attempted execution of Pike described how they heard her breathing raggedly after receiving the first injection. She was then hospitalized after a second dose failed to kill her and was declared to be in critical condition.

After the botched execution attempt, Spivey called the process “cruel and torturous”, adding that he had counted seven needles in Pike’s left arm, “one of which appeared to be bent when it was pulled from her”. Pike had lifted her head from the execution gurney to say her arms were burning as the drug was administered.

Her lawyers had warned before the execution that officials might struggle to locate veins and cause her suffering in the process, but the state had pushed to proceed and the US supreme court allowed the prison to move forward.

There is speculation from medical experts that perhaps some or a lot of the drug missed her vein and went into tissue, that one of her veins, which were problematic and this fact flagged by her lawyers months earlier – blew, or that the drug was faulty, or a combination.

Pike was 18 and her then boyfriend 17 at the time when they killed 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer, one of their classmates at Knoxville Job Corps, in Tennessee in 1995.

According to prosecutors, Pike was worried Slemmer was trying to take the boyfriend, Tadaryl Shipp, away from her. Together, they lured Slemmer into a wooded area, tortured and killed her.

Shipp was convicted of first-degree murder in 1997 and was given a life sentence with the possibility of parole. Pike has never denied her involvement in Slemmer’s killing. She was later diagnosed with bipolar disorder and post-traumatic stress disorder but, because she was 18, she was sentenced to death and has been on death row, isolated, for 30 years.
