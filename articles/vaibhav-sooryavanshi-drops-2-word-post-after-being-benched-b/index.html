The wait continues for Vaibhav Sooryavanshi. The 15-year-old opening batter has not featured in a single match for India since the start of the West Indies series, remaining on the bench for all four games as head coach Gautam Gambhir and the team management continue to back the opening combination of Sanju Samson and Abhishek Sharma. With the series level at 2-2, pressure is mounting on Gambhir and captain Shreyas Iyer to hand the teenage sensation an opportunity in the fifth and final T20I. While Gambhir has already made his stance on Sooryavanshi's selection clear, the youngster has offered a glimpse into his mindset amid the prolonged wait.

Taking to social media, Sooryavanshi shared a cryptic message that read, "still believing". The post appears to reflect his determination to remain hopeful about earning a place in the playing XI for the series decider against the West Indies, where he could potentially help India secure a series victory.

— Cricket Central (@CricketCentrl) October 16, 2026

Gambhir, known for his straightforward approach, has made it clear why Sooryavanshi has struggled to find a place in the team. The India head coach believes dropping either Abhishek Sharma or Sanju Samson to accommodate the youngster would be unfair, given their performances and established roles in the side.

"I think Samson showed what he is capable of. Someone doesn't become Player Of The Tournament in the World Cup without having talent, so he has done enough. Obviously, it was tough- probably the toughest decision I have taken in my coaching career, was leaving him out of that England series because of the way he had performed before that," said Gambhir.

Gambhir further explained that the management's decision to stick with the established opening combination was influenced by the players' past performances and the team's success in major tournaments. He stressed that a few disappointing outings should not be enough to undermine the contributions of players who have consistently delivered for India.

"But again, sometimes you go with the players form as well. From my point of view, I wanted to go back to the combination we had during the World Cup, because those guys after three or four failures, don't become bad players. If a team that has gone from one World Cup to another one unbeaten and has won the World Cup. Suddenly, it has become a team after one series and these guys not become bad players."

For Sooryavanshi, the message from the head coach leaves little room for ambiguity: he will have to be patient and wait for his opportunity. Although Gambhir acknowledged the teenager's immense potential, he made it clear that players with a proven track record would continue to receive preference.

"From Vaibhav's point of view, it is again a very clear conversation that he has to wait for his opportunity. We know the kind of talent he has. We know what he can do. But again, someone who done extremely well over a period of years obviously will always be ahead of someone who has got into the team. Whenever he gets that chance, you will get a longer run as well," he explained.

While Gambhir does not consider Sooryavanshi's continued absence from the playing XI a controversial selection call, the teenager has enjoyed comparatively more consistent opportunities under VVS Laxman. Laxman has taken charge of the Indian team on occasions when Gambhir has been occupied with other assignments, giving the young batter a different experience in terms of opportunities at the international level.
