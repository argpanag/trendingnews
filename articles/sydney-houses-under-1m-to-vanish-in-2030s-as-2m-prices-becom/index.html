Sydney houses under $1m to vanish in 2030s as $2m prices become norm - realestate.com.au

TheTools · 17.10.2026 22:03 · source · permalink

Sydney’s housing market has been in a slump for months but is expected to bottom out in mid-2027. Picture: Jane Dempster

Sydney houses priced under $1m have been placed on the endangered species list, with new figures showing these deals will likely be phased out of the market almost entirely by the 2030s.

This will coincide with house prices well above $2m becoming the norm across the bulk of Sydney, including in major Western Sydney hubs such as Liverpool and Fairfield, new modelling has revealed.

The realestate.com.au figures projected what houses and units would cost in every suburb by 2031 and 2036, with the current market downturn would likely end by the second half of 2027.

Prices were projected to grow by more than $500,000 over the next five years in 141 Sydney suburbs if they continued on the current trajectory of growth from the last 20 years.

Sluggish home construction has meant an ongoing shortage of housing that will eventually put pressure on prices to rise again.

Sydney suburbs with the most affordable houses were revealed to have the most upside price growth potential once the current downturn abated, largely because of the larger market for cheaper homes.

And it meant the bottom rung of the housing ladder would likely rise much faster, leading to a rapid deterioration in sub-$1m house deals.

“The reality is that houses under $1m are already quite rare in most of Sydney,” said Angus Moore, chief economist at Realestate.com.au.

“Prices will not keep falling forever. We expect them to bottom out next year and as prices grow, more and more suburbs will get pushed out (past $1m).”

Mr Moore said the projections showed that despite many homeowners losing thousands in equity in the current downturn, with recent buyers vulnerable if they had to sell, affordability challenges hadn’t disappeared.

Realestate.com.au’s projections showed no suburbs in the Sydney basin would have a median house price under $1m by 2031. Across The Central Coast and Blue Mountains combined, there would be just three suburbs.

Meanwhile, nearly 246 suburbs, about half of Sydney, would have house prices averaging higher than $2m.

The latter included 92 suburbs where the median house price was on track to surpass $3m by 2031. By 2036, prices above $3m would be the norm in just over a third of Sydney.

Greater Sydney’s most affordable suburbs in 2031 were projected to be Central Coast enclaves Tuggerah and Mannering Park and Blue Mountains area Mount Victoria.

All were more than 90km from the CBD and each with house prices averaging around the $970,000-$995,000 mark in 2031.

Mr Moore explained prices in cheaper outer suburbs had been rising rapidly ever since the Covid pandemic as remote working arrangements made less accessible areas more popular.

“We would expect that to continue,” he said, adding that the city was not building enough housing to contain population growth.

Government tax changes, including negative gearing restrictions, also incentivised investors to buy in Sydney’s cheaper outer areas where rental yields were higher.

This would keep sustained pressure on prices at the bottom of the market, leading to an eventual erosion of sub-$1m house deals, Mr Moore said.

REA Group economist Angus Moore said outer suburb prices had been growing faster over recent years.

The gradual disappearance of deals under $1m would mark a huge turning point in Sydney’s history considering the citywide median only surpassed this point for the first time in 2016.

For most of the period before then, a house costing $1m was cultural shorthand for Harbourside opulence.

Units were set to remain much more affordable than houses into the 2030s.

About half of Sydney areas would still have median unit prices below $1m in 2031, with most of the sub-$1m suburbs in the range of $800,000-$950,000.

Western Sydney agent Sanjeev Kumar of LJ Hooker Schofields said prices across the region were primed to bounce back strongly because coming transport improvements would broaden the lifestyle appeal.

“The community is growing,” he said. “Long-term, property does grow. People are cautious now but it is also a great time to be upgrading because home prices are temporarily lower.”

Martin Kennedy, group manager for social housing provider Home in Place, said a return to growing prices, while reassuring for homeowners who had stretched themselves, did come with major drawbacks.

It signalled coming generations would struggle to afford houses in locations that were once home to the working class, Mr Kennedy said.

“The most likely outcome is that Australian housing will remain exceptionally expensive by historical standards for the foreseeable future,” he said.

“Young adults increasingly don’t move out of home, don’t get married, don’t have kids and, because they have no stake in society and nothing to conserve, become easy prey for ideologues peddling the politics of redistribution.”

First-home buyers Sayani and Anirban Das said it was a buyer's market. Picture: Richard Dobson

Anirban Das recently secured his first home, buying a house in western suburb Riverstone, and said he was confident there would be value growth.

“Once the RBA has finished doing something about inflation, I think the value will go up,” he said. “We feel quite lucky because we got a really good deal on the property.

“It’s a buyer’s market right now. We could be patient. We are very happy with the size and location. We will get some equity by the time the market recovers.”
