David Jones struggles with declining relevance and faces administration as 5,000 jobs hang in the balance

David Jones, the iconic Australian department store, faces a crisis as it falls into administration after 188 years. The retailer’s struggle highlights the broader challenges facing department stores in adapting to modern retail trends.

Robert Crawford from RMIT argues that David Jones is experiencing ‘death by a thousand cuts’. He explains that the traditional department store model is outdated in the face of online shopping and specialised retail outlets like H&M and Uniqlo. Crawford points out that while these international brands have thrived, local department stores have seen a slow decline.

Challenges for department stores

Crawford notes that the convenience once offered by department stores has been overshadowed by the rise of malls and online platforms. “If I want everything, I go to Amazon,” he says, highlighting the shift in consumer behaviour. The loss of exclusive brand deals and the inability to adapt to changing consumer preferences have further weakened David Jones’ position.

The company’s failure to maintain its unique brand offerings has been a significant blow. As Crawford explains, once competitors gained access to these brands, David Jones lost its competitive edge.

This erosion of exclusivity has led to a reduction in store size and offerings, contributing to its current predicament.

With over 5,000 employees facing an uncertain future, the impact of David Jones’ administration is far-reaching.

The traditional mall model, which once relied on department stores as anchor tenants, is also under pressure. Crawford suggests that the future may lie in focusing on flagship stores in key locations and enhancing customer experiences.

Looking ahead, Crawford believes that David Jones must harness nostalgia while innovating to survive. The challenge will be to create unique experiences that draw customers back into stores, a strategy that has worked for some international counterparts.
