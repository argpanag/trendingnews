122nd over: South Africa 390-7 (Bavuma 164, Harmer 62) Hazlewood drags down and Harmer is into position in a flash. He doesn’t get all of it with a pull, but he muscles it over the leaping man at midwicket as it scampers away for four. South Africa now have as many as Australia managed in the first innings in Durban.

121st over: South Africa 385-7 (Bavuma 163, Harmer 58) Marnus into the attack! He’s bowling leggies and mixing up his pace. A floater and a half tracker to Harmer to close out a set that went for just three runs before that. But Harmer’s plinked drive and his off-balanced cut shot can’t find a gap in the ring so no damage.

120th over: South Africa 382-7 (Bavuma 160, Harmer 58) Hazlewood thwacks Harmer’s pad with a nip-backer. He considers a review but thinks better of it. Just as well as replays show it was going over the top. Harmer then steers a three behind point before Bavuma gets an immediate single. The pitch is playing really well. Predictable bounce and no real menace off the surface.

119th over: South Africa 378-7 (Bavuma 159, Harmer 55) Maddinson into the attack. Might as well. Two singles off the first over after drinks.

118th over: South Africa 376-7 (Bavuma 158, Harmer 54) Hazlewood returns. He hasn’t looked sharp. Then again, he is recovering from food poisoning. This over is OK. Back of a length, tight line, the things you expect from the metronome. Four runs off it. And time for drinks. Bavuma takes off his gloves and leaves them behind with his bat. He’s not declaring yet.

117th over: South Africa 372-7 (Bavuma 157, Harmer 51) On they go. Just one run off this Webster over that offered a little something off the surface. One spat up at Bavuma but his soft hands means it remains harmless.

Geoff Wingall writes in:

Hello Daniel,

And if it’s to be a draw, then why not put more miles into the legs of the Aussie bowlers before the 3rd Test - especially with one of them already struggling?

Hello Daniel,

I can understand your enthusiasm for a declaration with only two and a bit days remaining, but I can’t see it happening unless the follow on target is at least 200, preferably 250 if that’s achievable.

And if it’s to be a draw, then why not put more miles into the legs of the Aussie bowlers before the 3rd Test - especially with one of them already struggling?

It’s a good point, re the follow on score. I just want to see a result. If this was a four or five Test series I’d agree. Or if the first Test was drawn. But this SA team has a chance of winning the series, though they need the overs to get 20 Aussie wickets.

116th over: South Africa 371-7 (Bavuma 157, Harmer 50) Boland drops short and Harmer gets a whole lot of wood on it to pull him a dozen rows back over square leg. That shot takes him to 49, his highest Test score. He desperatrely wants his 50 and almost gets there with a sweet flick off his pads that doesn’t beat midwicket. He gets there off the last ball of the over with a little tickle off his hip. Well batted Simon Harmer, a half century in front of his home fans. Does that bring us closer to a declaration?

115th over: South Africa 363-7 (Bavuma 156, Harmer 43) A maiden from Webster to Harmer, who is eyeing up his first Test half century. His highest score is 47 against Australia. Perhaps that’s what they’re waiting for. They’re in no rush if so.

114th over: South Africa 363-7 (Bavuma 156, Harmer 43) Boland replaces Cummins. Just a pair of singles off that set. Tellingly both Rabada and Nortje still have their pads on. I’d rather see them stretching to let rip at the Australian openers in this gloom. But Bavuma and Conrad have other plans.

27 overs remain. The clouds are gathering. Temba, send the Aussies in!

113th over: South Africa 361-7 (Bavuma 155, Harmer 42) Webster’s first ball is a ‘hit-me’ long hop and Bavuma duly does, leaning back and cracking it well in front of square for four. Big Beau tightens up so Bavuma is watchful until the last ball when he bunts a single down the ground to keep the strike.

150 for Bavuma!

Just sublime. What a sensational knock this has been. Truly, one of the greatest innings by a South African skipper. He got to 149 with a scything cut for four off Cummins anf then reached another milestone with a bunted single into the covers.

112th over: South Africa 356-7 (Bavuma 150, Harmer 42)

111th over: South Africa 351-7 (Bavuma 145, Harmer 42) They keep ticking along. Two singles off this Webster over.

110th over: South Africa 349-7 (Bavuma 144, Harmer 41) The lights are on. Cummins hammers in a bumper that Harmer takes on. It arcs towards the fielder at a wide fine-leg but falls teasingly short. There’s an umpire’s review for a direct hit run-out after Renshaw shot down the stumps, but Harmer’s dive means he’s back safe for a second. Cummins digs in another bounce that Harmer swivels for a single.

What a shame we’ve lost so much time due to the rain across the first two days. I fear we might not get enough time for a result. But Test cricket does move fast. I think the only chance of something materialising is a declaration from the Saffas sooner rather than later.

109th over: South Africa 343-7 (Bavuma 142, Harmer 37) 100-run partnership between these two. What a turnaround. They were 110-6. Renshaw is flighting it up and to me it looks like Bavuma has lost his timing. A full blooded drive just about reached the man at cover. He’ll keep strike after collecting the third single of the over off the last ball.

108th over: South Africa 340-7 (Bavuma 140, Harmer 36) Things are in a bit of a holding pattern for now. Neither side really taking the game on. Cummins is banging in bouncers that harmer is ducking. Are they waiting for Bavuma to reach his 150? Just two runs off that over. When do they pull the trigger? Sure, it’s not a monster score, but they’ll want to get to the Aussie batters before the close.

107th over: South Africa 338-7 (Bavuma 139, Harmer 35) Renshaw into the attack, which is a good move from the Aussies. If the South Africans are going to look to be aggressive, the slower pace of Renshaw could create a bit of chaos. They’re watchful though across this over, showing respect as they settle on three singles.

106th over: South Africa 335-7 (Bavuma 138, Harmer 33) Bavuma throws his hands at a pull shot to Starc and misses. The he almost flings himself out of his boots as he throws his hands and the rest of his body at a full ball and misses. He finally makes contact with a plinked lofted drive, the bat turning in his hand, as it balloons over cover for two. There’s the intent from the captain. He holds his shape off the last ball as he eases a gorgeous drive down the ground for four. I predict an exciting half hour coming up.

105th over: South Africa 329-7 (Bavuma 132, Harmer 33) Boland is full to both batters, inviting the drive. After a pushed single to mid-on from Bavuma, Harmer finds the middle of his bat with a sweetly struck off-drive, but the fielder didn’t have to move. Not much zip off the surface. Looking a little benign to be honest. Calm before the storm?

104th over: South Africa 328-7 (Bavu,a 131, Harmer 33) They’re running well between the wickets. But that over is worth just three, including a leg-bye. A full toss from Starc is met by a cautious prod from Bavuma who pings it to the fielder at deep point.

103rd over: South Africa 325-7 (Bavuma 129, Harmer 33) Seven runs off that Boland over, all run. A three that was dinked off the pads by Harmer to go along with a clipped two from Bavuma and a pair of singles. This partnership is now wroth 83. Some fightback from the Saffas.

102nd over: South Africa 318-7 (Bavuma 125, Harmer 30) A maiden from Starc, round the wicket, looking to bang a length at Harmer, to start the session.

The players are back out. Do the South Africans look to press the accelerator and biff their way to a declaration? Of course a draw keeps the series alive for Cape Town, but they need a victory here if they want to win the whole thing.

And this is a pitch that has favoured the new ball. If Australia are two or three down before the close that could set things up nicely.

But that’ll all come out in the wash. Starc to get us going again.

Tea! South Africa 318-7 (Bavuma 125, Harmer 30)

An eventful last over before the break. Carey thought he’d pulled off a stunning leg side stumping to dislodge Bavuma off the bowling of Webster, but the SA captain’s back foot shot back down before the bails were removed.

Excellence from Bavuma who has been well supported by a swashbuckling Jansen and a resolute Harmer.

Back after the break.

100th over: South Africa 318-7 (Bavuma 125, Harmer 30) Another drag down from Hazlewood, another crunching pull from Harmer who climbs into this tame half-tracker and hits it well in front of the sweeper square of the wicket. Lovely shot. The Aussies look flat. This tea break can’t come soon enough.

99th over: South Africa 313-7 (Bavuma 124, Harmer 26) Less than 10 minutes to the tea break. These Saffas won’t want to lose their wicket now. Just the one run off that Webster over.

Steve Smith is receiving treatment on his finger. The camera has cut to him getting some heavy strapping. So the finger is not broken, or if it is he’s soldiering on.

98th over: South Africa 312-7 (Bavuma 123, Harmer 26) Hazlewood returns but he looks a shadow of what he was in Durban. His first ball back is a yuck half tracker that Harmer swats in front of square for four. A few balls later Bavuma is on strike and gets a thick edge that is dropped by Smith! It was a tough chance that dropped in front of him, but that is definitely a drop and he’s off the park as his finger needs attention. Could be bad news for him when he has to bat!

97th over: South Africa 306-7 (Bavuma 122, Harmer 21) Webster over pitches and Bavuma leans forward and plays a textbook perfect on drive. Phew, that should come with a parental warning sticker. That stroke also brings up the 300, a milestone that looked incomprehensible at the start of the day. But Bavuma’s not done. Another half volley, this one on his pads, is clipped through midwicket for four more.

96th over: South Africa 298-7 (Bavuma 114, Harmer 21) Cummins has found some extra zip. Perhaps a consequence of his frustrations that this South African lower order has produced the goods. His first ball is a sharp bouncer that lifts off a tricky length and has Bavuma leaping and fending without any control. Then he hits the back of Harmer’s helmet; the batter ducking and turning his head away at the last second. The four leg-byes will only irritate the Aussie skipper. After some attention from the physio, Harmer flicks Cummins through midwicket for four more. That’s a proper cricket shot and another productive over for the home side.

95th over: South Africa 289-7 (Bavuma 113, Harmer 17) A tidy over from Webster who could have had the wicket of Bavuma, if only the thick inside edge onto the back thigh was held by Carey up to the stumps. Webster thought it was lbw, and they burned a review finding out. A single to Bavuma means he’ll be on strike for the next set. Starting to wonder now what the plan is for the home side. Just bat to tea and have a think?

They’re reviewing for lbw against Bavuma. They don’t look confident. Carey, up to the stumps, isn’t sure. But worth a punt I guess, especially as Webster was certain.

Ooooh, unless there’s an inside edge this could be close.

But there is a thick inside edge. Which means, that is technically a dropped catch from Carey! But it was a half chance at best.

94th over: South Africa 288-7 (Bavuma 112, Harmer 17) Maiden from Cummins to Harmer. I wonder if this little period raises the case for Lyon’s selection. Webster, Renshaw and Maddinson have been used as support acts, but with Hazlewood unwell (which of course they couldn’t have planned for) the lack of a proper spinner has been telling. Just a thought.

93rd over: South Africa 288-7 (Bavuma 112, Harmer 17) Big Beau Webster into the attack – SA won’t mind that – and his first delivery is a tame throw down that Bavuma leans into and drives for four through the covers. When Webster drags his length back a touch there is some movement off the pitch. Another thing the South Africans, especially their bowlers, won’t mind.

92nd over: South Africa 284-7 (Bavuma 108, Harmer 17) A flick through midwicket adds two more to Bavuma’s tally. Cummins dangles a couple of tempters outside the off stump that Bavuma lets go, bringing a smile to Cummins’ face. Cummins closes the set by going wide of the crease to create a new angle, but a thick inside edge sees it bobble down to fine leg for a single.

91st over: South Africa 281-7 (Bavuma 105, Harmer 17) Hazlewood finds the thick edge of Harmer’s prodding bat with a back of a length banger, but it flies between second and fourth slip and trickles away for four. You want to scream as a bowler when that happens. This eighth wicket stand now worth 39.
