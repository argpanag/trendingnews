Delta and Kai’s samba

Last dance of the night now. We got there, gang! Australian singer and actor Delta Goodrem has relocated to the UK for the autumn, so will be hoping for a decent Strictly stint. She’s paired with homegrown pro Kai Widdrington, complete with his new hair. Flames and red lights for an intense intro. The samba is tricker than many assume and not sure Delta’s entirely nailing the technique but she’s got Brazilian bounce, great leg action and loose hips. Loads of movement around the floor with real swagger and Latin fire. Woof.

Song: Baianá by Barbatuques. The Brazilian group’s 2005 body percussion song is a traditional samba favourite which has been much sampled and remixed.

Judges’ scores: 7, 8 (first of the year), 8, 8 for a total of 31 points. Top of the table.

Judges’ comments: Anton says “I’m a bit stunned, extraordinary, reminded me of Darren Gough who went on to win, just saying.” Craig says “lacked swing and sway but classy, elegant and graceful”. Motsi says “a difficult dance for your first one and best of the night for me”. Shirley concludes “very sophisticated, spiffing, storytelling and ease of movement, unexpected but best of the night gor me too”. Sevens on their way?

John and Katya’s foxtrot

For the night’s penultimate dance, football content creator John Nellis and Russian pro Katya Jones slow things right down for the night’s only foxtrot. Graceful Hollywood glamour. I’m pleasantly surprised by this. Posture and hold could improve but clean footwork, strong partnering skills and gliding elegantly. Romantic and really rather lovely.

Song: Ordinary by Alex Warren. The American singer’s soppy breakthrough hit was one of last year’s biggest tunes, spending 13 non-consecutive weeks on top of the UK singles chart. It soundtracked a wafty number by the Strictly pros last series.

Judges’ scores: 6, 5, 5, 6 for a total of 22 points. Endearingly, Dani is “over the moon just to be dancing here”. Joint fourth with two dances remaining.

Judges’ comments: Motsi says “hello sir” to Danny Snr in the audience but tells Dani to “handled the speed and content but work on your feet”. Shirley says “aced the personality, brought the party, pocket rocket, be more solid downstairs but moved your tush”. Anton says “flung your limbs about but otherwise great”. Craig concludes “turned-in feet bothered me but great hip rolls and isolation”.

Dani and Nikita’s samba

Time for the reunited couple, after their false start last year when Dani Dyer was an injury dropout. She’s delighted to be back with Ukrainian pro Nikita Kuzmin and they’re celebrating with a rhythmical Rio carnival dance. Wobbly opening but then into a banging, hip-shaking tropical number. Supple, fast spins, strong synchronisation and musicality. Has she got the requisite bounce and samba rolls? Throwing away the arms and losing timing in place but upbeat, clubby and cracking entertainment.

Song: Lush Life by Zara Larsson. The Swedish singer’s 2015 electropop banger was widely compared to Rihanna with its dancehall-tinged island groove.

Judges’ scores: 5, 5, 5, 6 for a total of 21 points. Mid-table. “I’m a rule-breaker by nature,” admits Jaime. “Grateful not to get twos across the board.”

Judges’ comments: Anton says “a great number, moved well, work on your topline but the rest looked a million dollars”. Craig says “quite like the disco/tango fusion but your posture and frame really let you down, it didn’t entirely win me over”. Motsi says “beautiful movement, natural and true but like Cach, learn the rules before you break them, you are fire”. Shirley concludes “great performance, power and musicality, fix the frame, elongate, you’re gifted but show more quality”.

Jaime and Carlos’ tango

Self-styled raver Jaime Winstone was thrilled to be paired with reigning pro champion Carlos Gu, saying their “energies matched”. Can the hyperactive, high-volume couple channel it into a controlled, passionate tango? White furs for a Studio 54-style disco tango. Stalking promenades, flat across at the floor, with sharp swivels. Bit of gapping in hold, which could be more compact. Lacking clean finish points and staccato clip but cracking choreography from Carlos, charismatically handled by Jaime.

Song: I Feel Love by Donna Summer. The hugely influential 1977 disco classic has somehow only been used for Strictly group routines before, never a couple’s dance. Legend has it that Brian Eno ran into their Berlin recording studio and told David Bowie that he’d “heard the sound of the future”.

Judges’ scores: 6, 6, 6, 6 for a total of 24 points. Number of the beast and then some. Third place. “Good boy,” says Lauren.

Judges’ comments: Shirley says “full of content, you’re an amazing student, entertained us and even made Craig smile”. Anton says “old-school style, a proper cha cha, really super”. Craig says “made me laugh and I can’t work out why, great attention to detail, absolutely loved it”. Motsi concludes “love your vibe” and leads the crowd in a chorus of the Baha Men. Woofs continue on the balcony.

Graeme and Lauren’s cha cha cha

Canine expert Graeme Hall said pre-series “Sooner or later, I have to dance to a dog song, right?” Well, they’re getting it out of the way early. He’s done a bit of ballroom before, so could be a dark horse. Homegrown pro Lauren Oakley secretly has high hopes. Swaggeringly confident in the solo sections. Lots of Cuban fun with hip action, basic steps and synchronised movements. Flirt factor is low but the fun factor is high. Loses timing briefly but wiggles his booty and wags his tail. “Who let the dogs out, darling?” as Craig Revel Horwood drawled last week.

Song: Hound Dog (Smokey Joe’s Café version). Elvis Presley’s version of the 1950s blue classic sold 10m copies globally and was his bestselling single. Smokey Joe’s Café is the longest-running musical revue in Broadway history, clocking ip 2,036 performances between 1995 and 2000.

Judges’ scores: 3, 6, 5, 6 for a total of 20 points. Mid-table. Jojo tells Cach he’s gorgeous to soften the blow.

Judges’ comments: Craig says “too much freestyle for me, not enough quickstep and posture not great”. Motsi says “music lives in your body and your musicality was genius, learn the ballroom rules so you can break them, do the fiunademtnals but bring who you are to the dancefloor”. Shirley says “extremely light on your feet, natural feel but I agree with Craig, it was streetdance not quickstep but you’re gifted”. Anton concludes “terrific, this was TikTok era, sort your posture and hold but you’re a talent”.

Cach and Maddie’s quickstep

Oh hello, he’s here. Love Islander and influencer Cach Mercer is an experienced dancer, albeit self-taught and not versed in Latin and ballroom, so he has high expectations. Paired with new home-grown pro Maddie Ingoldsby, he certainly looks the part for this wedding-themed number. Too much messin’ abaht to start, then into hold for a ripping routine. Energetic yet elegant, light on their feet and full of frothy fun. Disco moves, leaps and dips. Charleston sections strong, leading well, strong finish. Wow.

Song: I Just Might by Bruno Mars. The retro disco-funk hit from this January was compared to both Leo Sayer’s You Make Me Feel Like Dancing and Junior Senior’s Move Your Feet.

Judges’ scores: 2 (what’s occurring?), 4, 4, 5 for a total of 15 points. Joint bottom with the two sportspeople.
