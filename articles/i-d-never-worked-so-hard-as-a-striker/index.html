“I’d never worked so hard as a striker…”

The FC Porto forward believes his style of play meets the team's demands, but acknowledges that the role has required him to work harder than ever before, including in the team's defensive organisation. As he returns to Feyenoord for a Champions League match, his affection for his former club sits alongside his desire to win and score for Porto, while keeping one thought firmly in mind: “I'm a team player and I don't like to think individually.”

His return to Rotterdam brings together two sides of the player's career: the bond he built at Feyenoord and the responsibilities he now shoulders in an FC Porto shirt. Between memories of a special spell and the demands of the present, the forward keeps his contribution to the team at the heart of his message.

Asked whether his style suits FC Porto's needs, he expressed confidence in what he can offer. At the same time, he acknowledged that playing up front involves much more than looking for goals.

“I believe so, otherwise I wouldn't be here. I can make that contribution to the team. I've never worked so hard as a forward, but I want to keep giving everything for the team,” he said. “I'm a team player and I don't like to think individually. A forward's job is very demanding and important, and here I've also come to understand how important it is to the team's defensive organisation.”

His answer combines confidence in his own qualities with an awareness of the increased demands. Defensive work emerges as an essential part of the role, reflecting a view of the game in which a forward is not judged solely on what he does near the opposition goal.

That commitment to the present goes with him on a trip full of personal significance. Returning to Feyenoord brings back fond memories, but it does not change his objective when he takes to the pitch for FC Porto.

“It's incredible that my first Champions League game after leaving was here, and that I'm now back again. My time at this club was very good and special, so it's a pleasure to return,” he explained. “This time, I'm back in an FC Porto shirt and I hope to win. If possible, I also want to score, because that's what football and the Champions League are all about. I'm still in touch with some of my former teammates who are still here.”

When the question turned to what he misses most about Rotterdam, his answer moved away from results and goals. He returned to the fans and the relationship that had also embraced his family.

“The bond I built with the fans. Not just me, but my family too,” he revealed.

That same bond came up when he chose his best memory of his time at Feyenoord. To sum it up in one match, he singled out his home farewell against Bayern Munich.

“The bond I built with the fans. If I have to choose one game, I'd pick the match against Bayern Munich, which was my last at home,” he recalled. “It was very special to win, score two goals and say goodbye that way.”

The emotional significance of his return lies as much in the people as in a farewell marked by a win and two goals. Now, his desire to score again comes in a different shirt: his gratitude towards Feyenoord remains, but his ambition lies with FC Porto.
