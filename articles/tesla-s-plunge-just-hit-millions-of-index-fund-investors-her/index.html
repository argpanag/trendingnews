Tesla's Plunge Just Hit Millions of Index Fund Investors. Here's How Much Your ETF Lost

TheTools · 05.11.2026 16:03 · source · permalink

You never bought a single Tesla share, yet your retirement account still felt Thursday's selloff. One popular sector fund carries far more exposure than anyone expects, and overlapping funds can quietly multiply the risk.

The ETF Examiner desk. Editor: Ryne Mauck.

This post may contain links from our sponsors and affiliates, and Flywheel Publishing may receive compensation for actions taken through them.

Tesla (NASDAQ:TSLA | TSLA Price Prediction) fell 9.46% in Thursday’s regular session, slipping to $348.74 as of 10:00 AM ET from $385.19. That works out to roughly $36.45 per share. Plenty of retirement savers who have never bought a Tesla share took part in that loss anyway, because the stock sits inside the index funds that anchor most 401(k) and IRA accounts.

The drop came on a second straight day of broad tech and AI-linked selling after Democrats swept the midterms, as investors priced in more regulation of AI and data center expansion. On Wednesday, headlines reported that Tesla and SpaceX shares slid after Democrats swept Congress and Musk-backed candidates lost in Texas. Tesla put out no company press release Thursday, and no same-day source points to a Tesla-specific catalyst. The stock is now down 13.73% over the past week and 22.45% year to date.

How Much Tesla Your ETF Actually Owns

Tesla shows up in three major fund categories, and the size of the position decides how much of Thursday’s damage reached you. The last column uses each fund’s latest reported Tesla weight to estimate how much of Thursday’s decline came from Tesla alone.

Broad Index Funds Absorbed the Blow

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (NYSEARCA:SPY) holds Tesla at 1.87%, well behind NVIDIA at 7.58% and Apple at 6.66%. SPY fell 1.81%, and Tesla explains only a small piece of that. Most of the S&P 500 losses came from the wider tech selloff.

The Invesco QQQ Trust (NASDAQ:QQQ) reported 38,410,623 Tesla shares, worth $16.16 billion, out of $490.1 billion in net assets. QQQ dropped 3.89% Thursday and is down 12.06% over the past month, so Nasdaq-100 holders are taking losses on several fronts at once.

Sector Funds Carry Far More Tesla Risk

The Consumer Discretionary Select Sector SPDR Fund (NYSEARCA:XLY) held $4.44 billion of Tesla against $22.59 billion in net assets. Only Amazon, at 22.22%, is larger. Between them, those two stocks make up about 41.9% of the fund.

Based on those weights, Tesla made up a significant portion of XLY’s Thursday decline. A sector fund that sounds diversified across retailers, restaurants and travel names is, on days like this, largely a Tesla-and-Amazon fund.

Overlap Risk Hiding in Your 401(k)

Overlap builds up quietly. Say a saver splits money equally across SPY, QQQ and XLY. Tesla then makes up about 8.3% of the combined portfolio, more than four times its weight in the S&P 500 fund alone. Each fund looks reasonable by itself. The concentration only shows up once you add them together.

Fund documents spell out the risk. An iShares prospectus warns that a fund “may be susceptible to an increased risk of loss” to the extent its investments are concentrated in one or more issuers.

Tesla also swings harder than most index giants. The stock trades at a P/E of about 401. Third-quarter operating income fell to $718 million from $1.62 billion as R&D rose to $2.76 billion. The CFO said on the October call, “We’re in a huge investment cycle to set us up for the future.”

What to Check in Your Own Accounts Now

  • Look-through holdings: Use your plan’s portfolio analyzer to see your total Tesla exposure across every fund, target-date funds included.
  • Sector fund weights: Look up the top 10 holdings of any sector or growth fund you own. A few names can cause most of the returns.
  • Holdings dates: Published weights lag. After a 22.45% drop this year, Tesla’s current weights are probably below the reported figures.
  • Allocation drift: Compare where your tech and consumer exposure stands today with the target you set.

Coming up for Tesla: management said it would launch Robotaxi service in Dallas the month after the October call and is planning to roll out Cybercabs “by the end of this year, 2026.” For index fund holders, the more pressing question is whether the post-election tech selling spreads, since that will move SPY and QQQ far more than any one stock.
