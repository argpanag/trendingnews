Synopsis

A cold front has settled south of the islands today. A gusty north wind is bringing in a cooler and drier airmass. Dry trade wind weather is forecast through Saturday, then deeper moisture may return early next week bringing better rain chances.

Discussion

The front has cleared the island chain today. Scattered clouds and showers in the cyclonic flow are slowly dissipating this afternoon as drier air works in on gusty north winds. A few showers may remain into early Friday on north facing slopes, but the rest of the area should remain dry. For lower elevations, expect cooler temperatures and low humidity to continue into Saturday morning with daytime temperatures in the mid to upper 70s and morning low temperatures in the upper 50s to mid 60s.
The base of a 100 kt mid level jet is situated over the state this afternoon which is resulting in strong winds on the Big Island summits. The strongest winds are on Mauna Kea where the summit has been measuring frequent gusts of 100-120 mph. The jet will slide off to the east this evening and winds should begin subsiding during the next few hours before dropping below warning criteria late tonight. The High Wind Warning continues until 3 AM.
A surface high will build in from the northwest behind the front and winds will gradually veer northeasterly on Friday, then moderate easterly trades will return for the weekend. A weak trough breaks through the ridge Sunday and Monday. Latest model runs indicate a piece may break off and become a cutoff low west of the state early next week. This would result in deeper moisture advecting northward and increasing rain chances.

Aviation

Northwest winds behind a cold front have been gusty today, and this should continue well into tonight. Scattered showers will continue through tonight, with isolated MVFR conditions. Otherwise, the area will have VFR conditions Friday.
AIRMET Tango remains in effect for low level turbulence associated with the gusty northwest winds behind the front, and also for upper level turbulence due to a strong jet moving overhead. The strong jet will produce widespread moderate turbulence in the upper levels and areas of severe turbulence today into tonight between FL200 to FL380.
SIGMET OSCAR for severe turbulence aloft continues over a large area surrounding the state.

Marine

High pressure will build northwest of the islands today through the weekend as a cold front quickly exits the region to the east later today. Strong NW to N winds have been observed today and winds are expected to veer to the NE and weaken tonight into the weekend as the front moves further east and the high pressure develops NW of the area. An extra large NW swell will continue to impact coastal waters and N facing shores through tonight as it declines. A Small Craft Advisory (SCA) remains in effect for all Hawaiian coastal waters, covering advisory-level winds, seas greater than 10 feet, or a combination of the two. As winds decrease tonight, they should drop below SCA criteria, however seas 10 ft or greater may linger a little longer, so a partial extension of the SCA is possible later tonight. Winds could increase and veer further to east on Monday as the high strengthens and moves closer to the State. Around that same time, an upper level low could increase showers and possibly isolated thunderstorms for the western waters.
An extra-large north-northwest (330 to 350 degree) swell will continue bringing extra-large to giant warning-level surf to north and west- facing shores. The Waimea Bay buoy was hovering around 16 to 18 feet this afternoon although the peak period has been dropping throughout the day. Warning level surf is likely to continue overnight and possibly linger into Friday as it gradually decreases. Therefore, a High Surf Warning is in effect for north and west-facing shores of Niihau, Kauai, Oahu, Molokai, and north facing shores of Maui and the Big Island. The swell will fade further through the weekend. A small to moderate northwest swell could arrive by Monday followed by another by the middle of next week.
Surf heights along east-facing shores will be somewhat elevated through Friday due to wrapping from the north-northwest swell, but are expected to remain below advisory levels. As the north- northwest swell fades, a medium-period northeast swell generated by a storm force low to the northeast of the islands will keep surf elevated for east-facing shores Friday and Saturday. More typical, short-period trade wind swell will return by Monday. Surf along south-facing shores will remain tiny to small through the next few days, with a small bump possible this Saturday through Sunday.

Fire weather

Breezy north winds and drier air with dew points in the 50s is leading to elevated fire weather concerns this afternoon. It will remain cool and dry through Saturday with winds gradually veering northeast. Inversion heights range from 7,000 to 8,000 feet this afternoon.
