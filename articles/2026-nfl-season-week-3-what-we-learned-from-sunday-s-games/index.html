Nick Shook's takeaways:

  1. Bills have plenty to clean up after win. Buffalo did everything in its power to hand Sunday's game to the visiting Chargers, who were the more aggressive and disciplined team in the first half. By most football standards, the Bills should have lost: five turnovers and 10 penalties (for 88 yards) are a recipe for a lopsided defeat, and Josh Allen was not his usual superstar self for most of the day. Most of the usual standouts had their ugly moments. James Cook fumbled on the opening possession, Allen threw a shockingly ugly interception, had another forced-into-traffic shot result in an interception, tight end Dalton Kincaid fumbled away possession on a long catch and run, and Gregory Rousseau and Bradley Chubb committed roughing the passer penalties to extend Chargers drives. Fortunately, a timely interception and a wise pivot back toward their Pro Bowl running back helped the Bills overcome their self-inflicted wounds and emerge from Week 3 with their third straight win to open the season. After two weeks of explosive offense that at times seemed unstoppable, Buffalo looked more mortal than mythical and won't be as fortunate against better opponents.
  2. Chargers suffer a demoralizing defeat. The Bolts arrived in Buffalo with their backs against the wall after an 0-2 start and began Sunday's contest with a sense of urgency. Defensively, the Chargers were flying around the field while clogging Buffalo's high-powered offense and swung the game's early portion in their favor by intercepting Allen twice. But the signs of their eventual disappointment appeared shortly after their trio of first half takeaways: After an interception in Buffalo territory, the Chargers gained 15 yards and settled for a field goal. The second interception set up a 63-yard drive that ended in a missed field goal. Three takeaways (including James Cook's early fumble) produced just 10 points, which was never going to be enough against these Bills, who scored just before halftime to tie a one-sided game entering the break. From there, the Chargers reverted to their previous form, committing penalties, failing to finish drives and turning it over twice in the fourth quarter (a Justin Herbert interception and a turnover on downs). The meltdown was unsurprising and the latest example of why Chargers fans feel as if their team is doomed. They'd landed a right hook on the Bills in the first half and played well enough to win for two quarters, but collapsed again for their third straight loss.
  3. Cook remains elite. When Joe Brady rose to offensive coordinator in Buffalo, a commitment to the running game followed, propelling James Cook into the top tier of NFL running backs. It's remained an essential part of the Bills' offense since then, and was just as important Sunday. Cook effectively sealed Sunday's win in the fourth quarter, gaining 77 of his 154 rushing yards and his rushing touchdown on 11 carries in the final period. With Cook gaining chunks of yards, Buffalo swung the game's time of possession battle much closer to even in the fourth, holding the ball for more than nine minutes and methodically burying the Chargers by winning up front -- an area in which the Chargers had previously dominated -- and leaving the rest for Cook to handle. Over his last two games, Cook has rushed for 289 yards and two touchdowns. Both have been home victories that required the Bills to finish strong. Luckily, they know they can turn to No. 4 to get that job done.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Chargers-Bills (via NFL Pro): After throwing as many completions (two) as interceptions (two) on his first four drives, Josh Allen completed 14 of his final 20 passes for 195 yards.

NFL Research: The Bills turned the ball over five times in Week 3 versus the Chargers, the most in any regular season start by Josh Allen (Bills had five turnovers in 2025 Divisional Round loss at the Broncos). It’s the Bills’ first win when committing at least five turnovers since 1997, having lost each of their last 18 such games, and their eight-point victory is tied for the largest by any team to commit at least five turnovers in a game in the last 20 seasons.

Nick Shook's takeaways:

  1. Browns defense shines again. After a horrid Week 1 showing, Cleveland bounced back significantly as a defense in Week 2. It might have been the sign of a reliable trend. Cleveland's defense stifled Bryce Young and the Panthers all afternoon, generating a pressure rate of 51% while blitzing on less than 30 percent of defensive snaps. It all starts with the Browns' defensive front. Jared Verse led the way with six QB pressures (including three quick pressures), Derek Barnett added five QB pressures, and Mason Graham had himself a career day, piling up two sacks, including one that came at a crucial moment in the game's final two minutes. With the front feasting, the remaining defenders were free to converge on pass catchers, forcing Carolina to punt three times and three turnovers on downs. It's familiar territory for Browns fans, who have grown accustomed to watching former defensive coordinator Jim Schwartz produce masterful game plans that suffocated most offenses on a weekly basis, while simultaneously hoping Cleveland's offense could muster enough lengthy drives to give it a break and possibly produce some points. This time, though, these performances are coming under the direction of new DC Mike Rutenberg, whose unit is playing excellent ball in the last two games.
  2. Young takes step back without favorite target. Bryce Young's start to the 2026 season was electric and gave the Panthers reason to believe he'd finally blossom into the franchise quarterback they've waited to see since selecting him No. 1 overall in 2023. Sunday didn't match that sentiment, especially after go-to receiver Jalen Coker left with a quad injury. Under constant duress from a ferocious Cleveland defense, Young was unsettled for most of the afternoon and was frequently subjected to crushing blows delivered by Browns pass rushers. He appeared to tweak his leg early in the contest but played through it, battling to put together drives that largely ended in field goals. In the most crucial moment, Young briefly found his form of the previous two weeks, going 3 for 3 for 42 yards and a go-ahead touchdown. The final two possessions, however, saw more of the same struggles — missed targets under pressure, hesitance to throw. The Panthers will need their quarterback to be better going forward than he was Sunday, but they also need to protect him better.
  3. Watson is ... a decent quarterback? For two straight weeks, the Browns have faced fourth-quarter deficits and have overcome them with touchdown drives in the final six minutes. Both scoring marches required above-average execution from Deshaun Watson, who confidently led Cleveland to two victories by completing passes under duress, extending plays, occasionally scrambling for positive yards, and executing in pressure-packed situations. On Sunday, he delivered once again. Facing third-and-10 from Carolina's 14-yard line and in need of a touchdown, Watson dropped to pass, stepped up and out of the pocket to the left, rolled away from his throwing arm and spotted a crossing Harold Fannin Jr. in the end zone, ripping an off-platform bullet to Fannin for the decisive score. The score capped a familiar fourth-quarter story. Watson turns to rookie Denzel Boston when he needs a completion most (he found Boston on the ensuing two-point conversion to push Cleveland's lead to three), using his legs to pick up key yards and firing accurately with the game on the line. It followed a forgettable first half and third quarter (save for Watson's fourth-and-goal touchdown pass to Fannin), which is not surprising. What's more stunning is that Watson has managed to rediscover his clutch gene, which seemed lost to the sands of time, in the most important moments.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Panthers-Browns (via NFL Pro): The Browns' 51% pressure rate is their third-highest in a game since 2024. Led by Jared Verse (who finished with a team-high six pressures), nine different Browns defenders generated multiple pressures on the day.

NFL Research: After back-to-back wins over the Buccaneers and Panthers, the Browns are now 14-13 versus the NFC South since their reactivation as a franchise in 1999 -- the only division Cleveland has a winning record against in that span.

Highlights from the Week 3 matchup between the New York Jets and the Detroit Lions during the 2026 NFL season.

Kevin Patra's takeaways:

  1. Gibbs, Lions offense powers win over Jets. Jahmyr Gibbs did his best Barry Sanders impression, carrying Detroit to a win with jukes galore. The stud running back scored three second-half touchdowns, starting with a 17-yard against-the-grain jaunt in which No. 0 pulled off a No. 20 imitation. Gibbs was a mismatch against the Jets' older linebackers in the passing attack, repeatedly finding space in the open field. He finished with 99 rushing yards and two scores on 20 carries, plus seven catches for 65 yards and the game-winning TD. Gibbs’ ability to gash defenses on the outside while also muscling for short-yardage games shows complete-back ability. His talent as a pass catcher makes him unstoppable. Powered by Gibbs, the Lions offense moved up and down the field for the final three quarters, scoring on five of six drives. They avoided a three-and-out all day while putting up 30-plus points for the third time in three weeks to open the season.
  2. Jets battle but come up short. Geno Smith and the offense needed time to kick things into gear. After going three-and-out in two of the first three drives, Smith diced up the Lions secondary. Displaying good pocket acumen and a willingness to sit in and take a hit, Smith threw for 321 yards on 31 of 37 with three TDs. The Jets took advantage of their tight end matchups. Rookie Kenyon Sadiq had the best game of his young career, proving to be a good outlet with good hands and the ability to dust LBs. He finished with 105 yards and his first career receiving TD on seven catches. Sacks stymied the Jets offense early, but the offensive line cleaned things up in the second half, allowing the Jets to tie the game late after falling behind 14 -0. Then the Lions threw a big blitz, and Chuck Clark got away clean and sacked Smith, who fumbled with a chance to score late. Once again, the Jets showed they can compete. The next step is turning close losses into actual wins.
  3. Lions D makes a late play after struggling to slow the Jets. Kelvin Sheppard dialed up a blitz that confused the Jets' blocking and allowed Clark to come up with the game-sealing strip. It’s a good thing Chuck Clark made the play, because Detroit’s defense once again showed an inability to get a stop in the second half. With defensive backs getting burned so often, the pass rush rarely gets a chance to get to the quarterback. When the defensive line is blocked up, it’s almost assuredly a big play for the opponent. Former Jet D.J. Reed continues to struggle, getting picked on and racking up defensive interference calls. Reed versus Garrett Wilson was a clear mismatch. When a team's most veteran CB is a liability, it’s going to be a problem. At this stage, the defense’s hope is to get just enough stops while the offense keeps racking up points. It’s a defense that requires a near-perfect offense to rack up Ws. 

Next Gen Stats Insight for Lions-Jets (via NFL Pro): The Jets blitzed Jared Goff on 44.4% of his dropbacks, with Goff completing all 14 of his attempts for 148 yards and a touchdown when facing extra rushers (+27.8% CPOE). Goff also fared well on play-action, completing 11 of 13 attempts for 181 yards.

NFL Research: With his two rushing touchdowns in Week 3, Jahmyr Gibbs (43) passed Billy Sims (42) for the second-most rushing touchdowns in Lions franchise history, trailing only Hall of Famer Barry Sanders (99). Gibbs also recorded his fifth career game with three touchdowns, tying HOF Jim Brown and Todd Gurley for the most three-plus TD games by a player before turning 25 in NFL history. His seven career games with 150-plus scrimmage yards and multiple scrimmage touchdowns trail only Hall of Famers Jim Brown (11), Barry Sanders (nine) and Walter Payton (eight) for most by a player under the age of 25 in NFL history.

Highlights from the Week 3 matchup between the Houston Texans and the Indianapolis Colts during the 2026 NFL season.

REWATCH: Texans-Colts on NFL+ Premium

FULL BOX SCORE

Bobby Kownack's takeaways:

  1. Indy snaps pair of losing streaks. Sunday’s win exorcised a few demons for the Colts, who had lost nine in a row since last season -- a string of defeats dating back to before Daniel Jones tore his Achilles. The Colts also ended a five-game losing streak to the Texans. All five of those losses had come in one-possession games, so -- perhaps just for good measure -- the Colts finally pulled out a tight one over their AFC South rivals. Even after looking like the better team for most of the day, it didn’t come easy. The Colts blew a nine-point fourth-quarter lead, only to rebound with a 29-yard two-minute drill to set up a 53-yard game-winning boot by Spencer Shrader, the kicker’s second make of 50-plus in the game despite tweaking his groin. There is a lot to clean up after Jones committed three turnovers, bringing his giveaways through three games to five, but the QB also did a lot of heavy lifting with Jonathan Taylor (23 carries for 68 yards) too often swallowed up by Houston’s defense. Jones was able to do just enough with the Colts’ big three pass catchers now that Alec Pierce is out for the foreseeable future: Josh Downs, Keenan Allen and Tyler Warren combined for 30 of his 36 targets, 20 receptions, 195 yards and a touchdown. Hopefully Indianapolis can carry the momentum of finally finding the win column into next week.
  2. Texans in familiar trouble. The Texans defense did its darndest to will Houston to a win. The D set up C.J. Stroud and Co.  at Indy’s 40-yard line with a Will Anderson Jr. strip-sack on the Colts’ opening possession, only for the Texans offense to turn the ball over on downs in four plays. It was just the beginning of a stellar performance by Anderson that would go wasted, as the edge rusher logged nine pressures and 2.5 sacks. Reed Blankenship later intercepted Daniel Jones at the 1-yard line with Indy driving in the third quarter, and Danielle Hunter made a Texans go-ahead drive possible by forcing another Jones fumble near midfield with 4 minutes and 40 seconds remaining. That time the offense capitalized by going 54 yards to take the lead, after which the defense uncharacteristically gave it away for good, but this loss is not on Houston’s defenders. The offense slogged through the game for far too long. Houston simply can’t seem to get it going in September under DeMeco Ryans. Already 0-2 for the third time in four years, the Texans dropped to 0-3 on Sunday for a second consecutive season. The caveat, of course, is that Ryans’ previous teams have been able to dig themselves out of it. Only seven teams since 2023 have started 0-2 and made the playoffs, a group that includes the ’23 and ’25 Texans. Only seven teams in NFL history have ever reached the postseason from 0-3, with Houston joining that group last season. Is another turnaround possible? It’ll help if the Texans get Nico Collins (hamstring) back soon, but that side of the ball must play far, far better. The margin for error is now almost nonexistent.
  3. Colts defense finally shows up. Indianapolis’ defense two weeks into the season was a travesty. The Colts entered Sunday’s game against the Texans having allowed an NFL-worst 74 points and 1,029 yards -- the second-most yards allowed through two games of a season in league history, per NFL Research. It was a far better performance against Houston. The Colts completely dominated the first half, allowing only 67 yards. Although the Texans would get a bit more on track to finish with 223 total yards, this was still a huge step forward for defensive coordinator Lou Anarumo’s group. Indy held David Montgomery and Woody Marks each to 3.0 yards per carry and limited C.J. Stroud to 167 yards through the air. In the first and third quarters combined, the Colts allowed only 27 yards across five possessions, forcing the Texans to scramble for points at the end of both halves. Indianapolis also prevented the Texans from getting into field-goal range after inheriting a two-point lead with 16 seconds remaining. Frequently thrust into defending following sudden-change situations, the Colts D held up for the first time this year.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Texans-Colts (via NFL Pro): Daniel Jones averaged more yards per attempt under pressure (7.7) than from a clean pocket (5.7) in the Colts' Week 3 win over the Texans.

NFL Research: The Colts defeated the Texans despite turning the ball over three times and generating zero takeaways themselves. It is the first time the Colts have won a game with a minus-three or worse turnover margin since Week 15, 1999 versus Washington. Indianapolis had lost 28 straight games in which it lost the turnover margin by three or more.

Highlights from the Week 3 matchup between the New England Patriots and the Jacksonville Jaguars during the 2026 NFL season.

Dan Parr's takeaways

  1. Jaguars dominated. Jacksonville never trailed on Sunday, and it didn’t relent when New England’s miscues piled up. It seems a healthy sign for the Jags that they didn’t simply survive against the sloppy Patriots – they buried the reigning AFC champions. The offense bounced back after disappearing in the second half against the Broncos last week, with Trevor Lawrence throwing touchdown passes to three different players. Lawrence now has 105 career passing TDs, surpassing Blake Bortles for second in franchise history. It was particularly encouraging to see the offensive line perform much better than it did against Denver. The Pats were down some starters on defense, and Lawrence did throw an interception for the second week in a row, but Jacksonville had put the game out of reach by the third quarter. Liam Coen has a legitimate chance to be the first head coach to lead the Jaguars to consecutive playoff appearances in the current millennium.
  2. Nightmarish day for Maye. The vibes around Drake Maye have not been lower since he was drafted third overall in 2024. He threw two interceptions, including one in the end zone that killed a Patriots drive. Also, he lost a fumble when the ball slipped out of his hand as he reached back to pass in the first quarter. The Jaguars scored a TD on the ensuing possession and New England never recovered, suffering its most lopsided loss since Mike Vrabel was hired as head coach prior to the 2025 season. In a sign of just how bad things were on Sunday, Vrabel sat Maye for the second half of the fourth quarter, inserting backup Tommy DeVito at quarterback with the game out of reach. A.J. Brown (ankle) is still weeks away from returning and a trip to face the division-leading Bills awaits, so things are not about to get easier for Vrabel’s squad.
  3. Hunter is on the board. Travis Hunter, the second overall pick of the 2025 NFL Draft, finally has his first career interception, picking off Drake Maye in the second quarter on a pass to the end zone intended for Efton Chism III. It’s been a bumpy ride for Hunter in the NFL due, in large part, to the injury that ended his rookie year in Week 7 and the team's struggle to maximize his ability as a two-way player. He played a season-high 42 snaps on defense in Week 3, and perhaps Sunday’s INT will be a new beginning of sorts for the franchise’s massive investment.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Patriots-Jaguars (via NFL Pro): The Jaguars generated pressure on 58.4% of Drake Maye's dropbacks, the third-highest pressure rate he has faced in the regular season. Against pressure, Maye went 6 of 11 for 81 yards and an interception. Without pressure, he went 8 of 14 for 118 yards and an interception. 

NFL Research: Drake Maye’s seven turnovers are the most by a Patriots player in the team's first three games of a season over the last 30 years.

Highlights from the Week 3 matchup between the Kansas City Chiefs and Miami Dolphins during the 2026 NFL season.

Bobby Kownack's takeaways:

  1. Chiefs score ho-hum win. Although Kansas City hardly felt in danger of losing, this was more of a slow-developing victory for the Chiefs than their fans probably would’ve liked. Patrick Mahomes found completions pretty much wherever he wanted, going 20 of 24 for 246 yards, two touchdowns and a tipped interception, but it didn’t result in opening up a sizeable lead until late. The pick was partly to blame for that, as was a turnover on downs at midfield during the drive preceding it. And while it’s difficult to harp on flaws after a defense forced two turnovers and only allowed 10 points, the group’s performance didn’t feel dominant in a way that the Week 1 victory over the Broncos was. The Chiefs let the Dolphins hang around until late, allowing numerous extended drives even if several ultimately proved fruitless. Mansoor Delane did look good coming off his shoulder injury. He had 10 tackles and a pass deflection while allowing just 24 yards on nine targets. Nick Bolton was also all over the place with a game-high 11 tackles, and Chris Jones tipped a Malik Willis pass in the air for a George Karlaftis interception to end any hopes of a Miami comeback. Perhaps wanting more after a double-digit win is a sign the Super Bowl-contending Chiefs are back, though.
  2. Fins hang tough on both sides. The final score does not indicate just how hard the Dolphins fought to stay in the game until the very end. Kansas City didn’t pull ahead by more than one possession until just under three minutes remained. Although the Dolphins offense scored under 14 points for a third straight week, the unit represented itself well while undermanned due to injuries. The team’s leading receiver, Caleb Douglas, missed Sunday’s action due to an ankle injury, and running back De'Von Achane injured his knee on Miami’s first drive, but Miami nonetheless was able to sustain a handful of lengthy marches. On the same possession Achane went down, the Dolphins went 91 yards on 17 plays for a touchdown. Miami’s first three possessions in the second half also took at least nine plays. Even if it didn’t result in a win, the Dolphins’ ability to play keep-away from Patrick Mahomes -- winning the time of possession by nearly nine minutes and ending with just 5 fewer total net yards than the Chiefs -- was admirable. Malik Willis was a bit more open to rushing than the previous two weeks with 56 yards on nine carries, which helped keep Kansas City off-balance. The QB also got Chris Bell as involved as he’s been yet, hitting the rookie four times for a team-leading 67 yards. Defensively, the Dolphins buckled down after looking anemic allowing two straight TD drives to open the game. They didn’t allow another point from Kansas City until the fourth quarter, keeping the score in reach with a forced turnover on downs and Jacob Rodriguez’ first career interception. It’s still a loss for the 0-3 Dolphins, but the process looked promising for a rebuilding squad.
  3. KWIII continues to deliver. It wasn’t as electric as his two 100-yard rushing games to kick off his Chiefs tenure, but Kenneth Walker III was again the sparkplug for Kansas City’s offense against the Dolphins. He scored the team’s first two touchdowns, a 10-yard run up the gut of Miami’s defense and a 5-yard reception on an underhand toss from Patrick Mahomes. Walker did surprisingly get stuffed on a fourth down in the second quarter and was held to a season-low 3.9 yards per carry, but a lot of credit belongs to Miami’s penetration. He was still making plenty of his opportunities, with three runs of 10-plus yards and 16 rushing yards over expected as he finished with 70 yards on 18 attempts. That brings the tough-running Walker to 360 rushing yards already on the season, with another 82 receiving. He has completely changed the complexion of the Chiefs offense in just three short games.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Chiefs-Dolphins (via NFL Pro): Kenneth Walker forced 12 missed tackles in the running game against the Dolphins, the third time that Walker forced 12 or more missed tackles on carries in a game this season.

NFL Research: Patrick Mahomes picked up his 98th career win, breaking a tie with Tom Brady for the third-most quarterback wins in a player’s first 10 seasons since quarterback starts were tracked in 1950. The only quarterbacks with more than Mahomes are Peyton Manning (105) and Russell Wilson (104), with Mahomes still having 14 games remaining in his 10th season.

Jeremy Bergman's takeaways:

  1. Giants lean on ground game in slog. In its first game without Jaxson Dart after the starter's regular season-ending knee injury, Big Blue was shut out of the end zone – but not out of the win column. New York was the better team for three quarters Sunday, maintaining possession in wet conditions at the Meadowlands (34:26 to 25:34). Jameis Winston was not sharp, completing 14 of 22 passes for just 118 yards, but the maximalist QB played small ball against Tennessee and avoided the mistakes that have plagued his career. He took three sacks but threw no picks and lost no fumbles. (Coach John Harbaugh said after the win that Winston's mediocre performance would not affect his status as starter, all the while the Giants are reportedly considering adding another vet to the QB room.) The game was won on offense by Cam Skattebo and Najee Harris, who combined for 112 yards on 29 carries. Not the most efficient afternoon from the tailbacks, but they kept drives alive with hard running, picked up first downs and kept the Titans off the field. Credit should be due to Dominic Zvada, who was perfect on four field goal attempts between 27 and 39 yards and served as New York's only scorer. The Giants didn't play their best ball, but it wasn't needed against the laggards of the AFC.
  2. Too little, too late for Titans. As familiar as Robert Saleh and Brian Daboll were to Sunday's venue, the former Jets and Giants coaches, respectively, didn't have the Titans ready to play in their returns to MetLife Stadium. Tennessee was lifeless yet again out of the gate, finishing the first half with 54 yards and just one first down. Out of halftime, the Titans didn't get the ball back until there were under seven minutes left in the third quarter after allowing a lengthy Giants field goal drive. From there, Cam Ward's offense was competent. Tennessee finished each of its three second-half marches in the red zone, but it came away with only seven points. Ward is to thank for most of the missed opportunities. First, Ward skied two passes from the Giants' 7-yard line late in the third quarter to turn the ball over on downs. Then, after a 13-play TD drive cut New York's lead to five, Ward and the Titans got the ball back with three minutes to go and picked up five first downs, looking destined to score a game-winning touchdown. But on first-and-goal, again from the 7-yard line, Ward was flushed out to his left and instead of trying to pick up yards with his legs, tried to find a blanketed Elic Ayomanor in the back of the end zone. Ward's off-footed pass didn't even make it there and was snagged by Giants safety Jevon Holland, hiding in plain sight, to end Tennessee's day. The Titans ended up with more first downs and total yards than New York on the day but came up short where and when it mattered most.
  3. Injury bug bites G-Men again. The Giants' Monday night loss saw Jaxson Dart go down for the year and Brian Burns banged up all week with a sprained ankle. The star pass rusher was a surprise active after not practicing all week and was a force in New York's front seven, logging a game-high four QB pressures of Cam Ward. Big Blue needed to tee off on Tennessee on a soggy day like Sunday and did with Burns leading the charge. But the edge rusher went down awkwardly with a knee injury in the fourth quarter after getting blocked by center Austin Schlottmann on a play-action rollout. Burns went straight to the locker room and did not return. New York has a deep bench at edge rusher with Abdul Carter, Kayvon Thibodeaux and even Arvell Reese able to fill in, but if Burns misses an extended period of time, the Giants will be in a bad position just three games into the season without their offensive and defensive leaders.

Next Gen Stats Insight for Titans-Giants (via NFL Pro): Cam Skattebo generated a season-high 74 yards after contact (and the second-most of his career) in Sunday's win over the Titans. His 30.0% forced missed tackle rate was the third-highest single-game rate of his career. Skattebo has gained 92.3% of his rushing yards after contact in his career, the third-highest rate among players with at least 75 carries since last season. 

NFL Research: The Giants are 2-1 or better to start a season for the first time since 2022. They finished that season 9-7-1 and made the playoffs. New York's seven points allowed against the Titans are its fewest in a game since Week 12, 2023 vs. the Patriots. It's also tied for its sixth-fewest points allowed in a game at MetLife Stadium.

Highlights from the Week 3 matchup between the Cincinnati Bengals and the Pittsburgh Steelers during the 2026 NFL season.

REWATCH: Bengals-Steelers on NFL+ Premium

Brooke Cersosimo's takeaways:

  1. Harmon to the rescue. On a day when Joe Burrow was dealing, staying relatively clean in the pocket and often finding his favorite targets, Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins, the Steelers' defense flexed its muscle when it mattered most. With the Steelers obtaining a 30-27 lead with 2:18 remaining in the game, the Bengals were driving and that's when Derrick Harmon went to work. On first-and-10 at the Steelers' 38-yard line, the Steelers' 2025 first-round draft pick strip-sacked Burrow with Sebastian Joseph-Day recovering the ball to secure the Steelers' victory -- reminiscent of the Super Bowl LVI ending. It was the only time Burrow was sacked Sunday. Harmon logged three sacks as a rookie and is already halfway to that total three games into his sophomore campaign. 
  2. Steelers' offense comes alive. Entering Week 3, the Steelers had not scored a touchdown on 20 consecutive offensive drives. That unit proved early Sunday that the sky was indeed not falling with a pair of scores in the first quarter — the Steelers' first game with multiple offensive touchdowns in the first quarter since Week 13, 2018, snapping a 123-game drought. Aaron Rodgers led consecutive touchdown drives, a four-play, 73-yard march that resulted in a 38-yard connection between Rodgers and Roman Wilson, followed by an 11-play, 86-yard series that ended in Rodgers' 23-yard toss to Ben Skowronek. It marked the first time Cincinnati's revamped defense allowed a passing touchdown this season. Pittsburgh put points on the board on six drives and totaled 411 yards of offense in the victory. Rodgers looked spry and completed 19 of 34 pass attempts for 292 yards with three TDs and one interception, while Jaylen Warren deserves his flowers for running over a Cincinnati defense that hadn't allowed more than 90 rushing yards in a game in 2026. Warren finished with 127 yards on 17 totes. This is the offense fans expected to see in the Mike McCarthy and Rodgers reunion.
  3. Late mistakes doom Bengals. Cincinnati's offense looked solid for much of the contest, scoring on five of its first six offensive possessions and earned its first lead with 8:26 remaining in the third quarter. It felt as though the tides had turned and Zac Taylor's unit would find its way to a road win. However, the team had two costly mistakes on special teams: a roughing the punter penalty that continued a stalled Pittsburgh drive and a muffed punt return by Ke'Shawn Williams.
