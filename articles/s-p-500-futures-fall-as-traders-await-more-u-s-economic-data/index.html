S&P 500 futures fall as traders await more U.S. economic data: Live updates

TheTools · 14.10.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Futures tied to the S&P 500 slid on Wednesday night, with traders looking ahead to more earnings reports and economic data following a losing session brought about by hotter-than-expected inflation data.

S&P 500 futures slipped 0.2%, while Nasdaq-100 futures dropped 0.7%. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures rose just 22 points.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite shed 1.9% during regular trading, marking its worst day since July 23 — when it dropped 2.2%. The S&P 500 dropped 1.1%, its biggest one one-day slide since July 29. The Dow was the relative outperformer, falling just 0.1%.

In Asia, South Korea's Kospi fell over 6%, dragged by technology heavyweights SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Japan's Nikkei 225 was down 4.4%, while the Hang Seng Index declined 1%. Mainland China's CSI 300 lost 1.41%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.85%.

There are growing concerns that the Federal Reserve could tighten monetary policy more aggressively before year-end, after new data showed consumer prices in September rose faster than expected on a year-over-year basis.

"Inflation is cooking with gas," said Jamie Cox, managing partner for Harris Financial Group. "It is abundantly clear where the inflation impulse originates, but two wars need to end before we get any relief. The Federal Reserve made the mistake of calling Covid era inflation transitory, and now it appears they are set to make the same mistake in reverse on the energy supply shock."

Investors will get even more inflation data on Thursday, with the September reading on producer prices due out at 8:30 a.m. ET. New retail sales figures and weekly jobless claims are also slated for release at that time.

The corporate earnings season continues as well, with regional banks U.S. Bancorp and PNC due to report. Charles Schwab is also scheduled to post earnings.

A sell-off in global technology hardware stocks deepened Thursday, with Asian chipmakers tracking Wall Street losses as concerns over further Federal Reserve interest rate hikes, financing of artificial intelligence investments weigh on sentiment.

South Korean chip giant SK Hynix plunged over 10%, while rival Samsung Electronics fell 7.1%. Other South Korean chip-related stocks also came under pressure. LG Innotek sank 8.1%, Seoul Semiconductor shed 7.7% and Samsung SDI declined 6.8%.

In Japan, technology investment giant SoftBank Group tumbled 7.9%. Semiconductor testing equipment maker Advantest dropped 6.9%, while chipmaking equipment supplier Tokyo Electron lost 5.4%. Renesas Electronics declined 5.4%.

Asia-Pacific markets fell Thursday, with Korean stocks leading declines.

South Korea's Kospi declined 6.1% at the open, while the small-cap Kosdaq Index fell 5.7%. Index heavy weights SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics lost 9% and 7%, respectively.

Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 3.2%, while the Topix fell 1.8%.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.71%.

— Lee Ying Shan

Asia-Pacific markets were set to fall Thursday, tracking losses on Wall Street as hotter-than-expected U.S. inflation data fuels concerns that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates more aggressively.

Escalating attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz have also dented sentiment, as they add to uncertainty around the Middle East conflict, clouding the outlook for energy supplies.

Futures for Hong Kong's Hang Seng index stood at 24,074, lower than the index's last close of 24,314.17.

Japan's Nikkei 225 was also set to fall at open, with its Chicago and Osaka futures contracts last at 66,365 and 66,480, respectively, compared with the index's previous close of 67,744.29.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.85% in its first hour of trade.

— Lee Ying Shan

Rocket Lab shares slipped in extended trading after the company pushed back the launch date of its Neutron rocket to 2027.

Shares last traded lower by about 7%.

The company recently identified an issue with the manufacturing process on one of its propellant domes, according to a Rocket Lab video that shared details on the launch delay.

Considering the length of time it would take to build a new dome, as well as to investigate and resolve the  manufacturing issue, the company pushed Neutron's first launch date into the new year, according to the video.

—Darla Mercado

CoreWeave shares were lower by 0.6% after the bell following a sharp pullback for the company's stock and bonds on fears some tech companies are borrowing too much for AI.

The neocloud name nosedived 15.5%, while yields on its debt jumped. CEO Mike Intrator dismissed the market moves in a memo obtained by CNBC.

"When you are building a new type of technology company there will be periods of time when the market misunderstands and acts irrationally," he wrote. "We are confident in our backlog which more than supports all of our debt obligations."
