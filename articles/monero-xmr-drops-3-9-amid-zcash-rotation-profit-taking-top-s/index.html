Monero (XMR) Drops 3.9% Amid Zcash Rotation, Profit Taking | Top Stories | CoinMarketCap

TheTools · 09.09.2026 00:02 · source · permalink

The recent 3.9 percentage point move in Monero (XMR) appears driven by a combination of sector rotation into Zcash (ZEC), profit taking after XMR's strong rally, and leveraged trading dynamics, rather than any Monero-specific fundamental news.

Monero's price movement is occurring within a robust privacy coin cycle currently dominated by Zcash. Privacy coins are the only crypto sector up since Bitcoin's peak, with the segment up 213%, and Zcash and Monero together account for about 90% of the sector's value.[^sector] Zcash has been exploding higher, driven by the launch of the Grayscale Zcash ETF (ZCSH) and aggressive speculative inflows, explicitly eclipsing Monero in privacy coin performance.[^zec_etf] Traders describe flows rotating out of XMR into ZEC, with one market watcher noting ZEC up about 11.4% while XMR was down about 4%, adding that "ZEC sucked liquidity away from XMR as buyers rotated hard into the leader."[^zec_rotation_tweet] This intra-sector rotation is common when a narrative stock or token inside a theme gets a new ETF, high-profile coverage, or a technical breakout, often becoming the "chosen vehicle" for traders. In this situation, even fundamentally similar peers like XMR can sag as capital and attention consolidate into the current leader, which here is ZEC.

The move also fits a normal corrective pattern after a very strong run in XMR itself. Monero ran from about 350 dollars in early August to around 570 dollars by early September, pushing its market cap to roughly 10.1 billion dollars and briefly overtaking Chainlink to enter the top 10 coins by size.[^xmr_vs_link] XMR was trading well above key moving averages, with the 20-day average near 470 dollars and longer averages in the 380–410 dollar band, and its RSI around 70 signaled high momentum but increased risk of a short-term correction.[^xmr_vs_link] Over the last 24 hours, XMR's price has oscillated in a roughly 524–544 dollar range, with the net 24-hour change around minus 1% against an altcoin market that is also modestly down. The broader altcoin market cap is roughly 1.24% lower over the same period, so XMR's move is only slightly weaker than the alt basket. This suggests that XMR was extended after a near-vertical rally into a top 10 market cap slot. In that context, even a small downtick in the overall altcoin market plus a hot competitor like ZEC is enough to trigger profit taking, which shows up as a few percentage points of giveback without any obvious Monero-specific headline.

Short-term trading signals and order flow show that leverage amplified a relatively modest rotation into a sharper intraday "flush." Multiple trading accounts on X shared short setups in XMR in the hours leading up to and during the move, posting plans such as "$XMR /USDT SHORT" with entries around 545–539 dollars, tight stops in the low 550s, and profit targets down into the low 500s.[^short_plan_1][^short_plan_2] This kind of clustered public short interest tends to concentrate orders around the same levels. A separate trader flagged that "$XMR JUST SAW A SHARP FLUSH," citing a 5.9% one-hour selloff inside a 3.39% 24-hour drop and a more than 700% spike in trading volume.[^flush_tweet] That pattern—heavy volume plus a single aggressive red candle—is characteristic of leveraged liquidations and stop cascades rather than a slow, fundamentally driven drift. At the same time, privacy coin coverage and ZEC ETF news were pulling in fresh longs on ZEC, which can indirectly pressure XMR as margin traders short XMR versus long ZEC to express a relative value view. When those pairs unwind, they can create sharp one-hour swings in XMR even if its 24-hour move remains modest.

Based on current data, there is no clear Monero-specific catalyst such as a hack, delisting, or major regulatory action behind the recent 3.9 percentage point move. Instead, the evidence points to a combination of strong rotation into Zcash, a natural pullback after XMR's own steep rally into the top 10, and short-term leveraged trading and concentrated short setups that produced a brief high-volume flush, magnifying what would otherwise have been a smaller, more orderly consolidation. So the movement appears to be a mix of sector-level rotation and technical positioning rather than a discrete Monero fundamental event.
