Nasdaq gains as investors buy the dip in the AI trade: Live updates

TheTools · 18.10.2026 22:09 · source · permalink

The Nasdaq Composite rose on Monday as traders moved back into tech following last week's artificial intelligence-related rout.

The tech-heavy index added 1.2%, while the S&P 500 gained 0.5%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average traded around the flatline.

Intel advanced 2% after JPMorgan analysts upgraded their rating on the chipmaker. Advanced Micro Devices and Micron Technology were also up 1% and 2%, respectively.

However, neocloud mainstay CoreWeave reversed course from earlier gains, falling 3%, even after receiving an upgrade at Goldman Sachs. The stock plunged more than 25% last week — its biggest one-week drop since November 2025 — over debt concerns.

The S&P 500 lost nearly 2% last week — its biggest weekly decline since late June. The Nasdaq tumbled 4.5%, snapping a four-week winning streak. Information technology also shed more than 6% to post its worst weekly performance since April 2025. It was the most net sold global sector last week, with hedge funds selling the largest percentage of tech on record, per Goldman Sachs.

Concerns around the health of the AI trade took center stage after CNBC reported, citing sources, that Anthropic's third-quarter revenue totaled $16.8 billion. And while that's up from $11.6 billion in the second quarter, it raised flags among investors about the pace of growth with the AI build-out.

Investors in the corporate bond market have started to shun CoreWeave, with the company's credit default swaps reaching distress levels.

LNW chief investment officer Ron Albahary believes the recent moves in CoreWeave's spreads is a sign that the narrative surrounding the AI trade's relative insulation from the rise in interest rates has changed. If earnings growth moderates, he thinks the market could become "increasingly more fragile."

"Investors are going to become much more discriminating in terms of looking at whether these companies are going to generate the kind of return on capital to justify the debt that they're taking on," he said. "Prior to this wave, this sell-off, I think investors were assuming that all AI investments were going to pay off, and that's not the case."

Traders were also keeping a wary eye on oil markets with ship attacks in the Persian Gulf picking up and Ukraine and Russia trading attacks on energy infrastructure. Ukraine hit an oil terminal in St. Petersburg, Russia on Sunday. Longer-dated oil futures have begun to ratchet higher on expectations energy infrastructure disruptions may last well into 2027.

On Monday, Brent and West Texas Intermediate crude futures continued to trade higher. Brent rose slightly to trade at roughly $110 per barrel, while WTI was around 2% higher at nearly $104.

A slew of earnings from major companies such as Meta Platforms and Tesla hit the Street this week, with optimism those results will stabilize the market.

The consumer discretionary sector was leading the S&P 500 higher in afternoon trading on Monday.

The sector was last up nearly 3%, followed by information technology, which rose more than 1%. Communication services and materials were also higher on the day.

The rest of the S&P 500 sectors were in the red. Among those seven, utilities was the biggest laggard, dropping 1.9%.

The S&P 500 was last seen up 0.5%.

— Sean Conlon

Wall Street research and advisory firm Gartner is standing by its forecast that $3.5 trillion in AI investment could be on the rocks due to profitability concerns among the top AI models.

Gartner's forecast, issued last week, helped send shockwaves through the AI trade. The firm's chief forecaster, John Lovelock, told CNBC Monday that returns are not yet backing up the full scale of investment.

Consolidation and takeovers among AI-dedicated companies known as neoclouds could ensue, due partly to a mismatch in timing, he added.

"At the moment, we have more compute capacity than we have demand, and we have a lot of the tokens and processing power being given away in freemium solutions," he told CNBC. "Everyone in the AI race is running for a last-man standing position. Pretty much every market that Gartner is tracking in our AI race is likely to collapse down to a duopoly." Read more.

U.S. homebuilder confidence fell to its lowest level since June 2012 in October, as rising mortgage rates, energy and material costs, and worsening labor and lot conditions continue to weigh on housing demand.

The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) and Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (HMI) dropped three points to 29 in October, its lowest reading since June 2012. The index remains well below the key 50-point threshold that signals positive sentiment. 

"Housing remains squeezed by supply-side constraints and significant affordability challenges," said NAHB Chief Economist Robert Dietz. 

Dietz said that while demand persists, higher mortgage rates, elevated construction costs and weaker consumer confidence are keeping more buyers on the sidelines. 

"As a result, buyer traffic has fallen to its lowest level since April 2012, excluding the pandemic's onset in April 2020," he said.

The S&P 500 information technology sector saw substantial pressure last week amid a sell-off of artificial intelligence-related names, according to Goldman Sachs.

In a recent note, the firm said that the sector — which saw a more than 6% drop in the week ended Oct. 16 — was "by far the most net sold global sector" last week. Notably, it saw the largest percentage of de-grossing in two months as well as the largest percentage of net selling on record dating back to 2016.

The firm also said that the move was "driven by long and to a lesser extent short sales."

Intel's sell-off over the last few months may be ending, according to JPMorgan.

The bank upgraded the stock to neutral from underweight, while maintaining its price target of $85. That indicates a 5% loss from Friday's close. Analyst Harlan Sur wrote in a Sunday note that as the stock has approached his price target — as it has declined 36% from its late June peak — the risk/reward profile of the company has improved, though only to become more balanced. 

"Since Lip Bu Tan's appointment to CEO, we believe the team's execution and competitiveness in their core client/datacenter compute market has improved, the team's execution on next-gen manufacturing has significantly improved," Sur said in the note. "Despite near-term concerns on AI infrastructure spending sustainability, we believe increasing agentic AI workloads (CPU intensive) and a very tight supply chain for leading edge silicon should drive continued positive EPS revisions in the upcoming earnings season and through CY27."

Shares of Intel were up 4% in premarket trading Monday. 

— Davis Giangiulio

Barclays shares fell almost 2.5% following reports that the Bank of England is examining whether traders at the London-headquartered lender misrepresented derivatives linked to private credit exposures.

The Financial Times reported Monday, citing people familiar with the matter, that the BoE's Prudential Regulation Authority received a complaint about how Barclays classified total return swaps linked to illiquid private credit loans. The classification could affect the amount of regulatory capital the bank is required to hold.

CNBC has contacted Barclays and the Bank of England for comment.

China's economic growth accelerated to 4.5% in the third quarter from a year earlier, as robust foreign demand for the country's technological goods largely offset drags from a deepening property slump and sluggish consumer demand.

Growth beat Reuters-polled economists' expectation of a 4.4% expansion, and was also higher than the three-year low of 4.3% in the second quarter.

Indonesia's benchmark stock index plunged over 5% on Monday to its lowest level in six years, as concerns over a potential downgrade by MSCI intensified fears of further foreign investor outflows.

The Jakarta Composite Index tumbled to its lowest level since early October 2020, extending a bruising sell-off in Indonesian equities. MSCI, the index provider, on Friday proposed reclassifying Indonesia directly from an emerging market to stand-alone status, raising the prospect of the country's removal from major global equity benchmarks.

MSCI is expected to make a final decision by June, with the potential reclassification hinging on whether Indonesian authorities can demonstrate meaningful improvements in market governance and transparency.

Goldman Sachs warned in a Sunday note that a full removal from MSCI's emerging-market indexes could trigger approximately $5.6 billion in passive fund outflows, equivalent to 18 days of average daily trading volume for constituents of the MSCI Indonesia Investable Market Index.

— Lee Ying Shan

Shares of Australia's Cleanaway Waste Management surged 19.84% on Monday after Swedish private equity firm EQT announced an agreement to acquire the company in a deal valued at about $9.3 billion Australian dollars ($6.5 billion).

Under the agreement, EQT will acquire all outstanding shares of Cleanaway for A$3.095 per share in cash, subject to adjustments for any dividends or other distributions.

The offer represents a 32.1% premium to Cleanaway's closing share price of A$2.37 on Aug. 12, the last full trading day before the company first disclosed a potential transaction.

EQT said it sees opportunities to invest in Cleanaway's infrastructure and improve the performance of its waste management network to meet Australia's evolving waste management needs.

Cleanaway's board unanimously recommended that shareholders vote in favor of the deal, citing the significant premium and certainty of cash consideration, subject to no superior proposal emerging and an independent expert concluding that the transaction is in shareholders' best interests.
