Alexander Zverev’s match with Ben Shelton at the ATP Finals angers tennis fans, ‘I feel so bad’

TheTools · 19.11.2026 00:00 · source · permalink

Peter Lynch

Alexander Zverev is the first player into the semifinals of the ATP Finals after a straight-sets win over Ben Shelton in Turin.

But the encounter between the two ATP superstars wasn’t free of controversy, with many tennis fans angered by a statistic that emerged from the match between Zverev and Shelton.

It was confirmed that the ball was in play during the encounter between Zverev and Shelton for 10 minutes (active time), and one hour and 23 minutes in non-active time. Additionally, half the points in the ATP Finals tie ended in just one shot.

Who will win the title?

Tennis fans angry with Alexander Zverev match vs Ben Shelton

Sharing their dismay at the truly extraordinary figures, one tennis follower commented on X: “I feel so bad for the fans in the stadium.”

A second fan agreed, saying: “Anyone who claims to love watching indoor tennis is lying to themselves and others,” with a third noting: “Oh my ticket charge would be disputed!”

Others concurred with verdicts like: “Paying premium prices for poor quality matches. I’d be fuming,” “And people wonder why they keep slowing courts down,” and “How do people find this entertaining?”

The anger certainly didn’t end there, with others making comments such as: “I’d rather watch clay tennis with no ELC with disputed points for 5 hours,” and “Absolutely disgusting.”

Summarising the thoughts of many, another fan said: “The best thing the ATP ever did was slow down the courts in the 00s and 10s so we’d get more rallies. The fact that they are actively choosing to make the sport worse to watch by speeding them up again makes zero sense.”

Another said: “A match between these two wouldn’t be entertaining on any surface of any speed. But it’s still baffling to me that the Finals are played on what is basically an ice court. What is even the point?”

The bemusement is certainly understandable, and it looks set to continue as the tournament progresses. And intriguingly, the venue for the ATP Finals won’t be changing anytime soon, with Turin set to host the year-end event until 2027. The indoor hard courts of the Inalpi Arena currently welcome the eight best ATP players in the world.

But perhaps action will be taken given the fan complaints, which followed a scintillating service display from Zverev in particular. The German, who triumphed 6-2, 7-6(7-4), won 25 points in a row behind his serve during the clash. He also fired down 14 aces, although his American opponent managed one more.
