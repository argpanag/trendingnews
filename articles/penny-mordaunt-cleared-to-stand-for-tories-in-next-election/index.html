Penny Mordaunt cleared to stand for Tories in next election

TheTools · 06.10.2026 07:20 · source · permalink

Former defence secretary passes assessment board after Kemi Badenoch blocked other senior figures including Sir Grant Shapps

Dame Penny Mordaunt will be allowed to stand as a Conservative candidate at the next general election.

The former defence secretary revealed that Kemi Badenoch had allowed her to put herself forward on a Tory ticket at the next national poll, which many expect to take place in 2029.

There had been speculation about whether Dame Penny would be able to stand after Mrs Badenoch blocked other senior Tories, including Sir Grant Shapps, from standing.

But, speaking to BBC’s Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg, she said: “I have passed my parliamentary assessment board. I am waiting to see what seats I can go for, but I have a full pass from the candidates committee.”

Dame Penny’s remarks came at the start of the annual Conservative Party conference in Birmingham, where Mrs Badenoch is expected to set out a broad vision for how a future Tory government would run Britain.

Approval from the committee does not guarantee that Dame Penny will have the chance to return to Westminster because she would still need to be selected by a local Tory association as its candidate.

The former cabinet minister added: “I would love to be back in the squad. If I am not selected, I will be in the stands cheering on the team and volunteering to chop the oranges at half-time.”

Dame Penny twice ran for the Tory leadership in the summer and autumn contests of 2022. She came third in the race to succeed Boris Johnson, eventually endorsing Liz Truss.

Ms Truss’s premiership came to an end after only 49 days, prompting Dame Penny to announce that she would run again.

However, she withdrew two minutes before the nomination deadline, leaving Rishi Sunak to enter Downing Street unopposed.

Dame Penny was elected as the MP for Portsmouth North in 2010, becoming Britain’s first female defence secretary and later serving as the leader of the Commons from 2022 to 2024.

In her role as Lord President of the Privy Council, Dame Penny held the 8lb (3.6kg) Sword of State during the King’s Coronation ceremony at Westminster Abbey in 2023.

Mrs Badenoch has declared that all Tory candidates must agree with her policies on leaving the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) and scrapping the 2050 net zero target.

Dame Penny was asked whether she now backed the ECHR and to confirm that she no longer believed in legally binding net zero targets introduced by Baroness May of Maidenhead in 2019, which she voted for as an MP.

She replied: “I’m very happy with the party position on all these issues. And I think Kemi is right. I’ve always towed the party line… I haven’t always had my first choice as leader, but I’ve followed the party line.

“And I think that Kemi is right to require her potential MPs to say they will deliver the manifesto they stand on.”

‘I’ve not agreed with every policy’

Dame Penny suggested she had only supported the flagship net zero target and membership of the ECHR while in government because of her Cabinet positions.

“Have I agreed 100 per cent with every policy? No, I haven’t, and that’s what collective responsibility is about,” she said.

“But I’m very happy with the policy position as it is. And I think we’ve tried a lot of things to deal with some of the big issues that are facing this country and now things like leaving the ECHR are the only course of action.”

In her appearance on the BBC on Sunday, Dame Penny noted that Mrs Badenoch was the first party leader since Lord Cameron for whom she had personally voted.

“The more people see of her, the more people are going to like her,” she said.

“She thinks not just about what should happen but how she’s going to make it happen. And this conference, there’ll be policy announcements, but she’s also focused on first principles.

“And that’s so important because there will be challenges that we know about, but she’s also thinking about dealing with challenges that we don’t know about yet, and that’s impressive.”
