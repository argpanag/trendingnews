Yella Beezy found guilty of capital murder for MO3's death

Dallas rapper Yella Beezy was found guilty of capital murder by a Dallas County jury for his role in the 2020 death of rival rapper MO3. FOX 4's Alex Boyer has more.

DALLAS - Dallas rapper Yella Beezy was convicted and sentenced to life in prison in the murder-for-hire case involving rival rapper MO3. The guilty verdict was delivered on the sixth anniversary of the murder.

Yella Beezy, whose real name is Markies Conway, was accused of paying another man to gun down MO3, whose real name is Melvin Noble, in an ambush on Interstate 35 in Dallas in 2020.

Prosecutors said Conway hired Kewon White to carry out the killing over a long-running feud.

Conway pleaded not guilty and denied involvement. White was previously convicted of capital murder for Noble’s death.

A third defendant, Devin Brown, is also charged with capital murder. He's still awaiting trial.

Closing Arguments

State gives closing arguments in Yella Beezy trial | FULL

Assistant District Attorney Krystal Biggins gave closing arguments in Yella Beezy's murder-for-hire trial. He faces up to life in prison if convicted.

During closing arguments, Assistant District Attorney Krystal Biggins detailed an "Ocean's 11"-style plot where shooter Kewon White ambushed Noble on I-35, firing over 40 rounds. She argued Conway placed a bounty on Noble, utilizing tipster Devin Brown and middleman Jerry Johnson to coordinate and funnel payments to White. Biggins pointed to wire transfers, seized cash, and testimony from Cedric Bradley, who stated White bragged about the murder and demanded payment.

Defense gives closing arguments in Yella Beezy trial | FULL

Defense attorney Toby Shook urges jurors to find his client, Markies Conway (Yella Beezy) not guilty of capital murder.  He argued the state's case is based on assumptions and lies.

Defense attorney Toby Shook pushed for an acquittal, asserting the state's case relied on speculation, rap lyrics, and investigator "tunnel vision." Shook highlighted conflicts in star witness Theodore Thomas's testimony, showing cell data placed Conway in Frisco rather than at an alleged barbershop meeting. He also challenged the financial narrative, demonstrating Conway routinely withdrew large sums in crisp $50 and $100 bills for family expenses, whereas White possessed rubber-banded, low-denomination street cash. 

More Yella Beezy defense closing arguments | FULL

Defense attorney John Gussio continued with closing arguments in the trial for Markies Conway (Yella Beezy) for the murder of rival Dallas rapper Melvin Noble (MO3).

John Gussio further argued for the defense that both artists were successful and lacked motive, directing jurors toward Brown, who had an intense personal dispute with Noble and logged 46 calls with White around the time of the shooting. The defense also dismissed Bradley’s testimony as a rehearsed bid to dodge a federal life sentence.

State gives final arguments in Yella Beezy trial | FULL

Prosecutor Tommy Adams makes his final augments in the trial for Markies Conway (Yella Beezy). The Dallas rapper is on trial for capital murder for the death of Melvin Noble (MO3) in 2020.

Prosecutor Tommy Adams delivered the final rebuttal, urging jurors to focus on "motive, money, and the man." He cited Conway’s return to North Texas, where he withdrew $40,000 cash, followed by 15 calls between Johnson and White that directly coincided with Conway's meetings. Rejecting claims of police tunnel vision, Adams maintained that the state proved both payment and remuneration.

MO3 Murder

VIDEO: Rapper Mo3 chased down by hitman on I-35

Prosecutors released video of rapper Mo3 being shot at and chased down by a hitman on I-35 in Dallas. The video was shown during a bond hearing for fellow rapper Yella Beezy who allegedly hired the hitman Kewon Dontrell White.

MO3 was shot 11 times and killed on I-35 back on Nov. 11, 2020. Prosecutors allege the shooting was part of a murder-for-hire plot organized by rapper Yella Beezy, whose legal name is Markies Conway. They claim he paid Kewon White to carry out the murder over a long-running feud. 

In September, jurors found White guilty of capital murder and sentenced him to life in prison after viewing video and hearing testimony about the shooting. Prosecutors also presented evidence of an alleged payment from Conway to White.

Conway has pleaded not guilty to capital murder and denied involvement. His trial was delayed by a month so that his lawyers could get a copy of White’s trial transcript.

A third defendant, Devin Brown, is also charged with capital murder.
