Queen Mette-Marit shares a thumbs-up as she's seen for the first time in months

TheTools · 18.11.2026 21:23 · source · permalink

The Norwegian royal made a surprise public return on Tuesday during a visit to Trondheim, which had not been announced on the Palace's official agenda

Queen Mette-Marit made a surprise outing this week, as she was seen in public for the first time in months. 

The Norwegian royal, 53, was pictured leaving the Britannia Hotel with her husband, King Haakon, during a visit to Trondheim on Tuesday.

Mette-Marit is currently on medical leave following her lung transplant in June and is not expected to resume official duties until summer 2027. The outing was not listed on the Royal House's official agenda.

The royal couple met Norwegian actor Alexander Jensen, who shared selfies from their meeting, with Mette-Marit, wearing a black winter coat and a polka-dot shirt, giving a thumbs-up in one.

Alexander wrote: "Nice talking to you, my king and queen! And it's extra fun to use some sign language. Today is a big day for me!

"Before I went to play the theater play Døvejævler, I had the great honor of meeting and greeting King Haakon and Queen Mette-Marit.

"A very special and unforgettable moment that made this day extra nice. This is a memory I will carry with me for a long time!"

Queen Mette-Marit and King Haakon prepare for milestone

The couple was visiting Trondheim, the city that will host their consecration ceremony next August.

They privately visited Nidaros Cathedral and the Archbishop's Palace, where the crown jewels are on display, as well as Stiftsgården, the royal residence in Trondheim.

"We have high expectations for that. I think it will be very nice," King Haakon said, speaking of their consecration, which is understood to be in the early planning stages.

The royal blessing will take place on 22 August 2027 at Nidaros Cathedral, followed by a gala dinner at Stiftsgården.

Following the consecration weekend, the King and Queen will travel on board the Royal Yacht to Bergen for a dinner at Håkon's Hall, before beginning an extensive consecration tour of Norway, lasting two years.

A statement on the Palace's website says: "Royal consecration in Norway is a tradition stretching back a thousand years. The custom of undertaking an extended tour of large parts of the country in connection with the consecration also dates back to the kings of the Middle Ages."

Queen Mette-Marit's last public appearance

Previously, Queen Mette-Marit was last seen in public at Princess Astrid's funeral in September.

She also attended King Harald's funeral earlier that month, before joining her husband on the balcony at the Royal Palace.

The Norwegian Royal Court shared an update on 7 October, stating: "Her Majesty the Queen is still in rehabilitation after her lung transplant and will initially have no official program until next summer."

Mette-Marit became Norway's Queen Consort in late August when her husband ascended the throne after Harald's death.

She underwent a lung transplant on 17 June, with the Palace announcing she was starting a six-month rehabilitation programme.
