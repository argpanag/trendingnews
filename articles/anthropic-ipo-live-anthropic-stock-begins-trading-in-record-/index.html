Anthropic IPO live: Anthropic stock begins trading in record $81B raise with $1.5T valuation

TheTools · 19.11.2026 20:22 · source · permalink
  • ANT +14.63%

Anthropic (ANT) began trading on Thursday at $111.50 per share, well above its $83 IPO price, giving it a market capitalization of $2 trillion.

The company, which was founded in 2021, raised a record-breaking $81.3 billion in its initial public offering at a valuation of $1.5 trillion.

CEO Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei were at the Nasdaq in Times Square to ring the opening bell ahead of the first trade, with Dario saying that safety will be key to how AI is developed and deployed.

Daniela Amodei, meanwhile, addressed concerns about AI growth, saying, "To anyone watching who is uneasy about AI or unsure what it will mean for you. This technology needs you. Not your trust, not your use, but your voice."

Anthropic, which Yahoo Finance named its Company of the Year for 2026, reported in its most recent amended S-1 that its third-quarter revenue climbed to $17 billion, a 12x year over year increase.

Now the market will determine how that risk factors into the company's future prospects.

  • Anthropic stock heads into close up 16%

    Anthropic (ANT) stock is heading into the end of the trading day up more than 16% from its initial price of $83.

    Shares of the AI lab began trading at $111.50 earlier in the, hitting $115.10, before retreating throughout the afternoon.

    Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in its initial public offering at a valuation of $1.5 trillion.

  • Retail investors pile into Anthropic debut, but net buying lags SpaceX

    Anthropic's (ANT) public debut drew intense retail trading activity, though net inflows have started at a slower pace than SpaceX's (SPCX) June 2026 listing, according to Vanda Research.

    The firm's data shows that during the first 30 minutes of trading, retail net buying reached $18.6 million, ranking second among 2026 debuts behind the rocket company's $25.6 million

    However, total retail turnover hit $255 million over the same window, more than double SpaceX's early trading volume, signaling that day traders were rapidly buying and selling shares.

    "Retail are trading the stock hard... but taking both sides rather than a one-way bid," wrote Vanda Research analyst Viraj Patel, noting retail buy share steadily declined from 57.2% to 52.5% across early trading.

    Meanwhile, retail investors sold single stocks to fund their Anthropic purchases, rotating out of AI names led by AMD as Nvidia's buying stalled

  • Anthropic has the 'first-mover advantage' over OpenAI: Strategist

    With Anthropic (ANT) stock showing a massive public debut, what does it mean for rival frontier AI lab OpenAI (OPAI.PVT)?

    Earlier this week, OpenAI's CEO Sam Altman reaffirmed the company's plans to go public. Reports earlier this year suggested OpenAI would try to IPO in 2026, but the company is now aiming to go public sometime in 2027.

    Anthropic is OpenAI's younger sibling: a group of OpenAI employees spun off their safety-focused startup in 2021. Now, it has the first IPO advantage, according to The Connor Group managing partner Jim Neesen

    "There is a lot of power in that first mover advantage," Neesen told Yahoo Finance. "The thing that really jumped out on the S-1 is their Fortune 500 clients, they have 50% market share. They're spending 0.1% of their expenses on AI specifically on Anthropic, so there is a massive opportunity that it really can grow and expand because of that first-mover advantage."

    "Now, if you look at OpenAI, … I think for them, they're really going to look to expand in their enterprise, get more market share there and demonstrate, hey, it's not just individual, but enterprises are buying as well," Neeson continued. "But we have here numbers that, when they're compared against Anthropic, we have a parabolic revenue trajectory as well."

  • Anthropic stock sales have 23 lock-up dates

    Anthropic (ANT) has set 23 different lock-up expiration dates for sales of its stock from as early as the initial trading day to as late as the second full trading day after the company releases its Sept. 30, 2027 earnings results.

    The tranches vary in size from 174 million shares to up to 3 billion.

  • Anthropic’s first half hour is the worst start for a big IPO since 2008

    The biggest IPO ever drew $15 billion in the first minute. Then the selling started.

    Anthropic (ANT) opened at $111.50, 34% above its $83 IPO price, and slid to about $97 by its 30th minute of trading, a 13% drop. That erased nearly $200 billion in market value, based on the 13.1 billion shares Yahoo Finance counts as implied shares outstanding.

    No big US IPO since 2008 was further below its first trade 30 minutes in, according to a Yahoo Finance review of 1-minute trading data for 21 of them. Coupang (CPNG) came closest at nearly 13%. SpaceX (SPCX) was up almost 10% at the same point in its June debut.

  • 'Kind of cool': Early Anthropic investor and actor Ashton Kutcher weighs in on IPO

    Ashton Kutcher celebrated Anthropic's blockbuster IPO on Thursday, and with good reason: Kutcher's venture capital firm A-Grade made a $435 million investment in Anthropic that's now worth roughly $7 billion, according to Newsweek.

    With Anthropic now worth well over $1 trillion, Kutcher's stake could make the "That '70s Show" actor a billionaire.

    "People usually see the one deal and think that's the whole thing, but the ones who know realize that it's years of work, micro victories and failures stacked on top of one another," Kutcher wrote on X.

    "Kind of cool when that works out," Kutcher said.

  • Ives says Anthropic IPO provides a 'read on demand' for frontier AI companies

    Yorkville's Dan Ives says Anthropic's (ANTH) market debut offers a look at demand for frontier-lab AI exposure among investors.

    "For the first time a US frontier lab carries a standalone public market price, uncluttered by an ad business or a rocket business," he wrote in a note to investors, referring to Google (GOOG, GOOGL) and SpaceX (SPCX).

    "At a time in which investors are very nervous on the overall tech trade and the Nvidia black cloud moment casts a shadow over the tech sector, Anthropic's debut is a big near-term win for the tech sector and should help restore some investor confidence that the AI Revolution growth thesis is not waning," Ives wrote.

    The analyst admitted that the AI trade is in the midst of a "very turbulent, white-knuckle period," but said it doesn't change his long-term view that companies will spend $3 trillion to $4 trillion on AI capital expenditures in the years ahead.

  • Anthropic stock's first trade was worth $12 billion — 34% above its IPO price

    Wall Street waited until nearly noon for Anthropic's first trade. That one print was worth about $12 billion.

    Anthropic (ANT) opened Thursday at $111.50 a share, 34% above its $83 IPO price, as 108 million shares changed hands in the opening trade. That single print was bigger than SpaceX's (SPCX) entire first minute of trading in June, about $9 billion.

    The first full minute was bigger still. About 139 million shares, roughly $15 billion of stock, traded in Anthropic's first 60 seconds, by Yahoo Finance calculations. That's nearly twice SpaceX's first minute, about three times Alibaba's (BABA) in 2014, and about 14% of the 980 million shares sold in the offering.

    The rush capped the biggest IPO ever. Anthropic raised $81.3 billion after pricing above its $70-$78 range, and the opening price valued the company at nearly $2 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

    The 34% opening pop was about three times SpaceX's 11%, though well short of Cerebras' (CBRS) 89% in May. IPOs priced above their marketed range have averaged a 50% first-day gain since 1980, according to data from University of Florida professor Jay Ritter. Read more.

    But if you dig deeper into Anthropic's business, there are more interesting factoids you will also have to get comfortable with if you want to be a shareholder.

    1. Anthropic has a donation-matching program in which employees can pledge a meaningful portion of their equity awards or the equivalent cash proceeds to a donor-advised fund or other nonprofit organization, and the company then matches those donations with shares of its Class N common stock. The company incurred $2.1 billion in expenses in the first nine months of 2026 related to this program, representing about 6% of total operating expenses. It saw $541 million in these expenses in 2025.

    2. Water is a legitimate risk to Anthropic's ability to grow its business quickly. "Our facilities separately depend on access to reliable water for cooling, which in some regions is scarce, subject to use restrictions, or increasingly costly. Any of the foregoing could adversely affect the availability of the power or water we require, increase our costs, or limit our ability to develop, expand, or operate our data center facilities, which could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations," the company noted in its S-1 filing.

    3. Anthropic may have to pay for compute whether or not it uses it. The company noted in its S-1 filing, "Our equipment supply framework agreements and similar arrangements may expose us to further risks, including minimum purchase or take-or-pay commitments, price escalation, and obligations that we are unable to align with our actual deployment needs."
