Grand Theft Auto VI's 30 FPS Console Cap Could Reach 120 FPS via Free SmoovPlay Tool, but Controls Still Respond Like 30 FPS

TheTools · 17.11.2026 16:10 · source · permalink

Grand Theft Auto VI launches in only two days on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and in true Rockstar Games tradition, the first release of the game targets 30 FPS gameplay on all systems, including the PlayStation 5 Pro.

However, some fans are coming up with all sorts of ways to enjoy the game at 60 FPS or more at launch, and one of them has developed a clever tool called SmoovPlay, which promises to deliver a high framerate experience leveraging game streaming from the console to a PC and AI Frame Generation, though the input delay won't likely make it a very enjoyable experience.

"Play 30 fps console games at 60, 90 or up to 120 fps on your PC, with real-time AI frame generation and upscaling," reads the official description of the tool on GitHub. "SmoovPlay streams your console to your PC, where your graphics card draws new frames between the ones the game sends, live. A 30 fps game plays at 60, 90 or up to 120 fps to match your screen, sharpened to fit it. There's nothing to install on the console, and it's free and open source."

SmoovPlay is powered by RIFE, an open-source AI model that draws 1, 2, or 3 new frames between each pair the game sends. The 1080p stream from the console is then sharpened and upscaled to the PC monitor resolution using either AMD FSR 1 (in 0.1 ms) or NVIDIA RTX Video Super Resolution. Although the tool has only been tested with PlayStation 5 Remote Play so far, Xbox and other streaming apps are supported as experimental options. Grand Theft Auto VI hasn't been tested either, as the game only launches in two days, but the tool should work regardless of the game played on a console.

While what SmoovPlay promises sounds almost like a miracle, there are plenty of caveats, as the more tech-savvy users already know. The tool promises a smoother experience, but not a faster one. "The extra frames are generated: motion gets smoother, but the game still responds like 30 fps, and a little later than without SmoovPlay. Great for driving, exploring and story missions; not for competitive online play," the tool's developer warns.

Gameplay generated by the tool has about 36 ms of delay at 90 FPS and about 20 ms at 60 FPS, in addition to the streaming delay, which depends on the user's network setup. As such, it's clear that Grand Theft Auto VI played through this setup is not going to be a very responsive game, adding to the fact that using frame generation with a 30 FPS base framerate is never ideal for a good experience, although the end results could be surprising, as Rockstar Games has ensured input latency is vastly reduced over Grand Theft Auto V and Red Dead Redemption 2.

Still, trying out SmoovPlay to get an early taste of a smooth 60 FPS Grand Theft Auto VI could definitely be worth it for those with a capable GPU. While shooting is likely to be atrocious with such high delay, taking a leisurely trip through Vice City at anything above 30 FPS could still be quite enjoyable, high delay and all, until real 60+ FPS gameplay and possibly NVIDIA DLSS 5 support become real options with the yet-to-be-dated PC version.
