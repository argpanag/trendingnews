Gambhir, Gill chase their defining SENA win in Christchurch

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:36 · source · permalink

It rained all morning in Christchurch on Thursday, and continued all the way through to about 1.30 pm. This was heavy rain. Not the kind of light pitter-patter you expect to witness in one of New Zealand’s driest major cities. Almost the type of tropical downpour that you might associate with some parts of India, one that a lot of players in the current Test squad would have been able to relate with.

It came only a few days after they’d batted and bowled on a surface in Wellington where they would have felt quite at home as well. Not in very fond fashion if you were one of the bowlers, of course. But it was also a Test where they found themselves in a position where they haven’t often been during a Test on New Zealand soil: bossing the game and completely in control for large periods of the contest.

There’s been an understandably extensive post-mortem of India’s inability to close out the first Test at the Basin Reserve. While the total lack of pace on the pitch as the Test wore on has been identified as the major culprit, other questions have been asked around the timing of the declaration as well as team selection. A lot of the anguish around the Test eventually finishing in a draw, though, and that too with Shubman Gill deciding to shake hands with 15 overs still left, stems from the fact that India had done so much right in Wellington till that point. They’d won the toss and reduced the hosts to 70/5, and then despite the Henry Nicholls-Glenn Phillips partnership pulling things back slightly, the visitors went on to put on one of the most dominant team performances with the bat to take pole position in the match.

They’d come so close to taking a 1-0 lead in a country which has historically seen India win so little. And in a fashion which could easily have meant that the Black Caps were totally demoralised after their bowlers were taken for over 700 runs for the first time in New Zealand cricket history. Instead, the two teams will square off at Hagley Oval with the series still 0-0 and with the possibility that India walk away from another Test tour to New Zealand with nothing to show for it.

We are now into the third year of Gautam Gambhir at the helm, and his second season with Gill as captain. And though India did pull off two famous wins in England last year, it feels like the time has come for the pairing to get a series win outside the subcontinent under their belt, ideally in a SENA country to really establish their partnership on the road. Like Virat Kohli did with his series win in Australia or MS Dhoni before him when he won the series in New Zealand of all places. That one will be a special memory for Gambhir, whose terrific rear-guard in Napier played a huge role in India sealing the deal, and in establishing the Indians as being viable challengers outside their comfort zone.

It is something the Gambhir-Gill duo would like to do as well before they head back home in a week’s time.

When: New Zealand v India, 2nd Test, November 27-December 1, 2026, 11:00 hrs Local Time, 03:30 hrs IST, 09:00 hrs AEDT,

Where: Hagley Oval, Christchurch

What to expect: The inclement weather meant that the Kiwis had to cancel their training session in the morning while India started their practice indoors. The rain did clear up post 2 pm allowing the tourists to get a good hit out in the outdoor nets at Hagley. But also more importantly, get a first-hand look at the pitch, which as per norm, had a nice tinge of grass-cover on top. The curator Jayden Tohill played it pretty safe in terms of making predictions with regards to the pitch but did cite that the weather, except for Thursday, had been very cooperative in terms of how he likes preparing the pitch. Last year, the Test against the West Indies went into Day 5, and Tohill was confident this promises to do the same.

Team News

New Zealand

The most obvious call for any team following a runfest like the one in Wellington would be to drop the most expensive of the bowlers. And Jacob Duffy did have a few days to forget at the Basin as he went for 201 runs at over 6-an-over. But in reality, there was little he could have done to stem the flow on that pitch. And it looks like the Black Caps will stick with him for Christchurch, despite having added the much quicker Matt Fisher to the camp. Duffy was spotted on the eve of the Test, as the only New Zealand player at training, going through his paces with bowling coach Jacob Oram while head coach Rob Walter and selector Gavin Larsen watched on.

Possible XI: Tom Latham (c), Devon Conway, Henry Nicholls, Rachin Ravindra, Daryl Mitchell, Tom Blundell (wk), Glenn Phillips, Nathan Smith, Kyle Jamieson, Matt Henry, Jacob Duffy

India

For India, the choices to make here are the same as last week, as the debate around Washington Sundar over Ravindra Jadeja continues to rage on. Jadeja, who was in great spirits on Thursday, was barely needed back in 2020, when he bowled only 15 overs in a fast-paced match, and Sundar did look decent in parts in Wellington. Gurnoor Brar has had a lot of focus on him as his extra pace is being looked at a tempting option for the Indians, but bowling coach Morne Morkel played his cards close to his chest and gave little away. Mohammad Siraj is still in the mix, but don’t be surprised if India stick with the same XI.

Probable XI: KL Rahul, Yashasvi Jaiswal, Devdutt Padikkal, Shubman Gill (c), Rishabh Pant (wk), Dhruv Jurel, Washington Sundar/Ravindra Jadeja, Anshul Kamboj, Akash Deep/Mohammad Siraj/Gurnoor Brar, Jasprit Bumrah, Prasidh Krishna

Did you know?

  • New Zealand have won 6 of the last 9 Tests against India
  • The average innings scores in Christchurch read 291 in the first innings, 343 in the second, 272 in the third and 248 in the fourth.
  • India have lost the only Test they have played in Christchurch, in 2020.

What they said:

"It does make life a lot harder, especially when the surface also loses a little bit of pace with the softer ball. That is my experience, playing for 16 years with the Kookaburra ball, that you need to make that new ball count. You need to get the ball in the right area, sometimes be a bit braver in terms of your lengths and your lines, even going for a few runs, because when the ball gets soft, you can always put a ring field in and bring the run rate back." - Morne Morkel, India's bowling coach

"The way we stayed patient with the ball in areas, there's some great signs for us from a control point of view. And then I think tired legs and tired minds, it's pretty tough to bat. And I think the way that the guys were able to navigate the pressures early on and see out a really, really good draw for us was really impressive." - Matt Henry, New Zealand pacer
