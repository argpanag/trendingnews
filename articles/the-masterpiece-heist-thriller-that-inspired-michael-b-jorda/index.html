Heist films are one of cinema's most timeless genres. Whether set in modern times, the 1960s, or the deep past, a good movie about people willing to break the law in exciting ways remains one of the most reliable forms of filmmaking. As Michael B. Jordan prepares to introduce audiences to a new and updated version of a Hollywood classic, the original film that served as its inspiration is now available to stream for free.

Premiering in 1968, The Thomas Crown Affair stars Steve McQueen and Faye Dunaway in one of the best heist movies ever made, and one that remains endlessly cool decades after release. The movie inspired another remake in 1999, starring Pierce Brosnan and Rene Russo, which swapped the original's bank robbery for a more elaborate art theft, something that Jordan's upcoming remake seems to be incorporating. With Jordan's upcoming adaptation starring himself alongside Adria Arjona set to release early in 2027, there's no better time to revisit the original classic. Fortunately for audiences, it is now available to stream for free on platforms including Tubi, Pluto TV, and Roku.

Steve McQueen's 'The Thomas Crown Affair' Is One of the Coolest Heist Movies Ever Made

The central plot of The Thomas Crown Affair centers on Thomas Crown (McQueen), a wealthy businessman who appears to have everything anyone could want: He's handsome, successful, and enjoys a luxury lifestyle that many can only dream of. Despite his success, Crown finds himself incredibly bored with his comfortable existence. This boredom manifests itself in a rather interesting way: he orchestrates an elaborate bank robbery, despite having no need for the money. Unlike traditional crime thrillers that tend to follow criminals who are down on their luck or desperate for money, The Thomas Crown Affair introduces a criminal who is motivated entirely by the thrill of getting away with something extraordinary.

Crown's confidence is soon challenged by an investigator, Vicki Anderson (Dunaway), who begins looking into the robbery to discover who could be behind the audacious crime. What follows is a cat-and-mouse game between Crown and Anderson, leading both to realize the romantic attraction that builds as each learns more about the other. It's a fascinating psychological battle between two intelligent adversaries, a duel that is only more exciting to watch thanks to the performances of McQueen and Dunaway. McQueen's effortlessly charismatic performance exudes cool, and it's easy to see how someone could be swayed by his charm, while Dunaway is the perfect foil. She knows what it would take to stop a man like Crown, but she also struggles to resist the temptation to be with him.

Michael B. Jordan's 'The Thomas Crown Affair' Has a Lot to Live Up To

The enduring appeal of a story like The Thomas Crown Affair extends far beyond just McQueen and Dunaway's performances. Norman Jewison's direction gives the film a sleek, immersive style through its split-screen photography and editing, creating a distinctive look. The styling, costumes, on-set location shoots, and incredible score set the stage for an unforgettable experience. The Thomas Crown Affair helped establish a format for films like Ocean's Eleven and Entrapment that followed later, so it's easy to see how timeless this movie is and why a modern remake makes sense.

Michael B. Jordan's remake will take inspiration from the original and the 1999 version with Pierce Brosnan and Rene Russo, swapping a bank robbery for art thefts. This will mark Jordan's second time in the director's chair following Creed III; he will also produce and star alongside Adria Arjona, Kenneth Branagh, and Lily Gladstone. While many heist movies emphasize action, often using it as the anchor for the project, The Thomas Crown Affair demonstrates that compelling characters with psychological tension can be just as entertaining. The greatest strength of both films was the romantic and sexual tension between their leads, and, judging by the trailers, Jordan seems to be following in their footsteps.

Jordan's interpretation will certainly bring its own unique perspective to the source material, but the original film with McQueen and Dunaway remains a must-watch and the gold standard. For those eager to sink their teeth into a good heist movie and see the one that started it all, it's available to stream and, best of all, it's free on Tubi, Pluto TV, and Roku. Needless to say, McQueen's crime thriller is still essential viewing even nearly 60 years later.
