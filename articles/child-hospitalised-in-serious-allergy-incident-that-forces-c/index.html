Child hospitalised in 'serious' allergy incident that forces closure of Edge centre

TheTools · 08.09.2026 03:40 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Child Care

Topic:Child Care

Edge Early Learning is in voluntary administration, with several centres facing problems, including in Port Augusta. (ABC News)

In short:

Edge Early Learning in Port Augusta has been forced to close for two weeks after a "serious food allergen incident" resulted in a child being hospitalised.

It is the latest in a series of problems at Edge, which has at different times been forced to close several of the more than 60 centres it operates.

What's next?

Edge had been ordered to ensure all service staff go through "independent training in emergency response management and the identification of allergic reactions and anaphylaxis".

Edge Early Learning in Port Augusta has been forced to close for two weeks after a child with allergies was hospitalised because they were given food "they shouldn't have been served".

The Education Standards Board (ESB) found the provider contravened it's responsibility to protect children from harm and hazards, describing what had occurred as a "serious food allergen incident".

The centre will be closed until Monday, September 21 at 6:30pm.

Education Minister Lucy Hood described the incident, which occurred on September 3, as "an allergen management issue".

"[It was] in regards to a young child being served food that they shouldn't have been served and I understand it led to the hospitalisation of a child,"

"My understanding was that the child had several allergies."

The ESB ordered the centre to provide written communication to the families of all children enrolled at the service, to outline the incident and the ESB's subsequent directions.

"[The ESB] has issued an emergency action notice to Edge Early Learning Port Augusta after identifying concerns relating to child safety,"  ESB regulation and quality executive director Sean Heffernan said in a statement.

Edge would also need to undertake an investigation into the incident and "ensure all service staff complete independent training in emergency response management and the identification of allergic reactions and anaphylaxis".

Edge has been plagued by controversy in recent months, and has at different times been forced to close several centres around the country.

Late last month, Edge — which has more than 60 centres across SA, the ACT and Queensland — entered voluntary administration.

In a statement at the time, it said administrators KordaMentha had been appointed to "review the business", and that the process would allow the company "to assess its financial position and consider options for its future".

"Allergen management has clearly been a challenge for the Edge Early Learning centres — there are a number of other centres that have experienced allergen issues, and so there'll be an investigation," Ms Hood said today.

"They have entered into voluntary administration. They have several compliance actions on their centres.

"I think, through the voluntary administration process, we will either see some of those centres sold off or the current centres secured."

Edge Early Learning has centres across the country, including in Brisbane. (ABC News: Steve Kelly)

Ms Hood, who said she had previously met with Edge, said she would be getting a full briefing on the Port Augusta incident later today.

"The service has been closed for 14 days. Parents have been notified, and they'll be encouraged to seek alternative options during this closure period," she said.

She said there were just over 100 enrolments at the centre and advised parents to check the federal government's Starting Blocks website "to look at alternative options within their community".

Edge Early Learning has been contacted for comment about the incident.
