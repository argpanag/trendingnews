Elena Rybakina arrived at the 2026 U.S. Open already carrying significant momentum, and win or lose, she leaves it as the new world No. 1. The Russian-born Kazakhstani player, known for her powerful serve and almost unsettlingly calm demeanor on court, secured the top ranking just by reaching the semifinals. Behind that rise is a coaching team that has had its own turbulent chapter, and come out the other side intact.

Below, get to know more about her coaches:

Stefano Vukov

A former tennis pro representing Croatia, Stefano Vukov reached a career-high ATP ranking of No. 1122 before retiring in 2009. He took over as Rybakina’s first traveling coach in February 2019 when she was ranked just inside the top 200. Under Vukov’s guidance, she rose rapidly into the top 30 and eventually to the top of the sport.

“I’m a very calm person and he is very active,” Rybakina told The Guardian, noting he even followed through on a bet by getting her name tattooed after she won Wimbledon in 2022. “He has so much energy and I think it works well for us because we are completely different. He’s always pushing me to be better and this works. I’ve had great results with him. We’ll see how long and how many more achievements we can make together.”

The partnership was briefly interrupted when Vukov was fired ahead of the 2024 U.S. Open and was subsequently suspended by the WTA under a code of conduct investigation in early 2025. The ban was upheld in February following a report in The Athletic that Vukov had subjected Rybakina to mental abuse, calling her “stupid,” among other derogatory terms, while making her cry, and pushing her beyond her physical limits to the point of illness.

The suspension was lifted in August following an independent tribunal hearing, and Rybakina surprised many by rehiring him before the Cincinnati Open that year. “It’s just nice to see him in the box. We obviously have good communication,” she told reporters later. “We never had issue, so…”

Stanislav Khmarskyi

Stanislav Khmarskyi, also known as Stas Khmarsky, joined Rybakina’s coaching setup in 2025 and has worked alongside her primary team through major tournaments. He previously served as a coach for Latvian Grand Slam champion Jeļena Ostapenko and is married to WTA player Nadiia Kichenok.
