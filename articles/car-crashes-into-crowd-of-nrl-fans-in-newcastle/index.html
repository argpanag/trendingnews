Car crashes into crowd of NRL fans in Newcastle

TheTools · 04.10.2026 16:44 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / World

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._Q.VKhexyL5oal5uCV3AUQmI1EOdryHi3hh73RdibV20lY/world/1697086%2Fcar-crashes-into-crowd-of-nrl-fans-in-newcastle

  • Ten people have been injured after a car hit a crowd of Newcastle Knights supporters in New South Wales
  • The crowd had gathered as part of a pre-grand final farewell to the team, in Newcastle
  • NSW Police said the driver has been arrested at the scene, while the injured had been transported to hospital.

Two people are in a critical condition and eight others have been injured after a car ploughed into a crowd gathered for the Newcastle Knights grand final procession in New South Wales.

Australian police arrested the driver of the car at the scene shortly after the crash on Newcastle Link Road in the town of Wallsend, in Newcastle's west, about 9.30am Saturday local time (12:30pm NZT).

NSW police said three girls under six years old had been injured, as well as seven adults.

Of those, a five-year-old girl and a 67-year-old man were in critical condition. The others were in a stable condition and being treated for their injuries in hospital.

NSW Police said Newcastle Link Road was closed in both directions while investigations continued.

"Members of the public are asked to safely disperse from the area and keep roadways clear to assist emergency services and traffic management efforts," NSW Police said in a statement.

Newcastle Mayor Gavin Morris told ABC News the driver of the vehicle was reportedly revving his engine before the crash: "He was excited, he was a supporter, he was, you know, a fan, he was there for the joy of the moment as well," he said.

"Somehow, yes, he has lost control of the car."

'Everyone was yelling, screaming for help'

Emergency services helicopters arriving on scene in the town of Wallsend, New South Wales.

ABC News - Piper Duffy

Thousands of supporters had gathered along roads in Wallsend to see off the Newcastle Knights. The team's bus was meant to make its way through the area as the team travelled to the NRL grand final, to be played in Sydney on Sunday.

The route of the bus was later diverted due to the accident.

Newcastle Knights fan Dylan Prior told ABC News that he witnessed the incident. He was with his two young daughters and his mother, having arrived at 8:30am, expecting the Knights' team bus to go by around 10am.

He said the mood was upbeat, with "everyone there, cheering and beeping", but at around 9:30am: "A BMW came around the corner, it was revving a bit, and it ran into a group of people".

"We've had a bit of rain here, and weather played a part in it.

"I saw some young kids go flying, and people running to help the victims.

"Everyone was yelling and hysterical, yelling at the driver.

"They were screaming for help."

Prior said there was only one police officer on site, and he quickly joined the effort to help the victims.

"But I wish there had been more police around. This could have been avoided if more police were in sight," he said.

"The mood is very deflated at the moment. I can't help but think of the victims."

Newcastle Knights give short statement

Four helicopters and 15 ambulances responded to the scene.

ABC News - Piper Duffy

Knights' director of football Chris James said the team's "thoughts are with everyone impacted by the incident, including those at the scene and their families".

"As this is a matter for NSW Police, the club will not be making any further comment at this time."

Earlier in the morning they said on social media that the team bus had been diverted from the planned route due to a major incident.

"We apologise to anybody who was waiting and missed the boys, but public safety is our priority."

NSW Premier Chris Minns said the incident was "heartbreaking", and the community would "come together for each other".

"It's heartbreaking to see fans who were sending off their grand final team involved in such a terrible incident," he said.

"My thoughts are with the families and friends of loved ones who have been involved or injured in the crash.

"I also want to thank emergency services, including police and ambulance, who are responding to this tragedy.

"We've seen how this grand final has brought the Newcastle together, and I hope the whole community remains united as we continue to respond."

- ABC
