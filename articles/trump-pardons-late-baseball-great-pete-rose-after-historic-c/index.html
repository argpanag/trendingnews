Trump pardons late baseball great Pete Rose after historic China trip

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:11 · source · permalink

Once banned from baseball, Rose was convicted on federal tax charges in 1990.

President Donald Trump said Thursday he issued a "complete and total pardon" to baseball legend Pete Rose, who died in 2024. 

Trump posted the announcement to his social media platform as he was returning from his trip to Asia where he met with other world leaders, notably the first-ever trilateral meeting with the leaders of China and Russia.

Rose, who holds the Major League Baseball record for most career hits, was sentenced to five months in prison after being convicted of tax charges in 1990.

Rose was also permanently banned from baseball in 1989 following an investigation into his betting on baseball games while managing the Cincinnati Reds. Rose also pleaded guilty to federal income tax charges in 1990.

He and 16 other former players were removed from baseball's permanent list of banned players last year, making him eligible for the Baseball Hall of Fame posthumously. The decision applied only to dead players who had been placed on the ineligible list.

"I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND, whose record of 4,256 Hits in a Season will never be broken," Trump, then rattled off a number of Rose's other baseball accomplishments.

Rose had 4,256 hits over his career, not in a single season as Trump wrote. 

"And, just like the Great Roger Clemens, who won 354 games, was treated terribly by Major League Baseball in not allowing him to go into the Baseball Hall of Fame. Politics, I guess!" Trump wrote.

Clemens, who is third on baseball's all-time career strikeout list with 4,672, was a seven-time Cy Young Award winner with a career record of 354-184 and a career earned run average of 3.12. He came up short in Hall of Fame balloting in the 10 years he was eligible because of allegations he used performance-enhancing drugs, which he denied.

MLB removed Rose from its permanently ineligible list in May 2025, making him eligible for future Hall of Fame consideration. Trump has previously said he wanted Rose to be pardoned and up for reconsideration for the Hall of Fame. 

Rose is expected to be on the ballot next year for the Hall of Fame's Classic Era Committee, which considers those who played before 1980 but weren't elected to the Hall.

Just last week during a surprise briefing at the White House today with Ultimate Fighting Championship President Dana White, Trump said he would pardon Rose. 

"I think Pete Rose was very mistreated. He was, you know, 4200 and some odd hits, way ahead of anybody else. A record can never be broken," Trump said then. "And he was caught gambling. Now it's illegal, right? But he was gambling on his own team."

"He shouldn't have done it. But he went through 40 years of hell, and they never -- and they should have given him a pardon," Trump said.

"They should have given him into the Baseball Hall of Fame a long time ago. And I spoke to the commissioner about it, and he agreed to do it. And then he waited, though, and Pete Rose died. So it didn't help him too much. But, I would give Pete Rose a pardon. I would do that," Trump added.
