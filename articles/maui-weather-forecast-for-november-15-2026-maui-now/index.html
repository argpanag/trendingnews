A strong high pressure system will build north of the Hawaiian Islands today on a slow eastward drift. Strong gusty winds will develop over and down stream of island mountain ranges today. Wind speeds will exceed advisory thresholds in the windier areas of all islands except for Kauai and Niihau. Passing showers are possible over windward and mountain areas through much of this week, higher rainfall amounts will favor the overnight to early morning hours.

A combination of satellite and radar imagery shows unsettled 'open cell' cumulus clouds riding in on the trade winds producing periods of rain showers across much of the state. Higher rainfall amounts will favor windward mountain areas of all islands, especially in the overnight to early morning hours. Upper air weather balloon soundings from Lihue and Hilo at 2 AM HST (12Z) observed subsidence temperature inversion heights around 6,000 feet, high enough for periods of passing showers to remain in the forecast. Stronger low level winds will also help to lift unstable clouds up over island mountains orographically forcing enhanced rainfall activity.
A satellite scatterometry pass from last night around 9:40 PM HST, measuring wind speeds through microwave scattering of wind blowing over the ocean water, reported near Gale Force winds in the Kaiwi and Alenuihaha channels last night. This satellite wind report and our forecast for increasing winds through the day led to issuing Gale Warnings for the windiest channels and Wind Advisories through 6 PM HST for windier land areas over the down wind of island mountain ranges from Oahu eastward to the Big Island of Hawaii. Kauai and Niihau will likely remain just below our Wind Advisory thresholds today.
Enhanced showers will likely continue through much of this week. Initially, we see a low level cloud band moving across the islands from the north from Monday afternoon into Tuesday. Then a passing weak upper low tracks from west to east across the islands on Wednesday and Thursday lifting cloud heights, and finally we see yet another low level cloud band riding into the islands on the trade winds by Friday. In essence, these passing weather systems will keep periods of enhanced showers in the forecast into next weekend. Light to moderate beneficial rainfall amounts are expected in this weeks wet trade wind weather pattern.
