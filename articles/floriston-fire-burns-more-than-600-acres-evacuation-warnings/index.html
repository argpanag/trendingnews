Floriston Fire burns more than 600 acres, evacuation warnings in place

Crews are working to control a brush fire burning near 1-80 and Floriston Way in Floriston, California, that broke out Wednesday morning.

The fire began around 10 a.m. on Sept. 16 and has burned 640 acres and is 5% contained as of Thursday morning.

Authorities say the fire remained within last night’s perimeter, with dozer line along portions of the south side and hand line around portions of the fire. Two spot fires were identified to the north.

The north side of the fire, toward Reno, remains the priority due to open line and areas where existing line needs improvement.

Westbound I-80 remains closed from approximately the state line through the fire area due to safety concerns, including the potential for material to roll onto the highway.

Evacuation warnings are in effect for the following locations:

  • NCO-379-B (Nevada County)
  • NCO-E105 (Nevada County)
  • NCO-E106 (Nevada County)
  • SIE-E099 (Sierra County)
  • SIE-E100-B (Sierra County)

Evacuation orders are in effect in the following locations:

  • NCO-E379-A

Authorities say those who require additional time to evacuate, and those with pets and livestock, should consider leaving now.

“Be Ready to Evacuate" warnings in Washoe County are available here.

Residents within these areas should remain aware of current conditions and be prepared should conditions change.

Northern Nevada Public Health’s (NNPH) Air Quality Management Division (AQMD) issued an Air Quality Advisory for the Reno-Sparks area due to smoke from regional wildfires until further notice.

NNPH officials say the Air Quality Index (AQI) is expected to reach the Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (USG) to Unhealthy range for PM2.5 in the Verdi and North Valleys areas Wednesday afternoon into Thursday morning due to the Floriston Fire, based on air quality models and sensors.

In addition, AQI in Eastern Sparks and Spanish Springs may reach the Moderate to USG Wednesday afternoon and into Thursday morning due to the Dome Fire, based on air quality models and sensors.

Truckee Meadows Fire & Rescue (TMFR)says TMFR units are staging at Gold Ranch in the event the fire spreads to Washoe County. The Reno Fire Department is sending crews to assist.

The cause of the fire is under investigation.
