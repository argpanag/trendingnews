Ryan Fox explains the challenge of playing with Rory McIlroy — 'I'm still getting used to it' - AOL

TheTools · 20.09.2026 18:11 · source · permalink

Ryan Fox and Rory McIlroy have seen plenty of each other at the BMW PGA Championship, and the Open champion admits he is still getting used to playing in the six-time major winner's orbit.

The New Zealander was paired with McIlroy for the opening three rounds at Wentworth, and he rose to the occasion of playing alongside the crowd favorite. Fox is handily placed at 10 under par, just three shots off the lead shared by Manuel Elvira and Filippo Celli.

McIlroy is one shot better than Fox after a sensational birdie-eagle finish at the West Course to fire a warning shot to the leaders.

The Northern Irishman is the main attraction most places he goes, but the spotlight is squarely on him this week as the superstar of the DP World Tour and a new resident of the Wentworth estate.

Glance behind McIlroy while he plays and you will inevitably see a large group of security, VIPs and TV operators following his every move.

It is new territory for Fox, and he will have to get used to it. As the Champion Golfer of the Year, he is going to be in plenty of marquee groups alongside the biggest names in the game. He knows the exposure leaves him with nowhere to hide.

"It's been pretty incredible the last three days. It's still something I'm trying to get used to a little bit. But I felt like I handled it pretty well the last couple of days," he said.

"It's great to see that the fans are right behind him and pretty much shouting at every step he takes, and it shows his class to finish how he did and put himself right in the hunt for tomorrow. That atmosphere on 18 when he hit that shot, it's pretty hard to beat.

Fox vanquished Cam Young and Sam Burns to win the Claret Jug at Birkdale in June, and his hopes of winning at Wentworth for the second time are alive after a two-under-par 70.

On the added commotion inside the ropes, the 39-year-old said: "There's obviously a lot more going around. I've played in some big groups in the past, and I think I'm going to play in a few more going forward

"It's good. I feel like it really gets you into the tournament straight away. There's no hiding from anyone, and it's great when you're playing well.

"I played pretty well those first two days and kind of got it round OK for the most part today. ButI think it's tough when you're playing bad. There's nowhere to hide as well. Thankfully, I didn't have much of that this week."
