Trump strikes deal with Putin for Russia to supply diesel to US

TheTools · 09.10.2026 22:43 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Oil and Gas

Topic:Oil and Gas

Donald Trump (right) says he had a "highly successful" phone call with Vladimir Putin over diesel supply.  (Reuters: Kevin Lamarque)

In short: 

Donald Trump says he has reached an agreement with Vladimir Putin for Russia to supply diesel to the US.

Mr Trump signed sweeping sanctions against Russia last month aimed at clamping down on Russia's energy revenues. 

Analysts have cast doubt on how much the deal will ease near record-high diesel prices in the US.

US President Donald Trump says he has struck a deal to obtain diesel from Russia, up-ending years of Washington pressuring Moscow over its war with Ukraine. 

The price of diesel, used for agriculture, heating homes and trucking, sits near a record high in the US. 

Mr Trump said on social media he had a "highly successful" discussion with Russian President Vladimir Putin in which they agreed Moscow would immediately supply more than 300,000 metric tonnes of diesel to US and global markets.

Energy minister Chris Bowen told Weekend Breakfast that Australia "doesn't need or want Russian fuel". 

"We have the arrangements in place as best as we possibly can with our trading partners to ensure that Russian fuel doesn't flow to Australia," he said.

"We do not want Vladimir Putin to benefit from this war in the Middle East." 

Mr Bowen stopped short of condemning the United States but reiterated Australia was firmly against Russia being rewarded "for their illegal and immoral invasion of Ukraine by any arrangements put in place". 

An envoy for Mr Putin, Kirill Dmitriev, praised the cooperation between Russia and the US in a post on X shortly after the call.

Only last month Mr Trump signed a sweeping sanctions law aimed at clamping down on Russia’s energy revenues and other areas that are fuelling its war against Ukraine.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy criticised the US leader's new stance.

"Gifts to Putin will not bring peace or any benefit to the civilised world," he said.

"Russia will repay the diesel with further terror and perfidy."

The US Treasury Department issued a general licence on Friday, local time, allowing the importation of Russian diesel until April 7.

Mr Trump said Russia would supply another 500,000 tonnes in November and a further 1 million tonnes "immediately thereafter". 

Even more would follow, Mr Trump said, "based on the condition of their diesel refineries", which have been damaged by Ukrainian attacks.

The White House did not immediately respond to questions about who was paying for the diesel from Russia and when the fuel being supplied under the agreement would become available.

It also did not clarify whether subsequent supplies of Russian diesel set to come next month would be supplied prior to the US midterm elections on November 3.

Doubts over deal's effect on fuel prices 

Analysts said the agreement was unlikely to lead to sustained lower fuel prices. 

"I cannot overstate how much of a nothing burger this is," Rory Johnston, an oil market researcher and founder of CommodityContext.com, said on X, adding that Russia usually exported much more diesel when its refinery fleet was not under attack.

The 300,000 tonnes to be immediately delivered is equal to about 2.25 million barrels of diesel. 

The US exports about 1.5 million barrels of diesel per day.

"It's clearly not a fix, but another stream to aid a very tight diesel market," Jim Mitchell, an analyst at consultancy Wood Mackenzie, said.

Diesel prices, which can drive inflation, are up 70 per cent since the US and Israel launched the war with Iran on February 28.

The wars in Iran and Ukraine have triggered a severe global fuel supply crunch, pushing average US diesel prices to $US6.28 a gallon ($2.37 a litre) on Thursday, according to the AAA motorist group.

The price climb is despite Mr Trump's two recent efforts to boost fuel supplies: pressuring allies ‌to release emergency reserves and expanding access to tax-exempt red-dyed diesel, which is normally used for farm equipment.

US diesel futures HOc1 fell sharply following news of the deal and were trading at $US4.64 a gallon, down more than 4.8 per cent. 

"Lower prices for Americans, especially our Great Farmers, Ranchers, and Truckers, is my Greatest Priority," Mr Trump said.

Future disruptions to diesel supply expected

Three industry sources said Mr Trump would, in the coming days, direct some US department heads to find ways to control diesel prices.

The directive, which they said could take the form of a presidential memo, will push officials to find ways to bypass local and state regulations blocking energy production and use the Cold War-era Defense Production Act (DPA) to increase the output of oil and fuel.

Under the DPA, the president can authorise US-backed loans or loan guarantees to expand domestic manufacturing of critical materials and require companies to prioritise government contracts for essential goods.

Donald Trump says Russia will immediately deliver 300,000 tons of diesel.

The White House has been weighing how to use the DPA to expand refining capacity as the war with Iran has exposed US vulnerability to supply disruptions and price spikes.

Refining executives told administration officials last month that federal money would be better directed ​toward making refineries more efficient or expanding plants rather than financing a new refinery, which would be more costly and ⁠take years to complete.
