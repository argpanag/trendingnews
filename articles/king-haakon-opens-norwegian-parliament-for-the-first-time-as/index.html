King Haakon Opens Norwegian Parliament for the First Time as Sovereign, But Without Queen Mette-Marit

TheTools · 02.10.2026 13:40 · source · permalink

Queen Mette-Marit, however, did not attend the ceremony.

King Haakon VIII and Crown Princess Ingrid Alexandra arrived at Norway’s parliament building in Oslo on Friday for the state opening of the 171st session of the Storting, the country’s unicameral legislature. It was the first time the king had presided over the ceremony as sovereign, following the death of his father, King Harald V, in August.

However, like at King Haakon’s Oath of Allegiance ceremony last month, Queen Mette-Marie was noticeably absent. According to Norwegian broadcaster NRK, the palace said she was unable to attend due to a respiratory infection while continuing her recovery from a lung transplant this summer. The Queen and the heir to the throne traditionally accompany the King to the state opening, forming part of the royal procession and standing alongside him during the ceremony—making Queen Mette-Marit’s absence particularly visible on an occasion that is, by design, a public display of the royal family united before the nation.

Under Article 74 of the Norwegian Constitution, the monarch delivers the Speech from the Throne, a presentation of the government’s policy agenda for the coming year written by the government itself and handed to the king by the prince minister during the ceremony. A member of the government then delivers the State of the Realm Address, traditionally given by the youngest minister and delivered in Nynorsk, one of Norway’s two official written languages.

Crown Princess Ingrid Alexandra, who stood to her father’s left during the ceremony, appeared dressed entirely in black, in observance of the royal family’s ongoing official mourning period following King Harald’s death on August 28. The mourning period was set to run for one month after the state funeral on September 9, concluding on October 9.

Coincidentally, Friday also marked 35 years to the day since King Harald V opened the Storting for the first time, on October 2, 1991. The Norwegian parliament usually opens every autumn, traditionally during the first or second week of October.
