UP Public Service Commission Lacks Legislative Competence To Decide Reservation Policy: Allahabad High Court Quashes 2020 Office Memorandum

The Allahabad High Court has struck down the Office Memorandum dated 09.01.2020 issued by the Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission, holding that the Commission has no legislative competence to decide the stage at which a more meritorious reserved category candidate is to be placed in the unreserved category, reservation in public services being a matter within the domain of the State.

The Office Memorandum provided that candidates belonging to the reserved categories who had taken no relaxation in an essential condition would be accommodated in the unreserved category at the time of final selection if they had secured more marks than the cut-off marks fixed for the unreserved category, and would otherwise remain in their respective category.

Justice Manish Kumar Nigam held,

“the Office Memorandum dated 9.01.2020 issued by the Commission on the basis of its meeting dated 9.01.2020 lacks legislative competence and is not supported by any law made by the State Government or any of the Government Orders issued by the State Government and, therefore, is liable to be struck down.”

Petitioners, who had appeared in the preliminary examination of the APO Examination, 2025 conducted by the Commission, pleaded that the Office Memorandum was without jurisdiction, as reservation in public services lies in the domain of the legislature or the Government, and every action of the Commission is subject to the statute relating to reservation and to the rules and Government Orders issued in that regard. It was submitted that Article 320, which provides for the functions of the Commission, does not authorise it to issue any office memorandum contrary to the statutory provisions, rules or Government Orders.

Counsel for the State submitted that the Commission acts as an independent body with authority to frame its own enactment and to be guided by the rules made under it, and that in view of clause (3) of Article 315 of the Constitution, it is empowered to frame such law as may be necessary or desirable for giving effect to the functions it is to discharge under the Constitution.

The Court held the submission to be wholly misconceived. It observed that clause (3) of Article 315 refers to the law mentioned in clause (2), that is, a law which Parliament may make providing for the appointment of a Joint State Public Service Commission to serve the needs of a group of States, and permits such a law to contain incidental and consequential provisions.

“By any stretch of imagination, it cannot be held that clause (3) of Article 315 provides a legislative competence to the Public Service Commission to enact any law.”

The Court relied on the decision of the Supreme Court in State of Punjab v. Manjit Singh, in which the stand of a Commission that, being an independent body not subservient to any authority or the State Government, it was competent to lay down the minimum efficiency standard including matters falling within the purview of Article 335, was held to be erroneous.

It also rejected the contention that the Commission comes within the expression 'other authorities' in Article 12, agreeing with the Division Bench of the Mysore High Court in B.S. Kesava Iyengar v. State of Mysore, where it was held that the functions of the Commission are those enumerated in Article 320, which include the duty to conduct examinations for appointments to the services.

“The Public Service Commission can only frame rules for the purposes of providing procedure for recruitment.”

The Court relied on the judgment of a Division Bench of this Court in Bhavna Yadav v. State of U.P., decided by the Lucknow bench on 29.09.2026, holding that the reservation policy applicable to public services in Uttar Pradesh is governed by the Act of 1994 and the Government Orders issued by the State, these being matters purely within the domain of the State and referable to Articles 16(4) and 16(4-A), and that conditions of recruitment and service are the domain of the employer under Article 309, which in this case is the State of U.P.

It held that the Commission is not the employer under Article 320(1) but has the duty to conduct examinations, and Article 320(4) prohibits its being consulted as respects the manner in which provision under Article 16(4) may be made or effect given to Article 335. Its power extends to the procedure or method of recruitment, subject to the enactments, rules and Government Orders applicable to the post in question, held the Court.

The Division Bench had also held that Section 11 of the Uttar Pradesh Public Service Commission (Regulation of Procedure) Act, 1985, relied upon by the Commission to defend the Office Memorandum, empowers it only to make rules for the regulation of its procedure, and cannot be read to confer a jurisdiction which the Constitution does not confer upon it.

Counsel for the respondents submitted that the Division Bench had not held the Office Memorandum to be ultra vires, but had merely asked the Commission to consider whether it had the authority to decide matters pertaining to reservation, and had observed that it would be better for the State Government to come up with a comprehensive policy.

The Court observed that the Division Bench had held the Office Memorandum to be beyond the competence of the Commission, and had refrained from setting it aside only because the Commission had taken the stand that it had no application to the recruitment before it.

The Court further recorded that no other statutory rule or Government Order prohibiting the placement of a more meritorious reserved category candidate in the open category had been brought to its notice.

“Except for the Office Memorandum dated 09.01.2020 issued by the UPPSC, there is no provision prohibiting the shift of more meritorious reserved category candidate to the open category.”

Accordingly, the Court quashed the Office Memorandum dated 09.01.2020. In the same judgment, it also quashed the result of the preliminary examination of the APO Examination, 2025 declared on 30.04.2026 and directed the Commission to redraw the preliminary examination merit list and hold the mains examination afresh on the basis of the revised result.
