UC Berkeley Opens On-Campus Twitch Studio For Student-Athlete Content

TheTools · 16.10.2026 12:38 · source · permalink

Tech

By

Dragomir Stojkov

StreamU, the college athletics livestreaming network run by TheLinkU in partnership with Twitch, has opened its first on-campus content studio at the University of California, Berkeley. The space gives Cal student-athletes across all sports a dedicated location to produce streams for the school’s StreamU channel on Twitch, with a content calendar already set for 10 hours of weekly programming.

The planned content includes athlete workouts, teammate challenges, gaming sessions, cooking contests, campus tours, watch parties, and game-day coverage. The studio was built with technology partner UnlimitedIRL and designed around Cal’s branding. It pairs Canon and Sony cameras, professional lighting and audio, and a Blackmagic Design video switcher for multi-camera streams, alongside a PlayStation 5 gaming lounge. A portable Sony FX3 camera and LiveU encoder let athletes stream from locations around campus.

“We are excited about this new venture with StreamU,” said Ron Rivera, Cal Football General Manager and Athletic Director, in a press release. “Providing our student-athletes with the resources and infrastructure to tell their stories and enhance their NIL potential is a significant priority for our football program and athletic department.”

Taylor Webb, Chief of Operations at UnlimitedIRL, said the studio was designed so athletes “can share their story with fans and go live with a professional, multi-camera show,” calling it “the model we’re bringing to every StreamU campus.”

TheLinkU, founded in 2022 by Austin Elrod, manages contracts, fund distribution and reporting for the network. Landon Goesling, chief revenue officer of StreamU, said the goal is to give Cal “the infrastructure to make athlete-driven content a regular part of its athletics program, while giving student-athletes more ways to build an audience and create value from it.”

Additional StreamU studios are planned at the University of Arkansas, Texas A&M University, Baylor University, and the University of Houston in the coming weeks.
