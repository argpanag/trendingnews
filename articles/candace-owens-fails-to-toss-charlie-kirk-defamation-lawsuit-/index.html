Candace Owens Fails to Toss Charlie Kirk Defamation Lawsuit - LAmag

TheTools · 23.11.2026 20:16 · source · permalink

The conservative podcaster suffered a setback in federal court after a judge refused to dismiss a defamation lawsuit brought by Charlie Kirk’s former security chief

Lauren Conlin

Candace Owens wanted Brian Harpole’s defamation lawsuit tossed out…and she wanted him to foot her legal bills, too.

A federal judge has now rejected both requests, handing the controversial commentator a bit of a setback in the lawsuit against her by Charlie Kirk’s former security chief.

Chief U.S. District Judge William L. Campbell Jr. denied a motion filed by Owens and her companies, Candace Owens LLC and GeorgeTom Inc., to dismiss the lawsuit and strike portions of Harpole’s complaint. The decision was filed Friday, Nov. 20.

The judge also shot down their attempt to use Tennessee’s anti-SLAPP law, which is designed to protect people from certain lawsuits targeting their free speech, to have the case dismissed and have her attorney’s fees covered.

Harpole, who previously oversaw security for Kirk, sued Owens and retired Army sergeant Mitchell Snow in April, accusing them of spreading false claims that linked him to an alleged conspiracy around Kirk’s assassination. Kirk was shot and killed Sept. 10, 2025, while speaking at Utah Valley University in Orem, Utah. Current defendant Tyler Robinson has been charged with his murder and faces the possibility of the death penalty if convicted. His trial is scheduled for 2028.

As Los Angeles previously reported, Harpole alleges Owens’ coverage went from questioning the security failures around Kirk’s murder to pushing claims and conspiracies that he attended a secret meeting with high-ranking military officials at Fort Huachuca, Arizona…just one day before Kirk was killed.

Harpole denies going to the alleged meeting; he said he was in Dallas at the time and has never even been to Fort Huachuca. Harpole’s lawsuit details that Owens had access to travel records contradicting the story but kept going with her version of events anyway.

Owens has denied wrongdoing and argued that her commentary consisted of protected opinions, questions and criticism about a matter of significant public interest.

In an August filing, Owens’ attorneys argued that Harpole had voluntarily entered the public debate surrounding Kirk’s assassination (he appeared on the Shawn Ryan Podcast) and should be held to a higher legal standard when trying to prove defamation. Her lawyers also argued that Harpole had failed to show that Owens’ claims caused financial and reputational damage he says he suffered.

But Campbell wasn’t convinced.

In his order, he wrote that Owens’ attorneys presented arguments based on facts favorable to their own client, something the court can’t do when considering a motion to dismiss. Judges must temporarily accept the plaintiff’s factual allegations as true and give the plaintiff the benefit when deciding whether a lawsuit can move forward.

Campbell found that Harpole’s complaint had enough facts to meet that standard.

Owens’ attorneys also submitted transcripts from several podcasts and interviews to support their argument that the lawsuit should be dismissed. But Harpole’s attorneys objected, and the judge decided not to consider those transcripts when making his ruling.

Owens’ second argument involved Tennessee’s Public Participation Act, commonly known as an anti-SLAPP law. The law is meant to protect people from certain lawsuits that could discourage them from using their free speech rights.

Her attorneys argued that the lawsuit concerned protected speech about Kirk’s assassination and that Harpole should be responsible for their legal expenses. Campbell disagreed, explaining that federal courts in Tennessee have repeatedly ruled that the state procedures for dismissing lawsuits don’t align with federal court rules.

He noted that Owens’ attorneys had not identified a federal court decision or case law supporting their position. The ruling means Owens and her companies have failed in their latest attempt to end Harpole’s case before it goes further.

The lawsuit is now able to move toward discovery, during which both sides may be required to share documents, communications and other evidence related to Owens’ podcasts and the sources behind her claims. Owens previously claimed that she welcomed discovery because she wanted access to additional information about Kirk’s murder and the security operation around him.

The lawsuit also names Mitchell Snow, who allegedly claimed to have witnessed Harpole leaving the secret military meeting. Snow has separately filed a dismissal, arguing that the Tennessee federal court lacks jurisdiction over him.

It looks like Owens’ legal fight is far from over.
