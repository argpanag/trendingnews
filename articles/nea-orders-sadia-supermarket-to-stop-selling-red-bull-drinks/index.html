NEA orders Sadia Supermarket to stop selling Red Bull drinks without deposit labels

TheTools · 21.11.2026 09:29 · source · permalink

AI-translated article with transliterated names. Read original Chinese article here.

Sadia Supermarket at Tuas South Recreation Centre has been ordered to immediately stop selling Red Bull drinks it imported because they lack the deposit labels required under the Beverage Container Return Scheme. The supermarket had not applied for a grace period to clear beverage stocks that did not comply with the new rules.

The National Environment Agency (NEA) said in a Facebook post on Saturday (21 November) that it had found Red Bull drinks sold at Sadia Supermarket without the deposit labels required under the Beverage Container Return Scheme (BCRS). It instructed the supermarket to stop selling the drinks immediately and issued a stern reminder of the legal consequences of such breaches.

To help small retailers and non-profit organisations clear old stocks, NEA had allowed eligible operators to apply for a grace period to sell drinks without deposit labels until 31 October. Sadia Supermarket, however, did not apply. It has complied with NEA’s instructions and removed the affected drinks from its shelves.

NEA stressed that the incident involved only Red Bull drinks imported and sold by Sadia Supermarket. Other drinks registered under and compliant with the scheme are not affected. Consumers can continue to return eligible empty bottles and cans at reverse vending machines to claim their 10-cent deposit for each container.

The Beverage Container Return Scheme was fully implemented on 1 October this year, covering drinks in plastic bottles and metal cans with capacities ranging from 150ml to three litres. About 1,500 reverse vending machines are now operating islandwide.
