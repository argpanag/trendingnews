Malcolm Offord has once again shown he has no respect for Holyrood

MALCOLM Offord has once again shown that he has no respect for the Scottish Parliament.

Reform UK’s Scottish leader could have used his time at First Minister’s Questions on Thursday to press John Swinney on the controversy surrounding NHS Grampian.

After all, this is what Scottish Labour leader Michael Marra did, and Russell Findlay followed suit for the Conservatives.

Today’s FMQs also took place less than one hour after news broke that Scotland’s chief medical officer Gregor Smith had resigned, citing mental health pressures.

Logically, that would have been the most obvious line of attack for Offord. Yet instead, he chose to spend his time talking about EU students – a topic that may have appeased supporters on social media, but did little to hold the Scottish Government to account on an issue which is dominating the political agenda.

Reform UK Scotland leader Malcolm Offord (centre) pictured in September (Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)

Swinney did not hold back in his response, comparing Scottish Labour’s sensitive approach to FMQs with Offord’s question.

“Can I also establish quite a contrast between the issues and the manner of Mr Marra's approach on an incredibly sensitive issue to try to make progress in the public interest in Scotland, and that,” the First Minister said, gesturing to Offord.

“We wonder why we've got a problem in discourse in our country, when the leader of the third largest party in this parliament talks about ‘sellout’, ‘grubby deal’.

“The language is just awful, and it's awful every single week. No wonder we've got a problem about discourse in our country, because of this discourse that we get.”

Opposition parties were equally unhappy with Offord, with Tory MSP Murdo Fraser saying he was “completely missing an open goal” and letting Swinney “off the hook” when the SNP Government is facing increasing pressure over NHS Grampian.

Fraser went on to say that Offord is “a gift to John Swinney” and that “all Reform are now doing is splitting the anti-SNP and anti-Labour vote”.

Less than 24 hours earlier, Offord demonstrated a similar disregard for Holyrood after failing to turn up to a debate on violence against women and girls.

Every other party leader was present for the debate and gave speeches, yet the Reform Scotland leader only showed up for the final vote.

His own deputy, Thomas Kerr, was missing for much of the debate, too (and was one of only two MSPs to vote against the motion, the other being ex-Reform MSP Amanda Bland).

When their absence was raised by SNP MSP Alex Kerr, who pointed out that both politicians were “self-proclaimed defenders of women”, Thomas Kerr raised a point of order to claim that he had only been missing “briefly”.

A quick check back through the Scottish Parliament recording shows that he was missing from the chamber for more than an hour, which arguably does not fall under the definition of “brief”.

The most frustrating thing is that this is a deliberate strategy. Reform have repeatedly pledged to "disrupt the establishment", and its approach to Holyrood looks increasingly designed to do exactly that.

The problem is that disruption and scrutiny are not the same thing. Opposing the Scottish Government is one thing; using Holyrood effectively is another. Time and time again, Reform MSPs seem more interested in generating controversy and clicks than engaging with the substance of parliamentary debate.

Compare this with the recent “influencergate” controversy sparked by social media star Max Balegde, who filmed himself outside No 10 miming explicit lyrics after attending a creator’s event with the Prime Minister.

Politicians and journalists were quick to accuse Balegde of “degrading” public discourse.

Perhaps they should instead be asking how different that is from politicians such as Offord treating Holyrood like their own personal reality TV set.
