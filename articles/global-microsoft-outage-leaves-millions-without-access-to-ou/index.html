Global Microsoft outage leaves millions without access to Outlook email, Teams and other tools | CNN Business

TheTools · 13.11.2026 04:38 · source · permalink

Where things stand

• Hours-long outage: A massive Microsoft outage has left millions around the world without access to Outlook email, Teams meeting tools and other staples of modern office life. Banks, governments and courts in multiple countries are affected.

• Global disruption: Outage reports “reached 74 countries and territories in about an hour,” according to Downdetector. In the US, fed-up workers at some companies went home early on Thursday. In Australia, businesses couldn’t print invoices and some schools couldn’t hold online classes on Friday.

• No quick fix: As Europe woke on Friday morning, the outage was still widespread. Microsoft initially said it was “seeing positive results” with efforts to resolve the issue. But hours later it said there was no “sustained recovery.” Microsoft said the outage was not caused by a cybersecurity breach.

Updates

Here's where we're seeing the outage's impacts

At first, Microsoft said the connection issues were happening “in the Americas,” but over time it became clear the troubles were global.

Microsoft’s outage reports “reached 74 countries and territories in about an hour,” according to Downdetector, a website that tracks cyber outages.

Here’s where we’ve seen reports of outages:

  • Australian schools, courts and government services started their Friday morning with Microsoft Outlook and other apps out of service.
  • In Hong Kong, workers from banks including Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs told CNN their work was distrupted, as they were unable to access emails or Teams from the moment they signed on and all through their lunch break. “Sporadically some mails come in but there is probably millions of backlog to process,” one banker told CNN before heading off for (a rather longer) lunch break.
  • The disruption has also hit Japan’s government services, Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary Masanao Ozaki said in a Friday presser. Government employees are responding to the outage by creating physical copies of documents, he added. Government ministries and agencies have been unable to send emails through most of the Friday workday.
  • Courts in the Philippines are also being slowed down by the Microsoft disruption, the Supreme Court said. “During this period, responses to emails and other transactions may be delayed,” it added in a post on X.

CNN’s Brian Stelter, Hilary Whiteman and Junko Ogura contributed to this report.

Global outage not caused by cyber attack, Microsoft executive says

The global Microsoft outage was not caused by a cybersecurity breach, a Microsoft executive confirmed late Thursday.

“We will continue to share more information as soon as we have it, and for now I can confirm that there is no indication this is the result of malicious or cyber activity,” Charles Lamanna, executive vice president of Copilot, Agents, and Platform at Microsoft, said in a post on X.

Earlier, Australia’s Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said the company had told him that they “don’t believe they are dealing with a cyber attack.”

“They believe they are dealing in some ways similar to what we saw with respect to CrowdStrike where there is an update that has gone awry,” Burke added during a press conference in Sydney.

Burke was referring to the 2024 software glitch at cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike that crashed computers, canceled flights and disrupted hospitals all around the globe, causing billions of dollars in losses.

Australia’s spy agency and federal police were among the organizations hit by the Microsoft outage on Friday local time.

Microsoft outage shows global risks of companies relying on a handful of tech giants, says expert

The Microsoft outage highlights the “inherent dangers” of companies that depend on a handful of tech giants for critical global infrastructure, an expert told CNN.

“If one of those firms suffers a major outage, it has the potential to dramatically reduce business productivity globally during the outage time,” Computing Professor Toby Murray, of Australia’s University of Melbourne, said.

Murray added that the outage affecting Outlook and other major services like Microsoft Teams is “incredibly widespread, and its ramifications extend well beyond being able to send and receive work emails,” making it difficult for businesses to operate.

Microsoft says still no "sustained recovery"

Microsoft said it had not yet observed a “sustained recovery” in areas affected by the widespread outage.

“We’re continuing our efforts to improve server stability to improve traffic flow,” it said late on Thursday in a post to its X account for Microsoft365 service incidents.

“We’re observing partial recovery across regions in which we’ve applied these adjustments; however, these actions aren’t resulting in sustained recovery.”

Outage causes headaches for some and jokes for others

Some US office workers celebrated the email blackout and joked that they could start the weekend early.

Others barely missed a beat, connecting with colleagues via Slack, phone and even the old-fashioned in-person way.

But the outage, which began around noon on the US East Coast, caused serious headaches, especially when it dragged on for more than an hour or two.

In Australia, Friday began with a realization that the Microsoft cloud was unresponsive. The outage took out live feeds from courts, and schools texted parents to phone in any absences until email systems were restored.

Australia’s national broadcaster, ABC, reported people missing job interviews because they couldn’t connect to virtual meetings, and even some supply issues with distribution centers stocking supermarkets due to the outage.

And there was no quick fix. As of 3:30 a.m. GMT on Friday, the outage was still widespread, affecting untold millions of people.

Microsoft is posting updates on X and on its “service health status” page.

But in another post at 3:13 a.m. GMT, the company said, “We’ve monitored the status of the changes applied to improve serviceability and confirmed they’re not taking as expected.”

“We’re identifying alternate workstreams that can be applied to increase capacity in the face of sustained traffic management imbalances,” the company added, leaving users on X befuddled.
