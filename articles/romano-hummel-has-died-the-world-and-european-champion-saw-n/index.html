The track racing community mourns the loss of Romano Hummel, one of the most successful riders from the Netherlands. The 27-year-old took his own life on 21 October 2026.

By

Ivo Schützbach and Rudi Hagen

22.10.2026 - 07:19

This article is an automatically generated English version. The Original Article was published in German.

To be so desperate that one’s own life no longer makes sense, that darkness takes the place of hope, is a terrible thought. When the person affected feels that this is the only way out. Such a step leaves behind questions and bewilderment; it is almost impossible to understand or come to terms with. What remains is the loss of a loved one. On 21 October 2026, Romano Hummel, aged 27, decided to take his own life.

Advertisement

Advertisement

He is one of the most successful speedway riders the Netherlands has ever produced. In 2021, Hummel became the World Long-Track Champion and in 2023 the World Team Champion; in 2022 and 2023, he also won the European Grass-Track Championship. At the end of September 2024, following a nasty crash at the Grand Prix in Vechta, he announced his retirement due to health problems.

Once he had recovered, Hummel returned to the oval and was immediately successful again. In 2026, he became European Champion for the third time on his home track in Aduard and, riding for the Netherlands, won gold for the second time in the Team World Championship in front of a home crowd in Roden.

Hummel had already announced at the end of August that he would be retiring for good at the end of this season. The reasons were of a personal nature, he told SPEEDWEEK.com. Looking back, he offered deep insights at the time, but the signs were difficult to interpret. “Now is the time to get my life back on track. I’ve got so many things on my mind, especially because of my children. My career is coming to an end; I’ve experienced some glorious times.”

Advertisement

Advertisement

We will remember Romano as a magnificent fighter with a unique riding style. He wasn’t one for textbook left-foot control; his leg was already at the rear by the time he entered a bend. Whilst others needed their left leg as a balancing aid, Hummel had such complete control over his bike that he didn’t need it. Also striking were the fluttering fringes he wore on both forearms from early on in his magnificent career.

Our thoughts are with his children, family and friends. Romano, rest in peace.
