Fire grows to 390ha at Queenstown's Walter Peak

TheTools · 06.10.2026 14:59 · source · permalink

Firefighting efforts are set to resume at Queenstown's Walter Peak Station tomorrow as a vegetation fire grew to more than 390ha and sent smoke billowing across the area this afternoon.

Fire and Emergency NZ Assistant District Commander Nic McQuillan said about 25 firefighters and incident management support personnel were at the scene on the 1800m mountain, supported by six helicopters.

Initial efforts were focused on protecting the homestead as it began "spreading rapidly up the steep slopes of Mount Nicholas", he said.

"Smoke from the fire is being blown across the lake towards Jacks Point and Hanleys Farm, and is likely to move towards Queenstown."

Large plumes of smoke from a vegetation fire on Walter Peak. (Source: Dave Noble) (Source: Supplied)

At 3pm, the fire was mapped at 146ha with a perimeter of almost 6km. The fireground had since increased to 390ha as of this evening.

In an update this evening, McQuillan said there were "three areas of active fire", which was not contained.

"Efforts to protect the Walter Peak homestead and other buildings in its vicinity have been successful," he said.

"As the fire is still travelling quickly downwind it is feeding on patches of vegetation, which is causing smoke to intensify as it moves through those areas. Smoke will continue to drift across the lake towards Jacks Point and Hanleys Farm, so people in areas affected by the smoke should keep doors and windows shut to minimise their exposure."

Flames could be seen as fire crews respond to the blaze spreading up Mount Nicholas. (Source: Dave Noble) (Source: Supplied)

He said firefighters from Queenstown, Frankton, Glenorchy and Mossburn were "working on the western flank to consolidate the perimeter".

"They and the air attack will continue working until sunset, when operations will be suspended overnight."

Firefighters and aircraft will resume tackling the fire tomorrow morning.

"People should expect to see signs of fire overnight and into tomorrow."

In a final update just after 7.30pm this evening, McQuillan said a "significant area of fire" had been extinguished this afternoon, but the fire "is not yet contained or controlled".

"The main area of active fire is around McKinlays Creek, east of the Walter Peak homestead. Conditions remain windy with norwesterly winds forecast to continue tomorrow with some light rain likely in the afternoon."

McQuillan said people could expect to see signs of fire and smell smoke overnight.

"Those in areas affected by drifting smoke, including Hanleys Farm and Jacks Point, should keep doors and windows shut to avoid exposure to the smoke," he said.

Both ground and air operations will resume in the morning, with up to 15 firefighters supported by three helicopters with monsoon buckets, and a fourth helicopter used for directing the air attack.

Smoke billows across Queenstown

Flames visible from a fire at Walter Peak Station in Queenstown. (Source: 1News)

Earlier today, Fire and Emergency NZ said smoke from the fire was "clearly visible from Queenstown".

Photographs and footage sent to 1News by a witness from Bob's Cove, on the other side of the lake, showed thick smoke billowing out from the mountain over Lake Wakatipu.

The coal-fired passenger steamship TSS Earnslaw – which offers scenic cruises and excursions to Walter Peak High Country Farm, a working sheep station – appeared to have been diverted, the witness said.

Flames from Walter Peak in Queenstown. (Source: Dave Noble) (Source: Supplied)

Residents in areas affected by the smoke were advised to keep their doors and windows closed to avoid exposure.

Boat operators and people in private watercraft were advised to "stay well away" from the Walter Peak Station shore where helicopters are filling monsoon buckets, he said.

The fire had reignited from a planned burn in the area last Wednesday.

Fire crews and helicopters are working to control the blaze. (Source: Dave Noble) (Source: Supplied)
