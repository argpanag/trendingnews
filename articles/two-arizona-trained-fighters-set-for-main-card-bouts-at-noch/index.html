Two Arizona-trained fighters set for main card bouts at Noche UFC

TheTools · 13.09.2026 00:44 · source · permalink
  • Two Phoenix-area MMA fighters, Tommy McMillen and Waldo Cortes Acosta, are set to compete at Noche UFC.
  • Both fighters have expressed a connection to the Mexican fan base expected at the Glendale event.

Tommy McMillen and Waldo Cortes Acosta are pro mixed martial arts fighters from vastly different backgrounds who happen to live and train in the Phoenix area. And both have a big opportunity on Saturday, Sept. 12, at Noche UFC at Desert Diamond Arena in Glendale.

McMillen, a brash Montana native, will look to back up his words about his featherweight opponent, Marwan Rahiki.

"I was a pretty depressed kid growing up," McMillen said. "I just had this fire burning in me that I just really wanted to be in the UFC to be able to change my life financially and inspire other kids around the world to do the same thing."

To that end, McMillen left Montana for Peoria's Red Hawk Academy MMA and ju-jitsu school years ago, and is now 11-0 going into his fight against Rahiki.

The two got into a scuffle after Noche UFC media day interviews at a downtown Phoenix hotel.

"I'm knocking dudes out for 100k bonuses and I'm breaking world records in the middle of all of it," McMillen said on Sept. 9. "I just really like to fight, bro. I am so calm and confident when I get in that cage. It feels like home to me.

"People are saying to me it's got to feel good to be fighting at home (in Arizona), but I'll go beat this kid's ass in the backyard in Morocco and I'll still feel at home."

McMillen is big on social media and insists that anyone dropping his name on Instagram gets more views and followers. His detractors say he's too much about the social life, but McMillen feeds off it.

"I love the food, the parties, the nightlife, it's everything a guy like me needs," McMillen said. "So just having my home and my gym here is just the cherry on top."

He plans to appeal to the Mexican fan base that will fill the seats on Saturday.

"There's no more fighting spirit than in a Mexican fighter, and I think I am one of the most 'Mexican' fighters to appear on the card," McMillen said.

Cortes Acosta (17-3) is a 6-foot-4, 264-pound heavyweight from the Dominican Republic who came to Arizona to rehabilitate his right elbow after Tommy John surgery. He was a pitcher in the Cincinnati Reds organization.

He found the area to his liking and stayed, getting married and enjoying the lifestyle. He would cut short trips to the Dominican Republic to get back to Arizona.

In 2015, he made his amateur MMA debut and went 6-1 before switching to boxing in 2017. He went 6-4 in that sport and made his pro MMA debut in 2018, but COVID forced him to stick with boxing only, and it wasn't until 2021 that he could return to MMA.

Nicknamed "Salsa Boy," Cortes Acosta won his first eight pro fights and by 2022 was on UFC Fight Night rosters. He's continued to stack victories, and on Sept. 12, faces Curtis Blaydes on the main card at Noche UFC.

"Every time I walk out, I represent Arizona, I represent the Latinos, I represent Mexico, everyone," he said, noting he is married to a Mexican woman.

Cortes Acosta maintains he can still throw a fastball in the low-90s, but feels shoulder tightness when he does.

"Last week I taught at the batting cages," he said. "The kids listen to me."

Saturday in Glendale, McMillen and Cortes Acosta step to the plate and aim to let their fighting do the talking.
