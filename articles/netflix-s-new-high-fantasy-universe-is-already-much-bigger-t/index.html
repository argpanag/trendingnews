Netflix's New High Fantasy Universe Is Already Much Bigger Than Peak 'Game of Thrones'

TheTools · 20.10.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

Few current fantasy series compare to Game of Thrones, whether you're looking at the scope and scale of their storytelling or their popularity. The HBO show brought a sprawling and detailed fantasy narrative to a broad audience that actually embraced it. It's a juggling act even well-known follow-ups like The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power and The Witcher have yet to accomplish. However, there are several upcoming shows that could rival Game of Thrones, including Netflix's looming high fantasy universe.

Netflix has several exciting fantasy projects in the works, but none so much as its Dungeons & Dragons TV show. Titled Ravenloft, the live-action series will bring the tabletop RPG's gothic horror setting to the small screen. Described as a "fantasy series where adventure collides with gothic horror and tragic romance" (via Deadline), the show will tell the origin story of Darklord Strahd von Zarovich. It's an exciting prospect for anyone familiar with the villain, but it's also promising for anyone seeking the next sensation like Game of Thrones.

The two fantasy stories will clearly be very different, but in many ways, Ravenloft is already bigger than the HBO adaptation. It certainly has greater potential, given how much lore it can tap and the sizable audience that comes with it.

Netflix's 'Ravenloft' Has More Material and Lore to Pull From Than 'Game of Thrones'

In terms of world-building and lore, Ravenloft already has Game of Thrones beat. The Netflix show may be focused on a specific Dungeons & Dragons backdrop, but it can work decades' worth of material into its storytelling. The world of D&D is larger and far more complex than even George R.R. Martin's A Song of Ice & Fire universe. It gives the upcoming Netflix series plenty to work with.

While the origin story for Darklord Strahd von Zarovich comes with expectations, it's also not as constricted by its source material as Game of Thrones. The HBO show was always going to disappoint viewers by making changes to and going past the books. By contrast, Ravenloft has many different materials to pull from. It can combine the established lore however it sees fit, without limiting itself to certain specific plot points. That will allow it to avoid one of the biggest pitfalls of Game of Thrones: the book comparisons.

It could also cause the show to go on longer, as there's not necessarily a set timeline. If successful, Ravenloft could even branch out into a bigger on-screen universe. There's been demand for a Forgotten Realms series for years, and there are so many D&D settings and campaigns that would make for compelling television. Spin-offs are basically baked into Netflix's upcoming fantasy project. While that was also true of Game of Thrones, D&D makes the possibilities almost limitless.

'Dungeons & Dragons' Has the Cultural Influence to Reach 'Game of Thrones's Heights

Paramount Pictures

Paramount Pictures

In addition to having more expansive lore to dig into, Ravenloft also has something else going for it: Dungeons & Dragons's massive cultural influence. The most successful fantasy projects tend to be the ones with recognizable IPs behind them. They come with built-in audiences — which means that, as long as they don't alienate that established base, they're starting off with traction.

Of course, to reach Game of Thrones's heights, such series also need to appeal to mainstream viewers who don't necessarily follow fantasy. Most people have at least heard of Dungeons & Dragons, so it has an advantage in that. The streaming success of projects like Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves and The Legend of Vox Machina also bodes well. Stranger Things is another positive sign, proving that a series centered on D&D could resonate with newcomers and familiar audiences alike.

Needless to say, there are good reasons for Netflix to pour time, money, and effort into its Ravenloft series. With Apple TV tackling Sanderson's Cosmere, HBO still capitalizing on Game of Thrones, and Prime Video juggling multiple high-profile fantasy projects, this gives Netflix a leg up in the fantasy race.
