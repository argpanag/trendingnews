"By the fifth take, I was in love": Meryl Streep on falling for Robert Redford - 3DVF

TheTools · 04.09.2026 14:12 · source · permalink

Sitting on stage at Cannes in May 2024, fresh off receiving her Honorary Palme d’Or, Meryl Streep didn’t hold back when the conversation turned to Robert Redford and their 1985 film “Out of Africa”. She described the river scene where his character washes her hair while reciting Coleridge, and admitted the on-screen intimacy got the better of her: “By take five, I was so in love.” The moment, she explained, felt more charged than any conventional love scene, “a sex scene in a way, because it’s so intimate”, especially once her longtime hair and makeup artist Roy Helland had quietly coached Redford on technique between takes. Streep also recalled the shoot’s real dangers on location in Kenya, from imported lions to hippos, reportedly the continent’s deadliest animal to film crews. More than a year after Redford’s death in September 2025, that Cannes confession reads less like festival gossip and more like a tender goodbye to one of cinema’s great on-screen pairings.

A Cannes moment to remember

Meryl Streep rarely lets her guard down in public, but the Cannes Film Festival got a genuine confession out of her. On May 14, 2024, the actress received an Honorary Palme d’Or during the festival’s opening ceremony, then sat down for an onstage conversation with journalist Didier Allouch. The subject that stole the evening was Robert Redford and a river in Kenya.

The magic of the river scene

The anecdote centers on the hair-washing sequence from Out of Africa, Sydney Pollack’s 1985 film in which Streep played Baroness Karen Blixen opposite Redford’s big-game hunter Denys Finch Hatton. In the scene, his character gently shampoos her hair while reciting Samuel Taylor Coleridge’s poem The Rime of the Ancient Mariner. She admitted she’d fallen for her co-star well before the cameras stopped rolling, adding that the moment was “a sex scene in a way, because it’s so intimate.” She confessed she didn’t want the day of filming to end.

A shampoo perfected behind the camera

The tenderness on screen owed something to preparation. Streep’s hair and makeup artist of many years, Roy Helland, personally coached Redford on how to wash her hair properly, and his technique improved considerably after the first attempts. That blend of craft and genuine connection helped turn a quiet gesture into one of the most romantic passages of 1980s cinema, one Streep still ranks as her favorite love scene.

Lions, hippos and real danger in Kenya

The shoot itself was far less serene than the finished film suggests. Streep recalled that the production imported lions billed as tame, while the river location came with warnings about hippopotamuses, described to the crew as the animal that kills the most men in Africa. Even so, she said the intimacy of that day overrode the risk, hippos included.

A posthumous glow for two legends

The confession has taken on new weight since. Robert Redford died on September 16, 2025, at his home in Sundance, Utah, at age 89, making Streep’s Cannes recollection one of the last public tributes he heard in his lifetime. Four decades after its release, Out of Africa remains the only film the two stars made together, a single pairing that produced one of Hollywood’s most enduring on-screen romances.
