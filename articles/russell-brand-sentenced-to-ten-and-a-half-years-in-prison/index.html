Russell Brand Sentenced to Ten and a Half Years in Prison

TheTools · 09.11.2026 15:42 · source · permalink

United Kingdom

ARCHIVE – Russell Brand leaves Southwark Crown Court in London. Photo: Kin Cheung/AP/dpa

Keystone

From the spotlight to a prison cell: British actor and former comedian Russell Brand has been sentenced to ten and a half years in prison for rape and sexual assault. The judge announced the verdict at London’s Southwark Crown Court.

“Twenty years ago, you became famous, Russell Brand; you were intelligent, and in the eyes of many people, you were funny and charismatic,” Judge Joel Bennathan said, addressing the 51-year-old. He added, “You also became a sexual predator.” He wanted to “pay tribute to the determination and courage of all those women whose statements I have recounted here,” the judge continued, before announcing the sentence.

On Thursday, Brand was found guilty by a jury of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault and was immediately taken into custody due to the risk of him fleeing. According to the verdict, the former TV and stage star assaulted a female fan, who was 21 at the time, after a performance at a theater in London in 2009. The three counts of sexual assault took place between 2004 and 2009, at the height of his popularity. He was acquitted of other charges, including allegations of rape.

"As if it would never end"

For weeks, the British media followed the trial, during which the women involved gave harrowing accounts of the assaults. According to PA, one victim reported that after the assault, she had “lost all self-love.” Whenever she saw Brand in the press, she felt “physically sick.” In the media, the former comedian had been celebrated “for his repulsive behavior toward women”—“as if it were all just a joke.” Another woman described how she was unable to work for a while and had to move in with her parents. “This is something that haunts me every day, and it often feels as though it will never end.”

Brand himself had always denied the allegations. In court, he described being accused of rape as “devastating.” Friends and colleagues had turned their backs on him. His wife, Laura, stood by him during her testimony and painted a picture of a loving husband who knows how to respect boundaries. However, she hardly convinced anyone with her testimony.

From the outset, the prosecution had accused the actor of serious character flaws, describing him as unscrupulous and manipulative, for example. Brand had “abused the influence and power that came with his fame,” said prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones in her closing statement.

The Downfall of a “Colorful Character”

In court, there was no trace left of the star’s former self-assured demeanor; not a single provocative remark passed his lips as he was besieged by reporters outside the London courthouse. “The flamboyant figure with his grand gestures and provocative remarks was not the man we saw in the courtroom,” wrote BBC journalist Sima Kotecha, for example.

The 51-year-old rose to fame in the 2000s through his often provocative performances as a stand-up comedian and as a radio and television host for the BBC and MTV. He usually performed like a rock star. With roles in films such as “Never Have Sex with the Ex” (2008), he made the leap to international cinema.

He also made headlines for his tumultuous marriage to pop star Katy Perry (42), which fell apart after just 14 months, and for his drug use, which was often described as excessive. Most recently, old TV clips have been circulating on social media in which Brand expressed questionable views on sex and women. His defense attorney, Ian Winter, stated that Brand is now a “fundamentally different person” from the man who committed the crimes.

In court, the 51-year-old at least admitted that he used to be obsessed with sex. He said his popularity made it easy for him to engage in sexual encounters. Looking back, he considers this behavior to have been wrong. During the trial, he presented himself as a Christian penitent, appeared in the courtroom with a Bible in his hand, and said he felt “blessed.”

Police Chief: "Not Surprised" by Further Allegations

It is considered unlikely that the negative headlines will now subside. Just minutes after the guilty verdict was announced, new allegations against the former comedian began to surface. The London Metropolitan Police are investigating rape allegations made by two women. Brand has reportedly already been questioned about the allegations.

"I wouldn't be surprised if many, many more came forward," London Police Chief Mark Rowley told the BBC on Friday. He also praised the courage of the women who had gone public with their allegations.

British Minister for Media Lisa Nandy described Russell Brand’s conviction as a “wake-up call” for the media industry. “That’s because people in these industries often work on short-term, flexible contracts,” the Labour politician told Sky News. “There are enormous power imbalances, and anyone who speaks out against abuses risks their reputation and their career prospects.”
