Pidcock says Del Toro was 'working for McNulty' at Worlds. Is that actually true?

TheTools · 28.09.2026 05:21 · source · permalink

Members Only

A closer look at the finale shows one UAE rider doing his fair share of chasing another.

After coming into the elite men's road race at Montreal Worlds as an outside favorite on a climber-friendly course, Tom Pidcock ultimately had to settle for seventh place on Sunday. Although the 27-year-old Brit put in plenty of work, digging deep to follow moves in an aggressive final hour and generally looking to be in form, he arrived at the finish with a chase group a little ways behind solo winner Brandon McNulty.

In the aftermath of the race, Pidcock reflected on a finale where he and the other chasers faced the difficult task of trying to close McNulty down without simply towing other riders along. Having spent much of that time in close proximity to McNulty's American teammate Matteo Jorgenson, who policed one surge after another in the chase group, Pidcock duly tipped his cap to a "super strong" American team.

He did not stop there, however, instead making multiple callouts of another rival: Isaac del Toro, the young Mexican star who spends much of the year riding alongside McNulty with UAE-Team Emirates XRG.

"It was hard to make the difference, to get the gap with the wind the way it was, with the USA team and with Matteo, who was super strong once Brandon attacked, and obviously Isaac is on the same team as well," Pidcock said to television cameras after the race.

"Del Toro was obviously working for McNulty in the end there," Pidcock told Cyclingnews in a separate interview.

One big name calling out another after a world championship battle is, frankly, exactly the sort of thing that sports fans love to read about on websites just like this one (we can see the traffic). What's more, it is not only Pidcock who was saying it on Sunday evening. The idea was mentioned during the race broadcast and plenty of internet commenters have brought it up in various places as well, echoing a somewhat common refrain for this time of year when loyalties become muddled in the unusual environment of athletes representing their home nation rather than a professional bike racing team. At this point, it is not just one rider's spicy opinion, but a conversation, and one that we shouldn't ignore.

That said, it didn't feel right to simply broadcast Pidcock's comments to the world without providing some context – so we went back and watched the finale. Was Del Toro actually working for McNulty?

Obviously we can never actually know what's going on inside someone's head, and frequent Escape Collective readers might also point out that this author did just write about Team Mexico's chances and may therefore be predisposed to defend Del Toro here. Maybe that's true, but the tale of the tape has no such bias, and what it shows is that Del Toro did more work at the front than any of the big contenders on Sunday, he continued to spend time there pulling the chasers in the finale, he was dropped more than once in the last two laps, and he never jumped onto Pidcock's wheel the way Jorgenson did over and over again.

In short: If Del Toro was really "working for McNulty," he did not do a very good job of it.

McNulty's big attack came with around 32 km to go when Del Toro was at the front of the lead group, although Del Toro was clearly looking around to see if anyone else would take up the pace-setting. That made sense; he had spent quite some time working to bring back a group that included Mathieu van der Poel (Netherlands) and Quinn Simmons (United States) and then again working to chase down Jorgenson. McNulty was several riders back in the group when he launched, coming out from behind Van der Poel and sailing right by Pidcock as he went.

Pidcock, like everyone else in the group, watched McNulty go without reacting.

Did we do a good job with this story?
