Donald Trump arrives in Ireland as major security operation in place - follow live

TheTools · 12.09.2026 09:06 · source · permalink

Summary

  • US President Donald Trump has arrived in Dublin for the start of his two-day visit to the Republic of Ireland - watch live above

  • Trump is greeted with a guard of honour from the Irish military as he meets Irish President Catherine Connolly at her official residence in Phoenix Park

  • He will later hold a meeting with Taoiseach (Irish Prime Minister) Micheál Martin

  • Trump will then travel to his golf resort in Doonbeg, County Clare, where the Irish Open golf tournament is being held

  • Irish authorities have been planning for Trump's visit for months in what will be one of the biggest security operations in the history of the state

  • Protests against the president's visit are expected to be held in Dublin and Doonbeg

  13. What has Ireland's president Catherine Connolly said about Trump?published at 09:06 BST
    09:06 BST

    One of President Trump’s more interesting events today could be the courtesy call with Irish President Catherine Connolly.

    She is due to greet him at shortly at Ireland's presidential residence Áras an Uachtaráin.

    The two have very different political outlooks.

    During last year’s presidential election campaign, Connolly described Trump as “volatile, unpredictable” and accused him of “acting like a bully”.

    She has also been a long-standing critic of US foreign policy and the use of Shannon Airport by the US military.

    Connolly has been less directly critical of Trump since becoming president, but has continued to speak strongly about war and international law.

    Today’s courtesy call will be their first meeting since she took office last November.

  14. 'One of the biggest policing and security operations' in Ireland's historypublished at 08:57 BST
    08:57 BST

    As President Trump makes his way to Phoenix Park in Dublin for the arrival ceremony, a reminder that a huge security operation is in place for his visit.

    The head of Ireland's police force, Garda Commissioner Justin Kelly, has described it as one of the biggest operations the force has ever undertaken.

    He spoke to journalists at a press conference on Friday morning.

    An extraordinary event has been declared, with 10,000 12-hour Garda shifts planned between Friday and Sunday.

    Road closures and traffic restrictions will be in place in Dublin and County Clare, where the president is due to travel later on Saturday, with rolling closures around Dublin Airport this morning.

    “We’re doing everything we can to minimise disruption to the public but during an event such as this, it is inevitable so I would ask for people’s patience over the weekend particularly in Dublin and Clare."

    Gardaí say they are aware of protest groups planning to gather and will facilitate peaceful protests, but added "there will be no tolerance for public disorder, or attempts to impact critical infrastructure or movements of the President of the United States”.
