Daniil Medvedev doesn't believe Alexander Zverev has improved in one area, ‘I wouldn’t say it's better'

TheTools · 21.11.2026 22:58 · source · permalink

Daniil Medvedev lost to Alexander Zverev in the ATP Finals semi-finals.

The Russian star is a former ATP Finals champion, but he won’t add to his 2020 triumph this weekend. Zverev defeated Medvedev, 7-6 (7-0), 6-4, in Turin, taking control when it mattered most.

After the match, Medvedev was asked several questions about Zverev’s game during his press conference. One journalist asked the world number five whether he believed Zverev’s forehand had improved.

Daniil Medvedev doesn’t think Alexander Zverev’s forehand has improved

“I think his serve is better. Forehand? I think I will put it this way: I think his forehand was very good even before, and then sometimes he can miss. Today he also missed a couple, so I would not say it’s better,” said Medvedev.

Medvedev has a point, as evidenced by the statistics from his semi-final defeat to Zverev. The German hit six forehand winners, but seven forehand unforced errors on Friday evening. It was far from an error-strewn performance, although if you wanted to highlight an area of improvement, it would be the serve, not the forehand.

Zverev’s serve had always been dangerous, but more often than not, it fell apart when the pressure was on. That’s not the case anymore. Zverev’s serve has held up in some of the biggest moments of his season, like when he won the French Open and US Open titles. Comparing his numbers from previous years, you can see just how much the shot has improved.

It’s been the serve that’s guided Zverev to the ATP Finals championship match this year. He will likely be tested on the forehand, too, against Carlos Alcaraz, whom he faces for the title on Sunday, November 22.

Daniil Medvedev responds when asked if he will become more ‘aggressive’ like Alexander Zverev

“He got more aggressive and changed his game to sort of keep up with the top players. Is that something that you think you’ve done, or you’d like to do, or would consider doing?” another journalist asked Medvedev.

“I think nowadays you don’t see many defensive players. I feel like everyone knows how to hit the ball, otherwise, you’re going to get beaten. People are going to get winners to you, and you’re going to lose the match,” he replied.

“I think all the players now are aggressive when they need to be. Did he maybe, like, step it up more, and that’s why he became better? I feel like he just became more consistent, stopped losing matches, you know, matches he shouldn’t lose.

“So, yeah, tough for me to say, because, again, when we played just now, did I feel that he was more aggressive than before? Not really. But he definitely probably served better, maybe serve plus one [too].”

Medvedev continued, sharing his thoughts on how much aggression he plays with, after finishing his 2026 campaign fifth in the world with a 54-22 record.

“When I’m playing good, yes. When I’m not playing good, no. It’s not because I don’t want to be. It’s because I miss. I can be aggressive or not aggressive if you don’t play well,” he said.

“I could give you the racquet now, and I would ask you, like, be aggressive. Doesn’t mean you’re going to become number one in the world. So I think, yeah, let’s say I like the way I play, so is it aggressive or not? That’s for other people to decide.”

Medvedev will have the off-season to ponder whether any extra aggression is needed. After a difficult 2025 season, the Russian surged back towards the top of men’s tennis this year, but still has work to do to catch the likes of Zverev, Alcaraz, and Jannik Sinner.
