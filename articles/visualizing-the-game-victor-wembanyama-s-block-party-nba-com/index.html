Visualizing the Game: Victor Wembanyama’s block party | NBA.com

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:55 · source · permalink

Top Stories

The reigning Kia Defensive Player of the Year has taken his defensive impact to a new level this season.

Brian Martin

Victor Wembanyama enters Tuesday’s action averaging a career-high 4.3 blocks per game.

In April, Victor Wembanyama made history by becoming the first unanimous winner of the Kia NBA Defensive Player of the Year award (receiving all 100 first-place votes) and the youngest player to win the honor (22 years, 106 days old).

This season, he’s been even better.

Entering tonight’s matchup with the Pistons (NBC/Peacock, 8 ET), Wembanyama is putting up the highest blocks average (4.3) and lowest defensive rating (95.1) of his career. He’s led the Spurs to 12 straight wins after a 0-2 start to the season. And while we’re zooming in on his defense today, he’s also the league’s third-ranked scorer (28.6 points per game) and fifth-ranked rebounder (11.6 rebounds per game).

Just a month into his fourth NBA season, Wembanyama is already putting himself in the conversation of most impactful defenders of all time. It took him only 192 career games to crack the top 200 on the all-time blocks leaderboard — a list that dates back to the 1973-74 season and features over 5,000 players.

He enters Tuesday’s game ranked No. 195 on the all-time list in 195 career games.

Wembanyama still has a long way to go before he can challenge the career totals of players like Hakeem Olajuwon (3,830), Dikembe Mutombo (3,289), Kareem Abdul-Jabbar (3,189), Mark Eaton (3,064) and Tim Duncan (3,020) — who are the only five players with over 3,000 career blocks.

But he has already passed each of them — and every other player in NBA history for that matter — when it comes to career blocks per game.

While Wembanyama is on pace to lead the NBA in blocks for the fourth time in his four years in the league, his current mark (4.3 per game) would rank 11th on the single-season blocks per game leaderboard, which is led by Eaton (5.6 in 1984-85) and Bol (5.0 in 1985-86) as the only two players to average at least five rejections per game.

In addition to chasing the all-time leader, Wembanyama ranks 25th among active players in career blocks and is on pace to track down and pass a number of his peers over the course of this season.

In the span of five days, Wembanyama posted the 2026-27 NBA season high in blocked shots with eight rejections against both the Mavericks on Nov. 4 and the Suns on Nov. 9.

Those were two of Wembanyama’s seven games already this season with at least five blocks. The rest of the league has combined for nine such games. Wembanyama now has 61 career games with at least five blocks, which ties him with Rudy Gobert for fourth among active players and ties him for 30th all-time.

 

One aspect of Wembanyama’s game that makes him so unique is his ability to impact shots all over the court. He’s more than just a rim protector; his range allows him to change the geometry of the court defensively, similar to what Stephen Curry does offensively.

While most of Wembanyama’s blocks come inside the paint, he has already blocked six 3-point shots and 18 2-point jump shots this season, which showcases his unique versatility for a player standing 7-foot-4.

One metric that we can’t measure is the number of shots that Wembanyama prevents due to his mere presence on the court. How many times does a guard driving to the basket kick the ball out rather than challenge Wembanyama as they would against any other defender? How many post players use three or four pump fakes to try to get Wembanyama to bite, only to pass it back out so a shot doesn’t get swatted away?

When players do decide to challenge Wembanyama, he has held opponents below their normal field goal percentage from every zone of the court. Opponents shoot a 4.6% lower field goal percentage on 3-pointers against Wembanyama than they do normally. Meanwhile, their 2-point field goal percentage drops by 22.6%.

This season, the NBA and AWS introduced the Leverage Score metric, which measures the impact each play has on a team’s chance to win the game and distributes the credit for those plays to the offensive and defensive players involved using real-time defensive matchups and expected field goal percentage to quantify each player’s contributions.

When looking at the defensive Leverage Score leaderboard, Wembanyama holds a commanding lead on the rest of the league. He has a similar advantage in other catch-all metrics such as defensive win shares (1.3, with second place at 1.0) and defensive box plus/minus (5.2, with second place at 3.2).
