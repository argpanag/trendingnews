Haaland calls for unity after Man City guilty verdict as Maresca expresses 'trust' in club

Erling Haaland called on everybody at Manchester City to “stick together” in the striker's first public comments since the club was found guilty of large-scale financial rule-breaking in the Premier League.

City manager Enzo Maresca also spoke for the first time about English soccer's biggest modern-day scandal, expressing his “trust” in the club's ownership and saying his players “don’t care too much about the noise."

City will play Liverpool on Sunday in its first match since the publication of an investigatory panel’s verdict that City artificially inflated its revenue by more than $1 billion from 2009-18 to circumvent financial fair-play rules that restricted rival clubs in that period.

City was found guilty by the panel of all 80 of the serious financial charges brought by the Premier League and could be hit with severe sanctions — including being expelled from the top flight. City has lodged an appeal against the findings.

In an interview with Sky Sports published Thursday, Haaland — City's most high-profile player — said: “We have to stick together and we have to spread the love to each other. So yeah, stick together, enjoy the ride and smile.”

Asked what his message would be to City fans traveling to Anfield, where a potentially hostile reception awaits the team, Haaland said: “We have to stick together, we have to stay with each other and we have to stay positive.

“No matter what happens in life, we have always have to have a smile. So we have to stay positive, look ahead and try to win football games and then try to enjoy the situation and the game.”

City later published an in-house interview with Maresca, who gave his first remarks on the matter with domestic soccer having been on hold for the past three weeks because of an extended international break.

“Personally I’m very calm to be honest,” he said of City's situation. “The club has been quite clear. We trust completely the club. We understand the noise but the focus for us is just what we can control and we can control just the football side."

Maresca said the feeling in the squad was “fantastic” ahead of City's next two games — against Liverpool and then Paris Saint-Germain in the Champions League on Wednesday.

City has won all seven of its matches this season in all competitions and is top of the Premier League.

“The players are just focused on that. They know what we can control is the football side – they are not worried at all about different things," Maresca said.

“They know their profession and they know that the noise is there but they don’t care too much about the noise."

Maresca is likely to face more severe questioning Friday when he holds his pre-match news conference with reporters.

The return of the Premier League offers rival team coaches a first opportunity to react to the City case that has rocked the world’s most popular soccer league and is likely to have a major impact on the remainder of the season.

They weren't rushing to judgment on Thursday.

“We’ve all seen the news. It’s obviously big news,” said Frank Lampard, the Coventry manager who played for City for one of the seasons — 2014-15 — that has been under investigation.

“Man City are appealing the situation so I guess my reaction is that: what can you say when an appeal is going on? I’m not inside the story, I’m outside it completely — I played for Man City a long time ago, for one year — so we’ll wait and see what the result of the appeal is.”

Bournemouth manager Marco Rose preferred to stay away from the matter, saying: “I’m a football coach, not a politician,” while Newcastle manager Matthias Jaissle said he was “not able to judge” as a manager of another club and “there are authorities taking care of it.”

In a similar vein, Leeds manager Daniel Farke said there were “more qualified people ... able to speak about it.”

Speaking at the Leaders in Sport conference in London, Leeds chairman Paraag Marathe – president of the club’s U.S.-ownership group, 49ers Enterprises – said he believed “the most just result, I’m sure, will be delivered.”

“In any sport, you know, everybody wants to play by the same rules,” he added, without mentioning City.

PARIS (AP) — A 29-year-old Indian man was sentenced to 20 years in prison Friday in a Paris trial over his alleged involvement in an international criminal network that targeted vulnerable children in the U.S., Britain and Canada and coerced them into producing violent and sexual videos.

The child victims were recruited via the online platform Discord to become “slaves” to a group known as CVLT, according to court documents in the case obtained by The Associated Press. At least three other members of the group have been arrested in the U.S.

Rohan Rane, one of the international online group's leaders, was found guilty of sexual extortion and acts of torture against a minor, according to lawyer Hippolyte Marquetty, representing five U.S. victims. The 20-year sentence was the maximum possible for the charges he faced.

Rane showed no reaction when the jury's verdict was announced Friday night, according to those in the courtroom. Judges had ordered the trial held behind closed doors in a rare move because the case involves abuse of children.

Following alerts from U.S. authorities, Rane was arrested by police in Antibes in southern France in 2021. While he initially claimed the situation was a “misunderstanding,” court documents show that he later admitted to being the administrator of the Discord group and stated that he enjoyed watching violent videos, specifically those depicting murders, self-harm and corpses.

A lawyer for five U.S. victims, Hippolyte Marquetty, described “extremely damaging consequences for the victims; they have shattered lives.”

“What the victims expect from the court is a sentence that matches the severity of the actions,” Marquetty told The Associated Press. "And a sentence that, in some way, also protects society a bit and says no, this is truly unacceptable, and prevents this man from being released immediately and potentially reoffending.”

Rane’s lawyer did not respond to requests for comment.

Rane, originally from the Indian city of Pune, was studying in France at the time. French authorities chose to complete criminal proceedings in France before considering extradition.

An examination of videos found on his computer and phone showed young girls engaging in self-harm on his orders, including carving the name “Rohan” or swastikas into their skin or damaging their genitals, according to the court documents.

The psychiatrist who evaluated him described the suspect as being in a state of denial, saying Rane understood the allegations against him but refused to accept any responsibility and projected the blame onto others.

Rane told authorities that he had arrived in France in 2016 to pursue studies. He stated that he relied financially on his parents, who lived in India, and had a girlfriend living in the United States, whom he had met online but never met in person, for several months. In reality, she was one of the CVLT victims whom Rane and others were threatening, the court documents said.

The documents said that since his detention in 2021, Rane has continued to communicate with others via Discord, and similar types of images were discovered on his phone while in custody. In June 2021, he was still corresponding with minors aged 15 and 16, urging them to join “his cult.”

In February 2022, writings covered in swastikas were found in his cell. He has since been placed in solitary confinement.

Kaleb Christopher Merritt, 26, another senior member of the group, is currently in federal custody in Los Angeles but is also serving a 33-year prison sentence in Virginia, according to a U.S. Department of Justice statement. Merritt, of Spring, Texas, pleaded guilty to one count of engaging in a child exploitation enterprise. He faces a sentencing hearing in January.

Clint Jordan Lopaka Nahooikaika Borge, 42, of Pahoa, Hawaii, and Collin John Thomas Walker, 24, of Bridgeton, New Jersey, were other members of the group, according to the statement.

Intelligence from various countries helped identify other accomplices, including a British national, a New Zealander, an Australian, and another American. Others could not be positively identified.

The U.S. Justice Department statement said CVLT sought to create an army of followers, and espoused neo-Nazism, nihilism, and pedophilia as its core principles.

Among the civil parties in the case is ECPAT 360, an NGO that fights sexual exploitation and trafficking of children. A representative for the group, Anna Rodriguez, attended the trial, in which some of the children's videos were shown as evidence. She described the abuse that the child victims suffered, such as being coerced to drink their own urine during a livestream.

“There is an explosion of violence and cruelty in the sexual violence committed against children and minors online,” she told AP.

Rodriguez said the civil parties advocated for an open trial to bring public attention to what’s happening in the dark corners of the internet.

"This violence is currently minimized because it is digital, because they are happening online, we tend to consider them virtual, confined to a bubble that supposedly doesn’t impact the victims themselves," she said. "This case demonstrates the exact opposite.”
