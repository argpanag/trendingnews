Abdullayev Misses Weight Ahead of Matthews UFC Qatar Fight

TheTools · 21.11.2026 10:05 · source · permalink

A weight-cutting setback has added another development to the UFC Qatar welterweight matchup between Azerbaijan's Tahir Abdullayev and Australia's Jake Matthews, scheduled for November 21, 2026, at ABHA Arena in Doha, Qatar. Despite missing the official limit, Abdullayev will proceed with the fight.

According to OperativMM, Abdullayev registered 172 pounds (78 kilograms) at the official weigh-in, exceeding the welterweight limit by one pound, approximately 450 grams. The Azerbaijani fighter will forfeit part of his purse as a consequence.

Matthews, meanwhile, entered the contest with a professional record of 23 wins and eight losses. Abdullayev holds a 20-3 record and is preparing for his second appearance inside the UFC Octagon.

The Australian secured his latest victory against Carlston Harris in May 2026, earning a unanimous decision. That result followed a third-round submission defeat against Neil Magny in September 2025.

Abdullayev made an impressive promotional debut at UFC Fight Night in Baku on June 27, 2026. He defeated Brazilian fighter Jefferson Nascimento through a third-round technical knockout, extending his run of consecutive finishes to five, according to the available fight records.

Statistical comparisons published by DocSports show Matthews averaging 3.36 significant strikes landed per minute, compared with Abdullayev's 2.56. Matthews has a 44 percent striking accuracy rate, while Abdullayev records 42 percent.

Defensively, Matthews absorbs 2.58 significant strikes per minute and blocks 60 percent of incoming significant strikes. Abdullayev absorbs 2.42 per minute and has a 51 percent striking defense rate.

The fighters share an identical 73-inch reach, although Matthews has a height advantage. He stands 5 feet 11 inches tall, compared with Abdullayev at 5 feet 8 inches. Both compete at the 170-pound welterweight division.

Abdullayev discussed his preparations and ambitions in an interview with Idman.biz published on November 19. He described himself as confident following his successful UFC debut and said his training approach had remained largely unchanged.

The Azerbaijani fighter also identified Matthews' preference for standing exchanges as an aspect of the matchup he welcomed.

"My goal is to enter the UFC rankings by the end of 2027," Abdullayev said in his interview with Idman.biz.

He explained that his immediate priority was securing victories and continuing to develop his position within the organization. Breaking into the UFC's top 15 remains his longer-term objective.

Abdullayev also expressed confidence about receiving support from spectators in Qatar. He noted that he expected encouragement from local fans during the event.

The matchup represents a significant experience gap between the competitors. Matthews has spent more than a decade competing against established UFC welterweights, while Abdullayev joined the promotion in 2026 after building his professional record outside the organization.

Despite the weigh-in violation, the scheduled November 21 contest remains on the UFC Qatar card, with Matthews and Abdullayev set to meet in the welterweight preliminaries in Doha.
