Chelsea 1-4 Leeds: Blues dismantled as pressure grows on manager Xabi Alonso after Noah Okafor's double

TheTools · 21.11.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

Report and free match highlights as Leeds beat Chelsea in the Premier League; Noah Okafor scored twice after Emmanuel Emegha had given hosts lead; Dominic Calvert-Lewin put Leeds ahead with a penalty; Brenden Aaronson capped Leeds' first victory at Stamford Bridge since 1999

Football Journalist

Saturday 21 November 2026 17:15, UK

Leeds dismantled Chelsea and increased the pressure on manager Xabi Alonso with an outstanding performance in a stunning 4-1 win.

Chelsea were booed off at half-time and full-time after suffering their third Premier League defeat in four games, a run which has seen them slip to eighth, as Leeds earned their first victory at Stamford Bridge since 1999.

Alonso's side has also been dumped out of the Carabao Cup and are already without European football this season, having now lost at home to their rivals Leeds and Tottenham for the first time in 67 years.

Emmanuel Emegha gave the hosts a first-half lead on his first start, but Noah Okafor deservedly equalised before half-time, and Leeds ran riot after the break. Dominic Calvert-Lewin put the visitors ahead with a penalty, Okafor smashed in his second and Brenden Aaronson capped an outstanding away win to move Leeds sixth.

Four of Chelsea's five league defeats have now come without the injured Joao Pedro, with his absence felt again despite Emegha smashing in the opener. The 23-year-old, making only his third appearance, caught James Trafford out at his near post although the striker was starved of service.

His goal came after Moises Caicedo was forced off following a heavy fall, and against the run of play after Calvert-Lewin had sent a header against the crossbar, but Leeds equalised within three minutes.

Calvert-Lewin slipped in Okafor, who, with three Chelsea defenders around him in the box, still managed to send a low, left-footed shot through the legs of Levi Colwill, across Emi Martínez and into the bottom right corner.

From there, Leeds were dominant until the end. Calvert-Lewin flicked a shot just wide, had a header saved, and Tarik Muharemovic's goal was ruled out for offside, all before half-time. Chelsea got worse still after the break.

Martinez's return in goal did little to settle a shaky defence, which was under pressure right from the restart, before Jayden Bogle's rocket struck the right arm of Morgan Rogers and referee Michael Oliver pointed to the spot. Calvert-Lewin sent Martinez the wrong way after VAR had backed the on-field decision following a lengthy check.

Chelsea never once looked like being able to recover and Leeds soon added a third. Ethan Ampadu's low cross from the right deflected up into the middle, and Okafor slammed a close-range volley into the top-left corner in the 58th minute.

It was too soon for the home fans to head for the exits, but they groaned every time Chelsea lost the ball, and a few boos followed Alonso's substitutions before they left en masse when Aaronson tucked in a fourth in added time.

Alonso: We're fragile and don't recover when punched

Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso:

"After the equaliser, after the change [Caicedo], we lost the momentum completely. Until then, we were doing okay. We were creating, we were coming out from the pressure, but from that moment they started believing and we started not believing and it changed totally.

"And after that, we never got the good feeling mentality at any moment, and we were not able to cope with the pressure, and we didn't get close to the goal, so it completely changed it.

"We need to find solutions for sure because there will be setbacks during any game and to be tougher and to be able to go through that and to know that it's still so long to play, it's something mandatory in this competition.

"Today I felt that probably we are in a fragile moment mentally, confidence, when we receive a punch that we don't recover that strong, that tough enough."

Calvert-Lewin: Chelsea couldn't handle intensity

Leeds striker Dominic Calvert-Lewin:

"I expected Chelsea to come out with a bit more oomph in the second half. But our intensity and the way that we played and the way that we set up against them, they couldn't handle it. That reflected in the scoreline."

Farke: We dominated - I can't wait to watch it back

Leeds head coach Daniel Farke:

"Great performance. I would have been disappointed with a draw today. I was a bit disappointed even at half-time when it was just a draw because from the first to the last [minute], we were the better side.

"We dominated this game, made sure that our plan for this game worked. We wanted to play on the front foot; we wanted to press Chelsea high; we wanted to make sure that they couldn't show their world-class potential.

"I still think that Chelsea came out motivated today and also played with intensity. I just checked the physical data, they were there with higher physical intensity than in their last games, but we still have outperformed them.

"It's important in this period of the season to collect points. It gives us more confidence but we travelled here with confidence and belief that it would be possible to give such a dominant performance.

"I can't wait to be on the sofa with coffee and cake and to watch this game back."
