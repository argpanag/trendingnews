Spiked nightcaps and invitations to strangers: how Philip Young sexually abused his wife for 15 years

TheTools · 19.11.2026 16:00 · source · permalink

Joanne Young, who has waived anonymity, was subjected to years of drugging and rape by former Swindon councillor, who invited other men to assault her

When Philip Young served as a Conservative party councillor for Swindon council, his contemporaries viewed him as a perfectly normal family man with “no edge to him”.

But behind closed doors he orchestrated a systematic campaign of abuse against his then wife, Joanne Young, secretly administering sedatives to render her unconscious, disoriented and amnesiac. Over a period of at least 15 years, he and other men raped and sexually assaulted her in her own bed at their home in Swindon.

The father of three pleaded guilty to more than 50 offences, including drugging, raping and sexually assaulting Joanne, who has been praised for her bravery in waiving her anonymity. His co-accused Connor Sanderson-Doyle, Richard Wilkins, Norman Macksoni and Mohammed Hassan were convicted on Thursday by a jury of their involvement. Another man, Dean Hamilton, pleaded guilty in February to rape.

The high-profile case has drawn comparisons to the Gisèle Pelicot trial in France, and it can now be revealed that the Crown Prosecution Service and Wiltshire police met the French team of investigators and prosecutors for advice.

Testimony and police interviews paint a picture of a carefully maintained domestic facade masking a predatory reality. Joanne, who was married to Young for 20 years and had been with him since 2002, described their relationship as traditionally monogamous and her own preferences as strictly “vanilla”.

Because of his heavy snoring, they slept in separate rooms, with Joanne eventually occupying a spacious second-floor loft conversion featuring a large black headboard and purple bedding.

Unknown to her, Young was exploiting this arrangement, and taking advantage of a common evening routine – sharing a nightcap of vodka after returning from social occasions – to spike her drinks with sedatives including zolpidem and ketamine that he is now believed to have bought online from countries including India.

The nightmare began to unravel on the night of 18 February 2023 after an evening out at the Blunsdon Arms. Joanne half-woke to find herself wearing a soft blindfold and wrist restraints, with a stranger’s hand on her wrist. She realised an unknown man was in her bedroom. When she attempted to move, Young whispered “get down”, causing the intruder to duck. Severely shortsighted and without her glasses, Joanne fled to the en suite bathroom and locked herself inside.

The following morning, Young apologised, claiming he had invited a man over from an online forum just to watch. The confession left Joanne deeply traumatised and questioning her marriage. She struggled to access counselling, while also managing impending redundancies at their shared business, a consulting firm.

The full scale of Young’s twisted operation was exposed after the National Crime Agency flagged illegal activity linked to him – the uploading of a bestiality image – on the messaging and livestreaming app Kik. Wiltshire police raided his home in July 2024.

Officers discovered digital caches of videos showing his then wife incapacitated, restrained and being sexually assaulted by a range of strangers, who it later emerged had been recruited via Craigslist, Grindr, Reddit and swinging websites. Young used multiple online aliases – such as “londonmonkey” – and posted advertisements across major cities such as London, Bristol and Bath, often messaging under the guise of Joanne.

He used at least 20 unique advertisement IDs and the titles included “Fuck my tied and blindfolded wife – mw4m – 35 (North Swindon)” and “Fuck my wife – 18th September – Central Bath hotel – mw4m”. The abbreviation “mw4m” was used in the posts to denote a man and woman looking for a man.

In a police interview on 4 July 2024, Young openly discussed his sexual fantasies, describing himself as “monogamous” – he had no interest in sleeping with other women – but saying he enjoyed “wife sharing”, or seeing his wife have sex with other men. He admitted to sharing images exclusively of his wife and claimed her interest in involving third parties varied, adding that “she changes at times”.

He told officers that in intimate moments his wife would sometimes tell him to “do what you want” and he would use Grindr to find men nearby to quickly arrive and “strip and join in”. He said his wife would usually be in bed, “blindfolded and tied” by her arms to the bed head, acting “submissive” while engaging with other men.

But Joanne later told detectives and the court she did not consent to other men having sex with her, adding the relationship “was monogamous, always monogamous”.

The following day, 5 July 2024, Young answered “no comment” to all questions put to him by officers. Hair sample analysis conducted by a forensic toxicologist who also worked on the Pelicot trial revealed traces of ketamine and zolpidem in Joanne’s hair. Residues of zolpidem were also detected inside a plastic bag found in Young’s old bedside table.

In January, Young pleaded guilty at Winchester crown court to 11 counts of rape and 11 counts of administering a substance with intent to stupefy Joanne. He also admitted 14 counts of voyeurism, including a single count that covered at least 200 acts of voyeurism; seven counts of assault by penetration; four counts of sexual assault and one count of publishing obscene articles on at least 500 occasions. He later admitted to charges of making indecent images of children, one count of possessing a prohibited image of a child, and four charges of possessing an extreme pornographic image.

But four of his co-accused denied charges of rape and sexual assault, which meant Joanne was subjected to the brutal process of a trial, in which she was again praised for giving evidence to the court. The jury rejected the defendants’ claims that they believed the acts were consensual, securing guilty verdicts across all counts for the co-accused.

Sanderson-Doyle, a Ministry of Defence contractor recruited via Craigslist in 2016 and captured on video assaulting the victim while wearing a balaclava, was convicted of sexual assault and assault by penetration.

Wilkins, a 62-year-old taxi driver who participated in July 2016, recorded performing a sex act on the sleeping and snoring victim, was found guilty of assault by penetration and rape.

Macksoni, a waste management worker who communicated with Young using the codename “Blunsdon” and was filmed abusing Joanne in February 2017, was convicted of rape.

Hassan, who was involved in the February 2023 incident in which Joanne woke up and discovered a stranger in her room, was found guilty of sexual assault.

Friends who socialised with the couple – sharing meals at the Blunsdon Arms and Greek restaurants, and playing Trivial Pursuit – recalled an outwardly normal family life with three children.

However, friends such as Andrew and Emma Brown and Rachel Williams said they had frequently noticed Young glued to his mobile phone during gatherings, an obsession now recognised as coordinating with strangers online.

It emerged in the trial that a potential opportunity to catch Young 10 years earlier may have been missed. In 2015, officers investigated an impounded package from India addressed to Young containing 120 zolpidem tablets – a class C drug known as a potential “date rape” substance. Young claimed ignorance, suggesting someone had misused his business details. An intelligence report was filed but no further action was taken.

A spokesperson for Wiltshire police said it was “understandable that questions may be asked” with the benefit of hindsight. “However, based on the information available at the time, the attending officer’s actions were both appropriate and proportionate,” they added.

“At that point, Young was a person of previously good character, with no criminal convictions, and there was no intelligence, information or evidence to suggest he posed a risk of committing the serious offences that have since come to light. The interaction was dealt with reasonably and in line with the information known at the time.”

Young later obtained multiple legitimate prescriptions for zolpidem and zopiclone from his GP between 2018 and 2022.

The revelations have sent shock waves through political circles in Swindon. A senior non-elected official close to Swindon borough council when Young served expressed profound shock at the former councillor’s conviction, telling the Guardian the revelations were “horrific” and “hideous”.

Young, who served as a cabinet member for regeneration and economic development between 2007 and 2010, was remembered as a progressive and engaging colleague. He held the political lead for Forward Swindon, an urban regeneration company responsible for revamping the town’s railway station forecourt among other projects, and liaised with the local business improvement district.

According to the source, the council was a difficult authority with challenging members at the time, but Young stood out as a clear exception. “He was one of the much more easy-to-get-along-with members [and] seemed to be considerate of staff,” the official noted, describing his approach as representing the “more modern thinking of councillors”.

The official said Young had shown no warning signs of his future criminal behaviour, in either his language or demeanour. “There was absolutely … no reason given to think that there was something unusual about this fellow,” the official said, adding that colleagues had generally considered Young to be “one of the good guys”.

A Conservative party source, who was close to the council at the time Young served, told the Guardian of the anger and betrayal they had felt upon learning of the former councillor’s crimes, saying the revelations had come as a “complete shock”.

Young was viewed as a highly capable and “brilliant councillor” who played a hands-on role in trying to revive Swindon’s town centre after the financial crash, they said. They revealed that Young had been so highly regarded for his public speaking and specialised skills that he was actively encouraged to run for council leader before he stepped down to become a director at the New Swindon Company.

Publicly, Young presented as a “perfectly normal family man” and successful business owner with “no edge to him”. “He wasn’t creepy in any way … I would never, ever have suspected anything untoward,” the source said.

Learning of the offences from a national newspaper reporter last Christmas had left former colleagues “beyond shocked” and “speechless”, they said. Expressing sympathy for the victims and for Young’s children, regarding the legacy he had left them, the source said the scale of his deception made them wonder “if you ever know anybody”.

After the convictions, questions still remain about the extent of Young’s offending. Police later discovered a receipt for a camcorder dating back to 2011, raising investigators’ suspicions that the covert recording and abuse may have started earlier than the window covered by the charges.

Meanwhile, authorities continue efforts to identify an elusive offender captured in the digital evidence who has yet to be identified despite several police appeals. Young has been questioned via his lawyers about the identity of the individual but has not assisted with inquiries.
