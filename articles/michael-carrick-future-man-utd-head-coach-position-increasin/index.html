Michael Carrick future: Man Utd head coach position increasingly in doubt with Mauricio Pochettino open to talks

TheTools · 10.11.2026 22:17 · source · permalink

Man Utd head coach Michael Carrick's position under increasing threat, but no final decision; Sky Sports News understands Mauricio Pochettino would be open to a discussion with Man Utd if he approached by club; it is thought the cost of buying him out of his USA contract is around £7m

Tuesday 10 November 2026 13:34, UK

Michael Carrick's position as Manchester United head coach is looking increasingly in doubt, with Mauricio Pochettino open to a discussion if he were approached by the club.

CEO Omar Berrada and Wilcox are due to meet minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco for a scheduled meeting on Wednesday, with the duo to discuss the findings of their season review and give their recommendations on Carrick's future, while also assessing their options.

Sky Sports News has been told that, as yet, no final decision has been taken on Carrick's Old Trafford future.

With Pochettino open to talks with United, it is thought the cost of buying him out of his contract with his current job as USA head coach would be around £7m.

The former Argentina defender recently extended his deal with the USA until 2030 and is preparing to face Haiti in the Nations League quarter-finals.

In the past, United have had discussions with Pochettino on at least two occasions regarding the United job.

Carrick arrived at the club's training ground on Tuesday morning as the club carries out a full review of the team's performance so far this season, led by director of football Jason Wilcox.

Carrick was given a reprieve following the 3-0 home defeat to Bournemouth a fortnight ago, with the bosses keen to give him a further three matches to try to turn the team's fortunes around.

But the pressure has continued to mount on Carrick after an eighth straight Premier League match without a win. There are now real doubts about whether he will be in charge of the team which goes to Anfield to play Liverpool the weekend after next.

The United hierarchy is determined that results have to improve, but they want to be sure about which direction to take the team before any action is taken.

If they decide to remove Carrick from his position, they want to avoid having an interim head coach take over, but as yet, there have been no substantive talks with other potential candidates.

Sky Sports News understands the club's hierarchy are planning to use the international break to fully evaluate the season so far and reassess their options.

Carrick and the players have all had Monday off after rescuing a late point against Villa on Sunday, but the head coach and his staff are due back into Carrington on Tuesday.

With most players away on international duty and no first-team training sessions planned in the coming days, that has bought some breathing space for a full re-evaluation of the season.

One win in 11 games, 30 goals conceded so far in all competitions, the worst record of any team in the top-five European leagues, and a worst-ever start to a Premier League season has ramped up the pressure on Carrick.

Those problems have outweighed the positivity brought about by the comebacks of the last two matches.

It is thought the United hierarchy feel Carrick over-achieved last season, with a third-placed League finish earning Champions League football.

But it is equally clear the bosses are adamant that the club's current crop of players have woefully under-achieved so far this season.

Sky Sports News understands there are three key reasons why United are not rushing a decision over the head coach position - all of which have given Carrick an extended stay of execution.

Firstly, the supporters are not singing for him to be sacked. It is unlikely that will ever happen, such is the affection of the fanbase for a legendary player who won 11 major trophies with United, including the Champions League and five Premier League titles.

As such, the pressure to change the boss which other clubs often experience from the stands has not materialised at Old Trafford.

Secondly, there is a feeling in the boardroom that Carrick and his coaching staff still retain the support of the players.

Despite the chaotic nature of the last two games, they have produced a Champions League win against Roma and a draw against a good Villa side - with notable comebacks in both.

As such, the assessment seems to be that the players have not downed tools, and they are still fighting for Carrick.

Thirdly, United's bosses remain unconvinced at this stage that changing the head coach will solve the team's problems.

If they did believe that, they could have already made a move to sack Carrick and appoint a candidate such as Julian Nagelsmann or Eddie Howe - both of whom are out of work, available, and thought to be keen to talk to United if a vacancy becomes available.

'It doesn't matter who's in charge at Man Utd'

Sky Sports News chief reporter Kaveh Solhekol:

"The club needs to become Manchester United again. They need to turn into a club who are capable of winning the title regularly, winning the European Cup again.

"It doesn't matter who's in charge, in my opinion. You could have Carlo Ancelotti, Pep Guardiola or Jurgen Klopp. But with the present ownership structure, I don't think things are going to change.

"You could bring in another manager but you would just paper over the cracks. We're talking about a club that used to rub shoulders with Barcelona and Real Madrid.
