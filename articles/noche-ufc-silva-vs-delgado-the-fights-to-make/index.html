Noche UFC: Silva vs Delgado, the fights to make

TheTools · 13.09.2026 05:54 · source · permalink

Jean Silva stood in the middle of the cage in Glendale on 12 September 2026 with a finish on his record and a title shot on his tongue. He is not getting one yet. What he has earned is the fight that settles the argument, and our model has no hesitation about who that is.

Lerone Murphy.

That is the call, and it is the best fight the Featherweight division can make right now. Murphy is the one man at the top of the division whose case sits in the same place as Silva’s, a wrecking-ball run and no belt to show for it, with title talks stalled and no opponent signed while he studies Diego Lopes tape. Silva’s grappling was the story of the main event. Murphy’s counter-striking is the cleanest in the weight class. Put the two of them in a cage and the winner walks out with a claim nobody in the division can talk their way past.

What next for Jean Silva

Silva took a short-notice replacement opponent, got dragged into a real fight for two and a half rounds and then submitted him. The official line is a rear-naked choke in the third. That is his most complete performance in the UFC and it came against a man who was not supposed to be there.

So the fight to make is Lerone Murphy, and if he signs elsewhere first the clean fallback is Aljamain Sterling. Silva does not need another finish over a short-notice opponent. He needs a name at the top of the division to sign his name to. Our model lands on Murphy because the stylistic gap is where the value sits, a striker who punishes over-commitment against a man who has built his run on over-committing and getting away with it.

Jose Miguel Delgado

Delgado took the main event on short notice after Yair Rodríguez withdrew injured, walked into the hardest assignment on the schedule and made Silva work harder than anyone expected. That is not a loss that sets a career back.

The call is Luke Riley. Two young Featherweights with finishing rates and no ceiling established yet, matched against each other while both are still interesting. Delgado does not need a step down and he certainly does not need a rebuild.

What next for Alexa Grasso

The biggest divisional shift on the card did not happen in the main event. Grasso walked into a designated title eliminator against the best Flyweight not holding a belt, dropped her and took a clean decision, then said out loud what everybody in the building already knew.

She wants the title back. She should get it.

The fight to make is the winner of Natalia Silva against Wang Cong, who meet for the vacant belt after Valentina Shevchenko was stripped through injury. Grasso already has a loss to Silva to avenge and a live stylistic puzzle against Cong. Either version sells itself and neither needs a single word of build. Anything else put in front of Grasso now is the UFC wasting an eliminator it commissioned itself.

Manon Fiorot

A bad night for one of the most technically sound Flyweights on the roster, and one that does not require a rebuild so much as a reset. She got out-worked in open space by somebody willing to throw first.

The call is Rose Namajunas. A former champion at Strawweight against a fighter who has beaten almost everyone else in the division, with the loser drifting out of the title conversation entirely. Fiorot has always looked her best against opponents who come to her, and Namajunas comes to everyone.

What next for Brandon Moreno

A split decision over Joseph Morales is not the statement Moreno wanted in front of a Mexican crowd, and the scorecards say the division’s former champion is now in genuine fights with opponents he used to handle. He is still one of the two or three most valuable names at Flyweight. He just needs an opponent who tells us something.

Our model says Tim Elliott, who won on this same card and who presents the exact problem Moreno has never solved cleanly, a grappler with no regard for position who is happy to make the fight ugly for fifteen minutes. If Elliott gets booked first, Rei Tsuruya is the fight.

Joseph Morales

Took a former champion to a split decision on the biggest stage of his career. He gets Fabia Sintes, a matchup between two Flyweights who both just proved they belong, with a place in the back half of the division’s pecking order on it.

What next for Tommy McMillen

A decision win over a live prospect, and the sort of performance that earns a step up rather than another peer. McMillen is ready for one.

The fight is Josh Emmett. A veteran with fight-ending power who tells you immediately whether a rising Featherweight can hang at the level above the one he has been fighting at. Beat Emmett and McMillen is in the conversation. Lose and nobody holds it against him.

Marwan Rahiki

A first UFC setback against a better-established opponent. Fernando Padilla is the right next assignment, a Featherweight with a similar profile and a similar need to get back on track.

What next for Curtis Blaydes

Blaydes did what Blaydes does. He took the most dangerous puncher available at Heavyweight, put him on the canvas and never let the fight become a striking match. Three rounds and every card his way.

The call is Vitor Petrino. The division’s incoming generation against its most reliable measuring stick, and the fastest way to find out whether Petrino’s Heavyweight run is real. Blaydes has spent years being the fight that separates contenders from pretenders and there is no reason to stop using him that way.

Waldo Cortes Acosta

Got wrestled and had no answer for it, which is the answer the division has been looking for. Valter Walker next, a fight between two big Heavyweights whose ground games are the open question, and one that keeps Cortes Acosta in the top half of the division without asking him to solve elite wrestling twice in a row.

What next for David Martinez

Three rounds, two 30-27 cards and a performance built on a pace Dan Ige simply could not match. Martinez is now the most interesting young Bantamweight the UFC has.

Which is why the model puts him in against Umar Nurmagomedov. Nurmagomedov is coming off a knockout loss to Song Yadong and needs a win over someone the division cares about. Martinez needs a name with a title-shot claim on his record. The styles are a nightmare for each other, a pressure fighter who never stops throwing against a technician who wants space and time.

Dan Ige

Three straight losses and a night where his output was not enough to bank a round. Ige has been one of the division’s most dependable operators for years and that record buys him a matchup he can win. Juan Diaz is the fight, a step back in level without a step back in entertainment.

Tim Elliott and Edgar Chairez

Elliott turned in another three rounds of controlled chaos to take a clear decision at catchweight. The model’s pick for him is Rei Tsuruya, and it is a good one, a veteran gatekeeper against one of the division’s best young Flyweights. If the Moreno fight materialises instead, take that.

Chairez gets Alex Perez. Two Flyweights who need to stop the slide, matched against each other rather than fed to somebody on the way up.

Ignacio Bahamondes

Three rounds against Muslim Salikhov at Welterweight and a decision that went the way of the younger, longer man. Jacobe Smith next. A wrestler with real credentials against a striker with genuine distance control, and the fight that tells us whether Bahamondes belongs in a second division or should head back down.

Salikhov gets Charles Radtke, which is the fun version of a Welterweight rebuild fight.

Yousri Belgaroui

A first-round stoppage with barely thirty seconds left in the round, the most emphatic finish on the prelims and a performance that earns a step up at Middleweight. Donte Johnson is the call, a matchup of two fighters nobody has managed to slow down yet.

Djorden Santos draws Luis Felipe Dias, a Middleweight matchup between two men who both need to prove the first impression was not the whole story.

Tommy Gantt and the rest of the prelims

Gantt caught Drakkar Klose in a triangle variation off his back and got a submission that nobody on the card saw coming. Axel Sola next at Lightweight, which is the level his win earned. Klose takes Kyle Nelson, a fight between two veterans with heavy hands and a shared need for a result.

Rongzhu out-worked Rafa Garcia across three rounds and our model pushes him straight into Mateusz Gamrot, which is a hard ask and exactly the right one. He has spent his UFC run grinding through opponents at his own level and a Lightweight in the upper reaches of the division is the logical next assignment. Garcia gets Diego Ferreira, a Lightweight matchup that stays entertaining regardless of where either man sits.

Sean King III needed thirty-six seconds and a slam to end Jessie Rosas, the shortest fight of the night. Javier Reyes is his next assignment, a Featherweight test against somebody far harder to put away. Rosas draws Toshiomi Kazama.

Regina Tarin took a close decision off JJ Aldrich and lands Carli Judice, two Flyweights on the fringe of relevance with a real opportunity on the line. Aldrich faces Juliana Miller.
