Martin: Public sector pay deal must be 'value for money'

TheTools · 17.10.2026 23:00 · source · permalink

Taoiseach Micheál Martin has said the new pay deal to be agreed at the Workplace Relations Commission (WRC) should advance productivity in the public service as well as provide protections to low-paid workers.

He added that any deal should represent "value for money".

He was speaking after unions and Government last night agreed to enter formal discussions at the WRC tomorrow after weeks of deadlock, including Wednesday's national strike by 300,000 public workers.

Speaking after the annual Fianna Fáil Wolfe Tonne Commemoration in Co Kildare, Mr. Martin said: "I welcome the fact that a process has been agreed between the unions and the Government side...what now needs to happen is for the negotiations to take place and that we can have a resolution which would manifest itself in a new pay agreement similar to the ones we've already negotiated".

Mr Martin refused to be drawn on whether the Government would go beyond the €1.2 billion allocated in this month's Budget for a new deal amid suggestions the Coalition could dip into a €1.6 billion pot for unallocated budgetary spending.

Unions have expressed the belief that the cost of a new deal could run to €2 billion.

"Nothing is decided, no precise figures have even been mentioned yet, other than what we've provided in terms of the Budget", he said.

However, he indicated he wanted to see greater productivity in the public service out of any agreement.

"I'm open to an agreement that obviously is affordable, that can advance the productivity within the public service But again that would look after, in particular, protections to low-paid workers," said Mr Martin.

"We've always had a view of endeavouring always to protect lower paid workers within the public service. There are a broad range of roles, different areas to be covered in terms of the size and scale of our public service. But that's a matter that has to be ironed out in the context of negotiations," he said.

Earlier Minister of State for Transport Seán Canney said a deal would be found in the "not too distant future".

Mr Canney said a successor to the deal will be found soon, adding that the €1.2 billion set aside in the Budget towards a deal was a sign of the Government's willingness to reach a deal.

Speaking on RTÉ's The Week in Politics Mr Canney said no dispute is ever resolved without all sides sitting around a table, adding that a pay deal could not be negotiated before formal talks start.

Mr Canney said hindsight was a great tool and efforts were made to reach out to union leaders prior to the strike.

Speaking on the same programme, Sinn Féin's Claire Kerrane said Wednesday's strike was a mess of the Government's making and was totally avoidable.

Ms Kerrane said the unions' ask for pay to be discussed first was a fair one.

The Galway-Roscommon TD said public servant workers know their worth and added that she doesn't know of any nurse who could be more productive in their role.

Aontú TD Paul Lawless said the Government misread the room, adding it was time for the Government to get around the table with union leaders.

Mr Lawless said failure to address the cost of living and the cost of housing for young people drove Wednesday's strike.

Last night's announcement came after meetings throughout Saturday aimed at exploring whether the formal process could begin, with a resolution not initially expected after a single day of engagement.

Independent Chair Kevin Foley met a trade union representative, ICTU General Secretary Owen Reidy, and a Government representative, Oonagh Buckley, who is Secretary General of the Department of the Environment, at the offices of the Forsa trade union in Dublin.

Other key stakeholders were briefed on the phone. Shortly before 9pm, the trio confirmed an agreement had been reached for formal pay talks to start on Monday.

In a statement, they said: "Following engagement with the parties, we believe that discussions at the WRC should get under way on Monday, 19 October.

"The discussions should commence with pay-related matters, including the trade unions' claims on cost-of-living effects between 2021–2025, this year, and for the duration of any agreement."

However, the trio also sounded a word of caution, adding that "there is a need to rebuild trust and confidence in industrial relations in the public sector by addressing commitments made in the context of the previous agreement".

Unions insist some of the commitments made by Government in the previous agreement have been reneged upon.

Mr Foley, Mr Reidy and Ms Buckley said they will remain available should there be any impasse on any particular matter during the negotiations at the WRC and they would "put forward a means of resolution which should be accepted by the parties".

"We wish to make it clear that this special arrangement should be understood as an exceptional measure reflective of the scale of this dispute and its impact on the economy and the people who rely on public services.

"This measure is specific to this dispute and its unique nature and is not in any sense a template or precedent for any other dispute between these or any other parties," they added.

Trade unions and Government will spend today finalising negotiating teams for the talks. Arrangements on how the talks will proceed will also be finalised.

Sources on both sides welcomed last night's development, while expressing positive surprise that this part of the process has concluded quicker than expected.
