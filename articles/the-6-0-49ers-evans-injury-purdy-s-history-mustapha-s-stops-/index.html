The 6-0 49ers: Evans’ injury, Purdy’s history, Mustapha’s stops, and a nail-biting finish

TheTools · 20.10.2026 03:26 · source · permalink

Kyle Shanahan’s team led 20-3 at halftime, but receiver Mike Evans’ Achilles injury left Brock Purdy’s offense without its top receiver in a narrow win.

With a 23-17 win over the Washington Commanders on Monday at Levi’s Stadium, the 49ers improved to 6-0 for the fourth time in franchise history and the first time since 2019.

The 49ers led 20-3 at halftime, but Mike Evans’ Achilles injury left quarterback Brock Purdy without any of his top weapons at wide receiver.

Fortunately for Purdy and coach Kyle Shanahan, defensive coordinator Raheem Morris’ unit had just enough left in the tank to slow Jayden Daniels and the Commanders’ offense. On Washington’s second-to-last drive, safety Malik Mustapha delivered a punishing third-down hit on running back Jeremy McNichols then broke up a fourth-down pass intended for receiver Treylon Burks at the goal line.

When Washington got the ball back with less than a minute remaining, rookie pass rusher Romello Height and veteran Keion White combined to sack Daniels on fourth down to seal the win.

Purdy completed 21 of 29 passes for 264 yards and a touchdown. He also passed 1,500 career attempts, the minimum need to qualify for NFL leaderboards. He entered Monday with a 105.4 career passer rating, and his 111.9 rating against Washington pushed it higher. That mark now stands as the best in league history. 

Thirty-one teams have started 6-0 since 2001, and all but two — the 2003 Vikings and 2009 Broncos — made the playoffs. After the Seahawks (3-3) lost to the Broncos on Thursday and the Rams (2-4) fell to the Cardinals (2-4) on Sunday, the 49ers hold a commanding lead in the NFC West.

The 2019 49ers started 8-0, finished 13-3, and earned the NFC’s No. 1 playoff seed with a dramatic Week 17 win in Seattle. Shanahan’s team advanced to the Super Bowl, but squandered a fourth-quarter lead and lost 31-20 to Patrick Mahomes and the Kansas City Chiefs.

Here are our instant takeaways from Monday’s game: 

Purdy’s passing history

By the end of the first half, Purdy was without his top six wide receivers — Evans, Ricky Pearsall, Deebo Samuel, De’Zhaun Stribling, Demarcus Robinson, and Christian Kirk were all unavailable.

But it didn’t matter, and perhaps it’s fitting that Purdy officially became the highest-rated passer in NFL history in yet another game that demanded turning to spare receiving pieces. Purdy has been incredibly resourceful since the beginning, whether it be his first career throw, a long completion to Ray-Ray McCloud, in garbage time against the Chiefs in 2022 or his first extended action against the Miami Dolphins and Tom Brady’s Tampa Bay Buccaneers later that season.

How the 49ers’ maligned draft classes are rebuilding a top defense

For nearly 30 minutes Monday, Purdy had Evans. The future Hall of Fame wideout racked up 94 yards, outgaining Washington (90 yards) by himself in the first half. But even after Evans exited the game, Purdy continued impressing. Other targets, including second-year receiver Jordan Watkins, benefitted. One of Purdy’s best throws came on a 20-yard laser to Watkins with Washington defensive lineman Daron Payne clutching Watkins’ ankle. Purdy’s legs also moved the chains and set up touchdowns.

He was in total command until the fourth quarter, when the offense stalled and the defense had to step up.

Evans’ injury

How hurt is Evans? Only this much is certain: That question will likely linger for the rest of the season.

Even if Evans manages to play through pain and continue piling up catches for the 49ers, the situation feels tenuous. He seems to limp away from almost every catch, and it’s fair to wonder just how long the 49ers can lean so heavily on him — especially because all the other injuries at wideout are intensifying the load on the 33-year-old.

With all that said, could receiver be a bigger trade need than any defensive position? Shanahan’s team is scheduled to return several big names at the position, but more injuries are possible. The 49ers would probably be wise to add as much pass-catching depth as they can.

With a group that creates even some separation, Purdy has been close to unstoppable.

Special teams miscues

Kicker Eddy Piñeiro missed a first-half extra-point, Jacob Cowing caught a punt inside the 5-yard line and ran into a wall of Commanders players, and special-teams ace Siran Neal committed a terrible penalty that gave Washington hope late in the fourth quarter.

Piñeiro has missed seven extra-points as a 49er, including three this season. If Washington had scored a second fourth-quarter touchdown, the Commanders would have only needed their own extra-point to take the lead.

Cowing won the punt returner job during the preseason, but he has struggled so far and gained just two yards after making an ill-advised decision to field a punt that may have rolled into the end zone for a touchback.

Neal, meanwhile, has been a game-changing player for the 49ers on punt and kick coverage, but his 15-yard penalty for kick-catch interference after Luke McCaffrey called for a fair catch set Washington up with the ball at its own 41-yard line in the final minute of Monday’s game.

The Commanders didn’t take full advantage of the 49ers’ special teams miscues, but a better opponent would have.
