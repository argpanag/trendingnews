Small asteroid hit Earth’s atmosphere on Sunday

A small asteroid burned up in Earth’s atmosphere over the Indian Ocean on Sunday, September 6, 2026. About 6 hours before the space rock hit our atmosphere, the Mount Lemmon Observatory – as part of the Catalina Sky Survey – spotted it, marking the 13th time scientists have ever discovered an asteroid before impact.

The asteroid turned out to be just about 80 centimeters or 2.6 feet across. Its small size is a testament to how well the space surveys are able to find asteroids in the near-Earth environment.

The impact happened over the Indian Ocean off northwestern Australia. While there were no confirmed sightings of the asteroid as it burned up in our atmosphere, the space rock should have hit around 16:07 UTC (11:07 a.m. CDT).

At first the asteroid was described as CERNQ52. Now it has an official name: 2026 RW1.

More about the asteroid 2026 RW1

Adrien Coffinet, a member of the Minor Planet Mailing List, wrote:

If I’m not wrong, this will be the first Aten asteroid (a < 1 AU) detected in space before impact. All the previous impactors detected in space before impact were Apollo asteroids (a > 1 AU).

Aten asteroids are those that have an orbit inside Earth’s, so under 1 astronomical unit (AU). Apollo asteroids are those that are farther from the sun than Earth on average.

Bottom line: A small asteroid impacted Earth’s atmosphere harmlessly on Sunday. Scientists spotted it just 6 hours earlier. It was the 13th asteroid spotted before impact.
