REVIEW: Last Seen (S1 E6/6 The Choice)

TheTools · 07.10.2026 18:33 · source · permalink

At the end of episode five, we left Henry, a badly wounded Jenna and Maggie/Sarah hurtling towards god knows where, with Ian in hot pursuit and Pena also on the trail. By now, Henry and co are making a beeline for his father’s compound, believing this ‘healer’ might save his wife and offer him and Maggie/Sarah refuge. Thanks to Don’s information, Ian is heading there too, setting up the inevitable confrontation.

What follows is a classic, but impressively tense, shootout, culminating in Ian and Henry finally coming face to face. More interestingly, though, Ian suddenly finds himself with his daughter; things don’t go quite as smoothly as he might have expected. The smart money might have been on a neat ending in which Sarah accepts that her mother and father weren’t the people she thought they were and heads off into the sunset with Ian to restart her old life. But with poor Jenna dead, how could her predicament possibly produce such a tidy conclusion? If you have spent your entire life believing two people are your parents, how can you simply think otherwise?

And so it proves. The episode is called The Choice, and Maggie/Sarah has one to make: escape with Henry or go with Ian. She chooses Henry.

I liked this little twist. Even though Henry doesn’t survive, Maggie/Sarah’s decision makes a strange kind of sense. It acknowledges that a situation this crazy, with every character carrying their own guilt and trauma, cannot provide easy answers. Ian remains estranged from his daughter, even after she moves in with Debbie and Sarah – a name she has kept – is back in everyone’s lives.

Some have hailed Last Seen as the best crime drama of the year, but I’m not quite there. It has been very solid, covering themes of male protection and parenting particularly well, but it never connected with me emotionally in the way it clearly has with others. Perhaps the biggest reason is that Maggie/Sarah’s identity was revealed too early. Still, The Choice ends the series on a thoughtful, appropriately messy note.

Paul Hirons
