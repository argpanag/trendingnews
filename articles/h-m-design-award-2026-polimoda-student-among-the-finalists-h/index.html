H&M Design Award 2026: Polimoda student among the finalists - HUB Style

TheTools · 27.10.2026 16:48 · source · permalink

Featured photo of Ann-Sofie-Johansson H&M creative advisor

The new generation of fashion is preparing to take the international stage. H&M announces the eight finalists of the H&M Design Award 2026, among them Evelina Kryvopust, a student at Polimoda in Florence. The award, dedicated to emerging designers, aims to champion new creative visions, supporting young talent as they move from education into the professional world. The result also shines a light on Italian education and the role of fashion schools in developing the creative languages of tomorrow.

H&M Design Award 2026: meet the eight finalists

The eight selected designers come from Japan, the United Kingdom, Italy, France and Belgium. Their collections explore different languages and sensibilities, spanning womenswear, menswear and projects that move beyond the traditional boundaries between the two worlds.

Here are the finalists for the 2026 edition:

  • Chico Yu, Bunka Fashion Graduate University, Japan.
  • Ennis Finnerty Mackay, Central Saint Martins, University of the Arts London, United Kingdom.
  • Evelina Kryvopust, Polimoda, Italy.
  • Fantin Delattre Pirenne, École Nationale Supérieure des Arts Visuels de La Cambre, Belgium.
  • Jessica Lin, Institut Français de la Mode, France.
  • Macy Grimshaw, Central Saint Martins, United Kingdom.
  • Yichun Liu Royal, Royal Academy of Antwerp, Belgium.
  • Yvonne Schichtel, Royal Academy of Antwerp, Belgium.

The young designers’ sources of inspiration range from love and memory to lesser-known neighbourhoods of London and Hong Kong, taking in stories passed down by mothers, the Arte Povera movement and the research of archaeologist Marija Gimbutas. What unites the projects is the desire to develop a recognisable vision of fashion, in which experimentation, cultural research and craftsmanship come together to create personal and distinctive collections.

The selection was made by analysing the portfolios of students on undergraduate and postgraduate degree courses at more than 60 international fashion schools. Creativity, innovation and savoir-faire were the central criteria used to identify the designers called upon to represent the new directions of contemporary fashion.

An international jury spanning fashion, creativity and visual culture

The work of the eight finalists will be assessed by a jury bringing together figures from different areas of the creative industry: from design to communications, journalism to styling. The exchange between different perspectives is intended to identify projects capable of standing out not only for their aesthetics, but also for their research, identity and potential.

Here are the members of the jury for the H&M Design Award 2026.

Ann-Sofie Johansson, creative advisor and head of design at H&M, has more than thirty years’ experience within the group and has helped define the brand’s style, from everyday collections to collaborations with international designers.

Beka Gvishiani, founder of Style Not Com, has turned his platform into a reference source for news, features and commentary on the fashion system, with an immediate and recognisable language.

Calum Knight, a London-based creative director and digital strategist, has led creative projects for artists and names such as Lady Gaga and SHOWstudio, while also collaborating with brands including H&M and Maison Margiela.

Emma Chopova and Laura Lowena, founders and designers of Chopova Lowena, are known for an approach combining folkloric references, punk influences and contemporary experimentation, creating a highly recognisable aesthetic identity.

Hillary Taymour, founder and creative director of Collina Strada, brings to the jury a vision of fashion rooted in experimentation and a focus on sustainable materials.

IB Kamara, stylist, creative director and imagemaker, has worked with names and maisons including Rihanna, Chanel and Dior. He has also led Dazed as editor-in-chief, collaborating with international publications including British Vogue, Vogue Italia, System and W.

Luke Leitch, fashion journalist and critic, writes for Vogue Runway and Vogue Business and is an authoritative voice on the analysis of collections and the industry’s transformations.

Paloma Elsesser, supermodel and activist for greater inclusivity in fashion, is one of the most recognisable faces in the contemporary landscape, appearing on Vogue covers and in international campaigns.

“Each of their portfolios perfectly embodies the spirit of creativity, savoir-faire and innovation that we set out to promote,” said Ann-Sofie Johansson, H&M creative advisor.

IB Kamara also highlighted the value of individual creative identities, reaffirming the importance of supporting designers in the early stages of their professional journeys. The winner of the H&M Design Award 2026 will be announced in London in autumn 2026.

150,000 euros and a mentoring programme up for grabs

Alongside international visibility, the award aims to turn creative potential into a tangible professional opportunity. Each of the eight finalists will receive 10,000 euros, a contribution intended to support the next stage of their career. The overall winner, meanwhile, will receive 150,000 euros and a personalised one-year mentoring programme.

The programme will cover key aspects of developing a fashion project: from production to marketing, public relations and sustainability, through to models linked to the circular economy.

So it is not only financial support, but also the tools and skills needed to understand the industry’s dynamics and guide a creative vision towards commercial realisation. The winning collection will be produced and sold online and in a selection of H&M stores worldwide. This opportunity will allow the selected designer’s work to reach an international audience and engage directly with the market.

H&M and Central Saint Martins: new scholarships for fashion talent

The 2026 edition expands its commitment to education through a scholarship programme created in collaboration with Central Saint Martins, part of the University of the Arts London.

The initiative provides financial support for two students, from the United Kingdom or overseas, accepted onto the BA (Hons) Fashion course, for the full duration of their studies. This is joined by maintenance grants awarded annually to two final-year students, for a period of three years.

The aim is to make fashion education more accessible, giving young creatives the opportunity to begin or complete their studies regardless of their financial circumstances. The scholarships therefore complement the support offered to the finalists, extending the award’s reach beyond the competition and strengthening its educational dimension.

The future of fashion lies with new designers

Returning after a six-year hiatus, the H&M Design Award renews its focus on a generation of creatives called upon to engage with an ever-changing industry. Research, experimentation, sustainability and the ability to build a recognisable identity are increasingly important in turning an academic project into a professional journey.

The presence of Evelina Kryvopust, a student at Polimoda, among the eight finalists represents recognition for Italian education and for the work of the schools that support young designers as they develop their own language.

The award’s ambition is to go beyond celebrating talent: to support creatives in the early stages of their careers, provide them with tools for growth and create real opportunities to connect with the public. Because the future of fashion is built not only on the catwalk, but also through the ideas, skills and opportunities that enable new designers to turn their vision into reality.
