Virtus falls, and it does so heavily. It's the first home defeat for a Black V that, last year, finished the season undefeated at the PalaDozza. Finding itself up against Zalgiris, a traditional bête noire, Mumbrù's boys are confused and unable to run as they would have liked. Indeed, it's Kaunas who makes better use of the transition than Bologna, who, after feasting under Olimpia's baskets on Sunday at the Forum, succumbs to Valanciunas' physicality and aggression.

Mumbrù tries early with Kokila, but the Lithuanian is too big and bulky to be contained. Virtus tries to attack him, trying to tire him out, but the game plan lasts a quarter, or rather, a half.

In the first round, Virtus soars thanks in part to Derrick Alston's desire. Left out in Milan, Derrick is incredibly eager to showcase his talent.

On the other side of the court is Carsen Edwards, the former player. In the end, the Bulldozers' score will be only six points, two fewer than the current Edwards (Kessler). But despite Carsen (more boos than applause, but he salutes everyone at the end of the game) being overshadowed on defense by Moore's talent, Kaunas is on a different path.

The last truly close encounter came early in the third quarter, when a triple from the usual Alston brought Bologna back to within 1, 38-39, but Kaunas always found a way to escape. At 51-65, the game seemed over. But remembering Milan, and their previous encounters, Virtus tried to give it their all. But the comeback attempt by a team that was still young and lacking Euroleague experience stalled at 62-70. Afterward, on a night when the Black V celebrated Augusto Binelli—a Hall of Fame inductee for the record-holder for appearances for the Bianconeri—there was room for coaching skirmishes. With the game over, with nine seconds remaining, Masiulis called a one-minute timeout.

It's clear right away that Mumbrù didn't appreciate it. Virtus gets the ball back. With nine-tenths of a second to go, the Catalan calls a timeout. Virtus loses, but Alex's message is clear: his Virtus can win or lose. But it always deserves the utmost respect. It's hard to argue with him.
