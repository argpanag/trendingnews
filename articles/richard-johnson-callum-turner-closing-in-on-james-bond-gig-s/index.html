RICHARD JOHNSON | Callum Turner closing in on James Bond gig: source

TheTools · 15.11.2026 14:00 · source · permalink

Who will be the next James Bond? Best bet is Callum Turner.

My most reliable source in Hollywood says Turner, 36, will try to fill the shoes of Sean Connery, Roger Moore, Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig.

Turner is a former soccer player and fashion model married to pop star Dua Lipa, who could provide the film’s theme song, joining the likes of Shirley Bassey, Tom Jones, Madonna, Tina Turner and Adele.

Amazon MGM Studios paid over $1 billion to the Broccoli family for the rights to 007. “Henry Cavill, who played Superman, would have gotten the role if he was five years younger,” said my source.

“Amazon would like someone who is in their 30s. And Turner being married to one of the biggest pop stars in the world adds to his allure.”

Several other British actors have been considered, led by Jack Lowden of “Slow Horses,” and Robert Pattinson, who garnered raves for “Primetime.”

Dean McDermott, who had five children with Tori Spelling before they divorced, credits her with saving his life.

“Tori was there for me 1,000 percent, and tried to help me,” McDermott said. “I didn’t think I had a problem.”

The actor started “black-out drinking” at 16, snorting cocaine at 20, then opiates and Vicodin. “It was off to the races,” he told me. It took years before he realized he needed help.

“You have to hit rock bottom,” he said. “Getting clean and sober doesn’t work the first time. Relapse is part of the journey.”

McDermott spoke this week at Nancy Davis’ Cure Addiction Now event in Beverly Hills. Davis lost her son Jason to overdose after he battled his addiction for a dozen years.

“For far too long, we treated addiction as a moral failure instead of a life-threatening disease. People suffering from addiction are not bad people; they have a bad disease,” Davis told the crowd.

Davis introduced Siedah Garrett, who performed “Love You I Do” and “Man in The Mirror,” bringing the crowd to their feet. Alec Benjamin sang “Water Fountain” and was joined by composer and musician David Foster for “Let Me Down Slowly.” X Ambassadors’ Sam Nelson Harris, introduced by McDermott, finished with “Renegades” and “Unsteady.”

Guests included Brandon Davis, Catherine Bach, Dyan Cannon, Jane Seymour and Tom Arnold.

McDermott just auditioned for a part in the second season of “Neagley” on Amazon Prime Video, a spinoff of “Reacher.”

The role is that of a crooked congressman. “Those parts are fun to play,” said McDermott. “Bad guys are much more fun.”

Melissa Etheridge opened up about how money and fame can’t shield one from depression at Audrey Gruss’ Hope for Depression Research Foundation’s 20th anniversary luncheon at The Plaza Hotel.

The two-time Grammy winner, discussing the death of her son Beckett at 21 from fentanyl, said, “How come I had everything that one could want, but I could do nothing for my child?

“The grief was so very difficult that when my fan club started a GoFundMe just because they wanted to help, I realized I had a few thousand dollars and maybe I could start something. Maybe I could help.”

She founded the Etheridge Foundation to discover new treatments for opioid addiction.

As for her own depression, “I thought you just get famous, you get rich and then everything’s great, right? … I never said, oh, I’m depressed or anything, I didn’t think I had the right to be depressed.”

She thinks that treating her depression helped her fight cancer. “If I let my brain, if I let my emotional body take over my physical body with what we call depression or these darker thoughts, my body’s going to believe it and that cancer’s going to return. This is my belief and I’m now 23 years cancer free.”

Cornelia Guest, whose godfather Edward VIII was once the king of England, hosted an exhibition of her mother’s clothes in Atlanta, Georgia.

“C.Z. Guest: Portrait of an American Style Icon” at SCAD FASH Museum of Fashion + Film features the work of designers Carolina Herrera, Chanel, Oscar de la Renta, Adolfo, Valentino and Courrèges.

CZ Guest was one of Truman Capote’s legendary Swans, a muse to Salvador Dalí and Diego Rivera, and an inductee into the International Best Dressed List Hall of Fame in 1959.

Cornelia donated her mother’s extraordinary wardrobe and accessories to SCAD because, “I knew this collection would be in a place where students could truly learn from it and understand how the clothes were made.

“My mother’s clothes are timeless and so beautiful, and I hope generations to come will learn from them and be inspired by them.”

Stefano Tonchi, editor-in-chief of Palmer magazine, and the museum’s Ariel Stark curated the show, which includes photographs of CZ by Slim Aarons, Irving Penn, Bruce Weber, Cecil Beaton, Lord Snowdon, and Ron Galella.

Guests at the party given by the show’s sponsor, Max Mara, included photographer Bruce Weber, philanthropist Kimberly Paige Bluhm, Kevin Kwan (author of “Crazy Rich Asians”), Robert Dupont, R. Couri Hay, and Darren Star, who created “Emily in Paris.”

Former Knicks dancer Marcella Hymowitz and her husband, businessman Gregg Hymowitz, were honored at the 20th anniversary of the Challenged Athletes Foundation’s Heroes, Heart & Hope Gala this week in the newly renovated Waldorf Astoria.

The night raised over $3 million, thanks to the generous couple, who gave a 250k matching fund to get the bidding started.

The Hymowitzs have been longtime supporters of CAF’s commitment to increasing access to sports for athletes with physical disabilities.

Marcella, the proprietor of The Pearl NYC workout and dance studio on East 84th Street, said, “Movement is vital to mental health. I believe that the freedom to move, to compete, and to express ourselves physically is one of the most precious gifts we have.”
