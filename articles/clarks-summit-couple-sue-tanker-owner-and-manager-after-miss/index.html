Clarks Summit couple sue tanker owner and manager after Mississippi River collision

SCRANTON, LACKAWANNA CO. (WOLF) — A retired Marywood University professor and his wife are suing the owner and manager of a tanker that struck their riverboat in New Orleans, alleging the collision caused serious injuries and the passenger vessel to capsize and partially sink.

Jay and Elizabeth Clymer of Clarks Summit were aboard the M/V American Heritage on Nov. 13. The lawsuit says the 525-foot asphalt tanker M/V Atlantic Asphalt struck the riverboat at about 12:29 p.m. as it was docked at the Thalia Street Terminal for passengers to disembark.

The Clymers’ attorney, Robert Moran, said they were seated near the area where the tanker breached the vessel. He said they narrowly escaped going into the frigid water shortly before the riverboat sank. The collision prompted an emergency rescue and evacuation, according to the lawsuit.

The complaint, filed Nov. 19 in Orleans Parish Civil District Court, names the tanker’s owner, Nave Shipping S.A., and ship manager, Thome Ship Management Pte Ltd. It alleges the tanker was unseaworthy and that the defendants failed to operate and maintain it safely, properly train its crew, account for river conditions and prepare for an emergency. The allegations have not been proven in court.

The lawsuit says Elizabeth Clymer, a retired registered nurse, suffered neck, back and ankle injuries, a concussion and post-traumatic stress disorder, among other injuries. Jay Clymer, a retired biology and environmental science professor at Marywood University, also alleges physical injuries and post-traumatic stress disorder. Both claim mental and emotional distress, loss of personal property and reduced enjoyment of life.

Before the collision, Elizabeth Clymer had helped a fellow passenger experiencing a medical emergency during the cruise, attorney Rico Alvendia said. She was hospitalized after the crash, and the couple remains under medical care in Pennsylvania, their attorneys said.

The Clymers are seeking damages for medical expenses, pain and suffering, lost wages, property loss and other claims, as well as punitive damages. The complaint does not state a dollar amount and requests a jury trial. The case was assigned to Judge Lori Jupiter.

The attorneys said this is the second lawsuit filed over the collision. An earlier case was brought on behalf of first responders injured during rescue operations.
