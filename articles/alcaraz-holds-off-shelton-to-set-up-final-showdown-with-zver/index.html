Alcaraz holds off Shelton to set up final showdown with Zverev at ATP Finals

TheTools · 21.11.2026 21:35 · source · permalink

  • Spaniard beats American 3-6, 6-3, 7-6 (1) in semi-final

  • Alexander Zverev sees off Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (0), 6-4

Spaniard beats American 3-6, 6-3, 7-6 (1) in semi-final

Alexander Zverev sees off Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (0), 6-4

The man himself was not present in Turin on Saturday afternoon, but one of the most intriguing developments on and off court in men’s tennis this year was Ben Shelton’s surprise decision to hire the former world No 1 Juan Carlos Ferrero as his new coach alongside his father, Bryan. Ferrero is one of the most highly respected coaches on tour, but he also knows Carlos Alcaraz better than any other coach in the world.

However, there is only so much that anyone can do against one of the sport’s greatest talents. Shelton, the third seed, played a spectacular match, serving monstrously, attacking without hesitation and volleying well in decisive moments, but it still wasn’t enough to defeat the second seeded Alcaraz, who maintained his focus after an uncertain start as he reached the final of the ATP Finals with a 3-6, 6-3, 7-6 (1) win.

The indoor hard court swing has long been the most difficult part of the season for Alcaraz, both because of his difficulties with fatigue at the end of a long year and his relative vulnerability in faster conditions. However, at the end of a turbulent 2026 he has made significant progress indoors. Alcaraz will face Alexander Zverev, the world No 1, in the final after the German reached the final with a 7-6 (0), 6-4 win against Daniil Medvedev.

Zverev, a two-time ATP Finals champion, is yet to drop a set or concede a service game in Turin, with the rapid conditions further fortifying his enormous serve. Despite a tight opening set, Medvedev failed to generate a single break point in their 88 minutes on court.

This will mark Zverev’s first meeting with Alcaraz since he won his first two grand slam titles this year, at Roland Garros and the US Open. In their last meeting, Alcaraz dramatically defeated Zverev in five sets at the Australian Open in January.

One of the interesting developments of this indoor season has been the continued trend of hard courts across the tour speeding up. Paris and Turin represent two of the fastest courts of the past decade and these are exactly the conditions a big server like Shelton would prefer against Alcaraz. However, Alcaraz’s level has been supreme throughout this tournament.

After his loss to Shelton at the quarter-finals of the US Open two months ago, one issue Alcaraz felt he needed to address was the quality of his serve. He made a number of changes to his action once he returned home, particularly simplifying the start of his motion to find greater balance and lowering his toss. After starting slowly and conceding his opening service game, the quality of Alcaraz’s serve was perhaps the defining point of the match.

It improved throughout the match and by the final set he simply did not seem at risk of losing his serve. That security behind his first serve allowed him to start the final set tie-break completely relaxed. After Shelton fired down an ace on the first point, Alcaraz immediately followed him with two unreturned serves. While the Spaniard’s confidence skyrocketed, a forehand error from Shelton and a rare missed volley on consecutive service points quickly sealed his fate.

Despite ending his year with a defeat and understandable frustration about his wasteful errors at the close, Shelton played an excellent match. Along with his monstrous serving, the most impressive part of his performance was his netplay. Time and time again, he made excellent volleys on key points, particularly on his serve in the final set.

Shelton still has very clear weaknesses that he must continue to work on, but he ends 2026 having put together by far the best season of his career. His highlights included a first grand slam final at the US Open, four titles including the Montreal ATP 1000, and the solidification of his position as the world No 4.

This match was accompanied by ample intrigue. The added dynamic of Ferrero’s presence in Shelton’s team now makes their matches a must-watch but there were also questions over Alcaraz’s fitness after he felt pain in his left thigh during his win over Medvedev on Wednesday. Alcaraz had an MRI scan after that match.

His thigh heavily taped, he responded to that setback by dismantling his good friend Flavio Cobolli to advance to the semi-finals without dropping a set and then he rallied from behind here to reach the final in Turin for a second consecutive year. He now stands on the brink of securing one of the few significant achievements that have eluded him so far.
