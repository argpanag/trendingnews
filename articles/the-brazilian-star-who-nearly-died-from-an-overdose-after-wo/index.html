The Brazilian star who nearly died from an overdose after World Cup snub

TheTools · 12.11.2026 15:45 · source · permalink

Once considered one of the world’s best strikers, the former Porto and Sporting star opens up about the personal struggles that derailed his career.

Soccer is full of stories about players who looked destined for greatness but never lived up to their potential. One of the most striking examples from the late 1990s and early 2000s is Mário Jardel. The Brazilian striker burst onto the scene as one of the most prolific goalscorers in the game, averaging almost a goal per game during the early years of his career. He also won the Golden Shoe twice as the world’s top goalscorer. There was little doubt he was a generational talent.

But Jardel’s career fell apart in a remarkably short space of time. Weight problems, addiction, disciplinary issues and poor decisions sent him into a downward spiral. Now, in an interview with La Gazzetta dello Sport, he has opened up about the events that derailed his career. One moment stands out above all others: his omission from Brazil’s squad for the 2002 World Cup.

Brazil’s World Cup snub still haunts Jardel

“Nobody ever gave me an explanation. Between the late 1990s and early 2000s, I was one of the five best strikers in the world. I scored 168 goals in 175 games over four years at Porto, and in the season before the World Cup in South Korea and Japan, I scored 55 goals in 42 games for Sporting Lisbon, winning my second Golden Shoe. I understand that Brazil had some phenomenal players, but I deserved a call-up,” Jardel said.

He still cannot understand why he was left out of the squad that would go on to win Brazil’s fifth World Cup title.

The summer of 2002 marked the beginning of the end. Jardel does not blame all his subsequent problems on missing the World Cup, but he sees his omission as the first domino to fall in a chain of events that would ultimately destroy his career.

“I fell into depression and started drinking heavily. Playing in a World Cup was my lifelong dream. My first wife left me, and there were problems involving custody of our children. I felt like everything was slipping away from me,” he said.

From depression to cocaine addiction

The breakdown of his marriage, depression and alcohol proved too much for Jardel to handle, but his troubles did not end there. A year after his life began to unravel, the Brazilian started experimenting with drugs.

“In the summer of 2003, I tried cocaine. I was on vacation and knew I could use it during that period because there was no drug testing. I liked it. It made me feel alive instead of depressed. Once, I stayed awake for an entire week. Then I overdosed and nearly died. A friend who was with me saved my life. That was when I hit rock bottom,” the former striker admitted.

At the time, Jardel was playing alongside a teenage Cristiano Ronaldo.

After that harrowing experience, Jardel began trying to put his life back together, although by then his professional career was already beyond repair. His determination, along with the arrival of someone who would become hugely important to him, helped him turn things around.

“I spent months talking to psychiatrists. I finally got out of it for good when I met my second wife, Sandra. She helped me understand the meaning of life, and I’m happy today.”

Weight problems and a spell with Italy’s worst-ever Serie A team

During the darkest period of his personal life, Jardel ended up at English club Bolton Wanderers. After a particularly difficult year, the Brazilian began neglecting his fitness and put on considerable weight, eventually leading to the termination of his contract.

He subsequently joined Italian side Ancona, who hold the dubious distinction of being the worst team in Serie A history after being relegated with just 13 points.

“I have nothing but bad memories. They didn’t pay me for six months, and I only played three games,” he said of his time at the club.

Jardel’s move to Italy came through someone who would go on to become one of soccer’s most influential agents.

“Jorge Mendes brought me there. He wasn’t my agent, but he was a close friend. He had contacts in Italy. I was 31, overweight and looking for a club where I could play regularly,” Jardel explained.

A career without direction

Jardel’s decline on the field turned him into a journeyman. Between 2003 and 2010, he played for 11 different clubs, making only sporadic appearances and rarely spending a full season at any of them.

His career took him to England, Italy, Argentina, Brazil, Portugal, Cyprus, Australia and Bulgaria. He played in eight different countries, and also had a brief spell in Spain after signing for Alaves, although he never made a single appearance for the club.
