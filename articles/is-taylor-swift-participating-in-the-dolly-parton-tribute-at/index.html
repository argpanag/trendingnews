Is Taylor Swift Participating in the Dolly Parton Tribute at CMA Awards 2026? The Truth

TheTools · 11.11.2026 20:07 · source · permalink

Ahead of the 2026 CMA Awards on Nov. 18, where Dolly Parton is set to be honored, a source confirmed to E! News whether Taylor Swift will be taking the stage.

Taylor Swift won't be honoring the life of this showgirl in Nashville. 

While a tribute to Dolly Parton will be performed at the 2026 Country Music Association Awards on Nov. 18, the "Patient Zero" singer won't be feeling the magic onstage, a CMA source confirmed to E! News.

The annual award show didn't announce which honky tonk angels would be hitting all the right notes in honor of the "Jolene" singer—who died at age 80 in August after a brief battle with cancer—but Kacey Musgraves, Miranda Lambert, Cody Johnson, Ella Langley, Luke Combs and Megan Moroney are among those performing at the Bridgestone Arena.  

Swift's love for Parton was like a butterfly, as soft and gentle as a sigh. And although she won't be part of the tribute, the Grammy winner never left a blank space when it came to her feelings.

"A world without Dolly doesn't feel possible, real, or right," Swift said in a statement to E! News after Parton's death in August. "She was truly the gift that kept on giving. Dolly gave us someone to look up to while encouraging us to be who we are as individuals. Somehow she was able to give us her lessons about the world gently and honestly, simultaneously, and oftentimes hilariously."

"Along with her other fans, I am so grateful for all of it," she continued. "For the melodies and lyrics she wrote that thawed even the coldest hearts. For the millions of books she provided children all over the world so that they might fall in love with stories the way she did. For the tightropes she walked so that young girls like me could grow up dreaming of joining the circus too."

And that's not to say the "Fate of Ophelia" singer hasn't paid tribute to Parton on stage. In fact, she dedicated her Video of the Year win at the 2026 MTV Video Music Awards in September to the "9 to 5" singer.

"I want to thank and dedicate this award to one person, without whom this wouldn't be happening, and that person is the ultimate showgirl, Dolly Parton," Swift said while accepting the Moon Person trophy. "Every single song that she wrote, her storytelling was so vivid and so rich." 

"Listening to her songs, it's like watching the best music video you have ever seen in your life every single time you hear what she has written," she added. "She handled this thing with such grace and joy and love and gratitude for the fact that she got to do this and everything that happened in her life or her career. She channeled back into love and gratitude and appreciation for her fans." 

As country music fans prepare for the CMA Awards, keep reading for more sweet dedications to Parton.

Stella Parton

Parton's younger sister shared moving insight into their special bond in her tribute.

"Every Tuesday about this time, before the sun came up and the world was still asleep, she and I, for many years, had our coffee together over the phone," she wrote on social media. "Being little farm girls we've continued to be early risers."

She added, "As she liked to say; 'shoot Tedda, we get more work done before the sun comes up than most folks do all day long!' We would catch up on gossip, as sisters do, and drink coffee and make plans, as it has always been our way"

Billy Ray Cyrus

Cyrus reflected on his longtime friendship with the "Love Is Like a Butterfly" singer.

"I have so many moments and memories with Dolly, it’s hard to know where to start," he explained on Instagram. "Like every little boy, I grew up loving Dolly Parton. She was a part of our lives, on our TV’s, right in our living rooms."

He also recalled a sweet note he received from Parton before her death.

"Dolly recently sent me a beautiful message, telling me she loved me with her whole heart," Cyrus added. "I will always love her in the same way ….. with my whole heart … and I always will."

 

Miley Cyrus

The "Malibu" singer paid tribute to her godmother as she shared insight into their last conversation.

"The feeling of losing my Aunt Dolly is beyond what feels right to share," Cyrus wrote on Instagram. "Our most sacred moments were private, andthey'lll stay there behind the stage, beneath the glamour & between our hearts."

"What I KNOW is Dolly would want me to feel it, write a song about it, and then continue and be strong. I have an angel by my side," she continued. "I always have. One of our last conversations was about music and metamorphosis."

Wynonna Judd

The "Kentucky Queen" singer shared how Parton supported her after her mom Noami Judd died by suicide in April 2022.

"To imagine a world without @dollyparton is almost unthinkable," she began on Instagram. "The mark she left on music, our culture, and the human spirit is greater than any of us could ever hope to leave behind."

"When Mom passed, Dolly showed up for me in a way I'll never forget," she continued. "That's who she was—she showed up. She was family. She was a light in my life and in the lives of millions of people who may never have had the privilege of knowing her personally, but somehow felt like they did. There will never be another Dolly. I will miss my friend more than words can say."

She ended her tribute with a message to her longtime friend: "From one mountain woman to another, I will always love you."

Seth Meyers

The Late Night host—who was joined by Parton in 2019—recalled two stories from the country singer's appearance.

"I told her backstage that my three-year-old's favorite song was 'Jolene,'" he explained on Instagram, "and she made me FaceTime so she could sing it to him."
 
"Later she shot a promo for her NBC Christmas special," he continued, "and when she got on a stool wearing her short skirt she said to the audience, 'I gotta be careful or else I'm gonna show y'all my box office!'"

The former Saturday Night Live member praised Parton's joke.

"Too dirty for church, too clean for the bar," he added. "Just like her, it was just right.  What a light."

Barbara Eden

"I know I speak for many when I state that there is now a void in the heart of Americana... no, the whole world, that may never be filled," the I Dream of Jeannie star wrote on X. "Dolly, words cannot define the enormity of your talent, the breadth of your kindness, the depth of your compassion and the expanse in which your legend will carry on in perpetuity."

She recalled meeting Parton at the Country Music Awards.

"You were not only someone whose smile and presence were larger than life, your heart was even larger," she continued. "I lift my eyes upward and know that while you've left us here, you're singing amongst the angels, assuredly giving the host of Heaven a run for their money."

Derek Hough

"Heartbroken to hear about the passing of Dolly Parton," the dancer shared on Instagram. "She was an absolute icon, a true legend not just for her music, but for her spirit, her kindness, her joy, her light."

The Dancing With the Stars pro also detailed working with the country singer and fellow dancers Mark Ballas and Cheryl Burke for a performance of "9 to 5" and "Better Get to Livin'" on the dance competition in 2007.

"I was lucky enough to share the stage with her, something I will never take for granted," he added. "Her music and the way she made people feel will live on forever. Sending love to all she touched. Thank you, Dolly. You will never be forgotten."

Kelsea Ballerini

"My East Tennessee angel," Ballerini—who narrated part of Parton's 2022 audiobook Run, Rose, Run—began in her Instagram Story, "Thank you for every ounce of magic and butterflies, acceptance and joy, heart and music you brought to this world. We will always love you Dolly."

Katy Perry

For me, she was an example of a woman who defied the stereotypes for decades. She proved that big boobs, big brains, and a big voice can all exist in one tiny body!"

But the "Dark Horse" singer emphasized that Parton had an even bigger heart.

"I had the honor of meeting and singing with her several times. Every time I saw Dolly, she was a pure, sweet (but not saccharine) sassy, cup of joy," she wrote. "Her way of looking at life with a sense of humor has always been an inspiration to me…"


"If there’s someone you wanna look up to and model your life after, it’s Dolly," Perry concluded. "Dolly was glam, funny, and kind as fuck. Her star will forever shine bright and I bet you she’s already remodeling them pearly gates with rhinestones."

Mick Jagger

"What a wonderful songwriter and singer Dolly was," he praised on X. "She brought so many people together, we will all miss her so much."

Daryl Hall

"I've known Dolly since the '70s," the Hall & Oates alum explained on X. "Her loss is immeasurable. She was such a kind and genuinely nice person. And I still don't know how she played so well with those long fingernails! What an amazing mystery…"

The Muppets

"We will miss her and her presence on this earth," the message continued, "but we will reap all the rewards of her life dedicated to making it a better place."

He also reflected on a recent visit to Parton's amusement park Dollywood.
