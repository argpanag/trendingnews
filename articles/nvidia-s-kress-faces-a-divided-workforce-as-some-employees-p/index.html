Nvidia’s Kress faces a divided workforce as some employees push for Huang’s return

TheTools · 18.11.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

In this article

The day before Colette Kress led her first earnings call as Nvidia's interim CEO, she did not want to talk about her predecessor, Jensen Huang.

"This is a new era for Nvidia," she told employees in an internal meeting on Monday, according to a person present, who spoke on the condition of anonymity. When someone brought up Huang, Kress quickly changed the subject, according to the person.

On Wednesday, Kress addressed the whole company at the first Nvidia all-hands meeting since the public chaos over Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy and other reported relationships. And while Kress will be tasked with filling the leadership vacuum created by Huang's departure, she is also tamping down public calls to bring him back.

Huang himself weighed in with his first public statement Tuesday saying he would be "honored" to return to Nvidia.

Kress, who has served as the company's CFO since 2013, must steady employees and convince customers and investors that Nvidia can execute on its ambitious targets without the man who made them.

At Wednesday's meeting with staffers, Kress was joined by newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones as well as Ian Buck, who oversees Nvidia's hyperscale and high performance computing business, according to an attendee.

Kress' message was, "Let's move forward," said the person, who asked not to be named because the event was private.

Nvidia's leaders have made clear that the board does not plan to bring Huang back.

"The board has determined that they will not reinstate Jensen," Kress said in an interview with CNBC's Jim Cramer on Tuesday. "We want to grow in terms of everything that we need to do with our employees, our customers, our shareholders, and that is where we are going to focus moving forward."

Jones echoed the sentiment on Tuesday's earnings call. "The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," he said in opening remarks.

Nvidia declined to comment for this story.

Kress stressed on the earnings call that the company was focused on the workers who remain, who she said are "committed to doing their life's work at Nvidia." The company also reported better-than-expected earnings and seemed to calm investors' nerves, at least for the moment.

Some employees are telling a different story. Support for Huang's return is growing inside Nvidia, sources tell CNBC. An online petition circulating since the weekend has nearly 200,000 signatures, with more than 10,000 of them claiming to be current Nvidia employees. Nvidia currently has roughly 42,000 employees.

Outside Nvidia, Huang's reinstatement has been called for by at least two major investors and tech heavyweights such as Elon Musk, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney. President Donald Trump said he "would not mind" seeing Huang return as CEO.

Several employees posted on X over the last few days, voicing support for Huang and calling for his return. One robotics engineer said the founder's departure led to his own decision to leave. 

Another senior Nvidia employee, Nader Khalil, sent a letter to the company's board and top executives warning that failing to reinstate Huang could lead to a loss of "irreplaceable talent," according to The Wall Street Journal. 

Huang was known for his deep day-to-day involvement in his company's operations, often responding directly to engineers and chiming in personally on technical projects.

Engineering employees, in particular, were "extremely devoted to him, almost like a demigod," according to a former Nvidia employee who was at the company for more than 15 years and spoke to CNBC on condition of anonymity.

Wednesday's all-hands is a standard meeting: Nvidia traditionally holds one on the morning after each quarterly earnings call, according to the former employee.

Still, the meeting is unlike anything the company has experienced. Employees told CNBC that they plan to share their grievances at the all-hands, though it wasn't clear ahead of time if they would be allowed to ask questions.

Huang took time to address employee questions during previous post-earnings all-hands meetings, the former Nvidia employee told CNBC, adding that Kress is "eager to demonstrate there's a new sheriff in town."

Kress ended Tuesday's earnings call with an upbeat message: "Our customers and our partners are with us. And employees are committed. We've got this."

— CNBC's Lora Kolodny contributed to this report.
