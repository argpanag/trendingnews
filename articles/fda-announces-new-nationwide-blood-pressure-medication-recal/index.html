FDA Announces New Nationwide Blood Pressure Medication Recall

TheTools · 27.09.2026 17:06 · source · permalink

The September 3 recall involves more than 13,000 bottles.

  • The FDA has classified an ongoing voluntary recall of certain chlorthalidone 25 mg tablets as a Class II recall.
  • The September recall involves more than 13,000 bottles of one lot of the medication due to failed dissolution specifications, which could mean the drug doesn’t dissolve properly and may not work as intended.
  • If you have the recalled medication, contact your healthcare provider or pharmacist before stopping or changing your medication.

If you take blood pressure medication—or know someone who does—there’s a new recall you’ll want to know about.

Inventia Healthcare first recalled certain lots of chlorthalidone tablets, which are used to treat high blood pressure, back in June. Then, on September 3, the company initiated a separate recall involving an additional lot of the medication. On September 17, the FDA classified the September recall as Class II. (The June recall has also been classified as Class II).

Chlorthalidone is a diuretic, or “water pill,” that makes your kidneys shed excess water and salt. This helps lower high blood pressure and reduce water retention, lessening your chances of organ damage that could cause heart attack, heart disease, heart failure, stroke, kidney damage, or damage to your eyes that could impact your vision.

A Class II recall, according to the FDA, means that if you use or are exposed to a recalled product, you may experience temporary or medically reversible health issues, but the probability of those issues being serious is considered remote.

The reason for the recall is listed as “failed dissolution specifications.” The FDA defines proper dissolution as the active ingredient dissolving from its dosage form so it can be absorbed by the body. In other words, if a medication doesn't dissolve as intended, your body may not absorb enough of the active ingredient, which could make the drug less effective at treating your condition. No further information about the reason for this specific recall has been provided by Inventia Healthcare or the FDA.

Recalled items

There are more than 13,000 bottles involved in the September recall. Check the label of any bottle you have for the following information:

  • Chlorthalidone tablets, USP, 25 mg, 100 tablets per bottle, Rx only,
  • Manufactured by: Inventia Healthcare Limited
  • NDC: 64980-599-01
  • Batch#: RISA24002
  • Expiration date: 04/2027

If you can’t confirm all of this information on your prescription label, contact your pharmacist for help.

What to do if you have the recalled medication

Contact your healthcare provider right away. You don’t want to make any decisions about stopping this medication without their medical guidance. If you feel ill in any way, make sure you tell your doctor about that immediately, too.
