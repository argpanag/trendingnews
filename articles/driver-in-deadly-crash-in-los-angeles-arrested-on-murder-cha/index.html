Driver in deadly crash in Los Angeles arrested on murder charges

TheTools · 11.11.2026 18:29 · source · permalink

The driver who officials say plowed into a group of pedestrians and food vendors, killing three and seriously injuring two others, has been arrested for murder, police said.

Ihor Demianenko, 61, was driving a minivan at an "unsafe speed" in the North Hollywood area just before 6 p.m. local time on Nov. 10 when he veered to the right, collided with a traffic light and struck a group of people on the sidewalk, the Los Angeles Police Department said in a statement. Demianenko remained trapped in his vehicle while pedestrians and property were "thrown in different directions," police said.

Paramedics with the Los Angeles Fire Department pronounced three women dead at the scene and transported two more to a local hospital in critical condition. Police said the victims were all Hispanic women between the ages of 40 and 60, but did not identify them pending notification of their next of kin.

Demianenko, who suffered minor injuries, was "determined to be under the influence" and later arrested on three counts of murder, police said. His bail was set at over $6 million.

"I’m heartbroken for the families of everyone impacted," Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass said in a Nov. 10 statement on social media. "We will continue to provide updates as more information becomes available."

What happened in the deadly North Hollywood crash?

Police said that a white Toyota Sienna was traveling south on Lankershim Boulevard, which runs through North Hollywood, a commercial and arts district located in the San Fernando Valley, when it ran off the road and struck people on the sidewalk near the metro station. Los Angeles Police Department Captain Warner Castillo said at brief news conference that the driver was conscious after being removed from the vehicle and appeared to be in shock.

Aerial footage from KCAL-TV, a local CBS News affiliate, showed a white vehicle stopped on a sidewalk beneath a blue canopy and against the side of a public transit shelter. Aerial images from KTLA 5, another local television station, showed a large emergency response near the North Hollywood Metro station as firefighters, paramedics and police officers worked at the scene.

Joe Everett, an assistant chief with the fire department, said a battalion chief happened upon the crash scene at the corner of Chandler Boulevard and Lankershim Boulevard shortly before 6 p.m. local time and found a vehicle that had "careened into a number of people on a sidewalk."

Police initially responded to what was reported as an assault with a deadly weapon involving a vehicle, according to Castillo. Investigators gathered surveillance video and interviewed witnesses as they worked to determine what caused the crash, police said.
