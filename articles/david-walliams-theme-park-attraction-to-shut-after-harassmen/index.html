David Walliams theme park attraction to shut after ‘harassment’ scandal

TheTools · 03.11.2026 16:00 · source · permalink

Merlin Entertainments says Alton Towers attraction will close at the end of Tuesday

The David Walliams attraction at Alton Towers is to close permanently, after The Telegraph exposed claims that he had harassed young women.

The owner of the theme park has announced that following a review of its attraction the World of David Walliams – which featured rides such as Gangsta Granny and Raj’s Bouncy Bottom Burp! – is to close its doors at the end of Tuesday.

Almost a year ago, Walliams was dropped by his publisher following an investigation by The Telegraph into allegations of inappropriate behaviour.

Walliams, one of the country’s best-selling children’s authors, faced accusations that he had “harassed” junior female employees at Harper Collins.

One woman who raised concerns was given a five-figure payoff by the publisher and left the business.

Former staff also said employees were told to work in “pairs” when meeting Walliams and were advised not to visit his home.

He has strongly denied the allegations.

Merlin Entertainments, which has operated Alton Towers since 2007, said on Tuesday that it had taken the decision to shut World of David Williams after carrying out its annual review process at the end of the main season.

A spokesman said: “As the 2026 peak season at Alton Towers Resort comes to an end and following our regular review of rides and attractions across the resort, we have taken the decision to permanently close the World of David Walliams.”

The announcement coincides with the seasonal closure of Alton Towers until next year.

The World of David Walliams opened in 2021, promising “brilliant rides” inspired by the characters in his books, including one called the Royal Carousel and a merchandise outlet called Raj’s Shop.

Alton Towers said it had yet to decide the future use of the area occupied by The World of David Walliams.

Walliams, who rose to fame more than 20 years ago through the comedy series Little Britain, has since become one of Britain’s most successful authors, with more than 40 books and short story collections to his name and sales of more than 60 million copies.

In 2019 it was estimated that he and his publisher had sold £100m worth of books.

Walliams’s books have been widely used in schools and translated into 55 languages, as well as being adapted for television by the BBC.

In September, Harper Collins announced it would no longer print his books, which include Gangsta Granny and The Boy in the Dress.

The publisher had maintained his catalogue of previous titles after dropping him as an author, but has now dropped his entire backlist.

It will also phase out its bookshop stock under the plans and remove Walliams’s e-book and audiobook versions that remain available online.

A spokesman for Walliams said in December last year: “David has never been informed of any allegations raised against him by Harper Collins.

“He was not party to any investigation or given any opportunity to answer questions.

“David strongly denies that he has behaved inappropriately and is taking legal advice.”

Walliams was contacted for comment.
