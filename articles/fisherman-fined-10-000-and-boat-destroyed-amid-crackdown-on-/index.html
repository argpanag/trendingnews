Fisherman fined $10,000 and boat destroyed amid crackdown on illegal trend in Aussie waters

TheTools · 22.09.2026 09:17 · source · permalink

Authorities across the country are clamping down on illegal activity in Aussie waters, with tough penalties being handed out to those involved.

As one Aussie state continues cracking down on the illegal seafood trade, fishers are being warned they could lose their boats and face hefty fines.

A Queensland man was fined $10,000 after Queensland Boating and Fisheries Patrol (QBFP) officers uncovered an illegal seafood operation involving the sale of mud crabs, king threadfin salmon and mulloway.

The marine animals were taken from Queensland waters without the required commercial authority, QBFP said.

The man was targeted and surveilled by officers who suspected wrongdoing as part of their ongoing crackdown on the black market seafood trade.

Australia's illicit seafood trade is estimated to be worth tens of millions of dollars annually.

The man was found to have been selling seafood to community members at prices well below those charged by legitimate retailers.

Video footage shows the man rummaging through crab pots on his boat, which he used to capture and contain the mud crabs.

Officers then executed a search warrant and seized the vessel, trailer, outboard motor, and marine electronics that had been used in the black-market activity.

His boat was subsequently seized and destroyed.

Fishing authorities dish out strong warning to Aussies

Authorities issued a strong warning to anyone planning to profit illegally from Queensland's fisheries resources.

"If you profit from illegal fishing, you could lose your boat," QBFP warned.

"Illegal operators undermine legitimate commercial fishers, seafood businesses and the sustainability of Queensland's fisheries."

The Russell Island man was one of three fishermen whose boats were seized and permanently destroyed in separate incidents across southeast Queensland.

All three forfeited vessels are now scheduled for crushing, ensuring they can never again be used to commit fisheries offences.

Deputy Director-General of Fisheries and Forestry, Pauline Jacob, said these penalties were intended to send a very clear message about using boats to break the law.

During a search, officers found undersized mud crab in the Russell Island man's possession.

"These offenders weren't simply catching a few extra fish. They were profiting from Queensland's fisheries resources through ongoing illegal seafood sales that undermine honest commercial fishers and legitimate seafood businesses," Jacob said.

"Licensed commercial fishers invest heavily to meet strict licensing, sustainability and food safety requirements. Black-market operators avoid those costs while undercutting businesses that do the right thing."

Specialist Services Group Superintendent David French said the Queensland Police Service (QPS) has "zero tolerance for illegal activity on the water"

Australia's tough stance on illegal fishing activity

The crackdown was part of a broader battle against illegal fishing across Queensland's waterways, where abandoned crab pots, often known as "ghost pots", also threaten marine life.

Previously, NSW announced a crackdown on illegal fishers and seafood pirates, introducing tough new laws to stop illegal fishers from poaching high-value species such as abalone, lobster, and mud crab.
