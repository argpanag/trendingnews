Fever Star Sophie Cunningham On WNBA Commissioner Dreams

TheTools · 18.11.2026 16:25 · source · permalink

Maxim’s November/December cover star is calling the shots.

It’s been quite a year for Sophie Cunningham.

Sure, the Indiana Fever’s season ended with a tough playoff loss to the Las Vegas Aces. But for the striking 6-foot-1 guard, that was perhaps the only thing that didn’t go her way. Consider this highlight reel: Cunningham went from WNBA standout to sensation, got a player-exclusive Crazy Energy Adidas shoe that sold out instantly, watched her social media following explode, made her Sports Illustrated Swimsuit debut, and became a viral star—largely thanks to a now-infamous stare-down and finger-point incident.

But despite the newfound fame, which now requires security, the blonde beauty insists not much has changed. Beneath the swagger and sass that fans see on the court, Cunningham’s still the Missouri farm girl who grew up riding horses and four-wheelers. “I’m still just me,” she says. “I’m just a very goofy, down-to-earth, go-with-the-flow, chill type of human.”

We caught up with her in Arizona, where Cunningham discussed her viral “pointing” video, weighed in on the “enforcer” misperceptions, revealed a side people seldom get to see, and even floated the idea of running the entire WNBA someday.

You’re obviously incredibly competitive. How do you feel about the way your season ended?

Being a pro, you’re playing every day sometimes, every other day, every two days. You don’t really have a lot of time to sit with the losses or really sit with the wins. You’ve gotta learn how to move on. It’s an art—you have to flush it and stack your next day.

Normally that’s a pretty easy thing, but when it’s the final game of the year, it can be sad. You feel a lot of feelings. And the competitor in me, you wanna win. You know you can win. You know your team had the potential to make it all the way. Although our team had great chemistry, we struggled with being consistent throughout the year. In the end, that inconsistency played a role in ending our season.

My body still thinks it’s playing. I’m still eating like a linebacker right now, so I’m just like, “Geez, calm down.” I haven’t worked out since. It’s kind of nice just to get away from everything, all the noise, and be able to step back, relax, and think about how grateful and how awesome this season was.

Let’s talk about your origin story. You spent a lot of time on your grandparents’ farm. What’s the most farm-girl thing about you?

My sister and I were just raised to have fun and to be where your two feet are at. We would get on four-wheelers, go play in the creeks, go play in the mud, go mess around with the cows. We learned to love the outdoors, truly, and to get away from stuff. When we were playing sports, we knew to give 110 percent. But when practice was done, we were taught to go enjoy the pool, go enjoy the farm, go enjoy your friends. Our love for the farm and outdoors runs deep in our family.

Was there a moment when you realized basketball was your sport?

I actually loved soccer, and I was really good at soccer. I also really loved volleyball. I was trying to dual-sport in college, but logistically we couldn’t make it work. Humbly, I say this: Whatever sport I decided to pursue, I knew I was going to attack, commit, and perform at a high level. Regardless of the sport, my goal was to always become a professional athlete, and that’s what I set my mind to from a young age. You know how some people are really good at art, music, or school, and they just have that natural sense? Being an elite athlete is what came natural to me from a very young age. Whatever I put my mind to, I knew I wouldn’t let myself not be successful. I obviously ended up choosing basketball, and I think I made the right choice!

How much do you hate losing?

I hate losing more than I love winning. It’s the worst. In basketball, I hate to lose in any drill. I think that’s what makes us so good. If you enjoy losing, then you’re probably not gonna be very successful, and you’re definitely not gonna be a professional athlete. Those just don’t coexist at the same time. But I will say I have learned how to channel my competitiveness, because being so competitive and wanting to always win is not always a good thing. The older I get, I’ve learned to enjoy the process. But now that I’m actually thinking about what I’m saying, even when it comes to cards and stuff, I hate to lose. So no, disregard all those comments. I like to win.

You also played football in high school. How did that happen?

I actually played two football games. One of my best friends was the kicker for our team, and he blew out his knee. The coach came up to me and was like, “Hey, do you wanna be the kicker for the football team?” And I was like, “Shit. I’ve never done this before, so why not?” I had to make sure my parents, my high school coach, and Mizzou were on board. But at the end of the day, I was gonna do it regardless, ’cause what a cool experience.

I ended up playing two games and actually missed my first one because I had a hole in my block, so I got tackled. But the guys had my back. I still have the scar. Every time I tell that story, I look down and look at the scar.

You have this reputation as the “enforcer” on the court. Do you embrace that?

Nope. I don’t really know what I’m enforcing. I get it, and maybe I feel that a little bit because of how I was raised; we were always taught to protect the ones you love. You go to war for the people to the left and right of you. So I do have a sense of protecting myself and my teammates. But to call me the enforcer, I know people love it, but I just don’t view myself as that. It’s become a fun storyline on the internet.

All my family and friends, they get why people are saying that because they know how passionate and protective I am about my people. But they also know I’m the biggest goofball, just trying to laugh, smile, and truly enjoy the game of basketball. I’m unapologetically me. I really am a very chill, down-to-earth person. I love people. I want people to feel confident and feel successful, and whatever I can do to help them feel that way, I’m gonna do.

Of course, when I’m on the court, I’m gonna do whatever it takes for my team to win. I’m the type of person who can flip the switch and also flip it off. I think a lot of people aren’t used to people who can do that. A lot of people who don’t know me think that I’m like this 24/7, but really, I’m just a very goofy, down-to-earth, go-with-the-flow, chill type of human.

You’re 6-foot-1, which clearly helps on the court. Are there any downsides to height that people might not expect?

We have very tall, strong women in our family. My aunt is 6-foot-2, my mom’s 5-foot-10, my grandma was 5-foot-10. And they all played sports. My mom and aunt had a really negative experience. They got a lot of heat for it. But because of that, they made sure that we felt loved and confident, and that we knew being tall, broad, and athletic was gonna be our biggest superpower one day. And it totally was.

Are people intimidated by your height?

I get told that all the time, that I’m intimidating to walk up to or intimidating to be around. I think once people meet me they’re like, “Oh, wait, you, you’re normal.” And I’m like, “Duh.”

People see the pointing video and this very intense competitor on the court, and they probably think that’s who you are all the time.

I’m just sassy sometimes, and that’s totally okay. I know how to get in people’s heads. Not saying that’s a good thing or a bad thing, but sometimes it is to my benefit. Once I get in someone’s head, my sassiness and my competitiveness come out. Sometimes it comes out in pointing. Sometimes it comes out in knocking someone’s head off.

[As for the pointing incident], I was just messing around. She said something, and I went to the ref, and her comment was, “Don’t point at me.” I was like, “Oh, shouldn’t have said that.” And that’s when I just held it. She was just going ballistic, and I was like, “Oh, this is fun.”

What do you look for in a partner?

I have a very high standard for my future husband. He’ll be someone who is Christ-centered and can lead me in my walk with Jesus. Someone who’s friendly, confident, and secure. Someone who is taller than me and athletic and just a good human with similar morals and values. With what I do for a living, I have to have someone who’s confident, secure, and able to kind of shoot the shit a little bit because that’s who I am. I don’t live a typical lifestyle. I hope one day to kind of have more of a traditional lifestyle of being in one spot, having a husband and kids and whatnot. But I prayed for this life, and I’m still having fun with it. But when it comes to a guy, I want someone who could push me in my faith, someone who is loyal, and someone who also loves every little piece of me.

And it’s important that he’s taller than you?

Yeah, I think so. I’m already so tall. The last thing I wanna do is bend down to kiss my husband goodnight. No, thanks.

This has been a huge year for you. Has your life changed as you’ve become more recognizable?

Oh my gosh, yeah. I have security now. I can’t really go anywhere without being approached. And I’m all for that. I want to be someone that people feel like they can come have a conversation with. But I do think there are boundaries, and I’m starting to say no to things and certain people. We’ve had to take my privacy and protection much more seriously. Truly. As your platform and fame grow, no one really ever thinks about the negative side of things. So I just have to be more careful. Sometimes when I do say no, I do feel bad, but at the same time, I can’t feel bad because I have to make sure I’m safe, too.

But with that, I can’t go anywhere anymore without people saying, “Thank you for sticking up for women. Thank you for sticking up for girls.”

What do you think it is about you that people have connected with so strongly?

Sometimes people in this position feel untouchable. They have egos, and they move a certain way where people don’t feel like they can relate to them. And truly, I can understand why people do that. It gets exhausting. Sometimes you just wanna be left alone. But I think maybe people cling onto me because, at the end of the day, I’m just goofy, I’m fun-loving, I relate to people, I can have a conversation, and I want people to feel good.

I don’t know why I’m a professional athlete. I don’t know why I got the platform that I did, but I’m so blessed, and I’m so happy. But I’m still just me—I’m just a girl from the country.

With pet donkeys.

We have Kevin, who is a mini donkey. We have Mark, who is a mammoth donkey. Daniel is a mule, and then we have Molly. They’re mainly yard ornaments. We just kinda let them have a good life, but when we wanna ride them, they’re rideable. We just love animals. We grew up with 25 horses. Our grandparents had cattle, always dogs and cats.

It’s also just fun to be able to live a city life—and a crazy life—but also go back and enjoy the farm. They’re a source of calm, fun, and unconditional love to me.

Where do you see yourself in 10 years?

I don’t know. I am doing things now to get my feet wet in different industries or things that I might be interested in. But to the core, I love to talk. I believe my life experiences have allowed me to have a unique perspective when it comes to leadership. In that role, you’re going to have people who support and love what you’re doing. But you’ll also have people on the contrary. Regardless, I’m built for it. So I do think about doing commentary. Sometimes I wanna get away, have my own podcast, and just work for myself. That sounds great. I think coaching sounds cool.

And maybe I shouldn’t be saying this, but why not? I do think being the WNBA commissioner would be awesome. Sometimes you just need someone to lead with love and experience, who has played in the league, and understands what the players need—but also understands the business side of things. And I think that I am developing that type of mindset. We’ll see what happens. I think it’d be fun. It’s not out of the question.
