Issued at 404 PM HST Sat Oct 31 2026 Trade wind showers anchored along the eastern slopes of Kauais mountains have persistently produced heavy rain through the day in a moist and unstable environment, as evidenced in this afternoons upper air sounding at Lihue. Rainfall rates and gage stream reports prompted the issuance of a Flash Flood Warning for the entire island, with the main trouble spots including the Hanalei Bridge along the Kuhio Highway and the South Fork of the Wailua River. This warning remains in effect through 5:45 PM this evening and will likely be extended. Heavy showers and isolated thunderstorms firing along a surface trough edging over Niihau and Kauai this afternoon will likely maintain the flood threat for the next couple of hours. Showers have also been persistent across Oahu through the day, but rainfall rates and stream gage responses have been unimpressive so far. However, the heavy showers and isolated thunderstorms currently over Kauai may impact Oahu overnight into Sunday morning as an upper level trough helps drive them eastward in advance of an approaching front, depending on how well the surface trough and the various outflow boundaries hold together.
The next pattern change arrives late Sunday into Sunday night as the tail end of a shallow front pushes down the island chain before dissipating. While this boundary will deliver an uptick in showers to windward and north-facing slopes, its primary impact will be the development of breezy and gusty northeast trade winds spreading from west to east. Deep high pressure building behind the front will usher in a drier, more stable air mass. This will lead to a break from the muggy conditions weve been experiencing this past week as dew points are expected to drop into the lower 60s on Monday and Tuesday.
Breezy and stable easterly trades will persist through much of the workweek as the northern high becomes elongated in response to broad low pressure system tracking across the North Pacific. Dew points will gradually rebound closer to seasonal norms by midweek. While rainfall will remain largely focused along windward slopes with only brief leeward drift, a minor uptick in shower coverage appears possible around midweek as trade moisture interacts with added instability from a passing weak mid-level shortwave trough.
Heading into next weekend, forecast attention shifts back to the tropics. While significant spread exists regarding timing and track details, model guidance continues to hint at potential tropical cyclone development far southeast of the state late in the week, with the system potentially nearing or passing south of the islands over the weekend. While background trades look to hold, questions remain concerning their ultimate strength and how much deep moisture is drawn into the region. The National Hurricane Center continues to monitor this area, maintaining a medium (40 percent) chance of tropical cyclone formation over the next 7 days.

Scattered showers currently between Kauai and Oahu will start to push towards PHNL and PHJR (and the rest of Oahu) over the next couple of hours. Persistent MVFR and some IFR ceilings and visibilities are possible in the heavier showers. If these hold together, they could affect Molokai, Lanai, and Maui by morning.
AIRMET Sierra remains in effect for IFR conditions on Kauai which may need to be extended into Oahu. AIRMET Sierra is also in effect for mountain obscuration on Oahu and windward areas of the Big Island due to low ceilings and reduced visibility in showers.
