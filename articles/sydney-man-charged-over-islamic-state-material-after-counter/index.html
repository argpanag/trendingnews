Sydney man charged over Islamic State material after counter-terror raids

A Sydney man has been charged with terrorism offences after police allegedly found Islamic State-inspired extremism material in his possession during sweeping raids across Sydney’s south-west.

Officers from the NSW Joint Counter Terrorism Team, comprising NSW Police, the Australian Federal Police, the NSW Crime Commission and the nation’s spy agency, ASIO, arrested three men after swooping on several properties in the dawn operation on Friday.

Friday’s raids, carried out at least half-a-dozen properties, come amid a heightened awareness of potential threats ahead of the first anniversary of the Bondi Beach terror attack next month.

Detectives charged a 35-year-old man with knowingly collecting or making a document connected with terrorism and using a carriage service to possess violent extremist material.

He was refused bail to appear in Campbelltown Local Court, where police will allege that the material was related to religiously motivated violent extremism and the Islamic State. There is no ongoing threat to public safety, police said.

A second man, aged 34, was arrested at the same property and is expected to be charged with hindering a police investigation. Footage of a raid on the Campbelltown property showed heavily armed police escorting a man from the home.

A third man, aged 33, was arrested at Wolli Creek, in Sydney’s south, and is expected to be charged with firearm offences.
