Benfica vs Celtic: Uefa Europa League stats & head-to-head

Celtic opened their Europa League campaign with a disappointing home loss to Ferencvaros

Manager Martin O'Neill wants Celtic's historic European Cup lift in Lisbon to inspire his players as they seek a first Europa League win of the season away to Benfica.

O'Neill and his squad are in the Portuguese capital, which played host to their 1967 victory over Inter Milan, as they became the first British club to win the competition.

"First of all, looking forward to the game, it's a big match for us and lovely to be involved," O'Neill said.

"Secondly, the history of the football club and the connection to Lisbon, it's terrific, something the football club should be really proud of.

"I think it's a separate entity in many aspects.

"By next year, it'll be 60 years, a historical occasion, absolutely fantastic. A lot of the boys have seen the stadium from a trip there last season and are aware of the history.

"It's really lovely, I must admit. It's something to look forward to and just the whole history of it, it should have a galvanizing effect on us."

Key players Cameron Carter-Vickers and Camilo Duran did not train on Wednesday morning, with the former ruled out and the latter potentially available after his partner gave birth on Wednesday.

"Cameron has a bit of a strain, so he's missing, but Camilo is hoping to be out here later on," added O'Neill.

Benfica got their Europa League campaign off to a flying start with victory over AC Milan at the San Siro, while Celtic slumped to a home loss to Ferencvaros.

Making mark in Europe important for Celtic

Some observers feel that Rangers' lack of European football will throw them an advantage in the battle for the Scottish title with last season's runners-up, Heart of Midlothian, now engaged in Conference League football as well.

O'Neill insists Celtic are taking the Europa League seriously and are targeting a place in the knock-outs in the early spring.

"The Premiership is very, very important obviously, but while we're here, our ambition is to try and qualify," he said.

"The players are looking forward to the game and to contesting European football.

"Last season, sometime in March, we had four games in about 10 days or something, really hectic, and it really took its toll. But I think we are equipped for it. We're ready to go.

"I thought we played some really fantastic football on Sunday [2-0 win at Motherwell], but this is a different team, different stadium, different everything.

"They've got a lot of good players and have gained a great deal of confidence by winning in Milan, that's not easy.

"They have good players, but so do we and we want to cause them a few problems."

Among those Celtic will have to watch out for are Greece striker Vangelis Pavlidis, who has 14 goals for the season already, and Argentina winger Gianluca Prestianni, who is on nine.

Benfica, who won the European Cup twice in the 1960s, sit second in the Portuguese top flight, with their only defeat coming away to leaders Porto.

Head coach Marco Silva had earlier suggested he may use some "B team" players but the hosts have a free weekend while Celtic are at home to Hearts on Sunday.

"It will be very important to pick up points," the former Fulham boss said. "After winning a tough away game, we need to keep the momentum going.

"Celtic are a big club and a strong name in Europe, with a rich tradition and capable of making things difficult for their opponents and fighting until the final whistle.

"They want to bounce back from a poor start and deal with pressure frequently, so it will be a tough challenge."

Silva says Ukraine's Anatoliy Trubin will definitely start in goal instead of Samuel Soares, who played on Sunday.

Trubin's compatriot, record signing Heorhiy Sudakov, could start after his first goal of the season as a substitute on Sunday after Manchester City's on-loan Claudio Echeverri started in the number 10 role.

Silva said he had a difficult choice to make at centre-back, with Alessandro Circati having taken time to adjust to a different system since arriving from Parma challenging Tomas Araujo and Clement Lenglet, who is on loan from Atletico Madrid, for the two places.

Match stats

  • Celtic have lost their past four away matches in Portugal in all European competitions, with this their first visit since losing 2-1 to Benfica in November 2012.

  • Benfica have a 100% record at home against Celtic in Europe, winning all four games by an aggregate score of 9-1.

  • Celtic have kept just two clean sheets in their past 29 Europa League matches.

  • Benfica are unbeaten in their past 11 Europa League group stage matches (W8 D3), scoring at least two goals each time since a 1-0 defeat to AEK Athens in October 2009.

  • Celtic reached the knockout phase play-off in the Europa League last season, losing to Stuttgart, after finishing 21st.

  • Benfica were 24th in the Champions League league phase last time, failing to reach the last 16 after a 3-1 aggregate loss to Real Madrid.
