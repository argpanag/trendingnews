Hype around Ottawa Senators' top prospect Jonas Lagerberg Hoen intensifying

TheTools · 12.09.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

The 18-year-old winger is still working back into form after a serious knee injury last season. Ottawa drafted him 25th overall in June.

Amongst all the red, white and black ripping up and down the ice at Canadian Tire Centre on Friday morning, Jonas Lagerberg Hoen’s yellow jersey served as a spotlight for those getting their first in-person glimpse of the Ottawa Senators‘ top forward prospect.

The 18-year-old, who had ACL surgery in December, will be held out of weekend Prospect Challenge games against the Toronto Maple Leafs (Saturday, 6 p.m.) and Montreal Canadiens (Sunday, 6 p.m.) at Slush Puppie Centre in Gatineau. But the fact that he was nearly a full participant on Day 1 of rookie camp was extremely encouraging.

“You want to compete with the best guys that are here, so it’s a bit boring,” Lagerberg Hoen said. “You see the guys competing and you want to join, but you’ve got to be patient. I know I’m going to get more chances.”

For the first hour of practice, Lagerberg Hoen confidently pushed the pace with the main group through flow drills, easily looking like one of the most offensively gifted players on the ice. He stayed on after the session, showing off his explosive speed with quick stops and starts, perhaps further testing his knee.

He’s come a long way in a short period of time.

After scoring 27 goals in 38 games for Leksands IF Jr. in 2024-25, Lagerberg Hoen was off to a brilliant start in his NHL draft season. He had 16 points (nine goals, seven assists) in nine games and had even got called up to play in two games with Leksands’ pro squad in the Swedish Hockey League.

Then disaster struck.

“I was going for a puck in the corner. We were skating fast and I fell before,” he recounted. “And, on my way up, the guy hit me from behind. He took my leg into the boards.

“It hurt very much, so I thought it was bad. But, like, hours after, it felt pretty good and some doctors said that there was no problem. But then we found out later that it was bad.”

Lagerberg Hoen has been skating all summer, but there were many months prior that he was trapped in a gym. He learned a lot about himself.

“You’ve got a lot of new emotions,” he admitted, “but you learn to control them and find other stuff to make you happy.”

The injury undoubtedly impacted his overall National Hockey League draft ranking, but not so much with Ottawa. The Senators had him at No. 16 on their big board, and he fell into their lap at No. 25.

General manager Steve Staios believes the winger has “home-run potential.” Chief scout Don Boyd has claimed Lagerberg Hoen owns “one of the better releases in Europe for his age group.”

European amateur scout Anders Östberg watched the young Swede for years before Ottawa made the pick, raving about his skating and offensive abilities the night of the draft.

“He’s not the typical Swede, so to speak, you know?” Östberg chuckled, giddy about the player’s potential. “He’s more like a North American goal scorer. Like, he’s not going to make too many high-end plays in traffic and speed. It’s more like he’s going to work hard, he’s going to win pucks, he’s going to go to the right areas and score a lot of goals.”

Below the mingling media members and team brass in the stands Friday, veteran Senators Tim Stutzle, Drake Batherson, Fabian Zetterlund and Linus Ullmark poked their heads out of a tunnel to check on some of their future teammates. Lagerberg Hoen had gotten the opportunity to skate with the NHLers ahead of camp, leaving with a better understanding of what it would take to one day make the big club.

“I’ve got to be better at everything, of course,” said the teenager, still with a mouthful of braces. “These guys are so good, you know, faster, stronger. But for me it’s going to be everything on the ice, getting bigger, being better defensively so the coach can trust me everywhere.

“It’s (about) getting stronger on pucks. That’s a big thing for me. I’ve always been fast, always been able to use my speed, but here it’s more physical, so I’ve got to work on that.”

Lagerberg Hoen, turning 19 in October, will make the transition to the pro level with Leksands this season (though, he’ll play in Sweden’s second-tier league, HockeyAllsvenskan, after the franchise was relegated in 2025-26). There’s no concrete timeline for his return to play, but the finish line is on the horizon.

“It’s been tough,” he said. “It’s a very long rehab, but everything has been going according plan, and it’s been going good … It’s close now, so next week I’m going to start to practise fully. It’s very close now.”

Providing extra motivation for Lagerberg Hoen is a roster spot up for grabs with Sweden for the world juniors in Edmonton and Red Deer, Alta., this winter. It was something he painfully missed out on last year.

“I’m going to have a short time to prove myself,” he said. “There’s a tournament coming up in November. I hope I can get in that one and prove myself that way.”
