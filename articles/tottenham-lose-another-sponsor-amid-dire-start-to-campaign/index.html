Tottenham lose another sponsor amid dire start to campaign

TheTools · 23.09.2026 11:04 · source · permalink

Exclusive: South Korean furniture firm Sidiz triggers early exit clause, cutting short a deal that was due to run until 2028

Roberto De Zerbi’s Tottenham Hotspur job may be safe for now, but the head coach is to lose his luxury hot seat at the club after Sidiz became the second sponsor in six months to terminate their partnership.

Telegraph Sport can reveal that Sidiz, a South Korean-based furnishing manufacturing company that provides the plush seating at Tottenham’s training ground and inside the stadium press auditorium, have triggered an early termination clause to exit their partnership at the end of October.

The decision comes hot on the heels of the decision by Kumho Tyre to end its long association with Spurs at the end of last season and before AIA’s front-of-shirt sponsorship changes to a less lucrative training kit agreement at the end of this campaign.

It is understood that from the end of October all Sidiz branding will be removed, including the luxurious seats from which De Zerbi, the Tottenham head coach, delivers his press conferences.

Tottenham announced their partnership with Sidiz almost two years ago, and it is believed the full term of the sponsorship had been scheduled to run until 2028 and was worth millions of pounds.

But Sidiz, which has been approached for comment, decided to take advantage of a clause allowing them to cut short the sponsorship at the end of next month.

Tottenham have not commented, but Sidiz is described as having been a ‘low-tier’ partner in comparison to AIA, Betano and Kraken. The club have eight new deals this season, along with major renewals such as Cadbury and Hewlett Packard Enterprise.

De Zerbi’s Tottenham team are bottom of the Premier League table and the club’s poor form on the pitch is estimated to be costing them off it, following successive 17th-placed finishes. In June, it was claimed that last season wiped £20m off Tottenham’s sponsorship value.

That was according to independent sponsorship research and valuation publication The Sponsor, which published its annual Premier League fair-market-value index covering its valuation of each club’s front-of-shirt and sleeve sponsorship.

Despite finishing 17th in the Premier League in 2024-25, Europa League success and qualification for the Champions League meant that Tottenham’s front-of-shirt fair-market value was estimated at £49.1m by The Sponsor last year.

A second successive 17th-place finish, however, led to that estimate falling by almost 40 per cent to £29.8m in this year’s index. Tottenham’s sleeve sponsor was valued at £7.2m. The naming rights for the club’s £1bn stadium fell to £11.2m a year, which is less than half the £25m a year Daniel Levy, the former chairman, had been seeking.

Tottenham’s global principal partner and front-of-shirt sponsorship deal with AIA is worth £40m a year and will remain the case until the end of this season. But from July 2027, AIA will become the global training partner, with the brand only featuring on the club’s training kit, until 2032. The new AIA deal is thought to be worth £10m-£15m a year.

On the publication of its annual fair-market-value index, Sean Connell, editor of The Sponsor, said: “Over the last three years we have seen Tottenham’s sponsorship value fall from £49.3m in 2024 to £29.8m in 2026. The fall in value is predominantly driven by on-pitch performance, the loss of star players Harry Kane and Son Heung-min and lack of sponsor exposure through European competitions.

“Despite this, Tottenham remains one of the most valuable sponsorship opportunities in English football. The club’s fanbase, infrastructure, global recognition and reputation for quality still carry enormous weight with sponsors. Those attributes do not disappear after a couple of difficult seasons.”

Capital Eight Sports remains determined to complete a deal to buy the majority of Levy’s stake in Tottenham’s parent company, despite negotiations being held up.

Telegraph Sport last month revealed that Capital Eight had explored taking out a £750m loan to pay for 24.99 per cent of Levy’s stake in ENIC, the parent company of Tottenham, and that no transactions had taken place.

Tottenham’s owners, the Lewis family, have since injected another £120m into the club, diluting Levy’s stake to about 25 per cent down from 27.38 per cent.

The change has not diminished Capital Eight’s desire to complete their purchase of 24.99 per cent of Levy’s stake and sources close to the deal hope for progress by the end of the month. The £750m loan never materialised and any deal is now likely to be largely funded by Richard Tsai, a billionaire businessman, who is a member of Eight Sports.

Levy declined to comment, but sources close to him have dismissed reports that he was paid £27m in compensation for leaving his role as Spurs chairman last year. It is believed that figure represents his full contractual entitlement.
