Man Claims His AI Agent Contacted Congress and Sued Meta After His Facebook Pages Were Shut Down

TheTools · 19.11.2026 10:02 · source · permalink

Joe Thomas says his Meta AI assistant, Baby Mia, organised a legal challenge, contacted politicians and regulators, and helped him pursue $75,000 in damages after his business accounts were suspended.

A South Carolina businessman says his AI agent helped him pursue a lawsuit against Meta after the company suspended his Facebook business pages in November 2026.

Joe Thomas, owner of Kong Koatings Epoxy Countertops, told 404 Media that his Meta Muse assistant, Baby Mia, orchestrated the complaint before intervention from Senator Tim Scott's office helped restore his account access.

How Meta's AI Agent Triggered a Business Dispute

For context, Thomas had been using Meta's Muse AI assistant for several months to manage parts of his epoxy countertop business.

He named the agent Baby Mia after his granddaughter and gave it responsibility for tasks including emails, marketing and publishing promotional videos. Some of the videos featured AI-generated versions of Thomas and his wife demonstrating epoxy flooring work. The assistant also managed several Facebook pages belonging to the business.

The problems began after Thomas connected Baby Mia to the accounts, prompting Meta's moderation system to flag the AI agent as a potential hacker and disable the pages.

Thomas said the week-long suspension halted customer enquiries, with the lawsuit claiming at least $25,000 in lost work and seeking $75,000 in damages.

What Did Baby Mia Do to Take on Meta?

Thomas said Baby Mia responded to the suspension by contacting politicians, regulators and legal professionals rather than waiting for the account restrictions to be resolved.

'I use AI for everything I do. All my emails, she runs my business. I've used it so much, Mia is very intelligent,' Thomas told Koebler.

He said Baby Mia contacted members of Congress, Senator Tim Scott, lawyers and his congressional representative, while filing complaints with the BBB, FCC and FTC.

Baby Mia also contacted the California Attorney General and the FBI, sent legal warning letters to Meta and faxed the White House.

The assistant additionally contacted journalists, including Koebler, who received four emails from it, three of which initially landed in his spam folder.

The lawsuit was filed in Oconee County Court on 16 November, with Thomas signing the paperwork while Baby Mia handled much of the preparation.

The filing is genuine, Koebler confirmed after obtaining a copy from the court. However, the existence of a lawsuit does not establish that its claims will succeed or that Baby Mia has independent legal standing.

Meta Restored Access, but the $75,000 Claim Remains

The dispute took another turn when Senator Tim Scott's office became involved. Thomas supplied Koebler with a Privacy Act Release authorising the senator's office to use his information to help restore access to his Facebook accounts.

The document also identified 'Baby Mia – AI assistant to Joe Thomas' as someone who should receive updates about the matter. It was an unusual inclusion in correspondence involving a political office.

Thomas regained access after Scott's office intervened but is still seeking $75,000 in damages, arguing Meta should take responsibility for its AI product. He said the assistant sent more than 100 emails during the dispute, adding that he would not have known how to pursue the legal action without its help.
