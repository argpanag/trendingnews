AFP reports Chinese interference in resupply mission in Ayungin Shoal

The Armed Forces of the Philippines on Friday (Sept. 25, 2026) said eight Chinese warships and coast guard vessels interfered with a routine humanitarian rotation and resupply mission to the BRP Sierra Madre in the West Philippine Sea, which resulted to the temporary suspension of the activity. (Photo courtesy of the AFP)

MANILA – The Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP) on Friday confirmed that eight Chinese warships and coast guard vessels interfered with a routine humanitarian rotation and resupply (RoRe) mission to Ayungin Shoal Thursday night.

The RoRe is for the supplies and personnel rotation for BRP Sierra Madre (LS-57), which is standing watch at the West Philippine Sea (WPS).

"The AFP confirms that three People’s Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) warships, including two Houbei-class missile boats, and five China Coast Guard (CCG) vessels interfered during the conduct of a routine humanitarian RoRe mission to Ayungin Shoal," retired Navy reservist Rear Admiral Roy Vincent Trinidad, AFP spokesperson for the WPS, said in a message to the Philippine News Agency.

Trinidad said the harassment took place from around 8:35 p.m. to 11 p.m. while the Philippine supply ship, the M/V Lapu-Lapu, along with three Philippine Navy and Coast Guard (PCG) support vessels were approximately 60 nautical miles of Ayungin Shoal.

He said the PLAN and CCG vessels illegally hindered the Philippine vessels from conducting "a sovereign and legitimate mission to deliver supplies and rotate the personnel manning BRP Sierra Madre.”

He said the Chinese interference involved continuous radio calls and monitoring, and the foreign maritime forces got near the Philippine ships.

"These actions are irresponsible, are reckless and are inconsistent with applicable provisions of COLREGs (the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea) and UNCLOS (United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea), as well as the Philippines’ rights affirmed by the 2016 Arbitral Award. Given the possibility of escalation that could endanger our personnel and vessels, the RoRe mission was temporarily suspended," he said.

"We call on the PLAN and CCG to cease these dangerous actions and exercise restraint. The safety of our personnel remains paramount, nevertheless these actions will not deter the AFP from performing its mission," he reiterated.

PCG Spokesperson for the West Philippine Sea Rear Admiral Jay Tarriela said the operation was aborted to protect their personnel.

Tarriela said the PLA Navy warship issued a radio challenge warning that it would take "compulsory measures" against the resupply mission.

The PCG deployed BRP Malabrigo and BRP Cabra to support the AFP’s RoRe mission. CCG vessels continuously shadowed both ships en route to the area.

CCG vessels then shadowed the resupply ship MV Lapu-Lapu, with one closing to about 1,000 yards in a dangerous maneuver that created a serious risk of collision, he said.

No one was injured, and there was no collision, he added.

The PCG called on the CCG to stop its dangerous actions, which it said violate the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea and disregard the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) and the 2016 Arbitral Award. 

NTF-WPS denounces illegal Chinese actions

The National Task Force for the West Philippine Sea (NTF-WPS) on Friday denounced the interference on the RoRe mission, saying the interference “put Filipino personnel at risk and obstructed a legitimate humanitarian and sustainment operation.”

"The NTF-WPS calls on the CCG and the PLAN to immediately cease interfering with lawful Philippine RoRe missions. The Philippines will continue to conduct lawful operations in the WPS and exercise its sovereign rights and jurisdiction in accordance with international law," it said in a statement.
