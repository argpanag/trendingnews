Metro Atlanta rapper who abused puppy during livestream pleads guilty to animal cruelty charge

TheTools · 22.10.2026 19:24 · source · permalink

DEKALB COUNTY, Ga. (Decaturish) — Aspen Easterling, known to her fans as up-and-coming rapper Aspen Kartier, on Oct. 21 pleaded guilty to one misdemeanor count of cruelty to animals.

Easterling, a 19-year-old, was livestreaming from her apartment in Brookhaven, Georgia, on Jan. 29 when she repeatedly struck a three-month-old puppy named Louie and later lifted the puppy up by the skin of its back.

DeKalb County Judge Alvin Wong presided over the case with prosecutor Samantha Wicker representing the state of Georgia.

The video recording of the incident played on the courtroom projector. Easterling became angry after Louie peed on the floor off-screen. The dog is heard whimpering and yelping throughout the three-minute clip as Easterling yells to viewers about the dog’s behavior after the attack.

“Frankly, I believe the video truly speaks for itself,” Wicker said. “I mean, she admits to hitting the dog. She says that the dog acts like it can’t breathe when she does hit it.”

The Brookhaven Police Department received a Compassionate Police Department Award from PETA to honor the first responders who rescued the dog.

Easterling stood alongside her attorney Pat Dixon as they discussed a plea deal for the misdemeanor. Initially charged with two counts of animal cruelty, one count was dropped due to the guilty plea.

Easterling’s boyfriend Cristian Denis, also known as Lil Crix, owns Louie and was also in the home during the incident. Denis said he would relinquish custody of the dog to DeKalb’s Lifeline Animal Project. Easterling will pay $3,348 to Lifeline for care and custody. According to examinations, Louie appeared in good health following Easterling’s initial arrest.

Dixon stated that Easterling was raised in “undesirable conditions” in Chicago and started rapping as a way out. After her manager in Los Angeles discovered her, he arranged for her to leave the poor living situation and move to Brookhaven.

After the incident, Easterling moved to Gwinnett County because her former address was released online.

In 2025, Easterling signed to Signal Records, a joint venture with former Capital Records CEO and Chairman Jeff Vaughn and Columbia Records, a division of Sony Music Entertainment.

Following the incident, Signal Records “shelved her,” meaning it postponed or delayed any planned release or project. Easterling also lost all sponsorships, had all shows canceled, and has been “cyber bullied and harassed on the internet,” according to Dixon.

“These 10 minutes don’t define who she is,” Dixon said.

Judge Wong officially sentenced Easterling to 10 days in jail, including two days already served. She was also given one year of probation, must attend 24 anger management classes, and serve 100 hours of community service.

“People do stupid things without thinking about what they’re doing. You wouldn’t want to be treated like that and you wouldn’t want to be punished like that, physically, by anybody,” Judge Wong said.

In addition, Easterling must participate in an animal cruelty education and prevention program and is not allowed to own, possess, or be in the presence of a canine during probation.

“I think some sort of punishment is appropriate so that you understand what it feels like to be on the other end of that,” Judge Wong said. “I was going to sentence you to 30 days in jail. I’m not going to do that, but I am going to sentence you to 10 days and give you credit for the two that you have served.”

“This is a very, very symbolic punishment,” Judge Wong added. “It won’t kill you, but it’s not pleasant.”

Easterling said in court she hopes to keep pursuing music as her career and is still signed with Signal Records. Her name also remains listed as a signed artist on the record label’s website.

A spokesperson from Advocates for DeKalb Animals attended the trial and was pleased that the dog is on its way to a “safe and loving home.” However, the group urged investigators to investigate Easterling’s boyfriend, the dog’s owner, after the verdict.

Here is Advocates for DeKalb Animals’ official statement:

“This case started with a three-month-old puppy who could not speak for himself. All he could do was cry out in pain while the abuse was broadcast for the world to see. Today, he finally has a path to a safe and loving home, and that matters deeply to us.

Advocates for DeKalb Animals hopes today sets a clear standard in DeKalb County: when animals are abused, there should be real accountability, meaningful protections for the animal, and responsibility for the cost of their care.

And while Ms. Easterling’s case has been resolved and this dog is now safe, the broader question of accountability is not finished. AfDA continues to advocate that Brookhaven Police fully investigate the man claiming to be the dog’s owner, fellow rapper Cristian Denis, AKA Lil Crix, and pursue any charges supported by the evidence and the law.”
