Blue Jays cut Davis Schneider, creating space for future adds

TheTools · 20.11.2026 18:36 · source · permalink

TORONTO — Davis Schneider’s Toronto Blue Jays tenure started with dramatics only to come to an end rather quietly.

Schneider, the moustachioed fan favourite who homered in his first big-league at-bat and later took Blake Snell deep to lead off Game 5 of the 2025 World Series, was non-tendered by the Blue Jays Friday evening. The move cleared a 40-man roster spot on a day the Blue Jays also shipped right-hander Chase Lee to the Colorado Rockies for cash.

Both transactions were prompted by MLB’s Nov. 20 deadline for tendering contracts to arbitration-eligible players like Schneider, who had been projected by MLB Trade Rumors to earn $1.3 million in 2027. Money aside, there was likely less playing time for Schneider in an outfield also including Brett Bateman, Nathan Lukes, Addison Barger, Jonatan Clase and Anthony Santander.

Utility players Josh Smith and Charles McAdoo further clouded the picture for Schneider, who’s now free to sign with any team. The Blue Jays’ seven other arbitration eligible players were all tendered contracts as anticipated: 

• Ernie Clement ($7.6 million projected salary by MLBTR)

• José Soriano ($5.6 million)

• Louis Varland ($3.8 million)

• Josh Smith ($3.7 million)

• Nathan Lukes ($2.1 million)

• Spencer Arrighetti ($1.9 million)

• Bowden Francis ($1.3 million)

A patient hitter with strong knowledge of the strike zone, Schneider has a lifetime .206/.322/.396 batting line with 37 career home runs in 978 plate appearances. Yet his production fell off in 2026, when he hit five home runs with a .145 batting average and .624 OPS.

Schneider was not a top prospect out of high school but despite being a 28th-round draft pick, he still forced his way to the big leagues by the age of 24 — a credit to his work ethic and the team's player development staff.

The Blue Jays now have one open spot on their 40-man roster, but it likely won’t remain that way for long as the club is actively working to add to their big-league team before an anticipated Dec. 1 lockout. Agents and executives continue to predict a burst of activity before then, with the Blue Jays described by all involved as motivated to add both hitting and pitching. 

Two-time Cy Young Award winner Tarik Skubal’s likely to sign by Dec. 1 with former Mets and Orioles GM Jim Duquette reporting that the Mets' initial offer was for nine years and almost $400 million. Calling that a strong offer is akin to saying Skubal has a chance to be a decent pitcher, as a deal in that range would set records for both AAV and total value for any pitcher.

Beyond Skubal, the starting pitching market is said to be strong, with clubs including the Blue Jays, Padres, Cubs, Guardians, White Sox and Orioles among those looking to add. Even the Rockies are doing their best to acquire pitching by trading for Luis Castillo and Davis Martin Thursday followed by Lee Friday. But while Colorado has to trade for their pitchers due to Denver’s difficult pitching conditions, others will simply spend.

Elsewhere, the relief market keeps moving, with Jonathan Loaisiga obtaining an $18-million guarantee to return to the Yankees through 2028. One agent predicts Adrian Morejon has a chance at a five-year deal, a rarity for relievers. Once he and Alex Vesia sign, there could be movement for Steven Okert and A.J. Minter, two other left-handers who are well-positioned for multi-year deals.

Those involved in the market for position players say right-handed hitting is in real demand, a good thing for Seiya Suzuki and Randy Arozarena. Speaking of which, some say the Mariners are highly motivated on Suzuki, who’s also an excellent fit for the Blue Jays.

One way or another, this is just the beginning. With 11 days remaining before the sport’s collective bargaining agreement expires, the deals are now trickling in slowly but surely. The deluge may not be far behind.
