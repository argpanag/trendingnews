USDA releases decades of Bigfoot files, including 1982 investigation

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:55 · source · permalink

A metal sculpture of Bigfoot welcomes visitors to the tiny community of Happy Camp in the Klamath Forest. The McKinney fire has charred more than 51,000 acres and killed four people in the area. (Luis Sinco / Los Angeles Times via Getty Images)

The Trump administration has slowly released a trove of government documents about one of the biggest and most enduring mysteries in American folklore: Bigfoot.

On Oct. 15, the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) released three files from its "Bigfoot/Sasquatch Records," The Washington Post first reported, adding fresh intrigue to the legend of the towering, hair-covered creature said to roam North American forests without ever being captured.

The records include an official U.S. Forest Service (USFS) investigation from June 1982 into reported Bigfoot footprints discovered in the Mill Creek Watershed of Washington state's Umatilla National Forest.

Investigators documented track measurements, took color photographs and made plaster casts across several field sites to preserve the unusual physical evidence.

Another file details a reported encounter with the creature in Montana dating back to the 1940s. Separately, USFS staff once hosted a Bigfoot in Your Backyard forum in California's Stanislaus National Forest to discuss local sightings, noting at the time the agency could neither confirm nor deny the creature's existence.

A six-page document included in last month's release, which was written in 1980, recounts a historical report from Petty Creek, Montana, by Leo Kinney, who described spotting a large set of tracks alongside three hunters in the 1940s.

"At this point we held a conference and decided that we were not particularly anxious to catch up to whatever it was, and though we didn't confess it at this time, in later conversations we found out that we all had a very uneasy feeling and could feel chills running up and down our backs," Kinney wrote.

The release also includes details of a 2003 sighting in Sanders County, Montana.

In an interview with The Washington Post, Jeremiah Byron of the "Bigfoot Society" questioned the timing of the federal disclosures.

"Why is all this cryptid and Bigfoot-related stuff happening now?" Byron asked. "I’m hoping what this means is that they could be ramping up government entities to start releasing information."

While Bigfoot lore has traditionally centered on the Pacific Northwest, the creature has also surfaced in pop culture, with players reporting sightings in the video game Grand Theft Auto VI.

Gamers have shared clips of the cryptid wandering the woods of Leonida, a fictional U.S. state based on Florida, before sprinting away in a panic when spotted, according to GameSpot.
