  1. Travel disruption around UK after second air traffic control issue in two weekspublished at 15:42 BST 21 September
    15:42 BST 21 September

    Dan Woodland
    Live reporter

    Passengers across the UK are facing flight delays and cancellations for the second time this month after "a technical issue" with air traffic control systems.

    Air traffic control organisation Nats reported a problem "early this morning" at its Prestwick centre in Scotland, but stressed it was "unrelated" to a software issue on 8 September that led to the cancellation of 2,000 flights.

    In an update a few hours later, it reported the problem had been "fixed" and blamed a "connectivity issue" in the systems network.

    But the knock on effects are still being felt. Aviation analytics company Cirium notes that as of 13:45 BST, around 200 flights have been cancelled or did not fly today.

    Among the passengers impacted by the disruption was Nicole McCloskey, from Kilmarnock, whose family holiday to Disneyland Paris has been put in jeopardy. Meanwhile, Steve Davies, 74, and his wife Sandie were left "cheesed off" after being told to disembark their easyJet flight to Edinburgh.

    The incident also prompted several major airlines operating in the UK to question whether Nats is fit for purpose. Ryanair has called for Nats chief Martin Rolfe to resign.

    Suffering two significant issues in a short space of time is likely to attract a great deal of criticism for Nats, and Rolfe could face more pressure to step down, our business correspondent writes.

    Have you been affected by the disruption? Here are your rights.

  2. Disrupted passengers 'unlikely' to get compensation - UK Civil Aviation Authoritypublished at 15:23 BST 21 September
    15:23 BST 21 SeptemberBreaking

    Passengers facing disruption today after the latest Nats technical issue are "unlikely" to be entitled to compensation, the UK Civil Aviation Authority says.

    It says the technical issue is "likely to be considered the result of 'extraordinary circumstances' under passenger rights rules", meaning that "although passengers should be looked after by airlines, they are unlikely to be entitled to compensation for delays or cancellations that were directly caused by the incident".

  3. 'Public will want answers' on air traffic control issue, says travel agents' trade bodypublished at 15:06 BST 21 September
    15:06 BST 21 September

    Flight disruptions from an air traffic control fault twice in a fortnight is "simply not good enough", says travel agents' trade body Abta.

    Abta's Luke Petherbridge says the public will "want answers" after the latest Nats error, adding: "This is the second Nats incident to cause disruption in the last fortnight, and fourth in recent years."

    "The industry and travelling public will want answers – not only on what has gone wrong, but critically how the system is going to be made more resilient to avoid future outages."

    Petherbridge also calls on the government to "seriously consider" whether Nats should be made to financially compensate the "wider industry for such events".

    14:28 BST 21 September

  11. Affected by the disruption? Here's what rights passengers havepublished at 13:36 BST 21 September
    13:36 BST 21 September

    Kevin Peachey
    Cost of living correspondent

    Once again, air passengers are being affected by air traffic control issues. But once again, those whose travel plans have been disrupted do have various rights.

    The most important is that your airline must get you to your destination - even if that’s with a rival carrier - unless you opt to take a refund.

    For lengthy delays, you may have the right to food, or a hotel stay, depending on the length of the wait.

    When delays and cancellations are the fault of an airline, then passengers may be entitled to extra compensation.

    But, as was the case with the last episode of air traffic disruption, these events are beyond the control of airlines, so no extra payments will be made beyond the basic rights mentioned above.
