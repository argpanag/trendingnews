Giants vs. Rams: Three must-know storylines for Monday's Week 2 prime-time game

TheTools · 21.09.2026 16:52 · source · permalink
  • WHERE: SoFi Stadium (Inglewood, Calif.)
  • WHEN: 8:15 p.m. ET
  • WAYS TO WATCH: ESPN, ABC, NFL+

Stream live local and prime-time games on mobile with NFL+ .

The Rams ended Week 1 as the only NFC West team without a win, and the Giants seamlessly transitioned into the John Harbaugh era by enforcing their will on the Cowboys in the Sunday night opener -- just as everyone expected!

Los Angeles’ stumble out of the starting blocks was the more surprising of the two results, and it sets up a pivotal early-season game between two NFC clubs.

While the Rams look to get into the win column, Big Blue has an opportunity to make a huge statement. It would be one thing to open the season by taking down an opponent in a divisional matchup, games which often end up being tossups, but following that up by traveling cross country to defeat the preseason Super Bowl favorites would put the league on notice regarding New York’s emergence as a surprise contender.

Such a statement would require the Giants to earn a win over L.A. for the first time in a decade. The Giants have churned through four different head coaches and dropped four consecutive results to the Rams since the club’s last head-to-head victory in 2016, but that means little to New York’s new era of stars, a Jaxson Dart-led contingent that has quickly proven capable of taking the fight to opponents no matter the odds.

Three must-know storylines

1) Stafford seeks rebound performance

Matthew Stafford’s first start of the 2026 season didn’t only pale in comparison to his ’25 Most Valuable Player campaign -- it was a low point of his six-year tenure with the Rams. Los Angeles scored seven points, the fewest the team has mustered in Stafford’s 75 starts, and his 61.2 passer rating was the second-worst by a reigning MVP in Week 1 history, per NFL Research. Davante Adams managed just 26 receiving yards, his second-lowest output as a Ram, while Puka Nacua, who is questionable to play with a hip injury, was relatively quiet outside of his 41-yard catch to set up Los Angeles’ lone score. The opening week of the NFL calendar is always home to weird results, though. While it’s strange seeing last year’s best offense rank 32nd in points after one game, few people, if any, assume such a result is indicative of how the rest of the season will go. The Rams caught a bit of a break with Giants cornerback Paulson Adebo going on injured reserve and CB Deonte Banks (questionable; calf) missing two practices this week, but they'll still have their work cut out for them against a New York defense that looked dangerous behind the new off-ball linebacking duo of Tremaine Edmunds and Arvell Reese and a speedy pass-rushing group led by Abdul Carter and Brian Burns. The Giants minimized damage against Dallas, which finished second in total offense last year, holding the Cowboys to 244 yards. Will they make it a trend by doing the same to last season’s top total offense, or will Stafford return to MVP form?

2) Can Giants win through another game of keep away?

Everything was working offensively for New York in John Harbaugh’s first game at the helm. Jaxson Dart delivered perfect throws with regularity, going 23 of 29 for 230 yards, three touchdowns and a 134.2 passer rating. He also looked more mature and settled running the ball, taking a late hit out of bounds early but otherwise protecting himself while rushing for 54 yards. Cam Skattebo and Malik Nabers both looked good coming off injury, and new tight end Isaiah Likely led all pass catchers in the game with eight receptions for 78 yards and two TDs. Most impressive of all was the Giants’ ability to sustain long drives, tiring out Dallas’ defense and preventing Dak Prescott and Co. from making it a shootout. Four of New York’s seven possessions hit double-digit plays. Five drives went 60-plus yards. In the second half, New York fielded two drives of eight-plus minutes and a third that took the clock from 5:21 to triple zeroes. It didn’t matter that Dallas scored both times it touched the ball after halftime; there was no way to stage a comeback with possessions so limited by New York’s methodical marches. That’ll likely be the strategy again versus the Rams, and it’ll be even more impressive if Dart and Co. can pull it off facing a defense of L.A.’s caliber.

3) Rams must stop sky from falling.

It’s far too early for Chicken Little to sound the alarm, but it’s difficult to imagine a worse start to the season for Sean McVay’s crew. The Rams could neither stop nor score on the 49ers while opening the campaign in Australia. Then, after returning to L.A., star acquisition Myles Garrett underwent knee surgery that will knock him out at least a month. Though there was no shortage of criticism after McVay’s plan to travel to Melbourne 24 hours before game time failed to yield results, the other explanation is potentially more concerning: If the Rams did feel 100% to play so soon after arriving, that would mean they simply didn’t measure up to a rival that is likely to challenge them for the division. Either way, L.A. is now staring at an 0-1 record and a matchup against an upstart Giants squad. Some panic could creep in among the fan base should the Rams lose, especially with difficult games on deck against the Broncos, Eagles and Bills.
