BYD Is a Battery Company That Happens to Build Cars

TheTools · 18.10.2026 00:48 · source · permalink
  • 002594.SZ
    +0.58%

Ask most people what BYD does and they will say electric cars. That is true, and it undersells the company by a wide margin. BYD is a Chinese multinational manufacturing conglomerate that builds rechargeable batteries, stationary energy storage, solar panels, semiconductors, forklifts, and entire monorail systems. The car business is the loudest part. It is not the whole company, and it was not even the first.

BYD Company Ltd. operates across electronics, autos, renewable energy, and rail transit. That spread is not an accident of acquisition. It is the result of a battery maker deciding, over three decades, that it could build the things its batteries power, then the things that move those things, then the infrastructure they run on. To understand BYD you have to start before the cars existed.

Wang Chuanfu founded BYD to make rechargeable batteries in February 1995, formally registered as Shenzhen BYD Battery Company Ltd. Before that he worked as a vice supervisor at Beijing's Nonferrous Research Institute. He saw an opening as Japanese companies shifted from NiCd toward higher-value nickel-metal hydride and lithium-ion chemistries, and he went after the segment they were leaving behind.

The early strategy was labor, not automation. BYD built its business on manual labor rather than the capital-intensive, highly automated processes Japanese rivals used, and that approach, combined with in-house production of key machinery, cut its unit costs to roughly five or six times lower than Japanese competitors. It worked. BYD won an order from Sanyo in 1996 that marked its arrival in the global supply chain, and by July 2002 it had surpassed Sanyo to become the world's leading NiCd battery maker, accounting for 65% of global production.

The car business came later. BYD Auto was established in 2003. For a battery company that had just won the NiCd crown, building cars was the logical place to put all those cells.

BYD is well known for its Blade Battery, and one sits in every BYD EV. The headline feature is safety: it has passed the nail penetration test, and its compressive strength reaches 445 kN, the equivalent of a 45-ton truck. The warranty runs eight years or longer, covering 310,685 miles and in some cases up to 621,371 miles.

BYD does not keep the batteries to itself. It functions as an OEM battery supplier, and FinDreams Battery, its subsidiary, ranked as the world's number-two EV battery producer in 2024, holding 17% of the EV battery market behind CATL. Its customers include rivals. The Toyota bZ3, a China-only sedan co-developed with BYD, runs a BYD Blade Battery underneath, and regulatory filings show Tesla has fitted BYD cells in the Model Y.
