Here’s MMAmania.com’s UFC 333 betting odds ‘Weekend Lock’ … what’s yours?

UFC 333 is less than 24 hours away …

It all goes down this weekend (Sat., Oct. 24, 2026), inside Etihad Arena on “Fight Island” in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. In the main event, Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) Featherweight champion, Alexander Volkanovski, defends his 145-pound crown against Movsar Evloev … who FINALLY gets his shot at gold.

We have a weekly series at MMAmania.com called “Weekend Lock,” where we share one bet that we predict will slap in the desert when the chaotic dust settles. We also want to hear what our readers think (that’s you!), so please tell us your most confident UFC 333 betting lock in the comments section below (see full UFC 333 odds here).

Last week’s recap: After a week off (I was on vacation), I return to get back in the win column after my last weekend lock did not hit whatsoever, as Andre Fili starched Kai Kamaka with a head kick from hell (see that signature knockout here).

Let’s keep rolling below:

No. 2-ranked Heavyweight contender, Alexander Volkov, looks to continue his march toward a long-overdue title shot when he collides with rising contender, Rizvan Kuniev, on UFC 333’s main card.

This week, I’m locking in Volkov’s moneyline at -118.

Here’s why:

I genuinely don’t understand the reason Volkov is being disrespected by oddsmakers.

Over the past five years, Volkov has lost to just two men: former Heavyweight champion, Tom Aspinall, and current champion Ciryl Gane (twice). And let’s be real, he was brutally robbed in that second Gane fight, which just about everyone (except Adalaide Byrd) thought he won.

Other than those two elite Heavyweights, Volkov has been damn-near perfect.

Indeed, he’s won six of his last seven fights, scoring victories over quality opponents such as Sergei Pavlovich, Jailton Almeida, Tai Tuivasa and Waldo Cortes-Acosta, among others. More important, the 37-year-old has improved tremendously over the years, evolving from a dangerous kickboxer into one of the most complete fighters in the division.

Frankly, Volkov shouldn’t even be fighting Kuniev right now — he should be challenging for UFC’s Heavyweight title. He’s been one of the most consistent fighters in the division for years, and if the judges hadn’t completely screwed him against Gane, he’d have an even stronger case for a long-overdue title shot. Instead, he’s being forced to defend his spot against another hungry contender. And somehow, he’s barely the betting favorite? Give me a break.

Stylistically, I think this is a fantastic matchup for him, too.

Volkov stands a towering 6’7” with an 80-inch reach, giving him a three-inch height and four-inch reach advantages over Kuniev. He’s excellent at using those physical advantages, keeping opponents at the end of his jab, punishing them with kicks, and controlling distance.

Simply put, unless your name is Ciryl Gane, you’re probably not beating Volkov in a technical kickboxing match.

Now, Kuniev is no slouch.

The 34-year-old Dagestani has looked somewhat impressive since joining UFC, notching victories over Almeida and Tyrell Fortune after a narrow split decision loss to Curtis Blaydes. He’s physically strong, possesses solid boxing and can make things ugly in the clinch.

But, that’s where I think the problem lies.

Kuniev’s clearest path to victory is getting Volkov to the ground and keeping him there. However, he has never shot for a takedown inside the Octagon, preferring to strike and work from the clinch.

And trying to outstrike Volkov for 15 minutes is a dangerous game.

Volkov has also improved his defensive wrestling significantly, which should make it even more difficult for Kuniev to turn this into a grinding grappling match.

What could go wrong?

Kuniev is good, and there’s a reason he’s already knocking on the door of the Heavyweight Top 5. He’s durable, strong and has enough power to make Volkov uncomfortable. If he can consistently close the distance, push Volkov against the fence and turn this into an ugly clinch battle, he could absolutely steal rounds.

Volkov is also getting older, and Father Time eventually catches everyone.

Still, I trust Volkov’s experience, technical striking and ability to control distance far more than Kuniev’s relatively unproven offensive wrestling.

At nearly even money, I’m happily backing the fighter who has spent years beating some of the best Heavyweights in the world.

So, again, give me Volkov’s moneyline at -118 as this week’s “Weekend Lock.”
