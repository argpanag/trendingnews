Is el clásico still the world’s biggest match? Three questions before Barcelona v Real Madrid

TheTools · 23.10.2026 09:00 · source · permalink

The fixture’s relevance waned as the Messi-Ronaldo rivalry faded. Can Lamine Yamal and Mbappé lift it into a new era?

For the best part of a decade, el clásico was more than just soccer’s biggest fixture. It was the defining narrative. At the peak of the Lionel Messi v Cristiano Ronaldo era, there were clásicos everywhere. Yes, when the two historic rivals clashed on the pitch, but also in the voting for the Ballon d’Or every year and in so much of the discourse around the sport.

As the Messi-Ronaldo rivalry faded, though, so too did the clásico’s relevance. This isn’t to say matches between Barcelona and Real Madrid no longer matter. They always have and always will. In 2026, however, it’s difficult to measure a clásico’s standing. Is it still the sport’s biggest fixture?

Sunday’s match at Camp Nou will be watched by hundreds of millions of viewers all over the world. A clásico always carries significance, but this weekend’s edition will be especially significant. A Barça win would move them nine points clear of their rivals at the top of La Liga. A Real Madrid victory could be a catalyst for the rest of their season.

Lamine Yamal may be the best player in the world right now, and he could have the Ballon d’Or trophy to prove it by Monday. Kylian Mbappé is up there too. The Clásico doesn’t lack for star power, but the framing of the fixture has changed in recent times. Not so long ago, the world stopped to watch it. Now, the world doesn’t seem so bothered.

Is the Premier League’s dominance a factor?

Unless Manchester City’s financial rule-breaking has a tangible effect on the Premier League’s reputation, the English top flight remains soccer’s dominant league. The league with more fans around the world than any other. A behemoth so omnipresent that even the uninterested are at least aware.

April’s title clash between Manchester City and Arsenal set a new record as the most-watched Premier League match in US TV history, nearly doubling the mark for the most-watched clásico in US TV history set in October 2025. Audience trends point towards the Premier League being European soccer’s most compelling product.

The packaging of the division is possibly part of this. While La Liga has bounced between different broadcasters, the Premier League made a long-term commitment to Sky Sports in the UK and NBC in the USA to properly broadcast and promote matches. Premier League matches are filmed with more cameras. They look better. More vibrant. It also helps that fans know where to consistently find games in their TV guide.

La Liga has tried to broaden its global appeal, setting up an office in New York and even floating the idea of playing a competitive match in Miami. Past clásico encounters have been scheduled for a primetime audience in Asia. The Premier League, however, has the advantage of being an English language-first league which appears to be an advantage in the modern media market.

Are Barcelona and Real Madrid still the best?

At the peak of the clásico’s relevance, there was an unavoidable sense that the fixture was a showcase for the very best that the sport had to offer. That, however, is no longer the case. Barcelona are Europe’s most exhilarating team to watch, but the inherent chaos of their playing style under Hansi Flick comes with problems (primarily their high defensive line) that have prevented them from truly challenging for the Champions League.

It’s been a full decade since Barça last won the Champions League. While Real Madrid have enjoyed continental glory more recently, winning a 14th European Cup in 2024, they are a long way from being the team who dominated Europe under Carlo Ancelotti and Zinedine Zidane.

Real Madrid find themselves behind the curve of the modern game and the appointment of José Mourinho in the summer didn’t change that. If anything, the Special One’s not-so-special return to the Santiago Bernabéu has highlighted the club’s stagnancy. Xabi Alonso dared to introduce new ideas and was hounded out for it.

Instead, Paris Saint-Germain are setting the standard at the top of the game – and doing so with a former Barcelona player and manager at the helm. And with a former Barcelona forward performing a false-nine role pioneered by Messi.

Is Mbappé v Yamal the new Messi v Ronaldo?

Lamine Yamal has 20 goal contributions in just 12 games for Barcelona this season. Mbappé has 15 in the same number of games for Real Madrid. Both players represent a new direction for their respective teams and could dominate the clásico as individuals for years in the way Messi and Ronaldo did.

Perhaps Sunday’s encounter will be the first truly memorable clash between the two. Vinícius Júnior is suspended while Raphinha is only just returning to fitness after a minor muscle injury, meaning there will be even more focus on Mbappé and Lamine Yamal.

Messi scored 26 clásico goals, the most of any player in the fixture’s history. Ronaldo had the second most (18), tied with Alfredo Di Stéfano. Mbappé and Lamine Yamal have a long way to go to reach such heights as the two new faces of Spain’s Big Two, but with every meeting their personal rivalry will grow. The narrative will build.

Meaning comes out of history. Shared experience. A consensus that something matters can take time to be reached and while the consensus is that Mbappé and Lamine Yamal are two of the best players in the world, their careers have yet to be intertwined as two rivals whose story can only be told as one. When that happens, the clásico may reclaim its status as soccer’s most meaningful game.
