Powerful Thanksgiving storm hammers US East Coast

A powerful storm has disrupted the Thanksgiving holiday along much of the US East Coast, knocking out ‌power for hundreds of thousands of users as wind gusts of up to 110km/h toppled trees and power lines.

Heavy rains flooded coastal areas on Thursday, while utilities warned rain-soaked ground and ‌strong winds could increase the risk of falling trees and power outages.

Nearly 200,000 homes and businesses had no power in Virginia and another 177,000 in Maryland, according to poweroutage.us, which also showed Pennsylvania, New Jersey and North Carolina each ‌had tens ‌of thousands ⁠of utility customers without electricity.

Utilities and state officials had warned the ​combination of strong onshore winds and rain could cause widespread power disruptions.

Wind gusts of 80-110km/h were reported or forecast across parts of Virginia, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, New York, and coastal New England, according to the US National Weather Service.

New York City Emergency Management ⁠reported winds intensified on Thursday afternoon, reaching 90km/h at LaGuardia Airport.

The NWS said strong onshore winds associated with the storm, combined with king ​tides, were ‌causing coastal flooding in low-lying communities along portions of the East Coast.

The threat of ​storms had already led officials to postpone Thursday's 100th edition of the annual Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City, marking the first time the ​event ​has been rescheduled due to severe weather.

The ​storm also disrupted holiday air travel, with ‌more than 400 US flights cancelled and some 1600 delayed by early Thursday afternoon, according to FlightAware.

Major airports in the Washington and New York City areas were among the hardest hit.

"A powerful coastal storm will continue to bring severe impacts to the region through ​today into this evening due to damaging high winds accompanied by heavy rainfall," ​the NWS said.

"The storm departs ⁠overnight into Friday."
