News from Denmark.

Since people sometimes like to share updates on other teams and leagues in these weekend posts, I'll give a summary of Denmark's Superliga, a league I know everybody here follows closely but would be interested in a write-up of anyway.

Sadly, it looks like business as usual is going to be restored at the top. FC København, having finished in the bottom half for the first time since the late 90s last season, are top and in outstanding form. Clear favorites for the title. The club with whom they have typically fought for the title over the past 12 years, FC Midtjylland, currently sit second.

If there are going to be any surprise challengers for the title, it looks like it will be the club in my name, FC Nordsjælland. FCN are a club who quite often have a decent-to-excellent attack, but are porous at the back. This season so far, they have the joint-fewest goals conceded and have looked quite solid defensively. It looks like their best chance in years to grab their second-ever title, though we'll see how they cope with only their third-ever European league/group stage.

Speaking of Europe, Denmark have for the first time since (I think) 2014 improved their coefficient to the point where this season they have two Champions League spots. Sadly, they have no teams in the Champions League or Europa League, but this might be a blessing in disguise, because they have a whole four teams in the Conference League, and they might be able to pick up enough coefficient points to improve their standing.

Last season's shock champions, AGF, are off to a shocking start, sitting second-bottom after seven games and not having yet won a game. After winning their first title in 40 years last season (think Hearts without the heartbreak at the end), nobody really expected them to repeat, but still...

The two promoted clubs, Lyngby and Horsens, are off to good starts. Lyngby are not surprising, they are quite the yo-yo club over the past decade, but spent three straight seasons in the Superliga before going down for a season to the second tier. Horsens' start are more impressive, given that it's their first in the top flight in 13 years.

Not many Mancunians in today’s Manchester derby but Kobbie Mainoo will be one of them.

There has been a spotlight on Mainoo, with some fans wondering how he would react to the suffocation of his World Cup dream; his treatment by Tuchel, who preferred Elliot Anderson and Declan Rice in the deeper midfield positions and, also, at various times, Reece James, Jude Bellingham, Jordan Henderson, Morgan Rogers and Nico O’Reilly. Nobody should have worried. Mainoo is made of stern stuff. United regeared their midfield to the tune of £155m during the transfer window, adding Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos and Carlos Baleba. Baleba has not yet played because of injury, but as United prepare for Sunday’s derby at Old Trafford, it is Mainoo who has reasserted himself.

Fulham are among the Premier League’s winless wonders but Alvaro Arbeloa is not panicking, particularly not after that goalless draw at Liverpool.

“I know that when you are not winning games it’s not so easy that your players believe in your ideas. But they believe in the way that we have been working and I am so happy and so proud of the way that we played.

“I want that my players show this character, that they want the ball, they want the ball under pressure. We know what we have to do, how we could beat the pressure of Liverpool and we did so, so well against a team that press so high and so well.

I think there were so many things that we did well, but we have been making mistakes and of course in this league, if you make mistakes, they will punish you. If you have chances, try to take those chances but also to be a little bit more balanced. I think we did better and we realised how important it is to be balanced.”

Nottingham Forest defender Nikola Milenkovic is set for three weeks on the sidelines with a hamstring injury.

The Serbia international suffered the problem on the eve of Saturday’s 2-1 win at Aston Villa and is definitely out of next week’s clash with Coventry. “Nikola is a little bit more serious,” boss Oliver Glasner said. “He will also miss Coventry. We are hopeful that he can be back after the international break. Let’s say this is the first time I feel three weeks without a game is good.” (Press Association)

Roberto De Zerbi says his Tottenham players continue to lack physical fitness and must “unblock” themselves in psychological terms after they drew their latest Premier League blank.

He explained: “There is a word in Italian – sbloccarsi – that literally translates as if we unblock ourselves.

“[It is] a mental block because four games, no wins, no goals scored … it is tough for the players. The team is extremely strong, we can score goals and we can win games. And then we have to respect the history of the players in their careers. Forget Dominic Solanke and Marmoush – the other players … they didn’t score many, many goals in their careers and we have to find these goals. I think we can do so with the quality of our play.”

De Zerbi added: “I said we started this season late for many, many reasons; too late. Many players at the World Cup, many injuries, many problems in Australia. Micky van de Ven suffered an injury in Australia. Domi Solanke the same. James Maddison the same. Mateus Fernandes suffered an injury in Australia. He lost 15 days and it’s not so quick. Sávio and Marmoush, they came 10 days ago. I started to work with this [whole] group not last Thursday but two Thursdays ago. So, I think we have not the right physical condition yet.”

Mikel Arteta was left furious with referee John Brooks last night, despite Arsenal’s 2-0 win against Sunderland.

Before Bruno Guimarães gave Arsenal the lead in a tight match that Régis Le Bris’s team were arguably unlucky to lose, David Raya made a brilliant save to deny Enzo Le Fée from the penalty spot. Arteta, however, did not believe that Ezri Konsa had hauled Dan Ballard to the ground, instead blaming the Sunderland man for pulling down his opponent.

He said: “We had to overcome a really tough opponent in a difficult place. Sunderland can suffocate you and they decided to be really physical and very direct and we also had to overcome a situation that, in my opinion was unacceptable at this level.

“I’m very worried and sad because this can never be a penalty. It’s a judo roll from the opponent. Ezri does nothing. We have to take responsibility to protect the game. Today the game was unprotected and it could have cost us the win and our momentum. I’m really sad. It should never have happened.”
