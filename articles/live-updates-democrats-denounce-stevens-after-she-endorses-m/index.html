Live updates: Democrats denounce Stevens after she endorses Michigan GOP Senate nominee | CNN Politics

Here's the latest

• Michigan Senate race shock: Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens broke with her party to endorse GOP nominee Mike Rogers following days of tension with her former primary rival, Abdul El-Sayed. The Democratic nominee said he remains focused on issues that matter to voters: “I actually don’t think about Haley Stevens,” El-Sayed told reporters.

• Swift fallout: Democrats have widely condemned Stevens’ move, with outrage coming from state and national party leadership, while the top Democrats in Congress signaled they have El-Sayed’s back. Questions still loom about whether any of Stevens’ backers will join her.

GOP Speaker Johnson says he spoke to Stevens and "told her how great she was"

House Speaker Mike Johnson said he spoke to Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens after her endorsement of Michigan GOP Senate nominee Mike Rogers, saying, “She’s standing for truth.”

“I sent her a note, spoke to Haley, and told her how great she was, how much we appreciate that. She’s standing for truth,” Johnson told reporters after traveling on Air Force One with President Donald Trump to a midterm rally in Iowa.

Johnson added that he also encouraged Stevens “to stay safe.” When asked whether that meant he did not think she was safe, the Louisiana Republican replied, “I don’t know.”

“We’re in a heightened threat environment. Everybody in politics is, and there are dangerous people out there. Of course, she and her fiancé have been getting a lot of threats,” he said.

Trump said he had not spoken to Stevens but that there was not much for him to reach out on other than to “thank her and say she did the right thing.”

When asked whether Trump would want Stevens to join the Republican Party, the president was unequivocal.

“I’d love to have her,” he said.

Kansas AG claims group is offering "monetary incentives" to voters

Kansas Attorney General Kris Kobach said his office has received evidence from a voter that a group identified as Bluestem Kansas has been offering $5 gift cards to voters and urged the group to stop.

“As attorney general and before that as secretary of state, I have prosecuted many cases of voter fraud in Kansas, including voting by non-citizens and double voting. But I never thought that a group in Kansas would be so brazen as to go door-to-door three days before a general election offering voters monetary incentives,” Kobach said in a statement on Saturday. CNN has reached out to Bluestem Kansas for comment.

President Donald Trump raised alarm about Kobach’s announcement on Sunday, writing in a Truth Social post that Kobach told him “a Radical Left PAC operative was caught on camera going door to door in Wichita offering gift cards to people for votes” and urged people to reach out to him with any information.

The Bluestem Kansas website promotes two separate organizations, Bluestem Foundation for Economic Freedom and Bluestem Fund PAC. It describes it’s mission as “Educating Kansans on the issues that affect our daily lives. Providing the tools and information to take action and vote.”

"This is not high school": State party chair slams Stevens for Michigan defection

Michigan Democratic Party Chair Curtis Hertel sharply criticized Haley Stevens’ decision to endorse GOP nominee Mike Rogers after the Democratic congresswoman said she was not welcomed in Abdul El-Sayed’s campaign.

“This is not high school. This is not some kind of drama club or a glee club,” Hertel told CNN’s Brian Todd this afternoon. “Not invited … why would that matter in any way, shape or form about how she treats the constituents and the people she said that she was gonna be there for?”

El-Sayed zeroes in on Michiganders' struggles and faith after flashpoint church service

What was meant to be a show of Democratic unity at a Detroit church on Sunday morning was completely upended when one lawmaker skipped the service, joined Republicans in the pews of a separate church and then promptly endorsed one of them.

But for Democratic Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, embracing fellow party members at the event less than 48 hours before Election Day, his focus remains locked on Michiganders, their struggles and faith.

“I want to live in an America where all of our kids can look at each other and say, ‘It doesn’t really matter the color of your skin or how you pray or who you love. It matters that you share this place with me, and I am mutually invested in you,’” El-Sayed told churchgoers Sunday afternoon. “That opportunity is in front of us, and we got a choice in this election.”

A late-week spat between El-Sayed and his former primary opponent Rep. Haley Stevens deepened over the weekend before reaching a fever pitch Sunday morning when Stevens crossed party lines to endorse El-Sayed’s GOP opponent, Mike Rogers, much to the Democratic Party’s dismay.

El-Sayed didn’t mention the clash with Stevens in his remarks following the church service.

“As the Muslim in the room, I’m going to ask us all to be evangelists here,” El-Sayed appealed to the crowd.

He said: “I’m going to ask you to preach the good word about what is on the line.”

Stevens “betrayed” working-class people by endorsing Rogers, Mamdani says

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani criticized Michigan Rep. Haley Stevens for endorsing Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers, saying she “betrayed” working-class people in doing so.

“Working-class people’s lives are on the ballot on Tuesday — their healthcare, their rent, their bills,” Mamdani posted on X, responding to Stevens’ video endorsement.

Brad Lander, the Democratic nominee for New York’s 10th Congressional District and ally of Mamdani, also rebuked Stevens’ endorsement.

“I didn’t think it was possible to combine primary-loser-sour-grapes & Islamophobia into a more toxic cocktail than Andrew Cuomo. But Haley Stevens has managed to do it,” Lander posted on X.

“If she wants to betray civil rights, reproductive freedom, immigrant protections, and working families by endorsing Mike Rogers and trying to give control of the Senate to J.D. Vance and Donald Trump, I guess that’s her business — but it’s appalling, oddly patronizing, and simply false to claim that it’s in the interest of Michigan’s Jewish communities,” Lander, a self-described liberal Zionist, wrote.

5 key things help demonstrate how Americans feel about the economy

Every incumbent on the ballot this fall, Republican or Democrat, is running on a set of numbers. Unemployment at 4.2%. Record household income. Inflation “cooling.”

But over six months of meeting with voters across the country, I heard directly from Americans why those numbers aren’t landing. Why do people feel this bad about an economy that, on paper, is this good?

Here are the five things that more than four dozen interviews taught me:

  • When Washington says inflation is cooling, it means prices are rising more slowly. It doesn’t mean anything got cheaper. That distinction sounds like economist hair-splitting. At the kitchen table, it’s everything.
  • No single catastrophe wiped out the people I met. It was the grind: housing, insurance, food, childcare, electricity — all of it climbing, none of it optional. That left them one surprise away from crisis: a $70 tire, a $400 vet bill, an $1,100 utility bill due Monday, a dead battery in a snowstorm.
  • When a system breaks quietly enough, the people it breaks blame themselves. But self-blame doesn’t stay self-blame forever. More than three-quarters of Americans say they’re dissatisfied or angry about the direction of the country, and 37% call themselves flat-out angry.
  • The anxiety I heard wasn’t only about this month’s bills. It was about who gets to stay. First-time buyers are getting older and buying less frequently. Voters under 35 are among the most pessimistic in the country: 84% rate the economy poorly. It isn’t a mood. It’s math about their own futures.
  • The people most consumed by the cost of living are the least sure their vote changes anything. Many Americans feel that policymakers aren’t listening, or don’t understand, when it comes to their financial situation.

Democrat Haley Stevens backs Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan Senate race. What to know

Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens broke with her party and endorsed the Republican nominee for Michigan’s Senate seat today.

“As you may have seen, our Democratic nominee for US Senate, Abdul, said that I was no longer welcome in his campaign and that I should stay away. And he’s right, and that’s why I am endorsing Mike Rogers for the United States Senate today,” said Stevens, a moderate Democrat who lost a contentious primary to Abdul El-Sayed.

Stevens’ shocking decision to endorse Rogers and make an unexpected appearance with him at a church service in Detroit on Sunday is a jarring culmination to a dayslong public and private war of words between Stevens and El-Sayed.

If you’re just joining us, here’s what else to know:

What Stevens said:

  • “We can’t stand with hate,” the lawmaker said when she addressed reporters outside of Perfecting Church in Detroit. Stevens said that while she has not cast her ballot yet, “90% of the votes I’m going to cast are going to be for Democrats.”
  • Stevens also repeatedly said that El-Sayed did not want to include her in his campaign, despite her previously endorsing the Democratic nominee.
  • Asked how she reconciles standing by Rogers amid repeated GOP attacks on El-Sayed’s faith, Stevens said, “This is not anti-Muslim at all.”
  • After Stevens and Rogers stopped taking questions from reporters, a person in the crowd shouted, “Haley’s a traitor.”

El-Sayed’s response: The Democratic Senate nominee largely dismissed questions about Stevens’ endorsement of Rogers. “At the end of the day, I actually don’t think about Haley Stevens,” he said, adding that he remains entirely focused on issues affecting Michiganders.

Rogers called Stevens: According to a GOP official close to Rogers’ campaign, the Republican Senate nominee called Stevens after CNN and other outlets reported that El-Sayed’s campaign had asked his former primary rival to stay away from his events.

Stevens’ staff blindsided: Stevens’ chief of staff and communications director have quit following her endorsement of Rogers, sources told CNN. Sources familiar with the matter told CNN Stevens’ announcement blindsided several of her staffers, outside political advisers and longtime Democratic friends. One person who has spoken to Stevens every day this week called her behavior “a complete and embarrassing betrayal.”

Democrats rally behind El-Sayed: Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries reiterated support for El-Sayed. Officials from state and national party leadership issued scathing statements, and many elected Democrats and other party figures have joined the chorus criticizing Stevens and backing El-Sayed.

Trump weighs in: President Donald Trump said Stevens was brave for endorsing Rogers. “Haley said what few people are willing to say,” he said.

CNN’s Dana Bash, Katherine Dautrich, Eric Bradner, Jeff Zeleny, Catherine Nicholls, Manu Raju and Elise Hammond contributed to this report.

"It's her choice": Some Democratic voters unfazed by Stevens' endorsement of Rogers

Though Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens has caused a stir in breaking with her party to endorse GOP nominee Mike Rogers, some Democratic voters in Detroit were unfazed by her decision.

Jacqueline Duncombe, an 80-year-old Detroit resident, told CNN it was Stevens’ choice to endorse Rogers.

“It’s her choice, you know, that’s what I like about America. You could vote for who you want to vote for, you know, and be Democratic or Republican or independent, whatever,” Duncombe said. She said she already cast her ballot, voting for Democrat Abdul El-Sayed.

Asked for her thoughts on Stevens’ endorsement of Rogers, Gloria Gooden, another Democrat who lives in Detroit, told CNN that she doesn’t pay much attention to issues like that.

“I don’t pay much attention to that, and I voted, and I vote Democrat. And I’m through with it. Whoever said what or did what, I don’t ask nobody else what they did or who they voted for. I just think about me,” Gooden said.

Rep. Debbie Dingell says voters are not focused on Stevens’ "efforts to get attention”

Rep. Debbie Dingell, the Michigan lawmaker who advised Rep. Haley Stevens to stay away from Democratic Senate Nominee Abdul El-Sayed’s events amid their rift, sharply dismissed her House colleague’s surprise endorsement of GOP Senate nominee Mike Rogers.

In a statement posted on X, Dingell said Michiganders are talking about issues like affordability, over “Congresswoman Stevens’ continued efforts to get attention.”

“If we are going to fight to improve the lives of working men and women in Michigan, we must stay focused on electing Democrats up and down the ballot, and that includes electing Abdul El-Sayed as Michigan’s next Senator,” Dingell said.

The statement came after Dingell said told MSNOW earlier today that Stevens appeared poised to support the El-Sayed.

“So let me be very clear. And she represents a lot of the Jewish community, but she was very clear, and I asked her if I could say this last night that she’s a passionate Democrat. She’s going to vote for Abdul,” Dingell said during an interview on MSNOW’s “The Weekend”

Last night, as the rift between the two Democrats continued to loom, Dingell said she suggested to Stevens that she stay away from El-Sayed’s events.

“No. I’m the one that talked to Haley, and I am the one that said, ‘This isn’t working,’ so I want to be really clear on that,” when asked by CNN’s Jessica Dean about reports that El-Sayed disinvited his former primary opponent from upcoming campaign events.

Dingell said she told Stevens to, instead, focus on matters impacting voters, such as affordability and healthcare.

Former Democratic primary rival says Stevens putting "feelings" above Michigan voters

Michigan state Sen. Mallory McMorrow said Rep. Haley Stevens, her former rival in the Democratic Senate primary, was putting her “feelings” above the best interests of their state by endorsing GOP candidate Mike Rogers.

McMorrow, who dropped out of the primary race in early July as a fierce battle shaped up between Stevens and now-Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed, pointed to Stevens’ references to being booed at the state party’s convention this spring, as well as being encouraged by El-Sayed’s team to stay away from his recent campaign events.

“It feels like it’s more about her feelings than Michiganders. Politics is not easy. I was in this primary too. It was one of the most brutal, ugliest primaries I have certainly seen, having lived through it. But there is too much on the line right now for us to be in our feelings,” McMorrow said in an interview on MS Now.

The Michigan Democrat said she received the news Stevens was backing Rogers while at church in the Detroit suburbs with El-Sayed, as well as prominent Democratic Reps. James Clyburn and Debbie Dingell.

“It is just a sad, desperate attempt to be relevant when we’ve got a couple of days in this election left,” she said.

McMorrow, who represents a similar part of the state to Stevens, said the news was “not a surprise to a lot of us who have known Haley Stevens for a long time.”

“This is how she operates behind the scenes. If she doesn’t get her way, she threatens people, and people are finally starting to see who she is,” she claimed, adding, “Michiganders deserve so much better.”

Michigan's Democratic senators condemn Stevens' GOP endorsement for open seat

Retiring Democratic Michigan Sen. Gary Peters said he is “extremely disappointed” in Rep. Haley Stevens’ move to break from the party with her endorsement of Mike Rogers, the Republican vying to flip Peters’ open seat.

“Colleen and I are extremely disappointed in Haley’s decision,” Peters wrote on X. “Donald Trump’s corruption and chaos must be checked by a Democratic Senate majority to stop the economic pain crushing Michiganders.”

Peters said he will cast his own vote for Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, along with every other Democrat on the ballot.

Sen. Elissa Slotkin also took to X on Sunday afternoon, saying that since the shocking endorsement, “there is simply too much at stake.”

“Anyone who makes an election this important about themselves, and not Michiganders and our democracy, should think hard about whether they are serving the greater good,” the Michigan Democrat wrote. “The election on Tuesday is bigger than any one person. It’s about who we are as a country and the values that most represent us.”

Troy Jackson teams up with progressive neighbor for town hall in Maine

Maine Democratic Senate nominee Troy Jackson teamed up with Sen. Ed Markey, a progressive from the neighboring state of Massachusetts, for a town hall two days before the election.

Markey is expected to win his own reelection bid after decisively winning a primary challenge from a younger, more moderate lawmaker.

“Maine has a great working-class warrior,” Markey said of Jackson. “The 51st seat to the Senate goes through Maine.”

Jackson is vying to unseat GOP Sen. Susan Collins, a battle-tested incumbent whom Democrats almost certainly need to defeat to win Senate control.

Issues of Michiganders matter most, the rest is "just a distraction," El-Sayed says

Despite the furor surrounding Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens breaking with her party to endorse GOP Senate candidate Mike Rogers, Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed said “none of this matters.”

Facing a wave of questions from a crowd of reporters on the campaign trail today, El-Sayed stressed that he cares more about the issues of everyday Michiganders. Everything else, he said, is “just a distraction.”

“I would hope that you all want to ask questions that are relevant to that family. They’re the ones who matter. The rest of this — just a distraction,” El-Sayed told journalists.

When asked about Stevens, the Democratic Senate nominee said he didn’t think about her, adding: “If you’re somebody who believes that we ought to have more rights, not less, then I would think you would support me in this race.”

Sen. Sullivan voters express confidence in Alaska ballot, concerns linger on certification

Brandon Daigger, who voted for Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan, the incumbent in Alaska’s Senate race, said he didn’t worry about what he saw on his ballot.

Alaska has been at the center of controversy over how a second Dan Sullivan ended up on the state’s Senate ballot, which has sparked lawsuits, criminal investigations and threats to block certification of the election if the incumbent is defeated by Democrat Mary Peltola.

Justin Andersen also stood in line to shake hands with his senator, who handed out candy to costumed children. “It doesn’t matter,” Andersen said about the two Dans on his ballot. Nodding to the senator Andersen voted for, he said, “He’s out here with the people. That’s what matters.”

FBI agents began knocking on doors this weekend of Dan J. Sullivan donors as the investigation picked up pace on the ground in Alaska. Andersen said the scrutiny gave him confidence in his ballot, which he already sent in, voting for Sen. Sullivan.

Daigger had one lingering concern, even as he remained confident in his fellow Alaska voters — a delay in certification that Gov. Mike Dunleavy raised.

Schumer accuses Stevens of "despicable betrayal" over Rogers endorsement

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, a New York Democrat, said Sunday that Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens’ endorsement of Michigan Senate Republican candidate Mike Rogers is a “despicable betrayal.”

The comment by Schumer, released on X, goes further than an earlier statement he released that did not mention Stevens. He said in that statement that Senate Democrats were “united” behind their nominee in the battleground race, Abdul-El Sayed.

Stevens’ decision to endorse Rogers has drawn further scrutiny to Schumer’s support for her in the primary.

Jeffries urges support for El-Sayed after Stevens' endorsement of Rogers

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, a New York Democrat, encouraged people to support Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed on Sunday, after one of Jeffries’ Democratic members, Haley Stevens, endorsed El-Sayed’s Republican opponent.

In an X post, Jeffries contrasted El-Sayed with the GOP nominee, Mike Rogers, and called the election “critical to flipping control of the United States Senate.”

“Vote [El-Sayed] in Michigan!” Jeffries said, without mentioning Stevens.

Jeffries rarely weighs in on Senate races, but his post underscored the extent of the backlash to Stevens’ decision to back Rogers. The top Senate Democrat, Chuck Schumer, also released a statement urging support for El-Sayed, without mentioning Stevens’ defection.

Rep. Haley Stevens’ mother, Maria Marcotte, said she supported Republican Senate nominee Mike Rogers before her daughter endorsed him and denounced claims that anyone encouraged Stevens to support Rogers.

“Haley is her own woman. She always has been. Suggesting a man made her decision for her is insulting to her, and it is insulting to me as her mother,” Marcotte said in a statement to CNN. Stevens’ fiancé, Jason Turkish, previously endorsed Rogers.

Marcotte also pointed to Stevens’ calls for El-Sayed to condemn an outside group’s advertisement that blames Americans’ cost-of-living struggles on US foreign aid for Israel. While El-Sayed denounced the ad on Saturday, CNN and other outlets reported on Friday that his campaign had asked Stevens to skip all campaign events in the race’s closing days,

“Abdul should not have thrown her out of the campaign for speaking her truth. Haley made this choice herself, with her eyes open, the way she has made every hard choice in her life,” Marcotte said. “And I know Haley will support whoever wins this election, because that is who she is. I have never been prouder of her.”

DNC chair calls Stevens GOP endorsement a "betrayal" of party values

Democratic National Committee head Ken Martin said Rep. Haley Stevens’ decision to break with the party and endorse Michigan’s GOP Senate nominee Mike Rogers was a “betrayal” of party values, calling the move a “last-minute political stunt.”

Stevens endorsed Rogers this morning after appearing with him at a Detroit church. CNN and other outlets previously reported that El-Sayed’s campaign had asked Stevens to stay away from his events despite the two of them originally being scheduled to appear together at a church service on Sunday.
