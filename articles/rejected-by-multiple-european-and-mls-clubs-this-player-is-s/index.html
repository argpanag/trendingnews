Rejected by multiple European and MLS clubs, this player is set to become one of soccer’s biggest stars

TheTools · 08.09.2026 13:16 · source · permalink

Yan Diomande is in the frame to make his UEFA Champions League debut today, continuing his swift recent ascent to the pinnacle of men’s soccer.

A month after completing a club-record transfer to Real Madrid, Yan Diomande is today in line to make his first ever appearance in the UEFA Champions League, as the 15-time tournament winners kick off their continental campaign with a heavyweight clash against Inter Milan.

For Diomande, a debut in Europe’s premier club competition would be the latest milestone in a journey to the top that has well and truly clicked into gear over the past couple of years: from rejection by multiple teams on both sides of the Atlantic, to performing on the elite stages of men’s soccer.

Will Diomande start for Real Madrid against Inter?

A $145 million signing from RB Leipzig in early August, Diomande is yet to feature in the Madrid lineup: in four games in LaLiga, Bernabéu boss José Mourinho has limited the newcomer to a substitute appearance on each occasion. Most recently, the 19-year-old played just over 25 minutes in Friday’s 1-0 loss at Real Betis, as Madrid dropped points for the first time in LaLiga this season.

However, as Mourinho deals with a number of key absences ahead of tonight’s Champions League opener against the Nerazzurri, Diomande is in the frame to mark his tournament debut with his first start for the club.

Madrid face Inter, Mourinho’s former employer, without midfielders Arda Güler, Bernardo Silva and Eduardo Camavinga, who are all suspended. What’s more, today’s league-phase matchup is likely to come too soon for Aurélien Tchouaméni to return in the middle of the park, as the Frenchman finalizes his recovery from injury.

Against this backdrop, Mourinho could opt to drop Jude Bellingham into a deeper midfield role, alongside club captain Federico Valverde at the heart of the engine room, leaving Brahim Díaz to deputize for Bellingham at No. 10. With Díaz operating centrally, and Güler unavailable, a berth on the right wing could well open up for Diomande to step into.

“Relax”

One of multiple players who are poised to wear the Champions League’s new “debut” patch on their sleeve this midweek, Diomande is yet to score or assist for Madrid; however, Mourinho has called for patience with the teenager, as he settles in at the Bernabéu.

“Relax, he has to go little by little,” the Portuguese told reporters on August 30, after Madrid’s 4-0 league win over Málaga. “He’s working really hard. He’s an open, humble kid who wants to learn, who wants to work. He listens to me, and he also listens to his teammates. He is going to grow.”

Mourinho added: “I can’t look at [Diomande’s nine-figure price tag]. I have to look at the young player with enormous potential who had a high-level season in the Bundesliga with Leipzig.”

Certainly, Diomande’s figures in 2025/26 augur well for Madrid. After joining Leipzig from Spanish club CD Leganés a year ago, the forward was a standout star for the Germans, amassing 23 goal involvements in 36 games as he helped his team to a third-placed finish in the Bundesliga. Diomande’s displays also earned him a spot at this summer’s World Cup in North America, where he started all four games for Ivory Coast.

However, until a career-launching move to Leganés just 21 months ago, Diomande did not appear to have an obvious path to such exalted arenas.

“He did slip through the cracks”

After moving to the U.S. in his mid-teens, the Abidjan native excelled for AS Frenzi, a semi-professional youth side affiliated to Florida’s DME Academy. In 2023, Diomande was one of the stars of Frenzi’s United Premier Soccer League title win, leading DME to laud his “extraordinary” contribution to the trophy triumph.

As he sought to break into the professional game, Diomande was then offered to several potential suitors in Europe, but the likes of Bournemouth, Crystal Palace, Olympiacos and Rangers all turned him down. A buyer could not be found in Major League Soccer, either; indeed, a coach who worked with Diomande at DME has revealed that Orlando City missed out on the winger when they could not be persuaded to make the short, intrastate trip to the academy’s Daytona Beach base to assess his talents.

“I think he did slip through the cracks,” Todd Eason told FourFourTwo after Diomande’s summer move to Madrid. “We were in Daytona, 45 minutes from Orlando City, and at the time, I had told some representatives at Orlando City, ‘Hey, I’ve got a kid coming that looks pretty special, and I would love him to come down and train with the B team.’ But they just kept delaying and delaying.”

Eason added: “I was very disappointed that Orlando City wouldn’t come 45 minutes to watch him or at least take them into camp.

“I do think MLS teams have probably dropped the ball a lot on some domestic talent that have come through, but every team’s a little bit different, so you can’t really speak on all that. Orlando City was the one that I was really disappointed in: you couldn’t go out of your way to see this kid play, and now you lost your chance.”

Finally, as other clubs hesitated, it was Leganés who stepped in to sign Diomande - and within less than a year, Los Pepineros had turned a more than tenfold profit. After purchasing the winger for just under $2m in November 2024, the Spaniards gave him his debut the following January. Only 10 appearances and six months later, he had been snapped up by Leipzig in a $20 million-plus deal.

What’s more, the return on Leganés’s investment didn’t end there: the club has now netted a further $7-8 million from Diomande’s subsequent move to Madrid, thanks to a sell-on clause included in his transfer to Germany.
