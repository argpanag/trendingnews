'It's Time for a Change'--'The Chosen' Actor Endorses James Talarico for Senate Ahead of Faith Coalition Launch

TheTools · 16.10.2026 15:05 · source · permalink

Share

Jordan Walker Ross, best known for portraying Little James in the hit Christian television series “The Chosen,” has endorsed Democratic U.S. Senate candidate James Talarico in Texas and is helping launch a new coalition aimed at reaching Christian and other religious voters.

The initiative, called “Faithful Texans for Talarico,” comes as Talarico faces Republican Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton in a closely watched Senate race in which both candidates are making faith a central part of their campaigns.

In a video shared on social media, Ross, who has also appeared in Taylor Sheridan’s mini-series “1883,” explained why he believes Talarico represents Christian values.

“I grew up in Texas, surrounded by faith my entire life, so that’s exactly why I’m proud to be supporting James Talarico, and launching Faithful Texans for Talarico,” Ross said.

“You know, a lot of politicians like to talk about Christian values, but you don’t really see that reflected in their legislation or the way that they live,” Ross said. “But you do see that in the way that James lives.”

“The Chosen” actor continued, “He loves his neighbor, he takes care of the needy, the sick, the immigrant, and he shows that in his legislation by bringing Democrats and Republicans together to take on corporate special interests and lower costs for his neighbors.”

Ross concluded by encouraging Texans to join the coalition and help spread Talarico’s message ahead of the Nov. 3 election.

“Our political system isn’t working for Texans. It’s time for a change,” Ross told his social media followers.

The 37-year-old Talarico is a former middle school English teacher who serves in the Texas House of Representatives. He is representing the Democratic Party in this year’s general election for Texas’ U.S. Senate seat against Republican candidate Ken Paxton.

Talarico is pursuing a Master of Divinity degree from Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary. He already holds a Master of Arts in theological studies from the school.
