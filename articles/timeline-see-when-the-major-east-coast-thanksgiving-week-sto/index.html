Timeline: See when the major East Coast Thanksgiving week storm will impact your area

“This storm could be one of the worst Thanksgiving Day storms for the I-95 corridor in terms of rain and wind impacts," the FOX Forecast Center said.

Strong East Coast storm to blast millions traveling for Thanksgiving with heavy rain, damaging winds

A powerful storm is developing off the Florida Atlantic Coast that will move up and soak the Eastern Seaboard through the week creating slick road conditions, as well as potential airport delays for millions traveling ahead of Thanksgiving. The storm is forecast to reach the heavily populated Northeast corridor on Thanksgiving, slamming the region with heavy rain and coastal winds of up to 60 mph.  

A powerful storm developing off Florida’s Atlantic coast will bring yet another flash flood threat to the Sunshine State before intensifying as it tracks north, threatening travel disruptions and power outages across the coastal Carolinas and Mid-Atlantic on Wednesday before slamming the Northeast on Thanksgiving Day itself.

East Coast storm overview

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

This system also has the potential to snarl road and air travel through the holiday week, delivering heavy rain, damaging winds, and coastal flooding. "This storm could be one of the worst Thanksgiving Day storms for the I-95 corridor in terms of rain and wind impacts," the FOX Forecast Center said.

Wednesday AM — Flood-weary Florida faces more rain and flash flooding  

The developing storm system is already lashing parts of Florida's East Coast, with some places along the coastline still dealing with historic flooding lingering from last week's storms that dropped more than 2 feet of rain in some locations. Rain could exacerbate flooding that remains along the St. John's River along Florida's Central East Coast.

Heavy rain is expected to last through Wednesday, with periods of downpours targeting South Florida, where the greatest rain totals in the state are currently expected.

Florida Flood Alerts

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

A Flood Watch extends from Titusville to Miami through Wednesday.

Additionally, increasing onshore winds could produce days of rough surf, dangerous rip currents and minor coastal flooding that could last through Thanksgiving Day.

Into early Wednesday, heavier rain will likely be moving through portions of Louisiana, Mississippi and Alabama and could cause travel delays. There is also a Level 1 out of 4 flash flood risk for costal Mississippi and Alabama, which includes Mobile, AL into Wednesday.

Worst airport travel times

(FOX Weather)

Wednesday PM through Thanksgiving Day — Flood threat shifts northward to the Coastal Carolinas, Mid-Atlantic

Potential airport delays: MIA, FLL, MCO, JAX, CHS, SAV, ATL and CLT

Interstates impacted: I-95, I-75, I-26 and I-20

The storm is expected to strengthen off the Southeast coast Wednesday afternoon, spreading more rain from Florida into Georgia and the Carolinas.

A Level 2 out of 4 flash flood risk stretches from Charleston, South Carolina, to the area just south of Kill Devil Hills, along North Carolina's Outer Banks Wednesday into Thanksgiving morning.

Onshore wind gusts could reach up to 50 mph and are expected to exacerbate King Tide flooding.

Places like Charleston Harbor could reach major coastal flood stage on Wednesday or Thursday.

Coastal flood threat

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

Thanksgiving Morning — Record flooding possible across Delaware Bay and southern New Jersey

The system will track up the Mid-Atlantic coast into early Thanksgiving morning. Even if the storm's center comes ashore, the worst of the impacts will remain along the coast.

Record coastal flooding is possible in Delaware Bay, as well as South Jersey, which may see the highest water levels since Superstorm Sandy during Thursday morning's high-tide cycle.

Northeast Thanksgiving storm setup

(FOX Weather)

Thanksgiving Day — Heavy rain soaks the Northeast; powerful wind gusts threaten power outages along the coast

Potential airport delays: DCA, EWR, LGA, JFK, PHL and BOS

Interstates impacted: I-95, I-80, I-87, I-89, I-90 and I-91

A solid shield of heavy rain is expected to spread from Washington, D.C. into the New York City metro area by early to midday on Thanksgiving. Heavy rain will then spread over the entire Northeast by later in the afternoon and into New England. 

High Wind Advisories

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

Wind gusts could reach 70 mph along the coast with gusts between 40 and 60 mph in inland areas. High Wind Watches are now in place across Maryland, New Jersey, eastern Pennsylvania and New York's Long Island, as well as New York City and Philadelphia.

This wind will increase the threat of widespread power outages across portions of the Mid-Atlantic and southeastern New England. 

Power Outage Forecast

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

A Level 1 flash flood risk covers 60 million people from Virginia through New England on Thursday. A widespread 1–2 inches of rain is expected, with localized totals of 2–3 inches possible along the coast.

This storm could be one of the worst Thanksgiving Day storms for the I-95 corridor in terms of rain and wind impacts.

Thanksgiving travel planner

(FOX Weather / FOX Weather)

Black Friday — Storm lingers across parts of New England, high elevation snow possible 

By Friday, the storm is expected to continue tracking north toward New England and the Canadian Maritimes. This could keep periods of rain and gusty winds going across portions of the Northeast, particularly eastern Massachusetts, coastal New Hampshire and Maine.

Depending on the storm's track and how quickly colder air arrives, some precipitation could change to snow across the higher elevations of interior New England.

Conditions should gradually improve from south to north as the storm moves away.
