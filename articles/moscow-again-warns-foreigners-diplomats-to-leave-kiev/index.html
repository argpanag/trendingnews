The Russian Foreign Ministry on Saturday repeated its warning to foreign visitors and diplomats to leave the Ukrainian capital Kiev or, failing that, avoid areas near military facilities and government buildings.

The ministry said Russia's armed forces were continuing "systematic targeted strikes against military targets in Kiev and other Ukrainian cities" in response to what it described as "terrorist actions by the Ukrainian armed forces against the civilian population and civilian infrastructure of our country (Russia)."

Moscow had warned all foreign nationals against visiting or staying in Kiev in May.

While some countries had warned their citizens against travelling to Ukraine, Russia noted "that not everyone has heeded these warnings and is knowingly exposing their lives to mortal danger." Responsibility for "possible negative consequences" lies entirely with those who ignore this warning, it said.

Russian forces have been launching large-scale attacks on the Ukrainian capital using drones and various types of missiles.

The strikes have killed and injured numerous people in Kiev and caused extensive damage across the city.
