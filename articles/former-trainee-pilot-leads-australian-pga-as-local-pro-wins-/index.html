Former trainee pilot leads Australian PGA as local pro wins BMW with hole-in-one

A decade ago, before quitting to focus on his golf career, Nicolai von Dellingshausen was on track to become a pilot with Lufthansa. Though he didn’t get his wings, the unheralded German is still flying high after carding an eight-under par second round 64 to go with his opening 67 to lead by three shots at the halfway mark of the Australian PGA Championship.

The same couldn’t be said of Australian star Lucas Herbert, whose hopes of a maiden Australian PGA crown quickly nosedived on Friday afternoon after the 30-year-old, who finished tied-sixth in this year’s Open Championship at Royal Birkdale, had horror round of 77 to miss the cut.

The news was better for compatriot and fellow former LIV defector Cameron Smith, who rocketed into contention by carding 64 to move into outright third at nine-under. Tournament favourite Adam Scott battled to get going in the blustery afternoon conditions, but managed to make the cut despite some struggles after the turn to be two-under.

And there was jubilation for Camden Golf Club pro Robbie Minns, who wowed the crowd late on Friday with a hole-in-one on the 18th to win a BMW X5 worth more than $150,000.

“I’m still in shock,” Minns – whose official world ranking is 5002 –said. “I hit a decent shot, it was a little bit heavy ... I’ve looked down, and I see everyone going off – and it’s gone in the hole.”

Von Dellingshausen’s round was headlined by a spectacular albatross on the par-four 14th at The Lakes Golf Course in Sydney, as the 33-year-old went to the clubhouse as the soaraway outright leader at 13-under.

The world No.333 leads rising Northern Irishman Tom McKibbin, who carded a seven-under to sit three shots back at -10. The 23-year-old is mentored by Rory McIlroy, having played at the same Holywood Gold Club, and will join the double Masters champion in Melbourne for the Australian Open next week at Kingston Heath.

Sitting one place further back in third is Smith. The 33-year-old has not won in three years but roared back to form on Friday and will go into the weekend firmly in contention to claim his fourth Australian PGA championship.

With storms expected on Saturday afternoon, the third round will start an hour earlier and Smith will tee off alongside the overseas pair in a three-ball.

Smith was one of two Australians to finish inside the top 10 after 36 holes, with Cam Davis sitting tied-eighth after carding a one-under to head into the weekend at six-under.

Round-one leader and world No.560 Haydn Barron failed to back up his stellar 64 on Thursday, but is still in the mix alongside fellow Aussie Travis Smyth, who carded a four-under. Championship drawcard Min Woo Lee endured an up-and-down day to finish at three-under after 36 holes.

While Smith set the pace early, von Dellingshausen stole the show and made use of the friendlier morning conditions.

The German started his round with a birdie on the first and was four-under through 13, before stunning the gallery by holing out with his second shot on the par-five 14th. Von Dellingshausen threatened the hole with his tee shot on the next, the par-three 15th, before settling for a tap-in birdie.

“Honestly, if that went in, I would have gone home, rebook the flight. Thanks, I’m done,” he quipped.

Von Dellingshausen only decided to make a proper fist of turning pro after a restructure at Lufthansa convinced him to pursue his other great love.

“I applied for the flight training and went through everything … but at that time the whole Lufthansa group was restructured, so pilots that were already in flight training couldn’t get a job,” he said.

“That means there was a queue of a couple 100 pilots that were not employed, and I was way behind them. I could have gone to flight school, but I never did and decided it was now or never with my golf.”
