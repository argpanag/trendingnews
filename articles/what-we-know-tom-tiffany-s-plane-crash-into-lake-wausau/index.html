What we know: Tom Tiffany's plane crash into Lake Wausau

A plane carrying Republican candidate for governor Tom Tiffany lost power and crashed into Lake Wausau Saturday night, injuring him and his pilot and causing them to swim to safety.

Tiffany said the plane, described as a four-seat Bonanza, was returning home from the La Crosse County Lincoln Day Dinner.

"After the crash, we called 911," Tiffany said in a statement. "As the plane began to submerge, we exited the aircraft and swam to a shallow area, where we waited until emergency responders reached us by boat."

The Marathon County Sheriff's Office said the initial 911 call came in at 8:49 p.m. Saturday. The sheriff's office identified the pilot as 73-year-old Leonard Boltz from Pearson.

In a new statement Sunday morning, Tiffany's campaign said he received stitches above his right eye at Aspirus Wausau Hospital overnight and was released. The pilot also received stitches and was released.

What we know

  • Republican candidate for governor Tom Tiffany’s plane lost power and crashed into Lake Wausau on Saturday night.
  • Tiffany said he and the pilot called 911, swam to a shallow area and waited for rescue by boat.
  • Tiffany received stitches above his right eye at Aspirus Wausau Hospital and was released, along with the pilot.
  • The sheriff's office identified the pilot as 73-year-old Leonard Boltz from Pearson.
  • Democratic candidate for governor David Crowley said he was grateful Tiffany and the pilot were not seriously hurt and wished them a quick recovery.
  • The plane is being removed from the lake Monday.
  • The FAA and NTSB are investigating why the plane lost power.

The rescue

WISN 12 spoke with Wausau Fire Battalion Chief Kraig Kruzan on Sunday. He described the rescue.

He was asked about the upgrades to boats that fire stations have done over the past few years.

"The boat that we use for a river system is very important," Kruzan said. "You have to be able to manage very shallow water, as well as very deep water. Having a shallow-running boat is very important to our success. Even crossing from the boat landing, we had to cross water that was as little as a foot deep. Standard tiller boats might run aground and get you stuck. Having the proper equipment is key to the success of a situation like this."

When he realized the crew had rescued a sitting member of Congress who was running for governor, he said, "That reaction didn't come until I watched them both safely walk onto shore from the boat, and I was just pleased that regardless of who they are. I'm glad they were safe. And for the most part had very minor injuries. So I think everybody on our crew was very relieved to find that the injuries were so minor, they were able to walk under their own power. Things could have been a lot worse."

Full statement

Tom Tiffany issued the following statement after the crash.

"While returning home from the La Crosse County Lincoln Day Dinner, the plane I was traveling in lost power as we approached the Wausau Downtown Airport. The pilot made an emergency water landing in Lake Wausau.

"After landing, we called 911. As the plane began to submerge, we exited the aircraft and swam to a shallow area, where we waited until emergency responders reached us by boat.

"I want to express my deepest gratitude to the pilot, the Wausau Fire Department, and all of the first responders who came to our aid so quickly. They got us safely off the water and transported us to Aspirus Wausau Hospital, where we received excellent care. Thankfully, both the pilot and I sustained only minor injuries.

"God was watching over us tonight, and I am incredibly grateful to be here in the hospital with my wife, Chris, by my side. An experience like this reminds you just how precious life is."

Emotional thanks

In a news conference Sunday afternoon, Tiffany thanked everyone who reached out with prayers and kind words, and said he was especially grateful to the pilot, the Wausau Fire Department, law enforcement, first responders and the team at Aspirus Wausau Hospital for their care and quick response.

"(I'm) just grateful for the Lord for sparing us another day," Tiffany said.

Plane removal

Officials removed the plane from Lake Wausau Monday. It was partially submerged since the crash happened and people were asked to avoid the area.

The investigation

Both the Federal Aviation Administration and National Transportation Safety Board are investigating why the plane lost power.

The NTSB said it is still waiting for the FAA to do its assessment of the plane to see if the crash meets criteria for an NTSB investigation. The agency said it typically investigates plane accidents where there is substantial damage or serious injury.

WISN 12 has put in a request for body camera footage, images and the 911 call.

Tiffany this week

While the U.S. House returns to Washington Monday, Tiffany will remain at home for at least the early part of the week after advice from doctors. Tiffany said he will continue campaigning for governor and anticipates flying again during the campaign.

Gov. Tony Evers responds

When WISN 12 spoke with Wisconsin Gov. Tony Evers at an event on Monday, he said he hadn't personally reached out to Tiffany, but that he was aware of the situation. He noted that he wanted to give Tiffany time to get stitches and rest.

"He seems good and I'm certainly glad that he came out of it alive and well," Evers said.

Crowley responds to the incident

Democratic candidate for governor David Crowley spoke after hearing about the crash.

"I’m grateful to God that Congressman Tiffany and no one else was hurt in this horrifying incident, which could have been a tragedy if not for the heroism of the pilot. I wish them and the Congressman a quick recovery and that they can find solace with their loved ones," he said.

Crowley leads Tiffany among likely voters 49% to 44% ahead of the November election in the first Marquette Law School poll unveiled on Aug. 26. But, when widened to include all registered voters, Crowley and Tiffany are tied at 44%.
