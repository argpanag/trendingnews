Ferrari benches Hamilton for Las Vegas FP1 as Beganovic gets fourth F1 outing | GRANDE PRÊMIO

TheTools · 17.11.2026 12:30 · source · permalink

Lewis Hamilton will hand his Ferrari SF-26 to Dino Beganovic for the opening practice session of the Las Vegas Grand Prix, completing the Italian team's mandatory rookie appearances for the 2026 Formula 1 season.

News Summary

  • Lewis Hamilton will sit out FP1 at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, with Ferrari academy driver Dino Beganovic taking his place.
  • Beganovic will make his fourth practice appearance of 2026, having already driven Ferrari’s SF-26 in Spain, Austria and São Paulo.
  • Ferrari will complete its mandatory rookie sessions in Las Vegas, while Haas will also field Ryo Hirakawa in place of Oliver Bearman.

Lewis Hamilton will miss the opening practice session of the Las Vegas Grand Prix after Ferrari confirmed that Dino Beganovic will take over his SF-26 on Thursday, November 19. The Swedish driver will make his fourth FP1 appearance of the 2026 Formula 1 season, completing the Italian team’s mandatory rookie-running programme.

Beganovic, a member of the Ferrari Driver Academy since 2020, previously replaced Hamilton at the Spanish Grand Prix and Charles Leclerc in Austria and São Paulo. His latest outing will take place at the Las Vegas Strip Circuit, where teams face unusual challenges because of low temperatures, limited grip and the track’s long straights.

The decision means Hamilton will have only FP2 and FP3 to prepare for qualifying, scheduled for Friday night in Las Vegas. The seven-time world champion will return to the car for the second practice session.

Beganovic completes Ferrari’s rookie programme in Las Vegas

Formula 1 regulations require every team to allocate at least two FP1 sessions per car to rookie drivers during the season, producing four mandatory appearances across a two-car operation.

Eligible drivers must have competed in no more than two Formula 1 Grands Prix.

Ferrari has already completed three of its four required sessions with Beganovic, who drove Hamilton’s car in Barcelona before replacing Leclerc in Austria and Brazil.

The 22-year-old finished eighth during his Barcelona appearance and ninth in Austria, where he completed 26 laps.

His most recent outing came at Interlagos, when Ferrari was among the teams using rookie drivers during the opening session of the São Paulo Grand Prix.

As GRANDE PRÊMIO reported ahead of that session, Beganovic was one of six replacement drivers confirmed for FP1 in Brazil.

The Las Vegas appearance will complete Ferrari’s obligations under the regulations, leaving the team free to use Hamilton and Leclerc throughout the remaining practice sessions of the championship.

Las Vegas presents different challenge for Ferrari rookie

Beganovic’s fourth appearance comes at one of the most unusual circuits on the Formula 1 calendar.

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit combines long straights, heavy braking zones and a street surface that changes significantly as rubber accumulates throughout the weekend.

The first practice session is particularly challenging because of the limited grip available before the circuit has been extensively used. Low temperatures during the night-time programme can also make tyre preparation more difficult.

For Beganovic, the session will provide further experience with Ferrari’s 2026 machinery as he prepares for an expanded role with the team.

The Swedish driver is competing in Formula 2 with DAMS and currently occupies fifth place in the championship, with 137 points, two victories and seven podium finishes.

Ferrari has also confirmed that Beganovic will take on a full-time reserve and development role from 2027.

The appointment follows his involvement in the team’s simulator programme and his growing experience during official Formula 1 practice sessions.

Speaking before his first appearance in the SF-26 at Barcelona earlier this season, Beganovic explained the importance of combining technical work with the experience of driving a Formula 1 car.

“My expectations for this weekend and for FP1 are a mix of technical focus and emotion,” he said.

“Clearly, this is still a very new car for me because of the regulation changes and everything that comes with them, so the priority is to get up to speed as quickly as possible.”

He also outlined the objectives of his work with Ferrari.

“My aim is to do the job the team requires of me, provide useful feedback, and adapt to the car as much as possible.”

Hamilton loses practice time during close F1 championship fight

Hamilton’s absence from FP1 comes as the Ferrari driver fights for third place in the Drivers’ Championship.

The seven-time world champion enters Las Vegas just one point behind George Russell, following a series of results that significantly reduced the gap between the former Mercedes teammates.

As GRANDE PRÊMIO reported in its analysis of their recent performances, Hamilton outscored Russell by 36 points across five Grands Prix.

However, Ferrari struggled during the São Paulo Grand Prix, where Hamilton finished ninth after encountering difficulties with tyre temperatures and car handling.

Team principal Frédéric Vasseur subsequently acknowledged that incorrect tyre temperatures could cost the team approximately half a second per lap.

The problem could be particularly relevant in Las Vegas, where the cool night-time conditions make tyre warm-up an important part of car preparation.

Hamilton will return for FP2, giving him two practice sessions before qualifying.

Haas also replaces Bearman as Câmara prepares for second F1 race

Ferrari will not be the only team making a change to its driver line-up during the opening session in Las Vegas.

Haas has confirmed that Ryo Hirakawa will replace Oliver Bearman in FP1, while Brazilian Rafael Câmara remains in the other car for his second Formula 1 Grand Prix.

As GRANDE PRÊMIO reported in its coverage of the Haas announcement, the Japanese driver will take part in the opening session as the American team completes its own rookie-running programme.

Câmara, meanwhile, will continue racing for Haas after making his Formula 1 debut at São Paulo.

The Brazilian’s promotion followed Esteban Ocon’s departure from the team, changing the plans previously considered for Ferrari’s young-driver programme.

Beganovic will therefore complete Ferrari’s remaining practice obligation in Las Vegas, while Câmara continues his racing programme with Haas.

The Las Vegas Grand Prix takes place from November 19–21, followed by Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

GRANDE PRÊMIO is following every session of the Las Vegas Grand Prix and the final rounds of the 2026 Formula 1 season.
