FBI Arrests Georgia Man Accused of Funneling $26K to ISIS-Linked Groups

Georgia man faces federal charges after allegedly sending more than $26,000 to ISIS-linked individuals and groups, with FBI agents discovering damaged cellphones and shredded documents submerged in liquid during a search of his apartment.

A Georgia man is facing federal charges after allegedly sending more than $26,000 to individuals and groups affiliated with ISIS, the Department of Justice (DOJ) announced.

Younes Moustafa Younes, 34, of Lavonia, Georgia, was charged with one count of international money laundering and one count of destruction of evidence, according to the DOJ.

Younes was arrested Nov. 18 and made his initial appearance Thursday in federal court.

Federal prosecutors allege Younes altered and destroyed evidence leading up to his arrest. During a search of his apartment, FBI agents discovered three cellphones submerged in an unknown solution, including one that had been smashed and another with its identification number removed, according to the criminal complaint.

Investigators also found shredded documents submerged in an unknown solution inside a bathtub.

According to the complaint, Younes sent approximately $16,807 through cryptocurrency transactions between December 2025 and June 2026, using multiple phone numbers and accounts allegedly intended to conceal his identity.

Investigators say at least some of the funds were intended to support ISIS operations in Syria.

Prosecutors also allege that Younes sent approximately $9,206 through 11 cryptocurrency transactions between December 2021 and April 2022, using an alias to conceal his involvement.

The FBI first identified Younes in 2020 after he allegedly accessed ISIS propaganda online, according to the complaint.

During an interview with law enforcement, Younes acknowledged viewing the material but denied supporting extremist ideologies.

Prosecutors allege he later became increasingly radicalized, expressing support for ISIS and other terrorist figures through encrypted communications.

If convicted, Younes faces up to 20 years in federal prison on each charge, according to the Justice Department.

The international money laundering charge also carries a potential fine of up to $500,000, while the destruction of evidence charge carries a potential fine of up to $250,000.
