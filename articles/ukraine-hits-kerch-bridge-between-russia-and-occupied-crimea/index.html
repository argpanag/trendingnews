Ukraine hits Kerch bridge between Russia and occupied Crimea

TheTools · 16.11.2026 12:45 · source · permalink

Traffic temporarily restricted on key connection after bridge targeted during overnight drone barrage by Ukraine

Ukraine has hit the Kerch bridge connecting occupied Crimea with Russia, in a symbolic blow that temporarily paralysed road traffic and left thousands of drivers stranded.

A Ukrainian kamikaze drone crashed into one of the bridge’s arches at 5.38am local time, local channels reported. Earlier drones could be heard buzzing above the structure, as Russian air defence units opened fire.

The extent of the damage was unclear. One report said the arch was listing at a 15-degree angle and “looked tired”.

The Kremlin-appointed governor of Crimea, Sergey Aksyonov, confirmed the “enemy” had launched an attack. “As a result, traffic will be restricted, likely until the afternoon,” he wrote on Telegram.

Russian officials said the crossing would remain closed while experts assessed its condition. About 2,000 vehicles were reportedly stuck in traffic jams on both sides.

In a call with reporters, the Kremlin spokesperson, Dmitry Peskov, downplayed the incident. He said: “Minor damage is being assessed. ⁠Traffic across the bridge continues.”

Ukraine says the bridge is a legitimate target for its armed forces, and has made at least two previous attempts to blow it up.

In 2022 a truck laden with explosives damaged part of the road section. A second strike using a “sea baby” drone took place the following year. Russia has subsequently strengthened air defences and installed metal barges and boom barriers.

Overnight Ukraine launched 1,336 drones, in what the Kremlin says was one of the biggest attacks so far this year. About 600 were intercepted on their approach to Moscow, claimed the capital’s mayor, Sergei Sobyanin.

The barrage took place days before a summit in Shenzhen between China’s president, Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Putin. US officials say the talks will not focus on the war in Ukraine, where Russia’s military offensive has largely stalled.

Ukraine says its retaliatory strikes are justified retribution for Russia’s own missile and drone barrages of its cities and energy grid – attacks that have killed hundreds of civilians and regularly knock out power and heating for millions. In recent weeks Russia has repeatedly hit two critical bridges in Kyiv over the Dnipro river.

Diplomatic efforts on ending the almost five-year war have so far gone nowhere. After a fresh round of talks in Abu Dhabi last week, Putin reiterated his hardline territorial demands and said any deployment of foreign troops would be seen in Moscow as a “continuation of war”.

The Ukrainian president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, said on Friday the Trump administration had proposed that Kyiv gives Russia the eastern Donbas region – including towns and villages Russia does not control – in exchange for Nato membership or Nato-like guarantees.

Zelenskyy ruled out handing over territory. The details of possible US security guarantees were vague and did not feature American troops on the ground, he said, citing conversations with Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner and the real-estate developer Steve Witkoff. The emissaries last week saw Putin in Moscow.
