Bonnie Greer: British-American author and critic dies at 77

TheTools · 16.09.2026 16:31 · source · permalink

British-American author and cultural commentator Bonnie Greer has died at the age of 77 after a brief illness, her husband has said.

In a statement delivered with "profound sadness", Greer's widower David Hutchins noted how the acclaimed playwright and critic had made "an enduring contribution to British cultural and public life" with her "intelligence, courage and distinctive voice".

He added: "To me, she was my partner, confidante and inspiration, and I feel profoundly privileged to have shared my life with her."

Hutchins continued: "Those closest to Bonnie will remember her warmth, humour, generosity and extraordinary ability to bring people together."

'Courage, humour and humanity'

BBC presenter Clive Myrie led the tributes from elsewhere, posting on X: "This is such a shock. She was a friend and mentor and I will miss her with all my heart."

Former deputy prime minister David Lammy called Greer "a powerful wordsmith and artist, she was eloquent and exacting".

He added that she was "a true life force" and he would "never forget her quiet fearlessness taking on Nick Griffin" during an appearance with the then-British National Party (BNP) leader on the BBC's Question Time in 2009.

Lammy added: "I will miss her mentorship, and her friendship."

London Mayor Sadiq Khan said Greer's voice brought "courage, humour and humanity to Britain's cultural conversation".

He said: "She inspired and opened doors for so many others. And she was great fun too - I will miss her friendship enormously."

Journalist and broadcaster Sangita Myska, who interviewed Greer several times, recalled her as being a "smart, sassy, firebrand who will be missed from our public discourse".

Plays, novels and Question Time

Born in Chicago in 1948, Greer wrote plays from a young age and went on to study theatre in her home city and before attending the Actors Studio in New York with director Elia Kazan.

Greer moved to the UK in 1986 and became a familiar face on UK TV, appearing as a frequent panellist to debate the topics of the day on the BBC's Question Time and Newsnight Review.

She penned award-winning works like Munda Negra, which won her the Verity Bargate Award for best play, and novels such as Hanging by Her Teeth (1994) and Entropy (2009).

Once named by the Observer as one of the country's leading public intellectuals, Greer's work - across a range of disciplines and platforms - often tackled issues around culture, race and public institutions.

Speaking to the Guardian in 2016 in the aftermath of the Black Lives Matter movement, Greer said: "When I wake up in the morning, I'm just myself. It's only when I leave the door that I become a black woman to the world."

She added: "I tell black women all the time, 'Don't apologise for yourself and keep your hair nappy. Make sure you are heard'."

Greer served on the boards of cultural institutions including the British Museum, Royal Opera House and London Film School, and was chancellor of London's Kingston University.

Her other published works include a memoir titled A Parallel Life and a biography of US poet and playwright Langston Hughes.

Also in 2009, her part-memoir, part-music-history book, Obama Music, was published.

Greer was made an OBE in 2010 for services to the arts.

In 2022, she was appointed as a fellow of the Royal Society of Literature.
