Matty Nicholson Extends

TheTools · 26.11.2026 02:07 · source · permalink

WRITTEN BY

ON

Matty Nicholson has extended his time at the Canberra Raiders through 2030. It’s a strong endorsement of Nicholson’s potential by the club, and puts them in a good position in their backrow despite losing Hudson Young. The only question now is whether Nicholson can live up to expectations.

Nicholson surprised in his rookie season, providing a strong runner on the right edge, with surprisingly decent hands. We all remember Kaeo Weekes’ try against the Storm at Magic Round started with his deft offload, right? Since then, he’s confirmed that he’s willing. To hit a hole. To make a tackle. To do what it takes.

What has held him back are two interrelated factors. First and most obvious is his injuries. He’s suffered horrible injuries in back to back seasons. These have held him back not only from building his experience and capabilities in the NRL, but also in the physical development needed to succeed.

Early in his rookie year, Coach Stuart noted how much better he’d be with a couple of pre-seasons under his belt. I don’t think he envisaged both of them being spent rebuilding a body after major injury during the season. Unfortunately Nicholson hasn’t had the privilege of that again this offseason.

That leads into the second factor, which is his lateral mobility. It’s hard to improve that while you’re fixing an ankle (and since, two injuries to his shoulder). Even though 2026 was a reinjury of a shoulder issue, it still meant less time to build his speed, and his agility, in order to keep pace with the menacing halves and fullbacks across the league that want to test his outside shoulder.

With a bit of health, the Raiders should get a better idea as to how Nicholson fits into the side. If Nicholson can stay healthy, and can improve his lateral agility, then he’ll prove a useful weapon. Those hard lines he runs will mean the Raiders now have two backrowers attracting the eyes of edge defenders, and should mean that Kaeo Weekes and Ethan Strange don’t have to head towards the corner post in order to find space.

But even if he never develops the fancy feet to be elite, he’ll always have value to the Raiders because of his workrate and his ability (and willingness) to play in the middle. The Raiders offseason recruitment has been characterised by a desire to inject a bit of mobility and pace into their middle rotations. In the past that had been in the form of Zac Hosking, but more minutes for Joe Roddy, the signatures of Jaiyden Hunt and Lipoi Hopoi, and now Matty Nicholson, are all part of an attempt to insert players who can play across the entire backrow into the game-day mix. It’s a season too late to adjust to Vlando’s shenanigans, but better late than never.

A final note – many will see this as a knee-jerk response to losing Hudson Young, or indicative that the flirtation with Liam Martin will come to nothing. But we first outlined this deal being done in September, before Hudson was leaving, and before Liam Martin was even an idea for the Milk. I don’t know why these things take so long to be official. I’m sure there are reasons.

It doesn’t dispel the fact that Canberra were already all-in on Matty Nicholson. This deal was going to be done whether Hudson stayed or not, and likely won’t affect whether Martin comes, because the money was allocated to Nicholson months ago. As we’ve said, Nicholson will be fine in the middle if Martin does come, and he even provides insurance if Father Time catches the Penrith backrower in a back-alley at some point.

This is a good deal for the Raiders. It’s a big bet. Canberra have made plenty of those lately. It’s good to see them swinging. In this case they’re backing Nicholson’s hard-edged work-rate, their own talent identification, and the idea that his health problems will just be a memory in a few years’ time.

The Canberra Raiders have high expectations, and that the peaks we’ve seen so far will be more constant in the future. Now it’s time for Matty Nicholson to prove them right.
