Ballon d’Or winner Harry Kane returns to action today in Der Klassiker, which is a 5.30pm (GMT) kick-off at the Allianz Arena. His record against Borussia Dortmund is, well, quite good – six goals in six Bundesliga games. But you’d expect nothing less, to be honest.

“Kane was a top player in England already,” Dortmund manager Niko Kovac said this week, “but to make another step up at his age that’s sensational and shows the danger and the quality of a player like that. We won’t get this done one on one, we’ll need to do it together.”

Kane was given a guard of honour by his teammates before Wednesday’s DFB Pokal game against Magdeburg, which he sat out on the bench.

Kane’s compatriot Aaron Danks will take charge of Bayern Munich for the second weekend in a row with Vincent Kompany still absent. For those of you in the UK, today’s Milan derby is being shown free-to-air on BBC Two. How about that?

Inter and Milan this week unveiled plans for their new stadium that will replace San Siro from the 2031-32 season, in time for Italy to co-host Euro 2032.

The stadium, sponsored by Generali – Italy’s largest insurance company – will have a capacity of 71,500 seats and is expected to cost the two clubs €1.2bn (£1bn). The plans were revealed at a gala event at Milan’s Castello Sforzesco on Thursday – but some fans in attendance voiced their opposition to San Siro being demolished.

Shortly before the briefing began, a group of Inter fans walked into the courtyard of the castle and unfurled a banner reading: “You will never extinguish the lights at San Siro,” and chanting: “Hands off San Siro.” Work on the new stadium is set to begin in 2028, on the site of the existing 75,000-capacity venue.

Suspicious bets for predicting Ballon d'Or under investigation

A series of suspicious bets that correctly predicted the exact top-five finishing order of this year’s Ballon d’Or and generated almost $200,000 (£149,000) in winnings is being investigated by the event’s organisers, France Football, who have also contacted the police.

It has been alleged that 12 Polymarket accounts – an American cryptocurrency-based prediction market – placed the bets, with more than $264,000 wagered on the outcome before Monday’s ceremony. Their extraordinary accuracy has raised serious questions about a potential leak of confidential results before football’s most prestigious individual award.

Harry Kane was crowned the winner at the ceremony at the London Palladium, finishing ahead of Kylian Mbappé, Lamine Yamal, Rodri and Lionel Messi. Remarkably, this was the exact order predicted by the group of accounts identified in French media outlet Upside’s investigation.

Aston Villa to take legal action against Carragher for 'unacceptable insults'

Aston Villa have announced plans to take legal action against Jamie Carragher following his “unacceptable insults” about the club and its supporters.

Carragher told Villa fans to “shut the f*** up” after facing criticism for suggesting Unai Emery should consider taking the Manchester United job. Villa responded on Friday evening with a statement on X signed by their chair, Nassef Sawiris.

“Aston Villa will initiate legal action against Jamie Carragher for his unacceptable insults about the club and its fans,” the statement said. “Aston Villa will not allow anyone to stop our ascension in English and European football. Nassef Sawiris, chairman.”
