Inside The Diplomat’s Shocking Windsor Castle Episodes

TheTools · 15.10.2026 14:00 · source · permalink

The fourth season kicks off with two episodes set entirely on the grounds of Windsor Castle. Here, creator Debora Cahn gives T&C the full scoop—and invites King Charles to make a cameo on the series.

Spoilers ahead for the first two episodes of The Diplomat season four.

At the end of the season four premiere of The Diplomat, the Americans are preparing to leave Windsor Castle after an unexpected stopover when their convoy is diverted due to protests. After President Grace Penn (Allison Janney) says her goodbyes to the staff and the King’s Guard and walks towards her car, she is shot.

The guards—played by background artists who were a combination of reservists and former soldiers—spring into action. “Those were real soldiers,” Keri Russell tells T&C. Rufus Sewell adds, “there’s something so cool about seeing them in action—I’ve never seen that. It did make a difference [with] them all being real, trained soldiers. Because when shit gets real, you can really tell.”

At the start of episode two, Vice President Hal Wyler (Sewell) and his wife, U.S. Ambassador to the UK Kate Wyler (Russell), are rushed back inside the Castle as Penn is medevaced to the USS Ford for emergency surgery and Hal becomes acting president. While the attempted assassination storyline wraps up relatively quickly, it has a lingering impact on Penn’s decisions and the Wylers’ evolving relationship with her.

Later in the season, the President makes a major decision that is explicitly illegal, and expects her administration to go along with her. “I wanted to start with the question of what happens if you get an order that you don’t like and you think is illegal?” creator Debora Cahn tells T&C about the impetus for this season. “It’s a question that’s been in the press a lot and it creates a lot of big feelings, as they would say in the kindergarten room, among different people.”

She continued, “I wanted to try, in an honest way, look at what is it like to be inside moments like that and how do you know when you’re in one? When are you overreacting and when are you under-reacting? I wanted to see a lot of different people who we respect have wildly different opinions about what they’re looking at, and to give us something happens that we can sort of look at as an excuse for the president’s behavior. You can kind of explain it all because [the assassination attempt] happened, but what if it isn’t that? What if it’s just, ‘okay, this is who this person is when president.’ Was that always there? Is anything really different? Seeing a lot of people wrestle with that, and never really reach the same conclusion twice, felt compelling.”

Here, Cahn reflects on beginning the season at Windsor Castle—including Hal and Kate’s vow renewal at St. George’s Chapel—and why her plans for The Diplomat have changed dramatically since the series premiere.

Season four picks up right where season three left off, no gap at all. Tell me about this storytelling decision, which has been consistent throughout the series.

I always want to skip ahead! I want to skip ahead a year, I want to skip ahead five years! I’m desperate to do it. We always talk about it and then we don’t do it because I feel like anything that we come up with that is worthy of a finale is something really intense and you want to see where those chips are going to fall. You don’t want to not see them dropping; you don’t want to hear about two years down the road how people look back at that really dramatic moment in their life. You want to be in that moment. We always wind up like a second leader.

How soon did you know that Windsor Castle was going to serve as the backdrop for the attempted assassination at the start of the season?

Sometimes I write these things in scripts as a joke just to see, “Well, production department, what are you going to do with this?” And “exterior Windsor Castle” was one of those. It felt a little bit like a cheat that we had never encountered a royal environment having been in the UK for that long; it just isn’t realistic that diplomats don’t wind up there in heads of state.

It felt exciting to find a way to try and do that, that also wasn’t The Crown—they did it, they were amazing at it. I don’t want to try and swim in those waters, but to find a way to give us just a little bit of a taste of what is it like for our scrappy bunch of warriors to be knocking around Windsor Castle. We ended up filming that on two different continents, nine different locations. It took a lot to [create] that world. It’s hard to get a good castle these days.

How do you get a good castle? I assume you didn’t film at Windsor...

We did not. We didn’t want to suggest that something like that could happen at the real Windsor. With Windsor, the bar is not to replicate, but it’s to imitate the vibe of it enough that you feel like you’re in a similar reality. We do a certain amount of filming in the U.S. and we’ll film things that are in the UK here when we can find them. We did that with Chequers last season, which was a beautiful estate in Long Island in Oyster Bay. But it turns out the U.S. just doesn’t build rooms that big. You just can’t find the scale, which is just unimaginable. All the money that anybody’s ever had in the United States, they’re still just not thinking that big. Whereas you go to the UK and you turn a corner and you’re tripping over another just monumental edifice to wealth and power.

Can you share where you filmed?

Hal’s office is in Belvoir Castle, which we also used the exterior for that. The room outside that room, the other offices that members of our delegation are in are in Long Island [Woolworth Manor as the equerries room, Sleepy Hollow Country Club as the office they give Grace Penn to use, and the ladies restroom, and St. Josaphat’s Monastery as the royal library]. And they’re in Long Island, but they’re also not in the same location in Long Island. The church [that stands in for St. George’s Chapel] is Cathedral of the Incarnation in Long Island. Long Island, turns out, has a lot to offer.

Other filming locations for Windsor Castle:
Burghley House as exterior Windsor, inner courtyard
Lancaster House as interior Windsor hallways and grand staircase
The British Foreign Office as the small reception room
The Church of the Intercession as the panic room and the courtyard corridor

Rufus Sewell and Keri Russell told me that the soldiers had real military backgrounds. What was that like?

Getting to know the King’s Guard, and having a sense of what they’re doing in those hats, and what is it actually like to be inside that red coat... To discover that [while] we only see them standing stock still, in fact, they are trained to do a lot of really fast work. Talking [with them] about if something were to happen, what are the commands? What do people say? What do you scream? What do you whatever? And the first answer was nobody screams—you just talk fairly quietly. And at a certain point we were like, “Well, can you yell a little? It’s a TV show. Can you boost the volume just a little bit?” But the level of professionalism and precision is breathtaking.

The assassination plot is the first time we really see Hal and Kate grapple with what being one person away from the presidency means.

The thing that I knew was going to be there going into it, but didn’t realize how prevalent it would be, is they’re returning to the roles they were in, the dynamic, the power relationship, the status relationship that they were in when they met and fell in love. It’s certainly a very uncomfortable moment: Something really terrible has happened, but in a way they’re both kind of like exhaling into a pair of pants they’ve been in before and a pair of shoes they know how to walk in. There’s something comforting for both of them about going back to that and being able to kind of rely on the leaning on each other that they do—the double act that they are when he’s got a big forward-facing job and she is contributing to shaping his thinking behind the scenes.

Why was their vow renewal at St. George’s Chapel a key scene at the very start of this season?

Recently, some friends of mine got married and they’d already been together for 18 years. And watching them—oh my God, I’m actually getting emotional—watching them say vows to each other, knowing what they’re getting into seemed so, in a way, so much more powerful than me and my husband [who married when we’d] known each other for 14 months, starry-eyed and blushing and giddy. We knew what we were agreeing to, but we didn’t really know what we were agreeing to. And seeing two people say, “Okay, we’ve been through a lot. There’s been good and there’s been ugly, and we are committing to this,” was something that I wanted to see [Hal and Kate] go through.

Alex Graves, our director, is brilliant. Initially, I was like, “Okay, so they walk down the aisle and they stand up front and turn and face each other.” And he was like, “What if they sit over on the side?” And I was like, “Oh, that’s so much better. So much better.”

When you began writing The Diplomat, what was your plan and have you deviated or stuck to it?

Oh my god, I’ve completely deviated. First of all, there was no Grace Penn. I mean, Grace Penn is mentioned in [season one,] episode one, and Grace Penn was sort of out there as a popcorn kernel that maybe would pop someday, but this was never the plan. Then Allison Janney walks in—and you better find a story that is worthy of her. But every year, I try to go into each season with a goal and a place that I think I want to get to. And my question for the writer's room is always: We’re steering toward this, but how can we beat it? Is there something better out there? Is there something more exciting and dynamic?

I don’t like biting off too much story. I don’t like telling it in a way that the audience can’t really feel [it] organically unfolding. So I would rather dump story [than] barrel through it in a way that requires some character to come in and say, “Well, given the fact that there was an explosion on the boat, now we can assume...” It feels like crappy storytelling to me. I would rather slow it all down and find some different place to end a season than push through [to a planned] end in a way that feels crappy.

I know you talked a bit about not having the royals really be there, but you sort of hint at them, the King being away at Balmoral. Do you ever want to bring a member of the royal family into the world of The Diplomat?

I always want our world to exist as though it could be running parallel to the real one, but I don’t want to do crossovers. I don’t want to do any live cameos, although I’ve got to be [honest]: If King Charles would like to be on The Diplomat, I am absolutely prepared. But short of that, I think it would be distracting and it would be about something else.

Perhaps they watch The Diplomat...

King Charles, the door is open.
