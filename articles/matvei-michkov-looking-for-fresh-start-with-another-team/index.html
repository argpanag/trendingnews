Matvei Michkov Looking For Fresh Start With Another Team

TheTools · 10.11.2026 22:13 · source · permalink

Philadelphia Flyers forward Matvei Michkov is reportedly seeking a fresh start with another team, according to multiple league sources relayed by ESPN’s Kevin Weekes. Weekes pointed to the Flyers’ challenging start and internal conflicts as the reasons behind the decision.

Of all the trade requests this season, this one is the least surprising. Michkov and Philadelphia’s relationship has deteriorated since the Flyers hired Rick Tocchet as head coach. That’s not to say the blame is squarely on Tocchet’s shoulders, but it is the one event almost everyone can point to as when things began to sour.

That relationship began almost four years ago when Philadelphia selected Michkov with the seventh overall pick of the 2023 NHL Draft. Some viewed Michkov as the second-most talented draft-eligible player behind Connor Bedard, but his contract with the KHL’s SKA St. Petersburg was a burden other teams weren’t willing to broach. St. Petersburg signed Michkov to a five-year extension that would have kept him in the KHL through the 2025-26 campaign.

Surprisingly, Michkov negotiated a release from his KHL contract and joined the Flyers for the 2024-25 campaign. He quickly showed his offensive prowess, scoring 26 goals and 63 points in 80 games with a -18 rating, averaging 16:41 of ice time per game. His offensive production was enough to finish fourth in Calder Trophy voting. Still, there were concerns.

Michkov played very individual games, and his 82 giveaways drew the ire of many. He also managed an 85.7% on-ice SV% at even strength. It is common for wingers, particularly younger ones, to have weaknesses in their defensive skills. However, Michkov had so many glaringly obvious defensive weaknesses that, surprisingly, he thrived in John Tortorella’s environment as well as he did.

Then the Flyers hired Tocchet. It began immediately, as Tocchet remarked on how Michkov came into training camp out of shape. Tocchet expressed concern about Michkov’s conditioning and commitment, claiming he would avoid competing in practice.

After several conversations that played out in the media, Michkov took accountability for his actions, and the team was willing to move forward with him. Still, Tocchet cut his ice time, and his production fell in lockstep. Michkov finished his sophomore campaign with 20 goals and 51 points in 81 games with a net-zero rating, averaging 14:50 of ice time per game.

This year, Michkov came into training camp and preseason much better prepared. After reaching the postseason last year, Philadelphia hoped to build on last season’s success. They collapsed out of the gate.

The Flyers went 0-6-3 in their first nine contests, quickly falling to last in the Eastern Conference. Despite having decent offensive talent on paper, Philadelphia managed only 11 goals in those games. Their success has mildly improved since then, with the Flyers going 5-4-2, averaging nearly 3.1 goals per game.

Although he still struggles with puck possession and defensive responsibility, Michkov has contributed to the team’s success. The former top-10 pick has 10 goals in 20 games this season with an exceptional 34.5% shooting percentage. Still, despite leading the team in goal-scoring, Michkov ranks sixth in ice time among forwards. Tocchet remains uncomfortable giving him meaningful minutes.

Last night, in their win over the Vancouver Canucks, things really boiled over. During the contest, video footage captured Michkov verbally berating defenseman Nick Seeler (video link). Michkov got so heated that he threw his water bottle and had to be held back by teammates Noel Acciari and Porter Martone.

Clearly, Michkov’s frustrations have reached a crescendo, and he no longer wants to continue his career with the team that drafted him. He’s in the final season of his three-year, entry-level contract, is projected to become a restricted free agent in the upcoming offseason, and is eligible for an offer sheet.

Given his offensive skill set, his age, and contract status, there will be multiple teams interested in Michkov if the Flyers make him available. Still, interested clubs will want to do their homework, as Michkov has displayed multiple warning signs early in his career that he may not gel well in the locker room, despite his perceived ceiling.
