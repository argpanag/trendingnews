US officials defend plan to destroy unstable explosives in Iowa after Trump pause

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:02 · source · permalink

The US Army is set to detonate the country's entire stockpile of lead azide on Thursday despite health warnings.

2 min read

Published 25 November 2026 6:56 am

Source: AFP

In brief

  • Residents near a small Iowa town have been urged to evacuate before the planned destruction of unstable explosives.
  • The operation has raised concerns about potential lead contamination and public safety.

The United States Army has urged residents of a small Iowa town and nearby areas to evacuate ahead of the planned detonation of some 4,500kg of unstable and degraded explosive material.

The plan — which has sparked fears of lead contamination — is to destroy the country's entire stockpile of lead azide, a material previously used to trigger other explosives in munitions but which officials say is now at risk of spontaneous detonation. The detonation is set for Thursday.

The US Federal Emergency Management Agency is "supporting the recommended relocation for all residents of Middletown, Iowa, and immediate surrounding impacted areas" out of "an abundance of caution", the army said in a public safety advisory. Middletown has a population of around 360.

The destruction of the lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant had been scheduled for 20 November.

But it was postponed by three days, after which US President Donald Trump ordered a pause in the detonation, which is now scheduled to proceed.

"There is consensus across the experts across the country that a controlled, mitigated detonation of the lead azide ... is far preferable to an uncontrolled, spontaneous detonation," acting army secretary Adam Telle told a news conference on Wednesday.

On Tuesday, Trump ordered a pause on the planned detonation, saying it would proceed only if an appeals court signed off and if Iowa's incoming governor, Democrat Rob Sand, agreed to let it go ahead.

The court cleared the way for the destruction of the explosives in a ruling on Tuesday, while Sand said the "administration should make this decision based on the information they have from the people they brought in to do the job months ago".

The army released two maps as part of the public safety advisory, showing potential exposure to toxic lead dust on surfaces and in the air in areas extending south-west of the planned blast site.

"Consider avoiding the highlighted area or remain indoors with windows/doors closed," the maps said, warning that "children under six are especially vulnerable to lead exposure."
