Emma Raducanu falls out of world’s top 100

TheTools · 16.10.2026 06:52 · source · permalink

Briton slips from No 24 in March to No 107 after stress fracture and ranking rules provide incentive to stay on sidelines

This week’s new rankings chart shows that four months of inactivity have sent Emma Raducanu tumbling out of the world’s top 100 players for the first time in more than two years.

Raducanu’s season has been another story of promise and disappointment, as her eye-catching run to the final of Queen’s in June suggested a woman coming into form at exactly the right moment to make a strong run at Wimbledon.

But it turned out that Raducanu’s five matches at Queen’s – which included three outings in 28 hours across a rain-disrupted weekend – had aggravated a pre-existing shin problem, which she suffered during a late and ill-fated attempt to play the tail-end of the clay-court season.

Having been seen in an orthopaedic boot just five days before Wimbledon, Raducanu then pulled out at 10pm on the eve of the tournament, explaining that “the niggle I’ve been managing has developed into a stress fracture and I’ve been medically advised to stop pushing through”.

With no rankings points to log since the 325 she collected at Queen’s, Raducanu has slipped quickly down the ladder. From No 31 after that tournament – and No 24 at Indian Wells in early March – she has dropped to the less exalted position of No 107. This makes her the British No 3, behind Katie Boulter (No 54) and Fran Jones (No 104).

Even though Raducanu is understood to be back training at the National Tennis Centre in south-west London this week – the first sighting for some time – there is little prospect of her returning to the match court this season.

Rankings incentive to stay on sidelines

Apart from the ever-present concerns surrounding her fragile physique, Women’s Tennis Association (WTA) rules offer her an incentive to remain on the sidelines until January, because any athlete who misses at least six months through injury is entitled to claim a so-called “protected ranking”.

The WTA regulations say that you can effectively freeze your ranking at the point at which you suffered the injury – which, in Raducanu’s case, would probably mean at No 31 in the world – and then use that number to enter up to eight tournaments in a season, including a maximum of two grand slam events.

Without a protected ranking, however, Raducanu could find herself struggling to gain direct entry to the main draw of January’s Australian Open and needing to go through the qualifying event – something she has not done at any tournament since her miraculous victory at the 2021 US Open.

Raducanu’s management team has declined to respond to inquiries about her plans. But it is no wonder, in the circumstances, that her name appears to be absent from the seven remaining WTA Tour entry lists for the 2026 season.

There are only four more weeks of regular events in any case, including the ongoing 1000-point tournament in Wuhan, before the eight leading players of the year go to Indian Wells for the WTA Finals on November 8.

We know very little about what Raducanu has been doing since she announced her stress fracture on June 28, although she did post photographs from a couple of holidays on her Instagram page, including one that took her to the exclusive Suvretta House resort in St Moritz and another to Puglia near the southern tip of Italy.

As on-court training resumes, she is understood to still be working with her coach Andrew Richardson, the old ally who guided her to the US Open trophy, and whose record with her remains outstanding after her strong performance at Queen’s in June.

In the short term, Australia is sure to be Raducanu’s primary target. Her record at Melbourne’s Australian Open is indifferent, with four of her five appearances having ended in the second round. In 2025, she went one better, scratching out nuggety wins over Ekaterina Alexandrova and Amanda Anisimova – two quality players – before being annihilated by Iga Swiatek in the third round.

Assuming that Raducanu regains full fitness in time for 2027’s Australian Open, she should be able to gain entry to the event via her likely protected ranking. However, you cannot claim a seeding through a protected ranking, so she would be vulnerable to a potential first-round meeting with any of the world’s best players.

Raducanu’s old schoolgirl rival Sonay Kartal has also been using a protected ranking after her own six-month absence with a spinal stress fracture, but Kartal has looked in decent form since her return at the recent Billie Jean King Cup finals in Shenzhen.

On Monday morning in Wuhan, Kartal defeated world No 48 Anhelina Kalinina in straight sets to make it three wins from four tour matches on the Asian swing to date.

Later, in Shanghai, an off-colour Carlos Alcaraz made an unexpected exit as he lost 7-5, 7-6 to Zizou Bergs, the world No 39 from Belgium.
