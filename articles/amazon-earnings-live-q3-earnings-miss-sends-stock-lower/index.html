Amazon earnings live: Q3 earnings miss sends stock lower

Amazon (AMZN) reported third quarter results Thursday, missing on the top and bottom lines, sending the stock falling.

Amazon says Q4 net sales will come in between $240 billion and $247 billion versus expectations of $244.5 billion

For the quarter, Amazon saw earnings per share (EPS) of $1.89 on revenue of $201.9 billion. Analysts projected EPS of $1.97 on revenue of $202.25 billion, according to Bloomberg's analyst consensus estimates. The company reported EPS of $1.95 and revenue of $180.16 billion in the same period last year.

Amazon stock fell more than 9% on the news.

AWS revenue came in at $45.9 billion versus expectations of $45.68 billion, while online store sales hit $68.47 billion. Wall Street was anticipating $69.6 billion. Amazon's advertising business saw sales of $19.6 billion.

  • Jassy says in two to three years, six to eight AI models will be about as good as each other

    "I think there are going to be six to eight models that are about as good as each other, or at least good enough for 95 plus percent of the workloads," Jassy said during Amazon's earnings call.

    "Most customers are going to use multiple models, and many will use multiple models in the same generative AI application. What customers really want to do, and we see this validated over and over again when we talk to customers, is they want a service that has really broad selection of those frontier models, and that has very good price performance, and it has very strong security and access control, and they want that service so that they can kind of decide what combination of models they want to use."

  • 'Workloads are moving': Amazon's Jassy says AWS still growing amid cloud move

  • Amazon has more than a million robots in its fulfillment network

    According to CEO Andy Jassy, Amazon has more than a million robots in its fulfillment network.

    That, he says, has had an impact on costs, productivity, and safety.

    "We're still so early with respect to both what we have built and deployed, and what we're working on. And I think that you'll see we will always have a lot of teammates in our fulfillment centers, but I think a lot of the jobs that we have that are more repetitive that we have humans doing will be done by robots."

  • More AWS capacity is coming across the next two years

    Jassy says that Amazon is adding "quite a bit" of capacity to AWS.

    "If I look at how much we've added this year and what we have planned over the next, I'll call it eight quarters or so, which is about as far out as we get eight to 10 quarters, we have a lot of capacity coming as well. "

  • Prime Video is profitable on its own

    Jassy says Amazon's Prime Video has become a significant business with a revenue run rate in the tens of billions of dollars and that it's now profitable "in its own right."

  • Amazon's Alexa shopping agent has been used by 400 million customers

    CEO Andy Jassy says that more than 400 million Amazon customers have used the company's Alexa for shopping in the last 12 months.

    Customer interactions, he added, were up a whopping 570% year over year.

    "US customers using Alexa for shopping are spending 50% more per order than those who don't," he said.

  • Jassy: AWS is still growing as more companies continue to migrate to the cloud

    "It's important to remember that the vast majority of global IT spend, about 85%, is still on-premises, and we're in a 10-year cycle where that equation will flip to the cloud," Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said.

    "Conventional wisdom has been that linchpin workloads like mainframes or storage array networks would never move to the cloud, but these workloads are moving as AWS has built unique cloud substitutes and AI-driven migration capabilities to make this transition much easier. And enterprises get the benefits of less capex, lower operating costs, faster innovation, better developer productivity, and data accessibility to more easily leverage AI," he added.

  • Prime Day shifted revenue from Q3 to Q2

    While Amazon's net sales increased by 12%, the company noted that they would have been higher if Prime Day had taken place in Q3, as it did last year, rather than Q2.

    "As a reminder, Prime Day timing shifted this year, with the event occurring in Q2 for most large countries. In 2025, Prime Day occurred entirely in Q3. Excluding this shift, third quarter year-over-year growth would have been approximately 400 basis points higher," the company said in its earnings report.

  • Amazon's Bedrock is seeing adoption across large companies.

    CEO Andy Jassy says Fortune 500 companies spent nearly 7x more year over year on its Bedrock inferencing service and that total Bedrock token volume grew nearly 17x.

  • Dan Ives reacts to Amazon results

    In a quick reaction note to investors, Yorkville Ives senior managing director Dan Ives said the question for Amazon going forward is how long it can keep up AWS growth.

    "AWS exited the quarter at a $184 billion annualized revenue run rate, and Jassy projected AWS revenue growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028.

    For Amazon the debate is how long AWS can run as capacity and Trainium come online, and a 39% print with management projecting more than 40% growth in both 2027 and 2028 is a stronger answer than the quarter alone."

  • Q4 revenue outlook also misses midpoint

    In addition to misses on the top and bottom line for the third quarter, Amazon's outlook for Q4 sales came in between $240 billion and $247 billion, with the midpoint at $243.5 billion.

    Analysts were looking for a midpoint of $244.5 billion

  • Amazon's Q4 operating income outlook falls short

    Amazon says it anticipates Q4 operating income of between $28.5 billion and $33.5 billion, with the midpoint falling at $31 billion. That's lower than the $32.99 billion analysts were looking for.

    The company, however, has issued operating income forecasts below consensus estimates for the last eight quarters.

  • Amazon CEO Andy Jassy says AWS investments are paying off

    "Our AWS investment in functionality and infrastructure is working, with over 39% year-over-year revenue growth in Q3 ($184 billion revenue run rate) and projected revenue growth rates of over 40% both in 2027 and in 2028," Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said in a statement.

    "Our broader selection and speedier delivery are helping us capture more retail market segment share with improving margins, and our newer businesses, ranging from Grocery to Pharmacy to Amazon Leo to Zoox, are progressing encouragingly. There has never been a trillion-dollar revenue business, and we may have two of them in the next 5-10 years."

  • Google results a warning sign to investors: Analyst

  • BofA keeps Buy on Amazon with $320 target ahead of earnings

    Bank of America expects AWS, Amazon's cloud unit, to drive upside in third-quarter results from new capacity, price increases, AI lab contracts, and growing enterprise demand.

    "Ultimately, we continue to believe the biggest drivers of the stock are expectations around the AI opportunity and returns on capex," the company wrote in a note to clients. "The key risk is high expectations (possibly at 42%+), but results should suggest strong ROI on capacity capex."

    Risks include rising capital spending, 4Q cost pressure from higher gas prices, and soft Q3 ad revenue, which could fuel concerns about AI shopping agents.

    The company maintains its Buy rating on Amazon with a $320 target ahead of earnings.

  • AWS operating margins in focus

    UBS analyst Stephen Ju writes in an investor note that he'll be focusing on AWS's operating margins, which he pegs at 46% for 2027 versus street estimates of 39% as revenue continues to ramp.

    Oppenheimer's Jason Helfstein concurred in a note, saying that chip efficiency and revenue growth should lead to better-than-anticipated margins going forward.

  • AWS is well positioned to capitalize on AI demand: Analyst

    "We continue to believe AWS is well positioned to capitalize on AI demand, from workforces modernizing tasks and emerging consumer applications (e.g., AI assistants)," KeyBanc Capital Markets' Justin Patterson wrote in a note to investors.

    "As agentic workloads are more CPU-intensive, AWS's Graviton chip offers both price and performance advantages and is attracting customers like Meta. Further, we believe services like Bedrock make it easy for enterprises to switch between models, providing AWS the ability to benefit from open-weight and closed-weight models," he added.
