TCL Tablet and Phone Deals for Prime Day

TheTools · 08.10.2026 00:59 · source · permalink

You can still get TCL's paper-like Nxtpaper tablets and phones at some of their lowest prices. You'll need to hurry, as Prime Day is nearly over.

Writer

Writer

October 7, 2026, 8:59 pm ET

Save up to $142: Prime Day is in its final hours, so if you’re looking to save on some TCL gear, now’s the time. Nxtpaper tablets and phones are discounted right now at Amazon, for up to $142 off. Real talk: Some of these markdowns are the lowest prices we’ve seen on these devices. If that wasn’t enough, TCL is tossing in a free stylus and flip case with every Nxtpaper tablet, adding more value to this solid deal. If you’re not looking to spend money on big-ticket items like TVs and laptops, a new TCL tablet or phone may be the right move.

TCL Prime Day deals

Save up to 38%

Nxtpaper is TCL’s matte display tech. It isn’t E Ink, like you find in Kindle and other e-readers. Rather, TCL adds an anti-glare layer over a regular LCD screen so you get something that looks and feels more like paper. You can switch between a standard tablet mode and paper-like color and black-and-white reading modes, so you basically get an e-reader and a regular tablet in one device.

My favorite deal of the bunch is the Nxtpaper 11 Plus for $228, down 38% from its usual $370. You could have gotten it for $220 in August, so this isn’t its lowest price, but you’re still pretty close. You get an 11.5-inch, 120Hz display, a pressure-sensitive stylus and an 8,000-mAh battery. You won’t get the fastest tablet out there, and you’ll find the 8-megapixel cameras rather basic, but you probably aren’t buying this one for photos.

More from CNET

If you want a bigger screen without spending much more, you can get the Tab A1 Plus Nxtpaper for $260, down 35% from $400. That’s its lowest price ever, and you’re even paying less than you would have during TCL’s back-to-school sale, when it dropped to $300. You get a 12.2-inch, 2.4K display with a 120Hz refresh rate, so the overall experience should be pretty smooth. I think this is the best pick for you if you want one tablet for watching shows at night and reading during the day.

If you want the biggest screen TCL makes, you can get the Nxtpaper 14 for $330, down from $470. You might remember it dropping to $300 in December, so this isn’t its lowest price, but I’d still call $330 a solid deal for a 14.3-inch tablet. TCL says it’s great for reading sheet music, and you can also use it as a second monitor for your PC in extend mode, which is what makes me want one the most.

If you’d rather have the paper-like screen in a smaller form factor, you can get the TCL 60 XE Nxtpaper 5G with 256GB of storage for $190, down 33% from $285. That’s the lowest price it’s ever been. The phone impressed CNET expert Tyler Graham, who said in his review, based on his testing, you should get through a full day on one charge even with frequent use. TCL also has its flagship Nxtpaper 70 Pro on sale, starting at $260 for the 128GB model (down from $330).

TCL has a couple of other solid deals, too. If you’re looking for something closer to a Remarkable, you can get the Note A1 Nxtpaper e-note for $450, which is $150 off. And if you’re shopping for gifts, you can get TCL’s CrystalClip Swarovski Edition earbuds for $89, down from $190. None of these deals needs a Prime Membership, so you’re good to shop these without spending anything extra.

If you’re looking to spend and save more during this October Prime Day, check out our roundups of the best Prime Day laptop deals and budget picks you can get under $50.

Alternatives to consider

If you mostly read and take handwritten notes, you might prefer a true E Ink tablet like the Boox Go 10.3 Gen II, which you can get for $420, though it’s not currently discounted. You won’t get a color display, but you’ll get an excellent E Ink screen and much longer battery life. If you prioritize speed, consider the Samsung Galaxy Tab S10 FE for $385. It comes with an S Pen, and you’ll find far more accessories for it than for TCL’s tablets.
