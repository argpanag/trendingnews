Casper officials give sixth cent project updates, fifth cent data at Wednesday press conference

TheTools · 30.10.2026 00:51 · source · permalink

CASPER, Wyo. — Casper officials gave progress updates on past municipal tax initiatives while detailing spending plans for proposed revenue from Natrona County’s countywide fifth-cent sales tax ahead of the upcoming election at a Wednesday press conference.

If voters choose to renew the fifth-cent tax on Nov. 3 for another four-year term running through 2030, Casper is projected to receive $80.8 million. Mayor Ray Pacheco said the tax has funded critical municipal needs for decades, including supporting 97% of Casper’s street repair work since 2022 and equipping first responders with new equipment.

The mayor said that, should the tax not be approved at the ballot box on Nov. 3, core operational services like police patrols would continue but funding for critical capital purchases — such as new fire trucks, police vehicles and emergency management software — would become difficult to sustain. Pacheco also emphasized the broader economic fallout, pointing to municipal assets like the Ford Wyoming Center and saying that facilities maintained with fifth-cent dollars drive local economic stability by generating vital sales tax revenue for Casper’s restaurants, hotels and shops.

“General purpose fifth cent [funding] has been crucial in purchasing equipment, police officer cars, snow plows and vehicles used to maintain streets, as well as our fire trucks… and equipment used to provide public safety services,” Pacheco said.

Based on a community survey, Casper officials previously established broad funding priorities for future revenues, led by street repairs, stormwater drainage, public safety, parks and recreation, facilities, cybersecurity and technology and community transit services.

During the meeting, Pacheco also detailed status updates for each of the four projects funded by the SPEC tax revenue. Approved by Casper voters in 2024, the SPEC tax was a temporary 1% municipal sales tax enacted specifically to fund four voter-approved capital projects. Under state law, specific purpose taxes expire after two years or as soon as the approved funding goal is raised. Casper’s tax reached its $18.2 million collection goal in 15 months, allowing the local sales tax rate to return to 5% on July 1.

Construction on the new Metro Animal Services facility, which received $7.3 million in SPEC funding, is set to break ground Nov. 5. Architectural design for replacing Fire Station No. 1 is approximately 90% complete, with the overall $23 million construction project expected to go out for competitive bidding in spring or early summer 2027 and take 18 months to build. The city allocated $4.4 million in SPEC tax revenue to fund the initial design phase and plans to pledge local tax funds as a match for a $2 million state mineral royalty grant. Additionally, new radio and telephone equipment contracts have been locked in for the regional 911 dispatch center using $5 million in tax funds, while renovation drawings for the competitive bidding on the Washington Park Bandshell renovation project are planned for February 2027.
