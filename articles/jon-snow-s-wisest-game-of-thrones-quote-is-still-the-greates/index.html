Jon Snow's Wisest Game of Thrones Quote Is Still the Greatest in Fantasy History

TheTools · 06.11.2026 12:30 · source · permalink

Jon Snow may not be as clever as Tyrion Lannister or as strategic as Robb Stark, but he may have an acute wisdom that many of the other characters in A Song of Ice and Fire lack. George R.R. Martin's fantasy world of Westeros is littered with impressive individuals, but perhaps none so much as the bastard of Winterfell, who overcomes his controversial birth to become one of the most fabled heroes in history.

Popularized even further by Kit Harington's portrayal in HBO's Game of Thrones, Jon Snow is a fan favorite among fantasy enthusiasts. However, he is best known for his nobility and strong fighting skills. Even so, the young boy on the cusp of manhood proves time and time again that he possesses a wisdom beyond his years. One line from A Song of Ice and Fire proves as much.

Jon Snow's Wisest Game of Thrones Quote Still Goes Hard Today

Jon Snow's wisest quote is also one of his earliest memorable moments in the Song of Ice and Fire series. At the beginning of A Game of Thrones, King Robert Baratheon rides to Winterfell with his royal retinue with one difficult request of its lord. The king asks that his old war buddy, Lord Eddard Stark, join him at King's Landing to become his new Hand. Ned reluctantly agrees, knowing he cannot refuse the king, and brings his two daughters, Sansa and Arya, with him to become ladies of the court.

At this very same time, Ned's bastard son, Jon, elects to leave Winterfell as well, riding with his uncle Benjen to the North, where he will become one of the Watchers on the Wall. The last three remaining Stark children, Robb, Bran, and Rickon, remain in their ancestral castle with their mother, Catelyn. In but a single moment, the Stark family fractures in three directions, with none of them sure when they will ever see the others again.

It is in this parting of ways that Arya expresses her concern to her brother, Jon: "I wish you were coming with us." In a moment of brotherly comfort, Jon merely reassures his sister that "different roads sometimes lead to the same castle." With that, the three different Stark factions head off on their separate roads, which will take them each on adventures they could have never anticipated.

At this point in the story, Jon is only 14 years old, barely a teenager. Going off into the unknown, he was just as scared as Arya was when leaving Winterfell. But seeing the concern in his sister's face, Jon didn't let his own fears overtake him. Instead, he offered words of encouragement, voicing his hope that they would see each other again someday, even if it wasn't for a very long time.

These words became even more painful for readers as the events of A Song of Ice and Fire ​​​​​​play out. Indeed, the Starks suffered through many hardships on their road back to one another. Some never see each other again--but others do.

Does Jon Snow's Promise Come True?

Does Jon ever get to keep his promise to Arya that they will be reunited someday? The answer is more complicated than one would expect. Shortly after the Starks went their separate ways, the Seven Kingdoms began to fall apart in civil war. The death of Robert Baratheon leaves his son, Joffrey, to take the throne. In a fit of rage, Joffrey has Ned executed for treason, prompting Winterfell and other kingdoms to rebel. At this, the Starks are all scattered to the winds, some of them never to see each other again.

Jon and Arya still haven't ever reunited in the books, though not for lack of effort. An entire storyline sees Jon send Mance Rayder to rescue his sister, only to discover that the target was an imposter commissioned by the Boltons. Arya, as it turns out, is off on her own adventure, which leads her away from Westeros and to the Faceless Men of Braavos.

The most recent book in A Song of Ice and Fire, A Dance with Dragons, ends with Jon Snow betrayed and murdered by his own men. As he lies dying, his final thoughts drift to Arya, coming to the conclusion that their diverging roads would never lead back to the same place. However, fans of the show know that Jon is resurrected shortly after his death, embarking on many additional adventures thereafter. If the books follow the same storyline, Jon may still have a chance to keep his promise.

In Game of Thrones, Jon and Arya finally do reunite in Season 8. Ironically, their separate roads lead them back to the same castle from which they started. After his many adventures beyond the Wall, Jon returns to Winterfell to prepare an army for the Night King's invasion. Arya returns home as well after learning that her siblings have retaken the castle from the Boltons. By this point, their siblings, Robb and Rickon, and parents had been murdered, leaving only Sansa, Jon, Bran, and Arya.

Arya and Jon's reunion remains one of the most emotional scenes in Game of Thrones. They meet in much the same way as they departed, inspecting one another's weapons and sharing a few good-natured jabs at one another. It's a reunion that fans of the books can't wait to see, provided that George R.R. Martin ever completes The Winds of Winter.

Why Doesn't Jon Snow's Best Line Make It Into the Show?

Although the show is the only medium in which Jon and Arya actually reunite, the line about different roads leading to the same castle only appears in the books. Game of Thrones takes a slightly different approach to the siblings' goodbye in the series premiere. As they prepare to leave Winterfell, Jon gives Arya a gift, her very own sword, and reminds her to "Stick 'em with the pointy end."

This becomes a recurring quote in Arya's story, and she later repeats these very same words to her brother when they reunite at Winterfell years later. While the series still manages to bring the necessary weight to the siblings' reunion, however, it would have been nice to have had a wise line like the one from the books to fall back on during the emotional scene.

Nevertheless, Game of Thrones manages to do what George R.R. Martin has yet to do: bring the story of the Starks to a close. While many fans have heated opinions about the way the series wrapped everything up in Season 8, the story at least has a definitive conclusion. The books, on the other hand, are in a state of perpetual stasis, as Martin finds himself endlessly pulled in other directions. The Winds of Winter is more than a decade overdue, and there is no end in sight for those waiting on its release.

Perhaps one day Jon Snow and Arya Stark will cross paths again. Maybe it will even be right where they started, as is the case in Game of Thrones. Or perhaps Jon really will stay dead in the books, putting a new, more tragic twist on his final words to his sister at Winterfell. Still, it would be nice to finally know.
