Jet ski seized, swimmers rescued as 30-degree heat hits Tasmania

TheTools · 22.10.2026 21:36 · source · permalink

Surf Life Saving Tasmania says there were “a number of near-drowning incidents” in southern Tasmania yesterday, as temperatures reached 30 degrees in parts of the state on the Royal Hobart Show public holiday.

On the same day, police seized a jet ski from a 22-year-old man following reports of riders behaving dangerously around Kingston Beach and Sandy Bay Beach.

Police said they seized the jet ski about 2pm “in relation to a number of alleged offences”, but did not specify what those offences were.

“The seizure follows reports of jet skis being operated in an unsafe and irresponsible manner in the area during a very warm public holiday,” police said.

“Most people do the right thing, but those who choose to operate irresponsibly need to understand that police can and will take enforcement action.”

“Popular beaches and waterways are shared spaces and operators need to be conscious of other people around them.”

Surf Life Saving Tasmania said large numbers of people headed to beaches, rivers and lakes ahead of the start of its patrol season on December 1, leaving popular swimming spots without lifeguards.

The organisation’s lifesaving services manager Ned Reardon said the near misses should serve as a warning.

“… Conditions can change quickly and people need to take responsibility for their own safety and the safety of those around them,” he said.

“Before entering the water, reassess your swimming abilities, understand your limitations and check the conditions.”

Reardon said emergency response teams were available year-round to support police, but strong swimming skills and safe decisions remained vital.

Tasmania recorded 10 drowning deaths last season, 25% above the 10-year average.

“Every one of these near misses could have ended very differently and our message is simple,” Reardon said.

“Enjoy our beaches, rivers and waterways, look out for each other and make sure everyone comes home safely.”

With similar weather expected today, Surf Life Saving Tasmania is urging people to check conditions before entering the water and use the Beachsafe app.

Police are asking anyone who witnesses dangerous behaviour on the water to report it on 131 444 or call Triple Zero (000) in an emergency.
