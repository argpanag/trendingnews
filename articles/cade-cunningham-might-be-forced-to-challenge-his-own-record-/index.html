Cade Cunningham might be forced to challenge his own record against the Spurs

TheTools · 18.11.2026 00:25 · source · permalink

The Pistons offense has been struggling against good teams in recent games, which could force Cade Cunningham into a tough position against the Spurs. Last season, in a similar position, Cade set the NBA record for most misses in a game with 31 against the Wizards in an overtime win. That performance proved that Cade has the right mentality to approach any challenge head-on and live with the consequences, and it'll be needed again in San Antonio.

Cade needs to carry the Pistons

Detroit has been without All-Star center Jalen Duren for several games, but got even more bad injury news ahead of the matchup with San Antonio. For this contest, the Pistons' second leading scorer this season Duncan Robinson will also be out. Even further depth pieces who can sometimes add offensive aggression in Isaiah Joe and Ron Holland II will be missing the game.

All of that leaves a lot of weight on Cade's shoulders. He'll be surrounded with a largely mismashed rotation of supporting pieces, but need to make it work one way or another. He will have defensive help from his teammates and strong coaching, but the offensive help will be severely limited.

That's been the case throughout the last couple weeks, but Robinson's absence and the Spurs' elite defense make the situation even more dire. With Victor Wembanyama protecting the paint for San Antonio, Cade might have to resort to desperate measures with lots of jumpshot attempts. His Pistons teammates generally score the best when attacking the rim, but that option could be neutralized by the reigning Defensive Player of the Year.

There is some possibility of help for Cade

Cade has shown he's not scared to miss a ton of shots if the game conditions require him to keep firing. If it comes down to that, he will likely be ready to shoot as much as needed against the Spurs. But the Pistons do have some contributors who could help avoid another historic outing.

Daniss Jenkins and Kevin Huerter have recently emerged as secondary ball-handlers and perimeter scorers who can also create their own shots. Both might end up in starting roles with other players injured. They could not only help keep Cade from needing to shoot so much, but also set him up for easier shots - and hopefully less misses as a result.

Ausar Thompson has also made strides as a creator and playmaker this season, although his shooting range is still severly limited. He can help Cade by creating openings for him, but is unlikely to have a big scoring performance of his own with Wembanyama in the paint.

Cade and the Pistons face a tall task in San Antonio. His efficiency and workload in this tough setting could speak volumes about the team's overall offensive system. Hopefully they're able to roll out something that keeps him from rewriting his own records.
