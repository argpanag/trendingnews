South Korea's 'Debt-Fueled Buying' Surges by ₩1.6 Trillion Amid Market Crash as Credit Line Balances Top ₩44 Trillion — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 18.10.2026 10:35 · source · permalink

As South Korea's stock market has plunged, demand for "debt-fueled investing"—borrowing money to invest—is flowing in rapidly as investors position for a rebound. Credit line balances at the country's five major commercial banks have grown by more than 1.63 trillion won (approximately $1.2 billion) this month, while unsecured personal loan balances, which had declined in September, surged by nearly 1.5 trillion won (approximately $1.1 billion) last week. Investor sentiment interpreting the stock decline as a buying opportunity is being directly reflected in the lending market.

However, cautionary voices are emerging, warning that leveraged investing during a period of extreme market volatility can simultaneously amplify losses and interest burdens. In fact, variable-rate mortgage rates at major banks are rising across the board, further increasing repayment pressure on borrowers who hold loans.

Credit Line Balances Top ₩44 Trillion... Up ₩1.5551 Trillion in Just Four Trading Days

According to financial industry data on the 18th, credit line balances at the five major commercial banks—KB Kookmin, Shinhan, Hana, Woori, and NH Nonghyup—stood at 44.6053 trillion won (approximately $33.5 billion) as of the 15th. That represents an increase of 1.6335 trillion won compared with the end of September (42.9718 trillion won, or approximately $32.3 billion).

The surge in balances was concentrated during the period when the stock market's decline intensified. The KOSPI fell 16.17% over four trading sessions, from a close of 6,625.93 on the 8th to 5,554.22 on the 15th. During that same period, credit line balances rose by 1.5551 trillion won (approximately $1.2 billion), from 43.0502 trillion won to 44.6053 trillion won. Notably, on the 14th, balances increased by 525.3 billion won (approximately $395.1 million) from the previous day to reach 44.4771 trillion won (approximately $33.5 billion), crossing the 44 trillion won threshold for the first time.

Total unsecured personal loan balances, which include credit lines, followed the same trajectory. As of the 15th, unsecured personal loan balances at the five major banks totaled 109.7122 trillion won (approximately $82.5 billion), up 1.4819 trillion won from the end of September (108.2303 trillion won, or approximately $81.4 billion). As of the 14th, when the KOSPI had been pushed down to the low 6,000 range, balances had risen by 1.451 trillion won (approximately $1.1 billion) compared with the 8th.

Banking industry analysts attribute the increase in unsecured lending primarily to bargain-hunting demand from investors expecting a rebound after the sharp market decline. "The recent increase in credit line balances appears to reflect bargain-hunting demand from investors who view the stock decline as an opportunity," said an official at a major commercial bank. "Historically, credit line balances have tended to rise when the stock market undergoes short-term corrections."

Interest Burden Grows Even as Market Rebounds... Variable Mortgage Rates Rise Across the Board

The KOSPI managed to rebound on the 16th, snapping an eight-session losing streak. According to the Korea Exchange, the KOSPI closed at 5,668.05, up 113.83 points (2.05%) from the previous session, while the KOSDAQ finished at 794.78, up 33.96 points (4.46%). However, given the preceding seven consecutive sessions of declines that wiped out 1,449.52 points (20.70%), the recovery was limited.

Starting the same day, variable-rate mortgage rates at major banks rose across the board. KB Kookmin Bank raised its six-month variable-rate mortgage rate, which is linked to the new-loan-based COFIX (Cost of Funds Index), from an annual range of 4.39–5.79% to 4.64–6.04%. Woori Bank also raised its rate on the same benchmark from 4.75–5.95% to 5.00–6.20%. Both banks implemented a 0.25 percentage point increase. NH Nonghyup Bank likewise raised its six-month variable-rate mortgage rate by 0.19 percentage points, from 4.49–6.29% to 4.68–6.48%.

The direct driver behind these increases is rising funding costs. The Korea Federation of Banks disclosed on the 15th that the September new-loan-based COFIX stood at 3.43%, up 0.25 percentage points from the previous month (3.18%). That marked the largest monthly increase in three years and ten months, since November 2022.

For a borrower with an outstanding loan balance of 400 million won (approximately $300,000), a 0.25 percentage point rate increase translates to an additional 1 million won (approximately $750) in annual interest—roughly 83,000 won (approximately $62) per month on a simple calculation basis. This assumes a constant loan balance; actual burden varies depending on repayment method and remaining principal. Nor do all existing variable-rate borrowers see their rates rise simultaneously—each loan reflects the benchmark rate for its product at its own rate-reset date.

According to Bank of Korea statistics, variable-rate loans accounted for 64.7% of new mortgage originations at deposit banks in August. Roughly two-thirds of newly issued mortgages are structured to be affected by future rate movements.

"Debt-Fueled Buying for a Rebound Requires Caution"

Private bankers are urging prudence when it comes to leveraged investing. Borrowing to invest in anticipation of a short-term rebound can compound investment losses with interest burdens if stock prices fall further.

"It's difficult to conclude that now is the time to buy the dip, given interest rate trends, winter energy price volatility, and policy variables around the U.S. midterm elections," said one private banking division head. "Rather than investing all at once, investors should consider dollar-cost averaging based on their investment horizon and financial capacity." The banker added, "In particular, investments using borrowed funds such as credit lines must account for both interest costs and the possibility of further declines."

Some analysts also point to the need to first assess uncertainties surrounding the market, including semiconductor industry conditions, foreign investor supply and demand, and geopolitical risks. Expectations regarding the future profitability of artificial intelligence companies are already priced into stock valuations. "With foreign investors moving to sell, investors should be wary of buying South Korean stocks simply because prices have fallen significantly," said a private banking specialist at a major commercial bank. "If an unexpected variable emerges over the weekend, the shock could be fully absorbed in Monday's session."

According to South Korea's Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service, household loans across the entire financial sector decreased by 2.2 trillion won (approximately $1.7 billion) in September from the previous month—the first decline this year. The drop was largely driven by a 5.5 trillion won (approximately $4.1 billion) decrease in other loans, including unsecured personal loans. Mortgages, by contrast, increased by 3.3 trillion won (approximately $2.5 billion). In effect, unsecured lending that had contracted through September has rapidly revived in October, triggered by the stock market crash.

"It's difficult to be optimistic about a near-term recovery in stock prices," said one wealth management expert. "If you've made plans to purchase a home or repay loans based on the assumption that stock prices will recover, you should recalculate the monthly payments you can afford based on your current income and cash on hand."
