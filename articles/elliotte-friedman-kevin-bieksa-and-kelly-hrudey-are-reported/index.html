Elliotte Friedman, Kevin Bieksa and Kelly Hrudey are reportedly joining a major NHL broadcast

TheTools · 03.09.2026 05:50 · source · permalink

Elliotte Friedman and Kevin Bieksa are entering the season without new deals at Sportsnet.

Kelly Hrudey is already gone, with Sportsnet confirming it won't renew his contract for the upcoming season.

Now two of the network's biggest names are sitting in the same uncertain spot, and the timing could not be worse for Sportsnet.

Because Amazon isn't just circling from the outside anymore. It actually has a seat at the table now.

Prime Video struck a 12-year deal with Rogers, landing exclusive Canadian rights to Wednesday Night Hockey plus select early playoff series.

That package starts this season and includes at least 26 national regular-season games, real inventory for a platform that used to have none.

So the question isn't just whether Friedman and Bieksa stay unsigned. It's whether Amazon uses this moment to go get them.

Why Friedman and Bieksa are not interchangeable pieces

Friedman's value isn't personality-driven the same way. It's built on relationships across the league and the insider reporting that comes with them.

Bieksa brings something completely different, a former player's voice with the kind of charisma that actually carries a broadcast.

Losing one changes the feel of Hockey Night in Canada. Losing both would be something closer to a full rebuild of the brand.

Nothing here is officially confirmed. Neither broadcaster has been reported as signed with Amazon, and Sportsnet hasn't announced departures either.

But reports and speculation have already put Amazon squarely into the conversation, and that alone says something about where this is heading.

There's also a viral claim that both men are furious over Hrudey's exit, with all three reportedly deep in talks with Amazon.

That specific claim hasn't been independently confirmed. The larger situation around their contracts, though, is very real.

Ron MacLean is still expected back at Sportsnet, and the network's broader lineup keeps evolving even as this uncertainty plays out.

Replacing two personalities this recognizable isn't like swapping analysts. It's replacing the familiarity a broadcast spends years building.

Amazon doesn't need to copy Hockey Night in Canada. If it lands even one of these two, it inherits credibility Sportsnet spent a decade earning.
