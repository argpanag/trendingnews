Qantas plane aborted Sydney landing to avoid collision with New Zealand carrier

A Qantas plane was forced to abort its landing at Sydney Airport to avoid a potential collision with two aircraft crossing the runway, prompting a federal investigation months after a string of near misses on the tarmac.

Pilots on QF426 from Melbourne were preparing to land on runway 16R on Wednesday morning while an Air New Zealand plane and another aircraft were on the tarmac.

It prompted a last-minute “go-around”, which involved the pilots continuing to fly past the airport towards Sydney Harbour as the other aircraft crossed the runway.

Air traffic control was yet to provide clearance to the Qantas pilots to land when they began the go-around. The flight landed safely at 10.25am about 10 minutes after the reported incident.

“Our pilots were following air traffic control instructions and elected to conduct a go-around as the other aircraft was crossing the runway,” a Qantas spokesperson said.

The Australian Transport Safety Bureau (ATSB) began investigating the close call on Thursday. That inquiry will include interviewing flight crew and air traffic controllers to examine any potential errors that may have led to the incident. Qantas, through its spokesperson, said it would support the ATSB investigation.

It comes less than two months after the ATSB released four preliminary reports related to six recent incidents at Sydney Airport.

These included a near-miss involving a Qantas Boeing 737 that was cleared to cross a runway in front of a Qantas Bombardier DHC-8. The incident was ranked as the most serious on an international scale used to grade runway incursions.

It was the third incident since Australian records began in 2003 assessed as a “Cat A runway incursion”.

Other incidents probed by the ATSB include a Jetstar A320 suddenly braking to avoid a Qatar Airways Boeing 777 on August 9, causing a flight attendant to fracture their wrist. The same day, a Qantas Boeing 737 was cleared to cross the runway in front of a Virgin 737 that had just been cleared for take-off.

Nine days later, a Qantas A321 had to brake to let a Qantas 737 pass in front of it. Strain on air traffic control staff was identified as an ongoing issue in the investigation of runway incidents at Sydney Airport, though the investigation didn’t determine whether waning staffing resources directly contributed to any error.

The ATSB expects to release a preliminary report on Wednesday’s close call by January.
