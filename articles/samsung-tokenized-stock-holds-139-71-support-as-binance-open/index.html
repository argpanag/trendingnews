The divergence between rising leveraged positioning and short-term selling pressure leaves the support level as the key line traders are watching.

The tokenized equity product, which tracks Samsung Electronics through a blockchain-based representation on Binance, has held the $139.71 level despite the latest one-hour taker flow skewing toward the sell side. Taker sell volume exceeded taker buy volume in the most recent hour, a signal that aggressive market participants were pressing the bid. Yet the support did not break, suggesting either passive bids absorbing the flow or sellers unwilling to chase price lower.

Samsung Tokenized Stock Price Action: $139.71 Support Holds As Taker Sellers Dominate

The $139.71 level has functioned as a short-term floor for Samsung's tokenized stock on Binance. The most recent one-hour window showed taker sell volume running ahead of taker buy volume, which typically indicates that sellers are initiating trades at market rather than waiting on the order book. When taker sellers dominate but price does not fall through a visible support, it often means resting limit orders are absorbing the aggressive flow.

The exact taker buy-to-sell ratio for that hour is not disclosed in the available data. What is confirmed is the directional skew: more volume executed on the sell side of the book than on the buy side. That imbalance, taken alone, would normally point to downward pressure. The fact that $139.71 held through that selling suggests the level has real bid depth behind it, at least for now.

Traders tracking the pair should treat the support as conditional rather than structural. A single hour of taker selling that fails to break a level is not the same as a level that has survived multiple tests across sessions. The open interest data adds a second layer: if leveraged longs are building while price stalls, the positioning itself becomes part of the story.

Binance Open Interest Jumps 10.50%: Long Positioning Builds Despite Price Stalling

Open interest on the Samsung tokenized stock pair rose 10.50% over the past 24 hours, according to the latest Binance data. Open interest measures the total number of outstanding derivative contracts that have not been settled. A rising figure means new positions are being opened faster than old ones are being closed or liquidated.

The 10.50% increase is notable because it arrived while price stayed near $139.71 rather than breaking decisively in either direction. When open interest rises alongside a flat or drifting price, it often signals that both sides are adding exposure, but the balance of new positioning matters. Binance accounts lean long on the pair, meaning the marginal new contract is more likely a buy than a sell.

That long skew creates a specific risk profile. If price breaks below $139.71, a cluster of leveraged longs could face liquidation, and forced selling could accelerate the move. Conversely, if the support holds and price turns higher, the same long positioning could fuel a squeeze as short-side liquidity gets run. The dollar value of the open interest increase is not disclosed in the current data, which limits the precision of any liquidation estimate.

The 24-hour open interest change is the headline number, but the composition matters more than the percentage. A 10.50% rise on a small base is less significant than the same percentage on a large one. Without the underlying dollar figure, traders can only note the direction and the long lean, not the absolute size of the leveraged exposure at risk.
