Trump Repeats Disputed Yarn About His 9/11 Role

TheTools · 09.09.2026 17:39 · source · permalink

President Trump is telling iffy 9/11 stories again.

On Tuesday, he took part in the Tunnel to Towers Foundation’s Steel Across America tour, delivering remarks from the Ellipse in Washington, D.C., in honor of the upcoming 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks. But naturally, his appearance couldn’t pass without a bit of weirdness.

Trump did honor first responders and the nearly 3,000 victims of the attacks, posthumously awarding the Medal of Freedom to Welles Crowther, an equities trader and volunteer firefighter who died while helping people flee the collapsing South Tower. But the president also used his speech to repeat a questionable anecdote about his whereabouts and actions in the aftermath of the tragedy and later defended his recollection as questions arose.

The president told the assembled crowd that he had been working on a “big building” when the attacks took place and that he took the entire construction crew down to ground zero “right after this happened.” It was then, Trump claims, that a pair of firefighters rushed him to safety out of concern that the nearby U.S. Steel Building, which is now known as One Liberty Plaza, was at risk of collapse. Trump said Tuesday that the building was “creaking” at the time. It wasn’t clear if by “right after” he meant the day of the attacks or sometime in the following days.

“I’ll never forget. Two firemen — we thought it was coming down on top of us, and two firemen, big strong guys — and I’m not the smallest guy in the world — they grabbed me under the arms and said, ‘Gotta get out of here.’ And they literally lifted me up. This is not easy to do. I’m big. They lifted me up and started running with me. I said, ‘Fellas, I can run myself.’ But they were amazing,” Trump said.

On Tuesday night, Trump defended his remarks in a Truth Social post, attacking our coverage and writing that everything he said at the Ellipse event was “exactly accurate.” But the president notably clarified that while he did visit ground zero, he was there “obviously not on the same day as the collapse, but shortly thereafter, with a large group of Construction Workers.”

“I was there as soon as the Law allowed,” he wrote.

He also attached an old video of himself talking to reporters in lower Manhattan on September 13, 2001 — entitled “What Donald Trump Saw at.. Ground Zero hours After 9/11 — in which he decries the attacks and says he just visited ground zero and has men working at or near the site. He does not mention any encounters with firemen.

Trump’s latest rendition of his 9/11 stories raised eyebrows. CNN’s stalwart fact checker Daniel Dale quickly responded to the president’s claims. “President Trump was not in the vicinity of the World Trade Center when it was hit. He was in Trump Tower, miles away from the towers and nearby One Liberty Plaza, which is/was also known as the US Steel building,” he wrote on X.

Dale continued in an more in-depth review of Trump’s remarks, noting that the first time the president publicly told a similar version of this story was on the campaign trail in 2016. At that point, Trump specified that the firemen anecdote happened “a couple of days” after the attack. Dale added that there’s no evidence Trump was even inside One Liberty Plaza following the 9/11 attacks:

With his comments about how he feared the US Steel building was “coming down on top of us” and about how a firefighter said “gotta get out of here,” Trump left open the impression that he was inside an unstable building. But there is no public evidence he was actually in the building he was referring to (better known as One Liberty Plaza in 2001 and still today) in the days after the attacks.

Similarly, PolitiFact noted that while there were concerns at the time One Liberty Plaza might collapse and it had to be evacuated on September 12, Trump was not mentioned in recollections about it. In addition to his retelling on the 2016 campaign trail, Trump also appears to have told a version of this story during a 2021 Newsmax interview, calling it a “scary situation.”

There are existing images and videos showing Trump in the vicinity of ground zero days after the attacks. On September 13, he spoke to a German outlet in lower Manhattan and claimed in the interview that he had 100 men working at the ground zero site with another 125 slated to join them. But USA Today and CNN have both noted that there’s no evidence a crew was sent. In another September 13 interview, with NBC News, Trump recounted that workers at ground zero frequently had to run from the site over fears other buildings would collapse, but that was as specific as he got:

Every few minutes, a whistle would go off and everybody would just run, because you have all the buildings around it, which are in such a weakened state that people just don’t know. And so they just have to take off, and then they come back, and they’re working under 50-story buildings that you don’t know if they’re going to fall down.

Trump did not mention the firemen story or himself needing to evacuate in either interview. It’s still not clear exactly how close Trump got to the collapsed towers. On September 14, Newsday reported that Trump was near the site the previous day:

The workers are so worn out that they barely glance at the sight of Donald Trump, every hair in place and impeccably dressed in a black suit, pressed white shirt and red tie, walking into the plaza with his cellular phone to his ear. “No, no. The building’s gone,” he says into the phone.

Trump has a long history of seeming embellishment when it comes to his post-9/11 actions. In 2019, the New York Times dug into a claim the president made while signing a bill to fully fund the medical care of 9/11 first responders. During the Rose Garden event, Trump reportedly claimed he spent a large amount of time alongside emergency workers at ground zero in the days following the attacks. “Many of those affected were firefighters, police officers and other first responders. And I was down there also, but I’m not considering myself a first responder. But I was down there. I spent a lot of time down there with you,” he said, per the Times.

But Richard Alles, a retired New York Fire Department deputy chief, told the Times he personally never saw Trump at the Twin Towers site. “I spent many months there myself, and I never witnessed him,” he told the outlet. “He was a private citizen at the time. I don’t know what kind of role he could have possibly played.”

Former NYPD commissioner and longtime Trump ally Bernie Kerik claimed in 2019 that Trump repeatedly visited ground zero without seeking media fanfare.

In 2015, Trump told the crowd at a Columbus, Ohio, campaign rally he personally saw people jumping from the World Trade Center. “Many people jumped, and I witnessed it — I watched that. I have a view, a view in my apartment that was specifically aimed at the World Trade Center,” he said, per CNN. “And I watched those people jump, and I watched the second plane hit … I saw the second plane hit the building, and I said, ‘Wow, that’s unbelievable.’” But CNN also noted at the time that Trump’s penthouse apartment in midtown Manhattan was four miles away from the towers, which would make it unlikely for Trump to have seen what he claimed.

Trump has also invoked inflammatory rhetoric when discussing 9/11 in the past, falsely claiming in 2015 that he saw Muslim Americans in nearby New Jersey celebrating the attacks — comments that state officials and critics swiftly denounced as false and Islamophobic.

Trump’s most infamous comments about 9/11, however, came hours after the attacks, when he phoned into Secaucus’s WWOR-TV that afternoon and described seeing the explosion from a window in Trump Tower, which gave him a view of the city’s skyline. “I’d see the Empire State Building in the foreground and the World Trade Center in the background. And now I’m looking at absolutely nothing. It’s just gone. It’s just hard to believe,” he said during the interview. But when the anchor asked whether a Trump building in the Financial District, 40 Wall Street, had been damaged, the future president boasted that his building was now the tallest in the city following the attacks, a statement that was ultimately debunked.

“40 Wall Street actually was the second-tallest building in downtown Manhattan, and it was actually, before the World Trade Center, was the tallest — and then, when they built the World Trade Center, it became known as the second tallest. And now it’s the tallest,” he said.

As New York City prepares to mark the solemn 25th anniversary of the attacks on Friday, Trump will be taking part in a ceremony held at the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia, instead. The New York Times reported last month that the president won’t be present at the ground-zero ceremony because he wanted to give a speech, something that is prohibited at the apolitical event. Instead, Vice-President J.D. Vance will represent the White House. Former presidents Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, Barack Obama, and Joe Biden are all expected to attend as well.
