What to know as the Sacramento area’s rainiest storm of the season continues

TheTools · 18.11.2026 23:32 · source · permalink

Meteorologists expect the strong winter storm to subside by Friday, before more rain over the weekend and next week.

Published on November 18, 2026

by Amelia Wu

The Sacramento region is experiencing a winter storm system, bringing rain and gusty winds to the valley.

Martin Christian

The Abridged version:

  • Meteorologists expect the heaviest rainfall to continue through Thursday and lighter rain next week into the holiday weekend.
  • Sacramento County officials say best practices for heavy rainfall and potential flooding include preparing a go-bag with ponchos, food, water and medicine. 
  • The strong winter storm doesn’t show signs of escalating to the severity of some of the area’s most severe storms of recent years, weather experts say.

An early winter storm is rolling through Sacramento, bringing heavy rain to the valley and strong snow to the Sierra Nevada. 

The storm, expected to continue Wednesday through Thursday, is significant but not historic, with the most severe weather occurring in the mountains, where heavy snowfall may make travel difficult, former KCRA meteorologist Mark Finan said. 

Chain controls have gone into effect on eastbound Interstate 80 in the mountains, where some westbound stretches of I-80 closed due to multiple spinouts, according to Caltrans.  

In the valley, the main concerns are gusty winds, power outages and clogged drains that could cause street flooding.

“It’s a pretty good storm,” Finan said. “For November to have something like this, it’s rarer … It has happened before, but it’s rare for us to get a storm that will provide this much snow in mid-November.” 

Finan said an active storm pattern entered the Sacramento area, with the heaviest rain expected Wednesday and Thursday, and chances of more rain over the weekend. The area will likely see more rain next week, but not as much as this week’s storm.

What’s happening

Courtney Carpenter, a warning coordinator meteorologist with the National Weather Service office in Sacramento, described the weather system as a strong winter storm, with heavy rain and gusty winds in lower elevations. Meanwhile, the Sierra is continuing to get heavy mountain snow throughout Wednesday and Thursday.

In the Sierra, a winter storm warning runs through early Friday, with 2 to 4 feet of snow forecast in lower elevations and as much as 5 feet on higher peaks. The storm follows a strong snowfall Nov. 13, which dropped nearly 12 inches of snow at Donner Pass.

But the snow has helped ski resorts: Heavenly Mountain Resort and Northstar California Resort are scheduled to open early Friday. Palisades Tahoe expects to open Saturday, followed by Sugar Bowl on Sunday.  

How does this stack up against the 2023 storms? 

Carpenter said Thursday will likely bring more rain and mountain snow, but with less wind. She added that the storms fall short of the magnitude of more severe storms in recent years. 

But a storm of that strength could happen later in the winter, she said. 

“This isn’t out of the norm,” Carpenter said. “This is right on track for what we start to see this time of year.”

The last time Sacramento saw high gusts and heavy rainfall was the 2023 atmospheric river. Sacramento County was the hardest-hit county in the state, with five deaths from the December and January storms. 

How does El Niño factor in? 

Finan pushed back on tying the current storm to El Niño, the weather pattern that forecasters expect to bring a particularly rainy winter. Even though El Niño conditions can increase the likelihood of wetter conditions, that doesn’t mean the weather will be more or less severe because of the ocean condition.  

In particular, Finan added that the 2023 storms, which were much more severe than this year’s prediction, were during a “non-El Niño” year. 

“There is no way to put any individual system on El Niño,” he said. “Some years it happens like this and some years it doesn’t.” 

He also said it is too early in the season to worry about flooding in streams, creeks and rivers. While the water levels may rise, area rivers and creeks are not expected to overflow, he said.  

No stranger to the rain 

Sacramento County spokesperson Macy Obernuefemann advised residents to be prepared in the event of a flooding emergency. She said good measures to take include updating a go-bag for the winter weather and looking into flood insurance.

She also encouraged residents to sign up for the county’s emergency alerts.

“One thing in particular about Sacramento County, we’re not strangers to rain during the winter and we’re not strangers to flooding,” she said. “So we stay prepared year-round at the Office of Emergency Services. And we’re urging people to stay prepared too.”

The county has opened three sandbag sites for residents of unincorporated areas. Residents should bring their own shovels, and there’s a 20-bag limit per visit: 

  • Branch Center (3847 Branch Center Road)
  • Orangevale Community Center (6826 Hazel Ave.) 
  • Sacramento Metropolitan Fire Station 116 (7995 Elwyn Ave.) 

Various situations can arise in weather conditions like these, the county spokesperson said. She said to refer to three phone lines, depending on the situation.  

Who to call:

  • 911: emergencies, including downed power lines
  • 311: nonemergency city services such as clogged drains, non-working traffic signals and fallen trees. (Call 916-264-5011 outside city limits.)
  • 211: community and social services

Stormy weather cleanup 

Jesa David, city of Sacramento spokesperson, said in an email that the city has extra crews on hand during heavy storms, working overtime to monitor conditions and respond to reports, including clogged storm drains.

Stormy weather causes the leaves to become heavier, which slows down leaf-season collections, David added. 

David advised residents waiting for the Claw to put leaves in the weekly collection container and maintain a neat pile, out of the way of storm drains and bike lanes. This makes cleanup after a storm easier, she said.

The city also opened two weather respite centers during the week.

The Outreach and Engagement Center at 3615 Auburn Blvd. accepts walk-ups and can accommodate up to 50 guests. The center will be open through 8 a.m. Friday, Nov. 20.

North 5th Street Navigation Center at 700 North Fifth St. operates at night, from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. The center does not accept pets and accommodates up to 20 guests.  

Through the city’s partnership with SacRT, those who need a means to reach the centers can ride public transit at no cost. All that is required is a screenshot or printed weather-respite flyer to present to the bus or light rail operator.

The city news release also recommends using libraries and community centers as shelter from the weather.
