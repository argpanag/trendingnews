The Canadian Open champion has now won a whopping 77.8% of his hard-court matches in 2026. 

While Shelton was a semifinalist at the US Open in 2023, he stalled out in the third round in each of the past two seasons and is hoping to make a deeper run this time around.

He likely enters this particular matchup with loads of confidence. Shelton is 4-0 against Denis Shapovalov and defeated the Canadian earlier this year in Dallas.

Shapovalov is just 27 years old, yet he seems far removed from the player who cracked the top 10 in 2020 and once looked like a rising star. From 2020 to 2022, the Canadian reached at least the quarterfinals at three different majors, most notably making the semifinals at Wimbledon in 2021 before falling to eventual champion Novak Djokovic.

However, he hasn’t been able to reach those heights in the years since, and 2026 has been a particularly trying campaign. 

Even with his two US Open victories, Shapovalov has a middling 16-17 record and has suffered multiple injuries this summer, forcing him to retire at both Wimbledon and, more recently, at the Canadian Open.

While Shapovalov is coming off solid wins over Miomir Kecmanovic and Luca Van Assche, his inconsistent form and questionable fitness level entering the US Open make it difficult to see him flipping the script in this one-sided rivalry. Shapovalov has also struggled with double faults, already committing 20 through two rounds.

According to Tennis Abstract’s Elo ratings, Shelton is projected to win this match 78.1% of the time.

While Shelton has dominated this head-to-head, their matches have generally been quite close. 

In their meeting earlier this year, Shelton needed three sets to win 4-6, 6-4, 7-6(4). In their only previous Grand Slam match, the American outlasted Shapovalov 6-7(4), 6-2, 6-4, 4-6, 6-2 at Wimbledon in 2024. 

The two also had a match where Shelton was up 7-6(5), 6-6 before the Canadian was disqualified for yelling at a fan.

Despite having a big serve, the American could have a difficult time generating aces against Shapovalov. The Canadian has been aced just 3.0% of the time on hard courts this year, the best mark among the top 50 players.  

Shelton has scored 24 and 15.5 fantasy points in his first two matches.

Ben Shelton is looking to advance past the third round of the US Open for the second time in his career. Can he take care of business against an opponent who’s winless against him?
