Dollar General overcharged millions at checkout for years, FTC and 20 states allege

TheTools · 27.10.2026 20:02 · source · permalink

Updated on: October 27, 2026 / 1:53 PM EDT / CBS News

The Federal Trade Commission and 20 states on Monday sued Dollar General, accusing the budget retailer of charging customers more for products than it advertised.

The chain, which operates 21,000 stores nationwide, allegedly mislabeled prices for years, "extracting millions of dollars in unlawful overcharges," the FTC alleged.

"Dollar General has betrayed the trust of millions of American consumers and imposed significant financial harm on communities and consumers who are already struggling to make their paychecks last," Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection, said in a statement. 

The FTC added that American consumers depend on Dollar General's advertised low prices to make ends meet on tight budgets, while prices that don't reflect advertised deals make it impossible for them to manage household finances.

The agency filed its complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee, Dollar General's home state. 

"Our goal is 100% accuracy between shelf and register prices in every store," Dollar General said in a statement. "As with other retailers and despite our policies and procedures, occasional price discrepancies can occur. When that happens, our store teams are instructed, empowered, and expected to make things right for the customer. In recent years, we have enhanced our price-accuracy processes and remain committed to continuous improvement."

Dollar General said it was made aware of the FTC's investigation in early February.

"Inaccurate pricing"

According to the suit, thousands of Dollar General stores unlawfully overcharged shoppers.

Dollar General "has for years engaged in deceptive conduct that harms the very consumers it claims to serve," the FTC and states allege in the suit. "Although Dollar General promises convenience, low prices, and value, the company has routinely charged customers more at the register than the prices displayed on shelves, price tags, and advertisements. This pattern of inaccurate pricing has persisted for years and affects millions of purchases across the country."

The company also faced thousands of consumer complaints claiming it failed to honor advertised prices at checkout, a sign Dollar General knowingly harmed customers, according to the FTC. 

The suit seeks a permanent injunction, along with monetary and other relief.

In its statement, Dollar General said, "FTC staff also demanded a monetary amount that was untethered to — and orders of magnitude larger than — any realistic assessment of amounts attributable to pricing discrepancies."

The company said it "worked tirelessly and in good faith to resolve this matter without litigation," including an "alternative, and in our view, more reasonable, manner of calculating potential settlement ranges."

"Although we respect the FTC's role in protecting American consumers and the State representatives' role in protecting the citizens of their respective states, we will defend this litigation vigorously," it said.

The FTC leveled the unfair pricing allegations against Dollar General as other retailers face scrutiny over how they charge shoppers. Consumer advocates say retailers are developing new artificial intelligence-enabled technology that could be used to charge consumers different prices. 

Groundwork Collaborative, a nonprofit think tank focused on economic policy, led an investigation into grocery-shopping platform Instacart and found that in a trial, it had used AI to simultaneously show customers different prices for the same goods from the same store.
