Missile alerts cost Ukraine $45m for every hour they halt businesses

in Kyiv

Missile alerts are costing Ukraine an estimated $45m (£34m) for every hour they bring business to a halt, according to the country’s economy minister, as Kyiv searches for ways to keep the economy running under an intensifying Russian bombardment.

Russia has increasingly targeted the infrastructure underpinning Ukraine’s economy, striking industrial sites, warehouses, logistics hubs and datacentres in what Oleksandr Kravchenko, the minister for economy and the environment, described as an effort to “destroy pretty much the entire economy”.

“What we had not fully anticipated was how quickly Russia would develop these jet-powered drones and use them to target everything,” he said, referring to Russia’s use of the new weapons that have pounded Kyiv in recent weeks.

Speaking from the heavily protected government building in Kyiv, Kravchenko, a former McKinsey executive who joined the government in the summer, said the attacks were forcing large parts of the economy into repeated standstills as workers were sent to shelters, with sirens lasting up to 10 hours a day.

“If people are in a bunker, business stops,” he said.

Romanian PM candidate loses key vote as political deadlock continues

The Romanian parliament has again failed to elect a prime minister, prolonging a political crisis that has been ongoing since a pro-European coalition government collapsed in May.

Liberal European parliament member Siegfried Muresan failed to get lawmakers’ backing with just 182 of the 233 votes needed to approve his government, according to the official count, AFP reports, bringing the prospect of snap elections closer.

President Nicusor Dan has now nominated three candidates for the post of prime minister, but all have failed to successfully form a government.

The Council of Europe has said that Ukrainians have made nearly 200,000 claims for compensation over Russia’s invasion since 2022.

AFP reports the body said about 65,000 of the 195,000 damages claims made since its Ukraine register opened in 2024 in The Hague had been accepted and the rest were being reviewed.

The council said claims can now be made in 43 different categories ranging from loss of human life or injury, to deprivation of medical care and education and fallout on the environment and natural resources.

EU's Kubilius calls for 'peace through strength' and further support for Ukraine

European defence commissioner Andrius Kubilius called for a push to achieve “peace through strength” as Russia continues to be not interested in any diplomatic talks on Ukraine.

Speaking at the Euronews event, he warned there were clear signs that “Russia is escalating,” but there is no immediate sign of political decision to strike further.

“Russians have capability to do some big things - one drone, ten drones, hundred drones, flying into Lithuania. I would not be surprised if that were to happen.

But do they have a really clear political decision to do something? I don’t know, and for me all our discussions about [whether] the Russians will do something are based on some sort of theoretical predictions.”

But do they have a really clear political decision to do something? I don’t know, and for me all our discussions about [whether] the Russians will do something are based on some sort of theoretical predictions.”

He said Europe in any case “needs to be ready” as “winter could be very rough,” but more attention should be paid to Ukraine’s growing capabilities, including its ability to strike targets within Russia.

“Ukraine really is defending not only themselves, but they are defending the whole Europe. And we need to make that a real priority for us,” he said.

He said it was “difficult to predict” what Russia could do in the future, but any signs of being afraid of Russia would only “encourage Putin to be even more escalatory.”

“Our support [for] Ukraine should be very clear answer to whatever Putin is trying to escalate,” he said.
