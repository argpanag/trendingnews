Bryson DeChambeau breaks silence on LIV 2.0 ambitions

Bryson DeChambeau has spoken out on failings from LIV Golf 1.0, while looking ahead to LIV 2.0 in the hopes it will be a league that lives forever.

The two-time US Open champion was speaking to Raj Shamani on the Figuring Out podcast.

In the two-and-a-half-hour long chat, DeChambeau spoke about how he got started in golf, his transformation to one of the game’s longest hitters and, of course, LIV Golf.

The 33-year-old explained that mistakes from those previously at the helm of the now-bankrupt golf league led to player payments being missed and, ultimately, the precarious financial situation the league finds itself in.

“The team golf stuff I was very excited about. I really cared and I still do care about the team golf stuff. The top co. wasn’t really managing that effectively. I know that now, I didn’t know that during the time,” DeChambeau explained.

“Our teams were doing very well. Our teams, we generated almost 29 million dollars in revenue last year. We had 21 (million) in costs from payment of players and expenses and travel and everything. We made eight million dollars EBIDTA last year.

“All the players payments and everything got pushed down to the teams when they went bankrupt. The top co. wasn’t being run effectively. No fault to each other but we thought there was a lot more capital that was going to be at play from the guys that were funding it.”

Despite this, DeChambeau went onto explain that he is actively working on LIV 2.0. While details of what the next iteration of LIV Golf might look like are, as of yet, largely unconfirmed, DeChambeau pointed to a format where team golf is at the forefront and where finances are under control.

“The business model makes a lot of sense. We’re actually working through that right now for this new model,” he said.

“It will make full financial sense. No funny business, no nothing. It’s going to be done right and this is going to be a league that exists hopefully forever.

“It’s not combative, we’re not trying to compete against the tour, we’re trying to be creative. We’re trying to bring value to anywhere and everywhere in the golf market across the world.”

The comments come at a time where it appears the future of LIV Golf is precarious. Over the past weeks, several of the league’s biggest names have declared they will be playing their golf elsewhere in future.

For now, however, it appears DeChambeau is fully behind LIV 2.0.
