Princess Anne's 'withering' question left Earl Spencer red-faced at royal dinner

TheTools · 23.09.2026 07:28 · source · permalink

Charles Spencer has claimed an encounter with Princess Anne left him feeling red-faced. The uncle of Prince Harry and Prince William has been sharing memories surrounding the death of his late sister, Princess Diana, in his latest book, Swan Song - Diana, my sister.

Among revelations concerning King Charles, he has also revealed an alleged uncomfortable exchange with the monarch's sister, Princess Anne. Earl Spencer recounted being "warned" to be on his best behaviour while attending a dinner party featuring various members of royal families from across the globe.

Having been informed he would be seated beside Anne, the Earl prepared what he thought would be an appropriate conversation topic. With her daughter Zara Tindall's christening taking place that very day, he considered this the ideal ice-breaker.

He wrote: "It transpired, to my alarm, that I was actually placed next to Anne, and when she turned my way, I asked, 'And how did your daughter's christening go today, Ma'am?' I expected her to run away with the happy topic of her baby Zara's baptism.

"But instead she stopped, looked at me witheringly, and asked, 'Tell me, how's a christening supposed to go?' And I, being seventeen and clueless about dinner-party small-talk, turned red and looked at my plate, unable to conjure up a reply."

Charles revealed that his brother-in-law Neil, by contrast, enjoyed a far more pleasant evening. Seated beside the Crown Princess of Japan, Neil engaged in relaxed discussion about rice cultivation.

"It was the luck of the draw that night, whether you were placed next to a royal who was happy to make you feel small, or one who had the grace and manners," the Earl observed.

Within the pages of his memoir, the author has also put forward allegations concerning his former brother-in-law. He claims Charles remarked: "Rest assured, we'll forget her soon enough," during a tense telephone exchange after Diana's death.

A Buckingham Palace representative responded to the allegation, stating: "While we do not comment on books as a matter of principle, His Majesty is mindful that the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory in ways others do not recognise, even many years after such a loss."

Charles further alleged that his brother-in-law appeared "giddy elated" while discussing arrangements for the funeral.

When questioned about this assertion during a conversation with BBC journalist Laura Kuenssberg, he maintained his position: "I felt there was that sort of giddy tone. I felt – I say in the book – I felt there was a giddy tone... and I just felt there was a lightness in his tone about Diana's death that really jarred with me."

After Laura suggested the Earl had implied the King was "pleased his former wife was dead", he responded: "Well, every word in this book I report it exactly as I saw it and felt it at the time."

When pressed further by the host, who described it as an "incredibly damning thing to say," he responded: "I cannot be sure. I'm saying that's what I assume from the tone is a possibility, and that's all that I can do."
