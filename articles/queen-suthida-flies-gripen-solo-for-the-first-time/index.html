Queen Suthida Flies Gripen Solo for the First Time

TheTools · 08.10.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

Queen Suthida of Thailand completed her first solo flight in a Saab JAS 39C Gripen on 8 October 2026, becoming the country's first female Gripen pilot.

Queen Suthida of Thailand completed her first solo flight in a Saab JAS 39C Gripen on 8 October 2026, making her the first female Gripen pilot in the Royal Thai Air Force (RTAF). The 41-minute sortie took place at Wing 7 in Surat Thani province and was witnessed by King Maha Vajiralongkorn himself.

What Happened at Wing 7

According to AeroTime, the flight departed from Runway 22 at the base in Phun Phin district at 10:06 local time. The Queen carried out a briefing on the flight plan and a pre-flight inspection of the single-seat fighter before taxiing for takeoff.

Air Chief Marshal Seksan Kantha, commander of the RTAF, and the Wing 7 commander were also present to observe the sortie. After she landed, King Maha Vajiralongkorn presented her with a bouquet.

The Golden Gripen Order Patch

Following the landing, the King attached a Golden Gripen Order Patch to the Queen's left sleeve. AeroTime reports that the Gripen Order, an honorary association in Sweden, presented the patch and accepted Queen Suthida as a full member.

The palace account identifies her as Thailand's first female Gripen pilot, marking a notable milestone for both the RTAF and the wider Gripen operator community.

The Training Course Behind the Solo Flight

The solo sortie was the final step in a conversion course for Fighter-Attack Aircraft Type 20, which is the RTAF's designation for the Gripen. The Queen undertook the course at the air force's invitation, following the RTAF's standard Gripen pilot training syllabus.

The programme began between June and July 2026 with 45 academic and emergency-procedure subjects, followed by 20 simulator sorties. She then flew the two-seat Saab JAS 39D Gripen with an instructor, accumulating approximately 10 hours before transitioning to the single-seat JAS 39C.

Queen Suthida's Flying Career

The Queen's aviation background is extensive. AeroTime notes that she first flew on 13 May 2010, in a Cessna T-41D of Squadron 604 at Wing 6 in Bangkok.

She subsequently gained experience on the CT-4E Airtrainer, Pilatus PC-9, Mooney M20M, and Piper PA-34 Seneca. She also serves as co-pilot alongside King Maha Vajiralongkorn on royal flights aboard Boeing 737-400 and 737-800 aircraft, with more than 450 hours logged on the 737-800 alone.

Her total flying experience across all aircraft types now exceeds 1,188 hours, according to the palace account.

Thailand's Gripen Fleet and What Comes Next

Wing 7 is the home of the RTAF's Gripen C/D squadron, which entered service in 2011. Thailand originally received 12 Gripen C/D fighters and currently operates 11, after losing a JAS 39C in a crash in January 2017.

The fleet is set to grow. AeroTime reports that Thailand ordered four Gripen E/F fighters in August 2025. In May 2026, an RTAF delegation led by Air Chief Marshal Seksan reviewed the start of assembly of the first Thai Gripen E at Saab's facility in Linköping, Sweden, with Saab confirming deliveries under that contract will take place by 2030.

The milestone comes at a busy moment for the Gripen programme globally. Earlier this month, the first Gripen E assembled in Brazil by Embraer completed its maiden flight from Embraer's industrial complex in Gavião Peixoto, São Paulo, on 6 October 2026, as Saab continues to build out its international production network.

The Bottom Line

Queen Suthida's solo Gripen flight is a significant moment for the RTAF and draws considerable attention to Thailand's long-standing commitment to the Swedish fighter. With a new batch of Gripen E/F aircraft on order and assembly already under way in Linköping, the type will remain central to Thai air defence well into the next decade.

Her completion of the full RTAF training syllabus, from academic study through simulator and dual instruction to a solo sortie in a front-line fighter, underlines the depth of the royal family's engagement with the air force and its equipment.
