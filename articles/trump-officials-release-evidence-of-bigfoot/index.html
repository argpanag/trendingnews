Trump officials release ‘evidence’ of Bigfoot

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:55 · source · permalink

‘Sasquatch files’ detail encounters with ‘shapeshifter’ and dog with a pig-like snout

On the eve of Donald Trump’s inauguration, he promised to release tranches of top-secret files held by the US government.

But it is not until now that the Trump administration has released perhaps some of the most hotly anticipated dossiers: the Bigfoot files.

The documents, locked away for years, chronicle sightings of a mysterious, hairy creature that crashed through forests, leaving behind extraordinarily large footprints.

Almost a quarter of Americans believe in the beast, also known as Sasquatch, according to a 2021 YouGov poll.

The most famous sighting was by Roger Patterson and Robert “Bob” Gimlin in 1967.

They were making a Bigfoot docudrama in Bluff Creek, northern California, and claim they filmed the creature ambling away on a near-60 second video.

Stills of the alleged beast are beloved by enthusiasts and the film continues to be a highly scrutinised, analysed and debated subject.

A file released by the Trump administration deals with what was known as the Utah dog investigation.

It was carried out on behalf of the US department of defence in the spring of 2010 by Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies.

The 60-page report was carried out by a four-person team despatched to Fort Duchesne, a small community in Uintah County, north-east Utah.

More than 25 people were interviewed, including a shaman, Mormon church members and the local fish and wildlife director.

Several digital cameras were also put in place.

Witnesses reported seeing a “skinwalker” – a witch in Navajo culture – capable of disguising itself as an animal. According to one witness, it moved like a “track star”.

Other bizarre phenomena were sighted.

Witnesses have reported unidentified balls of light at close range, daytime sightings of “dog-like creatures running on two legs, unnatural animalistic howling, screaming little people”, the report noted, as well as “prehistoric-like birds and a maggot which measured about 8ft long and 2-3ft in diameter”.

Then there was what was described as a humpbacked, pale and fur-less “shapeshifter” which “jumped over the fence and ran using his hands in a chimpanzee-like manner”, the report said.

The other creature was more like a dog with a pig-like snout, covered in fur, according to the document.

“The creature, which was the size of a big man, appeared to be hostile. He was scared and fled the area,” a witness stated.

Fur found at the scene was tested and found to belong to a dog and a raccoon. The report concluded the incidents were a result of local folklore.

Another report details an alleged encounter near Petty Creek, Montana, in 1941. The six-page account written in 1980 by Leo Kinney describes a sighting of bizarre tracks by a hunting party.

The party had killed an elk and when they went to fetch the carcass, only its entrails remained. The tracks suggested that the body had been carted off by a massive creature.

“At first we guessed it was a bear,” he wrote. “Except bears don’t walk on their hind legs, only for shorter distances and ordinarily would drag a carcass this large.

“Whatever it was picked up the elk whole and clear off the ground.”

Apart from the massive tracks, there was a strong odour and the hunting party could hear a loud grunting noise.

After a brief discussion, the men thought it wise to go home rather than try to track down the beast.

Then, in June 1982, there was an investigation into some tracks found at Low Creek in Oregon.

The file is detailed, complete with photographs of footprints beside a tape measure to demonstrate the sheer size.

One print was of a 15in-long right foot, which was also 7in wide – roughly twice the size of that of an average man.

Researchers concluded the tracks had not been made by a bear.

“Fine forest litter had been brushed aside prior to the making of the track. It was obvious that the litter had been displaced aside from the track area in an unnatural manner,” the report concluded.

‘Attempt at hoax’

The file contains two memoranda. One from biologist Rodney Johnson and the second from Border Patrol tracker Joel Hardin.

Mr Hardin concluded that the tracks were “a very clever attempt to perpetrate a hoax”.

The tracks had been left with the use of a fake foot made of either rubber or soft plaster, he suggested.

In 1972, the Bigfoot Research Institute was formed, “dedicated to the scientific investigation and understanding of Bigfoot and other unknown creatures”.

According to its website, the institute’s aim was to uncover “the truth behind the Bigfoot mystery”.

“Belief in Bigfoot is pretty widespread in the US and has been increasing – though this has to be understood in the context of widespread belief in angels, the power of prayer, ghosts and UFOs,” Andrew Bartlett, sociologist of science at Sheffield University and co-author of the book Bigfooters and Scientific Inquiry, told The Telegraph.

“Bigfoot is obviously a pretty important cultural object in America. Even if we agree it doesn’t exist, most Americans ‘know’ a lot about it; they could describe it and might even be able to tell you something of its behaviour.

“This is in some part due to the popularity of ‘reality’ TV programmes which follow Bigfooters on expeditions of varying degrees of authenticity.”

David Daegling, professor of anthropology, was sceptical about the importance of the files.

“The articles released don’t provide anything novel that would cause a sceptic or believer to change their mind,” he said.

“Bigfoot has always been a mythological figure, even in the unlikely event that there is a real animal behind it.

“The problem with proving Bigfoot is that there is a long record of witness reports, people finding tracks, taking photographs and making films, such that there is a rich trove of information to draw upon when trying to understand a weird experience in the woods.”
