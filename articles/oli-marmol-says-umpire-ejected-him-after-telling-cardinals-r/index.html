Oli Marmol says umpire ejected him after telling Cardinals reliever to fix his hat

TheTools · 05.09.2026 10:13 · source · permalink

MLB Latest

Oliver Marmol argues with umpire Doug Eddings after getting ejected from the game as catcher Leo Bernal looks on in the sixth inning at Coors Field. Justin Edmonds / Getty Images

During the St. Louis Cardinals’ 7-6 victory over the Colorado Rockies on Friday, home-plate umpire Doug Eddings ejected Cardinals manager Oli Marmol in the bottom of the sixth inning. Marmol had a unique explanation for the on-field conflict.

“Some words exchanged there. Quite honestly, I thought it was pretty embarrassing on his end,” Marmol said on the Cardinals’ broadcast when asked about his run-in with Eddings. “We have a kid making his debut, and in between an at-bat, he tells him that he needs to fix his hat and, ‘The kid’s not going to wear his hat like that on my field.’ Last I checked, this is Coors Field, so I took issue with it.”

The player making his first major-league appearance was reliever Hancel Rincon, whom the Cardinals called up from Triple-A Memphis on Sept. 1. While Rincon faced Cole Carrigg in the sixth inning, Eddings halted the game with the count 2-0 and motioned to Cardinals catcher Leo Bernal, and Rincon adjusted his cap.

Carrigg hit an inside-the-park home run three pitches later. After the next batter, Connor Norby, hit a single, Marmol replaced Rincon with Justin Bruihl. While Bruihl was warming up, a visibly angry Marmol approached Eddings and appeared to adjust his cap. Not only did Eddings eject Marmol, but he also called a pitch-clock violation that gave Rockies designated hitter Troy Johnston a 1-0 count to start his at-bat against Bruihl.

“And then the pitch violation while I’m out there having a discussion, that’s also stupid, in my opinion,” Marmol said. “So there’s a lot of moving parts to that one. At the end of the day, I thought they handled it poorly, and I’d like an explanation at some point.”

Cardinals broadcasters Chip Caray and Brad Thompson tried to make sense of what happened after Marmol’s ejection.

“He is telling Rincon to put his hat on straight,” Thompson said while watching a replay of the initial interaction between Eddings, Bernal and Rincon while Carrigg was at bat. “That’s why Leo was laughing behind the plate. Like, ‘Are you serious, dude?’ If that’s what happened, if Doug Eddings behind the plate was telling Rincon to fix his hat, that is a flat-out embarrassment. That is not his job to do. He’s not wearing his hat backwards. This wasn’t a uniform violation.”

“Sometimes umpires think it’s their game, their field,” Caray said. “It’s not. They are the officials. They’re in charge, but it’s a player’s game, I thought.”

Added Thompson: “Again, if that’s in fact what that was all about, that is overstepping big time, and the league should look in.”

The bottom of the sixth inning was an interesting one for Eddings, and it didn’t stop with Marmol’s ejection. The Cardinals had challenged a tag play at third base earlier in the inning on TJ Rumfield’s triple, and Eddings enthusiastically told the crowd in Denver after the review that the call was confirmed, which meant St. Louis was out of challenges. Johnston would later score on a sacrifice bunt by Zac Veen on a play where it seemed pretty clear that Bernal tagged Johnston.
