Florentino: Portman Road Crowd So Important - Ipswich Town News

TheTools · 08.11.2026 14:21 · source · permalink

Town midfielder Florentino Luís hopes the Portman Road crowd can play their part as the Blues prepare to host in-form Bournemouth on Sunday.

After a slow start to the campaign under new boss Marco Rose, the resurgent Cherries have won each of their last seven matches in all competitions, including their last three in the Premier League without conceding.

That run has propelled Bournemouth to eighth in the table, with their tally of 12 points seeing them sit one point ahead of Town after the opening nine league matches of the season.

Portman Road has been relatively kind to the Blues so far though, with Gary O’Neil’s side having already won twice on Suffolk soil this term thanks to late winners against Sunderland and Fulham.

Florentino, who has started the last four matches, is confident that Town can register another strong result on their own turf in the final match before November’s international break.

“It will be a very tough game because they are a team that like to play,” he said. “It doesn’t matter the stadium that they are playing, so we are prepared for that.

“For sure, we are looking forward to get a win because it will be very important for us. Every time that we play at Portman Road, it’s a big opportunity for us. We want to have the help of the supporters, because it is very important every time.

“We can see in the games that they go until the end. The victories that we have had at home, they were there at the end of the games.

“The atmosphere is very good. We want to have this every time that we play there going forward. We want the people to continue with us, because for sure we will give our max on the pitch.

“Our mindset for this game is very clear. It’s to get the three points, and we know how important it is. We want to enjoy every game at home because with our supporters it’s easier. We are looking forward to getting the three points.”

Bournemouth’s rapid rise over the last decade continued with a record-breaking sixth-place finish last season, which earned the club qualification for the Europa League for the first time ever.

As a result, the Cherries are currently dealing with the tricky balance of playing European fixtures in midweek with domestic matches at the weekend.

Thursday’s victory away to Sparta Prague continued Bournemouth’s perfect start to the league phase of the Europa League, with Rose’s side showing few signs of fatigue at the busy schedule.

Florentino has dealt with similar fixture congestion with former side Benfica, and says the Cherries’ success at handling multiple competitions is commendable.

He said: “It’s not a thing that is easy to do, but we are looking forward to playing against them and we know that will be a good game to have the win for us.

“It’s not easy to have a lot of travel during the week, but we know that the clubs give the best conditions to recover. This is not an excuse to perform, because you can see they have seven games winning.

“We are ready for what they are bringing to us and to the stadium. We are not looking for if they are tired or not, but we are looking for our game, and our focus is on our game to get the win.

“We can see that they have good organisation when you see the past games and the [lack of] goals that they conceded. But playing at home, we have always the possibility to score goals and I think it will happen.

“We are preparing our team to see the spots that they are a bit weak to get the goals.”

While with boyhood side Benfica, Florentino played 124 matches alongside Bournemouth defender António Silva during three years together in Lisbon.

“He’s a very good player, he’s a player that I’ve spent some years with him,” Florentino said. “I know him very well and I’m very happy for him, but this weekend he cannot win.

“He’s a very good player and I’m very happy that he has settled well in the Premier League.”

By his own admission, Florentino is not a prolific goalscorer and had scored only four goals across 257 appearances in his senior career.

That was until he notched his first goal in English football last weekend, slamming high into the roof of the net to open the scoring against Hull City at the MKM Stadium.

“It was a great feeling to score my first goal for Ipswich and in the Premier League, and hopefully the first of many,” the 27-year-old reflected.

“We want to continue in this shape and we want to continue to have good results. In the past week we had a draw, but we would like to win. This weekend we have another opportunity to continue our way.

“It’s one thing that he’s trying to give to the team every week, and step-by-step we are getting there. The team is doing very well on the things that the coach is asking of us.

“We want to keep this consistency to every game and every goal like that, because we see that our work during the week is working at the weekend.”
