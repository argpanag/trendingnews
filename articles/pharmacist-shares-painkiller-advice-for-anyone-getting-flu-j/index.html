Pharmacist shares painkiller advice for anyone getting flu jab | InYourArea

TheTools · 03.10.2026 20:50 · source · permalink

National

Ibuprofen and paracetamol can help with vaccine side effects, but caution is needed

  • By Sara Tilley
  • •3 October 2026

If you're getting ready for your flu jab, you might be tempted to take painkillers beforehand to ward off a sore arm or headache, but it's actually better to wait. Taking them ahead of time doesn't usually help and may do more harm than good, according to one pharmacist.

Nasir Ali advises against taking paracetamol or ibuprofen to prevent possible side effects from the jab. At best they will be unnecessary – and there's a chance they could reduce its effectiveness.

He said: "Most people won’t know whether they’re going to get any side effects, so there’s usually no reason to take a painkiller before you need one. A lot of people won't need pain relief at all, so taking it beforehand could simply mean you're taking medicine you didn't need."

He adds that some research suggests that taking paracetamol or ibuprofen before certain jabs could affect the immune response slightly. "We don't know how much difference that makes in practice, but there's usually no real benefit to taking a painkiller before you've even got symptoms," said the superintendent pharmacist with SwiftDoctor.

"My advice would be to have the jab, see how you feel and deal with any side effects if they actually appear, rather than trying to prevent them with medication beforehand." However, the NHS says you can take painkillers after having the flu vaccine to provide relief from any mild symptoms, reports the Mirror.

Paracetamol

"If you develop a headache, a fever, general aches, or a sore arm that's bothering you, you can take something suitable for you when you need it. Paracetamol is often the simplest first choice for a temperature, headache or general aches," he said.

However, be careful if you’re already taking cold or flu remedies – some products contain paracetamol and it can be surprisingly easy to take more than you realise. "If you're not sure what's already in a cold and flu remedy, ask a pharmacist before adding another painkiller. It's a simple check, but an important one, because taking too much paracetamol can be dangerous," said Ali.

Ibuprofen

While ibuprofen can also help with pain, fever and inflammation, Ali warns that it's not suitable for everyone – some people with stomach ulcers, kidney problems or certain types of asthma may need to avoid it. "If you've got a health condition that affects what painkillers you can take, stick to the advice you've already been given or check with a pharmacist," he said.

Which side effects are normal and when should you worry?

A sore or tender arm is common after a flu jab. Some people also develop a slightly raised temperature or feel achy. These symptoms are usually mild and ease within a day or two. You should s seek medical advice if symptoms are severe, getting worse, unusual or not settling down as expected.

A severe allergic reaction is extremely rare. However, difficulty breathing, significant swelling of the face or throat, collapse or a rapidly developing widespread allergic reaction needs immediate emergency medical attention.

Can you have your flu jab if you're unwell or taking antibiotics?

Taking antibiotics doesn't automatically mean you need to delay your vaccination – what matters more is how unwell you feel. If you're being treated for a minor infection, have no fever and otherwise feel reasonably well, you may still be able to have your jab.

If you're seriously ill or have a high temperature, the vaccination will usually be postponed until you've recovered. If you're unsure, tell the pharmacist or person giving your vaccination about your symptoms and any medicines you're taking.
