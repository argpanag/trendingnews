Furyk: I will bury hatchet with Reed if he wants Ryder Cup return

TheTools · 07.10.2026 06:59 · source · permalink

US captain will talk with compatriot before next year’s edition despite personal outburst following 2018 defeat

Jim Furyk has said that he will bury the hatchet with Patrick Reed before next year’s Ryder Cup, despite the latter’s extraordinary outburst after the 2018 match in Paris.

In the wake of the 17½- 10½ defeat at Le Golf National, Reed took aim at Furyk’s captaincy. “For somebody as successful in the Ryder Cup as I am, I don’t think it’s smart to sit me twice,” Reed said after being benched for two sessions.

Reed also accused Furyk of running “a buddy system” and of “blindsiding me” when partnering him with Tiger Woods. The pair lost both of their fourballs together.

Reed had asked Furyk whether he and Jordan Spieth could resume their partnership that had been so successful in the previous two Ryder Cups, but Spieth wanted to play with his long-friend Justin Thomas.

“I don’t have any issue with Jordan,” Reed said. “When it comes right down to it, I don’t care if I like the person I’m paired with or if the person likes me, as long as it works and it sets up the team for success… Every day, [in the US team room] I saw ‘leave your egos at the door’. But they [the Europeans] do that better than us.”

Reed, 36, has not played a Ryder Cup since that year. He was overlooked in 2021, but was a member of LIV for the last two tournaments. However, he quit LIV at the start of the year and, sitting on top of the DP World Tour order of merit, he is all but guaranteed to play on the PGA Tour in 2027. The self-styled “Captain America” has declared it is his intention to turn out in the stars and stripes again and will be buoyed by Furyk’s comments on Tuesday.

When asked by Telegraph Sport whether he would “let bygones be bygones” when it came to Reed, Furyk was magnanimous. “We are going to put the best 12 guys on this team and if Patrick Reed is one of those, that’s great,” Furyk said. “It’s been a long time since he’s been in our team room and, yeah, you know, Patrick and I will have that conversation. I’m looking forward to welcoming our best team of 12.”

Furyk was talking at the “year to go celebrations” at Adare Manor, the Co Limerick venue which will host the biennial tussle next September. The 56-year-old was a surprise choice to take over from Keegan Bradley, considering the humiliation he suffered during his first time in the hot seat. But with the US team reeling after back-to-back losses, the PGA of America entrusted the popular figure with the task of winning on European soil for the first time in 34 years.

Sat next to Luke Donald, his opposite number who will be looking to become the first captain to win three Ryder Cups in succession, Furyk pinned his cap to “preparation”. “I’m going to ask this team to do more than any other United States team has in the past,” Furyk said. This includes practising foursomes, the format in which they have struggled, including at the recent Presidents Cup.

He also expects his prospective players to attend the reconnaissance mission two months before the match. “To absolutely be here at Adare Manor next summer would be first and foremost,” he said. “We have guys coming over for both the Scottish and the Open Championship and the ask will be out there and I expect everyone to make their best effort to be here.

“But I do expect a lot of folks to be here and on-site, and I’m looking forward to it. I want them to see the golf course. We had a pretty good turnout back in ’18, as well, for Le Golf National in the summer. But I’m looking for a bigger out-turn this time.”

Furyk’s mind must have been playing tricks on him, as only five players joined him on that visit – and only three made the team. Furyk also acknowledged that he could not demand they all show up. “Things come up,” he said. “Family obligations, whatever it may be.”

For his part, Donald, as he does, kept his scorecards close to his chest, although he did refuse to acknowledge Furyk’s claim that the US will be the underdogs. “For us, underdog is a mentality,” Donald said. “We always want to prove something to ourselves, to the rest of the world. Yeah, we understand that being at home is an advantage, but we also know that the US are very, very strong and there will be absolutely no complacency as far as we are concerned.”
