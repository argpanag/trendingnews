News account only disclosed it was authorised by LNP after ABC questions

  • Topic:Government and Politics

By Claudia Williams

Queensland Breaking News only disclosed it was authorised by the Liberal National Party following questions from the ABC. (Supplied)

In short:

An Instagram page called Queensland Breaking News started sharing posts from government press conferences this month.

After questions from the ABC, the page disclosed it was authorised by the Liberal National Party in Queensland.

It is among a string of politically linked accounts promoting themselves as news sources at both a state and federal level.

A social media account promoting itself as a Queensland news source only disclosed it was authorised by the Liberal National Party following questions from the ABC.

This month Queensland Breaking News started sharing Instagram reels from government press conferences spruiking announcements made by ministers.

Its posts on transport and infrastructure projects have been re-posted by Premier David Crisafulli and Deputy Premier Jarrod Bleijie, with its best performing content reaching almost 10,000 views.

The account's maroon and white logo featuring a map of Queensland is in stark contrast to LNP's trademark blue.

Following questions to the government from the ABC an authorisation statement was added to the account's bio to appear alongside its tagline "latest updates from across QLD".

An LNP spokesperson said: "Queensland Breaking News is one of many pages used by the LNP to share information about what the LNP Government is delivering for Queensland."

An authorisation was added to the bio for the Queensland Breaking News Instagram account after questions from the ABC. (Supplied: Instagram)

While an authorisation was added, it is not a legal requirement. In Queensland, authorisation of election material, including social media accounts, is only required 12 months before a state election.

For the next state election in 2028, this will apply from October next year.

The Electoral Commission of Queensland does not regulate authorisation of election matter outside of the authorisation period.

It is among a string of politically linked accounts promoting themselves as news sources at both a state and federal level.

Queensland Politics Newsfeed, which is authorised by Queensland's Labor opposition, has been active since 2024 when the party was in office.

It posts reels from press conferences, with Opposition Leader Steven Miles prominently featured and uses the hashtag #qldupdates.

Across the border, an Instagram account with the handle @nswnewsroom states that it is authorised by the Labor Party. According to the Sydney Morning Herald, this detail was added after the government was approached for comment.

Australian National University marketing lecturer Dr Andrew Hughes said the accounts "purport" to be reporting on the news but only tell one side of the story.

"They are growing in popularity in use because they fall outside a hard definition of being election material which would then have to be authorised," he said.

"It is attractive to a party because it might take some time for someone, like a normal person, to deduct that is being done by a party."

The Instagram page posts press conferences from LNP politicians in Queensland. (Instagram: @qldbreakingnews)

Dr Hughes said political advertising laws should be changed to require the authorisation of content at all times to reflect the changing media landscape and strategies used to reach voters.

"In the age of digital and social media, it is ongoing, it does not stop," he said.

Dr Hughes said including an authorisation on an account allows a political party to say they are not hiding who they are and where the information is coming from.

"In the modern age of social media, you don't want to go for paid; you want to get the viral effect, which is it is shared by people among their networks," he said.
