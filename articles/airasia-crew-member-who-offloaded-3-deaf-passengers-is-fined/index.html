AirAsia crew member who offloaded 3 deaf passengers is fined P50,000, 9 years later

TheTools · 07.11.2026 09:35 · source · permalink

LIVE

A Puerto Princesa court convicted a former AirAsia senior cabin crew member of discriminating against three deaf passengers and fined her P50,000, nine years after they were removed from a flight. Disability advocates call it a landmark for equal access to transport. #DisabilityRights #AirAsia #PWD #Philippines

Three deaf passengers were ordered off their AirAsia flight in Puerto Princesa City in 2017 because they had no hearing companion. On October 29, a court convicted the former senior cabin crew member who removed them and fined her P50,000.

The Municipal Trial Court in Cities, Branch 1, in Puerto Princesa found Richie Rojas guilty of violating Republic Act 7277, the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons, as amended by RA 9442. The decision was dated October 29 and carried a P50,000 fine.

Her passenger, Khalil F. Haj, spent nearly a decade on the case. He was traveling with two other deaf Filipinos to a conference of the Filipino Sign Language Association when the crew stopped the group at Puerto Princesa City International Airport on May 30, 2017.

What the court found

The court rejected Rojas’s argument that she acted for flight safety. Haj was not violent and had harmed no one, the decision said, and the court found no genuine safety issue with him.

It also ruled that the three passengers were capable of traveling without an escort and were eligible to fly under AirAsia’s own policies, Inquirer.net reported.

The court found no malice on Rojas’s part but held that she intentionally refused to transport Haj because of his hearing disability, IMT News reported. That intent is what made the act a violation.

Why the rule she cited did not apply

Rojas had demanded that the group produce an able-bodied, hearing companion. The court examined AirAsia’s Ground Operations Manual, which describes a deaf passenger as a “self-reliant passenger” who may refuse escort assistance.

An able-bodied companion is required only for groups of four or more deaf passengers, radar.ph reported from the decision. Haj’s group had three.

The court said Rojas confronted the group again after ground staff had already guided them to their assigned seats, which showed she treated them as a safety problem simply for being deaf.

“This mistake is possible or even common among illiterate passengers, senior citizens, passengers unused to other alphabets/scripts or first time travellers, but they are not branded as security threats,” the decision read.

A settlement refused, and nine years of appeals

The complaint was filed in May 2017, but the trial began only in 2025. The decision traced the delay to repeated appeals filed by the defense, radar.ph reported.

IMT News reported that the Court of Appeals and the Supreme Court both upheld Haj’s position before the case returned to the trial court.

AirAsia and Rojas offered a reported P500,000 settlement during the proceedings, a figure the National Council on Disability Affairs also cited. Haj refused it, saying no amount of money could compensate for the discrimination, and pursued the case with student interns from the De La Salle University Legal Aid Clinic.

His two companions accepted the settlement terms, radar.ph reported.

What disability advocates want next

The National Council on Disability Affairs said the ruling came ahead of Deaf Awareness Week, which runs from November 10 to 16. “Equal access to transport is a right, not a privilege,” the agency said.

The agency urged airlines to train staff, review policies and ensure every passenger is treated with dignity, and called for mandatory disability inclusion training across the aviation sector.

Liza Martinez, convenor of the Filipino Sign Language National Network, called the ruling a victory for the Deaf community. She said Haj pursued the case to defend the right of persons with disabilities to equal treatment, IMT News reported.

Key facts

Why the case reaches beyond one flight

The ruling lands while the government is rebuilding its registry of persons with disabilities. By early November, about 190,000 PWDs held the new unified ID, and 2.7 million more are required to register by 2027.

Separately, the Anti-Red Tape Authority has told agencies and banks they cannot demand two valid IDs from applicants.

None of the three reports carried a statement from AirAsia Philippines on the ruling.
