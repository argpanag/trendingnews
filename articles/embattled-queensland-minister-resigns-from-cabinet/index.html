Embattled Queensland minister resigns from cabinet

Queensland MP Amanda Camm has stepped down as a cabinet frontbencher.

The member for Whitsunday held two portfolios, standing as Families, Seniors and Disability Services Minister, and Child Safety and the Prevention of Domestic and Family Violence Minister.

Shortly after 8am on Friday, the ABC reported Camm would no longer act in those roles.

Her resignation is expected to force a cabinet reshuffle in the Crisafulli LNP government.

Camm has been under fire in recent months and under mounting pressure to step down.

Earlier this year, she defended the state government’s decision to cut millions in funding from the domestic violence sector, following the alleged murder of a mother west of Brisbane.

Questions swirled over children in need of emergency shelter sleeping on the floor of government facilities, a $38.5 million cut to the domestic and family violence services budget, and a bungled IT system roll-out compromising responses to time-sensitive child safety matters.

She came under more scrutiny in August amid revelations that statements made during estimates might have been flawed.

And in September, reports revealed the group said to have taken up the work of a dumped domestic violence reform oversight office had not given Camm any progress update in the 16 months since it was set up.

Camm was caught in a public grilling when her undeclared romantic relationship with Sport Minister Tim Mander was brought to light earlier this year.

Questions centred on whether the pair had fulfilled their obligations to report any conflicts of interest and adhere to the parliamentary code of conduct.

The extramarital affair was again scrutinised when the Australian Federal Police investigated reports Mander had not been living at his declared address – which had been the home he shared with his wife Gayle, before their marriage ended in April 2025.
