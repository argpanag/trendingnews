John Glen on Royal International Air Tattoo returning to Boscombe Down

TheTools · 06.11.2026 02:10 · source · permalink

This week, I have been reflecting with great optimism and excitement on the major news that the Royal International Air Tattoo is making its return to Boscombe Down after 35 years, coinciding with the eve of the Salisbury 800 celebrations.

I met representatives of RIAT in Westminster last week and will be reaching out to people across the constituency in the coming weeks and months to ensure that south Wiltshire experiences maximum benefit and minimum disruption, while delivering a magnificent event.

The RIAT is one of the largest ticketed events in the country and the economic boost that so many national and international visitors could bring to the area is substantial.

I am encouraged to hear that hotels in a wide radius around Boscombe Down will see the benefit and I am told that advance block bookings have already been made.

Inevitable concerns about traffic have been anticipated and will be met by a vast ring of park and ride sites and specially laid on public transport connections both inside and outside the constituency and served by dedicated hopper buses.

There will be no general parking or direct public access to the site.

I am delighted to be able to bring RIAT to the upcoming Place Partnership meeting, to connect with local stakeholders and kick off the important conversation about how Salisbury can deliver for RIAT, while RIAT delivers for Salisbury.

In addition to the public spectacle, visitor influx, associated jobs and local economic boost, the prestigious STEM and defence industry connections associated with the event will be of great interest to our educational establishments and our existing defence science community.

The STEM/Science hub exhibition is hugely popular with children, and the event includes a dedicated day for schools.

The RAF Charitable Trust also directly supports STEM education and aviation engagement.

My constituency diary this week also includes the latest Pride in Place workshop, as some of the interesting and imaginative bids for funding to offer transformative facilities and experiences in Bemerton Heath come under scrutiny.

Over the weekend, I am attending Salisbury’s Remembrance Day observances as usual, as well as accompanying the Leader of the Opposition, Kemi Badenoch, to the Festival of Remembrance at The Royal Albert Hall.

Finally, thank you to all those local businesses who attended my annual budget breakfast analysis last Friday, hosted by Moore.

Of course, what we got from the budget was a lot of small measures but nothing of any substance — and still more burdens on the squeezed middle and the engines of growth — small employers.

Above all, last week’s Budget should have been the moment to properly fund defence, but instead we got more dither and delay and yet another plan to make a plan.
