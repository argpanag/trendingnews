Lawson equals career-best, to start third in Brazil after chaotic qualifying

TheTools · 07.11.2026 20:48 · source · permalink

Subscribe to listen

At a time where his Formula One future is largely out of his hands, qualifying third for the Brazilian Grand Prix is as good a statement Liam Lawson could have made.

In a chaotic qualifying session – delayed by more than an hour by torrential rain at Interlagos – the Kiwi earned himself his 10th top 10 start of the year, and potentially his most important in the context of his future in the sport.

While his best lap time, 1m 28.321s, was only enough for fifth place in the final stage of qualifying (Q3), grid penalties to Mercedes’ George Russell and Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc – five and 10 places respectively – saw the Kiwi promoted to the second row for the second time in his career.

Starting third sees Lawson equal the best qualifying position of his career, achieved at last year’s Azerbaijan Grand Prix, where he also managed his best result of fifth-place.

As another nudge to Red Bull’s decisionmakers, Lawson was the organisation’s fastest car across its two teams, with his closest stablemate being Isack Hadjar, who qualified seventh but will start fifth.

Lawson’s best lap time saw him 0.851s off the pace set by McLaren’s Lando Norris on pole, while newly-crowned world champion Kimi Antonelli starts alongside him on the front row.

As a sign of the chaos on track during the session, Lawson will be joined on the second row by Audi’s Nico Hulkenberg.

And with more rain expected over the course of the 71-lap race, Lawson will back himself to achieve something special in conditions he excels in.

The Brazilian Grand Prix begins at 6am on Monday.

As rain fell over Interlagos, a red flag less than two minutes into Q1 triggered a serious rethink of strategies for all teams. Once the session resumed close to 90 minutes later, drivers faced an effective shootout as the track dried and favoured cars that completed their laps as late as possible.

Despite regularly falling down the leaderboard, Lawson was never in any real danger of elimination, and saw his best time of 1m 32.085s enough for seventh place in Q1 to progress with more than 1.2s up his sleeve.

In a huge win for Racing Bulls, though, neither Alpine car could escape Q1, with Franco Colapinto and Pierre Gasly qualifying 17th and 18th respectively.

Entering Q2, Lawson’s chances were boosted when Max Verstappen didn’t return to the track, given his 30 place grid penalty, in order to save tyres, to free up a place in the top 10.

As conditions on track cleared, Lawson’s first effort of 1m 30.922s had him comfortably in the top 10. And once conditions cleared, the Kiwi changed for intermediate tyres to post a lap of 1m 27.049s to advance into the top 10 in second, just 0.304s off the pace of Russell as the session leader.

His teammate wasn’t as fortunate, as Arvid Lindblad stopped on track at turn five, and was eliminated in 12th, as Q2 was stopped when Haas’ debutant Rafael Camara hit the barriers to trigger a red flag with less than a minute left.

As more rain fell between Q2 and Q3, Lawson wasn’t able to better the time he set in the second part of qualifying, given the conditions, but was able to keep his composure to equal his career best, as another tick in the column of why he belongs in Formula One next year and beyond.
