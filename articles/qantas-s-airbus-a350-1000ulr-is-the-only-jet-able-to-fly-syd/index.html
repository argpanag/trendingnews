Qantas's Airbus A350-1000ULR Is the Only Jet Able to Fly Sydney to London Nonstop, Launch Now Slipping to Late 2027 | AviNews

TheTools · 20.11.2026 12:19 · source · permalink

Why It MattersUltra-long-range nonstops are gated by a single aircraft type, so any production or delivery slip moves the whole launch, and the route's economics rest on trading seats for fuel capacity.

What happened

Qantas plans to launch the world's longest commercial service, a nonstop Sydney–London flight covering 9,188 nautical miles (10,573 miles or 17,016 km) and scheduled to exceed 20 hours. The aircraft designated for it is the Airbus A350-1000ULR (Ultra Long Range). Qantas ordered 12 of the type from Airbus in May 2022 under its "Project Sunrise" plan to fly direct from Sydney Airport (SYD) to both New York John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) and London Heathrow Airport (LHR).

The standard A350-1000 has a range of about 9,000 nautical miles (16,668 km), just short of what the route requires. The -1000ULR adds structural improvements, reinforced landing gear for a higher maximum take-off weight, and a permanently fitted extra rear center tank holding 5,283 US gallons (20,000 liters). According to Airbus airport and maintenance planning documents, this lifts usable fuel capacity to 48,893 gallons (185,076 liters) and extends range to up to 9,800 nautical miles (18,150 km).

Qantas will configure its -1000ULRs with 238 seats in four classes: 6 first suites, 52 business suites, 40 premium economy seats and 140 economy seats. The standard -1000 typically carries 350 to 410 passengers in three classes and up to 480 at maximum. Singapore Airlines, operator of the Singapore–New York route, made a similar trade-off with its A350-900ULR, which carries 161 passengers versus 300 to 350 for the A350-900.

Per Boeing, the 777-8 has a range of 8,745 nautical miles and the 777-9 7,285 nautical miles. The Boeing 787 family, which enabled Qantas's Perth–London service from 2018, has a maximum range of 7,865 nautical miles.

The launch has been repeatedly delayed. The most recent delay moved first delivery to April 2027 and the inaugural commercial flight toward the end of 2027. Qantas said the first -1000ULR was in the paint shop in Toulouse as of late May, test flights were "just weeks away", and pilot training was underway at its new A350 simulator in Sydney. The airline said it will announce the first route and timing next month, that passengers will save up to four hours on the journey, and that three aircraft are needed for a daily nonstop service on either route.

Industry impact & what to watch

Ultra-long-range nonstops are an aircraft-gated market. When only one type can fly a sector with commercial payload, the airline's launch date is tied to that manufacturer's delivery slots, certification of the variant and crew training, and each slip in one of them moves the whole schedule. The repeated delays to Qantas's start date show how little room such a program has to absorb a setback.

The seat count shows the economics behind this kind of route. A cabin of 238 seats, against the 350 to 410 of the standard three-class A350-1000, means the operator is pricing the flight on premium demand and on the value of removing a connection, since the extra fuel tank displaces revenue capacity. Singapore Airlines made the same trade on its 161-seat A350-900ULR. Routes of this length tend to depend on a small number of premium cabins carrying the load.

The next dated milestone is Qantas's announcement, due next month, of the first route and its timing, which will show whether Sydney–London or Sydney–New York leads. Test flights, described as weeks away, and the April 2027 first delivery are the steps that would settle whether the end-of-2027 inaugural date holds.
