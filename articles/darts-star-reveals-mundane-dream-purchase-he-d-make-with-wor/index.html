Darts star reveals mundane dream purchase he'd make with World Championship cash

TheTools · 15.11.2026 19:47 · source · permalink
  • Jack Johnson, Sports Writer
  • Published: 19:47, 15 Nov 2026
  • Updated: 19:47, 15 Nov 2026

Andrew Gilding has revealed how he would 'sensibly' spend the PDC World Darts Championship's £1million prize money.

And it seems like Gilding certainly won't be making any flash purchases should he win the record amount of cash...

Instead, the 55-year-old, who will be going for glory at Alexandra Palace at the end of the year, has a more mundane plan.

He has revealed his dream buy if he claimed the money would be to have a full-size snooker table in his house.

Gilding would even put a chunk of it into a pension, chuckling at his own boring, but clever, decision.

Gilding 'sensible' £1m prize money plans

The World No.27, who is currently playing at the Grand Slam of Darts, had been asked about playing at a high level despite his age.

Two-time world champion Peter Wright has been struggling hugely at 56 years old, but Gilding is still thriving at 55.

"I imagine Peter's quite financially stable at the moment and I never really saved that much," he said, via Oche 180.

"I mean, I've got a bit in the pension but I still need to do it. I think it's need (to play) more than anything."

Gilding was then asked what he would do with the money should he win the World Championship at Ally Pally in the New Year.

"If I won the £1million, I've got real dreams about having a full-size snooker table at some point," Goldfinger admitted.

"I'm too sensible, that's the trouble. I'd put it into a pension. I can't help myself, I'm too sensible!"

How much money is on the line at the World Darts Championship?

Last year, for the first time ever, £1m was awarded to the winner of the PDC World Darts Championship at Alexandra Palace.

Luke Littler picked up that record-breaking prize in January, deciding to buy football cards with some of his winnings.

The same amount will be available this time around, with Gilding hoping to pull off a shock and lift the Sid Waddell Trophy.

But even if he doesn't, a huge cheque could be on its way...

The runner-up will take home £400,000, with the semi-final losers leaving with a hefty £200,000 each.

Quarter-finalists claim £100,000 each, with those who reached the fourth round walking away with £60,000 each.
