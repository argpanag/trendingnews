The Fall 2026 Anime Preview Guide - Overgeared

TheTools · 03.10.2026 17:11 · source · permalink

Youngwoo Shin is a deadbeat in real life, working odd jobs to barely pay off his loans. Even his family has written him off as a hopeless video game addict. But when he logs in to the massively popular VR game Satisfy, Youngwoo becomes Grid the Warrior…who is also painfully mediocre. Everything changes when he stumbles across a tome containing the lost knowledge of a legendary blacksmith. The catch is, using it resets all his levels. In order to survive after being forced back to square one.

Overgeared is based on the webtoon series by Team Argo and Monohumbug. The anime series is streaming on Crunchyroll on Sundays.

Outside of an attempt at World of Warcraft that lasted, as I recall, less than an hour, I've never played an MMORPG. I have loved ones who are devoted Final Fantasy XIV players and, being entrenched in the anime scene, I've seen my share of shows that are about playing MMOs. I've even enjoyed one or two of them! But I can't say I have a fair idea of whether or not a show is accurate to the experience of playing one.

Let's take Overgeared, for example. The game it features, Satisfy (the title rendered in letters that zoom toward the viewer like it's 2002 and we're watching a 3D movie), is so popular that it has two billion players. In-game events are featured on the news, and in-game status is linked to the players' real lives. One would expect a game that popular to sanitize player activities from one another so that, say, a single player can't cause the full destruction of an entire dungeon. Or a low-level player from accidentally stumbling into high-level quests for reasons that aren't entirely clear to me. Or to keep players' levels from going into the negative, rather than having an extended scene of suits talking about the potential effects. Like… wouldn't that cause an error unless you specifically programmed for it? And not just make the character stronger once he levels up?

It doesn't really matter, but it sure stuck in my craw, and the writing did nothing to assuage it. The plotting and pacing are totally amateurish, which doesn't surprise me because it originated as a web novel before being turned into a Webtoon and finally an anime. Stuff just kind of happens, and then more stuff happens, and I don't have a great grasp of just why. Grid is simultaneously the kind of self-centered clod who would straight-up ask a guy for his magic ring, but also genuinely concerned for the well-being of NPCs. His supposed super-powerful job and negative levels barely come into play, because the plot structure is non-existent.

I don't know what Overgeared is trying to do. It seems like it wants to be a comedy, but the jokes are rarely funny. It has some solid action, but the protagonist Grid is too weak to really take part. There are gestures at just how awful Grid's real life is, but he's enough of a putz that I really don't feel bad for him in his dark bedroom littered with empty soda bottles.

These shows about cynical teenagers or young adults just playing video games are a dime a dozen at this point, but I was pleasantly surprised at how entertained I was by Overgeared. While there is definitely some foreshadowing and implications about the show's in-game mechanics that could have a good payoff later on, most of the entertainment came from the show's voice acting and directing. The way main character Grid reacts to everything and the comedic timing is so over the top that I laughed anytime there was a quick cut or dramatic shift. It also helps that this show isn't trying to take itself too seriously by blasting through a lot of the standard exposition to get to those scenes. This doesn't feel like a show that really wants me to fully engage with its drama, even if there are one or two dramatic beats here because I'm supposed to just be along for the ride.

Even the generic art style of the game world and music feels like it's at least partially intentional. This is supposed to feel like every RPG I've ever seen, and the main appeal is going to be in how Grid specifically ends up breaking and redefining the game. The idea that he starts as such a loser at a negative level because of a weird exploit in the system isn't that interesting on its face. But the implication that his base level could end up getting so strong it surpasses everybody else, since he still gains experience points by leveling up negative levels, does at least offer a plausible explanation for how Grid could end up very powerful in the future. What I'm concerned about is whether or not the show will be able to maintain this fast-paced cynical comedy while also being that potential wish fulfillment power fantasy.

Part of what makes the show so funny is how much of a loser and money-grubby Grid is. If he ends up becoming this broken soldier in the game, then there's no way that humor would work. I wonder if this means the show will constantly introduce bad luck and other obstacles for him to deal with, even if he ends up getting stronger. I'll give the show another episode or two to see what the actual narrative progression will be. If it can maintain this level of comedy, then it can definitely be a good junk food show that I can laugh at, which I will always take over something boring and derivative.

I can't believe it. For so many years, it has been an immutable law written into the stone of the anime industry's foundations: “When a new season begins, thou shalt put up with yet another Sword Art Online ripoff that sucks harder than Nancy Reagan at the 1976 Republican National Convention after-parties.” Here we are, though, at the kick-off of Fall 2026, and Overgeared…doesn't suck? In fact, it's somehow really quite good? I can hardly find the words to express what I am feeling right now. I would toss out a reference to that line from Contact where Jodie Foster looks out into the vast beauty of infinite space and says, “They should have brought a poet…,” except I got my damned degree in writing poetry and fiction, and I'm still at a loss.

Overgeared has a few things going for it that make it stand leaps and bounds above the dregs of also-rans and has-beens choking up the mass graves of the RPG-Lit slop pits. For one, I love the fact that we're not just spending all of our time stuck in the generic wastelands of another generic fantasy MMO. I mean, it is true that “Satisfy” is not going to be beating the plagiarism allegations from Bethesda Softworks' lawyers any time soon, but I can deal with that considering how Overgeared is actually taking the time to flesh out how the hyper-popular video game actually interacts with the real world.

This goes hand-in-hand with making our hero Takumi into a likeable protagonist with a discernible personality and human character traits. It's already nice enough that his dorky and pathetic tendencies help to balance out his inevitable climb up the ranks of the overpowered echelons. His story is even more compelling when you can contrast his almost-epic adventures in the world of Satisfy against his humdrum everyday life. The biggest laugh of the premiere comes from Takumi trying to call up the news to report his involvement in the headline-generating clash between the world's most legendary heroes, only for the operator to immediately hang up on him once she hears that his level is officially negative three. The English dub of the first episode doesn't give many other characters enough time to truly shine, which means Ricco Fajardo has a lot to do to essentially carry the premiere, and I think he's a great fit for Takumi. He manages to deliver a performance that makes Takumi sound believably capable while still coming across as a giant doofus, which is key.

I also have to commend J.C. Staff for producing an adaptation that looks pretty decent. It's not amazing, by any means, and if I'm being honest, I think the studio's color grading and effects usage can look fairly tacky here. A “tacky” is a very different beast from an “ugly” anime, though, and I can deal with some chintzy visuals if it means there's some actual life in the storyboards and animation. Besides, the real secret sauce of Overgeared's production values lies in its editing and pacing. This show has jokes, like so many anime do, but Overgeared's jokes are actually funny most of the time, which is a crucial differentiator.

Overall, I would probably be losing my mind if Overgeared had premiered five years ago and wasn't fighting against the inexorable tides of the 21st century's most oversaturated genre trends. As is, I will remain appropriately impressed that Overgeared is as entertaining and watchable as it is. Let's just hope it doesn't lose too much of that steam as the season continues.

There's an art to making your protagonist a “likable asshole.” They need some redeeming quality to balance their negative tendencies. Maybe they are just that good in actual combat or their past is so filled with misfortune that you can't help but empathize with them. And while Overgeared may be trying to do the latter with Grid, his tragic backstory of—let me check my notes here—“being a normal, average guy with a normal average family” does nothing to outweigh the mountain of negative character traits he embodies.

Grid is selfish, cowardly, and has a victim complex. He wants fame and fortune and has decided playing a video game is the way to get it. The fact that he's failed spectacularly despite what he sees as working hard is just more proof to him that the world is out to get him. Or to put it another way, there is nothing “likable” about this asshole.

But perhaps I've taken this all the wrong way. Maybe I'm supposed to hate Grid—laugh at the misfortune of his own making and the temper tantrums he throws in their wake. The issue with this is that Grid has done nothing to make me hate him enough to revel in his misfortune.

He's done nothing to hurt anyone else—well, beyond waking his family up in the middle of the night during a bout of impotent rage. And while he may be freeloading in his parents' house, he pays for his game expenses by working a hard manual-labor job. And, in his own way, he is trying his best—even if it's borne no fruit as of yet.

Far from being someone I love to hate, I just find Grid annoying. One episode in, I don't really care what happens to him. I'd rather just avoid him. I have no investment in his (mis) adventures—and that means I have no real interest in watching the next episode. And that is why this premiere episode fails so spectacularly despite ample budget and solid voice acting.
