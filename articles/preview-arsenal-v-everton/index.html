Preview: Arsenal v Everton

TheTools · 24.10.2026 14:25 · source · permalink

We’re back in Premier League action on Saturday (3pm) as Everton head to Emirates Stadium, where we’ll look to claim a seventh straight win on our own patch.

After our midweek draw against Bayern Munich, we can turn our attention back to the league, as Mikel Arteta welcomes his former Toffees manager, David Moyes, to north London hoping to end the day closer to league leaders Manchester City than we started.

Both teams enter this game having been beaten just once this term, while the Toffees haven’t lost on the road. They would be delighted to leave N5 with that record intact, having emerged victorious in just one of their last 30 away Premier League matches against us.

HARD TO BEAT

After a second consecutive 13th-place finish last term, Everton remained thrifty in the summer, spending less on new players than they did in recuperating £65m from the sale of winger Iliman Ndiaye to Manchester City. New additions include Hayden Hackney from Middlesbrough and former Gunners Ainsley Maitland-Niles and Christian Norgaard, while Jack Grealish returned for a second loan spell.

The prospects for an improved campaign are encouraging, despite their unbeaten start ending with a 2-0 home defeat to Chelsea last weekend. They enter this round of games in eighth position with 10 points, including Merseyside wins over Crystal Palace and Ipswich Town, plus a late 2-2 draw against Manchester United. 

However the Toffees are winless in their last eight league away games, with all three this season ending level – 1-1 at Bournemouth, 0-0 at Tottenham and 2-2 at Hull City. They have also progressed to the fourth round of the Carabao Cup after knocking out Wolves and Preston North End.

WHAT THE MANAGERS SAY

Arteta: "We’re having three games in six days so we know what’s happening, especially when the games are so demanding and the necessity is to win. We know that, so we prepare the players for those scenarios and do the best we possibly can.

"I know David so well and how he sets the team, the quality they have in their organisation and individually. It’s no coincidence they didn’t lose before their last game. They have a really good team, which we know because in recent clashes, it’s always been a really difficult game for us."

Moyes: It's not an easy game and never has been over many years. We look forward to it; they had a game in midweek, and we have to try and capitalise on that. I thought we did really well last year against them; we played really well down at Arsenal, and I hope we can do the same.

"When I look back at the team we had out, we were pretty threadbare at that time, but we did a good job, and I hope we can do it again. Obviously Arsenal have some nany different tools in their bag and people they can use in different positions, so we're fully aware of the team we're up against and the level."

TEAM NEWS

Bukayo Saka came off early on against Bayern Munich on Wednesday night with a quad issue, which Mikel Arteta has suggested will continue to be assessed by specialists over the coming days, but he will miss this game.

Piero Hincapie is back in training after his ankle injury, but Christos Tzolis (hamstring) and William Saliba (back) are continuing their recoveries.

Moyes meanwhile has indicated he has a few injury issues of his own, which could include Jarrad Branthwaite after his heavy collision with Emiliano Martinez last weekend, which left the Chelsea goalkeeper with a broken jaw.

Former Gunner Christian Norgaard recovered from a groin injury to make a belated debut against the Blues, but Moyes believes he isn't quite match-fit yet, while it remains to be seen if another familiar face in Ainsley Maitland-Niles lines up against us, or whether Jake O’Brien starts at right-back.

TALKING TACTICS

Adrian Clarke, writing in the official matchday programme: Overall, Everton have a balanced style, but physicality and aggression are always at the heart of what they do best. Centre-backs James Tarkowski and Branthwaite want to dominate their duels and lay the foundation for the rest of the side to thrive. The duo, alongside striker Thierno Barry, boast serious aerial power, with three set-piece goals registered already.

A new-look central midfield of the technically excellent James Garner and 19-year-old Harrison Armstrong is also functioning extremely well. With Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall also working in close proximity, the engine room is an area of the pitch Everton aren't dominated in too often.

Pressing has become a core part of their armoury. While they are content to drop deep and defend their own box when necessary, the Merseysiders are also not afraid to hunt in packs higher up the pitch. They have enjoyed a joint league-high 14 shot-ending high turnovers so far, and are very dangerous in those situations.

Moyes will be disappointed with their finishing. They have created an impressive 22 big chances but have converted just four into goals. The full-back areas are also slightly concerning, with multiple errors from them leading to goals.

FACTS AND STATS

We are unbeaten in our last seven Premier League matches against Everton, our best run against the Toffees since going 13 without defeat between December 2007 and December 2013.

Under David Moyes, the Toffees have never won away at us in 13 league attempts.

No side have conceded fewer Premier League goals than us this season, while we have also faced fewer shots on target (21) and have a lower xG against (5.6) than any other side.

Everton have conceded just once in the first half, fewer than any other Premier League team. Meanwhile, each of our last six goals in the competition have come after half-time.

We have won our last two Premier League games against Everton without conceding a goal; only in March 1999 have we achieved three in a row in the competition.

Everton are winless in six Premier League games in London (D3 L3), failing to score in their last two.

Martin Odegaard has made more line-breaking passes leading to a goal than any other player in the Premier League. He has also been directly involved in four goals (2 goals, 2 assists) in his last four Premier League home games against Everton.

Declan Rice has assisted four of our last six league goals, with the last three being match-winning goal contributions.

MATCH OFFICIALS

Tony Harrington has been given this game, which is only the fourth time he has refereed us. We have won the previous three, a 3-1 success against Southampton in October 2024, a 2-0 win against Brentford last December and the 4-1 FA Cup triumph at Portsmouth the following month.

The Yorkshireman is better known to the Toffees, who have only lost three of 12 games he’s presided over. They have won the last three, which have all been on the road, including victories at Old Trafford, Villa Park and a Carabao Cup victory at Preston back in August. His three top-flight outings this term have seen 10 yellow cards, one red and two penalties.

EVERTON’S RECENT VISITS

The Toffees haven’t enjoyed playing at Highbury and Emirates Stadium in the Premier League era, winning just two of their 34 trips, in 1996 and 2021. Indeed, they have lost 10 of their last 12 visits, during which time we’ve scored 30 goals, or an average of 2.5 per game.

Last season’s encounter will live long in the memory as we left it late to snatch a priceless victory, with Viktor Gyokeres netting in the 89th minute before Max Dowman became the Premier League’s youngest-ever scorer with his first Gunners goal.

The season before brought a goalless draw in December 2024, while in May 2024 we faced each other on the final day of the season. The visitors did take the lead when Idrissa Gueye saw a free-kick deflect past David Raya, but still in with a chance of the title, we came back and Takehiro Tomiyasu equalised before Kai Havertz grabbed a late winner.
