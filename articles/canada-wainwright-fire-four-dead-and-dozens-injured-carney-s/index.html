Canada Wainwright fire: Four dead and dozens injured, Carney says - follow live

TheTools · 18.10.2026 20:22 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Four service members have been killed and dozens wounded after a fire at a Canadian military base, Prime Minister Mark Carney says - here's what we know so far

  • Carney says it's "one of the worst tragedies" for the military in peacetime, as he describes a "violent, fast-moving" fire on Saturday

  • "It was like a scene out of a war movie," says a hospital resident who witnessed a military vehicle and ambulances bringing injured people in

  • A defence spokesperson says that, while investigations are under way, "at this time, there are no indications that it was the result of any terrorist activity"

  • Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, where the fire broke out about 16:00 local time (22:00 GMT) on Saturday, is one of the country's busiest military training bases

  • This is particularly challenging for Mark Carney, as the incident comes just days before Albertans vote in a referendum on whether to pursue independence, writes our Canada digital editor Jessica Murphy

Four service members have been killed and dozens wounded after a fire at a Canadian military base, Prime Minister Mark Carney says - here's what we know so far

Carney says it's "one of the worst tragedies" for the military in peacetime, as he describes a "violent, fast-moving" fire on Saturday

"It was like a scene out of a war movie," says a hospital resident who witnessed a military vehicle and ambulances bringing injured people in

A defence spokesperson says that, while investigations are under way, "at this time, there are no indications that it was the result of any terrorist activity"

Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, where the fire broke out about 16:00 local time (22:00 GMT) on Saturday, is one of the country's busiest military training bases

This is particularly challenging for Mark Carney, as the incident comes just days before Albertans vote in a referendum on whether to pursue independence, writes our Canada digital editor Jessica Murphy

Live Reporting

Edited by Tinshui Yeung and Dulcie Lee, with Nadine Yousif reporting from Alberta, Canada

  1. 'It was like a scene out of a war movie,' says hospital residentpublished at 21:19 BST
    21:19 BST

    Brandon Simmons, who lives in long-term care at the Wainwright Health Centre, witnessed the aftermath of the fire at the military base last night.

    He tells Reuters news agency that the site went into code orange and that nurses had dropped everything and immediately started grabbing stretchers, gurneys and any medical equipment they could before running to the front entrance.

    At the same time, he says, a military vehicle arrived at the centre and started unloading injured people, while nurses tried to stabilise everyone and “calm them down”.

    Brandon says he remembers seeing people with burns, while one person had their clothes "blown off" and was wearing only underwear.

    "I heard people screaming inside of those vehicles that will never end up being out of my mind," he recalls, saying it was "like a scene from a war movie".

    Brandon says ambulances kept coming "throughout the night", with between 12 and 20 parked along the road at one time.

  2. 'Code orange' emergency designation deactivatedpublished at 21:00 BST
    21:00 BST

    All “code orange” activations in Alberta have now been halted, Acute Care Alberta says in a statement.

    A code orange is an emergency designation used in Canada during a disaster or mass-casualty incident involving multiple severely injured patients who need medical care.

    The statement also reports the scale of the emergency response.

    It says Emergency Health Services Alberta deployed 30 units directly to Wainwright, including 22 ground ambulances, six fixed-wing units and two rotary-wing units.

    The agency says the active emergency response was sustained across the system for more than 10 hours.

    As of today, patients have been transferred from Wainwright Health Centre to four facilities: the University of Alberta Hospital, Royal Alexandra Hospital, Foothills Medical Centre and Red Deer Regional Hospital Centre, the agency adds.

  4. Loss of life 'heartbreaking', says Alberta premierpublished at 20:20 BST
    20:20 BST

    Alberta Premier Danielle Smith says the province is in mourning alongside those affected by the “tragic incident” at the Wainwright military base.

    “The loss of life and injuries are heartbreaking,” she writes on social media, adding that her thoughts are with those “facing uncertainty and grief during this incredibly difficult time”.

    Smith says she will travel to Edmonton later today to meet provincial ministers and federal officials. An Emergency Management Cabinet Coordination Committee has also been convened.

  6. Members of the Princess Pat's infantry among those affectedpublished at 19:32 BST
    19:32 BST

    During his address, Prime Minister Mark Carney said those affected by the fire at Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Wainwright included reservists and soldiers from the Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI).

    Carney described the infantry as a force that has "fought for freedom, democracy, human dignity", noting it served in the first and second world wars, Korea and Afghanistan.

    The PPCLI is one of several front-line infantry regiments in Canada’s armed forces. It has about 2,000 soldiers, divided into three battalions, two of which are based at CFB Edmonton, in Alberta, while the other is based at CFB Shilo, in Manitoba.

    The regiment was founded in Ottawa in 1914 for service in the First World War, joining Allied forces in France and Belgium. Several PPCLI companies and platoons also served in Afghanistan from 2002, following the 9/11 terror attacks, until 2014.

  7. What we know and don’t know about the deadly fire at Wainwright military basepublished at 19:00 BST
    19:00 BST

    After Mark Carney’s earlier statement, we now know a bit more about the incident at Wainwright military base.

    Here’s where things stand:

    What we know

    • Four service members have been killed and dozens more wounded in a large fire near a Canadian military base in Alberta
    • The fire broke out at about 16:00 local time (22:00 GMT) on Saturday at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, one of the country’s busiest military training bases
    • Royal Alexandra Hospital in Edmonton declared a code orange - a disaster response protocol for an incident with many casualties
    • Authorities say there are no indications the incident was the result of any “terrorist activity”
    • A “detailed account and formal briefing” will be provided later today, Carney said

    What we don’t know

    • The exact cause of the fire remains unclear
    • Whether more fatalities will be announced
    • The identities of those killed and injured in the fire

  8. Military base fire comes days before Alberta referendumpublished at 18:38 BST
    18:38 BST

    Jessica Murphy
    Canada digital editor

    This would be a challenging moment for any prime minister. For Mark Carney, this comes at an especially delicate time politically in Alberta.

    On Monday, Albertans will vote in a referendum on whether to pursue independence - on of the most significant challenges to Canadian unity in decades.

    Carney just now made no mention of that vote - it would not have been the place.

    But he made clear that Canada as a whole would be there for the wounded and the families of all the victims, many of whom will hail from that western province, just like Carney.

    "In Canada, we take care of each other," he said in his brief remarks.

    He also highlighted the close collaboration between the federal government and the provincial government in the immediate response to the crisis.

    It is also a significant blow to a military that is only now beginning to receive significant government support after decades of underfunding.

    Ottawa is funneling in billions to shore up old equipment, boost pay and get the armed forces up to Nato spending compliance.

    It will now face scrutiny over how it handled this tragedy - one Carney called "one of the worst tragedies for the forces on Canadian soil in peacetime".

  9. Wainwright military base is one of Canada’s busiest training sitespublished at 18:23 BST
    18:23 BST

    Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Wainwright, where the incident took place on Saturday, is one of the country's busiest military training bases.

    Established in 1939, it is 210km (130 miles) east of Edmonton, Alberta's capital.

    It is one of the main bases used by the Canadian military for live-fire training exercises. About 690 military personnel and 300 civilian workers are on site on a typical day.

    The chairperson of Nato’s Military Committee, Admiral Giuseppe Cavo Dragone, said the base houses troops from Canada and other allied countries.
