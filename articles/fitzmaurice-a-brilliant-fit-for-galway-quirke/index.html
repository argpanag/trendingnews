Fitzmaurice a 'brilliant fit' for Galway - Quirke

TheTools · 05.10.2026 12:05 · source · permalink

As newly appointed Galway football manager Éamonn Fitzmaurice settles into his first full week in the job, his former Kerry team-mate Mike Quirke believes it could be the start of an 'exciting time' for the Tribesmen.

Fitzmaurice was officially appointed as Padraic Joyce's successor last week on a three-year term and will now look to guide his new charges to a first All-Ireland crown since 2001.

The ex-Kerry boss is returning to the inter-county game after an absence of eight seasons, having stepped down as Kingdom manager following the conclusion of the 2018 championship.

During his six years in charge, Fitzmaurice helped Kerry to the 2014 All-Ireland title, having collected three Celtic Crosses during his playing career - including one at Galway's expense in 2000.

Quirke - who also spent time as part of an All-Ireland winning Kerry management team in 2022 - says that it looks like a natural move for the Finuge clubman.

"I think from a football perspective, it’s a brilliant fit," Quirke said on the latest edition of the RTÉ GAA Podcast.

"Éamonn has obviously been out of the game at senior inter-county level for a number of years, he obviously was so embedded in the FRC and looking at the rules and through his media stuff then as well had a really close connection with the game.

"I think Galway are a team that have been everyone’s second favourite team or close enough to it for a number of years, so I think from a football point of view it makes loads of sense."

Fitzmaurice may not have been involved in inter-county management in recent years, but he has been heavily involved in other strands of the game, as a pundit with RTÉ and the Irish Examiner, while he also played a key role alongside Jim Gavin as part of the FRC in formulating football’s new rules two seasons ago.

He has also been heavily involved in coaching at club and schools level in Kerry and, in some quarters, had been touted as a possible contender to return to the manager’s role in his own county whenever Jack O’Connor decides to step away.

However, Quirke believes the prospect of taking on a Galway team viewed as All-Ireland contenders was enough to tempt him back into the inter-county fold.

"I just think this opportunity was too big to turn down," he said.

"Galway under Padraic Joyce have been the nearly men, they could have won two All-Irelands at least. I was involved with Kerry in 2022 when we narrowly beat them in the final, Shane Walsh put on an unbelievable exhibition that day and they could easily have won that game. It was the same against Armagh a couple of years later, they were right there and they’ve become a really relevant force.

"There’s a lot of symbiosis between Kerry and Galway, there’s a lot of traditional ideals of how the game is played in both of those counties and I think it would have taken something like that for Éamonn to get back involved.

"I don’t think it was going to be a county that didn’t have a lot of similarities to the way Kerry like to play football and they way it is held within the county, it’s such a big deal in Galway.

"Obviously hurling is huge, but it’s a big, big footballing county with a lot of proud history in the way the game is played. I think that was a huge factor in it as well, it just suits the way Éamonn himself who has been so embedded in Kerry football for a huge number of years through minor, Under-20 and senior and then into management, I think it would have taken something like that to really pique that interest to get back into the game."

In terms of how Galway might play under their new manager, Quirke says Fitzmaurice will likely try and implement his own style.

"I think Éamonn is a traditionalist, he’ll want to move the ball with the foot as often as possible, from back to front with as many kick passes as you possibly can.

"His ideals are born out of Páidí Ó’Sé playing football, Jack O’Connor, Pat O’Shea, guys he would have played under as a player and those ideals are the pillars that Kerry football try to play through.

"Kicking the ball, essentially defend a little bit more man to man but then at times but then at times you have to adapt to the new game and get yourself set in a zone and play the game that way.

"I think Éamonn will do a good job of marrying those traditional ideals that are very important to Galway football as well."

The Galway club championship has reached the semi-final stage, with the last four matches in the senior grade down for decision next weekend. Fitzmaurice is likely to be in attendance as he turns his attention to assembling a backroom team and selecting a panel to begin the 2027 season.

Quirke says that Fitzmaurice’s arrival in the county is likely to provide a sense of enthusiasm within the current playing squad and could provide the spark for a big season.

"This will give a lot of those guys a real burst of energy that we can still do this.

"Not that Padraic Joyce didn’t do a great job, but everything comes to a natural conclusion and I think it probably had for Galway and the idea of Éamonn Fitzmaurice coming in with new ideas, a really clear perspective on the new rules and how to get the very most out of those, I think that will give guys a real shot in the arm.

"Then you’d look at some of the older guys, will Paul Conroy go now or will he stay, can he get the most out of Damien Comer and Shane Walsh and these guys?

"Injuries have been a really big part of the Galway story for the last four or five years, getting their best team on the field has been a challenge for them for a myriad of different reasons.

"That side of things is going to be crucial for Éamonn and I think it will bring a freshness that guys will want to play, guys that haven’t been involved for the last few years of Padraic’s term will want to really make a stake and get back in there, because it could be a really exciting time."
