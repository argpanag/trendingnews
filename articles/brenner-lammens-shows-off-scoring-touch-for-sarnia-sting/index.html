Brenner Lammens shows off scoring touch for Sarnia Sting

TheTools · 09.10.2026 19:38 · source · permalink

Brenner Lammens likes to be a headache for goalies.

Brenner Lammens likes to be a headache for goalies.

He’s doing that now by putting pucks behind them, not just causing mayhem in front of them.

The second-year forward is happy to provide more offence for the Sarnia Sting than he did last season.

“I had something to prove that way,” said Lammens, 17. “Everybody knows that I could play the physical game and be in the dirty areas, but . . . I needed to prove that I could score goals and help our team win games by putting some goals up.”

He went into the weekend sharing the team lead with five goals in seven games. Last season, he didn’t score his fifth goal until the 31st game.

He played a bigger role as his rookie year went on, finishing with nine goals and nine assists in 59 games.

“I learned a lot throughout the year,” said the No. 4 pick in the 2025 OHL draft. “I got some opportunity and I proved that I could help our team out in the big situations.”

He started this season with seven points in seven games, thanks to his five-game goal streak.

“What we were seeing at the end of last year is kind of what we’re seeing now,” head coach Mathieu Turcotte said. “He was getting chances, he was playing the right way, he was playing really hard.

“I feel that he took his summer so seriously. He’s in the best shape of his life. His skating improved. His shot improved. He’s basically (getting) a point a game and playing a type of game that not many kids play at his age.

“He’s had a great start and he’s definitely an important part of our team going forward.”

Lammens feels like himself again. Last season, his self-belief waned at times.

“Getting my confidence back is really important for me,” he said. “I lost it a bit last year. I wasn’t playing a whole lot.

“Once I started to get in that groove with Turcs, my game took a jump and I knew the momentum I had from the end of last year was going to be big for my summer.”

Staying on the ice helps as well. He has a better idea when to be an agitator and when to stay out of opponents’ faces.

“He’s a lot more disciplined,” Turcotte said. “He only has 10 minutes in seven games, which is not very much for a guy that plays on the edge like him.

“He’s able to stay physical, to stay engaged, to stay hungry around the net and not take very many penalties. Even that’s improved.

“He’s just improved on absolutely everything. I’m happy to see him go. He’s a big part of our team, a big part of our offence.”

Turcotte complained a few times last season about opposing teams going after the then 16-year-old, but Lammens knew why they did.

“I know I’m not innocent, too,” he said, laughing. “I’m always doing something. I get under the other team’s skin once in awhile.”

Even as a rookie, he was eager to throw his body around. A seven-time Canadian amateur motocross champion, he’ll take on all comers.

“One of the big things I had to learn last year was when to go be more physical,” he said. “We had a little bit of a smaller team, so try to make space for some of my linemates.

“This year, we’re fortunate we’ve got a lot bigger team, so there’s a lot more space for me, too. You’ve just got to pick your moments in the game when to take that jump or finish the hit.”

The Sting have 10 players who are at least six-foot-two.

“Last year, I was one of the bigger guys, and I wasn’t too big,” the six-foot-one, 195-pound Lammens said. “This year, I feel like a small guy, and I’ve gotten bigger.

“I think that’s good for us. It creates space for everybody. It puts the D (defence) on their heels a bit when we’re forechecking them and allows us to get the puck back, which we do a lot on the forecheck.”
