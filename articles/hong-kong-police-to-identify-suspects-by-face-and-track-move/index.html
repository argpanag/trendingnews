Hong Kong Police to Identify Suspects by Face and Track Movements

TheTools · 15.11.2026 11:35 · source · permalink

Starting from the 16th, Hong Kong police will integrate AI facial recognition into their smart surveillance network to track the past movements of suspects in video footage and monitor their real-time locations. While the police explained that this technology is for national security and crime prevention, concerns have been raised that it could infringe on personal privacy and human rights.

Searching for faces in videos starting from the 16th

The ‘Smart View’ smart surveillance network will add features that allow for the input of a specific person's facial features to locate them in past surveillance footage and track their movement paths captured by multiple cameras.

Real-time location tracking is also possible. It can be used not only for criminal suspects but also by registering photos of missing persons to receive alerts as soon as they are captured by cameras; police explained this could help in quickly locating high-risk missing elderly people suffering from dementia.

60,000 cameras by 2028

This function will be added to the Smart View project, which began in 2024. Hong Kong has been connecting CCTV cameras from police and public institutions, focusing on areas with frequent crimes or high foot traffic, and aims to have approximately 60,000 cameras installed across Hong Kong by 2028. More than 10,000 cameras have been installed so far.

The scope of integration may include major public and transportation facilities such as the Hong Kong International Airport, Kai Tak Sports Park, cross-harbor tunnels, MTR stations, and public housing estates. Video footage from cameras operated by third parties, such as government departments and public institutions, can also be connected to the system.

Hong Kong police stated that Smart View operates on a dedicated fiber-optic network to respond to external hacking and cybersecurity risks. They explained that the structure allows footage from external or commercial cameras to enter police servers through a firewall. Police stated that as of June 30, 2026, Smart View footage had contributed to solving 1,659 cases, including major crimes such as murder, robbery, and assault.

Clash between public safety and privacy protection

Hong Kong Police Commissioner Siu Chak-yee presented facial recognition technology as a means to safeguard national security, prevent and detect crime, and ensure public safety. He said that they have the ability to arrest people who commit crimes and that this technology can act as a safety net to protect law-abiding citizens.

However, as there has been a massive crackdown on anti-government protesters in the past in Hong Kong, concerns are being raised that the expansion of the surveillance network could be used to broadly track the movements and identities of citizens. In one private residential complex that attempted to introduce a facial recognition access control system, some residents raised issues, leading authorities to launch an investigation.

Hong Kong’s personal data-related laws and authority guidelines regulate the collection, retention, processing, and use of personal information. However, the specific retention period for this facial recognition data, the scope of the database, and the procedure for filing objections in cases of misidentification were not disclosed in the police's recent announcement.
