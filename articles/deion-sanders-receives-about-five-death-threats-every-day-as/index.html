Deion Sanders receives 'about five' death threats every day as Colorado football coach

TheTools · 20.11.2026 11:04 · source · permalink

Police records showed at least 15 documented cases of harassment or suspicious activity since 2023

Just a few years ago, Colorado’s football program had largely been forgotten. Then Pro Football Hall of Famer Deion Sanders arrived in Boulder, bringing his larger-than-life personality and celebrity to the program.

Colorado’s on-field results under Sanders have been uneven, but his tenure has also been marked by significant off-field challenges. Sanders has continued coaching while managing serious health issues. More recently, police records obtained by The Athletic detailed how his personal safety has been at risk at times.

According to a September report, Sanders regularly received death threats. At one point, he said he was receiving "about five" threats per day. Public records from Boulder shed light on how authorities handled many of those situations.

In May 2023, a pair of university police officers waited for Sanders outside his office. Once the two-time Super Bowl winner met the officers, they told Sanders about a series of threatening voicemails on a Colorado football ticket office phone line that mentioned him.

At the time, Sanders was just six months into the job and had yet to coach a game. "I guess this confirms what you are working on," Sanders told Evis McGee, a member of his personal security team, after learning about the voicemails, per the police report.

The voicemails proved not to be an isolated incident. The Athletic ultimately discovered 15 closed University of Colorado police cases involving harassment or suspicious activity directed at Sanders since he left Jackson State and was named the Buffaloes head coach. Two additional cases remain open, with records unavailable, and most stem from 2023.

In a YouTube video posted in January by Sanders’ son, Deion Jr., Michael Rhodes warned players to "be mindful of who comes in [the facility] with you, especially on the elevator." Rhodes delivered those comments during an introductory meeting ahead of the 2026 season.

"Don’t let somebody coerce you into scanning them up on the floor, because everybody’s trying to get to Coach Prime," he continued. Rhodes is the head of Sanders' university-paid security team.

"Some people say, ‘Coach, what do you need a head of security for?’ We get death threats all the time," said Sanders. "Why? I’m a Black man making it happen. … That stuff is real.

"How many death threats we get?" Sanders was shown in the video asking Rhodes.

He responded: "About five every day."

Colorado athletics said its security efforts involve Sanders’ personal security team as well as the CU Boulder Division of Public Safety.

"The safety of Coach Prime is our top priority, and we take every threat seriously," the athletics department said in a statement. "CU Athletics works closely with the CU Boulder Division of Public Safety to coordinate security efforts with Coach Prime’s entire team, including his security detail, and respond appropriately to reported threats."

Nearly half of the 15 closed cases The Athletic reviewed were found to have ties to one individual who repeatedly mailed threatening letters. The letters contained incoherent passages and biblical references, along with racist remarks directed at Sanders.
