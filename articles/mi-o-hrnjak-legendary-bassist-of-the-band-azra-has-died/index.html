Mišo Hrnjak, legendary bassist of the band Azra, has died.

TheTools · 01.11.2026 09:13 · source · permalink

He spent the last years of his life in England and Canada, where his children moved, and Croatian media wrote that he had been in very poor health for a long time.

Mišo Hrnjak (76), the legendary bassist of the original line-up of the band Azra, died after a long and serious illness, confirmed the drummer of the band's first line-up, Boris Lajner, Croatian media reported just before midnight between Saturday and Sunday.

Hrnjak became part of the three-member Azra in the second half of 1979, after the leader and sole author of the group, Branimir Štulić, unsuccessfully tried to form a band with the team that later created the very successful band – Film.

The group's first single with the songs "Balkan" and "A šta da radim" was released in late 1979 and attracted significant attention from critics, and the then very influential Džuboks magazine declared it the best single of the year.

In mid-1980, they recorded their first album – "Azra" – which was released just a few days after their first performance in Belgrade, at an open-air handball court not far from the Pionir Hall. That same evening, on August 26, they also played at the Belgrade Youth Center, and Hrnjak and Lajner immediately proved to be an excellent (and would prove to be indispensable) accompaniment to the unbridled and energetic Štulić.

The group played with an unchanged lineup on all of its most significant albums, the double "Sunčana strana ulice" (1981), the double "Filigranski pločnici" (1981), and the triple live album "Ravno do dna", recorded in Kulušić, Zagreb, a year earlier.

In 1982, Hrnjak went to serve his military service (Surdulica, Leskovac), where he played in a military orchestra, but upon returning to Zagreb, he decided to end his musical career. He joined the religious community Božja djeca (Children of God), traveled around Europe, and made children's toys.

He later moved to Hungary, started a family, had five children with his wife Ursula, and, among other things, worked as a carpenter.

Almost 20 years later, while filming a documentary about 1980s music ("Happy Child"), he met up with Liner again, and in 2010 the two recorded the not particularly notable album "Higher Power".

Five years later, he wrote the autobiographical book "Heart of Gold, Stories But Not Really for Children," revealing lesser-known details about his life and struggle with addiction.

He spent the last years of his life in England and Canada, where his children moved, and Croatian media write that he was in very poor health for a long time, caused by multiple strokes.
