Govt sees economy growing up to 5.3% this year, but slowing in 2027

TheTools · 09.10.2026 07:30 · source · permalink

Strong domestic demand, private investment and robust E&E exports are expected to sustain expansion despite global uncertainties.

According to the finance ministry’s Economic Outlook 2027 report, the economy grew by 5.7% in the first half of 2026 and is forecast to expand by 4.7% in the second half.

The report said that domestic demand would remain the key driver, supported by private consumption, a firm labour market and continued income growth.

“Household consumption continues to be supported by government measures to sustain purchasing power through SARA and STR, as well as the expansion of the PJRM,” the report said, referring to the Sumbangan Asas Rahmah, Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah and Payung Rahmah initiatives.

The continuation of BUDI95 and targeted BUDI Diesel subsidies is also expected to “cushion households against energy price shocks and inflationary pressures”.

On the supply side, the services sector will likely remain the primary growth driver, helped by tourism, retail and transport in conjunction with Visit Malaysia 2026-2027.

The manufacturing sector is expected to benefit from the technology upcycle and expansion in high-value activities, particularly semiconductors and electrical and electronics industries.

The report also cautioned that the outlook “remains subject to external risks”, including geopolitical tensions, shifts in international trade policies and global financial market volatility.
