Cheslin Kolbe to inspire next generation with autobiography 'Sidestepping the Odds'

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:17 · source · permalink

Celeste Martin

The two-time Rugby World Cup winner said he wants his story to show others that their background does not have to determine their future.

'This is more than a rugby story. It’s a story of faith, family, sacrifice and the courage to defy the odds.'

That's an excerpt from the synopsis of Springbok star Cheslin Kolbe's autobiography 'Sidestepping the Odds'. 

Co-authored by veteran journalist David O’Sullivan, Kolbe said he hopes his story will show others that their background does not have to determine their future.

Speaking to CapeTalk's Lester Kiewit, Kolbe spoke about how rugby changed the course of his life, giving him a way out of the challenges he faced growing up in Kraaifontein in Cape Town, and how he was inspired to help young people facing similar circumstances. 

"Everybody asked me the question... why did you think of doing or letting the book out now? Like, the easiest thing was probably to do it once I was done with rugby because I would have had more time. But I think because I had so much time in Japan, while I was busy with the book with David, I just felt it was relevant now, while I'm still in the game and playing club and international rugby, to put my story out there.

"Why I did the book was literally to inspire not just the young generation but anyone out there who is facing the challenges I've faced growing up and the things that I've seen, so they can basically just take anything inspiring out of the book and into their daily life."

The two-time Rugby World Cup winner reflected on growing up in a community affected by crime, gangsterism and poverty, and the role sport played in keeping him focused on his ambitions. 

"Rugby definitely saved my life, but there are many other ways where sport plays a massive role in changing your life and the way you do things and takes you out of difficult circumstances, and that's what rugby has given me… that platform to get away from the distraction, from falling into the easy way out. 

"In our communities where there's gangsterism, there's crime, there's poverty… and that's probably the easier way to fall into the trap is if you don't have that motivation, whether that's sport, whether that's education, whether that's becoming a businessman or entrepreneur or anything. 

"Like, for me, my early days were, if I look back at it now, the things that I saw, that I experienced, I wouldn't change it for the world, to be quite honest, because it made me a stronger person, and it made me want to achieve a lot more in rugby.

"My dad played a massive part in me following him throughout the week when he goes to training and games on weekends, and for me rugby definitely has changed my life; it's given me a platform to do better and go back into communities and try and inspire and just like be a mentor to the younger generation."

Kolbe also opened up about the doubts he faced because of his size, recalling how people in rugby questioned whether he could make it professionally.

"I've obviously been faced with a lot of doubt in terms of my size, my weight, because from players to coaches, I had guys telling me that I wouldn't be able to make rugby a professional career… that was tough to obviously take in, but I looked at it in a positive way and tried to use that to prove people wrong.

"It doesn't matter how big or how small you are; if you're willing to put in the hard work and sacrifice and just be determined to achieve the goals that you set up for yourself, then anything is possible.

"It doesn't matter the circumstances, your background that you come from, but if you have that resilience of not giving up and just wanting to work and have that goal in mind that it is achievable. The coaches play a massive part in that because once you have the trust and buy-in from coaches, that just gives you that freedom to express on the field at the end of the day."

Now an international rugby icon, Kolbe said staying connected to his roots remains important.

He has even bought his grandmother's home to keep it in the family, preserving the memories of where his journey began.

"Kraaifontein will always have a special place in my heart, and it's always a community that I'm proud of. There's so much talent in Kraaifontein, across South Africa as well, where I can go back to, and obviously through rugby has given me that financial stability to obviously purchase my grandmother's house and keep it within the family because there are so many memories in that household that I will keep with me, things that I've learned, developed as a young kid till today and still being able to go back to the community to just drive past our house and just go back in time to just remind myself where you started, where everything happened and that for me, it's important to not forget where I come from, what the people has done for me in the community.

"... for me, it's just to remain humble, keep my feet on the ground and continue to be a better person than what I was yesterday and rugby player as well... just to show the kids in not just my community, but in communities out there, that it doesn't matter where you're from, how difficult things might look, that there's always a way out and someone always keeping an eye out on you."

Kolbe hopes to use his success to mentor younger generations and remind them that there are opportunities beyond the circumstances they grow up in.
