NI employers named for failing to pay staff minimum wage

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:35 · source · permalink

Thirty-six businesses in Northern Ireland, including a care provider, hotels and solicitors, have been named for failing to pay their employees the minimum wage, according to the Department for Business and Trade (DBT).

Across the UK, more than 600 employers were ordered to pay affected workers the outstanding wages, with £4m returned to workers.

The firms across the UK have also been issued penalties worth £7m.

Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds said the government was determined to stamp out the practice of "short-changing your staff".

"The best businesses know that looking after your workers isn't just the right thing to do, it's the smart thing to do," he said.

In April 2026, the hourly rate for over-21s rose by 50p to £12.71, with workers aged 18-20 seeing an 85p rise to £10.85.

Sixteen and 17-year-olds and apprentices saw their minimum wage increase by 6%.
