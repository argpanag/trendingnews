A familiar Christian athlete on stage can make promises of big money feel safe. When reports of aggressive sales tactics at one faith-based seminar surfaced, the claims stayed somewhat quiet until Tim Tebow's repeated appearances there pushed everything into wider view. That connection has now drawn scrutiny that shows little sign of slowing.

Pablo Torre and Mother Jones reporter Kiera Butler published their joint investigation on September 24, 2026. The Pablo Torre Finds Out episode carried the title "We Investigated the Christian 'Scam' Fronted by Tim Tebow." An accompanying Mother Jones piece was called "The Gospel of High Yields: Inside Tim Tebow's Favorite Wealth Seminar." Those reports set up Torre's later assessment of how far the claims might extend.

"We are actively in the thick of that story," Pablo Torre replied when Front Office Sports Tonight reporter Bridget Condon asked about the Tim Tebow's investigation. "The story of Life Surge, the Christian Roadshow, that has used him as a headliner more than 40 times. And now is being beset by allegations that this is all allegedly a scam."

"The Christian classes, financial education that they're selling, apparently to the tune of tens of thousands of dollars for some of the most vulnerable people among their faith. And so Tebow is saying, I'm going to pause this relationship after years of deep connection to it. I think that's just the tip of the iceberg." Torre added further.

Reporting cites around 44 appearances by the former NFL quarterback as a Life Surge headliner. He spoke at about 10 events each year in 2024 and 2025, and his schedule was on pace for around 20 appearances in 2026. His long-standing Christian image appears to have helped some attendees feel comfortable trusting the organization.

Tebow received speaking fees of about $50,000 to $100,000 per appearance, though the company has not confirmed the exact figures. Some estimates place his overall earnings from the relationship in the millions. His compensation has been described as not tied to sales commissions. Those numbers remain reported estimates rather than verified payment records.

Tebow addressed the allegations on September 25, 2026, the day after the investigation appeared. He said his team would look into the claims and, for the time being, he was pausing his relationship with Life Surge until more facts were clear.

He noted that he had been caught off guard while attending an anti-human-trafficking conference on September 24. That pause is the step Torre referenced when he described the story as larger than one celebrity's decision to step back.

Life Surge operates as a faith-based motivational and financial education company. It stages nationwide one-day events that feature Christian speakers, worship, personal testimonials, and celebrity appearances.

Entry tickets often run around $20 to $30, which keeps the gatherings open to a broad crowd. After those sessions, attendees are offered expensive financial education and mentorship programs.

According to the investigation, some packages cost around $10,000 to $50,000. Former attendees alleged that sales presentations used urgency and religious language to encourage purchases. Some former attendees said the pressure made it hard to seek outside advice before deciding. Those sales tactics led some people into debt, according to customers interviewed for the reports.

Cathy Luebke and her husband bought a package that cost around $20,000 after watching demonstrations of potential profits. They borrowed about $40,000 through promotional credit cards, saw none of the expected returns, and later took a home-equity loan to handle the remaining debt.

Other complaints have also surfaced through the Federal Trade Commission, the Better Business Bureau, and online forums. These accounts raise questions but do not amount to a proven finding of fraud.

Life Surge has reported holding around 30 one-day events each year across the country. Those figures list 117,196 attendees at one-day events in 2025 and 25,541 reported "decisions for Christ."

Torre has described the Life Surge investigation as a continuing series and indicated that follow-up reporting is planned. In mid-October comments, he said more is still to come, and that further details could open people's eyes. Later reporting has already examined a Life Surge event at Ohio State that involved Urban Meyer and Ohio State players Jeremiah Smith and Tavien St. Clair.

Life Surge has also featured other prominent names, including Joe Montana, Russell Wilson, Magic Johnson, David Ortiz, and Meyer. Boston Celtics coach Joe Mazzulla cut ties, canceled a scheduled October appearance, and asked that his image be removed from the company's website.
