Managing Tottenham as Kroupi signs, Endrick replaces £65m star and three leave

TheTools · 15.11.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

Tottenham Hotspur have a lot of work to do in the January transfer window if they want to qualify for either of UEFA's three club competitions this season. The Lilywhites have enjoyed a promising start to the Premier League campaign, distancing themselves from another relegation battle and mounting a bid for a place in the top half of the table.

Although Spurs currently sit 11th on just 12 points, they're just two points away from a place in the top eight, which, last term, earned Brighton qualification for the Conference League. A modest return to the European stage would certainly be a step in the right direction, but Vinai Venkatesham has set his sights on becoming champions of England.

Speaking on The Sports Agents Podcast earlier this month, the Tottenham chief executive announced his target to win the Premier League. Vinai said: "There will be good moments this season and bad moments this season, but I firmly believe we’re on the right track to where we want to get to.

"Ultimately, I guess the best way to answer your question (about targets) is: what are we here for? We’re here to win, right? The people that we’ve attracted to the football club, the long list of people, everyone is here to win.

"If you work for Tottenham, when we talk about being here to win, that means we’re here to win the Premier League. That’s what this football club wants to do. It wants to win the Premier League, and it wants to be consistent in the Champions League and ultimately win the Champions League.

"Can I tell you what year that’s going to happen in? Obviously I can’t, but that’s what we’re aiming for, and that’s what I want every single person that works for this club to think about every single day."

If Vinai wants the Lilywhites to one day mount a bid for the Premier League title, he'll need to put his money where his mouth is and continue to back De Zerbi whenever possible. So, with that being said, football.london has looked at what Tottenham need to do in the upcoming transfer window.

Tottenham offload THREE players

Before Johan Lange can begin thinking about who to sign, he will need to streamline the Spurs squad and raise a little cash. Richarlison should be first out of the door at Hotspur Way, having fallen out of favour with De Zerbi.

The Lilywhites will, of course, have the chance to register the striker to play again in the Premier League, but, with bridges burned, players returning from injury, and months left on his contract, he should be offloaded. In an ideal world, Richarlison and the Tottenham boss put their differences aside – as it just seems like a waste of a much-needed goalscorer – but that seems unlikely.

Next, Spurs should terminate Mykhailo Mudryk's loan from Chelsea. Unless the winger can make a miraculous return to full fitness and stake a strong claim for a place in the starting lineup between now and the new year, De Zerbi would be much better off allocating more minutes to Luca Williams-Barnett, who's shown he's capable of holding his own at the highest level.

Wilson Odobert, who suffered an anterior cruciate ligament injury earlier this year, could also become available for selection again soon. Although Tottenham may damage their relationship with the Blues, they need to prioritise their players' health and development.

By axing Mudryk, De Zerbi will be better equipped to manage two exciting young players who could have long, promising futures in north London. Then, last but by no means least, it comes with a heavy heart in proposing that Dejan Kulusevski spend the rest of the season on loan.

Although the midfielder would perhaps be better off under the watchful eye of medical staff at Hotspur Way, having only recently returned from injury, he needs minutes in the tank to rebuild his fitness.

With Xavi Simons on the mend, Omar Marmoush capable of playing behind the striker, James Maddison ahead on the road to full recovery, and more mobile options on the right, it could be difficult to find Kulusevski the chances he deserves.

To be sold: Richarlison.

To be loaned: Dejan Kulusevski.

To be let go: Mykhailo Mudryk.

Lange splashes the cash

De Zerbi has done well to settle the core of his squad, so Lange shouldn't necessarily pull up any trees in January. But Tottenham are in desperate need of a new striker or two, and the sporting director needs to splash the cash.

Eli Junior Kroupi would be an excellent addition to the starting lineup, bringing great quality to the frontline positions. Even if Spurs were to land the Bournemouth star, they would still need to bring in an out-and-out number nine to replace the profile of Dominic Solanke: a box striker.

It would be brilliant to see Will Lankshear have a go at leading the attack under De Zerbi, but he should see out the season at Middlesbrough for the sake of his development and he has only just been permanently let go by the club. So without a clear and obvious solution at the moment in January, Endrick would be an exciting wildcard signing on loan from Real Madrid.

He has an eye for goal, X-factor quality, and is deceptively physical, so he shouldn't have many problems adapting to life in the Premier League. Of course, he's still a little raw, and it would be a gamble. But with no strings attached, the upside would be superb.

To be signed: Eli Junior Kroupi.

To be loaned: Endrick (from Real Madrid).
