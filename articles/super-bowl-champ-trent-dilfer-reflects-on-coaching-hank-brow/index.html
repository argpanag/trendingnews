Super Bowl champ Trent Dilfer reflects on coaching Hank Brown in high school

TheTools · 27.11.2026 16:26 · source · permalink

Dilfer knew he had something special early on.

Dilfer knew he had something special early on.

Trent Dilfer played on the biggest football stage imaginable, quarterbacking Baltimore to a Super Bowl XXXV win over the Giants in 2001. These days, he's the head coach at Lipscomb Academy in Nashville, Tennessee.

He coached current Iowa QB Hank Brown during Brown's magical senior season in 2022. Watching his former quarterback play in college is a gut-wrenching experience, even more nerve-wracking than playing in a Super Bowl.

"Watching Hank, by far," Dilfer said. "It's like watching my kids. My daughters were Division One volleyball players, played in big-time matches, Final Fours, national championships, all that stuff. And I would just be sick to my stomach, because I have no control. It's very similar. Watching Hank, he has an amazing family, so I don't pretend to be another parent, but I feel that kind of affection towards him. Gosh, the Michigan game. After the walk-off, I'm running around the house like a 12-year-old and FaceTiming his younger brother, Brooks. They're doing the same thing and texting Leslie and Eric and, I mean, we kind of live and die by Iowa football in the Dilfer household these days. We kind of plan our whole weekends around whenever Iowa's playing."

Brown was the backup quarterback his junior year at Lipscomb Academy in Nashville, so he only played one season: his senior year. But boy, did he make it count. He quarterbacked Dilfer's team to a title in 2022.

"I knew early on that we had something special," Dilfer said.

Brown passed for 3264 yards and 47 touchdowns, fifth most in state history. He completed over 73% of his passes and led Lipscomb to a perfect 13-0 record. In one game, Lipscomb fell behind big early. Dilfer asked his QB to put the team on his back.

"I said to him, 'We don't ask you to do this very often, but go bring us back.' I think he threw for 400 yards. It was probably 450. He just absolutely went out there and said, 'Over my dead body are we going to lose.' He was clearly the best player on the field that night and many other nights, too."

Dilfer says Brown has got incredible, natural accuracy.

"Hank very rarely misses what he's throwing at, and he usually chooses the right speed and the right anticipatory qualities to throw it."

Even better, Dilfer says, is Brown's leadership.

"He just never flinches. He has tremendous faith. He has ice in his veins. Nothing bothers him. He doesn't get too high. Like, even the other night after the touchdown, the fourth-and-two touchdown run, that's about as much positive emotion you're going to get out of him."

For Hank, that time with Dilfer is priceless.

"Way more of a blessing than I realized," Brown said. "Both on and off the field. I learned a lot from him, you know, on the field, mechanically. And then off the field, he's a man of God and really taught me where my foundation really lies."

Brown began his career at Auburn before transferring to Iowa. He won a prolonged QB battle this year and has put together a memorable season and proven capable of leading Iowa to big wins.

"There's a million dudes with a lot of talent on the sidewalks," Dilfer said. "The guys that make it are surrounded by good people. That was always my encouragement. It was, 'Hank, you keep getting better. You do your part, but you got to find the right people.' And I think he's really found his people at Iowa."

Iowa hosts Nebraska Friday at 11 a.m. on KCCI. A win keeps Iowa in contention for the Big Ten Championship and the College Football Playoff.
