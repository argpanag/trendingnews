Over two years since the end of the hit CBS sitcom Young Sheldon, series star Annie Potts is making her feelings about the polarizing TV finale achingly clear.

In the new tell-all book about the making of the series, Young Sheldon: The (Juicier-Than-Meemaw's-Brisket) Inside Story of the Hit Comedy, Potts spoke to author Jessica Radloff about her thoughts. onthe series' finale, which saw the return of Jim Parsons and Mayim Bialik reprising their roles as Sheldon Cooper and Amy Fararah Fowler, respectively.

As fun as it might have been to see the return of The Big Bang Theory's most beloved characters, Potts believed this abrupt cameo at the end of Young Sheldon ultimately took away from the accomplishments of the show's newer cast.

In addition, the actress also felt the finale undid much of the natural growth and progression that underlined young Sheldon's transformation throughout the series, reverting him back to the same irresponsible brainiac he was at the start of the series.

"That broke my heart. It was like, 'Really? Still no responsibility?' It was just selfishness," Potts said. "It's just not how I wish to see Sheldon off into the world. … I thought the show could have ended [with young Sheldon] and not to the dreaded scene with Jim and Mayim as Sheldon and Amy revealing he was unchanged and unrepentant. That gave me a stomachache. It was like, 'Who crashed our party?! Who invited you?'"

Premiering on CBS in late 2017, Young Sheldon acted as the first official spinoff to the network's wildly popular Big Bang Theory. An inventive prequel series, the show focused on the exploits of a young Sheldon Cooper (played by Iain Armitage) as he navigated life as a child prodigy in 1990s Texas.

The series ran for a total of seven seasons on CBS before its finale premiered on May 16, 2024.
