Do Barcelona have another La Masia gem on their hands? Xavi Espart is taking his chance

TheTools · 13.09.2026 15:52 · source · permalink

Espart has started for Barcelona in their opening three games of the season Francisco Macia/Getty Images

Just when you thought you’d lost your capacity to be amazed by La Masia, Barcelona’s youth academy comes along with another exciting prospect.

The latest to establish himself at La Liga’s champions is Xavi Espart, a versatile 19-year-old who has worked himself into Hansi Flick’s plans at full-back, despite initially being trained as a midfielder.

Espart was part of the same youth teams as Lamine Yamal, Pau Cubarsi and Marc Bernal growing up, but he has had to wait a little longer than them for a chance to impose himself on the senior squad.

He made his Barcelona debut off the bench in a Champions League round-of-16 first-leg at Newcastle last season and caught the eye despite only being on the pitch for about nine minutes. Five days later, he started Barca’s home La Liga match against Sevilla and impressed again.

As Barca successfully defended their Spanish league title last term, Espart remained in Flick’s plans. All but one of the seven appearances he made in all competitions were at right-back.

Picking up where he left off this pre-season — after also starring in midfield in Spain’s Under-19 European Championship victory this summer — he has been ever-present in this campaign, having moved position again.

Flick has picked Espart at left-back for each of Barca’s opening three matches of the new season — and the youngster has grabbed that chance with both hands, putting in exciting displays and picking up two assists.

It has created one of those good problems for German boss Flick, whose other options for the position include Joao Cancelo (who returned to Barcelona on a permanent transfer on August 20) and Alejandro Balde (who ended up staying despite Manchester United interest).

Espart, from Vilassar de Mar, a small town about 20 minutes up the coast from Barcelona, arrived at La Masia in 2015, aged eight. He was often compared to Xavi (who was also his idol) because of his playing style.

That was until Flick arrived at Barcelona and said he reminded him of Philipp Lahm.

“When I watch him play, I really like the confidence he shows on the ball,” the 61-year-old said at a press conference last March. “He reminds me of Lahm, who can play as a No 6 or a No 2. I like watching him both with and without the ball.”

Espart’s start at Barca, and Flick’s words about him, mirror his development at the club. He often alternated positions at the academy, too, partly due to other talents emerging ahead of him and the challenges that posed.

Marc Serra, Barcelona’s seven-a-side football coordinator at the time, alongside academy senior figures Aureli Altimira and Jordi Roura, approved Espart’s signing.

“He was the typical player we were looking for,” Roura tells The Athletic.

“He was technically very good, had a good understanding of the game, thought quickly and anticipated the play. But not everyone was convinced he’d make it. He wasn’t a Lamine Yamal, so to speak.”

Up until under-14 level Espart had been playing as a No 6. But then his coach, Pol Planas, moved Bernal from attacking midfield into a holding midfield position, and as a result Espart lost some of his prominence.

It was during the next season, at under-15 level, when Espart began to play more regularly as a full-back, alternating between that position and his old midfield spot.

With coaches feeling his development was being hampered, Sergi Mila — La Masia’s 11-a-side football coordinator — then recommended to Espart’s coach, Ivan Carrasco, that he play him at right-back. Espart settled into the role and began to really take off.

Even now, many within the club still believe that Espart will eventually end up in midfield, despite the path being very congested. Frenkie de Jong is 29 and the recently arrived Rodri is 30, while Pedri and Fermin Lopez are 23, Gavi is 22, and Bernal is 19.

But the switch did bring the best out of him. He gained a lot of confidence. Until then he had been considered a rather introverted character, someone who kept a low profile. He began to open up more as his football really came into its own.

By under-17 and under-18 level, Espart had fully established himself in Barca’s youth teams and started to receive call-ups to Spain’s youth sides — where he alternated between midfield and full-back.

At Barca, Espart has had to watch on while his former team-mates have been propelled to stardom. Yamal made his debut aged 15 in April 2023. Cubarsi followed in January 2024, aged 16. Bernal broke through the following August at 17, before rupturing his anterior cruciate ligament.

His journey has not been the easiest, nor did he seem like a player touched by a magic wand when coming through. But he didn’t lose heart and kept persevering, hoping that his moment would eventually arrive. He has learned resilience on the way.

This summer, aware that Flick had already shown him faith, Espart took pre-season training very seriously, knowing also that there would be a greater opportunity to impress given several of the senior squad would be late in returning from World Cup duty with Spain.

What comes next? There is a long way to go for any 19-year-old footballer hoping to progress in the game, let alone one seeking a career at a top club like Barcelona.

But what we have seen already suggests they have unearthed another local gem in Espart, and fans are revelling in it.
