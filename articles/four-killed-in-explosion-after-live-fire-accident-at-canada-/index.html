Four killed in explosion after ‘live-fire accident’ at Canada military base

TheTools · 19.10.2026 23:27 · source · permalink

Gas line reportedly hit during a training exercise, triggering a large blaze in Alberta

At least four people have been killed and dozens more injured after a large explosion at one of Canada’s busiest military bases.

A gas line was hit during a live-fire training exercise at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, in Alberta, at 4pm on Saturday, according to reports.

Mark Carney, the Canadian prime minister, called the incident “one of the worst tragedies for Canadian forces on Canadian soil in peacetime”.

At an address on Sunday, Mr Carney said: “Four service members have lost their lives and their next of kin has been notified. Dozens more are seriously injured.”

The Canadian military has not officially confirmed the cause of the blaze, but army sources told Global News it was the result of an accident during live-fire training.

David McGuinty, the Canadian defence minister, said the military and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police were investigating the incident.

He added: “I’m saddened to share that there have been both fatalities and injuries as a result of an incident on the base... I cannot recall an incident like this in recent memory.”

Seven people were reported to have been killed in the blast before Mr Carney revised the death toll.

Wes Streeting, the Defence Secretary, on Sunday night sent his “deepest condolences” to those affected by the fire.

He wrote: “My deepest condolences to all those mourning the Canadian Armed Forces personnel who tragically lost their lives at the base in Wainwright – and with those who have suffered injuries during this terrible incident.

“To our Canadian friends – we stand with you and grieve with you.”

David Reed, Britain’s shadow defence secretary, said he was “deeply saddened to hear this devastating news”.

He wrote on X: “My thoughts and prayers are with the four members of the Canadian Armed Forces who have tragically lost their lives, and with their families, friends and fellow service personnel at this incredibly difficult time.”

Firefighters worked late into the night as changeable winds, with gusts as strong as 62mph, moved the fire towards the nearby town of Wainwright, home to 7,000 people. The flames were stopped from reaching the town before midnight local time.

Hospitals close to the base were overwhelmed, according to witnesses.

The number of injured continued to rise on Sunday morning, with a “code orange” issued to hospitals in Edmonton, Alberta’s capital, denoting a mass casualty incident.

Dr Warren Thirsk, an emergency department physician at the Royal Alexandra Hospital, told CBC News: “It is more patients than any of us want to count right now. It’s a disaster. It’s a one in every 15 to 25-year disaster.”

Danielle Smith, the Albertan premier, described the fire as “significant and tragic” and said it was affecting many military personnel.

Canadian Forces Base Wainwright is one of the country’s premier training bases bringing together infantry, artillery and air support.

The base hosts approximately 690 personnel and 300 workers, according to CBC.

The fire also disrupted advance voting in Alberta’s referendum on Monday to decide whether it should become independent of Canada.

Election authorities said two polling locations, in Lloydminster and Vermilion, had closed early on Saturday, but the ballots had been transported securely.

Polls strongly suggest that the province will remain part of the country, but what was once considered a fringe issue has now entered mainstream politics.
