Police expert sacked for stealing energy drinks and snacks

TheTools · 06.10.2026 12:08 · source · permalink

A "deliberately dishonest" veteran drugs expert working for Lincolnshire Police has lost his job after stealing energy drinks and snacks while on the job.

PC Andrew Cookson stole several bags of items from a cannabis factory which had been discovered in the old bus depot in Gainsborough, a police misconduct panel concluded.

He has been dismissed without notice and will be placed on the barred list for police forces.

Initial CSI photos showed large amounts of energy drinks and snacks in the factory’s living quarters, including Red Bull, Coca Cola, biscuits, sweets and chocolate.

However these had disappeared by the next day, casting suspicion on those who had been at the crime scene.

CCTV showed PC Cookson returning to South Park police station in Lincoln with two bulky Lidl bags, identical to those which had been spotted at the factory.

PC Cookson moved the bags from his police van to his car, appearing to take care not to take them into the station.

He claimed this was kit smelling of cannabis which he was taking home to wash, but the panel concluded it was stolen items from the crime scene.

The panel heard that he had debts of between £15,000 and £20,000 at the time of the incident on January 3, 2024.

While the panel found that theft of drinks and sweets hadn’t harmed the investigation, they said it could undermine the public’s trust in the police.

It concluded that he had breached the standards for honesty, integrity and discreditable conduct, which amounted to gross misconduct.

PC Cookson, who had worked for the force for nearly 24 years, maintained his innocence throughout, saying he didn't know who had taken the items.

He left the hearing after it was announced on Tuesday, October 6 that he was guilty of gross misconduct, and didn't return to hear the fate of his career.

Dan Penman, for Lincolnshire Police, told the panel: “Not only has he stolen but he has maintained his innocence throughout the proceedings.

“He has not shown any indication he wouldn’t do it again.”

Assistant Chief Constable Kate Anderson, who chaired the panel, said: “He was deliberately dishonest, removing items from the scene of the crime.”

She said the heavy bags seen on footage were “consistent with stolen property” rather than clothes, as he had claimed.

The hearing was told he had good character and had no previous misconduct, but may have been struggling with money following the Covid pandemic.

Mr Reed, representing PC Cookson, previously told the hearing: “If he is in financial difficulty as it’s claimed, would this really assist him?

“Would someone throw away a 20-plus year career for cans of pop, sweeties and detergent?”

He had suggested other people may have been hiding in the large cannabis factory, which had several purpose-built rooms full of plants.

However ACC Anderson said it was “implausible” that any third parties had snuck in and stolen the items, and all the evidence pointed to PC Cookson.

PC Cookson will be unable to apply to join any other police forces.
