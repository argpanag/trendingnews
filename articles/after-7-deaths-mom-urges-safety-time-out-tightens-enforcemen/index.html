After 7 deaths, MOM urges safety time-out, tightens enforcement in construction, transport

TheTools · 05.10.2026 18:14 · source · permalink

Singapore

The latest measures include higher fines, longer stop-work orders and possible curbs on migrant-worker hiring for serious lapses.

SINGAPORE: Companies in the construction industry and the transportation and storage industry are being urged to conduct a voluntary safety time-out from Oct 5 to 18, following seven workplace deaths since last month.

In a Facebook post on Monday (Oct 5), the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) said that the recent deaths in the two industries were a "grave reminder that workplace safety and health cannot be taken for granted".

MOM asked companies to review their workplace safety and health procedures, strengthen risk controls and address safety gaps.

It added that particular attention should be paid to the safe use of concrete pumps, and measures to prevent vehicles from rolling away, including ensuring that handbrakes are engaged before drivers leave their vehicles and using wheel chocks where appropriate.

Alongside the voluntary safety time-out, MOM said that it would introduce enhanced enforcement measures in both industries from Oct 5 to Nov 4, "to strengthen deterrence and accountability".

The composition fine for first-time offences will increase from S$2,000 (about US$1,550) to S$3,000, with higher fines for repeat or more serious offences.

The minimum duration of stop-work orders from also increase from five weeks to eight weeks, while companies may be barred from hiring new migrant workers for three months in "egregious cases" involving serious workplace safety lapses that result in fatal or serious accidents.

The same enhanced measures were introduced nationwide in June, following a spate of workplace deaths then. They were in place from Jun 26 to Jul 31.

Mr Dinesh Vasu Dash, Minister of State for Manpower, said on Monday that the recent fatalities have been concentrated in the two industries, which was why MOM was calling on companies in these sectors to conduct a voluntary safety time-out and stepping up enforcement.

"The seven workplace fatalities since September are deeply concerning, and we must act decisively to prevent further loss of lives," he added.

A 22-year-old worker died on Sep 28 after falling from height at a construction site along Tanjong Rhu Road, while two workers died after falling into the sea at Tuas Port on Sep 22. 

MOM's figures showed 21 workplace fatalities in the first half of 2026, with Singapore's annualised fatal injury rate to 1.1 per 100,000 workers.

Vehicular incidents were the leading cause, accounting for nine deaths. Separately, four of seven work-related traffic fatalities during that period involved platform workers.

Mr Melvin Yong, MP for Radin Mas and executive secretary of the Built Environment and Urban Trades Employees' Union, wrote in a Facebook post that he supported the tougher enforcement measures.

He said that these send a clear message: companies that "cut corners on safety" must face serious consequences.

In September, four of the seven workplace deaths were in construction, while three were in transportation and storage, he added.

"This cannot become another statistic that we acknowledge today and forget tomorrow. Because behind every fatality is a family left grieving." 

Mr Yong, who is also assistant secretary-general of the National Trades Union Congress (NTUC), asked companies not to treat the time-out as a "paper exercise".

"Employers must provide safe systems of work, supervisors must stop unsafe work immediately, and workers must be able to speak up without fear," he added.

"NTUC and our unions will work with our tripartite partners and employers to strengthen accountability on the ground."

Mr Dinesh also called on industry partners and stakeholders, including the labour movement, to reinforce these efforts within their networks.

"Continued vigilance and collective effort will help us strengthen safety practices and build safer workplaces," he said.

MOM said that employers should use this opportunity to engage workers and supervisors on workplace risks and reinforce safe work practices at all levels.
