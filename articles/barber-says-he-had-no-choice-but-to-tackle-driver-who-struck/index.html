Barber says he had 'no choice' but to tackle driver who struck Australian MP Helen Haines

TheTools · 20.10.2026 04:08 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Australian MP Helen Haines remains in a critical condition after being hit by a car in what Prime Minister Anthony Albanese calls "a deliberate act"

  • The suspect allegedly did a U-turn in order to strike the MP and then reversed to ram her a second time, Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke says

  • The 65-year-old independent MP, who was taken by helicopter to hospital in Melbourne, has reportedly lost a leg

  • A 43-year-old man, from nearby Tarrawingee, was detained at the scene by a passer-by before being taken into custody

  • Police say it was an 'isolated incident' and they are working to identify potential charges

  • Both MPs and experts have warned that threats to Australian politicians were rising before the Haines incident, writes Tiffanie Turnbull in Sydney

    04:48 BST

    New Zealand Prime Minister Christopher Luxon says he spoke with Anthony Albanese in the aftermath of the alleged attack on Helen Haines, and passed on "thoughts and prayers from New Zealand".

  4. What we know so farpublished at 03:57 BST
    03:57 BST

    Helen Haines, the independent federal MP for Indi, remains in a critical condition in hospital after she was hit by a car outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, Victoria, yesterday afternoon.

    Here's the latest:

    • Haines, 65, was airlifted to the Alfred Hospital in Melbourne where she underwent surgery last night. Some local media outlets are reporting she has lost one of her legs
    • A 43-year-old man from nearby Tarrawingee was arrested at the scene after he was detained by bystanders. He has now been named by local media as Brendan Nugent, who was previously well known in the local area as an environmental campaigner
    • Police are questioning the suspect and weighing up charges, which could include causing harm to a public official, an offence which carries a ten-year jail term
    • The driver reportedly did a U-turn to get into position before hitting Haines, then reversed and hit her again, the home affairs minister has told reporters
    • Politicians from across the political spectrum, including PM Anthony Albanese, say the incident has left them shaken
    • The country is in shock as political violence is rare in Australia, though fears of such attacks have been growing

    Stay with us as we bring you the latest updates.

  8. 'People feared there'd be some sort of violent incident' says Australian MPpublished at 02:50 BST
    02:50 BST

    Fellow independent MP Allegra Spender has been telling ABC News "many people have feared that there would be some sort of violent incident against Australian politicians".

    "This is part of a trend that we I think a country, not just as politicians, we need to step up and say, we don’t want to see this sort of violence in language and violence in attitude."

    "We are collectively responsible for that."

  9. Australian political leaders condemn apparent attackpublished at 02:33 BST
    02:33 BST

    Political leaders have condemned the apparent attack on independent MP Helen Haines who remains in a critical condition in hospital.

    Australia's opposition leader Angus Taylor says it is a "shocking day for our nation" and that Australians will be "angry and appalled" by the attack.

    "Our nation cannot become a place where violent attacks are normalised," he says.

    The Greens leader David Shoebridge says the notion that Haines was targeted for her work is "horrifying, senseless, and shameful".

    “We cannot let political violence become the norm and I believe that politics grounded in non-violence is a powerful way to address this threat.

    And Senator Pauline Hanson, leader of One Nation, says: "Violence against any elected representative, whoever they are and whatever they stand for, is never acceptable in Australia".

  11. Police working to identify potential charges for suspectpublished at 01:52 BST
    01:52 BST

    The Australian Federal Police (AFP) Commissioner has just released a statement about Helen Haines with an update on the 43-year-old man who was arrested shortly after the incident.

    The AFP and Victoria Police are "working closely together to identify potential charges," says Krissy Barrett, with possible state and Commonwealth offences including causing harm to a public official which carries a maximum penalty of ten years in prison.

    "At this stage it appears this is an isolated incident, and there are no known threats connected to this matter," she says.

    The investigation is being led by Victoria Police's security investigation unit with support from the state's joint counter terrorism team, which is includes the AFP and ASIO, Australia's spy agency.

    Federal police have regularly raised concerns about "growing threats against federal parliamentarians and high office holders," Barrett says, and last October, a special unit was established to probe politically-motivated violence and hate, with 41 people charged as a result.

  12. Spy chief says 'grievance' and 'polarisation' have made violence more likelypublished at 01:31 BST
    01:31 BST

    The head of Australia's intelligence services, Mike Burgess, has issued a statement warning that increased levels of "grievance, intolerance and polarisation continue to create a permissive environment of violence" in Australia.

    He says that the agency, ASIO, is concerned about the "use of violent rhetoric in public discourse".

    "This incident occurred in an environment where politically motivated violence is more likely than previous years," he writes.

    He also repeats an earlier warning, first made in 2024, that "As polarisation, frustration and perceived injustices grow, ASIO anticipates an increase in politically motivated violence – including terrorism – across all ideological spectrums.

    "Attacks are likely to occur with little to no warning and will be difficult to detect.”

  13. 'Huge rise' in hate and aggression towards politicians, says MPpublished at 01:15 BST
    01:15 BST

    Zali Steggall, an independent MP in Sydney, says she's seen a "huge rise" in hate and aggression towards politicians, especially female ones.

    "It's deeply concerning," she said in an interview with the BBC's The World Tonight programme.

    "It is unfortunately a reality of the role, but it shouldn't deter us. I think the haters win if we let this kind of aggressive behaviour deter in particular women, from entering public life and serving in politics."

    Steggall, who sits next to Helen Haines in federal parliament, described her colleague as an "amazing woman" who is "deeply loved and respected".

    "A key part of her advocacy in politics has, in fact, been around integrity for a national anti-corruption commission, but also around treating each other with respect, recognising our differences."

    Haines had recently become a grandmother, Steggall says, and "would often share with me the photos of her baby grandchildren".

  14. Suspect hit MP twice with car, says ministerpublished at 00:56 BST 20 October
    00:56 BST 20 October

    The driver who allegedly hit Helen Haines with his car reversed so he could ram into her a second time, Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke has said.

    "What witnesses have described is the driver doing a U-turn to get into the car park and to ram Helen against a vehicle, then to reverse and do it a second time," Burke told national broadcaster ABC this morning.

    As we reported earlier, a bystander ran towards the car, pulled the driver out and held him until police arrived.

    "I think every Australian is grateful for those moments when a hero like this runs towards the most dangerous place," Burke said.

  15. Helen Haines is a 'beacon of light' in her communitypublished at 00:41 BST 20 October
    00:41 BST 20 October

    Simon Atkinson
    Australia reporter

    Helen Haines may not be a household name in Australia, but since Monday afternoon's incident it has become clear she is held in high esteem by her fellow politicians and her local community.

    There is clearly a lot of love for the former nurse and midwife who came to politics fairly late after a career in health - including at the Wangaratta hospital where she was first taken after the attack - before being flown to more specialist care in Melbourne.

    As an independent MP, Helen Haines isn't someone he always agreed with, Anthony Albanese has said but he talked warmly of her "integrity" as a "hard-working" politician who is a "beacon of light" for her rural electorate.

    These are words coming up again and again, along with "respected". Perhaps her biggest win in politics has been leading the campaign for a national anti-corruption body.

    Another independent MP, Allegra Spender, calls her a "wonderful woman" and "an extraordinary representative for her community".

    "When I have an ethical dilemma, when I want to understand rural communities, when I want to hear commonsense, I speak to Helen."

    Not much unites politicians in Australia at the moment, but across the divide nobody has had a bad word to say about Helen Haines - adding to the shock that is being felt here.

  16. Threats to Australian politicians were rising before Haines incidentpublished at 00:17 BST 20 October
    00:17 BST 20 October

    Tiffanie Turnbull
    BBC News, Sydney

    As increasingly frequent acts of violence against politicians have shocked the globe in recent years, Australia has watched on with a contradictory feeling: that attacks like that could not happen here, and that it was only a matter of time until they did.

    But MPs and public figures – backed by experts – have been warning of a volcano of threats against them as social cohesion cracks, polarisation intensifies and the tone of debate grows hotter.

    According to government statistics, there were 1,067 reports of "harassing and threatening behaviour" against "high office holders" in the year to June: an increase on last year.

    "We tend to bury our head in the sand and say, 'Not here', but this has been happening now for well over a decade," Josh Roose, a Deakin University Professor on violent extremism, tells the BBC.

    Prime Minister Anthony Albanese swiftly described Monday's incident as an apparent attack, and though he stressed no motive had been confirmed, he got emotional as he talked about the growing fear amongst MPs that they would end up targets.

  18. Suspect was not known to policepublished at 23:48 BST 19 October
    23:48 BST 19 October

    In an update about the suspect this morning, police say there was "no recent intelligence... that put him on our radar".

    A 43-year-old man from Tarrawingee - about 12km east of Wangaratta - was arrested shortly after the incident and remains in police custody.

    Acting Superintendent Ashley Mason says police are treating the case as an "isolated incident".

    "There's no intelligence that we're aware of to indicate that there is any further or ongoing threat to any individual or the broader community," he says.

    He appealed to any witnesses who were near Murphy St, Wangaratta around 3.35pm on Monday and anyone who was driving nearby who may have dashcam footage to contact police.

  19. Local barber among 'heroes' who helped detain driverpublished at 23:30 BST 19 October
    23:30 BST 19 October

    A local barber in Wangaratta was among a group of by-standers who were first on the scene and detained the driver of the car that hit Helen Haines.

    "It happened directly out the front of my shop," Ricky Jefferies tells national broadcaster ABC, recalling the moment he grabbed the man out of the white Toyota Landcruiser.

    "When I pulled him out from the vehicle I searched him and found a knife in his pocket.

    "I had him sit on the ground and police got there within minutes. A number of selfless locals intervened, they were all heroes."

    Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese praised Jefferies, calling him a hero, during an interview on Channel Nine’s Today show this morning.

    23:16 BST 19 October

"It happened directly out the front of my shop," Ricky Jefferies tells national broadcaster ABC, recalling the moment he grabbed the man out of the white Toyota Landcruiser.

"When I pulled him out from the vehicle I searched him and found a knife in his pocket.

"I had him sit on the ground and police got there within minutes. A number of selfless locals intervened, they were all heroes."

Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese praised Jefferies, calling him a hero, during an interview on Channel Nine’s Today show this morning.

Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has been on local radio this morning, with more details on what he calls a "deliberate act" against Helen Haines, the independent MP for Indi.

He says the suspect drove past Haines and did a U-turn "in order to get back" to where she was before hitting her with the car.

"It's clear this was a deliberate act," he tells ABC News, adding it was not an "accident".

The motive for the attack is not clear, he says, and we shouldn't speculate at this point as police continue their investigations.
