British Airways exits entire market after 10 years

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:16 · source · permalink

A branch of the main national airline British Airways, BA City Flyer was established in 2007 with the goal of expanding the carrier's network of regional flights to smaller destinations in Europe.

This came at a time when British Airways had just sold its regional carrier BA Connect to Flybe and was left without a presence out of the London City Airport (LCY).

The name was a resurrection of the older CityFlyer Express that British Airways operated for 20 years from the 1980s until 2001, while the smaller but more central London airport remained the carrier's key hub over the next 20 years.

British Airways' City Flyer cancels 5 flights from London Stansted

This week, aviation watchdog Sean M spotted that City Flyer removed five routes out of London Stansted Airport (STN) that it had initially hoped to run in summer 2027 from its flying schedule.

The airline first entered the larger London airport used primarily by low-cost airlines in May 2016. Over the years, it has run more than 20 different routes to different corners of Europe from this hub.

British Airways pulled its Stansted operations in 2020 due to the Covid pandemic but resumed them in 2024 with routes to Florence and Ibiza.

According to the new flying schedule posted for the summer of 2027, the cut City Flyer routes include flights to Bergerac Dordogne Périgord Airport and Nice-Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE) in France, Florence Airport (FLR) in Italy, and Olbia Costa Smeralda Airport (OLB) and Palma de Mallorca Airport (PMI) in Spain.

As those were the only routes City Flyer had initially been scheduled to run, their suspension suggests that City Flyer may be exiting the Stansted market entirely in the coming year.

Representatives of the airline could not be immediately reached for confirmation or comment.

What to know about British Airways City Flyer Stansted flights in 2026 and 2027

At the moment, the airline continues to run weekend flights to Florence, Chambéry, and Geneva as part of its 2026-2027 winter schedule, but the latter two are seasonal routes that were never scheduled for the summer season.
