Grand Slam of Darts: Luke Littler lands triple-bull checkout, as Gerwyn Price benefits from maths error

TheTools · 17.11.2026 03:31 · source · permalink

Luke Littler (2024 and 2025) and Gerwyn Price (2018, 2019 and 2021) have both won the Grand Slam of Darts on multiple occasions

  • Published
    4 hours ago

Gerwyn Price and Luke Littler both made it through to the Grand Slam of Darts knockout stage on a night featuring a bizarre maths error and a sensational triple-bull checkout.

Littler suffered a 5-4 defeat by Dutchman Niels Zonneveld but claimed the three legs he required to progress from the group stage.

The 19-year-old, egged on by the crowd at the Aldersley venue in Wolverhampton, improbably landed three darts on the bullseye to produce a remarkable 150 checkout and level the match at 4-4, only to lose the deciding leg.

Price, meanwhile, claimed a 5-2 victory over Sebastian Bialecki - although he was the beneficiary of an unusual mathematical mistake.

The Welshman was 2-1 up against Poland's Bialecki and required 135 to win the fourth leg.

Price's first dart hit treble 19, leaving him with 78, but he asked referee Charlie Corstorphine what he had left to score and was incorrectly told 88.

In good faith, Price promptly threw treble 16 and then double 20 to win the leg, having scored 145 - which should have bust him.

"A rare scoring error was made by the referee in the fourth leg of the match between Gerwyn Price and Sebastian Bialecki," organisers the PDC told BBC Sport in a statement.

"Under Darts Regulation Authority (DRA) rule 6.4, as the error was not corrected before the next leg began, the leg score stands as announced."

Price said he was unaware of the miscalculation until it was flagged up to him before he walked into a post-match news conference.

"I hit the first treble and I asked 'what have I got left?' and he said 88, so I threw treble 16 and double top, then he said 'game shot'," said fourth seed Price.

"I'm glad I didn't know, because it probably would have played on my mind."

What do DRA rules say about Price error?

The DRA's rulebook is clear if there has been an error in scoring which, although rare in professional darts, can sometimes occur.

The following clauses are what the rulebook, external says about the mistake involving Price:

  • 6.4.6 Errors in arithmetic shall stand as detailed on the scoreboard unless corrected prior to the next throw of the player whose score is in error. Any error in the score can be corrected by the referee at any time until game shot is called in that leg.

  • 6.4.7 If the incorrect score resulted in a leg win the result will stand unless corrected prior to the start of the next leg.

  • 6.4.8 If the incorrect score resulted in the winning of a match the result will be considered as final when both players and the referee have left the playing area believing the match to be successfully completed.
