NVIDIA RTX Remix Was Built for Older Fixed-Function Games, but Modders Have Now Brought Path Tracing to Dark Souls and Fallout 4

TheTools · 16.11.2026 14:00 · source · permalink

NVIDIA's RTX Remix platform is really starting to flourish, thanks to the work of modders who are using it to remaster some of the most classic PC games.

When it launched in Open Beta in January 2024, RTX Remix targeted only older games built with fixed-function pipelines. Those titles sent standardized geometry and texture data rather than running opaque runtime shader code, allowing NVIDIA's tool to cleanly intercept draw calls and inject modern path tracing, custom PBR assets, and DLSS features, among other things. Games released after 2005, such as Half-Life 2 or Doom 3, heavily adopted per-pixel programmable shaders, making them much harder to reconstruct with automated fixed-function capture tools.

However, between the modders and NVIDIA, RTX Remix was slowly expanded and can now remaster even far more recent games, as evidenced by the debuts of Fallout: New Vegas RTX and Grand Theft Auto IV RTX. Over the weekend, another couple of PC classics received their first public RTX Remix versions: Dark Souls: Prepare to Die and Fallout 4.

The latter is being developed by Kim2091, one of the leading modders in the RTX Remix community. Following her work with New Vegas, she has now released Fallout 4 RTX Alpha 1, though this build is only available to her Bonfire and Radiant supporters via Ko-fi and Patreon. The Bonfire tier costs $7 monthly, while Radiant is priced at $15. Of course, once the mod becomes stable enough, it will be released for free on Nexus Mods and its source code will be made public.

As for Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition RTX, this one is already available on Nexus Mods. Author gabdeg, one of the most active in the RTX Remix community, has shared the following feature set:

  • Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition fully rendered with path tracing in RTX Remix
  • New RTX Remix-compatible PBR maps created based on the game's assets 
  • New dynamic light sources for in-game effects such as spells, fires, etc.
  • Game image pipeline faithful to the original presentation 
  • Emissive particle rendering
  • RTX Remix particle effects
  • Subsurface scattering for player characters
  • Fluid surface simulations for water and mud
  • High frame rate support through Frame Generation
  • DLSS 5 NR support 
  • Experimental HDR10 support

This version is also still very much a work in progress, so users shouldn't expect a full remaster. The author also said that while the focus is currently on the Prepare to Die Edition, Dark Souls Remastered might eventually be compatible if NVIDIA's latest RTX Remix updates can be ported.
