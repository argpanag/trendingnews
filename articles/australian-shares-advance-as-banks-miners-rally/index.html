Australian shares advance as banks, miners rally

Australia’s sharemarket has started the week higher after banks rallied in anticipation of US earnings season, while rebounding metals prices supported mining stocks.

The S&P/ASX 200 rose 66.6 points on Monday, up 0.76 per cent, to 8783.2, as the broader All Ordinaries gained 59.3 points, or 0.67 per cent, to 8937.

Resurgent copper prices lit a match under BHP shares, which surged more than 2 per cent to $62.39, adding a cool $7.3 billion to the market value of Australia’s largest company.

BHP ended the session up 2.45 per cent. Other miners joined the rising tide. Fortescue climbed 2.87 per cent and Rio Tinto 1.6 per cent. Gold miner Northern Star lifted 2.5 per cent and Evolution Mining ended the day flat.

Among financial stocks, Westpac led the big four banks higher (2.7 per cent) amid optimism around US banking earnings due later in the week. Commonwealth Bank climbed 1.15 per cent, National Australia Bank 2.26 per cent and ANZ Bank 1.96 per cent.

Technology stocks also followed their Wall Street peers higher.

Oil prices eased during the session despite ongoing supply risks and conflict in the Middle East. Woodside Energy shed 0.43 per cent and Santos was flat. Refiner Ampol slumped 3.11 per cent, while rival Viva Energy fell 0.93 per cent.

Brent crude was near $US105 a barrel after King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh came under attack again overnight by Iran-backed Houthi militants, a day after another strike killed 12 people.

Major Middle Eastern oil producers have managed to resume crude exports through Hormuz close to pre-conflict levels but the spike in tanker attacks, and heightened tensions across the region, could threaten those flows.

US President Donald Trump announced this morning that Russia and Ukraine have agreed to an “energy ceasefire”, effective immediately. “Both parties have agreed. Do not break it!” he wrote on his Truth Social platform. He didn’t provide further details.

The Australian dollar was buying US69.77¢, down from US69.83¢ on Friday at 5pm.

On Friday in New York the S&P 500 climbed 0.6 per cent for its first gain since setting its all-time high three days earlier. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 423 points, or 0.8 per cent, and the Nasdaq composite rose 0.6 per cent.

Treasury yields flipped up and down in the bond market, following their own sharp moves earlier in the week. The yield on the 10-year Treasury eventually climbed to 5.24 per cent from 5.22 per cent late on Thursday.

It recently touched its highest level since 2002 because of worries about high inflation, the US government’s huge debt load, signals that the US economy is continuing to grow, and other factors. High yields threaten to slow the economy by making borrowing money more expensive for everyone, and the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage has already leapt to its highest level in nearly three years.

Yields are also shooting higher in other bond markets around the world, which makes investors less willing to pay high prices for stocks and other investments. But the high yields have attracted investors to recent auctions of Treasurys, and that demand is helping to limit further increases for yields.

The pressure is on companies, broadly, to deliver strong growth in profits after their stock prices ran towards records despite the downward push from high bond yields. Next week will bring high-profile profit reports from the country’s biggest banks, including Citigroup, JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo, all on Tuesday.

Expectations are high for corporate profits, even as Americans say they’re feeling more squeezed by high inflation. A preliminary report from the University of Michigan said on Friday that US consumers are raising their own forecasts for how bad inflation will be over the coming year.

They’re expecting inflation of 4.7 per cent, up from their forecast of 4.6 per cent a month ago and just 3.4 per cent in February before the war with Iran began. Sentiment among consumers is falling sharply for Americans with lower incomes and smaller investments in stocks, who can least afford the fast increases in the cost of living.

In sharemarkets abroad, indexes rallied in Europe with gains of at least 1 per cent in France, Germany and the United Kingdom.
