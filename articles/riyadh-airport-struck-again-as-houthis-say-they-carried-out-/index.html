    The Houthi group in Yemen has taken responsibility for multiple "targeted" attacks on Saudi Arabia in the past 24 hours, including the deadly attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on 10 October.

    The Houthi group reiterated its stark message to all airline companies, warning them not to use Saudi airports or airspace, as "they are unsafe".

    Earlier today, the same airport was struck again, resulting in "some injuries," the Saudi civil aviation authority says.

    These are the latest in a spate of attacks on the country, representing a heating up of the conflict between the Houthis and Saudia Arabia - which is backing the internationally-recognised government in Yemen that the Houthis are working to topple.

  5. Houthis outline operations carried out in Saudi in response to 'aggression'published at 17:16 BST 11 October
    17:16 BST 11 October

    The Houthi group has put out a statement taking responsibility for Saturday's deadly attack on the King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, and reiterating its warning to all people not to operate flights to Saudi airports.

    Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree says on Telegram that it is in response to "brutal Saudi aggression against our country and our people" and claims Saudi Arabia launched 124 airstrikes in the past 24 hours at Yemen's territory controlled by the group.

    Saree says in the past 24 hours the Houthis carried out four operations, which include:

    • Three phases of attacks on King Khalid International Airport, starting Friday evening, which he claims resulted in the complete closure of the airport
    • An attack on Shugadam gas facility, belonging to Saudi's national petroleum and natural gas company Aramco, which he says caused "large fires"
    • An attack on Aramco's Tanajib oil refinery, resulting in "ignition"
    • And a fourth operation targeting King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, which he says caused air traffic disruption

    The statement adds: "We reiterate our warning to all international companies, experts, employees, workers, and travelers from all over the world to not operate flights to Saudi airports, as they are unsafe, except for the airports and airspace of the holy cities of Mecca and Medina."

  8. Situation in the Middle East may have taken a new turn after airport attackpublished at 16:25 BST 11 October
    16:25 BST 11 October

    Frank Gardner and Jon Donnison
    BBC security correspondent and Jerusalem correspondent

    As part of the "axis of resistance", the Houthis are a vital strategic partner for Iran.

    The Red Sea shipping route has become increasingly important, because the Strait of Hormuz, on the other side of the Gulf, has still not fully reopened since the US and Iran have failed to reach a deal to end their war.

    Targeting ships in the Red Sea is another way that the Houthis are trying to exert leverage - the attacks cause global economic damage, which is a key bargaining chip for Tehran.

    In early September, the White House stated it would not directly intervene in the Yemen conflict, but could provide intelligence support to the Saudis. It later confirmed that it was supplying intelligence and had deployed military experts to Saudi Arabia.

    However the situation may have taken a new turn following the latest attack on Riyadh, which killed a US citizen.

    Speaking to reporters, US President Donald Trump called Saturday's attack "terrible" and indicated he may intervene militarily with the Saudis.

    "We just found out about the recent attack, so we'll make a decision. We move very quickly," Trump said.

    But that now seems unlikely, at least before crucial US midterm elections next month.

The UK Foreign Office is advising against all travel to parts of Saudi Arabia.

It advises against:

  • All travel to within 10km of the border with Yemen
  • All but essential travel to Aseer province, Riyadh province, Najran province, Jizan province and Taif city and Taif International Airport

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband earlier said he was "appalled" by the attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, adding "we stand in solidarity with those affected".

He told British nationals to avoid the airport, following Saudi authority advice, and to follow the government's travel advice, external.

Houthi warning intended to amplify the psychological and economic impactpublished at 12:41 BST 11 October
12:41 BST 11 October

Hesham Shawish
BBC Monitoring Middle East Journalist

This morning we saw the fresh warning from the Houthis, warning people against using Saudi airports and airspace.

The latest warning shows that Houthi signalling towards Saudi civilian aviation is part of the pressure campaign against the Saudis to try and end their blockade of Houthi controlled areas in Yemen.

This type of warning is really intended to amplify the psychological and economic impact. We've already seen hundreds of flights cancelled or delayed by airlines, so it does have a major impact.

It will make travellers and insurers more wary too, so it is intended to cause a lot of damage beyond physical damage.

We'll likely see an increase in the diplomatic lobbying for countries to either condemn the Houthi escalation against Saudi airports or for an intervention from Pakistan and Turkey - the two other signatories of the Mecca pact, a joint defence agreement.

The United Arab Emirates (UAE) has condemned Saturday's attack on its neighbour Saudi Arabia.
