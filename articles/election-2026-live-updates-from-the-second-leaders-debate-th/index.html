Election 2026: Live updates from the second leaders' debate | The Spinoff

TheTools · 14.10.2026 06:05 · source · permalink

Chris Hipkins and Christopher Luxon face-off once again in the second leaders' debate.

Catch all the action as Red Chris goes up against Blue Chris.

The polls have Labour and National neck-and-neck. Hang with us for this high-stakes debate.

The poll of polls

Calling all Spinoff members! Cast your vote below to decide who's the winner in this debate. You only get one vote, so choose wisely.

Ready, set, go!

And we're off! Hipkins and Luxon entered the stage with a polite handshake. As winner of the coin toss, Hipkins gets to answers Ryan Bridges' first question: "have you ever told a porkie?"

"Not deliberately, no," Hipkins said - it's just that sometimes you say something that you realise wasn't true.

"I don't think a politician needs to do that," was Luxon's reply.

You heard it here first: this is the first time the Chrises have told lies.

The Chrises have entered the building

T-minus 10 minutes until the Chrises take the stage. There have been endless echoes of "NO NEW TAXES" from a strong crowd of National Party supporters in a pack outside the NZME building. On the other side of the road, Labour supporters are doing their best to out-vibe the blue team. When Chris Hipkins arrived, it was to the dulcet tones of The Soca Boys' 'Follow The Leader'.

Here we go again...

Welcome to a bout of déjà vu. Yes, you're once again witnessing Chris Hipkins and Christopher Luxon heading into the ring for a debate, but this time it's hosted by the NZ Herald's Ryan Bridge. I know what you're thinking: we've really got to stop meeting like this. Sorry, but that's out of the question. This is only the second Chris v Chris debate – there's another one next week.

Tonight's comes after Tuesday1News/Verian poll saw National and Labour deadlocked on 28%. If there's no overhang come election day, a result like this would leave neither the right- or left-bloc with enough seats to form a government, meaning they'd both need to pick up the phone to Opportunity, sitting at 8% in the poll, which would have the party bringing 10 MPs. Of course, Luxon has already ruled out this possibility, but when push comes to shove, you never know what one might do to stay in power.

Now that Chris and Christopher have had some practice with Jack Tame at TVNZ last week, audiences will be hoping for a bit more fire. Hipkins, widely crowned the winner of the last debate, poked the bear a few times but shied away from swinging the bed leg. With the purple vote looking worse than ever, both Chrises will be hoping to sway undecided voters. Showing a bit of personality wouldn't hurt either.

After the debate wraps, The NZ Herald's Katie Bradford will host a panel with Newstalk ZB's Heather du Plessis-Allan. In a pre-debate chat, Plessis-Allan highlighted the one thing the campaign has been missing so far: a big vision. "They're hearing this, but I think they're too scared," Plessis-Allan said. "A big vision opens you up to criticism, and this is too tight to take any chances."

There's still 25 days to go until November 7 (and 11 days left before enrolments end and advance voting begins, eek!), so there's still some time to shift the dial. If only slightly. Our editor Veronica Schmidt will be the judge of that; come back to The Spinoff first thing tomorrow to see what she makes of the leaders' efforts.
