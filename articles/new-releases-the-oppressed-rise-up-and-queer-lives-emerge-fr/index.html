The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (《饥饿游戏：黎明收割》) (PG13)
159 minutes
★★★

Story: A young Haymitch (Joseph Zada) takes part in the 50th Hunger Games. This edition marks the Quarter Quell, held every 25 years, when each district must send twice as many “tributes”, making the fight to the death even more brutal. Haymitch forms an unexpected alliance with Donor (Mckenna Grace) in the arena, before joining the resistance against President Snow (Ralph Fiennes). As the Games progress, Haymitch is no longer fighting merely to survive. He risks everything to bring down the killing contest from within and ignite a rebellion that could change Panem’s fate. In English with Chinese subtitles.

Review: Following 2023’s The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes (《饥饿游戏：鸣鸟与游蛇之歌》), the new film is set 24 years before the first Hunger Games film. It reveals how the teenage Haymitch becomes the hard-drinking mentor, played by Woody Harrelson, who guides Jennifer Lawrence’s iconic character Katniss.

American director Francis Lawrence returns to helm his fifth Hunger Games film. This time, the Quarter Quell doubles the number of tributes as the embers of rebellion begin to glow. Yet the deaths come thick and fast without delivering a corresponding emotional impact. Some plotlines arrive with too little groundwork and are hastily wrapped up, promising much but delivering little.

A particularly interesting development comes after Haymitch wins, when the organisers manipulate the footage to misrepresent him. Those in power turn truth into lies and lies into truth: they not only decide who survives, but also control what audiences accept as reality. That is more thought-provoking than the killing in the arena.

When I spoke about The Hunger Games with a viewer born in the 1990s, he said the series had long become part of his memories of growing up. That nostalgia alone is enough to keep fans buying tickets, whatever the story. (Swa Xin Ying)

La Bola Negra (《黑球》) (R21)
159 minutes
★★★★

Story: Co-directed by Spanish filmmaking duo Los Javis — Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi — the film draws inspiration from Spanish poet and playwright Federico García Lorca’s unfinished work La Bola Negra, of which only four manuscript pages survive. Spanning 1932, 1937 and 2017, it interweaves the fates of three generations of gay men to explore queer memories and emotions suppressed and buried by history.

Review: After watching this hugely ambitious film, it is easy to understand why Los Javis, who are also a couple, won the Best Director award at the 79th Cannes Film Festival. Both the film and novel Cloud Atlas (《云图》) left my head spinning. La Bola Negra could well be called a gay Cloud Atlas.

With its many characters — several of whom look alike — tangled relationships, blend of fact and fiction, sweep across past and present, and weighty themes of homosexuality and war, the film is a veritable web of narrative traps. The directors skilfully carry viewers between settings, drawing them into the emotional lives of characters from different eras. Their ambition is matched by genuine craft. With clever, sly manoeuvres, the film sidesteps historical tragedy and redirects our attention, offering a different view of history and an empathy that transcends time and place. The real focus is a parade of male bodies; Hollywood stars Penélope Cruz and Madonna are merely decorative.

Special mention must go to cinematographer Gris Jordana, whose fluid, poetic camerawork is the most stunning I have seen recently. Visual beauty like this demands a big screen. I knew little about modern Spanish history before watching the film, but it left me wanting to explore the origins of the conflicted love, hatred and repression that marked those turbulent times. In Spanish with English subtitles. Showing exclusively at Filmhouse. (Chong Yen Ling)
