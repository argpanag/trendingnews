Everything to know about 2026 NBA Cup ahead of Celtics-Bulls opener

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:10 · source · permalink

NBA Cup season has arrived.

The fourth installment of the league’s annual in-season tournament tips off Friday night, with the Celtics hosting the Chicago Bulls at TD Garden in one of 10 group-stage games.

The reception to commissioner Adam Silver’s attempt to spice up the NBA’s early-season slate by replicating the European soccer model has been mixed since its inception in 2023. But the NBA Cup does at least give this otherwise forgettable stretch of the schedule a fresh coat of paint (literally).

“I think it’s great,” Celtics wing Sam Hauser said. “You get sort of a playoff feel in the middle of the season, or I guess at the start of the season. You saw last year that the Knicks and the Spurs played in the NBA Cup championship, and those two met in the (NBA) Finals.

“So obviously it means something, and we’ve got to be better with getting into the bracket rounds and then advancing after that.”

Here’s everything you need to know about this year’s edition:

How does the format work?

Teams are split into six groups of five (three per conference). Each team plays the other four in its group on designated “Cup Nights” from late October through November.

The three group winners from the East and West, plus the top second-place finisher from each conference, advance to the single-elimination knockout rounds. If teams finish with identical group-stage records, the following tiebreakers are used (in order):

1. Head-to-head record in group play
2. Point differential in group play (excluding overtime)
3. Total points scored in group play (excluding overtime)
4. Record from the 2025-26 NBA regular season
5. Random drawing

Point differential has been needed to break at least one tie in every NBA Cup/In-Season Tournament to date.

The quarterfinals and semifinals will be played in the home arena of the higher-seeded team. That’s a change from previous years, when the semis were held at a neutral site in Las Vegas.

The 2026 NBA Cup championship also was moved out of Vegas. It will take place at Butler University’s famed Hinkle Fieldhouse — the former stomping grounds of Celtics president of basketball operations Brad Stevens — on Dec. 11.

Teams that do not advance out of group play and the losing quarterfinalists will play hastily scheduled consolation games against other eliminated clubs.

Who will the Celtics play?

Boston got a favorable Cup draw. Of its four opponents in East Group C, just one (the Atlanta Hawks) made the playoffs last season.

The Bulls and Washington Wizards are mid-rebuild, and the Charlotte Hornets, one of the best teams in the second half of last season, traded away multiple starters this summer.

Here is the Celtics’ full group-play schedule:

Friday, Oct. 30 vs. Chicago (7:30 p.m. ET)
Friday, Nov. 6 at Charlotte (7 p.m.)
Tuesday, Nov. 24 at Washington (8 p.m.; NBC/Peacock)
Friday, Nov. 27 vs. Atlanta (8 p.m.)

The Celtics were knocked out in the group stage in each of the last two NBA Cups and have yet to advance beyond the quarterfinals.

How is this different from the regular season?

In most ways, it’s not. Every NBA Cup game, excluding the championship, counts toward a team’s regular-season record, and all standard rules apply.

There are, however, two notable differences — one aesthetic and one tactical.

First, NBA Cup games are played on special courts that differentiate them from non-Cup contests. This year’s Celtics court is a deep green with a black shamrock logo at center court; NBA Cup trophies in both lanes; a gray parquet pattern along the sidelines and baselines; and oversized “CELTICS” lettering, in the spray paint-esque font the league has gravitated toward this season, covering most of the playing surface.

The other difference is the impact point differential has on how teams handle late-game scenarios. Since the Cup format encourages teams to win by as many points as possible in the group stage, it behooves coaches to push for blowouts rather than easing up.

In one memorable example of this, Celtics head coach Joe Mazzulla had his players intentionally foul then-Bulls center Andre Drummond with Boston up double digits in a 2023 In-Season Tournament game. The Celtics’ 124-97 victory was just enough to get them through to the knockouts, giving them a five-point edge in their tiebreaker over Orlando.

The Celtics were on the other end of group-play stat-padding last year, when the Magic cranked up their offensive pressure to score 14 points in the final two minutes of a 123-110 win.

“Yeah, it’s weird,” Hauser said. “You’ve got to win by certain amounts. I know the last couple years, we’ve been up big on a team and you have to play until literally the final buzzer goes off. So that’s a little weird, but you’ve got to do what you’ve got to do to get in.”

What’s the prize?

To incentivize players to actually care about the NBA Cup, the league hands out increasingly lucrative bonuses as teams advance through the tournament.

An NBA spokesperson told The Athletic that every standard-contract player on this year’s NBA Cup champion will receive $604,985. That number drops to $241,994 for the runner-up, $120,997 for the losing semifinalists and $60,499 for the losing quarterfinalists. Players on two-way contracts will receive half of that value.

Those bonuses might not move the needle for a highly paid superstar making $50 million per year, but they would mean a great deal to a mid-tier or bottom-of-the-roster player. Eight of the Celtics’ 14 rostered players are making less than $3.2 million this season.
