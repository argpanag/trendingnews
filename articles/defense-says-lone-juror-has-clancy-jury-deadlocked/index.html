Defense says lone juror has Clancy jury deadlocked

TheTools · 04.09.2026 17:28 · source · permalink

Support WBUR

A single juror appeared to be in disagreement with the other 11 jurors in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy, as deliberations concluded for the sixth day.

At around 3 p.m., Plymouth County Superior Court Judge William Sullivan reminded the jurors of how they should view his instructions and reiterated the meaning of "beyond a reasonable doubt," before ordering them to resume their deliberations.

Attorneys from both sides were called to side bar, and at one point Clancy, who is in a wheelchair, was brought forward to join the discussion.

After the sidebar, Sullivan again reminded jurors to follow his instructions and explained the nature of reasonable doubt, before asking them to return to their mulling.

Once the jury exited, defense attorney Kevin Reddington was allowed to speak by the judge and complained about a single juror, accusing them of "not following" the law related to what beyond a reasonable doubt means, and suggested they should be removed. The judge declined, saying he could not take a side in the deliberations.

In all, jurors have spent more than 35 hours deliberating, trying to decide whether Clancy, 36, can be held criminally responsible for the killings of her three young children before trying to take her own life in 2023.

Reddington has argued Clancy should not be held criminally responsible for the killings because she was experiencing post partum psychosis at the time, employing what's often called an insanity defense.

Jurors will return to court Friday to continue their work.
