AMD surges on AI chip boom, forecasts revenue to jump over 80% next year

TheTools · 04.11.2026 02:59 · source · permalink

U.S. semiconductor corporations AMD posted record quarterly results in the third quarter this year on the back of rising demand for artificial intelligence (AI) data center chips. Operating profit rose 123% from a year earlier, and data center revenue nearly doubled. AMD projected that total revenue next year will grow more than 80% from this year as it expands supply of next-generation AI accelerators and server central processing units (CPUs).

AMD released its third-quarter results on the 3rd (local time). Under U.S. Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP), revenue was $13,377 million (about 17.9386 trillion won) and operating profit was $2,832 million (about 3.7977 trillion won). Year over year, revenue increased 44.7% and operating profit rose 123.0%. Compared with the prior quarter, revenue grew 16.0% and operating profit rose 42.3%. The operating margin was 21%, up 7 percentage points from the same period last year (14%) and 4 percentage points from the prior quarter (17%).

Third-quarter net income was $2,428 million (about 3.2559 trillion won), up 95.3% from a year earlier. Diluted earnings per share (EPS) under GAAP were $1.46, about twice the level of the same period last year ($0.75).

Excluding one-time expense and other items, non-GAAP operating profit was $3,923 million (about 5.2607 trillion won), up 75% from a year earlier. The non-GAAP operating margin was 29%, an improvement of 5 percentage points from the same period last year.

Lisa Su, AMD chief executive officer (CEO and chair), said on the earnings conference call, "The third quarter became the largest and most profitable quarter in our company's history as we began ramping mass production of next-generation data center products," adding, "Data center revenue growth accelerated each quarter this year, and we expect the growth to continue in the fourth quarter as we expand production of new products."

◇ Data center revenue nearly doubles… AI accelerators and server CPUs grow together

AMD's third-quarter data center institutional sector revenue was $8,546 million (about 11.4602 trillion won), up 96.9% from a year earlier. It also rose 27.2% from the prior quarter. Data centers accounted for 63.9% of total revenue, up sharply from about 47% a year earlier. The data center institutional sector operating profit was $2,882 million (about 3.8648 trillion won), up 168% from a year earlier. The operating margin rose from 25% to 34% over the same period.

In the cloud and enterprise server markets, rising sales of "Epyc (EPYC)" CPUs, along with expanded supply of the AI accelerator "Instinct," supported results. Su said, "Both cloud and enterprise server revenue more than doubled from a year earlier," adding, "As corporations adopt AI agents for software development, supply chain management and information technology (IT) operations, CPU demand is rising significantly."

In the third quarter, AMD began shipments of the sixth-generation Epyc processor "Venice," accompanied by revenue. According to Su, server manufacturers and original design manufacturers are preparing more than 240 platforms featuring Venice, and initial systems are scheduled to launch in the fourth quarter.

Mass production of next-generation products also began in the AI accelerator business. AMD expanded shipments of the Instinct MI450 series in the third quarter and said major customers including Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle have begun initial operation of the AI server system "Helios."

Helios is an AI computing system that combines GPUs, CPUs, memory and networking into a single rack unit. AMD is targeting the large-scale AI training and inference market by supplying next-generation Instinct GPUs and Epyc CPUs together.

Su said, "The third quarter marked an important inflection point, as it was the first quarter with revenue from MI450-series GPUs and Helios racks," adding, "Supply will increase significantly in the fourth quarter, and production expansion will continue in 2027."

◇ Fourth-quarter revenue seen nearing 23 trillion won… Total revenue next year expected to grow more than 80%

AMD forecast fourth-quarter revenue of about $17,000 million (about 22.7970 trillion won). The expected range is $16,490 million to $17,510 million (about 22.1131 trillion won to 23.4809 trillion won). Based on the midpoint of the outlook, that implies about 66% growth year over year and about 27% growth quarter over quarter. The non-GAAP gross margin was projected at about 55%.

In particular, it projected fourth-quarter data center institutional sector revenue will increase about 50% from the prior quarter. It also projected that revenue from the AI accelerator business will surpass revenue from the server CPU business for the first time.

Su said, "We expect total company revenue to grow more than 80% in 2027," adding, "Server CPU business revenue will roughly double, and data center AI business revenue will grow well beyond double." She added, "In 2027, the data center business will account for more than 80% of total revenue," and "Data centers will drive most of the company's overall revenue and profit growth."

However, securing the supply chain to meet explosively growing AI Semiconductor demand remains a task. Su said, "It is true that demand exceeds supply," adding, "We are working closely with customers and supply chain partners to secure the necessary components, including wafers, semiconductor package substrates, advanced packaging and memory."

On rising prices for components including memory semiconductors, she said, "We have long-term contracts with some suppliers and customers," adding, "Not only memory but other components are also facing upward price pressure, and we are discussing with customers ways to share these expense."

◇ PC CPUs face pressure from higher memory prices… PC market expected to fall by double digits next year

In contrast to the data center business, the PC and gaming institutional sector was weak. Third-quarter client and gaming institutional sector revenue was $3,722 million (about 4.9912 trillion won), down 8% from a year earlier. Operating profit was $573 million (about 768.4 billion won), down 34%.

Client business revenue, which sells PC processors, was $2,949 million (about 3.9546 trillion won), up 7% from a year earlier but down 4% from the prior quarter. Gaming business revenue was $773 million (about 1.0366 trillion won), down 40% from a year earlier.

Meanwhile, embedded business revenue was $1,109 million (about 1.4872 trillion won), up 29% from a year earlier. Increased demand for semiconductors in aerospace and defense, automotive, communications and industrial applications had an impact.

Regarding the weakness in the PC market, Su said, "Rising component prices are affecting overall system prices, and the consumer market in particular is more sensitive to price changes," adding, "We have seen some slowdown in actual sell-through." She added, "We are planning our business on the assumption that PC shipments will decline by a double-digit rate in 2027," and "We will focus on higher value-added areas such as enterprise and premium consumer segments and the desktop market."

AMD plans to flexibly allocate production resources to expand supply of data center chips and respond to slowing demand in the PC market. Su said, "The supply chains for PCs and data center businesses can be leveraged in large part in common," adding, "We will adjust supply based on market demand and respond to additional data center demand."
