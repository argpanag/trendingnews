Warner questions CA’s ‘safe space,’ calls for ‘independent inquiry’

David Warner has also reacted to Cricket Australia’s Sandpapergate forum idea before play in Hobart.

Warner welcomed the idea in principle but questioned “is it really going to be a safe space?” and “is everyone going to be able to talk?”

CA said that they would be able to, but Warner called for “an open independent inquiry” that would presumably be run separate to the national body.

“It’s not about penalties, it’s about accountability and transparency,” Warner - one of the three banned players - said on Fox Cricket.

Former Australian vice-captain David Warner cries. AAP

Khawaja says CA will ‘cherry-pick’ Sandpapergate findings

Usman Khawaja has applauded Cricket Australia for creating a forum on Sandpapergate but has serious questions about whether the public will ever really know the truth.

Khawaja told Fox Cricket he wishes CA had come up with the idea “a week and a half ago” after his book created a civil war for the game.

He liked the idea of a “safe space” for players to air their views but questioned whether that would amount to anything tangible.

CA boss Todd Greenberg was vague as to what extent the findings of the forum would be made public.

Usman Khawaja and David Warner. Getty Images

“The biggest question I have, and I wrote it down, is the board makes the decisions on what comes out at the end,” Khawaja said.

“How is that an actual way of trying to present what people have said? So I could go in there and say something, but then the board can decide that they’re not going to release that, and I just feel like then we just go in a circle again of, ’this is happening, that’s happening…”

Khawaja said he didn’t want anyone to be punished and said that the “price has already been paid” for David Warner, Steve Smith and Cameron Bancroft.

He said the CA board “can choose whatever they want to come in and come out” from the forum and predicted they would “cherry-pick.”

Khawaja also claimed staff on the 2018 tour “aren’t allowed to talk” but was unsure as to whether they had signed non-disclosure agreements.

“We all want to move on,” he said.

David Warner and Usman Khawaja. Getty Images

CEO says Cummins ‘doesn’t need to be cleared’

Todd Greenberg says there is no heat on captain Pat Cummins or any of the other bowlers from the 2018 tour.

Cummins and his fellow bowlers have maintained they didn’t know sandpaper was being used on the ball.

Greenberg says this new forum will not be used to find any other culprits from 2018.

“The captain doesn’t need to be cleared,” Greenberg says of Cummins

“The investigation was complete.

“We’ve got almost everyone’s version of events, and they differ substantially. We have to give respect to each individual.”

Pat Cummins addresses a media pack. Getty Images

Greenberg’s message to Bancroft

Todd Greenberg is asked whether he’s afraid of what Cameron Bancroft might say in coming days.

There is speculation the batter may go public with his version of events from 2018, when he was busted using sandpaper on the ball.

“No, I’m not (concerned),” Greenberg says.

“Cameron is entitled, just like any other player, to share their views.

“He can use this (forum) mechanism to share his views, he can share them publicly. Every other player should be afforded the same right.”

Did more than three people know?

Steve Smith, David Warner, and Cameron Bancroft are still the only people who have ever been punished for the events of 2018.

Several players have stated more people knew about the sandpaper use prior to them being caught in Cape Town.

Todd Greenberg admits he doesn’t know what the truth is.

“I don’t know the answer to that question,” Greenberg says when asked if more than three people knew.

“The events conflict with each other, people’s views of the events, and that is the most complex part of this.

“Anyone who comes into this forum. I encourage them to be as specific as possible, it is hard to deal in generalities.”

Greenberg has ruled out ever making the results of the 2018 investigation public given it was conducted under confidentiality.

He is asked if the 2018 investigation was “botched” but he denies that was the case – he does confirm only EIGHT of the 11 players in that Cape Town Test were interviewed.

Results of forum may not be made public

Todd Greenberg says the new forum is not a reopening of the Sandpaper investigation from eight years ago.

“No, the investigation has been completed,” Greenberg says.

“The forum is designed where people who weren’t interviewed, or felt they weren’t afforded the opportunity to contribute, to now contribute.

“We want to be able to give them the opportunity they deserve.”

Greenberg can’t guarantee the results of the forum will be made public.

Greenberg admits ‘hurt’ over Sandpapergate

Cricket Australia CEO Todd Greenberg:

“Over the past week we have had several different and conflicting accounts of what transpired in Cape Town in 2018, and it has been an incredibly difficult and uncomfortable time for everyone involved.

“What happened then, and continues now to play out, has a lot to do with the culture that existed back at that time – a culture CA played a part in creating.

“We are very proud of the culture this team has built over the past eight years. Our leaders, our coaches, deserve enormous credit for that, in particular Pat (Cummins) and Andrew (McDonald) who continue to lead this team with distinction.

“We recognise the hurt the events around sandpaper have caused. The game is genuinely hurting at the moment.

“It’s not good enough for anyone, we need to find a path through and find time to heal.”
