BMWs, Louis Vuitton bags, Rolex watches seized amid charity money-laundering allegations

TheTools · 22.10.2026 23:08 · source · permalink

Gardaí have conducted a number of searches and seized assets, including luxury vehicles and designer goods, as part of an investigation into allegations of money laundering at a registered charity.

Liberty Recycling, the charity under investigation, collects clothing via a vast network of clothing banks nationwide. It has a major processing facility in Bluebell, Dublin, and processes a 40ft container of clothing every week.

It has a significant presence in Dublin and also has a national footprint, with dozens of full-time staff and volunteers. Most of its clothing goes to destinations in Asia and Africa, and some is sold via its network of nine shops across Ireland.

The most recent financial records for the organisation suggest its income and expenditure increased significantly in the period just after the pandemic and had reached €5 million in 2024.

The group has also proven adept at attracting well-known figures in politics and the charity sector to become involved in its activities, so boosting its profile and reputation.

Sinn Féin TD Aengus Ó Snodaigh is listed as the current chairman, while Tony Geoghegan, who ran the Merchants Quay drug treatment organisation in Dublin for decades, is also a trustee. There is no suggestion whatever that either man is under investigation.

Liberty Recycling did not reply to a series of queries from The Irish Times. However, as Ó Snodaigh is involved as chairman, and is a prominent TD, Sinn Féin responded, saying it had become aware of the investigation by the Garda and Charities Regulator.

“Sinn Féin fully supports any investigation,” it added. “Deputy Ó Snodaigh has engaged with the office of the Charities Regulator. He has not been contacted by Gardaí, but if required, he will make himself available to assist with their inquiries.”

Some of the group’s expenditure is now under investigation on suspicion it effectively amounts to money laundering.

Specifically, gardaí are investigating if money intended to fund the charity’s services, helping impoverished and addicted people, has been misappropriated.

The assets seized during two days of searches this week were taken on suspicion they had been funded, or part funded, by money from the charity.

Any such activity – converting money into assets to conceal the origin of the funds, and for personal gain – would fall under the legal definition of money laundering.

However, sources familiar with the investigation stressed that no findings had been reached. They added the inquiry was still at the stage of probing allegations and gathering evidence, with no conclusions reached.

Garda Headquarters issued a short statement on Thursday morning confirming searches took place as part of an investigation, in conjunction with the Charities Regular, into a “Dublin-based charitable organisation”.

“As part of an ongoing investigation by detectives attached to the Money Laundering Investigation Unit at the Garda National Economic Crime Bureau, gardaí carried out a number of searches at locations in the Dublin, Eastern and Southern Regions,” it said of the searches on Tuesday and Wednesday.

“During the course of the searches, gardaí seized two BMW X5 cars with a total estimated value of €250,000 and other high value items including Louis Vuitton bags, Rolex watches, and jewellery with a total estimated value of €40,000.”

The Charities Regulator confirmed to The Irish Times that is has been assisting with the Garda investigation and is engaging with the trustees of the charity involved. Neither the Garda nor regulator have identified the charity or the precise nature of the investigation.

The charity has been in operation for more than two decades and while its main presence is in Dublin, it is well established, with centres offering services, in at least counties across the country.

It also has a much higher number of points around the country where people can make contributions to help fund the charity’s work and supply items towards the services it provides.
