Free, live K-pop moves to the centre of FAST schedules

Samsung TV Plus streams the 2026 MAMA AWARDS free in 14 countries on Nov. 20–21, after SMTOWN LIVE (18) and KCON LA (17). It added creator channels in eight markets in September. Korea's K-FAST Alliance now has 82 members.

Samsung TV Plus, Samsung Electronics' free ad-supported streaming (FAST) service, will carry the 2026 MAMA AWARDS live from Kyocera Dome Osaka in Japan on Nov. 20 and 21. The broadcast runs on CJ ENM's K-POP channel in 14 countries, and viewers can watch both days, from the red carpet to the ceremony, without paying.

2026 MAMA AWARDS livestream schedule (KST). Source: Samsung Electronics, CJ ENM

The 14 markets include Australia, Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Italy, Mexico, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Spain, Sweden and the U.K. When the channel launched in 2024 it served the U.S., Canada, the U.K., France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Brazil and Mexico; India, Australia, New Zealand, the Netherlands and Sweden were added this year. Samsung says viewers can set reminders and adjust TV sound and lighting to match the show.

CJ ENM gives artist awards on the first night and performance awards on the second. It is building a 360-degree stage in the dome and a segment made with Google's Gemini generative AI. BIGBANG, in its 20th year, performs with the cartoon character Dooly the Little Dinosaur (아기공룡 둘리), and ENHYPEN with the film Dune: Part Three. Panic (패닉), a duo in its 32nd year, shares the stage with WOODZ and members of CORTIS, ZEROBASEONE, ALLDAY PROJECT and Hearts2Hearts, and an "Art Meets MAMA" segment puts the work of contemporary artist Lee Bae (이배) on stage.

Actor Park Bo-gum (박보검) hosts for the eighth time since 2017 and is MAMA's first ambassador. The Korea Herald reported the lineup is the largest the show has fielded in a single host city. MAMA also streams worldwide on Mnet Plus; Samsung serves viewers on the TV set. "Together with CJ ENM, we're bringing fans two unforgettable days of live performances and special collaborations they can enjoy from the comfort of their homes," said Hun Lee, executive vice president of Samsung's Visual Display business.

The 2026 Melon Music Awards (MMA2026), held at Gocheok Sky Dome in Seoul on Nov. 14–15, streamed live on Melon and Wavve, U-Next in Japan, TVBS42 and CatchPlay+ in Taiwan, and 1theK. Of the two K-pop award shows held this month, MAMA used a free TV channel for international viewers and MMA used subscription services and a YouTube channel.

SMTOWN LIVE in 18 countries, KCON LA in 17, MAMA in 14: three K-pop livestreams in 18 months

Samsung TV Plus's K-pop livestreams began with MAMA in November 2024. It streamed SM Entertainment's SMTOWN LIVE 2025 in L.A. to 18 countries in May 2025 and CJ ENM's KCON LA 2026 to 17 countries in August, while its MAMA coverage grew from nine countries in 2024 to 14 this year. Over 18 months, three major K-pop shows went out live to the world on a free TV channel.

SMTOWN LIVE 2025 in L.A., SM Entertainment's 30th-anniversary concert at Dignity Health Sports Park on May 11, 2025, was streamed exclusively in 18 countries on the new SMTOWN channel on Samsung TV Plus. TVXQ!, SUPER JUNIOR, SHINee, EXO, aespa, RIIZE and NCT 127 were on the bill. Samsung then offered exclusive replays in 14 countries for six months, and Asia Economy reported cumulative viewership above 10 million.

On Aug. 14–16, 2026, Samsung TV Plus carried CJ ENM's KCON LA 2026 live in 17 countries, including France, India, Thailand, the U.K. and the U.S., from Crypto.com Arena and the Los Angeles Convention Center. A year earlier, KCON LA 2025 had streamed on Amazon Music's Twitch channel and Prime Video.

In May 2026 Samsung and SM Entertainment launched Monthly SM Concert, airing concert films every Saturday at 7 p.m. on the SMTOWN channel and Samsung's STN channel in Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Brazil and Mexico. Junheon Choi (최준헌), head of the TV Plus group, said the series builds on the SMTOWN LIVE exclusive to give global fans a steady place to watch K-pop.

CJ ENM took its K-POP by CJ ENM channel to Samsung TV Plus in the U.K., France, Spain, Italy, Germany, Sweden and the Netherlands in January 2025. In North America it launched K-Dance, exclusive to LG Channels, as its sixth FAST channel, and in September it announced CJ ENM K-Pop and CJ ENM K-Stories FAST channels on Amazon Prime Video. Omdia's 2025 forecast put Samsung TV Plus third in global FAST share at 13%, behind Roku (21%) and Pluto TV.

100 million users, 4,300 channels; former Google Korea chief runs Samsung's TV business

Figures Samsung released in January put Samsung TV Plus above 100 million monthly active users, with streaming hours up 25% year on year and three-month retention at 92%. In September 2024 it counted 88 million active users on Tizen-powered TVs.

In May Samsung named Won-jin Lee (이원진), a former head of Google Korea, to lead its Visual Display business and its service business team. In a March newsroom column, Visual Display EVP Gwi-ho Lee (이귀호) wrote that the TV is evolving beyond a device that delivers content into a platform that actively connects users with content.

Google Freeplay: 300 channels and 10,000 titles; Apple and Amazon add free content to the home screen

FAST channels have long been criticised for running mostly re-runs of Korean variety shows and dramas such as Infinite Challenge (무한도전) and 2 Days & 1 Night (1박 2일). As the Samsung TV Plus case shows, free streaming platforms that now sit on the living-room TV are gathering more formats, led by live events and news. Samsung has added exclusive K-pop livestreams that viewers watch together at a set time. The service passed 100 million monthly active users in January and runs about 4,300 free channels in 30 countries.

Gracenote, the Nielsen data unit, counts 4,500 news programmes on FAST channels in August 2026, up 45% from 3,100 a year earlier, in its 2026 State of Play report.

FAST's growth is, paradoxically, also a product of the flood of paid streaming. Nearly 35% of Gracenote's respondents said there are so many subscription services that they now spend more time on FAST channels, and more than 51% pay for three or more services. Streaming has become the norm, but as subscription bills rise, more viewers pair a few core paid services with FAST.

Twelve minutes to choose: nearly 35% say overload sends them to FAST

More than 51% of Gracenote's respondents said the abundance of content and services makes it harder to find something to watch. The average search takes 12 minutes, 15 for 18-to-24-year-olds. 27.3% frequently give up on a programme they cannot find, and 51.6% would cancel a service if they cannot find anything of interest, up from 48.5% a year earlier.

Nearly 35% said they spend more time on FAST channels because the subscription landscape has become overwhelming. 72.3% want one guide across all their services, and 87.4% say user experience matters when deciding which services to keep. Ampere Analysis puts the share of consumers overwhelmed by choice at 46% and says smart TV operators are using FAST to bring back sit-back, scheduled viewing.

Big Tech platforms are moving too. On Aug. 11 Google added more than 10,000 on-demand films and shows to Google TV Freeplay and lifted its free live lineup above 300 channels, including titles from A24 and Lionsgate and channels such as Bloomberg TV+, Yahoo! Sports Network and The Martha Stewart Channel. Freeplay is U.S.-only. Google put free channels into the Google TV Live tab in 2023 before bundling them as Freeplay.

Advanced Television on Nov. 16 looked at how Apple, Amazon and Google are adding free content to smart TV home screens. Apple plans to put subscription-free video podcasts into the Apple TV app, which reaches more than a billion devices; 35% of its video-podcast customers switch between audio and video at least once a month. Amazon links Fire TV devices, its free Fire TV Channels and its ad business in one ecosystem. The publication also cited niche channels, such as gaming and casino streams, that build regular audiences on small budgets.

Free content on major TV platforms. Sources: Google, Advanced Television, Samsung Electronics

When a platform carries channels directly, publishers reach viewers without another app to install, but the platform decides which channel sits in the first row. Advanced Television concluded that the companies controlling home screens gain influence over which services viewers encounter first.

From casino and gaming streams to golf and science: niche channels build loyal audiences on small budgets

As FAST grows, platforms are drawing audiences with creators and channels built around specific interests rather than expensive originals.

Advanced Television argues that, as FAST grows, platforms are drawing audiences with channels built around specific interests rather than expensive originals: classic TV, documentaries, sports highlights, technology and single genres. It cites gaming streams, where audiences watch other people play, and casino streams, where viewers follow gambling sessions and see how other players approach the games, as programming traditional TV schedules rarely carried.

CasinoStreamers is one site that aggregates such streams. It ranks live casino streams from Twitch and Kick by viewer count on one page, awards points for watch time and offers a monthly prize to the top of its leaderboard. The page is labelled free, no purchase needed and 18+. When captured on Nov. 19, 95,365 people were watching 345 live streams.

A channel serving a specific audience may draw fewer viewers overall but can still generate repeat visits and steady ad exposure, Advanced Television says, letting advertisers place campaigns next to relevant programming rather than buying broad TV audiences. It adds that revenue depends on viewing time, return visits and ad demand more than on the number of channels, and that viewers will tolerate only so many ads.

Samsung TV Plus's Golf Life and Mark Rober TV, and the K-beauty, K-dance and vertical-drama channels the K-FAST Alliance is building this year, are interest-led channels of the same kind. Gracenote collects listings for about 2,100 FAST channels. Genres with a defined fan base, such as K-pop concerts and music shows, are programming units that lend themselves to repeat viewing and ad tie-ins in this structure.

Creator channels in eight more markets; Mark Rober TV dubbed for Brazil and Mexico

On Sept. 28 Samsung TV Plus launched new creator-led channels in Australia, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and the U.K., and extended existing creator channels to Brazil and Mexico. In the U.K. it partnered with 208 Studios on three channels: Happy Place, interviews from Fearne Cotton's podcast (more than 15 million social followers); Golf Life, golf and comedy from Ange, Jimmy and Tubes Bullard (more than 3 million combined followers); and JOE Media, the U.K. news and entertainment brand with more than 30 million followers.

Australia, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand get Luke Nguyen's World, following the Australian-Vietnamese chef through food, culture and travel across more than 95 episodes in nine seasons. Brazil and Mexico get Mark Rober TV, a 24-hour science and engineering channel of dubbed content from the former NASA engineer, who has more than 79 million followers across platforms. Samsung TV Plus already carries Dhar Mann, Michelle Khare, Smosh and The Try Guys.

"The creator economy has become a global phenomenon, reshaping how audiences discover and engage with content," said Salek Brodsky, head of global TV Plus at Samsung Electronics. On the Samsung home screen promoting the MAMA livestream, the first "Now Playing" tile is Mark Rober TV.

More viewers are watching influencer content. Across the 24 markets Ampere surveyed, weekly viewing of influencer content rose from 63% in 2020 to 70% in 2026. 83% of 18-to-24-year-olds watch weekly, while the biggest gains came among 45-to-54-year-olds (22%) and 55-to-64-year-olds (20%). Music and music videos top the list of content watched on social media, which helps explain why free TV channels now schedule K-pop livestreams alongside YouTube creators.

News on FAST up 45% in a year; CNN Headlines audience up 192%

As FAST platforms scale, the genres that perform on them are becoming clear: news, sport and information programming, which are also areas where K-FAST channels are relatively thin. Gracenote counts 4,500 news programmes on FAST channels in August 2026, up from 3,100 a year earlier; with episodes, the count rose from 5,900 to 7,400. The report cites Adweek's August finding that CNN Headlines, the network's FAST channel, grew its audience 192% in a year.

Sports programming on the six global subscription services Gracenote tracks rose 25% in a year. Live sport (54.4%) ranks behind only movies (91%) and original series (71%) as a reason to subscribe. The report says streaming is coming to resemble the scheduled TV experience viewers once left.

12.4% of U.K. viewers prefer FAST; ad tolerance up from 44% to 54%

Asked which streaming option they prefer, 50.4% of 3,000 respondents in Brazil, France, Germany, Mexico, the U.K. and the U.S. chose ad-supported tiers, ahead of premium ad-free services at 41.8%. FAST averaged 7.8%, with 12.4% in the U.K., 10.8% in the U.S. and 9.6% in Germany. Gracenote ties the shift to streaming price rises outpacing inflation.

Dataxis estimates North American streaming ad revenue made up 51.1% and 50.5% of the total in the first and second quarters of 2026, ahead of subscriptions. Grand View Research projects European FAST revenue to grow 15.6% a year and pass $8.5 billion (about ₩11.34 trillion) by 2033. Streaming ad loads average about five minutes an hour, against 12 to 16 minutes on traditional TV.

In markets where Netflix has an ad tier, the share of consumers willing to see ads for a cheaper or free service rose from 44% in Q3 2022 to 54% in Q3 2026, Ampere found. Monthly use of free ad-supported platforms was 13% across its markets and 35% in the U.S.

30% in four English-speaking markets watch Korean titles; frequent subtitled viewing up from 43% to 56%

As streaming grows, the shape of TV viewing is changing. Ampere Analysis's November report on ten years of its Media Consumer survey finds daily scheduled-TV viewing across 14 markets has fallen by almost an hour from 1 hour 48 minutes in 2017. Subscription streaming overtook scheduled TV in the past two years.

Across Australia, Canada, the U.K. and the U.S., frequent viewing of subtitled TV shows and films ("very often" or "sometimes") rose from 43% in 2020 to 56% in 2026, Ampere found, and reached 59% among subscription streaming (SVOD) users. 30% of consumers in the four markets watch Korean titles, which Ampere attributes to hits such as Squid Game and Parasite; 23% frequently watch Spanish-language content. Audiences for subtitled content are 22% more likely than average to be 18 to 34, and frequent viewers of dubbed content 30% more likely.

Consumers worldwide now watch more than an hour of video a day on smartphones, while TV viewing has fallen 15 minutes since early 2024, according to Ampere. MAMA splits its coverage between Samsung TV Plus on the TV set and Mnet Plus on mobile.

K-FAST Alliance grows from 22 to 82 members; 20 AI-dubbed channels reach 100 million viewers in 22 countries

Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (과학기술정보통신부) and the Korea Association for ICT Promotion (KAIT, 한국정보통신진흥협회) launched the Global K-FAST Alliance on April 23, 2025, chaired by Kim Sung-chul (김성철), a professor at Korea University's Graduate School of Media. Its 22 founding members included Samsung Electronics, LG Electronics, KBS, MBC, SBS, CJ ENM, JTBC, NEW ID, KT, SK Broadband, LG Uplus, Hudson AI and ESTsoft. K-EnterTech Hub is also a founding member.

Membership reached 82 companies by July 2026, and the alliance reorganised into five working groups: content and channels, technology, advertising and platforms, global, and coordination. With government support, about 1,200 Korean titles (1,400 hours) were AI-dubbed into English, Spanish and Portuguese for 20 K-FAST channels. Carried on Samsung TV Plus and LG Channels in 22 countries including the U.S., Canada and Mexico since December 2025, they drew about 100 million cumulative viewers by the end of April. This year three consortia led by Hudson AI, ESTsoft and UnAI (언에이아이) are building four new channels for vertical drama, K-beauty and K-dance, with about ₩230 million (about $172,000) per channel.

"We will continue our support to build a sustainable K-FAST ecosystem," Nam Seok (남석), director general for telecommunications policy at the ministry, said at a July working-group meeting. A Statista forecast cited by Money Today puts the FAST market at about ₩16 trillion in 2025, rising to about ₩23 trillion by 2030, a 7.47% annual growth rate.

The Korea Communications and Media Commission (방송미디어통신위원회) met FAST industry representatives at Samsung Innovation Museum in Suwon on June 4. Participants said Korean FAST channels largely re-schedule previously produced content and do not reflect local demand, and discussed programming around North American viewer preferences and linking viewing data to advertising.

Conclusion: live rights, English metadata and home-screen placement

FAST began as a re-run model, packaging older programmes into 24-hour channels. Google, Amazon and Samsung built those channels into their TV home screens, and this year K-pop livestreams such as MAMA, KCON and SMTOWN LIVE joined creator channels such as Mark Rober TV, news, sport and video podcasts on the grid. Gracenote's report concludes that content drives sign-ups but user experience decides whether viewers stay.

The K-FAST Alliance's AI-dubbed channels re-version existing titles, and the commission's June meeting flagged the limits of re-run channels. The MAMA, KCON and SMTOWN LIVE streams show that live assets held by Korean companies, from award shows to music programmes and concerts, can reach overseas viewers through FAST. For Korean broadcasters to send year-end music award shows or weekly music programmes out the same way, overseas FAST rights have to be secured at the production stage.

Gracenote collects listings for about 2,100 FAST channels, and 72.3% of viewers want a unified guide. Channels with poor listings data drop out of consolidated guides and recommendations. For the same reason Gracenote cites in sports, K-pop and Korean channels aimed at overseas viewers need English-language programme, performer and episode information.

As Google, Amazon and Apple pull free content forward, whoever runs the TV operating system decides which channels lead. Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics own such home screens and both belong to the K-FAST Alliance. The placement and ad-revenue terms that Korean broadcasters and labels agree with them are where the K-FAST ecosystem starts.

Market shifts such as 15.6% projected annual growth in European FAST revenue, 54% ad tolerance in Netflix ad-tier markets and streaming ad revenue passing half the North American total are part of the picture. If Google takes Freeplay beyond the U.S. and Apple adds video podcasts to its TV app, free content will take up more of the home screen. Which K-pop shows beyond KCON and MAMA go out live on FAST next year, and whether Korean terrestrial music programmes use the same route, will set the direction for the next phase of K-FAST.
