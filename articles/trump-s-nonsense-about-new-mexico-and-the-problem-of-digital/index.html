Trump’s nonsense about New Mexico and the problem of digital diarrhea among politicians

TheTools · 20.10.2026 10:00 · source · permalink

Being a good politician is incompatible with being a loudmouth—that is, with speaking more than prudence and responsibility dictate.

An immoral reward from Trump to Putin for massacring civilians in Ukraine
Trump's verbal incontinence comes to Sánchez's rescue with an attack on Spain

Once again, yesterday, the current president of the United States was kind enough to illustrate to the world the risk posed by a loudmouthed politician online. On his Truth Social account, he posted the following message:

"New Mexico should immediately change its name to New America. We are not Mexico, we are the United States of America! The name would add great value and prestige to the State! Home Values and Incomes would rise, and you would be even prouder while saluting our beautiful American Flag! Maybe even the Crooked Elections, that New Mexico is known for, would become honest again!
President DONALD J. TRUMP"

To begin with, New Mexico has borne that name since long before Mexico gained its independence from Spain. The Spanish began calling this territory "San Felipe del Nuevo México" in the 16th century, under the mistaken belief that it was inhabited by peoples related to the Mexica—a notion that was, in fact, incorrect. Mexico adopted this name following its independence in 1821, as it encompassed what had once been the Viceroyalty of New Spain; thus, the name "New Mexico" was not coined by the Mexican state. In 1850, the United States established the Territory of New Mexico, which would go on to become the present-day state in 1912.

Trump’s remark regarding New Mexico begs the question: doesn't this guy have more important things to do? What is the point of demanding that a state established in 1912 change its name simply because he happens to dislike it? What Trump has said is a classic example of cheap populism—distracting people with nonsense to steer the conversation away from more significant matters, such as his immoral pact with the dictator Putin to purchase large quantities of diesel from Russia—thereby helping to fund a terrorist regime that assisted the Iranian dictatorship in killing American soldiers, an act for which Trump ought to be tried for treason.

There is one thing that really stands out when reading Trump’s account on Truth Social. Despite being 80 years old, the U.S. president posts messages compulsively, much like a 13-year-old teenager. He maintains a posting pace that I—who am 30 years his junior—have never adopted on any social media platform, if only to avoid overwhelming those kind enough to read my work. Such "digital diarrhea" means casting aside essential elements of writing—reflection, prudence, and a sense of responsibility—virtues that are conspicuously absent from his posts.

Behavior like this should be a source of vicarious embarrassment for the followers of any politician, regardless of their ideology; yet, as is so often the case, it seems the "what" matters far less than the "who." In Spain, we have politicians who suffer from this same kind of "diarrhea"—such as the Socialist Óscar Puente—who spends his time insulting anyone who says or does something he dislikes. Clearly, taxpayers in both Spain and the United States do not fund a politician’s salary so that they can spend all day spouting nonsense on social media; rather, they are paid to work in the service of their citizens and their country. Politicians afflicted with "digital diarrhea" are a problem that must be addressed, as their condition degrades political discourse and—worse still—stokes societal conflict with foolish, impulsive remarks. We should demand better from adults who hold government office, given the significant harm caused by this type of behavior.
