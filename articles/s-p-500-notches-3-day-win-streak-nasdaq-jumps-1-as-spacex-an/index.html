S&P 500 notches 3-day win streak, Nasdaq jumps 1% as SpaceX and Tesla rally

TheTools · 20.10.2026 22:06 · source · permalink

Stocks rose on Wednesday as the latest developments surrounding SpaceX and Tesla led investors to move back into technology.

The Nasdaq Composite added 1.05% to end at 26,702.86, and the S&P 500 rose 0.58% to 7,757.17. The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 360.21 points, or 0.69%, to close at 52,856.09.

Shares of Elon Musk's SpaceX surged 7% after the company said its revenue grew 161% in the third quarter. Musk also said that SpaceX and Tesla, his auto manufacturer, have formed special committees to discuss a merger. Tesla, which is due to report earnings later, saw shares rise more than 3%.

SpaceX's artificial intelligence business grew ninefold, reigniting enthusiasm for stocks related to the trade. Shares of Advanced Micro Devices advanced more than 3%, while Nvidia's stock rose more than 2%. Shares of Micron Technology and Marvell Technology grew nearly 2%. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) was up close to 2% as well.

"SpaceX suggests there is still a longer runway here, but that fundamental tension or experience of getting overly enthusiastic and overly capitalizing things I don't think has changed," said Joe Zidle, Cresset's chief market strategist.

Concerns around the AI trade's sustainability have weighed on market sentiment lately, as traders focused on debt-backed AI investments and whether the infrastructure demand justifies companies' spending. But Zidle anticipates that the current reporting period will show that a number of AI-related companies are continuing to see stronger demand.

"I think when we get to the end of earnings season and we look back, SpaceX is not an anomaly; it's not a phenomenon; it's actually something that's going to be, I think, more common to the sector," he said.

Investors are seeking more insights into the AI trade on Wednesday, with Meta Platforms also set to report after the bell. Earlier this month, the company rolled out its latest AI model, Muse Wonder, which is only available to paying users.

Traders are also watching for results from IBM and Southwest Airlines, both of which are due after the close.

Netflix was a key laggard during Wednesday's session. Shares tumbled more than 5% after the streaming mainstay's full-year revenue forecast came in below analysts' expectations. The company also cut its ad revenue guidance for the full year.

The three leading U.S. indexes rose on Wednesday.

The S&P 500 added 0.58% to finish at 7,757.17, while the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.05% to 26,702.86. The Dow Jones Industrial Average increased 360.21 points, or 0.69%, to 52,856.09.

— Sean Conlon

CME Group's outgoing CEO Terry Duffy said innovation needs to be balanced with effective regulation to protect market integrity and participants, speaking to CNBC's "Power Lunch" Wednesday.

His comments come amid a wave of innovation in financial products like perpetual futures contracts and tokenized assets – ideas made popular in the crypto market that have challenged the status quo in the traditional market, particularly demand for always-on markets. Duffy said the push to compete in the 24/7 trading boom mustn't outpace regulation, however, especially as retail access increases.

"Innovation is critically important, it's the lifeblood to any institution … You've got to be careful not to have innovation for the sake of innovation without regulation. And sometimes that's what I'm seeing with the products," he said.

He raised concerns about the CFTC's handling of more than 4,000 self-certified contracts and the risks of extreme leverage without enforceable limits to protect retail traders and broader financial stability.

"Innovation is great," Duffy added, "as long as it's not at the cost of efficiencies and credibility to the marketplace."

— Tanaya Macheel

Fears about massive artificial intelligence spending has sent spreads wider in the credit market, but Barclays is expecting a shift in sentiment in the coming weeks.

That could lead to some tactical, short-term opportunities in tech credit, analyst Andrew Keches said in a note Tuesday.

"The demand side of the AI equation remains incredibly strong, IG [investment grade] balance sheets remain entrenched, and there are near-term catalysts that could restore market confidence," he wrote.

Those include hyperscaler earnings, such as Meta's results coming after the bell, and Oracle investor day, expected Oct. 28. Alphabet and Microsoft also report quarterly earnings on Oct. 28.

Apple is set to report on Nov. 2, while Oracle hasn't set a release date yet for its next quarterly results.

That said, Keches doubts this is the last round of volatility and he remains underweight tech in the medium term. "[F]unding will remain an overhang through 2027."

Oppenheimer says that Zoom Communications' AI platform is "working better than expected," and it's changing the outlook for the stock. 

The bank upgraded the stock to outperform from perform, and placed a $130 price target on the stock. That implies a 33% gain from Tuesday's close. Analyst Timothy Horan wrote in a Wednesday note that Oppenheimer is gaining confidence in Zoom's transition from a video meeting platform to a larger AI communications and workflow one. 

"Its AI platform is enjoying strong uptake, growing ARPU," average revenue per user, "and retention," Horan said in the note. "Integrating communications with AI applications should deepen customer relationships and raise the growth rate to double digits, all on stable margins."

Shares of United Airlines up more than 4% on Wednesday.

The carrier provided a weaker-than-expected fourth-quarter profit outlook as rising fuel costs weighed on profitability but reported revenue of a record $17.9 billion, which is a 17% increase year over year.

"Our employees met the challenge of our ever-changing environment and our results prove the resilience of focusing on building a brand-loyal airline for customers," United CEO Scott Kirby said in an earnings release.

United expects adjusted earnings of 50 cents to $1.50 per share in the fourth quarter, below the $1.76 analysts expected.

The airline reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $2.54 per share, missing estimates of $2.57.

Fuel expenses surged 62% from a year earlier, even as premium cabin and corporate travel revenue climbed 17% and 26%, respectively. The carrier expects to pay an average of $4.80 a gallon for fuel in the fourth quarter, up from an average price of $3.90 a gallon in the third. 

— Deena Zaidi

Asked about embattled neocloud CoreWeave, Altimeter Capital CEO and vociferous AI bull Brad Gerstner acknowledged the uncertainty in future demand levels for computing power.

"The reality is, nobody knows what the demand is going to be in 12 months," he told CNBC on Wednesday. "Most of us who are in the game are saying this is accelerating. We're forecasting that demand is going to accelerate. But of course there's a distribution of future unknown probabilities."

Gerstner told CNBC earlier this month that he had taken his exposure to the AI trade down, though he didn't get into specifics during the interview.

He reconciled his professed bullishness on AI with his diminished exposure by saying that he lives in "two simultaneous but different worlds," distinguishing between his hedge fund investments and his other investments.

"One world is I manage money for people in a hedge fund, and so my job is to make sure I avoid some of that drawdown. In my long-only fund and in my growth funds and venture funds, we're investing in the next five to ten years," he said.

Profitability concerns have rattled the AI trade in recent weeks as revenues coming in from frontier models Anthropic and OpenAI have disappointed analysts.

Some on Wall Street think that AI, which is quickly becoming more of a base-level commodity than a premium product due in part to the proliferation of open-source models, is not profitable enough to substantiate trillions in capital expenditures from equipment builders.

"We have more compute capacity than we have demand, and we have a lot of the tokens and processing power being given away in freemium solutions," John Lovelock at Gartner told CNBC on Monday. "Everyone in the AI race is running for a last-man standing position. Pretty much every market that Gartner is tracking in our AI race is likely to collapse down to a duopoly."

— Tobias Burns

Mortgage demand took another leg lower, despite a brief respite from rising interest rates.

Total mortgage application volume fell 5.3% last week compared with the previous week, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association's seasonally adjusted index.

The average contract interest rate for 30-year fixed-rate mortgages with conforming loan balances, $832,750 or less, decreased last week to 7.41% from 7.50%, with points decreasing to 0.75 from 0.83 (including the origination fee, for loans with a 20% down payment.

"Mortgage rates decreased for the first time in over two months," said Joel Kan, an MBA economist in a release. "However, lower rates were not enough to boost application activity, as both purchase and refinance applications continued to decline."

The U.K. government has approved new drilling in the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea.

The Jackdaw project, whose operator Adura is a joint venture between Shell and Equinor, lies about 150 miles east of Aberdeen, Scotland.

Adura said the field can potentially provide 6% of Britain's total gas supply, and could be connected to the U.K. grid within months. That would help boost the U.K.'s energy security at a time when the country — a net energy importer —remains vulnerable to external price shocks.

Japan's exports in September rose 16.7% from a year earlier, missing market expectations by a huge margin. Reuters-polled analysts had pegged exports growth at 20.1%.

This is the second straight month that shipments from Japan have recorded a slowdown, after hitting a near four-year high in July.

Growth was supported by continued strong semiconductor exports, with shipments rising 49.4% year on year by value.

Exports of machinery also bolstered growth, with semiconductor-related machinery shipments up 43.9% and power generation machinery exports climbing 22.6%.

In terms of destinations, shipments to mainland China — Japan's largest trading partner — rose 8.6%, while exports to the U.S. climbed 22.9%.
