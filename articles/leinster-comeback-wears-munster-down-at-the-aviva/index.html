Leinster comeback wears Munster down at the Aviva

TheTools · 24.10.2026 19:04 · source · permalink

Leinster overpowered Munster in the second half at Aviva Stadium, as Leo Cullen's side came from 13-0 down to record a bonus-point 24-18 win against their rivals.

Tries for Tommy O'Brien, Ryan Baird, Charlie Tector and Jamie Osborne gave the defending champions their second URC win of the season, as Munster's hefty injury list proved decisive down the stretch.

While both sides' internationals will head off to Ireland camp this week, there could be a significant fallout in the days ahead, with an allegation of foul play late in the first half seeing play stopped for several minutes.

Munster's Gavin Coombes alleged he was grabbed in the groin area by a Leinster player at a ruck, and while referee Andrew Brace stopped play for several minutes to consult his TMO, they could not find a conclusive angle to pursue it, although the incident could still be referred to the citing commissioner.

Munster had been dominant in the first half, as their backrow tormented Leinster at the breakdown, and they were 13-0 in front after 28 minutes, with Shane Daly racing in for a stunning try, and 20-year-old out-half Charlie O'Shea kicking a conversion and two penalties on another impressive outing.

Clayton McMillan's side had used the scrum as a major weapon so far this season, but they were on the backfoot at the setpiece as Andrew Porter got on top of Kieran Brookes, which proved crucial in Leinster's second half comeback.

It was 13-5 at half time, Tommy O'Brien with Leinster's only score, before they wore Munster down with scores from Baird, Tector and Ryan, but a missed conversion from Joey Carbery after Osborne's try gave Munster a window to save a losing bonus-point, Ben O'Connor giving them something to take away from Dublin in the final minute.

A strong crosswind favoured no team in particular in the opening half, but it was Munster who settled into the game better in the first quarter of an hour.

McMillan's side kept the ball as much as they could, and kicked well when they had to, with O'Shea giving them a 3-0 lead from the tee after James Ryan's high tackle on Kieran Brookes.

The hosts were slow to warm to the game, and eventually some half-breaks from Carbery and Hugo Keenan had the Munster defence under pressure either side of the 20-minutes mark, but Josh Wycherley and Gavin Coombes came up with vital interventions at the breakdown, winning jackal penalties which allowed them clear the lines.

Their tenacity in defence was rewarded on 25 minutes with a sensational try, as Munster exposed Leinster's rush defence; Jamie Osborne shot up and left a hole for O'Shea to play Sean O'Brien into, and the centre had the support of Craig Casey, whose pass out the back door sent Daly running under the posts for a converted try and a 10-0 lead.

Munster's were dominant at the breakdown, and three minutes after the try the province extended their lead with an O'Shea penalty, the out-half judging the wind to perfection from right on the halfway line to make it 13-0.

On 33 minutes, Leinster hit back, although there was a touch of fortune to the try.

Referee Andy Brace got bundled over while Leinster were attacking the Munster 22, and the scrum to restart the game gave them an ideal platform to attack, with Jamison Gibson-Park exploiting a narrow Munster defence, throwing a fantastic wide pass to Tommy O'Brien for the first Leinster try, but Carbery's conversion was wide of the mark, as the visitors brought a 13-5 lead into the half time break.

Leinster came close to landing another blow early in the second half when a needless shoulder charge by Kieran Brookes on Rónan Kelleher was followed by two more penalties, before Munster just about held Robbie Henshaw up short of the line.

They kept the foot on the accelerator though, as Andrew Porter began to dominate Brookes at the scrum, and after back-to-back penalties they eventually broke through for a try on 55 minutes, scored by Baird and converted by Carbery to make it 13-12, while Munster lost Alex Nankivell to the sin-bin after an accumulation of penalties.

Heading into the final quarter, Leinster's dominance was growing. Munster repelled one attack with a huge jackal penalty from Beirne, but a missed lineout immediately after led to another attacking wave from the hosts, which saw Tector (above) dive over to give his side the lead for the first time, 17-13 on 62 minutes, although Carbery's conversion came back off the post.

Munster looked out on their feet in the final quarter, and when they lost Daly to a head injury, it forced McMillan to send O'Shea out to the right wing, with Leinster continuing to hammer home their dominance.

With eight minutes left, the hosts made sure of the win as they reaped the reward for a dominant second half, with Osborne diving over for their bonus-point try, converted by Carbery for a 24-13 advantage.

That score essentially sealed the full five points for Leinster, but there was still time for Munster to grab a deserved losing bonus-point, as struck with the final play of the game, O'Connor diving over in the corner to leave six points between the sides at the final whistle.
