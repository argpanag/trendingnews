Mets To Sign Tarik Skubal

TheTools · 23.11.2026 00:10 · source · permalink

The Mets have made another blockbuster addition, as the club has signed two-time AL Cy Young Award winner Tarik Skubal to a nine-year, $450MM contract.  The deal is a straight nine-year pact with no opt-outs and a full no-trade clause.  Skubal will get the first $45MM of his deal as a signing bonus, and $30MM of the southpaw’s future salary will be deferred.  The contract will become official once Skubal passes a physical.  Skubal is represented by the Boras Corporation.

As many expected heading into the offseason, Skubal has landed the largest contract ever given to a pitcher, with his $450MM payday far exceeding the $325MM Yoshinobu Yamamoto received from the Dodgers (over a 12-year term) in December 2023.  Skubal’s contract is the fourth largest guaranteed salary given to any player, topped only by Juan Soto’s $765MM from the Mets two years ago, the $700MM Shohei Ohtani received from the Dodgers, and Vladimir Guerrero’s $500MM extension with the Blue Jays.  Skubal’s $45MM in average annual value also puts him fourth all-time in that category, behind Kyle Tucker, Soto, and Ohtani.

MLB Trade Rumors naturally ranked Skubal in the top spot on our list of the offseason’s top 50 free agents, and his $450MM deal is even further north of our nine-year, $425MM projection.  Steve Adams and Darragh McDonald each predicted Skubal would sign with the Mets, which was a logical choice given both New York’s severe need for starting pitching, and the fact that the Mets are one of the few teams willing and able to meet Skubal’s huge asking price.

The contract is a heck of a birthday present for Skubal, who turned 30 years old just two days ago.  The left-hander enters his age-30 season on the heels of four outstanding seasons that have cemented his case as perhaps the single best pitcher in baseball.  A relatively under-the-radar ninth-round pick for the Tigers in the 2018 draft, Skubal emerged as an elite pitching prospect and was starting to really come into his own in 2022 before a flexor surgery kept him on the shelf for almost 11 months.

Skubal returned from that surgery on July 4, 2023, and has since dominated baseball.  Even though he missed half the 2023 season and missed six weeks this year (due to a NanoNeedle procedure to remove loose bodies from his throwing elbow), Skubal’s 21.3 fWAR since Opening Day 2023 is still the highest of any pitcher in baseball.  He has delivered a 2.47 ERA over 626 1/3 innings for the Tigers and Dodgers in those four seasons, as well as a superb 31.1% strikeout rate and 4.5% walk rate.  The left-hander was a unanimous choice as the AL Cy Young winner in 2023, and garnered 26 of 30 first-place votes in 2024 to capture the trophy a second time.

The loose-bodies issue threatened to interrupt Skubal’s platform year and perhaps might’ve impacted his contract if it wasn’t for his remarkably quick return to the mound, and the fact that he continued to look great upon his return.  The 2026 campaign was also marked by a blockbuster deadline trade that sent Skubal to Los Angeles for a package of three young players.

Detroit’s struggles left the club as a borderline contender at best by the deadline, and with little hope of re-signing Skubal, the Tigers opted to swing the trade with a look ahead to 2027.  While draft pick compensation wouldn’t have impacted Skubal’s market, this midseason trade made him ineligible for a qualifying offer, so the Mets don’t have to surrender any picks or international bonus money as part of the signing.

The switch to the National League didn’t curb Skubal’s effectiveness, and pitching for the NL West champs allowed Skubal his third straight year of postseason play.  Skubal has a 2.13 ERA over eight starts and 50 2/3 innings in the playoffs, and at this point in his career, a World Series ring is basically the only thing missing from what is looking like a Cooperstown-worthy resume.

The Mets haven’t won the World Series since 1986, as Steve Cohen’s six years of ownership have resulted in a ton of big spending but only mixed results on the field.  The Amazins made the NLCS in 2024 and won 101 games in 2022 to reach the playoffs as a wild card, but these are the club’s only two playoff appearances of Cohen’s tenure.  The good vibes of the 2024 playoff run seemed to carry over for the first part of the 2025 campaign, yet a slow and steady collapse left New York out of the NL postseason field entirely.  This year, a brutal 22-33 start basically wrote the Mets off before the start of June, and the team finished last in the NL East with a 74-88 record.

A below-average rotation was one of the biggest reasons for the Mets’ struggles, but Skubal’s addition naturally makes the situation much brighter.  Skubal and star rookie Nolan McLean now may be baseball’s best one-two punch, Christian Scott and Zac Thornton are two more younger arms who delivered solid results in their first full MLB seasons.  Sean Manaea is penciled into the fifth starter’s role, with Kodai Senga, Jonah Tong, and (returning from Tommy John surgery) Tylor Megill among the names lined up as either depth starters of perhaps bullpen arms.

Another rotation addition probably shouldn’t be ruled out, as New York could look to add a lower-cost veteran to augment the back end of the starting five.  President of baseball operations David Stearns has traditionally eschewed major long-term commitments to starting pitchers, yet Skubal is now obviously the biggest exception possible to this strategy.  While it is safe to guess that Cohen was driving the push for Skubal, the left-hander is such a clear-cut ace that Stearns was surely also open to going above and beyond to lock in a frontline star for the next nine years.

As one might expect, not even Skubal can turn around a 74-win team.  Beyond any other additions to the rotation or bullpen, the Mets are known to be looking for another big bat for the first base and/or DH spots.

It remains to be seen what will happen with the Competitive Balance Tax under the next collective bargaining agreement, but assuming the luxury tax survives in a somewhat similar fashion, the Mets will surely again soar over the highest penalty level.  MLBTR’s Payroll Tracker projects the Mets for a tax number of around $287.2MM even before Skubal’s deal is added.

CBT penalties clearly don’t faze Cohen, and the owner might be feeling even more aggressive in trying to finally get the Mets on a path to consistent success.  This inflated payroll may become an issue if the league is successful in its long-desired goal of instituting a salary cap in the next CBA, yet the Mets seem prepared to only worry about that problem if it happens.

Scott Boras’ clients have a tendency to wait until deeper into the offseason to sign, in order for the agent to fully access the market and to perhaps draw out another suitor or two into the bidding war.  It didn’t seem like Skubal would last too long in free agency, however, due to the limited number of clubs that could afford him and due to the impending lockout at the end of day on December 1.  In agreeing to a deal now, Skubal locks in his $450MM in advance of whatever the next CBA may bring, and he’ll get a little over a week to acclimate himself to his new front office and coaching staff before the lockout ends all communication between players and team employees.

The Padres, Blue Jays, and Dodgers were known to be interested in Skubal, with a few other “mystery teams” also reportedly checking in on his services.  The Dodgers are no strangers to mega-deals, yet their interest in Skubal reportedly seemed limited to the type of short-term, higher-AAV deal they gave Tucker last offseason.  ESPN’s Alden Gonzalez writes that Los Angeles was “willing to ultimately go long-term” for Skubal, but the Dodgers didn’t make an offer, due to factors like “the uncertainty of a new CBA and what the economic model might look like” once the lockout is over.

With Skubal already off the board and other notables like Sonny Gray and Dustin May already landing new contracts, the free agent pitching market figures to be red hot in the final days before the lockout ends all transactional business.  Trevor Rogers, Freddy Peralta, Michael King, Clay Holmes, and other top arms figure to get plenty of attention from the teams that missed out on Skubal, plus other clubs who weren’t willing to spend to Skubal’s price range in the first place.

The New York Post’s Jon Heyman was the first to report that the Mets had signed Skubal, with ESPN’s Jeff Passan providing the $450MM figure and the contract length.  SNY’s Chelsea Janes had the detail about the lack of any opt-out clause, The Athletic’s Ken Rosenthal reported on the $45MM signing bonus, The Athletic’s Will Sammon wrote about the no-trade protection, and the New York Post’s Joel Sherman had the detail about the $30MM in deferred money.
