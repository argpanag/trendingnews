Dow drops more than 500 points; S&P 500 falls for fifth day as Nvidia sells off: Live updates

TheTools · 17.11.2026 23:04 · source · permalink

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell on Wednesday as Nvidia shares lost momentum following the chipmaker's better-than-expected earnings report.

The 30-stock index shed 561.25 points, or 1.11%, to end at 50,198.17. The S&P 500 lost 0.23% and closed at 7,323.63. It was the fifth consecutive losing session for the broad market index and its longest slide since June. The Nasdaq Composite outperformed and rose 0.42% to 24,429.88.

Shares of Nvidia were 2.9% lower, reversing course from earlier gains after the company's profit and revenue in the third quarter beat analysts' estimates. The company also lifted its revenue guidance for the fiscal year ending January 2028, calling for 75% growth year over year.

The report marked the chipmaker's first since Jensen Huang resigned last week as CEO, a move that came days after he admitted to an affair with a businesswoman alleged to be a Chinese spy. The company's board chair, Harvey Jones, said at the start of the earnings call that the board doesn't intend to bring Huang back as chief executive.

"It's going to be an adjustment, but it's one that the market will work its way through, just as it always has in the past," said Eric Parnell, chief market strategist at Great Valley Advisor Group.

When it comes to the broader artificial intelligence trade, however, Parnell noted that the underlying fundamental challenges, including those related to capital expenditures and debt financing, haven't changed — and might not anytime soon.

"It doesn't take much of a slowdown in earnings growth in the environment that we're in with the AI buildout and the AI spend to take these types of stocks down pretty quickly," he said. "It's this type of volatility that I suspect we're not only going to continue to see but it's likely to increase as we go through 2027 and into 2028."

The Dow was also weighed down by losses in healthcare stocks like UnitedHealth as well as energy name Chevron and industrial giant Caterpillar. UnitedHealth lost 10%, while Chevron and Caterpillar declined almost 4% and more than 2%, respectively.

On Tuesday, the blue-chip index dropped more than 1%, while the S&P 500 notched its lowest closing level since July 29. The Nasdaq shed almost 1% and touched its lowest level since April during the session.

Investors are looking ahead to a blockbuster initial public offering from Anthropic later this week. The AI giant's shares are expected to be priced above the current range between $70 and $78, multiple sources told CNBC's David Faber on Tuesday.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 closed lower on Wednesday.

The 30-stock index lost 561.25 points, or 1.11%, to close at 50,198.17, while the broad market S&P 500 dipped 0.23% to 7,323.63.

The Nasdaq Composite, however, rose 0.42% to end the day at 24,429.88.

The stock market might not be fully acknowledging the threat China poses to the U.S.'s standing in the artificial intelligence race, according to UBS.

"US AI leadership remains powerful, but we heard that the market may be underestimating Chinese competition and the potential fragmentation of corporate demand," said strategist Reinout De Bock in a Tuesday note.

"The question is not just how quickly AI capex grows, but who will own the infrastructure, control the data and capture the monetization," he added.

— Sean Conlon

Federal Reserve officials expressed confidence in October that further interest rate increases will be needed to stem inflation, though they decided to hold off while gathering more information, according to meeting minutes released Wednesday.

Policymakers put artificial intelligence at the core of their discussions, indicating that demand associated with the technology would be "a material source of inflation risk."

More broadly, officials said "inflation pressures in the economy were broad based" and would warrant action, with "some" policymakers advocating for a quarter percentage point hike at the meeting. A couple participants even noted the possibility that a larger increase might be needed at the next policy meeting in December.

The meeting summary said "almost all participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labor market, further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate … would likely be appropriate, in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2 percent objective."

SpaceX shares are up more than 3% over the last five sessions as of late morning trading Wednesday, while the Nasdaq Composite is down more than 4%. That separation may be a sign of things to come, according to Morgan Stanley. 

"As the AI trade diffuses into its next era, we see scope for further decorrelation and a more durable place in investor portfolios," analyst Adam Jonas wrote in a Tuesday note about SpaceX shares. 

Two key things lead Jonas to this prediction: revenue from the company's connectivity — Starlink — vertical, the increasing need for SpaceX's Terafab as the U.S. seeks to create a more independent semiconductor supply chain.

However, a merger between SpaceX and Tesla, Elon Musk's other megacap company, creates a ceiling for the former's stock for now, he said. "Most investors we speak with are broadly comfortable with the merits of a potential combination," Jonas wrote, "but put off by the lack of certainty on timing and potential dilution risk."

The recent crypto sell-off has led Citi to trim its targets for bitcoin and ether.

"We revise our base-case price targets for BTC and ETH to $99,532 and $2,838, respectively, as crypto's holding pattern has broken to the downside, with both assets down since our last update. BTC's break below its 200-day moving average prompts us to cut our near-term flow outlook to neutral," wrote strategist Alex Saunders. Richard Reyle, chief investment officer at New Jersey-based Questar Capital Partners, said in a note Wednesday that Nvidia is "uninvestable."

"Nvidia is the Jenga tower of the whole AI trade, and that is not a compliment," he said. "A week ago, we thought this was a pedestrian story about a CEO whose ego got the best of him. Then we found out there was no clear succession plan, and poor corporate governance, with no Chairman until this scandal came to light."

He added that Nvidia's delayed 10-Q was a "red flag, as it's out of character from its previous releases."

"We believe there are significant questions about where Nvidia has been selling its chips," Reyle said. "For investors who own the stock, this is an immediate sell in our view. We have never owned Nvidia and believe the stock has been and remains uninvestable."

Shares of Nvidia were up around 4% in pre-market trading on Wednesday.

"There's another company set to begin a spectacular run (Anthropic), and every moment spent worrying about Nvidia is time that could have been spent finding the next winner," Reyle added. "News flash: NVDA ain't it."

President Donald Trump has landed in China for the APEC summit, where he is due to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in the first trilateral talks between the three leaders on Thursday.

The meeting — taking place in Shenzhen, a major tech hub — puts trade and technology in sharp focus. Markets will be watching for any sign of progress on U.S.-China trade tensions or restrictions on advanced AI chips.

Xi opened the summit by calling for fewer trade and investment barriers and closer cooperation on artificial intelligence.

Traders are also watching for any signal on Ukraine, sanctions and energy, and the conflict in the Middle East, which could shape expectations for Russian oil exports and the geopolitical risk premium.

— Hugh Leask

Precious metals sold off Wednesday morning, with gold falling to its lowest level in over a year and silver also plunging to a 52-week low, as expectations of interest rate rises from the Federal Reserve next month drew investors away from non-interest bearing assets.

Gold futures fell 2.34% to $3,761.84, while spot gold was down 2.38% at $3,758%. Silver futures slipped 3.5% to $45.72, as spot silver shed 3.6% to reach $45.67.

Nannette Hechler-Fayd'herbe, Lombard Odier Group's chief investment officer for EMEA and head of investment strategy, sustainability and research, said she remained optimistic on gold over the next 12 months, but warned that investors may need to be patient.

Hechler-Fayd'herbe does not expect the Federal Reserve or other central banks to embark on a sustained series of interest-rate hikes. But with U.S. real yields moving back up to 2.9%, from 2.6% not long ago, conditions are not yet supportive of an immediate rally, she said.

"This of course is not an environment where gold can immediately perform, so a little bit of patience on gold I think is warranted," she told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" Wednesday.

— Hugh Leask

Indonesia's central bank on Wednesday raised rates by 25 basis points in a surprise move, bringing its policy rate to 6% — last seen in December 2024.

Economists polled by Reuters had expected the bank to keep rates steady at 5.75%.

The Bank of Indonesia said the move was to strengthen the currency and a preemptive measure to keep inflation in its target range of 1.5% to 3.5%.

The Indonesian rupiah depreciated to a record low of 18,285 against the greenback on Tuesday, while the inflation rate in the country was at 3.23% in October.

Japan's exports in October surged 20.5% from a year earlier, snapping a two-month slowdown in shipments, official data released Wednesday showed.

Exports growth beat expectations of economists polled by Reuters that had pegged it at 20%, and it was sharply higher compared to 16.5% in September.

Shipments of semiconductor equipment that have been propping up Tokyo's exports jumped 49.7% by value. Exports of motor vehicles climbed 19.9% year on year.

In terms of destinations, shipments to China rose 13.3%, while they climbed 23.9% to the U.S.
