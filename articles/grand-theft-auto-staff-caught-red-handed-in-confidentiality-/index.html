Grand Theft Auto staff 'caught red-handed' in confidentiality breach, tribunal hears

TheTools · 21.11.2026 09:23 · source · permalink
  • Published
    18 November 2026

A group of workers who were dismissed from Rockstar Games were "caught red-handed" flouting contractual policies and obligations, an employment tribunal has heard.

The gaming giant behind Grand Theft Auto said it took action after staff discussed confidential information on a Discord server which had hundreds of members.

It came during the company's closing speech in a case brought by former employees who are claiming unfair dismissal for trade union activities. A judgement is due at a later date.

Representing 23 workers, the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB) said the former staff were penalised for discussing working conditions in a private online forum.

The conclusion of evidence comes ahead of the release of the highly anticipated Grand Theft Auto 6 game on Thursday.

It is expected to break sales records when it is released. Pre-orders of the game are already though to be worth hundreds of millions of pounds.

Some 34 people are believed to have been dismissed from Rockstar North in October last year across the UK and Canada over what the company described as "the sharing of confidential company information".

The company's Rockstar North studio is in Holyrood Road, Edinburgh.

Thomas Cordrey, representing Rockstar, said employees made disparaging comments about the company on a semi-public Discord server and shared confidential information.

This included unreleased game features for Grand Theft Auto 6 and the timing of its release.

In his closing statement, he said moderators of the Discord server did not want to "over police" it and chose to "let the taps of disparagement and confidential information flow".

He added that the claimants "were caught red-handed" while Rockstar was under "enormous pressure" and on the "precipice of announcing further delays".

Cordrey said the alternative would have been 34 extended disciplinary hearings, all in the face of "inevitable dismissals for gross misconduct".

He urged Judge Lucy Wiseman to take into account the impact of going through these lengthier processes.

'Obsessed by confidentiality'

Cordrey also said the claimants did not appear apologetic about their conduct and described them as "blameworthy".

He told the tribunal: "This was a case of 'we know the policies, we know our employer would be opposed to the use of a semi-public Discord to disparage and breach confidentiality, but we did it anyway and we would do it again'.

"The reason why these individuals were dismissed was for that reason – flouting of contractual policies and obligations."

Managers were also said to be "vilified" in some posts on the server and the "most vitally protected resource" - Grand Theft Auto 6 - was discussed "liberally".

Cordrey added Rockstar was "obsessed by confidentiality".

He referred to evidence from the games developer's head of licensing, Jennifer Kolbe, who described being "shocked and appalled" at what was discovered on the Discord server in October last year.

He said union membership and activities had nothing to do with the dismissals.

In a statement, a spokesperson for Rockstar Games said: "Rockstar Games' position has been clear and consistent throughout.

"Individuals across the UK and Canada were dismissed for gross misconduct following the sharing of confidential company information, not because of alleged trade union membership or activity.

"The tribunal has heard our evidence and seen the facts, and the hearing has not yet concluded. We remain confident of a positive outcome."

The evidence concluded on Wednesday afternoon.

A timeframe for judgement was not given.
