World Rugby rankings: All Blacks risk record-equalling low as Springboks enter three-way battle for top spot

TheTools · 20.11.2026 09:59 · source · permalink

World Rugby rankings ahead of the final Nations Championship group games.

Springboks are once again putting their place at the summit on the line with three teams in the race for the number one spot in the World Rugby rankings this weekend.

Losing their position at the top would conclude a fraught week for South Africa, which began with their head coach, Rassie Erasmus, being banned for his tirade against the officiating in the France clash last Friday.

The Boks went down 21-19 to the French and briefly knocked them down to second ahead of Saturday’s matches, which included New Zealand’s encounter with Wales in Cardiff.

Many expected Dave Rennie’s men to swat aside the Welsh with ease and rise to the summit of the standings, but the Springboks staggeringly maintained their place in first after the hosts stunned the All Blacks 35-28.

It meant that the top two remained unchanged but set up another set of games where the Kiwis are gunning for the South Africans in the penultimate weekend of the Nations Championship.

The 12 countries are set to play their last game of the group stages before Finals Weekend at Allianz, Twickenham.

England and New Zealand therefore won’t have to move from their bases as they collide in London in one of the early Saturday encounters.

Rennie’s outfit can duly set the target for South Africa to chase this time if they manage to see off the English with a victory.

They will not get too much reward with a positive result this weekend, however, considering the hosts go into the game off good performances against the Wallabies and Japan.

How All Blacks can move top

A triumph of any kind for the All Blacks will give them 0.63 points while a win by more than 15 will increase their tally by 0.95.

Even so, it will be enough to usurp the Boks, who will drop down to second should they succumb to Ireland by any margin.

Erasmus’ team will obviously increase their buffer if they defeat the Irish and the All Blacks lose, which will give them slightly more breathing space going into Rugby World Cup year.

There is a scenario where the top-two drop behind their respective opponents, though. It would require margins of more than 15 for Ireland and England but, should that happen, the former would rise to the top of the rankings.

The English would only move up one place to fourth – providing France don’t lose to Argentina – but New Zealand could go down to as low as fifth with a heavy defeat at Twickenham, equalling their worst ever position in the standings.

It is part of a weekend which could shake up the top 10 with a number of intriguing battles taking place on Saturday.

That includes 11th place Italy taking on Fiji, who are in 10th, and Wales (ninth) facing the Wallabies (seventh) after their heroics in Cardiff last weekend.
