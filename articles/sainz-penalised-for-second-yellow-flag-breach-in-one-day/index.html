Sainz penalised for second yellow flag breach in one day

TheTools · 25.09.2026 15:44 · source · permalink

Valentin Khorounzhiy

Williams Formula 1 driver Carlos Sainz has received a five-place grid penalty for ignoring yellow flags during Azerbaijan Grand Prix qualifying.

The penalty denies Williams what was set to be its best grid position of the year, superseding Alex Albon's 11th place on the Monaco GP grid.

Remarkably, the sanction came on a day on which Sainz was already investigated - and found guilty - of a different yellow-flag breach, although on that occasion he was let off with a reprimand due to mitigating circumstances.

Sainz and Williams were originally hauled in front of the stewards after Friday morning practice for a yellow flag breach through Turn 4. The stewards ruled that Sainz "failed to sufficiently reduce speed" under the yellow flag - but felt there was sufficient mitigation to avoid the imposition of the grid penalty in "the very short period between the single yellow being displayed and Sainz entering the relevant marshalling sector".

No such mitigation was present in a different incident, recorded during the third qualifying segment. In fact, the stewards pointed out that Sainz received advanced warning from the team that a yellow flag was out on the approach to Turn 3, and simply failed to heed it sufficiently.

The warning in question was "yellow ahead, yellow ahead, [Lando] Norris, just do a lift, do a lift, and complete the lap" and then an apparent "clear-clear" that followed seconds later - seemingly creating a situation that led Sainz to believe the yellow flag was no longer acutely relevant.

"[Sainz] explained that he intended to demonstrate a reduction in speed across the yellow area as a whole," the stewards wrote as part of a lengthy explanation.

"He stated that he did not consider it necessary to reduce speed on the straight and instead intended to demonstrate his response when approaching and exiting Turn 3. He considered this to be a 'global lift' covering the consecutive yellow sectors. He further explained that the car [Norris] which had originally caused the yellow was no longer in the runoff area and that he expected the yellow flags to be withdrawn.

"The stewards did not accept this interpretation. Where consecutive marshalling sectors are subject to yellow-flag conditions, a driver must demonstrate a significant reaction to reduce speed in each individual yellow marshalling sector. A reduction in a subsequent sector cannot retrospectively satisfy the requirement applicable to the preceding yellow sector. The obligation is also not dependent on the driver's own assessment of whether the original hazard remains present within a particular part of the yellow sector."

McLaren driver Lando Norris had indeed vacated the relevant runoff by that point - but as the FIA stewards do not want the drivers to self-assess the validity of a yellow flag, they found the lack of any "meaningful reduction in speed" in the relevant marshalling sector was sufficient grounds for the penalty.

In addition to the five-position penalty, which demotes Sainz from ninth to 14th, he has also received two penalty points - taking him to a total of four for the 12-month period.

Sainz was not alone in incurring a grid penalty after Baku qualifying.

A three-position penalty has been assessed against Cadillac driver Sergio Perez for impeding McLaren driver Oscar Piastri in the opening segment.

The stewards wrote that Perez "received two radio warnings" from the team but still forced a needless lift from the McLaren driver through the Turns 18-19-20 section, by "not managing his preparation lap in accordance with specific traffic-management instructions" - namely the race director's order that a driver on a prep lap stay to the right-hand side and avoid weaving in front of incoming cars.

Because of the engine component penalties to the Aston Martin cars, however, Perez is only set to lose one position on the grid - going from 19th to 20th (the position he originally qualified in), behind team-mate Valtteri Bottas.
