Policy must alleviate pain in equities; Sensex at 95k by 2027-end: Morgan Stanley

TheTools · 17.11.2026 03:32 · source · permalink

New Delhi: Pitching for the rollout of policy measures to alleviate pain in equities, foreign brokerage Morgan Stanley on Monday estimated the BSE Sensex to reach 95,000 points by the end of next year.

Stressing that there is hardly any wrong with the Indian economy except inflation, it said, adding that given that 13 crore countrymen invest in stocks, policymakers need to respond to the goings-on.

"Stock prices reflect into the economy, so a bear market can slow down the economy," the American brokerage said in a note.

"While we see far-reaching reforms that could lift growth rates in the coming quarters, policymakers could also roll out policies to directly alleviate the pain in equities."

The note also said that India offers a '3D edge' of durable, diversified, and decoupled growth, and the markets stand to gain as the artificial intelligence story wanes or geopolitical troubles settle down.

It sees a 16 percent upside from the present levels on the BSE's 30-share benchmark through to the end of next year, and expects the Sensex to be at 95,000 points by December 2027 from the present 82,104.79 points.

The country possesses strengths including inexpensive valuations, a resumption in the upward journey for the earnings cycle, an emerging private capex cycle and releveraging of corporate balance sheets, it said.

It added that there is a case for re-rating of Indian markets on factors like falling intensity of oil in GDP and potential for rising share of exports in GDP, especially services, and fiscal consolidation.

The base case estimate for end-2027 is 95,000 for the Sensex, it said, adding that the benchmark can also move up to 1.05 lakh points in a bull case, which has a 30 per cent likelihood of playing out and also crash to 68,000 points if the 20 per cent probability of a bear market plays out.
