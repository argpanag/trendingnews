SPCX Stock Reclaims $165 as Upgrade and Microsoft Deal Hopes Lift Revenue Outlook

TheTools · 05.10.2026 17:13 · source · permalink

SpaceX shares have regained momentum above $165 as Wall Street turns increasingly bullish on its computing business, while potential Microsoft demand and Starship progress strengthen the long-term growth story.

SpaceX shares have climbed back above $165 following fresh Wall Street coverage, bringing the psychologically important $200 level back into focus. The recovery has also pushed the company’s implied valuation toward the $2 trillion range, highlighting the substantial expectations already reflected in the stock.

The latest catalyst is the possibility that Microsoft could become a major customer of SpaceX’s rapidly expanding computing business. The Information reported that the two companies held talks during the summer about Microsoft leasing computing capacity from SpaceX. The status of those discussions remains unclear, while Microsoft declined to comment.

A Microsoft agreement could nevertheless become an important addition to SpaceX’s rapidly expanding revenue base.

Bernstein analyst Douglas Harned expects SpaceX’s computing-related revenue to increase from approximately $24.6 billion in 2026 to $115.1 billion in 2027. That forecast implies nearly fivefold growth and would require SpaceX to add more than $90 billion in revenue within a single year.

The company already has major customers. Anthropic reportedly pays approximately $1.25 billion per month, while Alphabet has agreed to pay around $920 million monthly. Another unidentified customer is expected to begin paying roughly $1.11 billion per month in December.

Together, those agreements could generate approximately $3.28 billion per month, or more than $39 billion annually once fully operational.

However, the gap between that revenue base and Bernstein’s $115.1 billion forecast remains substantial. SpaceX will therefore need to expand beyond traditional computing leases and generate additional revenue from related services.

Microsoft could provide another significant source of demand. The company has said demand for computing capacity continues to exceed supply despite plans for approximately $175 billion in capital spending during 2026.

SpaceX expects to finish 2026 with slightly more than 2 GW of computing capacity and plans to increase that to between 5 GW and 10 GW next year. The company is also reportedly preparing to bring approximately 420,000 additional Nvidia GPUs online in November.

The expansion provides SpaceX with a potentially lucrative new business, but it also requires enormous capital investment and rapid execution.

Wall Street sentiment toward SpaceX has strengthened. Morgan Stanley analyst Adam Jonas maintained an Overweight rating and reiterated a $300 price target.

TD Cowen initiated coverage with a Buy rating and a $200 target, while Pivotal Research has established a $220 target. These forecasts suggest significant upside from current levels, although they also rely heavily on aggressive assumptions about future computing and launch revenue.

SpaceX’s opportunity extends beyond commercial computing. The company has secured major U.S. national-security contracts, including a $4.16 billion Space Force award for the Space-Based Airborne Moving Target Indicator program.

It subsequently received approximately $1.6 billion in task orders covering 18 Falcon 9 launches and a further $2.29 billion award to develop the Space Data Network Backbone.

Together, the three programs represent approximately $8.05 billion in potential contract value. However, contract awards should not be treated as equivalent to future revenue or earnings.

SpaceX’s Falcon program continues to provide a reliable launch platform, while Starship represents the company’s larger long-term opportunity.

If Starship eventually achieves reliable and frequent launches, SpaceX could dramatically increase launch capacity while reducing the cost of deploying larger satellite constellations. The system still faces substantial execution risks, however, including reliability, rapid turnaround and launch frequency.

SpaceX shares reversed sharply higher during the U.S. session, gaining about 0.5% and extending their recovery following a volatile August.

The move pushed SpaceX stock above the $150 level, an important technical barrier that had limited the recovery. A sustained break above this area could strengthen the bullish setup and put $200 firmly on investors’ radar.

Fresh Wall Street coverage has also added momentum. Pivotal Research Group initiated coverage with a Buy rating and a $220 price target, providing a significant institutional valuation signal for the newly public company.

Additional share supply could also complicate the recovery. Approximately 319 million shares were expected to become available in September, with further unlocks scheduled during October and December.

If early investors, employees or insiders sell into the rally, the additional supply could create resistance and make it harder for buyers to sustain momentum.

For now, $150 has become an important technical reference for SpaceX stock. Holding above that level would keep buyers focused on $200, while a failure to maintain the recent breakout could trigger renewed selling.

With Microsoft potentially adding to computing demand, Wall Street raising its targets and Starship offering a major long-term growth opportunity, the SpaceX story is becoming increasingly ambitious. The challenge is that much of the company’s valuation already assumes exceptional execution, leaving little room for delays, weaker demand or slower revenue growth.
