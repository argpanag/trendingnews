Atlanta chef turns experience with homelessness into Thanksgiving mission to feed 1,000

TheTools · 26.11.2026 17:38 · source · permalink

ATLANTA — An Atlanta chef who says he once experienced homelessness is using his experience to make sure seniors and unhoused people have a hot meal this Thanksgiving.

Chef Tim Morgan, with Aprons for Change, spent Thanksgiving morning alongside volunteers handing out 1,000 meals to people across the community.

“It is deeply personal for me,” Morgan said. “It’s an unwavering commitment to help those who are underserved.”

Morgan said this is his 10th year giving back to people in need. This year, the organization doubled its efforts, providing 1,000 meals compared with 500 last year.

Preparing that many meals required days of work. Morgan and a team of volunteers chopped, cooked, packaged and distributed food ahead of Thanksgiving.

“I’ve literally personally been up for 72 hours,” Morgan said.

Volunteer Sandra Lee Williams said she wanted to be part of the effort to help her neighbors.

“We wanted to be here, a part of that outreach, to do whatever we can to serve our community,” Williams said.

For Morgan, the mission is personal. He said he once experienced homelessness and knows firsthand how difficult and isolating it can be to find help.

“There were no opportunities,” Morgan said. “When I knocked on doors, I had to build a door.”

Morgan said those experiences helped shape his commitment to serving others.

“I feel like it’s just a part of humanity, us being human beings, having a level of compassion,” he said.

The meals also reached seniors, including Estella Gibbs, who said Thanksgiving can be especially difficult for older adults who do not have family nearby.

“It makes me feel real good because a lot of seniors here don’t have families, and a lot of times it’s the only way they can get a good free meal,” Gibbs said.

Gibbs said she and other seniors appreciate having organizations and volunteers willing to provide a hot meal.

“We are very appreciative because a lot of people just can’t get food, a hot meal and stuff,” Gibbs said.

And she gave the Thanksgiving spread a positive review.

“The food is really, really good,” Gibbs said.

Morgan said donations made this year’s 1,000-meal giveaway possible. Aprons for Change plans to continue the effort next year, with donations helping support future meals for people in need.
