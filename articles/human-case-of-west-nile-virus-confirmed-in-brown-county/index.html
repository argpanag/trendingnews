Human case of West Nile virus confirmed in Brown County

TheTools · 16.09.2026 00:13 · source · permalink

West Nile virus has been confirmed in a human and a horse in Brown County, the Brown County, De Pere and Oneida Nation health departments announced on Sept. 15.

“While these two cases are not related, they show mosquitoes in the area are carrying the virus and could continue spreading it to humans or animals,” said Michelle Tipple, community/public health officer for the Oneida Nation Health Department.

They are the first cases this year in a person and a horse. The virus has been found in birds and mosquitoes in other parts of the state.

Brown County lists one human case and one hospitalization, according to the state Department of Health Services.

In 2024, a Brown County man and a Fondu Lac County man died from West Nile virus. An Outagamie County woman was hospitalized. Wisconsin typically sees about 18 illnesses from West Nile virus annually.

West Nile Virus can cause mild, flu-like symptoms two to 14 days after being bitten by an infected mosquito. In fewer than 1% of the cases, a victim needs to be hospitalized, the health departments said.

“Anyone can get sick if infected with West Nile virus,” said Anna Nick, health officer/administrator for Brown County Public Health. “However, people who are older, have certain chronic medical conditions, or have weakened immune systems are at higher risk of severe illness.”

The virus is spread to humans, birds and other animals through the bite from an infected mosquito. The mosquitoes get the virus by feeding on infected birds. The virus is not spread human to human, animal to animal, or animal to person, the DHS said.

Health officials advised people to protect against West Nile Virus by:

  • Using an insect repellent.
  • Wearing long, loose-fitting shirts and pants.
  • Avoiding being outside between dusk and dawn when mosquitoes are active.
  • Using screens on windows and doors to keep mosquitoes outside.

They also urged people to drain any standing water as mosquitoes need water to breed.
