    13:19 GMT 19 November

    Joe Coughlan
    Reporting from Leonida

    Just like that, Grand Theft Auto 6 has become part of the wider gaming world, after launching to rave reviews and staggering estimated pre-order sales.

    The game had a phased midnight launch, but that didn't stop UK gamers from jumping online as soon as it became available - with one logging on despite having to get up for work in five hours.

    Younger players were warned against doing the same, with schools telling students not to skip class. One school in Essex even offering a raffle to those who attended as normal today.

    The long 13-year wait came to an end for me this morning, bringing back poignant memories of my mum handing me a copy of the previous GTA game on its release day in 2013, when I finished school.

    Goodbye, Los Santos. Hello, Vice City.

  3. Accessibility features in GTA 6 include large subtitles and tinnitus reductionpublished at 12:41 GMT 19 November
    12:41 GMT 19 November

    Joe Coughlan
    Reporting from Leonida

    Accessibility settings have become a more prominent feature of video games as the technology has progressed.

    From browsing the game's settings, I can see various options to increase text sizes and apply closed captions.

    Screen narration is also available for text on most menus, as well as audio descriptions during cutscenes, although it’s listed as being available only in English.

    Finer details include tinnitus reduction to reduce ringing effects after explosions, alongside high-contrast shaders to make various entities in the world stand out more visually.

    One criticism, though, is the apparent inability to fully remap the buttons in the game’s control scheme. Players can instead switch between three control presets.

  4. 'GTA saved our relationship', say siblings in USpublished at 12:30 GMT 19 November
    12:30 GMT 19 November

    Madeleine Lake
    Live reporter

    Siblings Nate and Jamie Wyatt say they became "estranged" over the years as they lived in different US states.

    But after Nate suffered a spinal injury, the two reconnected on a call.

    He suggested they play GTA 5 to spend time together.

    Jamie agreed and went out to buy a PlayStation. She said she initially felt "intimidated" by consoles but quickly learnt how to play, with Nate as her teacher.

    What started as a few hours of gaming has become part of their daily lives.

    While the game is virtual, Nate says the social aspect brings it to life: "The fun is real, the interaction is real, and the time we spend together is real."

    Jamie says having companionship has been essential for her.

    "We are both in our 50s, and we don't have as many friends our age who game," she says. "We have been separated by distance and this now fills in some gaps that were there before. I don't think they exist anymore."

    The two are excited about GTA 6 and plan to call each other as they start their playthroughs.

  6. Expert concerns over 'widening gap' between winners and losers in gaming industrypublished at 11:48 GMT 19 November
    11:48 GMT 19 November

    Madeleine Lake
    Live reporter

    University lecturer Stephanie Farnsworth says the success of GTA 6 could set a standard that some games will struggle to reach.

    She says that a lot of games have promised huge open worlds but failed to deliver, and had to scale back in the end.

    Many studios will not be able to compete with the new standard Rockstar has set, she says, so they may need to start thinking about designing smaller worlds with unique features.

    Farnsworth is also concerned about the widening gap in the games industry between studios that produce blockbusters and those that face layoffs when targets are not met.

    "This does come at a huge time of cuts and layoffs in the last 18-24 months, including BioWare closing, which has been going for some decades.

    "If you're not a huge top dog, you're at risk in this climate."
