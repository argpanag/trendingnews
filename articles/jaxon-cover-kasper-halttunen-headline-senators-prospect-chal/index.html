Jaxon Cover, Kasper Halttunen headline Senators Prospect Challenge roster this weekend

TheTools · 12.09.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Ottawa Senators fans will get their first live look at one of the club's newest, most promising young forwards this weekend in Gatineau.

Jaxon Cover, selected 32nd overall in the 2026 NHL Draft, will suit up for games against the Toronto Maple Leafs (Saturday, 6 p.m.) and Montreal Canadiens (Sunday, 6 p.m.) at Slush Puppie Centre.

Cover's unconventional route to hockey fame — he mostly played roller hockey in the Cayman Islands before transitioning to ice hockey in Canada at age 13 — is just as impressive as his skill and creativity.

As London Knights general manager Mark Hunter told the Ottawa Citizen, despite Cover still learning the fundamentals of the game and getting more comfortable with his edges on the ice, "the upside is huge on this young man."

The Prospect Challenge will also be the first time that newcomers Kasper Halttunen and Eskild Bakke Olsen slip on a Sens jersey.

Halttunen, a physical right winger with a hefty shot, was acquired from the San Jose Sharks in the William Eklund trade. He is a year or two away from potentially breaking into the NHL, recording 35 points (16 goals, 19 assists) for the AHL's San Jose Barracuda last season.

Bakke Olsen, one of the oldest players in the showcase at age 24, had a bit of a coming out party at the world hockey championship in May for Norway, helping upset Canada 3-2 in overtime in the bronze-medal game. The day after the Stanley Cup was handed to the Carolina Hurricanes, Ottawa signed the forward to a one-year, entry-level contract worth $910,000.

Another name on the roster that caught many fans' attention Thursday morning was the team's first pick this past June, Jonas Lagerberg Hoen. General manager Steve Staios was surprised when Lagerberg Hoen was still available at pick No. 25 — the Sens had him at No. 16 on their big board — believing the winger has "home run potential."

The only worry was a brutal knee injury that forced him to miss the majority of last season and required ACL surgery. Though he missed development camp in early July, he has been back skating for months now.

Unfortunately, the Senators tell the Citizen that the 18-year-old winger will not participate in games this weekend.

Still, it's another step in his recovery and introduction to the club.

He was a man amongst boys in Sweden's top junior league to begin the 2025-26 season, racking up 16 points (nine goals, seven assists) in nine games. Lagerberg Hoen can absolutely rip the puck, skates like the wind, attacks the middle of the ice and has a sizeable frame to build out at 6-foot-2.

Other noteworthy forwards on this weekend's roster include Blake Montgomery, a 6-foot-4 speedster heading into his first full pro season, and Adam Nemec, who was a point-per-game player this past season for both the OHL's Sudbury Wolves and Team Slovakia at the world junior championship.

Is Carter Yakemchuk playing?

More indication that the Senators intend to give defenceman Carter Yakemchuk every opportunity to make the big club later this month is his omission from the rookie squad.

Yakemchuk, who had 40 points (10 goals, 30 assists) in 54 games with Belleville of the American Hockey League in 2025-26, got multiple stints with Ottawa last season.

He had multi-point performances in both his regular-season debut against the Detroit Red Wings and his Stanley Cup playoff debut in Game 4 versus the Carolina Hurricanes.

Especially with Artem Zub still rehabbing the injury he sustained in Round 1 against Carolina, Yakemchuk could be needed to bolster the right side of the defence corps right out of training camp.

Ottawa's blue line will still feature some intriguing players this weekend. Matt Andonovski is fresh off a Memorial Cup with the Kitchener Rangers and Hoyt Stanley has wrapped up his college career, one of more than a dozen players on the roster destined for Belleville.

Ottawa's best young goalie is here

Lucas Beckman, the franchise's potential future in goal, will get at least half a game this weekend, as the club likely splits the back-to-back equally amongst its four netminders.

Beckman backstopped the Chicoutimi Saguenéens to a QMJHL championship in the spring, going 16-4 with a .918 save percentage. He signed a three-year, entry-level deal in the midst of the run.

Last season, he said he wanted to be the best goalie in the Q and, after arguably accomplishing that objective, it'll be interesting to see what he has in store for an encore.

Is Gavin McKenna playing for the Leafs?

The No. 1 pick from this year's draft will have to wait until pre-season play to make his unofficial Maple Leafs debut.

Gavin McKenna, who will undoubtedly play NHL games this season, is on Toronto's rookie roster and is practicing with his fellow young guns, but will be held out of games this weekend in Gatineau. McKenna was a star in college for Penn State University last season, racking up 51 points (15 goals, 36 assists) in 35 games. He had two points per game for Canada at the World Juniors during the holidays.

The Jets are bringing their eighth-overall selection at the 2026 draft, forward Viggo Björck. The 18-year-old, who officially left Djurgårdens of the Swedish Hockey League this summer, will be given a real shot at cracking Winnipeg's NHL roster after signing his entry-level contract in July.

None of the Habs' top three picks from the 2026 draft, including Russian power forward Gleb Pugachyov, will be attending. Four later-round picks — Brayden Klimpke (117th overall), Parker Trottier (189th), Wesley Royston (190th) and Jean-Samuel Daigneault (221st) — will suit up.
