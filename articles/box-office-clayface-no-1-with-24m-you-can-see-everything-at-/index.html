Box Office: 'Clayface' No 1 With $24M+, 'You Can See Everything' At Near $20M

TheTools · 25.10.2026 15:07 · source · permalink

SUNDAY AM: Elizabeth Holmes and Nathan Fielder couldn’t draw enough blood to keep Clayface out of No. 1 as the lower-budget DC genre film flexed some muscle with a $24.5M opening, but the 3-hour A24 documentary, You Can See Everything remained widely seen with an excellent $19.5M second weekend, up 25%. Global start for Clayface is $43M.

While percentile surges in second weekend theater jumps are always par for the course, to see a second frame dollar wise outgrossing its opening, well, that’s just spectacular. A24 had a plan to go in under 1,000 theaters with You Can See Everything last weekend, creating a cultural buzz with sellouts, before going wide this weekend. That platform has paid off immensely. One non-A24 distribution head remarked over the weekend that more ‘tease’ platforming ala You Can See Everything‘s should be executed more by the industry to create anticipation for audiences (tell that to the studio bosses who are always eager to monetize the next window on a wide release).

Elsewhere, DC co-Boss James Gunn pointed out on Threads that while Sinners is the only horror movie to get an A CinemaScore, Clayface is in rare air with Obsession, Weapons, and Get Out as the only horror movies to land A- CimemaScores.

Toronto.

DC went full throttle on promoting this movie at San Diego Comic-Con with Watkins and star Tom Rhys Harries in tow for appearances. That’s what you’re suppose to do with a tentpole comic book movie by the way (too often, more and more studios are side-stepping Comic-Con out of fear that if they reveal too much, they’ll scare off the audience. Not true. DC was wise not to skip the opportunity).

It should be noted that the newly combined Paramount and Warner Bros crossed the billion mark at the domestic box office on Monday, becoming the fourth studio behind Universal, Disney and Sony to do so year-to-date. This is only the beginning as to what kind of formidable box office force the new Paramount/Warner Bros can become.

5.) Wicker (Black) 1,471 theaters, Fri $2.36M, Sat $1.8M Sun $1.55M 3-day $5.75M/Wk 1
After being popular with female audiences, Wicker will play in 97 AMC Dolby Cinemas today with 76 4DX locations on Wednesday which are already sold-out. As we mentioned this is an opening up there with Conclave ($6.6M) and Olivia Colman’s The Roses ($6.2M), and with an excellent PostTrak of 92% positive and 74% definite recommend, this bodes well for a great fall season awards-driven run. Upon dropping the first trailer for Wicker back in August which focused on a woman of agency choosing a husband of her own design, the spot took off with several female-driven brands tagging it (a maneuver that wasn’t triggered by Black Bear, rather was entirely organic). IKEA Australia was the first to tag the Wicker trailer followed by Home Goods where female customers took pictures in store with baskets exclaiming they were looking for their own wicker husband. Sephora, Toms (shoes), Central Park, Audible, and HBO got in the action with BookTok also a huge driver. The toothbrush Quip tagged the film with a shot of a wicker toothbrush handle, writing, ‘IYKYK’. Black Bear spotted the opportunities immediately and capitalized on them.

After establishing the Wicker Husband as the campaign’s lustful lynchpin, the studio’s marketing team brought the Alexander Skarsgard character into the real world. Standing six feet, six inches tall, the custom Wicker Husband standee appeared in theaters and bookstores where fans shared sightings, theater chains created their own content, and audiences openly plotting to take the basket guy home. The outdoor first appeared with no copy, just the Wicker Husband’s chest. In the final stretch, Black Bear opened a one-day-only retail pop-up where fans stepped inside the Basketmaker’s workshop, shopped exclusive merch, and wander the village market.

Observes Social Media Marketing Exec Mara Freedman of Storyd Media about Wicker‘s wattage, “In August, when the Wicker trailer came out, I instantly knew it would be a hit. A movie that gets it? A movie that women can relate to? Olivia Coleman’s character is a stand-in for all women in America (and worldwide) fed up with men, and forced to find love with a wicker man. That brands like Ikea instantly saw that connection and started posting tie ins wasn’t only genius, but it was a great way to get consumers to come in the store and post, and to tag their brands on social with the film. Other homegoods store like TJ Maxx, Homegoods, the Container Store, and Marshalls also saw many excited fans posting photos up against their basket walls or even resharing items that consumers already had at home, calling various bins, boxes, and baskets their ‘husbands’. What Blackbear could not have predicted though, was the timing of politically charged moments in pop culture, such as Shane Gillis’s SNL monologue or Vivek Ramaswamy’s reprehensible ad helping to promote the very wish fulfillment and fantasy that Wicker sets out to create.”

UPDATED FRIDAY PM: Right now, Warner Bros/DC’s R-rated Clayface is puffing its chest with a $9.5 million Friday (including previews) and an estimated $23M-$25M opening weekend at 3,458 theaters. If that comes to be it’s a solid start in the vicinity of other mid-October genre movies like Smile ($22.6M opening) and last year’s Black Phone 2 ($27.2M).

But it still remains to be seen what type of blood transfusion audiences give Nathan Fielder and Elizabeth Holmes this weekend as the A24 documentary You Can See Everything is coming on strong with a $5.8M Friday, up 5% from a week ago, and what’s looking to be an awesome $19M+ second weekend after its wide expansion from 855 locations to 2,655.

That will get the 10 -day total for the doc to $44.6M. Some rivals think a $20M+ second weekend remains a possibility. But at a $19M second frame, You Can See Everything is up 22% from its opening. Obsession was +39% in its second weekend (though that was during a Memorial Day weekend).

At this point in time, Clayface is keeping himself together for a No. 1 finish atop the domestic charts after great audience scores (93% on RT) and solid reviews (75%).

Viewers are also spinning Clayface against Spider-Man villain universe, ‘This as the blueprint nobody asked Sony to copy but should have.’ plus, ‘No Dialogue, only horror sounds, and some eerie song playing in the background, THIS is what a horror teaser should be like.'”

PREVIOUSLY, Thursday PM EXCLUSIVE: Peter Safran and James Gunn’s DC entry into lower-budget genre movies, Clayface, is off to a solid start in previews Thursday night with an estimated $3.5 million, if not more.

Domestic weekend forecasts are between $20M-$25M, which if that occurs would deliver the newly combined Paramount-Warner Bros its second back-to-back No. 1 opener at the box office after last weekend’s Street Fighter ($31M).

Previews started at 2 p.m. today for the James Watkins-directed Clayface, which though based on the Batman villain is a stand-alone story about babyfaced Hollywood actor Matt Hagen (Tom Rhys Harries), who in reaching for the stars falls short, kisses the wrong girl at the party, and — oops — there goes his face. The screenplay by Mike Flanagan in December 2024 got Gunn and Safran excited, and it was off to the races to make this movie, which is coming in at a net production cost of $44M before P&A, shot in the UK.

In regards to preview comps, Clayface‘s money is higher than the R-rated Sony/Marvel movie Kraven the Hunter ($2M previews, $11M dismal opening), and not far from the pre-Covid PG-13 DC spinoff Birds of Prey ($4M previews, $33M opening). Next to horror films, Clayface‘s cash is similar to Blumhouse Atomic Monster’s Other Mommy ($3.6M previews, $27.8M opening) and ahead of 2022’s Smile ($2M previews, $22.6M opening).

The hope is that the DC movie isn’t frontloaded tonight, as all eyes are on the potential hold of A24 documentary You Can See Everything following its great midweek business and fantastic $15.6M opening last weekend. The documentary co-directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim about Theranos architect Elizabeth Holmes expands to 2,600 locations Friday after bowing in 855 sites.

Clayface glides into the weekend with a 76% certified fresh Rotten Tomatoes critics score, which is higher than The Flash (63%), Supergirl (52% Rotten), Joker Folie à Deux (31%), Shazam: Fury of the Gods (49%), James Wan’s Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom (33%) and almost as good as Birds of Prey (79% certified fresh).

Note this isn’t the first time in DC movie history that it has ventured into low- to midsized-budget fare. In the early aughts there was DC/Vertigo’s The Losers, in 2010, which cost $25M before P&A and opened to $9.4M and finaled at $23.5M domestic. There was also the Josh Brolin-Megan Fox 2010 fantasy western Jonah Hex, which bombed with a $5.3M opening, $10.5M stateside final next to its $47M production cost. And there was the Wachowskis’ R-rated 2005 feature take of the DC Vertigo title V for Vendetta, which yielded a No. 1 $25.6M North American opening off a $50M production cost. Its final domestic take was $70.5M, and $134.7M global.

We’re hearing that previews for the Fathom release of Laika’s stop-motion animated title Wildwood is around $1M. The hope is that the Travis Knight-directed movie can break double-digits in its opening. The 100% certified fresh critical score and 98% with audiences on RT will certainly help.
