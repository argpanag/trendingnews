Alonso will not race with the 2030 V8s: the definitive farewell to the Asturian

TheTools · 23.11.2026 00:11 · source · permalink

Fernando Alonso has confirmed it bluntly: when Formula 1 brings back V8 engines in 2030, he will no longer be on the grid. The Asturian, who will be 49 by then, considers that this will be his definitive retirement from the big circus. His continuity seems assured until 2028, but no further, and with this decision a chapter closes for the generation that still raced with naturally aspirated engines.

From the naturally aspirated V8 to the hybrid: a decade of technical transition 🏎️

Alonso is one of the five drivers on the current grid who competed with V8s until 2013, when the FIA made the leap to the hybrid era. The Asturian laments that this change meant losing more than a decade of real racing, referring to the weight and complexity that hybrid power units brought. The return to V8s in 2030, according to the FIA's plan, will recover a naturally aspirated architecture that the new generation of drivers has never known in competition.

The only driver with a licence to drive without a battery 🔋

While the rest of the grid learns to manage energy and talk about deployment, Alonso will be able to say that thing about how he was already racing without batteries when this was a sport. The irony is served: the drivers who arrive in 2030 will have to study videos to understand what a V8 was, and the Asturian, from home, will be able to watch them with the peace of mind of someone who has already lived it and has no intention of explaining it again.
