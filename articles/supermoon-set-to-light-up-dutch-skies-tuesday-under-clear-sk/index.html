Supermoon set to light up Dutch skies Tuesday under clear skies

TheTools · 23.11.2026 15:10 · source · permalink

A supermoon is expected to be visible across much of the Netherlands on Tuesday evening, November 24, as mostly clear skies are set to offer favorable conditions for those interested in viewing it. The moon will reach its full phase at 3:53 p.m., before it raises above the eastern horizon.

The November full moon is traditionally known as the Beaver Moon. It is also classified as a supermoon because it occurs close to the Moon’s nearest point to Earth in its elliptical orbit. The moon will reach its closest point to Earth on Wednesday, November 25, one day after reaching its full phase.

In Amsterdam, the moon is expected to rise at about 3:51 p.m., shortly before sunset. It may appear orange or golden as it approaches the horizon before taking on a brighter, whiter appearance.

The weather is expected to cooperate in much of the country, with sunny spells during the day. Temperatures will remain low, reaching about 4 degrees Celsius during the day before dropping to around freezing overnight, according to Weeronline and the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute (KNMI).

Little change in the weather pattern is expected Thursday, with a mix of sunshine and cloud cover and no significant rainfall forecast. Morning temperatures will fall below freezing before afternoon highs reach just 1 to 3 degrees Celsius, well below the seasonal average of 7 to 9 degrees.

Friday is also expected to remain cold, with overnight frost and afternoon temperatures of 0 to 3 degrees. There will be a mix of sunny spells and cloud cover, while forecasters say some areas could experience an ice day, when temperatures remain below freezing throughout the day.

Reporting by ANP and NL Times
