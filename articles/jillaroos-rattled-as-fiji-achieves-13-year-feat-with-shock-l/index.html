There’s a lot of emotion here

Jocelyn Kelleher has dropped the ball cold 20 metres out from her defensive line.

And haven’t the Fijians let her know about it, pushing her around!

Tarryn Aiken took exception and there was plenty of pushing and shoving going on.

“The Australians are rattled,” Emma Veran said on Seven.

Jillaroos and Fiji. Seven

A few tackles later and Fiji gets a penalty and takes the two.

They now have a 2-0 lead. It’s the first time since the 2013 World Cup final that the Jillaroos do not take the lead first in a women’s international.

Fiji has come here with a plan

No points yet but Fiji looking a lot better than the Jillaroos.

They have been physical in defence and went very close to being first to score. They were probably unlucky to not get a penalty from a high shot close to the line too.

“It is an ambush,” Yasmin Meakes said on Seven.

There’s been some uncharacteristic errors from the Jillaroos too.

We’re underway

Fiji get us started and Millie Elliott moves past the 20m line off the first hit-up.

Emotion in the anthem

There were tears in the Fiji anthem with the passion on show.

Now we’ll have the traditional hymn before the match kicks off.

Tears in the Fiji anthem. Seven

When’s the last time Fiji played the Jillaroos?

1998.

It’s been a long time between battles for these sides.

The Jillaroos come into this match as overwhelming favourites as Fiji prepares for its first-ever World Cup semi-final appearance.

Both sides are 1-17

No late changes for the Jillaroos or Fiji.

Jocelyn Kelleher is back at five-eighth while props Millie Elliott and Simaima Taufa and hooker Olivia Higgins also return after not playing against Wales.

Tiana Penitani Gray (foot) is back on deck too.

Kiwis captain learns fate ahead of World Cup final

New Zealand captain James Fisher-Harris is free to play in the World Cup final next Sunday night after escaping with a warning for dangerous contact in the Kiwis’ semi-final win over Tonga.

Fisher-Harris lifted his knee into Salesi Foketi as he took a hit-up early in the New Castle clash, but the World Cup match review committee has neither suspended nor fined him.

New Zealand defeated Tonga 34-10.

Kangaroos receive visit from Blues legend

NSW Blues legend Brad Fittler has addressed the Kangaroos, as coach Kevin Walters called him to present the squad with their jerseys ahead of Sunday night’s blockbuster against Samoa.

Walters called in his Origin rival to the camp to rev up the players as they look to book a spot to take on New Zealand in the Rugby League World Cup final.

“Freddy’s had some big times as a player and coach, so he understands the lay of the land,” said Walters.

“It’s all those little, little things about their preparation, and getting Brad in will be a big thrill for the players, and also myself.

“I love Freddy, he’s a great fella. His direction towards the Kangaroos when he was playing was absolutely outstanding, so he’s a good guy to come in.”

Australia could be swamped by ‘Blue Army’

Although the Kangaroos have the home-team advantage at Allianz Stadium, Samoa supporters are expected to paint the stadium blue as they cheer on their team to a potential final berth against New Zealand.

Kangaroos coach Kevin Walters brushed off concerns that the “Blue Army” could knock the Aussies’ focus.

“I feel like there’s gonna be a lot of Australian supporters here, I think we’ll outnumber the Samoans,” said Walters.

“And if we don’t, well that’s OK as well.”
