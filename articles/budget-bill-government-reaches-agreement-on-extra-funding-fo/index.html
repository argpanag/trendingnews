Budget Bill: Government reaches agreement on extra funding for healthcare

There is a willingness to increase funding for healthcare. However, there will be no further resources allocated to the 850 million fund per year for early retirement, which the Lega wants to cap at 64. There may also be a reduction in the cut to pension indexation for those earning between four and six times the minimum pension. These are the main outcomes of the budget summit which the Prime Minister, on her return from the ‘Volenterosi’ summit on Ukraine in Berlin, held last night via videoconference with the leaders of the majority parties and the Minister for the Economy, Giancarlo Giorgetti.

Issues to be resolved

A face-to-face meeting that has not yet produced a resolution, but which is necessary to tighten the screws on a coalition that has shown a lack of discipline, as evidenced by the 2,000 amendments tabled to the Budget Bill. All the more so following yesterday’s sensational split in the Senate over the FdI amendment requiring publishers to sell e-books at a 50 per cent discount, with Forza Italia voting against it. Four days on from the first summit, which was sealed by a promise to proceed in a “united” manner, all the key issues remain on the table: pensions, healthcare, the Superbonus, the tax amnesty and the banks.

More resources for healthcare

However, progress has been made in certain areas. Starting with healthcare. In response to pressure from the regions, the government – as confirmed by Matteo Salvini on Tg2 Post – is in fact working on a further refinancing of the healthcare fund, with sums exceeding the figures that have been circulating. This means more than the 500 million (to be allocated to doctors’ and nurses’ allowances) that has been discussed in recent days (in addition to the already planned additional allocation of 3.4 billion). All of this is conditional upon certain conditions being met, which will be discussed with the regions in the coming days. The funds are to be found within the budget. “There are certain expenditure items that can be reviewed or postponed; the banks are already making a contribution, and discussions are still ongoing with credit institutions, insurance companies and the online gambling sector,” explained Salvini.

Salvini: working to raise the threshold for full revaluation to 100%

However, the League’s request for additional resources to be allocated to the fund of 850 million a year (for three years) for flexible retirement has been rejected. However, whilst upholding the principle that higher pensions will be revalued by a smaller percentage than lower ones, Salvini clarified that ‘we are working to raise the threshold for the total revaluation to 100 per cent’.

Agreement to be reached on the building amnesty

The majority must also grapple with an agreement on the building amnesty that has yet to be finalised. Tajani insists on the need to provide an answer to those still awaiting the outcome of their previous applications, but reiterates his assurance: there will be no blanket amnesty (“We will see what can and cannot be done”). Pending the selection of the proposed amendments, Forza Italia is also focusing on remote working to cut public administration costs. A proposal signed by treasurer Fabio Roscioli establishes a 10 million fund to reduce office space, energy consumption and expenditure.

Leo: limited resources but finances under control

The League, meanwhile, is back on the offensive regarding the Superbonus, calling for construction firms that have applied discounts on their invoices to be excluded from the ten-year instalment scheme for tax credits. The Deputy Minister for the Economy, Maurizio Leo, is quick to point out that any adjustment comes at a price. ‘We only have 26 billion,’ he warns once again, leaving the door open to a few adjustments, once more euphemistically labelled as ‘fine-tuning’ with the relevant sectors. This is also why the Deputy Minister is defending the doubling of the Tobin tax against criticism from Borsa Italiana and industry associations: a decision dictated by the need to ‘find resources to finance the planned measures’.
