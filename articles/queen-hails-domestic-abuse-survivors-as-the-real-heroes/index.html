Queen hails domestic abuse survivors as ‘the real heroes’

TheTools · 24.10.2026 20:37 · source · permalink

Queen Camilla, who revealed she was indecently assaulted as a teenager, praises campaigners fighting to end violence against women

The Queen has hailed domestic abuse survivors as “the real heroes” in the campaign to end violence against women.

Queen Camilla, who revealed last year that she was indecently assaulted on a train as a teenager, made the comments at the Ending Violence Against Women and Girls Summit in Wiltshire.

She said: “I want to say the real heroes are the survivors, not the victims. The survivors who come forward and tell us all their stories.”

Queen Camilla made an informal speech about her “heroes” in the field, which she has championed in her royal work, at the summit, organised by Lady Landsdowne, the Queen’s Companion.

She said her own determination to do whatever she could to help those affected by domestic and sexual abuse was inspired by a visit to the charity Safe Lives, where she heard Hetti Barkworth Nanton, the best friend of Jo Simpson, who was killed by her abusive estranged husband, speak about her case.

The Queen, 79, said of Ms Barkworth Nanton, chairman of the board of the charity Refuge: “It was one of the bravest things I’ve ever seen. As her best friend, she got up with such dignity and told this horrific story. She is one of the real heroes of this cause.”

She has spent more than a decade campaigning against domestic and sexual abuse. Last year, she revealed she was groped as a schoolgirl on a train and is said to have fended off her attacker with the heel of her shoe.

She described the incident during a special edition of BBC Radio 4’s Today programme in December, and said: “I had been attacked but I remember anger, and I was so furious about it.”

Lady Landsdowne said she had been inspired to set up the summit by the work the Queen was doing in the sphere to end abuse against women.

The Queen arrived on Friday morning and listened to speeches by Jess Philips, MP and former parliamentary under-secretary of state for safeguarding and violence against women and girls, and Dame Nicole Jacobs, the domestic abuse commissioner for England and Wales.

In her impromptu speech, she also praised Jo’s mother, Diana Parkes, who spoke at Friday’s event.

She said: “She is a fantastic lady. She’s of a certain age, she lost her beloved daughter, and a lot of people would have hidden away and shut the door and buried themselves forever.

“But she got up and got out there and she spoke to people. I have such admiration for her. She survived the ordeal and she survived it by helping others and stopping others from suffering the same fate.”

She also said Britain was “doing more than any other” to address the issue of domestic violence.

“I want to say a huge thanks to you all and to everyone,” she said. “As a resident of Wiltshire, I am deeply impressed that they are leading the way to ending domestic violence.”

Praising the survivors’ bravery, she added: “It’s terrifying to get up onto a platform and talk about something so horrific, the things that have happened to them, to stand in front of an audience and talk. I just want to applaud them.

“Talking is what we have to do. Domestic abuse was a taboo subject and nobody talked about it. It was hush-hush. Now people are getting up and talking about it. We might be able to put an end to it.”

At Friday’s event, Her Majesty met Zara McDermott, a documentary maker and influencer who has also become a campaigner in the field after becoming the victim of revenge porn.

The Queen told Ms McDermott, who is also the girlfriend of One Direction star Louis Tomlinson: “It’s people like you who speak out.”

Ms McDermott said afterwards: “I think it is my responsibility to [use what happened to her] in a positive way. Especially in my world, the world of social media and the digital space, we have so much power to connect with people, speak to them directly.

“I believe that every single person who has a platform has a responsibility to be positive with it.”
