Cessna 172 Pilot Describes Airplane Overturning at John Wayne Airport

The pilot of a Cessna 172 Skyhawk said he was "struck by a sudden jet blast" that caused his aircraft to pitch forward and overturn completely at John Wayne Airport (KSNA) in California.

The incident took place Saturday at approximately 2:40 p.m. PST, according to a report shared with FLYING by the Orange County Flight Center (OCFC), which owns the C172.

Officials from the airport and Orange County Fire Authority (OCFA) told CBS News Los Angeles that firefighters were called to the airport's ACI Jet ramp after learning of the flipped airplane. Firefighters were told that the aircraft was unoccupied and parked in its stall when the incident occurred, CBS LA reported. Airport officials later said the pilot and a passenger were on board and that both were uninjured.

Neither the airport nor OCFA said the Cessna 172 flipped due to a jet blast. However, the FAA said in a statement that the single-engine aircraft was blown over while taxiing behind United Airlines Flight 2611. According to air traffic control audio, the pilot of another C172 told the airport's ground control that the flipped aircraft was "blasted by the United 737 jet blast."

Footage from John Wayne Airport shows the moment the Cessna 172 flipped over. [Credit: Orange County Flight Center]

"We were following [N57] Bravo Mike, and Bravo Mike just flipped over," the pilot told ground.

OCFC director Niki Shetty told CBS LA that the United jet was "allowed to take off as our aircraft was leaving the ramp towards the taxiway." Shetty said the occupants were a flight instructor and student.

"Currently there is a major reconstruction and realignment project ongoing at the airport, and general aviation aircraft are being adversely impacted by this project, and flight safety may be jeopardized," Shetty said in a statement.

Cessna 172 Pilot Recounts the Incident

Per the report of the incident obtained by FLYING, the C172 flipped over at John Wayne Airport's Dove Street South Parking Entrance, near Taxiways A and D. Jason Ryu was the airplane's pilot, and Kunal Gawli was the student pilot. Fortunately, neither sustained injuries.

According to a summary, the C172 was stopped on the taxi route to the Dove Street parking area. A Boeing jet holding short of the airport's Runway 02L at Taxiway D increased its engine power to enter the runway, and the resulting blast struck the Skyhawk.

Ryu recalled that after clearing the runway, ATC told him to "Taxi via Charlie, through midfield run-up, then Alpha to Dove Street." As he approached the Dove Street South entrance, Ryu observed the Boeing jet holding short of the runway. His route to the entrance took the C172 behind the United airplane.

As Ryu began a left turn toward the entrance, the Skyhawk was "struck by a sudden jet blast" as the Boeing jet increased engine power, causing it to overturn.

"The airflow moved the aircraft and I was unable to maintain control of its movement," Ryu said in the report.

Ryu said that after the jet blast and before the C172 flipped, he immediately secured the engine and electrical system, pulling the mixture control to idle cutoff and turning off the ignition and master switch.

Per the report, the extent of the damage to the C172 has not yet been fully assessed.
