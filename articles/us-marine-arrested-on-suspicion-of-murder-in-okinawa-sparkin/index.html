US Marine arrested on suspicion of murder in Okinawa, sparking outrage in Japan | CNN

TheTools · 04.10.2026 04:50 · source · permalink

A US Marine was arrested Sunday on suspicion of murder and robbery after a woman’s body was discovered in a hotel room in Okinawa, prompting outrage across Japan, with the local governor saying the incident showed the US efforts to maintain discipline among its troops were “completely ineffective.”

Anna Yagi, 39, was found unresponsive in her hotel room in Naha city on Okinawa Island and pronounced dead at the scene early Saturday, Okinawa police said.

Police told CNN that Yagi had “died from suffocation after being strangled.”

Lance Corporal Devin Jacob Ballard, a 20-year-old Marine assigned to the III Marine Expeditionary Force at Futenma Air Station, was in the custody of local authorities, police and the US military said.

The case is the latest in a string of criminal allegations against US personnel in Okinawa. The island chain, which host US military facilities, including some of the largest US military bases overseas, have for years been the center of local protests against American presence.

CCTV footage reportedly showed a man, believed to be a foreign national, with a shaved head, entering the hotel room with the woman and later leaving the building with her belongings in hand, police said.

Okinawa police told CNN that the suspect initially denied the charges, but that police were refraining from commenting “on how the suspect is responding during the interrogation.” Prosectors will now decide whether to lay charges.

The III Marine Expeditionary Force said Monday it would not provide further personal or administrative details about Ballard, citing the need to protect the integrity of the ongoing investigation.

In a statement Sunday,, the US Marine Corps extended condolences to the victim’s family and said it takes the allegations “very seriously and expects every Marine to adhere to the highest standards of behavior.”

“We are actively cooperating with Japanese authorities in response to allegations of serious criminal actions by a Marine,” it said.

But the alleged murder has prompted outrage from politicians and leaders in Japan.

“The fact that this kind of event has happened again leaves us no choice but to conclude that the US military’s efforts in maintaining discipline, educating personnel, and other such measures are completely ineffective,” Okinawa Governor Genta Koja posted on X.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said her government “has strongly protested to the US side and has called for the fullest possible cooperation with the investigation, thorough discipline among US military personnel, and comprehensive measures to prevent the recurrence of crimes and accidents.”

The US embassy in Tokyo said on X it was “aware that a US service member is in custody following the death of a Japanese national in Okinawa.”

“As an investigation is underway to determine the facts of the case, the United States is communicating and cooperating with the Government of Japan and local authorities,” the embassy added.

The woman’s body was discovered at around 4.30 am local time on Saturday and the US Marine was arrested less than 24 hours later, at 1am local time, while walking around the city, the force added.

Japan’s public broadcaster NHK said Monday that the woman’s backpack was found near the crime scene and that her wallet was still inside, citing sources.

Last year a US Marine was sentenced to seven years in prison after he was convicted of strangling and sexually assaulting a woman in Okinawa. In 2016 tens of thousands of people demonstrated calling for an end to the US military presence on Okinawa following the killing of a local woman by a civilian worker stationed at the US Kadena Air Base.

“We are committed to being disciplined, responsible neighbors and preserving the trust of the local communities with whom we live and serve,” the III Marine Expeditionary Force said Monday.

This article has been updated with additional information and to update the rank of the Marine based on a statement from the US military.

CNN’s Aleena Fayaz, Bruna Horvath, Yumi Asada and Hana Saeki contributed to this report.
