Storm Team4 Forecast: Steady rain all day as temps drop into breezy 60s

TheTools · 22.09.2026 18:41 · source · permalink

Storm Team4 has the weather forecast for D.C., Maryland and Northern Virginia.

Storm Team4’s Gary McGrady is tracking showers and drizzle today and into Wednesday.

4 things to know about the weather:

  1. 100% rain chance Tuesday, Wednesday
  2. Chilly northeast wind
  3. Genuine drought relief
  4. Drying out by Friday

Stream NBC4 newscasts for free right here, right now.

What you see is what you get: clouds and rain all the way through the end of Wednesday.

High pressure over northern New England and low pressure over the Tennessee Valley will combine to deliver us a steady northeast wind that will deliver and abundance of moisture over the next 48 hours.

Most of the area will get at least 1 to 1.5 inches of rain with this storm with higher amounts to the southwest of the D.C. metro area. Central Virginia and the foothills of the Blue Ridge could easily get 2 to 2.5 inches.

The steadiest rain will come Tuesday and Tuesday night but the final drops won’t fall until late Wednesday night or Thursday morning.

Download the NBC Washington app on iOS and Android to check the weather radar on the go.

Drying out by end of week

Drier air will finally arrive Thursday afternoon and the weekend still looks good for outdoor activities.

With full cloud cover and rain, our temperatures are stuck right where they are: mid-to-upper 50s and low 60s all the way into Thursday morning. Once the rain tapers off, temperatures will recover a bit for Thursday afternoon with most areas returning to the mid-60s.

Increasing sunshine for Friday and the weekend will help us get back to around 70° but north winds still in the 10-15 mph range will prevent us from getting much warmer than that.

Once this storm moves away, the long range looks dry all the way into the first half of next week. Temperatures will finally get back into the mid-to-upper 70s by next Tuesday. In the meantime, let’s just enjoy this rainy, season changing soaker of a storm.
