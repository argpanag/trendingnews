MCCAYSVILLE, Ga. — McCaysville city leaders met Tuesday evening to provide an update on recovery efforts nearly three weeks after devastating flooding in the area, as officials described mounting costs and residents pressed for answers about what could change in the future.

Mayor Terry Arp says recovery and cleanup from the flood has been costly and the city is working to secure grant funding.

“We’re working as hard as we can and calling everybody we can to try to get the monies here on the grants,” Arp says.

Police Chief Micheal Earley says the financial strain has forced staffing cuts in the police department.

Earley says he had to lay off two officers as the city works to cover excess costs tied to the flooding.

“We have to do it efficiently and effectively with what we have left after this floods,” Earley says. “So, hopefully one day we can get back and hire those officers back.”

During the meeting, one resident urged the community to keep moving forward...

"We go things to do... and if we keep holding onto God’s hand, he’s going to take us through this.”

Another resident asked how the flood happened in the first place. Arp says he has been asking the same question, but pointed to the amount of rainfall and the city’s geography.

“I’ve gotten to wondering the same thing. But when you get 11 inches of rain and you live in a bowl, something’s going to give somewhere,” Arp says.

City officials say storm drains and the river reached their maximum capacity. Earley described water backing up through the drainage system as the river rose.

“Once that river comes up and crest the bottom of the bridge and you got rain, actively come into the storm drains. It has nowhere to go but back up,” Earley says.

As residents asked about preventative ordinances, Arp said the city has limited control near the river because other agencies regulate what can be done.

“It’s an Army Corps of Engineers, TVA and NPD, and they really regulate what we can and can’t do,” Arp says.

Officials also shared a positive update about a key local business.

Edward Vest, owner of I.G.A Hometown Food, told community members he plans to one day reopen the family-owned grocery store after it was damaged in the floods.

“The people that in our town, we had ladies sliding up in that mud and the store, they just get up and laugh about it and load them buggies and hitch it over,” Vest says.

City council members also voted to approve a joint declaration with Fannin County.

The city attorney says the declaration means the city and county would join in recovery efforts and work to reach the threshold for FEMA grants.
