Donald Trump has canceled his final two rallies before Election Day while also giving out candy to children at the annual White House Halloween event.

The president and first lady Melania Trump greeted Trick-or-Treaters dressed up as dinosaurs, racecar drivers and bananas Thursday evening. But he was most amused by the kids in costumes close to his interests such as police officers, the Founding Fathers and himself.

Trump was supposed to travel to Kansas on Saturday and Iowa on Sunday to fire up his base days before the midterms, but he canceled both visits, according to reports.

“They are going to do a series of tele-rallies but I don’t have any information about those,” Kansas GOP Chair Danedri Herbert said, per The Kansas City Star.

Earlier Thursday, the president hosted a rally in South Carolina amid a feud over Barack Obama’s jab comparing the White House to a Cheesecake factory.

After his team posted an AI-generated photo of Trump smashing cheesecake into Obama’s face, the president decided to hold back on more insults while campaigning Thursday for Republican Senator Darline Graham.

“They mobilized a whole government response to that joke – not inflation – but to that joke,” Obama said Wednesday.

Trump cancels final Kansas and Iowa rallies before Election Day — and unloads on reporters asking about it

President Donald Trump has canceled two campaign-trail appearances scheduled for the weekend before Election Day, opting instead for a series of telerallies amid dimming chances of Republican victories in midterms.

The president unloaded on a reporter who questioned why he canceled a Saturday appearance at a Kansas rally, one of last appearances on the road following two weeks of near nightly events. Hours later, the president pulled out of Sunday’s rally in Iowa.

Trump, who appeared to be sweating as he briefly addressed reporters following his remarks at a campaign rally in South Carolina, called a reporter with Reuters “way out of shape” and mocked him for asking whether the president was “feeling well.”

“You know what the reason is? I don’t feel I have to go to Kansas,” Trump told reporters Thursday. “He said, ‘You’re not going to Kansas, sir, are you feeling well?’ You’ve been following me for two weeks. I feel perfect.”

The real winner in the Trump vs. Obama fight over The Cheesecake Factory might be the restaurant’s bottom line

The Cheesecake Factory finds itself in the middle of a presidential tussle, and it’s doing just fine.

After former President Barack Obama compared President Donald Trump’s White House decor to The Cheesecake Factory, the restaurant announced during an earnings call Tuesday that sales soared 15.3 percent from July to September. Those months were before the two leaders started their public feud that sucked in the restaurant.

Obama’s comments were met with outrage from MAGA, who called them “elitist.” Trump’s Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent responded by ordering a French dip cheeseburger at a Cheesecake Factory in Washington, D.C. Monday. Obama defended his comments at a Wednesday campaign rally in Georgia, saying the restaurant was his “jam” while he raised his daughters.

Though The Cheesecake Factory was swept up in controversy without asking, it’s a valuable win for the company, Marin Richardson, CEO of publicity firm Disrupt PR, told The Independent in an email.

“Both sides are fighting over who loves it more,” Richardson said. “Most brands would pay a fortune for that.”

Trump’s Cabinet members are violating federal law by joining him on campaign trail, experts allege

Ethics experts have accused some of President Donald Trump’s Cabinet of violating federal law by joining him on the campaign trail to rally for Republican candidates ahead of the midterm elections.

At least nine of Trump’s 15 cabinet secretaries have been accused of violating the Hatch Act —a federal law prohibiting federal employees from engaging in certain partisan political activities in their official capacity — over the past few months, according to The New York Times.

The president and vice president are exempt from restrictions under the 87-year-old law, but it applies to all other civil employees of the executive branch.

Trump rally attendee trolls Fox News reporter with MAGA jibe in South Carolina

An attendee at a Donald Trump rally in South Carolina trolled a Fox News reporter by comparing the MAGA faithful to Jim James and the Jonestown suicide cult.

The pro-Trump station’s reporter Lucas Tomlinson was interviewing members of the audience gathered in Rock Hill on Thursday to hear the president stump for Darline Graham, the sister of the late Senator Lindsey Graham, who is facing off against Democrat Annie Andrews.

Tomlinson asked the man, who gave his name as Tom, why he was at the rally.

 “Well, in the late '70s, I was a little too young to get involved and go to South America for the Jonestown Massacre, but this is as close as I'll ever get, so that's why I'm here,” Tom replied.

When a confused Tomlinson asked him to clarify he meant the Jonestown massacre, in which 900 members of the Jonestown cult committed suicide in Guyana, Tom replied, “ there's nothing like seeing a cult right up front, man.”
