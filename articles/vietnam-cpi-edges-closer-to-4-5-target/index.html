Vietnam CPI edges closer to 4.5% target

TheTools · 04.09.2026 13:24 · source · permalink
  • Economy

Vietnam's average consumer price index (CPI) rose 4.45% in the first eight months of 2026, just 0.05 percentage point below the 4.5% target set by the National Assembly.

With the room for containing prices narrowing, energy prices and essential services will be key variables for inflation management in the final months of the year.

A fruit shop in Vietnam. Photo courtesy of the Ministry of Industry and Trade.

CPI rebounds, narrowing gap with target

According to the National Statistics Office , CPI rose 0.47% in August from the previous month and 4.89% from a year earlier. For the first eight months, CPI rose 4.45%, while core inflation increased 4.24%.

The 4.45% increase puts CPI close to the government's target of keeping average inflation at around 4.5% in 2026. With the National Assembly, the country's legislative body, targeting GDP growth of 10% or more, containing inflation and maintaining macroeconomic stability are important conditions for sustaining high economic growth.

Notably, CPI rebounded in August after declining for two consecutive months. The main driver was the transport group, which rose 4.09% from July and contributed 0.41 percentage point to the overall CPI. Diesel prices jumped 22.15%, while gasoline prices rose 9.53%.

The National Statistics Office said the August CPI increase was mainly linked to domestic fuel price adjustments in line with global fuel price movements. This highlights energy as a variable capable of having a rapid and significant impact on the overall price level.

On the positive side, inflationary pressure has not yet spread across the economy. Prices of food and catering services edged down 0.03% in August, while core inflation in the first eight months was 0.21 percentage point lower than headline CPI.

Energy prices and public services key variables

Besides fuel, movements in essential service prices could add to inflationary pressure. The education group rose 0.47% in August, with education services increasing 0.57% as some localities adjusted tuition fees for the 2026-2027 academic year.

In the first eight months, the housing, electricity, water, fuel and construction materials group rose 6.71%, making it one of the major sources of pressure on CPI.

The figures show that efforts to contain inflation in the final months of the year will depend not only on global oil prices, but also on how and when prices of goods and services that directly affect household living costs are adjusted.

At a forum on market and price developments in Vietnam in 2026, held in early July, Can Van Luc, chief economist at state-controlled BIDV and a member of the National Financial and Monetary Policy Advisory Council, forecast average CPI growth in 2026 at between 4.3% and 4.6%. He said essential goods as well as state-regulated goods and services would remain major factors affecting the overall price level.

Nguyen Duc Do, former deputy director of the Institute of Economics and Finance, told the media recently that full-year inflation could range between 4.5% and 5%, with exchange-rate stability, flexible management of state-regulated prices, and measures to limit spillover effects from energy price volatility being key.

The 4.45% average CPI increase after eight months does not mean the 4.5% target is out of reach, but the buffer has clearly become very thin. A positive scenario remains possible if energy prices cool and state-regulated prices are managed appropriately. Conversely, a sharp rise in fuel prices, exchange rates or public service prices could quickly alter the inflation outlook.

Experts said that as Vietnam pursues a high-growth target, price management in the final months of the year should therefore be viewed as part of a broader policy equation: limiting cost shocks, protecting purchasing power, and anchoring inflation expectations while preserving room for monetary policy to support growth.

With CPI already close to the 4.5% threshold, the final months of 2026 will be decisive in determining whether Vietnam can meet its inflation-control target for the year.
