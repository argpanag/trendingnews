What we know so far about the deadly 'disaster' at CFB Wainwright in Alberta | CBC

Deaths, injuries after fire at Alberta military base training area, officials say

The Latest

  • Multiple people have been hurt or killed after a significant fire burned at a large training area on a Canadian Forces Base in east-central Alberta on Saturday.
  • Canada's minister of national defence did not confirm how many people died, but an emergency care physician in Edmonton described the incident at CFB Wainwright as "a one in every 15 to 25-year disaster."
  • Officials called a "code orange" response protocol in Edmonton to help care for a high number of patients coming from the base, while the general public was asked to avoid the University of Alberta Hospital emergency department.
  • The Department of National Defence told CBC News there were no indications that what unfolded at CFB Wainwright was the result of "any terrorist activity."
  • Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said the incident affected "many of our brave military personnel." Prime Minister Mark Carney also described the fire as "serious."

What we know so far about the deadly incident at CFB Wainwright

CBC News senior defence writer Murray Brewster breaks down what is known — and not known — about Saturday's incident at a Canadian Forces Base in Alberta that officials say resulted in deaths and injuries, as well as how the investigation might unfold and the implications for Canada's military.

A large fire at a training area at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in east-central Alberta on Saturday resulted in multiple fatalities and injuries, Canadian Armed Forces officials say.

Here's what we know so far. 

  • National Defence Minister David McGuinty confirmed that people were killed and injured in the fire but did not provide numbers. The Canadian Armed Forces says it is waiting until next of kin have been notified before releasing further details. McGuinty called the fire "very active and delicate" and said it was the worst mass casualty event he knew of in "recent memory."
  • The Canadian Armed Forces says the fire started around 4 p.m. local time Saturday and spread beyond the training area. Strong winds contributed to the fire's rapid spread.
  • Health staff said a "code orange" was declared to co-ordinate care for a high number of patients transported from Wainwright. Dr. Warren Thirsk, an emergency department physician at Edmonton’s Royal Alexandra Hospital, described the incident as a "one in every 15 to 25-year disaster."
  • The cause of the fire remains unclear. The RCMP confirmed it sent resources to assist at the base. The Canadian Armed Forces has said further details will be released as they become available.
  • Alberta Premier Danielle Smith called it a "significant and tragic fire," while Prime Minister Mark Carney described it as a "serious incident." Saskatchewan has deployed resources to assist Alberta, and British Columbia has also offered support.
  • Bruce Pugh, the mayor of the neighbouring community of Wainwright, said the fire had crossed the base fence, but was being managed and held by firefighting teams. He said the local hospital was acting as a triage centre and that the fire would have a profound impact on his community, noting, "we share our lives" with the members of the Armed Forces base.
  • CFB Wainwright is a major military training facility. It is the Canadian Armed Forces' principal base for live-fire training exercises, bringing together infantry, artillery and air support for combined training.
  • Gen. Jennie Carignan, chief of the defence staff released a statement early Sunday morning: "This is a profoundly difficult moment for the entire Canadian Armed Forces. Our immediate focus remains on supporting those affected, assisting emergency response efforts, and ensuring the well-being of our members, their families, and the surrounding community.… I extend my deepest condolences to the loved ones of those who have lost their lives."

    What goes into the next of kin notification process

    David Common

    The Canadian Armed Forces train as they fight. This means they use live fire in realistic training exercises, like those at CFB Wainwright. While many safety protocols are in place, the work is inherently dangerous.

    Deaths have occurred on the base before, and there is unfortunately a well-exercised process for notifying next of kin of both those injured and killed.

    The main operations centre at National Defence Headquarters is immediately involved, as next of kin may be anywhere in Canada or beyond. Where possible, a senior CAF officer will be sent to a home (or local law enforcement where it can arrive faster). In some instances, the news will be delivered by phone.

    In cases of both injuries and death, members of a family are flown by the CAF, to either a hospital or the location of their loved one's remains. This may be by airline or military aircraft. 

    They are supported by a designated officer: a member of the CAF who is there to act as an in-person connection between the family and the military.

    There have been instances where it can take more than 24 hours to locate the next of kin. The military has tended not to publicly announce deaths, preferring that a family hears from them first and not through the news.

    'Different fingers of the same glove'

    Verity Stevenson

    Pugh's community is closely interconnected with the nearby Armed Forces base, which is among the largest in the country. Of the town's 7,000 inhabitants, many are either members, former members or have family and professional ties to it, the mayor said. 

    "We're different fingers of the same glove," Pugh said. "Our kids attend the same schools. We play on the same sports teams. We enjoy the same rec facilities."

    Pugh said the incident would be seared in his community's memory and that of its first responders and health workers. 

    "This will be an incident where it's a heartbreaking, impossible-to-understand event, but it's being met with an equal reaction of humanity," he said.

    I've spent time at CFB Wainwright, and can speak to its massive size. It is the largest ground training facility in Canada's Armed Forces, where troops have come from across the country to face realistic battlefield exercises for generations.
