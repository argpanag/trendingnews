Las Vegas Grand Prix: Kimi Antonelli fastest but penalty means Max Verstappen will start on pole

TheTools · 21.11.2026 09:24 · source · permalink

Kimi Antonelli won both the previous two grands prix in the United States this season, in Miami and Austin

  • Published
    21 November 2026, 05:19 GMT

Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli was fastest in qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix but a grid penalty will hand pole position to Red Bull's Max Verstappen.

Antonelli, running at the back of the pack of cars in qualifying and maximising his tow, beat Verstappen by 0.258 seconds.

But the new world champion has a five-place grid penalty for using more than permitted number of engine components and will start sixth.

The Italian's team-mate George Russell was just 0.073secs behind Verstappen and will start alongside the Dutchman on the front row.

McLaren's Lando Norris qualified fourth, and will start third, ahead of Red Bull's Isack Hadjar.

Both Ferraris were eliminated in the second session and qualified 11th and 12th with Charles Leclerc ahead of Lewis Hamilton.

Each will be promoted a place by a 10-place penalty for McLaren's Oscar Piastri, who qualified seventh.

Racing Bulls' rookie Arvid Lindblad, sixth fastest, is the last driver to be promoted as a result of Antonelli's penalty.

Racing Bulls' Liam Lawson will start seventh with Alpine's Pierre Gasly eighth and Franco Colapinto ninth.

Antonelli's final run did not start auspiciously as he and Russell became tangled up with the Alpines and Racing Bulls as they sought the best track position to optimise the slipstream effect.

Antonelli ended up the last driver to run and made it to the line with only one second to spare before the chequered flag, one second behind Lindblad.

Normally, being so close to the car in front would be a compromise but in Las Vegas it magnified the tow effect and Antonelli leapt to the front. "That was all the tow," he said over the radio after being told he was on pole.

"Starting the lap, I was only one second behind the car in front and I thought I was screwed," he said. "But the tow is so powerful here."

Verstappen, who has won two of the three Las Vegas Grands Prix, said: "I have been struggling with some weird issues that were also in the car in Brazil and just trying to get my head around how to deal with that.

"In qualifying we managed to make it work. Had a solid qualifying. My first lap in qualifying was pretty much as good as it could be with the tow, so to be on the front row is good for us.

"It was solid in the corners, and you have a bit of dirty air, but it is insane how much you gain on the straights."

Russell had looked in the running for the pole, but was happy with his session despite missing out.

"Overall it's been a very solid session and a solid day," he said.

"I wanted to be on pole but I am probably more pleased with the performance than in Mexico when I was on the front row.

"Kimi did a great job and he probably thought it wasn't going to work out so well being so close but with the tow it worked out."

'Nightmare' for Ferrari

In four grands prix in Las Vegas, Lewis Hamilton's best qualifying result is 10th

Leclerc and Hamilton were separated by 0.056 seconds and both had tales of woe that explained their early exit - but very different ones.

Leclerc said: "Just a very poor qualifying. We are losing one second in the straights which makes it difficult enough already, and then I did a mistake when I had to put the lap in in Q2, following multiple mistakes we have done as a team and this we need to look at because it builds a lot of frustration that doesn't help.

"But in the end the mistake is on me and I will look at that."

Hamilton added: "It was a nightmare. Just low grip, felt like the tyres weren't working, had some damage on the floor that appeared out of nowhere and was losing 0.3-0.4secs and then had a deployment issue that lost us 0.6secs and with that we were nowhere, and I had no-one ahead of me. That wasn't great. So everything wasn't great.

"We knew we would struggle a bit here but maybe more than we anticipated."
