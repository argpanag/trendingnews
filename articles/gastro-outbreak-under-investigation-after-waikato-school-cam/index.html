Gastro outbreak under investigation after Waikato school camp

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:54 · source · permalink

Health NZ's National Public Health Service is investigating an outbreak of gastroenteritis (gastro) linked to a Waikato school camp last week.

Around 360 students, 13 staff and 48 parents attended the Marian Catholic School camp between November 18 and 20.

Public Health was notified of the outbreak on November 24.

"A number of students have been unwell with vomiting and diarrhoea since returning, and at least two staff and some parents have also been unwell. Public Health is working to confirm how many people are affected," Medical Officer of Health Kate Meerkerk said.

Gastro symptoms included diarrhoea; nausea and vomiting; stomach pain, including abdominal cramps, aching or bloating; low-grade or high fever; a loss of appetite, tiredness, headache, and muscle aches.

Meerkerk said the cause of the illness was not yet known.

"Public Health staff are working closely with the school and the camp to understand what may have caused it, including potentially food, water or the wider environment," she said. "Stool sample kits have been provided to the school, and we are asking people with recent vomiting or diarrhoea to help by providing a sample as soon as possible so we can find the cause."

She added that some families had reported illness in a household member who did not attend the camp "which shows that gastro can spread from person to person at home".

Anyone with the illness was advised to stay away from the school until they were free of symptoms for 48 hours.

Most people with gastro recovered at home within a few days without needing medical treatment.

Anyone with concerns was urged to call their healthcare provider or Healthline on 0800 611 116.

General tips for preventing gastroenteritis

- Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water, clean under fingernails, rinse well and dry with a clean towel: after using the toilet, before and after preparing food, and after cleaning up vomit or diarrhoea.

- People with gastro should not go to work, school, university or early childhood centres until they have been symptom-free for 48 hours.

- People should wait at least two weeks after their last episode of diarrhoea before swimming in a pool.

- People with gastro should not prepare food for others for at least 48 hours after vomiting and diarrhoea stop.
