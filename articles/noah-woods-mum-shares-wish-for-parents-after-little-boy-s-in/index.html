Noah Woods' mum shares wish for parents after little boy's inquest

TheTools · 25.09.2026 12:24 · source · permalink

Noah Woods' family has spoken out following an inquest into his death, with his mum sharing her heartbreaking wish for other parents.

Three-year-old Noah was found after a widespread public search, and an inquest has now heard that he left a play area 'through a gap in the fence.'

Paying tribute to their son, Noah's family said he was 'a happy, cheeky, playful' little boy 'who brought joy wherever he went.'

Noah’s family said in the tribute: 'He was part of a loving and caring family, living with his mummy, daddy and his little baby brother. He was at the centre of our world.

'Noah was being assessed for autism, although he had not received a diagnosis. He also had mild hearing loss and was speech-delayed. Despite the challenges he faced, he was learning every day.

'He was saying new words all the time and had only just started finding and using his little voice.

'One of the things we miss most is the special sound of his voice. It was unique, wonderful, and something we will carry with us forever.

'He absolutely adored his little baby brother. He loved spending time with him, entertaining him and making him laugh.

'He would follow him around and always wanted to be close to him. The bond they shared was incredibly special.”

Noah’s mother, Charlie, added: 'If there is one thing we could ask of people, it is to hold their children a little tighter, cherish every moment, and never take those precious memories for granted.'

The family added that they 'want Noah's last memory to be one of joy' and for 'people to remember him running, playing chase, giggling and being the happy little boy that he was.'

Noah's inquest heard that he left a play area 'through a gap in the fence' after he became separated from a relative on the afternoon of September 15. The inquest has been adjourned to a later date.

Around 1,300 members of the public joined a search to find Noah, and Met Police divers recovered his body from a lake, known locally as Decoy Pond, on the afternoon of September 16.

The day after Noah was found, his father, Rhys, also shared a statement, saying the family was 'absolutely heartbroken.'

'There are no words to describe the pain of this loss; we are all absolutely heartbroken. Noah was the sweetest and most innocent little boy, with nothing but love to give, and we are going to miss him so very much,' he said.

He extended a 'heartfelt thank you to everyone … who came together to search for our boy.'

A JustGiving fundraiser set up to support the family has since received more than £113,000 (approx. €131,000) in donations.
