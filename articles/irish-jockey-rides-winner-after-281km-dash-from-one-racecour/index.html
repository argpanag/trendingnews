Irish jockey rides winner after 281km dash from one racecourse to another

TheTools · 22.09.2026 13:33 · source · permalink

Jockey Sam Coen's decision to fly from one race meeting to another paid off as he rode Shesthedevil to victory at Fairyhouse on Monday.

The leading apprentice's first ride of the day came in Listowel and saw him finish second on Zaraahmando (11/4 fav) for trainer Andrew Slattery. The four-year-old was beaten three lengths and three-quarters of a length by the Denis Hogan-trained Majestic King under Daniel King in the GAIN The Advantage Series Handicap over a mile.

Coen then jumped on a plane with fellow jockey Sean Flanagan, who rode a 12/1 winner at Listowel on Sunday. As well as being a good pilot on a horse, the Champion Chase-winning jockey also holds a pilot's licence.

Shortly after landing at Fairyhouse, Coen landed a seven-furlong claiming race on Shesthedevil (11/4) for trainer Patrick Magee, beating the Ger Lyons-trained and Colin Keane-ridden Cudawudashuda (6/5 fav) by half a length.

Flanagan previously explained how he got into flying planes and stated that he would love to work for Ryanair when he retires from the saddle.

He said: "Years back, I got a present of a voucher for an introductory flying lesson. I loved it.

"At the time I wasn’t able to pursue it. Later I was able to pick it up and decided to have a crack and built a bit of momentum.

"I was chatting with a fella who sponsored me at the time and told him about it. He told me to come and see him the next day. I did and it turned out his son had just qualified as a pilot and was joining Ryanair.

"The chap had bought his son a plane, a Cessna 150, for him to train in. He said I could use it. That made my training affordable and it took me two years to get my licence.

"Things skyrocketed from there. I really enjoy it, and it is very handy. For instance, it is a three-and-a-half-hour drive for me from where I am based in Kells to Mallow racecourse in Cork. Flying only takes me 45 minutes, and I land beside the track!

"I qualified five years ago. I’ve flown all over. When I came second (on board Vanillier) in the 2023 Grand National, I flew six other jockeys over!

"It is so convenient. At Cheltenham I land at Gloucester Airport which is only 15 minutes from the track. Cost-wise it is not much dearer when you share the plane."

He added: "It’s definitely an option (becoming a full-time pilot). I always said the only way I could find the time at the moment is if I have an injury that keeps me out for a while. That is very possible in our game.

"If it did happen, I’d be straight on studying for the commercial exams. There are 14 written exams I’d have to take and 250 hours and a lot of SIM instrument training. I already have about 400 hours of solo flying.

"It really appeals to me - it would be the dream job. Flying for Ryanair would be the ultimate. Imagine Captain Flanagan! If anyone I knew heard me welcome them aboard, they’d turn round and jump off quick enough."
