AccuWeather Alert: Tornado watch for parts of N.J. | Live update

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:24 · source · permalink

Stay with Eyewitness News and the AccuWeather team for continuing updates on the coastal low.

NEW YORK (WABC) -- A storm system is moving in on Thursday, bringing heavy rain, possible thunderstorms and a sharp drop in temperatures by Friday.

Separate tornado warnings issued for Queens in New York City, and Monmouth and Burlington counties in New Jersey on Thursday expired, but a tornado watch remains in effect for parts of New Jersey until 7 p.m.

A severe thunderstorm warning has expired for parts of southern New Jersey, including Monmouth and Ocean counties, but has been replaced with a flash flood warning for the area until 10 p.m.

Thunderstorms are producing heavy rain across the area. Between 1 and 2.5 inches of rain have already fallen in some areas, and slow-moving bands of storms could bring more before the evening commute.

Eyewitness News meteorologists are keeping a close eye on the potential for slow-moving bands of rain and thunderstorms that could repeatedly track over the same locations. If that occurs, localized flooding could develop in some communities and create difficult travel conditions for the evening commute.

The system is also pulling much colder air into the Northeast.

As rain continues Thursday night and into Friday, precipitation will begin changing over to snow across the Catskills and Poconos. Winter weather alerts are in effect for Ulster County from 7 p.m. Thursday through 1 p.m. Friday. Around an inch of snow is possible north of the I-84 corridor in parts of Pike, Sullivan, Ulster, and northern Dutchess counties, with 1 to 3 inches at higher elevations in the Catskills.

In addition to the rain and snow threat, minor coastal flooding is possible. Areas along the western Long Island Sound and southern Nassau County face the greatest risk around high tide, while Ocean County is also under a coastal flood advisory for minor flooding concerns.

Behind the storm, the coldest air of the season will settle into the region. Gusty winds on Friday will make temperatures feel more like the low-to-mid 30s, a dramatic change from Thursday's mild conditions. Rain and snow showers will gradually diminish Friday evening as the storm departs.

The unsettled pattern lingers into the weekend. Saturday will remain blustery and cool with highs near 47 degrees. Conditions improve on Sunday with brighter skies, though it will stay windy and cool. Early next week looks calmer, but temperatures are expected to remain below the mild readings seen before the storm arrived.
