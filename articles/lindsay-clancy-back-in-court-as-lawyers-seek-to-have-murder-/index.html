Lindsay Clancy back in court as lawyers seek to have murder case dismissed

TheTools · 29.09.2026 16:13 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Courts

Topic:Courts

Lindsay Clancy's murder trial ended without a verdict earlier this month. (Reuters: Josh Reynolds)

In short:

Lindsay Clancy killed her three young children in their Boston home more than three years ago.

Her murder trial ended with a mistrial after the jury could not reach a unanimous verdict.

What's next?

Ms Clancy's lawyers have asked the court to throw out a retrial and find her not guilty by reason of insanity.

Lindsay Clancy has returned to court for the first time since her mistrial, with her lawyer asking for the murder case against her to be dismissed. 

The 36-year-old Massachusetts woman has never denied killing her three children, aged five, three and eight months, in the basement of their Boston home in January 2023.

But her lawyers had argued Ms Clancy was delusion and not in control of her actions at the time due to a rare condition known as postpartum psychosis.

Earlier this month, her murder trial ended in deadlock with the jury unable to reach a unanimous verdict: 11 members wanted to find her not guilty by reason of insanity, but one member would not acquit her.

Judge William Sullivan then declared a mistrial. 

But Ms Clancy returned to court on Tuesday, local time, with her lawyers saying her first trial should not have been declared a mistrial and she should be spared from a second trial.

They told the court a second trial would violate Ms Clancy's right to avoid being tried twice for the same crime, known as double jeopardy.

Her attorneys have also asked the judge to allow them to access normally confidential records about the jury, including the truthfulness during the jury selection process and a juror's use of a mobile phone.

Lawyer Kevin Reddington told the judge there was a "total and complete lack of evidence" at the trial to prove Ms Clancy knew what she was doing was wrong when she killed the children.

"This is coming from politicians, from President Trump all the way down," he told the court on Tuesday local time.

"This entire case is based on speculation."

But prosecutor Shanan Buckingham told the court that argument was "laughable" and there was enough evidence to convict Ms Clancy.

While they have not said whether they planned to retry the former maternity nurse, prosecutors did argue during the first trial that Ms Clancy, despite having mental health issues, knew what she was doing when she strangled the children.

Prosecutors have several options before them, including trying to reach a plea deal or retrying Clancy on lesser charges, which would give her the option to waive a jury trial and proceed before a judge.

If Ms Clancy is found not guilty by reason of insanity, she would be committed to a state psychiatric hospital for evaluation and would then be subject to regular reviews by the court.

The judge did not make a ruling on a retrial and the case is expected to return to court in November.

Until then, Ms Clancy is expected to remain at a psychiatric hospital.

The court heard if there was to be a new trial it would not be heard before next June.
