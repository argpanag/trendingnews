'I expect us to go there and win' - O'Neil hints at tactical switch for Man City trip

TheTools · 17.10.2026 16:13 · source · permalink

Ipswich Town manager Gary O'Neil has told his players that he expects them to them to go to Manchester City and win tomorrow.

The newly-promoted Blues go the Etihad with their tails up following last weekend's 2-1 home win against Fulham. That made it three wins from six in the Premier League, all of them coming courtesy of dramatic last-gasp goals.

City, who are playing their first home league game since being found guilty of 114 charges of breaching financial rules, sit top of the table with a 100% record though, with striker Erling Haaland in ominous form.

Upon taking the Town job back in June, O'Neil referenced a trip to the Etihad as a moment he would have to take a more pragmatic tactical approach. Asked if that remained the plan, he replied: “I don't know how to answer that without giving too much away! I haven't fully decided, but the lads are aware that we're considering moving a few bits around.

“I love where we're at. I love what we've been able to do, performance-wise, and I am reluctant to move too far away from it. I also have a lot of respect for the tens of world-class players that Manchester City have, so it would be silly of me to go there and not to change a few things, I think.

“But the lads fully understand what I expect from this weekend. They know that I expect us to go there and win the game. There's no other thought in my mind.

“However we decide to go about it, if we decide to change to something else, that is not just for the sake of it, it's to win. The lads know that the only reason we're going there is to win."

O'Neil continued: “We understand the size of the task. Load of the lads have been in games like this, whether it be the boys from France who have played PSG, whether it be the boys that have been in the Premier League and have played City before. They know that you need to go there and you need to be absolutely perfect. It won't feel like playing Fulham at home. It won't.

“But that's okay. We just do the best version of us this weekend and I fully believe that we can win a football match.

"They’ve won every game in the league. The PSG game in the week (4-2 home loss in the Champions League) was a good game. We need to defend well, of course, but we also need to create chances and need to score because I don't think many teams manage to go there and keep Manchester City out. We've done well at that this year so far, and that needs to continue tomorrow."

With City facing a potentially relegation-guaranteeing points deduction following their seismic guilty verdict, O'Neil reiterated his comments from last week that he hadn't put any thought into their situation or possible punishment.

"If we win the game tomorrow, I'm sure we get three points - so let's win the game," he smiled, when asked if he not fully knowing the stakes of this game was an issue.

"The group is ready. We’re in in a really good spot. The players should be really excited with the challenge. It's not a challenge that we are fearing in any way.

"We go there and we try to be ourselves. We try to be on the front foot. We try to equip ourselves well enough to deal with the many threats that they pose.

"We know how much work it's going to take from everybody – the starting 11, staff, subs, non-subs, everybody travelling up there. We go together as a united group to try and do something special on the road against an excellent side.

"Of course, the challenge is to stay in it as long as possible at Manchester City, but it's not like the plan is not to go there and hang on. At times it will look like that, I'm sure, but the plan is to go there, to be really disciplined, but to make sure that we still carry a threat.

"This is why you work so hard to get into the Premier League, to be able to test yourself against the best. We get to do that tomorrow, and we don't go there just for a, ‘Oh, this is nice, let's see what this is like’. That's not what we're about.

“The fact they've played two big games this week (winning 3-0 at Liverpool last Sunday before hosting PSG on Wednesday) will test them. Of course it will. There is always something in the atmosphere after a loss and I'm sure City will have felt that after losing to PSG.

"Their three-game week probably makes it more difficult for me to know their starting XI. I think there's probably more chance that they rotate a little bit.

"You might get away with a small crack or two against some teams in the Premier League, but if you go to Manchester City and something's not quite right then it very, very quickly ends up in you taking a kick-off. So we need to be at the very, very top of our level. Standards have been unbelievably high all week from everybody to make sure that the group is ready and the ship is tight.

"Ultimately we go there to make sure that we have a real good go and make it as difficult as possible for them."
