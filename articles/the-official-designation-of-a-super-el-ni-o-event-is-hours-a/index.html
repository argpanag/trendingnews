Forecasters have said for months that El Niño would reach "super" status, the point when water temperatures are at least 2 degrees Celsius above average in the equatorial Pacific for at least three consecutive months.

They predicted it would happen in late fall, maintaining status until spring 2027, when La Niña conditions are forecast to take over. 

Evaluations completed by NOAA's Climate Prediction Center (CPC) found that water temperatures during the three-month period from July, August and September were not high enough to meet the "Very Strong" or "Super" El Niño category. 

However, on Thursday morning, the CPC is expected to say that the August, September and October period did reach the 2 degrees Celsius threshold needed for Super El Niño. 

Despite not being an official Super El Niño yet, the climate pattern has proved to have significant impacts, especially when it comes to hurricane season in the Pacific and the Atlantic. 

El Niño's impacts on 2026 hurricane season 

The Eastern Pacific has reported one of the busiest seasons on record, with only four names left on the list for the season. 

The Atlantic, on the other hand, has had a record under-active season, with only 1 hurricane and 10 named storms for the season so far.

In the Atlantic, wind shear limited storm development, while in the Pacific, warm ocean temperatures and limited wind shear helped fuel tropical activity, all thanks to El Niño. 

El Niño's impacts won't stop there, as the climate pattern is also forecast to have significant impacts on the winter ahead. 

What Super El Niño is likely to mean for winter 

NOAA's Winter Outlook for the 2026-27 season said more storms are expected from Southern California across the entire Southern Tier to Florida. 

During a Super El Niño, the Pacific jet stream tends to become more extended and active, acting as a conveyor belt that directs storms farther across the southern tier of the U.S.

Super El Niños of the past vs today

In past years, we have seen Super El Niño, but forecasters aren't sure how this Super El Niño will look compared to others in history just yet. 

Winter 1997-98 and 2015-26 were both Super El Niño years.
