After a solid month-long run in theaters, one of the most divisive movies of 2026 is heading home. Interestingly, the film is headlined by the star of another highly controversial film, also distributed by the same company earlier this year. The first film emerged as a runaway hit, grossing $135 million worldwide against a reported $28 million budget, buoyed by positive reviews and a provocative central conceit. Starring Robert Pattinson and Zendaya, the movie in question is The Drama. Pattinson's latest film has essentially matched The Drama's box-office performance domestically, and has yet to be released in most overseas markets. It will, however, become available to rent and purchase on PVOD platforms this week.

The film was released theatrically in September, and has so far grossed more than $46 million at the domestic box office against a reported budget of under $15 million. The chances of it touching the $50 million mark seem slim, given that it's playing in only around 175 theaters nationwide. The Drama ended its domestic box-office run with $48 million, and is now sitting at a "Certified Fresh" 76% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes. Pattinson and Zendaya were also a part of Christopher Nolan's record-breaking epic The Odyssey, and will soon be seen in Denis Villeneuve's hotly anticipated sci-fi threequel Dune: Part Three.

The Thriller Has Received Excellent Reviews

But we're talking about Primetime, the controversial thriller based on the television series To Catch a Predator's short-lived run in the mid-2000s. Directed by Lance Oppenheim, who also co-directed the recent word-of-mouth sensation You Can See Everything, the movie holds a "Certified Fresh" 86% score on Rotten Tomatoes. The aggregator website's consensus reads, "Turning the camera's scrutinizing eye back onto To Catch a Predator's journalistic legacy, Primetime interrogates the slippery boundary between justice and lurid spectacle with crackling style and an outstanding Robert Pattinson." Also featuring Merritt Wever, Skyler Gisondo, and Phoebe Bridgers, Primetime will debut on the PVOD market on November 3. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
