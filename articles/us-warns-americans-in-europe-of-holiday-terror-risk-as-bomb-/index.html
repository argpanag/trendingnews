  • The US Embassy in Bulgaria warned Americans across Europe to stay vigilant over the holidays, citing terror risks at crowded events, more frequent strikes and November bomb hoaxes that disrupted flights in numerous European countries this month.

The alert is precautionary, and alerts like this rarely move markets on their own. The bigger point for markets is travel reliability heading into the busiest stretch of the year. US carriers are still clearing backlogs from last week’s FAA ground stop, and Monday’s United outage shows how little slack there is in airline systems. Any further disruption in Europe or the US during the Thanksgiving peak would bring airline and travel stocks into focus. The banking outages were resolved and described as technical. Washington is warning Americans to watch their step in Europe this holiday season. Back home, a week of grounded flights and frozen bank apps has shown that the systems people rely on can fail without any attacker involved.

  • The US Embassy in Bulgaria issued a Europe-wide security alert urging Americans to stay vigilant during the holiday season.

  • Americans are advised to monitor local news, check with airlines, avoid demonstrations and enroll in the State Department’s STEP alert program.
  • In the US, an FAA ground stop, a United Airlines outage and online banking problems at major lenders caused disruption in recent days.

The US Embassy in Bulgaria has issued a Europe-wide security alert urging American citizens to stay vigilant over the holiday season. It warns that terrorists, including lone actors, may strike with little or no warning, and that disruptions to travel and daily life remain possible across the continent.

The alert, dated November 16, says large gatherings and public events could be a target for terrorist groups and criminals. It singles out tourist locations such as markets, transport hubs, shopping malls and places of worship, and urges extra caution at holiday festivals and other crowded events.

On disruption, the embassy notes that strikes and demonstrations have become more frequent in recent months. It also says hoax bomb threats targeted civil aviation in numerous European countries in November, disrupting flights. Americans are advised to:

  • monitor local news and check with their airline for disruptions
  • avoid demonstrations and areas with heavy police activity
  • keep a low profile
  • enroll in the State Department’s Smart Traveler Enrollment Program for security updates

The warning comes after a run of disruption on the US side of the Atlantic. A nationwide Federal Aviation Administration ground stop on Friday halted departures for several hours and led to around 10,000 cancellations. The government blamed a communications failure during equipment work. On Monday, United Airlines suffered a short-lived systemwide technology outage. The same day, customers at JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and other major lenders reported problems accessing online banking.

No agency or company has tied any of those US incidents to a cyberattack or a foreign actor. The FAA said its investigation found no sign of an attack, and United and Chase both said their problems were technical rather than cyber-related. Given Trump's wars I'd be sceptical of these glib explanantions. Regardless, the cluster has highlighted how exposed travel and payments are to technical failures at a busy time of year.

The timing matters for travellers. US airlines expect close to 29 million passengers over the Thanksgiving period from November 20 to 30. Europe’s holiday season brings the large markets and crowds the embassy alert focuses on. For now the advice is precautionary, and the alert does not cite a specific threat.
