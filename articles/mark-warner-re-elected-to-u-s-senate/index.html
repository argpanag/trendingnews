Michigan Senate race puts two sharply different LGBTQ records head to head

2026 Midterm Elections

Abdul El-Sayed challenging U.S. Sen. Mike Rogers (R-Mich.)

Poll workers across the country are pulling out the ballots and “I voted” stickers, bracing for one of the most divisive and consequential midterm elections in modern times.

Few states have drawn as much media attention, fiery rhetoric, and last-minute political shake-ups as Michigan.

Tomorrow, Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed will face off against Republican nominee Mike Rogers for the Great Lakes State’s Senate seat.

In a state where LGBTQ adults make up nearly 11 percent of the population, it is important to understand where each candidate stands on issues affecting the community.

El-Sayed, a former public health official and politician, has a record of advocacy and healthcare policy experience related to the LGBTQ community.

He led Detroit’s Ryan White HIV program and partnered with LGBT Detroit to make public health services more open and affirming for LGBTQ residents. Drawing on his public health expertise, he has also committed to restoring federal HIV and PrEP funding gutted by the Trump-Vance administration. He refuses corporate PAC money, has been outspoken in his support for trans rights, and has vowed to be a “lion for LGBTQ+ rights” in the Senate.

Earlier this year, El-Sayed spoke to PrideSource, where he reaffirmed his support for the LGBTQ community, specifically the transgender community, which has faced the brunt of political attacks and hateful rhetoric.

“There’s a reason that if I’m asked, I always say that I support trans rights because I believe in all rights,” El-Sayed said. “I want folks to see their own rights in the plight of a trans person who’s being discriminated against.”

He continued, explaining that if he gets elected to the Senate, he will champion LGBTQ rights wherever he can.

“I want the community to know that I intend to be a lion for rights. When somebody’s coming for you, I intend to stand in the cut to protect you. We have to answer this broader question — do we want to live in the kind of America where we leave some of us behind, or do we want to live in the kind of America where we understand that government of the people, by the people, and for the people means all the people?

“My job as a public servant is to stand up for folks. It’s what motivated a lot of my efforts in Detroit and Wayne County to make sure that our services were deeply accessible and empowering of LGBTQ+ residents — and it’s why I take the idea of protecting all of our rights so seriously.”

Rogers, meanwhile, last served as a public servant in 2015, when he represented Michigan’s 8th Congressional District. Before entering politics, he worked as a special agent for the FBI from 1989 to 1994. He unsuccessfully ran for Congress again in 2024 and is now seeking a seat in the Senate.

Compared to El-Sayed, Rogers has a lengthy anti-LGBTQ record, according to the Human Rights Campaign and GLAAD.

Rogers has called being gay a “lifestyle choice” saying he wouldn’t support his own child if they were gay, has a record of multiple votes against marriage equality, as well as opposition to the repeal of “Don’t Ask, Don’t Tell,” the discriminatory military policy that led to LGBTQ service members being discharged for who they are or who they love.

Rogers also voted against the Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act, which expanded federal hate crime protections to people targeted because of their gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, and disability, alongside existing protections based on race, color, religion, or national origin.

Additionally, he voted against a fully inclusive Violence Against Women Act (VAWA), which sought to ensure survivors received protections and housing regardless of their gender or sexual orientation. He also voted against the Affordable Care Act, which prohibits insurance companies from discriminating against people with pre-existing conditions, including LGBTQ patients.

His record has become a point of contention, which saw an unexpected political shake up that has started to dominate coverage. 

Just yesterday, U.S. Rep. Haley Stevens, a Michigan Democrat who previously ran against El-Sayed for the party’s Senate nomination, withdrew her endorsement of him and instead endorsed Rogers, surprising her team and members of the Democratic Party.

Jeremy Moss, an LGBTQ state lawmaker who is also running for federal office in Michigan that the Blade interviewed earlier this election cycle, condemned Steven’s sudden about face in a statement on Instagram.

“I strongly disagree with Haley Stevens’ decision to endorse Mike Rogers.

Abdul and I have talked about our shared commitment to fighting MAGA extremism, dismantling ICE, and expanding affordable healthcare … But openly cheerleading Mike Rogers is unacceptable. He would be a rubberstamp for Donald Trump, from engaging in voter suppression and election denial, to continuing ICE’s cruel and unlawful operations, to defending disastrous tariffs, to slashing affordable healthcare, and more …

And Rogers, with his abysmal anti-LGBT record, would gleefully confirm Supreme Court justices who will rollback the rights that I have fought hard to protect.”

Despite himself not being a member of the LGBTQ community, HRC has become a vocal supporter of El-Sayed since he emerged as the Democratic nominee.

“I’m proud to have the Human Rights Campaign standing with us in this race,” said El-Sayed. “LGBTQ+ Michiganders deserve leaders who will have their backs when it matters most, not just when it’s politically convenient. That’s the commitment I’ve carried throughout my career in Detroit and Wayne County, and in the United States Senate, I’ll fight to ensure every Michigander can live without discrimination and never have to wonder whether their rights will still be there tomorrow.”

HRC President Kelley Robinson issued a statement today ahead of tomorrow’s election, urging Michigan voters to choose equality over discrimination.

“Mike Rogers voted for a constitutional ban on marriage equality — more than once. He opposed the repeal of Don’t Ask, Don’t Tell. And he’s gone after trans kids. There’s only one pro-equality Senate candidate on the ballot in Michigan: Abdul El-Sayed. And he has HRC’s unequivocal support,” Robinson said in a statement. “We have boots on the ground in Michigan, and we’re seeing this thing through until the end.”

The Washington Blade reached out to Rogers regarding his anti-LGBTQ policy record but did not receive a response by the time of publication.

Ryan Fecteau spoke with Blade about pivotal Senate race

BIDDEFORD, Maine — Maine House Speaker Ryan Fecteau on Wednesday said U.S. Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine) “should not be underestimated.”

“I’m not going to count her out,” the gay Democrat told the Washington Blade during an interview in Biddeford, an old textile mill city near the mouth of the Saco River in York County that is roughly 20 miles south of Portland, Maine’s largest city. “I don’t think anyone should, and I don’t think anyone is because we know that she is a very strong campaigner. She is someone who doesn’t take time off when there is an election. She is out there, cutting the ribbons and announcing this and announcing that. She is someone who is consistently putting in the work to build those relationships and communities.”

Collins, 73, has represented Maine in the U.S. Senate since 1997.

Her Democratic opponent, Troy Jackson, is a former logger from Aroostook County in northern Maine who was president of the state Senate from 2018-2024.

Graham Platner in July suspended his campaign after a former girlfriend accused him of sexually assaulting her. Maine Democrats subsequently nominated Jackson to run against Collins.

“The great thing about what occurred over the summer is we had a process that allowed everyday people to make the decision about who was going to replace Graham on the ballot,” said Fecteau. “Instead of having sort of this anointment of some kind, we had a real process where people were able to put themselves out there. We had a convention. Delegates ran, got nominated by their peers from their individual counties and Troy Jackson had a very strong ground games and he acquired and organized the most delegates and ended up winning.”

“That is the kind of process that I wanted to see,” he added.

Polls indicate the race between Collins and Jackson remains close.

Fecteau during the interview specifically criticized Collins over her decision to vote for U.S. Supreme Court Justice Brett Kavanaugh’s confirmation in 2018.

“[She] claimed over and over and over again that she had this assurance from him that Roe v. wade was settled law,” Fecteau told the Blade. “She put her confidence behind someone who then almost immediately voted to upend this precedent, this settled law, and thus abortion rights.”

Fecteau also cited Collins’s “inability to have a Margaret Chase Smith moment.”

Smith was a Republican who represented Maine in the U.S. Senate from 1949-1973. She famously challenged then-U.S. Sen. Joseph McCarthy (R-Wis.) in her “Declaration of Conscience” speech on the Senate floor on June 1, 1950.

“Margaret Chase Smith is a senator that we hold in high regard here in Maine, a senator who stood up to McCarthyism, against her own party, and held a moral high ground that was above any party allegiance,” Fecteau told the Blade. “What we’ve seen with Sen. Collins is she hasn’t had that moment, and she’s had the opportunity to seize those moments and has chosen this allegiance to Donald Trump.”

Fecteau criticized Collins for her decision to allow President Donald Trump’s “big beautiful bill” to advance in the Senate in 2025, even though she ultimately voted against the tax and spending measure. Fecteau said Maine lost $2 billion that specifically funded health care in the state.

Fecteau also said tariffs have had a significant impact on Maine’s economy.

Maine Public Radio notes Collins last week announced the Trump-Vance administration implemented tariff exemptions on Canadian products on which the state’s agricultural, aquaculture, and logging industries rely.

“He’s using tariffs as a way of giving Sen. Collins some sort of victory as we head towards Election Day,” said Fecteau. “That’s what that was all about.”

“We need to actually acknowledge the fact that Sen. Collins and her colleagues in Congress have given away the power that is supposed to rest in Congress around tariffs, and they’ve given it away to the Executive Branch, and she doesn’t question that,” he added. “She doesn’t do anything to prevent that. Instead, she’s going to go parade the fact that she got the president to change his mind about this, that, and the other thing. Why not take back the power that is supposed to rest in the Congress? How about you do that instead? And so it’s all charade. It’s all smoke and mirrors and that’s what we’ve continued to get out of Sen. Collins since the Trump administration has come into office.”

Collins co-sponsored Respect for Marriage Act

Collins co-sponsored the Respect for Marriage Act that codified marriage rights for same-sex couples in federal law.

Then-President Joe Biden signed it on Dec. 13, 2022. Collins was among the lawmakers who attended the White House ceremony.

Collins, who chairs the Senate Appropriations Committee, has also worked to protect PEPFAR funding.

“She has been in the corner on some key issues while in the U.S. Senate, but I have to maintain the proper perspective here and I know that Troy Jackson will always be supportive of LGBTQ rights,” Fecteau told the Blade. “I know Troy personally. I trust that he’ll be in our corner when we need him.”

Fecteau is a Catholic University alum who grew up in Biddeford.

He represented Maine House District 11 from 2014-2022. Fecteau since 2024 has represented Maine House District 132 in which Biddeford is located.

Fecteau in 2020 became the first openly gay speaker of the Maine House of Representatives. He was once again elected speaker in 2024.

He noted to the Blade that Democrats have a 3-seat majority in the House “that we won by 60 votes two years ago in the three districts that made a difference.”

“We are not deeply blue,” said Fecteau.

Fecteau also noted the race to succeed retiring U.S. Rep. Jared Golden (D-Maine) in the state’s 2nd Congressional District.

Former Gov. Paul LePage, a Republican, is running against Democrat Matt Dunlap.

“We obviously don’t want to send Paul LePage to Washington,” said Fecteau. “He’s got a pretty bad track record from his time as governor; tons of offensive statements.”

President has previously used slur against S.C. Democrat

President Donald Trump referred to Democrat Annie Andrews as “Tranny Annie” while campaigning for Republican Senate candidate Darline Graham at the Rock Hill Sports and Event Center in Rock Hill, S.C, on Thursday.

“If Tranny Annie does not have the judgment to know that sex changes for children should not be allowed, she’s too crazy, too dangerous, and too radical to serve in the United States Senate. And certainly, certainly not from this day,” Trump said while speaking to a crowd in the Palmetto State.

This is not the first time Trump has used the slur against Andrews.

Earlier this month, the president called the candidate a “trannie” in a post on Truth Social.

Andrews, the Democratic nominee in South Carolina, is a cisgender pediatrician who lives in Charleston with her three children. Earlier this month, she told NBC News that she would “work with anyone to put South Carolina first.”

“As a mom and a pediatrician, I’m in this race to fight for my patients, my three kids, and South Carolina,” Andrews said, adding that “lowering costs and making life better for families across our state is going to take all of us working together.”

However, after Trump used the slur against her, Andrews appeared to shift her position on transgender rights, emphasizing her opposition to certain policies and pointing out that, as a physician, she had never performed gender-affirming surgery on minors.

“I want you to hear me loud and clear: I don’t support biological men playing women’s sports, and I don’t support and haven’t ever performed gender surgery for minors,” Andrews said in a political ad.

The Washington Blade reached out to Andrews’s campaign both times Trump used the slur but did not receive a response.
