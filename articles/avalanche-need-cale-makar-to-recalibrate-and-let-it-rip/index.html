Avalanche need Cale Makar to recalibrate and let it rip

TheTools · 08.10.2026 00:50 · source · permalink

CENTENNIAL — Is it too early to jump to conclusions with the Avalanche?

That takes on a whole new meaning when you consider the Avalanche season wouldn’t even be underway under the NHL’s old 82-game schedule.

Instead, the Avalanche are two games in and it’s way too early to jump to any conclusions based on such a small sample size. But here’s one.

The Avalanche need Cale Makar to let it rip on the power play.

Granted, this is based off a bit more than a two-game sample size. Makar’s shot attempt rate has dipped over the past three seasons while the Avalanche have a five-on-four advantage. During the 2023-24 season, he attempted 32.58 shot attempts per 60 minutes while on a five-on-four advantage. The next season, that number dipped down to 27.89 and, last year, it dropped again to 27.64.

Coincidentally, or not, the Avalanche power-play numbers have dipped each of the past three years.

“Ideally, yeah,” Jared Bednar said when asked if he wants Makar to shoot a bit more.

Over that time, teams have started to play Makar a little differently. Bednar acknowledged as much last season. It makes sense. If you’re the opposition, why wouldn’t you want to limit a huge threat like Makar? His shot attempt rates have dropped, and, on top of that, the rate at which his shots are actually getting through to the goaltender has really dropped.

During that 2023-24 season, 13.23 of his shot attempts per 60 at five-on-four were hitting the net. The next season, it dropped to 11.86. Last year, there was a significant drop to 9.61. Opposing teams deserve some credit for that. During the 2025-26 campaign, he had around one more shot attempt per 60 minutes blocked by a penalty killer. If someone on the opposing team is willing to block a shot, you have to tip your cap to them.

Other teams getting in the lane may have led to a bit of an overcorrection from Makar, though. Just over 43% of his shot attempts that did not get blocked just missed the net at five-on-four last year. That’s his highest missed shot percentage at five-on-four since his rookie season and it was about 8% higher than his missed shot percentage from the year before.

This year, there’s just not enough sample size to go off of. In the 9:37 of ice time Makar has gotten at five-on-four through two games, he’s attempted just two shots, with one hitting the net and one getting blocked. You’d like to see Makar shoot more than twice on the power play through two games, but it’s not a large enough sample size to draw conclusions.

The Avalanche need Makar to recalibrate and shoot more. They also need to adjust when teams won’t let him, something they’ve struggled to do over the last year.

“They take Cale away, we got to find something else. They take (Nathan MacKinnon) away, we got to find something else,” Bednar said. “Now, what are our other options? If you’re just going to take guys away, other guys should have more space, so how do we execute around that?

“We’ve talked quite a bit. They’re both aware teams that teams are going to want to take those two guys away because they basically run our power play. We have to have the skill and poise and playmaking ability around that to be able to create dangerous scoring chances.”

Just two games into the season, it looks like the Avalanche will be making their first big power-play change for Wednesday’s game against Winnipeg. Nazem Kadri replaced Gabriel Landeskog on the top unit, a unit that has not scored at five-on-four while allowing two short-handed goals in the season opener. Kadri allows a bit more versatility, as he can move around a bit more than Landeskog, but it could take away from Colorado’s net-front presence.

All options are on the table to “fix” that top unit, but Makar shooting more would certainly help.

Avalanche notes from practice

  • Bednar said he thinks both Zachary L’Heureux and Logan O’Connor will play during his quick two-game road trip for the Avalanche. Whether they each play in both games remains to be seen, as Bednar had previously indicated he may use L’Heureux in one game and O’Connor in another to ease them into the lineup. Either way, the Avalanche look like they will return to the traditional 12-forward, six-defenseman lineup.
  • When asked if he was chomping at the bit to get into the lineup, L’Heureux responded, “(Expletive) yeah,” before apologizing for cursing.
  • Gabriel Landeskog returned to practice after a maintenance day on Sunday.
  • If both L’Heureux and O’Connor play in the same game, that would give the Avalanche 13 healthy forwards. Bednar said it’s no guarantee T.J. Hughes, who sits at minus-3 through two games, would be the odd man out.
