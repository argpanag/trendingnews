Phantom Blade Zero PC In-Game Settings

Phantom Blade Zero is made using Unreal Engine 5 (5.7.4). Visually, the game looks stunning, and there are plenty of options to tune the game as per your PC's hardware.

The game kicks off with a Shader Compilation screen which takes a good amount of time. This is a one-time process.

First up, we have the "Display" menu where you can adjust "Image Calibration" and change the "Display Mode" settings, such as selecting the "Output Display", switching between "Window Mode", setting the resolution, and enabling the frame-rate limit. Here, you will also find a few settings under the "Visuals" bar, which include:

  • V-Sync
  • HDR
  • Motion Blur
  • Sharpness
  • Camera Shake
  • Film Grain Intensity

Moving over to the "Graphics" menu, the first thing you can do is apply a recommended graphics preset. The game will automatically check your hardware and select a preset.

Under that are "Super Resolution" settings. These allow users to pick an SR method, which comes in the form of TSR, NVIDIA DLSS, AMD FSR, and Intel XeSS. You can also pick the Super Resolution mode with the standard Ultra Performance, Performance, Balanced, Quality, Ultra Quality, and DLAA/FSR AA/XeSS AA modes available. Dynamic Resolution Quality is also available but will be disabled when using DLSS/XeSS/FSR technologies.

The game also features integrated Frame Generation support with AMD going up to 2x, Intel going up to 4x, and NVIDIA going up to 6x modes. Users can also enable Dynamic MFG mode through the NVIDIA app, and a faster frame-generation model is already available since the last update.

Phantom Blade Zero is one of the latest titles to feature Path Tracing technology. The technology isn't enabled by default, and users can select between Native Rendering, Ray Tracing, and Path Tracing. NVIDIA RTX GPUs further support Ray Reconstruction technology based on DLSS 4.5 that offers better image quality when running RT/PT modes.

The full list of graphical options in the "Details" bar is listed below:

  • Shadow Quality
  • Reflection Quality
  • Post-Processing Quality
  • Lighting Quality
  • Effects Quality
  • Material Quality
  • Ambient Occlusion Quality
  • Depth of Field Quality
  • Motion Blur Quality
  • Anti-Aliasing
  • View Distance
  • Character Texture Quality
  • Character Model Quality
  • High-Quality Hair
  • Environment Texture Quality
  • Environment Model Quality
  • Environment Detail Quality

Our test setup featured an Intel Core i9-13900K running on an MSI MEG Z790 ACE motherboard with 32 GB of DDR5-7600 (CL36) memory. The drivers used are 581.42 for NVIDIA, 25.10.1 for AMD, & 32.0.101.8136 for Intel.

Phantom Blade Zero PC Performance Benchmarks

Before we start the performance benchmarks, we first want to see how the game scales across its various quality presets.

The Low preset looks very basic, with most of the meshes and models missing while the textures are very low quality. Moving to Medium, we see the same level of detail with a slight uptick in the texture quality and a few more objects in the world, such as vegetation, but the lighting remains the same between Low and Medium presets. That's why the performance between both modes is also very similar, with just a 3-5 FPS difference.

Switching to High, we notice the first big upgrade in visuals as the lighting looks much better, textures look crisper, and meshes/objects are filled in. We also see a 14% drop in performance. The Very High Preset further elevates the visual fidelity, and we see another 15% hit in overall performance. And lastly, we have the Ultra preset, which looks slightly better and yields a 6-7% drop in performance.

Turning on Ray Tracing, you will see a 20-22% drop in performance over the Native "Ultra" preset, while enabling Path Tracing has a 35-40% hit on the FPS versus Ray Tracing, and 50% over native "Ultra" presets.

This is at 4K with a 5090, so actual mileage may vary depending on the resolution and graphics card used.

Coming to performance, we first want to share the native results. At 4K, there are hardly any GPUs besides the RTX 5090 that can handle 60+ FPS; all other cards run at sub-60 FPS, which makes Phantom Blade Zero a very graphics-intensive title to run.

At 1440p, 60 FPS can be achieved on cards such as the 5070 Ti, RX 9070 XT, and above. The rest of the cards will have to rely on lower quality or upscaling + frame-gen to achieve over 60 FPS.

On entry-level and mainstream GPUs, Phantom Blade Zero requires lowering the quality and upscaling technologies for 60+ FPS.

Upscaling and Frame-Gen options are available in the game. Most enthusiast-grade cards won't require frame generation, as upscaling will easily deliver over 100 FPS, but if you want to go the extra route for that 240Hz 4K experience, then MFG is a must. If you plan on using Path Tracing or Ray Tracing, having frame-generation enabled is a must.

Phantom Blade Zero PC Impressions

Phantom Blade Zero is the latest Unreal Engine 5 title to hit PC, and as expected, it offers some great visuals, but to achieve those visuals, you also need powerful hardware. On PC, the game is backed by a fantastic set of tuning options, and the developer has amped things up by leveraging NVIDIA's RTX and DLSS technologies.

In our tests, we found that running the game natively on Very High and Ultra settings is very hard even on existing GPUs without using upscaling or frame generation. High and Very High settings do provide a good balance of performance+visuals, but if you want anything above that, upscaling and frame-gen are necessary.

Talking about the RT and PT side of things, the visuals are further lifted thanks to Ray Tracing, but Path Tracing, as expected, takes things to the next level. The world in Phantom Blade Zero is very dense and rich; in combat, you will see various particle effects, plus certain sections that include rainy and thunderstorm weather patterns look absolutely stunning when shadows and lighting are path traced. DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction also adds that finer touch, boosting the path-traced scenes further.

Overall, S-GAME has really outdone themselves by releasing a gorgeous, fast-paced, and superb game in the form of Phantom Blade Zero, and NVIDIA's RTX technologies really bring out the best in this game.
