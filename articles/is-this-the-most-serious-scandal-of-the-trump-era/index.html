Is This the Most Serious Scandal of the Trump Era?

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:20 · source · permalink

Jensen Huang may have resigned from the AI-chip behemoth he founded, but the investigation into what he knew and when he knew it—about his mistress Alice Huang, and about where her company was delivering Nvidia’s most advanced chips—that’s just beginning.

Congressional investigators will soon be asking whether Jensen Huang helped Alice Huang, an alleged Chinese spy, to circumvent U.S. export controls to supply powerful chips to China’s AI companies. Did he have access to—and pass on—U.S. classified information? As a member of the President’s Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, Jensen Huang was eligible for a security clearance and may have been briefed on sensitive intelligence. And given Alice Huang’s alleged ties to the Ministry of State Security (MSS), was she then feeding information to the Chinese government?

We’ll return to Alice Huang and the MSS in a minute.

But first we mustn’t overlook the companion narrative that only adds to the scandal. That is a story of brazen regulatory capture: how Jensen Huang gained personal, persistent access to the president of the United States, the secretary of commerce, and other key officials—and how he used that access to advocate policies that have put America’s foremost adversary, the People’s Republic of China, within reach of becoming the dominant power in artificial intelligence.

“China is going to win the AI race,” Jensen Huang told the Financial Times last November. He blamed American “cynicism” about AI and contrasted it with Beijing’s lavish subsidies for the technology. Jensen Huang released a statement later the same day saying, “It’s vital America wins” the AI race.

You wouldn’t know it from his lobbying efforts.
