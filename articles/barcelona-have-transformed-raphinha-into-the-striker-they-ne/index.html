Barcelona have transformed Raphinha into the striker they needed

TheTools · 12.09.2026 22:07 · source · permalink

Champions

League

Raphinha's double against Feyenoord took his tally to eight goals from five matches this term (Josep Lago/ AFP via Getty Images)

Just a month ago, there were some worried voices around Barcelona. They didn’t have a striker.

Robert Lewandowski had been allowed to leave on the expiry of his contract at the end of June. Ferran Torres was sold to Paris Saint-Germain in mid-August.

Julian Alvarez was the player Barca wanted — but Atletico Madrid were determined not to sell to them.

“There are no centre-forwards,” said one concerned Barcelona board member back during the team’s pre-season training camp at St George’s Park (speaking anonymously as they did not have permission to comment).

Raphinha has provided the answer to those anxieties. The Brazilian 29-year-old has been recast as a No 9 by Hansi Flick this season, moving inside from his previous spot on the left flank. He is a winger dressed up as a centre-forward, in a costume that seems tailor-made for him.

His brace against Feyenoord in Wednesday night’s 5-1 Champions League opening victory brought his tally to eight goals from this season’s first five matches. The first was a thing of beauty.

Latching on to Lamine Yamal’s pass in the third minute, he entered the box and, with a single touch, left two opponents in his wake with a brilliant feint. Time seemed to stand still at the Camp Nou as the Dutch side’s defenders looked back towards him. Raphinha calmly slotted home.

His second was great in a different way; a perfectly timed run to get in behind, an emphatic finish from a player who just looks so full of confidence.

Everything he does makes sense right now. He presses, he makes dangerous movements, he simplifies Barca’s game and has been prolific with his chances. He doesn’t look forced despite being out of his natural habitat. Maybe this is where he was always meant to be.

The No 9 position isn’t one Raphinha had played to any significant extent at any of his previous clubs. After coming back from his break following the World Cup, Flick told him in training that he wanted to try him out there.

He was not daunted by the challenge. It wasn’t the first time he had faced such a scenario.

Raphinha arrived in Barcelona from Leeds United in July 2022 as a signing for the right wing, his established position.

Back then, Barca’s first choice in the role was Ousmane Dembele. Xavi, the manager at the time, was a big fan of the Frenchman, and Raphinha had to wait. After Dembele left for PSG the following summer, Xavi turned to Raphinha but would often substitute him in games for Torres, which led to several incidents where Raphinha would storm off to the bench and have to be calmed down by his team-mates. Then came the full emergence of Lamine Yamal.

By January 2024, Yamal had fully forced himself into a starting spot on the right, his talent impossible to ignore. When Raphinha returned from a hamstring injury, a 16-year-old had overtaken him.

Raphinha spoke to Xavi and made himself available to the team: if Yamal was to have a key role, he could play wherever he was needed. Xavi moved him to the left, and Flick kept him there when taking over for 2024-25.

He scored 34 goals and provided 26 assists that term as Barcelona won a domestic treble and reached the Champions League semi-finals. He went from being a substitute to being voted the fifth-best player in the world in the 2025 Ballon d’Or. He was one of the team’s captains and one of the most beloved players in the dressing room — and among fans.

Last season he had a tougher time. He suffered five injuries and was sidelined for a total of 125 days with several hamstring problems. But his dedication and attitude were never in question. Nor was his importance. For this term, Flick appointed him first captain with the blessing of his team-mates.

On Tuesday, the 30-strong shortlist of Ballon d’Or nominees for 2026 was released, and Raphinha was not amongst them. Nor was Pedri. Both omissions are striking.

“He (Raphinha) is far too good not to be on that list, but we can’t change it,” Flick said at his press conference after the Feyenoord win.

Raphinha arrived at Barcelona in Dembele’s shadow, eventually lost the right wing to Yamal and then found a different way to become indispensable. Dembele, meanwhile, took his own route away from the wing, one that led him to that Ballon d’Or prize last year.

Different careers, different turns, different reasons for moving inside — yet somehow they have ended up in the same position. On October 20, in Paris, the two wingers-turned-centre-forwards will meet again, this time leading their teams from the middle.

That is Barca’s next real test in the Champions League. One that will provide a true benchmark of what they, and Raphinha, are capable of.
