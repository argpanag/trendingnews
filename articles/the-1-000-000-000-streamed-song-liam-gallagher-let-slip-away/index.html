The 1,000,000,000 streamed song Liam Gallagher let slip away

TheTools · 10.10.2026 12:45 · source · permalink

Liam Gallagher wasn’t the kind to mince words about what he liked half the time.

He was willing to say what he wanted and let the chips fall where they may, and when it came to music that he didn’t like, it didn’t matter whether you were an industry legend or someone who was gaining momentum on the charts. Everyone was fair game when it came to him, and while he could be more than a little bit cutthroat at times, he was the first to say that some of the best songs in British history were up there for a reason.

There isn’t a day that goes by that Liam doesn’t give his due credit to John Lennon for turning him into a rock and roll star, and from the moment that he heard Ian Brown sing when he was a kid, he knew that was what he wanted to do for the rest of his life. But when you have one of the great songwriting geniuses in the country right by your side, you sometimes don’t need to worry about whether or not the songs have the magic.

When Noel came up with a tune like ‘Live Forever’, there was no way that anyone else was going to be able to take that away from him. Sure, Oasis might get dogged as Beatles rip-offs every time they play, but there’s no way that anyone would have been able to pull some of Noel’s melodies out of thin air just by listening to Abbey Road and Revolver a couple of hundred times.

It all came down to the sense of craftsmanship, and while Liam was willing to play the rock star alongside his brother, he never lost the respect he had for the songs. Even during the moments where their petty arguing got in the way of everything, Liam was still the one shellshocked that his brother could come up with something as beautiful as ‘The Masterplan’ and manage to put the thing out as a B-side.

That’s the work of someone who clearly had a never-ending stream of classics, but during What’s the Story Morning Glory, Noel did put his foot down about singing a couple of tunes. He didn’t fashion himself as a frontman by any stretch, but when ‘The Chief’ talked with his brother about what songs he wanted to have on the album, Lia was given the choice to either sing ‘Wonderwall’ or ‘Don’t Look Back in Anger’.

Sure, Liam did manage to walk away with one of the biggest singalongs in 1990s history, but Noel remembered the reasoning behind his brother choosing that song over ‘Don’t Look Back in Anger’, saying, “He hated ‘Wonderwall’ and wanted to sing ‘Don’t Look Back In Anger’, but then someone from Creation said to him, ‘That’s going to be a worldwide hit.’ Then he said, ‘I quite like it actually.’” Given how well their voices mesh on each song, though, Liam didn’t need to lose sleep over it.

Noel might have been the more competent singer when it comes to singing a tune like ‘Anger’, but Liam was always meant to sing a tune like ‘Wonderwall’. He does have a lot more grit in his delivery than what you’d expect out of a ballad, but some of the best moments in his career have come from when he has the power to take that thick rock and roll cadence in his voice and actually manage to get sentimental with it.

Not everyone is able to do that, and while ‘Don’t Look Back in Anger’ remains the second-most played Oasis song and has gained upwards of a billion streams, Liam knew that he had walked away with a stunner. It became an albatross around his neck for a while, but it’s hard to really complain when millions of people are singing your song back to you every single time you perform it.
