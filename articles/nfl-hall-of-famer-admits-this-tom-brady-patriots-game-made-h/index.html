Joe Thomas hated Tom Brady.

So when Brady and the New England Patriots came to Cleveland during the 2016 season, Thomas wanted nothing more than to beat the future Hall of Fame quarterback. 

Instead, the Patriots handed the Browns a 33-13 loss in Week 5, with Brady throwing for 406 yards and three touchdowns in his return from a four-game suspension.

For Thomas, the loss became a breaking point.

"I f—ing hated Tom Brady, as everybody did in the league. I hated the Patriots. I just wanted to smash him so bad," Thomas said on "Bussin' With The Boys."

Thomas recalled sitting in the car after the game with ice on both elbows and knees while his wife drove. He began crying as he looked out the window. 

His wife eventually asked if he was OK and whether he had played a bad game. 

"Then the tears really came because I said, 'I played a perfect game, and we lost by 30,'" Thomas said. 

While Thomas' memory of the final score was off, the feeling remained the same. The Browns had lost by 20 points, dropping to 0-5, while Brady immediately looked like himself in his return. 

"It really sunk in at that moment that you don't control the situation anymore," Thomas said. "The feeling of loss of control is really hard."

Thomas had spent years convincing himself the Browns could be a Super Bowl team because he believed that mindset was necessary to get the most out of himself. 

But by his 10th season, he said the team's rebuilding direction had become too obvious to ignore. 

"I'd been kind of fooling myself for awhile, and we are not going to win no matter what I did," Thomas said.

Thomas eventually met with the Browns' team psychologist to work through what he described as nearly a decade of building toward that realization. 

"We're not that team and we really don't have a chance," Thomas said. "And I don't impact this game the way I'd hoped."
