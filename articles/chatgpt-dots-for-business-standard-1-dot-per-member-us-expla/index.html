ChatGPT Dots for Business Standard: 1 Dot per Member (US) | explainx.ai Blog

Part of OpenAI and ChatGPT

OpenAI is rolling out Dots to eligible ChatGPT Business Standard members in the US: one personal dot each, with usage limits. Who gets it, what it does, open questions.

OpenAI is rolling out Dots to eligible ChatGPT Business Standard members in the United States. The ChatGPT Business release notes for November 19, 2026 say that once you have access, "you can create one personal dot in your Business workspace and ask it to help with ongoing projects, like preparing a weekly update." The note adds one line that matters: "Usage limits apply."

That is a plan-perk change for paying users. Until now, Dots on Business went to Premium seats. This post covers what the note says, how it fits the Dots rollout so far, what is not stated, and what a workspace admin should check.

Quick reference

What is a dot?

A dot is OpenAI's always-on agent inside ChatGPT. Our launch coverage of Dots at DevDay describes it as a persistent agent with a dedicated cloud computer, which you chat with and which keeps working on tasks between conversations. The Business note uses the plain version: a personal assistant for ongoing projects.

Two details from the November 19 note are worth underlining.

  • "Personal." The dot belongs to one member. It is not a shared team agent. For shared recurring work, the Business notes describe a separate feature, Team tasks, which lets teammates share scheduled jobs that run with approved company tools.
  • "Ongoing projects." The example, preparing a weekly update, is a recurring job with the same inputs each week. That fits an agent that keeps context.

How the Dots rollout has moved

The path from launch to Business Standard is short.

Business Standard is the lower of the two paid Business seat types. The Help Center says Premium seats offer more included usage for Work and Codex than Standard seats, so a dot on a Standard seat will likely have less room to run. That is the likely reason for the explicit "usage limits apply."

Standard versus Premium: what the release notes show

The Business release notes are the best public record of how OpenAI splits the two seat types. Three entries show the pattern.

  • Voice limits. In the September 9 note, Business Standard seats get up to 3 hours with GPT-Live-1 per day, and Business Premium seats get up to 15 hours. Extra Voice usage costs 1.25 credits per minute, down from 5.
  • Work and Codex allowance. The October 29 note says Pro 100 and Pro 500 plans now include twice their previous Work and Codex allowance, and that the Pro 200 reduction announced on September 29 was canceled. Business Premium is named in the heading, so the heavy agent usage sits on Premium and Pro.
  • Models. The November 17 note brings GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol to Work and Codex in Business workspaces, with access depending on seat and workspace permissions.

OpenAI's ChatGPT Business models and limits page is where per-seat figures are published. The takeaway is that Standard seats get the same features at smaller allowances. A personal dot on a Standard seat is likely to follow the same rule, but OpenAI has not published the numbers. Treat any estimate you read elsewhere as a guess until the Help Center says otherwise.

What else changed in Business this week

Dots were not the only Business update on November 19. The same release notes list SSO exemptions for external workspace members. Workspace owners can now choose "Allow login without SSO" or "Follow workspace SSO policy" for external members in the Admin Console. Existing external members of workspaces that required SSO receive an exemption automatically, and newly added external members are not exempt by default.

That matters if you plan to give a dot to a contractor. An external member who is exempt from SSO is still bound by workspace permissions, but your identity provider no longer gates their login. Review who is exempt before you let their dot connect to company apps.

For admins, the sensible order is: review the new SSO setting, review connected apps, then decide who should try a dot.

What the note does not say

Several things a buyer would want are missing from the release note.

  1. Numbers. There is no figure for how much a Standard dot can do per day or month.
  2. Pricing. The note does not mention an added cost. The Help Center describes a credit pool that eligible activity can draw on after included usage, subject to workspace settings and spending limits. Whether Dots draw on it for Standard is not stated in the note.
  3. A date outside the US. The rollout is US only.
  4. Admin controls. The note does not list admin toggles. Our earlier coverage notes that Enterprise and Edu admins had to enable a beta. Business admins should look in the Admin Console and Help Center for Dots settings.
  5. Eligibility. "Eligible" is not defined in the note.

None of this is a criticism. Release notes are short. But if you are deciding whether to move people between seat types, wait for the Dots article in the Help Center or test with a few seats.

Safety and data questions for admins

A dot works with a cloud computer, connected apps and messages, so it needs the same scrutiny you would give any agent.

  • Data use. Our Dots launch post notes that Business, Enterprise and Edu data are not used to improve models by default. Check the current terms in the Help Center for your workspace.
  • Email and actions. We covered a safety issue around a dot sending email without approval. Check how approvals work for your connected apps.
  • Connected accounts. Per OpenAI's Business notes on app connections, the connected provider account determines which information and actions a task can access. Give a dot only the scopes it needs.
  • HTTPS and certificates. There is a separate claimed-versus-verified post on the dot cloud computer and a CA certificate, useful if you run security review.

How Dots compare with other assistants

Dots are one of several proactive agent products. Our comparison of Claude Duet, OpenAI Dots, Meta Muse and Grok Bot covers the design differences. The Business angle is new: OpenAI is moving Dots from premium consumer and top-tier business seats toward the standard work seat. Apple is also hiring for an agent of its own, as we noted in the Apple agent job listing.

What to do if you run a Business workspace

  1. Check eligibility. Confirm you are on Business Standard and in the United States. Ask members to look for Dots in ChatGPT.
  2. Pick one recurring job. A weekly update is the example OpenAI gives. Choose a task where the inputs are stable.
  3. Measure usage. Because the limits are undisclosed, run a pilot with two or three people and note when they hit a ceiling.
  4. Compare with Team tasks. If the job should be shared, Team tasks may be a better fit than a personal dot.
  5. Review connected apps. Limit scopes and make sure approvals are on for email and sends.
  6. Read the Help Center article. OpenAI's article on managing dots in workspaces covers access, personal dots, computer capabilities, connected apps and messaging.

What people are asking

Is Dots available on ChatGPT Plus or Free?

Our earlier coverage described Free and Plus as not part of the first wave. The November 19 note concerns Business Standard only. Check the Help Center for current plan availability.

Why is it US only?

OpenAI does not say. Launch rollouts for agent features have often started in the US.

Will my existing Premium dot change?

The note does not mention any change for Premium seats.

Can a Standard member share their dot?

The note describes a personal dot. For shared work, use Team tasks and shared plugins.

Where will the official answer on limits appear?

In the ChatGPT Business release notes and the Help Center article on managing dots. Check those pages before you promise a team a specific allowance.
