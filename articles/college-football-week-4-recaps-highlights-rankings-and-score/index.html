How Week 4’s action can impact the rankings

This week was LOADED with ranked vs. ranked matchups! Here's how it could shake up Sunday's AP Poll rankings.

  • No. 1 Texas should get more first-place votes after picking up a road ranked win over No. 14 Tennessee. The winning UT got 58 first-place votes last week so don't expect the Longhorns to fall from the top spot.
  • There will be a new team in the top five after No. 4 Mississippi lost. Only 52 points separated No. 5 Indiana, No. 6 Miami and No. 7 Ohio State. Typically, each team would just move up one spot. However, Indiana's six-point win over Northwestern could cause some voters to drop the defending national champions after a win. We've seen ranked teams drop after wins every week this season, so it wouldn't be surprising.
  • That begs the question, "Where will the Rebels land." That's where things get interesting because the No. 4 Rebels didn't just lose, the got blown out. One would think there's a floor thanks to a head-to-head win over LSU last week, but the Tigers picked up a  ranked win of their own this week.
  • No. 10 LSU might make head-to-head look like a joke this week. The Tigers defeated No. 23 Texas A&M in a rout, 35-6. LSU does have one loss to Ole Miss, which as mentioned before, got blown out 52-28. LSU could move in front of a team it lost to. The Bayou Bengals will almost certainly jump in front of No. 9 BYU after the Cougars had a bye
  • No. 12 Southern California, No. 13 Penn State and No. 14 Tennessee all lost this week. That means the edge of the top-10 will feature some new faces. 
  • No. 16 Louisville could be the first two-loss team to be ranked this year. The Cardinals lost by just three points to Wake Forest, but have a head-to-head win over a ranked SMU team. It's other loss comes to a ranked Mississippi squad. Louisville has a strong strength of schedule and resume that could keep it from dropping out of the rankings.
  • No. 17 Iowa and No. 20 Oregon should fill in some of those spots in the top 15 after picking up ranked wins.
  • No. 21 Florida should have the largest jump of any team. One could argue the Gators should enter the top 10 after a 24-point win over the No. 4 team in the country. UF is undefeated after all. When looking at common opponents, No. 10 LSU lost to an opponent Florida beat. Ranking the Gators will be a hard decision for voters this week.
  • No. 24 Mississippi State will climb. The Bulldogs entered the rankings for the first time last week. They responded with a hard-fought 31-24 win over No. 19 Missouri. Mississippi State could climb into the top 15 with over 10 spots of movement. Meanwhile, Missouri should hang on near the bottom five teams after a ranked loss.
  • How many new entrants could we have this week? I think we could see three. It's almost a guarantee that No. 23 Texas A&M and No. 18 Michigan fall out of the rankings. The Aggies have lost back-to-back games and dropped 14 spots after their last loss. The Wolverines still have the Western Michigan debacle on its resume in addition to their first official loss. Voters don't seem to be high enough on either team to keep then ranked. I mentioned Louisville earlier, but I'd give the Cardinals more than a 50% chance of falling out of the rankings.
  • Kentucky, Oklahoma State and Boise State are the top-three teams that could enter the rankings this week.

I'm sure most of us heard this stat this morning: For the first time since Dec. 1, 2023, there are six AP ranked matchups on the same day. That stat wields quite lofty expectations for a Saturday in September. It's a stat that almost feels like a jinx, like when an announcer talks about a kicker who hasn't missed.

Surely college football can't be that good today. Wrong. It was. Let's talk about it. 

Today’s top headlines:

  • No. 1 Texas survived No. 14 Tennessee 20-17 in Knoxville to start the day. 
  • No. 13 Penn State and No. 16 Louisville were both upset by unranked teams (Wisconsin and Wake Forest, respectively.)

Night games deliver in loaded Week 4 Saturday

Midnight is knocking here on the east coast and the night slate is winding down. Here’s how things shook out:

No. 10 LSU cruises past No. 23 Texas A&M 

No. 23 Texas A&M was no match for No. 10 LSU in Death Valley. The Tigers steamrolled the Aggies 35-6, despite Trey'Dez Green’s absence and Sam Leavitt’s sidelining injury.

Despite Leavitt’s pain, he managed to go 17-of-23 for 312 yards with two touchdowns and an interception. 

Backup QB Landen Clark took over for Leavitt in the second half after the Tigers held a commanding lead. The FCS Elon transfer went 0-5 through the air, but added 41 yards and two scores on the ground. 

LSU’s defense had solo tackles from 16 different defenders, holding Texas A&M to just two field goals.

No. 24 Mississippi State survives No. 19 Missouri 

No. 24 Mississippi State outlasted No. 19 Missouri in Starkville, winning 31-24 in the most exciting game of the ranked-vs-ranked buffet we had today. 

Before we dive into the game action, let's take a quick look at the historical significance of this win for Mississippi State.

The Bulldogs had not beaten a ranked team since 2014. The Bulldogs now have two SEC wins in two weeks. They had two SEC wins over the previous three seasons combined. And for maybe the craziest stat, Mississippi State is 4-0 for consecutive seasons for the first time since 1944-45. 

But this historic win wasn't without drama as Missouri had a Hail Mary attempt to force OT that came up just short—rather surprising based on how the first three weeks of the season delivered jaw-dropping finishes. 

And of course, there’s great numbers on the field: 

  • Mississippi State quarterback 'Super' Kamario Taylor has surpassed 1,000 yards for 14 touchdowns on the season so far. Taylor went 27-of-38 for 312 yards and three touchdowns tonight.
    • Taylor has thrown three touchdowns in four straight games — a program record.
  • Missouri’s Cayden Lee recorded 135 yards on nine receptions for two touchdowns
  • The Bulldogs' Fluff Bothwell had 25 carries for 122 yards and a touchdown.
  • Taylor has thrown three touchdowns in four straight games — a program record.

But, there’s oen stat from tonight that stands out above the rest — the Bulldog faithful set a world record, ringing 41,819 cowbells simultaneously. Time to party in Starkville.

🚨 Top-5 FCS UPSET: UNI shocks No. 4 Illinois State

Unranked vs. ranked upsets were all across college football today, including in the FCS. 

The University of Northern Iowa stunned Illinois State, ranked No. 3 in the Stats Perform poll and No. 4 in the AFCA coaches poll, 28-21 in home territory.

Headed into tonight’s game, the Redbirds were undefeated while the Panthers were just 1-2. UNI, however, controlled the game, never surrendering its lead after a leveled game in the first quarter. 

Panthers QB Jaxon Dailey went 13-for-17 for 217 yards and three total touchdowns, including a 28-yard rush into the end zone.

Down goes No. 13 Penn State! 🚨

We’ve got ourselves another upset!

After overcoming a 17-0 deficit at the half, an unranked Wisconsin team rallied to upset No. 13 Penn State, 24-20.

The Badgers' last win over Penn State was in 2011, losing their last six matchups against the Nittany Lions. Better yet, they haven't won in Happy Valley since 2003. 

Before tonight, Wisconsin was 1-13 against ranked opponents over the last five seasons. 

Despite throwing three interceptions, Wisconsin signal caller Colton Joseph showed up when the Badgers needed it most.

Joseph connected with Jacob Harris on a short pass, resulting in a 72-yard touchdown to take the lead with under two minutes to go in regulation. Harris finished the night with 105 yards on five receptions. 

This is Wisconsin's largest comeback win since 2006, when they trailed Illinois by 18.

Looking ahead at tonight’s lineup 👀🌙

We certainly have a night ahead of us.

Three of today’s six ranked matchups are slated for tonight’s window, so let’s talk about them.

No. 10 LSU vs. No. 23 Texas A&M

In a day chock-full of top-SEC clashes, this game will be a great way to tie it all together. 

With both teams coming off pretty significant losses, this game means a lot.

The Aggies, who lost 31-21 to an unranked Kentucky team last weekend, will head to Death Valley to face off against LSU. 

The Tigers fell 32-24 to Lane Kiffin’s ex-program, Ole Miss, in Oxford in Week 3’s spotlight game.

LSU needs quarterback Sam Leavitt to step up to the plate when it comes to moving the football through the air, while Texas A&M needs Marcel Reed to be a weapon on the ground.

Regardless, it should be a good one, and the stakes could not be higher with both teams needing to avoid a second loss in four weeks to keep their CFP hopes alive and well. 

No. 12 Southern Cal vs. No. 20 Oregon

Oregon has won the past four games over USC, taking eight out of the last 10 matchups. 

Both teams have struggled against unranked teams, with Oregon losing 39-31 to Oklahoma State and USC narrowly escaping Rutgers, 42-35, last week.

A second loss would put the Ducks well behind the sticks with preseason CFP aspirations, while the Trojans can pick up a signature resume builder heading into the middle of the season. 

No. 19 Missouri at No. 24 Mississippi State

In an effort to break the world record for most cowbells rung simultaneously, this game in Starkville is bound to get loud.

Each team is undefeated, but Mississippi State’s offense stands out.

Averaging 544.7 yards per game, the Bulldogs are second in the FBS in offensive yardage, backed by quarterback 'Super' Kamario Taylor, who has 832 yards for 11 touchdowns so far.

Missouri has lost their last five road games against AP-ranked teams.

What this 52-28 win over No. 4 Ole Miss means for Florida

What an afternoon in the Swamp.

No. 21 Florida steamrolled No. 4 Ole Miss, 52-28 in Gainesville, improving to 4-0 on the season. 

For the last few years, Florida has had a tough ride. The quarterback room was rarely stable, coaching pressure never let up and their SEC schedule was simply unforgiving.

The last time the Gators began their season with four wins was in 2019 under Dan Mullen, who was fired in 2021 after the team unraveled. 

Billy Napier was brought on to rebuild, but fell short, posting a 22-23 record in his four seasons with Florida. 

But this season’s turnaround has been one Gators’ fans have wished for on birthdays candles and loose eyelashes. 

In his first year of coaching the Gators, John Sumrall came to Gainesville to “win championships,” rebuilding a Florida team that only won two conference games last season.

We are underway with our four midday games, and they are certainly not to be underestimated.

No. 2 Georgia vs. Oklahoma

For the second time ever, Oklahoma will take on Georgia. The first and only time these teams met was to ring in the new year on Jan. 1, 2017, when the Bulldogs barely snuck by the Sooners in a 58-48, double-overtime thriller in the College Football Playoff semifinals.

Today marks the first time Oklahoma has played a game in Athens and its first matchup against Georgia as an SEC team.

No. 4 Ole Miss vs. No. 21 Florida

Ole Miss is coming off a stellar win over LSU in the Lane Kiffin return. They now turn their focus to the Florida Gators. The last time these two teams faced off, Ole Miss left with a 34-24 victory.

One name you won't see on the Ole Miss offense is No. 5 running back Kewan Lacy. Lacy has been a key part of this undefeated Ole Miss team, but the Rebels will be in The Swamp without him as they look to hand the Gators their first loss.

No. 15 Utah vs. Iowa State

Utah heads to Iowa State hoping to keep its perfect record going after three dominant performances this season. After defeating Idaho 66-15 in their season opener, the Utes have continued to stay solid.

Iowa State hopes to repeat its last matchup against Utah. The Cyclones won 31-28 in a close game in 2024 after Utah missed a late field-goal attempt.

No. 17 Iowa vs. No. 18 Michigan

This undefeated Big Ten matchup that will be a must-see, as the Iowa Hawkeyes head to the Big House to take on Michigan.

The Wolverines, coming off a big win over Oklahoma earlier in the season, have kept the momentum going. The Hawkeyes are also coming off a dominant 55-0 win over Northern Iowa.

It's been almost three years since the Wolverines and Hawkeyes have met. Michigan came out on top, winning 26-0 before going on to win the College Football Playoff National Championship Game.
