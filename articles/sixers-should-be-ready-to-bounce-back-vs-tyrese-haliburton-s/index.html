Sixers should be ready to bounce back vs. Tyrese Haliburton’s Pacers

TheTools · 28.10.2026 13:17 · source · permalink
  • Sixers Game Previews

Following their first loss of the season, the Sixers should have a good chance to return to winning against a depleted Pacers team.

Update: Tyrese Haliburton is now out for tonight’s game. Shams Charania has reported that Haliburton has a left hamstring strain and the Pacers are working out a timeline for his return.

It’s clearly been an exciting start overall to the 2026-27 season for the Sixers, but we got our first taste of a loss for this team on Sunday. On the second night of a back-to-back, with Joel Embiid out and a host of injuries to the backup center rotation, the Sixers lost to the Pistons, 106-93.

The Sixers shot just 7-of-25 from three, made only 14 of their 23 free throw attempts, Jaylen Brown struggled (as did most of the team) and shot 5-of-20, and they gave up 15 offensive rebounds. From the lacking center rotation in Embiid’s absence to the rebounding issues, it was a look at where the Sixers could use some improvement.

On Wednesday, after getting an extra day of rest, the Sixers will be on the road with a chance to bounce back against the 2-2 Pacers.

Having Embiid back on Wednesday is huge. The already thin center depth was obvious against Detroit, and that was even with Jalen Duren out. The rest of the Sixers’ injured bigs are still included on the injury report, though. Dominick Barlow, Tacko Fall and Jabari Walker all remain out.

The Pacers are banged up themselves. Johnny Furphy (ACL surgery), T.J. McConnell (right hamstring strain), Ben Sheppard (left meniscus tear), and Ivica Zubac (left ankle sprain) are all out. Zubac’s absence in particular will make life far easier for the Sixers’ smaller lineups. Andrew Nembhard (neck soreness) and newly returned star Tyrese Haliburton (left hamstring soreness) are both questionable. Obi Toppin (left hand sprain) is probable.

While the Sixers’ frontcourt remains shorthanded, we’ve seen a fair dose of LeBron at the 5. Which, despite not being something he or the team want to rely on using too much, has been effective in spells. The physicality, IQ, still versatile defense when he’s locked in, and offensive skill that needs no introduction makes LeBron a unique smaller option.

Apart from getting healthier at center, rebounding has to improve, too. As Nick Nurse emphasized after Sunday’s loss, it’s not just about one position: it’s a team effort.

“All five guys got to rebound. I just look at the numbers there and the defensive rebounding, just across the board, no matter position, just aren’t good enough,” Nurse said to reporters after the game. “So, everybody’s got to chip in with a few more.”

Let’s see if they can take advantage of Zubac being out and up their rebounding on Wednesday.

The Pacers aren’t off to a bad start this year. They haven’t faced tough opponents yet and are coming off a 110-102 loss to the Bucks on Monday, but did at least take care of business with wins against the Pelicans and Nets.

First and foremost, as a basketball fan, it’s a joy to see Haliburton back. After missing all last season with an Achilles injury, he’s here to lead the Pacers again.

Considering it’s been 486 days since he last played a game, Haliburton looks like he’s barely missed a beat. He’s already putting up 21.8 points, 9.5 assists to only 2.3 turnovers, 4.5 rebounds and 1.3 steals per game with an excellent true shooting percentage of 63.4. He started with a bang, dropping 23 points and 12 assists and delivering in the clutch to win Indiana’s season opener.

Maxey and VJ Edgecombe have both been playing impressive defense to open the season, and have well and truly played their part in the Sixers’ ranking fifth in defensive rating so far. Watching how they handle Haliburton (if he plays) on the ball, keep his drives in check, ideally reduce his breathing room to fire passes all over the floor, and contest his creation at the three-point line will be the key matchup to follow in this contest.

We’ll also get to watch former Sixer Kelly Oubre Jr. go up against Philly again for the first time since leaving. After doing so much for the Sixers with his high motor, rangy defense and athletic scoring, he’s stepped straight into a solid role with the Pacers, including two starts. He just hasn’t hit his usual stride on offense yet, averaging 7.5 points on only 38.7/18.8/50.0 shooting splits through his first four games in 22.5 minutes a night.

The next big matchup for the Sixers to handle in order to fully take control in this game is Pascal Siakam. With his range, speed and ability as a driver, he’ll be a good physical test for LeBron and Dean Wade. With how well the Sixers’ forwards have played defensively so far, though, and how spry Embiid has looked protecting the paint, that bodes well for them containing Siakam and ideally providing sharp interior rotations and rim protection to Haliburton, Siakam and Oubre in check enough downhill.

Apart from having Embiid’s scoring back, his size and defense will be a welcome presence against the Pacers’ bigs, led by 7-foot-1 starting center Jay Huff. Huff is a weak rebounder (averaging just 6.7 per 36 minutes for his career) which helps the Sixers, but he still can’t be overlooked as a rim protector and finisher with his size and 7-foot-5 wingspan.

Overall, there should be some fun matchups in this one, especially between LeBron vs. Siakam, and the Sixers’ guards against Haliburton, if he can play. If Philly can pair that with better rebounding and center play thanks to Embiid’s return, and ideally some hotter three-point shooting (they’re missing plenty of open looks so far, ranking 22nd as a team at 31.7 percent from three), this is a contest they should be winning. It’s a good opportunity to bounce back before heading into a tougher matchup in Friday’s rematch against the Knicks.
