    FT: Wales 2-3 Portugal

    That's it from Cardiff City Stadium, where Wales went toe to toe with the heavyweights of Portugal tonight but ended up with nothing.

    Wales caused plenty of problems for the Portuguese, with David Brooks and Sorba Thomas striking in the first half to give the home side a 2-1 lead after Bruno Fernandes' opener.

    But Portugal quickly drew level through Pedro Neto before substitute Carlos Forbs scored a second-half winner, with Wales unable to conjure an equaliser despite Neto's 74th-minute red card.

    Portugal win this Nations League group, while Wales - as we knew they would - finish bottom having lost five of their six games.

    But all is not lost. A relegation play-off awaits in March - with Wales set to discover their opponents on Friday - and we will of course be there to take you through it.

    For now, it's goodbye.

Portugal, beaten by Denmark on Saturday, have not lost back-to-back matches since 2014

Wales fell just short of ending their Nations League group stage with a marquee win as reigning champions Portugal edged a five-goal thriller to triumph in Cardiff.

Wales were already guaranteed to finish bottom of Group A4 – albeit with a relegation play-off in March to keep their place in the top tier – but there was no hint of this being a dead rubber as Craig Bellamy's side dazzled in a blistering encounter.

In their second match since the international retirement of the great Cristiano Ronaldo, Portugal led through Bruno Fernandes' fine curling effort after Josh Sheehan had given the ball away to Joao Neves.

Wales equalised two minutes later as the impressive Rubin Colwill set up David Brooks for a smart first-time finish.

Bold and enterprising, the home side showed no fear as they played their way out of trouble and attacked from all directions to rattle their star-studded opponents.

Wales created a glut of chances before taking a deserved first-half lead as Sorba Thomas scampered clear to finish from Brooks' through ball, only for Portugal to level before the break as Pedro Neto volleyed in from Francisco Trincao's cross.

Although Wales continued to press and had opportunities to regain the lead, Portugal pounced on another defensive lapse – this time a loose header from Ben Cabango – as substitute Carlos Forbs fired into the bottom corner.

The visitors were soon down to 10 men after Chelsea's Neto picked up two yellow cards in two minutes to see red, but Wales could not take advantage, despite their valiant efforts.

As group winners, Portugal will advance to the Nations League quarter-finals in March, with the draw to come on Friday.

On the same day, Wales will discover which Group B runners-up they will face in their two-leg relegation play-off.

Wales analysis: Colwill, Wilson and Brooks star for exciting hosts

Although Wales knew this result would have no effect on their Nations League top-flight survival or Euro 2028 qualifying prospects, there was still the opportunity to claim a rare victory against one of Europe's heavyweights.

It had been three years since Wales had beaten a team ranked inside the world's top 10, though last month's win over World Cup quarter-finalists Norway showed they can be competitive at this level.

Bellamy's side emphasised that point with a performance brimming with attacking ambition.

At times, they were overly ambitious, such as when Sheehan attempted to turn Neves as he received a pass, only for the Paris Saint-Germain midfielder to pick his pocket and set up fellow Ballon d'Or nominee Fernandes to fire Portugal in front.

And while there were other moments when the visitors got through the home defence a little too easily, Wales were a joy to watch whenever they poured forward.

Harry Wilson and Brooks combined beautifully, with the former coming close to finishing some sensational team moves, while midfielder Colwill caught the eye in and out of possession on his long-awaited first start under Bellamy, earning a standing ovation when he was substituted.

Wales will discover on Friday whether they will face Slovenia, Hungary, Republic of Ireland or Bosnia-Herzegovina – who knocked them out of World Cup qualifying earlier this year – in their relegation play-off in March.

If Bellamy's side can replicate the attacking verve of this display, while tightening up in defence, they should be confident of beating any of those.

Portugal analysis: Shaken again but quality tells

Ronaldo's exit had cast a shadow over Portugal's campaign, with the 41-year-old walking out on the squad in September after being told he would not start a Nations League match against Denmark, resulting in a fine and suspension.

Despite his legendary status, Ronaldo's place in the national team had been under scrutiny for some time and, without him, Portugal cantered to the top of Group A4.

However, in the first fixture after his international retirement was announced earlier this month, they were humbled 4-1 at home by Denmark.

Having looked like runaway leaders until that point, Portugal travelled to Cardiff needing at least a draw to be mathematically sure of winning the group.

That would have been the least of their expectations given the riches at manager Jorge Jesus' disposal, and Wales' performance – coupled with Denmark taking an early 2-0 lead over Norway – will have had the visitors nervously looking over their shoulders.

Portugal looked uncertain defensively once again, conceding a total of 22 shots, and might have wondered if they were facing successive defeats for the first time since 2014.

But they weathered the storm of Welsh pressure – and torrential rain – to win, top the group and march on in their bid to retain their Nations League crown.
