Van Vs. Pantoja Odds, Full Fight Preview & Prediction

TheTools · 19.09.2026 16:15 · source · permalink
  • UFC 331

Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) Flyweight champions Joshua Van vs. Alexandre Pantoja will rematch TONIGHT (Sat., Sept. 19, 2026) inside Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles, Calif., for UFC 331.

There is only one takeaway from the snake-bitten first fight, which ended almost instantly in a freak accident (see it again here): this fight is going to be incredible. In the 20 seconds prior to Pantoja’s unfortunate arm injury, the two Flyweights wasted absolutely zero time in trading, engaging right away in a full-power firefight that was sure to entertain for however long it lasted.

Technically, it did, but well … you know.

Almost one year later, Van has risen into his championship status. His five-round war with Tatsuro Taira proved that Van is here to stay, opening a lot of eyes to his overall skill and potential. Pantoja, conversely, has spent all this time healing and waiting for his moment to prove that he’s still the best in the world.

Let’s take a closer look at the betting odds and strategic keys for each athlete:

How Van Wins

Van has quickly developed into one of the sport’s best boxers. He’s so comfortable in the pocket, able to initiate, counter and build combinations fluidly. He does his best work when pressuring his opponents and countering their attempts to exchange, as his eyes in the pocket are excellent.

The champion also pushes a remarkable pace.

At first glance, this is an obvious enough striker vs. grappler matchup. Van wants to sprawl-and-brawl his way to victory against the jiu-jitsu ace, a stylistic clash so common it’s a pillar of the sport. In this case, however, there’s a more specific goal for Van than merely stopping the takedown: he has to keep Pantoja working.

A major part of Pantoja’s late-career success is that he’s able to manage his output so well. “The Cannibal” knows how to explode in a violent flurry of strikes and takedowns, then find his way to a dominant position and rest. He doesn’t have to keep up with younger opposition, not when he can pin them down for long periods of time and catch his breath.

Against Taira, Van did strong work in using his own jiu-jitsu, even in bad positions. He was able to use butterfly guard to stand or hip escape away from being mounted. That same skill will be tested tonight, because Pantoja will get him to the canvas at some point.

The question is whether Van can keep the scramble going and continue to put pressure on Pantoja’s gas tank.

How Pantoja Wins

Nobody really expected Pantoja to become an all-time great Flyweight champion. An The Ultimate Fighter (TUF) also-ran, the Brazilian established himself as a Top 10 contender, but ran into the wall several times as he approached title contention. At 31 years of age, he suddenly turned a corner and ripped off an incredible win streak that saw him capture the Flyweight crown and score four defenses.

In addition to being the best backpacker in the sport, Pantoja is a crafty kickboxer who is apparently made of steel. Remember the Kai Asakura head kick that just knocked out Aoriqileng? Pantoja walked right through the same shot without flinching.

In this matchup, Pantoja’s ability to time his shots will be tested. He’s going to have a difficult time converting his takedowns into successful control if he dives straight into Van’s defenses, as the champion keeps his base beneath him well. He’s going to have to trade with Van and time his entrance perfectly, otherwise it’s going to take large amounts of energy to ground his opponent.

In the Taira fight, Van did well to avoid giving up his back in scrambles. Pantoja has to be more active than the Japanese fighter in landing elbows from top position. Ground strikes will force Van’s hand and expose the back take.

From that position, Pantoja is a finishing threat to anyone, including the current champion.

Van vs. Pantoja 2 Prediction

MMA moves fast.

Less than one year ago, I predicted Pantoja would win via submission, arguing that volume punching was not the solution to Pantoja’s brick chin and elite jiu-jitsu. In short, I believed that Van wouldn’t get enough time on the feet to wear down the veteran champion.

Ten months later, I think the opposite. Van’s defensive grappling in the Taira fight demonstrated a level of experience and comfort on the floor that I wouldn’t have expected. He’s a quick study on the canvas in addition to the boxing, which means he’ll be able to return to the feet and make Pantoja pay for his age and output. Steve Erceg nearly upset Pantoja in 2024 by continually forcing the Brazilian to work and being more refined on his feet … and Van now has the skills to build on that success.

I still wouldn’t be shocked by a Pantoja submission inside the first three rounds — he’s an incredible fighter and champion. Coming off an injury at 36 against an opponent who is rapidly improving, however, the odds are against him.

Prediction: Van via decision
