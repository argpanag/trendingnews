US Open 2026 – men’s final: What to expect? - Roland-Garros 2026 - The official site

TheTools · 13.09.2026 18:45 · source · permalink

Ben Shelton could end a 23-year wait for an American men’s singles champion in NYC.

All the talk, hustle and bustle in the city that never sleeps has built to quite the crescendo at the 2026 US Open.

Six years ago, top seed Alexander Zverev suffered major final heartbreak on Arthur Ashe Stadium, having led Dominic Thiem by two sets to love. The German wants to re-write that script.

American men’s tennis has suffered plenty of Grand Slam heartbreak this century too. So much so that it’s been an astonishing 8407 days wait for an American man to win a major singles title since Andy Roddick triumphed at Flushing Meadows in 2003. Ben Shelton, who was approaching his 1st birthday during the 2003 US Open, wants to re-write that script.

A new name will feature on the Roll of Honour inside Arthur Ashe Stadium on Sunday evening. Who will it be?

Alexander Zverev (No.1) vs Ben Shelton (No.8)

💪 The context / The current form

With the ultra-dominant duo of world No.1 Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz in and out of the picture, Alexander Zverev has more than seized his opportunity to rule the men’s tennis landscape.

The 29-year-old has been supremely consistent for many seasons, 2026 represents his seventh year of his career tallying over 50 victories. He’s on 52 right now and is in the Grand Slam groove to make it 53.

Just three months ago, Zverev erased devastation from three major final defeats to capture his maiden Grand Slam at Roland-Garros. Now he’s found the formula, Zverev is hoping to become just the fourth man in the Open Era to win their first two Grand Slams in a single season.

Over to Shelton, the home charge has been building up for this moment with steam since his breakout semi-final run in New York City in 2023. The rocket fuel serve helped collect silverware and his game has developed throughout, gaining second week journeys at all three of the other majors.

Retaining his Masters 1000 Montreal title last month portrayed a player in prime position for a major move. The 23-year wait for an American man to lift a Grand Slam has been distorted by greatness. Roger Federer halted Roddick in four finals during that time span and the Swiss maestro also defeated Andre Agassi in the 2005 US Open silverware showdown. Most recently, Taylor Fritz was denied glory at Arthur Ashe Stadium two years ago by Jannik Sinner.

It’s been close, ever so close. Over to Shelton to secure a special slice of history.

🏆 Their 2026 tournament

Nine and a half hours – that’s what it took a lacklustre Zverev to escape the opening two rounds.

Since then, the Roland-Garros champion has risen through his gears to storm past four challengers.

The German has demonstrated the tennis and major knowhow that has propelled him into three successive Grand Slam finals. He’s going to take some stopping.

1st round: victory against Lorenzo Sonego 6-4, 3-6, 6-7(7), 7-5, 6-4 (4hrs 53mins)

2nd round: victory against Quentin Halys 6-4, 4-6, 7-6(3), 6-7(3), 6-3 (4hrs 33mins)

3rd round: victory against Alejandro Tabilo 6-2, 7-6(2), 6-2 (2hrs 26mins)

Round of 16: victory against Luciano Darderi 6-2, 6-2, 7-6(3) (2hrs 31mins)

Quarter-finals: victory against Botic van de Zandschulp 6-2, 7-5, 6-1 (2hrs 17mins)

Semi-finals: victory against Karen Khachanov 6-3, 7-6(7), 7-6(6) (2hrs 58mins)

The way Shelton navigated the opening few rounds was impressive. However, the way the fiercely focused and extraordinarily acrobatic American has overcome defending champion Carlos Alcaraz, then his countryman Frances Tiafoe back-to-back, proves this is a different version of Shelton.

The 23-year-old has spoken about his commitment to the sport, “the pursuit and hunger to get better,” and it’s worked a treat. There has been the usual muscular shotmaking, the serve has been scorching, but there has also been grit, tactical tweaks, resilience, versatility, it’s been one hell of a combination to watch on his electric yet methodical path to a maiden major final.

2nd round: victory against Hubbert Hurkacz 6-3, 5-7, 7-6(3), 7-5 (3hrs 24mins)

3rd round: victory against Denis Shapovalov 7-6(3), 6-7(5), 6-3, 6-4 (3hrs 22mins)

Round of 16: victory against Stefanos Tsitsipas 6-2, 6-3, 6-4 (1hr 52mins)

Quarter-finals: victory against Carlos Alcaraz 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6[10-7] (4hrs 28mins)

Semi-finals: victory against Frances Tiafoe 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 (3hrs 17mins)

🆚 Face-to-face

Pure and simple, with his Grand Slam experience and success, Zverev is the standout favourite.

Boosting that argument, the 29-year-old holds a 5-0 record over the world No.9. Their last two duels have been on hard courts in 2025. Zverev soared to a 6-2, 6-2 Cincinnati Masters scoreboard and then edged to a 6-3, 7-6(6) victory at the season-ending ATP Finals in November.

Both of these players have monster serves, that’s not news. However, it’s surprising Zverev has clattered more aces than Shelton 87-71 this fortnight in the Big Apple. The top seed has also held 101 of 110 service games with a 92 per cent success rate, narrowly pipping Shelton’s 88 per cent.

The American powerhouse’s return has evolved in recent campaigns, attacking from deep, more variety, but it’s going to need to be near perfect on Sunday night to have a chance.
