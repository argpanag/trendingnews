Australia's Middle Percy Island seeks volunteer caretakers

TheTools · 13.09.2026 00:43 · source · permalink

The group managing Middle Percy Island in the Australian state of Queensland is looking for volunteer caretakers willing to live there for at least a year. Accommodation on the island is free, but candidates must provide for their own needs, ABC News Australia reports.

The tropical island, covering nearly 20 square kilometres, is located 125 kilometres southeast of the city of Mackay. Two people currently live there, but the number of residents could increase fivefold.

Most of Middle Percy has national park status. The remaining 316 hectares, which are part of Percy Island Conservation Park, are managed by Percy Island Conservation Group Inc., whose members are volunteer island residents.

Volunteer responsibilities

The group's director, Catherine Radcliffe, said the organization is looking for volunteers with relevant skills. They are expected to maintain infrastructure, tend gardens and an apiary, control the population of grazing animals, and uphold the tradition of hospitality for crews of vessels visiting the island.

The island also produces goods made from hides and beeswax, which are sold to visitors. The proceeds are used for the maintenance of Middle Percy.

Self-sufficiency and isolation

One of the residents, Martin Papai, said he found a way of life on Middle Percy in a treehouse 15 years ago. According to him, self-sufficient living requires skills in cooking, carpentry, animal care, and gardening. Residents largely eat what they can grow or catch on the island.

Ralph Ender, who moved to the island at the beginning of the year, listed pumping rainwater, watering the garden, caring for poultry, and bottling honey among his daily activities. Papai also mentioned hunting goats and working with beehives.

There is no regular transport connection between Middle Percy and the mainland. However, from around May to October, the island is frequently visited by yachtsmen. Ender said that after moving there, he spent about four months without meeting other people because Papai was off the island at the time. According to him, remote living can be emotionally challenging and requires the ability to cope with such experiences independently.
