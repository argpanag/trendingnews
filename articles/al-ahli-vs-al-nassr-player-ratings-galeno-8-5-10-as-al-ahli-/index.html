Summary

  • Cristiano Ronaldo put Al Nassr ahead in the 14th minute with a right-footed strike, but missed a penalty shortly after.
  • Galeno equalized for Al Ahli just before the break, and Ivan Toney completed the comeback in the 71st minute with a calm penalty.
  • A Roger Ibañez header in the 75th minute put the game beyond the visitors' reach.

We watched Al Ahli vs Al Nassr expecting fireworks, and honestly, this Saudi Pro League clash delivered. Al Nassr exploded out of the blocks, riding an early wave driven by Cristiano Ronaldo and his relentless shooting.

His goal for 0-1, assisted by Samú Costa after Al Ahli made a horrendous mistake in passing out from the back, felt like a statement that the visitors were in full control.

However, the game twisted sharply. The missed penalty from one of the greatest footballers of all time and another shot off the post completely changed Al Nassr's body language.

Confidence seemed to drain from Al Nassr, while Al Ahli grew braver, especially just before half-time when Wenderson Galeno struck to level it 1-1. The second half became a fascinating mental battle as much as a tactical one.

What stood out to us was Al Ahli’s aggression and belief after the break. Ivan Toney coolly converted from the spot for 2-1, and then Roger Ibañez’s towering header for 3-1 felt like a hammer blow. Fans noticed Al Nassr’s defenders arguing and gesturing in frustration, while Al Ahli’s midfield compacted intelligently and steamrolled past their opposition.

Overall, this Saudi Pro League match turned into a showcase of Al Ahli’s resilience and ruthlessness inside the box. Check out the Al Ahli vs Al Nassr Player Ratings below.

Al Nassr Player Ratings vs Al Ahli

Here are the Al Nassr Player Ratings from the Al Ahli vs Al Nassr Player Ratings.

Goalkeeper

Bento - 5.5: From our perspective, Bento never looked fully comfortable. He conceded three and made only one notable save, which is simply not enough at this level. His distribution was mixed, with several long balls going astray and inviting pressure. We noticed hesitancy on crosses and set pieces, especially around the equaliser phase. Although the defending in front of him was chaotic, he did not impose himself as a commanding presence.

Defenders

Mohamed Simakan - 5.5: Simakan’s night felt tense from early on. He produced seven clearances and won his aerial duel, but his yellow card and frequent fouls showed a defender under stress. We noticed him constantly shouting at teammates, trying to reorganise a back line that kept losing shape. His passing was reasonably clean, yet he never really stepped out to break lines. Overall, he battled hard but could not prevent Al Ahli’s momentum or the late collapse.

Nawaf Boushal - 6.5: Boushal was one of the few Al Nassr players trying to inject positivity. We liked his energy down the flank and those ten passes into the final third. Defensively, he chipped in with clearances and interceptions, plus a perfect tackling record. However, we also saw him caught high a few times, leaving space behind for Galeno to attack. His output was decent, but he lacked a decisive contribution in either box.

Iñigo Martínez - 6: Martínez used the ball calmly, completing 21 of 26 passes and hitting useful long balls. From our perspective, though, his yellow card summed up a slightly late, reactive performance. He won all his duels but didn’t dominate physically, especially when Al Ahli piled on crosses. We noticed him dropping deeper and deeper, which invited more pressure. Not disastrous, yet far from the commanding display Al Nassr needed.

Awad Aman - 6: Aman mixed tidy passing with some rash moments. His yellow card and a couple of heavy challenges showed frustration as Al Ahli turned the screw. We liked his 93% passing accuracy and how he occasionally stepped into midfield. Still, he lost several duels and failed to stop Toney’s clever movement around the box. From our perspective, he worked hard but lacked composure in the key phases.

Midfielders

Abdullah Al-Hamdan - 6.5: Al-Hamdan’s performance was busy and complicated. He racked up five successful tackles and battled in 17 duels, showing real work-rate. We noticed him driving forward to shoot several times, though his finishing was inconsistent. The yellow card and penalty conceded were big turning points, shifting the match towards Al Ahli. From our perspective, he tried to do too much at once and lost control in crucial moments.

Samú Costa - 7: Costa’s assist for Ronaldo’s opener was a clever, well-weighted pass and really impressed us. He worked hard off the ball, making tackles, recoveries, and keeping the midfield ticking. His foul count and duel losses showed he was under pressure, but he never hid from the contest. We noticed him dropping deep to help build play when others panicked. Overall, he was one of Al Nassr’s better performers despite the team’s collapse.

Abdullah Al-Khaibari - 6: Al-Khaibari kept things simple before being substituted. His passing was secure, with 18 accurate from 20, and he rarely lost the ball. From our perspective, he lacked vertical impact and rarely threatened between the lines. When the game became more physical, he seemed bypassed and struggled to assert himself. The substitution for Coman felt inevitable once Al Nassr needed more dynamism.

Ângelo - 5: This was a frustrating night for Ângelo. He hit the post early and missed more chances, which clearly affected his confidence. We saw his body language drop after repeated wayward shots and heavy touches. Losing the ball 11 times and winning only one duel hurt Al Nassr’s attacks. He showed flashes of dribbling quality, but overall his decision-making was poor and wasteful.

João Félix - 6.5: Félix tried to orchestrate moves, drifting between lines and creating two chances. His passing was sharp, with 88% accuracy and one big chance created. However, he also surrendered possession 13 times, often while attempting risky dribbles. We noticed moments where he seemed frustrated with teammates’ movement and rushed shots. From our perspective, he was inventive but lacked the killer final ball on the night.

Forwards

Cristiano Ronaldo - 6.5: Ronaldo’s evening was a rollercoaster. He scored a precise opener at 0-1 and looked sharp initially. Then the saved penalty and another shot off the post visibly rattled him. We saw his gestures of frustration, arms thrown up, and more isolated positioning. He still produced five shots and one key pass, yet missed two big chances. From our perspective, he was dangerous but ultimately wasteful, and that shaped the match.

Substitutes

Kingsley Coman - 6: Coman entered needing to change the tempo but struggled under Al Ahli’s compact block. We noticed a few positive runs, yet he rarely found real space out wide. His link play was neat without being decisive or penetrative. With Al Nassr chasing the game, he looked slightly isolated and short of support. Overall, he added energy but not the cutting edge the team desperately required.

Manager

A. Postecoglou - 5.5: From our perspective, Postecoglou got the initial plan right, pressing high and exploiting Ronaldo’s movement. However, he seemed slow to react once the momentum flipped after the missed penalty. The midfield lost control, yet there was no strong adjustment to protect the back line. We expected earlier changes to stabilise things, but they arrived too late. His side faded badly in the final thirty minutes and looked mentally unprepared for Al Ahli’s surge.

Al Ahli Player Ratings vs Al Nassr

Have a look at Al Ahli Player Ratings from the Al Ahli vs Al Nassr Player Ratings.

Goalkeeper

Edouard Mendy - 7: Mendy conceded once, but he felt calm and authoritative throughout. We noticed his positioning on Ronaldo’s later shots was excellent, narrowing angles well. He made two important saves that kept Al Ahli in control after 1-1. His long passing helped launch counters, especially towards Toney and Galeno. As captain, he projected confidence and settled his defenders during nervy spells.

Defenders

Roger Ibañez - 8: For us, Ibañez was arguably Al Ahli’s best defender and one of the highest rated players. His decisive header for 3-1 completely killed Al Nassr’s hopes and showed huge desire. Defensively, he stacked up tackles, interceptions, and eight ball recoveries. We loved how he stepped out aggressively yet kept his timing clean. He also passed crisply from the back, helping Al Ahli play out under pressure. Overall, an excellent all-round performance at both ends of the pitch.

Merih Demiral - 7.5: Demiral had a dramatic evening, with a goal first disallowed by VAR for a foul. We noticed his reaction, a mix of anger and determination, which seemed to fuel him. He kept attacking set pieces, forcing saves and winning aerial battles. At the back, he cleared danger and won most duels, showing typical physical dominance. His presence gave Al Ahli real steel against Ronaldo’s movement and crosses.

Saeed Baattia - 5.5: Baattia had some difficult moments, including a penalty conceded and an error leading to a shot. His passing was okay, but he never looked fully comfortable defending wide spaces. We noticed he sometimes mistimed challenges, allowing crosses or shots under little pressure. He worked and tackled, yet his overall defensive impact felt shaky. From our perspective, he was one of Al Ahli’s weaker performers despite the win.

Saad Balobaid - 6.5: Balobaid contributed solidly before being replaced. His crossing was accurate, and he created a chance with a good delivery. Defensively, he battled well in duels and picked up a yellow card in a committed display. We noticed his willingness to overlap and stretch Al Nassr’s defensive line. Not spectacular, but he played a useful role in building pressure down the flank.

Midfielders

Valentin Andy Franck Atangana Edoa - 6: Atangana Edoa kept the ball efficiently, completing 27 of 28 passes. He also produced two interceptions and several recoveries, reading danger intelligently. However, his error leading to a goal was a major negative moment. We saw his shoulders drop after that mistake, though he gradually recovered composure. Overall, he was tidy but blemished by that costly lapse.

Francisco Trincão - 6.5: Trincão’s left side work-rate was impressive. He tracked back, made tackles, and chipped in with a couple of accurate crosses. Going forward, he had a blocked shot and one key pass, linking well with Galeno. We noticed he chose sensible passes instead of forcing low-percentage dribbles. From our perspective, he quietly helped Al Ahli control transitions without stealing the spotlight.

Eduard Spertsyan - 7: Spertsyan’s yellow card early on showed he was intensely involved from the start. His right-footed shot being blocked in the second minute set the tone for his attacking intent. He produced four key passes and several dangerous crosses, constantly testing Al Nassr’s defence. Despite losing the ball at times, we liked his bravery on the ball. He played a big part in sustaining pressure, especially around the equaliser.

Galeno - 8.5: Galeno was, in our eyes, the man of the match and highest rated player. His goal for 1-1 just before half-time completely changed the mood in the stadium. He followed that with an assist for Ibañez’s 3-1 header, showcasing superb timing and delivery. We noticed his relentless running, smart duels, and ability to attack the back post. Even when he collected a yellow card, his aggression remained controlled and purposeful. Overall, he drove Al Ahli’s comeback with quality and sheer belief.

Ziyad Al-Johani - 6.5: Al-Johani worked tirelessly in the middle, winning five of nine duels. His tackling and blocking helped screen the defence against Félix and Ângelo. We saw him constantly communicating, pointing to spaces and urging teammates to stay compact. On the ball, he was simple but effective, rarely forcing risky passes. From our perspective, he offered vital balance without making headline-grabbing plays.

Forwards

Ivan Toney - 8: Toney’s display oozed confidence and intelligence. His assist for Galeno’s equaliser was sharp and well-weighted. Later, his cool penalty for 2-1 showed his strong mentality under pressure. We noticed his clever movement, pulling centre-backs around and winning fouls to relieve pressure. Though he missed one earlier shot, he never looked rattled. Overall, he was a huge reason Al Ahli turned the match around.

Substitutes

Zakaria Al Hawsaw - 6: Al Hawsaw came on and did a decent stabilising job. We noticed his main focus was defensive shape rather than adventurous runs. He helped close spaces on the flank and tracked runners diligently. On the ball, he kept passing safe, avoiding risky turnovers. His impact was quiet but useful in protecting the lead late on.

Manager

M. Pusic - 7.5: From our perspective, Pusic managed the emotional swings of the game brilliantly. Even after the early 0-1 setback, Al Ahli kept their structure and belief. His use of wide overloads with Galeno and Trincão slowly stretched Al Nassr’s defence. We appreciated how his side pressed selectively, not recklessly, especially after going ahead. Overall, his game plan and in-game adjustments deserved a lot of credit for the 3-1 win.
