ScotRail adds 178 carriages and extra trains this weekend for two huge events

TheTools · 03.09.2026 17:40 · source · permalink

ScotRail has announced a major boost to rail services to support two of Ayrshire’s biggest events.

Extra trains, express services, and 178 additional carriages will be running on Saturday, September 5, to help passengers reach the International Ayr Show and the Largs Viking Festival.

The special timetable will see three trains per hour between Glasgow Central and Ayr, helping to spread demand and offer more flexibility before and after the events.

Phil Campbell, ScotRail’s customer operations director, said: "We know just how popular the International Ayr Show is, and with the Largs Viking Festival taking place at the same time, we’re expecting a busy day for the railway across Ayrshire.

"That’s why we’re doing even more this year, with three trains an hour between Glasgow and Ayr, dedicated express services, additional shuttle trains, and 178 extra carriages compared with a normal Saturday."

An hourly express service between Glasgow Central and Ayr will only call at Paisley Gilmour Street, Johnstone, and Kilwinning, while other trains will stop at more stations across Ayrshire.

Additional shuttle trains will run between Kilwinning and Ayr, serving Irvine, Barassie, Troon, Prestwick, and Newton-on-Ayr, to boost capacity for event-goers.

Campbell said: "Our plan is designed to provide more seats on board before the events and, importantly, more options to get people moving again afterwards when thousands of customers will be making their way home at the same time."

Passengers should note that services between Largs and Glasgow Central will only run as far as Kilwinning on the day.

Travellers will need to change at Kilwinning for onward connections.

Engineering works in the Kilmarnock area will also affect some routes.

Trains between Kilmarnock and Girvan or Stranraer will run only between Ayr and Girvan or Stranraer.

Rail replacement buses will operate between Kilmarnock and Ayr, with extra vehicles to meet higher demand.

A queuing system will be in place at Glasgow Central before the event, and at Ayr station afterwards, to manage the large crowds.

ScotRail is advising passengers to allow extra time for their journeys and to follow staff instructions on the day.

Campbell said: "We’re asking everyone travelling to plan ahead, check their journey before setting off, and make sure they have a valid ticket before boarding.

"Queuing systems will be in place at Glasgow Central before the event and at Ayr afterwards, so customers should allow some extra time and follow the advice of our staff, who’ll be there to help keep everyone moving safely and smoothly."

Passengers are also reminded that a valid ticket is required before boarding any service.
