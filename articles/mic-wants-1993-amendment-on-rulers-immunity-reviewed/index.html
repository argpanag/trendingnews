MIC wants 1993 amendment on rulers’ immunity reviewed

TheTools · 28.11.2026 05:38 · source · permalink

Party president S Vigneswaran questions whether consent was obtained for the constitutional changes.

MIC president S Vigneswaran, who made the same call in 2020, said Articles 38(4) and 159(5) of the Federal Constitution require the conference’s consent for laws affecting the rulers’ privileges and amendments to certain constitutional provisions.

However, he said Hansard records showed that the then Dewan Rakyat Speaker informed the House on Jan 18, 1993, that he had received a letter from the Keeper of the Rulers’ Seal stating that the Conference of Rulers had rejected the bill.

Objections to passing it without the Conference of Rulers’s consent were raised again the next day, he said, but he said the amendment process went ahead.

“I believe this matter deserves serious attention. We are not talking about returning absolute power to the rulers.

“We are talking about respecting the original consent enshrined in the Federal Constitution,” he said when opening the party’s 80th general assembly in Johor.

Vigneswaran stressed that MIC was not seeking to restore absolute power to the rulers but to uphold the original constitutional agreement.

“The Federal Constitution must remain greater than the government of the day. No majority is above the constitution, and no constitutional safeguard should be treated as optional,” he said.

The 1993 amendment removed the rulers’ personal immunity from civil and criminal proceedings and established a special court to hear cases involving them.

The first bill was passed by the Dewan Rakyat in January 1993, but the Conference of Rulers withheld its consent. A revised bill was tabled on March 8 and passed the following day. The amendments took effect on March 30, 1993.

Constitutional law scholar Shad Saleem Faruqi previously questioned whether the Conference of Ruler’s consent was obtained for the final version of the amendments, saying the issue raises serious constitutional questions.
