XRP Traders Brace for Warsh's First Rate Call as Fed Meeting Nears End — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 28.10.2026 18:29 · source · permalink

Crypto traders holding XRP are counting down the hours to the Federal Reserve's policy announcement on Wednesday, with the central bank's two-day meeting in Washington set to conclude and Chair Kevin Warsh scheduled to face the press shortly after the decision lands.

The consensus among market participants is that the Federal Open Market Committee will lift its benchmark rate by a quarter percentage point, a move that analyst and content creator Austin Hilton says is already reflected in the price of both equities and digital assets.

"I think that the market, and I'm talking about the stock market and the crypto market, is expecting that 0.25% basis rate increase," Hilton said in a video published ahead of the meeting. "Everybody will pretty much agree with that."

If the rate move is a foregone conclusion, the real event, in Hilton's reading, is what comes afterward. The Fed under Warsh has stopped issuing traditional forward guidance, which means each public statement carries more weight than it once did and is open to wider interpretation.

The chair, who served on the Board of Governors from 2006 to 2011 and took the top job in 2026, has moved away from telegraphing policy intentions. Hilton argues that this shift turns the post-decision press conference, expected between 2:00 and 2:30 p.m. Eastern time, into the main catalyst for risk assets.

A hawkish Warsh, signaling further tightening, would be read as bearish and could weigh on Bitcoin and XRP alike, according to Hilton. A softer, dovish tone would offer more support to riskier holdings, though he considers that the less likely path given current conditions. He has also cautioned that the buildup may prove anticlimactic, saying the outcome "could be nothing at the end of the day."

The macro backdrop is unusually awkward for policymakers. Inflation has been climbing, with recent consumer and producer price readings both coming in hotter than forecast, while growth appears to be losing momentum. Hilton has described the combination as "a dangerous setup for stagflation."

Energy costs are adding to the squeeze. The US-Iran conflict has pushed gasoline and diesel prices higher, feeding through into the cost of goods and complicating the inflation picture further.

Hilton's assessment of the Fed's options is blunt. "The only tool the Federal Reserve has to use in their arsenal is the lowering or the raising of that base interest rate," he said, a constraint that magnifies the significance of every policy move and every accompanying remark.

Yields are a particular concern. Hilton notes that a bullish tone from the chair could push bond yields higher, potentially drawing capital out of crypto and into government debt offering guaranteed returns of roughly 5.7%.

He also points to strains beneath the surface of the economy: a housing market that continues to struggle, borrowing costs that remain elevated and companies trimming payrolls, all of which raise questions about labor market stability.

For XRP specifically, the setup has been deteriorating. In his earlier video, Hilton flagged that the token was down 0.26% while the wider market was off 0.2%, describing conditions as a waiting game ahead of the Fed. He has spent weeks framing this meeting as a make-or-break moment for the asset.

His advice to holders is to watch the whole press conference rather than just the headline rate number. "They say a lot, but they don't say a lot," he said of central bank communication, adding that "inflation is rising, and the economy is weakening, and the Fed has very little room for error."

For crypto investors, the practical takeaway is that direction in the near term will be set by tone rather than by the quarter-point move itself. With positioning already crowded around the expected hike, the scope for a sharp move in either direction is elevated, and XRP, as one of the more rate-sensitive large-cap tokens, sits squarely in the path of whatever Warsh says.
