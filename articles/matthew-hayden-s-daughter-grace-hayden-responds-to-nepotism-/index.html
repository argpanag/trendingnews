Matthew Hayden’s Daughter Grace Hayden Responds To Nepotism Trolls

TheTools · 03.10.2026 03:41 · source · permalink

Ascendants Dot In

Share

Australian sports presenter Grace Hayden has responded to claims that her famous father’s reputation helped build her broadcasting career, insisting that her achievements are backed by research, preparation and hard work.

Gracie Hayden, who is the daughter of the former Australian cricketer Mathew Hayden, is responding to critics who have been accusing her of having a successful career in sports broadcasting solely because of her well-known family name.

The 24-year-old presenter and model, who has worked across cricket, motorsports, tennis and rugby league, opened up about the issue in a recent interview with Stellar, revealing that questions surrounding her individual achievements have followed her since childhood.

Hayden acknowledged that growing up in a prominent cricketing family brought opportunities and public recognition. However, she rejected suggestions that her professional journey could be attributed solely to her father’s reputation.

“People want to throw in whatever they want to throw at me. But I definitely know I do the work. I do the hard yards and it’s a lot of research,” she told Stellar.

Why Has Grace Hayden Faced Nepotism Criticism?

Much of the criticism surrounding Grace Hayden stems from her connection to Matthew Hayden, one of Australia’s most recognised former international cricketers, who represented the country in 103 Test matches.

As she established herself in sports media, questions arose over whether her surname had played a decisive role in securing professional opportunities.

For Hayden, however, such assumptions are nothing new.

She recalled being subjected to similar remarks during her younger years, including suggestions that she had earned places in sporting teams simply because of her father’s identity.

“I definitely wrestled with it when I was younger when people would say I only made a sports team because I was ‘Matthew Hayden’s daughter’ or I only did something because I was ‘Matthew Hayden’s daughter’,” she revealed.

Rather than continuing to distance herself from her family background, Hayden said a piece of advice helped her approach the criticism differently.

She was encouraged to embrace her identity and recognise that being proud of her parents did not diminish her own ambitions.

“And at the end of the day, I’m so proud of my dad and mum, for what they’ve achieved together,” she said.

Her response reflects a distinction she is keen to establish: recognising the influence of her family while also seeking acknowledgment for the work she puts into her broadcasting assignments.

From Racing Fashion Reporter to IPL and NRL Coverage

Hayden’s professional journey began in an unexpected setting.

In 2022, while studying occupational therapy at university, she was selected as a fashion ambassador for Oaks Day at Eagle Farm Racecourse in Brisbane.

Her confidence during interviews attracted the attention of Seven Network executives, who subsequently considered her for a roving fashion reporter position during the Melbourne racing carnival.

Despite having limited broadcasting experience and competing against six other candidates, Hayden secured the role.

That initial opportunity eventually helped her transition into broader sports coverage, including Australia’s Big Bash League (BBL) and the Indian Premier League (IPL) in India.

She has since expanded her portfolio to include motorsports and tennis, while joining Fox League as an NRL sideline reporter in July 2026.

Describing the current phase of her career as her “year of yes”, Hayden said she has consciously chosen to accept unfamiliar opportunities and develop her experience across different sporting environments.

Her response to critics comes as she continues to balance television presenting, modelling and public appearances.

Grace Hayden Returns to Racing With Sydney Everest Carnival Role

Hayden has been appointed an ambassador for the Australian Turf Club’s Sydney Everest Carnival, marking a return to the industry where her television journey began.

She is scheduled to attend TAB Everest Day on October 17, 2026, as the race celebrates its 10th anniversary with a total prize pool of A$20 million.

Beyond her public-facing commitments, Hayden shares a quieter rural lifestyle with her partner, business executive Wilson Statham, on a farm in Moree, northern New South Wales.

She credits her close relationships with helping her remain grounded amid the public attention surrounding her career.

Although her family name continues to attract scrutiny, Hayden appears determined to establish a professional identity through her own broadcasting experience.

Reflecting on her journey so far, she told Stellar: “And I feel like I’m just getting started.”

Industry Comment: Aparna Agarwal, Founder of Sandbox Events

“Coming from a well-known family may bring visibility, but sustaining a career in a competitive industry requires individual effort, discipline and consistency. Grace Hayden should have the opportunity to be recognised for her professional journey rather than having every achievement reduced to her father’s identity. A family legacy can be a source of pride without taking away from an individual’s hard work.”
