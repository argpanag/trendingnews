Ludwig season 2 ending explained: Who killed [spoiler]?

As with season 1, season 2 ended with some major developments in the show's ongoing mystery.

*Warning - contains full spoilers for all six episodes of Ludwig season 2.*

The final three episodes of Ludwig season 2 have now been released on BBC iPlayer, bringing to a close this latest chapter of the Cambridge-based comedic detective series.

Season 2 ends on a dramatic note, with David Mitchell's John and the team coming together to investigate a crucial murder, while also learning more about the ongoing mystery surrounding James's disappearance.

But what more did we learn in the episode, who was killed and by whom, and where does the finale leave the characters ahead of the 2026 Christmas special?

Read on for everything you need to know about the ending of Ludwig season 2.

Ludwig season 2 ending explained: Who killed Chief Constable Ziegler?

At one point, it seemed as though local MP Joanne Kemper was banged to rights for the murder of Chief Constable Ziegler. However, it all seemed a bit too easy, too convenient. Her gun had been used for the murder, and been left in the fireplace at the scene of the crime. She was also captured on CCTV visiting Ziegler's house on the night of the murder.

Lucy and John then came to wonder whether something else was at play - whether James had framed Kemper for Ziegler's murder in order to bring her to justice and end her reign of terror.

Thankfully, it turned out he hadn't gone so far over to the dark side. Instead, someone else had been framing Kemper, that being Ziegler himself.

It turned out that, the night she had died, Ziegler's daughter Mandy, who was also Russell's fiancée, had been on her way to meet James, to hand over evidence of her father covering up crimes for Kemper.

Ziegler himself hadn't known this, but had later found it out after being sent a book of Ludwig puzzles by an unknown party. He had cracked a code enabling him to read a letter from James about the case and what happened with Mandy, and this threw him into despair.

He had formed a plan to frame Kemper, as she couldn't be tied to any of the murders she had actually committed. He stole her gun, timed his suicide around her visit to his house, and left clues suggesting she was the guilty party. He believed he was getting justice for his daughter.

What did we learn about Joanne Kemper?

At the end of the season, local MP Joanne Kemper was still riding high and comfortable in her position - in fact, she was on track to potentially become Prime Minister, according to Lucy and soaring poll numbers.

We still don't know exactly what dodgy deeds she's been up to or why.

However, what we do know so far is that she and her family had been behind two suspicious deaths, ten years apart - those of Dominic Kennedy and Ryan Oakley.

Conspiracy theorist Roger Sinclair had stumbled upon this connection, and suggested on his blog that Kemper and her family had been committing ritual sacrifices for generations in order to maintain political power. This was, of course, nonsense, but the link being made apparent meant Sinclair had to die.

Kemper had Chief Constable Ziegler in hock to her, and it seems he then orchestrated the murder of Rhys Bowen, a local burglar who Sinclair's death was pinned on and who was awaiting trial. His death was made to look like a suicide.

Where did we leave John, Lucy and the team?

As for the police team, they were more unified than ever. While Ethan and Caitlin believed John was secretly a member of MI5, everyone was otherwise on the same page, brought into the investigation surrounding Kemper.
