Garvaghy Road spokesperson reveals friendship with late Orangeman

TheTools · 14.10.2026 06:05 · source · permalink

Garvaghy Road residents’ spokesperson Breandán Mac Cionnaith has revealed that he was good friends with an Orangeman.

The leading member of the Garvaghy Road Residents Coalition said that the pair had met many times and discussed issues around parading without ever falling out.

Speaking to the Ireland Podcast on Sunday, two days after Secretary of State Sir Chris Bryant banned the Drumcree parade, Mr Mac Cionnaith said the friendship showed people on opposite sides of the dispute could still get on.

“This may surprise people, it may shock people, but a good friend of mine was a member of the Orange Order. He died recently,” he said.

“Him and me met on many occasions and discussed this issue and other issues. We never fell out, we remained friends. When I heard about his death, I went and spoke to his wife, and I went to the wake. I was welcomed there.

“It shows that, despite the differences, on a human level we could still get on.”

He declined to name the man, saying “I’ll not say his name because I don’t want the family targeted”.

For years, the Portadown lodge refused to sit down with Mr Mac Cionnaith because of his past as a republican prisoner.

He said he had not met the Order in talks during the latest dispute either, with the Secretary of State instead moving between the two sides.

Mr Mac Cionnaith said he believes the Order should come home from Drumcree Church the way it goes out.

He added that if the Order tried “to come into conflict”, the acceptability of its outward route could also be called into question.

“The Orange Order had a route here that takes them out to Drumcree Church. They can take that route back home again,” he said.

“If they fail to do so and try to come into conflict, well, then that will raise the whole question about the acceptability of the route out to Drumcree. A lot of the homes that are now along that route have been built in recent years and they’re primarily nationalist homes.”

He said the Parades Commission faced “a major task” to win back the trust of the community. He pointed to a residents’ petition he said was signed by around 3,500 adults, about 95 percent of the area.

“The Parades Commission chose to ignore it. You have to ask why that was,” he said.

Asked if the commission’s credibility was beyond repair, he said: “That could well be.”

He dismissed unionist claims that the dispute showed Orangemen were being treated as second-class citizens. He said there are around 50 to 60 Orange, Black and band parades in Portadown in an average year.

“They have ample opportunity to exercise their freedom of movement, their freedom to parade across Portadown, without coming to the most contentious area of all. What have they to fear?” he said.
