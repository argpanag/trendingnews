What Kauaʻi County residents, visitors need to know Friday about Hurricane Lowell recovery | Kauai Now

TheTools · 18.09.2026 14:35 · source · permalink

Kaua‘i County Mayor Derek Kawakami on Thursday encouraged visitors to participate in the Garden Isle’s recovery by supporting local businesses, adds people should avoid the Wainiha and Hāʻena areas in wake of the hurricane.

Kaua‘i County is in Day 11 of recovery from Hurricane Lowell.

More signs of progress. Emergency repairs to address erosion damage from Hurricane Lowell were completed on Wainiha River Bridge No. 3 on Kūhiō Highway on the North Shore of Kaua‘i. Today, 15-ton traffic can use the bridge again after access was weight-limited to 5 tons or below during the repairs.

Kaua‘i County Mayor Derek Kawakami on Thursday encouraged visitors to participate in the Garden Isle’s recovery by supporting local businesses. 

“Kauaʻi is welcoming visitors, and we are grateful for the support you bring to our local businesses and our economy,” Kawakami said. “At the same time, our island is still in active recovery from Hurricane Lowell, and we are asking everyone who comes here to be part of that recovery.”

He said the best way to help is by spending money locally.

“Eat at our locally-owned restaurants, shop at our boutiques and small shops and book with local activity providers,” the mayor said. “Many of our small businesses are carrying this recovery on their own and every dollar you spend with them makes a real difference.” 

Kawakami added that people should avoid the Wainiha and Hāʻena areas in wake of the hurricane. 

State staff will visit the county’s hurricane recovery resource centers in Hanalei and Kōloa next week to help residents with unemployment insurance applications and other work-related questions.

The Kauaʻi Bus extended its post-hurricane fare-free service through Sept. 30 to support residents during the recovery. Meanwhile, water conservation notices are being lifted in additional areas as conditions improve, though conservation measures remain in place where needed.

Visit KauaiRecovers.org for recovery help, resources and update. You also can call Aloha United Way at 2-1-1, text “HELP” to 877-275-6569 or chat with a local specialist at AUW211.org for any non-emergency information.

Water conservation

Water conservation notices remain in effect for:

  • ʻAnini Water System
  • Hāʻena-Wainiha Water System
  • Kīlauea Water System

In these areas:

  • Use water only for essential needs.
  • Outages can affect pumps and wells, so water could cut out or lose pressure.
  • Do not use water if you think it is contaminated by flooding.
  • Some water systems are operating partially on emergency generators. High demand could result in low water pressure and temporary service outages. 
  • Call 808-245-5444 to report a water service outage or leak.

All other areas had their water conservation notices canceled. Visit the Water Department website and follow the department on social media for additional information and updates. Receive alerts by signing up for Everbridge notifications.

Hurricane resource centers

To help residents with state unemployment insurance applications and other employment-related questions, staff from the state Department of Labor and Industrial Relations will be on-site at both county hurricane resource centers next week:

  • Hanalei Neighborhood Center (North Shore): Monday and Tuesday, Sept. 21-22.
  • Kōloa Neighborhood Center (South Shore): Wednesday and Thursday, Sept. 23-24.

Residents are encouraged to meet in-person with government and nonprofit staff at the recovery centers.

Centers are open 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily. New hours of 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. begin Monday.

The Hanalei Initiative operates an hourly shuttle starting at 9 a.m. from The Hanalei Colony Resort in Wainiha directly to the Hanalei resource center.

Kīlauea refuge area remains open

With power restoration efforts progressing around Kaua‘i, one refuge area remains open at Kīlauea Neighborhood Center, 2460 Keneke St. People using refuge areas have the opportunity to connect with case management and other wraparound services and support. 

Many County park and park facilities reopen

Many of the hard-hit county park and park facilities reopened Tuesday. When re-entering, use caution and heed isolated amenities that might remain closed. Certain beach parks, including Hāʻena, Blackpot, Poʻipū and Salt Pond along with Hanapēpē Stadium football field, remain closed because of storm-related damages. 

Power restoration updates

Power is now restored for more than 91% of the island as of Thursday afternoon.

  • Work is ongoing in the North Shore area. No timeline is available for when repairs will be completed.
  • Flaggers will be on site to assist with traffic control. Use extra caution around coned work areas.
  • Motorists should expect extended delays and are asked to slow down and remain alert.
  • Stay away from downed utility lines and call 9-1-1 if a line poses an immediate danger.
  • Peak outages after Lowell impacted about 33,000 customers.

Kaua‘i Island Utility Cooperative and partners continued Thursday prepping for repairs in Wainiha/Hā‘ena, with a focus on debris removal and vegetation management. Support for this effort was provided by Hawai‘i Department of Transportation Highway Division, county Department of Public Works, Earthworks Pacific and Goodfellow Bros.  

The utility cooperative and mutual aid partner Southern California Edison continued addressing remaining spot outages in communities from Anahola to Kekaha and restoration planning for Kōke‘e, which remains without power. 

Pending service orders and line extensions: 

The utility cooperative suspended processing routine service orders and line extensions to focus all efforts on Hurricane Lowell restoration efforts, including safety-related service orders. Refrain from calling to check on the status of your service order or application so the co-op can remain focused on restoration priorities. 

Outage verifications: 

If your insurance carrier requires verification of an outage caused by Hurricane Lowell, download, fill out and then submit this form per instructions on the form. Kauaʻi Island Utility Cooperative will process the request as quickly as possible. It must be submitted by the member of record for your account. Remember to sign the form before submitting it. You also can find this information on the cooperative website.

Remember to make sure your contact information with Kauaʻi Island Utility Cooperative is updated by calling 808-246-4300 to get alerts. Check ongoing outages online.

Financial assistance:

Members financially impacted by Hurricane Lowell and anticipate a need to establish a payment plan with Kaua‘i Island Utility Cooperative or interested in exploring financial assistance options are encouraged to visit the utility co-op website for more information.

Note: Kaua‘i Island Utility Cooperative suspended collections activities for delinquent accounts. The timing of resumption of collections activities will be evaluated once Hurricane Lowell restoration is completed.

Find links to helpful resources on the Kaua‘i Island Utility Cooperative website. Resumption of collections activities will be evaluated once Hurricane Lowell restoration is completed.

Generator safety

  • Keep generators outdoors and at least 20 feet away from your home, with the exhaust pointed away from doors, windows and vents to prevent deadly carbon monoxide from entering.
  • Never plug a generator directly into a household outlet; use properly rated extension cords or a professionally installed transfer switch to prevent dangerous back feeding onto utility lines.
  • Keep the generator dry, allow it to cool before refueling, and follow all manufacturer instructions for safe operation.

SNAP replacement 

Households impacted by Hurricane Lowell on Kaua‘i could be eligible for replacement of Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits.

The U.S. Department of Agriculture is allowing Hawai‘i to approve automatic mass replacements of 60% benefits for residents who lost food as a result of power outages. The waiver applies to Kaua‘i residents. More details are available through the Hawai‘i Department of Human Services.

SNAP participants in areas hardest hit by the disaster may have a portion of their August benefits replaced.

Avoid unnecessary travel on roadways

Kauaʻi Police Department continues to urge the public to travel only if necessary. Officers are escorting work crews, and extra traffic is slowing debris removal, power restoration and road repairs.

The Kauaʻi Bus

Regular bus and paratransit service resumed, with fares now waved through Sept. 30. The 900 and 950 Anahola-Waimea express routes are not running until further notice. Call 808-246-8110 or visit The Kauaʻi Bus website for additional information or with any questions.

State road closures and openings

Numerous roads continue to be closed islandwide; however, the state Transportation Department reports no state highway or road are closed in a way that keeps people from reaching their homes.

  • Kaumualiʻi Highway (vicinity of Halfway Bridge): Tree/debris removal ongoing; crews will use the shoulder to minimize traffic impacts. 
  • Kūhiō Highway (vicinity of Princeville transfer station to Kapaka Street): Tree/debris removal and one-lane shifting. (8:30 a.m. to 3 p.m.).
  • Kūhiō Highway (vicinity of Kalihiwai Bridge): Tree/debris removal ongoing and one-lane shifting. (8:30 a.m. to 3 p.m.).
  • Crews will resume debris clearing under the mauka Wailuā River Bridge on Sept. 20.
  • Kōkeʻe and Waimea Canyon roads are still being cleared.
  • Halewili Road is open from the ʻEleʻele side of Kaumualiʻi Highway but remains closed from the Kalāheo side of the highway to Kauaʻi Coffee because of downed utility poles.
  • Kūhiō Highway: Princeville to Keʻe Beach open to local traffic only.

Hawai‘i Department of Transportation reopened Wainiha River Bridge No. 3 to 15-ton traffic after access was weight-limited to 5 tons or less to address scour damage from Hurricane Lowell. This reopening is happening ahead of the originally scheduled date of Sept. 21.

Crews were able to complete the concrete pour to strengthen the foundation and pour a new abutment, resulting in the lifting of the vehicle weight restriction Thursday morning.

Additional work in the area is ongoing, including installing grouted riprap to protect the bridge abutments and piers. Stream bank stabilization methods also are being implemented to protect against future scour damage.

A crew also will be dispatched to clear rocks that piled up at the river fork upstream so water can flow again. Work will begin today, weather permitting, and is expected to take 1 to 2 days to complete.

Help manage post-storm waste

  • Separate waste into green waste, metals and putrescible waste (food and other materials that can spoil).
  • Prioritize disposal of putrescible waste first to help reduce odors, pests and other impacts while collection services are disrupted.
  • Mixed waste should be kept free of rainwater and any other liquids such as cooking oil, etc.

Tipping fees for storm-generated debris are waived at Kekaha Landfill and county transfer stations as residents and businesses clean up from Hurricane Lowell.

Anaina Hou remains without power and cleanup continues. Tripp’s Hale, located next to the playground, was severely damaged during the storm.

County, state and other closures/service disruptions

  • Waimea and Kapaʻa pools, along with Wailuā Golf Course, remain closed.
  • All neighborhood centers are closed until further notice, except in Hanalei and Kōloa, which are serving as hurricane recovery resource centers, and Kīlauea Neighborhood Center, which continues to operate as a refuge area. 
  • County board and commission meetings are suspended. 
  • The Sept. 16 county council meeting is canceled.
  • All state Department of Land and Natural Resources parks and forest reserves will be closed for the foreseeable future because of widespread damage from Hurricane Lowell. The public is especially urged to avoid Kōke‘e State Park and Kōke‘e Road, where many catastrophic, unmitigated hazards exist throughout the landscape. Road clearing may require many more weeks. These areas will reopen as soon as it is safe to do so.
  • Trees have fallen across Waimea Canyon Drive, Kokeʻe Road and across virtually all internal park dirt roads that access facilities, including approximately 100 leased recreational cabins.
  • Division of State Parks closed the road into Kōkeʻe State Park as part of the total park closure. Hawaiʻi Department of Transportation also closed Waimea Canyon Road above the lower junction.
  • Following reports of people disregarding the closure and trying to access cabins, Division of Conservation and Resources Enforcement officers are posted at the junction of Kōkeʻe Road and Waimea Canyon Road, maintaining a 24/7 roadblock, controlling traffic and access. Access is allowed during daytime hours until 6 p.m. for people who have legitimate business. All others continue to be denied entry.

Brown water advisory

An islandwide brown water advisory remains in effect for Kauaʻi. Stay out of floodwaters and ocean water near stream mouths, drainage outlets and harbors, which could contain sewage, animal waste, chemicals and debris.

Courthouse reopen

The Puʻuhonua Kaulike courthouse in Līhuʻe resumed operations Monday, with hearings, trials and other court proceedings proceeding as scheduled. The public can visit the Judiciary website for updates and information.

Parties also can monitor the state’s online public case information system eCourt Kokua for the most current information regarding hearings, trials and case activity.

Mold warning

State health officials urge residents to address any mold growth following flooding. Mold exposure carries serious health risks, including breathing problems, allergic reactions and infections. For information on how to clean mold and mud post-flood, Click here for additional information about how to clean mold and mud post-flood.

Avoid disaster recovery scams

Kauaʻi Police Department continues to urge residents remain vigilant for scams during ongoing Hurricane Lowell recovery.

The public is reminded to:

  • Verify who is contacting them before providing information or accepting assistance.
  • Do not share passwords, financial information, Social Security numbers or verification codes.
  • Do not send money, gift cards or cryptocurrency to unsolicited contacts.
  • Be cautious with links, QR codes and requests for donations.
  • Use official government and agency channels to verify disaster-related information.

Visit Kauaʻi County official channels and KauaiRecovers.org for verified information about Hurricane Lowell recovery.

Hawaiʻi State Federal Credit Union

Hawaii State Federal Credit Union is expanding its Emergency Member Relief Program to provide immediate financial assistance to members impacted by Hurricane Lowell.

Qualifying members have access to a range of support options under this program, including:

  • Emergency assistance loans: Access up to $5,000 with no payments required for the first three months and no prepayment penalty for qualifying members.
  • Deferral on loan payments: Payment deferrals on eligible consumer loans for one to three months. Interest will continue to accrue during the deferral period, and the loan term will be extended, resulting in a final balloon payment. Deferral options for mortgages, home equity lines of credit and revolving credit may vary.
  • Penalty-free term share withdrawals: Withdrawals from existing term share accounts without an early withdrawal penalty.

To learn more, visit any of the credit union’s 14 branch locations or call the Member Service Call Center at 808-587-2700.

Document your damage

Residents are urged to closely document damage to their homes and property. A damage assessment form is available through the Kauaʻi Emergency Management website.

Resident-submitted damage assessments Sunday surpassed a total of 550.

The information provided will help the County of Kauaʻi understand the scope of residential damage and impacts to our community. Damage reports may be used to determine whether our jurisdiction qualifies for state or federal assistance.

Affected parties still need to file claims with their insurance companies for residential damage and personal property losses. Residents can also notify authorities about public hazards online. This form is for reporting non-life-threatening roadway and public space flood damage and hazards.  

Stay connected

T-Mobile has restored coverage across major population centers and throughout the northern part of the island, however, recovery work remains ongoing in southern Kauaʻi, where downed trees and access challenges continue to impact some locations.

Verizon is waiving domestic calls, texts and data charges for consumer value, postpaid and small business customers through Sept. 14 for those on Kauaʻi.

Satellite messaging provides backup if terrestrial services are unavailable.

The rest of the state continues to welcome visitors, but it urges anyone with upcoming trips to the islands to monitor conditions.
