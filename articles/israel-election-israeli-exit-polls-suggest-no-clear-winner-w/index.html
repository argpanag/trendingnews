Israel election: Israeli exit polls suggest no clear winner with Eisenkot's bloc just ahead of Netanyahu

TheTools · 27.10.2026 23:42 · source · permalink

Summary

  • Israeli exit polls suggest Gadi Eisenkot's bloc has won the largest number of seats in the general election but has fallen short of a majority

  • Eisenkot leads the newly formed Yashar party and is the main figure in the "change bloc", a group of opposition parties with the shared goal of ousting Benjamin Netanyahu

  • The exit polls also suggest Netanyahu's Likud party is set to be the biggest single party in the Israeli parliament

  • Israeli elections can be decided by just a handful of seats and exit polls have been wrong in the past

  • Official results will start being released from midnight local time (22:00 GMT), but counting could take days

  • What lies ahead is likely to be weeks or months of protracted negotiations between the parties, with numerous options on the table, our reporter in Tel Aviv writes

  • All 120 seats in the Knesset, Israel's parliament, are up for grabs, but no party has ever won a majority and in the past governments have been made up of coalitions

  • It is Israel's first legislative election since the Hamas-led attacks on 7 October 2023 - what were the big issues on the campaign trail?

Israeli exit polls suggest Gadi Eisenkot's bloc has won the largest number of seats in the general election but has fallen short of a majority

Eisenkot leads the newly formed Yashar party and is the main figure in the "change bloc", a group of opposition parties with the shared goal of ousting Benjamin Netanyahu

The exit polls also suggest Netanyahu's Likud party is set to be the biggest single party in the Israeli parliament

Israeli elections can be decided by just a handful of seats and exit polls have been wrong in the past

Official results will start being released from midnight local time (22:00 GMT), but counting could take days

What lies ahead is likely to be weeks or months of protracted negotiations between the parties, with numerous options on the table, our reporter in Tel Aviv writes

All 120 seats in the Knesset, Israel's parliament, are up for grabs, but no party has ever won a majority and in the past governments have been made up of coalitions

It is Israel's first legislative election since the Hamas-led attacks on 7 October 2023 - what were the big issues on the campaign trail?

  1. Israel voted to end Netanyahu's term, Eisenkot tells supporterspublished at 23:41 GMT
    23:41 GMT

    Tom Bennett
    Reporting from Eisenkot's HQ

    Gadi Eisenkot has just arrived at his party’s HQ here in Tel Aviv, taking a long walk through the crowd onto stage, surrounded by the candidates from his Yashar Party.

    “We made history,” he says. “This morning, the people of Israel went to a referendum on the leadership of the coming years. The public decided in favour of change.

    "The Israeli public voted against the continuation of Netanyahu's term," he says, to big cheers from the crowd.

    “In an election with such high voter turnout, it would be a mistake to go to additional elections. It is our duty, and my personal responsibility, to do everything to respect the will of the people.”

    The national anthem was then played before he took an Israeli flag from the crowd and waved it, before exiting the stage.

  4. 'A very, very bad night' for Netanyahu's bloc, commentator sayspublished at 22:49 GMT
    22:49 GMT

    Netanyahu's party being the largest in the Israeli exit polls is a "meaningless consolation prize" compared to the prediction of Gadi Eisenkot's bloc being the biggest group, a leading Israeli commentator has said.

    "He's had incredible comebacks before, but for the Netanyahu coalition - this is a very, very bad night," Anshel Pfeffer, the Economist's Israel correspondent, tells BBC News.

    Pfeffer says he feels the Likud leader could only make a comeback if he manages to convince centrist parties to join him in a unity government, adding: "I'm not going to put that past him."

  6. Who is Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot?published at 22:18 GMT
    22:18 GMT

    Gadi Eisenkot is a former head of the Israel Defense Forces, now leading the centrist Yashar party. He has built his campaign on calls for accountability over the security failures that led to 7 October.

    His popularity stems partly from his military credentials and his status as a relative political newcomer, having formed his party only last year.

    He lost his son, a soldier, in the first months of the Gaza war, and served in Israel's war cabinet under Benjamin Netanyahu until resigning in 2024 over the PM's handling of the conflict.

    On Gaza, Eisenkot does not differ hugely from Netanyahu. He recently pledged to end Hamas rule in the territory within 18 months, through a US-led peace plan or, if necessary, military means.

    He supports Israeli settlements in general, but opposes proceeding with the controversial E1 settlement project which would, in effect, split the West Bank in two.

  7. Arab-majority parties could win record number of seatspublished at 22:08 GMT
    22:08 GMT

    Paul Adams
    Diplomatic correspondent

    Reports suggest that a high turnout among Israeli Arab voters has been translated into electoral success, with the two Arab-majority parties (The United Arab List - or Ra’am - and The Joint List, an alliance of three smaller parties) projected to win as many as 19 seats between them (which would be a record).

    The main parties in Israel have traditionally been extremely reluctant to include Arab parties in their coalitions, but with the gap between the two main electoral blocs projected to be narrow, the Arab-majority parties will be hard for Eisenkot and Netanyahu to ignore.

    In past Israeli elections, Arab turnout has generally been lower than Jewish turnout. But this time, the desire to see the back of Netanyahu seems to have driven large numbers of Arabs to the polls. Netanyahu, clearly aware of this danger, spent much of election day warning of an effort by Arab voters to "steal the election".

    Until now, Eisenkot has said the Arab parties do not meet his conditions for joining a coalition. The mathematics of the new Knesset may force him to reconsider, either by relying on their informal support in the event of a minority government or by bringing in Ra’am as a formal coalition partner.

    After the 2021 elections, Ra’am was part of a coalition led by Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid. It lasted a year before collapsing.

  9. Weeks or months of protracted talks may lie aheadpublished at 21:31 GMT
    21:31 GMT

    Tom Bennett
    Reporting from Eisenkot's watch party in Tel Aviv

    The positive news for Israelis who want to oust Netanyahu is that these projected results suggest it will be very hard for him to form a government.

    The bad news is that his key challenger, Eisenkot, will also struggle.

    It is the Arab parties who may hold the key to breaking the political deadlock. They’re projected to defy expectations to win a record 17 or 18 seats, in part driven by a strong turnout from Israel’s Arab population, many of whom refer to themselves as Palestinian citizens of Israel.

    What lies ahead is likely to be weeks or months of protracted negotiations between the parties, with numerous options on the table - but none of them easy.

    Could Eisenkot form a minority government propped up by the Arab parties? Could Netanyahu persuade Eisenkot to break his election promises and form a unity government of Israel’s centrist and right-wing parties? Could Netanyahu lure one of the parties from Eisenkot’s bloc to break away and join him - or vice versa?

    These are all options being discussed by Israeli commentators.

    For a government to be formed, it seems likely someone will have to break their election promises.

  20. If no-one wins this election, how long could Netanyahu stay on?published at 19:10 GMT
    19:10 GMT

    Raffi Berg
    Middle East digital editor

    If neither current Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc nor the opposition wins a majority of seats, Netanyahu will stay on as caretaker PM as attempts are made to form a government.

    President Isaac Herzog will nominate the party leader whom he thinks stands the best chance of being able to do this.
