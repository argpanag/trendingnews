Verstappen wins exhilarating Las Vegas Grand Prix

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:58 · source · permalink

Max Verstappen triumphed in a breathless Las Vegas Grand Prix, the Red Bull driver ultimately sharing the podium with McLaren's Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri.

Max Verstappen has taken victory in a thrilling and frenetic Las Vegas Grand Prix, the four-time World Champion managing to hold off a race-long challenge from the Mercedes pair – who both dramatically fell back from the podium positions in the final laps – to seal his third win on the streets of Nevada ahead of the McLaren duo.

Verstappen had made a good start to lead from pole position when the race got underway – but with fine margins throughout the pack, the order continued to evolve as battles played out across the field, with George Russell frequently overtaking the Red Bull only for Verstappen to retake the position moments later.

The Dutchman also held onto P1 amid a Safety Car phase mid-race following a collision between Sergio Perez and Rafael Camara – and while Isack Hadjar dropped backwards after damaging his front wing, the fight continued amongst the leading cars as the event headed into its second half.

Despite the lead still changing hands multiple times – with Kimi Antonelli also getting involved – Verstappen continually grabbed it back, but question marks remained over whether he could do this to the final lap. However, the threat from Mercedes was nullified when Russell locked up and fell to ninth in the closing stages, while Antonelli appeared to suffer an issue that saw him slip down to fourth.

This meant that Verstappen claimed a stunning win ahead of the McLarens of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri. Antonelli had to settle for fourth, while Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto secured a solid points haul for Alpine in fifth and sixth respectively.

Lewis Hamilton was seventh for Ferrari in an eventful race for the Scuderia, the Briton having taken a 10-second time penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage in an earlier battle with Norris while he also made contact with team mate Charles Leclerc later on, for which the Monegasque received a 10-second penalty of his own.

Race results

Carlos Sainz scored for Williams with P8 in his 250th Grand Prix, while Russell and Leclerc were likely to be less happy in P9 and P10. The Audis of Nico Hulkenberg and Gabriel Bortoleto just missed out in 11th and 12th, as did Haas’ Ollie Bearman in 13th place.

Hadjar wound up in P14 on a disappointing day for the Red Bull racer, whose evening had started strongly in Las Vegas. An incident between Hadjar and Russell will be investigated after the race. It was also a tough outing for Racing Bulls, with Liam Lawson crossing the line in P15 after starting from the pit lane due to a fuel leak on his car.

Camara ended his second Grand Prix in 16th ahead of Lance Stroll in the Aston Martin and Cadillac’s Valtteri Bottas, while Fernando Alonso retired near the end of the event.

Arvid Lindblad was amongst the retirees after spinning his Racing Bulls machine behind the Safety Car, with Perez also unable to continue following his collision with Camara. Alex Albon, meanwhile, had been wheeled off the grid prior to the start and could not participate due to damage from overheating brakes.

AS IT HAPPENED

After an action-packed two days on track in Nevada, the attentions of the paddock turned to Saturday night’s 50-lap Las Vegas Grand Prix, for which Verstappen – a two-time winner of the event in previous years – would start from pole position.

While Antonelli had set the fastest time in the dying moments of Friday’s Qualifying, a five-place penalty due to a power unit change dropped the Italian down to P6 on the grid – meaning that Verstappen and Russell were promoted to the front row.

That did not mark the only change to the order, as other drivers also served their respective penalties. Piastri slipped from P7 to P17, while Sainz’s P14 in Qualifying ultimately became P20 and Stroll was demoted from P20 to P21. Bearman, meanwhile, did not set a competitive time due to his penalties putting him at the back of the order.

Heavy rain had affected running when the weekend began on Thursday, but dry and cool conditions looked to be in store as the grid started to assemble for the Grand Prix. One driver yet to make to his P7 slot as the start time neared, however, was Lawson, with Racing Bulls confirming that he would start the race from the pit lane following a fuel leak on the car.

Meanwhile there was also trouble for Albon, whose Williams was wheeled off the grid from P14 following an issue with the brakes overheating. The team subsequently confirmed that the Thai driver would not be able to race due to the damage sustained by the heat on the way to the grid.

When the rest of the field were in place and the tyre blankets had been removed, it was revealed that the majority of the pack would start on the soft compound with the exception of Antonelli, Lawson and Bearman on the medium.

After the formation lap had been completed and the five lights went out in Las Vegas, a strong launch for Verstappen saw the polesitter lead away from Russell, while behind them Hadjar tried to go around the outside of Norris in a fight for third before having to back down.

It did not take long for the Frenchman to find a way through, however, with the Red Bull utilising the power of the slipstream to overtake on the straight. Meanwhile Gasly had moved ahead of team mate Colapinto to go up to P6 and Hamilton was now in front of Leclerc following Ferrari’s tough Qualifying.

Russell surged ahead of Verstappen on Lap 2 but his time at the front was brief, with Verstappen sweeping ahead around the outside again at the final corner. Hadjar also looked to be getting involved with the battle and subsequently repeated Verstappen’s move on Russell – this time to the inside – one tour later to move into P2.

The scrap was far from over, however. Russell momentarily overtook again on Lap 4, only for Hadjar to quickly retake the position – and soon Lindblad and Norris had also joined the party, the Racing Bulls having also briefly moved ahead of Russell before the Mercedes retook the place.

Up ahead the lead was potentially changing hands too, with Hadjar going side-by-side alongside Verstappen – leading to the Frenchman being told over the radio to avoid losing time with the team due to the threat of Russell behind. The Silver Arrows machine had indeed closed up again moments later, with little to separate the pack.

“Can he stop defending? Because otherwise we’re just going to lose time,” Hadjar radioed in on Lap 8. Soon afterwards the French driver pulled alongside but did not overtake – but one tour later, Russell had made a move on Hadjar to split the Red Bulls.

By the Lap 10 mark, the order stood as Verstappen, Russell, Hadjar, Gasly, Lindblad, Norris, Antonelli, Hamilton, Leclerc and Piastri, the gaps remaining fine throughout the pack. Russell subsequently managed to grab the lead, only for Verstappen to snatch it back again moments later.

Elsewhere Antonelli had taken P5 while Norris was engaged in a close scrap with the Ferrari duo, the Briton ultimately losing out to both cars before also slipping backwards behind Colapinto and Piastri. The stewards went on to confirm that they would investigate Hamilton for leaving the track and gaining an advantage in the fight with Norris.

As another brief swap for the lead played out at the front – with Russell ahead for a moment before Verstappen retook the position – it looked as if the top five had slightly broken away. Gasly had managed to stay involved and engaged in a scrap with Hadjar for third, the Alpine driver enjoying a strong outing in Las Vegas.

Over at Ferrari, Leclerc – running behind Hamilton and Piastri – reported: “He needs to be careful with his squeezing because it’s not going to end well.” Hamilton was soon on the radio himself, asking his engineer if he could tell Leclerc to “back the **** off” as the Briton fought to keep Piastri at bay.

There was bad news for Hamilton moments later as the seven-time World Champion received a 10-second time penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage in his earlier fight with Norris. Meanwhile Hadjar had overtaken Russell for P2 – before the yellow flags were thrown due to Perez hitting the wall at Turns 14/15.

Replays showed that the Mexican had collided with the Haas of Camara, the latter continuing on while the Cadillac was out of the race. The Safety Car was deployed, leading to a flurry of action in the pit lane. Hamilton, however, was informed that he would be staying out due to the impact that his penalty would have had on a double stack.

Other teams did use the double stack, including Red Bull – with Russell feeling that Hadjar had backed the rest of the pack up in order to give Verstappen more of a breather ahead. Meanwhile Hamiton was brought into the pits one lap later, the Ferrari man voicing his annoyance at his penalty as he dropped out of the top 10.

There was heartbreak for Racing Bulls as Lindblad spun behind the Safety Car and was unable to get the car going again, putting the Briton out of the race. It marked a disappointing day for the squad, with Lawson running at the back of the order following his earlier fuel leak.

The stewards announced that the Perez and Camara collision would be investigated after the race as the rest of the pack continued to circulate behind the Safety Car, with the order on Lap 23 shaking out as Verstappen, Russell, Hadjar, Gasly, Antonelli, Norris, Leclerc, Bortoleto, Colapinto and Sainz.

As the Safety Car period came to an end on Lap 25 – with most drivers at the front now running the medium tyres, the exception being Antonelli, Bearman, Bottas, Camara and Lawson on the soft – Verstappen executed a solid restart to lead away.

There was drama behind just moments later, however, for the other Red Bull, as Hadjar suffered a broken front wing and dropped down the order. As the French driver headed to the pits – leaving him down in last place – Norris had moved up into P3 only for Gasly to snatch it back, while Russell was again trying to get ahead of Verstappen.

Once again the Briton pulled off an overtake, and while he initially managed to hold on through the last corner – the spot where Verstappen had frequently got back in front – the Red Bull ultimately returned to P1 later in the lap.

Hadjar’s day seemed to be going from bad to worse, with the stewards noting him for an unsafe release – though it was subsequently confirmed that there would be no further investigation. However, an incident involving Hadjar and Russell was set to be investigated after the race.

With Norris having moved ahead of Gasly for P3, the Alpine was then overtaken by Antonelli. Further back, the Ferrari pair were duelling over P7 on Lap 33, while the start of the next lap saw Norris slip ahead of Russell to slot into second place behind Verstappen.

Moments later the McLaren had briefly taken the lead – only for Verstappen to yet again grab P1 back. Meanwhile Piastri was now in fourth ahead of Antonelli and Hamilton had picked off Leclerc for sixth, with Gasly dropping back to eighth as the Alpine seemingly struggled with cooling issues.

The order rarely stayed in place for long as the laps ticked down. Antonelli had moved into the final podium position by Lap 37 – followed by Piastri and Norris – while Russell was again ahead of Verstappen, only for the Dutchman to retake P1 once again.

Further drama was in store as the Ferraris tangled behind, with Hamilton spinning and falling back to P10 while Leclerc was in P8. The incident was noted by the stewards.

In another intra-team scrap, Antonelli was now in front of Russell for P2 – and while the Italian then took the lead from Verstappen, the regular scenario unfolded of the Red Bull quickly returning to the front.

The pattern was causing some concern for Verstappen, however, due to the fact that the other car was generally ahead at the line before the Red Bull retook the lead into Turn 1 – meaning that he could in theory be overtaken on the last lap and not have time to grab P1 back into the first corner.

Over at McLaren – with Norris and Piastri now in third and fifth – Piastri was told to be careful around his team mate, leading the Australian to respond: ”Stop telling me to ******* be careful.” Meanwhile the Ferrari contact had resulted in a 10-second time penalty for Leclerc.

With only a few laps to go, Verstappen was still continuing to retake the lead from Antonelli whenever he was overtaken while Norris and Piastri were just behind Russell. The papaya battle was not settled, however, with Piastri taking P4 from his team mate.

Russell tried to move ahead of Antonelli on Lap 48, only to lock up and run into the barriers. While the Briton managed to reverse and get back into the race, the incident had dropped him down to ninth place.

There was further drama for Mercedes to come, with Antonelli appearing to suffer an issue as he fell behind the McLarens of Norris and Piastri in the closing stages – giving Verstappen some breathing room for perhaps the first time in the race.

As such, the Dutchman crossed the line to clinch his third – and arguably most eventful – victory at the Las Vegas Grand Prix with a margin of 0.639s, while the McLaren pair of Norris and Piastri secured a double podium for the team that perhaps would not have seemed likely a few hours earlier.

Antonelli settled for fourth, with Gasly managing the cooling issues on his Alpine to take P5 ahead of team mate Colapinto. Hamilton followed in seventh, while Sainz claimed some valuable points for Williams in eighth and Russell and Leclerc rounded out the top 10.

Hulkenberg, Bortoleto, Bearman, Hadjar, Lawson, Camara, Stroll and Bottas completed the classification, with Alonso retiring one lap before the finish. This brought the list of non-finishers to four, the Spaniard joining Lindblad, Perez and Albon.

Key quote

"Really, really surreal and something that I've never seen before in Formula 1 myself," Verstappen commented on the number of lead changes in the race. "I think in the beginning we had the deployment immediately correct, but of course people are learning and watching what you're doing. At one point, I couldn't get back into the lead before the start-finish line, so that's why I asked the question. But I think we had it set up well, and it was just a crazy amount of fighting. The slipstream was so big that it was just impossible to drive off, and it was crazy how many people were following."
