Gold hits 3½-month low on Fed hike fears, set for monthly loss

TheTools · 29.10.2026 04:49 · source · permalink

In this article

Gold prices ​fell to their lowest since ​mid-July on Friday ​and were headed for a second straight monthly decline, as rising crude oil prices and persistent inflation worries reinforced expectations of further US interest rate hikes.

Spot gold was down ⁠0.6% ‌at $3,995.42 per ounce after ⁠hitting its lowest since July 17 earlier. US gold futures for December delivery dropped 0.6% to $4,005.50. Bullion has declined 3.8% so far this month.

"Gold prices are affected by a combination of ‌two factors, the US GDP data which came in stronger than expected and the increase in oil prices putting pressure on the Fed ​to hike further interest rates," said Giovanni Staunovo, a UBS analyst.

Data on Thursday showed US gross domestic product grew more than expected last quarter, while inflation accelerated in September as consumers paid more for gasoline and ⁠other goods, bolstering economists' expectations of a rate hike in December.

Traders are pricing in a ‌near 74% chance of a rate hike in December, ‌according to the CME's FedWatch Tool

"Gold could climb to $5,000 or higher next year, as the Fed will eventually have to cut rates again and inflation will no longer pose ⁠a problem," Staunovo said.

Major central banks hit pause on rate rises this ⁠week but made clear the tightening cycle isn't over, with ⁠the Fed and the European Central Bank both holding rates steady and pointing to inflation risks from surging energy costs.

Oil prices climbed and ​were set for a fourth monthly ‌gain as an attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz highlighted continuing supply risks from the US-Israeli war on Iran.

Higher energy prices strengthen expectations of a prolonged period of elevated interest rates, making non-yielding assets such as gold less attractive.
