Eskom fraud scandal: Hawks target luxury Mercedes and R7.7m in assets

TheTools · 10.10.2026 00:43 · source · permalink

The Hawks have secured a R7.7 million restraint order targeting properties and vehicles following convictions in an R8.5 million fraud and money laundering case involving Eskom's Duvha Power Station in Mpumalanga.

Image: AI-Generated

The Hawks have secured a High Court restraint order targeting properties and luxury vehicles worth R7.7 million following the conviction of a former Eskom buyer and her co-accused in an R8.5 million fraud and money laundering scandal.

Former Eskom buyer Nozipho Ntuli, 39, was convicted of fraud after investigations uncovered how millions of rands in procurement contracts at Eskom's Duvha Power Station in Mpumalanga were awarded to companies linked to her personal associates.

Hawks spokesperson Lieutenant Colonel Magonseni Nkosi said the restraint order targets four properties and eight movable assets, including several vehicles.

"The restraint order, issued on September 30, 2026, covers four immovable properties and eight movable assets, including several motor vehicles," Nkosi said.

He said the order formed part of efforts to preserve assets potentially linked to corruption, fraud and money laundering involving procurement contracts at the power station.

Eskom buyer linked to R8.5 million procurement scandal

The restraint order follows the convictions of Ntuli, Edward George Sekatane, 46, Thulani Harries Mahlalela, 43, Alfred Lebogang Sekatane, 62, and three companies implicated in the scheme.

On August 18, 2026, the Middelburg Specialised Commercial Crimes Court convicted Ntuli of fraud, while her co-accused and their companies were convicted of money laundering-related offences under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Nkosi said investigations revealed that Ntuli abused her position as an Eskom buyer between October 2016 and May 2019 by awarding contracts to companies linked to individuals with whom she had personal relationships or associations.

"The contracts awarded to these companies amounted to R8 525 738, resulting in a reported financial loss to Eskom of the same amount," Nkosi said.

The companies identified in the investigation were Phoenix Motion Enterprise, Hustle Hard Development and Itireleng Masheleng.

Phoenix Motion Enterprise, directed by Edward George Sekatane, received contracts worth approximately R5.38 million.

Investigators also established that Sekatane continued running the company while serving a prison sentence for an armed robbery conviction.

Hustle Hard Development, directed by Mahlalela, secured contracts valued at approximately R2.23 million, while Itireleng Masheleng received contracts worth about R913 712.

Luxury Mercedes-Benz and multimillion-rand properties targeted

The Hawks have now moved to preserve assets valued at R7 746 050 following the convictions.

Nkosi said the restraint order covers four properties in Emalahleni and Middelburg, with a combined reported value of R6.82 million.

Among the properties is one situated in Aerorand, Middelburg, valued at R5.25 million, alongside properties in Emalahleni and Clewer.

"The total value of the assets is reported as R7 746 050," Nkosi said.

The movable assets include a Mercedes-Benz E63 AMG, Toyota Fortuner, Hyundai i20, Mazda Drifter, Citroën C3, Ford Bantam, Volkswagen Caddy and a motorbike trailer.

"The restraint order is a legal mechanism intended to prevent the disposal or dissipation of assets pending further proceedings," Nkosi said.

Any eventual forfeiture of the assets remains subject to further legal processes and a court determination.

Hawks warn fraudsters that convictions are not the end

Mpumalanga Hawks provincial head Major General Nico Gerber said the restraint order demonstrated the authorities' determination to pursue suspected proceeds of criminal activity beyond securing convictions.

"The outcome sends a clear message that investigations into alleged corruption and fraud do not end with identifying wrongdoing. Where the evidence supports further legal action, efforts will also be directed at preserving assets potentially linked to criminal activity," Gerber said.

The investigation was conducted by the Hawks' Secunda Serious Corruption Investigation unit, working with the Priority Crime Management Centre, Priority Crime Specialised Investigation and the National Prosecuting Authority.

The Hawks said it will continue working with prosecutors and other law-enforcement agencies to investigate financial crimes, trace suspected proceeds of unlawful activity and preserve assets where legal requirements are met.
