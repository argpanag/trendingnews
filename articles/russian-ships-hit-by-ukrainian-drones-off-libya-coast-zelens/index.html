Russian ships hit by Ukrainian drones off Libya coast, Zelensky says

TheTools · 23.11.2026 08:51 · source · permalink

A Russian warship was sunk by Ukrainian drones off the coast of Libya on Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelensky said.

The attack also hit a second Russian vessel, a cargo ship being used to transport weapons, the Ukrainian prime minister claimed.

On Sunday morning, the government that controls eastern Libya said there had been an explosion on the warship at the Benghazi port, while it was docking for fuel and supplies ahead of its return to Russia.

Meanwhile, the health ministry said it had received injured people from a commercial ship that was targeted off the coast of the port. It is not clear whether this is the same vessel as the one Ukraine has claimed responsibility for.

Moscow has not yet commented on the attack.

In a statement on Monday, Zelensky praised an "exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia's war".

Seven naval drones were deployed to target and sink the corvette Merkury, one of Russia's newest ships, Zelensky wrote. He added that a Ka-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed.

The Ukranian president said the second ship was the MV ANGARA, a Russian container carrier.

The UN mission in Libya expressed "serious concern" over the attacks, calling for "an immediate, independent and transparent investigation".

"Libya cannot afford, and must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts," the UNSMIL wrote on X.

Videos posted on social media and geolocated by BBC Verify show the ship that was struck in the morning engulfed in flames and smoke. Satellite imagery from Planet Labs PBC also shows a boat on fire at 05:47 GMT at the Benghazi port.

US-based intelligence firm Vantor collected high-resolution images that it said showed Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class corvette (Merkury) - a Russian navy ship - moored in the port on Saturday, and on Sunday showed most of the same ship capsized and sunk with only the forward bow still visible.

The eastern-based Government of National Stability (GNS) said the Russian ship - which it did not name or identify - was using the Benghazi port within the framework of an existing maritime agreement.

Sunday's incident is under investigation, and authorities are coordinating with their Russian counterparts to determine the source of the suspected attack and who is responsible for it, GNS added.

GNS condemned the attack on a ship inside a Libyan port, and called it a violation of maritime safety and an attack on their sovereignty. It said Libya was not party to the Russia-Ukraine war, or any other conflict beyond its borders.

Separately, Libya's internationally recognised government in Tripoli - the Government of National Unity (GNU) - warned against hosting foreign forces and allowing them to use facilities in a statement published by state media.

GNU said such activity raises "serious fears" about "risks that may result from involving ports and civilian facilities in activities related to external conflicts, and the resulting threat to the lives of citizens and the safety of national facilities and assets".

Libya fractured into violence and instability after its leader Muammar Gaddafi was overthrown in 2011, leading to the opposing governments.

Benghazi is more than 2,000 km (1,243 miles) away from the Russian capital Moscow.

In March, Russia accused Ukraine of targeting the Russian tanker Arctic Metagaz in the Mediterranean between Libya and Malta with "uncrewed sea drones" launched from the Libyan coast.

As well as having an economic impact on Russia, hampering the ability of their so-called shadow fleet of tankers to export oil and gas, attacks like this are seen as humiliating to the Kremlin, according to Harchaoui.

But it is not only Moscow that will want to react to this latest incident. The ship was in the region as part of a deal with the government of eastern Libya - allied with Khalifa Haftar, commander of the Libyan National Army.

Harchaoui said Haftar will take the attack as a personal slight. "You're going to have a response by the Haftar family. They will ask questions about who is hosting the Ukrainians."
