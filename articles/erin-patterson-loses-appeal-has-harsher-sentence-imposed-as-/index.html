Hi Wes and Brett,

It is a little confusing, but you must first convince the Court of Appeal that your argument has merit before they'll decide to actually deeply consider the legal ramifications of your complaint.

Erin Patterson's lawyers brought seven different reasons they believed there was a miscarriage of justice.

The Court of Appeal judges said they would only grant leave to appeal for the first two, and dismissed the other five (things like the lead prosecutor being unfair with her questioning) outright.

They then considered the first two grounds, which were:

  • The mistake that occurred with the jury staying in the same hotel as a police witness
  • The use of Patterson's mobile phone data as evidence

The judges considered these two grounds, but decided there were no issues and dismissed them because they were not convinced.

Why is the prosecution appealing?

T

By Tim Callanan

By Tim Callanan

Victoria's Director of Public Prosecutions appealed on the basis that the sentence handed down to Patterson was "manifestly inadequate".

Patterson was sentenced to life in prison with a 33-year non-parole period over the murders of relatives Don and Gail Patterson and Heather Wilkinson.

She was also convicted of the attempted murder of Heather's husband Ian Wilkinson, who survived the lunch but spent weeks recovering in hospital.

Patterson was spared the harshest penalty under the law — a life sentence with no parole — meaning with time already served she could be freed in 2056.

She would be 82 years old then.

The judge who handed down the sentence, Justice Beale, said he had given Patterson the chance for parole due to her harsher-than-usual prison conditions, which included being housed in a small cell in solitary confinement.

However, prosecutors argued this was an "error" by the judge because Corrections Victoria staff reviewed Patterson's conditions on a monthly basis.

They also argued Patterson remained remorseless over her actions and that her crimes fell into the "worst category".

Patterson's life behind bars

J

By Judd Boaz

By Judd Boaz

During both her sentencing and her appeal, Erin Patterson's lawyers told the court about her living conditions in prison, which they said were extremely challenging.

Her defence team said Patterson was forced to spend between 22 and 24 hours alone in a maximum security wing away from other prisoners due to her notoriety. They argued this constituted solitary confinement, as defined by the United Nations.

The court heard Patterson had little access to fresh air, had food delivered through a trap door, and only had permission to communicate with one other prisoner, who was a convicted terrorist.

Her lawyers said her confinement meant her prison term was all the more punishing, and meant her jail sentence was adequate.

Prosecutors disagreed, arguing Patterson's notoriety would eventually fade over time, and that she still had access to services such as a library and a chaplain.

What are the potential outcomes of today's judgments?

T

By Tim Callanan

By Tim Callanan

The three Court of Appeal judges are handing down judgments on two appeals today:

1) Erin Patterson's appeal against her convictions

2) The prosecution's appeal against her sentence

The judgment on Patterson's appeal against her convictions will be handed down first as the outcome could mean that a decision on the prosecution's appeal is not required.

The judges are ruling first on whether she can actually lodge an appeal and, if that is granted, on the outcome of any appeal.

That means there are four potential outcomes for her conviction appeal. The judges could rule:

a) Leave to appeal not granted

b) Leave to appeal granted, appeal dismissed

c) Leave to appeal granted, appeal upheld, retrial ordered

d) Leave to appeal granted, appeal upheld, acquittal entered

Should the judges hand down a judgment of c] or d], the sentence appeal will not be required.

For the sentence appeal, there are two potential outcomes:

a) The appeal is dismissed and the initial life sentence with a non-parole period of 33 years remains

b) The appeal is upheld and she is resentenced by the Court of Appeal

What was the basis for Erin Patterson's appeal?

T

By Tim Callanan

By Tim Callanan

The primary argument put forward by Erin Patterson's lawyers was that the verdicts in her triple-murder trial were compromised by a "catastrophic" jury accommodation arrangement.

Her legal team's argument centred on how the jurors considered their verdicts while housed in the same accommodation as the case's lead homicide detective and some prosecutors.

Her barrister, Richard Edney, argued this went against the notion that justice needed to be done, as well as being seen to be done in "perhaps one of the most high-profile criminal trials this state has ever had".

However, under questioning from Court of Appeal judges Lesley Taylor, Stephen McLeish and Peter Kidd, Mr Edney conceded there was no evidence police or prosecutors directly communicated with jurors.
