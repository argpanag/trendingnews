Can Carlos Repeat? Can Aryna Three-Peat? And Where Do Serena and Venus Stand? Your 2026 US Open Preview

TheTools · 31.08.2026 12:09 · source · permalink

The 146th installment of the US Open—tennis’s final Grand Slam of the season—kicked off yesterday at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in New York. (We were there for the epic, tragic, late-night, first-round five-set upset of Novak Djokovic at the hands of Mariano Navone, though we wilted and left before Venus Williams’s loss against Sofia Kenin, which ended at 2:01 am.) It returns to action today and for the next two weeks.

If demand for tickets has never been higher; hopes and expectations for players on the courts have never been more topsy-turvy.

Remember when—with the ghost of the so-called Big Three of Federer, Nadal, and Djokovic still lingering—Carlos Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner first rose to the cream of the tennis rankings? For a moment, there was a kind of despair induced by how far ahead of the pack Alcaraz and Sinner were running; tennis-watchers logically assumed that the finals of the foreseeable future would feature nobody but the two of them, swinging for the fences in a cosmos of their own creation.

Well, things haven’t quite turned out like that, have they? The two haven’t played each other since April, and with Sinner skipping New York with a knee injury, we won’t see them square off in a Grand Slam this year. Alcaraz, the tournament’s defending men’s champion, is, at long last, back on the tour after months away rehabbing a complicated right-wrist injury, but his match fitness is anything but assured—opening the Open to a host of possibilities.

Top-seeded Alexander Zverev has the most to gain: With Sinner out, Alcaraz only just back on the scene, and a very kind tournament draw, he would seem to have the easiest path to a championship on either the men’s or women’s sides. Grand Slams being complicated, multifaceted beasts, though, that outcome is anything but assured.

Could an American man step in and stop the 23-year-long title drought? (Andy Roddick was the last American man to win the US Open, in 2003.) It’s certainly possible, with eighth-seeded Ben Shelton and ninth-seeded Taylor Fritz the most obvious contenders. But there is also a small handful of players seeded just a bit lower who are capable of deep, thrilling runs.

Looking for the dark-horse, next-gen, heir-apparent player who could, just maybe, win it all? Watch the early-round matches of 10th-seeded Arthur Fils, who faces Stefanos Tsitsipas today at 11 a.m. Fils plays a thrilling game, no matter the outcome—as does 12th-seeded Rafael Jodar, who kicks off his Open tomorrow. Neither of them will likely win the whole thing, but either of them could.

On the women’s side, things are even more interesting. The world number one ranking is—after Aryna Sabalenka has held it down for more than 90 consecutive weeks—very much in play. Sabalenka hasn’t had a season to remember, and while she’d certainly settle for a third consecutive title in New York, even that might not be enough for her to hold on to the top ranking: It’s quite complicated, but if Elena Rybakina makes it as far as the semifinals, she becomes the new number one; if she doesn’t make it that far and Sabalenka, Coco Gauff, or Jessica Pegula win the tournament, they become number one.

Having said all that—and with Sabalenka’s disappointing season as the caveat here—she’s still the odds-on favorite. We already know that she’s capable of anything on the court; this will really be a matter of whether or not she can get out of the mental space that’s been sabotaging her lately.

Ready to pounce, of course, are all of the above-mentioned players, plus the rest of the draw, with Rybakina, Gauff, and Iga Swiatek the most eager, willing, and able to draw blood. The problem, particularly with Gauff and Swiatek, though: the luck of the draw, or absence thereof. Both players have a fraught path through to the finals, with Swiatek and Rybakina on course to face off in the quarterfinals, and fifth-seeded Mirra Andreeva and 10th-seeded Amanda Anisimova in the way of Gauff.

As for the outside-chancers here: Her fifth seed doesn’t exactly make her a dark horse, but Andreeva is eternally capable of punching far, far above her weight if she can maintain her focus and composure; and 11th-seeded Marta Kostyuk is having the season of her career (and is already easily past the first round).

As for the ever-popular Venus and Serena: Serena has opted not to play singles at the Open, while Venus, as mentioned, is already out of singles. They’ve teamed up to play doubles together, but we won’t find out until tomorrow who they’ll face in the opening rounds. Together, they’ve won 14 Grand Slam doubles titles, including the US Open in 1999 and 2009. Can they get it together for another great run to cap their historic careers? The odds are against it—but when have they ever played the odds?

It’s going to be an amazing fortnight—hope to see you out in Flushing. Enjoy the tennis!
