NES Token Recovery Splits Along Custody Lines as Binance and Kraken Set Different Rules — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 12.09.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

Holders of the Nesa network's NES token face starkly different recovery outcomes depending on where their assets sat when an exploit disrupted the ecosystem, with Binance Alpha applying a two-snapshot eligibility framework while Kraken has opted for a straightforward 1:1 migration on Ethereum only.

The divergence means the same token could qualify for a swap, a refund, or nothing at all, hinging on custody venue, blockchain network, and precise holding timestamps. For the roughly 700 million people across ASEAN who trade on venues like Tokocrypto, Indodax, and Coins.ph, determining which path applies has become the immediate priority.

Kraken's approach is the simpler of the two. The exchange confirmed that NES would migrate 1:1 to a new contract on Ethereum, while NES funding on BNB Chain would remain disabled going forward, according to its official incident status page. The new Ethereum contract address was published as 0x863F08A3d3B14fbD3D42fFD061D0e290F7dBC538, though the exchange's notice does not constitute an independent audit of the contract's bytecode or provenance.

Kraken's timeline began with an investigation notice on August 24, 2026, at 14:56 UTC, identifying Nesa funding on BNB Chain as affected. Withdrawals were paused on August 31 at 16:19 UTC. Ethereum NES deposits and withdrawals were scheduled to resume on September 10 at 14:00 UTC, and the exchange marked the incident resolved at 14:12 UTC that same day.

Binance Alpha's framework is more granular. CryptoSlate reported, citing a Binance notice, that the exchange is using two separate snapshots to determine eligibility: one at August 24, 2026, 14:51 UTC, and a second at September 5, 2026, 04:00 UTC, aligned with the trading suspension. To qualify for a 1:1 swap, a holder needed NES before the first cutoff and an eligible portion still held at the second snapshot.

NES acquired after the August 24 cutoff is excluded from the swap and falls under separate refund treatment. Eligible net purchasers in the specified window were told they would receive refund details by email within seven business days. Binance scheduled NES trading to reopen on September 10 at 08:00 UTC, though the reporting did not disclose the complete refund formula.

The two snapshots do not map cleanly to the two outcomes. The available reporting does not establish that one snapshot governs swaps and the other governs refunds, nor does it confirm whether holders can receive both treatments or choose between them. Binance's own announcement could not be independently read, leaving these details under secondary attribution.

Note: Kraken's migration is tied to network, not purchase date. Binance Alpha's terms are based on CryptoSlate's reporting of a Binance notice that could not be independently verified.

Nesa describes NES as the native asset used for network security, compensation of model developers and miners, and inference queryspace payments, per the project's official website. The homepage, however, carries no incident-specific migration procedure, leaving self-custody holders with the least clear path forward.

No universal self-custody migration route has been verified across Nesa's official channels. Holders who kept tokens in private wallets are advised to avoid connecting wallets or moving assets based on unverified instructions, and to confirm any contract address against official sources before interacting with it.

Several practical details remain unconfirmed. The exact snapshot dates, times, and time zones require direct confirmation against Binance's own notice. The treatment of trades and transfers between snapshots, whether distribution is automatic or requires user action, and any applicable deadlines are all open questions. Their absence in the available evidence does not mean Binance has failed to publish them.

A $286 million exploit loss figure circulating in one report title is not substantiated in that article's body or in Kraken's notice, and should not be treated as an established number. A separate CryptoSlate report on a BounceBit incident referencing roughly 286.5 million BB tokens concerns a different token in a different event.

Market data at observation showed NES trading around $0.1460 to $0.1504, with a 24-hour change ranging from a 9.2% decline to a 2.6% gain depending on the retrieval time, against a market capitalization near $20.7 million to $21.3 million. The broad crypto Fear & Greed Index read between 56 and 63, in Greed territory, a market-wide gauge rather than a measure of NES holder sentiment.

For regional platforms, the episode serves as a reminder that recovery terms are exchange policy, not insurance. As with Blockstream's stance during the Liquid incident and compensation questions raised in the DBS lawsuit in Singapore, ASEAN exchanges and their users should read exchange-specific notices closely and avoid treating swap or refund arrangements as a legal guarantee. No regulator action or statutory compensation entitlement has been identified in this case.

The near-term catalysts are procedural: confirmation of Binance's snapshot criteria, the refund-detail emails due within the reported seven-business-day window from reopening, and verification that Kraken's Ethereum funding restoration and BNB suspension hold as posted.
