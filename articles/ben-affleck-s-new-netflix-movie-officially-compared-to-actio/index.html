Ben Affleck's New Netflix Movie Officially Compared To Action Thriller Masterpiece

TheTools · 09.10.2026 20:57 · source · permalink

Ben Affleck returns to the director's chair for the first time in three years with his new Netflix film, Animals. The multi-hyphenate entertainer, who also serves as a co-writer, producer, and lead actor on the crime thriller, has spent decades building an impressive body of work, with Affleck directing many of his strongest creative efforts. His Animals co-star agrees, saying the new movie shows a skill Affleck used to extraordinary effect in one of his most acclaimed films.

Animals, a political thriller set to be released in select theaters and on Netflix on Oct. 9, 2026, stars Affleck as Los Angeles mayoral candidate Mark Kimball. He has a strained relationship with his wife Abigail, played by Kerry Washington. After their son is kidnapped, the couple scrambles to raise the ransom, exploring their own morality as devastating secrets threaten to destroy their lives. Equal parts kidnapping thriller and examination of a faltering marriage under extreme pressure, the film required a director with a confident vision. According to Animals actor Luis Gerardo Méndez, that is an arena in which Affleck thrives.

Speaking with MovieWeb, Méndez recounted his experience shooting an emotionally intense scene in the movie and the choices Affleck made that impressed him.

"Ben is a very confident director. The best example I have for that is one of the scenes that we did together in Cecilia's bedroom, when they're making these really tough decisions about what are they gonna do. I'm talking about the scenes that, as actors, you prepare for months, right? And we did one take, and Ben was like, 'I have it, that's it,' and we were sobbing, being like, 'What? Just can we do it again, please?' And he was like, 'Yeah, if you want to do it again, but I have it.' We did it again, and then he was like, 'Yeah, I have it.' It is really rare to have a director that is that ballsy, and he knows what he wants, and I think that translates to every single aspect of the movie."

The actor went on to describe Animals' gripping and relentless nature, which he says kicks into overdrive just minutes into the film, comparing its tense and captivating story to that of one of Affleck's most critically acclaimed projects.

"...after 10 minutes of the movie starting, like, you cannot move, you cannot blink, and Ben is really good at doing that. He did it in Argo, too. Argo is one of my favorite movies, too. He has a very good understanding of tension, which I think is very important."

"...after 10 minutes of the movie starting, like, you cannot move, you cannot blink, and Ben is really good at doing that. He did it in Argo, too. Argo is one of my favorite movies, too. He has a very good understanding of tension, which I think is very important."

Méndez's comparison is particularly notable, as the 2012 political espionage thriller won three Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Adapted Screenplay. The film was widely praised for expertly building suspense around a real-life historical event and never letting the tension drop, even though the outcome was already widely known.

With Méndez joining his co-stars in praising Affleck's approach to directing, Animals has plenty of support as it arrives on Netflix today. And if Animals can recreate the tense, pressure-driven scenarios that made Argo a widely celebrated film, it could join the Best Picture winner as one of the defining titles in Affleck's long filmography.
