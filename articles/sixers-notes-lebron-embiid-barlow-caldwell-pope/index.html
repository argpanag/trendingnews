Sixers Notes: LeBron, Embiid, Barlow, Caldwell-Pope

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:58 · source · permalink

The Sixers are tied for the NBA’s best record at 14-3, but they still aren’t finishing off opponents the way an elite team should, writes Kris Rhim of ESPN. The latest example came Saturday afternoon against the one-win Bucks, as Philadelphia nearly squandered a 10-point lead with a little more than two minutes remaining. The game would have gone to overtime, but Tyler Herro’s three-pointer was ruled to be a split-second after the final buzzer (Twitter video link).

“Obviously, we wanted to close the game out a lot better,” LeBron James said. “But a team like that, we know they’re scrappy. They’re going to play as hard as they can for their minutes. It’s about just getting the win, but we can learn from a lot of things that we didn’t do well tonight.”

The Sixers improved to 12-0 in clutch games, but they’ve rarely been able to pull away from their opponents. That could be the result of a revamped roster that’s still learning to play together after the offseason additions of James, Jaylen Brown, Anfernee Simons, Dean Wade and others, but James said he’s seeing progress in that area.

“I think early on, like preseason, it looked a little weird. Guys were like overpassing, you know, looking weird,” he said. “But that’s been great. And I think for the majority, our defense has been really good for the majority of the season. Tonight wasn’t so great, but we’ve been really good defensively throughout the course of the season.”

There’s more from Philadelphia:

  • After posting 26 points, nine assists and a season-high 17 rebounds on Saturday, Joel Embiid said he’s making more of an effort to control the boards, per Adam Aaronson of The Philly Voice. “I think the last few games, I’ve been more focused on boxing out my man and letting everyone else get it,” Embiid said. “And after last game, I just wanted to rebound the ball, too.”
  • Dominick Barlow was able to make his season debut after dealing with a back issue that has affected him since training camp, Aaronson states in a separate story. The 23-year-old forward, who was a regular starter last season, called the lengthy recovery process “miserable.”

    “The days just felt longer, you know? Trying to be engaged mentally, which I think I’ve done a good job with,” he said after Friday’s practice. “But the physical aspect wasn’t there. I’m somebody who’s always prided myself on competing and being available. So, not being able to do that was hard. But I’m excited to put this in the past.”

  • With Brown and Wade unavailable for Saturday’s game, free agent addition Kentavious Caldwell-Pope moved into the starting lineup and provided excellent defense along with 11 points in 35 minutes, Aaronson adds. The veteran swingman is shooting 46.3% from three-point range and has played in all 17 games.
