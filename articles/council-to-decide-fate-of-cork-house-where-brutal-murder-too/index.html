Council to decide fate of Cork house where brutal murder took place

TheTools · 14.09.2026 12:33 · source · permalink

Cork City Council will make a decision in the coming weeks on a proposal to knock down and replace a derelict old property where a horrific crime took place.

Planning permission has been sought to demolish Castlegreina House on Boreenmana Road. The property dates back to the 1900's and has been derelict for a decade after its previous owner entered a nursing home. It fell into major disrepair and became a site for squatters and antisocial behaviour. Homeless people frequently used the grounds to drink.

The large historic home, which stands out for its unusual Gothic castle-style design and decorative tower, crenellations, and parapet, was sold earlier this year for €485k. Now, its owner has applied to knock it.

The application is to demolish the existing two-storey property and outbuilding and build a new two-storey detached dwelling. The application documents note that a consulting engineer inspected the property in February 2026 and found it to be "beyond repair and is in a dangerous and unsafe condition."

It is infamous as being where the body of Cork man Francis 'Frankie' Dunne was found. In a crime that shocked the city, his body was discovered dismembered on the grounds of the period house in December 2019.

The horrific discovery was made just after Christmas, when a neighbour went to look for his missing cat on December 28.

Frankie, 64, from Knocknaheeney, spent his last Christmas with his family on the Northside just days before he was killed and dismembered by Ionut Cosmin Nicholescu.

The 30-year-old chef was found guilty and received a life sentence at the Central Criminal Court. Nicholescu, a native of Romania, had been squatting at the derelict Castlegreina House. He took advantage of Mr Dunne, who was living nearby in a support unit for persons living with addiction.

After the gruesome crime, the property was totally boarded up, and its sat empty since.

The Cork City Council planning board will deliver its decision on the demolition and construction of a new house on the site by October 5 next month.
