Suns compete well with Rockets' physicality in loss - Arizona Sports

TheTools · 26.11.2026 04:08 · source · permalink

PHOENIX SUNS

It was a good battle from the Suns in Houston to wrap up a five-game road trip.

Watch: Why the Phoenix Suns look completely different right now | 8 mins

The Phoenix Suns fly back to the Valley for the holidays with four wins on their five-game road trip, and the one loss on Wednesday still provided some positives too, a 116-104 defeat to the Houston Rockets on Wednesday.

This was a great test for the Suns, the first time this season they’ve played a marquee opponent that was at full strength.

The way they responded to that was strong. Phoenix (9-8) met and even at points surpassed Houston’s (14-5) signature physicality, doing everything it needed to in order not to get pushed around in all areas of the floor. The Suns were the better team for the majority of the first half except with rebounding, and the thing about the Rockets is they can win quarters, halves and games off just being better in that one area.

Offensive rebounds were 12-4 Rockets in the first half and the Suns’ smaller lead held for most of the half slipped away on a 20-7 close from Houston for the final 5:20 of the first half to lead by 10.

That was a buffer the Suns couldn’t afford to give up, because their shot-making wasn’t there to keep up with Houston’s league-best offense.

A Collin Gillespie and-one less than two minutes into the fourth quarter got Phoenix back within five after it had trailed by as many as 14, and a Luke Kennard middy a few minutes later had it a four-point game with under six minutes remaining.

Kevin Durant then missed an open 3, which Jalen Green used to fuel a layup in transition, making it a two-point contest.

A minute later with the Suns down three, Green and Dillon Brooks miscommunicated on a switch, leaving Fred VanVleet open for a 3 he drilled to put the Rockets back up six. Green then got caught in the air with the ball for a turnover, and Amen Thompson scored at the rim the next time down.

An eight-point gap at under four minutes left against an offense like Houston’s was the type of difference the Suns’ lack of shot-making on Wednesday wasn’t going to be able to support.

Phoenix shot 5-of-32 (16%) from 3 and its primary scorers on Wednesday — Devin Booker (6-of-18) and Green (8-of-21) — struggled to be efficient.

Khaman Maluach was fantastic with how he matched a Houston team trying to push him around. He made several plays on Wednesday strictly because of that, and was impacting the game everywhere as a true interior presence while remaining active on the perimeter too. This was the best game of his career thus far, the tape that shows his potential down the line. He finished with 12 points, six rebounds, two turnovers, a steal and a block in 25 minutes.

There were a combined 58 free throws in this one (30-28 Rockets), an affair that stayed chippy throughout and the Suns did a great job of turning that into free throws.

Offensive rebounding ended up 20-13 Houston. Alperen Sengun (7) and Steven Adams (6) made up for over half of those.

This is the type of result that will turn the dialogue around the team toward the direction of defensive rebounding. So let’s chat.

Phoenix’s roster was always going to struggle in this regard. It was 26th in defensive rebounding rate last season, making up for it inside other margins along the way so it didn’t drag the team down. This year, the Suns’ margin work isn’t as tight (specifically due to their own offensive rebounding dropping substantially), and their defensive rebounding rate is the worst in the NBA. Consistency will be more difficult to find until that is resolved.

Instinctually, the centers are the ones responsible. That, however, is not what the numbers say, and we’ll use defensive rebounding percentage to best help illustrate that.

The absence of Mark Williams looms large in theory, but Phoenix was actually a better defensive rebounding team when Williams was off the floor last season, per NBA Stats. Oso Ighodaro’s defensive rebounding percentage is the same as it was last year. So it’s not because of him statistically. Maluach’s 23.1 DREB% is right around the range of Williams’, so that’s the level you’re getting there. More or less the same.

How about the perimeter players? Miles Bridges is the one really making a push. It’s a career-high mark for him, 22.8%, just trailing Maluach for the team lead. The career numbers for Brooks and Green are right around where they sit this year. It’s actually Booker who is sporting a career-low percentage, as is Jordan Goodwin. Is that to say those two are responsible? Of course not. But a rebound here, a rebound there and suddenly you go from bottom-five to the worst team in the league.

Maluach’s development is the only real cure-all in sight. He profiles as an easy double-double candidate in the future, but he’s got some nuances to still get down on the glass and physical growth still to come.

Even if that comes for him, though, or the Suns trade for one great rebounder, they will still be poor in that department overall. It’s about making up for it elsewhere. Phoenix joins Toronto as the only two teams that sit top-6 in both turnover rate and opposing turnover rate, so that’s an outstanding job by the Suns in one of the primary margins while this one continues to work itself out.

Phoenix’s loss all but eliminated them from the NBA Cup at a record of 1-2. A two-loss team has never advanced beyond the group stage in the brief history of the tournament.
