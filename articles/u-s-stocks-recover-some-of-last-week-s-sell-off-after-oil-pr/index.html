U.S. stocks recover some of last week’s sell-off after oil prices drop

TheTools · 02.11.2026 15:05 · source · permalink

NEW YORK — The U.S. stock market on Monday is clawing back some of last week’s sell-off that was led by the tech and AI industries.

The S&P 500 rose 0.5 per cent, coming off its third straight losing week, and pulled within 2.6 per cent of its all-time high. The Dow Jones Industrial Average was up 391 points, or 0.8 per cent, as of 9:35 a.m. Eastern time, and the Nasdaq composite was 0.7 per cent higher.

Stocks got a lift from easing prices for oil, which headed back down in their latest yo-yo move on uncertainty about when the war with Iran will allow the global industry to return to normal. The price for a barrel of Brent crude, the international standard, fell 5.9 per cent to US$106.65.

Iran said Monday it had a two-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which would help the global energy industry, and resume nuclear talks with the United States. Over the weekend, Iran’s top diplomat said he hoped for “rationality” on the U.S. side as diplomatic efforts seek to avert a return to all-out war.

The price for the most actively traded contract in the Brent market has been veering between roughly US$70 and US$120 since just before the war began, and it’s been quick to reverse direction.

So have stocks in the artificial-intelligence industry. Worries are growing that all the hundreds of billions of dollars Big Tech companies are pouring into AI data centers won’t produce enough productivity and profit to make the investments worth it.

The latest profit reports from Alphabet and other Big Tech companies also showed how AI may be dragging on their own businesses. Not only did Google’s parent company burn through more cash during the latest quarter, as measured by something called free cash flow, it also reported slower-than-expected growth in its search business, which raised worries about AI cannibalizing its cash cow.

Alphabet rose 1.7 per cent Monday, recovering some of its drop of more than 12 per cent from Thursday, which was its worst in history.

Other tech giants also held steadier. Nvidia rose 1.4 per cent after its 4.2 per cent slide on Friday forced it to cede the title as Wall Street’s most valuable stock to Apple.

Outside of AI and tech, several stocks climbed after reporting better profits for the summer than analysts expected.

Marriott International rallied 5.5 per cent, for example, after reporting strength for its hotels around the world. Even in the Middle East, where the war has affected its business, CEO Anthony Capuano said that “trends improved meaningfully” from the spring.

The drop in oil prices, meanwhile, helped to lift stocks of companies with big fuel bills. United Airlines flew 5 per cent higher, Southwest Airlines climbed 3.2 per cent and cruise operator Carnival rose 3.4 per cent.

They helped offset a 5.7 per cent drop for Marvell Technology after it announced a deal to buy Lumentum, which sells lasers and other products that power the networks and infrastructure behind AI and cloud computing.

Marvell said it will give 4.5 of its shares for each Lumentum share, valuing it at roughly US$115 billion. Lumentum’s shares dropped 11 per cent.

In stock markets abroad, indexes were mixed in Europe amid mostly modest moves.

The swings were larger in Asia, where South Korea’s Kospi jumped 2.2 per cent on renewed strength for the tech giants that dominate its market.

In China, indexes rose 1.1 per cent in Shanghai and 0.4 per cent in Hong Kong to recover some of their steep recent losses. Stocks in Shanghai finished October more than 15 per cent below a peak reached in May.

The People’s Bank of China said Monday that around 100 billion yuan (US$15 billion) would be deployed under a new program could help support the market.
