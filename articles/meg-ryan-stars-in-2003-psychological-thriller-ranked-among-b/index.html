In the long list of notable crime drama films, there are some that deserve a second look, like a 2003 Meg Ryan film directed by Jane Campion that's a killer psychological thriller.

Entertainment Weekly developed a list of the best movies in the genre from classics like 1995's Heat to the well-loved Chinese film Hard Boiled from 1992. Among the list is Ryan's In the Cut, based on the 1995 novel of the same name by Susanna Moore. 

The film was directed by Campion, who won an Academy Award for her critically acclaimed film The Piano, and produced by Nicole Kidman. Following the novel's storyline, Ryan plays the role of an introverted writer and English teacher, Frannie Avery. Her student brings up the theory that famed serial killer John Wayne Gacy may not have been the one to commit the murders.

When the murders of young women somehow tie themselves to Frannie, she meets Detective Giovanni Malloy, played by Mark Ruffalo. She soon becomes entangled in the investigation and worries that the killer might set her sights on her.

In the Cut has a stellar cast of actors alongside Ruffalo and Ryan, including Jennifer Jason Leigh and Kevin Bacon. The film was given a limited release in late October before hitting audiences on Halloween, but it failed to win at the box office. It only made $23.7 million compared to its $12 million budget.

Critics commented that the director took "a stab at subverting the psycho-sexual thriller genre," but ultimately got "tangled in her own abstraction." While many praised Ryan's role and the artiness of the film, many felt the "story's unpalatable narrative holes" were a deal-breaker.

The Washington Post reviewed Ruffalo's role as having been "unsettlingly convincing as a man whose sexual magnetism lies not in leading-man good looks but in his ability to portray himself as both a protector and, if the situation warrants, a predator".

In the years since the release of In the Cut, the film has garnered a newfound retrospective popularity among movie fans. It's since been praised as a feminist erotic thriller that subverts the well-known tropes of the genre concerning the femme fatale and the male gaze.

"It is just her. Generally, the way she treats the sexuality in her other movies are cool and interesting and not exploitative," said Ryan about Champion's vision to Dark Horizons, and added that she "loved the sex scenes in this movie, because they are really, really not coy, but truly honest." 

Critics have come to list the film as one of the greatest of the 21st century, with one critic admiring how "it's the way that Campion, in scene after scene, juxtaposes powerful female desire with vulnerability that makes In the Cut a unique noir"

One interesting detail behind the film's creation is that Laurie Parker and Campion took five years to develop it. Kidman was also only credited as its producer because she had originally been considered for Ryan's role, but stepped away as she was going through a divorce.
