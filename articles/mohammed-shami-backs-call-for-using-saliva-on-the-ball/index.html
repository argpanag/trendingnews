Mohammed Shami backs call for using saliva on the ball

Mohammed Shami has no plans of slowing down. Having called time on his Test career, the India fast bowler is keen on continuing to play white-ball cricket and remains open to turning out in red-ball cricket when required. 

For now, his focus is firmly on staying fit, maintaining his rhythm and enjoying the game without putting unnecessary pressure on his body.

Shami, who announced retirement from Test cricket last month, dismissed suggestions that the decision had changed his approach towards the game. For him, the motivation to keep playing remains unchanged.

“I have said this before: I will continue playing white-ball cricket, and if required, I will play red-ball cricket as well,” he said.

While he may have moved on from Tests, Shami clearly hasn’t lost his affection for the longer format. Asked about India captain Shubman Gill’s suggestion that bowlers should be allowed to apply saliva on the ball, the pacer threw his weight behind the proposal.

“I back Shubman’s statement on allowing bowlers to apply saliva on the ball. Kuchh toh maza aye red ball mein... (Red-ball cricket should have some added fun!)”

There is also no regret about how his Test journey ended, despite having enjoyed considerable success in the format. 

When asked if he felt any disappointment about announcing his retirement after taking eight for 97 in the Ranji Trophy last season, Shami said, “I have no regrets. Whatever I got was what was destined for me. You get only what the Almighty has written in your destiny. I am happy with whatever I have received. Now, I will play as much as I am destined to and enjoy it.”

The fast bowler is also keen to remain involved in domestic cricket, where he believes his experience can be valuable to younger players. Having spent years at the highest level, Shami sees continuing to play for his State as an opportunity to give something back.

“As long as you love cricket and enjoy playing the game, you should continue. I have said this before as well. As long as you are enjoying it, you should keep playing. That is my motivation.”

“Also, if we can help a few youngsters in some way, that would be even better. Especially when you play for your State, experience matters a lot,” he added.

Shami has the flexibility to pick and choose his matches in the coming season, but that does not mean he intends to compromise on preparation.

“I have the option of picking and choosing. I can decide which matches I want to play and which ones I don’t. There is no pressure.”

He also pointed out that maintaining fitness and preparing for the demands of the game remain non-negotiable.

“I have been following the same routine for the last 15–20 years. Nothing has changed.”

While a return to international white-ball cricket remains a possibility, Shami is not spending his time thinking about the next World Cup cycle. The 2027 ODI World Cup and the 2028 T20 World Cup may be on the horizon, but Shami would rather concentrate on what is immediately in front of him.

“I have stopped thinking about all these things. Right now, my focus is on my game. Whatever decision they take will be better (sic).”

For Shami, the priorities are simple -- stay fit, keep playing and enjoy the sport.
