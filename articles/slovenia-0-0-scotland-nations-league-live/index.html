Sebastien Pocognoli: "We can take this positively"

“I think we had two games in the same game, but you can analyse in a positive way both of them,” said Scotland’s new head coach in an interview with the BBC. “First half, was not what we expected in terms of structure. We were in between for the press, actually we were a little bit surprised about the formation of Slovenia, but at half-time we fixed this and you can see that the second half was much better.

“We produced what we wanted to show every minute, but first half we learned to stick together, to defend the box, to be resilient and to be patient to change the game. We were also challenging at set-pieces. After five minutes, we had many set-pieces to defend in, so that’s the positive way to look at the first half. It was not always positive, but in the second half we had a game on the ball against a low block.

“So we had a lot of good movement through the side, through the lines and we should have scored to be honest. But the counter-press was good, the high [line] of the defence was good because the position of the players was better. Clean sheet, that’s also positive but we want to win. I think we can take this positively.”

Match report: Slovenia 0-0 Scotland

Nations League Group B1: While they may not have won, under blue skies in a dainty stadium in Ljubljana, Scotland put on a second half display that provided glimpses of optimism in respect of their world post-Steve Clarke. Ewan Murray reports from Stadion Sozice …

Billy Gilmour: “Yeah, the two halves were totally different,” says Scotland’s stand-out player. “I think in the first half we were still getting to grips with the situations off the ball and on the ball, but the second half is a good performance. Obviously, you need to score. We’ve had great chances. We never took them, but it’s definitely something to build on.

“I mean, we’re walking off gutted that we never scored, but in the second half it was really good. We came out in the second half, we were right at it. We stayed up there, we were putting pressure on them, creating chances and the final part just never came. Of course we’re disappointed, we created enough chances to maybe take the game, but yeah, we came off having given it all. I’m really happy to be back with the boys, I missed them.”

Stephen Welsh: “I thought we struggled to get into the game first half, but second half we completely dominated the game,” says the Scotland defender in an interview with the BBC. “They’ve got a top goalie who made really good saves. So many positives to take and a clean sheet as well. We changed shape a wee bit at half-time to get more control and that did really well for us. It’s a big game again on Tuesday so it’s important to recover now and go again.”

On being a right-footer playing on the left side of a back three: “I’ve played on the left-hand side for a couple of years now, so I’m comfortable doing that,” he says. “We’ve had a great week. A new manager, new way of playing and new faces in camp. It’s just the chances we need to take - the fact we’re creating the chances stands us in good stead.”

Next up for Scotland: Sebastien Pocognoli’s side face Switzerland in Hampden Park on Tuesday.

Oli McBurnie: "I'm not going to be able to sleep tonight"

“It took us a little bit of getting used to in the first half, the new formation and the way the gaffer wanted to play,” says the Scotland striker in an interview with the BBC. “I thought once we did, we looked really good. We created enough chances to kill the game and we’re really disappointed we didn’t get the three points. I think I’ve got to score and a couple of the other lads as well. So yeah, disappointing but a good start under the new manager, I think.

“I was asked to play a different position today and I quite enjoyed it. I enjoy the manager’\s ideas and we’ve had some good chats. I think it suited me in there but I’m not going to be bale to sleep tonight with them chances. It’s a tough one but I’d rather be getting in the positions than not getting the chances. Hopefully I can get another chance in the next game and put it to bed.”

Full time: Slovenia 0-0 Scotland

The spoils are shared and the deadlock remains intact in Ljubljana, where it was very much a game of two halves. Scotland were lucky not to be a goal or two down at half-time, but were hugely dominant in the second period.

Oli McBurnie and Kieron Bowie both missed excellent chances, while the Hull City striker also brought an excellent save out of Jan Oblak. It’s not a bad result for either team but Scotland will feel they should have won.

90+2 min: It’s all over in Slovenia, where Scotland have been held by their hosts.

90+1 min: A Ben Gannon Doak cross into the Slovenia penalty area is headed clear.

90 min: We’re only going to have two minutes of added time. You won’t hear any complaints from me but then, I’m not Scottish.

89 min: John McGinn floats the free-kick high towards the far post. Oblak comes for it and plucks it cleanly from the air on this occasion.

88 min: Scotland keep plugging away but Lewis Ferguson plays a poor cross that’s put out for a throw-in. John McGinn wins a free-kick.

85 min: Scotland have completely dominated this second half but missed two terrific chances that were squandered by McBurnie and Bowie. As the clock ticks on, can they find a late winner?

83 min: Robertson curls the ball into the mixer, where Oblak comes for it but doesn’t get it. Scotland recycle the ball and Stephen Welsh lashes the ball over the bar from distance. BOwie flung himself to the ground in the penalty area as the cross came in, claiming to have been fouled by Zabukovnik. Reader, he wasn’t.

81 min: Into the final 10 minutes we go and Slovenia have made another substitution: Lovric for Cerin. McBurnie has a shot blocked and Zarnicek is booked for a foul on Robertson. Free-kick for Scotland, wide on the left.

79 min: Ben Gannon Doak is on for Hickey and immediately gets involved. He stands the ball up at the far post with a cross from the right but it’s cleared.

78 min: Crikey! Bowie misses a sitter, latching on to a Gilmour pass and curling a shot wide of the upright from 12 yards out with only Oblak to beat and the goal at his mercy. It’s an awful miss.

77 min: Bowie has looked lively since coming on and now forces Oblak to cough up a cheap throw-in for Scotland with an undustrious press.

75 min: Slovenian double-substitution: Vipotnic and Balkovec on for Sporar and Brekalo.

73 min: Ferguson’s delivery is sublime and met by the head of McBurnie, whose header brings a reflex save out of Oblak from point-black range. Scotland are not so much knocking on the door as trying to kick it open.

72 min: Lewis Ferguson tries his luck with a pot-shot from distance but his effort is blocked. Moments later, Stephen Welsh picks out Bowie with a terrific slide-rule pass. The substitute is unable to lift the ball over Oblak, who puts it out for a corner.

70 min: Kieron Bowie chops inside a defender and sends a low shot straight at Oblak. The susbtitute kicks out in frsutration at a divot as he too glares accusingly at the cut-up pitch.

66 min: Kieron Bowie is on for Robbie Ure and Scotland have another corner. McGinn’s delivery is excellent and met by Aaron Hickey. He sends his header drifting narrowly wide of the far post.

64 min: Scotland finally win a corner and have to wait to take it as Slovenia make a double-substitution. Zec and Zabukovnik come on for Drkusij and Elsnik. Nothing comes of the set-piece.

62 min: McBurnie connects with a McGinn cross and his header goes over the bar off the head of David Brekalo. Slovenia are awarded a goal-kick, much to McBurnie’s disgust. Scotland’s wait for a first corner goes on.

61 min: Ure latches on to a first-time pass from GIlmour and wins a corner for Scotland, their first of the match. Actually it isn’t – the Sevilla man was offside.

59 min: Gilmour and McBurnie combine to good effect on the edge of the Slovenia penalty area but the Napoli midfielder’s low shot doesn’t trouble Oblak, who gets down to save comfortably.

59 min: Scotland have been much improved in this second half but with their high line, still look extremely vulnerable to counter-attacks.

56 min: John McGinn is clattered by Adam Cerin and wins a free-kick near the centre-circle in the Slovenia half. Lewis Ferguson lofts the ball into the penalty area, where Oblak comes for it and doesn’t get it. Stephen Welsh tries to lob the loose ball towards the vacant goal but Vanja Drkusic heads it away and wins a free-kick.

54 min: McGinn tries to stand the ball up for McBurnie near the byline and the Scotland striker leaps to contest it with Oblak. The Slovenian looked to have carried it wide for a corner but wins a free-kick because an outfield player had the audacity to jump in the same postal district as him. Goalkeepers, eh?

51 min: Oof! Oli McBurnie is played in behind the Slovenia defence and finds himself in a one-on-one with Oblak. The Slovenia goalkeeper’s legs are gaping and you could drive a bus between them, but McBurnie tries to take the ball around him instead of going for the nutmeg. Oblak almost certainly can’t believe his luck as he plucks the ball off the Scottish striker’s toe.

50 min: Scotland have started the second half on the front foot but are struggling to create anything much in the way an opening. They’re playing with a high line today but their wing-backs Robertson and Aaron Hickey are struggling to have any sort of impact on the game.

46 min: John McGinn spins his man, gallops up the right flank and plays the ball inside to Billy Gilmour. His pass wide to Andy Robertson takes a massive bobble off the poor surface and the Scottish captain is unable to control the ball.

Second half: Slovenia 0-0 Scotland

46 min: Scotland get the ball rolling, with no changes in personnel on either side and the sound of bagpipes audible in the Ljubljana air.

Half-time: Slovenia 0-0 Scotland

Peeeeeeeep! It’s scoreless at the break although both teams have hit the woodwork. Scotland finished the half with something of a flourish but were poor for the first 40 minutes. The Slovenian trio of Matko, Sturm and Sporar have caused Scotland’s defence all manner of problems and will rue their missed chances during a long period of first-half dominance.

45 min: Another excellent pass from McGinn and Robbie Ure’s low daisy-cutter from distance hits the foot of the right post. Much, much better from Scotland.

43 min: An excellent pass from John McGinn sends Robbie Ure on his way down the left and his cross drops at the feet of McBurnie whose back is to goal. He tees up Hickey, whose shot is blocked.

42 min: Andraz Sporar wins a free-kick wide on the left after the referee unfairly judges Aaron Hickey to have fouled him as the pair contested a 50-50 ball. Nothing comes of it.

40 min: We’re approaching half-time and Scotland have yet to have a shot on or off-target. They’ve had one touch of the ball in the Slovenia penalty area.

37 min: Gunn races off his line to collect a free-kick but gets nowhere near it. His blushes are spared by the linesman’s offside flag. The pitch at the Sozice Stadium is cutting up really badly.
