UK Begins Construction on Next National Supercomputer in Edinburgh

TheTools · 23.09.2026 10:53 · source · permalink

Construction on the UK's next national supercomputer reached a major milestone at the University of Edinburgh as Artificial Intelligence Minister Kanishka Narayan signed a commemorative steel beam, completing the facility's structural frame ahead of a planned 2028 operational launch.

Building contractor Robertson Construction Central East is erecting the facility, which spans roughly the size of a large supermarket, to house the nation's most powerful computing system. The project is funded by the UK Government through UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) as a joint initiative with the University of Edinburgh's EPCC.

Once operational, the supercomputer will support scientific research in sustainable aircraft engineering, flood defense modeling, volcanic activity analysis, and extreme weather forecasting. Medical researchers at the site demonstrated how high-performance computing currently assists in analyzing antibiotic resistance, identifying COVID-19 treatments, and evaluating stroke and dementia through population-scale data.

Government officials highlighted the strategic importance of expanding national computational capacity to drive scientific and economic growth.

"Seeing construction underway in Edinburgh is a powerful reminder that the UK’s ambition to build world-leading compute capability is becoming a reality. This Next National Supercomputer will be a vital national asset, giving researchers and companies the power to pursue transformational discoveries on British shores and strengthening our position at the forefront of global science," said Kanishka Narayan, AI minister.

University leadership emphasized the regional impact of the project and ongoing collaboration with government bodies to support technical innovation.

"This is an exciting moment for Edinburgh and the UK as the construction on the external build for the Next National Supercomputer nears completion. It has been a privilege to host Minister Narayan at the site, showcasing Edinburgh’s globally-impactful contributions in health innovation, supercomputing, AI and advanced technologies. We look forward to continuing to work with the UK Government to deliver on their priorities, boosting economic growth in our region and across the UK," said Professor Kim Graham, Provost at the University of Edinburgh.

Project managers noted that the completion of the structural frame signals a smooth transition toward the upcoming system installation phase.

"Now that the steel frame has been completed on the building, getting the Next National Supercomputer online feels tantalisingly close. It’s been fantastic to see construction progress smoothly and I look forward to working with our partners at UKRI and the UK Government as we progress towards installation and switch on," said Professor Mark Parsons, director of EPCC at the University of Edinburgh.

The construction partner acknowledged the continuation of institutional developments aimed at enhancing UK research capabilities.

"The University of Edinburgh and Robertson have shared a longstanding partnership and continue to deliver high-quality projects together that support research, innovation and discovery. It was a pleasure to welcome the Minister to site and showcase the strong progress being made on this nationally significant project," said Elliot Robertson, chief executive officer of Robertson Group.

The completed supercomputer is scheduled to go online in early 2028, providing compute access to academic and industry researchers nationwide.
