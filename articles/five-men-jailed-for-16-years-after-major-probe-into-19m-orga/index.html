Five men jailed for 16 years after major probe into £19m organised crime network

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:42 · source · permalink

Five men have been jailed for more than 16 years for serious and organised crime offences following a major investigation in Scotland.

Anthony Carroll Snr, 57, pleaded guilty to directing serious organised crime at the High Court in Glasgow in August.

He was found to have controlled around £19 million between January and May 2020.

Carroll Snr was sentenced to four and a half years in prison and given a Serious Crime Prevention Order (SCPO) for five years following his release.

Anthony Carroll Jnr, 37, Michael Monaghan, 37, Ryan Carberry, 46, and Marc Cooper, 34, also admitted involvement in serious and organised crime at the same hearing.

Carroll Jnr and Monaghan were each sentenced to three years in prison.

Carberry received three years and three months, while Cooper was jailed for two years and nine months.

All four were given SCPOs for four years after their release.

The offences related to the collection, storage, and movement of criminal cash between January and June 2020.

The convictions followed Operation Venetic, a Europe-wide investigation targeting organised crime groups using encrypted devices.

Detective Inspector Kevin Hughes, from the Organised Crime and Counter Terrorism Unit, said: "We work tirelessly every day to disrupt the activities of those involved in serious organised crime, who think they are above the law, and in some way untouchable.

"These convictions and sentences show this is not the case.

"This was a complex investigation, which involved a lot of work going on behind the scenes, including exploring forensic opportunities and analysis of digital devices.

"While this may not be always be as visible as some of our other tactics, please be assured we work tirelessly to build cases against those involved in organised crime.

"Today's sentences also highlight Police Scotland's commitment to the Serious and Organised Crime Taskforce and its national strategy.

"Information and support from the public is vital to our work and I would encourage anyone with concerns about serious organised crime.

"Alternatively, you can call Crimestoppers on 0800 555 111 where information can be given anonymously."

Prosecutor Sineidin Corrins, of the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS), said: "The public should be under no illusions that this kind of criminality is a blight on our communities.

"I hope these convictions and the sentences send a strong message to others involved in this kind of criminal behaviour and demonstrates the ability of police and COPFS to investigate, prepare and prosecute crime of this nature.

"Money laundering is not a victimless crime and plays an integral role in a complex, large-scale operation which facilitates the criminal activity of others.

"The Crown will continue working with the police and other agencies as a member of Scotland's Serious and Organised Crime Taskforce to ensure these crimes are detected and those responsible prosecuted using all measures at our disposal."

The court heard the five men had been kept under surveillance as part of Operation Delfin, an investigation into an organised crime group involved in the large-scale distribution of drugs and the collection, counting and storage of criminal cash.

Prosecutor Leanne Cross said the group's activities had a "very significant impact on local communities in Scotland".

Members of the gang communicated using the encrypted EncroChat network before it was dismantled by law enforcement in 2020.

Carroll Snr, from Milton, Glasgow, used the handle "Gorilla Caustic", while his son, from Maryhill, was known as "Livid Buffalo".

Carberry, from Kirkintilloch, used the handles "Shiptender" and "Luxury Squirrel", Cooper, from Maryhill, was known as "Lunatic Turtle", and Monaghan, from Drumchapel, used "Clifftender".

The court heard Carroll Snr was responsible for counting, storing and distributing large amounts of criminal cash and directed the other four men in their roles.

He, in turn, answered to senior figures in the organised crime group who were largely based overseas.

Carroll Snr kept records of the money under his control and sent lists to senior gang members through EncroChat.

The figures showed £4m coming into the operation in January 2020, £3m the following month, £2.6m in March, around £4m in April and £4.8m during the first half of May.

These sums were recorded as payments linked to cocaine, while another list showed around £1m from cannabis trafficking in early 2020.

None of the five men was involved in the supply or storage of drugs.

Police later discovered £780,000 hidden beneath floorboards at Carroll Jnr's home in May 2020.

The court heard this came shortly after Carroll Snr instructed his son to remove money from his home following police action against two other gang members.

Officers subsequently searched Carroll Snr's property, where they seized mobile phones, £5,000 and just over €1,000 from inside a cooker hood.

Carberry was described in court as a "cash collector" who helped organise 75 collections worth more than £6m.

Police searching his partner's home in June 2020 discovered £195,000 inside rucksacks.

Cooper was responsible for one of the gang's "cash counting houses" and reported back to Carroll Snr.

One of his final messages showed he had counted £1.8m in a matter of weeks during May and June 2020.

Monaghan carried out a similar role, with police recovering two cash-counting machines from a bedroom cupboard at his home in June 2020.

The court heard cash collected by members of the group was passed to counters before being "funnelled" to Carroll Snr and then passed to safehouse holders for onward transportation.

The five men were paid between £500 and £1,200 a week for their involvement.

Sentencing them, Judge Lord Scott said: "You each took part knowingly for months in a significant organised crime operation.

"Based on the snapshots and the scale of the money involved shows that it was a multi-million-pound operation.

"Carroll snr your role was significant. You came to be trusted with important parts of the working of the organised crime group.

"The other four, your roles might be lesser roles but serious organised crime groups would not operate as they do without people like you prepared to carry out lesser but crucial roles.

"It is necessary to punish you and deter you and others from behaving in this way."

Two other men linked to the criminal cash operation were jailed earlier this year.

Alistair MacKenzie, 60, and Daniel Kerrigan, 56, were arrested following a raid on a warehouse in Clydebank, West Dunbartonshire, in May 2020.

The court heard around £3m had passed through the counting house.

MacKenzie was jailed for five years and two months and Kerrigan for four years in January.
