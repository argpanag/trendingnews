Doctor Doom Now Speaks Japanese: First Listen to the 'Avengers: Doomsday' Dub Trailer and Its Emotional Tribute

TheTools · 03.11.2026 08:53 · source · permalink

A masked villain lives and dies by his voice, and few Marvel characters prove that better than Doctor Doom. Since Robert Downey Jr. took on the role, fans have debated every syllable of his low, Slavic-tinged delivery. Now audiences in Japan can hear how the character sounds in their own language, thanks to a new dubbed trailer for ‘Avengers: Doomsday’.

The trailer shows how seriously Marvel is treating localization for the film. A previous announcement revealed that 29 Japanese voice actors had been cast, including Yuichi Nakamura returning as Steve Rogers. The new footage is the first real chance to hear that cast at work.

The standout moment is the very first one. In a short clip from the trailer, Doom stands before a ruined landscape lined with towering, hulking figures, and a Japanese subtitle appears as he speaks, reading roughly “Yes, it is I…” The on-screen text also confirms the film opens in Japan on December 18, the same day as the United States.

The man behind that voice is veteran voice actor Toshiyuki Morikawa, who will perform Doctor Doom in the Japanese dub. Downey Jr. announced the casting himself in a special video message for Japanese fans, calling the news the best and thanking Morikawa. He said he looks forward to bringing the performance to Japanese audiences.

Morikawa has said he was moved by the message, joking that it surely wasn’t AI. According to Marvel’s Japanese site, he also described the role as the chance to deliver the culmination of 40 years as a voice actor. He added that the footage he saw far exceeded his expectations.

The announcement carries extra emotional weight because of who came before. Keiji Fujiwara voiced Downey Jr.’s characters in Japan starting with ‘Iron Man’, and he continued through ‘Avengers: Endgame’. He died on April 12, 2020, at age 55 after a battle with cancer.

Downey Jr. paid tribute in the message, saying Fujiwara’s will would be carried on. Morikawa, who has also taken on Fujiwara’s role as Hiroshi Nohara in ‘Crayon Shin-chan’, said the two were from the same cohort of newcomers in the industry. He said he wants to honor Fujiwara’s feelings in the performance and has a strong desire not to let anyone down.

Doctor Doom is a particularly tough role for a dub. The character wears a mask, so his voice carries most of his presence. Downey Jr.’s own take features a deep Slavic accent that sparked heated debate after the first full trailer, with some fans praising the clear separation from Tony Stark and others joking about the sound.

A Japanese dub has to capture that same menace without a literal accent. Morikawa will need to convey authority and threat through tone alone, which makes the casting a notable choice. If the first few seconds are any indication, the performance aims for the same cold, regal register.

Alongside the trailer, Marvel Japan released a set of format-specific posters. They include versions for INFINITY VISION, which uses a stained-glass design, as well as Dolby Cinema, 4DX, SCREENX and Premium Large Format screenings. The artwork shows Doom in armored and masked imagery.

The stained-glass design echoes the look of earlier key art for the film, and it suggests Marvel is leaning into a regal, almost gothic visual identity for its villain. The wide range of formats also reflects how much effort is going into the theatrical rollout.

Dubbed releases are a major part of the Marvel audience in Japan, and the film opens there on the same day as the US. That makes the dub cast and trailer a central part of the marketing, not an afterthought. Downey Jr.’s personal involvement signals how much the star values that audience.
