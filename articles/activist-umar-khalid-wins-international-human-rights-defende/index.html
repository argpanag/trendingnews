Activist Umar Khalid wins international human rights defender award

TheTools · 10.10.2026 00:37 · source · permalink

His incarceration is evidence of India’s ‘expanding use of anti-terror laws against dissent’, said Ireland-based organisation Front Line Defenders.

Activist Umar Khalid, who has been in prison for more than six years in connection with the 2020 Delhi riots conspiracy case, won the 2026 Award for Human Rights Defenders at Risk on Thursday.

The award is given out by the Ireland-based human rights organisation Front Line Defenders. It was presented at a ceremony in Dublin, which Khalid could not attend.

He was arrested on September 13, 2020, and has remained in jail since then without trial.

The other recipients of this year’s award are Ahmadreza Haeri, an anti-death penalty campaigner from Iran, Laurinda Manuel Gouveia, a feminist pro-participatory democracy defender from Angola, Adda Vyctoria Caetano, a Black trans woman leader from Brazil, and Ljiljana Nešić, a feminist peacebuilder from Serbia.

The police had arrested Khalid in connection with the communal violence that broke out in North East Delhi in February 2020 between supporters of the contentious Citizenship Amendment Act and those opposing it. The violence had left 53 dead and hundreds injured. Most of those killed were Muslims.

Khalid, activist Sharjeel Imam and the others accused in the matter have been charged under the Unlawful Activities Prevention Act, the Prevention of Damage to Public Property Act, the Arms Act and sections of the Indian Penal Code.

The police have claimed that the violence was part of a larger conspiracy to defame the Narendra Modi government and was planned by those who organised the protests against the amended Citizenship Act.

Front Line Defenders said that Khalid’s continued imprisonment was “evidence of a wider moment in India”, which is characterised by “the expanding use of anti-terror laws against dissent, the role of a compliant media in shaping public perception, and a pattern” of using restrictive bail provisions “as a form of punishment before, or entirely without, a conviction”.

“That pattern disproportionately affects human rights defenders, journalists and students who exercise their right to peaceful assembly and free expression,” added the organisation.

It also said that Khalid’s speech in Amravati in February 2020, which the prosecution holds as evidence of a “premeditated” conspiracy, does not amount to incitement.

In his speech, Khalid used the phrases “inquilabi salaam”, or revolutionary salute, and “krantikari istiqbal”, or revolutionary welcome, while praising the crowd for dissenting peacefully against the Citizenship Amendment Act.

He had also invoked Mahatma Gandhi and called for non-violent protests to coincide with the visit of the United States President Donald Trump in February that year.

On September 30, the Delhi High Court denied bail to Khalid and Imam for the third time.

The High Court said that it was “bound by judicial discipline” and could not modify conditions imposed by the Supreme Court in its January 5 order concerning the two men.

In January, the Supreme Court denied bail to Khalid and Imam, but granted relief to activists Gulfisha Fatima, Meera Haider, Shifa-Ur-Rehman, Muhammad Saleem Khan and Shadab Ahmed.

It had said that Imam and Khalid could renew their bail pleas after the examination of protected witnesses was completed or after one year, whichever came earlier.
