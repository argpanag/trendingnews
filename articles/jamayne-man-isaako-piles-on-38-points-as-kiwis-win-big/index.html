Jamayne man: Isaako piles on 38 points as Kiwis win big

TheTools · 02.11.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

Back

A rousing Test debut by Tanner Stowers-Smith and a record 38-point haul from Jamayne Isaako has powered the Kiwis to a 82-16 victory over Cook Islands in Christchurch.

Coming off a narrow loss to Australia in their tournament opener the Kiwis were keen to get their campaign back on track and they did that with a 14-tries-to-three romp.

Stowers-Smith entered the field around the half-hour mark and needed just five minutes to make his mark when he powered over from close range after taking a pass from crafty No.9 Jeremy Marshall-King.

The 22-year-old Warrior was in again in the 50th minute when he proved too strong for the tiring Aitu defence.

Player of the match Casey McLean also helped himself to a double, taking his career tally to 11 tries in just six Tests, while Ronaldo Mulitalo also crossed twice and Isaako snared a hat-trick.

The Kiwis didn't have it all their own way in the first half as an Esom Ioka hat-trick had Cook Islands daring to dream.

After Isaako and Mulitalo had scored early, Ioka picked off an intercept on his own 10m line and went the distance to put the Aitu on the board.

Five minutes later he had a second courtesy of a pinpoint crossfield kick from veteran playmaker Brad Takairangi before another intercept try had Cook Islands ahead 16-12 after 20 minutes.

Isaako made it a double in the 24th minute when he got around the outside of Cameron Brown and crawled over in the corner before converting from wide out to give the Kiwis the lead at 18-16.

Some sweet ball-play from Kodi Nikorima to put Scott Sorensen over on the left edge saw the Kiwis extend their lead to 24-16 before Stowers-Smith got his first.

With half-time approaching McLean finished off a brilliant move started by Erin Clark with a deft offload for Keano Kini before Kodi Nikorima also played his part.

The Kiwis made the ideal start to the second term when Kini burst through a poor attempted tackle by Maui Winitana-Patelesio and dotted down to make it 42-16 after Isaako's seventh successful conversion.

Stowers-Smith completed his double in the 50th minute before Nikorima outpointed Val Holmes in the air and scooted away for the Kiwis' ninth try.

Next to hit the scoreboard were McLean in the 59th minute and Mulitalo in the 65th minute before Isaako completed his hat-trick five minutes from full-time and banged over the conversion to give him 38 points, eclipsing the previous Kiwis record of 32 points set by Tasesa Lavea against Cook Islands in 2000.

Charnze Nicoll-Klokstad rounded out the scoring in the 79th minute as the Kiwis fine-tuned their game ahead of a Round 3 clash with Fiji on the Gold Coast.

Cook Islands will head to Townsville to take on defending champions Australia as they look to finish their tournament on a high.

Kiwis thrash Fiji Bati to advance to semis

New Zealand winger Ronaldo Mulitalo and Fiji halfback Brandon Wakeham were sent from the field at Cbus Super Stadium on Saturday as the Kiwis tuned up for the semi-finals with a 78-0 smashing of the Bati.

With the Kiwis cruising at 48-0 the match exploded in the 52nd minute when Mulitalo shot in off his wing and made high contact with Ronald Philitoga, who was ruled out of the rest of the game.

Wakeham came in to remonstrate with Mulitalo and threw a punch, leading to him being marched.

With both sides down to 12 men the Kiwis ran in five more tries, with gun centre Casey McLean bagging four to take his record to 15 tries in seven Tests.

While Mulitalo faces a nervous wait for Sunday's judiciary charges his team-mates will revel in the comprehensive nature of the victory that sends an ominous warning to their World Cup rivals.

Chasing their first World Cup title since 2008, the Kiwis' 13-try romp was started and finished by Keano Kini, who racked up 260 run metres and seven tackle breaks.

The clash at Cbus Super Stadium was a shootout for a semi-final berth but in the end the Bati simply had no answers as the loss of Api Koroisau (jaw) and Semi Valemei (head knock) in the first half left them rattled.

By the time the half-time siren sounded the Kiwis had racked up a 48-0 lead as Kini, McLean and Mulitalo broke tackles at will and Naufahu Whyte continued to enhance his reputation as one of the game's elite big men.

Skipper James Fisher-Harris got in on the action with his first Test try since the 2023 Pacific Championships against Samoa, trailing through to pounce on the ball after Mulitalo had tapped back a Jahrome Hughes bomb.

Hughes finished the day with three try assists as he tuned up for the knockout stage of the tournament where the Kiwis are a chance of welcoming back superstar Kalyn Ponga from a broken arm.

Ponga suffered the injury in the tournament opener against Australia on October 15 and most expected he was done for the campaign but the Knights No.1 has worked tirelessly on his rehab and looms as a real X-factor for the Kiwis next week.

Kiwi Ferns secure semi final spot with win in Gold Coast

The Kiwi Ferns have finished the pool stages of the Rugby League World Cup with a dominant 40-12 win over Papua New Guinea in the Gold Coast. This secures their spot in the semi-finals where they will face Fetu Samoa in Newcastle, Australia.

In damp conditions, it was a tug-of-war for the first half, but a try from Kaiya Atai just before the break pulled the Kiwi Ferns ahead to 14-12.

Opening the second half strongly, NRLW Premiership winner Otesa Pule scored a barnstorming 60m runaway try, which set the tone for the final 35 minutes.

Mackenzie Wiki followed with her second try, and fellow Canberra Raiders winger Leianne Tufuga ran in another. Two tries from Abigail Roache sealed the deal for the New Zealand side, with Papua New Guinea unable to retaliate.

Head Coach Ricky Henry said: “They turned up ready to play and put a bit of pressure on us, but then I thought the second half for us, [we] definitely got on top and were able to maintain our composure and start to put in some tries. So really happy with the second half, but obviously there are things we need to work on.”

The strong attacking game saw the Kiwi Ferns run 1313 metres, to Papua New Guinea’s 756, and make 11 line breaks while their opposition managed one.

Kiwi Ferns fullback Apii Nicholls took out Player of the Match, leading the stats boards for try assists (2), run meters (200), and runs (17).

The Kiwi Ferns v Fetu Samoa semi-final will kick off at 5.45pm AEDT on Saturday 7 November at McDonald Jones Stadium, Newcastle.

Like mother, like daughter: Kaiyah Atai carries on a proud Ferns legacy

Nine years ago, Kaiyah Atai was in the Suncorp Stadium grandstand to see the New Zealand women’s team go down in a classic Rugby League World Cup final against Australia.

The Kiwi Ferns newcomer is eyeing the opportunity to run onto the field at the same venue on November 15, most likely against the Jillaroos in a fifth successive trans-Tasman World Cup decider.  

Kaiyah’s mum, Sharlene Atai (née Johnson), was part of New Zealand’s 2017 World Cup squad – the conclusion of a remarkable international career spanning 17 seasons. The pair created history this month as the Kiwi Ferns’ first-ever mother-daughter representatives.  

“It was a very proud moment for myself and my family,” Kaiyah told NRL.com.

“My mum was very happy when I chose to play for Kiwi Ferns this World Cup, we always talk about history and just to be a part of that, especially in the black jersey, is so cool.”

Sharlene was a winger as New Zealand defeated Great Britain in the inaugural women’s Rugby League World Cup final in 2000 – less than a year before Kaiyah was born.  

Family commitments ruled her out of the Kiwi Ferns’ 2003 and 2013 RLWC campaigns, but she was on the flank as they routed the Jillaroos 34-0 in the 2008 final at Suncorp Stadium. At the age of 40, she ended a seven-year hiatus from the national side to win selection for the 2017 World Cup.  

“I think the only memory I have from her playing in the World Cup was in 2017,” Kaiyah said.

“I flew over to support her in the final – unfortunately they lost, but it was just so cool to be around that environment when I was young. And some of the Kiwi Ferns she played with, I'm now playing with in this World Cup.

“It’s such an honour to represent the Kiwi Ferns, and I think just being around all the girls has made it really easy for us debutants, being new into camp, and I’m really enjoying it.”

The Warriors back-rower made her Kiwi Ferns debut in their torrid tournament-opening win over Fiji in Newcastle, lining up alongside 2017 survivors Raecene McGregor, Georgia Hale and Apii Nicholls.  

Atai was a standout in New Zealand’s record-breaking 92-0 win over France in Christchurch, scoring a try, making three line breaks and producing a try assist.  

“We had to hold our nerve there, [France] showed us very different defence but we still stuck to our game plan, which I think our coach would be very proud of," she said.

A star in the Auckland competition with Richmond Roses and on the New Zealand domestic rep scene, she turned out for Cook Islands in 2023-24 prior to making her NRLW debut.  

“It’s so cool that I get to represent both my cultures,” she said.

“Obviously I was brought up in New Zealand, but I've lived in the Cook Islands for a bit. My dad's all Cook Island heritage, and whatever jersey I put on, I'm very proud to represent where I'm from.”

Atai has used the Warriors’ reintroduction to the NRLW as a springboard to becoming one of the game’s rising back-row talents.  

She was named the Warriors’ rookie of the year in 2025, while her industrious, high-quality performances – averaging 97 metres and 29 tackles a game – garnered the player of the year award this season for a team that collectively fell short of expectations.

“I played footy when I was 18 in New Zealand, the Warriors weren't back in the comp and we didn't have a team until two years ago," she said.

“So I guess that was my pathway, and then once the Warriors finally got back in I got a call from the coach. I’m just very proud that I get to represent home, the Warriors being the only New Zealand team [in the NRLW].

“It was kind of a tough season, but to get those honours was something really cool, and I think I can be proud of that. A disappointing season [for the team] but I think that's only going to make us hungrier for next year, so it’ll be a big off-season ahead after the World Cup.”

Following the wake-up call provided by World Cup debutants Fiji – recovering from an error-strewn display to prevail 18-12 – the Kiwi Ferns used the week on home soil to recalibrate and refocus.  

“It was good to just soak it all up, our second week in camp and our only home game in Christchurch,” Atai said.

“The weather, the fans and just the buzz around the town was so cool for us, and I think it showed in our performance as well.  

“But the rest of our games are in Australia, so we'll just need to reset and keep our momentum going.”

The Kiwi Ferns will look to clinch top spot in Pool B when they take on PNG Orchids as part of Saturday's triple-header at Cbus Super Stadium.

While a semi-final against Samoa or England looms and potential showdown with Australia on November 15 is the ultimate goal, Atai says the Ricky Henry-coached Kiwi Ferns can’t afford to look any further ahead than their next assignment.  

"We'll take a lot of confidence out of our performance [against France] and build into next week. We're here at a World Cup for a reason, and we're wanting to make that final and push the Aussies, who’ve won the last three World Cups," she said.

“You never take any side lightly and we give respect to all of the teams we're playing. It was a close one against Fiji, and we still want to stick to our game plan against PNG and come off with another win while still sticking to our processes.”

Whutupōro Rīki Whaikaha o Aotearoa, Physical Disability Rugby League NZ Kiwis prepare for World Cup

The inclusivity of rugby league will be on full display during IRL Rugby League World Cup 2026 with players in the second Physical Disability Rugby League World Cup to share the same stage as the stars of the Men’s and Women’s tournaments.

As it was in England in 2022, the PDRL World Cup will be played alongside the Men’s, Women’s and Wheelchair World Cups, with the final to be played at Allianz Stadium on November 8 before the Men’s and Women’s semi-finals.

The tournament featuring Australia, England and New Zealand will be held at AIIMS Stadium in Cabramatta, with each nation to play games against the other two.

The top two teams will meet in the final at Allianz Stadium on November 8.

“Physical Disability Rugby League Australia is proud to host the Physical Disability Rugby League (PDRL) World Cup at AIIMS Stadium Cabramatta, where PDRL was born 16 years ago,” NSW PDRL Vice-Chair Keith Povah said.

“Playing on the same global stage as the Men's, Women's, and Wheelchair tournaments means everything to our playing group because it gives them an opportunity to showcase their skills alongside the whole rugby league family.”

England are the current champions after defeating New Zealand 42-10 in the final of the inaugural 2022 PDRL World Cup at Halliwell Jones Stadium in Warrington.

“The second PDRL World Cup marks a significant landmark for the continued progress of PDRL across the World. Being placed on the same stage as the Men’s, Women’s and Wheelchair World Cups once more allows all to see that rugby league is a sport for them,” RFL National Inclusion Manager Chris Godfrey said.

“The event will shine a spotlight on these fantastic athletes and spark the imagination of everyone to dream of what is possible for them.”

The 2026 PDRL World Cup will kick off on October 30 with Australia taking on England, who then play New Zealand on November 2 before the trans-Tasman rivals clash on November 5.

"Physical Disability Rugby League New Zealand is thrilled to be part of the second Physical Disability Rugby League World Cup in Sydney in October and November 2026,” NZPDRL Chair Sandra Hickey said.

“Playing alongside the men’s, women’s and wheelchair competitions is a proud moment that celebrates the many forms of rugby league and the inclusive spirit of the game."

Kiwi Ferns squad named for clash with Papua New Guinea

Gold Coast-fostered talent, Zahara Temara, will start for the Kiwi Ferns this week in the team’s final pool match of the Rugby League World Cup against Papua New Guinea.

Temara attended Keebra State High School, a premier rugby league school based in the Gold Coast, from the age of 11 and is among many talents the school has produced, including teammate Chantay Kiria-Ratu, former Kiwi Fern Roxy Murdoch, current Kiwis halfback Te Maire Martin and former Kiwis captain Benji Marshall.

Temara and Kiria-Ratu returned to the school this week to connect with the aspiring young women hoping to also represent their country in rugby league.

Temara said it was one of the most rewarding things to have seen the Keebra girls rugby league programme grow to where it is now. 

“When I was there, we never had a footy programme; it was just the boys. To see the girls have a programme, and see them treated exactly like the boys are, that was pretty special to see.

“The mindset that the girls have and what the girls are doing is exactly what we're doing in camp - they're being professional, they're cutting clips, they're looking at shapes and learning wrestle and that type of thing. They've just started so much earlier and they'll probably kill it because they've had a taste of that professionalism. When they come here, it won't be different.”

Talent rotations by Head Coach Ricky Henry see Leianne Tufuga and Abigail Roache also return to the starting lineup at left wing and centre, respectively, with Shannon Mato back at prop. Meanwhile, the relentless Shaniece Monschau earns her first start in the Kiwi Ferns jersey at second row.

“I’m really happy with how the squad is progressing, and the performance last week saw us solidify a lot of our processes,” said Henry.

,“We showcased the style of footy we want to be playing, and are motivated to have put out another strong performance.”

The Kiwi Ferns will round out a triple-header at Cbus Super Stadium this Saturday, facing Papua New Guinea at 4.45pm local time, 7.45pm NZT.

Experienced players back for clash against Fiji

Five experienced campaigners return for the New Zealand Kiwis’ critical third-round Rugby League World Cup encounter with the Fiji Bati at Cbus Super Stadium in Robina on Saturday (2.30pm kick-off local time, 5.30pm NZT).

Dane Gagai, who made his Kiwi debut against the Kangaroos on October 15, is back in the centres for Charnze Nicoll-Klokstad after missing Sunday’s 82-16 win over the Cook Islands.

There are three changes in the starting forward pack with Moses Leota named at prop (Joseph Tapine returning to loose forward for Erin Clark) and fellow Test regulars Isaiah Papali'i and Briton Nikora team up again in the second row for Scott Sorensen and Xavier Willison, who move to the six-man bench.

Back on the bench is premiership-winning Roosters front rower Naufahu Whyte, who joins Te Maire Martin, Clark, Willison, Nicoll-Klokstad and Sorensen.

Gagai, Leota, Papali’i, Nikora and Whyte missed the 14-try rout of the Kukis as head coach Stacey Jones recalled Nicoll-Klokstad, Sorensen and prop Leo Thompson while handing Test debuts to prop Tanner Stowers-Smith (Kiwi #846) and Jacob Laban (Kiwi #847).

Apart from Keano Kini starting at fullback for the injured Kalyn Ponga, Jones has named the same starting combination and interchange used in the tournament-opening 22-24 loss to the Kangaroos.

The Kiwis arrived on the Gold Coast on Monday night to prepare for a difficult mission against a Fiji side which led the Kangaroos 16-12 early in the second half before the home side rallied to win 36-22 in their round two clash at Suncorp Stadium on Sunday.

The Kiwis have battled against Fiji in the last two World Cups, suffering a shock 2-4 loss in the quarter-finals in Wellington in 2017 and needing a late comeback to escape with a 24-18 quarter-final victory in Hull four years ago.

Kiwi Ferns score record points in win over France

One New Zealand Stadium was pumping on Sunday afternoon as the sold-out crowd cheered on the Kiwi Ferns to their highest-ever score, putting 92 points on the French, who had no answer for the onslaught.

The Kiwi Ferns crossed the chalk 18 times, with Apii Nicholls scoring four tries, and fellow Warriors teammates Stacey Waaka and Mele Hufanga achieving hat-tricks. 

Dominant defence by the home team restricted France to only 1 tackle inside the Kiwi Ferns 20m zone, and they managed only 14 tackles inside the Kiwi Ferns’ half. This saw a whopping 81% of the match played inside the French half, with the Kiwi Ferns maintaining 74% of possession. 

Head Coach Ricky Henry said: “We played our style of footy and stuck to it, which I'm really, really happy with. We had to be really disciplined there because we knew we were going to get a few looks at different things. So the big takeaway for us was sticking to what works for us and knowing that when we do that, we get some joy.”

Henry was complimentary of his debutants too, describing the three new additions as “awesome.” 

Tenika Willison scored two tries and showed her dynamic skills, switching from wing to centre in the second half. Chantay Kiria-Ratu notched 18 points off her boot, plus a try, and 19-year-old Trinity Tauaneai was immense through the middle with strong carries and played big minutes despite starting off the bench.

The Kiwi Ferns now travel to the Gold Coast where they will prepare for their final pool game against Papua New Guinea.

Go Media get behind the Kiwi Ferns for the Rugby League World Cup

New Zealand Rugby League strengthens their partnership with Go Media, who will continue as official outdoor media partner and join the Kiwi Ferns as a jersey sponsor for the 2026 Rugby League World Cup.

Since first coming on board as official outdoor media partner of NZRL in 2024, Go Media has been a valued collaborator in helping reach fans far and wide through their out of home network across Aotearoa.  

Their support now extends to the Kiwi Ferns as they compete in the World Cup, with the team winning their opening match against Fiji and now preparing to face France in Christchurch this weekend. As the Kiwi Ferns’ only dedicated partner, it’s a significant milestone for NZRL and Go Media in driving exposure and growth through the women’s game.  

NZRL CEO Andrea Nelson said,  

“We’ve built a great relationship with Go Media and we’re excited to see our partnership continue to grow.  

“Having them extend their support to the Kiwi Ferns for the World Cup is fantastic, especially as we continue to take great strides in growing the Kiwi Ferns fanbase and increasing female participation in rugby league as a whole.”  

Mike Gray Go Media Director shared, “Go Media has always been a passionate supporter of New Zealand sport, and we're incredibly proud to be extending our partnership with New Zealand Rugby League to support the Kiwi Ferns on the world stage.

“The Kiwi Ferns are outstanding ambassadors for our country, and we believe women's sport deserves the same visibility, recognition and commercial support as the men's game.

“As a New Zealand-owned business, having our brand on the Kiwi Ferns jersey at a World Cup is something we're immensely proud of. We look forward to using our national outdoor media network to help shine a spotlight on the team and inspire the next generation of young women to get involved in rugby league.”

Get ready for the NZ 16s & 18s Schools, Clubs & Residents Labour Day matches

The New Zealand Residents 16s Girls and Boys, New Zealand Secondary Schools 18s Girls and Boys, and New Zealand Clubs 18s Girls and Boys teams will play their representative fixtures this coming Labour Day, Monday 26 October, at Auckland's Bruce Pulman Park.

The NZ Secondary Schools teams were selected from students who represented their schools at the NZRL National Secondary Schools Tournament in early September. Their opponents, the NZ Clubs teams, were selected from players who represented their respective districts and zones at the NZRL National Youth Tournament in late September.

In the 16s grade, the NZ Residents teams were selected from both the Secondary Schools and National Youth tournaments. They will face the Pasifika Aotearoa Rugby League Collective 16s Girls and Aotearoa New Zealand Māori Rugby League 16s Tama, respectively.
