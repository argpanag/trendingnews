Dragons’ Den star’s retailer rescued by fridge magnate

TheTools · 24.09.2026 14:43 · source · permalink

Peter Jones sells camera chain Jessops to online white-goods store AO World

Dragons’ Den star Peter Jones has sold struggling camera retailer Jessops to AO World, more than a decade after he first saved the chain from administration.

Jessops has collapsed three times since 2013, when Mr Jones initially bailed out the camera chain, and has since shrunk to just eight shops, compared with more than 300 at its peak.

AO, the online white-goods store led by entrepreneur John Roberts, said it would use its scale to grow the business, while taking control of its specialist expertise in cameras and photography.

The deal, announced on Thursday, also includes Camera Jungle, Jessops’ second-hand business. It will sit alongside musicMagpie, which sells used phones and other consumer electronics that AO bought in 2024.

Mr Roberts said: “I am delighted to welcome Jessops into the AO family and look forward to growing the existing business as well as integrating the category into the wider AO business.”

Jessops was founded in Leicester in 1935 by Frank Jessop and grew into one of Britain’s best-known photography retailers. It eventually became a national chain with hundreds of shops, but was hit by the shift towards online shopping and the growing quality of smartphone cameras.

Mr Jones stepped in after Jessops fell into administration in January 2013, buying the brand and some of its assets and putting in £5m of his own money to relaunch business.

At the time, he said Jessops was an “iconic British brand” that could “lead the retail resurgence on Britain’s high streets”. He reopened 30 shops and re-employed hundreds of former staff.

The rescue ultimately failed to revive Jessops and the current business is a fraction of its former size. The retailer entered administration again in 2019 and a third time in 2021, after which Mr Jones agreed a company voluntary arrangement with creditors.

Its latest accounts show sales of about £19.9m in the year to September 2025, up 6.5pc, but a £1.1m loss. The business has increasingly moved its sales online and shifted towards second-hand photography equipment.

The sale brings an end to Mr Jones’s 13-year ownership of the brand. The entrepreneur made his fortune in mobile phones before becoming one of the investors on Dragons’ Den, the BBC One show that he has appeared in since it debuted in 2005.

The deal gives AO another foothold in the second-hand market after it bought musicMagpie in 2024 and turned the loss-making business profitable.

AO said the Jessops deal was funded from its existing cash and did not disclose the price. It had more than £200m of headroom at the end of September.

The acquisition comes as AO reported a 5.5pc rise in first-half revenue and said pre-tax profit was up more than 20pc to about £21.5m.
