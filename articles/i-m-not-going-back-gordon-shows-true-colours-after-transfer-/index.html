'I'm not going back' - Gordon shows true colours after transfer move backfired

TheTools · 22.09.2026 10:55 · source · permalink

Anthony Gordon has made a fine start to life in Barcelona.(Image: Getty Images)

Former Newcastle United star Anthony Gordon has revealed how time on loan at Preston North End sparked his phenomenal turnaround. The Magpies bid farewell to the forward in the summer as he landed a dream move to Barcelona for a fee in the region of £69million.

Gordon was rejuvenated under Eddie Howe after leaving his boyhood club Everton. The 25-year-old ultimately became a key figure at the Toffees, subsequently earning a £45million switch to the North East.

However, as he settles into life in Catalonia, he has reflected on a difficult period in Lancashire with the Lillywhites. He made just 11 appearances during a short-term loan at the Championship club and failed to register a goal or an assist at Deepdale, and he has revealed how he had to change his mentality to succeed as a professional.

He said in an interview with El Pais: "Around age 19, more or less. I was a very talented player, but my mentality wasn’t that strong or consistent.

"I was loaned to Preston from Everton and barely played. After that loan, I told myself: I’m not going back to this.

"I need to play in the Premier League; I can’t go back to the Championship. When I returned for preseason, I started reading every day, watching videos.

"From then on, I started paying close attention to personal development. I began to understand a lot of things."

Gordon has five assists in seven appearances for Hansi Flick's side after an impressive start to his Camp Nou chapter, despite failing to score.

The attacker was adored at St James' Park as he was a part of the Newcastle United side that returned to Champions League football and ended the wait for silverware after clinching the Carabao Cup with a dominant win over Liverpool.

After his move to the La Liga champions was confirmed, Gordon paid tribute to Newcastle and hailed them for bringing his love for football back.

He wrote: "I owe this club (Newcastle) a lot because, when I arrived, I was quite lost both in life and in football. The club has given me a sense of belonging and a sense of identity. It's allowed me to do what I always thought I could do.

"It's put me on the biggest stage and allowed me to perform for the shirt.

"Since coming to the club, I feel I've improved a lot on the pitch, but this club has played a big part in the person I've become over the last three-and-a-half years.

"It was really important for me to leave this place in a good way because I've loved every single minute of being a part of Newcastle United. This is an incredible club and one that I'll never forget. I'll be a fan for the rest of my life."
