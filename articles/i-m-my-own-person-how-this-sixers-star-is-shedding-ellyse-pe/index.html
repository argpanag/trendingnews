‘I’m my own person’: How this Sixers star is shedding Ellyse Perry comparisons

At 17, Caoimhe Bray is starting to feel old.

Two years ago, she became the youngest ever Women’s Big Bash League player. On debut, aged just 15, Bray dismissed West Indies international Deandra Dottin and hit the winning runs for the Sixers against the Melbourne Renegades.

Though still not an adult, the allrounder is now in her third WBBL season and continues to impress with her matchwinning contributions – like the 4-28 against crosstown rivals the Sydney Thunder earlier this month. But her burgeoning maturity is evident beyond her form on the field.

“I compare myself to when I was 15, coming into the squad for the first time,” she said at Sixers training this week. “I’m still energetic, but how energetic I was just seeing all these insane cricketers all around me and to know that I’m so much more comfortable with them now, and I’m just best friends with most of them, is crazy to think of.

“If I told my 15-year-old self that, she probably wouldn’t believe it.”

Having become teammates with the same players from whom she once sought autographs after matches, Bray has begun to find her voice within the Sixers side.

“I’ve noticed when I’m playing, I’m just putting more of a say into my own bowling and to my own body and knowing what’s better for me and the team, which is something I probably wouldn’t have had that much knowledge about two years ago,” she said.

Back then, much was made of Bray’s cross-code talents. She had, after all, been Australia’s goalkeeper at the under-17s soccer Women’s Asian Cup in 2024 aged just 14. Commentators drew obvious parallels with Ellyse Perry – the Australian Test superstar who scored three goals in 18 appearances for the Matildas early in her sporting career.

Like Perry, Bray is now wholly devoted to cricket.

And unlike in her first two WBBL seasons, when she had to get permission from her high school to take time off to play, Bray is now full-time, having chosen not to return for year 11 and 12 and moved from Newcastle to Sydney to pursue her cricket career – a move made more challenging by the fact she was still on her learner’s driving permit when relocating intrastate.

Moving to the big city at such a tender age has helped Bray become her own person. Having once picked the No.18 jersey as a homage to Perry (who wears No.8) and Phoebe Litchfield (the Thunder star who also wears 18), Bray now wants to make a change to play under her NSW number, 14.

“As much as I love those two players, and I think how cool they are, it’d be cool to have my own story to my number,” she said. “I think just growing up, realising I am my own person and not as big of a fangirl as I was.

“Actually, that’s a lie. I’m still a fan girl, that is such a lie.”

Bray is eager to follow her former teammate Lucy Hamilton into the Australian Under-19 side. Her deadline? “As soon as possible.”

“At the same time, I’m still only young,” she said. “And I can still have a few more years to catch up to those girls and I hope one day I can do it.”
