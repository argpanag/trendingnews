Double Olympic champ has perfect chance to send statement to heavyweight rivals

TheTools · 09.10.2026 08:35 · source · permalink

A double reigning Olympic champion appears on the O2 bill in London next week and it is neither Daniel Dubois nor Fabio Wardley.

The reason Bakhodir Jalalov is not the headline act is the double life he has been leading combining amateur and professional commitments. Though the policy delivered super heavyweight gold in Tokyo and Paris, it has undoubtedly stalled his development in the pros.

At 32, eight years after his debut in Connecticut, doubts must remain if he can ever make the transition successfully. He is at least giving it a go now. Next week’s engagement against Solomon Dacres is his second this year since joining Frank Warren’s Queensberry stable.

He knows this is his chance. He needs to get people excited. For that to happen he has to entertain, to blow guys away.

At 6ft 7ins and 18st, the southpaw from Uzbekistan brings a 21st century frame to the ring. Back to back Olympic gold is pretty impressive, too. That’s Teofilio Stevenson stuff.

But amateur boxing is not pro boxing. In the former you can stay out of the way for three rounds. In the pros, you don’t have that luxury. Opponents are at you every minute of every round.

And when you take off the padded 12oz gloves for the 10oz heavyweight variety the game changes, especially so for Jalalov, who does not have the skill-set for fighting on the inside.

Solomon Dacres is his 18th opponent. At 6ft 5ins and 17st, there was some excitement about Dacres in the amateurs.

He spent three years in the GB squad but was sparked in the first round defending his British title against the explosive David Adeleye two years ago and injured his bicep during a points win in his comeback fight last year.

At 32, Jalolov’s style is pretty much set. He will always want to keep it long and use his power. The problem is, as he moves up the food chain opponents will want to defuse him by getting close, working his body.

Dacres is unlikely to be that guy, which is why he is in there, of course. But the best Dacres is potentially Jalalov’s toughest test to date.

If he is to make the most of his Olympic legacy, Jalalov has to turn up the dial sharpish. He must learn how to block, then unload three or four combinations at close range.

If he can do that, he will at least gain the respect of opponents when the challenge gets serious.
