Lady Louise Windsor is supported by her mother, the Duchess of Edinburgh, at a carriage driving meet

TheTools · 05.10.2026 09:36 · source · permalink

Lady Louise Windsor, pictured here at The Royal Windsor Horse Show 2026, has spent the weekend competing in the final carriage-driving meet of the season

The Duchess of Edinburgh was the picture of a proud parent as she supported her daughter, Lady Louise Windsor, in a carriage-driving competition over the weekend. Sophie, 61, was pictured walking alongside St Andrew’s graduate Louise, 22, at the Windsor Grek Park carriage-driving meet. The event is close to home for the duo, who live at nearby Bagshot Park.

Lady Louise – who inherited her love of carriage driving from her grandfather, Prince Philip, and is known to ride his beloved horse, Balmoral Bertie – was in a warm fleece and riding helmet as she carried her crop atop the horse. Sophie, meanwhile, was wrapped up in a warm grey jacket, mocha scarf, and white jeans for the event on Saturday.

On Saturday, the young royal was spotted caring for her horse at the final meet of the carriage driving season. Commenting on photos of Lady Louise dressed in a long grey coat, jeans, and riding boots, royal watchers noted her striking similarity to her grandmother, Queen Elizabeth II, who was known for her love of all things equestrian and would try carriage driving in a carriage belonging to her own grandmother, Queen Alexandra.

Lady Louise Windsor, pictured here at the Royal Windsor Horse Show in 2026, was supported by her mother at Windsor Great Park this weekend

But it is Prince Philip whose legacy Lady Louise is truly continuing. After the late Duke of Edinburgh retired from polo in 2003, he took up carriage driving, introducing the sport to a wider audience, competing late into his 90s.

Lady Louise, who has won the hearts of the public thanks to her down-to-earth approach to life in the House of Windsor, spoke about continuing her grandfather’s legacy in a BBC documentary following his death in 2021.

‘The Duke of Edinburgh has been so involved in my driving, which has been so lovely although slightly scary because he invented the sport pretty much. It's incredible to have learned first-hand from him,’ Lady Louise said.

‘After a competition, he would always ask how it went. His eyes would light up because he just gets so excited when he talks about it.

‘When we would go carriage driving, he would take me on a different route every day, I do not know how he managed to do that, and tell me all sorts of anecdotes about anything and everything. He is honestly one of the most interesting people I have ever met.’

The Duke of Edinburgh's daughter, who is 18th in line for the throne, has won praise for her down-to-earth approach to royal life; she took an official job at the Royal Windsor Horse Show this year

When she is not supported by her mother, Lady Louise can count on the presence of her boyfriend, Felix da Silva-Clamp. The couple’s relationship is said to have begun when they joined the same student drama group at St Andrew’s, following in the footsteps of Lady Louise’s cousin, Prince William, who met his wife, Catherine Middleton, at the Scottish university. Felix has since become a regular at family outings for the Edinburgh family, joining Prince Edward, Sophie, and Lady Louise at a number of horse events and a trip to the theatre during the Edinburgh Festival this summer, and he even sat by the royal’s side when she collected her Duke of Edinburgh award from her father.

Felix is most often seen supporting Lady Louise at her carriage driving competitions. The duo have made a number of appearances together at the events, including at the Sandringham Horse Driving Trials last summer (his second time at the event) where Lady Louise raced her way to a bronze medal as da Silva-Clamp watched on proudly. He appeared to get on famously with the other guests, including Lady Louise’s mother, Sophie, and the late Prince Philip’s close friend, Penny Knatchbull – showing just how friendly he has become with the family of his girlfriend.

In September 2025, da Silva-Clamp accompanied Lady Louise to the National Carriage Driving Championships at the Ashfields Carriage and Polo Club, where he cheered the royal on from the sidelines. Dressed in a shirt from her family home, Bagshot Park, Lady Louise was seen getting close to da Silva-Clamp before the race and beaming as she completed the course.

‘Lady Louise was elated, [she] broke into a lovely little grin afterwards,’ an onlooker told HELLO! magazine. Noting that the pair were ‘mindful’ of any public displays of affection, the insider added that everyone could ‘tell they’re very close’.

Sara Hower, a trainer in the sport who is a regular on the competition circuit, reflected on the couple’s relationship, telling the outlet that Lady Louise’s ‘lad’ is ‘just lovely’.

The royal's boyfriend, who she is said to have met in a student drama club at St Andrew's, has become a familiar face by her side in recent months, joining Lady Louise at a special Duke of Edinburgh's award ceremony

‘He's always been very supportive, but also leaves her alone to get on with the job,’ the trainer explained, adding that, while Felix and Lady Louise value ‘their own privacy’, the pair ‘certainly aren't put out on a pedestal and that nobody can go anywhere near them. They're totally the opposite from that.’

Hower has also commented on the Windsor scion’s carriage driving career more widely. Noting: ‘She certainly tries. She gets frustrated when it goes wrong, as the rest of us do. So she still goes home and practises and comes back, and there's an improvement. She looks more than good.’

Up next for Lady Louise Windsor? A gap year, which is said to promise volunteering and travelling before a period spent focussing on potential future careers. No doubt royal watchers will be keen to celebrate the young royal any way they can.
