      From 1 Nov, travellers in Japan must pay tax upfront and claim refunds at the airport

      From 1 Nov, travellers shopping in Japan will have to pay full price in-store and claim their tax refund at the airport before leaving. Eligible purchases are currently exempt from the 10 per cent consumption tax at the point of sale. The change is aimed at curbing abuse by travellers who buy goods tax-free, then illegally resell them for a profit equal to the tax.

      The Straits Times likened the new process to Singapore's goods and services tax refund system for tourists. According to Japan's National Tax Agency and the Japan Tax-free Shop websites, purchases totalling 5,000 yen (S$40) or more per store per day are eligible. Gold and platinum bullion and airport duty-free items are excluded. Travellers must present their passport at eligible shops so the purchase can be registered.

      Under the pay-first, refund-later system, travellers must go through customs inspection at the airport within 90 days of purchase. They will have to show their passport at a tax-free procedure terminal. All items listed on a receipt must be presented for inspection. If even one is missing, perhaps because it has been consumed, none of the goods on that receipt will qualify for a refund.

      The terminals are at the international departure lobby. Travellers can also complete the process online through Visit Japan Web at Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose, and Naha airports. However, they must be within the departure lobby areas with dedicated Wi-Fi, up to the security checkpoint. Luggage should not be checked in before the inspection, as goods may need to be examined and checked baggage cannot be retrieved. Travellers should arrive early to allow enough time.

      The quantity of goods bought tax-free is limited to what a traveller can personally take out of Japan. This means goods shipped separately to Singapore will not qualify for a refund. The new system removes the daily purchase limit of 500,000 yen on consumables such as alcohol and cosmetics, and special packaging is no longer required.

      Travellers who have consumed all or part of these items should not complete the tax-free procedure and should instead report them to a customs officer at the counter. Items left behind in Japan after passing inspection will incur consumption tax equal to the exempted amount, along with penalties.

      Singapore air quality stays healthy overnight, with normal PM2.5 and good-to-moderate PSI

      Singapore's air quality remained healthy overnight, with hourly PM2.5 readings in the normal range and the 24-hour PSI in the good-to-moderate band across the island. At 6am on 27 Oct, the one-hour PM2.5 readings in all five regions ranged between 14 and 32. The 24-hour PSI stood between 62 and 72.

      PM2.5 readings are gauged hourly and reflect current air quality. Scores from zero to 55 are normal, while those between 56 and 150 indicate an elevated concentration of fine particles. The PSI is a 24-hour rolling average of six key air pollutants. Air quality is considered good to moderate when it is between zero and 100, and unhealthy between 101 and 200. Once it breaches the unhealthy threshold, people, especially the elderly, children, and those with health issues, should start scaling down outdoor activities.

      Residents saw some respite over the weekend after weeks of bad air quality. PM2.5 levels stood in the normal range for most parts of the country, although the 24-hour PSI readings remained unhealthy in some areas.

      In its daily haze advisory on the evening of 26 Oct, the National Environment Agency (NEA) forecast the PSI for 27 Oct to be in the good-to-moderate range of 60 to 90. Smoke haze was observed but confined over Kalimantan on 26 Oct, while it was cloudy over Singapore. The NEA said the likelihood of smoke haze affecting Singapore on 27 Oct is low, with winds expected from the north-east or south-east and showers expected over and near the island.

      Winds are forecast to become variable on 28 and 29 Oct, with showers expected over Singapore and the surrounding region. The wet conditions should help keep hotspot and smoke haze activity suppressed, the NEA said.

      All 10 Singapore-based firms respond to NEA haze law notices, most saying they will cooperate

      All 10 Singapore-based companies issued notices under the haze law have acknowledged the National Environment Agency's (NEA) requests for information on their links to fires in Indonesia. Most have said they will cooperate.

      Wilmar International was the first to announce it would comply, in a filing close to midnight on 23 Oct, about three hours after the NEA sent the notices. First Resources followed at 1.15am on 24 Oct, agreeing to provide the necessary details. Bumitama Agri and its subsidiaries said in a filing that afternoon that they would provide the requested documents and information.

      Asia Pulp & Paper told The Straits Times at about 6.30pm that day that it was cooperating with the authorities, and Golden Agri International later said the same. Kencana Agri, owned by the Maknawi family, said in a filing on 25 Oct that it would cooperate fully.

      Three firms did not explicitly say they would cooperate: Indofood Agri Resources, TSH Global Plantation, and TSH Oversea. Indofood Agri Resources said in a filing on 25 Oct that it received the notice on 23 Oct and would give more details when there were material updates. The two TSH firms were the last to respond, on 26 Oct, through Malaysian firm TSH Resources, which oversees them. In a Singapore Exchange filing at 8.12am, TSH Resources said the subsidiaries were notified to provide documents and information under Section 10 of the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act (THPA) by 2 Nov. It said it is reviewing the notice and will engage with the agency accordingly.

      Under Section 10, representatives of non-compliant firms can face a fine of up to $5,000, a jail term of up to one month, or both. Separately, entities found guilty of causing or contributing to the haze can be fined up to $100,000 for every day the pollution persists, capped at $2 million.

      The NEA issued the notices on the night of 23 Oct to Asia Pulp & Paper Company, Asia Pulp & Paper Company (China), Bumitama Agri, Golden Agri International, First Resources, Indofood Agri Resources, Kencana Agri, TSH Global Plantation, TSH Oversea, and Wilmar International. All 10 are in the oil palm or forestry business and either own land concessions in Indonesia or have related companies that do.

      The notices followed earlier ones, issued under Section 9 of the THPA to 10 Indonesian companies on 16 and 19 Oct, asking how they were preventing or suppressing fires on their lands. Firms face no penalties for not responding to notices under that section.

      Former PR firm director’s wrongful dismissal claim fails after she covertly recorded meeting

      A magistrate has ruled that secretly recording a company meeting could amount to misconduct that justifies immediate dismissal. The ruling threw out a wrongful dismissal claim by a former managing director of a public relations firm, identified only as JJT in court documents. In a judgment dated 23 Oct, Tribunal Magistrate Kerry Chan noted that no reported Singapore judgments had addressed the issue. The Employment Act allows employers to dismiss staff without notice for misconduct, she said, so the question was whether the recording warranted immediate dismissal.

      The Straits Times reported that JJT was told on 23 Feb that her employment was being terminated without cause. She could serve a three-month notice period or go on immediate garden leave with two months' salary, and chose to work until 22 May. Days later, she received a formal misconduct warning over several lapses during her notice period. The firm's leadership cited unauthorised contact with a client, reaching out to a colleague on his day off, and attending a business meeting where she forgot her own team member's name.

      On 3 March, JJT attended a regular weekly meeting with the firm's president and chief human resources officer, where she responded to the allegations. She recorded the conversation on her phone. The president noticed the recording on the screen as she showed a WhatsApp message she had sent to her colleague about her termination. She then deleted the recording and left her phone outside the room. The president later asked her to pause work and explain in writing what had happened, including whether she had recorded internal discussions before.

      On 6 March, she was handed an immediate termination letter saying the unauthorised recording was misconduct and a fundamental breach of trust and confidence.

      JJT argued that the dismissal was wrongful, saying she had placed her phone on the table to record openly, solely to help prepare an accurate written response. She said she deleted the recording as soon as she was asked, and that no confidential client information, financial figures, or other business information had been discussed. She also contended that the firm used the incident to deprive her of the salary she would have earned until 22 May.

      The firm, which was not named in court documents, said JJT was not an ordinary rank-and-file employee. It argued that if a senior leader covertly recorded her own management, every conversation afterwards would come into doubt. It added that as the most senior member of her business unit, she was held to the highest standard of professional judgment, and her contract named a breach of its confidentiality provisions as cause for termination without notice. Chan accepted that the firm understandably had stringent confidentiality standards, as its public relations work involved being entrusted with clients' confidential information.

      The meeting was convened to discuss the firm's business, so it was irrelevant that only JJT's lapses had been discussed when the recording was noticed, Chan said. With the recording deleted, she could not determine its true purpose, and questioned why one was needed when JJT was already reading from prepared notes.

      Some acts can destroy trust in an employment relationship whatever the employee's intentions, she said, and the covert recording was "sufficient to destroy the trust and confidence in this employment relationship". She stressed that this does not mean every covert recording warrants dismissal, as each case turns on its own facts.

It argued that if a senior leader covertly recorded her own management, every conversation afterwards would come into doubt. It added that as the most senior member of her business unit, she was held to the highest standard of professional judgment, and her contract named a breach of its confidentiality provisions as cause for termination without notice. Chan accepted that the firm understandably had stringent confidentiality standards, as its public relations work involved being entrusted with clients' confidential information.

    The meeting was convened to discuss the firm's business, so it was irrelevant that only JJT's lapses had been discussed when the recording was noticed, Chan said. With the recording deleted, she could not determine its true purpose, and questioned why one was needed when JJT was already reading from prepared notes.

    Some acts can destroy trust in an employment relationship whatever the employee's intentions, she said, and the covert recording was "sufficient to destroy the trust and confidence in this employment relationship". She stressed that this does not mean every covert recording warrants dismissal, as each case turns on its own facts.
