Here are the talking points the White House sent Republicans on Trump’s media ban

TheTools · 22.09.2026 17:50 · source · permalink

After MS NOW, CNN and Politico sued the Trump administration on Monday over the president banning their reporters from White House grounds, Trump’s communications team sent congressional Republicans talking points Monday night in defense of the president’s decision.

According to the document obtained by MS NOW, the White House wants Republicans to argue that “no president has been more transparent or offered more of his time to the press” and that the First Amendment does not grant reporters access to the White House.

“The First Amendment protects their right to publish; it does not entitle certain outlets and members of the press to a hard pass, briefing room seat, or place in the press pool,” the talking points say.

In response to MS NOW’s request for comment on the talking points, a White House official said, “We communicate the President’s wins for the American people and priorities with Congress and the American public every single day.”

In the talking points, the White House argues to congressional Republicans that Trump’s ban isn’t unprecedented, drawing a false equivalence with previous press restrictions from Democratic administrations — such as the Biden administration updating its credentialing rules in 2023.

That update — which required reporters to live near Washington, D.C., have gone to the White House within the last six months, and have a press pass with the Supreme Court or Congress — led to hundreds of reporters who didn’t meet the criteria losing their hard passes. 

But the Biden administration didn’t ban those reporters from the White House. Most of those reporters simply chose not to renew their hard passes, and the White House still allowed those reporters to apply for a day pass. Only one person — who did not meet all of the news criteria — was denied a hard pass.

The White House links to a press release it issued Monday detailing some Democratic controversies with the press titled “White House Access Is a Privilege — Not a Right.”

The talking points were included in an email titled “White House Highlights,” which was sent to GOP offices in the House and Senate, according to a House GOP aide and a senior GOP Senate aide.

Among other talking points supplied to Republicans are these arguments:

  • “No member of the public or the press has a freestanding constitutional right to access the White House complex.”
  • “Fabricating facts or reporting verifiable falsehoods from inside the White House complex is an intentional breach of professional decorum and it is severely disruptive to the mission and purpose of the White House.”

Despite the claims of “fabricating facts or reporting verifiable falsehoods,” the talking points do not identify any specific report by MS NOW, CNN or Politico as an example of such conduct, and Trump has said his ban isn’t because of any particular story.

Gabe Rottman, the vice president of policy at the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, told MS NOW that while the White House is correct that the president and his communications staff have “a fair amount of discretion in how it manages press access to the White House grounds,” the circumstances are different when decisions are based on viewpoints.

Rottman said, based on existing case law, one thing was “abundantly clear”: The White House can’t bar press access based on viewpoints and the content of reporting.

He said the president’s own statements, both on Truth Social and during a press conference on Friday, had provided “all of the evidence one would need to determine that the ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico from physically entering the White House at all is because the White House perceives their coverage as as overly negative.”
