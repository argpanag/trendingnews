7 Takeaways From the Coachella 2027 Lineup

TheTools · 11.11.2026 19:23 · source · permalink

It’s that time of year again where we all gather around our screens to yell about various inconsistencies in artist placement on a festival lineup poster. Put another way: Coachella just announced its 2027 slate, and we have many thoughts and questions about the selections, including “congrats to Underscores,” “Måneskin has way too high a billing,” and “who is going to a Murda Beatz set in 2027?” This and more sentiments can be found below.

The Current Headliners

Is it just me, or do the 2027 Coachella headliners seem like they should’ve been…the 2026 Coachella headliners? Dua Lipa, SZA, and Kevin Parker have not released full projects within the last 12 months, nor have they had especially visible years. (The “Dracula” remix TikTok challenge doesn’t count.) At least Dua’s inclusion seems, of the three, the clearest signpost of an imminent new album—I’d expect her to have one announced and maybe even released by the time April rolls around—while SZA is one of the few artists who could feasibly get Kendrick to finally show face. (Rumor has it the fest has been chasing him to headline for several years running.) As for Kevin, well, for his own sake he better hope they let him bring Ninajirachi along. —Walden Green

A Robust Undercard

Electronic acts dominate the 2027 lineup so heavily that DJs are even populating the undercard, the unofficial designated space for generally alt picks that record-store employees would approve of. But wade through the beats and you’ll find plenty of standouts within DIY punk (Twisted Teens, Lambrini Girls, First Day Back), bedroom glitch-pop (Bassvictim, Tiffany Day, Ear), indie rock hitmakers (This Is Lorelei, Mary in the Junkyard), and conjurers of whispery ambiance (Nirosta Steel, Chanel Beads, Love Spells). There’s plenty more tucked away in the fine print, be it After, Genesis Owusu, or the Womack Sisters, but you already know that if your first instinct when the lineup drops is to grab a magnifying glass. —Nina Corcoran

Speaking of Electronic Music…

Coachella’s dance ticket is an annual lesson in how to reformulate “crowd-pleasing” as a form of warcraft. Titanic EDM, house, and techno bookings dominate, with a seemingly endless phalanx of Rüfüs du Sol-type acts stepping up to the challenge of supplying abundant feels and zero surprises. Building on this storied tradition, failsafe pyrotechnics are in order from the likes of Martin Garrix, Solomun, Tiësto, Chase & Status, and Boris Brejcha. In fairness, there is vibe-forward talent aplenty too. One of the most intriguing bookings is Italian disco maven-turned-composer Giorgio Moroder, while Four Tet will return to the desert half a decade after sticking the landing on a hare-brained scheme to drop from “Smells Like Teen Spirit” into Hol!’s EDM behemoth “Country Riddim.” It’s a moment Kieran Hebden later described as “one of the most powerful feelings I’ve experienced in my whole life.” Also joining are Underworld and the Avalanches, while new-gen party-starters Peggy Gou, Barry Can’t Swim, and Amelie Lens x Sara Landry round things out. —Kiana Mickles and Jazz Monroe

Desert Yankee

Daddy Yankee at Coachella almost didn’t happen. In 2022, the burnt out reggaetón pioneer announced his retirement with what was supposed to be his last statement, Legendaddy, and a farewell world tour. He, of course, returned three years later, a new man converted to Christianity, trying to promote a devotional album, Lamento en Baile, which signaled his pivot away from the raunchy focus of his earlier classics. But if you’re itching to whine to “Yo nunca me quedo allá atrás, yo nunca me quedo allá atrás!” fret not—chances that the King of Reggaeton will transform the Colorado desert into a monastery are slim. He knows what the people want, and that’s to screech the opening tongue-tying lyrics of “Gasolina,” hold onto to someone’s waist for “La Despedida,” and to—fingers crossed—catch a glimpse of Bad Bunny for a performance of “Soltera.” —KM

There’s Almost No Rap

Drake was an early prediction (and, depending on which tabloid you subscribe to, nearly a lock) to headline. Who knows if that was even close to happening but even a 6 God appearance wouldn’t make up for the extremely low number of rappers appearing on the 2027 lineup. On the list are Don Toliver, Milo J, Drain Gang, Nettspend, Xaviersobased, Esdeekid, Freddie Gibbs, Genesis Owusu, Mike D, Murda Beatz, Fetty Wap, and… well, that’s it. Coachella’s yearslong shift away from hip-hop has been well-documented but it’s pretty bleak when one of the most high-profile music festivals in the world features about a dozen acts on a slate of more than 150. Someone send Goldenvoice our ambient rap feature. —Alex Suskind

Madonna Skips the Main Stage (Again)

When Madonna made her Indio debut two decades ago—right on the heels of 2005’s Confessions on a Dance Floor—she caused a stir by opting for the Sahara Tent over the main stage. Now, of course, the image of her emerging from a giant disco ball to the opening strains of “Hung Up” is as embedded in the mythos of Coachella as Daft Punk’s pyramid or Beyoncé’s HBCU tribute. The Confessions II era commenced in earnest when Madge popped up during Sabrina Carpenter’s headlining set back in April, so perhaps it was inevitable that she’d cap things off back where it all began. One imagines she’ll go lighter on the aerobics this time, and to that I say: You will watch the Madonna stand behind the decks and lip sync and you will like it. —WG

Unc Rock

Coachella bookings typically reflect the dewy skin and TikTok feeds of their average attendee. This year, though, they raided the AARP mailing list to remind everyone that age is just a number offstage. The sexagenarians are out in full force: Beastie Boys’ Mike D is rolling out the bass amps for Thank You, his solo debut; Nirosta Steel will pull up a folding chair to play queer art-pop for his rare festival appearance; and Social Distortion will rep old-school Californian hardcore as they have been for the past 48 years. The Damned may be grandparents, but their status as one of Britain’s founding punk bands doesn’t indicate their energy, as the axe-wielding group is still as wily live as ever in their 70s. Consider it a public service from Coachella, a festival that's spent the past decade embracing its looksmaxxing crowds and club culture, to drive home that youth is a mindset. —NC
