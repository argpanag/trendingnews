Down and down and down: Former commodore reacts to surface fleet halving in 30 years

TheTools · 27.11.2026 18:01 · source · permalink

The Royal Navy's surface fleet has been almost halved since 1997, according to new figures.

The number of vessels in the Senior Service has dropped from 97 in May of that year to 49 at the beginning of this month.

The statistics show a consistent decline in the overall number of ships the Navy can deploy over the last three decades.

Among them the amount of destroyers in the fleet was halved from 12 to six while the number of frigates plummeted from 23 to five.

The number of survey and ice patrol ships also dropped from six to three while mine countermeasures vessels and fleet minesweeper figures fell from 23 to just seven this year.

Responding to a question on naval sea power from Conservative MP Ben Obese-Jecty, Defence Readiness and Industry Minister Luke Pollard said that his government inherited an Armed Forces that was "underinvested and hollowed out" and the service is going through a period of transition.

He said: "This Government inherited Armed Forces that had been underinvested and hollowed out. 

"We are increasing defence spending and delivering new capabilities to meet the new threats the UK faces.

"The Royal Navy is currently managing a complex phased transition as ageing ships are being replaced with modern variants. 

"The Fleet is soon going to be bolstered by eight world leading Type 26 frigates and five Type 31 frigates as well as revolutionary uncrewed assets as the service transitions to a Hybrid Navy."

Former Royal Navy officer Steve Prest told BFBS Forces News he expects the situation to deteriorate further before vessel numbers increase.

He said: "I’m not sure we're quite at the bottom yet regrettably. 

"My read of the situation is that the first Type 26 should get to sea next year but it's got to do first of class trials and there's a capability insertion period and all sorts of things to go before it's actually in service. 

"So, I think that's still a couple of years away. 

"I think the first Type 31s will probably not be fully in service till the end of the decade um so I think we're looking at the end of General Gwyn's tenure 2029/2030 before we start to see the first of the new frigates come in and in the meantime I think we will see more ships having to be taken out of service because they've just run out of life.

"They're old and frankly they will be unsafe to be at sea. 

"We've seen that with HMS Lancaster who's still stuck alongside in Bahrain 

"I think it's going to be another tough two or three years and i think for the First Sea Lord it's going to be very hard to sell that narrative about the Navy being ready for war as we're seeing the ship numbers go down and down and down."
