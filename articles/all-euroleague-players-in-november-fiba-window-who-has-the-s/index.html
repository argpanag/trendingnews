Up to 66 EuroLeague players across 15 national teams could feature in the November FIBA World Cup 2027 European Qualifiers, with 28 already included in Thursday's official game-day squads and several stars expected to join for the second game.

It's that time of year again: international basketball returns, and EuroLeague players have to balance their club and national team duties.

The fifth and penultimate window of the FIBA World Cup 2027 European Qualifiers will take place from November 26-30, with 11 more European tickets to Qatar still up for grabs.

So far, Turkiye is the only European national team to have secured qualification.

While the EuroLeague calendar makes room for the FIBA window, the timing is far from ideal.

With Round 14 concluding on Wednesday and the first qualifying games tipping off on Thursday, several national teams will have to wait until their second games to welcome some of their biggest stars. Others are going the extra mile to represent their countries.

Based on the latest national team announcements and player availability updates, up to 66 EuroLeague players could feature in these FIBA Qualifiers, representing 15 of the 24 participating national teams.

A quick note:

Serbia leads the way with 11 EuroLeague players named to its extended squad, although the actual number of participants could be lower.

Among the big names are Vasilije Micic, Marko Guduric, Filip Petrusev, and Vladimir Lucic, who could make his first national team appearance in four years.

Italy follows with nine EuroLeague players, while Spain and Turkiye have eight each.

Greece is next with seven players, all representing either Olympiacos Piraeus or Panathinaikos AKTOR Athens.

Lithuania could have six EuroLeague players, with five representing Zalgiris Kaunas. However, Jonas Valanciunas' participation remains uncertain.

Meanwhile, France and Israel each have four EuroLeague players.

Reigning world and European champions Germany will feature just two – Bayern Munich teammates Andreas Obst and Oscar da Silva – after Justus Hollatz withdrew.

Nine national teams are not currently expected to feature any EuroLeague players: Estonia, Hungary, Iceland, Latvia, Montenegro, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, and Sweden.

Poland lost its only EuroLeague representative after Barcelona center Aleksander Balcerowski was ruled out of both qualifying games.

Meanwhile, Sweden's Bobi Klintman is not expected to play due to an ankle injury.

* Players whose participation in the November FIBA window remains uncertain.

FIBA has already confirmed the final 12-man rosters for Thursday's six qualifying games, with 28 EuroLeague players included across eight national teams.

Greece leads the way with six EuroLeague players available for its opening game, followed by Italy, Spain, and Turkiye with five each.

Despite Serbia having the most EuroLeague players on its extended roster, only one made the final squad for Thursday's game against Lithuania.

Not all EuroLeague players will be available from the start of the FIBA window.

With several clubs playing their Round 14 games on Wednesday, some national teams could look considerably different between their first and second qualifying games.

Serbia is perhaps the prime example. Despite naming 11 EuroLeague players in his extended squad, Dusan Alimpijevic can count on just one of them for Thursday's clash against Lithuania – Crvena Zvezda's Nikola Djurisic.

His Zvezda teammates Ognjen Dobric and Dejan Davidovac, as well as Dubai Basketball's Filip Petrusev and Nemanja Dangubic, are expected to reinforce Serbia for Sunday's matchup against Italy in Belgrade.

The availability of the remaining six EuroLeague players – Micic, Guduric, Lucic, Vanja Marinkovic, Arijan Lakic, and Nikola Tanaskovic – has yet to be confirmed.

Lithuania finds itself in a similar situation. Zalgiris released Marek Blazevic, Dovydas Giedraitis, and Deividas Sirvydis ahead of Wednesday's EuroLeague game against Real Madrid, allowing them to join the national team earlier.

Fenerbahce guard Ignas Sargiunas has also made Lithuania's final 12-man roster against Serbia.

However, Arnas Butkevicius played for Zalgiris in Madrid before his expected return to the national team for Sunday's matchup against Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Jonas Valanciunas will also miss the Serbia game, with his availability against Bosnia still uncertain.

Meanwhile, neither Rokas Jokubaitis nor Azuolas Tubelis, who made the preliminary roster, will participate in this window.

Italy will have to wait until its second game to welcome Real Madrid's Gabriele Procida and Partizan Belgrade's Alessandro Pajola.

Milan teammates Darius Thompson and Nicola Akele were also left out of Italy's final 12-man roster against Turkiye despite joining the national team on Wednesday.

Similarly, Spain will be without Alberto Abalde, Jaime Pradilla, and Santi Yusta against Montenegro on Thursday, with all three expected to join Chus Mateo's squad for Sunday's game against Greece.

Turkiye will be without three of its eight EuroLeague players against Italy: Ercan Osmani, Berk Ugurlu, and Omer Kutluay.

Bosnia and Herzegovina will also have to wait until its second game against Lithuania to welcome its biggest star, Dzanan Musa.

The Bosnian federation is still negotiating with Baskonia over the release of big man Kenan Kamenjas, who is willing to represent his country.

However, Kamenjas suffered a blow to his knee during Wednesday's EuroLeague game against Anadolu Efes, leaving his availability against Lithuania in doubt.

Meanwhile, Hapoel Tel Aviv forward Tomer Ginat will miss Israel's opening game against the Netherlands for personal reasons before joining the national team for Monday's matchup against Latvia.

Then there's Tornike Shengelia.

The 35-year-old Georgian veteran flew to Athens following Wednesday's EuroLeague game between Dubai and Partizan and has been included in Georgia's final 12-man squad against Greece.

Shengelia is therefore expected to be available for both of Georgia's qualifying games, with Portugal awaiting on Sunday.
