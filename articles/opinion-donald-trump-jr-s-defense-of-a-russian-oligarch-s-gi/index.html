Opinion | Donald Trump Jr.’s defense of a Russian oligarch’s ‘gift’ is ridiculous

TheTools · 16.09.2026 10:00 · source · permalink

Donald Trump Jr. hasn’t taken kindly to the recent scrutiny of his lavish wedding party, following the revelation that it was partially bankrolled by a Russian oligarch with deep ties to the Kremlin. In a joint statement with his wife, Trump Jr. argued that “friendship doesn’t require a political motive” and “sometimes a wedding gift is simply a wedding gift.” His defense might make an ounce of sense if he was not the son of the president. 

Investigative media outlet ProPublica, citing records it reviewed and interviews with three people familiar with the event, revealed on Monday that Trump Jr.’s three-day party held across two private islands was funded in part by Umar Kremlev, a “Russian oligarch close to President Vladimir Putin.” Kremlev heads the International Boxing Association, an organization that has been financed by the Russian state-owned energy company Gazprom. ProPublica reported that Kremlev “footed the bill for hundreds of thousands of dollars of wedding expenses,” including paying to rent one of the islands and the fireworks show. (A spokesperson for Trump Jr. did not dispute the wedding payments from Kremlev and told ProPublica that “Umar is a personal friend of Don.”)

Without even getting into the political and geopolitical concerns, this arrangement is, on its face, peculiar for a few reasons. Trump Jr. said on his podcast that the party was “really small, like, just really close friends,” which makes it all the more striking that Kremlev helped pay for the celebration and attended. ProPublica says Kremlev “speaks limited English,” and Kremlev’s office said that he and Trump Jr. met “a couple of years ago.” President Donald Trump said he’s “never heard of” Kremlev.

Furthermore, why would a new friend offer such an extraordinary contribution — hundreds of thousands of dollars to cover the cost of a private island? That level of expense is something that would typically be covered by either the bride and the groom or their families. Even a party of Gatsbyesque decadence would’ve been easily manageable for Trump Jr., who is estimated to have a net worth of hundreds of millions of dollars. And of course his father is a billionaire.

But given that Trump Jr. is the son of the president, questions of politics and government ethics cannot be set aside. And while there’s no indication that any laws were violated, it certainly violates common sense principles of ethical governance that would guard both against a quid pro quo with the Trump administration — or the appearance of one.
