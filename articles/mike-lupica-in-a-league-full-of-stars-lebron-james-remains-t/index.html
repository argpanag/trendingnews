Mike Lupica: In a league full of stars, LeBron James remains the NBA’s biggest

TheTools · 24.10.2026 14:09 · source · permalink

You want all the qualifiers about the 76ers? How much time do you have? Joel Embiid could get hurt — probably will get hurt — because that’s kind of his thing. LeBron James is going to be 42 years old the day before New Year’s Eve. Maybe the biggest qualifier of all is this one right here: They’re the 76ers, they’ve been finding ways not to win championships for a long time, haven’t been to the NBA Finals since Larry Brown was coach and Allen Iverson was his star. If you’re keeping score at home, that was 25 years ago.

Having said all that? These Sixers have a chance to be the biggest story of the year in the NBA, bigger than the Knicks trying to repeat, or the Thunder trying to come back, or Victor Wembanyama and them trying to show everybody they’re better than what they showed against the Knicks in the Finals.

It all starts with the OG, LeBron. Right now, today, he is once again the biggest star in the sport, same as he was when he signed on with Philly. We thought he’d had Act 3 of his career with the Lakers, where he won one more title and made his 10th trip to the NBA Finals. Only now here he is, in the late innings, trying to give himself a shot to win a fifth title with a fourth different team. Not only that, he is trying to give himself the final act that Michael Jordan — against whom he will be measured from now until the end of days — never got. Michael absolutely came back and was still able to average 20 points a game with the Wizards at the age of 40. But that team was nothing. The 2026-27 76ers have a chance to be really something, and not just because they clipped the Knicks the way they did on opening night, and then came back from 29 down to beat the Cavaliers two nights later.

The story here, for as long as it lasts, is that after two decades of being the greatest star since Michael, after all the years and the mileage, LeBron James might be playing on the most talented team of which he’s ever been a part. You want to know why? Because he’s only the third best player on it, and might look like the fourth on nights when Embiid is playing like one of the best big men around.

It might not be treated like the best possible narrative around here, certainly not with Knicks fans, but we are once again seeing that the NBA is always a lot more interesting when LeBron is in the center of the action and on a team with a chance.

Again: It’s only been three games, counting Philadelphia’s Saturday night game against the Bucks. But if you just watched the first two, it is plain to see that one thing hasn’t changed with LeBron James, even at 41 going on 42:

He’s not just the greatest all-around player in NBA history, one who could have been an All-Star at any of the five positions, he is as great a teammate as the sport has ever seen. I used to write that the young LeBron could pick up four other guys at the Y and still make the NBA playoffs. Well, guess what: Tyrese Maxey and Jaylen Brown and Embiid and V.J. Edgecombe are a bit more than four guys at the Y, even if LeBron isn’t young any longer.

Brown has long been one of the best players on the planet, and a winner, with the Celtics, and a tremendous teammate himself. Maxey has not just become a full-fledged superstar himself, but playing with LeBron now — and even going off an incredibly small sample — you can plainly see the difference LeBron has made with Maxey just when the kid has been off the ball. And when he has the ball, as Knicks fans well know, his range generally extends all the way to the first row of courtside seats.

We don’t have to go through the qualifiers again. We know exactly how old LeBron is. Embiid sometimes looks older than that. Nick Nourse, the coach, is obviously going to have to watch minutes with both of them, and do what he’s always done with Embiid, which means hold his breath every time Embiid hits the floor. But if you did watch that comeback against the Cavs on Thursday night in the Sixers’ home opener, it wasn’t just one of them, it was all of them making it happen.

I’ve always been clear on something: If I had to pick one guy to play for my life, it would be Michael. I saw the whole show from him, from North Carolina on. I was there when he won the national championship against Patrick Ewing and Georgetown in the Superdome and I saw him beat the Knicks all those times in the ’90s; and you better believe I was there at the Garden the night he dropped 55 on the Knicks less than two weeks after coming back to the NBA from minor league baseball.

But I’ve always said this about LeBron: From game to game, he does more things trying to help his team win the game — both ends of the court — than anybody who ever played. That’s in addition to scoring more points than anybody in history. He has already been a Hall of Fame player in three different cities, Cleveland and Miami and Los Angeles. Now he is balling as hard as ever in Philadelphia. And doing it with Brown and Maxey and Embiid. If this is his own Last Dance, he’s playing for a coach, Nurse, who was a champion himself with the Raptors a few years ago.

The Knicks absolutely turned themselves into the face of the NBA in winning that title, and winning it the way they did last spring. We’ve watched Shai Gilgeous-Alexander look like the most gifted mid-range player since Michael. And Wembanyama might very well be the future, and not just in the short run.

LeBron is still the star of all of them.

The league got more interesting when he shocked everybody by signing with the 76ers when everybody thought he might be on his way back to Cleveland or Miami. Opening night would always have been big at the Garden because of the raising of the banner and the handing out of rings — well, not to everybody – but LeBron made it much bigger by being the opponent.

Now he is a legit opponent for the Knicks again, the way he once was in Miami. And maybe, just maybe, he is even more of a villain for Knick fans than ever as he tries to be The Man one last time in pro basketball. Even if he’s just the third man on his new team.
