Dutch police join international crackdown on illegal IPTV services, €26 million traced

TheTools · 25.11.2026 14:20 · source · permalink

Europol has coordinated a major international operation targeting the financial networks behind illegal IPTV services across Europe. Police from 11 countries, including the Netherlands, traced more than €26 million in cryptocurrency transactions, disrupted 57 illegal IPTV services and identified 321 websites distributing copyrighted content without permission.

The operation, conducted in Seville, Spain, represents a shift in the fight against illegal streaming: authorities are increasingly targeting the money and people behind the services, rather than simply shutting down websites and streaming platforms.

The five-day operation took place from November 16 to November 20 at the Spanish National Police headquarters in Seville. Europol coordinated the operation, bringing together law enforcement officers and representatives from the cryptocurrency industry.

The 321 websites collectively receive more than 7.3 million visits annually. Europol has not specified how many individual users were affected or how many of the websites were permanently taken offline.

Around 40 people participated, including 30 law enforcement officers. Dutch police were among the international investigators working to trace the financial networks behind the illegal services.

Illegal IPTV providers often sell subscriptions for just a few euros per month, offering access to thousands of television channels, films and live sporting events without obtaining the necessary broadcasting rights.

Cryptocurrency has become one way for these operators to collect subscription payments and move their profits internationally. Investigators followed suspicious transactions across blockchain networks, looking for connections between iIllegal IPTV platforms and cryptocurrency wallets receiving subscription payments.

The operation targeted an industry that generates billions of euros in revenue and causes substantial losses to legitimate broadcasters and governments. A total of 60 million people across Europe use IPTV services.
