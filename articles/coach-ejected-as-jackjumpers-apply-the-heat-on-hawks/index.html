Coach ejected as JackJumpers apply the heat on Hawks

TheTools · 21.11.2026 09:27 · source · permalink

Tasmania have moved to the top of the NBL ladder with a 95-82 win over Illawarra, whose coach Justin Tatum was ejected from the game.

Josh Bannan (20 points, 11 rebounds) and Dakota Mathias (19) led a typically even team performance from the JackJumpers at Hobart's MyState Bank Arena on Saturday night.

Guard Taran Armstrong (nine points, eight assists, three steals) was also influential as they improved to 10-4, a win clear of Sydney (9-4) and Cairns (9-6).

"I thought our guys competed very well again," said JackJumpers coach Scott Roth, who waved to the fans and blew them kisses after the final siren.

"We continue to figure out ways to win games down the stretch."

Makur Maker (21 points) powered the Hawks, whose fightback was stymied by three technical fouls in the fourth quarter.

Import Kelan Martin had one of them and Tatum had two, the second coming with 0.5 seconds left and prompting his exit from the arena.

Illawarra started slowly, then closed to 20-17 at quarter-time, before pinching the lead when Maker's second alley-oop dunk capped a 9-0 burst.

Turnovers were an issue for Tasmania, Hawks co-captain Tyler Harvey's active hands producing four first-half steals.

But Illawarra couldn't buy a rebound, Bannan and Will Magnay leading the JackJumpers to a 26-15 advantage on the glass to create a 43-36 halftime buffer.

Tasmania then got on a roll after intermission.

Import Mathias scored eight unanswered points in 67 seconds and Kody Stattmann (15 points) drained his fourth three without a miss.

The 18-3 spree blew the margin out to 63-42.

Illawarra scored the next 10 points before the Jackies finished the period strongly.

Bannan's put-back on the three-quarter-time bell stretched the gap to 72-56 and epitomised the Hawks' woes on the glass, trailing the rebound count 38-22.

The visitors lifted their defensive intensity to start the fourth, and cut the deficit to seven when Harvey's fifth steal resulted in a lay-up.

But that was as close as they got, Tasmania catching a second win to storm to an 89-70 advantage as the Hawks' discipline evaporated down the stretch.

"I felt our guys gave it all they could," said Tatum, whose side is expected to be bolstered by the addition of Brazilian point guard Raul Neto.

Neto impressed as an injury replacement for Brisbane and is set to join the Hawks during the looming FIBA break.

"I asked multiple guys to play 35-plus minutes in 36 hours," Tatum said.

"I'm just happy my guys came out healthy, and we move on to the next one."
