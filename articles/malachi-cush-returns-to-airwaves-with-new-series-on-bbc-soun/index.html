Malachi Cush returns to airwaves with new series on BBC Sounds | We Are Tyrone

TheTools · 22.09.2026 10:00 · source · permalink
  • 22 September 2026

BROADCASTER and musician Malachi Cush is set to return to the airwaves with a new 12-part series of ‘Healing Sounds’, exploring grief, trauma, illness, loss, resilience and hope through the songs that have shaped the lives of his guests.

The third series of its kind, available on BBC Sounds, opens with a special hour-long programme marking the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks, featuring retired NYPD Lieutenant and 9
11 survivor Bill Cosgrove.

He tells Malachi Cush how he entered the World Trade Center to help save lives and recounts helping to carry NYFD chaplain Father Mychal Judge, one of the first official fatalities of the attacks, from the devastation.

He believes he is alive today because he helped bring Father Judge to what he describes as a ‘safe haven’.

Twenty-five years on, Bill Cosgrove reflects on how the events changed his life and the lasting impact of the trauma.

Tears

He tells the Domnaghmore musician that ‘the hurt is always there’ and admits that the anniversary still brings tears to his eyes.

Despite this, he has never visited the 9
11 Memorial in Manhattan, saying he has not done so ‘for whatever reason’,

Across the new series, Malachi Cush will speak to people from a wide range of backgrounds, with each guest choosing four songs that hold particular meaning for them.

The guests include country singer Lisa McHugh, Tyrone athlete Nick Griggs, Eurovision winner Charlie McGettigan, singer Andrea Begley, artist Jacqueline Rooney, singer Janet Devlin, Colin Bell, founder of the Kevin Bell Repatriation Trust, and Fr John Joe Duffy from Creeslough.

Former NYPD officer Denis Mulcahy and Eugene McVeigh, whose brother Columba was abducted and killed in 1975, will also feature.

Candid

Mr McVeigh speaks candidly about the disappearance of his brother and the decades of uncertainty endured by his family.

He says that while senior figures have met with him to express their sorrow over Columba’s disappearance, forgiveness is not something he feels he can offer, explaining that he is ‘not the ultimate judge or the person who forgives’, but rather ‘the person who can be consoled by other people’.

Other episodes will explore the devastating loss of a child, serious illness, family tragedy and the challenges of overcoming adversity.

Country singer Lisa McHugh will discuss her experiences of Functional Neurological Disorder, trigeminal neuralgia and sepsis, while Nick Griggs will reflect on the loss of his brother Josh and the role running has played in his life.

Charlie McGettigan will speak about the death of his son Shayne, while Colin Bell will recall the loss of his son Kevin and how the tragedy led to the creation of the Kevin Bell Repatriation Trust.

Malachi Cush first rose to national prominence in 2002 as a finalist on the first series of the BBC talent show Fame Academy, which launched his career in music and media. Cush has carved out a successful career as a radio and television presenter.

Speaking about the new series Cush said he was ‘privileged and humbled’ by the stories shared by his guests.

“’Healing Sounds’ is a deeply-personal series of conversations in which my guests share extraordinary life experiences and reveal the music that has helped them through some of their darkest and most defining moments,” he said.

“I believe the audience will be shocked, entertained, saddened, uplifted, surprised and will empathise with or relate to the stories being told.

“While every guest has a standout story of heartache to share and every episode is emotionally charged, I want the series to give the listeners an overarching sense of hope from the resilience of my guests.”

Through music, memory and honest conversation, ‘Healing Sounds’ explores the powerful role songs can play in helping people process grief, preserve memories, express emotions and find a way forward.

The first episode, featuring Bill Cosgrove’s 9/11 story, is available now on BBC Sounds.
