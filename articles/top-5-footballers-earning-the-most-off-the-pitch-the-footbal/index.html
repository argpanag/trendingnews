Top 5 Footballers Earning the Most Off the Pitch - The Football Week

TheTools · 15.09.2026 22:07 · source · permalink

In the modern sports economy, elite footballers earn enormous sums off the pitch, often rivaling or exceeding their on-field salaries. Through global endorsement deals, product lines, equity stakes, media ventures, and strategic brand partnerships, certain players have built commercial empires that stretch far beyond their club contracts.

Here’s a deep dive into the five footballers making the most in commercial income, and what makes them such powerful global brands.

Cristiano Ronaldo: The Unrivalled Commercial Monarch

Year after year, Cristiano Ronaldo stands at the top of the global earnings charts, not just among footballers, but among all athletes worldwide. In 2025, Ronaldo was once again named the highest-paid athlete, recording earnings as high as around $280 million for the year, largely fueled by endorsement income alongside his club contract with Al-Nassr.

Ronaldo’s commercial portfolio is monumental. His long-standing deal with Nike, reportedly a multi-year contract worth over €160 million through 2026, has been a cornerstone of his endorsement earnings for over a decade. Beyond Nike, he has partnerships with global brands including Clear shampoo, Herbalife, Binance, Perplexity, Yili and Therabody, among others.

Ronaldo’s appeal comes from scale. He has over 1 billion followers across social media platforms, giving huge global reach for any brand willing to work with him. His personal CR7 brand further expands commercial revenue through apparel, fragrances, and lifestyle products. His ability to drive global visibility, from Asia to the Americas and Africa, is unmatched, making him a blue-chip ambassador for brand partners.

Lionel Messi: The Endorsement Powerhouse

Lionel Messi consistently ranks among the highest-earning athletes in the world, and when it comes to off-field income, he is often second only to Ronaldo. In 2025, Forbes estimated Messi’s total earnings at around $130 million, with a substantial portion, roughly $70 million, coming from endorsements and commercial activities.

Messi’s endorsement portfolio includes lifetime partner Adidas, a long-term deal that has made him one of the sport’s defining brand ambassadors. In addition, he has high-value contracts with Lay’s, Mastercard, PepsiCo, and other multinational brands.

Recently, Messi expanded into entrepreneurial ventures with the launch of Más+ by Messi, a sports drink, and involvement in hospitality and branding extensions such as the MiM Hotel project in Miami. These ventures diversify his income beyond traditional sponsorships and align his personal brand with lifestyle and consumer markets.

Messi’s appeal is built on legacy, global respect, and cultural resonance. Considered one of the greatest players of all time, brands see not just reach but authenticity and trust in his association.

Kylian Mbappé: The Next Generation Commercial Star

At just 25, Kylian Mbappé combines elite performance with rapid commercial rise. According to Forbes and other earning estimates, Mbappé consistently appears among football’s top earners, with total annual compensation including endorsements often north of $90 million.

Mbappé’s commercial portfolio includes major partners like Nike and luxury fashion brands such as Dior, with his relationship with Dior standing out because of its rarity (few current athletes secure luxury fashion deals at this level). He also has multi-market sponsorship relationships that span North America, Europe and the Middle East.

Mbappé’s appeal is a mix of youthful excellence on the pitch, charismatic personality, global recognisability, and a brand-ready image that fits both performance and lifestyle categories. At the heart of the next generation of global football icons, Mbappé is both a cultural ambassador and a commercial magnet.

Neymar Jr.: Brazilian Icon and Global Marketer

Neymar Jr. has long been one of football’s most marketable figures. Even as his on-field situation evolves, his commercial value remains strong. In recent top-earner lists, Neymar’s total earnings have approached the upper tier of global footballers, with clubs and endorsements contributing to strong income figures.

Commercially, Neymar has partnered with Puma, Red Bull, Konami and other global entertainment and lifestyle brands. His vibrant personality, social media presence, and celebrity appeal have made him a favourite for campaigns that target younger, digitally engaged audiences.

Brazil’s massive global diaspora also means Neymar’s reach is especially valuable across Latin America and Europe, regions where global brands compete intensely for cultural relevance.

Mohamed Salah: A Commercial Idol from Africa

While often overshadowed by global superstars in pure earnings lists, Mohamed Salah has developed a powerful commercial profile that rivals many of his peers. According to recent earnings estimates, Salah earns significant income off the field, often around $20–25 million annually through sponsorships and brand partnerships.

Salah’s brand partnerships include global sponsors like Vodafone, Pepsi, Mountain View Egypt, and Adidas, with his social media reach nearing 100 million followers across platforms.

Salah’s appeal stems from authenticity, humility, and consistent excellence, traits that resonate particularly well with global consumer audiences in the Middle East, Africa and Asia. Brands seeking credibility over mere visibility often find Salah one of the most appealing ambassadors in the sport.

The Commercial Appeal: What Makes These Players Marketable?

Across these five athletes, a few common threads explain their commercial success:

  • Global Reach: Massive followings on social media multiply marketing messages organically.
  • Brand Fit & Versatility: Partnerships range from sportswear to tech, beverage, luxury goods, lifestyle, gaming and beyond.
  • Cultural Resonance: Performance excellence combined with personal storylines increases audience connection.
  • Authenticity: Sponsors prioritise athletes whose public personas align with their brand values.

In each case, the players are platforms through which brands access emotional fan engagement, global market penetration, and cross-category promotional opportunities.

The Top Female Footballer Commercially: Aitana Bonmatí

No discussion of commercial earnings in football today would be complete without highlighting the fastest-growing segment of the sport: women’s football. Among female players, Aitana Bonmatí of FC Barcelona stands out as the leading commercial earner.

Bonmatí is recognised as one of the highest-paid female footballers and has a growing portfolio of global partnerships. Her endorsements include Adidas, Alpro, Grupo Bimbo and Topps, alongside involvement in broader lifestyle branding efforts. adidas’s decision to shift its partnership from Nike to Bonmatí marked one of the most significant commercial moves in women’s sport, illustrating the surging investment and belief in her long-term brand potential.

What sets Bonmatí apart is the blending of world-class performance (multiple Ballon d’Or Féminin and major tournament success), cultural influence, and marketplace timing, she arrived as brands intensified investment in women’s football ahead of major tournaments like the Women’s EUROs and FIFA Women’s World Cup.

Conclusion

The global football economy today rewards influence as much as ability. While on-field salaries remain enormous, it is off-pitch commercial deals that often define the richest and most influential players in the sport.

From Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi’s billion-dollar-plus branding ecosystems to Mbappé’s next-generation marketability, Neymar’s entertainment appeal, Salah’s authenticity, and Bonmatí’s rise in the women’s game, the commercial landscape of football continues to evolve.

These players have fully transcended football; they are global brands in their own right, and for companies seeking relevance, emotion, and reach, they offer access to audiences that few other public figures can match.
