J.K. Rowling Will Officially Publish Two New 'Harry Potter' Books in February

TheTools · 06.10.2026 22:01 · source · permalink

It's been a great year for Harry Potter fans. The release of the critically acclaimed Audible Full-Cast Audio Editions gave fans months of enjoyment, and quite literally about a hundred hours of compelling new content. Meanwhile, the first trailer was released for the upcoming HBO series, which premieres on Christmas. Ahead of the new series adaptation coming in December, Bloomsbury Publishing is officially releasing two new additions to the Wizarding World we all know and love.

The Wizarding World started on the page, after all. Now, Bloomsbury Publishing is officially expanding its Pocket Potters line with two new books centered on three of Harry's closest allies: Minerva McGonagall, and the Weasley twins, Fred and George, due for release in February 2027. While they're aimed at younger readers, they're also exactly the kind of collectible releases that fans who definitely don't need more Harry Potter merchandise will buy anyway. Accio, books!

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

  • This is the only world on the list where doing good is understood as a very long defeat, fought anyway, by people who will never see how it ends.
  • You are drawn to grandeur, to white cities and deep forges and the sense that almost everything you touch is older than your entire family line.
  • The Rings of Power is set in the last good age before it all goes wrong, which gives every quiet scene in it a slightly unbearable weight.
  • You would take the hard road, and you would take it on foot.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

  • One Piece has run for decades on one very simple engine, which is a group of people who each want something impossible and have decided to go and get it together.
  • You collect people. Your ideal crew is a set of misfits who were each written off somewhere else before you turned up and offered them a better idea.
  • The world is enormous and deliberately ridiculous, and the emotional gut punches land harder because of that rather than in spite of it.
  • You would set sail knowing the map runs out, which is more or less the entire point of it.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

  • Witchers get paid to do what nobody else will and resented for existing at all, which is a trade most people would refuse and you would probably accept.
  • You have no real illusions about which side is the good one, largely because on the Continent that question tends to have a disappointing answer.
  • The best of these stories are not about the fighting. They are about somebody with a rigid code being repeatedly cornered into caring about people.
  • You would take the coin, finish the job properly, and be out of town before the story got told wrong.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

  • Percy Jackson takes the oldest stories we have and drops them into car parks and diners, which makes the gods considerably funnier and a great deal worse.
  • You would be the kid every school gave up on, who turns out to have been built for something nobody thought to test for.
  • The series is genuinely about friendship rather than destiny, and it says so out loud every time a prophecy tries to get in the way.
  • Camp Half-Blood is the real fantasy here: a place where the thing that made you difficult everywhere else is the thing that makes you belong.

What Are 'Pocket Potters' About?

Pocket Potters: Fred and George is illustrated by Helen Brady. The book will focus on Hogwarts' favorite troublemaking twins, from their years spent causing chaos at school to the creation of Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes. It'll explore their best pranks and inventions, their talent for getting around the rules, and their eventual decision to leave Hogwarts in spectacular fashion before opening their joke shop in Diagon Alley. Expect plenty of Puking Pastilles, Extendable Ears, and other products that probably shouldn't be anywhere near a classroom.

Likewise, Pocket Potters: McGonagall is illustrated by Natalie Smillie. The guide will explore the formidable Hogwarts professor's story, including her role as Head of Gryffindor House, her ability to transform into a tabby cat, and the Transfiguration lessons that generations of students learned not to arrive late for. It'll also look at McGonagall's relationship with Harry and her fellow professors, her fierce loyalty to Hogwarts, and the dry sense of humor hiding underneath that pointed hat.

The pair joins existing Pocket Potters releases for Harry Potter, Ron Weasley, Hermione Granger, Professor Dumbledore, and Luna Lovegood. Each book is full of quotes, story moments, illustrations, and small facts designed to introduce young readers to the Wizarding World in a colorful way.

Harry Potter will arrive on HBO and HBO Max on December 25.
