Devils' Penalty Kill Gets Better Since Connor Brown's Return From Injury

TheTools · 03.11.2026 15:24 · source · permalink

The New Jersey Devils have given up fewer slot passes and shots on the penalty kill since forward Connor Brown came back to the lineup Oct. 20. Sportlogiq tracked the data, and Bryce Salvador shared it.

A lower-body injury kept Brown out for the first couple of weeks of the 2026-27 season. As of the Oct. 30 report, he had played five games since returning. In that stretch he has two goals and an assist, and he averages 13:18 of ice time per game. Both goals came in a loss to the Colorado Avalanche.

Brown has a depth job this season. Last season he ended up in the top six. Now he skates on the fourth line with Amadeus Lombardi and Cody Glass, and he takes shifts on the penalty kill.

The Devils' penalty kill struggled until Brown joined the lineup. The Sportlogiq numbers below are per two minutes of penalty kill time, before and after he returned.

Completed passes to the slot fell from 1.74 to 1.17. Slot pass attempts dropped from 2.71 to 2.18. Shots on net went from 1.79 to 1.17, slot shots on net went from 1.12 to 0.67, and inner-slot shots on net went from 0.61 to 0.33.

According to Pucks and Pitchforks, that adds up to a 33% drop in completed passes to the slot and a 20% drop in slot pass attempts. Shots on net fell 35%, slot shots on net fell 40%, and inner-slot shots on net fell 46%. The samples are small, though. New Jersey played eight games before Brown came back and only five with him on the ice.

In those five games the Devils took six penalties and killed off five. They also scored a shorthanded goal. Brown got it.

Before the season began, the penalty kill lost a key piece when the Devils traded Dawson Mercer to Winnipeg. That deal brought goaltender Connor Hellebuyck to New Jersey.
