Olympus OM-1n Camera Review

TheTools · 18.11.2026 22:00 · source · permalink

The Olympus OM-1n is a beautiful reminder of an era when camera manufacturers were trying to make professional photographic equipment smaller without sacrificing the features photographers actually needed. Nearly five decades after its launch, this little beauty proved to me that it could not just turn heads with its looks, but also deliver the goods when it mattered most. It remains an enjoyable camera in an era when instant gratification seems to be the only thing that photography companies are aiming to deliver to their fans.

Olympus OM-1n Review Conclusions: Is It Worth Buying?

Verdict

Yes, buy the Olympus OM-1n camera if you find a reasonably priced unit (under $200) in mint condition today. Even if it needs a bit of CLA on arrival, as long as the inbuilt light meter works fine, you’ll find yourself enjoying the experience. This is the short version, but the full article goes much deeper, with image quality findings, our hands-on experience and the full tech specs.

If you enjoy analog photography, and the slowing down of your thought process, that comes with it you’ll find the Olympus OM-1n is a camera that ticks a lot of boxes for you. It’s small enough to carry almost everywhere and does make you wonder why many SLRs from the 70s and 80s were so bulky. This little Japanese camera was doing such a competent job back then with its inbuilt light meter and impeccable construction quality, that it makes you wonder why brands these days seem to be scrimping on quality.

The Olympus OM-1n was a camera that originally launched in 1979 as an updated variant of the OM-1, a camera billed as the lightest and smallest full-frame SLR of its time. The Olympus OM-1n model brought flash synchronization to the series and improved handling. My Olympus OM-1n is especially meaningful because it belonged to my grandfather and then to my younger aunt. There’s something wonderful about using a camera that was once part of more than one generation’s photographic life. The knowledge that the same shutter, film advance lever and viewfinder that I’m using today were once used by him is greatly humbling.

It’s kind of barebones in its features, compared to what cameras offer these days, but that’s what makes the experience of using the Olympus OM-1n special in some ways, to be challenged by the seemingly outdated limitations and come back with rewarding results.

⭐⭐⭐⭐

  • Exceptionally compact and unobtrusive for a 35mm SLR
  • Beautiful construction that’s strong enough to hammer nails into a brick
  • Large and clear optical viewfinder
  • Inbuilt light meter, but uses batteries that aren’t easy to source these days
  • Shutter speed dial around the lens mount takes a very long time to get used to
  • No auto modes, so get thinking creatively in order to get your shots

Experience: How Is the Olympus OM-1n for Photographers?

Pick up the Olympus OM-1n today, and you’ll probably be surprised by how small it is. At approximately 510g (a little over 1lb) without a lens, it feels closer to some compact modern cameras than the chunky mechanical SLRs we normally associate with the 1970s. It’s easily the smallest SLR in my analog collection and can get visually lost in my display cabinet. The only Olympus lens that I had was the Zuiko 50mm f1.4 lens that was attached to it, which too was small but quite capable. Olympus somehow created a working camera in a small sized body with a full mirror mechanism and pentaprism without compromising the usability of the camera. It even incorporated a mechanism to reduce mirror shock and shutter vibration, helping make the camera quieter than many of its contemporaries. Indeed the shutter here was a lot less loud than the (satisfying nevertheless) clunk my Nikon FM2 exhibits while taking a picture.

For street photography, this camera helps you blend into the background, not attracting attention at all (unless you pair it with a loud camera strap). It’s not a camera that announces your arrival at a scene, and for that reason especially you might find yourself getting more candid frames than with other SLRs of its time. But there is one thing that I still couldn’t find myself getting used to, and that’s the method for changing the shutter speed. It was nowhere to be found on the camea and for a few minutes when I first loaded film into the camera, I thought the ISO dial on the top somehow doubled as the shutter speed dial. Until I looked it up online and found that shutter speed adjustments weren’t to be done on the top or rear of the Olympus OM-1n, but in the front. The shutter speed dial, or rather the ring, is located directly in front of the lens mount of the camera body. It can look like it’s on the lens itself, such is the seamless looking design of the system when the lens is paired to the body. I almost always, by habit, moved my fingers to the top of the camera to change shutter speed, as I do with my Nikon FM2.

The built-in exposure meter is great, but this camera was made during an era when mercury batteries were commonly used in electronics. Of course, these batteries are no longer manufactured for ordinary consumer use because of environmental and safety restrictions. Your best bet today is the Wein MRB625 1.35V Zinc-Air Battery which can be found on Amazon or eBay.

Olympus OM Zuiko 50mm f1.4 Lens Image Quality Findings

Let’s step back for a moment and just admire the beauty of this lens, before we discuss the merits of the images it produces. Observe the front elements as you rotate the lens under light, and you’ll notice those gorgeous reflective coatings at some angles, showing shades of purple, amber, blue and green. Lenses these days have sophisticated optical coatings, that are arguably better at controlling reflections, but there is something visually captivating about the older Zuiko glass and other lenses from the analog era. The lens itself is compact, matching the Olympus OM-1n’s design philosophy perfectly. Don’t expect any lens from that era to compete with a modern one in terms of sharpness. But do expect oodles of character in them, just as I noticed on this lens.
