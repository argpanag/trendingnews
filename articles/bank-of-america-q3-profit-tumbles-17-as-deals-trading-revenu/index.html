Bank of America said Wednesday that its third quarter profit fell 17% from a year earlier as $1.2 billion regulatory penalty and a slowdown in its Wall Street machine dented higher lending income.

Bank of America's (BAC) stock fell more than 3% in premarket trading.

The nation's second biggest bank reported Q3 net income of $6.93 billion, or $0.91 per share, compared with the $1.10 per share analysts expected. "Excluding the regulatory penalty, Bank of America said it earned $8.1 billion, or $1.08 per share, with adjusted earnings per share rising 4% from a year earlier.

Revenue rose 3% to $30 billion, compared with expectations of roughly $30.6 billion.

US regulators charged the Charlotte, North Carolina-based bank $1.2 billion in fines on Tuesday afternoon over widespread failures in the banking giant's safeguards against money laundering and other illicit financial activity

Excluding the regulatory penalty, Bank of America said it earned $8.1 billion, or $1.08 per share.

"While investment banking and sales and trading revenues were lower, commercial lending grew in every client segment," Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan said in a statement with the release.

"Our momentum, reflected in growth in client deposits, wealth flows and consumer activity, supported solid earnings, balance-sheet growth and long-term shareholder value," he added.

The results also offered another glimpse into how America's biggest banks have navigated sharply rising borrowing costs, which has supported higher lending income while complicating their Wall Street earnings machines.

Bank of America's net interest income, a core measure for what the bank earns from lending, rose 8% from a year earlier to $16.4 billion, supported by higher loan and deposit balances.

Meanwhile, investment banking fees fell 11% from a year earlier to $1.8 billion while still surpassing analyst forecasts after CEO Moynihan telegraphed the bank's relatively softer period for dealmaking.

Fixed income trading revenue fell 17% from a year ago to $2.6 billion, offsetting an 11% increase in equities trading revenue to $2.5 billion.

"We saw healthy activity from our consumer, wealth management, business and commercial clients," Bank of America CFO Alastair Borthwick said in a statement with the release.
