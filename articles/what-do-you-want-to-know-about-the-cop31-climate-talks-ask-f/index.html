The Cop31 climate summit in Antalya is in its final days, and negotiators are fighting over a first draft of the deal.

It calls for “accelerated action to phase out fossil fuels, including through the immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems”, and for tripling adaptation finance for poorer countries. But with the world heading past the 1.5C limit and countries deeply divided over finance, adaptation and the “just transition”, can Cop31 deliver anything that matters?

The Guardian’s environment editor, Fiona Harvey, reported that the 18-page draft contains 144 separate items, many of which are unlikely to survive: “There are still at least four and a half days of heavy negotiating to go, so this text will go through many more iterations.” Campaigners are sceptical. Tzeporah Berman of the Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty Initiative said: “This mention in the draft text will last about 2 minutes and they know it.”

Ministers have now arrived in Antalya to start cutting the text down. Chris Bowen, the Australian minister presiding over the talks, admitted: “The first draft will look very different to the final draft.”

At the opening leaders’ summit, Hilda Heine, president of the Marshall Islands, warned: “Our response to the threat of overshoot must be overwhelming, or the world we know is over.” The UN secretary general, António Guterres, put it more bluntly: “It’s make or bake time.” Yet only 91 national leaders attended, and Donald Trump and Xi Jinping were among those absent.

The Guardian’s editorial argued that those pushing for a faster shift away from fossil fuels “are on the side of the future, while their opponents are stuck in the past”.

What do you want to know about what’s happening in Antalya? Can a summit co-hosted by an oil-producing Turkey and a coal-exporting Australia get a fossil-fuel phaseout over the line? Is 1.5C now out of reach, and what does “overshoot” really mean for the countries most at risk? And with the US government absent, who is leading?

Fiona Harvey and Ajit Niranjan will be live here at5pm GMT (12pm EST) on Tuesday, 17 November to answer your questions on the talks, the draft deal and what comes next.
