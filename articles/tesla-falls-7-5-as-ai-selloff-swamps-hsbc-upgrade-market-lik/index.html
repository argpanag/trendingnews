Tesla falls 7.5% as AI selloff swamps HSBC upgrade; market likely...

TheTools · 21.11.2026 15:40 · source · permalink

Research

The drop erased an estimated $112 billion of market value and left Tesla 3.6% below the $359 target HSBC set that morning on the chance of a SpaceX deal.

In brief

  • Tesla closed at $346.23 on Nov. 20, 2026, down 7.54%, about 2.1 times the S&P 500 fund's 3.51% loss and 1.4 times the Nasdaq 100 fund's 5.48% drop.
  • On Nov. 20, 2026, HSBC raised Tesla to Hold from Reduce with a $359 target, about 14% below the $417.53 consensus Longbridge reported as of Nov. 19.
  • XPeng fell 13.96% to $8.07 on Nov. 20, 2026, after HSBC cut it to Hold, its biggest one-day drop in the 289 sessions in Quantisnow's records.

Tesla closed at $346.23 on Friday, Nov. 20, down 7.54% from $374.48. It was the stock's biggest one-day drop since Nov. 5. Before the open, HSBC had upgraded the shares. During the session the S&P 500 fund SPY fell 3.51% and the Nasdaq 100 fund QQQ fell 5.48%. Taking Quantisnow's latest market value of $1.37 trillion, dated Nov. 21, as the post-drop figure, the drop erased about $112 billion.

The market rout plausibly explains roughly 3 to 5.5 points of Tesla's 7.5-point drop, and the data cannot pin the split down further. Rivian fell more than Tesla, and Lucid and the tech sector fell as much, which points to a high-beta electric-vehicle and growth selloff. Nothing in the data shows Tesla trading off SpaceX, which HSBC's note placed at the center of the bull case. XPeng's steeper fall after an HSBC downgrade the same morning leaves a much larger unexplained share, and HSBC gave a different reason for it.

Tesla also filed a DEFA14A at 5:27 PM ET on Nov. 20, after the close. It is the only fresh Tesla-specific item we found. The form is used for additional proxy-soliciting material. We have not read the filing and do not know whether it relates to SpaceX or the special committees.

Risk-off selling likely explains much of Tesla's drop, but the split is uncertain

The selloff was broad. In Quantisnow's data, semiconductors fell 10.55%, the tech sector fund 7.21% and the equal-weight S&P 500 fund 1.96%. Gold fell 3.63% and Bitcoin 5.90%, while long Treasuries rose 1.36% and consumer staples 0.68%, a pattern that points to risk-off liquidation. CNBC said the S&P 500 and the Dow closed in correction territory and tied the deepening selling to a Reuters report of an SEC probe into Nvidia's accounting. We could not confirm that report.

The chart shows Tesla fell more than either index fund and about as much as the tech sector fund. Quantisnow's records do not include a daily beta for Tesla, so we bracket the market's share with two assumptions. A response of about 0.8 times SPY's move would explain about 3 points. A one-for-one move with the Nasdaq 100 would explain 5.5. Neither is Quantisnow data, and the 0.8 figure comes from comparing cumulative returns over several sessions, which is not a true sensitivity.

Other evidence fits a market-driven day. Quantisnow's records show Friday was the biggest one-day drop since Nov. 5 for both Tesla and SPY. Rivian, which has no part in any merger talk, fell about 8% according to TIKR's report on Yahoo Finance. At midday, 24/7 Wall St. had Tesla down 4% alongside Rivian down 5% and Lucid down 4%. That suggests the weakness was market-wide or specific to electric-vehicle stocks. Tesla's volume on IEX, one exchange's share of trading, was 1.0 times its 20-day average while SPY's was 1.8 times, which hints at no unusual rush out of Tesla, though IEX is a thin slice of total volume. Benzinga had Tesla down 6% at $351.96 in the early afternoon.

HSBC's upgrade rests on a possible SpaceX deal

HSBC analyst Michael Tyndall raised Tesla to Hold from Reduce and more than doubled his target, from $157 to $359, Investing.com reported. The upgrade rests on a possible merger with SpaceX rather than on the car business. Tyndall wrote that with M&A likely to dominate discussion, "we see limited further downside from current levels." He argued that control is the main motive for a deal and estimated that a combination would dilute Elon Musk's stake to about 60%.

TIKR and Basenor described the target as two equally weighted cases. The first is $507 a share if SpaceX buys Tesla at a 33% premium to its 30-day average price. The second is $212 for Tesla on its own fundamentals. TIKR reported that both companies have formed special committees of independent directors and that no terms exist yet. These details come from TIKR and Basenor, a blog. We did not see the HSBC note, a filing or a company statement.

Measured against HSBC's two values, the stock moved from 55% of the way between $212 and $507 on Thursday to 46% on Friday, but most of that move could simply be the market.

SpaceX is a plausible channel for any leftover points. TIKR reported that SpaceX fell about 7%, citing a Bloomberg report that it sold $32 billion of bonds at yields above 8% on Thursday. We could not confirm either figure. Any offer paid in SpaceX stock would be worth less if SpaceX falls, but whether Tesla actually traded off SpaceX on Friday is not shown in our data or in the reports we read.

Friday's close is 3.6% below HSBC's $359 target. The target sits about 14% below the $417.53 consensus from 47 institutions that Longbridge reported as of Nov. 19. It is 13% below the $412.48 average StockAnalysis attributed to 44 analysts polled by S&P Global; we could not confirm the as-of date for that figure. The $212 stand-alone value is 39% below the close.

XPeng's fall leaves a far larger unexplained share

HSBC's case on XPeng had nothing to do with a merger. The Fly, via TipRanks, reported that HSBC cut XPeng to Hold from Buy, lowered its target to $10.10 from $17 and trimmed its 2026 to 2028 volume estimates by 1% to 7%, because older models such as the M03 are losing sales faster than it expected. Electric-Vehicles.com identified the analyst as Yuqian Ding and said the shares hit their lowest level since September 2024.

XPeng is a volatile China-based stock, so some of its 13.96% drop was the market. A one-for-one move with QQQ, an assumption our data does not establish, would explain 5.5 points and leave 8.5. A sensitivity twice the Nasdaq 100's would still leave about 3 points. A ratio to SPY is not useful here: XPeng's five-day drop of 13.9% against its one-day drop of 13.96% shows it was flat on net over the prior four sessions while the market fell. The size of the drop also stands apart: Friday's fall was XPeng's biggest in Quantisnow's 289-session file, while both Tesla and SPY had a bigger drop as recently as Nov. 5. Volume ran 1.9 times its 20-day average on IEX, about the same as SPY's 1.8 times, so trading activity alone does not mark it as unusual.

The timing suggests the downgrade drove much of XPeng's excess loss. The two HSBC notes gave different reasons, a possible deal for Tesla and an aging model lineup for XPeng. Neither note, as reported, cites a demand problem common to electric-vehicle makers, so the pairing may be two company stories on one bad day. Rivian and Lucid also fell without an HSBC note, however, so a group-wide electric-vehicle move cannot be ruled out.

Tesla is still valued at a premium to its falling profits

Over the past year Tesla is down 12.4% while SPY is up 5.9%, as the chart shows. Friday's close is 29.3% below the 52-week closing high of $489.79 and 16.1% above the low of $298.23. The stock is down 0.8% over three months, so it is roughly where it was in late August.

The selloff has not made the valuation cheap. Diluted earnings for the past four quarters add up to $0.91 a share in Tesla's SEC filings (financials), which puts Friday's price at about 380 times trailing earnings. TIKR put the forward multiple at 175 times on Thursday, against a five-year average of 109 times.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was flat at $28.0 billion. Operating income was $718 million, a margin of about 2.6%, compared with $1.6 billion, or 5.7%, a year earlier. Diluted EPS fell to $0.22 from $0.39.

Quantisnow's records show Tesla fell the day after each of its last four earnings reports, including 14.5% in July, an average absolute move of 5.4%. The stock has tended to trade hard on its own news.

Quantisnow has no record of open-market insider purchases of Tesla stock in the past 180 days. Analyst revisions in our records have run one way since Oct. 22, with four upgrades and no downgrades. CLSA moved to Hold with a $400 target and BNP Paribas Exane to Neutral, both on Oct. 22. Barclays went to Overweight at $450 on Oct. 26, and HSBC to Hold at $359 on Friday. Three of the four took bearish analysts to neutral. StockAnalysis's table shows Sell and Strong Sell ratings falling from six in June to two in November, while few analysts turned outright bullish.

What to watch next week

Monday, Nov. 23, shows first whether the market rout continues. Tesla's move against SPY and against SpaceX's shares will say more, though one session cannot cleanly separate market effects from company effects.

The unread DEFA14A is the second item. SpaceX's shares and debt are the third. Under HSBC's framework, a weaker SpaceX lowers the value of a stock-funded offer, and TIKR reported that terms could come as soon as December. The week is short: U.S. markets close for Thanksgiving on Thursday, Nov. 26. Quantisnow shows no scheduled events for Tesla in the next 45 days.
