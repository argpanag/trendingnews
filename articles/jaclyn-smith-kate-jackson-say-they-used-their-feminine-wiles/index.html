Jaclyn Smith and Kate Jackson are opening up about how they handled beauty standards while defying stereotypes while starring in Charlie’s Angels.

The duo spoke in an appearance on the Monday, Sep. 14, season premiere of The Drew Barrymore Show.

During the episode, host Drew Barrymore asked, “How did you guys handle beauty standards as you were literally also defying and defining them?”

Jackson, 77, then responded, “I think we didn’t think about it. We just did what we were supposed to do, and that was it.”

Smith, 80, acknowledged that fame comes with judgment, adding, “I do think it does bring judgment and takes and amplifies everything.”

“You’re judged every minute for beauty, but I think the nice thing about Charlie’s is, we sort of used our, our feminine wiles and our beauty to accomplish our case, and beauty, it’s a hard thing,” Smith added.

Barrymore — who starred in the 2000 film version of Charlie’s Angels alongside Cameron Diaz and Lucy Liu — then noted: “But I also love when women just open doors for other women. And to me, that’s what you did.”

“What you guys created with Charlie’s Angels is something that I think we’re still trying to emulate and hold up as women can be attractive inside, maybe outside, but I think you could meet the most attractive person and if they don’t have the goods, they’re not attractive,” Barrymore added.

Barrymore added of the film version: “I think that when we set out to make Charlie’s Angels, I always said that we should not pretend to be men.”

Added Jackson: “We don’t need to pretend to be men. Women are just as good as men.”

Jackson and Smith starred alongside the late Farrah Fawcett in the original Charlie’s Angels in 1976, before her departure at the end of the first season. Cheryl Ladd later replaced Fawcett in the second season and remained on the show until its 1981 conclusion.
