Russell lashes out at Verstappen after Las Vegas GP clash: ‘He’s screwing all of us’

TheTools · 21.11.2026 06:00 · source · permalink

  • Red Bull driver on front row alongside Mercedes rival

  • Antonelli quickest in qualifying but will start from sixth

Red Bull driver on front row alongside Mercedes rival

Antonelli quickest in qualifying but will start from sixth

George Russell has condemned Max Verstappen with a damning indictment of his driving at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, saying the Dutchman was “screwing” other drivers and that the four-time world champion believed he was “bigger and better than everyone else”.

After the pair came together on the street circuit during the final practice session on Friday, Russell had clearly had enough with Verstappen’s approach at overtaking him while he was preparing for a flying lap.

“There’s nothing in the rules to say you cannot overtake someone who is starting their lap but when you do this you are screwing this person over and he does this week-in, week-out with many drivers and everybody accepts it,” Russell said.

“I’m not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this, and walk around like they’re bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody has got to stand up to that guy.”

The clash in Las Vegas was investigated by the stewards where they banged wheels at turn 14 in final practice. Verstappen had overtaken Russell on the way into the corner then slowed down in front of him and, according to the FIA stewards’ decision, then changed direction. The move broke what is understood to be a gentleman’s agreement between drivers not to overtake on the warm up to a flying lap. Russell reciprocated by ducking back up the inside and the pair bashed wheels.

The Red Bull driver accused Russell of trying to push him into the barriers, while the British driver’s race engineer, Marcus Dudley said he had seen the Dutchman “cut in front of you”. After an investigation by the FIA Verstappen was found responsible and given a warning by the stewards but no penalty.

After qualifying – with Verstappen taking pole and Russell alongside him following a penalty that pushed Kimi Antonelli back to sixth on the grid – there was a frosty atmosphere between the pair. Verstappen was not even willing to address the incident. “Nothing. Nothing happened,” he said, adding when pressed on the matter: “I don’t even want to waste my time on that. It’s not worth it to me at all.”

Russell was equally stony-faced. “No confusion. I mean, it was pretty clear, I think,” he said of his outburst.

There is little love lost between the pair. In 2024 before the Qatar GP Russell said Verstappen had threatened to “put me on my fucking head in the wall” after the Dutchman lost pole position having been penalised for impeding Russell.

The British driver said at the time: “I’m not going to sit here and accept it. People have been bullied by Max for years now . You can’t question his driving abilities [but] he cannot deal with adversity whenever anything has gone against him.”

Last year Russell insisted that Verstappen should have faced disqualification after he crashed into the British driver at the Spanish GP, claiming he felt he had done so deliberately and that he was setting a bad example for young drivers.

Verstappen was bullish after a race where he received a 10-second penalty that dropped him from fifth at the flag to 10th, dismissed Russell’s comments, maintaining he had no regrets and mocking the British driver’s reactions with the comment: “Well, I’ll bring some tissues next time.” The penalty cost Verstappen nine points and he went on to lose the world championship to McLaren’s Lando Norris by two points.

In qualifying, Antonelli was only denied pole by a grid penalty. A five-place sanction for taking a new engine means Verstappen starts from the front of the grid in Nevada.

Russell, meanwhile, was clear he would have talks with Mercedes to discuss why, with the slipstream proving vital in Las Vegas, they did not engage the Italian for a tow given they knew he faced a grid drop. Antonelli still has every chance on Sunday, but it is Russell and Verstappen who hold the advantage.

In contrast, Ferrari floundered, forced to fold before even the final hand at a race where they enjoy huge support. Lewis Hamilton was struggling with a lack of grip and could not hook up a good lap. Neither he nor his teammate, Charles Leclerc, made it out of Q2, with Leclerc finishing in 11th and Hamilton 12th. They will start from 10th and 11th after McLaren’s Oscar Piastri also received a 10-place penalty for taking a new engine.

The impact of the new regulations threw an unexpected curveball into the mix at the Las Vegas Strip circuit, where the active aerodynamics of the straight-line mode proved to have a major impact on the long straight. Riding in the slipstream of a car in front was far more of an advantage than had been expected and it led to some jostling beneath the lights.

By the time to take the decisive runs arrived it was clear a tow was proving hugely beneficial. Verstappen, who had been squarely in the slipstream of his teammate, Isack Hadjar, was up by almost three-tenths on his first quick lap in Q3 to take provisional pole but Mercedes were using no such tactics and nor had Ferrari.

Russell then specifically asked the team if he could have a tow from Antonelli given the Italian had a grid penalty, a request that fell on closed ears.

As the field hustled for position for the final runs, with no driver wanting to be at the front, Antonelli decided to take a chance. He backed out and dropped to the back of the pack, losing time in the process. It was an immensely close run thing for the Italian, he was in the slipstream of all the other cars but in dropping back crossed the line to start his last lap with barely a second to spare.

It proved inspired, as he took the top spot with a time of 1min 33.893sec, two and half tenths up on Verstappen after a session where he had been far from comfortable.
