PlayStation Is Done With the PS VR Headset, Says New Report—What Happened?

TheTools · 08.10.2026 07:30 · source · permalink

A new report suggests PlayStation has stepped away from virtual reality, and the clue comes from a patent deal rather than an announcement.

Sony has transferred dozens of virtual and augmented reality patents to Meta, a major VR competitor. Neither company has commented publicly, so the full picture is still unclear.

Sony Is Reportedly Done With PS VR Headset

A report shared by ZDNet Korea has found that Sony transferred 43 United States patents to Meta between June and September 2026. The patents cover virtual, augmented, and mixed reality technology, along with head-mounted display systems. Road to VR noted that Sony filed them between 2010 and 2022, a span that ComicBook says covers the development of both PS VR headsets.

The transfer comes from a Confirmatory Assignment agreement the two companies signed in December 2025. Under that deal, 419 patents worldwide are expected to change hands. Of those, 180 are United States patents, while the other 239 were filed in Japan, China, Korea, Europe, and other regions.

ZDNet Korea sees the move as an apparent effort to shrink Sony's XR hardware business, and it expects the number of transferred patents to keep growing. Road to VR echoed that view, saying the sale may suggest Sony is "materially scaling back its XR hardware ambitions." Sony has not confirmed any change to its VR plans.

Sony US Patents of AR, VR, XR, and More

US Patent and Trademark Office records show the transfers happened in three batches. Two patents moved in June, 20 followed in July, and 21 more arrived in September.

The agreement covers far more than the 43 patents recorded so far. ZDNet Korea expects that list to grow, because 180 United States patents fall under the agreement.

ZDNet Korea also noted that Sony once turned down a request from Apple to expand production capacity for the OLED on silicon displays used in the Vision Pro. At the time, industry watchers took that as a sign Sony was reluctant to grow that side of the business.

Meta has recently announced new Meta VR Glasses, and research firm Omdia says Meta held 81 percent of the global AI glasses market in the first half of the year. Road to VR says the glasses are slim and light, priced at $1,300 and due in Spring 2027.

Is This the End for the PlayStation VR?

ComicBook says PlayStation's support for PS VR2 has been minimal since its 2023 launch, and it describes the patent sale as one more sign of PlayStation moving on. "Transferring a huge swath of patents to a big VR competitor all but signals PlayStation's unwillingness to keep looking into VR, at least for the time being," the outlet writes.

The outlet notes that PlayStation published only three new games for PS VR2: "Horizon Call of the Mountain," "Firewall Ultra," and "My First Gran Turismo." The developer of "Firewall Ultra" was shut down within four months, and Firesprite, the PlayStation studio with the deepest VR background, is now working on "Until Dawn 2." PlayStation also never remastered or ported "Astro Bot Rescue Mission" or "Blood & Truth" from the original headset.

Bloomberg reported in March 2024 that Sony paused PS VR2 production after making just over 2 million units. The headset launched at $549.99, about $50 more than the PS5 at the time. Sony later released a PC adapter that let owners play Steam games, which ComicBook says came across as PlayStation deferring to Steam's library.

ComicBook notes that PS VR2 games keep arriving on the PlayStation Store, supported by third-party and indie developers. Publisher Impact Inked also announced in August that it is porting a slate of mystery games to PS VR2 through its Unlock the Vault initiative.

PlayStation has also used paid partnerships to build up the headset's library. A Capcom data leak revealed that PlayStation paid the publisher $5 million to bring "Resident Evil 7: Biohazard" to the original PS VR. It is unconfirmed whether similar deals covered the VR modes in "Resident Evil Village" and the "Resident Evil 4" remake, and "Resident Evil Requiem" never received an official VR mode.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the PlayStation VR?

PlayStation VR is Sony's line of virtual reality headsets built for PlayStation consoles. The original PS VR launched in October 2016 for the PS4 at about $400 and tracked movement through the PlayStation Camera. It also works with the PS4 Pro, and with the PS5 when paired with a PlayStation Camera adapter that Sony offered to PS VR owners at no extra cost.

The second generation, PS VR2, launched on February 22, 2023 for $549.99. It works only with the PS5, connects through a single USB C cable, and tracks movement with cameras built into the headset itself. Features include OLED displays, eye tracking, and haptic feedback in the headset. The price included a pair of PS VR2 Sense controllers.

How Many PlayStation VRs Are There?

There are two PlayStation VR headsets, the original PS VR from 2016 and the PS VR2 from 2023. A PS VR2 PC adapter followed on August 7, 2024, which lets the headset play games from Steam on a compatible PC. Sony also sells an enterprise mixed reality headset built with Siemens, though it is aimed at businesses rather than gamers.

Bloomberg reported that the original PS VR took three years to sell 5 million units, while Sony said PS VR2 sold slightly under 600,000 units in its first six weeks. Ahead of the PS VR2 launch, more than 600 games were available for the original PS VR, compared with around 30 expected for PS VR2 in its first month.

What Will Happen to Old PS VRs?

Nothing in the current reports says that headsets already in homes will stop working, and neither Sony nor Meta has confirmed any plan to remove support. PS VR2 games are still arriving on the PlayStation Store from third party and indie developers, so owners can keep buying and playing new releases.

The reports also do not suggest that games already purchased for either headset will be removed from players' libraries. PS VR2 owners can also connect the headset to a PC through the adapter, which gives access to Steam's VR library.

Sony has not said whether its next console will support PS VR or PS VR2, so support on future hardware remains uncertain. Compatibility between PlayStation VR generations has not been automatic before, since PS VR2 cannot play original PS VR games and the PS5 supports the first headset only through a PlayStation Camera adapter.
