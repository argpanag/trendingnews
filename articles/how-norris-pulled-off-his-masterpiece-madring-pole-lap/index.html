How Norris pulled off his 'masterpiece' Madring pole lap

TheTools · 12.09.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Lando Norris clinched pole position for the Spanish Grand Prix with a stunning last-gasp effort in Qualifying.

A quite remarkable lap from Lando Norris at the end of Q3 in Madrid secured a third pole position in four races for the McLaren driver, and prompted his team boss Andrea Stella to describe it as a “bit of a masterpiece”.

This one was special not just in the context of the 2026 season, but also the whole of Norris' career.

Perhaps the most remarkable thing was that a slide at the 22nd and final corner, as his tyres began to cry enough, cost him over 0.3 sec – and yet he was already so far up on where he’d been before that it was still good enough to secure his 19th career pole.

It was a performance that was enjoyed as much by the McLaren pit wall as by watching fans, especially as it appeared to come out of the blue.

“I would say that definitely this was one of the most impressive laps that I witnessed, as somebody that watches telemetry in 450 Grands Prix,” said Stella afterwards.

“Because we were not honestly performing to the level of scoring of pole position. I think at no stage during the weekend we demonstrated that the car was performing at the level of being the best car.

“We were lacking quite significantly in the first sector – low-speed and kerbing – and for the drivers it was a very difficult car to nail a lap. We saw that there were spells of potential, but it's always very difficult to put it together.”

As Stella noted, the pole lap would have been more notable without the loss of time at the end of it.

“I think the most impressive thing is the fact that it's a pole position despite having lost three and a half tenths in the last corner,” said the Italian. “Which means that it would have been a pretty convincing pole position without this issue.

“At the same time, this issue is a consequence of pushing to the level that Lando managed to push. But today, everyone will push the limits.

"The thing that makes the lap unique, when you look especially at the telemetry, is the precision, the braking, the speed you carry an entry, the striking of the kerb, the going on power – everything is executed with a pretty unique level of precision.

“So for me, that's if anything the main attribute that would associate to this lap, just the incredible precision in the metre-by-metre execution.”

Through the weekend the McLaren engineering team focussed on that critical first part of the lap, with its pair of tight complexes at Turns 1 and 2, and 5, 5A and 6.

Progress wasn’t helped by the gearbox issue that cost Norris crucial track time in FP2, but into FP3 and Qualifying further tweaks gradually brought the improvements that the team were seeking.

“Shout out to Lando, but let me say also to the team,” said Stella. “Because we had the clear objective from the first runs in practice to add some low-speed performance and kerbing performance.

“And we kept doing that session after session, but obviously P2 was a bit of loss of ground, because Lando lost pretty much the entire session. So I think this makes his work in Qualifying even more remarkable. And also on Oscar, we were experiencing a little problem on the car, so we didn't have great references. We kept learning, we kept putting this together.”

Stella stressed that the challenge was to overcome the inherent characteristics of the MCL40.

“The car much more naturally performs well in high-speed, fast-flowing sections,” he said. “But struggles to be consistent when you brake and you carry the brakes into low-speed, and then you have to hit a kerb – it's very unpredictable.

“And here there's many of these kinds of corners. So to start with, in FP1, we saw the difficulties. What I appreciated in the work of the team is that we gave ourselves a clear objective, and we started to nail several areas of the setup that ultimately compounded and allowed the performance of the car to go to another level.

“So corner 5, corner 6, I think it's one of the most difficult corners of the entire championship. The fact that a McLaren could have the fastest Sector 1, it was not only in 5 and 6, we can see in the GPS overlays that we are also the fastest in corner 1 and 2.

“But once again, the amount of kerb that you take in corner 1 and landing without upsetting the car, this is why I say the lap was almost unrepeatable, because all this was nailed. So the gain in Sector 1 comes 50% in 5/6, but the other 50% in corner 1 and 2.”

The set-up tweaks also paid off as Stella further explained: “Like I said before, the car was completely changed from the start of FP1 to perform well in these two corners, and corner 7 and corner 20. These are the places where we focussed, because we knew that in the other corners the performance was coming quite naturally for the characteristics of the car.

“And in these places, we forced the car to work, but still the performance needed to be extracted. And like I said, this required a level of precision that thankfully we were able to deploy in the final run of Q3. But you know, that lap gives you a sense that it would be difficult to be repeated.”

He added: “Still, I think the exploitation of car potential for the pole position lap is pretty unique. I would say that if we put another set of tyres, I mean, we would gain three-tenths in the last corner, but I'm not sure we would be able to repeat what Lando was able to do in the first 21 corners.”

The only downside for Norris and the team was that slide at the very end of the lap as the tyres were past their best.

“I think in the last corner he just paid the price of the fact that the lap was coming like one second faster than any other lap,” Stella explained.

“Therefore, there was quite a lot more that was extracted from the tyres. But the tyres were tired, and somehow Lando did not take this into account, and he entered the corner with the same impetus that he had in some other corners.

“I think it's just a little bit of a toll that was presented by the tyres – he oversteered in entry, and this means that he loses completely the racing line. And like I say, he lost more than three-tenths.”
