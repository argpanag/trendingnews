Munster's Gavin Coombes feared backlash after accusation against Leinster's Tadhg Furlong

TheTools · 16.11.2026 21:05 · source · permalink

Munster's Gavin Coombes. Pic: ©INPHO/Ben Brady

Gavin Coombes feared a public backlash following his accusation of foul play against Tadhg Furlong last month.

The Munster forward alleged that the Leinster prop had grabbed his testicles in the course of a ruck during their URC interpro derby at the Aviva Stadium after which Furlong was ultimately cleared unanimously by an independent disciplinary committee.

More than two weeks have passed since the decision was made but the URC made the full report available to media late on Monday afternoon. The full document stretches to 21 pages and provides an exhaustive record of the process and of the end result.

It explained how Coombes “described how he felt a lot of pressure upon him, not from any person, but from a recognition of the consequences of making the allegation, because of the player's high status in the public eye and in Ireland."

“He described how he was having something of a "panic attack", although qualified this to say he did not mean a full panic attack in the literal/medical sense. He said that he was worried about the consequences of being the victim in this situation.” 

When asked more about this ‘pressure’, the Munster back row said that he knew social media would play a huge part and that being vilified was a real fear. However, he also said that he knew what had happened with the incident in question.

Guy Easterby, Leinster’s chief operating officer, also appeared before the committee where he gave a glowing testimony for his player. He also “attested to the upset and distress which the player had demonstrated following the allegation”.

Coombes’ written statement stated how he was an assist tackler against Leinster’s Joe McCarthy in the 37th minute of the game, and that he ended up on top of the lock in the ruck when there was an attempt to “clean” him away by another opposition player.

“During this I felt someone’s hand come up between my legs and then reach up and grab my testicles, then I felt a firm squeeze of my testicles from the players hand which lasted 2 or 3 seconds before I could react and push his hand away.

“I then turned to see who it was and why he was doing such a thing – “I said what the fuck”. I then let my captain know what had happened at the next stop of play and he referred it to the referee.

“I also said to the Leinster player after the match "You know what you did" he replies (sic) don’t point your finger at me and he offered no apology."

Furlong denied that this exchange had taken place.

Dr Jamie Kearns, head of Munster’s medical team, said in a statement that he assessed Coombes on the pitch and that he complained of pain around his testicle/groin area at the time having felt someone grab the area.

He was able to continue, complained of some soreness in the area after the final whistle but added that there was no visible swelling/discolouration. An assessment 36 hours after the match was normal as well.

Coombes went through an interview with the citing commissioner approximately 40 minutes after the incident itself, and explained that he had spoken to members of his team management before this took place.

“It was put to him that his verbal account to the Citing Commissioner made no mention of the duration of the alleged squeeze or that it had been his testicles that had been squeezed. He acknowledged that this was correct,” said the report.

“He was asked what had caused him to provide more detail in his later description. He said that, when he made his written statement, he did not feel under as much pressure and was in a “better headspace” to explain matters without emotion clouding what he wanted to say.” 

Furlong gave evidence while referring to a number of video clips and stated his wish to explain the incident in the context of how rugby is being played at present in terms of rucking and mauling.

“He explained that the new maul laws meant as a player defending a maul on the outside, he was not allowed to touch the ground with his hand and so his left hand would wraparound the opponent (what he called a “second row bind”) and then it would be hooked underneath and between the legs of the opponent.” 

In response to questions, Furlong said that his actions were very similar to the other legitimate instances of hooking of a leg shown in the clips and that “it would be nearly impossible to target someone at that speed and with that adjustment” to do so.

He stated he had not done so.

He repeated that he was 100% certain his hand had not made contact with Coombes’ testicles, “unless his hand brushed them on the way in, but accepted some contact could have been made with another part of him”.

The committee concluded that Coombes had given an honest account of the incident and had not sought to embellish the facts of the case.

However, it noted that he had been reticent about certain details, including the identity of the player whom he accused, the duration of the incident or how the pressure to his testicles made him feel, when asked by the citing commissioner after the game. And that he had then provided further information in his written statement.

Its conclusion was that Furlong was “entitled to call into question the accuracy of Mr Coombes' evidence, given the absence of a detailed account when he had an earlier opportunity to provide one”.

It reviewed the testimony and images – there were nine angles used of the incident in question – before coming to its decision, unanimously, not to suspend Furlong.

“The Committee therefore concluded that, on the balance of probabilities, the most likely scenario was that, during the fast-moving and dynamic ruck, Mr Coombes felt pressure applied to his testicles and believed at the time that the Player had grabbed them,” the summation read.

“The Committee accepted the Player’s account, which can also be seen on the video footage, that as Mr Coombes fell onto his side his legs closed tightly around the Player’s hand and forearm.

“In the Committee’s view, that is the most likely cause of what Mr Coombes felt: a static, all-round pressure arising from his own legs closing around the Player’s arm, rather than from any grip by the Player’s hand.

“When Mr Coombes then reached down and found the Player’s hand in the general area of his upper thigh and/or genitals, he honestly, but mistakenly, believed that it was the hand which had grabbed and squeezed him.”
