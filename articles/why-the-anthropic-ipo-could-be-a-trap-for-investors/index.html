Anthropic's IPO is Wall Street's Black Friday doorbuster. But IPO history says whatever you grab on day one usually goes on sale later.

Anthropic (ANT) is expected to price its shares Wednesday evening and start trading Thursday on the Nasdaq. Orders reportedly came in at nearly seven times the shares on offer, and the top of its $70-$78 range values it at up to about $1.4 trillion, fully diluted.

The doorbuster price goes mostly to big funds, though Anthropic is setting aside up to 20% of its shares for individual investors through brokers including Charles Schwab (SCHW), Fidelity and Robinhood (HOOD). Everyone else buys the first trade, and that's where the trouble usually starts.

This year's three biggest US IPOs, SpaceX (SPCX), SK hynix (SKHY) and Cerebras (CBRS), all trade below their first trades, by less than 10% to about 70%. SK hynix and Cerebras are below their IPO prices, too.

Every one of the 12 biggest US IPOs since 2008 fell below their first trade within a year.

The first-day low

Kathy Donnelly, co-author of "The Lifecycle Trade," has said most IPOs undercut their first-day low within three weeks. Seven of those 12 giants did it the next day. SpaceX took seven sessions.

The dip didn't decide the ending. Visa (V) and Facebook, now Meta (META), are each up more than tenfold from their first trades. Rivian (RIVN) and Coupang (CPNG) are still down more than 75%.

Doorbuster math

Anthropic's existing shareholders paid an average of $11.50 a share, according to its IPO filing. IPO buyers will pay nearly seven times that at the top of the range, and first-day buyers could pay a lot more. SpaceX opened about 11% above its IPO price. Cerebras opened nearly 90% higher.

Insiders can sell up to 316 million shares from day one, and up to 3.3 billion more before the usual six-month lockup ends. That's nearly four times the roughly 950 million shares in the deal.

Tusk Venture Partners co-founder Bradley Tusk told Yahoo Finance that Anthropic's valuation of about $1.4 trillion is "totally based on promise and potential."

The long game

The longer history of hot IPOs isn't much kinder. Jay Ritter of the University of Florida tracked more than 9,000 IPOs from 1975 to 2021. Three years after the first close, 60% were flat or lower, and nearly 4 in 10 had lost more than half their value. Only 16% more than doubled.

Bulls see a different kind of company. ARK Invest CEO Cathie Wood told Yahoo Finance that Anthropic could justify a valuation of almost $2 trillion. "Believe it or not, we are thinking it's a very reasonable valuation," she said of the roughly $1.4 trillion price tag.
