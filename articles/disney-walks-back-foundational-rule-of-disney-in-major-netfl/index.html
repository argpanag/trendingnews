Disney Walks Back Foundational Rule of Disney+ in Major Netflix Streaming Deal

TheTools · 02.10.2026 16:17 · source · permalink

Over the last seven years, Disney has helped change the entertainment world with its work on Disney+, the company's exclusive streaming service. Originally, the streamer largely hosted Disney's enormous cache of past movies and TV shows, including Walt Disney animation classics, Star Wars' Skywalker Saga, and the first decade of stories from the Marvel Cinematic Universe. From there, Disney expanded into exclusive shows hosted only on Disney+, adding new chapters to Marvel and Star Wars while also utilizing other franchises and original storytelling.More recently, Disney finalized its purchase of Hulu, leading to the two streamers being bundled together for purchase. By the end of the year, Hulu will be phased out entirely as its entire library will migrate to Disney+ alongside the rest of Disney's enormous cache of content. This comes amid a massive shift across the industry, especially given the Warner Bros./Paramount merger and its transition to Skydance, which will still keep both HBO Max and Paramount+ in play. Now, another major shift is underway for Disney+, as it partners with another top streamer.

According to Variety, Disney Entertainment and Netflix have entered into a massive new content licensing agreement. This will bring a wide mix of library titles and current-season TV shows to Netflix for undisclosed time periods. The first result of this deal will be the first two seasons of Percy Jackson and the Olympians coming to Netflix starting on Oct. 4, 2026, staying there for a three-month promotional window as Season 3 debuts on Disney+ on Nov. 20. Additionally, all five Ice Age movies from 20th Century Studios will be available to watch on Netflix on the same day, before Ice Age: Boiling Point hits theaters on Feb 5., 2027.Seasons 1-4 of Will Trent, the first two seasons of Shifting Gears, and Tracker's first three seasons will also be available on Netflix internationally outside of the USA between Nov. 2026 and Feb. 2027, as new seasons of all three shows are on the way. Furthermore, the deal includes a selection of non-franchised Disney-branded films from Walt Disney Animation Studios and Pixar, with titles like Soul, Elio, Raya and the Last Dragon, and more set to air globally on Netflix.

This will bring even more visibility to many of Disney's biggest properties for a new audience on Netflix, as both streamers navigate a shifting landscape. While both Disney+ and Netflix will continue to produce and release their own content separately from one another, this move further unites the two media giants and only adds to the intrigue of what will come for viewers in the immediate future.
