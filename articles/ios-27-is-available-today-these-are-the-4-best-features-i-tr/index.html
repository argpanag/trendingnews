iOS 27 is available today — these are the 4 best features I tried in beta

TheTools · 14.09.2026 20:52 · source · permalink

Siri AI stands out, but there's a ton of other great stuff present in Apple's new software.

It's official: Apple released iOS 27 today to everyone after a couple of months of beta testing. You can download iOS 27 by going to the Software Update tab under General in the Settings app. Select iOS 27 on the screen and follow the instructions from there — but make sure your Apple device will support iOS 27 first by checking the list at the bottom of Apple's iOS 27 page. iOS 27 is supported on iPhone SE (2nd gen) models and newer.

I tested the new software update in beta for weeks and found the changes to be a little less than life-changing, but nevertheless, they add up to a pleasantly practical and new-feeling experience. The best thing about iOS 27 is, hands-down, Siri AI. Apple has caught up in the AI race (by borrowing Google's capabilities) and now Siri has a much better understanding of the world. Siri AI has been intelligently baked into your iPhone's info and apps and the Visual Intelligence feature takes its cue from competitors with a distinctly Apple-esque ease of use. But the new software has tricks unrelated to the assistant, too. Here are some of my favorite new features.

1. Big widgets!

Your Music (or Weather or Calendar) widget can now take up an entire Home page

You could make larger widgets on your Home Screen before, but now you can make them really big — to the point that they take up a full screen. So far just Calendar, Weather, Photos and Apple Music have this ability, but as app developers get a hold of the new API, we'll no doubt see lots of fun, full-page widgets. 

You can add a widget the same way as in iOS 26: Long press on a blank spot on the Home Screen, tap the paintbrush icon (formerly Edit) then tap Add Widget. From there, you can select the app and type of info you want to display (such as your recently played in Music or an event list in Calendar). You can also long press the app icon in the Home Screen to create a widget, but you won't get options for the type of info the widget displays.

I made the Music app a full-page widget showing recently played music, which made it easy to start my focusing playlist without opening the app. I made a full-page Weather widget too, which I use constantly. The trick isn't shaving full minutes off my day, but it's a bit more convenient than opening the apps. 

2. Contextual suggestions in Messages help organize your life

Little buttons pop up so you forget less stuff.

Say your partner is the only one who knows how to use the new ventless dryer in the basement. He's out of town, so you ask for instructions. He texts them to you and right there, beneath his message are two little buttons. One says "Add to Reminders" and the other says "Add to Notes." That's pretty smart. In another scenario, your kid's dentist texts you a reminder about an upcoming appointment along with the address. Magically, two buttons appear beneath the message: "Add to Calendar" and "Show in Maps."

These new "contextual suggestions" delight me to no end. iOS 27 can glean what I might want to do based on the info from a text, and then suggest that I set reminders, add stuff to my calendar or map out my route to an appointment. When I chose an option, the information it added to the respective app was pretty spot on. I had to adjust the time on a Calendar entry or two, but it translated the gist of what I wanted. The same function is available in the Mail app, but I don't have Mail set up. 

3. Image Playground makes more sense now

The dog on the left is very slop. The dog on the right is my mutt with a hat.

To be fair, I'm not terribly "excited" about AI photo manipulation. But thanks to the photo-realistic capabilities in Image Playground, I could see myself using the app — if only to entertain my 9-year-old or creep out my husband. Now, you can import an image from Photos and, say, give a dog a hat and bow tie. Asking the same thing of the old Playground gives you a little kiddy cartoon of some animal that probably looks very unlike the original beast in the source photo. 

You can still generate illustrated versions of your friends and pets, but there are multiple styles now. And the addition of photo-realistic elements makes the app more functional; it's no longer the one-off novelty it was before. I spent some time making sunny days into stormy ones, adding a bee to a sunflower and otherwise adjusting reality in all manner of unnecessary and disquieting ways until I put the phone away and sighed for the Future of the Truth. 

Apple also added two new AI tools to the editor in the Photos app: Reframe and Extend. The former lets you slightly change the angle of a shot, while the latter lets you expand the area around your subject. I used both and, while the results were decent, I don't see myself using them often, particularly since I'm an avowed over-shooter and try to capture multiple angles during important photo sessions. 

4. A bunch of small quality of life improvements really add up 

The Liquid Glass is glassier in iOS 27 beta compared to iOS 26

The last thing I'm excited about is not so much a single thing as it is a feeling. I installed the iOS 27 beta on an loaner iPhone 16 and whenever I'd go back to my personal iPhone 16 — running lowly iOS 26 — it felt less-than. I've grown fond of the Siri bubble popping up at the top to answer my questions. Opening apps is a touch faster on the new OS (Apple says iOS 27 pre-loads data so apps open 30% faster). And things just look better on the updated phone, particularly the Liquid Glass elements. They look more… glassy, with shinier edges and sharper contrast. 

I'm excited to try out Apple's claim of smoother network transitions with iOS 27. With it, going from your home's Wi-Fi to cellular data should be more seamless. Anyone who has had to turn off Wi-Fi as they sat in the car because Maps kept trying to use the home's fading signal will appreciate this. I haven't been able to properly test whether it's actually smoother, because I simply haven't left the house enough times lately (there are certain drawbacks to working remotely and seeing far too much of the inside of your home is one of them).

Bonus: The Weather app got a minor lift

There's a new panel in the Weather app for conditions, rain and wind.

This might not matter to many, but as a person who checks multiple weather apps many times per day, I'm excited. The software update will bring a new panel to Apple's native Weather app that shows conditions for the next six hours. Tap on the cloud to see weather patterns, the raindrop to see precipitation or the wavy lines to see wind speeds. When you select a parameter, you'll also see predictions for clouds, rain, or wind on the 10-day forecast below the panel, too. It's minor, but I've already played with it many times. Now if weather apps can please become more accurate, I'd really appreciate it.

iOS 27 features I'm excited to try

iOS 27 includes expanded child safety features and I'm very much looking forward to trying them out. We use the Screen Time feature to curtail what would otherwise be 24-7 Roblox marathons on my kid's iPad. App limits and ask-to-buy features are also indispensable for parents. In iOS 27, those allowances and screen schedules will be more granular. They're a little broad and all-or-nothing right now so this could be a nice change.   

I also have yet to try the cool dual-capture FaceTime feature, in which you can show the person you're talking to the feed from both the front and selfie camera. That trick can only be performed by series 17 iPhones and the iPhone Air. The latest phones are also required if you want to adjust Siri's voice; iOS 27 will let you change the tone and pace of the way the AI talks. While I'm an upgrade-when-it-breaks sort of person, iOS 27 finally has enough going for it that I could consider breaking that rule.
