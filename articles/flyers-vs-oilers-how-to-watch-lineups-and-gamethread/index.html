Flyers vs. Oilers: How to watch, lineups, and gamethread

TheTools · 15.11.2026 21:00 · source · permalink

The Flyers are hosting Connor McDavid his Oilers in the second half of a back-to-back for both teams.

The Philadelphia Flyers are back at it. They’re traveling back home to the Xfinity Mobile Arena to face Connor McDavid and the Edmonton Oilers just 24 hours after playing in Columbus. Oh, we’re sure the good vibes will keep on rolling against arguably the best team in the league. Just a perfect situation.

Storylines to watch

Second half of back-to-backs with some travel? Sure why not

As we know, the Flyers played the Blue Jackets last night in Columbus — and it was a pretty solid win from the boys in Orange and Black — but they aren’t the only team who will be lacing up for this matchup who are just wrapping up some travel. Last night, the Oilers were in Toronto to deliver a 1-0 win over the Maple Leafs. It was a full team effort from them as well as they shutout Auston Matthews and his underperforming crew.

It’s not common that both teams are playing in the second half of a back-to-back and both teams had to travel the night before. Sure, we see all the time two teams colliding having both played the night before, but the home team is usually playing both of their games at home, playing host two nights in a row.

Will this have any major effect on the game? Well, we are much more confident that it will affect the Flyers more than Connor McDavid and Leon Draisaitl. The latter might feel a little ounce of fatigue as they turnstile a couple of Flyers defensemen, while the Flyers are going to get even slower than they normally are. It might not be a pretty sight in Philadelphia. But even if the Oilers were playing their second game in 24 hours and the Flyers had three days of rest, the result would probably be the same, even if they almost got the win in Edmonton.

Could we see a repeat of the last game?

The Flyers are getting their matchups against the Oilers over and done with. Just last Wednesday, they were in Edmonton to face the Oilers and truly played a very close game with a Stanley Cup favorite. They went down 2-0 early but fought back to force overtime and then it was fairly automatic as Draisaitl, McDavid, and Evan Bouchard took the ice at 3-on-3.

Could we see some sort of similar effort? Obviously fatigue will play a factor, but multiple Flyers really got up for that game, including Tyson Foerster who is looking much more like himself after scoring in Edmonton and settling up Trevor Zegras for an excellent goal on Saturday night in Columbus.

What it will take to get a similar performance

Honestly, if we’re talking about the one factor in the very close 3-2 overtime loss for the Flyers out there in Alberta, it was staying out of the box. The Oilers’ power play is guaranteed to score against you. Teams should just assume that they will get a goal whenever they have a player committing a penalty.

On Wednesday, the Flyers took just one penalty — an interference call on Oliver Bonk — and the Oilers scored within seconds. But that was it. Their one stab to the heart, but they largely kept the game even-strength or were able to go on the man advantage themselves (and actually score on it, too).

Tonight, the Flyers basically have to commit zero penalties. That’s the bar. If they want to secure any amount of points out of this game and continue this solid level of play this past week, they have to stay out of the box. It’s mandatory. We’re sure Rick Tocchet has already told them this multiple times ahead of tonight’s game.
