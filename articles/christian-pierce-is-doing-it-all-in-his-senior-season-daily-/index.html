Christian Pierce is doing it all in his senior season - Daily Trojan

TheTools · 20.11.2026 09:24 · source · permalink

Amid a USC football season largely defined by its star freshmen, senior safety Christian Pierce stands out as a veteran on the Trojans’ roster.

One of USC’s longest-tenured players, Pierce has been with the team since his freshman year in 2023. Now in his fourth season with the Trojans, Pierce finds himself staring down what will likely be his final game at Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum on Saturday.

Across four years, Pierce has dealt with nagging injuries, inconsistent playing time and frequent roster turnover, not to mention playing under three different defensive coordinators. Yet through it all, he has remained dedicated to his team — and, in his senior season, that dedication is finally paying dividends.

Pierce’s collegiate journey began as a three-star recruit out of Rancho Cucamonga High School, where he combined for 168 tackles and 12 interceptions over his last two seasons on the varsity squad and was named the Baseline League Defensive MVP as a senior. Pierce committed to USC in May 2022 as part of Head Coach Lincoln Riley’s first full recruiting class, just five months after Riley took the helm.

It didn’t take long for Pierce to make his way onto the Coliseum grass: He played in 10 games as a true freshman and all 13 of the Trojans’ games as a sophomore, though he spent most of his time on special teams both years. Still, Pierce made the most of his opportunities — including a three-tackle game against then-No. 6 Oregon in November 2023 — setting the stage to earn a larger role as an upperclassman.

And Riley took notice.

“Pierce has been really good. He’s been really good, which we weren’t shocked [by],” Riley said in April 2025. “We expected it, and it’s happening now.”

In his junior season, Pierce made his long-awaited leap to a full-time safety, earning nine starts while competing for time with star safeties Kamari Ramsey and Bishop Fitzgerald. It was a season of many firsts for Pierce — his first career sack came early in the 2025 season against Purdue, and he grabbed his first career interception in the Alamo Bowl against TCU. But as he battled a persistent injury all year, Pierce knew there was still room for more.

Now, as one of the veteran pieces on Riley’s current roster, that potential is being realized.

After Ramsey and Fitzgerald both left for the NFL, Pierce slotted in as one of USC’s starting safeties alongside redshirt senior Prophet Brown, and he hasn’t relinquished the role since. Through 10 games this season, Pierce has tallied 31 solo tackles and 51 overall, both of which lead the Trojan secondary and rank third among all USC defenders.

Pierce put up solid numbers in nonconference play, but his breakout came in the team’s Big Ten opener against Rutgers — a game in which he truly did it all. On top of a career-high 11 tackles — the most by a Trojan in any game this season — Pierce snagged an interception on the Scarlet Knights’ second play and ran it back 16 yards, setting USC up for an eventual touchdown to go up 10-0. He also broke up two passes throughout the day for good measure.

The fourth-year Trojan has been on fire ever since. The very next game, Pierce notched seven tackles against one of the Big Ten’s best offenses in Oregon; a week later, he took care of business himself, putting up nine tackles — all solo — in a narrow victory over Washington.

His biggest moment, however, came Oct. 10 against Penn State. With a 31-23 lead and just over four minutes to play in the fourth quarter, USC desperately needed a stop to escape the “White Out” game with a win. 

Pierce delivered: On the first play of the Nittany Lions’ final drive, he punched the ball out of a receiver’s hands, giving way for redshirt sophomore cornerback Chasen Johnson to dive onto the ball and secure the fumble recovery. The Trojans emerged with their first-ever win at Penn State, and Pierce had his one shining moment.

Of course, when asked about the play afterward, Pierce wasn’t focused on himself.

“We just got the win. That win [is] even bigger than the punch for me,” Pierce said in a post-practice news conference Oct. 14. “To be in that environment, and to play with my guys, and to go out and dominate as we did — I’m blessed for that.”

Pierce’s time at USC may be nearing its end, but the impact he’s had during his four years won’t be easily forgotten — especially with his younger brother, redshirt sophomore cornerback Carrington Pierce, waiting in the wings to grab his own spot in the starting lineup one day.

As for that final home game: Pierce isn’t treating it any differently than the 46 games that came before it.

“It’s a regular game for me,” Pierce said in a post-practice news conference Tuesday. “I know it’s going to be the last game [at] the Coliseum, but right now, all I’m worried about is getting to 1-0.”
