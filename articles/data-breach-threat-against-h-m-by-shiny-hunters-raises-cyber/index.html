Data Breach Threat against H&M by Shiny Hunters raises Cybersecurity Concerns

TheTools · 13.10.2026 05:55 · source · permalink
  • CYBER THREATS & ATTACKS
  • Data Breach

The cybersecurity landscape has come under renewed scrutiny following claims by the Shiny Hunters hacking group that it breached the servers of H&M, the Swedish fashion retailer, and put a portion of the allegedly stolen data up for sale. The announcement came shortly after FBI Director Kash Patel revealed the arrest of Edward Dubrovsky, described in reports as a Canada-based response expert associated with the Shiny Hunters group. The developments have raised fresh concerns about the growing threat posed by organized cybercriminal networks to major international businesses.

H&M is reportedly investigating the incident and has engaged a third-party digital forensics expert to examine the potential breach. The investigation is expected to establish whether the company’s systems were compromised, what information may have been accessed or stolen, and whether customer, employee, or business-related data was affected. Until the investigation is completed and the claims are independently verified, the full extent of the alleged incident remains unclear.

For readers of Cybersecurity Insiders, the latest development highlights the continuing risks faced by prominent retail brands. Shiny Hunters and other interconnected cybercriminal groups have previously been associated with high-profile incidents involving companies such as Jaguar Land Rover, Ticketmaster, Harrods, and Marks & Spencer. These incidents demonstrate how cyberattacks can disrupt business operations, expose sensitive information, and impose significant financial and reputational costs on organizations. In one reported case, a victim allegedly paid approximately $400,000 to recover encrypted data, illustrating the substantial financial pressure ransomware-related incidents can create.

The wider cybercrime ecosystem has also seen the emergence of groups described as Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters, an alleged combination of Scattered Spider, Lapsus$, and Shiny Hunters. Sometimes referred to as the “Trinity of Chaos,” these groups have attracted attention because of their association with sophisticated social engineering tactics and attacks against large organizations. However, the relationships between these groups and their individual roles in specific incidents can vary, making careful attribution essential.

One particularly concerning tactic is voice phishing, commonly known as vishing. Rather than relying exclusively on technical vulnerabilities, attackers use telephone calls, impersonation, and psychological manipulation to persuade employees to disclose credentials or approve unauthorized access. Reports of criminal networks recruiting individuals to conduct such attacks underscore the importance of employee awareness, robust identity verification, and strong authentication controls. 

Firms must ensure that sensitive requests are independently verified, even when they appear to originate from trusted colleagues or senior executives.

Meanwhile, concerns about artificial intelligence (AI) are adding another dimension to the global cybersecurity debate. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has warned about the potential risks AI-driven cyberattacks pose to the global financial system. IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva has also highlighted the possibility of a major digital shock affecting financial institutions. AI can help defenders identify threats more quickly, but criminals may also exploit it to automate attacks, generate convincing phishing messages, and improve social engineering campaigns.

The alleged H&M breach therefore serves as a reminder that cybersecurity is no longer merely an IT concern; it is a critical business and economic priority. Businesses must invest in proactive threat detection, incident response planning, employee training, and resilient data protection systems. As cybercriminal tactics evolve, collaboration between businesses, law enforcement agencies, and international institutions will be essential to reducing the impact of future attacks.
