Pakistan To Continue Fuel Relief Scheme Despite Falling Global Oil Prices - WE News English

TheTools · 20.11.2026 19:51 · source · permalink

Key Points

ISLAMABAD: Pakistan’s government has decided to continue its targeted fuel relief scheme despite recent reductions in petroleum prices, with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif directing authorities to maintain subsidies for motorcyclists and owners of small cars to provide sustained relief to consumers.

Federal Minister for Information and Broadcasting Attaullah Tarar announced the decision during a press conference alongside Minister of State for Finance and Revenue Bilal Azhar Kayani in Islamabad on Friday.

Tarar said the prime minister had instructed the relevant authorities to continue the initiative, under which motorcyclists receive Rs500 in weekly petrol relief, while owners of vehicles with engine capacities of up to 800cc also benefit from subsidised fuel.

He said the government wanted to ensure that the public continued receiving relief despite a decline in international petroleum prices.

The information minister said the government had taken several measures to promote transparency, merit and accountability across public institutions, adding that efforts to strengthen these principles had continued since 1997.

He cited merit-based recruitment of teachers and measures to eliminate examination malpractice as examples of improvements in governance.

Explaining the government’s response to the international petroleum crisis, Tarar said Pakistan had maintained uninterrupted fuel supplies despite challenging global circumstances. He claimed that petrol pumps across the country had not witnessed long queues or shortages, as the prime minister had continuously monitored the situation and directed timely measures to maintain supplies.

The minister said that when international petroleum prices were increasing by Rs50, Rs60 and Rs70, the prime minister allocated Rs129 billion in subsidies to prevent corresponding increases in domestic fuel prices.

He added that the government subsequently introduced the targeted fuel relief scheme despite further increases in international oil prices.

Tarar said the government would also issue further instructions to district administrations and price-control magistrates to ensure that essential commodities were sold at officially notified prices.

He emphasised that effective monitoring was necessary to prevent profiteering and ensure that reductions in prices translated into relief for consumers.

Highlighting the government’s governance initiatives, Tarar referred to the installation of surveillance cameras under Punjab’s Safe City project and the establishment of a forensic laboratory. He said the government had also undertaken a National Risk Assessment on the prime minister’s instructions.

The information minister said the government had closed several public-sector organisations, including the Pakistan Public Works Department (Pak PWD) and Utility Stores Corporation, while the Pakistan Agricultural Storage and Services Corporation (PASSCO) was also being phased out.

He said digital wallets had been introduced to ensure that financial assistance reached deserving families during Ramazan.

Speaking at the same press conference, State Minister for Finance Bilal Azhar Kayani said transparency remained the foremost priority of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, adding that the government had introduced third-party verification mechanisms to assess the effectiveness of its initiatives.

He said the government was using a track-and-trace system to monitor industrial production, improve tax collection and prevent underreporting of economic activity.

According to Kayani, the system was already operational in the sugar, tobacco, cement and tiles industries, while its implementation was being extended to beverages, textiles, iron and steel, paper and board, hatcheries and other sectors.

He said these measures were aimed at identifying tax evasion, improving documentation and bringing more economic activity into the formal tax system.

Kayani said the government was also working to reduce discretionary powers within the FBR to minimise opportunities for corruption and improve the consistency of tax administration.

He said a faceless assessment system had been introduced in the customs sector, while artificial intelligence and data-driven mechanisms were being employed to conduct income tax audits.

The state miniter for finance claimed that tax reforms and enhanced monitoring had generated additional tax revenues ranging between Rs100 billion and Rs150 billion.

Kayani said the National Risk Assessment report would also be uploaded on Friday.

Responding to questions about government expenditure, Kayani said the administration had identified several public-sector organisations performing overlapping functions and was merging or restructuring them under its right-sizing programme.

He cited the closure of Utility Stores Corporation and Pak PWD, along with the phased closure of PASSCO, as examples of the government’s efforts to reduce administrative costs. He said the government was reviewing public-sector expenditure to eliminate unnecessary spending while ensuring that the salaries of government employees were not affected.

The state minister added that the government had spent Rs129 billion to shield consumers from increases in petroleum prices following the outbreak of the conflict in the Middle East.

Responding to a question about the renovation of government buildings, Kayani said public properties were state assets and their maintenance was necessary. However, he acknowledged that repairs and renovation should remain within reasonable limits.

On the taxation burden, Kayani said the government wanted to reduce the tax burden on salaried individuals and gradually decrease its reliance on indirect taxation.

He said approximately Rs400 billion in taxes had been reduced in the previous budget after the government created additional fiscal space, providing relief to salaried employees and other taxpayers.

Kayani said the government intended to broaden the tax base, improve compliance and bring more sectors into the documented economy rather than relying excessively on existing taxpayers.

Responding to questions about governance and institutional performance, Tarar said there was always room for improvement in the government system and that structural changes and administrative reforms should remain an ongoing process. He said public opinion should be given importance when deciding the direction of future reforms and improving the delivery of public services.

The state minister maintained that improving governance required continuous evaluation of existing arrangements and the introduction of measures to address institutional shortcomings.
