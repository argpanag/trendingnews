Synopsis

Light to moderate trade winds will produce a hybrid trade wind and sea breeze wind flow each day lasting into early next week. Brief passing showers will drift into the islands on the easterly trade winds, favoring windward mountain areas during the overnight to early morning hours. A more significant change to our weather pattern continues to evolve for early next week beginning sometime Monday night or Tuesday. Deeply unstable tropical moisture moving across the state will bring the potential for heavy rain and thunderstorms affecting all islands.

Discussion

Satellite and radar imagery show isolated to scattered showers moving into windward and mauka areas on the light to moderate trade wind flow this afternoon, with the best coverage over the central part of the state. In addition, showers have also developed over some leeward areas due to sea breeze activity in the lighter trade wind flow, particularly across Oahu.
The trade winds have eased to become light to moderate in response to the weakening pressure gradient as the surface high gets pushed farther northeast of the state while a low pressure system sets up to the north. These light to moderate trade winds will more or less persist through the weekend, allowing for a hybrid trade wind and land/sea breeze pattern to commence. However, with high clouds expected to move in from the west- southwest Friday through most of the weekend, sea breeze activity might be somewhat suppressed. With the weakening mid to upper level low over the state today, the instability associated with this feature has helped to support the development of afternoon showers over some leeward and interior areas as sea breezes form. However, limited available moisture and weak lifting mechanisms at the surface will continue to keep the shower activity rather limited. This mid to upper level low will lift northeastward tonight into Friday and ridging will try to nudge in from the south, limiting shower activity even further. However, deeper moisture will move over the Big Island and maybe Maui from the southeast, increasing the potential for showers along the lower south- and east-facing slopes.
Remnants of a dissipating frontal boundary extending from the low to the north may move in Saturday through Saturday night as the low dips south-southeast, bringing a slight boost in the east- northeast trade wind speeds and trade wind showers over the western end of the state as ridging tries to build in from the north. The upper level trough supporting the surface low will form a cutoff low to the northeast of the state over the weekend, driving the surface low over the same area.
Meanwhile, to the west of the state, a low will try to develop somewhere near 16N and the dateline this weekend, with the National Hurricane Center giving this feature a low (30 percent) chance of developing into a tropical cyclone over the next 7 days. Regardless of the potential tropical development, the main threat for Hawaii is the potential for heavy rain and possibly isolated thunderstorms next week as this potential low and a large convergent band of deep tropical moisture to its east get swept eastward by a passing shortwave trough and drawn across Hawaii beginning sometime Monday night or Tuesday. The threat for heavy rain may persist through much of the week as this band of deep tropical moisture lingers over the area. However, its important to note that forecast uncertainty remains moderate to high as there continues to be a fairly wide spread among the global model solutions.

Marine

Easterly trade winds will be at gentle to moderate strength through Friday as surface low pressure far north of Hawaii and a nearby surface trough weaken the local pressure gradient. Trades are expected to hold at current strength and shift out of the northeast this weekend as high pressure far northwest of Hawaii pushes to low eastward. The weaker trade winds should persist Monday, but uncertainty increases on Tuesday, hinging on the potential development of a low southwest of the islands.
A small, medium-period northwest swell will arrive overnight and provide a small boost to surf along north-facing shores on Friday. A moderate, medium-period north-northwest swell will build late Saturday and peak around seasonal average on Sunday. As this swell declines on Monday, a long-period west-northwest swell will arrive, maintaining small to moderate surf into the middle of next week.
Small background level surf will prevail along south-facing shores through Friday. A small to moderate south-southwest swell is expected to boost surf to around seasonal average this weekend into early next week.
Along east-facing shores, trade-wind swell will continue to decrease through Friday, though a small, medium-period northeast swell may maintain small surf through Saturday. Aside from wrapping north-northwest swell in exposed areas, east shore surf should remain below seasonal average much of next week.

Passing showers will move into mainly windward and mauka areas on the trade wind flow and some showers may also be expected over leeward areas as seas breezes develop in the most wind-protected areas.
