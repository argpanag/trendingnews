Lindsay Clancy: five key moments from ‘America’s saddest murder trial’

TheTools · 04.09.2026 18:50 · source · permalink

Massachusetts mother accused of fatally strangling her children faces possibility of retrial after judge declares mistrial

  • Clancy judge declares mistrial – what happens next?

Full report: Judge declares mistrial in Lindsay Clancy case

Clancy judge declares mistrial – what happens next?

The trial of Lindsay Clancy that gripped headlines in recent weeks, drawing renewed attention to the state of maternal mental healthcare in the US and postpartum psychosis, has ended with a mistrial.

This now leaves Clancy, the Massachusetts mother accused of fatally strangling her three children in January 2023 who pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity, open to the possibility of a retrial with a different jury.

As the public now waits for what will happen next, here are five key takeaways from what has been called “America’s saddest murder trial”.

  1. 1. Patrick Clancy: Lindsay Clancy ‘described thoughts of hurting the kids’

    Lindsay Clancy’s ex-husband, 36-year-old Patrick Clancy, took the stand as a key figure in the case, testifying about his wife’s worsening mental health in the months leading up to the killings of five-year old Cora, three-year old Dawson and eight-month old Callan.

    Patrick Clancy described Lindsay as a “very dedicated” mother who struggled with her mental health and “just had a really hard time being separated from them”.

    Approximately two months before the killings, Lindsay “described thoughts of hurting the kids or them having some sort of ailment”, Patrick Clancy testified. He said his then wife told him that she did not have a specific plan to hurt the children and did not think she needed to be separated from them.

    “It was very confusing because a minute later, she was making them lunch or playing with them or getting ready for bed or whatever it was,” Patrick Clancy said, adding that Lindsay was not displaying “any intention” of hurting the kids.

    He also testified that Lindsay began expressing suicidal thoughts in December 2022, saying: “She started to say that she was suicidal.”

    After killing her children, Lindsay allegedly attempted to take her own life by jumping from her home’s second-floor window which left her paralyzed from the waist down.

  2. 2. Lindsay Clancy’s chaplain: “She said to me … ‘I’m so glad my children are safe’” after killings

    Sheila Cavanaugh, a chaplain at Brigham and Women’s Hospital in Boston, Massachusetts, who met with Lindsay Clancy numerous times after the killings of her children, also testified in court.

    “Her emotional state was very neutral, a flat affect, but I remember vividly the first thing she said to me,” Cavanaugh testified, recalling her conversation with Lindsay at the hospital where she was still “covered” in tubes and wearing a neck brace.

    “We had been praying together, and so when I went in on Tuesday the 31 [of January], she said to me as I held her hand to comfort her: ‘I am so glad my children are safe,’” Cavanaugh said.

    Cavanaugh further testified that Lindsay Clancy said she had heard a male voice several times, which “according to Lindsay, told her that if she did not follow the command, neither she nor her children would be safe”.

    “She alluded to the fact that it was a male voice and it was persistent,” Cavanaugh said. During cross-examination, when asked why her notes did not mention the voice, Cavanaugh explained that she was “not there to evaluate the patient” but instead to “bear witness to their suffering”.

  3. 3. Testimonies from Lindsay Clancy’s mother, sister and former mother-in-law

    Lindsay Clancy’s mother and sister also testified about the deterioration of her mental state in the months leading up to the killings of her three children, describing increasing paranoia, insomnia and suicidal thoughts.

    Paula Musgrove, Lindsay Clancy’s mother, testified that her daughter had become increasingly paranoid in the fall of 2022. Musgrove said Lindsay Clancy was afraid to sleep alone and believed the medications she was taking “were destroying her mind”.

    In December 2022, Musgrove testified she was in the kitchen with Lindsay Clancy and Patrick Clancy when “she told us she had thoughts of harming the children.”

    A text message from Lindsay Clancy to her mother shown in court also detailed her worsening condition. It read: “Mom, will you please come up and stay with me for a bit? I’m really sick. Something is wrong … I had horrible insomnia all night, and I just don’t know how I am going to get through the day … It’s just really scary, and I don’t want to be alone.”

    Lindsay Clancy’s sister, Allison Ozga, similarly testified about her sister’s declining mental state. During Thanksgiving 2022, Ozga said Lindsay Clancy’s “mood did not look good”. By December, Ozga testified that her sister “very much decompensated”.

    “I’m in a really tough spot,” Ozga recalled her sister saying, adding that toward the end of December 2022, Lindsay Clancy told her she “had suicidal ideation every day for a month.”

    Meanwhile, Lindsay Clancy’s former mother-in-law, Susan Clancy, testified that Lindsay Clancy was “begging for help” as her mental health struggles worsened. “Lindsay was struggling, we were all concerned,” Susan Clancy said.

    She also confirmed that her son went skiing after dropping Lindsay Clancy off at Women & Infants Hospital of Rhode Island for treatment in December 2022.

  4. 4. Lindsay Clancy supporters rally outside courthouse

    Over 300 supporters, mostly women, rallied outside the courthouse in Plymouth, Massachusetts, in support of Lindsay Clancy as the trial shed light on women’s struggles with mental health.

    Many of the supporters wore pink with the words: “Believe”, “She Needed Help” and “Peace For Lindsay”.

    Speaking to the Associated Press during the rally, April Vincent, a 52-year-old paralegal from Providence, Rhode Island, said: “Women are being dismissed, neglected and ignored when we speak up … We’re scared because nobody takes us seriously.”

    Jeanne Zaborski, a 72-year-old retired nurse from Rockland, Massachusetts, told the outlet from the outside of the courthouse: “It’s very sad … I am a mother. I am a grandmother. I have a sister who had problems after birth and it’s just very sad. I hope Lindsay gets the care she needs and deserves and other women will come together like we did today.”

  5. 5. Civil medical malpractice lawsuits remain under way

    Lindsay Clancy and her ex-husband, Patrick Clancy, have filed several civil lawsuits against doctors and hospitals involved in Lindsay Clancy’s care. Lindsay Clancy alleges negligence and medical malpractice, while Patrick Clancy alleges wrongful death.

    Lindsay Clancy’s lawsuit alleges that the defendants “each failed to properly diagnose Lindsay’s bipolar disorder with postpartum onset and instead subjected her to a disorganized, uncoordinated course of polypharmacy that exacerbated her condition and precipitated a severe psychotic break.”

    Patrick Clancy’s lawsuit similarly alleges that “the bevy of diverse and powerful medications they misprescribed coupled with their abject failure to appropriately monitor Lindsay resulted in Lindsay’s mental health deteriorating to the point of suicidal ideation and requiring in-patient care”.

    “Ultimately, defendants’ negligent treatment of Lindsay, including their complete failure to recognize and address the radical erosion of her mental health, resulted in tragedy, namely, allegations that Lindsay took the lives of her children,” his lawsuit adds.
