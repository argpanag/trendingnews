Influenza A can cause severe respiratory failure.

TheTools · 12.10.2026 17:37 · source · permalink

Many people still consider influenza A a common illness and assess its severity based on body temperature. However, the reality at the National Hospital for Tropical Diseases shows that influenza A can progress severely, causing rapid respiratory failure in the elderly and those with underlying conditions, threatening life within just a few days of onset.

Dr. Le Thi Huyen, from the Intensive Care Unit at the National Hospital for Tropical Diseases, shared that the hospital recently admitted patient HXT, 72 years old, who has a history of hypertension for many years, is regularly taking medication, and had previously suffered a stroke.

The patient was admitted with a high fever, productive cough, progressively worsening shortness of breath, and respiratory failure. A rapid test was positive for influenza A. Upon examination, doctors noted severe pneumonia and lung damage significantly affecting gas exchange capacity, requiring respiratory support with oxygen concentrations of approximately 40–50% to maintain blood oxygen levels.

Patients with influenza A are being treated at the National Hospital for Tropical Diseases.

Tests revealed an inflammatory response and elevated infection markers. The patient was treated for influenza A, along with treatment for the infection and intensive respiratory support. After three days of treatment, the patient was fever-free and hemodynamically stable. However, the lung damage remained severe, the patient was unable to sustain effective respiration independently, and continued to require non-invasive mechanical ventilation with an oxygen support concentration of approximately 40%.

The second case was admitted to the Emergency Department of the National Hospital for Tropical Diseases. The patient had a history of heavy smoking for many years, suffered from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and had been regularly using nebulized medication at home for the past 3-4 months. Approximately four days before admission, the patient began coughing, expectorating white phlegm, experiencing progressively worsening shortness of breath, accompanied by fatigue and poor appetite. Notably, the patient only had a mild fever, around 38°C, with no significant signs of high fever. However, the shortness of breath worsened, prompting the family to take the patient to a nearby medical facility for examination.

Here, test results confirmed the patient had influenza A. Upon admission to the Emergency Department, the patient was in severe respiratory distress. Doctors promptly intubated the patient, provided respiratory support, and implemented intensive resuscitation measures. However, the disease continued to progress rapidly, with serious systemic disorders appearing, and showed no improvement despite intensive resuscitation.

Influenza A is a respiratory infection caused by the influenza virus, primarily transmitted through respiratory droplets when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or talks. Symptoms typically include fever, cough, sore throat, body aches, and fatigue. In young children, the elderly, and those with chronic illnesses, influenza can cause pneumonia, worsen pre-existing conditions, and lead to respiratory failure and other dangerous complications.

Dr. Pham Van Phuc, Deputy Director of the Intensive Care Unit at the National Hospital for Tropical Diseases, advises that people should proactively get vaccinated against influenza annually, especially those in high-risk groups. In addition, it is necessary to maintain hand hygiene, cover the mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing, keep living spaces well-ventilated, and limit close contact with people who have symptoms of the disease. People with influenza should limit contact with others to reduce the risk of infection.

When children or individuals at high risk develop symptoms suspected of being influenza, families should take them to see a doctor promptly for assessment and appropriate treatment. Do not self-medicate, especially with antibiotics, without a doctor's prescription.

Patients should be taken to a medical facility immediately if they exhibit signs such as shortness of breath, rapid breathing, cyanosis, chest pain, lethargy, unusual irritability, or a rapid deterioration of their condition. In children, signs such as difficulty breathing, chest retraction, lethargy, seizures, inability to drink, or signs of dehydration require prompt medical attention.

Doctors note that the severity of influenza cannot be assessed solely by the fever level. Patients need close monitoring of respiratory status and any unusual health changes to detect early signs of worsening condition and receive timely treatment.
