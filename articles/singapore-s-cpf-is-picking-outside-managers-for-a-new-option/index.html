Singapore’s CPF Is Picking Outside Managers For A New Option

TheTools · 27.11.2026 06:17 · source · permalink

JPMorgan Asset Management, BlackRock, and Endowus are said to be in the running to oversee part of the SG$701 billion fund, with a decision expected in the first half of 2027.

1 min

What's going on here?

Singapore’s SG$701 billion Central Provident Fund is looking to hire outside firms – JPMorgan Asset Management, BlackRock, and Endowus are said to be leading contenders – to run a new opt-in “life-cycle” investment option.

What does this mean?

The Business Times reported that proposals are in, with a winner expected in the first half of 2027 and a planned launch in 2028. The idea is simple: members who choose the option would get a portfolio that automatically shifts over time, starting with more stocks and gradually moving toward more lower-risk assets as retirement gets closer. That kind of “set-and-adjust” design can help people avoid making big allocation decis..

decisions at exactly the wrong moment, especially during stressful market swings. For the shortlisted managers, the prize is a visible mandate linked to one of Singapore’s largest pools of retirement savings.

Why should I care?

For you: CPF’s 2028 life-cycle option is really about protecting your retirement pot from bad timing.

The core feature here is the glide path: automatic rebalancing that reduces stock exposure as you get older. That matters because a sharp market drop right before or around retirement can do lasting damage, since you’re more likely to be locking in balances or starting withdrawals when there’s less time to recover. By dialing down risk as the target date approaches, a life-cycle portfolio aims to make outcomes smoother and less “crash-sensitive.” The trade-off is that you also give up some potential upside if stocks rally late in your career, so the decision is less about chasing the highest return and more about how much timing risk you want to carry.
