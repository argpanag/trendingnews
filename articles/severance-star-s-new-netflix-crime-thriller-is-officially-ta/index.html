Netflix's hot streak this year refuses to end. Although the streamer has witnessed the decline of its Enola Holmes franchise — Enola Holmes 3 underwhelmed ratings-wise — and the failure of the star-studded non-performer In the Hands of Dante, it has also been consistently successful in executing its new strategy. Netflix has pivoted away from mega-budget tentpoles in recent months, choosing instead to release mid-budget genre movies. Two of them from this year — Swapped and War Machine — were able to break into the streamer's all-time top 10 list. The winning streak continued this past week with a psychological thriller that debuted at the top of the streamer's viewership chart despite mixed reviews.

Netflix shares weekly viewership data, and this week's report tracks the week of August 24 to August 30. The streamer has released several mid-budget movies this year that topped the coveted 100-million views mark over the course of their respective runs. Joining Swapped and War Machine on this list were The Rip, the crime thriller starring Ben Affleck and Matt Damon; Apex, the survival thriller starring Charlize Theron and Taron Egerton; and Thrash, another survival thriller, headlined by Phoebe Dynevor. More recently, Netflix released the sci-fi movie The Last House, starring Greta Lee and Wagner Moura; it has accumulated around 80 million views in four weeks.

Here's How Well Netflix's New Thriller Performed

The streamer's latest hit, however, is The Whisper Man. Starring Robert De Niro, Adam Scott, and Michelle Monaghan, the movie serves as a throwback to '90s serial killer thrillers such as Se7en and The Silence of the Lambs. The Whisper Man accumulated 23 million views in just three days of release, beating out the holdover hits Facing El Chapo, Don't Say Good Luck, and The Last House. Directed by James Ashcroft, the movie received mixed reviews and is now sitting at a 46% score on Rotten Tomatoes. In his review, Collider's David Caballero described the movie as "a solid and extremely eerie watch" while pointing out how derivative it is.
