Boy killed in Waterford fire had surgery two weeks ago

TheTools · 14.10.2026 10:34 · source · permalink

The nine-year-old boy who died in a house fire along with his parents and older brother was a 'heart warrior', who had an operation two weeks ago.

Jayden English died following an arson attack on his home in Waterford city yesterday morning.

His parents, Philip and Kim, and his 12-year-old brother Callum also died in the blaze.

Two other children who were in the house at the time were taken to hospital with critical injuries.

Jayden was born with a heart condition and attended hospital appointments in Dublin, travelling via BUMBLEance, Ireland's dedicated children's ambulance service.

Áine Phelan, a Waterford based paramedic who previously worked with the service, said she had the "absolute privilege" of travelling on ambulance journeys with Jayden.

She said that she was trying to comprehend what had happened and added that her thoughts were with the family in the "sad, sad days ahead."

'Always had a smile on his face'

Callum played for Johnville FC and was on the U11 team that won a league trophy two years ago, the club said.

His coach Alex O'Sullivan said it was "very devastating news to wake up to yesterday morning."

Speaking on RTÉ's Today with David McCullagh, Mr O'Sullivan described Callum as "always standing out from the rest" of the kids.

"Always had a smile on his face, always worked hard on and off the pitch, always looked out for his teammates, and always respected his coaches.

He said Callum's parents "were always there, supporting Callum and his teammates, they were very helpful to the club.

They did not miss a day at the club supporting him and the other children, he said, adding "they were very grateful for all our work."

"It's very sad now that unfortunately they're not going to be seen anymore."

The principal of Scoil Lorcáin, where Callum attended, described him as a "lovely young fella".

Caroline Douris was speaking as she and other staff members at the school laid flowers outside the house in the Birchwood Estate, where Callum, his parents and Jayden died.

She said the National Educational Educational Psychological Service is working with the school and they are receiving psychological support for the students and parents.

"We will get together as a school community tomorrow morning to give as much support as we can to the boys".

Ms Douris appealed for privacy and to let "kids just be kids", adding that they are going to try and keep "as normal as possible" as the well-being of the children is the most important thing at the moment.

Prayer vigil

A prayer vigil will take place in Waterford city this evening for the family.

In a post on social media, the Diocese of Waterford and Lismore said: "We invite the people of Waterford to come together in prayer for those who have lost their lives, their grieving families and loved ones and all those affected by this terrible tragedy."

"May those who have died rest in eternal peace, and may their families find strength, comfort and consolation in the prayers and support of our community," it added.

The vigil will take place at the Sacred Heart Church, The Folly, Waterford city at 5pm.

President Catherine Connolly expressed her shock at the deaths of the family members.

In a statement yesterday, she said: "May I join with all of those who have expressed such shock following the horrific killing of a couple and two of their children...in Waterford and express my deepest condolences to the family and friends of those who have lost their lives.

"This was an appalling attack and all of our thoughts are with the two children currently in critical condition in hospital for a full recovery and with the first responders who have been on the scene and treating them."

'Lovely couple'

Independent Councillor Donal Barry described Kim and Philip English as "an absolutely lovely couple".

Mr Barry, speaking on RTÉ's Prime Time, said he knew the family "quite well" and that he had talked to Ms English last Tuesday.

He said the couple "really just minded her own business and went about their thing" and that Ms English's "whole ethos was to look after her children".

"Kim done everything for her kids and that was the way they lived as a couple - the kids, the kids," he said, adding "everything was done to make the lives of the children better".

"Kim was a real advocate for her children, she wanted better services for children with additional needs and she was the whole time on to various politicians and ministers, the whole lot, looking for services for the kids," Mr Barry said.

After paint was thrown on the couple's car a few years ago, he said Ms English had asked him "why are people picking on us? We don't put in or out on nobody, we're causing no harm to nobody".

"And they didn't, they were very well thought of here in the community," Mr Barry said.
