Auckland FC’s title defence starts from scratch as championship core changes

TheTools · 12.10.2026 00:23 · source · permalink

RNZ / News / Sport

Link: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/eyJhbGciOiJIUzI1NiJ9._Q.VKhexyL5oal5uCV3AUQmI1EOdryHi3hh73RdibV20lY/sport/1825135%2Fauckland-fc-s-title-defence-starts-from-scratch-as-championship-core-changes

Auckland FC has a foundation of success that will be tested as the team starts from scratch in their A-League championship title defence this season.

The "glue in the changing room" has moved on, the average age of the playing group has dropped, and an entirely new coaching staff, led by head coach Pedro Moreira, has taken charge of a squad that has had 11 players leave in the off season.

With the A-League season kicking of next week, Auckland FC has little time left to fine-tune their new-look squad.

When Moreira arrived in Auckland, he said he was not going to overlook what had made the club champions.

"It's important to catch the good things that they did in the past. I'm trying to put my little touch on the details," he said.

For Auckland FC's day one players it has been an adjustment figuring out how this new version of the team functions in season three.

Sports teams have a flow during the season. Training sessions, gym sessions, meetings and video sessions all have a place in a strictly controlled schedule.

Drill down even further and each session runs based on how a coach likes to do things.

Auckland FC training sessions look different now than they did last season.

RNZ / Marika Khabazi

Defender Francis de Vries has been around since the club's inception and is now into his third A-League pre-season.

"Every coach has their own style," the All White said.

"I just liken it to how it was in Europe, sometimes I had a coach that was a little bit more set up the session and then let it run.

"And other coaches stopped it more and tried to adjust things on the fly, it's definitely a contrast and I think something that everybody's getting used to now as we go along.

"But it's a normal thing in football that there's personnel changes and you realise that it's also going to be a change of style and approach within the sessions.

"If you're a more experienced player like myself, you've kind of had those different styles before so you switch over to what you're used to having in your experience."

Playing style was also going to change a little this season.

Moreira was trying different rotations, wanting players to be "a little more fluid with the ball" all of which meant more time on the training pitch drilling this into the playing group.

"We've definitely gotten a lot younger as a team," de Vries said.

"So there's different ideas and different things that people are used to, so that's been interesting too but it's nice."

Louis Verstraete and Jesse Randall have left Auckland FC

Andrew Cornaga/www.photosport.nz

Among the experienced players that left were visa players Guillermo May and Louis Verstraete, All White Jesse Randall and proven winner Dan Hall.

"Of course, you miss the guys that leave, Jesse and Louis were two of my best mates, not even talking about football, outside of it.

"Obviously, you lose someone like Dan, who was the glue in the changing room, and people like that don't come around just like that.

"So there's some big shoes to fill there and for different people to step up and take different roles within the team is going to be super important."

De Vries and Randall were in sync on the field too, two experienced players who instinctively knew where the other would be.

With younger players beside him de Vries said there was "more talk, more back and forth," as he helped them understand the tactics.

Francis de Vries and Nando Pijnaker with Auckland FC's head coach for two seasons Steve Corica.

photosport

Another World Cup All White, Nando Pijnaker, was on the mend from a pre-season rib injury and in his third season with Auckland wanted to be in Moreira's starting line-up.

Pijnaker had an injury-hit 2025-26 A-League season, missing 11 games, and was hoping to avoid a repeat this season.

Instead he wanted something closer to his involvement in season one where he played 25 regular season games and only missed one match through suspension.

"I want to be as fit as possible.

"I want to play every single game and help the team out as much as possible."

While former Auckland head coach Steve Corica knew what Pijnaker could do and described him as "technically very good and very strong in the air which means he is a threat both defensively and also in attack" when he signed him in September 2024, that meant little now.

The 27-year-old knew Moreira arrived with a clean slate and all players wanted to catch his eye.

"If you're a player that hasn't been playing so much and a new coach comes in, you have a new opportunity to impress them and obviously try to push for your place in the starting team, and that goes for everyone," Pijnaker said.

"It's the same for the players that have been playing, they need to now impress the new coach again to make sure that they keep their spot on the team.

"It's good to have competition for places, it's a very competitive training environment gearing up for round one."

Nando Pijnaker wants to be on the field a lot in the 2026-27 A-League season.

Photosport

Changes to the salary cap and the promotion of young local talent meant most A-League teams would be different going forward.

"I think the last couple of years, it was maybe quite difficult for a young player to come into the team and also play on the weekend, and that's going to probably change this season because we have a lot more young boys coming into the training environment and coming into the team as well.

"They'll see that there's more opportunities than there were the last couple of years."

Like de Vries, Pijnaker was adjusting to having younger voices in the locker room.

"Definitely last year, I think the young boys, they wouldn't really speak up too much. You wouldn't really hear too much or get too much out of them.

"Now at lunchtime they're talking really loud. They get on really well with each other and with the older players."
