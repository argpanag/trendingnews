Prominent religious leaders condemn Houthi attacks

TheTools · 13.10.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

ISLAMABAD: Pakistan’s prominent religious leaders have strongly condemned Houthi attacks targeting Umrah pilgrims and expressed solidarity with Saudi Arabia, declaring that the Haramain Sharifain are a red line for Muslims.

The remarks were made at a press conference organised by the Pak-Saudi Solidarity Council and hosted by Senator Dr Hafiz Abdul Karim.

Senator Hafiz Karim said that Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia were condemnable, particularly the attempt to target Umrah pilgrims, which was unacceptable.

He said Makkah Mukarramah and Madinah Munawwarah were among the holiest places for all Muslims, and their security was of paramount importance to the entire Muslim Ummah.

He stated that Pakistan stood with Saudi Arabia and that such attacks could not be tolerated under any circumstances. “The entire Pakistani nation and the Muslim Ummah condemn these heinous attacks and declare their solidarity with Saudi Arabia,” he added.

He also praised the position adopted by the Pakistan government and expressed his full support for it.

Federal Minister for Religious Affairs Sardar Muhammad Yousaf said: “Killing Allah’s guests while they were in the state of Ihram is completely unacceptable. We strongly and unequivocally condemn this act. Within the sacred precincts of the Haramain, where even killing an ant is forbidden, murdering the guests of Allah is an immense act of oppression.”

He added that the Pakistan government stood with Saudi Arabia and would continue to support its defence.

According to the minister, these attacks were an attempt to provoke the Muslim Ummah, and Pakistan condemned them. He added that the government and the people of Pakistan were united on the issue of defending the Haramain Sharifain.

He further said Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal General Asim Munir had already announced their clear policy on the matter, adding: “Saudi Arabia is not merely a country but the centre of Muslims’ religious devotion”.

He said the time had come for Muslim countries to stand firmly alongside Saudi Arabia, like an impregnable wall.

Sahibzada Shah Owais Noorani said that Pakistan and Saudi Arabia might appear to be two separate countries, but they were bound together by the bond of faith.

He described Pakistan as the fortress of Islam and Saudi Arabia as its centre.

Others who addressed the press conference included Jamiat Ulema-e-Pakistan Secretary General and Vice President Sahibzada Shah Owais Noorani, Ahle Sunnat Wal Jamaat Patron Maulana Muhammad Ahmed Ludhianvi, Pak-Saudi Solidarity Council President Dr Ali Muhammad Abu Turab, Secretary General Maulana Fazlur Rehman Khalil, Coordination Secretary Maulana Atiq-ur-Rehman Shah, and council spokesperson Prof Sajjad Qamar.
