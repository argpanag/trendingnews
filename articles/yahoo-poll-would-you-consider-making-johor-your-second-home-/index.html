YAHOO POLL: Would you consider making Johor your second home once the RTS opens?

TheTools · 25.10.2026 20:39 · source · permalink

Passengers will be able to travel between stations in Johor and Singapore in about five minutes.

For many Singaporeans, Johor Bahru has long been a popular destination for shopping, dining and short getaways. But with a new rail connection to Singapore expected to open in early 2027, could more people begin considering Johor as a second home?

The Johor Bahru–Singapore Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link is expected to make cross-border journeys more convenient and predictable, potentially changing how Singaporeans think about spending time across the Causeway.

The upcoming connection is also attracting attention from property developers and buyers, particularly those interested in larger homes, lower living costs and retirement options in Johor.

According to a recent EdgeProp Singapore article, Johor's landed property market has enjoyed considerable growth over the past decade, supported by economic expansion, investment and closer ties with Singapore.

At a property event held in Singapore on 17 and 18 Oct, industry representatives highlighted how improved connectivity could make Johor more appealing to Singaporeans seeking a second residence or a place to retire.

For some, the attraction is easy to understand.

The proximity to Singapore means retirees could potentially maintain close relationships with family and friends while enjoying a different pace of life across the border.

Others might prefer to spend weekends or extended periods in Johor while retaining their main residence in Singapore.

However, owning a property in Malaysia is not quite the same as having the right to live there indefinitely.

Singaporeans considering a second home would need to understand Malaysia's property ownership restrictions and immigration requirements, particularly if they intend to stay for extended periods.

There are also practical considerations, including healthcare, transport, maintaining a property overseas and travelling between the two countries.

While the RTS promises improved connectivity, whether that convenience is enough to encourage more Singaporeans to establish a second home in Johor remains to be seen.
