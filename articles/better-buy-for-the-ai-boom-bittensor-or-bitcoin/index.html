Better Buy for the AI Boom: Bittensor or Bitcoin?

TheTools · 15.10.2026 16:00 · source · permalink

Bittensor plugs directly into the AI economy while Bitcoin rides it from the outside, and that difference makes choosing between them far more complicated than a simple risk tolerance check.

The Crypto Desk desk. Editor: Sam Daodu.

When it comes to investing in the AI boom through cryptocurrencies, Bittensor (CRYPTO: TAO) and Bitcoin (CRYPTO: BTC) often stand out as key options. The decision between Bittensor and Bitcoin largely depends on the level of risk you are willing to accept. As of October 15, 2026, Bittensor is trading at $262, having dropped 5.9% in the last 24 hours, while Bitcoin is priced at $81,233, down 2.4% over the same period.

Both cryptocurrencies have seen declines this week, with Bittensor down 7.5% and Bitcoin down 2.1%. However, over the past 30 days, Bittensor has gained 15.7%, while Bitcoin has risen 5.6%, highlighting their distinct approaches to the AI theme.

So, which cryptocurrency offers better exposure to AI, and which one is more likely to withstand a slowdown in AI investment?

Bittensor Pays for AI Work, While Bitcoin’s AI Link Is Indirect

Mindscape studio / Shutterstock.com

Bittensor operates as a decentralized network that rewards users in TAO tokens for contributing AI models and computational work. The token serves as both payment and reward within its AI marketplace. Thus, investing in TAO hinges on one crucial factor: the network’s adoption and use. If developers and users engage with the platform, demand for the token is likely to grow.

In contrast, Bitcoin’s connection to AI is indirect. Its protocol, the fundamental rules that govern the network, does not specifically include AI functions. Instead, its link to AI stems from two main sources: miners who secure the network using powerful computers (and who provide services to AI data centers) and the idea that AI agents—software acting on behalf of users—will require a neutral network for settling transactions.

Jordi Visser highlighted this viewpoint during an episode of The Pomp Podcast on October 10, titled “Bitcoin Is the Toll Collector of the AI Economy.” He noted that crypto is above its 200-day moving average and argued that the AI agent economy, rising stablecoin usage, and growing interest from traditional finance are fueling a bullish market.

Visser believes the most significant crypto moves will come from the agent economy. However, Bitcoin’s potential growth tied to AI depends on the agent economy favoring it as a settlement network.

Bitcoin Is 550 Times Bittensor’s Size, and That Cuts Both Ways

FellowNeko / Shutterstock.com

Bitcoin boasts a market value of approximately $1.63 trillion, while Bittensor’s stands at around $2.97 billion, making Bitcoin about 550 times larger.

This size disparity creates both opportunities and risks. A small influx of new investment could significantly impact Bittensor’s value, allowing it to grow much faster than Bitcoin. Conversely, the smaller market capitalization means Bittensor could also lose most of its value, an outcome that seems less likely for the larger and more established Bitcoin.

Historic price movements illustrate this dynamic. Bittensor trades 65.5% below its March 2024 peak, while Bitcoin trades 35.6% below its record high. Yet Bittensor’s 15.7% gain over the past month is nearly triple Bitcoin’s 5.6%, suggesting the smaller token swings more violently.

Bitcoin Fits Core Savings, While Bittensor Fits Speculative Money

ARMMY PICCA / Shutterstock.com

For investors looking to preserve wealth over time, Bitcoin is the more reliable choice for AI-related investments. Its established status as a tradable asset, combined with potential AI-driven growth, makes it a solid pick for core savings. However, this strategy carries risks if the agent economy chooses to utilize stablecoins or other blockchain technologies over Bitcoin.

On the other hand, for those willing to take on more risk, Bittensor may be a better fit for speculative investment. Its token is directly tied to an AI network, which can deliver significant returns if the network gains traction.

However, risks remain, including potential stagnation in usage or large AI companies opting to handle work internally instead of utilizing the network. Given its relatively small market capitalization, Bittensor may suit the high-risk portion of an investment portfolio.

Is Bittensor or Bitcoin the Better Buy for the AI Boom?

In summary, Bitcoin is the better option for foundational investments, while Bittensor suits those willing to take risks for potential gains. Bitcoin’s primary risk is whether its AI link, as Visser framed it, will translate into measurable demand. Meanwhile, Bittensor’s main risk involves its ability to attract and retain users, with the token’s value contingent on the network’s success.

For investors, the benchmarks are clear: Visser’s bullish outlook hinges on Bitcoin staying above the 200-day moving average. If it slips below that mark, his thesis may falter. For Bittensor, the critical test will be whether its 30-day gain, 15.7% on October 15, stays positive; if that reading turns negative while Bitcoin’s stays positive, the AI premium in TAO may begin to fade.
