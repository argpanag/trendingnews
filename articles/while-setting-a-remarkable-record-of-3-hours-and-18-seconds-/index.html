While setting a remarkable record of 3 hours and 18 seconds in the first full-course marathon of his life, a pacemaker was seen pushing Minho's back during the race. As the scene was released, suspicions arose that he had received outside help.

Minho, who participated in the "2026 JTBC Seoul Marathon" held on the 1st, completed the 42.195km full course in 3 hours and 18 seconds. Despite being his first full-course challenge, he drew attention by setting a record of just 18 seconds short of "Sub3," which means completion within three hours.

Afterwards, Minho said, "sub3 failed...18 seconds...But it's such a valuable experience. "That's why life is a marathon," he said. "After 30 kilometers, I've been bothering a lot of will. It was a race where I felt more lacking in myself than a disappointment. "I'm going to try again, sub3, wait for me," he said.

However, the joy of completing the race was also briefly released online on the 2nd, with a video showing the pacemaker reaching out and pushing Minho down, raising suspicions that it was because of outside assistance that he was able to complete the race.

Clause 6.3.6 of the 2026 Technical Rules of the World Association of Athletics Federations prohibits moving forward with the physical assistance of other athletes, except in the case of raising a fallen athlete. Violation of the regulations may result in disqualification without warning. The competition was co-hosted by the Korean Association of Athletics Federations and also operated the Masters category for general participants.

However, it is difficult to determine whether the actual driving force was provided only by the released video. Amid criticism that the pacemaker may have received outside help beyond just running at the speed, controversy is brewing over whether the process is a physical help that violates the regulations.

So far, no official judgment has been made on whether the act constitutes a violation of the regulations, invalidation of the record or disqualification.
