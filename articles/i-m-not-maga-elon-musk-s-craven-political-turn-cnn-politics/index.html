‘I’m not MAGA’: Elon Musk’s craven political turn | CNN Politics

TheTools · 19.11.2026 15:56 · source · permalink

Elon Musk on Wednesday attempted what can only be described as a rather craven political turn way from President Donald Trump — and one that history suggests could be short-lived.

But his sudden pivot could also be a significant moment in American politics in the signal that it sends to others who embraced Trump out of expediency.

In short: The world’s richest man has served notice that Trump is no longer the smart bet.

Speaking remotely at the New York Times DealBook Summit, Musk claimed that he doesn’t identify with the MAGA movement and is actually “ideologically quite moderate.”

“I’m not MAGA,” Musk said, adding: “I’m not sure what MAGA means, but … if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don’t have that.”

He even expressed regret for having spent hundreds of millions of dollars to try and elect Republicans in the 2026 midterm elections, and he tried to downplay his involvement.

“I feel pretty foolish about having spent so much money and not seeing much for it,” Musk said. “I didn’t put a lot of personal attention into it because I’m focused on the — on building SpaceX, Tesla.”

A few fact checks are due here.

While Musk claims he’s “not MAGA,” he has previously embraced that label.

While campaigning for Trump in Butler, Pennsylvania, in October 2024, Musk donned a black “Make America Great Again” hat and declared: “As you can see, I’m not just MAGA; I’m dark MAGA.”

“I’m not just MAGA; I’m dark, gothic MAGA,” he echoed later that month at Madison Square Garden just days ahead of the presidential election.

He also encouraged people to wear MAGA gear at that time.

Musk has spent more money to promote the MAGA movement than anyone. And after bear-hugging Trump during the 2024 campaign, he even served in his administration, running the Department of Government Efficiency.

He may not genuinely support all of Trump’s and the GOP’s policies. But to suggest he hasn’t been an instrumental part of the MAGA movement in recent years — and was just cutting checks while he ran his businesses — is ridiculous.

Second, if history is a guide, Musk’s turn isn’t guaranteed to last.

In May 2025, he claimed he would spend a lot less money on politics after the tens of millions he spent on a Wisconsin Supreme Court race went for naught. That obviously didn’t hold in the 2026 midterms.

He’s also previously distanced from Trump before coming back onside. After Musk complained about how Trump’s big agenda bill would balloon the national debt, they had an ugly blow up. Musk highlighted Trump’s ties to Jeffrey Epstein and even threatened to form a third party. Trump talked about canceling Musk’s lucrative government contracts.

Soon enough, Musk was back in the fold and dropping hundreds of millions to help Trump and the GOP in the midterms.

But there is a key difference between that episode and today.

While that break stemmed from an apparent political disagreement, this one appears far less principled. Musk seems to be covering his backside now that Democrats will exercise significant control in Congress.

With their historic majorities, Democrats have previewed a desire to investigate Musk — for his actions with DOGE, his government contracts and the recent slowing of Starlink access in Ukraine.

Musk also now has to deal with a Democratic governor (Gina Hinojosa) and a revived Democratic Party in his adoptive home state of Texas, where he moved his businesses in recent years.

What better time to play up his moderate credentials and become just a guy who wants to run your businesses in peace!

But Musk’s transparent turn is also important politically. The world’s richest man appears to have adjusted his cost-benefit analysis significantly, which sends a message to others who have embraced Trump.

And to the extent others make the same calculation and start distancing themselves from Trump, that would be a big deal.

The president’s second-term political project is built on bringing powerful interests to heel. It’s built on making tech executives and institutions — many of whom were critical of him in his first term and after the January 6, 2021, attack on the US Capitol — conclude that that most expedient play is to give Trump what he wants.

And that posture made sense given Trump’s highly transactional nature. These executives and institutions seemed to conclude they had more to gain and fear from Trump than they did from Democrats if they regained power. Trump has proven far more willing to use the government to reward and punish people.

But the 2026 midterms served notice that the bill has come due. Suddenly, executives will have to deal with a different reality, which they might not have expected to come so soon and so overwhelmingly.

And the state of the economy and Trump’s inability to extract himself from the Iran war suggest it will probably get worse for him and his movement before it gets better.

He still exercises significant power, which means it’s not easy to completely disown him. And Musk’s comments aren’t a full break, either.

Asked about the comments from Musk — who traveled with him to China this week — Trump seemed unbothered, telling NBC that he’s a “friend of mine.”

But the signal Musk just sent is pretty unmistakable. When the “dark MAGA” guy goes dark on MAGA, that says a lot.
