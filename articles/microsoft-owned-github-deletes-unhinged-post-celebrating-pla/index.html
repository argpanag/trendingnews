Microsoft-Owned Github Deletes Unhinged Post Celebrating PlayStation 5 Emulator's Success Following Launch Of GTA 6

TheTools · 26.11.2026 15:44 · source · permalink

EmulationGitHubGTA 6MicrosoftPS5Xbox

The official social media team behind GitHub, the Microsoft-owned collaborative coding platform, has published and subsequently deleted a bizarrely brazen post on X celebrating the fact that Grand Theft Auto 6’s release has caused AnyPS5, a PlayStation 5 emulator, to pass “58K stars since its August launch.” It then asked the question, “Does changing where games run cross a legal line?”

To be clear, we’re not crowbarring GTA 6 into this story. GitHub’s post about the popularity of its PS5 emu opened with the line “GTA 6 is finally here, sparking one of the biggest weeks in gaming and open source on GitHub.”

Likewise, the video that was contained within GitHub’s post highlighted that users’ efforts to improve the AnyPS5 emulator were primarily driven by the release of GTA 6. The video specifically mentions a comment from one AnyPS5 coder which states, “We have 3 days until GTA 6 everyone[…]get your agents or deevs[sic] and work.”

Thankfully, you don’t have to take my word for it, as one user on X had the foresight to download and reupload GitHub’s video before it was deleted.

Again, it’s important to note here that GitHub is owned by Microsoft, which purchased the company for “$7.5 billion in stock” in 2018. That makes it all the more confusing that GitHub’s own post posits the Sony-baiting question, “Does changing where games run cross a legal line?”

Well, uh, I’d wager that the publishers of Grand Theft Auto 6, Take-Two, would probably say that the answer to that question is a firm “Yes,” considering that its been actively shooting down all of the GitHub repositories attempting to vibe-code a PC port of GTA 6 over the past week.

It’s probably pretty obvious why they opted to delete the post, but I can’t help but wonder what compelled GitHub’s social media team to ask the question in the first place.
