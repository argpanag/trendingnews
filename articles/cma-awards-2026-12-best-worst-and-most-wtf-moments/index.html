CMA Awards 2026: 12 Best, Worst, and Most WTF Moments

TheTools · 19.11.2026 15:22 · source · permalink

Lainey Wilson may not have made CMA history by becoming the first woman to win Entertainer of the Year three times, but she sure cemented her reputation as one of the CMA Awards’ best all-time hosts. The effervescent Nashville star was fully in the zone as emcee of the 60th annual Country Music Association Awards — her monologue killed, her performances were show-stoppers, and, even when speeches and run-times were about to go off the rails, she was unflappable.

So consider Wilson the host an additional “Best” on this list. There’s a bunch more to celebrate too, from Taylor Swift’s return to Faith Hill’s reactions, plus some shake-my-head fails and a few just plain “wut?” moments.

  • Best: Taylor Swift’s Return Is Worth the Wait

    It’s been several years since Taylor Swift graced the CMAs stage — 13 to be exact, which can’t possibly be a coincidence — and she returned with a performance that made us forget any time had passed at all. We would have been perfectly happy with her delivering her delightful ditty “I Knew It, I Knew You” from Toy Story 5 and nothing more, but this is someone who spent weeks handwriting personalized, wax-sealed thank-you notes to her entire crew on the Eras tour. Going above and beyond is what she does best. Her performance began backstage, strolling past Lainey Wilson, Brad Paisley, Eric Church, Sugarland, and Brooks & Dunn, trying on sunglasses and a cowboy hat, before she retrieved her acoustic guitar from “Whispering Bill” Anderson. Onstage with Keith Urban, she seamlessly transitioned into “Our Song,” off her self-titled debut, which recently turned 20 years old. In the end, it didn’t matter that Swift lost to Ella Langley for Single of the Year. She was finally back. —A.M.

  • Best: Tucker Wetmore Brings the Heat and Horns

    Somehow the most R-rated moment of the night was whatever Tucker Wetmore was doing to that piano bench, made even more welcome during an evening that was a little more mid-tempo and ballad-heavy than we usually like to see. We loved Wetmore’s much needed shot of energy with his horn-driven performance of “Who Told You That,” followed by a version of the Hank Williams Jr. classic “All My Rowdy Friends Are Coming Over Tonight” on the keys, where he assumed a Tori Amos posture before getting rid of the seat altogether. Wetmore is full of surprises — at CMA Fest this past summer, he whipped out a trumpet. —M.M.

  • WTF: Squeaky Wheel Becomes Part of the Show

    Look, we are all for honoring our elder statesmen in country music, and Sammy Kershaw is responsible for his share of truly great hits. But making a stink about being originally denied a red carpet appearance — which is literally walking around and having your photo taken — was a little silly. It’s a photo opp, not the Hall of Fame. All the screen time afforded Kershaw during the actual show was overkill, if you ask us. But hey, at the end of the day, it’s nice to see him have his moment. The arena sure seemed to agree. —M.M.

  • Best: Stephen Wilson Jr. Proves It’s Never Too Late to Be a New Artist

    At 47 years old, Stephen Wilson Jr. was the CMA Awards’ New Artist of the Year. The former scientist (“a recovering microbiologist,” as he puts it) bested fellow nominees like Avery Anna, 22, who is literally half his age. But while Wilson’s win proved that, as 30 Rock taught us, it’s never too late for now, the victory also reinforced how important a personal, authentic story is to country music. Wilson lived many lives before he ever took the CMA stage: indie-rock guitarist, boxer, scientist, and grieving son. It all informed not only the music he makes, but the (new) artist he became. —J.H.

  • Best: Miranda Lambert Is the CMA MVP

    A voice so nice she had to sing twice — and in a year where the producer/writer/label owner isn’t even nominated for an album of her own. Joining Ella Langley for a moody performance of “Snakeskin Boots” and Kacey Musgraves for their cheeky, beef-quashing duet “Horses and Divorces,” Lambert brought some of the best moments of the evening while making sure to model solidarity, not competition, with her fellow female artists. That she’s as prolific as ever while settling into legend status makes it all the more impressive. —M.M.

  • Worst: This Year’s Nominees Were All White

    It’s a shocking statistic: Zero artists of color were nominated for any awards at this year’s CMAs. And it’s a problem that runs far deeper than just this particular broadcast. Black artists garner a minuscule proportion of airtime (think less than one percent, if we’re talking about Black women), which makes the CMA’s rigid eligibility requirements for nomination impossible to meet. But we saw how easily the organization could change course and allow squeaky-wheel, veteran white artists to walk the red carpet. Now it’s time to change the radio requirements that, intentionally or not, have been keeping things far too white for too long. —M.M.

  • WTF: Lifetime Achievement Award Winners Beat Out a New Class

    “I’ve run out of things to say,” said Kix Brooks as he accepted Brooks & Dunn’s Vocal Duo of the Year award. So have we, honestly… Not to hate on the legendary duo whatsoever, but if you win both this award and the lifetime achievement honor at the same show (with a speech from Taylor Swift, to boot), it feels like a sign that voters need a serious splash of cold water. It’s time to give this trophy to someone else, ideally with actual new music out, or rehab the category altogether. —M.M.

  • Best: Lainey Wilson Finally Finds Her Anthem, With an All-Women Band

    What better way to introduce a song about the expectations placed on a woman in a relationship than a performance with a stage full of them? For Wilson’s sultry new single, written with Luke Dick and Shane McAnally, she recruited an entirely female band, string section, and background singers and made the whole thing into a gorgeous, soulful moment. This musical lane is perfect for Wilson’s pipes, and she delivered it beautifully in a dramatic setting that really met the moment (and showed that, for as wonderfully goofy as she can be, she can also be dead serious onstage). If this is a tease of an album to come, we’re here for it. —M.M.

  • Best: Faith Hill’s Reactions Deserve Their Own Award

    “I met Faith Hill for the first time tonight,” Ella Langley told the crowd, during one of her many wins. “How sick is that?” Stars, they’re just like us! One of the many highlights of the CMAs wasn’t a performance, acceptance speech, or a wonky parody about ChatGPT. It was the many times we’d catch a glimpse of our Mississippi Girl reacting to the ceremony, seated next to her husband Tim McGraw, with whom she just celebrated 30 years of marriage. There she is, soaking up Halestorm’s raging tribute to Brooks & Dunn. There she is again, smiling at Langley, who admitted she was more nervous to meet Hill than McGraw. Hill hasn’t released a non-holiday album in more than two decades (her last proper LP was 2005’s Fireflies), and she’s been relatively quiet in recent years. But her cameo at the CMAs, and at McGraw’s Country Music Hall of Fame induction last month, have us more hopeful than ever that some kind of return just may happen. How sick would that be? —A.M.

  • WTF: That Brooks & Dunn Tribute

    The choice of Nashville hard-rockers Halestorm to cover “Boot Scootin’ Boogie” before Brooks & Dunn were honored with the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award was bound to ruffle feathers in some country music circles, and social media delivered. The most viral discussion centered around a series of clips of Ronnie Dunn looking stonefaced as the tribute played on — never mind that it’s the same expression Dunn has worn for the better part of his career. But those in the know, including Ronnie himself, are well aware of Lzzy Hale and company’s association with the duo: Halestorm recorded a studio version of “Boot Scootin'” for the B&D tribute album Reboot II. —J.C.

  • Best: Brad Paisley Helps Revive the Musical Monologue

    It’s been a wild few weeks when it comes to technology — doomsday-esque Microsoft outages and airplane groundings among them — and former CMA host Brad Paisley returned to help Lainey Wilson tackle it all. “We probably need to hit the old refresh button on country music, like a software update,” Paisley said before joining Wilson in a song that called out data centers (“I ain’t proud to be a data miner’s daughter”), Waymos, and social media while making time to riff on Taylor Swift’s wedding and plans for a new name for the Nashville airport. “Click our heels and say three times, ‘Dolly Parton airport!’” Wilson cheered. “Where every gate is ‘Double D!’” —M.M.

  • Worst: Sleepy Songs Were Musical Melatonin

    The CMAs kicked off with a show-stopping duet from Vince Gill and Lainey Wilson tackling “I Will Always Love You” — almost inarguably the greatest country ballad of all time — in tribute to Dolly Parton. When it comes to filling the house with wet eyes, how do you top that? You don’t. Nevertheless, the producers still tried, programming a glut of ballads or, at least, down-tempo performances. Even a Collin Raye fan club president might call that a bit much. This included a stretch of some particularly sleepy tunes: Mumford & Sons’ “Here,” which, despite featuring Chris Stapleton, couldn’t rouse the crowd; Megan Moroney’s Cloud 9 honey-dripper “Beautiful Things”; and Hall of Famer Tim McGraw’s “Don’t Take the Girl” (we were so hoping for “Something Like That”). To be clear, they weren’t lowlights — just low energy. —A.G.
