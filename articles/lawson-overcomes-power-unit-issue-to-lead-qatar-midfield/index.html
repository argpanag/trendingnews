Lawson overcomes power unit issue to lead Qatar midfield

TheTools · 27.11.2026 20:31 · source · permalink

Lawson started the day 14th in FP1 at Lusail with a 1m25.593s, almost a second off teammate Arvid Lindblad in ninth, after a fault meant his power unit was not responding correctly.

Racing Bulls was able to resolve the issue before Lawson returned to the track for FP2, with the New Zealander going more than a second quicker in the evening session to move up to ninth with a 1m24.425s, edging Lindblad by 0.122s.

Only Mercedes, McLaren, Red Bull and Ferrari had cars ahead of the Racing Bulls pair.

“It’s been a decent day for us today and we’ve got a fast car, which is positive,” Lawson said.

“We made some big changes in the right direction between FP1 and FP2, which put the car in a good place.

“We’ll continue to review the data and make any final tweaks to bring it all together.”

The fastest times of the day were set on medium tyres by the two Mercedes drivers, whose attempts to improve on softs in FP2 were interrupted by red flags.

“There isn’t a huge difference between the compounds around here, which is likely to play a bigger role in the race than it will tomorrow,” Lawson added.

“The rest of the midfield are looking strong, so we’ll have to see where everyone ends up in Qualifying. I’m looking forward to it.”

The day was more straightforward for Lindblad, who was ninth in FP1 and completed 60 laps across the two sessions.

“It’s a very fast circuit and certainly presents different challenges compared to Las Vegas, but I really enjoyed myself and felt comfortable in the car,” Lindblad said.

“The team did a great job in providing a strong baseline setup, so overall I’m quite happy with how things went.

“That said, the rest of the weekend won’t be easy. The race, in particular, is going to be challenging from a tyre management perspective, so we’ll need to be very delicate and stay on top of that.”

Racing Bulls team principal Alan Permane said both drivers had benefited from revisions to the balance of the car between sessions, with Racing Bulls looking to secure fifth in the constructors’ championship.

“We have plenty of data from today, which we’ll analyse over night to see where we can improve ahead of tomorrow,” Permane said.

“Our long run pace looks very encouraging, so if we can put everything together, we should have a strong race as we continue our fight for P5.”
