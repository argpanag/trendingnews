Can Bangladesh escape defeat as West Indies tighten grip on Mirpur Test?

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:47 · source · permalink

A familiar batting collapse has left Bangladesh staring at defeat in the first Test against West Indies, with the hosts stumbling to 79 for six in their second innings on Day 3 at the Sher-e-Bangla National Cricket Stadium in Mirpur today.

Leading by just 106 runs with four wickets in hand, Bangladesh face an uphill battle to keep the visitors at bay after bad light brought an early close to proceedings.

Jaker Ali and Nayeem Hasan were unbeaten on 18 each when the umpires decided to call off play due to deteriorating light. With Bangladesh's lower order now exposed, the hosts face an uphill task to set West Indies a challenging target and keep their hopes of avoiding defeat alive.

It was another disappointing batting display from Bangladesh, who lost four wickets in a devastating spell during the second session to find themselves reeling at 34 for four at tea.

Bangladesh had earlier bowled West Indies out for 245 in their first innings, taking a crucial lead.

Shamar Joseph struck early, removing Tanzid Hasan for a duck in the second over. The opener edged his second ball to gully, where West Indies captain Roston Chase took a comfortable catch.

Chase then took charge of the proceedings, producing a decisive spell that left Bangladesh's top order in disarray.

The West Indies skipper first bowled Shadman Islam, who had struck a century in the first innings, with a delivery that drifted past the left-hander and hit the stumps.

He struck again off the very next ball, as Tawhid Hridoy attempted an ambitious shot against a length delivery but could only sky the ball towards mid-off, where Jayden Seales completed the catch.

Chase's third breakthrough came when he trapped Bangladesh captain Najmul Hossain Shanto lbw for 10 with another straight delivery, further deepening the hosts' troubles.

Bangladesh were also without the services of experienced batter Mominul Haque, who suffered a concussion after being struck while fielding close to the bat. Jaker Ali came in as his replacement and joined Litton Das at the crease before rain and poor light forced an early tea break.

The situation worsened for Bangladesh after the interval as left-arm spinner Jomel Warrican removed Litton for just four before dismissing Mehidy Hasan Miraz for nine, reducing the hosts to 60 for six.

Earlier in the day, Bangladesh had enjoyed a productive outing with the ball, with off-spinner Nayeem Hasan leading the charge to dismiss West Indies for 245.

Nayeem claimed four wickets for 33 runs, including the crucial scalp of Justin Greaves, whom he trapped lbw for 46 with the final delivery before lunch. He returned after the break to clean up the West Indies lower order, dismissing Shamar Joseph, Warrican and Seales in quick succession.

Tagenarine Chanderpaul had provided the visitors with a solid start, reaching his third Test half-century. The left-hander struck Taijul Islam for a six during the morning session before Shoriful Islam ended his 184-ball innings on 56, with the batter caught at second slip.

Greaves and Chase subsequently added 51 runs for the sixth wicket, helping West Indies consolidate their position. However, Nayeem's late strikes ensured Bangladesh restricted the visitors to 245.

Despite that effort, Bangladesh's second-innings collapse has handed West Indies the upper hand heading into the fourth day.
 

Nayeem sets new record as Tigers secure 27-run lead 

Nayeem Hasan became the fastest Bangladeshi spinner to reach 50 Test wickets as the Tigers bowled out the West Indies for 245 in 82.5 overs in their first innings, securing a 27-run lead in the second session of Day 3 of the first Test at the Sher-e-Bangla National Cricket Stadium in Mirpur today.

The Tigers needed just eight overs after lunch to claim the remaining four West Indies wickets, with Nayeem accounting for three of them.

Mehidy Hasan Miraz, however, struck the first blow of the second session when he had debutant Joshua Bishop stumped after the latter's quickfire 15 off 11 deliveries.

The visitors tried to up the ante and score quick runs to reduce the first-innings deficit, but Nayeem kept striking at regular intervals to derail their plans.

The off-spinner reached the milestone when he dismissed Shamar Joseph. Playing his 16th Test, Nayeem took 2,856 deliveries to reach 50 wickets, surpassing Mehidy, who previously held the record for the fewest deliveries needed by a Bangladeshi spinner to reach the milestone.

Nayeem also dismissed Jomel Warrican and Jayden Seales to finish with figures of four for 33 in 10.5 overs.
 

 

 

 

Late delight for Tigers as Nayeem removes Greaves before lunch

Bangladesh off-spinner Nayeem Hasan provided the breakthrough the Tigers had been waiting for just before lunch on Day 3 of their first Test against the West Indies at the Sher-e-Bangla National Cricket Stadium in Mirpur today.

At the end of the opening session, the visitors were 213 for six in 74.5 overs, with Nayeem trapping Justin Greaves in front with the final delivery before the break. The West Indies trail Bangladesh's first-innings total by 59 runs.

Greaves walked back to the pavilion after scoring 46 off 103 deliveries, falling just short of his third Test fifty. His dismissal also ended a 51-run sixth-wicket partnership between Greaves and West Indies skipper Roston Chase.

Nayeem could have dismissed Chase a few overs earlier when the right-hander edged one to Mominul Haque at short leg, but survived on 27 as Mominul failed to hold on to the catch.

Chase remains unbeaten on 30 off 42 deliveries.

 

Shoriful ends Chanderpaul’s resistance

Tagenarine Chanderpaul struck his third Test half-century before falling for 56 off 184 balls as West Indies reached 179 for five in 66 overs, trailing by 93 runs, on the third morning of the first Test against Bangladesh at the Sher-e-Bangla National Cricket Stadium in Mirpur on Friday.

The son of former West Indies batter Shivnarine Chanderpaul, renowned for his gritty batting, Tagenarine brought up his third fifty in 30 innings since making his Test debut in 2022. He shared a 65-run fifth-wicket stand with Justin Greaves, striking six fours and a maximum. 

 



Shoriful Islam ended the partnership by having the left-hander caught by Najmul Hossain Shanto at second slip.

Bangladesh, who posted 272 in their first innings, had fought back on the second afternoon through Taijul Islam, who removed Kavem Hodge and Amir Jangoo in the same over.

The visitors resumed day's proceedings on 134 for four.
