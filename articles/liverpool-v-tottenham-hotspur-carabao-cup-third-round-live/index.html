Andoni Iraola speaks to Sky Sports. “We decided to change a lot of the team … we needed fresh legs … we have a lot of players willing to have a good performance … get us to the next round … also looking forward to seeing those players … I am sure they will take that chance … it’s a great chance to see [the younger players] … if we put them in, it’s because we think they are ready to help us and ready to perform … we will get a lot of information from this game … it is a matter of execution and keeping calm … I trust them … I hope they do well … we know we will face a strong team … but I trust my players.”

This is all a bit of a back-of-envelope scribble, so if I’ve forgotten someone you’ll have to sue me*, but it’s just occurred to me that quite a lot of the players on the team-sheets tonight have already tasted League Cup glory. For Liverpool, Alexis Mac Allister, Cody Gakpo, Joe Gomez, Ryan Gravenberch, Waturo Endo, Kostas Tsimikas and James McConnell played in either the 2022 or 2024 final victories over Chelsea, with Alisson, Trey Nyoni and Lewis Koumas getting medals for being on the bench in 2024, also Dominik Szoboszlai despite being injured. In addition, Alexander Isak scored for Newcastle in their win over Liverpool in 2025. As for Spurs, Andy Robertson is the only one of tonight’s squad to have played and won a final (for Liverpool) while Omar Marmoush and Savio were on the bench earlier this year for Manchester City’s victory over Arsenal, just as Martin Dubravka watched on from the dugout while Newcastle won in 2025. Sandro Tonali isn’t involved tonight, but he did play in that Newcastle victory, and can tell everyone about it when they get back.

* Please do not sue me.

Roberto De Zerbi speaks to Sky Sports. “I am lucky I have a great squad … many very good players … changing the first XI, I hope we don’t change the quality of the players, I am sure they are the same level … I would like to see the goals … the same attitude of the first 30 minutes against Everton … we have the qualities enough to score … it’s a tough moment … no pressure, but we play to win the games … playing at Anfield against Liverpool is always tough … but my focus is on my team … after one month of working together … keep our eyes on our goal … our organisation of the play and nothing else.”

Both teams make a slew of changes from their Premier League selections at the weekend. The only survivor from the XI that started Liverpool’s 0-0 draw with Fulham is Ronald Araujo. He’ll move from right back to form a central defensive pairing with Joe Gomez, who returns from his latest injury lay-off.

Spurs retain three members of their starting XI for the goalless draw against Everton. Rodrigo Bentancur, Omar Marmoush and captain Archie Gray go again. A couple of LFC old boys are on the bench: Dominic Solanke and Andrew Robertson, the latter having made 378 appearances for the Reds. Expect emotion if Robertson gets minutes.

Subs: Kinsky, Robertson, van Hecke, Maddison, Savio, Fernandes, Solanke, Davies, van de Ven.

Referee: Andrew Madley (West Yorkshire)

Preamble

Liverpool drew a miserable blank against Fulham on Saturday and have yet to beat an English team at Anfield this season, while Spurs have the sum total of zero goals in four matches against top-flight opposition. That doesn’t augur well, does it?

But look at it another way. It’s the League Cup tonight, which means the team selections are likely to be left-field bordering on all-over-the-shop. And when these clubs meet, things usually happen: in the last four seasons alone, their get-togethers have generated scorelines such as 5-1, 4-0, 3-6, 4-2 and 4-3, a good smattering of last-minute equalisers and even later winners, four red cards, and one egregious VAR balls-up. Good process, everyone! So anything could feasibly happen. The fun – and it should be fun, shouldn’t it? – begins at 8pm BST. It’s on!
