Say Goodbye to HBO as the King of Fantasy as Netflix's Next Hit Shows Game of Thrones How It's Done

TheTools · 24.10.2026 12:30 · source · permalink

Since its 2011 premiere, HBO’s Game of Thrones has dominated prestige fantasy television. The George R.R. Martin adaptation set the genre’s standard and turned fantasy into the cultural phenomenon it is today. Now, however, Netflix is making an impact of its own with Magic: The Gathering, which could rival Game of Thrones' legacy.

Netflix is developing and producing a television adaptation of the popular card game Magic: The Gathering, which is set in a genuine multiverse. If the streaming platform gets it right, it could very well overtake HBO in the fantasy battle.

Magic: The Gathering Is a Massive Fantasy IP

Launched in 1993, Magic: The Gathering is the world's first trading card game. Over three decades, it has accumulated more than 27,000 unique playable cards, over 60 novels, comics, video games, and a living mythology of interconnected planes, characters, and conflicts.

Magic: The Gathering is one of the biggest fantasy IPs in existence, in which powerful spellcasters known as Planeswalkers traverse different dimensions. They each have their own aesthetic, rules of magic, political structures, and a narrative history, and they fire powerful spells at each other in an attempt to bring their opponent's life force down to zero.

This franchise has been circling Hollywood since 2001, with the Russo Brothers attached to it at one point, but all of these projects eventually evaporated. In September 2024, Netflix restarted from scratch with a new showrunner, Terry Matalas, who is behind Star Trek: Picard's acclaimed Season 3, with Oscar winner Patrick Osborne (Feast) and DIGIC Pictures handling animation.

As of May 2025, Matalas confirmed that all scripts for the first season were complete and that production had moved into the casting and animation phase.

Netflix's Magic: The Gathering Will Have Fewer Constraints Than Game of Thrones

The biggest problem that every fantasy show, post Game of Thrones, has run into is the complexity of creating live-action universes. Fantasy is expansive, and as the imagination runs wild, so can budgets as they try to bring these worlds to life. Rings of Power reportedly cost over a billion dollars across its first two seasons, and House of the Dragon routinely shoots dragon sequences on budgets that would break lesser productions.

Magic: The Gathering does not have this problem because it is animated. This is one of the soundest decisions because animation removes most of the hurdles that live-action fantasy presents. Every new plane that the series will visit, be it the neon-soaked cyberpunk corridors of Kamigawa, the gothic horror of Innistrad, or the art deco grandeur of Ravnica, can be rendered in full, without restrictions on how things should look.

Where Game of Thrones was fundamentally constrained to one world, Magic: The Gathering can reinvent its visual language in every single episode, if it wants.

Magic: The Gathering's Multiverse Has Huge Potential

While Game of Thrones is an epic fantasy drama, it was also structured around a single world. Westeros is one continent, and the Seven Kingdoms within it are at the epicenter of geopolitical conflicts that lead to elaborate battles among kings, armies, houses, and over succession.

Producing the fantasy elements, like the dragons, White Walkers, and the deep-rooted magic in the land, was always a tough task. Additionally, these elements existed in service of a story centered on this specific continent.

Magic: The Gathering has no such ceiling, as the multiverse structure allows the viewer to travel to entirely distinct planes, wherever the story arc takes them. The showrunners have chosen to center the series on the Planeswalker Chandra Nalaar, a pyromancer whose lore involves themes of freedom and confronting her past. Beloved characters like Ajani and Jace will also appear on the show.

These three characters serve as the audience's anchors across dimensions on a single map, and the multiverse allows the story to veer into different aesthetics and even genres because the medium is animation. And Magic: The Gathering isn't only about visuals — it has a main character with an emotional journey that feels relatable. It is through Chandra that the many layers of this multiverse will be revealed. It is a solid strategy by Netflix.

Like Game of Thrones, this story features an ensemble of characters, interweaving plots, and morally complex themes, making it a worthy contender for the HBO Original. It does have an important ace in its pocket, and that's what could give it the edge in the fantasy race.

Netflix Could Overtake HBO in Fantasy TV

Currently, HBO has not been dethroned in the fantasy department. It has House of the Dragon going strong, as well as a fantasy-lite show, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms. However, it is clear that fans are aching for something new, which is why the latter proved to be a hit worldwide. The era in which GoT set the temperature for all other fantasy shows is coming to an end, and Magic: The Gathering is in the right place and the right time to take the throne.

With the incredible team that is involved in the show, it seems likely, too. Terry Matalas has created some of the most widely regarded works in Star Trek history, and 12 Monkeys is a cult classic in science fiction. This is a creative who understands mythology and adaptation of an IP without suffocating it. The fact that all the scripts are ready before a single frame of animation is a heartening sign that Magic: The Gathering won't be just CGI fluff.

Magic: The Gathering will be built on an epic story that spans decades, and it has the medium to execute its imaginative arcs perfectly. If that is done well, and if Netflix allows the animated adaptation to breathe and come into its own, it is all set to become the fantasy event of the decade, finally overtaking Game of Thrones at its own game.
