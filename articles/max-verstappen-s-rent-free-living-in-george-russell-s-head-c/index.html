Max Verstappen's 'rent-free' living in George Russell's head continues

TheTools · 22.11.2026 13:18 · source · permalink

Obsession is probably the best word to describe George Russell's mindset when it comes to Max Verstappen at this point.

And that didn't just start this past weekend in Las Vegas, with their latest free practice run-in again triggering sharp words from Russell about the four-time world champion.

Entering the 2026 Formula 1 season, Russell was the heavy favorite to win the world championship. Rookie teammate Kimi Antonelli struggled mightily in 2025, and Mercedes entered 2026 head and shoulders above the rest of the grid amid Formula 1's new set of regulations. The opening round in Melbourne solidified that status.

It has been a downward spiral for Russell quite literally ever since, and for as much as fans sometimes like to credit "the car" when it comes to the success of any dominant driver or team, Russell's struggles in the constructor championship-winning entry have proven that world championships are still won and lost on the race track.

Antonelli has dominated Russell in the same machinery the entire season. With nine wins compared to Russell's three, he eliminated Russell from world championship contention after just 19 of 23 races, and he's already ahead of him on the all-time wins list.

For as much as Russell tried to completely discredit the success of his teammate early on, it only became clearer and clearer that Antonelli is, in fact, the better driver. Even without some of Russell's mechanical failures (Antonelli has had his too), it has not even been a battle, with the 20-year-old Italian now 138 points clear of the 28-year-old Briton.

So naturally, with Russell fighting not for a title but simply for a top five spot in the championship standings, re-enter Verstappen.

George Russell is letting Max Verstappen live rent-free in his head, and it's doing more harm than good

It's no secret that Verstappen and Russell have not had a great relationship over the years. And we'll be the first time admit that it has, at times, been two-sided, with Verstappen's decision to run into Russell in Spain a year ago quite literally costing him the world championship (whether he wanted to address it after the season or not).

But the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend proved once and for all that if Russell wants to experience any semblance of success in Formula 1, much less within his own team, he needs to either start charging Verstappen rent, or he needs to evict the Dutchman from the space within his mind that he is seemingly occupying 24/7. Because at this point, something needs to change.

Russell isn't the only driver to have had run-ins with Verstappen over the years, but as far as sustained frustration over minor incidents that others would simply get over relatively quickly, the Mercedes driver's is simply unmatched.

The respect between Verstappen and the rest of the grid, whether it's Lewis Hamilton, Lando Norris, Charles Leclerc, Antonelli, or literally anybody else, is evident and unquestioned, even when tensions rise.

But with Russell, not so much.

Russell has been making comments about his disdain for Verstappen for years, in the past referring to him as a "bully". This is a driver who literally orchestrated a follow-up question from a specific reporter in 2024 just so he could execute a completely scripted rant against Verstappen to look like some sort of a hero for calling him out publicly.

You want "pure cinema"? This was almost straight from a movie, and it will never not be an all-time classic moment in whatever this "rivalry" is supposed to be.

And given the fact that the reporter is one who has had his own biases against Verstappen over the years, it's even more telling that he was willing to admit afterward that Russell had confronted him about enabling the staged rant.

After their run-in during practice in Las Vegas didn't trigger more than a warning, because it was quite frankly not remarkable whatsoever, Russell again went in on Verstappen, stating that "somebody's got to stand up to that guy!"

There are two options. Number one is to beat him on the race track. That's something that Russell, in a dominant Mercedes, hasn't even been able to do this year. Verstappen's Red Bull was a tractor until late June, and he's still managed to pass the preseason world championship favorite in wins, podium finishes, and points (despite having more DNFs/retirements).

And number two is to play mind games. Russell, for whatever reason, still hasn't figured out that if you try to play mind games with Verstappen, you will lose. Every time.

He simply cannot handle that kind of pressure, and it shows, just as it has for years.

The weekend ended for Russell in totally predictable fashion: in the barrier with just over two laps remaining, as he tried to pass Antonelli for second to challenge Verstappen for the victory in a race that ultimately saw 35 lead changes in 50 laps. He stayed in the race but only finished ninth, and he has now finished outside of the top five in more than half of the races contested post-Miami.

Verstappen laughed about Russell's blunder on the radio, and after the race, stated that "his comments don't motivate me, because he's nowhere near me. All talk off the track and a complete joke on it this season."

Verstappen's words are harsh, no doubt. But Russell has backed himself into a corner, and if we are looking strictly at performance, he is indeed nowhere closer to where Verstappen is, even in a year when he was supposed to dominate.

Talk all you want about the importance of humility, but given Russell's past comments, we can't blame Verstappen for standing up for himself here.

He now has 29 career victories in seasons his team has not (or will not) finish in the top two in the constructor standings, by far an all-time record, whereas Russell is tied for fourth in the standings in his first year in constructor championship-winning machinery.

Russell is seemingly concerned about Verstappen and only Verstappen, and he's doing himself no favors by trying to spark a rivalry with a driver he simply doesn't like, pretty much just because he simply doesn't like him and never has.

And if he wants to have any shot at a bounce-back season in 2027, his number one goal needs to be to focus on himself.

Russell is now just 14 points clear of dropping out of the top five completely. Verstappen is second, 40 points ahead of Russell with two races remaining on the schedule.

To be fair to Russell, it probably doesn't help matters that he knows Toto Wolff would prefer to have Verstappen in his race car. You're fooling yourself if you think otherwise, and that's got to weigh on you as a Formula 1 driver, especially after everybody told you from December to March that the world title was yours and 2026 was going to be "your year".

It probably doesn't help matters that there were rumors, up until the summer, that Wolff would take another swing at Verstappen to put him in the struggling Russell's seat. He doesn't need to admit it for it to be true; just look at the past 12 years, specifically the past three, of his actions. He has made multiple attempts to sign him, and none have panned out.

But at the end of the day, Russell still has the opportunity to control what he can control, both on the race track and off of it. Yet for whatever reason, he continues to let Verstappen control it, and the results are completely predictable.
