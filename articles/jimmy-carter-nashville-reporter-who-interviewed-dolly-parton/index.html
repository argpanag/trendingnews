Jimmy Carter, the Nashville entertainment reporter who spent decades covering country music and Hollywood, has died at 73. His family confirmed the news, while former employers remembered a broadcaster whose interviews connected viewers with stars.

According to The Tennessean, Carter died on Tuesday, October 13. Meanwhile, his granddaughter Kira Sparks said he passed away in his sleep, a detail the family also confirmed with WSMV.

In her announcement, Sparks wrote: "We received the news today that my grandfather, Jimmy Carter, passed in his sleep." She also shared: "He leaves behind three daughters who loved him deeply, five grandchildren, and two great-grandchildren."

However, his family has not publicly announced a medical cause of death. Additionally, funeral arrangements remained unannounced in reports published Wednesday morning.

How Did Jimmy Carter Become a Familiar Voice in Nashville?

Before his Nashville career, Carter grew up in Montgomery, Alabama, and developed an interest in radio while still young. Later, he became a recognizable presence at awards shows, red carpets, and events around Music Row.

Specifically, he spent a combined 21 years at WSMV Channel 4 across two periods. First, he worked there for six years during the 1980s. Then, he returned for another 15 years, from 2003 through 2018.

Beyond local television, Carter contributed to WSM Radio and the nationally syndicated country entertainment program Crook & Chase. Meanwhile, his interviews ranged from Dolly Parton, Alan Jackson, and Garth Brooks to Clint Eastwood, Sylvester Stallone, and Kevin Costner.

Importantly, his work earned honors, including duPont-Columbia and Edward R. Murrow awards, according to WSMV. Those achievements also reflected a career that extended beyond celebrity appearances into reporting and production.

After his death, WSM Radio posted: "Today, we lost a dear friend and a cherished member of the WSM family." The station also described him as a trusted source, gifted broadcaster, and friend to artists, listeners, and colleagues.
