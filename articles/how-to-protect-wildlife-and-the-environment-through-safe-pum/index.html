How to protect wildlife and the environment through safe pumpkin display and disposal practices 

TheTools · 01.11.2026 09:10 · source · permalink

Keep your jack-o'-lantern away from wildlife and landfills this Halloween

NewsNews | Oct 30, 2026

alongwell@vaildaily.com

From pumpkin pie to jack-o’-lanterns, millions of Americans turn to the orange gourd each autumn to celebrate the season’s ghoulish and gratitude-filled holidays. 

In 2025, the U.S. Department of Agriculture reported that producers across the country grew 1.47 billion pounds of pumpkins, sold not only for pureeing, canning and consuming, but for Halloween and Thanksgiving decorations. 

However, the vast majority of pumpkins left lingering after the holidays can pose a significant threat to wildlife and the environment if left unattended or disposed of improperly. 

Ahead of Halloween, Colorado Parks and Wildlife issued a warning that leaving out pumpkins for wildlife can have negative consequences for both humans and wildlife. Not to mention, feeding wildlife and knowingly luring bears are both illegal in Colorado.

Pumpkins can attract unwanted and dangerous interactions with all manner of wildlife, from deer, elk and raccoons to black bears and mountain lions.

During the fall, bears are extra active as they pack on the calories — up to 20,000 a day — before they can den for the winter. And in a year where drought and freeze events eliminated a lot of the animals’ natural forage, bear conflict and activity have already skyrocketed. Any human-related food source — trash being the most significant — can lead to habituation and have dangerous consequences for humans and bears.   

“Often, we see bird feeders bring bears into people’s yards and onto porches,” said Kris Middledorf, Parks and Wildlife’s area wildlife manager in Steamboat Springs, in a news release. “We then see escalating conflicts with bears that have gotten close to homes, then trying to or successfully gaining access to homes. It is the same story with pumpkins people put out every October. This can present serious human health and safety issues that we can prevent.”

Attracting deer and elk into your yard with pumpkins and jack-o’-lanterns can also bring in predators like mountain lions. Elk, moose and deer with babies can also become aggressive in defending their young. Bringing these ungulates closer to humans can also increase the likelihood of conflict with dogs and cars as well as increase the spread of disease between livestock, humans and wildlife. 

While Parks and Wildlife has recommended that individuals bring in pumpkins overnight throughout the season, they also suggest speedy — and safe — disposal of the gourds after the holidays. 

It’s estimated that each year, well over 80% of pumpkins purchased end up in landfills where, as food waste, they produce methane, a potent greenhouse gas that is 25 times stronger than carbon dioxide. 

Diverting the jack-o’-lantern — so long as it is free of candles, lights, wax, glitter, paint or other decorations — to a compost facility offers a safer way to dispose of them.  

Many communities offer pumpkin-specific composting, or local ranchers and farmers often use them to feed domestic livestock or create their own compost. Check with your local community and compost facility for opportunities to dispose of your pumpkins properly.
