'Small but mighty' Saanich Fair returns for 158th edition this weekend

TheTools · 05.09.2026 05:25 · source · permalink

Big crowds are expected this weekend at the Saanich Fair, the oldest agricultural event of its kind in Western Canada — and one of the largest.

The fair’s 158th edition has a new theme, “Small But Mighty,” which is meant to highlight the crowd-pleasing stable of mini horses that will be on display throughout the weekend.

But the tagline also aptly describes the fair itself, said events co-ordinator Wendy Kotorynski.

“Every inch is filled with something,” she said. “The Saanich Fair is a bustling, happy place. We are small, but mighty, so it fits.”

Kotorynski, one of the staff and volunteers with the North & South Saanich Agricultural Society, grew up in the Saanichton area and has been around the fair since she was a child.

The Labour Day tradition is expected to draw between 45,000 and 50,000 people this weekend.

The aim is to provide something for everyone, from animals and exhibits to the midway and entertainment. “We think we do a really good job with that,” Kotorynski said.

More than 35 performances will take place over two stages, with headliners Big Sugar (Saturday) and Queen: It’s a Kinda Magic (Sunday) expected to draw large crowds.

The performances are included free with paid admission to the site. Daily admission is $18 for adults, $13 for seniors and $12 for youth ages 7-13.

There are 19 exhibit departments and hundreds of classes for children, youth and adults, along with a bustling 4-H program. The agricultural show and farm animals component of the fair remains strong, thanks to an abundance of horses, llamas, alpacas, sheep, poultry, rabbits and dairy cattle.

A midway with more than 30 amusements and dozens of games opens Friday at 3 p.m., and runs from 10 a.m. until 11 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday and until 5 p.m. on Monday. Tickets are $4 to $10 per ride and can be purchased in advance through the Saanich Fair website, at saanichfair.ca.

Gates to the fair are open from 9 a.m. until 9 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday, and from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Monday. Traffic leading to and from the event is expected to be heavy, though traffic-control measures at nearby intersections will ease the burden at peak hours.

“There are certain times of day where everyone arrives at once, and those are when the back-ups happen,” Kotorynski said. “But it is not all day long.”

The administration and operations team had the site humming in early August, with three sold-out engagements at the fairgrounds with the RCMP Musical Ride. They turned their attention to the fair almost immediately, with a focus on expanding areas outside the popular midway.

New this year is a low-sensory tent for adults and children, run by the Evolutions Wellness Collective, complete with noise-cancelling headphones. “There’s nowhere on the fairgrounds that is quiet. But their tent will be as far away from the midway and everything else as possible. It’s a little oasis of rest.”

Kotorynski said the needs of parents and young children were also a priority. The Kids Zone and Tiny Town areas of the site offer family-friendly entertainment, games and activities.

“The midway can be quite loud, and full of older kids and adults. We really wanted to have an area where younger kids can run around and not feel overwhelmed. It’s also close to the animals, so they get that experience as well.”

After not hosting one in recent years, the fair will offer a pancake breakfast, served Saturday and Sunday morning from 9 .m. to 11 a.m. The proceeds will support the Lions Club, one of the many community partnerships the fair has with local businesses and organizations.

Those relationships are the backbone of the Saanich Fair, Kotorynski said.

“We have a lot of sponsors who have donated this year, and that keeps the fair going. What that allows us to do is keep our prices as low as possible. It is extremely expensive to run the fair and costs are going up.

“But we’re not going anywhere. We will be here year after year after year.”
