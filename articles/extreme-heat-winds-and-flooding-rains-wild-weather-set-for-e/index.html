Extreme heat, winds and flooding rains: Wild weather set for eastern states

TheTools · 04.09.2026 19:43 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Weather Forecasts

Topic:Weather Forecasts

Spring has brought record warmth to eastern Australia after unprecedented winter heat. (Supplied: Laurie Wilson)

A powerful front will generate a smorgasbord of wild weather across eastern Australia this weekend.

It will include: potential record hot early September temperatures in Brisbane and Sydney; damaging winds and gusty thunderstorms in parts of New South Wales, South Australia and Victoria; flooding rains in northern Victoria and northern Tasmania; and a dusting of snow across the Alps.

Parts of Victoria may experience flooding rains. (Supplied: Amruta Wazalwar)

Summer heat arrives early in NSW and Queensland

After unprecedented winter heat across much of eastern Australia, the start to spring has also brought record warmth.

Sydney's maximums through the first five days of September are on track to match the city's midsummer average of 26.4 degrees Celsius — that is warmer than any other start to spring since the Observatory Hill station opened in the 1850s.

For many parts of northern and eastern NSW, the highest temperatures of the week will arrive today, as an approaching front draws a strong north-westerly airstream south from the tropics.

Most Sydney suburbs are tipped to hit 32C or 33C this afternoon, as much as 13 above average for September, and again this will challenge a record.

The current hottest day for Sydney's Observatory Hill during the first week of spring is 31.1C from 2013.

Highs today are heading above 30C across a large part of southern Queensland and NSW. (ABC News)

The state's northern coast is also heading to the low 30s today, while maximums will reach the mid 30s in the north-west.

After a dry winter along the NSW coast and low soil moisture, today's hot winds will cause fire dangers to spike.

For Greater Sydney the first "extreme" fire danger day of the season is predicted along with a total fire ban, while a "high" fire danger is predicted for the Illawarra, Hunter and parts of the state's north.

Under an extreme rating, a burning fire could be fast moving, dangerous and threaten life and property, while a high danger indicates a heightened risk and a need to be on alert.

A cooler change will flush the hot air out of eastern and northern NSW overnight, although the Northern Rivers will still hit the low 30s on Sunday.

Cooler air from the south arriving on Sunday night is set to lower the temperature in south-east Queensland.  (Supplied: Trish Mears)

Southern Queensland is also sweating early this year and tomorrow will see temperatures peak in the mid 30s, and there is the slight risk of records for early September.

Thankfully though a lack of strong winds will keep the fire danger across south-east Queensland below 'extreme', before cooler air from the south arrives on Sunday night.

Gusty storms and gales trigger multiple warnings

Further south, the vigorous front made landfall on the SA coast overnight, and will track rapidly east through Victoria and southern NSW today.

The system has tightened up the pressure gradient across a large swathe of south-east Australia, causing winds to rapidly strengthen and activating warnings in multiple states.

Peak gusts today along stretches of the coast and ranges should reach around 100 kilometres per hour, strong enough to bring down tree branches and power lines.

The front will also trigger isolated thunderstorms today, which have the potential to draw even stronger jet stream winds down to the surface.

The highest risk of storms is across central and eastern Victoria, and along the NSW central and southern slopes and ranges, including the eastern Riverina.

Heavy rain to trigger river rises, followed by dusting of snow

The system is also bringing relatively widespread showers to south-east states, and a thin plume of tropical moisture on the leading edge of the front will bring moderate falls to northern Tasmania and the alpine region of Victorian and NSW.

While average totals should remain below 50 millimetres, wet catchments and elevated rivers after heavy winter rain have prompted a flood watch for 10 catchments.

At this stage only minor flood levels are expected, although the Bureau of Meteorology (BOM) has warned of the chance of isolated moderate flooding in areas of higher rainfall in north-east Victoria.

The rain will ease tonight as the front moves quickly into the Tasman, but colder air arriving off the Southern Ocean will lead to light snowfalls overnight across the higher Alps.

While snow should fall later today on the higher alpine peaks, the 2026 snow season is winding down early after the warmest winter on record. (Supplied: Robbo MCS)

However, it is a case of too little too late to save the ski season, with natural snow cover currently the worst on record across the alpine region for early spring.

The wild weather will rapidly ease across south-east Australia on Sunday as the front heads out into the Tasman Sea.
