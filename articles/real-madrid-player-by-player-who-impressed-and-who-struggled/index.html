Real Madrid player-by-player: Who impressed and who struggled against Atlético

TheTools · 20.09.2026 17:06 · source · permalink

Madrid struggled for long stretches at the Metropolitano, with only a few players emerging from a difficult derby with credit.

Blackout at the Metropolitano. It was a Real Madrid side that looked inferior against a superior Atleti team that controlled the game and won. Again. A Madrid team that completely lost its way and only woke up in the 81st minute, when Bernardo Silva came on. That was no coincidence. By then, it was too late, but the Portuguese had enough time to put a perfect ball on a plate for Rüdiger.

That late push, a battling Bellingham and a dangerous Diomande down the left were the only positives.

Everything else was cause for alarm. Vinicius was one of the biggest disappointments, coming off in the 54th minute. So was referee Ortiz Arias. Because if Real Madrid were invisible, so was the referee, who failed to send off Cristián Romero and Kang-in Lee.

One reality does not obscure another: Madrid are six points behind Barcelona, and several more behind in terms of what they are showing on the field.

Real Madrid player ratings vs. Atlético

Courtois: Reliable and decisive on the only occasion he was called into action in the first half, when he stopped a shot from Kang-In Lee. In the second half, even he could not prevent two goals when he was left exposed. One came from the penalty spot, the other from inside the six-yard box. He then made an impossible save to deny a Romero header. He stopped everything he could.

Dumfries: Restrained going forward, he never really contributed from the start and was more focused on shutting down Baena. Defensively, though, he did his job. Most of the danger and headaches came from the other flank.

Konaté: He cleared several Atlético attacks in the first half, although one poor clearance nearly ended badly. He was also nowhere to be seen on the penalty play. But he did not provide the usual sense of security, and he struggled with the ball at his feet. That said, he saved what would have been 3-0 by diving in headfirst to clear a rebound off the line.

Huijsen: He had been putting together a solid, assured performance, just as he has throughout the season. But everything changed in the 50th minute. The penalty on Giuliano was clear. The Atlético player was getting away from him, and Huijsen grabbed him. He was also sent off.

The Metropolitano was the scene of his first bad day as a Madrid player, and his first of the season. A stadium of nightmares for the defender.

Cucurella: Giuliano caused him huge problems. Even for a Cucurella accustomed to these kinds of battles. This time, though, he finally cracked.

He held up in the first half, but the punishment came after the break. He arrived a little late on the play that led to the penalty, and Kang-In and Giuliano combined to beat him on the move that made it 2-0. It simply wasn’t his day, and it was the worst possible day for that to happen.

That said, he was left alone to deal with the danger.

Valverde: Mourinho considers him indispensable, an unquestioned presence in the engine room. But he could not get it running. Nor could he and Tchouaméni impose themselves on an Atlético midfield that had more of the ball than Madrid.

He was strong in duels and his attitude was good, but on days like this, that is not enough.

Tchouaméni (71′): He and Valverde do not click. That is a reality, at least for now. Defensively, he did his job, and physically he moves around the field naturally. But he offered little going forward. Although that is not his job, or at least it shouldn’t be...

Güler (54′): He earned his place on the big stage and into the starting lineup on merit. But on the right wing, he was lost. He barely made an impact on the derby, aside from one counterattack where, unusually for him, he made the wrong decision. After Huijsen was sent off, Mourinho opted for Diomande’s directness and took Güler off.

Bellingham (82′): Amid Madrid’s struggles, he provided a little spark. Not much, but he tried. There were a few isolated plays in which he protected the ball well and tried to move the team forward.

He was effective when he took defenders on, and his work rate was commendable. He won virtually every duel. He would have stood out more if he’d had anyone around him to lean on.

Vinicius (54′): Nowhere to be found. He never found a way to beat his man and created virtually nothing with his passing. And after barely making an impact, he was the first player substituted, along with Güler.

Earlier than ever, in such an important game. He left the field accepting the difficult circumstances. Even he did not seem surprised by his own substitution.

Mbappé: Completely isolated. Nobody was creating anything, and he barely had a chance to get into the opposition penalty area. He dropped deeper to receive the ball on a few occasions, faced with the isolation up front, but his impact was minimal.

One shot was blocked, and that was about it. A major setback.

Substitutes

Diomande (54′): Lively, and clearly much more comfortable on the left than the right. He had Madrid’s first chance, creating it himself, although his shot went over the bar. He was the best.

Rüdiger (54′): Came on to try to hold things together with Madrid down to 10 men, then dragged them back into the game with a header right at the death.

Camavinga (71′): Mourinho continues to trust him.

Bernardo (82′): Came on with almost no time left, far too late. But it was enough for him to put a perfect ball on a plate for Rüdiger.
