Key symptom linked to 'American Covid' as HSE says 'stay home for five days'

TheTools · 05.10.2026 10:18 · source · permalink

Cases of Covid in Ireland are surging with hundreds of people being hospitalised thanks to the virus as a new dominant strain spreads.

The Omicron strain XFG - also known as Stratus - has been dubbed "American Covid" after spreading rapidly in the USA. It is now dominant in many countries around the world. In Ireland, where XFG now accounts for 55% of all tested cases, there were 479 cases of Covid recorded by the Health Protection Surveillance Centre in the most recent week- up from 319 the week before and 277 the week before that.

Routine testing is no longer carried out, so the recorded figures are the tip of the iceberg. There were also 187 hospitalisations and one death in the most recent week at the end of September.

The strain has been known since last year but has now become dominant in several countries, and medical experts say it has some unique symptoms. Pharmacist Daniel Cheung, writing in NowPatient, said there is a key symptom of XFG - also known as Stratus. He said: "The key symptoms and most noticeable symptoms of the Stratus variant include hoarseness, scratchy/raspy voice, which is considered a unique symptom distinguishing it from other Covid strains."

He said other symptoms include a dry cough, sore throat, fever, fatigue, muscle aches and a loss of sense of taste and smell. GP Dr Renée Hoenderkamp told the Mail patients with Covid now complain of sore throats, headaches, runny noses and fatigue. She said: "In the past 10 days to three weeks in general practice, my lists have definitely changed to people with these symptoms."

Dr Hoenderkamp added: "It's very flu‐like, actually, which is probably the fairest way to describe it." One woman who recently had Covid explained her symptoms, saying: "I was absolutely exhausted, with a temperature, cough and really painful flu-like aches in my legs and joints. There were times in the morning when I'd think: 'I'm fine, get your act together', but by lunchtime I'd have to go back to bed. I slept through a lot of it."

She said her daughter 'had a really bad headache, sore throat and cough, a raging temperature and was completely exhausted'. Her GP said they had seen three patients with similar symptoms that day.

GP Dr Saira Bano, founder of SwiftDoctor, said: "A loss or change in your sense of taste or smell is still a recognised Covid symptom, but it isn't the giveaway sign people may remember from earlier in the pandemic. It became much less common with Omicron compared with earlier variants, and an ordinary cold can also reduce your sense of smell, particularly if your nose is badly congested.

"So a change in taste or smell shouldn't automatically be taken to mean you have Covid. Nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea can also occur with Covid and other respiratory viral infections, but they aren't the dominant symptoms you'd normally expect with a straightforward cold. If vomiting and diarrhoea are the main problem, I'd also consider a stomach infection such as norovirus rather than immediately assuming it's flu or Covid."

If you take a test and test positive, the HSE says you should stay at home and avoid contact with other people for five days. The advice reads: "You can leave home after 5 days if your symptoms have fully or mostly gone for the last 48 hours."

You should also avoid meeting people who are at very high risk for 10 days. This starts from the day you first had symptoms.
