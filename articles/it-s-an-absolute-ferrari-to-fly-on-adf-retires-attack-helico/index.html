'It's an absolute Ferrari to fly on': ADF retires attack helicopters

TheTools · 26.09.2026 00:03 · source · permalink
  • T

    By Tilda Colling

  • Topic:Defence Forces

By Tilda Colling

Topic:Defence Forces

The Australian Defence Force is retiring its fleet of Tiger attack helicopters this month. (ABC News: Tristan Hooft)

In short:

The Australian Army will retire the use of the Tiger Armed Reconnaissance Helicopter. 

The 1st Aviation Regiment have farewelled the fleet with a flyover in Darwin.

What's next?

The fleet will be replaced by Apache helicopters, and the regiment moved to Townsville.

After more than two decades, the Australian Defence Force (ADF) is farewelling its $1.1-billion fleet of attack helicopters, known as the Tigers, and replacing them with American Apaches.

The Tiger Armed Reconnaissance Helicopters (ARH) will be retired from use in the ADF this month, and the 1st Aviation Regiment will be transitioned to Townsville away from Darwin.

Nicholas Bilton described flying the Tigers as a childhood dream come true. (ABC News: Tristan Hooft)

Captain Nicholas Bilton, who is based in Darwin and has been flying the Tigers for three years, said the experience has been "absolutely awesome".

"It's literally me living out my childhood dream — I've wanted to do it ever since I was a kid," he said.

"I think I'll just savour the moment, enjoy the memories Tiger brought.

"I really enjoyed flying it but I'm ready for the next steps."

Known for their speed and agility, the 22 Tiger ARHs were acquired by the Australian defence force in 2001 and have been in service since 2004.

Tiger ARHs have been used by the Australian Defence force for more than 20 years. (Supplied: Australian Defence Force)

According to a 2016 report, Australia was one of four countries operating the two-seater attack helicopters, with France, Germany and Spain also including them in their fleets.

It's unknown yet where the Tigers will be retired to, although last year the Albanese government considered offloading them to Kyiv to aid in Ukraine's war efforts

Captain Bilton last week completed a final send off for the Tigers with a flyover of the iconic Mindil Beach Markets.

"I'm going to miss most of the manoeuvrability of the Tiger — it's an absolute Ferrari to fly on … I'm going to miss it,"

From Tigers to Apaches

Lieutenant-Colonel Jason Perrins said the Tigers have been a "key and longstanding" part of the unit.

"It's been part of the furniture here really, and all of the people that have been through it, it is going to be a sad time but it's also been an excellent period through that and we're celebrating the people," he said.

Jason Perrins was one of the first to be qualified to fly the Apache helicopter. (ABC News: Tristan Hooft)

He said the Tigers have participated in international exercises, supporting the US and recently showing off defence capabilities to international partners in Malaysia.

The regiment's aircraft will be transitioned to AH‑64E Apache attack helicopters, which are being acquired from the United States in a $4.5 billion dollar deal.

Six Apaches have been delivered to Townsville, with all 29 expected to arrive in Australia by the end of 2029.

The Australian Army has acquired 29 new AH-64E Apache attack helicopters. (ABC News: Baz Ruddick)

Lieutenant-Colonel Perrins was one of the first army pilots qualified to fly the Apaches.

"I wouldn't say the Tigers can't keep up, both are excellent attack platforms, but what the Apache does, it is a step-up in a number of areas, connectivity and data systems and sensors," he said.

An ADF spokesperson said the transition to the Apache Helicopter reflected "Australia's need to operate in an evolving threat environment".
