Spain and Portugal roadmaps help build momentum to end fossil fuels at COP31  - Greenpeace International

TheTools · 18.11.2026 21:12 · source · permalink

Antalya, Türkiye – The announced roadmaps away from fossil fuels from Spain and Portugal at COP31 are a welcome development, but must now lead to an ambitious outcome at the UN climate conference to accelerate the phase-out of fossil fuels.

At a press conference of the Beyond Oil and Gas Alliance (BOGA) at COP31, Spain, Portugal and Denmark announced they would launch roadmaps for the transition away from fossil fuels, while Luxembourg said it would launch its roadmap before the joint Ireland-Tuvalu conference for the transition away from fossil fuels next year. The BOGA alliance called on greater action to end fossil fuels to limit the extent and duration of the looming 1.5°C overshoot.

Jasper Inventor, Head of COP31 of Greenpeace Delegation said: “This is a welcome push by the countries that have announced their roadmaps. We encourage other countries who are yet to do so to step forward and help secure a COP31 outcome that accelerates the phase-out of fossil fuels. These announcements send a clear signal that the era of fossil fuels will end. But the devil is in the detail: if we’re to keep 1.5°C alive we need to go harder and faster. Our collective future is on the line here at COP31.” 

Greenpeace Spain-Portugal also welcomed the announcement from Spain to not only present its roadmap for phasing out fossil fuels in Antalya, but also advocate for an electrification target linked to renewable energy and the phase-out of fossil fuels in the COP31 outcome.

Sara Pizzinato, Climate and Energy Campaigner, Greenpeace Spain-Portugal said: “We hope that the roadmaps will include 2040 as the deadline for the decarbonisation of the economy, along with guidelines to harness the potential of sufficiency, efficiency and electrification with renewables to accelerate the transition.

“Greenpeace Spain – Portugal has demonstrated that Spain and Portugal can phase out fossil fuels as early as 2040 by reducing its energy demand by 39 % and meeting the entirety of that demand with renewable energy. What Spain and Portugal must not do is resubmit the same weak targets as those in the current Integrated National Energy and Climate Plans for 2030. It must include dates for phasing out the use of coal, gas and oil (no later than 2040), and measures to end subsidies for fossil fuels.”

Simon Bradshaw, COP31 Lead, Greenpeace Australia Pacific said: “The only path to returning warming to 1.5°C is through leaving fossil fuels in the ground. To inject the momentum these talks critically need, Australia must lead by example through developing its own national roadmap away from fossil fuels, including exports. We have just heard Denmark, another fossil fuel producer, say it is putting end dates on oil and gas production. Australia must be willing to do the same.

“Australia’s Presidency of Negotiations will be judged on whether it builds further momentum and practical pathways for the global transition away from fossil fuels. This must include an electrification pledge that is powered by clean, renewable sources that actually displace fossil fuels and align with the science of 1.5°C, not a hollow pledge that risks driving up fossil gas production.”
