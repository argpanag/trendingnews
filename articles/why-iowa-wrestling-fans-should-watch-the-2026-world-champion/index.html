Iowa wrestling fans have spent years watching Spencer Lee dominate on the college mat. Now, the three-time NCAA champion is chasing another milestone on the international stage.

Lee headlines a group of Iowa-connected wrestlers competing at the 2026 Senior World Championships, scheduled for Oct. 24 through Nov. 1 in Astana, Kazakhstan.

The former Hawkeye will represent the United States at 57 kg in men's freestyle, while Kennedy Blades, an Olympic silver medalist who trains with the Hawkeye Wrestling Club, will compete at 68 kg in women's freestyle. 

Both give Iowa fans a reason to follow the world's best wrestlers beyond the college season.

With Olympic medalists, world champions and former college standouts in the field, the tournament offers a chance to see how Iowa's wrestling connections measure up against elite international competition.

Tomorrow · 4:30 AM UTC

Spencer Lee Is Chasing A Senior World Title

Lee remains one of the most accomplished wrestlers in Iowa history. He won three NCAA championships, earned two Hodge Trophy awards and became an Olympic silver medalist at the 2024 Paris Games.

Now competing at 57 kg, Lee returns to the senior world stage after finishing ninth at the 2025 World Championships. He has already demonstrated that his wrestling can translate to freestyle, earning Olympic silver in Paris after a standout college career at Iowa.

The next challenge is turning that success into a senior world championship.

International freestyle differs from the folkstyle wrestling Lee mastered in college. Wrestlers must adjust to different scoring opportunities, exposure rules and tactical demands, making the transition a compelling storyline for fans who know Lee primarily from his time in an Iowa singlet.

His matches in Astana will offer another opportunity to see one of the sport's most decorated American wrestlers pursue a world title.

Kennedy Blades Brings Olympic Pedigree To The Tournament

Blades gives Iowa fans another major name to follow, competing in women's freestyle at 68 kg.

The Hawkeye Wrestling Club athlete won a silver medal at the 2024 Paris Olympics, establishing herself among the world's elite. Her place on the U.S. team puts another Iowa-connected wrestler in position to contend against international competition.

Blades' tournament is especially interesting because the Senior World Championships bring together established champions and emerging contenders from around the world. Each match offers a different test of her technique, pace and ability to score against opponents with a range of international styles.

For Iowa fans, her performance is also a reminder that the Hawkeye wrestling community extends beyond the men's college program. The Hawkeye Wrestling Club provides a home for elite athletes preparing for the sport's biggest international events.

The Tournament Offers A Different Look At Wrestling's Best

The Senior World Championships feature men's freestyle, women's freestyle and Greco-Roman wrestling. Each discipline rewards different skills, giving fans a broader view of the sport than a traditional college dual meet.

Freestyle places a premium on exposure, leg attacks and quick scoring sequences. Women's freestyle showcases many of the same attacking and counterattacking exchanges, while Greco-Roman emphasizes upper-body wrestling and prohibits attacks below the waist.

For fans accustomed to Iowa's folkstyle schedule, the international tournament offers an opportunity to see familiar principles like pressure, positioning, conditioning and mat awareness applied under a different ruleset.

The event also provides a useful look at the level wrestlers must reach to contend for world and Olympic medals. College success is an important foundation, but international competition presents new opponents and tactical challenges.

Iowa's Wrestling Connections Extend Beyond The College Lineup

Lee and Blades are the most direct Iowa storylines on the U.S. roster, with both connected to the Hawkeye Wrestling Club. Their participation reflects the role of regional training centers in helping athletes continue their careers after college or develop toward senior-level international competition.

Lee's path from three-time NCAA champion to Olympic silver medalist is a familiar example of how a successful college wrestler can make the jump to the international stage. Blades' Olympic success provides another example of the high-level talent training in Iowa City.

Their performances can also give current and future Hawkeyes a look at what it takes to compete beyond the NCAA Championships. The transition requires technical adjustments, international experience and the ability to solve unfamiliar opponents under pressure.

The Senior World Championships Arrive Before College Wrestling Season

The timing makes the tournament an appealing early-season watch for Iowa fans. The international competition runs from Oct. 24 through Nov. 1, giving fans a chance to follow Lee and Blades before the college wrestling schedule takes center stage.

The U.S. team includes several other recognizable names, including Olympic and world medalists. The depth of the roster should make for a challenging tournament across all three styles, with athletes seeking world titles and valuable international experience.

For Iowa fans, Lee's pursuit of a senior world title is the headline, but Blades' return to international competition adds another compelling storyline. Both athletes represent the strength of Iowa City's wrestling community and the opportunities available to athletes beyond the college ranks.
