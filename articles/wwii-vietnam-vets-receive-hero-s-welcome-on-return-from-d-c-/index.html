WWII, Vietnam vets receive hero's welcome on return from D.C. Honor Flight

TheTools · 18.09.2026 10:53 · source · permalink
  • Eighteen veterans returning home from a just concluded Honor Flight to Washington D.C. received a hero's welcome in Pensacola.
  • The airport and the military town it serves showed up in force to greet the veterans, including 100-year old Willow Adams, whose soldiering career dates back to World War II
  • Those who traveled included nine U.S. Army veterans, three who served U.S. Air Force, five who served in the U.S. Navy and a single member of the Marine Corps.

Looking a bit like the weary travelers they doubtless were, but nonetheless exchanging waves and smiles with the hundreds who had come to greet them, 18 veterans returning home from an Honor Flight to Washington, D.C., received a hero's welcome.

The Pensacola International Airport and the military town it serves showed up in force to greet the veterans Sept. 17, mostly men and women of the Vietnam era, but also 100-year-old Willow Adams, whose soldiering career dates back to World War II.

They received a water cannon salute before they even left the plane, and as they entered the terminal, they were greeted by bagpipes, an honor guard and waving flags. It was the 20th such Honor Flight welcoming committee, one participant said, and some people had attended all of them.

Bobby Enfinger, who served in Vietnam in 1969-70, was returning from his rare second Honor Flight adventure to the nation's capital. Though he was a little anxious over some misplaced luggage (it was later located), Enfinger did let on that this trip was the more satisfying of the two.

"The last time, it rained the whole time," he said. "This time, it was beautiful both days."

All of the veterans in the group were wearing shirts bearing the words "Bobby Enfinger's Wiregrass Honor Flight Experience," and this honor apparently is what earned him his return trip to Washington. The Ozark, Alabama, resident has been a consistent donor to the Wiregrass Honor Flight Hub, according to hub president Travis Parker.

"It's not always a lot, but he's always there to help," Parker said. "That's why we thought Bobby should be this year's honoree."

Parker founded the Wiregrass Honor Flight Hub in 2022. It is one of more than 120 such hubs across the United States that help put these Honor Flights to Washington, D.C., together for veterans. The Honor Flight initiative was created to honor our nation’s veterans by bringing them to Washington, D.C., to visit the memorials built to commemorate their service and sacrifice.

A look at the Honor Flight National Hub website indicates flights are arriving and leaving the capital city every day.

Judi Temple, Judi with an "I" and Temple like Shirley, joined Parker at the Wiregrass Hub a year after it was founded. The two have found that their organizing and management skills coalesce nicely.

With a schedule to keep upon landing in Pensacola, Parker had time to give a quick rundown of what the veterans had been treated to in Washington, D.C.

The whirlwind visit included, but was not limited to, the United States Capitol, the Vietnam Wall memorial at night and the Lincoln Memorial.

The veterans visited the Iwo Jima Memorial, the National Museum of the U.S. Army, the World War II Memorial, memorials to veterans of Vietnam and Korea, the women's combat memorial and the still-under-construction Gulf War memorial, Parker said. There were also visits to the Air Force and Navy memorials and a stop by Arlington Cemetery for the changing of the guard.

For Donald Frel, a U.S. Navy veteran who served during the Vietnam War but never saw combat, there was no single travel visit that stood out over the others.

"There was so much to see," he said. "Pick anything, and it would be my favorite part of the trip."

Those who traveled included nine U.S. Army veterans, three who served U.S. Air Force, five who served with the U.S. Navy and a single member of the Marine Corps.

Attendees who served in the Army were Adams, James Frank Hughley, Thomas Gleland Prince, Wilbert D. Payne Jr., Melinda Early, Otis C. LeCompte, Richard William Barns, Robert Paul Norris and Robert Richard Green, who received two Purple Heart medals during his service in the Vietnam War.

The Air Force veterans were Gene Barrett, Jerome Louie Handley and Carl Michael Babski.

Those who had served in the Navy were James M. Levy, Marc Paul Blasetti, Michael Philip Domlin, Peter M. Sheff and Frel.

Bruce E. Gardner was the single U.S. Marine who made this Honor Flight trip.

Those attending the return of the Honor Flight included representatives from the Patriot Guard Riders, University of West Florida ROTC, Daughters of the American Revolution, Santa Rosa Pipes and Drums, Sons of the American Revolution Color Guard and the staff of the Pensacola International Airport.

The veterans who were honored traveled, as always, free of charge, thanks to generous donations from the Honor Flight Network.

The Honor Flight Network website states that the group initially focused its efforts solely on World War II-era veterans, but has since expanded its mission to include those who served during the Korean War and the Vietnam War eras.

The Honor Flight TLC program also honors veterans of any service era who are critically ill.
