Deal‑Maker vs Deal‑Finisher — Why Křetínský Looks Like the Future of West Ham

TheTools · 14.09.2026 07:30 · source · permalink

Amanda Staveley’s statement on Friday morning further signalled an intention to connect to West Ham fans, and send a signal to David Sullivan that her interest in the club is still very much live, even after failing to land Vanessa Gold’s shares.

Given her previous offer was for both Gold’s 25% holding and Sullivan’s shares, her actions confirmed at the very least her consortium has access to a minimum of £380m. Yet, to balance this out post the pre-emption rights, Daniel Kretinsky will have spent approximately £400m in total on acquiring shares at West Ham.

The key question on most supporters lips is quite simply “Where’s Staveley’s money coming from?”: And it’s a fair question. West Ham isn’t a cheap date and any serious involvement in the club demands long‑term financial muscle.

The mystery partner behind Staveley’s bid was revealed as Ashland Forest Capital Partners, a US‑based investment partnership led by Peter Bernick and Tom McCauley. They’re not Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, or any of the mega‑funds with billions ready to deploy,  but they do have decades of experience raising capital and turning underperforming assets around.

According to the firm’s latest form ADV data file registered with the SEC (source: Radient Analytics) Ashland Forest manages one active fund with a total Gross Asset Value of $30m. As such this suggests they raise money per transaction, assembling capital from their network depending on the deal size.

That means Staveley’s bid was potentially backed by a consortium of wealthy players and not a single mega‑investor writing a large cheque. A network of wealthy investors assembled by Ashland Forest Capital Partners is not a giant private‑equity fund.

The other concern would be whether the monies are part of an LBO (Leveraged Buy Out) which is allowed in the Championship yet not in the Premier League.

This would be unlikely and highly impractical as SCR is affected  – given LBO loads a club with debt and the high interest payments would cripple West Ham’s day to day revenue, leaving no money to spend on players without breaching the SCR Cap. There are also regulatory hurdles.

Would West Ham Be Loaded With Debt?

Supporters only have to look no further than Burnley’s US takeover which has reportedly left the club loaded with debt.

To allay any fears, Staveley would need to prove that she has the money, and this would have been the case regarding the previous offer to Vanessa Gold. Any purchase of West Ham shares requires proof of funds(POF) so Staveley’s consortium would need to demonstrate they have the capital available or committed.

This includes bank statements, investor commitment letters, escrow arrangements and legal confirmations from capital partners. Regulators will also require source‑of‑funds verification, anti‑money‑laundering checks and the relevant fit‑and‑proper ownership assessments.

Existing shareholders, notably Daniel Křetínský has the right to match any potential future offers made to David Sullivan. This is exactly what happened with Vanessa Gold’s shares where Křetínský exercised his rights along with other shareholders which effectively blocked Staveley’s purchase.

Set to be the largest shareholder, Kretinsky blocked Staveley’s bid to buy a majority shareholding

So even if Staveley had the money ready, she still needed shareholder approval  and she didn’t get it. Staveley is a deal‑maker, not a billionaire. She assembles capital from partners rather than deploying her own wealth. Many fans and insiders remain unconvinced Staveley’s consortium has the long‑term financial firepower to compete.

Too many people are beguiled by her involvement in the Manchester City and Newcastle United takeovers, and I’m very wary of the assumption that the same blueprint can be replicated simply by buying out Sullivan’s stake if he even decides to sell.

For that reason, I’m pinning my hopes on Daniel Kretinsky as the key to moving the club forward. For those saying he’s not a footballing person, look no further than the developments at Sparta Prague. He has working capital and is proven in the business world. A layer of investors via Stavely doesn’t inspire confidence.

Sullivan will sell eventually; it will happen regardless of suggestions that he will keep his stake for his sons, yet under Křetínský as the lead share holder I feel more confident, and hope others are too. Remember Křetínský‘s silence isn’t necessarily a bad thing. Lions don’t need to roar, while those who shout seek attention, and those who stay quiet already have it!

I have been a season ticket holder since the late eighties, so experienced the highs and lows of being a West Ham supporter. I previously wrote for OLAS and have contributed to a number of football publications in the past.
