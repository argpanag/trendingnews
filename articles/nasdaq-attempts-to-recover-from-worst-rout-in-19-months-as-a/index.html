Nasdaq attempts to recover from worst rout in 19 months as AI stocks see some buying: Live updates

TheTools · 05.11.2026 23:03 · source · permalink

The Nasdaq Composite rebounded on Friday as some investors bought the dip in chip stocks following a punishing sell-off that sent the index into correction territory.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq bounced by 1%. The S&P 500 rose 0.5%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average traded down 96 points, or 0.2%.

Worries of a potential slowdown in data center construction following big Democratic wins in the House and Senate and key state governorships hit U.S. markets hard on Thursday.

The heavy selling Thursday sent the three major stock averages to their worst one-day losses since April 2025. The Nasdaq Composite tumbled 5.5%, closing more than 10% below its most recent high and putting it in what Wall Street regards as a correction. The broad-market S&P 500 lost 4.4%, while the Dow fell more than 1,800 points, or 3.4%.

All 11 sectors in the S&P 500 closed lower, but shares of companies tied to the AI build-out led the decline.

During Friday's session, shares of Micron Technology were up 5% following a 17% decline on Thursday. Nvidia was up 4% after a 9% rout the day before and moved back into positive territory for the year.

"The scale of the falls in some of the market's biggest names has been striking. But it also highlights the concentration risk that has built up in U.S. equity markets," Stuart Clark, portfolio manager at Quilter, said by email.

"With a relatively small number of technology and semiconductor stocks now carrying so much weight, weakness in those names quickly translates into sizeable falls for the Nasdaq and S&P 500."

The "Magnificent Seven" stocks shed $1 trillion in total market capitalization on Thursday alone. Along with Nvidia, Tesla, Meta Platforms and Alphabet saw gains Friday.

Top Democrats have called for the curbing of the data center buildout since the party's big election win. Governor-Elect Gina Hinojosa of Texas — a key data center hub in the country — said in a CNN interview Thursday evening, "We will have laws to protect Texans, and we need to stop what we are doing with data centers until we have those protections."

Investors' fears will likely persist given that Hinojosa and others are not slated to take office until January, according to Chris Grisanti of MAI Capital Management. However, the chief market strategist is still optimistic on AI, as he believes that the deals related to the buildout will eventually get done.

"While there's definitely more of a headwind now after the election, this is an industrial revolution akin to railroads, akin to internet, and it's not going to stop," he said. "I don't think we're at the bottom, but I think you can see it from here."

Investors were also reacting to the latest nonfarm payrolls report, which came in much better than expected at an increase of 165,000 in October. That was well above the 59,000 that economists polled by Dow Jones had anticipated. The unemployment rate also moved down to 4.1%. Economists had expected it to hold steady at 4.2%.

All three major averages are poised for losses on the week, even after Wednesday's big rally in the wake of the election. The Nasdaq is off by almost 3% and the S&P 500 by 2%. The 30-stock Dow is the outperformer, lower by less than 1% week to date.

Looking at the history of Nasdaq corrections shows tech stocks often find their footing over the next three-to-six months, but trading can remain rocky in the near term.

Consumers grew a bit more optimistic about the economy heading into the midterm election, according to a University of Michigan survey released Friday.

The Survey of Consumers for November showed a reading of 51.0, up 6.3% from a month ago and better than the Dow Jones consensus for 47.5. The reading was flat compared to a year ago. Current conditions and expectations indexes also posted monthly gains, up 9.2% and 4.3% respectively.

The survey period covered Oct. 20-Nov. 2, just before the Tuesday vote.

On inflation, the one-year outlook slipped to a 4.3% rate, down 0.4 percentage point from October. The five-year view edged lower to 3.4%.

"While sentiment lifted for lower- and middle-wealth consumers, it fell 5% for those with the largest stock portfolios in the wake of a relatively turbulent period in stock markets," survey director Joanne Hsu said. "Moreover, consumers' comments revealed growing anxiety over a possible AI bubble. AI worries, however, were dwarfed by cost of living concerns, which continue to be at the forefront of consumers' minds across the population."

The U.S. economy isn't expected to have produced much job growth in October, with employers reluctant to hire workers but also not cutting staff amid a shrinking labor force and a growing problem of long-term unemployment.

Economists are looking for a meager nonfarm payrolls gain of 59,000, a slight uptick from the 29,000 jobs produced in September, according to the Dow Jones consensus. That result would continue a pattern of sparse growth.

But that's kind of OK: The forecast also is that the unemployment rate will hold at a very low 4.2% rate, the product of a climate where a stagnant labor force reduces the need for outsized job increases to keep the jobless level steady.

"Long-term, we're not going to need as many jobs added to the economy to have stable employment because we have fewer people who are available to work," said Nicole Bachaud, labor economist at jobs site ZipRecruiter. "An aging workforce is pushing a lot of people into retirement. We have had decades of low birth rates, meaning we don't have a lot of people who are coming in to replace those retiring workers. And immigration is now down pretty significantly, which is reducing some of that prime-age employment."

The sharp sell-off in AI-related names this week reflects an adjustment in growth expectations to a more sustainable level, according to William de Gale, co-founder and lead portfolio manager at Blue Box Asset Management — but it does not change "insatiable" levels of demand.

De Gale told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" that the technology sector had been delivering consistent profits and stock price growth of around 15% for nearly two decades. This was connected to a host of supply constraints, including in semiconductor equipment, raw materials, energy, staffing and permitting, which cap the growth rate, he said.

"AI has been able to grow a lot faster than that in recent years because it's been a lot smaller. It's a subset of the tech sector by and large, but eventually it becomes big enough that it runs into that overall constraint, and I think that's really what's happening now," de Gale said.

"The industry seems to be ahead of what expectations are, ahead of what is sustainable, and at the moment, all the models have 30% top line growth in them, going many years into the future, and nothing that big can grow that fast very long, so those models all need to come down to 15, to 20%, and that's the adjustment that's happening at the moment."

"I don't think there's any real issue with demand. The demand is insatiable, and the demand outside AI is pretty much insatiable as well."

Germany's Daimler Truck reported an 89% year-on-year increase in earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT) to 1.05 billion euros ($1.18 billion).

North American truck sales jumped 51%, which the company attributed to both a continued market recovery and the impact of tariff uncertainty in the same quarter last year. The North American market for heavy-duty trucks fell 7% in the first three quarters, but was offset by 8% growth in Europe.

Daimler, one of the world's biggest truck manufacturers, flagged ongoing market challenges, including slowing growth, rising energy costs and uncertainty in the global economy.

CFO Eva Scherer said the company showed "discipline in converting performance into cash" in the quarter and was "determined to maintain that discipline."
