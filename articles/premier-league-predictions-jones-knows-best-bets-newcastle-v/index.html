Liverpool vs Manchester United, Sunday 4.30pm, live on Sky Sports - PLAY SUPER 6!

Anfield. Liverpool. Manchester United. Maurico Pochettino making his Premier League return. This is a fixture with enough history, pressure and emotional baggage to turn an ordinary Sunday afternoon into complete bedlam.

Pochettino has walked into a mess at United though. A team find themselves in the relegation zone at this stage of a season for the first time in 40 years. A footballing catastrophe for a club of their stature.

The defensive numbers are particularly frightening. United have shipped 30 goals in just 15 matches across all competitions this season. Only Lens (31) have conceded more among teams in Europe's major leagues.

And here's the problem for Pochettino. You can't fix that level of dysfunction with a few training sessions and a motivational team talk. Not with the same personnel, anyway.

Liverpool should smell blood.

Pochettino's first priority surely has to be making United harder to beat, which could mean sacrificing attacking ambition to protect a defence desperately short of confidence.

That points towards a relatively controlled Liverpool victory rather than an Anfield demolition job.

Brentford vs Everton, Monday 8pm, live on Sky Sports

This looks a proper Monday Night Football arm wrestle. Two sides who are comfortable in their own skin, two managers who understand the value of defensive organisation and a fixture where the first goal could completely change the tactical landscape. It's classic Premier League where the margins are so fine and trying to dig out a prediction on the outright market is a tough task.

Goals it is then.

The numbers behind this particular scheduling sweet spot are rather tasty.

The last 15 Monday Night Football fixtures played immediately after an international break have averaged 3.1 goals per game. The latest instalment, Coventry's meeting with Newcastle, served up five goals for our entertainment.

Coincidence? Perhaps. But there's a theory worth exploring here.

Unlike the dreaded Saturday lunchtime slot, where managers are often scrambling to get their international players back into shape, Monday night offers an extra couple of days on the training ground. More preparation time, sharper attacking patterns and potentially more fluid football.

And when Brentford are involved at home, the goals argument gets even stronger. Their last 85 home matches have averaged exactly three goals per game. That's a substantial sample size and one that tells you plenty about the type of football we're likely to see.

Everton should have their moments, too. The market is offering Evens on both teams to score and over 2.5 goals with Sky Bet and the 2-2 correct score at 10/1 also worth a nibble.
