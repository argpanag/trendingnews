Possible measles exposure reported at the NICU at CHOP in Philadelphia, officials say

TheTools · 22.09.2026 21:52 · source · permalink

Health officials say that a possible measles exposure was reported in the NICU at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia last week.

Philadelphia Dept. of Health officials encourage all residents to get the measles, mumps, and rubella vaccine. NBC10’s Siobhan McGirl has the latest. 

A possible measles exposure was reported at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia last week, according to the city's Department of Public Health.

Officials said that the possible exposure happened at the CHOP building located at 3401 Civic Center Boulevard on Friday, Sept. 11, at various times throughout the day including:

  • Between 9:30 a.m. and 4:15 p.m. at NICU West
  • Between 9:30 a.m. and 4:15 p.m. at NICU West Family Lounge
  • Between 9:30 a.m. and 4:15 p.m. at the CHOP Atrium
  • Between 12:05 p.m. and 2:45 p.m. at the CHOP Main Cafeteria

Officials are working to make sure that all infants in CHOP's NICU get a "preventative antibody product" that will help prevent them from contracting measles and spreading it.

Symptoms of measles include:

  • fever
  • runny nose
  • cough
  • red, watery eyes
  • rash

For some who get measles, it could be serious and lead to pneumonia, brain infection and death.

So far in 2026, the Department of Health has reported four measles-related deaths and confirmed 767 measles cases in 38 counties in Pennsylvania.

The best protection against the measles is to get fully vaccinated. Health officials said the MMR vaccine – which is administered over two doses – boosts a person’s immune system and provides 97% lifetime protection.

"Given the ongoing outbreaks in Pennsylvania and other places, it is imperative that everyone who is not up to date on their MMR vaccine gets their vaccine as soon as possible. Remember, this is not just about protecting yourself, it’s about protecting our most vulnerable Philadelphians," Health Commissioner Dr. Palak Raval-Nelson said.

In the last month, more than 40,000 doses of the MMR vaccine have been administered in Pennsylvania, as the Commonwealth responds to the measles outbreak.

“Babies who are under 1 year old can’t be vaccinated. So our getting vaccinated and having everyone around the child get vaccinated helps protect them" said Communications Director of the Philadelphia Department of Health.

If you believe you were exposed to the measles or are experiencing symptoms, contact your health care provider immediately or call the DOH toll-free hotline at 877-PA-HEALTH (877-724-3258).
