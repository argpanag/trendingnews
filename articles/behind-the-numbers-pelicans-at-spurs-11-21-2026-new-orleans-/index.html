Behind the Numbers: Pelicans at Spurs (11/21/2026) | New Orleans Pelicans

TheTools · 22.11.2026 02:58 · source · permalink

A look at three key numbers related to Saturday’s game in Frost Bank Center between New Orleans and San Antonio (7 p.m. Central, GCSEN on TV; WWL on radio):

1:In four head-to-head meetings during the 2025-26 regular season, San Antonio beat New Orleans three times, but only outscored its Southwest Division counterpart by a single point in regulation (the Spurs notched a four-point overtime victory in the Pelicans’ Oct. 24 home opener, upping their total advantage to five points). Despite San Antonio eventually capturing the 2026 Western Conference title in the spring and the Spurs winning 36 more regular season games than the Pelicans, all four matchups last season were highly competitive.

New Orleans was also responsible for one of San Antonio’s eight home defeats, posting a 104-95 verdict in January. Individually, Trey Murphy was superb vs. the Spurs, averaging 28.5 points over his four games and shooting 53 percent from the field. Derik Queen averaged 16.3 points, 6.3 rebounds and 5.0 assists, highlighted by a 30-point triple-double (with four blocks) in a narrow 135-132 home loss to the silver and black. In two appearances, Zion Williamson averaged a double-double (25.5 ppg, 10.0 rpg).

39.4, 37.5, 39.3, 43.6: New Orleans three-point shooting percentages over its last four games, respectively. Are the Pelicans breaking out of their early-season slump from beyond the arc? Week 5 of the NBA schedule has been by far the club’s most effective stretch of perimeter accuracy – prior to this four-game span, New Orleans had not shot above league average (35.4 in ’26-27, via Basketball Reference) on deep shots for more than two straight games (the only instance of doing it twice yielded consecutive wins vs. Atlanta, at Memphis). The Pelicans also set their season high for most three-point makes in a game Friday by going 17/39 in Oklahoma City. New Orleans was the league’s third-worst three-point shooting club percentage-wise (29.0) during its 0-4 start, but since then has been ranked near the middle of the pack (16th at 36.1).

40.7: Speaking of improvements from downtown, Murphy made his return from injury to the New Orleans lineup Friday (as a reserve for the first time this season) and shot 3/8 from three-point range. Over his last four appearances, dating back to the start of November, he’s gone 11/27 on treys, which is 40.7 percent, By his standards, Murphy is off to an OK start at his specialty – shooting 37.7 percent beyond the arc in 10 total games this season – but he’s also been in uniform for New Orleans’ most impressive victories, highlighted by 18 points and five assists to help knock off unbeaten Memphis on the Grizzlies’ home floor. Unfortunately, Murphy won't play Saturday in this back-to-back game, with the team listing him as out in its afternoon injury report, due to injury maintenance for bilateral patellar tendinopathy.
