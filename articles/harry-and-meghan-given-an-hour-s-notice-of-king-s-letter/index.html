Harry and Meghan given an hour’s notice of King’s letter

TheTools · 11.09.2026 21:38 · source · permalink

Duke of Sussex is thought to be frustrated that the letter was shared publicly without consultation

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex were given one hour’s notice of the King’s letter clarifying their status.

The letter, issued to senior military and government officials by the Lord Chamberlain on behalf of the King, confirmed that the Sussexes should be treated as “private citizens” on their return to the UK, that they could not use their “HRH” titles, and that any charity work they undertook would not be on behalf of the Royal family.

It came before a crunch meeting of the Home Office committee responsible for making decisions about royal security, which is expected to take place this week.

The Duke is thought to be frustrated that such a letter, describing him and his wife as “private citizens”, was sent out without any consultation.

It is said to have been shared with the Sussexes’ office at 1.59pm on Monday. Exactly 20 minutes later, at 2.19pm, their staff responded, asking whether the guidance had already been sent out.

They wanted time to alert the Duke and Duchess before it was made public, and were also keen to “clarify one or two points”.

At 3.09pm, aides managed to get hold of the Duke, who had been in a meeting, to inform him that a letter had been drafted and that they were seeking guidance on whether it had been distributed.

Unbeknown to them, the letter had been shared with the media three minutes earlier, at 3.06pm. The aides were told this at 3.11pm.

Buckingham Palace sources, who insisted there was “nothing controversial or new” in the letter, queried why the Duke and Duchess should need to be consulted. The notion that they might wish to suggest edits was roundly dismissed.

The letter, the Palace said, was in full accordance with the Sussex family’s stated wishes to remain private citizens while in the UK, and was in keeping with the so-called “Sandringham summit” agreement made in 2020.

“It is merely making clear the continued position for the benefit of all parties,” one source said.

However, the Duke is understood to have taken particular issue with the notion that the couple were to be treated as private citizens. If that were the case, it was claimed, such formal guidance would not be required.

The Sussexes prefer the term “public figures”, and would have liked an acknowledgement that, on a personal level, they remained members of the Royal family, despite no longer receiving public funding.

Concerns have previously been raised that the timing of the announcement was deliberate, and was part of an effort to influence the Executive Committee for the Protection of Royalty and Public Figures as it prepared to discuss whether the couple should be granted police protection.

Palace sources strongly rejected that claim.

However, the tension caused by a letter that, on the face of it, simply reasserts the status quo, demonstrates the level of mistrust between the two sides.

The letter confirms that the terms of the 2020 Sandringham summit remain in place. It also suggests, in clear, formal language, that the King will allow no gradual softening of this agreement.

The Sandringham summit – a “family discussion” about the Sussexes’ future – was convened by Elizabeth II, and was held in the Long Library on the Norfolk estate in January 2020. It was attended by the late Queen, the then Prince Charles, Princes William and Harry, and their respective private secretaries.

Relations between the Duke and the rest of the family were already at an all-time low. The meeting took place only days after Sussexes announced their plans to become “financially independent” and to divide their time between Britain and the United States while also representing the Royal family.

The Palace contingent made clear that the “half in, half out” model was not an option. The Duke was told that it was simply not possible for a member of the Royal family to pursue private business interests while also representing the Queen on matters of state.

The Duke would later reveal in his memoir, Spare, that he had been presented with five options, but claimed that the Palace had only ever prepared for the fifth, which involved “full severance, no royal role, no working for Granny [the late Queen], and total loss of security”.

Five days after the meeting, Buckingham Palace issued a seismic statement declaring that the Duke and Duchess would no longer use their HRH titles or formally represent the late Queen.

It laid bare the magnitude of the couple’s split from the Royal family, making clear that they had not been allowed to adopt the “half in, half out” approach.

It stated that the couple would repay £2.4m of taxpayer’s money that had been used to refurbish the marital home, Frogmore Cottage, and would pay commercial rent on the property. They would not receive public funds for royal duties, and the Duke was to be relieved of his much-cherished official military appointments.

An update on the couple’s newly launched Sussex Royal website appeared to acknowledge that the plans the Duke and Duchess had declared some 10 days earlier would have to be amended.
