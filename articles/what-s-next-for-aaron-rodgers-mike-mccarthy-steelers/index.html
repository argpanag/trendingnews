What's next for Aaron Rodgers, Mike McCarthy, Steelers?

TheTools · 26.11.2026 14:54 · source · permalink

The Pittsburgh Steelers went from a 6-4 team vying for playoff contention with a four-time MVP quarterback under center Tuesday to a team entering its Week 12 game Friday against the Denver Broncos reeling from controversy and now without Aaron Rodgers on its roster.

Pittsburgh waived Rodgers after the quarterback said Wednesday on "The Pat McAfee Show" that he wouldn't return to the team after his stunning benching Tuesday night. It was a scorched-earth interview that included allegations that coach Mike McCarthy has been sleeping through team meetings, among other embarrassing details.

Rodgers said he found out from teammate Pat Freiermuth that he was being benched and still hasn't heard the news from McCarthy. The veteran quarterback and the coach's partnership goes back to when they were together in Green Bay as the Packers won Super Bowl XLV and extended to McCarthy being fired eight years later in 2018.

Turning 43 on Sunday, Rodgers had known limitations entering the 2026 season. Though his completion percentage (65.7%) in his first season in Pittsburgh was his highest since 2021 (with the Packers), his 3.7 air yards per completion were the lowest in the NFL. Rodgers' 3.9 air yards per completion this season were the third lowest in the past 20 years.

The Steelers had a 19.7% chance of winning the AFC North entering Week 10. But after back-to-back losses, they're down to roughly 5%. If Pittsburgh beats Denver, the Steelers' chances of making it to the playoffs will grow to 92%, but a loss reduces those odds to 82%, per ESPN Analytics.

Could Rodgers finish out his 22nd season in the league with another team? What does this move mean for the Steelers' offense moving forward? As the story out of Pittsburgh and from Rodgers continues to evolve, ESPN's NFL insiders tackle the most pressing questions on everything they know as of Wednesday afternoon.

What were some of the signs that happened in Pittsburgh for this divorce to escalate so quickly?

This past year has been a whirlwind of change for the Steelers organization. McCarthy, a then 62-year-old Pittsburgh native, was hired in January to become just the Steelers' fourth head coach since 1969. The news of McCarthy's hire came 11 days after Mike Tomlin told the team he was walking away from the role after 19 seasons.

Rodgers even "vouched" for McCarthy to general manager Omar Khan.

And then the Steelers brought Rodgers back because the organization believed the four-time MVP was their best option to contend in the AFC, and they inked him to a one-year deal worth up to $25 million after another prolonged offseason courtship. Rodgers previously told reporters that he will retire after this season.

Flash forward to this year's training camp and that's when the problems started, Rodgers alleged Wednesday to ESPN's Pat McAfee.

Rodgers said it's a "delicate situation because the coaching staff is worried about their job security and the players are worried about their money and their job security. And I don't. I've made money.

"And again, I'll reiterate, originally I'm talking about in training camp when this stuff was going on, when he was missing meetings and the word was he's sleeping, and then he's sleeping in the coaches' film sessions and he's not alert here. And he doesn't want to get to know the defense. I'm telling the defense, 'Hey, just trust me. You just got to give him time. He'll be fine.' And then he's telling me I don't need to get to know the defense at all," Rodgers said.

There was also an eye-raising occurrence between the pair that happened during the Steelers' Week 4 loss to the Cleveland Browns on Oct. 1. Cameras caught Rodgers shouting toward the sideline and McCarthy appearing to mouth a profanity in response. -- Brooke Pryor and ESPN staff

How was Rodgers playing before his release?

Not well. Rodgers had the sixth-fastest time to throw in the league (2.85 seconds) with an astonishing 35% of his pass attempts not getting beyond the line of scrimmage (second in the league).

He had the fourth-lowest success rate on pressured dropbacks and the fourth-lowest on-target rate on all dropbacks. He had disagreements with his receivers, as has been the case in recent years, and he was throwing low-probability heaves downfield to make a point when his pass catchers failed to separate. He saw the highest rate of man coverage this season (38.8%) of any quarterback of the past five seasons in large part because teams knew he wasn't accurate enough downfield to punish them for it.

Rodgers' play was good enough -- he threw for 2,241 yards (17th in NFL) with 16 touchdowns (tied for 13th) -- that the Steelers could win six games despite his limitations. If this were only a football issue and not an interpersonal issue as well, he would not have been benched. But it is, and he was. -- Ben Solak

If Rodgers is interested in playing again this season, which teams could make sense as fits?

Rodgers' play this season hasn't been stellar. He just publicly torched his coach for 40 minutes on "The Pat McAfee Show" and would hijack any offense he takes over. He told McAfee that he demanded McCarthy relinquish playcalling or he wanted out of Pittsburgh.

While there are teams with needs -- think of the Patriots, who just lost Drake Maye, and the Eagles, playing without Jalen Hurts -- it's hard to imagine any expressing interest. Yes, even at a minimum salary. The production (or lack thereof) does not offset the headache.

Perhaps the rival Ravens, who play Pittsburgh twice down the stretch, would kick the tires, but that seems unlikely at this point.

"Teams desperate on the playoff bubble, maybe, but it's going to be hard after watching him go scorched earth on people and an organization like that," an AFC team executive told ESPN. -- Jeremy Fowler

What are the Steelers' cap ramifications for releasing the veteran QB?

Likely none. Rodgers signed a simple one-year, $22,502,750 contract with the Steelers. Of that, $500,000 was a roster bonus (already paid), $2,750 was a workout bonus (already paid) and the remaining $22 million was a fully guaranteed salary, of which Rodgers has already made $13.44 million because he has been paid for the first 11 weeks of the 18-week regular season. Per the terms of the contract, the Steelers have to pay Rodgers the remaining $8.56 million because the salary was guaranteed, and his $22,502,750 cap number for 2026 will be unaffected.

Given Rodgers' public comments, the Steelers could pursue a grievance against him for conduct detrimental to the team and try to get out of the remaining guaranteed money. If they do that and succeed, they will get the remaining $8.56 million refunded to them in the form of a salary cap credit at the end of this season. Remember, cap room can be carried forward from one year to the next. -- Dan Graziano

What does this QB move mean for the Steelers' offense going forward?

It means a couple of things.

The first is that the Steelers are likely to run the ball more. Pittsburgh had the sixth-highest pass rate of all offenses this season (64%) and easily the highest of all offenses inside the 10-yard line (74%) -- in large part because Rodgers would audible the offense out of running plays and into his preferred dropbacks. This used to be one of his superpowers, but as defenses have improved disguises, Rodgers regularly hurt the offense by checking the unit into plays that didn't work against a disguised look post-snap. The Steelers' best avenue to team success -- both with and without Rodgers -- is leaning on their running backs (Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle) and defense to win low-scoring affairs. That will be the path going forward.

The second: The Steelers are going to try to access the middle of the field more in the passing game. Only two quarterbacks threw less frequently to the intermediate range of the field (10-19 yards) than Rodgers because of how often he checked down quickly and how often he threw up prayerful heaves against one-on-ones. Mason Rudolph should have the arm and experience necessary to throw between the levels of the defense and create a more balanced passing attack. -- Solak

Is there legitimacy to Rodgers' claims about McCarthy's past issues while with the Dallas Cowboys and Packers?

McCarthy and Rodgers had a tense relationship during McCarthy's last season with the Packers in 2018, and I reported a story that went deep on their falling-out. That season, a source close to Rodgers told me that because McCarthy had other duties as the head coach, he regularly missed several offensive install meetings or quarterback meetings, which became a source of frustration for Rodgers because McCarthy would add or change plays during the Saturday walk-throughs that messed with the established flow of the game plan.

"That drives Aaron crazy," the source close to Rodgers told me in 2018. "Especially over the years, [McCarthy] is not as present as he should be in meetings and all the game-planning."

A former Green Bay staffer told me after Rodgers appeared on "The Pat McAfee Show" on Wednesday that he remembers conversations with Rodgers in 2017 and 2018 in which the quarterback told him that McCarthy was missing meetings or falling asleep in them. The former Green Bay staffer said that based on those conversations, they were surprised that Rodgers signed back with Pittsburgh when the club hired McCarthy.

Rodgers told McAfee that McCarthy doesn't watch as much film as he should as a playcaller. "Multiple times he said he hasn't watched the film when I've talked to him on Tuesdays," Rodgers said Wednesday. "He hasn't seen the film yet, hasn't seen the film yet. I don't know how that's possible on a Tuesday after you play on a Sunday."

During McCarthy's first gap year in between jobs -- 2019 -- he set up a film lab in a barn to study football in depth with other retired and out-of-work coaches. In his introductory news conference in Dallas, McCarthy said he told Cowboys owner and general manager Jerry Jones during the interview process that he watched every play of the 2019 NFL season, but when asked about this during the same event, McCarthy admitted he didn't actually do so. "I wanted the job," he said. -- Kalyn Kahler
