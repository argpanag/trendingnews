Mammoth look to derail surging Predators

TheTools · 27.11.2026 17:57 · source · permalink

Fresh off halting Minnesota's 15-game point streak, the visitors bring the league's third-ranked power play into a matchup with Nashville's second-rated penalty kill.

Coming off perhaps their most impressive victory of the season, the Utah Mammoth look to cool off another hot team when they visit the Nashville Predators on Friday.

Utah's 4-3 road win over the Minnesota Wild on Wednesday ended Minnesota's 15-game (12-0-3) points streak. The Mammoth never trailed, but had to survive a late push from the Wild. Blake Coleman scored for Minnesota 15:56 into the third period, but the Mammoth prevented an equalizer despite playing short-handed for the final 3:45 of play.

In the aftermath of a six-game winless drought (0-3-3) earlier this month, the Mammoth have bounced back to go 3-1-0 in their last four games.

The Mammoth's 321 penalty minutes are the second-most in the NHL, with 28 minutes assessed on Wednesday. Utah has struggled to kill penalties for much of the season, but the penalty-kill unit is 12-for-13 in their last four games, including a 5-for-6 performance against the Wild.

Forward Lawson Crouse felt Wednesday's game had "a lot of emotion and (we were) just embracing it, using it, but not getting caught up in it. ... Obviously you don't want to be short-handed but those are big moments. Everyone embraces those on our team and everyone stepped up to the challenge."

Utah's penalty killers might have more opportunity for success Friday since the Predators are only 1-for-19 on the power play over their last seven games.

The other side of the special-teams battle features two elite units. Nashville's 89.2% (58-for-65) penalty-kill percentage is the second-best in the NHL, while Utah's 32.2% (28-for-87) power-play percentage ranks third in the league.

After ending the Wild's points streak, the Mammoth now visit the Predators who are 9-2-0 in their last 11 games. The Predators are coming off a big Central Division win of their own in Wednesday's 3-1 victory over the Dallas Stars.

"I thought we played a really good road game against a really good team. ... I thought we caused some problems in the offensive zone by getting pressure and a little bit of the identity of what our team looks like. I thought it painted a really good picture," Predators coach Andrew Brunette said.

In another disciplined performance for the club, Nashville logged only two penalty minutes against Dallas. The Predators' 163 penalty minutes are the second-fewest in the NHL.

Filip Forsberg leads the Predators with 26 points (nine goals, 17 assists). Steven Stamkos leads the club with 12 goals, and Roman Josi leads with 22 assists.

The Predators face the Detroit Red Wings on Saturday, so Juuse Saros and Justus Annunen are expected to split the back-to-back starts. Saros is likelier to face division rivals Utah, and the veteran has been in good form with a 6-1-0 record, 1.71 goals-against average, and .940 save percentage in his last seven games.

The Mammoth also have a back-to-back looming with a game in Dallas on Saturday, so goalies Karel Vejmelka and Sebastian Cossa will likely split the two starts in some order.

Clayton Keller leads Utah in assists (24) and points (29). Logan Cooley leads the club with 13 goals.

Cooley and Vincent Trocheck each have ongoing five-game point streaks. Cooley has seven points (two goals, five assists) during his streak, while Trocheck has six points (three goals, three assists) in his last five games.
