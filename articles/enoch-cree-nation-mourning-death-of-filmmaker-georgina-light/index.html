Hit musical Bear Grease co-creator Crystle Lightning confirmed her mother Georgina Lightning died over the weekend in a social media post.

“Today, she made her way to the spirit world and I am still in shock,” she wrote. “I have lost my mother, my son’s Kokhum, my matriarch, my role model, my confident.

“That’s all I can say right now. I’m wrecked.”

Georgina Lighting was a prominent writer, director and actress known for works such as Older Than America,Johnny Greyeyes and DreamKeeper. She was 63 years old and was the first North American Indigenous woman to direct a major feature film. Three projects of hers are still pending release —  as an actress in a made-for-TV-movie called Watcher and a film called A Life Less Empty and as executive producer of Fantasies of Flying. She was a registered member of Samson Cree Nation.

In addition to her own film making work, she was a strong supporter of Indigenous media arts and co-founded Tribal Alliance Productions, which supports Indigenous storytellers in all forms of media production. She also helped establish the Native Media Network, which helps First Nation artists establish themselves in film and media industries. She was awarded the White House Project’s Epic Award for Emerging Artist in 2010. She also has made appearances on prominent television shows The West Wing and Walker: Texas Ranger and lended her vocal talents to the Disney animated feature Pocahontas 2: Journey to a New World and the video game, What’s My Story?

Enoch Cree Nation announced in a statement on Saturday that the nation lost a community member in an incident during Friday evening’s Indian Relay at the Enoch Rodeo Grounds.

“Chief and council extend our most sincere condolences to the family,” reads the statement. “Our community is grieving, and we are holding them in our thoughts and prayers.

“The nation is working with the appropriate authorities as they conduct their investigation, and we will support that process fully. Out of respect, we will not be speculating on the circumstances.”

RCMP media relations officer Cpl. Mathew Howell told Postmedia a woman was standing in the media area when a horse left the track and struck her. She was later pronounced dead by emergency medical services.

Occupational Health and Safety is conducting an investigation with RCMP support.

Enock Cree Nation spokesperson Erin Stroud said the nation would not be commenting to ensure the integrity of the investigation.

“Our thoughts and deepest condolences are with the family, friends and colleagues of the individual who died,” she said. “Further information will be provided when it is appropriate and when doing so will not interfere with the investigation.”
