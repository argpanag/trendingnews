Philadelphia Semiconductor Index Plunges 3.83%, Triggering Catch-Up Selloff; Taiwan Stocks Open Down More Than 860 Points, Testing 46,200 — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 19.10.2026 02:05 · source · permalink

Technology stocks on Wall Street came under an all-out selloff on Friday (the 16th), with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunging 3.83% in a single session to mark its eighth consecutive losing day and its longest streak since 2008. Taiwan index futures also plunged 1,144 points in overnight trading, leaving Taiwan stocks facing heavy catch-up selling pressure at the open on Monday (the 19th). The Taiwan Weighted Index opened at 46,844.64, down 266.62 points, then fell sharply as electronics, financial and traditional industrial shares all sold off, touching an intraday low of 46,243.55, a drop of more than 860 points. The index is now fighting to defend the 46,000 level and testing its quarterly moving average. The TPEx Index, which tracks small and mid-cap stocks, fell more than 3%, the electronics sub-index dropped about 2%, and the semiconductor sub-index fell 1.82%.

Market heavyweight Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (2330.TW) opened at NT$2,395 and fell to an early low of NT$2,390, down NT$30 or 1.24%, losing the NT$2,400 round-number level. Other large-cap electronics shares also weakened: United Microelectronics (2303.TW) fell more than 4% to an early low of NT$126.5; Hon Hai (2317.TW) touched an early low of NT$239.5, down 1.84%; MediaTek (2454.TW) fell more than 2%, and ASE Technology Holding (3711.TW) also dropped more than 2%. Delta Electronics (2308.TW) fell more than 2% at the open before briefly turning positive, with choppy early trading.

Memory stocks were the hardest hit, with Nanya Technology (2408.TW) and Winbond Electronics (2344.TW) each falling more than 5% in early trade to lows of NT$418.5 and NT$151, respectively. Macronix International (2337.TW) fell 5% to 6%, Powerchip Semiconductor Manufacturing (6770.TW) dropped by half a daily limit, and its affiliate AP Memory (6531.TW) plunged more than 7%. The two passive-component leaders were also weak, with Walsin Technology (2492.TW) down more than 7% and Yageo (2327.TW) down 6%. Testing and packaging firms King Yuan Electronics (2449.TW) and Powertech Technology (6239.TW) fell more than 4% to 5%. Elite Material (2383.TW) and Unimicron Technology (3037.TW) each fell about 4% in early trade before paring losses.

Among high-priced shares, King Slide Works (2059.TW) extended its decline and fell below the NT$10,000 mark, while Jentech Precision Industrial (3653.TW) and Largan Precision (3008.TW) each fell about half a daily limit. Lotes (3533.TW) bucked the trend and held in positive territory. Fiberglass cloth-related Taiwan Glass Ind. (1802.TW) and Nan Ya Plastics (1303.TW) rose against the trend, and the three major telecom operators remained a safe haven for funds amid the broader market weakness. Market turnover was estimated to shrink to about NT$780 billion to NT$790 billion.

The source of the selling pressure was Wall Street. On Friday, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 175.28 points, or 0.34%, the S&P 500 dropped 1.09%, and the Nasdaq Composite tumbled 2.14%. AI-related technology stocks such as CoreWeave and Broadcom came under renewed pressure, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index has now fallen for eight straight sessions. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing's ADR fell $9.64 (about NT$310), or 2.28%, to close at $412.61 (about NT$13,000), directly foreshadowing the catch-up decline in the local spot market on Monday.

Beyond the valuation correction in technology stocks, international oil prices approaching $110 a barrel (about NT$3,600) also deepened concerns that inflation could reignite and that interest rates will stay elevated. The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) topped 22 for the first time in more than four months. Asian markets fell in unison on Monday morning, with South Korean stocks opening lower and plunging 3.16% to break below 5,500 points, while Japan's Nikkei opened down 1,493 points and fell below the 64,000 level, showing that the correction is not unique to Taiwan but reflects synchronized deleveraging across global risk assets.

Analysts said Taiwan stocks face a catch-up effect after the overnight futures plunge, and with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index posting its longest losing streak since the global financial crisis, semiconductor valuations are under pressure and the index faces considerable strain as it tests its quarterly moving average. However, turnover shrinking to around NT$780 billion suggests no panic selling has emerged, with funds rotating into defensive names such as telecom operators and fiberglass cloth makers, indicating the market structure still reflects sector rotation rather than a broad collapse. Investors will need to watch whether U.S. AI-related stocks can stabilize and how oil prices affect inflation expectations.
