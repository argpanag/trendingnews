Badenoch says using amputee athletes to justify cutting disability benefits was ‘message of hope’

Good morning. At PMQs yesterday Andy Burnham said that Kemi Badenoch should withdraw the “double amputees run marathons” comment that she used to justify the Tory plan to cut sickness and disability benefits by £14bn. It was “crass and insensitive”, he said.

This morning Badenoch defended what she was saying claimed that, in fact, she was delivering a “message of hope”.

In an interview on the Today programme, asked if she accepted that her remark was insensitive, Badenoch said she didn’t. She went on:

I will speak the truth and I will say what I believe is common sense. And what I know is that, if people can work, then we should encourage them to work.

And what I’m saying is actually a message of hope, that if people even with significant, problems can work, then those who have ADHD and mild anxiety should go to work.

I will speak the truth and I will say what I believe is common sense. And what I know is that, if people can work, then we should encourage them to work.

And what I’m saying is actually a message of hope, that if people even with significant, problems can work, then those who have ADHD and mild anxiety should go to work.

When the presenter, Nick Robinson, put it to her that it was unreasonable to use an exceptional case as a guide to what most disabled people should be doing, Badenoch did not agree. “We do use extreme examples to illustrate things,” she said.

Robinson tried again, saying she was taking “one extreme case [to] imply that anybody on disability benefits should just get on with it”. Badenoch accused him of “faux outrage” and “tone policing”. She added:

People are free to disagree, but they know where I’m coming from.

UK must decide whether it wants to be or not to be in EU, says Rosencrantz at EU summit

Jakub Krupa writes the Guardian’s Europe live blog.

Over in Brussels, EU ministers are arriving for foreign affairs talks, with questions already popping up about tomorrow’s EU-UK summit, 10 years on from Britain’s vote to leave the bloc.

Ireland’s EU minister Thomas Byrne said the main areas to be discussed on Friday “will have very significant benefits” for all parties, with the sanitary and phytosanitary agreement set to resolve “one of the biggest problems out of Brexit”.

But he was cautious talking about the future relationship with the EU, saying that while he had “no doubt that the EU and Britain will want to go further on that path, we have seen how complex even dealing with the issues for tomorrow is and how long it takes.”

Sweden’s Jessica Rosencrantz stressed it is “up to the citizens of the UK to decide how close you want to work with the EU”.

But, again, asked if Britain could rejoin the bloc and keep its historical opt-outs and rebates, she dismissed it as “a highly speculative question”. She added:

Let’s remember how the discussion was at the time of Brexit. In that light, I think it is amazing that we now can have a big summit together, with discussions on how we can come closer together when it comes to trade and mobility.

Let’s remember how the discussion was at the time of Brexit. In that light, I think it is amazing that we now can have a big summit together, with discussions on how we can come closer together when it comes to trade and mobility.

Poland’s EU minister Ignacy Niemczycki said Warsaw’s approach “has always been very pragmatic,” as he stressed that the EU after Brexit is “weaker.” He said:

I would like us, wherever possible, to work out such mechanisms so it works for both of us. … This is a country that thinks similarly to us in Europe on many issues and there is no reason for us to treat each other like enemies. We are friends and let’s find the right formula for that.

I would like us, wherever possible, to work out such mechanisms so it works for both of us. … This is a country that thinks similarly to us in Europe on many issues and there is no reason for us to treat each other like enemies. We are friends and let’s find the right formula for that.

In a cheeky comment, he added:

I would encourage everyone to think seriously about the benefits of being in the EU, … and I think the people of the UK see it [too], now that they are outside the union.

Tories choose James Cleverly as their candidate for London mayor in 2028

James Cleverly has been selected as the Conservative party’s candidate for the next London mayoral election.

The former foreign secretary and former home secretary won 86% of the votes in the ballot, beating Westminster councillor Festus Akinbusoye and former parliamentary candidate Sara Gezdari. A fourth candidate, communications professional Alex Challoner, was eliminated before the shortlist was drawn up for the ballot.

The next election for London mayor will take place in 2028.

Cleverly said he was “all in” for the race and would not be seeking reselection as an MP. He represents Braintree in Essex.

After the result was announced, Cleverly said the current mayor, Labour’s Sadiq Khan, was letting London down. He said:

People are leaving London, or not choosing to come here in the first place, businesses, job creators, families leaving London, and by the time of the election in 2028, Khan will have been mayor for 12 years, and those two things are related.

The problems London faces are the symptoms. Khan is the cause.

When I ask Londoners, do you feel safe walking home at night? The answer is no.

It’s shameful, it’s a failure, and it’s not just crime.

We have had the worst period of housebuilding since the second world war and only the highest-earning Londoners can afford the cheapest houses in London.

Minister admits government cannot force Google to remove Russell Brand prison video from YouTube

The government has no power to make Google take down a YouTube video with a message from Russell Brand from prison claiming he is innocent of his crimes, a minister has admitted.

As Nadeem Badshah reports, the government has asked Google to take down the video.

Google has reportedly denied the request because it determined its rules had not been broken, the Press Association reports.

Asked about this in an interview on BBC Breakfast this morning, Matthew Pennycook, the housing minister, said:

We don’t have any power to mandate a removal of content. That is up to Google. It’s based on their terms and conditions.

But as I’ve been very clear, we’ve made a request in this instance because we feel very strongly that victims shouldn’t have to see this content.

We hope they do so because they have taken action on their platform before. They banned, for example in 2023, Russell Brand for making money off the platform because his off-platform behaviour was seen to cause harm.

Kemi Badenoch was in a straight-talking mode on the media round this morning, calling the historic meeting between Trump, Xi and Putin “disturbing”, saying it looked like they were trying to “carve” up the world” between them.

Speaking on the Today programme, the leader of the opposition said the meeting was “absolutely not reassuring”.

She said:

There are three world leaders who look like they’re trying to carve up the world between them, a tri-polar world where Xi gets Asia, America gets the western hemisphere and Russia gets Europe, and that is very concerning, especially seeing Donald Trump call President Putin his friend.

Russia is not America’s friend, it certainly hasn’t been during my lifetime. But also at a time when we’re all worried about where the Ukraine war is going, the message that is sending to President Zelensky and of course the other summit that is taking place … is very concerning indeed.

And what I want to know is where we fit in in all of this. I don’t want to be in a polarised world run by Russia. I’ll do everything I can to make sure that doesn’t happen.
