Peru faces a morning of four earthquakes and extreme heat, with temperatures forecast to reach 38°C

TheTools · 14.11.2026 18:19 · source · permalink

The strongest tremor measured magnitude 4.6 near Pasco; authorities reported no casualties or structural damage as SENAMHI warned of intense heat and UV radiation.

The Briefing

  • Four sisms, the strongest a magnitude 4.6 near Pasco, struck Peru within a single morning window on Friday, November 13, with no structural damage or casualties reported across any affected zone.
  • SENAMHI simultaneously activated a red-level meteorological alert for Lima, Callao, and 18 additional regions, warning of daytime temperatures climbing to 38°C and intense UV radiation along the coast and highlands over a 71-hour window.
  • The dual emergency coincides with an extraordinary El Niño Costero season that Peru's national climate commission ENFEN projects will persist through at least January 2027.

Peru faced two simultaneous natural crises on Friday morning. Before 8 a.m., four seismic events registered across separate regions spanning Pasco and Arequipa while meteorologists finalized a red-level heat advisory covering nearly two-thirds of the country's departments. Neither emergency turned catastrophic, but their overlap placed civil defense authorities on heightened readiness as the holiday weekend approached.

Four Tremors Register Across Three Regions

The day's final tremor arrived at 7:37 a.m., when the IGP logged a 4.6-magnitude event with its epicenter 32 kilometers southeast of Puerto Bermúdez, in the jungle province of Oxapampa, Pasco. The focus sat at 139 kilometers — an intermediate-depth classification that lowers surface destruction risk while extending the radius over which shaking is perceptible. The event registered intensity III near the epicenter. No injuries or building damage were reported from Puerto Bermúdez or surrounding Pasco communities.

Three hours earlier, at 4:04 a.m., a 4.5-magnitude tremor struck 33 kilometers northwest of Ocoña, Arequipa, at a depth of 79 kilometers, reaching intensity III–IV with no casualties. Two additional 4.7-magnitude events were also recorded during the same morning hours, bringing the total to four significant seismic episodes before the workday began. Authorities urged residents living near older structures to remain vigilant for aftershocks throughout the day.

Peru's Seismic Baseline: Life on the Ring of Fire

Multi-tremor mornings are geologically routine for Peru. The country occupies a critical segment of the Pacific Ring of Fire, the 40,000-kilometer arc of fault lines and volcanic systems where roughly 90 percent of global seismic activity originates. Arequipa, Lima, and Ica consistently concentrate the highest tremor frequency. By mid-August 2026, the IGP had already registered 543 seismic events for the year, with July standing out as the most active month. No tsunami warning was issued for any of Friday's tremors, and civil defense authorities have not declared a state of emergency, though standard INDECI multi-event protocols remain in force.

SENAMHI's 71-Hour Red Alert: Extreme Heat Stretches Across the Coast

While the IGP tracked each tremor in real time, Peru's national meteorological service was activating a parallel emergency of a different nature. SENAMHI issued a red-level alert covering Lima, Callao, and 18 additional regions over a 71-hour window, warning that daytime temperatures would reach 38°C in coastal and highland zones. The advisory flagged intensified ultraviolet radiation and strong wind gusts along the central and southern shoreline as compounding factors. The agency called on residents to avoid direct sun exposure during peak hours — typically between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m. — to stay hydrated and to follow updates from official channels while the alert remains active.

El Niño Costero: The Force Driving Peru's Heat Surge

The heat crisis is not an isolated weather event. Peru's multisectoral El Niño monitoring commission, ENFEN, determined in September 2026 that El Niño Costero had escalated to extraordinary magnitude — the highest tier in Peru's official classification system. ENFEN placed the probability at 62 percent of the event maintaining that classification through January 2027 in the Niño 1+2 coastal region off Peru and Ecuador. That anomalous ocean warming feeds the elevated air temperatures now pressing down on Lima and the rest of the coast.

SENAMHI specialist Matt Nieto warned in September that the most intense rainfall period of the season — driven directly by El Niño's thermal energy — was set to begin in late November across affected regions. That timeline means current conditions represent an opening act rather than a peak. Flooding risk will be layered on top of the heat and seismic alerts already active this week as the Southern Hemisphere summer accelerates.

How to Stay Informed

The IGP publishes the magnitude, depth, and coordinates of every detected event immediately after recording, via its official X account @Sismos_Peru_IGP. SENAMHI updates its meteorological advisories continuously at senamhi.gob.pe. Residents in highland zones covered by the heat alert, particularly those in areas historically prone to slope instability, are encouraged to monitor both agencies throughout the advisory window.
