Live updates: Ground stop lifted after hourslong halt to US departures snarls air travel nationwide | CNN

TheTools · 13.11.2026 21:22 · source · permalink

What's happening now

• JUST IN: Domestic US flights are being allowed to depart again after federal officials issued an hourslong ground stop due to safety concerns from a telecommunications outage. Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy blamed old air traffic control technology and overloaded routers.

• Travel nightmare: An estimated 1.5 million people were stranded by flight cancellations, according to analysis by the data firm Cirium. The skies emptied as flights that were already in the air came in for landing and the ground stop dragged on for more than four hours.

• No known threat: Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the ground stop did not stem from a threat to the US and was not linked to this week’s disruptive Microsoft outage. His department has seen “zero evidence” that the issue stemmed from a cyber attack, he added.

Updates

Duffy says it's going to "take us some time to unwind" after ground stop

While the ground stop has been lifted, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy cautioned that it’s “going to be slow” to return to normal, depending on how many crews are available. However, Duffy said that it shouldn’t impact Thanksgiving travel in a couple weeks.

Depending on crew capacity, Duffy said they would try to move as many flights as possible tonight, adding that aircraft with more people will be prioritized.

“If not, we’re going to start tomorrow morning with our airline partners moving those aircraft. But it’s going to take us some time to unwind what’s happened this afternoon,” Duffy said.

“Hopefully, tomorrow we are going to start to see far better movement,” he added.

Duffy on system failure and subsequent ground stop: "I want you to fly safely"

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy acknowledged that the air traffic control system “had a failure” Friday but said his “number one job is America’s safety.”

FAA technology systems need "complete overhaul," Mullin says

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin called for a “complete overhaul” of FAA’s technology systems, citing new equipment overwhelming the old framework as the reason for the ground stop.

“We need someone to come in and build the whole system. Doing it nickel-and-dime-it together does not work. This is what happens when that takes place,” Mullin said during a Friday night press briefing.

Mullin described the system failure as a result of patching new technology into existing framework which caused the older system to get overwhelmed.

“That’s why the president has made it very clear: We can’t do this. It has to be a complete overhaul of the system, which we estimate to be about $50 billion,” Mullin said.

Duffy asks for more funding for air traffic control revamp: "I want $50 billion"

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy urged Congress to give “emergency funding” and approve $50 billion dollars to revamp the air traffic control system, increasing a previous ask for funding of more than $30 billion.

“I’ve asked, the president’s asked for $30 billion. I want $50 billion,” Duffy said while briefing the press at the White House tonight.

“What’s more important than having an air traffic control system that actually works?” Duffy continued. “And so the president said, ‘You know what? Let’s finish this job. New hardware, new software, new telecommunication.’”

Duffy and the Department of Transportation announced a plan in 2025 to build a new air traffic control system by 2028 that would cost roughly $31.5 billion. More than $12 billion of that funding was secured through the passage of President Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

“We are building in that 12 and a half billion dollars. That portion of the bill will be done by the time President Trump leaves office. We are moving at a speed that has never been seen before with regard to the FAA,” Duffy said. “But that’s a partial funding.”

Duffy blames old tech, overloaded routers for nationwide ground stop

Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy said old air traffic control technology and routers overloaded with data were to blame for the nationwide ground stop today.

“What we have is an incredibly old air traffic control system,” Duffy said at a news conference tonight. “The president, for the last year, has been asking Congress to fully fund a full build of a brand new air traffic control system.”

“Our routers actually got overloaded with data, and… they started to give us inconsistency in the data from those routers. And because of that, we shut the system down,” he added.

Duffy said some routers had been replaced during his tenure, but the old hardware went down.

Duffy says more than 3,500 planes in the air when ground stop issued

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Friday that more than 3,500 airplanes were in the air when the ground stop was issued earlier today.

“We had over 3,500 airplanes in the air. They all engaged, and last time I looked, we had less than 400 in the airspace as they were all coming in to safely land. And we navigated it with our well-known procedures that we have when these incidents happen,” Duffy said during a press briefing late Friday.

The secretary said that the ground stop was in response to instability in radar and communication centers across the country.

“We started to have inconsistency in the data from the radars and our communication with airplanes, and we have procedures for this,” he said.

US-bound flights from Europe suspended after FAA ground stop

Eurocontrol, Europe’s air traffic control authority, suspended all US-bound departures from its 42 member countries on Friday at the Federal Aviation Administration’s request.

The suspension was scheduled to run until midnight ET after a communications failure triggered a nationwide ground stop at US airports.

Flights from the US to Europe can still operate if the FAA clears them for takeoff.

Last week, Eurocontrol recorded an average of about 1,180 flights a day in both directions between Europe and the North Atlantic region. The US was the busiest destination outside Eurocontrol’s network in 2025.

"Zero evidence" of a cyber attack, but "we're still looking," Mullin says

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said his department has seen “zero evidence” that the telecommunications issue causing the ground stop was a cyber attack.

"The public was never at danger," Mullin says

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin stressed that the public was not in danger throughout tonight’s nationwide ground stop, telling reporters at the White House that it was initiated out of an “abundance of caution.”

Mullin also said no link exists between tonight’s events and Thursday’s Microsoft outage.

“There is no connection between this and Microsoft. I know a lot of people are making that jump,” Mullin said off the top of the briefing at the White House.

“I do understand the internet traffic and where all these conspiracies start running towards. I just can’t say this enough,” Mullin later said: “All your loved ones are safe.”

Mullin says things seem to be going "in the right direction" to fix FAA issue

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said “things seem to be working, as we can say, in the right direction” amid the nationwide FAA ground stop.

Addressing the press at the White House tonight in the briefing room alongside Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, Mullin stressed that “there was no cyber attack” that took down the system.

“The public was never at danger through this process. There was never any lives. There was no flights at risk,” Mullin said.

Nationwide ground stop lifted by FAA

The nationwide ground stop issued for all domestic flights has been lifted, according to the Federal Aviation Administration.

The order comes after over four hours of uncertainty due to a telecommunications outage, causing thousands of delays and cancellations at major airports.

US-bound flights from Asia also suffer delays and cancellations

US-bound flights from major airports across the Asia Pacific have also been affected by the nationwide ground stop.

Overnight flights from Incheon International Airport in the South Korean capital Seoul to Detroit, Guam, Atlanta and San Francisco face delays of up to two hours.

A Delta Air Lines flight from Hong Kong to Los Angeles was canceled, and another to the city was delayed.

A flight from Shanghai to Detroit and another from Singapore to San Francisco also faced delays.

JetBlue and Air Canada call off remainder of US flights for Friday

JetBlue Airways and Air Canada are calling off their remaining US flights for Friday, the airlines said in their own statements.

“Due to the nationwide FAA mandated ground stop, JetBlue is canceling all remaining flights this evening with the exception of Europe-bound departures from JFK and Boston,” JetBlue said. “Flights already in the air are continuing to their destinations.”

JetBlue said it expects disruptions to continue into tomorrow, with additional cancelations.

Air Canada said it would cancel all of its flights between Canada and the US for the rest of the evening.

“Flights between Canada, the Caribbean and South America that pass through U.S. airspace could also be affected,” Air Canada said in a statement.

Mullin and Duffy holding briefing on ground stop

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy are now holding a briefing at the White House on the nationwide ground stop.

Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport has run out of space to park planes

Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport does not have any more space to park planes, an airport source said.

The FAA has issued an alert that the airport can no longer accept diversions, the source added.

Some context: With so many aircraft unable to fly because of the nationwide groundstop, airports are running out of gates and tarmac space to park planes.

Here’s why US authorities have — so far — concluded aviation incident was not an attack

A “whole of government” response to Friday’s incident involving national air traffic by multiple law enforcement and intelligence agencies has still not identified a nefarious attack, a senior US security source told CNN.

Authorities continue to believe the issue was caused by a communication software issue rather than an intentional act, the source said, even as cyber specialists coast to coast are still investigating the incident.

US law enforcement and intelligence agencies began receiving internal updates from aviation officials notifying them something was amiss right around when television screens inside agencies’ 24-hour command centers in the agencies’ headquarters began alerting officials to the issue Friday.

It prompted officials at the FBI, Department of Homeland Security, and even the US National Security Agency — which leads efforts to counter foreign cyber threats — to spring into action.

“Still no indication this was an attack,” the senior security source said late Friday. The source added the collective security response included “drilling down on” the various means the US government’s aviation system connects to networks that could potentially be exposed to bad actors.

The source said efforts by US security services to identify any potential “node of attack” are expected to continue “at full speed” in the coming days, as cyber threats can sometimes eventually be located deep inside computer networks.

Compounding the problem are antiquated aviation networks spread across the country that need to be investigated, the source added.

“The (US) aviation networks and servers are old and spread across a wide swath of territory and won’t be totally cleared until we get boots on the ground to give a close look at the networks impacted,” the source said.

Man sold ticket after FAA had grounded flights

Passengers at Newark Liberty International Airport were seen walking through the TSA checkpoints, some cases they were rushing.

There looked to be some confusion because the boards were still indicating that some flights were delayed and will board at one point.

Passenger Jose said he is trying to get to Toronto for work and bought a ticket.

“This already happened and they sold it to me anyways,” the passenger said.

Just under 10,000 flights delayed today, tracking platform reports

Flights continue to be canceled and delayed amid the nationwide ground stop.

According to the aviation tracking platform FlightAware, there have been 9,814 total delays and 5,117 total cancellations within, into or out of the US today as of 8 p.m. ET.

The Federal Aviation Administration issued the ground stop today at approximately 4 p.m. ET. The delays and cancellations reported by FlightAware account for the entire day and not just following the ground stop announcement.

This traveler found out — too late — about the ground stop

Like countless others this evening, Adanma Uzor has no idea when when she can return home. She was already in an Uber en route to Chicago’s O’Hare International Airport when she read the United Airlines text alert warning her about delays and the possible cancellation of flights.

“We are sorry to let you know” about the nationwide ground stop, the 4:43 p.m. CT message began. She was set to return home to New York after a work trip.

The message had arrived minutes before she left for the airport, but she hadn’t checked her phone until the Uber was already on the highway, she told CNN.

On arrival, she was told to scan her boarding pass on a kiosk, to see options of vouchers for a hotel and meals.
