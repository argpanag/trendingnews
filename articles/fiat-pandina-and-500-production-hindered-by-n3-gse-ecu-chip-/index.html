Fiat Pandina and 500 Production Hindered by N3 GSE ECU Chip Shortage

TheTools · 04.11.2026 10:59 · source · permalink

First and foremost, N3 GSE is part of the FireFly engine family. Displacing just under 1 liter, the three-pot mill is a naturally aspirated lump that started production back in 2016. The BSG version of the N3 GSE adds 12-volt assistance in the form of a small battery and a belt-driven starter generator. The BSG provides additional torque in certain scenarios, lowering the load on the engine.

As implied, the BSG serves as a generator when coasting or stepping on the brakes, harvesting kinetic energy that is directly fed into the lithium-ion battery. A coasting mode also needs to be highlighted, along with an ultra-quiet restart procedure when the driver presses the clutch pedal to select first gear. To further maximize efficiency, the BSG version runs on the Miller cycle.

Having cleared that up, this chip shortage could not have arrived at a worse time for Stellantis Europe. The Pandina, which is what Fiat calls the old-school Panda nowadays, moved plenty of units in October. Even though Fiat could not make as many as it wanted, the Pandina remained the best-selling car in the Italian automaker's home market, posting 7,198 sales last month.

The chip shortage puts Fiat in a very frustrating position because demand for the Pandina certainly is there. Stellantis Europe, however, cannot supply as freely as it would like. Also used by the hybridized 500, the engine control unit of the 1.0-liter mill does a lot of things. From core engine management to advanced energy orchestration, diagnostics, and self-preservation when a critical fault is detected, that control unit is one of the most important in a modern vehicle.

Beyond said factories, Termoli is where the N3 GSE inline-three is assembled. Stellantis Europe has yet to announce downtime for Termoli, and the company hasn't quantified the expected production losses either. As of this writing, Fiat and Stellantis are working hard with suppliers to identify alternatives in order to progressively restore normal production outputs.

Speaking of disruptions, the Mirafiori plant had already entered a separate three-week stoppage in October due to an unrelated shortage. Weak demand for the 500 line also contributed to said interruption, and the latest shortage introduces yet another constraint at a plant that was already struggling to maintain its designated production schedule.

Looking at the bigger picture, Stellantis and Fiat should have known better. The industry already went through a far more painful version of this problem in 2021 and 2022, with US light vehicle production recovering to pre-crisis levels in early 2023. Back then, automakers responded by prioritizing higher-margin vehicles, deleting semiconductor-dependent features that were not absolutely essential.

On a related note, Stellantis has recently said that its 2026 financial guidance remains unchanged following its third-quarter results. Stellantis also maintained expectations for positive industrial free cash flow in 2027, meaning that Stellantis isn't forecasting any significant revisions linked to this chip shortage.
