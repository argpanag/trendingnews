Supercars to return to Phillip Island, plugging hole left by MotoGP exit

  • ABC Gippsland

  • Topic:Motor Sports

Topic:Motor Sports

Supercars will return to Phillip Island for the first time since 2019 as part of a five-year deal struck by the Victorian government. (AAP: Dave Hunt)

In short:

Supercars will return to Phillip Island for the first time since 2019 as part of a five-year deal with the Victorian government.

The event will help offset the $80 million drop in revenue caused by the loss of the MotoGP to Adelaide from next year.

What's next?

The first Supercars event will be held on Phillip Island in 2027.

Supercars will return to Phillip Island next year, helping plug an economic gap left by MotoGP's move to Adelaide.

Victorian Major Events Minister Anthony Carbines today announced a five-year deal to host a Supercars Championship final series round.

It will be the first time since 2019 that the island has hosted Supercars. 

"We know Supercars fans are wrapped with this news. We know our drivers are wrapped because they love Phillip Island, they love the circuit and they can't wait," Mr Carbines said.

"It was critical that we got back out there and made sure we locked in something that was going to make a big difference to the community of Phillip Island and the vast coast, but also to bring Supercars back to the Island."

Phillip Island will host its final MotoGP from October 23 to 25.

The track has hosted the event since 1989, but lost it to South Australia after the Victorian government rejected a request to move it from Phillip Island to Melbourne's Albert Park.

It later lost the Australian round of the FIM Superbike World Championships, which will be held on Phillip Island for the last time in 2027.

The loss of the two events has created anxiety in the local business community, which relies heavily on event-driven tourism.

Local tourism body Destination Phillip Island says racing is worth $80 million to the local economy.

Phillip Island will host a Festival of Motoring in March next year, with Labor and the Coalition both promising to back the event.

Opposition major events spokesperson David Southwick today said the Coalition would provide $100,000 to fund the event if it wins November's state election.

Victorian opposition major events spokesperson David Southwick and Bass Liberal candidate Rochelle Halstead say the Coalition will back a motoring festival on Phillip Island. (ABC Gippsland: Madeleine Stuchbery)

"We know the government has announced supercars today from Federation Square," Mr Southwick said on Phillip Island.

"They don't care about Bass Coast, they don't care about the island.

"We're here today because we care and we know it all starts with homegrown events."

Motorsport central to Phillip Island

President of the Association of Motoring Clubs Keith Mortimer said motoring culture connected many people to Phillip Island.

He said the goal for the next 12 months was to ensure the first motoring festival's success and make sure the event "is here to stay".

He said motorsport, whether it's bikes, classic cars, or Supercars, connected many visitors to the island.

Keith Mortimer says motorsport connects many visitors to Phillip Island. (ABC Gippsland: Madeleine Stuchbery)

"There are some wonderful things to see here, around Churchill Island, around the chocolate factories, around the local produce," Mr Mortimer said.

"It really does have something for everyone."

Destination Phillip Island chief executive Kim Storey said one in four jobs on the island were connected to the visitor economy.

She said the five-year commitment will boost confidence in local accommodation, hospitality, retail and tourism.

"Supercars represents meaningful progress towards rebuilding major event visitation," she said.

"There is still important work ahead to replace lost demand across the year and support the broader economic transition facing Phillip Island and San Remo."
