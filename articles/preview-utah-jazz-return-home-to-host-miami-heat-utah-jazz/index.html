Preview: Utah Jazz Return Home to Host Miami Heat  | Utah Jazz

TheTools · 11.11.2026 02:43 · source · permalink

The Utah Jazz are certainly glad to be home.

After a four-game, seven-day road trip, the Jazz are hoping that some time spent at the Delta Center can help turn their season around. Utah went 0-4 away from home over the past week, with a few close losses to Western Conference opponents. 

In Utah’s latest contest, struggles to hit shots (29 percent from beyond the arc) and the absences of Lauri Markkanen hurt the offense. Darryn Peterson impressed with a career-high 26 points (on 11-of-21 shooting, including 4-of-8 from three), but Keyonte George went down with an ankle injury and the Jazz couldn’t string together enough quality offense to keep pace. The Timberwolves overcame 22 turnovers to secure a 115-100 victory.

The Jazz have a brief stop at home for two games, which begins with a matchup against the Miami Heat on Tuesday night.

Miami (4-6) has struggled out of the gates following a blockbuster acquisition of Giannis Antetokounmpo in the offseason. The Heat are still searching for consistency on both ends of the floor, having entered the new week ranked 19th in offense and 26th in defense. With several veterans joining Erik Spoelstra’s squad in the summer, Miami is hoping that as the season progresses, the team will find its groove and compete in the Eastern Conference. 

The two teams split the season series a year ago.

Jazz injury report:

OUT - Trey Alexander (G League - Two-Way), Kobe Bufkin (G League - Two-Way), Keyonte George (Right Ankle; Sprain), Harrison Ingram (G League - Two-Way), Jaren Jackson Jr. (Right Knee; MCL Tear), Jusuf Nurkić (Right Foot; 2nd Plantar Plate Tear)

QUESTIONABLE - Lauri Markkanen (Right Wrist; Sprain)

Heat injury report:

PROBABLE - Giannis Antetokounmpo (Right Wrist; Sprain)

Here are three keys to Tuesday’s game.

Let it fly from deep 

Outside from the injuries that have depleted the frontcourt rotation, the most notable storyline of Utah’s season has been the extended shooting slump the team has suffered through.

After a 7-of-24 outing from downtown against Minnesota, the Jazz sit at 32.1 percent on the season from three, good for 26th in the league. When you combine the lack of success with Utah’s below-average frequency of attempts (35.8 per game, 22nd in the league), the long ball simply hasn’t been a reliable weapon for Will Hardy’s squad.

A matchup against Miami’s defense while playing at home may be just what the Jazz need to find their rhythm from deep. Opponents have had plenty of success attacking from beyond the arc against the Heat this season, with 43.2 percent of opponent shot attempts coming from downtown, the sixth-highest frequency in the league. On those looks, opponents have connected on 40 percent, the third-highest percentage in the association.

Utah will need to generate quality looks and if they can knock a few down early and find a flow, it will go a long way towards breaking down Miami’s defense.

Limit turnovers and fast break opportunities

Although the Heat have gone through some early season struggles, there are still facets of the game that Spoelstra’s squad thrive in.

One area that should be of concern for the Jazz is Miami’s ability to force turnovers. The Heat rank eighth in opponent turnover percentage through the first 10 games, which has helped fuel an aggressive transition attack. Miami ranks fifth in the league in frequency of possessions that begin with a transition play, while that number jumps up to second when specifically looking at possessions that begin after a steal. 

The Jazz did well to limit their turnovers against Minnesota (14), but still allowed the Timberwolves to get out on the break for 27 fast break points on the night. Utah must strengthen its ball security against a Heat squad that will look to take any advantage it can get. 

Peterson building momentum 

The story of Utah’s close to the road trip was the play of Peterson. 

The 19-year-old set career highs in scoring in back-to-back games against the Mavericks and Timberwolves. Peterson’s effort Sunday night stood out, as he scored 26 points on 11 made shots, four of which came from deep. 

Peterson mixed up his shot distribution and did well against Minnesota’s ball pressure, while he also sprinkled in four assists and ended the night plus-three in the box score. 

“I thought Darryn turned in his best game as a pro,” Hardy said. “There’s a variety of ways to attack. There’s a lot to take away from how he not only handled ball pressure, but also an elite big at the rim.”

Through four November games, Peterson has averaged 17 points and 5.5 assists per game, connecting on 37.5 percent of his 3-point attempts. Miami has some tough perimeter defenders in Davion Mitchell and Pelle Larsson, so Tuesday’s game will be another good test for Peterson.

Tuesday’s game tips off at 7 p.m. MT. Tune in to the pregame show with Alema Harrington and Mike Smith beginning at 6:30 p.m. MT on Jazz+!
