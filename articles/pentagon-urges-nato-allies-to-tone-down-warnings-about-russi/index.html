Pentagon urges NATO allies to tone down warnings about Russia, report says

TheTools · 30.10.2026 15:39 · source · permalink

Pentagon officials have urged several NATO allies to tone down their public warnings about Russia, arguing that some statements by European leaders could undermine security in Europe, according to a news report.

The requests were made in recent weeks during private meetings with officials from Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia, according to four people familiar with the discussions who spoke to Reuters on condition of anonymity.

Elbridge Colby, the Pentagon’s under secretary of defense for policy, was among the officials delivering the message, the news service reported Friday.

One person familiar with the discussions said Colby had urged a Baltic official to avoid “poking” Russia, citing statements by European leaders portraying Moscow as increasingly desperate as its war in Ukraine drags on.

Colby reportedly pointed to descriptions of Russia as “cornered like a rat” as an example of unhelpful rhetoric.

Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal used similar language in September, referring to Russian President Vladimir Putin as a “rat in a corner” while suggesting that Moscow was resorting to sabotage operations in Europe as its invasion of Ukraine stalled.

In another meeting, Pentagon officials urged an Estonian delegation to be “measured” in its public communications, according to Reuters. However, Estonia’s defense ministry pushed back Friday.

“No such suggestions have reached us through official channels,” ministry spokeswoman Liisa Tagel said in response to a query about the report.

The Pentagon and NATO acknowledged requests for comment but did not immediately provide responses Friday.

A Pentagon official quoted by Reuters declined to address the assertions of the four NATO representatives.

“Private conversations among allies are just that, so we will not comment on these allegations,” the official said.

Colby is a driving force of the Pentagon initiative to put European allies at the forefront of responsibility for collective defense of the Continent.

The aim is to take that burden off the United States so its troops can concentrate on the Indo-Pacific and the Western Hemisphere. Colby has dubbed the new arrangement NATO 3.0.

The reported requests come as President Donald Trump renews efforts to negotiate an end to the Russia-Ukraine war.

The Baltic states, which border Russia or its close ally Belarus, have repeatedly warned that Moscow could pose a broader threat to European security beyond Ukraine.

The Pentagon’s reported intervention also comes amid growing concern about suspected Russian sabotage operations across Europe. Moscow denies the accusations.

Reuters said it could not determine whether the Pentagon had delivered similar messages to other European governments.

Phillip Walter Wellman is a reporter and photographer for Stars and Stripes, based in Kaiserslautern, Germany. From 2016 to 2021, he covered the war in Afghanistan from Stripes’ Kabul bureau. He is a graduate of the London School of Economics.
