GYMNASTICS: U.S.’s Blakely stays solid, improves in three of four events to win Worlds women’s All-Around gold! - The Sports Examiner

≡ WORLD ARTISTIC CHAMPS ≡

The 2026 World Gymnastics Artistic World Championships in Rotterdam (NED) featured the women’s All-Around, with 2024 Olympic Uneven Bars champ Kaylia Nemour (ALG) – still just 19 – the leading qualifier during the team qualification program, followed by American Skye Blakely and Russian Liudmila Rochchina.

● Rotation one: The top six in the team qualifying started on Vault, with Roshchina starting well a 14.033, slightly better than 14.033 in qualifying. Blakely, 21, followed with a solid 14.200, +0.200 from qualifying.

That brought up Nemour, who scored only 12.366 with a scaled-down vault, way down from 14.033 in her qualifying, leaving her in disbelief (and in 21st place). China’s Qinqin Ke, fifth in the qualifying, scored a solid 14.033, also down from 14.266 in qualifying. France’s Elena Colas, the no. 4 qualifier, hit 13.200 on Vault, down just 0.033.

On Uneven Bars, 17-year-old Claire Pease of the U.S. started well with a solid routine and a stuck landing, earning 13.833, behind her qualifying score of 14.066. The rotation leader was Alena Glotova (RUS) with 14.400 on the Uneven Bars.

● Rotation two: Blakely led off on the Uneven Bars, and was elegant and fast, stuck the landing and scored 14.400, up from her 14.333 in qualifying. Nemour, the reigning World Champion, was next and had some difficulty, but the routine had so much difficulty, she scored 14.866 and got back into the podium race. Ke was good, scoring 13.900, down 0.233 from qualifying and Colas moved up with a 14.433, down just 0.100 from qualifying. Roshchina scored 14.333, up from the 14.266 she did in qualifying.

Pease was on Beam but fell, and scored 12.533; Glotova also fell, twice, and scored 11.066 and was out of contention.

So at halfway, Blakely led at 28.600, then Roshchina (28.366) and Ke (27.933). Nemour was up to sixth after her best event at 27.232. Pease was 15th (26.366).

● Rotation three: The lead group was on Beam, and Nemour had an early wobble, but scored a big 14.500. China’s Ke had a fall on the dismount, but still scored 13.833, followed by Colas at 13.933 and Roshchina was solid, with a 13.433 and into the lead at 41.799.

That brought up Blakely, who scored 14.166 in qualifying, stayed composed, took her time and had only a single step on the discount and scored 14.200 and had the overall lead by more than a point at 42.800. Roshchina, Ke and Nemour were within 0.067 of each other in 2-3-4, and Colas a couple more tenths back in fifth.

Pease was on Floor and was strong at 13.733 and was tied for 10th at 40.99.

● Rotation four: Blakely had to wait again, starting fifth out of six on Floor, where she scored 12.966 in qualifying. Ke, standing third, scored 13.266, nicely up from 12.400 in qualifying and totaled 55.032, and Colas scored 13.100 for 54.666. Roshchina scored 13.266 in qualifying and was very solid again, scoring 13.400 to take the lead at 55.199.

Blakely was next and had to score 12.400 or better to win. Her tumbling was good, but she danced beautifully and scored 12.933 to total 55.733 and win an amazing gold medal!

Incredibly, Blakely lost a 2024 Olympic berth to injury, did not contest the All-Around at the 2026 NCAA Championships for Florida and was just seventh in the All-Around at the U.S. Championships. In fact, her career A-A finishes at the U.S. Nationals have been 7th-6th-4th-2nd-(did not compete in ’25)-7th since 2021 as a senior. Now, she is World Champion.

Nemour was the last on Floor and scored 13.266 and finished at 54.998 and ended up fourth, missing a medal by 0.34 points after that terrible opening event. Pease stormed through an excellent Uneven Bars routine to score 14.133 – better than in qualifying – and finished at 54.232, good for seventh (at 17).

After an inquiry, Roshchina was confirmed for silver (55.199), then Ke for the bronze (55.032) and Nemour fourth.

¶

Was Blakely favored coming into the Worlds? Certainly not, but the American performance in this event has been striking: seven golds in a row in the women’s A-A from 2011 to 2019 – five from Simone Biles – then Biles again in 2023 and now Blakely in 2026. That’s nine wins in the last 12 editions … and silvers in the other three for Leanne Wong (2) and Shilese Jones. American gymnasts have now won a medal in the women’s A-A in 18 straight Worlds.

The Worlds will finish with the apparatus finals on Saturday and Sunday.
