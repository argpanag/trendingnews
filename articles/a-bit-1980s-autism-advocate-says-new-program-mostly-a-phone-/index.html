'A bit 1980s': Autism advocate says new program mostly a phone line so far

TheTools · 30.09.2026 04:20 · source · permalink
  • N

    By Niki Burnside

  • Topic:People With Disability

By Niki Burnside

Topic:People With Disability

The ACT's Education Minister and Disability, Autism and Neurodiversity Minister announced the beginning of the Thriving Kids program in the ACT. (ABC News: Callum Flinn)

In short:

The ACT has announced the first phase of Thriving Kids, a program aimed at helping children under nine experiencing developmental delay get access to support.

Those on the NDIS before 2028 will stay on it, subject to usual reassessment criteria, but critics say those who are diverted to Thriving Kids may face longer waits for diagnosis and care.

What's next?

Thriving Kids starts on October 1 and is expected to be fully implemented by January 2028.

The ACT government says children under the age of nine will not yet be removed from the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) as it begins to roll out the Thriving Kids program.

A federal government initiative, Thriving Kids was announced as a way to better support children experiencing developmental delays.

Today, ACT Disability, Autism and Neurodiversity Minister Suzanne Orr announced Thriving Kids would begin to operate in the ACT from October 1, ahead of its official start date in January 2028.

All states and territories have opted in except for Queensland, but there are concerns it could mean some children will lose vital support.

Suzanne Orr says Thriving Kids offers families an opportunity to ask questions. (ABC News: Mark Moore)

But Ms Orr stressed that those currently accessing the NDIS would not lose that support.

Thriving Kids offers a phone line for parents or guardians to ring if they have questions about their child's development, regardless of whether they have a diagnosis or not.

"If you have a question on your child's development or delay, if you want to check in with an allied health professional, we will have those supports here,"

'You've got a place to go'

Federal NDIS Minister Mark Butler released the model for Thriving Kids in February as part of a major overhaul of the way disability support is accessed and received in Australia.

Announced last August, the program aims to divert children under nine years old with "mild to moderate" developmental delays and autism away from the NDIS.

The first stage of the model centres on identification, where delays or support needs could be identified by a family member, a school or a health professional.

The next stage connects families to the appropriate support, and the later stages focus on providing that care depending on the individual needs of the child.

Some children will fall into categories deemed to be "low", "moderate" or "low to moderate" need.

Children with "significant permanent disability" will remain eligible for the NDIS. 

Those on the scheme before 2028 will stay on it, subject to the usual reassessment criteria.

The National Disability Insurance Scheme is being overhauled by the federal government.

"The first phase of the rollout will be a scaling up of our existing services, so we'll have more supports there to assist more people in our community and we'll continue to see that build," Ms Orr said today.

"And if you have a concern, if you just have a question, you've got a place to go."

Ms Orr said the program was "not about moving people off the NDIS".

"This is about meeting that unmet need within our community," she said.

"We'll continue to provide those supports and we'll continue to work through what it looks like for access to the NDIS and how Thriving Kids and the NDIS link."

Education Minister Rachel Stephen-Smith said the funding would provide a "single point of contact".

"[It's] a clearer pathway for parents, carers and families where they do have concerns about their child's developmental delay or any aspect of their child's development," she said.

'A bit 1980s'

Autism Awareness Australia chief executive Nicole Rogerson told 666 ABC Canberra they still knew little about what the Thriving Kids program would deliver.

"A phone line? Okay, great. It feels a bit 1980s, but sure,"

"What are the people on the phone line going to tell the parents when they ring?"

Autism Awareness Australia CEO Nicole Rogerson says they know little detail about Thriving Kids. (ABC News: Billy Cooper)

Ms Orr said that with early intervention created by Thriving Kids, they would be "getting more people the support they need when they need it, which will actually work in helping to move through the system more seamlessly when you need it as opposed to having to wait".

But Ms Rogerson said with children able to access it without a diagnosis, the service would likely be overwhelmed.

"It's going to capture children with ADHD, children with speech and language delays, it's going to capture children with behavioural issues," she said.

"All I see in the future is wait times.

"And the real reality is that children with autism need quality early intervention."
