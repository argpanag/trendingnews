​China Now Has Not Only KJ-2000s, Resembling russia's A-50s, but Also the Latest KJ-3000s – What Is Known About These Aircraft? | Defense Express

TheTools · 25.10.2026 07:25 · source · permalink

Publicly available satellite images show that China has four latest KJ-3000 AEW&C aircraft, which are based on the Y-20B military transport aircraft, suggesting that mass production may have begun.

In this context, it is interesting to note that the KJ-3000s are intended to replace a small fleet of four KJ-2000 aircraft, which are based on the Il-76 and resemble the russian A-50s.

The primary source is a TWZ report, whose authors cite satellite imagery provided by Vantor.

There is some disagreement among experts regarding the likely start of mass production of the KJ-3000s. According to one account, all four KJ-3000s seen in the photographs may be the first production aircraft. Others suggest that they are prototypes previously photographed elsewhere and have now simply been brought together at the XAC aircraft plant in Xi'an-Yanliang.

It is particularly important that the KJ-3000 is based on the Y-20B variant, which is equipped with Chinese-made engines. This provides the necessary foundation for reliable aircraft maintenance.

At the same time, little is known about the avionics suite installed on the KJ-3000, making it difficult to clearly distinguish the aircraft from its "predecessor," the KJ-2000, which is based on the russian Il-76.

According to reports on the KJ-2000, its electronic warfare system is said to have a target detection range of up to 500 kilometers and can simultaneously track "several hundred" targets. These aircraft entered service in 2007. It is more likely an entirely Chinese design based on the Il-76 that merely resembles the russian A-50.

According to Western analysts, it is currently difficult for China to obtain additional Il-76s or spare parts for its existing aircraft of this type from russia. For this reason, the Chinese decided to develop a new KJ-3000 AEW&C aircraft.

Ivan Kyrychevskyi, serviceman of the 413th Raid Regiment of the Unmanned Systems Forces of Ukraine, weapons expert at Defense Express.
