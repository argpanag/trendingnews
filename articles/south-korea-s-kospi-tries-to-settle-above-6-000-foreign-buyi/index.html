South Korea's KOSPI Tries to Settle Above 6,000; Foreign Buying Streak Lifts Index to 6,040 Range — BigGo Finance

TheTools · 29.10.2026 01:06 · source · permalink

South Korea's KOSPI, which reclaimed the 6,000 level after a 6% surge the previous session, is now fluctuating around the flat line just a day later. In early trading, profit-taking and bargain hunting are squaring off, with the index searching for direction around 6,040.

According to the Korea Exchange on the 29th, KOSPI was trading at 6,049.71 as of 9:14 a.m., up 0.62% from the previous session. The index opened at 6,017.22, up 0.08%, then modestly extended its gains to stay above the 6,000 mark. At the same time, KOSDAQ was down 0.98% at 763.76, with most of its largest stocks by market capitalization trading lower.

On the supply-demand front, foreign investors are extending their buying streak to a second day. Foreigners and retail investors net bought 195.5 billion won (approximately $146.4 million) and 13.3 billion won (approximately $10.0 million), respectively, while institutions net sold 205.7 billion won (approximately $154.1 million). On KOSDAQ, retail investors bought 59.1 billion won (approximately $44.3 million), while foreigners and institutions sold 34.9 billion won (approximately $26.1 million) and 14.7 billion won (approximately $11.0 million), respectively.

Large-cap stocks diverged by sector. SK Hynix rose 3.56%, extending the previous session's sharp rally, while SK Square (3.58%) and Samsung Electronics (0.93%) also traded higher. In contrast, LG Energy Solution plunged 7.57%, while Hyundai Motor (-1.86%) and KB Financial (-0.97%) also declined. Among KOSDAQ heavyweights, Ecopro (-2.61%), Ecopro BM (-3.28%), Rainbow Robotics (-3.09%) and Wonik IPS (-3.12%) all fell.

Overnight, New York stocks closed slightly higher after the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) decided to hold its benchmark interest rate steady, but mixed earnings from U.S. big tech are capping the upside for the South Korean market. Microsoft (MS) gained 2-3% in after-hours trading, while Alphabet plunged about 10% after hours on weak search advertising revenue and deteriorating free cash flow (FCF).

Han Ji-young, an analyst at Kiwoom Securities, noted that the previous day's SK Hynix earnings and conference call had partly dispelled the market's doubts about its profits. "For the time being, this could help stabilize supply and demand not only for SK Hynix but for the South Korean stock market as a whole," he said. SK Hynix has announced plans to buy back 20 trillion won (approximately $15.0 billion) of its own shares and pay 14 trillion won (approximately $10.5 billion) in cash dividends.

The exchange rate is cited as a burden. In the Seoul foreign exchange market on the 29th, the won-dollar exchange rate stood at 1,337.90 won, down 0.42% from the previous session, but Seo Sang-young, an analyst at Mirae Asset Securities, said, "It is worth noting that the dollar-won exchange rate rose into the 1,340 range despite dollar weakness." He added, "Foreign net buying has resumed recently, but if won weakness persists, the continuity of buying could be limited."

A key intraday variable is Samsung Electronics' third-quarter earnings conference call scheduled for the day. There is also lingering speculation that short-term profit-taking could hit the large semiconductor stocks that led the previous day's rally. Market participants are watching whether the index can settle above 6,000, the sustainability of foreign buying, and exchange-rate trends.
