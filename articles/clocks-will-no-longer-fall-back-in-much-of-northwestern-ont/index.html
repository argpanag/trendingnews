Clocks will no longer fall back in much of Northwestern Ont.

TheTools · 16.10.2026 12:11 · source · permalink

KENORA — On November 1, when the rest of the province turns their clocks back one hour, communities in Northwestern Ontario will stay on permanent central daylight time.

In an announcement on Tuesday, the province said that the regulatory change will “ensure there is no disruption for businesses, families and Indigenous communities in Ontario’s central time zone.”

The regulatory change will affect communities west of the 90th meridian, near Raith.

During the winter months, those communities will share the same time as the rest of Ontario.

“Maintaining alignment with Manitoba would help businesses operate more efficiently across provincial boundaries, while making it easier for residents and service providers to coordinate activities such as health-care appointments and travel,” read the province’s release.

The decision follows Manitoba Premier Wab Kinew's announcement that they too would end seasonal clock changes, which prompted engagement with leaders throughout the region who surveyed their respective communities.

The surveys all yielded results overwhelmingly in support of ending the time change.
