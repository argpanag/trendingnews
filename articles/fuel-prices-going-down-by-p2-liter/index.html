Fuel prices going down by P2/liter

TheTools · 22.11.2026 10:59 · source · permalink

MANILA, Philippines — A rollback of at least P2 per liter is looming at the pumps next week, potentially marking the third straight round of price cuts, even as the Strait of Hormuz remains closed.

Early industry estimates point to a reduction of P2.35 to P2.55 per liter for diesel and P2 to P2.15 per liter for gasoline.

The projected adjustments are based on the four-day trading in the regional benchmark Mean of Platts Singapore, although they have yet to account for freight and insurance costs.

Jetti Petroleum president Leo Bellas said oil prices declined this week due to growing expectations of a supply surplus, coupled with a weaker demand outlook.

The continued slide in prices comes despite fading hopes of an imminent reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a key shipping route whose closure has fueled concerns over supply disruptions.

“Increasing supply from the Middle East through alternative routes continue to weigh on the market, although risks are still being priced in as attacks on shipping and Saudi Arabia’s energy infrastructure continue, and the US blockade on Iran remains,” Bellas said.

While the Philippines sources most of its refined petroleum products like gasoline and diesel from other Asian countries, refineries in these markets remain heavily reliant on crude oil imports from the Middle East.
