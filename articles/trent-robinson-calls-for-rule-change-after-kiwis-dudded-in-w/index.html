Trent Robinson calls for rule change after Kiwis dudded in World Cup final

TheTools · 16.11.2026 01:44 · source · permalink

The Kiwis had a try overturned at a critical stage.

Champion Roosters coach Trent Robinson has called for a rule change around the interpretation of knock-ons during mid-air contests. And his comments came after New Zealand were denied what could have been a crucial try in the Rugby League World Cup final.

With Australia leading 28-6 with 22 minutes to play, Ronaldo Mulitalo thought he'd got New Zealand a little bit closer. The winger went up for a high ball in a contest with Tom Trbojevic, before coming down with it and planting the ball down.

To the naked eye it appeared a clean catch, but the Bunker official found a knock-on from Mulitalo after slowing it down to the nth degree. The Kiwi lost possession into Trbojevic's arm before regaining control and scoring.

To the latter of the law it was the correct decision, but according to Robinson it might be time for a rule change. Speaking in commentary for Channel 7, the Roosters coach said he believes whoever comes down with the ball after a contest should be declared as having possession regardless of whether there was a tiny knock-on.

Ronaldo Mulitalo was found to have knocked on. Image: Channel 7

"I'd say that there may be a discussion in the off-season about contests in the air and whether we allow that to be a try or not," Robinson said. Fans have been complaining for years that the Bunker tries to find reasons not to allow tries, often slowing down a replay of a contest to within an inch of its life.

Andrew Johns is often heard saying "the game isn't played in slow motion", and if Robinson gets his way there might be a tweak to the rules in the off-season. The try would have made the score 28-10 with a kick to come, and considering Isaiah Papali'i scored just five minutes later the Aussies' lead might have only been 10 points with 15 minutes to play.

Stacey Jones concedes New Zealand were poor

Instead the Kangaroos kicked away and won 32-12 to claim their fourth World Cup in a row. "It was our worst performance," Kiwi coach Stacey Jones conceded. "Got off to a bad start and couldn't fight our way out of it… Possession was too much for us.

"The start didn't help and then we came up with errors, our discipline was poor and we gave away penalties. They had all the energy. They were running harder, they had more energy with their line speed. When you make errors and tour discipline is poor, no matter what team you are, it's too hard."
