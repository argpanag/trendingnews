Updates

Huang has spent over 3 decades building Nvidia

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has spent more than three decades building a company that has made him one of the most powerful figures in tech.

If you walked into a San Jose, California, branch of Denny’s a couple of years ago, you may have spotted a sign above one of the diner’s red booths: “THE BOOTH THAT LAUNCHED A $2 TRILLION COMPANY.”

It was there, in 1993, that Huang cooked up the idea for Nvidia with co-founders and fellow electrical engineers Curtis Priem and Chris Malachowsky.

The trio wanted to design semiconductor chips that could create more realistic graphics for video games, signing a deal with Japanese gaming developer Sega. But they made a strategic blunder within their first decade that nearly tanked the firm: They had designed an unconventional 3D chip that few developers could use. It was a costly mistake, helping send Nvidia hurtling toward bankruptcy. An eleventh-hour $5 million investment from Sega provided Nvidia a crucial lifeline.

Nvidia spent the next decade in gaming. But by the mid-aughts, Huang realized that some of its chips – containing processing units that could perform many calculations in tandem – could have applications beyond gaming.

It developed CUDA, software that allowed computer programmers to use its chips more generally. In 2016, Huang was filmed delivering Nvidia’s first AI supercomputer to Elon Musk, the co-founder of what was then a little-known research lab called OpenAI.

A few years later, when OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public, Nvidia was already in position to capitalize on the ensuing boom.

Beijing says espionage claims are excuse for American "technological blockade on China"

Beijing has again denied allegations of espionage on Thursday, after having previously rejected claims that Alice Huang had worked as a Chinese spy while in a relationship with Jensen Huang.

“We have already made our position clear on the so-called claims of ‘Chinese espionage.’ China’s technological advances are driven by independent innovation and open cooperation,” Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Mao Ning told reporters at a regular briefing.

He added that the US and China were “maintaining close communication regarding interactions between the two countries’ leaders,” and that Beijing was “ready to work with the US to properly implement the important common understandings reached by the two heads of state.”

The Nvidia espionage allegations come at a particularly awkward time. US President Donald Trump is heading to Shenzhen next week for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, where he is expected to meet Chinese leader Xi Jinping for the third time this year.

Sources say Bessent confronted Lutnick over not labeling Nvidia customer a national security threat

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent challenged Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over his department’s reluctance to label Nvidia customer Megaspeed a possible national security threat in a Situation Room confrontation last week, two people familiar with the matter told CNN, illustrating the tension inside the Trump administration over Chinese access to Nvidia chips.

Trump officials have long harbored concerns that Megaspeed has circumvented US law and funneled advanced chips into China — a prospect that Treasury staffers argued should land it on Commerce’s so-called entity list of China-connected companies that could pose national security risks. But the issue gathered newfound attention and importance in recent days when Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an affair with a Megaspeed employee suspected of being a Chinese spy.

Senior Trump officials, including Lutnick, also were briefed months ago about Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, a former director at Megaspeed who the intelligence community suspected of being a Chinese spy, people familiar with the matter said.

But despite those warnings, Commerce has so far refused to put Megaspeed on the entity list, the people said.

Confronted on it by Bessent last Thursday, Lutnick did not immediately commit to taking any specific action, they said.

Megaspeed has denied any wrongdoing and said it is a “compliant and legal company.” China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs called the allegations that Alice Huang was a Chinese spy “groundless speculation and an attempt to smear and malign China.”

Chinese university bought Nvidia H200 chips despite US blacklist

A Chinese university linked with the People’s Liberation Army bought two of Nvidia’s powerful H200 chips last month, according to a filing on the university’s website, circumventing a US-imposed blocklist on the institution. CNN’s Will Ripley finds out more.

Kimberly Powell, reportedly involved with Huang, has been at Nvidia for "18 wonderful years"

The New York Times reported late Wednesday that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is said to have had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, the vice president of healthcare at the company.

CNN has reached out to Powell for comment.

This is the second time this week accusations of an affair with Huang has been reported, after his suspected relationship with an alleged Chinese spy made international news.

Powell has been with Nvidia since 2008, where she oversees the company’s healthcare business – which includes “hardware and software platforms for accelerated computing, AI, and visualization that power the ecosystem of medical imaging, life sciences, drug discovery, and healthcare analytics,” according to her bio page on the Nvidia website.

She previously led Nvidia’s higher education and research business, along with other programs.

According to her LinkedIn page, she worked at a display technology company before her time at Nvidia and has an electrical engineering degree.

In podcast episodes and media interviews, she has espoused a future vision of healthcare where AI is used across the medical system – from imaging and radiology to helping automate workflows.

At one point in the episode, Farr asked Powell how long she had worked at Nvidia. “18 wonderful years,” Powell responded.

China’s internet is riveted by the Jensen Huang saga, too

China’s internet has been lit up with discussion of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, with posts about the scandal around the tech mogul capturing billions of views on China’s Weibo, an X-like social media platform, in recent days.

Much of that is down to Huang’s celebrity status in China. The billionaire is a household name and has attracted huge attention during past visits to the country, where images of him eating noodles and sipping douzhi’er, the fermented soybean drink, have gone viral.

Many Chinese internet users expressed understanding about Huang’s behavior, with some also rallying around Alice Huang, a woman with whom he reportedly had a relationship. Alice Huang (no relation) has been accused by the US of working as a spy for China. Beijing has denied the allegations.

In videos posted on the platform RedNote, some users dissected outfits worn by businesswoman Huang and her interactions with the Nvidia chief.

Chinese state media earlier this week slammed reports that Nvidia’s Huang was the target of Chinese espionage, framing the allegations as part of an effort to stir up US-China confrontation while also blaming racism and McCarthyism.

After reports later emerged that Huang had another extramarital affair – with an Nvidia employee – some social media users picked up the thread, with one joking beneath a post about that affair: “honeytrap.”

CNN’s Fred He contributed to this report.

Unclear whether Alice Huang was working for China's top intelligence agency, analyst says

Though US officials believe Alice Huang was working as a Chinese spy while conducting a relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang (of no relation), questions remain about how she came to occupy that role – and whether she had been a spy from the beginning of their involvement.

“No one seems to have suggested that she herself was an MSS (China’s Ministry of State Security) officer,” said former CIA counterintelligence analyst Peter Mattis, now president of defense policy think tank The Jamestown Foundation.

It’s hard to tell whether she was already working for Chinese intelligence when she first met Jensen, he added.

Beijing has called the accusations of spying an attempt to malign and smear China.

The FBI raised concerns to lawmakers that Alice Huang might be feeding information to China nearly a year ago, two people familiar with the matter told CNN.

Alice Huang’s current whereabouts are unclear — and she has made no public statement or appearance since last year, when reports of the suspicions surrounding her relationship with Jensen Huang first broke.

Southeast Asia offers China a loophole for buying banned US chips, analyst says

The US has export controls on many advanced AI chips, banning their sale to China, but an analyst told CNN that some Chinese companies have found a way around it: going through Southeast Asia.

The Nvidia spy scandal has shone a spotlight on this loophole. The alleged spy whom Jensen Huang reportedly had a relationship with was a director at Singapore-based cloud computing and data center provider Megaspeed in 2023.

Trump officials have long harbored concerns that Megaspeed has circumvented US law and funneled advanced chips into China. Megaspeed has denied any wrongdoing and said it is “compliant and legal.”

Megaspeed has purchased more than $4 billion worth of Nvidia’s semiconductors, by some analyst estimates.

Chip shipments can pass through Southeast Asia before reaching China because “there is minimal scrutiny on US air data,” he added. “You can bring things into Singapore, Malaysia, Central and Southeast Asia, and then move those goods, sometimes through Thailand, into China.”

The stakes are high: the chips powering the AI race could determine who is the world’s greatest power, more than traditional weaponry or military might, said Taipei-based technology journalist Tim Culpan.

Who's on the board of directors discussing Huang's fate?

All eyes are on Nvidia’s board of directors, which is meeting late Wednesday night to discuss the fate of scandal-hit CEO Jensen Huang – with the company’s shares dropping sharply, and no clear successor.

The board is made up of 11 directors, including Huang himself. In an unusual arrangement, there is no board chair, according to the lineup on Nvidia’s website.

The directors include heavy hitters from various sectors – former executives from global brands like Taco Bell, Pepsi-Cola and the NFL – as well as academics, scientists and financial leaders.

Four of them have been with the company, and known Huang, since the 1990s. The others joined between 2013 to 2026.

The fact that many directors share a long history with Huang has raised questions about whether they can make objective decisions about his future at the company and the ongoing investigation, Reuters reported.

On Tuesday, Nvidia’s board announced it had retained high-profile New York law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead the investigation into whether Huang was a target of a foreign intelligence effort.

Jensen Huang has long been a bridge between US and China

Nvidia chief Jensen Huang may have unwittingly found himself in the middle of a spy scandal involving Chinese spies and American chips – but he has long positioned himself as a bridge between the two rival countries and top economies.

Huang moved from Taiwan to Thailand as a child and then to the United States – a background that gave him the language and cultural skills needed to navigate business both in the US and in China.

As Huang grew closer to President Donald Trump, he also gained visibility with Beijing; when Chinese leader Xi Jinping made a state visit in September, Huang was among several top tech executives having dinner at the head table with Trump and Xi.

And Huang joined Trump during a layover in Alaska on the way to Beijing in May, accompanying him and his top aides on Air Force One. A source told CNN that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had briefed Trump on intelligence that Huang was in a relationship with a potential Chinese spy before the Nvidia CEO got on the plane – though Trump on Monday publicly denied knowing about the allegations at that time.

It was during that visit to Beijing that Huang charmed much of the Chinese internet – and watchers around the world – when he took himself on an impromptu food tour of the Chinese capital.

“Jensen, how do you like China?” a woman is heard asking him in one video. The Nvidia CEO responded: “I love China.”

Trump is set to visit China again next week. When asked on Sunday whether he would bring Huang along on his trip, the president responded, “I don’t know, but I’d certainly take him.” It’s not clear if Huang plans to go.

Democrats urge Trump officials to preserve Nvidia documents, raise questions of inaction

Two Democratic senators, Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons, issued a letter to several top Trump administration officials on Wednesday, asking them to preserve all Nvidia-related documents – and questioning whether they had known about the allegations surrounding Jensen Huang and failed to act.

The letter was addressed to Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, urging their three respective departments to preserve all documents “regarding the export of NVIDIA’s advanced AI chips to China or to entities that may divert such chips to China.”

Huang is under intense scrutiny after reports that he had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, of no relation, who was working as a spy for China.

They also asked the officials to brief Congress on what risks the alleged foreign intelligence operation could have posed to the US’ national security.

For Taiwan, a mutually beneficial relationship just got complicated

Jensen Huang has spent years cultivating a personal relationship with Taiwan, the island of his birth.

Whenever he visits, he eats at local restaurants, speaks comfortably in Taiwanese Hokkien as well as Mandarin, poses for endless selfies and signs autographs – including at least once across a female fan’s chest – turning routine business trips into media events.

For many here, Nvidia’s success feels partly their own – reflected in the surging stock prices in Taiwan and the booming export figures driven by huge demand for AI.

And Huang – who was born in the city of Tainan and spent the first five years of his life on the island – is one of their own. He has lavishly praised Taiwan and given it massive global visibility.

Tim Culpan, a technology journalist who has known Huang for more than 25 years, says the affair itself isn’t necessarily what troubles Taiwanese people most; it’s the reported connection to Chinese intelligence.

Up until now it’s been a mutually beneficial relationship. Taiwan produces the advanced chips underpinning Nvidia’s dominance, while facing daily pressure from Beijing, which considers Taiwan part of its territory. The possibility that someone close to Huang was working for Chinese intelligence touches a particularly raw nerve.

There’s money at stake, too. Many Taiwanese own shares in Nvidia or its local suppliers. TSMC – the island’s crown jewel, which produces more than 90% of the world’s advanced microchips – makes up roughly 40% of Taiwan’s benchmark stock index alone.

Nvidia is also investing more than $1 billion in a new Taipei headquarters, expected to open in 2030 – a major bet on Taiwan that city officials insist remains on track despite the scandal.

Huang’s popularity won’t disappear overnight. But in Taiwan, where business, technology and homeland security are inseparable, this is no longer just a story about his private life.

Why Nvidia’s chips are so sought after by China

The allegations that China’s spy service targeted Jensen Huang through a woman he had an affair with will have far-reaching implications for the US-China race to dominate AI.

At the center of this fight are Nvidia’s AI chips, which China has long sought to access.

For years, the US government has restricted China’s access to advanced American technology in an effort to slow its progress on AI. During his first term, Trump imposed restrictions on Chinese giant Huawei; the Biden administration introduced further export controls on US-made semiconductors, advanced chips and chip-making equipment.

Since then, China’s chip technology has made significant strides. Experts say Huawei has developed chips with performance comparable to — and in some cases surpassing — some of Nvidia’s chips.

Perhaps sensing that China is getting ahead anyway, regardless of export controls, the Trump administration reversed its stance last year and approved the sale of a key Nvidia chip, the H20. At the time, Trump argued the H20 was “obsolete” as it lags behind Nvidia’s more advanced Blackwell or H100 chips – which are still blocked.

Despite China’s progress, Nvidia’s chips remain highly coveted by Chinese companies, and are sometimes smuggled in. Alice Huang is alleged to have helped facilitate this smuggling.

Just last month, a Chinese university linked with the People’s Liberation Army (PLA) bought two of Nvidia’s powerful H200 chips from Shaanxi Bosuan Network Technology, circumventing a US-imposed blocklist on the institution, according to a filing on the university’s website.

Reuters first reported the purchase. Neither the Northwestern Polytechnical University nor Shaanxi Bosuan Network Technology responded to phone calls from CNN seeking comment.

Timeline of events for Nvidia and its embattled CEO, Jensen Huang

Nvidia’s board is reportedly meeting Wednesday evening to discuss CEO Jensen Huang’s future as its leader, following a rough few days for the embattled CEO. Here is how Huang, and Nvidia, got here.

Wall Street weighs in on Nvidia

Nvidia shares (NVDA) were down 8.6% in after-hours trading as of 8 p.m. ET as Wall Street digests the developments surrounding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.

“If Jensen Huang is ousted as is expected, an already challenging environment for Nvidia investors and employees will become considerably more difficult,” Mike O’Rourke, chief market strategist at JonesTrading, told CNN in an email.

“Nvidia is currently the most important company in the world,” he said. “The new CEO will need to make an extensive appraisal of the company and Jensen’s successor will not have the same latitude or deference to pursue circular deals and pursue aggressive sales tactics.”

Dan Ives, partner and senior managing director at Yorkville Ives & Co., wrote in a note that he thinks “the writing is on the wall and Jensen’s days at Nvidia could be numbered.”

“While Jensen is the hearts and lungs of the Nvidia story… the company and its leading edge GPU technology will go on and it does not change the entrenched position Nvidia has in the AI Revolution chip arms race,” Ives said.

While Nvidia shares tumbled, shares of other chipmakers rose: Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) gained 4.3% and Broadcom (AVGO) gained 2.4%.

Huang is at “the epicenter for deal making in the AI complex,” said Michael Monaghan, portfolio manager of the Founders 100 ETF, a fund focused on founder-led companies. “Without him, that goes away, and so it opens up more opportunities for AMD, more opportunities for [Broadcom].”

“If Jensen the kingmaker is gone, it could really become a more free-for-all playing field,” Monaghan told CNN. “We still need to see what the board’s going to do.”

Nvidia scandal comes before Trump's high-stakes trip to Shenzhen

Jensen Huang’s spy scandal and the spectacular fallout for Nvidia comes at a fraught time for US-China relations, and a sensitive one for the White House, as President Donald Trump prepares for a trip to Shenzhen.

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit will take place next week in the southern Chinese tech and innovation hub, where Trump is expected to meet Chinese leader Xi Jinping for the third time so far this year.

Bilateral relations remain fraught. A fragile trade truce between the world’s two biggest economies will expire in January, and analysts say the risk of additional tariffs and other trade barriers will continue to loom in the new year.

Other experts say that Trump might be more willing to play hard ball during future negotiations, now that the midterm elections are over, and Trump no longer has to worry about how Chinese countermeasure might sway voters.

At the Shenzhen summit, Xi and Trump may also hold a historic meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin on the sidelines. Last month, Trump said he was open to this idea, telling reporters: “We would have it to talk about nuclear weapons and denuclearization … I’m sure we’d talk about other things like trade too.”

"Jensen has created a black eye moment." Wall Street tech analyst says CEO likely must go

Dan Ives, one of the biggest AI bulls on Wall Street, says the “writing is on the wall” that Jensen Huang’s days leading Nvidia are likely numbered.

Ives cited The New York Times report that Huang had an affair with a direct report.

“The reality is that the old Jensen is now gone in the eyes of the public, customer and technology world,” the tech analyst wrote. “Nvidia likely now has to move on without Jensen at the helm, which seemed like a Twilight Zone episode narrative before Sunday afternoon when the news broke.”

Huang is widely viewed as irreplaceable and Nvidia failed to groom a successor who could step in, a decision that now looks highly questionable.

If the board pushes out Huang, it risks spooking already-rattled investors about who will steer the ship. With Huang in charge, Nvidia is worth $300 per share, compared with $225 without him, Ives says.

"We’re past the tipping point." Ex-SEC chair says Nvidia is now likely to oust Jensen Huang

The allegation that Nvidia’s Jensen Huang had a second affair is likely to force the board of directors to oust the rockstar CEO, former Securities and Exchange Commission chairman Gary Gensler told CNN on Wednesday evening.

Gensler, now a professor at MIT, said Nvidia was already dealing with a massive public relation crisis caused by Huang’s admitted affair.

US officials have told CNN that Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, who they have concluded is a Chinese spy.

Alice Huang was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer under investigation for allegedly diverting cutting-edge chips to China.

Gensler said Megaspeed appears to be a front company, masking its true backers. “It’s a cardboard cut-out of a company,” he said. “It’s like a stage front on a Hollywood lot.”
