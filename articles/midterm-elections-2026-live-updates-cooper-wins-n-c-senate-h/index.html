Midterm elections 2026 live updates: Cooper wins N.C. Senate, House control 'leans' Dem

TheTools · 03.11.2026 17:00 · source · permalink

This is CNBC's live coverage of the 2026 midterm elections.

Polls are closing across the U.S. as voters are deciding control of Congress in an election that could define President Donald Trump's final years in office.

CNBC's Election Desk projects that control of the U.S. House "leans Democratic" and that Democrats will win a minimum of 205 of the chamber's 435 seats with Republicans winning at least 160. Democrats flipped the North Carolina Senate seat with a victory by former Gov. Roy Cooper.

All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are at stake, as Republicans try to defend narrow majorities. Voter frustration with Trump's war in Iran, inflation, perceived government corruption and immigration policy could weigh down Republicans. But the GOP has the bulk of billionaire campaign spending.

Deep-red Senate seats such as Kansas, South Carolina and Nebraska could be in play while Republicans could win in traditional Democratic strongholds such as Minnesota and New Hampshire. And toss-up races in states including Alaska, Iowa, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas will determine who wins the gavels next Congress.

The Associated Press provides vote data and race projections for CNBC. Polls began closing at 6 p.m. ET, with Alaska being the last to close at 1 a.m. ET.

Michigan's open Senate seat is a crucial Republican pickup opportunity and a must-win for Democrats hoping to retake the chamber. Progressive Democrat Abdul El-Sayed faces Republican former Rep. Mike Rogers in a state Trump narrowly carried in 2024.

The race tests whether economic populism can win back working-class voters who have drifted toward Republicans. El-Sayed, who would become the nation's first Muslim senator, champions Medicare for All and taxing billionaires. Rogers has embraced Trump while strategically distancing himself in recent weeks amid political headwinds. 

The race took a stunning turn Sunday when Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens, whom El-Sayed defeated in the primary, crossed party lines to endorse Rogers over disagreements about Israel. The defection could help Rogers court moderates, but it also energized El-Sayed's supporters, fueling a reported $2.4 million fundraising surge that day.

The implications of the race also stretch to the 2028 presidential election. An El-Sayed victory would strengthen progressives' case that their economic message can win in Trump territory. A Rogers win would end a 32-year Republican Senate drought in Michigan and sharpen questions about whether Democrats' ideological divisions are costing them winnable races.

Polls showing who looks likely to win key battleground districts and which party looks set to control Congress have widely circulated in recent weeks, but even the most methodologically rigorous polls don't always correctly predict the election winner.

Polls often contain errors that are hard to avoid, despite best efforts. According to the American Association of Public Opinion Research, the absolute average polling error in the 2024 election was 7.0 points in either direction for the House of Representatives, and 3.3 points for the Senate. Factors such as voter turnout, last-minute campaigning and changes in the president's approval rating can produce election results that ultimately differ from polls in the weeks and months leading up to it.

— Caleigh Keating

California voters will decide on a measure, Proposition 40, that would impose a one-time 5% tax on billionaires in the state. The tax would apply to about 200 residents worth at least $1 billion as of Jan. 1, 2026, excluding real property and certain pensions and retirement accounts. Most proceeds would go toward healthcare, with the remainder set aside for education and food assistance. Backers say the tax would be a response to Trump-era funding cuts that could result in some Californians losing coverage.

Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont supports the measure, as does Rep. Ro Khanna, a Democrat representing several Silicon Valley cities. Opponents include California Gov. Gavin Newsom, a Democrat, along with billionaires, from Alphabet's Sergey Brin to venture capitalist Peter Thiel. Early SpaceX and Tesla investor Tim Draper posted on X that he voted against the measure, saying it is "unconstitutional" and "would drive businesses and jobs out of state."

The tax requires a majority vote to pass. About 46% of likely voters said they would vote for Proposition 40 in an October poll from the Public Policy Institute of California, a nonpartisan think tank.

Advocates are telling voters to reject Proposition 41 and Proposition 42, which conflict with Proposition 40. Proposition 41 would block new taxes that exclude revenues from state spending limits, while Proposition 42 would stand in the way of new taxes on' personal property or past activities.

Proposition 40 might not become law if either of the other two receives more "yes" votes, according to the state voter information guide. Polls close at 11 p.m. ET.

— Jordan Novet

Markets may be nervous ahead of the midterm elections, but history suggests that anxiety often fades quickly. Recent CNBC analysis found that the S&P 500 gained an average of 13.7% in the year following midterm elections, with positive returns in 91% of cases.

"Once you are through it, it's a big relief, and markets tend to do pretty well," Ryan Detrick of Carson Group told CNBC.

A Nov. 3 Deutsche Bank note points to a similar pattern. The bank said midterm years are usually the weakest part of the four-year cycle because of political uncertainty, which eases once the election is over. "The market is buying a 'known' outcome," the bank said. The usual result is divided government and gridlock, which reduces the risk of major policy changes.

Deutsche Bank looked at the S&P 500's performance over the 12 months following every midterm vote since 1930 and found that the index rose after 22 of 24 midterms. The analysis showed that historically the broad market and most individual stocks are likely to be higher in 12 months, with both median and average gains in double digits.

"But given today's concentration, whether the run extends to a 22nd consecutive positive post-midterm period likely depends on the performance of just a handful of stocks," the bank said.
