The FAA in October gave Washington state officials a November 30 deadline to produce records supporting the state's assertion that aviation fuel tax proceeds are not being illegally diverted away from airports. In another move welcomed by AOPA, the agency ruled that a ground lease moratorium imposed by the city of Cle Elum violates the city's federal grant obligation.

While the two matters are not related beyond geography, they represent strong moves by the FAA to address concerns raised by general aviation advocates, said AOPA Northwest Mountain Regional Manager Brad Schuster. The October 14 letter from FAA Director of the Office of Airport Compliance Michael Helvey includes a detailed request for records:

While the U.S. Department of Transportation and the FAA have worked with AOPA for years to pressure officials in Washington and four other states (California, Kentucky, Nevada, and Tennessee) to stop illegally diverting fuel tax receipts into the general fund, Helvey's demand for detailed records "is arguably the most forceful that we have seen to date," Schuster said.

The agency's recent determination, which follows a Part 13 complaint filed  by users of Cle Elum Municipal Airport, is another example of the agency actively enforcing federal law and grant obligations. The municipality cited Chapter 9 bankruptcy proceedings as the reason it halted issuing ground leases to airport tenants, an argument the FAA flatly rejected.

"While the FAA acknowledges the City's financial circumstances, those circumstances do not relieve the City of its continuing obligations under the Federal grant assurances," wrote FAA Regional Compliance Program Manager Peter Doyle. "The FAA further notes that the City's response indicates the leasing moratorium is indefinite and will remain in effect until the City's bankruptcy proceedings are concluded and it determines whether it will continue operating the airport… Those considerations do not supersede the City's continuing obligations under the Airport Improvement Program grant assurances. In addition, a policy that categorically prevents all prospective aeronautical users from accessing airport property constitutes an unreasonable restriction on aeronautical access, even when applied uniformly."

The FAA directed the city to submit a corrective action plan.

"We may not always see eye-to-eye with the FAA on every issue, but they deserve a tip of the cap for responding forcefully," Schuster said. "We welcome these actions, and hope that both the city and state will respond as directed.
