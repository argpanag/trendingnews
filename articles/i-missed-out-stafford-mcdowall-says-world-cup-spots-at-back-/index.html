'I missed out': Stafford McDowall says World Cup spots at back of Scottish minds

TheTools · 21.11.2026 17:17 · source · permalink

Stafford McDowall is adamant Scotland will not let injuries derail them after they suffered another late call-off ahead of Saturday’s Nations Championship clash with Japan.

Having lost captain Sione Tuipulotu to a knee issue on the eve of last Sunday’s clash with Australia, the Scots had to contend with more pre-match disruption as Rory Darge, who had been named as skipper to face the Cherry Blossoms at Murrayfield this weekend, pulled out due to a shoulder problem.

That resulted in Matt Fagerson being added to the starting XV at number eight, with Ollie Leatherbarrow switching to flanker. Back-rower Freddy Douglas – not in the initial 23 – is now on the substitutes’ bench.

In addition to Darge and Tuipulotu, the Scots are also without Ben White, Jack Dempsey, Pierre Schoeman, Scott Cummings, Josh Bayliss, Fergus Burke and Alex Craig through injury, while other senior players such as Finn Russell, Huw Jones, Kyle Steyn and Ewan Ashman have been given the weekend off.

“I’d say, apart from Darge’s one, that a lot of the boys that were injured probably weren’t involved this week anyway,” said McDowall, promoted to captain after Darge’s withdrawal.

“As a group we’ve had a really good training week together. Darge actually didn’t do much training. It was probably a similar situation to Sione last week.

“Matt had run quite a lot at eight and Ollie at seven. We’ve had a really good training week now so there’s no excuse. The team that’s playing has had time together.”

McDowall is one of 10 players added to a heavily rotated Scotland side after last weekend’s hard-fought win over Australia.

The 28-year-old, who has spent most of his Test career in the shadow of fellow centres Tuipulotu and Jones, is viewing the Japan match as another opportunity to impress head coach Gregor Townsend less than a year out from the World Cup in Australia.

McDowall was named in Scotland’s 41-man training group ahead of the tournament in 2023 and made his debut in a warm-up match against Italy before being cut when the squad was reduced to 37.

“I think it’s a really good opportunity for guys like myself to try and put our hands up for that World Cup squad, and what an opportunity it is,” said McDowall.

“There’s a lot of rugby to be played between now and then.

“I think being captain tomorrow, I’m going to be focusing on what I can do tomorrow and how I can influence the team tomorrow, but it’s always going to be in the back of your mind.

“Obviously I missed out on the last World Cup, so any opportunity to play for Scotland here and put your hand up, you try and put your best foot forward.”
