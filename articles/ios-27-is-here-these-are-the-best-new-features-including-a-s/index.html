iOS 27 Is Here. These Are the Best New Features, Including a Smarter Siri.

TheTools · 16.09.2026 22:48 · source · permalink
  1. Software and apps

By Brenda Stolyar

Brenda Stolyar is a writer covering consumer technology. Her beats include smartphones, tablets, and wearables.

Apple just unveiled its latest iPhone lineup, which includes the iPhone 18 Pro, the iPhone 18 Pro Max, and the first foldable iPhone, the iPhone Duo. 

But you don’t need to buy a new iPhone to get new features. Apple just released its new iPhone operating system, iOS 27, and you can download it right now if you have an iPhone 11 or newer. 

The biggest change in the new version of iOS is AI — specifically, a smarter version of Siri. The opt-in Siri AI feature turns Apple’s long-maligned voice assistant into an app in which you can have conversations, similar to those you might have with a chatbot such as Claude or ChatGPT. Apple’s once-dumb voice-activated assistant is now impressively capable and contextually aware.

But iOS 27 brings other useful new features, too, even if you’re not interested in AI or don’t have a newer-model iPhone. 

We’ve been using the iOS 27 beta all summer. Below, we’ve rounded up all the features we think are worth trying once you’ve updated your device.

How to upgrade to iOS 27

Before installing a new version of the operating system, back up your iPhone. That way, you don’t have to worry about losing any important data, such as photos, text messages, and contacts. Go to Settings and tap your name. From there, turn on iCloud Backup and then select Back Up Now.

If you have Automatic Updates enabled on your iPhone, you’ll receive a notification when iOS 27 is ready to download and install. You can also check Settings > General > Software Update. You don’t need to upgrade right away, but we do recommend keeping your software up-to-date to ensure that you have the latest security fixes.

Our favorite new features in iOS 27

iOS 27 is compatible with the iPhone 11 and newer, but only the iPhone 15 Pro and newer support Apple Intelligence features such as Siri AI, the standalone Siri app, and Visual Intelligence. The following features are limited to those newer phones. To learn about iOS 27 features that are available on older iPhones, jump ahead to that section.

Rely on a smarter, more capable Siri

Siri has always been painfully slow, unreliable, and irritating to use. If you had a question or wanted to complete a task, doing it manually was often easier and faster. With iOS 27, Apple is finally delivering the new and improved Siri that it promised in 2024. 

Called Siri AI, the new version is smarter and fully personalized to your device. It’s also much more capable, with the ability to see what’s on your screen and on your phone to answer questions, as well as deeper integration across a slew of Apple apps. 

It also has a new look. When you say “Hey, Siri,” a shimmering orb appears in the iPhone’s Dynamic Island containing results to your questions presented as rich communication cards that you can pull down to automatically access the new standalone Siri app. 

The app keeps track of all your conversations (which we’ve found ideal for finding information from past searches), but you can also upload content if you have a request related to a photo or file. 

When you install the new OS, Siri AI pulls in a backlog of information already stored on your phone, including calendar invites, emails, texts, files, and notes. This process allows the virtual assistant to automatically deliver contextual suggestions and information relevant to you. As a result, rather than manually searching your phone for a specific link, photo, or event, you can just ask Siri.

And it works. Siri AI was able to recall the exact dates I went on trips to Las Vegas this year, identifying that I went twice.  It also pulled up the last book recommendation a friend gave me; when I asked for a recent photo of my dog, it quickly found several of those, too. 

But Siri still struggles sometimes. When I asked it to pull up a hotel confirmation for a wedding I’m attending at the end of September, Siri pinpointed whose wedding it was and which text threads discussed the hotel accommodations but couldn’t find the hotel name (even though the hotel confirmation is in my email). Once I manually found the confirmation, I asked Siri to add it to my calendar right from the Gmail app — and it did. 

Despite such hiccups, I rely on Siri a lot more now than I have in years. I used it to plan an itinerary for a day out in Soho, and it recommended various coffee shops, restaurants, and bookstores in the area. I used it to analyze screenshots of clothing items and accessories to find similar items online. I used it for looking up movie times at local theaters and for finding recipes using ingredients I had in my fridge. All of these queries worked without any issues. 

Siri is also far easier to interact with than it’s ever been. You can now have a more natural conversation with the virtual assistant, including continuous dialogue and the ability to ask follow-up questions within a single query. And, rather than fully dismissing a request or question when it doesn’t understand, it will specify which parts weren’t clear.

Of course, it still has room for improvement in a few respects, not just drawing on personal context. It’s also a bit slower to process requests than other virtual assistants, such as Google Gemini. Still, it’s a great first step toward the Siri everyone expected years ago. 

Note that you have to enable Siri AI in the Settings app — it isn’t on by default. And at this point Siri AI is still a beta feature (though anyone can try it), and it could improve before the final version is released.

Ask Siri about what your camera can see 

You can open the Camera app, swipe to the left to tap the new Siri tab, and then aim your camera at something you want more information about. You can point it at an event schedule and ask Siri to add each one to your calendar, scan and analyze nutritional labels to log them in your Health app, or take a photo of a restaurant and ask for its business hours or the menu.

Split the bill with help from the Camera app

In iOS 27, Visual Intelligence calculates what each person owes for a restaurant bill from a photo of a receipt, after which they can use Apple Cash to send it to whoever’s covering the bill. 

To access the feature, open the Camera app, tap the Visual Intelligence icon, and take a photo of the receipt. The feature calculates what each person owes (including tax and tip), and then each person in the party can select the items and send the total amount owed via Apple Cash. 

It works well depending on the type of receipt — printed receipts were a breeze in my experience, but it struggled to accurately identify prices on a handwritten receipt (which depends, of course, on how clear the handwriting is).

However, because this function works only through Apple Cash, you’ll have to load money onto your Apple Cash card in order to be able to transfer money to other people. It would be ideal if Apple could add the ability to route the payment through a debit card or a bank account, as you can with third-party apps.

Create custom wallet passes

Though it’s common to add specific cards to Apple Wallet (such as insurance cards, concert tickets, and boarding passes), some cards don’t offer this built-in option. With “Create a Pass,” you can create and add custom passes for cards, passes, or memberships that don't support the Wallet app.

To create one, go to the Wallet app, tap the + sign in the upper-right corner, and tap Create a Pass. Take a photo of your physical card, and Visual Intelligence will generate the pass. I used this function for my library card and a gift card for a local boutique — two cards I tend to leave behind in my other handbags — so that I always have them on me.

If you don’t have an iPhone that supports Apple Intelligence, or the physical card you want to upload, you can also create a pass manually. Just choose from three templates (Standard, Membership, or Event) and enter the necessary information. You can also customize it with images, colors, styles, and text.

Set Safari notifications to track updates on price, stock, and more

While shopping online, if you’re eyeing a specific item but want to wait until it’s on sale or back in stock, Apple’s Notify Me feature makes the process easier. Instead of constantly checking the product page for updates, you can have Safari notify you when it detects a change on the website. For example, I set a notification alert for a Longchamp tote bag I’ve been wanting and asked Safari to notify me when the price dipped under $100; I also customized it to check twice a week around 9 a.m. (Fingers crossed.)

To enable the feature, search for and find the specific product you’re looking for in Safari, tap Menu in the Tab bar, and then select Notify Me. You can then describe the update you want and customize how often you want Safari to check in. It’s a convenient feature for those who don’t want to miss a sale or a restock.

Create simple “if this, then that” shortcuts

Apple’s Shortcuts is a standalone app that allows you to create automations, such as turning on smart bulbs at a specific time in the evening, starting a specific playlist when you connect to Apple CarPlay, or texting someone at home when you arrive at the office each morning. 

But creating a shortcut hasn’t always been easy, mainly because the Shortcuts app has always been unintuitive and confusing to use. With iOS 27, Apple has simplified the process immensely, and now you can make a shortcut through natural language. All you have to do is type in a prompt, and the app will do all the work for you. 

To create a shortcut, open the Shortcuts app, tap the + sign, and type your specific prompt. It will automatically generate the shortcut and add it to your library of existing ones. I’ve quickly created shortcuts for playing Spotify when I leave my apartment or the office, texting my dad when I’m back home to let him know I’ve arrived safely, and starting an outdoor-walk workout whenever I leave home.

Clean up your photos more easily

With iOS 27, the photo-editing Clean Up tool is faster and better. You can now choose from different Clean Up modes: Fast (for minor touch-ups using on-device AI models), High Quality (for more complex retouching using Apple’s cloud models), and Auto (in which your device chooses the mode based on what you’re removing). 

My main issue with Clean Up has always been that it’s slow, but in the past it also wasn’t great at filling in removed items, leaving the vacant spot warped or looking off compared with the rest of the photo. Now, in Auto mode, Clean Up seems quicker (though it does take a bit to load), and it’s much better at filling in details. For example, when I removed a towel rack from the background of a photo I took, it nicely and accurately filled in what was missing to match the rest of the wall.

iOS 27 features for older phones

If you own a base-model iPhone 15 (or an iPhone dating back to the iPhone 11), you won’t have access to Apple’s AI features. But the new iOS has some useful new tools for those older phones, too.

Save video frames as photos

Sometimes, while shooting a video, you realize that some frames would make great photos. Now, you can save a video frame as a photo. 

Select the video, tap the three dots in the right corner, and choose the Save Video Frame as Photo option. The photo then appears in your library. I often take screenshots of frames from my videos; this function is much more convenient, because I no longer have to go through the extra step of cropping the image.

Organize your photo library with a new filter

When you’re sifting through the Photos app, telling the difference between photos you’ve taken and those that family or friends sent you can be tough. With Captured By Me, you can now filter photos that you’ve specifically taken on the Camera app (even if they’re from older phones).

Continuous send in Messages

If you send a text, photo, or video with subpar cellular service, you’re typically met with a slow-moving progress bar at the top of the Messages window, which keeps any additional messages you send after that from going through. With continuous send, Messages now shows the send indicator underneath each individual text while allowing other messages to go through.

Set independent alarm volumes

Instead of setting your ringer volume on high just for alarms, you can now adjust the alarm volume separately. Go to Settings > Sounds and Haptics and scroll down to “Alarms and Timers.” Toggle off Match Ringtone Volume and then set your preferred alarm volume. This feature is a real game changer, mainly because I usually set my phone’s volume as high as possible for fear that I won’t hear my alarms, but that also makes notification pings too loud. 

Add larger widgets with more information

Though app widgets on the home screen are great for viewing more information at a glance, I’d always find myself tapping them to see more details. With iOS 27, you can now make widgets extra large on your home screen. This presentation is useful for apps such as Calendar, News, and Reminders, where you want a more detailed overview.

I’ve loved using it for the Notes app — as someone who is constantly jotting down separate notes, I’ve found that the expanded widget lets me see more of what I’ve saved without having to tap into it each time. It’s also great for the Weather app, because the widget now shows a nine-day forecast instead of a five-day forecast.

Personalize your AirPods sound

If you have AirPods, you can now change the sound from Apple’s recommended setting to a customized one that you create yourself. While wearing the AirPods, go to the AirPods settings and select Audio & Routing > Equalizer > Custom. There, you can listen to an audio sample and fine-tune how it sounds for your preferences. 

The feature is available on any AirPods model with an H2 chip, including the AirPods Pro (2nd generation and 3rd generation), AirPods (4th generation and 5th generation), and AirPods Max (2nd generation).

Enable ‘Ask to Buy’ for children’s devices

With iOS 27, Apple is releasing new child-safety features that allow parents and guardians to better manage children’s phones, with more control over the content they can access. 

The company is introducing Ask to Buy, which requires kids to ask permission before adding new apps to their devices. A similar Safari feature, Ask to Browse, allows parents to manually approve what their children search for on the web.

Customize Camera controls

Apple offers a bunch of advanced camera settings, but if they’re not in the Camera Controls menu at the top of the viewfinder when you open the app, you have to dig to find them. This can be annoying when you’re trying to take a photo right away.

Now, you can customize the controls to include the ones you want quick access to and remove those you don’t need. You have the option to add Flash, Live Photo, Timer, Exposure, Styles, Filter, Aspect, Night Mode, Depth Control, File Format, and Shared Library. If you have an iPhone 18 Pro or iPhone 18 Pro Max, you can also add exclusive features such as Lens Aperture and Shutter Speed. 

To customize the Camera Controls, open the Camera app, tap Edit, and select the controls you want to include (you can pick up to eight). To remove any, tap the - icon to the left of each item. You can also rearrange them by long-pressing each one and moving it to your preferred spot.

Use multiple iPhones with the same phone number

With iPhone Handoff, you can link two devices to one phone number so that you can easily switch between them. This feature could be useful if you have a personal phone and a work phone but still want to be reached at your primary number. However, it’s currently available only through T-Mobile (it’s coming to Verizon later this year) and requires a $5 monthly charge through the carrier. Both iPhones must also be running iOS 27 for the feature to work. 

To set up the feature, go to Settings > Cellular, select This iPhone and Another, and tap Continue. If you want to keep the primary eSIM on your current phone, tap Other iPhone; if you want to transfer the primary eSIM to your new iPhone, select This iPhone. Once you’ve set it up, your phone number will switch between iPhones depending on which one is unlocked. The feature worked seamlessly in my experience — whenever I unlocked an iPhone, I saw a notification at the top of the screen that said “Switched to this iPhone,” which let me know that the swap was successful each time.
