Mavericks Face Clippers With Key Players Sidelined in Dallas

TheTools · 22.11.2026 00:29 · source · permalink

Dallas welcomed the Los Angeles Clippers to American Airlines Center on Saturday, November 21, for an NBA Western Conference matchup as the Mavericks looked to build on their latest victory. The home team entered with an 8-7 record, while Los Angeles arrived at 4-10 after consecutive defeats, with injuries affecting both rosters.

According to the injury reports published by Yahoo Sports and Sports Illustrated, five Clippers players were unavailable: Brandon Ingram, Bradley Beal, Nick Martinelli, Jordan Miller and Jalen Pickett. Ingram was recovering from right heel surgery, while Beal was sidelined for right knee management.

Dallas also faced several absences. Tarik Biberovic, Sergio de Larrea, Tobi Lawal and Dereck Lively II were ruled out, while P.J. Washington, Walter Clayton Jr. and Jett Howard were listed as questionable. Caleb Martin was available despite wearing a protective splint on his thumb.

The Mavericks entered the contest following a convincing 116-94 victory over the Golden State Warriors. That result ended a three-game losing streak and improved Dallas to 6-2 at home, according to the team's published statistics.

The Clippers, meanwhile, had lost 124-112 against the San Antonio Spurs in their previous outing. Their road record stood at 0-6, making their trip to Dallas another opportunity to secure their first away victory of the season.

Dallas coach Dusty May discussed the team's ongoing development during Friday's practice, emphasizing communication, effort and defensive improvement as the foundations of its identity.

"Identity is something we're developing in our practice every day with the way we work, with the way we communicate, our effort (and) our intensity," May said.

He stressed that supporters should expect consistent commitment regardless of the team's results or the quality of its opponents.

"Those are the things that our fans have to know when we step foot on the floor they're going to get that every single night."

May acknowledged that Dallas would encounter difficult performances and opponents throughout the season but said those challenges should not interrupt its progress.

"There's going to be nights where we play poorly and there's going to be nights in this league when we go up against guys who are on a heater, and that can't cause us to lose our fight or our continuous development. So, we're still figuring out exactly who we're going to be, but our ball pressure is improving, our gap defense is improving."

The coach identified ball movement as another important element of the Mavericks' offensive approach. Against Golden State, Dallas recorded 34 assists on 42 successful field goals.

"I think we pass the ball when we're at our best," May said.

He also highlighted the team's aggressive start against the Warriors and its ability to recover after a temporary decline in intensity.

"I thought we came out and we had an aggressive mindset."

May described how the players maintained their competitive edge during that performance.

"I think nine minutes into the game we felt like we were completely engaged at a high, high level every possession, and then we had a little bit of a dip, and then we were able to get that edge back. We're so much better when we're playing with that type of aggression and physicality and edge. It's on us to do it more consistent."

Cooper Flagg also identified improved communication as a major factor behind Dallas' defensive performance against Golden State.

"I think the intensity obviously starts with us and effort, and I think that comes with better communication. You're more into it and you're applying better pressure when you're hearing four teammates behind you talking to you and letting you know you're not alone," Flagg said.

The rookie explained that stronger communication helped the team apply pressure and defend more effectively.

"When the communication is better and the intensity is better, that just led to better defense."

May also praised Flagg's progress in drawing fouls and reaching the free-throw line. During his first 14 games of the current season, Flagg converted 85 of 95 free throws, compared with 36 makes on 46 attempts through his first 15 games as a rookie.

"He's made a big jump, and that was an intentional effort by him to draw more fouls," May said.

The coach also identified an area where the young forward could continue improving when attacking the basket.

"He sometimes gets stripped in the paint."

May explained that Flagg's ability to attack with greater force could influence how frequently officials award him free throws.

"Whether they're fouls or whether they're not we think is a matter of how forceful he is going into it. So, we expect him to continue to get to the line more."

Flagg's perimeter shooting has also improved. He made 12 of 30 three-point attempts, a 40 percent success rate, across his previous six games, according to the team's statistics.

"His three-point shot looks great," May said.

The coach credited Flagg's work during practice for the improvement.

"He's been shooting it well in practice and after practice."

May said the rookie continued making progress across different aspects of his game.

"He's been doing a nice job of just making incremental gains in all areas, and getting to the free throw line is one of them. I'm very happy with the way Coop played the other night."

Despite the Warriors victory, May cautioned against assuming Dallas could reproduce the same performance against Los Angeles. He said the team needed to prepare for a different opponent and maintain its concentration.

"I'm not sure you carry it over," May said.

Dallas entered the matchup having won four consecutive home games, while the Clippers were still searching for their first road victory. The Mavericks' next schedule included a three-game road trip beginning in Utah on Sunday, followed by a visit to Denver on Tuesday and a game against Phoenix the following Friday.

May also provided an update on injured center Dereck Lively II, saying the player was making greater progress during his recovery.

"doing a lot more now"
