Maaz’s blazing 96, Imran’s three-wicket burst power Pakistan to series triumph

TheTools · 11.10.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

RAWALPINDI: Maaz Sadaqat came within touching distance of a maiden T20 international century before Imran’s devastating three-wicket burst propelled Pakistan to a commanding 82-run victory over Sri Lanka, sealing the three-match series 2-0 in front of a packed Rawalpindi Stadium on Sunday.

In a compelling exhibition of aggressive batting and disciplined bowling, Pakistan piled up a formidable 215 for three after being asked to bat before restricting Sri Lanka to 133 for seven in their pursuit of a daunting 216-run target.

The emphatic victory underlined the growing confidence of Pakistan’s revamped T20 outfit, with young opener Maaz emerging as the undisputed star of the evening and Imran delivering the decisive blows to leave the visitors struggling almost from the outset.

Maaz’s explosive 96 off just 51 balls provided the foundation for Pakistan’s imposing total, while captain Sahibzada Farhan once again led from the front with a commanding 73. The pair combined for an 88-run second-wicket partnership that put Pakistan firmly in control and laid the groundwork for a total beyond Sri Lanka’s reach.

If Pakistan’s batting set the stadium alight, Imran’s incisive spell ensured there would be no late drama. The pacer returned exceptional figures of three wickets for just 11 runs in his four overs, dismantling Sri Lanka’s top order and effectively ending the contest as a realistic competition.

Sri Lanka, faced with the enormous challenge of maintaining a required rate of more than 10 runs an over, never recovered from Imran’s early strikes. He removed Sanath Jayawardana for 22 before dismissing captain Kusal Mendis for nine and Sahan Arachchige for a duck, leaving the visitors reeling under mounting pressure.

The early collapse exposed the gulf between the two sides on the night, as Pakistan’s bowlers combined discipline with intelligent variations to keep the Sri Lankan batters from building any meaningful momentum.

Sri Lanka eventually finished at 133 for seven, falling well short of the target despite some resistance from Wickramasinghe, who scored 29, and Maheesh Theekshana, who remained unbeaten on 32. Theekshana’s late contribution, however, could do little to alter the outcome of a match Pakistan had dominated in both departments.

Earlier, it was Maaz who provided the fireworks that had the capacity crowd on its feet. The left-hander blended power with timing, repeatedly clearing the boundary with remarkable ease and giving the innings the acceleration required to turn a competitive total into a match-winning one.

His 96-run knock featured seven sixes and six fours, making it one of the most entertaining innings of the series. The 21-year-old appeared destined for a memorable hundred but fell agonisingly short, his dismissal denying him a deserved landmark without diminishing the impact of his outstanding performance.

Maaz reached his second successive T20 international half-century in just 30 balls, having struck four sixes and three fours by that stage. Once he crossed the fifty-run mark, he shifted into an even higher gear, attacking Sri Lanka’s spinners and particularly punishing Theekshana, whose figures suffered as Maaz launched two more sixes off his bowling.

His fearless approach reflected Pakistan’s attacking intent, with the hosts refusing to settle for a merely respectable total after being put in to bat. Maaz’s ability to find the boundary consistently allowed Pakistan to maintain a healthy scoring rate and keep the pressure firmly on the opposition.

At the other end, Farhan continued his impressive run as Pakistan captain. After taking time to settle, he gradually found his rhythm before accelerating decisively, striking three sixes and four boundaries in a 44-ball 73.

Farhan brought up his second consecutive half-century off 32 deliveries, providing another demonstration of his ability to balance responsibility with aggression. His innings ensured Pakistan capitalised on the strong platform and remained on course for a substantial total throughout the middle overs.

The 88-run stand between Farhan and Maaz proved pivotal. With both batters finding the boundary regularly, Pakistan were able to build momentum without allowing Sri Lanka to regain control. Their partnership also highlighted the promise of a young batting combination capable of taking the attack to quality opposition.

Fakhar Zaman contributed 22 off 17 balls but was unable to convert his start into a substantial score. His departure, however, did little to slow Pakistan’s progress as Maaz and Farhan continued to dictate terms.

Pakistan’s final total of 215 for three was their second-highest in T20 internationals, reflecting the effectiveness of their batting approach and the depth available in the line-up.

For Sri Lanka, Theekshana finished with one wicket for 55 runs, while the visitors struggled to find an effective response to Pakistan’s sustained assault. Their batting subsequently failed to match the urgency required in such a demanding chase.

With the series now beyond Sri Lanka’s reach, Pakistan will take considerable confidence from a performance in which their young batters delivered a commanding total and their bowlers backed it up with an equally convincing effort.

For Maaz, the night marked another significant step forward in his international career. For Farhan, it was further evidence of a captain leading from the front. And for Pakistan, the comprehensive victory offered an encouraging glimpse of the aggressive, confident T20 side they hope to build for the future.

Score: Pakistan 215 for 2 in 20 overs (Maaz Sadaqat 96, Sahibzada Farhan 73, Dasun Shanaka 1-30, Ehsan Malinga 1-41)

Lanka for in 20 overs (Chamindu Wickramasinghe 29, Maheesh Theekshana 32 not out, Imran Randhawa 3-11, Salman Mirza 2-28)

Result: Pakistan won by 82 runs.
