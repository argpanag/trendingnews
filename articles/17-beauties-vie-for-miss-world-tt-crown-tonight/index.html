The next Miss World Trinidad and To­ba­go will be cho­sen tonight as 17 beau­ties vie for the cov­et­ed ti­tle.

Un­der the na­tion­al fran­chise held by Charu Lochan Dass, this year’s com­pe­ti­tion ar­rives at a par­tic­u­lar­ly sig­nif­i­cant mo­ment in the coun­try’s pageant his­to­ry.

Ac­cord­ing to Miss World Trinidad and To­ba­go, Dass is an ar­dent ad­vo­cate for the trans­for­ma­tion­al plat­form pro­mot­ed by the Miss World mis­sion and is a dri­ving force be­hind the de­vel­op­ment of ex­cel­lence. Trinidad and To­ba­go’s grow­ing in­ter­na­tion­al suc­cess has be­come a tes­ta­ment to the strength of its rep­re­sen­ta­tion, the cal­i­bre of its women and the in­vest­ment in de­vel­op­ing con­fi­dent, ar­tic­u­late and pur­pose-dri­ven am­bas­sadors.

Georgie Lee Gill’s re­cent Head-to-Head Chal­lenge vic­to­ry, se­cur­ing the Amer­i­c­as and Caribbean re­gion­al win, fur­ther re­in­forces this lega­cy. Gill’s achieve­ment al­so marks Trinidad and To­ba­go’s third Miss World Caribbean ti­tle among its last five rep­re­sen­ta­tives, along­side con­sis­tent place­ments in the Miss World Top 40 dur­ing this pe­ri­od.

In ad­di­tion to the three Miss World Caribbean ti­tles, Trinidad and To­ba­go has al­so se­cured re­gion­al Head-to-Head vic­to­ries. Con­sis­tent in­ter­na­tion­al recog­ni­tion has cer­tain­ly been a strong­hold, with rep­re­sen­ta­tives such as Tya Jané Ramey, Jea­nine Brandt, Aché Abra­hams, An­na-Lise Nan­ton and Geor­gia Lee Gill. The last three rep­re­sen­ta­tives were un­der the stew­ard­ship of Charu Lochan Dass and her team, com­pris­ing Giselle La Ronde-West, Adri­an Ray­mond, Rachel Thomas-Telemacque, Rana Ibrahim, Kylie Ja­cob, Jase Calderon, Do­minic Ross, Ra­jiv Sama­roo and Richard Young.

Cen­tral to this con­tin­u­ing suc­cess is the work of Richard Young, one of the Caribbean’s most es­tab­lished fash­ion and cre­ative in­dus­try prac­ti­tion­ers, whose ap­proach to train­ing and pre­sen­ta­tion has helped shape the de­vel­op­ment of na­tion­al rep­re­sen­ta­tives.

With a ca­reer span­ning four decades across fash­ion, beau­ty, per­for­mance, cre­ative di­rec­tion and pageant pro­duc­tion, Young brings a dis­tinc­tive phi­los­o­phy to the prepa­ra­tion of women for the in­ter­na­tion­al stage: con­fi­dence is not sim­ply an at­tribute; it is an ar­chi­tec­ture.

Through his method­ol­o­gy of Con­fi­dence Ar­chi­tec­ture and Stage Dy­nam­ics, Young de­vel­ops the pres­ence, com­mu­ni­ca­tion, per­for­mance in­tel­li­gence and per­son­al ex­pres­sion re­quired for del­e­gates to move be­yond the con­ven­tions of pageantry and be­come com­pelling rep­re­sen­ta­tives of their na­tion.

Lat­er to­day, Queen’s Hall be­comes the stage for a na­tion prepar­ing to take its next step on­to the world stage, mark­ing the start of the jour­ney to Miss World 2027.
