Essential 104-Minute Halloween Cult Classic Officially Leaves 2,895 Theaters After Box Office Disaster

TheTools · 12.10.2026 23:30 · source · permalink

There's a case to be made that Practical Magic 2 was never really a theatrical proposition in the first place, and the numbers now winding down its big-screen run arguably prove it. The 1998 original famously flopped in cinemas, limping to around $47 million domestically on a hefty budget before two decades of October cable rotations, home video rewatches, and Halloween traditions turned it into one of the most beloved comfort movies of its era.

The Box Office Run for Practical Magic 2 Ended Much Like the Original's

That cult afterlife is the entire reason Warner Bros. reunited Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman as the Owens sisters 28 years later, with Susanne Bier directing an adaptation of Alice Hoffman's The Book of Magic and Joey King, Maisie Williams, Lee Pace, and Xolo Maridueña rounding out a new generation. The sequel even opened respectably enough, dethroning Spider-Man: Brand New Day's six-week box office reign with a $30 million debut in September. But a soft multiplier and some brutal weekend-to-weekend drops have now brought the theatrical chapter to a quiet close, and in a way that mirrors its predecessor's fate almost beat for beat.

Per the latest weekend figures, Practical Magic 2 earned just $1.35 million, down 51.4% from the prior frame, playing in 1,268 locations after shedding another 795 theaters. That marks a staggering fall of 2,895 screens from its peak count of 4,163, with a per-theater average of roughly $1,068 signaling the run is effectively over. The film's domestic total now sits at $67.2 million, with the worldwide haul hovering around $103 million against a reported $75 million production budget. The exit is no accident of scheduling either, as Warner Bros. has moved the film to digital platforms as of Oct. 13, a fairly tight 32-day window, with the 4K and Blu-ray release set for Dec. 1.

'Practical Magic 2' Is Heading to Digital After a Short Theatrical Run

Dropping the film onto VOD two and a half weeks before Halloween puts it in front of the precise audience that streams the original every October, and it's not hard to imagine the studio banking on seasonal rentals to patch the theatrical shortfall. It's worth gently noting that Warner Bros. has had far bigger headaches this fall, so a mid-budget sequel landing somewhere near break-even is hardly its most pressing concern. Still, the broader takeaway is a slightly deflating one for anyone hoping legacy sequels aimed at nostalgic adult audiences could reliably open theatrically. Practical Magic 2 did everything right, timing the release for spooky season, and it still couldn't convert decades of couch-bound affection into sustained ticket sales.
