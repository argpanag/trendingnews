Anthropic’s new fund comes with fine print

Morning Brew Inc.

TL;DR: Anthropic announced a new equity fund it says could help address AI’s impact on workers, just two days after its own report showed the company has reduced its share of junior hires because of AI agents. But the details on who runs the fund and who gets paid are still murky.

What happened: Yesterday, Anthropic unveiled the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund—committing 5% of its shares (worth about $100 billion if it goes public at a $2 trillion valuation) pending stockholder approval, with an additional 2% for every 50% jump in the company’s value. Per Anthropic, the fund could give money to nonprofits or pay “specific populations” directly, and the company’s announcement singled out workers hit by AI. That’s notable, given what its own hiring data just revealed.

Junior varsity: Since early 2025, junior roles have dropped from 27% to 9% of Anthropic’s technical hires, and entry-level job postings have fallen sharply since December 2025, per the report. Roughly a third of Anthropic’s machine learning managers and a fifth of software engineering managers say they’re hiring less because of AI. Meanwhile, the AI agents employees use now log six times as many hours as the employees themselves. (No word yet on Claude’s overtime pay.)

While the report may look ominous on the surface, Anthropic’s headcount did triple last year. The company says it’s prioritizing moving current staff into new roles, but the report concedes that doesn’t directly address its starkest finding—the junior hiring slump. Its employees are already anticipating more changes: Over half think AI will be able to do at least 80% of their current work by 2030.

Fine print TBD: Anthropic cited labor disruption as one problem its newly created fund could tackle, but the how is still unclear. The fund is a wholly owned LLC, not a 501(c)(3) (IRS-speak for a conventional charity), which means Anthropic owns it and the company’s board decides whether it grows. Anthropic says the structure gives it flexibility to distribute the money, but it also means it gets to skip the required public disclosures traditional charities must make.

Bottom line: Anthropic is seemingly putting its money where its mouth is on AI and jobs. But until we know who runs the fund and where the money will go, it’s still just a promise and not actually a plan. —LC

AI comics steal the show

Screenshots via @georgebrettolson/Instagram

That comedian you saw telling a knee-slapper on Instagram might not be real—and neither are their jokes. Across social media, there’s now a scourge of uncannily attractive, AI-generated comics who lift material from real standups, right down to the dramatic pause. The sad punchline: The knockoffs often get way more views and gushing comments than the originals.

Sometimes the jokes are even about AI taking human jobs (which, as one human comedian noted in a Reel, makes this “the most ironic thing that’s ever happened” to him). Meanwhile, Meta’s response to the whole debacle has evidently been a giant shrug. One comedian told Wired the company’s position was that the AI mimic isn’t actually impersonating her, which would break its rules. It was just stealing her material. The lone silver lining might be that having your jokes stolen by AI is probably good fodder for a new bit. Just don’t post it on Instagram. —WK
