Macron: Malaysia-France Strategic Partnership To Reduce Dependencies, Strengthen Sovereignty

TheTools · 18.11.2026 12:56 · source · permalink

KUALA LUMPUR, Nov 18 (Bernama) -- French President Emmanuel Macron said the new strategic partnership between Malaysia and France aims to reduce dependencies and strengthen sovereignty through cooperation in critical minerals, defence, energy and advanced technologies.

Macron described the elevation of bilateral ties to a strategic partnership as an important step forward for both countries.

"Malaysia and France are taking an important step forward today: we are elevating our relationship to a strategic partnership.

"Less dependence, greater sovereignty. We are turning ambitions into action," he said in a Facebook post.

Macron concluded his two-day official visit to Malaysia on Tuesday, his first since assuming office in May 2017, at the invitation of Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim.

The French president cited the Carester-Malaco rare earths project, scheduled to begin operations in 2027, as an example of efforts to secure strategic supplies and strengthen industrial sovereignty.

“From 2027, the Carester-Malaco project will enable the processing of rare earths in Malaysia, with refining capabilities in France. We are securing our strategic supplies and strengthening our industrial sovereignty," he said.

The partnership also covers defence cooperation, including the supply of Airbus search-and-rescue helicopters, a joint roadmap for defence industries and the establishment of a centre to counter hybrid threats.

"In defence, we are strengthening our cooperation through an agreement for the supply of Airbus search-and-rescue helicopters, a joint roadmap for our defence industries and the establishment of a French-Malaysian centre to counter hybrid threats," he said.

Macron said energy security was another area of cooperation, with both countries seeking closer ties in liquefied natural gas, civil nuclear energy and electricity interconnections across Southeast Asia, in support of decarbonisation.

The two countries also plan to expand cooperation in advanced technologies, including semiconductors, high-performance microprocessors, data centres and digital infrastructure.

"In future technologies, we are moving forward together on semiconductors, high-performance microprocessors, data centres and the security of our digital infrastructure, particularly submarine cables," he said.

Their cooperation will also extend to education, research and cultural exchanges.

"We are also strengthening ties between our young people through the revival of joint scholarship and research programmes, as well as new partnerships between our major museums," he said.

Macron said the partnership also reflected the countries' shared ambitions as they approach the 70th anniversary of diplomatic relations in 2027.

"As we approach the 70th anniversary of our diplomatic relations, this strategic partnership reflects a shared ambition: sovereign nations, stronger economies and an international order based on law, respect and cooperation," he said.

He also thanked Anwar and Malaysians for their hospitality during his official visit.

"Thank you, dear Anwar Ibrahim and thank you to the Malaysian people for your warm welcome, your trust and your friendship.

"Long live the friendship between Malaysia and France!" he said.
