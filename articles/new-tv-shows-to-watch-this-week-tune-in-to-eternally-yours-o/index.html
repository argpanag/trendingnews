My recommendation: Eternally Yours

Why you should watch it: What does happily ever after look like for two vampires in a 500-year-old marriage? From the minds of Joe Port and Joe Wiseman, the duo behind CBS's Ghosts, comes another quirky supernatural sitcom — and it's pretty decent.

In Eternally Yours, we meet a quirky vampire coven residing in overcast Seattle. They're led by the married couple Charles (Ed Weeks) and Liz (Allegra Edwards), who have one adopted child, Emma (Helen J. Shen). Meanwhile, Jesse (Parker Young), a member of their coven, has two half-vampire tweens with his human wife, Phyllis (Rose Abdoo). The joke here is that the couple has a big age gap — in real life, Abdoo is 63, while Young is 38 — thanks to Jesse being hundreds of years old with nary a wrinkle. (He also has to frequently wipe Phyllis's memory so she doesn't remember she lives with a household of vampires; this feels problematic, but hopefully the show adds nuance along the way.)

The action really kicks off when Emma's boyfriend, Max (Jaren Lewison), finds out his girlfriend's family regularly drinks blood. Worse, a freak accident leads to the split-second decision to turn him into a vampire and save his life, inducting him into the coven against his will. Max then has to clumsily adjust to what being a vampire really entails — the special powers, the fangs and, of course, the thirst for human blood. (But don't worry — the coven ethically purchases blood from willing donors on the internet to avoid killing.)

Similar to Ghosts, part of what makes Eternally Yours so fun to watch is the way it pokes fun at vampire stereotypes and uses those clichés to build out its lore and mythology. Is there any way around needing to be invited into a building? Will garlic and sunshine really repel them? And what else happened in the last 500 years to make this family the way it is? Being packaged in zippy 20-minute episodes makes this an easy watch to add to your weekly rotation.

How to watch: New episodes of Eternally Yours air every Thursday at 8:30 p.m. ET on CBS and stream on Paramount+.

My recommendation: Yaga

Why you should watch it: Television has seen its fair share of mysterious disappearances in small, foggy towns, but none like this one. Yaga, created by Kat Sandler and produced by Crave, the Canadian network that brought you Heated Rivalry, is a witchy story inspired by the Slavic myth of Baba Yaga.

Initially, Yaga sets itself up to be like any season of True Detective. Police detective Carson (Clark Backo) is investigating the disappearance of a college student in the fictional Canadian coastal town of Whittock. She's assisted by Rapp (Noah Reid), a private investigator hired by the student's wealthy parents, whose cockiness is an entertaining foil to the more buttoned-up detective.

Potential suspects include Katherine (Carrie-Anne Moss), an osteology professor at the local college who taught the missing student. Hudson Williams of Heated Rivalry fame pulls double duty here as both Henry, the missing student, and Logan, Henry's very similar-looking roommate.

As the case deepens, things get weirder, for Rapp especially. There is something palpably supernatural going on here, although it's yet to be determined just how supernatural things will get. As the myth goes, Baba Yaga is often depicted as an old woman who flies around inside a mortar. Is that the version of the witch we'll find in this series? Or is she more human than we might expect?

Yaga was easily the buzziest television premiere at the Toronto International Film Festival in September, where the first two episodes debuted. (The Heated Rivalry of it all certainly helps.) And although Yaga is definitely a bit steamy, it is very much its own thing — a reminder of the versatility of Canadian storytelling. If these eight episodes follow the events of Sandler's 2019 play upon which the show is based, brace yourself for twists and turns that will definitely catch you off guard.

How to watch: The first two episodes of Yaga premiere on AMC+ on Friday, Oct. 23, with new episodes streaming weekly.

Another spooky mystery recommendation: Phony

Why you should watch it: What if you suspected your mother had been swapped out for an imposter, but you were the only one who noticed it? That's the underlying mystery of Phony, the new half-hour comedy created by Nick Paley (Marcel the Shell With Shoes On) for Hulu.

Ellen (Connie Britton) and Sonny Cooke (Sam Nivola) are an adorable mother-son duo. They have inside jokes and get along swimmingly. Ellen is a single mother and the principal at Sonny's high school, and despite spending so much time together, they are still essentially best friends. 

Things take a turn when Sonny is in a car crash that sends him into a coma for a week. When he wakes up, Ellen is … slightly off. Her face especially. With the help of some subtle hair and makeup changes, Britton herself looks different, but it's hard to pinpoint what has shifted, which is exactly how Sonny feels when she greets him in his hospital bed.

Unfortunately, neither Sonny's girlfriend nor his best friend sees it, and others dismiss his concerns as side effects of his coma and recovery. The show doesn't dwell too much on pathologizing Sonny, though, and we quickly find out there are pretty significant clues that point toward some kind of swap. Is she a lizard person? A twin sister who has always wanted to take over Ellen's life? Or something even more bizarre? 

Eventually, he starts to recruit help beyond his high school circle to get to the bottom of what might be going on. Meanwhile, the show slowly starts revealing to the viewer that something sinister did happen while Sonny was in a coma.

Phony is also stylistically interesting to watch. It feels like an indie mumblecore film at times, and the way teens are naturalistically written and performed reminds me of Prime Video's YA drama Sterling Point. There is a similar realism here and less of a prestige TV sheen, making it stand out as something unique against the many, many mystery shows that come across my desk.

Only four of eight episodes were provided to critics, so I'm itching to find out what happened to Ellen — or if this is in fact all in Sonny's head. 

How to watch: The first two episodes of Phony premiere Tuesday, Oct. 20 on Hulu, with a new episode streaming weekly.

💎 Hidden gems

Servant was one of Apple TV's early forays into original programming.

My recommendation: Servant

Why you should watch it: Servant, produced by showrunner M. Night Shyamalan and created by Tony Basgallop, is one of Apple TV's best-hidden horror gems. Fortunately, it ran for four entertaining seasons between 2019 and 2023. This month is the perfect time to rediscover it.

We begin with an unsteady household. Dorothy Turner (Lauren Ambrose) is a local television news reporter traumatized by the recent loss of her 13-week-old baby, Jericho. In the hopes of helping her heal, Dorothy's husband, Sean (Toby Kebbell), and brother, Julian (Harry Potter's Rupert Grint), bring in a therapeutic and very realistic baby doll to replace him. Dorothy, in some form of grief psychosis, is under the impression the doll is real. When young nanny Leanne (Nell Tiger Free) is hired to help out around the house, the doll mysteriously comes to life.

Servant plays a lot with your perception of what is actually happening. In the early seasons especially, it's unclear if Jericho's "rebirth" is a result of Leanne's supernatural abilities, or if their gorgeous Philadelphia brownstone itself is alive in some way. It's classic Shyamalan in that way — keeping you in the dark until a vital twist.

You might not expect it, considering the dark subject matter, but there is a lot of humor injected into this series — even just coming from the way Ambrose embodies Dorothy in a delightfully over-the-top performance. You might like Servant if you got absorbed with Widow's Bay earlier this year.

The production and food design are also phenomenal in their own right. Sean is a stay-at-home consulting chef who often makes the most bizarre dishes — everything from lobster ice cream to placenta croquembouche (yes, you read that right!). Over its 40 episodes, the show finds dynamic ways to showcase the house, where most of the story takes place. It always felt like there was a new room to discover, from the attic to the wine cellar.

Servant was never an awards frontrunner and was actually one of the first shows to ever premiere on Apple TV's streaming platform, but I remember it fondly and am definitely due for a Halloween rewatch.

How to watch: All four seasons of Servant are streaming on Apple TV.
