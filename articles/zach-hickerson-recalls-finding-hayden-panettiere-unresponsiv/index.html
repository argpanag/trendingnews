Zach Hickerson recalls finding Hayden Panettiere unresponsive and disputes mother's claims

TheTools · 27.11.2026 10:47 · source · permalink

Culture, Lifestyle, Society, World / United States of America, World Culture, World Society

Zach Hickerson says he canceled an Atlanta golf trip at Hayden Panettiere's request before finding her unresponsive in August, pushing back against claims from her mother.

Zach Hickerson has shared new details about the August afternoon Hayden Panettiere died, revealing that he canceled a planned golf trip to Atlanta at the actress’s request shortly before discovering her unresponsive.

Speaking with TMZ’s Charlie Neff in interview excerpts published on November 25, the 39-year-old brother of Panettiere’s on-and-off boyfriend, Brian Hickerson, recounted staying nearby in Greenville, South Carolina, where the 36-year-old “Heroes” and “Nashville” star was staying at a vacation rental.

Hickerson explained that he initially assumed Panettiere was asleep when he saw her leaning forward with her legs crossed and hair partially obscuring her face, until Brian lifted her and revealed her pale skin and discolored lips.

According to Hickerson’s account, he immediately dialed 911 while Brian administered Narcan, and both men performed CPR until paramedics arrived at the apartment. Hickerson remembered praying next to Panettiere as emergency personnel worked while his brother appeared frozen in shock.

“I was crying, I put my hand on her leg and said goodbye,” Hickerson recalled. A Greenville Police Department incident report later noted Zach’s visible distress during resuscitation efforts and documented that Brian became emotional after Panettiere was pronounced dead, subsequently showing officers a bag of medications she had been taking.

During the interview, Hickerson also pushed back against remarks made by Panettiere’s mother, Lesley Vogel, who told “Inside Edition” on November 16 that she blamed Brian for her daughter’s passing and implied Zach carried personal guilt over the tragedy.

While expressing sympathy for Vogel’s grief and admitting he has wondered if anything more could have been done, Hickerson rejected any suggestion of culpability or dishonesty, calling the loss a “tragic accident.”

The Greenville County Coroner’s Office officially ruled Panettiere’s August 16 death accidental in September, citing the toxic effects of fentanyl, 4-ANPP, alprazolam, methocarbamol, and quetiapine with no signs of physical trauma, and police closed their investigation in October without charges.
