Tom Brady built one of the most successful careers in NFL history, but his path was never based on being viewed as the obvious future star.

That's why one of his best-known quotes about self-belief carries particular weight.

"If you don't believe in yourself why is anyone else going to believe in you?"

Brady entered the NFL as the 199th pick in the 2000 draft and spent much of the early part of his career proving that draft position didn't have to define what followed.

His message is simple: confidence has to start internally before it can be expected from coaches, teammates or anyone else.

Brady's career was built on self-belief

That mindset became central to the way Brady approached competition.

"I think sometimes in life the biggest challenges end up being the best things that happen in your life."

The quote reflects how Brady often treated setbacks as opportunities to improve rather than reasons to lower expectations.

His career included injuries, defeats and constant questions about how long he could continue playing at the highest level, yet he remained one of the NFL's most demanding competitors.

"You have to believe in the process. You have to believe in the things that you are doing to help the team win."

That belief in preparation was as important as confidence itself.

Confidence still needs work behind it

Brady's philosophy was never simply about telling yourself that you were good enough.

It was also about preparing enough to justify that belief when the pressure arrived.

"If you don't play to win don't play at all."

That mentality helped Brady win seven Super Bowls and establish himself as one of the most decorated players in the history of American football.

The larger lesson behind his original quote is that external belief usually follows what a person demonstrates over time.

Confidence on its own isn't enough, but without it, even preparation and talent can be undermined.

Brady's message remains straightforward: if you want other people to trust what you can do, you first have to trust yourself.
