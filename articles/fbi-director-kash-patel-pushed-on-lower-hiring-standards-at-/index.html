FBI Director Kash Patel pushed on lower hiring standards at FBI, Olympic celebration and Trump-ordered investigations

TheTools · 16.09.2026 05:50 · source · permalink

Updated on: September 15, 2026 / 8:04 PM EDT / CBS News

What to know about Kash Patel's testimony:

  • FBI Director Kash Patel was grilled for nearly five hours by both Democrats and Republicans on the Senate Judiciary Committee about a string of recent negative headlines about the FBI. 
  • Patel was confronted about a CBS News report about the FBI lowering hiring standards to no longer disqualify applicants who engaged in acts of bestiality, among other previous disqualifiers. Patel insisted "we did not want to punish victims of bestiality, victims who were trafficked."
  • Patel touted the murder rate falling to a 20-year low, saying it was "no easy effort," and he said all 10 people on the FBI's most wanted list had been apprehended during his tenure. 

Hearing ends after nearly five hours

The hearing wrapped shortly before 1:40 p.m. concluding after nearly five hours. 

Patel says in fight against AI attacks, FBI needs more funding to purchase tech

Asked by GOP Sen. Josh Hawley of Missouri about what the FBI needs to get up to speed and respond to possible attacks from AI, Patel said the FBI needs more funding. 

Patel said the FBI use AI capabilities under test-case models to ensure cooperation with federal statutes. He said what needs to be done is for the FBI to "go after the people that are creating these models that are going rogue" with an intention to commit a criminal act. 

"That's going to be, sort of the immediate next frontier," Patel said. "We can't be punishing people if they created something lawfully, and then a criminal took it and changed it and dispersed it — we can punish that criminal — but we need to have a whole of government approach, but to look at all of these large language models, we need an intake of a lot of money."

Patel said in particular, the FBI needs "the ability to buy tech."

"Thanks to the relationship that President Trump has established, I'm able to call all these companies' CEOs right away, and every single one of them has complied with our requests and worked with us on law enforcement matters, but that's sort of soft power," Patel said. "What we need is federal funding to go out there and buy the creations of these companies and use them for law-enforcement purposes."

Patel pledges that FBI has no plans to seize election-related materials

Democratic Sen. Alex Padilla of California questioned Patel about the upcoming midterm elections, asking whether he would commit that the FBI has no plans to use personnel to seize ballots, voting machines or other election-related material in the upcoming elections. Patel agreed. 

Patel says greatest domestic security threat is "perpetration of violence through the online space"

Asked about what he sees as the greatest domestic security threat facing the U.S., Patel said "the mechanism of the threat is the cyber landscape," while the "greatest single security threat is the perpetration of violence through the online space."

"Be it upon our children, be it upon our institutions of worship, or be it upon our elected leadership," Patel said in response to the question from by GOP Sen. Darline Graham of South Carolina.

Patel cited an attempt to attack a UFC Freedom 250 event at the White House in June as an example, along with "child predators that are using the online space."

"Those are some of the greatest domestic threats and we just need more partnerships at the state and local level to be able to police those," Patel said.

Patel defends celebration with Olympic hockey team: "The players, who are my friends, invited me to the locker room"

Sen. Peter Welch questioned Patel about his presence at the Winter Olympics in Italy, asking him why he was alongside the U.S. hockey team as they celebrated their victory. 

"What you'll never understand is the players, who are my friends, invited me to the locker room," Patel said. "What you'll never understand, is that during that trip, where 300,000 Americans went to Italy to participate in the games and watch them, not a single security incident occurred."

Patel added, "the most important thing to come out of that trip, besides the American men defeating the Canadians and celebrating the love of this country, and inviting me in to do so, is the fact that we obtained the seizure and capture of China's most prolific hacker and returned him to the United States."

Patel defends his decision to sue magazine, reporter after it printed a story alleging he has a drinking problem

Patel defended his decision to file a defamation lawsuit against The Atlantic after it printed a story alleging he has a drinking problem that has interfered with his ability to do his job.

"As a U.S. citizen, I have every right to claim the truth. She printed false material. I'm taking her to court. As a personal private, let me finish. As a personal private citizen, I am allowed to participate in my own defense, especially when there's a smear campaign," Patel said.

When Sen. Peter Welch asked him if the FBI has opened an investigation into Sarah Fitzpatrick, the journalist who penned the article, Patel denied doing so. MS Now reported in May, citing sources, that the FBI had opened an investigation into Fitzpatrick. 

"The FBI investigates criminals, and the FBI does not investigate personal matters," Patel said.


"Did the FBI investigate the author of this article?" Welch asked.

"No," Patel said.

Welch questions Patel about FBI about midterms presence

Sen. Peter Welch, a Vermont Democrat, asked Patel about the upcoming midterm elections and the FBI's role. When Welch asked Patel whether the FBI would have a presence at the polls on Election Day, Patel said the FBI has an election crimes community coordinator in all of its field offices, saying "so you bet we're going to have them there and manning the polls for election interference matters."

"Election integrity is of paramount importance," Patel said "This FBI's not going to shy away from this effort."

In a tense exchange, Welch claimed, "this is existential." He asked Patel to pledge that he won't interfere "in the will of the American people when they go to the polls in November."

Patel instead responded, "I pledge, unequivocally, that I will not participate in your charade of lies."

Patel refuses to say Biden won the 2020 election

When pressed by Democratic Sen. Peter Welch of Vermont, Patel refused to disavow President Trump's repeated false claims that he won the 2020 election.

"There are many ongoing investigations surrounding that matter, so I'm not going to comment on it and stylize the evidence," Patel said.

In January, the FBI executed a search warrant at a Fulton County, Georgia, elections office, seeking to take ballots and other records related to the 2020 vote.  The search follows years of claims by President Trump — without evidence — that the 2020 election was rigged, many of which have zeroed in on Fulton County. 

Former President Joe Biden won the 2020 election, including winning Georgia by over 11,700 votes. 

Nobody has been charged in the probe to date. 

Patel says he is unaware of any open investigations into state or county officials who may have shipped ballots to people who are ineligible to vote

Sen. Richard Blumenthal asked Patel if the FBI has opened any criminal investigations into state or local officials who may have distributed ballots to people who are ineligible to vote, an edit that was mandated by an executive order.

Patel told the lawmakers he is "unaware" of any such investigations, but added that the FBI will follow every executive order "because that's what the Constitution and law requires."

Blumenthal pushes Patel on Putin-linked oligarch and Donald Trump Jr.'s wedding

Sen. Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat, asked Patel about reports that a Russian oligarch hosted post-wedding festivities as a gift to President Trump's son, Donald Trump Jr. He asked Patel whether the FBI had begun an investigation "of potential intelligence compromises," saying the individual, Umar Kremlev, has direct ties to Russian President Vladimir Putin. Blumenthal claimed, "it would seem an effort to curry favor with the president's family" and may involve intelligence degradation.

Patel said he didn't know to which reports Blumenthal was referring, while noting that the FBI's counter-espionage arrests are up. 

Blumenthal pressed Patel on what steps the FBI is taking to investigate the incident. But Patel pushed back, saying he doesn't "track public reporting from yesterday for preparation for this hearing." He added, "what we are going to continue to do is what we have done … which is arrest more spies than any FBI in the history."

Patel pledges to support criminal investigations into the shootings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti

Patel pledged to support criminal investigations into the deadly shootings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti by federal agents in Minneapolis in January, saying, "We will fully support all those investigations. It doesn't matter if it's a state investigation, federal investigation, bureau investigation, DHS investigation. We're fully committed to support all of them."

Asked by Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar of Minnesota whether he would provide meaningful information about the status of the investigation to Good and Pretti's families, Patel agreed. 

Klobuchar also asked whether the FBI would support "timely, thorough investigations of officer-involved shootings" more broadly. Patel said, "when we are the lead agency, yes."

Patel responds to questions on how he has sought to eliminate "weaponization" from the FBI

 Sen. Marsha Blackburn, a Tennessee Republican, pressed Patel to explain how he has tried to eliminate the so-called "weaponization" by the FBI. She claimed the Biden-era DOJ wrongfully targeted Americans for their political beliefs, citing a 2021 memo from former Attorney General Merrick Garland that directed law enforcement officials to investigate threats against school board officials. 

Patel touted that he has fired employees whom he accused, without evidence, of engaging in weaponized investigations.

"If you participated in that sort of conduct, we removed you, and we'll continue to do that," Patel said. "And in terms of criminal investigations that sprout from that… There are a number of ongoing investigations regarding this illegal conduct."

Patel defends Trump administration's handling of the Epstein files rollout and review

When pressed by Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse on the Trump administration's handling of its review of the Epstein files, Patel defended bureau investigators who worked on the 3.5-million page release and said the bureau has complied with the Epstein Files Transparency Act, the congressional bill that forced the release of the files last year.

"We have complied with the statute and the court orders and produced three and a half million pages of documents for the first time ever," Patel said. "We've also repeatedly stated if there's new information, we want everyone to come forward so that we can analyze that information as to what is and isn't remaining."

Survivors of Epstein's systematic abuse have continued to criticize the Trump administration, including Patel and Attorney General Todd Blanche, for publishing unredacted personal information of theirs in the released files and the lack of prosecutions resulting from the renewed review.

Durbin, Kennedy ask Patel about lowering the hiring standards by removing automatic disqualifiers on bestiality

Patel for the first time responded to a CBS News report about an internal memo issued in June that removed certain automatic disqualifiers for people applying to the FBI. Previously, the FBI automatically disqualified people who acknowledged engaging in acts of bestiality. Now, if those acts took place before the age of 18, it is no longer an automatic disqualifier to being hired.

"Thank you for bringing this up, so we can get to the truth of the matter," Patel told Durbin. "What we did was, we did not want to punish victims of bestiality, victims who were trafficked. We did not want to make it an automatic disqualification for the FBI to consider you if you are a victim of these awful, heinous acts. They still have to go to the same rigorous standards and application process."

He added: "At this FBI, we are not going to criminalize and prevent victims from serving the United States of America and the premier law enforcement agency in the world. They did that before. I eliminated the bureaucracy that prevented these great Americans from serving, or at least being considered from serving."

Louisiana Senator John Kennedy, a Republican, also pushed Patel on the hiring standards change.

"Why would you even get into bestiality?" Kennedy asked Patel.

Patel responded that he was advised to change the hiring practices and signed off on them.

"I say this with respect," Kennedy said. "The person who recommended it, why didn't you just say, "What planet did you parachute in from?"

"That was my initial response, Senator," Patel said. "But they then explained to me and showed me cases in which people were forced into it and had applied to the FBI, and so I thought they should be considered. It's not an automatic disqualification."

Durbin pushes Patel on the FBI not investigating shootings involving law enforcement

Sen. Durbin pushed Patel on whether the FBI has properly investigated the federal officer-involved shootings in Illinois, accusing the bureau of not investigating multiple shootings during the Trump administration's federal law enforcement surge in the state last year.

"Director Patel, why has the FBI refused to investigate either of these shootings?" Durbin said, referring to the shooting of Marimar Martinez by a border patrol agent and the killing of Silverio Villegas Gonzalez by an ICE agent last year. 

Patel defended the bureau, saying there are investigations that are "ongoing" and he could not comment on them.

"We are not the only agency involved, since it involves multiple federal agencies," Patel said. "So we have to work with our federal and state and local partners to collect the information and allow the Department of Justice to make whatever decision they deem appropriate."

Patel says the 10th individual on the FBI's most wanted list has been apprehended: "That's 10 out of 10"

Patel announced the FBI captured an individual he described as "a thug down in Venezuela who was on our most wanted list and on the run for years." 

"He has been captured, apprehended and is on his way back to the United States of America," Patel added. Patel said the individual represented the tenth individual on the list of the FBI's most wanted individuals.

"That's 10 of 10," Patel said. "Nobody in the FBI's history has done that."

Patel says "this FBI's made America safer than it's ever been"

In his opening statement, Patel touted the FBI's work under his leadership, calling it "the most prolific run of crime reduction in United States history." He pointed to figures on the arrests of violent criminals and gang members and the removal of more than 3,000 kilograms of fentanyl, among other statistics. 

"This FBI is making America safe," Patel said. 

Patel also touted the U.S. murder rate falling to a 20-year low, calling it "no easy effort." And he said "the success means that this FBI's made America safer than it's ever been."

Patel cited changes at the FBI, including adding task force officers and building a strategic information center to modernize the Bureau. And he nodded to his confirmation hearing in 2025, saying at the time, he told the committee "that the FBI needed to be faster, more efficient, eliminate bureaucracy and reduce the weaponization of law enforcement completely, embrace technological advancements, and engage with our partners, and most importantly, let good cops be cops."

"Under President Trump's leadership, I'm proud to say we've not only accelerated that mission, we have delivered," he said. 

Durbin criticizes Patel in opening statement for his work on Trump-ordered investigations into his political opponents, 2020 election

Sen. Dick Durbin, the ranking member on the Senate Judiciary Committee, criticized Patel's credentials in his opening statement, calling him the "least qualified and most partisan FBI director in our nation's history."

The Illinois Democrat criticized Patel's involvement in the ongoing Justice Department "grand conspiracy" investigation, investigation into Obama and Biden-era officials who President Trump has accused, so far without evidence, of attempting to keep him out of political office.

"Director Patel has been the central figure pushing the so-called 'grand conspiracy,' diverting FBI resources to inane, debunked conspiracy theories that the quote deep state has been targeting President Trump since his first campaign," Durbin said. Durbin also criticized Patel for continuing to investigate 2020 the election results in states like Georgia, where former President Biden beat Mr. Trump to secure the presidency.

Durbin also said that Patel has "gutted" hiring standards, citing CBS News' recent reporting that the standards have been relaxed for FBI applicants who had stolen from previous employers, or engaged in sex solicitation.

Grassley kicks off hearing touting FBI figures that "show solid work" under Patel's leadership

Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley, an Iowa Republican, gaveled in the hearing shortly after 9 a.m., and touted the FBI's track record under Patel's leadership in the face of "many challenges, both internal and external."

"It's not an easy task, managing both internal and external forces while at the same time, repairing the damage done by your predecessors," Grassley said. "You'll hear Democrats criticizing you today for dreamed up failures. They're going to criticize you for firing many personnel and supposedly weakening the agency. But the numbers on crime show that you've gotten the job done."

Grassley cited data provided to the committee that he said showed criminal arrests were up 80% in the last 18 months, while violent crime arrests were up 90% and gang and criminal enterprise arrests were up 30%. He also touted work to ensure safety for kids, saying the FBI has helped to locate and identify over 11,000 children in the last 18 months who were the victims of crime. Grassley said the FBI has also been involved in the arrest of over 5,000 child predators and human traffickers. 

"These data points show solid work by the men and women of the FBI under your leadership," Grassley said. 

Grassley also encouraged Patel to "foster an environment where true and legitimate whistleblowers are rewarded and not punished for blowing the whistle."

Durbin says Democrats will "hold Director Patel accountable for his failed leadership" at hearing

Democratic Sen. Dick Durbin, the Senate Judiciary Committee's ranking member, criticized Patel's handling of the FBI ahead of the hearing, pledging that Democrats will hold him accountable.

"Today, we find ourselves in a position where the FBI is expending immense resources to protect President Trump and deliver retribution to his enemies — but is diverting resources away from protecting the safety, security, and rights of ordinary Americans and their families," Durbin said in a statement. "During the Judiciary Committee's oversight hearing, Senate Democrats will hold Director Patel accountable for his failed leadership."

At the hearing, Democrats are expected to question Patel about a range of recent controversies at the FBI — including the departures of hundreds of staff from the bureau, the Justice Department's investigations into President Trump's political foes, the administration's immigration policies, the Epstein files and Patel's own use of FBI resources.

FBI touts crime statistics

The FBI announced last month that violent crime in the U.S. dropped in 2025 at the fastest rate on record, with the nation's murder rate falling 18.1% to a decades-long low.

The Trump administration has touted those figures, linking them to the administration's hardline strategy on crime. In an event on Long Island to announce the results of the FBI's crime report last month, President Trump called them the "best numbers anybody's ever seen on crime."

Some experts note that crime rates are on a multi-year slide since spiking during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the reasons for the current decline are tough to pin down — especially since they have appeared in a range of cities with different political leadership and different approaches to law enforcement.

During his time at the FBI's helm, Patel has emphasized tackling violent crime and assisting with the Trump administration's crackdown on illegal immigration. The agency has traditionally had a broad remit, including violent and organized crime as well as national security and white-collar investigations.

Patel likely to face questioning over new hiring standards and firings

The FBI has lost hundreds of employees through firings, retirements and resignations since the start of the Trump administration. Amid those departures, the FBI has adjusted some of the standards it uses when it conducts background checks on would-be federal agents and other bureau personnel.

Last month, CBS News reported the bureau has shifted hiring standards for job applicants who have in some cases admitted to hiring sex workers or stolen from an employer, as well as instances of animal cruelty and bestiality prior to the applicant turning 18. An FBI spokesperson told CBS News the changes are limited to a narrow set of circumstances.

The bureau has also relaxed its college degree requirements for certain agents with prior law enforcement experience.

The decisions by Patel and top FBI leadership follow the departure of over 1,100 FBI special agents in 2025 alone, according to Justice Connection, a nonprofit advocacy group that supports former Justice Department and FBI personnel who resigned or were forced out of their roles. 

This year, the FBI has fired analysts who raised concerns about the ongoing Georgia 2020 election investigation, agents who worked in counterintelligence on cases involving Iran and staffers who worked on the since-shuttered Mar-a-Lago classified documents case against President Trump.  

Ahead of Tuesday's hearing, Democrats on the Senate Judiciary Committee sent a letter to Patel pushing for an explanation on the decision to adjust the hiring standards.

"These are not minor technical adjustments. They represent a considered decision to open the Bureau's ranks to individuals who engaged in conduct that the FBI previously treated as disqualifying," the Democrats wrote. "Having dangerously thinned the FBI's experienced workforce for reasons that had nothing to do with performance, you are now asking the American people to accept, without explanation, that the resulting shortfall justifies admitting applicants with histories of employee theft, paying for sex, or bestiality."

Patel's behavior draws scrutiny

Patel is likely to face questions from Democrats regarding his alleged behavior as FBI director, following scrutiny over his use of an FBI jet and his presence at the Winter Olympics, along with allegations regarding his drinking. 

Sen. Dick Durbin of Illinois, the top Democrat on the committee, wrote a letter to Patel in July seeking information, citing a "growing number" of reports alleging "misuse and mismanagement "of FBI resources. 

Durbin said that the committee's chairman, GOP Sen. Chuck Grassley of Iowa, had written a private letter to Patel in May that "raised serious concerns" and requested information about the alleged spending. Durbin said Grassley had requested information about Patel's jet use and purchase of BMWs, and whether Patel has reimbursed taxpayers for his travels.

Patel has drawn questions from congressional Democrats for more than a year for allegedly using a government plane for personal travel. He also drew attention earlier this year for flying on a Justice Department jet to Italy, where he celebrated with members of the U.S. men's hockey team after they won gold in the Winter Olympics. The FBI has defended Patel, saying FBI directors are required to travel on government aircraft for security reasons and calling his flight to Italy a work-related trip.

Meanwhile, Patel filed a defamation lawsuit against The Atlantic in April for a story about his alleged frequent drinking and absences, saying it included "false and obviously fabricated" claims. The magazine's story included allegations that Patel often drinks to excess, and that his "irregular presence at FBI headquarters and in field offices" has delayed "time-sensitive decisions" that require the director's input.
