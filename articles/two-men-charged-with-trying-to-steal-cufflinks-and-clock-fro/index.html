Two men charged with trying to steal cufflinks and clock from royal home

TheTools · 26.11.2026 23:36 · source · permalink

Two men charged with trespassing at the Prince and Princess of Wales's country home are accused of trying to steal cufflinks and a clock.

Police were called to Anmer Hall, on the Sandringham estate in Norfolk, shortly before 18:00 GMT on Monday after reports of suspicious activity.

Mirgen Doku, 34, and Arilen Ndoci, 32, both of no fixed address, have been charged with entering a dwelling with intent to steal and trespassing on a protected site.

Both appeared at Norwich Magistrates' Court earlier and have been remanded in custody until a hearing on Monday. Prosecutors said a third suspect remained at large.

The men, who appeared in court in black sweatshirts, are accused of stealing six pairs of cufflinks and a clock.

Both spoke through an Albanian interpreter, but did not enter pleas.

The court heard one of the pairs of cufflinks were described as "the Duke of Edinburgh cufflinks".

William and Catherine do not live at Anmer Hall full-time, but use the estate as a country retreat during the holidays.

The property was unoccupied at the time of the incident, police added. On Monday, Kensington Palace said it did not comment on security matters.

Dogs, drones and a helicopter were used to search the area before two men were arrested within the estate's grounds.

Anmer Hall is located on the private estate and is about two miles (3km) east from Sandringham House, one of the King's private residences.

The country retreat was gifted to the Prince and Princess of Wales from the late Queen Elizabeth II following their wedding in 2011.
