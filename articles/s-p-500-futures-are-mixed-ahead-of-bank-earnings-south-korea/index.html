S&P 500 futures are mixed ahead of bank earnings; South Korea's Kospi falls below 6,000: Live updates

TheTools · 12.10.2026 22:02 · source · permalink

Futures tied to the broad market S&P 500 were mixed early Tuesday, with traders bracing for a slew of corporate earnings reports from major companies such as JPMorgan Chase and UnitedHealth.

S&P 500 futures inched down about 0.2%, while Nasdaq-100 futures lost 0.7%. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures rose 122 points, or 0.2%

In Asia, South Korea's benchmark Kospi closed 5.46% lower, after dropping below 6,000 to its lowest level since July 31. Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 1.82%, while Australia's benchmark S&P/ASX 200 was flat. Mainland China's CSI 300 slipped 0.29%.

Big banks JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo are slated to release their results before the open on Tuesday. Dow member UnitedHealth is also on deck.

The early reports could set the tone for an earnings season with lofty expectations. Analysts on average expect S&P 500 profits grew by nearly 30% from the year-earlier period, according to FactSet.

"Three groups will likely shape how investors read the season: 1) Banks (consumer and business health), 2) Big Tech (AI monetization), and 3) Energy (the durability of oil-driven profits)," wrote Anthony Saglimbene, chief market strategist at Ameriprise. "Underneath these areas, the health of consumers and businesses amid higher interest rates, elevated costs, and concerns about the macroeconomic environment will be closely monitored across sectors throughout the season."

Investors on Tuesday will also parse through existing home sales figures along with commentary from Federal Reserve officials Christopher Waller, Tom Barkin and Susan Collins.

Later in the week, inflation data for September will be released – which could jolt Treasury yields after they reached multidecade highs recently. The U.S. bond market was closed Monday due to the Indigenous Peoples' Day holiday.

Wall Street is coming off a mixed session. The Dow ended Monday more than 195 points higher, while declines in memory stocks pushed the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 into negative territory for the day.

Japan's Nikkei 225 closed 1.82% lower at 67,777.52, while South Korea's Kospi dropped 5.46%.

Australia's benchmark S&P/ASX 200 was flat at 8,783.90.

Hong Kong's Hang Seng index was down nearly 1% in its last hour of trade on Tuesday, while mainland China's CSI 300 closed 0.29% lower at 4,275.68.

—Justina Lee

European chip stocks sank in early morning trade, with semiconductor equipment maker ASML down 3.3%, and STMicroelectronics and German chip maker Infineon both down 5%. Dutch firms BE Semiconductor and ASM International were both around 4% lower at the open.

— April Roach

Oil giant BP said Tuesday that its third-quarter results are expected to include post-tax adjusting items relating to impairment charges in the range of $3 billion to $4.5 billion.

"These charges are primarily attributable to transition businesses in the gas & low carbon energy segment and are excluded from underlying replacement cost profit," the company said in a trading statement.

Its third-quarter earnings will be published in full on Oct. 30.

— Chloe Taylor

The euro was higher against the U.S. dollar and the British pound on Tuesday morning.

By 2:25 a.m. ET, the single currency was up 0.2% versus the greenback, after falling the previous day.

In a Monday note, ING analysts said French fiscal concerns could keep weighing on the euro into the new year. They said they expected EUR/USD to remain in a 1.10 to 1.15 range in the coming months.

It was last seen trading at around $1.1172.

— Chloe Taylor

The Korea Exchange activated a sell-side sidecar on the Kospi, temporarily halting program trading, as South Korean stocks drop. 

A sell-side sidecar is triggered when the Kospi 200 futures index falls 5% or more for at least one minute, stopping trading for five minutes.

—Justina Lee

Asian technology stocks fell Tuesday, tracking U.S. peers that declined ahead of the release of U.S. inflation data later this week.

In Japan, SoftBank Group dropped 6.7%, while chip equipment maker Tokyo Electron fell more than 5%. Advantest lost 6.1%, and Japanese memory chipmaker Kioxia declined 8.8%.

In South Korea, SK Hynix fell 6.5%, while Samsung Electronics slipped 5.6%. Seoul Semiconductor declined 3.1%.

—Justina Lee

Asia-Pacific markets traded lower early Tuesday.

Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 1.20% while the Topix was flat.

The Kospi declined 3.29% at open, while the small-cap Kosdaq fell 2.49%.

Australia's benchmark S&P/ASX 200 was 0.33% lower.

—Justina Lee

Asia-Pacific markets were set to open lower Tuesday, amid concerns over elevated oil prices.

Japan's Nikkei 225 was poised to decline as trading resumes after a holiday, with its Chicago futures contract at 68,320 and its Osaka counterpart last trading at 68,380, compared with the index's previous close of 69,030.92 on Friday.

Hong Kong Hang Seng index futures were last at 24,228, compared with the index's last close of 24,271.20.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was 0.13% lower in early trading.

Renewed concerns over oil supply disruptions drove Brent past $106 per barrel, following the deadly Saudi Airport attack last Saturday which left 12 people dead and more than 300 injured.
