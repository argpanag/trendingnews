Russian oil firm Lukoil’s overseas assets sold to investors led by US billionaire Todd Boehly | CNN Business

TheTools · 25.11.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

US billionaire Todd Boehly is leading a consortium to acquire the vast portfolio of international energy assets belonging to Russia’s second-largest oil producer, his investment firm Eldridge announced Wednesday.

Eldridge said in a statement that the consortium, which includes the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation, had agreed to buy Lukoil International from its sanctioned Russian parent. The sale price was not disclosed, but the deal will result in the US government receiving an equity stake in Lukoil via the DFC.

The deal will “secure critical global energy assets under American and allied ownership,” the statement added.

Lukoil’s upstream operations span 11 countries and currently produce around 350,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day. The portfolio includes oil and gas fields; three refineries in Europe; 2,000 retail gas stations across 16 countries in Europe, North America and Central Asia; and an international trading business.

Lukoil’s overseas portfolio of assets will be acquired via a share swap, with restricted shares in its Russian parent and other Russian companies exchanged for Lukoil International’s equity. This “avoids any direct cash payment to Russia under the transaction,” Eldridge said.

Other investment partners include UAE-based Catena Investment Holding and US investment bank Xtellus Partners. The transaction is subject to conditional approval from the US Treasury Department’s Office of Foreign Assets Control.

CNN has contacted Lukoil and the DFC for comment.

Petras Katinas, a research fellow at the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI), a defense and security think tank in London, argued that the deal “demonstrates the efficiency of sanctions.”

“Western sanctions are forcing one of Russia’s largest oil companies to relinquish international assets accumulated over decades,” Katinas told CNN earlier this month, ahead of the deal being announced.

Transferring refineries and fuel stations to non-Russian owners will “reduce Moscow’s trade influence over important energy infrastructure and remove a source of future earnings for Lukoil,” he added.

The deal caps a long and tumultuous sale process. Lukoil put its international assets up for sale more than a year ago after the Treasury Department sanctioned the company in a bid to pressure Moscow to end its war in Ukraine by cutting off an important source of revenue. Rosneft, Russia’s top oil producer, was also sanctioned at the time.

The months that followed saw potential deals come and go. Last year, Swiss commodities trading firm Gunvor Group, now Centalion, made an offer for the assets—though the deal was scuttled when it could not win approval from the Treasury. Then, in January, private equity firm Carlyle agreed to buy the foreign assets, excluding those in Kazakhstan. That deal also fell apart this year.

The transaction is an unusual investment for Boehly, a part-owner of sports teams including the Los Angeles Dodgers and Los Angeles Lakers. Boehly’s asset management and insurance holding company Eldridge, which manages more than $75 billion in assets, is focused mostly on sports, entertainment, diversified credit and real estate.
