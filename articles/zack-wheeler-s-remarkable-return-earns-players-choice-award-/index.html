Zack Wheeler's remarkable return earns Players Choice Award from his peers

TheTools · 29.10.2026 09:37 · source · permalink

The Phillies' right-hander was named National League Comeback Player of the Year by the Major League Baseball Players Association.

What a start for Zack Wheeler on Tuesday against the Reds. He strikes out 14 over seven strong innings, tying his career-high.

Zack Wheeler's return to the mound was one of the Phillies' biggest success stories of 2026. Now, he's being recognized by the players who witnessed it firsthand.

The Major League Baseball Players Association announced Wednesday that Wheeler was voted the National League Comeback Player of the Year as part of its annual Players Choice Awards.

The 36-year-old right-hander received the honor after returning from venous thoracic outlet syndrome, which required surgery to remove a rib after doctors discovered a blood clot near his right shoulder in August 2025.

Wheeler made his season debut April 25 and returned to form, posting a 14-5 record with a 3.00 ERA and 193 strikeouts across 162 innings in 28 starts.

In a video released by the MLBPA announcing the award, several pitchers around the league praised Wheeler's ability to overcome an injury that initially raised questions about his future.

"He's a special player. He's had a tremendous career," Yankees right-hander Cam Schlittler said. "You see the injuries can be, you know, a setback and then rehab can be a tough process. I was very impressed."

Orioles pitcher Chris Bassitt recalled his initial reaction to Wheeler's diagnosis, believing the injury could potentially bring his career to an abrupt end.

"What I thought at the time was like he doesn't deserve to go out like this," Bassitt said. "He deserves to have a path where he can choose when he's done."

Wheeler also offered a glimpse into the uncertainty of his recovery, detailing the multiple procedures required to restore proper blood flow.

"This is one of these surgeries that, you know, just isn't really that common," Wheeler said. "So a lot of uncertainty around it. At the same time, you got to keep a positive mindset."

That mindset helped Wheeler return to the level of performance his peers had grown accustomed to seeing.

And for a pitcher who has spent more than a decade competing against some of baseball's best, the recognition carried particular significance.

"I always thought when we vote on these every year that this would be one of the cool awards to win," Wheeler said. "The ones that the players vote on is the guys that you're competing against, playing with, guys who know what it takes to be good day in and day out."

Wheeler could soon add another honor to his résumé.

He is also a leading candidate for MLB's separate NL Comeback Player of the Year Award, which will be announced Nov. 11. Unlike the Players Choice Awards, which are voted on by major league players, MLB's honor is determined by a vote of the 30 MLB.com club beat reporters.
