The sell-off in chip stocks deepened on Wednesday as a hotter-than-expected inflation read raised worries about more rate hikes by the Federal Reserve and a higher cost of capital, potentially throwing a wrench into the AI trade.

Long-dated bond yields, tied to borrowing costs, rose before trimming gains after September's inflation data was released.

That put pressure on Tech (XLK) stocks, which were already on jittery ground amid elevated yields, rising debt, possible disruptions to AI buildouts, and overall concerns about a bubble.

Chip giant Nvidia (NVDA) closed the session down more than 3%, leading the sector lower, as seen on Yahoo Finance's AlphaSpace heatmap below.

Nvidia stock fell almost 2%.

(NVDA )

The chip sell-off started earlier this week as memory makers Micron (MU) and SK Hynix (SKHY) fell in recent sessions amid worries over Chinese competition.

On Wednesday, worries about borrowing costs also hit the AI trade.

CoreWeave (CRWV) stock fell more than 16% after a chart of the neocloud providers' bond yields circulated on social media.

The stock's sharp sell-off highlighted concerns about the company's debt-heavy balance sheet and whether it can scale fast enough to manage its growing interest expense.

Any slowdown in AI spending could spell trouble for chip makers, which have been the main beneficiaries of infrastructure spending as hyperscalers race to build out data centers.

On Thursday, investors will get another glimpse of the health of the AI build-out when TSMC (TSM) reports quarterly results. Investors expect quarterly profits to rise 60%.

Investors will pay close attention to the foundry's capacity, given ASML's (ASML) results on Wednesday. The maker of advanced chipmaking machines warned it may not be able to fully meet demand in 2027 and 2008.

Ines Ferre is a senior business reporter for Yahoo Finance.
