Googlebook uses built-in AI to help you finish tasks faster and easier. You can use the "Magic Pointer" to ask questions about anything on your screen or use "Rambler" to turn messy voice notes into organized lists. It even lets you build your own custom widgets without needing to know how to code. It’s basically a smarter laptop designed to do the boring work for you.

Explore other styles:

Great hardware needs software that can actually keep up, so we designed Googlebook with an intelligence system that brings Gemini directly on your device. It anticipates what you need, simplifies your daily tasks, and proactively helps you get things done. Here are a few features that make Googlebook uniquely helpful.

Work smarter with Magic Pointer.

Magic Pointer reinvents the decades-old point and click computer experience.

With a quick wiggle of your cursor, Magic Pointer — which understands text, images, and context — brings Gemini exactly where you’re working and makes previously tedious tasks trivial. Here are just a few examples of how you can use Magic Pointer.

  • Instant scheduling: Highlight a complex marathon training plan on a web page, and ask Gemini to automatically map out those runs and schedule them in Google Calendar.
  • Keeping you secure: Hover over a suspicious email and ask Gemini to analyze the text and images to verify if it’s spam or a legitimate message.
  • Combining visuals: Select a set of images with Magic Pointer and ask Gemini to visualize them together. No downloading, uploading, or prompting. Just point, ask, result.

From day one, we built Magic Pointer with privacy at the core. It only springs into action when summoned by a wiggle, and stays off when you’re not using it.

Talk naturally to your Googlebook with Rambler.

Standard voice dictation writes down everything that comes out of your mouth — including the stutters, mistakes, and filler words. And cleaning those up can take longer than typing your thoughts on your keyboard. Rambler actually understands the context and nuance behind your words.

Say you’re drafting team meeting notes when you’d rather be wrapping up your day. Just open Rambler and let your stream of consciousness fly. Rambler will take a disorganized verbal brain dump and clean up the errors, organize action items into structured bullet points, and even add relevant emoji. It’s also natively multilingual, so you can switch languages mid-sentence without losing a beat.

Make Googlebook your own with Create My Widget.

Create My Widget on Googlebook lets you build your own widgets, with no coding experience required. Build bespoke widgets simply by describing what you want. Make one to track your favorite sports team’s scores, or create a visual countdown to your next family trip.

If you want to go further, Google Antigravity comes with every Googlebook. Use it to write your own apps then deploy them directly to your Googlebook. Developers also have access to a full Linux terminal environment to run tools like Claude Code or Antigravity CLI and do serious agentic coding right on your Googlebook.

Get started right away with Gemini and Chrome.

The powerful Gemini features that you know and use on Chrome and Android are available on Googlebook out of the box, including task automations, Gemini Live, and proactive suggestions that show your next move based on what’s on your screen. You can even close your laptop while Gemini Spark works in the background to process complex requests and finish your tasks.

Pricing, availability, and pre-order details

Starting at $899, every Googlebook includes 12 months of Google AI Pro providing 5TB of cloud storage and higher usage limits in Gemini, plus 3 months of YouTube Premium, Adobe Photoshop, and more. Googlebook OS also comes with regular feature drops and updates for up to 10 years.

Pre-orders are now open on the Google Store, Best Buy, and other select retailers. Devices hit shelves on October 4 in the U.S., and October 5 in Canada, the U.K., Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia.
