Phillies icon, chairman emeritus Bill Giles dies at 92

TheTools · 26.09.2026 01:17 · source · permalink

Bill Giles, the chairman emeritus and longtime executive of the Philadelphia Phillies, has died at the age of 92.

The Phillies are mourning the loss of a legend. The team’s chairman emeritus and longtime executive Bill Giles died at the age of 92. NBC10’s Miguel Martinez-Valle has the details.

Bill Giles, the chairman emeritus and longtime executive of the Philadelphia Phillies as well as the honorary National League president, has died.

The Phillies announced that Giles passed away at his home in Gladwyne, Pennsylvania, on Friday, Sept. 25, 2026. He was 92-years-old.

“The Phillies family lost an icon today,” said the team’s Managing Partner and CEO John Middleton. “Bill helped to reshape our franchise from a traditional baseball club into a highly visible entertainment and business operation.  His profound impact in overseeing one of the greatest eras in franchise history, both on and off the field, will forever be his legacy. Our hearts are heavy for the Giles family and all those who had the privilege of knowing Bill.”

The son of former National League president Warren C. Giles, Bill Giles was born in Rochester, New York, in 1934. He began his Major League Baseball (MLB) career as a teenager when he worked various jobs in the Cincinnati Reds’ public relations department. He then worked in Houston as a traveling secretary, publicity director and promotions director.

Giles’ legendary career with the Phillies began in 1969 when he served as the team’s vice president of business operations. He was then promoted to executive vice president in 1972.

In 1981, Giles organized a group that purchased the Phillies and served as the team’s president until 1997. He was then named the Phillies’ chairman before being named chairman emeritus in 2015.

During his time with the Phillies, Giles played a pivotal role in opening Veterans Stadium and later Citizens Bank Park. He used clever marketing ideas to draw in fans and helped create the iconic mascot, the Phillie Phanatic.

During his time as the team’s president, the Phillies won two National League East titles and National League pennants.

In 1999, the Phillies established the Bill Giles Award which honors minor league players who share his attributes, including a love and respect for baseball, outstanding work ethic and pride in the organization.

Giles was also named honorary president of the National League in 2001 and won the Bud Selig Executive Leadership Award for lifetime achievement in baseball in 2010.

Giles was also part of a council that expanded the MLB to 30 teams, incorporated interleague play and restructured the league’s playoff format with a wild card slot.

Giles was also heavily involved in Philadelphia, serving on numerous boards for various organizations, including the Philadelphia Convention and Tourism Bureau, Philadelphia Chamber of Commerce, Philadelphia Sports Congress, Police Athletic League and Salvation Army. Giles also served as president of the Boy Scouts of America and Corporate Alliance for Drug Education.

Giles is survived by three sons and eight grandchildren. Information on his funeral arrangements will be announced at a later date, according to the Phillies.
