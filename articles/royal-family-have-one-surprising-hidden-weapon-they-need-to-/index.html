Royal Family have one surprising hidden weapon – they need to use it

TheTools · 14.11.2026 14:28 · source · permalink

A royal expert has revealed what the family could do to help with the dwindling numbers of working royals.

The Royal Family are facing dwindling numbers, but there’s one thing they could do to gain popularity and bump up the number of working royals.

Royal expert Richard Fitzwilliams has said that people often forget that Edward and Sophie’s children are important people. He said: “As the monarch’s grandchild, Lady Louise technically has the option of becoming a Princess, so she’s quite a senior royal really.

“Edward and Sophie are also popular and do well on YouGov ratings, but much of their hard work goes unnoticed. If they were to involve their children then they’d be introducing younger royals into the world, which they’ve really got to do, because there are now very few senior royals under the age of 70.”

Lady Louise was born on November 8, 2003, at Frimley Park Hospital in Surrey. She and her brother James, Earl of Wessex are not working royals. Louise studied English at the University of St Andrews in Scotland, the same university Princess Catherine and Prince William attended. She graduated this summer.

James received his A-level results earlier this year, with Buckingham Palace sharing the news that the young royal would start university in September. The statement said: “Having achieved the grades he needed, the Earl of Wessex will be going to the Royal Agricultural University to study Rural Land & Property Management. He will head straight there without taking a year out.”

To their credit, Sophie and Edward have spent years establishing themselves as the dependable representatives of the monarchy. But perhaps their next important contribution should be preparing their own children for a more prominent role.

That does not necessarily mean thrusting them into the limelight or sacrificing their privacy. Rather, it could involve taking them on selected overseas visits, involving them in diplomatic and charitable engagements and allowing them to build relationships with their counterparts in other royal households.
