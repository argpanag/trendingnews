Huge Test fears as Aussie duo struck down after teen star’s blitz sparks win in ODI finale

TheTools · 30.09.2026 22:56 · source · permalink

Australia has picked up a consolation 45-run victory via the DLS, but suffered fresh injury concerns to Cameron Green and Matt Renshaw, as rain brought an early to the third ODI against South Africa in a wet and freezing cold Potchefstroom.

Australia piled on 9/322 courtesy of half-centuries from Mitchell Marsh and Alex Carey, as well as teenager Ollie Peake’s career-high knock, after being sent it and in reply, series victors, the Proteas were 4/145 after 28 overs when rain ended the day/night affair.

The Australians walked away with concerns over the fitness of Green and Renshaw for the Test series starting on October 9, however.

Green ended up on the ground when struck on the goin by a Corbin Bosch full toss while batting, but he shook that blow off and hit the next delivery for six.

The next ball was what sounded the alarm for Australia, however, as Green grabbed at his abdominal area after miscued hook shot ballooned to wicketkeeper Quinton De Kock to end his innings.

Green then attempted to bowl in the warm-up during the change of innings, but was clearly uncomfortable and did not take the field for any of the Proteas’ innings.

The carnage continued as Renshaw sustained a nasty blow to his wrist in the field after trying to stop a Ryan Rickleton pull off Adam Zampa at square leg.

Renshaw and Green been sidelined along with injuries to Nathan Ellis and Jack Edwards sustained in the second ODI left Test players Marnus Labuschagne and Beau Webster having to substitute field.

Josh Inglis is already in doubt for the Test series after fracturing his finger in the ODI series opener, and the Australians could now be without three of the batters in the squad for the first Test in Durban.

South Africa also had an injury concern of their own with Test batter Tony De Zorzi injuring his hamstring in the field and not batting.

Meanwhile, the visitors welcomed back Test captain Pat Cummins for his first ODI outing in two years, but Marsh remained as skipper.

“Obviously would have been nice to have a full game. I thought the way we batted, showed improvement,” Marsh said at the post-match ceremony.

“Disappointing series...Looking forward to the World Cup in 12 months’ time. SA are a team that plays well at home and lot of learnings from that.

“It was a great challenge and always a privilege to captain Australia. I felt good, but would’ve been nice to get a couple of big scores for the team.”

Marsh continued his fine form at the top of the order, crunching 56 off 42 balls before falling lbw to left-arm spinner Bjorn Fortuin (1/50) - the third time in the series he has been undone by spin following a blistering start.

Fellow opener Travis Head (24) and No.3 Cooper Connolly (23) both got starts before Head dragged onto his stumps and Connolly was knocked over by a vicious yorker from Gerald Coetzee (2/61).

Australia had been crying out for middle order runs in this series and they finally got them from teenager Oliver Peake and gloveman Alex Carey.

Peake was left out of the first two games against South Africa but was recalled following a hamstring injury to all-rounder Jack Edwards.

The 19-year-old Victorian was not a straight swap at No.7 - where he had batted in six of seven previous ODI innings - however, with the left-handed rising star given an opportunity at No.4.

He repaid the faith by compiling his highest score in international cricket - a well-made 48 off 53 balls that included four fours and a six.

Carey was the one to kick on as the No.5 top-scored with a busy knock of 74 off 88, including four fours and two sixes, and was named player of the match for his highest ODI score in two years.

Both Carey and Peake fell to seamer Corbin Bosch (4/60), who did the bulk of the damage for the hosts.

Renshaw (30 off 29) and Green (23 off 18) chipped in late in the innings - before their injury issues - to ensure that every member of Australia’s top seven passed 20 and to help lift the total beyond 300.

“Yeah, the guys got off to a good start - they have done it all series - but today was nice to get past 300,” Carey said.

“Rain is never a good thing in cricket, but we were ahead of the curve. Full credit to SA for winning the series.

“I know my role in the team - catching balls and sometimes playing as a batter. Unfortunately, Inglis went down. The guys are excited and the Test players are here. Hopefully, the weather will be okay. I know everyone is keen for the Test series. We will start the prep tomorrow.”

Mitchell Starc returned in place of Josh Hazlewood as Australia’s ‘Big Three’ managed their workloads ahead of the Test series starting on October 9, but it was Xavier Bartlett who did the damage with the new ball.

The Queenslander removed Quinton De Kock for a five-ball duck in his opening over and four overs later trapped fellow opener Aiden Markram lbw for 15 to put South Africa behind the eight ball early in their run chase.

No.3 Ryan Rickelton played a lone-hand of defiance as he was unbeaten on 71 off 81 when the rain came down - with all-rounder Bosch (13*) for company.

A 72-run partnership between Rickelton and Matthew Breetzke (25) threatened to take the game away from the Australians but Adam Zampa bounced back from poor outings in Durban and Johannesburg with two big wickets.

Zampa (2/34) had Breetzke stumped by Carey and four overs later he hit Tristan Stubbs (14) on the pads to put Australia well on top.

Having clinched a sixth straight ODI series victory against Australia in Johannesburg, the South Africans also had an eye towards the Tests in making five changes to their XI.

Captain Temba Bavuma, who scored a century at the Wanderers, towering twins Marco and Duan Jansen, player of the series winning spinner Keshav Maharaj, and another Wanderers centurion David Miller all came out of the the side.

“The result didn’t go our way, but we wanted to give guys opportunities,” Bavuma said.

“Rickelton got some runs under his belt, Coetzee with the ball...from that point of view, it was good. We managed to go 2-0 up and we had the luxury of resting guys and giving others opportunities.

“It’s never a dead rubber and always something we look to achieve. I feel we are making good strides. If we look at batting and bowling...a lot of it is about momentum and overcoming challenges in front of you.”
