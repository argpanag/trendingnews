Luka Doncic said the new-look Lakers will need time and reps to build consistency but stressed that he "really likes playing with these guys" and took responsibility himself after scoring 37 points in a 112-95 loss to the Warriors.

Slovenian superstar Luka Doncic made it clear that he remains confident in the Los Angeles Lakers' new-look roster despite a disappointing 112-95 loss to the Golden State Warriors on Friday night.

The Lakers blew a 25-point first-quarter lead in their NBA Cup opener, falling to 4-2 after Golden State completely turned the game around over the final three quarters.

Doncic still led Los Angeles with 37 points, seven rebounds, and five assists, shooting 12-of-27 from the field and 6-of-16 from three-point range.

Asked what it will take for the Lakers to develop the consistency needed to remain a strong team throughout the entire season, Doncic pointed primarily to time and experience together.

"I don't know what it's going to take," Luka said. "Obviously, reps and just playing. This game is a good example. They play hard defense and show a lot, so it was a good game for us. Not good because we lost, but good to practice those things."

"It's going to take some time for sure," he added. "We've got a lot of new guys, but I really like playing with these guys."

"Everybody can be better than this. Me first. I've got to be better. We lose together, we win together."

The Lakers will look to bounce back at home against the Toronto Raptors on Sunday, November 1.

One of the Lakers' biggest issues against Golden State was handling the additional pressure Luka faced when the Warriors brought their big men higher up the floor.

Doncic expects opponents to continue using similar coverages against him, but believes the Lakers should be able to turn that attention into an advantage.

"I think there are going to be a lot of shocks on me," Doncic said. "A lot of times, even going back to Europe, there were a lot of shocks, so I'm kind of used to it."

"We just need to do better with that. You create the advantage. You put two on the ball, and then you've got to go score. It's four-on-three, so we've got to be better."

The 27-year-old said the Lakers must attack the four-on-three advantage after he gets the ball out of the initial pressure.

"You've got to read the low man first," Doncic explained. "It's not just because I made the pass. You throw it to the short roll, and then you've got to read the low man."

"But mostly, we've just got to be aggressive to score. It's an advantage, four-on-three, and I think we've got to be way better at this."

Despite Friday's loss, Doncic has opened the season in dominant individual form, averaging 36.2 points, 6.8 rebounds, and 7.6 assists per game for Los Angeles.
