    14:45 BST

    Lorraine McKenna
    BBC Sport Journalist at Monza

    I’ve ventured out into the vast Monza park to see how fans are cooling off in the break between third practice and qualifying. Every shaded spot available is taken while the queues for the water stations are extremely long.

    On my travels I spot Martina, Giada and Giulia carrying 10/10 signage for Mercedes’ George Russell. So the Italian contingent are also supporting Briton Russell. Or are they?

    When I ask if they think Russell can beat Antonelli in the title race this year, the trio shout loudly “noooo!! We love Kimi!!”

    The girls, while still in the Russell fan club, “we love him a lot,” says Martina, it’s Italy’s own Antonelli they’re cheering on.

    The girls have travelled this weekend from Emilia-Romagna - right on the doorstep of championship leader Antonelli home town of Bologna.

    So what makes him so good? “He’s Italian,” says Giulia. “And we’re right next to him!” Giada says excitedly.

    The grid penalty for Antonelli is “necessary for the championship overall”, Giada adds. “But why here, at Monza?!” say Martina and Giulia.

  3. FIA appeal court refutes Briatore impartiality claimpublished at 14:39 BST
    14:39 BST

    Andrew Benson
    BBC F1 correspondent at Monza

    The FIA court of appeal has refuted a claim by Alpine boss Flavio Briatore which it says "question the independence and impartiality of the judges" in a case brought by McLaren and Red Bull against the French team.

    Briatore claimed on Friday that one of the court of appeal judges in the case that ended with the court re-instating a penalty on Alpine's Pierre Gasly in the Monaco Grand Prix had "links" with McLaren.

    The statement by the highest legal authority of motorsport's governing body said that court of appeal judges are "subject to the independence and confidentiality requirements contained in the FIA judicial and disciplinary rules, make annual disclosures of interests to the FIA compliance officer, and sign a specific declaration of independence for each particular case in consideration of the issue at stake and the parties involved".

  7. Report: Russell fastest with slipstream battle already under waypublished at 12:46 BST
    12:46 BST

    Andrew Benson
    BBC F1 correspondent at Monza

    Mercedes driver George Russell set the pace in a final practice session at the Italian Grand Prix dominated by drivers trying to find a silpstream to boost their lap time.

    Russell set his best time apparently without a tow and was 0.226 seconds quicker than Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton, with Red Bull’s Max Verstappen third fastest.

    But Mercedes spent some time in the session practising Kimi Antonelli towing Russell on fast laps.

    An early slipstream from the first to second chicane vaulted Russell to a fastest time that was comfortably quickest at the time.

    Antonelli, leading the championship by 59 points from Russell and Hamilton, has a grid penalty and will start the race from the back.

    Mercedes have made it clear they will try to use Antonelli to boost Russell’s chances in qualifying - and the Briton is already favourite for pole based on pace.

    And there was a fracas involving Verstappen and Hamilton at the end of the session, when they ran wheel to wheel to the first chicane, where the Dutchman dived for the inside, forcing Hamilton into the run-off area.

    Antonelli, whose penalty is for exceeding the permitted number of engine parts, was fourth fastest, 0.361secs off the pace.

    Verstappen, 0.350secs slower than Russell, appeared to have made a step forward after a difficult Friday.

    But McLaren, winners of the past two races with Lando Norris, were still struggling.

    The world champion was fifth fastest, 0.406secs off the pace, his team-mate Oscar Piastri eighth behind Ferrari’s Charles Leclerc and Racing Bulls’ Arvid Lindblad.
