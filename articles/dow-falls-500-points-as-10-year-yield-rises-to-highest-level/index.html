Dow falls 500 points as 10-year yield rises to highest level in 24 years: Live updates

TheTools · 06.10.2026 22:04 · source · permalink

U.S. equities fell on Wednesday, a day after the S&P 500 reached a fresh all-time high, as oil prices moved higher alongside Treasury yields.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 503 points, or 1%, while the S&P 500 shed 0.6%. The Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.7%.

U.S. crude prices moved back above $90 per barrel after rising 1%. International Brent crude futures, meanwhile, traded nearly 1% higher at around $102 per barrel. Those moves led yields, which took a breather in the previous session, to resume the recent surge that drove them to multiyear highs.

The benchmark 10-year Treasury note yield climbed more than 8 basis points to trade at 5.356%. The yield reached its highest level since April 2002. The 30-year bond yield gained more than 8 basis points to 5.725%. It had hit its highest level since May 2002.

The moves come ahead of a planned auction by the Treasury Wednesday, in which it's planning to sell $39 billion of 10-year notes.

The S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time on Tuesday, led by gains in chipmakers. Bond yields also eased, giving equities a boost.

"Stocks have gotten cheaper since January which is ironic, since we're hitting all-time highs," said Nancy Tengler, CEO and CIO of Laffer Tengler. "I don't mind multiple compression because it tells me that this bull market is sustainable and it elongates it. I like where we sit for this bull market, and earnings growth does ultimately drive stock price performance."

Investors are awaiting the minutes of the Federal Reserve's last meeting in September, at which the U.S. central bank raised interest rates for the first time since 2023. Minutes are set to shed light on how monetary policymakers are thinking about economic conditions.

Gold hit a low of 4,091.2 on Wednesday, reaching the lowest level since Aug. 3, when it traded as low as 4,074.

The move lower is helped in part by a strong dollar which make dollar-denominated metals more expensive to foreign buyers.

Gold has not traded below 4,000 since July 20 when gold hit a low of 3,986.

Gold Miners (GDX) are down 3.85% on pace for worst day since Sep. 28 when the GDX fell -5.36%

Silver also hit a low of $59.23, the lowest level since Aug. 4 when silver traded as low as $58.18.

Morgan Stanley upgraded Gold Fields to Overweight, saying the stock's underperformance has created an attractive risk-reward setup. 

Gold Fields has lagged global gold peers by about 16% this year. But according to Morgan Stanley's analyst Christopher Nicholson much of the uncertainty around its Tarkwa mining lease is already priced in. 

"We think Tarkwa lease risk is largely priced; in addition, a potential Northern Star deal looks accretive on a pro forma basis. We update earnings estimates and price targets on the back of our latest price deck – the risk-reward for gold skews upwards," said Nicholson.

Overall, the analyst remains bullish on the gold, naming the yellow metal as its top commodity pick and forecasting an average price of $5,050 an ounce in 2027. Nicholson said gold could benefit from concerns around government debt, potential intervention to cap bond yields, swings in oil prices and continued central-bank buying.

Constellation Brands shares fell 5% even after the company reported better-than-expected results for its fiscal second quarter.

The beer distributor earned an $3.74 per share on revenue of $2.63 billion. Analysts expected a profit of $3.56 per share on revenue of $2.54 billion.

That said, beer operating margins decreased by 160 basis points year on year. Depletions, a key demand metric for the company, fell slightly from the year-earlier period as well.

The chief executive of U.S. oil giant Chevron on Wednesday warned it would be "unwise" for the Trump administration to move forward with a diesel export ban, saying the policy runs the risk of making the supply situation worse.

"The U.S. has been a reliable supplier to the world at a time when it needs it," Chevron CEO Mike Wirth told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" in an exclusive interview on Wednesday.

"And I think it would be unwise for the U.S. to create questions in the minds of our allies and our partners as to whether or not we will be there with reliable supply when times are difficult — and so, there are other options," he added.

Average house prices in the U.K. were flat in September, according to data from lender Lloyds, after falling 0.3% the previous month.

The average property price in the country is now £298,441 ($395,005), compared to £298,395 in August. The index was also flat on an annual basis.

"While the market overall has been fairly subdued, property prices have so far proved resilient during a period of higher mortgage rates, which has been driven by changing expectations around the future path of Base Rate," Andrew Asaam, mortgages director at Lloyds, said in the data release. "That's mirrored in wider economic data, with household spending holding up better than many expected despite energy and other cost pressures arising from the Middle East conflict."

Yields on bonds issued by various governments moved higher on Wednesday morning, amid lingering economic concerns.

The yield on the benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury was up 4 basis points to 5.309% as of 2:30 a.m. ET. Yields on the longer-maturity 30-year Treasury note added almost 5 basis points to 5.688%.

Bond yields and prices move in opposite directions.

Yields on U.K. government bonds, known as gilts, also rose across the curve, with 10- and 20-year bond yields rising by 5 basis points.

Meanwhile, French and Italian 10-year government bond yields rose by 8 basis points. Japanese bonds bucked the trend, with yields trading marginally lower.

NLFI, a non-profit organization that manages shares of nationalized or bailed-out financial institutions owned by the Dutch state, said Wednesday that it would sell part of its stake in lender ABN Amro.

The sale would be carried out through a pre-arranged trading plan, executed by BofA Securities Europe, it said. Once completed, it would reduce NLFI's holding of ABN Amro to a 10.5% stake, down from 20.7%.

The Reserve Bank of India on Wednesday raised interest rates for the first time since 2023, joining several major central banks in tightening monetary policy to arrest accelerating inflation.

The central bank increased the benchmark repo rate by 25 basis points to a 1-year high of 5.50%, in line with expectations of economists polled by Reuters.

India's economic growth has been strong despite global challenges, Sanjay Malhotra, RBI governor, said in his address on Wednesday, while adding that "inflation and its outlook are not benign, as they were last year."

The monetary policy committee decided to change the policy stance "to calibrated tightening," Malhotra said.

The hike comes as retail inflation in India has been on the rise for 10 straight months, touching 4.8% in August, higher than the RBI's medium-term target of 4%.

— Priyanka Salve

Oil rose Wednesday as attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis on Saudi Arabia raised concerns over crude flows from the Middle East even as supplies have been recovering.

Futures for international benchmark Brent crude for December delivery gained 1.17% at $101.76 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures for November advanced 1.26% at $90.57 per barrel.

Naeem Aslam, chief investment officer of Zaye Capital Markets, said Tuesday that oil remains "caught between improving physical supply and persistent geopolitical risk."

"The sustained ability of the Houthis in Yemen to target oil facilities hundreds of kilometers from the border keeps the risks of a renewed large-scale crude supply disruption present and high, and these risks could worsen if the Houthis feel the need to apply more pressure as a result of losing more territory," said Samer Hasn, senior market analyst at forex trading platform XS.com.

— Justina Lee

Investors will be scrutinizing the minutes of the Federal Reserve's September meeting on Wednesday, which are set to be released at 2 p.m. ET. Futures markets are betting on a hold from the central bank at its next meeting at this end of this month, followed by a quarter-point rate hike at the subsequent meeting in December.

"The September [summary of economic projections] and subsequent communications point to broad support for additional rate increases, but less agreement over the pace and total magnitude of these moves," Amy Yang at Deutsche Bank wrote in a Monday note to clients.

"The center of the Committee, represented most clearly by Williams and Jefferson, favors taking time to evaluate the data, while the most hawkish wing sees September as the beginning of a multi-step adjustment toward a sufficiently restrictive stance," she added.
