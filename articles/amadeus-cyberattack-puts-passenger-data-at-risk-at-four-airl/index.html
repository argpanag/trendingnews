Amadeus cyberattack puts passenger data at risk at four airlines

A cybersecurity incident at Amadeus, the Spanish technology provider for the travel sector, has put the reservation systems used by several Asian airlines under scrutiny. Japan Airlines (JAL) and its subsidiary Japan Transocean Air have confirmed indications of unauthorized access to passenger information. All Nippon Airways (ANA) has reported a similar incident, while Korean Air and Asiana Airlines have also warned of customer data exposure through systems from the same provider.

The information disseminated by the cyber threat specialist account H4ckmanac on X alerted about the incident. However, the most relevant confirmation comes from the companies themselves: their official statements verify that Amadeus notified them of unauthorized accesses or indications of external consultation of reservation data.

JAL estimates 92,000 potentially affected passengers

Japan Airlines reported this past October 28 that Amadeus IT Group, provider of its reservation management, ticket issuance, and check-in system, had communicated indications of external access to part of the passenger records. The company received the notice on October 23.

According to the official statement, the incident could affect approximately 92,000 people with reservations for flights operated under the JAL and Japan Transocean Air codes. In about 90,000 cases, the potentially accessed data includes the reservation code, passenger name, flight date, route, and service class.

For around 2,000 customers, the information could also include email addresses, phone numbers, passport numbers, nationality, frequent flyer number, ticket number, and assigned seat, among other data.

The airline assures that credit card numbers and passwords have not been compromised. It has also not confirmed any fraudulent uses of the information and states that the incident has not affected flight operations.

The period during which indications of external access have been identified ranges from August 10, 2023, to September 23, 2026. This corresponds to the interval of potentially affected records, not a confirmation that the attackers maintained continuous access throughout that time.

JAL has announced that it will individually contact affected customers and warn them about possible fraudulent emails, calls, and websites impersonating the airline.

ANA also investigates possible data exposure

All Nippon Airways confirmed on October 29 that it had received communication from Amadeus about unauthorized access to its systems. The company is working with the provider to determine the scope of the incident and what information may have been exposed.

For now, ANA has not published a figure of affected passengers nor detailed the categories of potentially accessed data. The airline has indicated that it will inform affected customers when confirmed information is available.

Korean Air and Asiana warned earlier about the exposure

South Korean companies Korean Air and Asiana Airlines have also reported unauthorized access to information managed by Amadeus. Both communicated the incident on October 23, after receiving notification from the provider on the 20th.

According to their statements, the affected data includes reservation numbers of some customers. The companies indicate that the investigation points to unauthorized use of access credentials associated with an Amadeus system account.

Korean Air and Asiana assure that they have found no indications that their own internal databases have been compromised. Both have reported that they are collaborating with the provider to clarify what additional information may have been exposed and have implemented the corresponding communication and protection measures.

There is still no consolidated balance of the number of affected individuals in the four airlines. The approximately 92,000 passengers reported by JAL and Japan Transocean Air correspond exclusively to these two companies.

The risk of concentrating critical services in a common provider

The incident highlights the risks of the technological supply chain. Airlines outsource part of their reservation, ticket issuance, and passenger management systems, which allows for integrated operations and services, but also means that an incident at a provider can impact multiple companies.

The exposure of names, reservation codes, and flight data can facilitate phishing campaigns. If attackers also have phone numbers, emails, or passport numbers, they can craft more convincing messages to try to obtain credentials or induce passengers to visit fraudulent pages.

Amadeus has communicated to the companies that it has applied the necessary security measures. However, the full technical details of the incident, the identity of those responsible, and the specific method of access have not been published in the communications consulted.

The investigation will need to clarify whether the cases of the four airlines respond to the same intrusion, what the real scope of the accessed data was, and if there are other affected companies.
