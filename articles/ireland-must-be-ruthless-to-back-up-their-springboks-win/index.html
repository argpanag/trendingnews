Ireland must be ruthless to back up their Springboks win

TheTools · 26.11.2026 17:23 · source · permalink

There's been a trend with Ireland in the last year that big wins and big performances have been followed by a regression.

Last year's record win against Australia came a week before a brutal defeat to South Africa, the record win away to England in February was followed by a stuttering performance against Wales, and after hammering Scotland to finish the Six Nations, they needed a miraculous comeback to beat the Wallabies in their Nations Championship opener.

Even this month, Ireland started their run of November games with a slick win at home to Argentina, before going through the motions a week later against Fiji.

That might go some way to explaining Andy Farrell's team selection for this evening's renewal with Australia, which rounds off their Nations Championship campaign.

After such an attritional game against the Springboks last week, and with a six-day turnaround to what is essentially a fifth-place play-off against the Wallabies, there was an expectation that the Ireland head coach would use the final game of 2026 as an opportunity to get some gametime into the wider squad and wrap some of the frontliners in cotton wool.

Instead, Farrell has run his selection back, with just two changes made from the XV that started last week's win, both changes enforced through injury to Mack Hansen and suspension for Dan Sheehan.

It's the fewest number of changes to an Ireland side since their opening game of the 2025 Six Nations, and considerably down on the average of six changes Farrell (below) has been making to his team across 2026.

Last week's 20-17 win against South Africa was desperately needed for Ireland to remind themselves that they can still mix it with the best in the world, and it's clear Farrell sees this game as an opportunity to double down on their best performance of the season, rather than spread the minutes around.

Nick Timoney comes back into the starting team to replace the suspended Sheehan, while Jacob Stockdale has beaten Tommy O'Brien and Jimmy O'Brien to Hansen's left wing spot, and the Ulsterman now has a huge opportunity to make an early claim for a World Cup spot in such a competitive position.

Along with Nathan Doak, who is on the bench, it brings the number of players used by Farrell in this window to 31, with Jimmy O'Brien and Cian Prendergast, both of whom started the win against Australia in July, missing out on any gametime, as well as Billy Bohan and the uncapped Jamie Benson.

Of the starting XV this evening, only seven started the corresponding fixture against the Wallabies in July, where Ireland needed a dramatic second half comeback to win 33-31 in Sydney.

While the likes of Jack Conan, Jamie Osborne and Garry Ringrose are unavailable due to injury, and Dan Sheehan is suspended, it's offset by the return of Andrew Porter, Ryan Baird and Robert Baloucoune, all three of whom have been integral to Ireland's form this month.

The Wallabies side is also much-changed from the July meeting, with Les Kiss using this game to rotate his side around in their final game of a 13-match Test season.

There are four changes from the team that beat Wales a week ago, as tighthead prop Allan Alaalatoa comes in to win his 100th cap, with second row Jeremy Williams, scrum-half Kalani Thomas and uncapped wing James Ramm brought in, while uncapped scrum-half Teddy Wilson is also in line for a debut off the bench.

While only eight of the XV started the previous meeting with Ireland, those who did caused Ireland real problems.

Second row Josh Canham was outstanding in July on his first Wallabies start, with his combination of power and an ability to distribute proving a constant threat for the Irish defence, while he and Jeremy Williams did a great job putting Ireland's lineout off their throw.

The 10-12-13 axis of Carter Gordon, Len Ikitau and Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii (below) is also retained, and that trio terrorised the Irish defence in Sydney, particularly in the first half.

There's also a blast from the past on the Australian bench, with 37-year-old Kurtley Beale named among the replacements, with the versatile back winning his 96th cap, and first for five years.

It's eight years and six defeats in a row since Australia last beat Ireland, and as the clock ticks toward them hosting the World Cup next year, breaking that streak could be the springboard they need heading into 2027.

Joe Schmidt was still in charge for the first three games of the Nations Championship, and they have continued to struggle for consistency since Kiss took charge in late July.

A win against South Africa was the headline result, but that came against a heavily rotated Springboks side, while there were two victories against Japan and one against Argentina before that.

Their two Bledisloe Cup games against New Zealand saw them comfortably beaten, while further losses to England and Scotland saw them go on a run of four defeats in a row before they got the better of Wales last week.

If they are to produce a surprise and beat Ireland, slowing down Jamison Gibson-Park's ability to play with quick ball will be top of the list.

Against the Springboks last week, Ireland's average ruck speed was a terrifying 1.47 seconds, which gave Sam Prendergast time to run the attack and get Hansen, Baloucoune (below) and Hugo Keenan into space.

The Irish lineout has also shown considerable improvements from the summer, with Baird proving his worth to Farrell on both sides of the setpiece in the last three games.

Given last week's result, the bar has now been set for this Ireland team, and with such a strong side picked to go again this weekend, a similarly strong performance is the expectation – rather than hope – at Allianz Stadium.

A fifth-place finish in the Nations Championship would have felt like an underwhelming return if you had offered it to Farrell back in early July, but if Ireland finish out 2026 with another fluid performance, the Ireland coach will be feeling good heading into a World Cup year.

Verdict: Ireland

Ireland: Hugo Keenan; Rob Baloucoune, Robbie Henshaw, Stuart McCloskey, Jacob Stockdale; Sam Prendergast, Jamison Gibson-Park; Andrew Porter, Rónan Kelleher, Tadhg Furlong; Joe McCarthy, Tadhg Beirne (capt); Ryan Baird, Josh van der Flier, Nick Timoney.

Replacements: Tom Stewart, Tom O'Toole, Thomas Clarkson, James Ryan, James McNabney, Nathan Doak, Ciarán Frawley, Bundee Aki.

Australia: Tom Wright; Max Jorgensen, Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii, Len Ikitau, James Ramm; Carter Gordon, Kalani Thomas; Isaac Kailea, Brandon Paenga-Amosa, Allan Alaalatoa (co-capt); Josh Canham, Jeremy Williams; Tom Hooper, Carlo Tizzano, Rob Valetini.

Replacements: Josh Nasser, Angus Bell, Taniela Tupou, Lachlan Shaw, Harry Wilson (co-capt), Fraser McReight, Teddy Wilson, Kurtley Beale.

Referee: Nika Amashukeli (GRU)
