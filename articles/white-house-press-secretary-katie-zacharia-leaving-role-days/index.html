White House press secretary Katie Zacharia leaving role days after taking job

TheTools · 16.10.2026 20:49 · source · permalink

Summary

  • The White House says press secretary Katie Zacharia is leaving the role just days into the job - who is she?

  • The confirmation came just after Donald Trump said he told Zacharia to "reconsider" her role, adding she had been treated badly by the media - watch the moment

  • With the midterms just weeks away, this is a critical time for Trump - and he now has no press secretary, our North America correspondent writes

The White House says press secretary Katie Zacharia is leaving the role just days into the job - who is she?

    21:43 BST

    Bernd Debusmann Jr
    White House reporter, travelling with the president

    Donald Trump has just begun speaking at Dolton Municipal Airport in Georgia, campaigning for Republican candidates ahead of next month's midterm elections.

    It's an odd set-up - the crowd is on large sets of riders set up between two aircraft hangers.

    Zacharia's departure seems to be far from the minds of the small but enthusiastic crowd. A short while ago, Marine One - the presidential helicopter - flew over the venue to the applause of the crowd, which was chanting "USA, USA" as Trump emerged.

    He has yet to mention the departure. The position of White House press secretary, after all, is one of immense importance to journalists covering the presidency in Washington DC, but much less so to voters across the country.

    They are here to see Trump and most likely care very little for the ins and outs of the White House staff that ultimately are there at his pleasure.

  4. At a critical time, Trump finds himself without the help of a press secretarypublished at 20:10 BST
    20:10 BST

    Anthony Zurcher
    North America correspondent

    The White House press secretary job is a challenging one, even under the best of circumstances.

    These were not the best of circumstances.

    Katie Zacharia was thrown into the whirlwind, joining a White House just weeks away from what is shaping up to be very difficult midterm congressional elections for Donald Trump and his Republican Party.

    The White House has been beset by a series of controversies – including allegations of corruption that forced the resignation of US Ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle and an abrupt reversal of plans to publicly air a military execution in December after widespread bipartisan objection.

    A skilled press secretary may have been able to navigate these political landmines and continue to push the White House message in the face of a building media frenzy. Zacharia, a relative novice, only fanned the flames.

    Her halting Tuesday press conference performance, combined with the questions about her legal background, essentially sealed her fate. And subsequent press appearances, including a less-than-welcoming interview with conservative talking head Sean Hannity on Thursday – which Trump said he watched – did nothing to change that.

    As president, Trump has essentially acted as his own press secretary, making major announcements and changing policies from the Oval Office, White House lawn and airfield tarmacs. But his press secretaries, when they’ve been effective, have smoothed relations with the media, provided context for inquisitive journalists and helped tamp down simmering issues.

    At a critical time in his second term, Trump will be without such help.

  10. I was in the room for Zacharia's first - and maybe only - White House briefingpublished at 19:24 BST
    19:24 BST

    Sarah Smith
    North America editor

    It's lucky I was in the room for Katie Zacharia’s first White House press briefing - because it seems it might also have been her last appearance in the job.

    She seemed very nervous and stilted on Tuesday as she read out prepared remarks about how proud she was to be working for President Trump. It made me wonder why she had rushed to hold a briefing - live on camera - on her second day in the job.

    As she abruptly ended the briefing after only 14 minutes, the assembled journalists in the White House briefing room speculated about how long she might last in the job. But we assumed that she would not be sacked before the midterm elections, which are coming up on 3 November.

    We did not predict that she would be gone by the end of the week, lasting even less time than Anthony Scaramucci, who served as Donald Trump's communications head for only 11 days during his first administration.

  17. Who is Katie Zacharia?published at 18:33 BST
    18:33 BST

    Katie Zacharia, 42, was appointed as the US president's sixth chief spokesperson across his two administrations.

    The political commentator is a frequent fixture on the conservative networks Newsmax and Fox News.

    She previously had a brief stint as a spokesperson at the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and worked as a communications adviser for Trump's social media platform Truth Social, according to her LinkedIn.

    In the early days of her career, Zacharia worked as an intern for the Republican National Committee and for the White House Office of Political Affairs during the Bush administration in 2006.

    Trump called her "loyal" and "a devoted fan and member of Maga" in a social media post on Friday, adding that she had "an unblemished record of support".

  19. Trump says Zacharia should 'reconsider' White House press jobpublished at 18:28 BST
    18:28 BSTBreaking

    US President Donald Trump has suggested maybe the role of White House press secretary is not for Katie Zacharia, following her shaky debut earlier this week.

    "I said Katie, you know you don’t have to do this if you don’t want," Trump said earlier today, speaking to reporters outside Marine One.

    "If I were you I'd reconsider," he added.

    The biggest scrutiny has come over her legal credentials, having been introduced by Trump as an "attorney" yet Zacharia has never passed the bar and does not meet the legal definition for the term.

    Zacharia has spent the week playing defence, answering tough questions from conservative outlets about her credentials and preparedness for the job.
