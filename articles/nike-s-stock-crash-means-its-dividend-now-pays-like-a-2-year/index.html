Nike's (NKE) stock crash has won the athletic-wear maker the distinguished (or maybe not-so-distinguished) honor of making its dividend yield as meaty as that of the 2-year Treasury note (ZT=F).

At the time of this writing on Monday, Nike's dividend yield — which moves inversely to the stock price — stood at 4.87%. The yield on the 2-year Treasury stood at 4.85%.

"For investors willing to look beyond near-term volatility, NKE offers both income and recovery potential," Jefferies analyst and Nike bull Randy Konik said in a note.

(NKE )

Nike reminded investors why the stock is trading at record lows — and why the dividend yield is nearing 5% — on its earnings day late last week.

For one, comments from Nike CEO Elliott Hill suggested that the weak sportswear market will continue in the medium term. That is causing Nike to discount slow-moving product, which could pressure others in the sector to do the same.

The company's earnings guidance for its full fiscal year was also well below analyst estimates.

The other factor at play is just Nike's raw results for the quarter. They are rough on the eye and have probably had investors in Nike competitors wondering if their companies are sitting on similar ugliness.

Nike Brand sales fell 4%. Online sales plunged 13%. Converse sales tanked 28%. China sales crashed 26%. And the company signaled a fresh round of major layoffs was coming.

Nike stock is now down 47% year to date and 77% in the past five years.

"We are not ready to call a bottom yet with shares trading at 28x P/E at the midpoint of FY27E guidance," Stifel analyst Peter McGoldrick said.
