  3. 'In the Champions League there are no easy games'published at 22:22 BST 22 September
    22:22 BST 22 September

    FT: Arsenal 1-0 HB Koge

    Arsenal

    Arsenal defender Steph Catley, speaking to Disney+: "It was a pretty crazy five minutes. It's football sometimes and we left it late.

    "It's tough at the moment and in the Champions League there are no easy games. They defended great, we didn't score early and it didn't come until the end.

    "In the Champions League you never think it's an easy day. I was expecting a tough game and it became really difficult but credit to the girls, they did everything and Mariona [Caldentey] was very calm in that moment.

    "It's hard to put your finger on it [what is going wrong], it is a bit of a slog and the goals are not coming as freely as they have in the past. It's not a lack of trying, we're doing everything we can to put the ball into the back of the net.

    "Sometimes it does not feel easy and that's where we are at but we're working hard every day trying to fix it. We have great history in this competition and it's important we are in it every year and challenging to win. We have a real belief that we can go on and win it. It's a long season and we're working really hard so to get the wins early on is very important."

    On the penalty: "I'm never in doubt when she is on the ball. You always felt she [Caldentey] was going to score. Selina [Cerci] made a big impact dribbling into the box."

Arsenal 0-0 HB Koge

Arsenal break forward and Olivia Smith has her shirt pulled by Laura Munk Hermann, who gets a yellow card.

Arsenal struggled in front of goal but scored from the penalty spot in the third minute of stoppage time

Mariona Caldentey scored a stoppage-time penalty to secure victory for a wasteful Arsenal at home to Danish side HB Koge in the first game of their Women's Champions League campaign.

Arsenal, who had struggled in front of goal against a side made up of several part-time players, were heading towards a disappointing draw when Caldentey scored from the spot after Selina Cerci was tripped in the box.

The Gunners earlier thought they had scored when Chloe Kelly sent an outside-of-the-boot prod past Alberte Vingum, but her strike was ruled out after a VAR check deemed Stina Blackstenius to have been in an offside position and in the goalkeeper's eyeline.

But otherwise Arsenal looked toothless all too often, enjoying dominance of the ball but unable to find a way through.

The strength of the defensive performance from the visitors made a difference, too, with the energetic half-time celebrations of Koge boss Kristian Morch highlighting the determined nature of his side's display.

Arsenal lacked aggression in the final third and were often sloppy in possession, gifting opportunities for the Danish side to press and snatch the ball.

Gunners boss Renee Slegers had made eight changes from the side which salvaged a 1-1 draw with Manchester United at the weekend, but opted to switch things up at the break and introduce Alessia Russo in the place of Blackstenius in search of a goal threat.

But it provided little of the sort as the second half proceeded in the same manner as the first, with Arsenal dominating but struggling to fashion clear-cut chances.

They rescued it from the spot, however, as Vingum was unable to deny Caldentey from 12 yards despite managing to get a hand to her strike.

Analysis: Familiar frustrations as Arsenal struggle in front of goal

There was a clear sense of disappointment surrounding Arsenal going into this game, given their struggles in front of goal which played a large part in their 1-1 draw with Manchester United and stalemate with Crystal Palace.

Making eight changes following their United draw, in a week where they face Chelsea on Sunday (16:30 BST), was an understandable decision on Slegers' part.

However, as Arsenal's frustrations wore on throughout the game it only contributed to a feeling that they may not have afforded Koge the required respect.

The game progressed to a state where Arsenal seemingly prioritised quantity, not quality of chances as their decision-making continued to fall wide of the mark.

While they managed to find a way to win, it came only as a result of a piece of clumsy defending from Koge which gifted them the penalty.

Given the strength of opponents on the way for the Gunners in this competition, including a trip to the Nou Camp to face Barcelona and a game against European heavyweights Lyon, much improvement will need to be done.
