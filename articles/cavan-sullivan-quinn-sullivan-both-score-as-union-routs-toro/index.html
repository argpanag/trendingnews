Cavan Sullivan, Quinn Sullivan both score as Union routs Toronto, clinches sixth

TheTools · 07.11.2026 23:50 · source · permalink

CHESTER — The Philadelphia Union put the ball in the back of the net six times Saturday. Four of them counted. Against Toronto FC, that was more than enough.

Despite two first-half goals sketchily chalked off, Quinn Sullivan and Cavan Sullivan scored in the same game for the first time as professionals in a 4-1 thumping of Toronto FC.

The win halts a four-game unbeaten run for the Union (14-12-8, 50 points), who solidify sixth place in the Eastern Conference. They will take on third-seeded New England in a first-round best-of-3 series to take place after the November international window.

The first half was a dizzying array of goals that did and didn’t count.

Quinn’s goal set up by Danley Jean Jacques in the 11th minute didn’t. Cavan’s in the 28th off an Indiana Vassilev layoff did.

So did the penalty that Cavan drew and Quinn finished in the 41st. Not so for Frankie Westfield’s goal set up by Cavan in the 45th, thanks to Quinn being in an offside position and, per referee Tim Ford in a superfluously officiated game, being in the sightline of Toronto goalie William Yarborough.

Toronto, who was eliminated from playoff contention last week, rotated much of its squad. One of the starters was Josh Sargent, who in the seventh minute of first-half stoppage time swooped in unmarked and thumped home a header to make it 2-1.

Adding consternation was Andre Blake exiting at halftime due to an undisclosed injury. Interim coach Ryan Richter said postgame that Blake “felt something at halftime, in his groin. We don’t have how serious that is. It doesn’t seem like it’s something that’s super serious.”

The second half was more straightforward.

Quinn Sullivan headmanned a 2-on-1 and dished to Cavan to stroke home his ninth goal of the season. Quinn was felled in the box in the 71st by Jonathan Osorio, and Bruno Damiani potted his 13th goal of the season from the spot.

Andrew Rick had little to do in goal, the Union outshooting TFC, 19-7. Richter did get a curtain call for Alejandro Bedoya, who earlier this week announced he was retiring after his 11th season with the Union.
