Northwest Notes: Wolves, Avdija, Lillard, Thunder, Clifford

TheTools · 20.11.2026 02:13 · source · permalink

After Terrence Shannon Jr. scored 34 points to lead the Timberwolves to a win over Portland on Monday, Jaden McDaniels lauded the team’s depth and suggested that several players besides star guards Anthony Edwards and LaMelo Ball can be difference-makers on a given night.

Two of the players McDaniels identified as capable of winning a game for the Timberwolves were Ayo Dosunmu and Bones Hyland. And on Tuesday, Dosunmu and Hyland proved their teammate right by playing significant roles in a 106-105 victory over the Trail Blazers.

The Wolves outscored Portland by 14 points during Hyland’s 17 minutes on the floor and were a +11 in Dosunmu’s 39 minutes as he racked up a team-high 30 points to go along with five assists — including one on the game-winning McDaniels basket with three seconds left in the fourth quarter (Twitter video link).

Even with Edwards out since October 26 and starting center Rudy Gobert struggling mightily on offense, the Timberwolves now have an 11-4 record. The fact that the team is getting contributions from up and down its roster is a promising sign for its ability to contend in a competitive Western Conference.

“You look at any championship team the last 10 years, it’s all been about who stayed the most healthy and who can adapt the most,” Dosunmu said, per Jon Krawczynski of The Athletic (free link). “I think we’re showcasing that now. We’re adapting with guys being out. We’re trusting each other.”

“We’ve got a really good roster,” head coach Chris Finch added. “We’ve got good spirit. We’ve got a lot of guys that really embrace defense and small plays right now and whatever it takes.”

We have more from around the Northwest:

  • Already down Ja Morant and Shaedon Sharpe, the Trail Blazers were also missing Deni Avdija on Tuesday vs. Minnesota due to left foot inflammation and soreness. As Joe Freeman of The Oregonian writes (subscription required), two-way player Jayson Kent filled in admirably for Avdija in his first career start, but the star forward’s absence was deeply felt by a Portland team that made just 39.6% of its shots from the floor. It’s unclear whether Avdija will miss additional time, according to Freeman, who describes the forward’s speedy return as “paramount.”
  • In a pair of interviews conducted by Sam Amick of The Athletic, Damian Lillard said he’s enjoying his time back in Portland with a Trail Blazers team that he considers “special,” while Alex Caruso explained that the injury-plagued Thunder aren’t feeling sorry for themselves because they’re a “no-excuses” organization. “Given the offseason moves that we were forced to make with the way that the payroll is set up, you’re going to lose some of the depth that kind of made us a super-team the year before,” Caruso told Amick. “No matter who was out or in, we had guys that would go out there with experience and play. So it’s just a different challenge now.”
  • Second-year guard Nique Clifford admits that it wasn’t easy to leave the Kings after building several strong relationships in Sacramento over the last year-plus, but he’s a firm believe that everything happens for a reason and said he has been impressed with the Thunder organization so far, per Justin Martinez of The Oklahoman. While Clifford’s contributions in his first few games following his trade to Oklahoma City have been modest, he has made a strong first impression on his new teammates. “I think he’s done a great job,” Ajay Mitchell said. “He’s a great dude and a great competitor. Obviously, it takes time to adapt and adjust, but he’s done a great job. He’s going out there and he’s competing every minute. And even off the floor, he’s just connecting with all of the guys. It’s fun to have him.”
