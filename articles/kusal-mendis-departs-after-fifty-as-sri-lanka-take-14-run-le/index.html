Kusal Mendis departs after fifty as Sri Lanka take 14-run lead against Pakistan in second Test

Email Story

Sri Lanka fought back from a difficult position to take a 14-run overall lead against Pakistan, reaching 241-6 at tea on the third day of the second Test at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium on Thursday.

The visitors added 167 runs during the day for the loss of three wickets, recovering from 74-3 at the start of play.

Captain Kusal Mendis led the resistance with a half-century before falling to Salman Ali Agha, while Sonal Dinusha and Milan Rathnayake remained unbeaten at the interval.

Pakistan had secured a commanding 227-run first-innings lead after bowling Sri Lanka out for 98 and then making 325 in reply. The hosts had strengthened their position further by reducing the visitors to 74-3 at stumps on the second day.

Resuming on Thursday, Sri Lanka lost both overnight batters as Pakistan’s fast bowlers struck in the morning session. Aamer Jamal dismissed Dhananjaya de Silva for 37, trapping him lbw with a sharp incoming delivery. The batter reviewed the decision but the original call was upheld.

De Silva had struck six fours in his 47-ball innings and his departure left Sri Lanka at 99-4.

Razaullah then ended Pasindu Sooriyabandara’s resistance, having the batter caught behind by wicketkeeper Ghazi Ghori for 38 off 116 deliveries. Sooriyabandara, who struck three fours, had battled for nearly two hours before edging a delivery down the leg side.

Sri Lanka reached lunch at 166-5, having added 92 runs in the morning session. Kusal was unbeaten on 32, with Dinusha on 19, and the pair continued their recovery after the break.

Kusal completed his 24th Test half-century, scoring 53 from 126 balls with eight fours. He and Dinusha put together an 81-run partnership before Salman provided Pakistan with their only breakthrough of the afternoon.

In the 70th over, Kusal attempted a sweep against the off-spinner but played the ball onto his leg stump, departing at 215-6. His dismissal gave Pakistan a much-needed wicket after a prolonged period of resistance.

Dinusha, however, continued to frustrate the hosts, reaching 46 not out from 101 deliveries, with six boundaries. He was struck on the hand by a rising delivery from Jamal during the afternoon but continued after receiving treatment.

Rathnayake joined him following Kusal’s dismissal and scored an unbeaten 17 off 16 balls, including three fours. The pair added 26 runs for the seventh wicket to take Sri Lanka to 241-6 at tea.

Pakistan continued to miss the services of Saqib Khan, who has been ruled out of further bowling after sustaining a left hamstring strain on Wednesday.

With the new ball due after 80 overs, Pakistan will seek quick wickets in the final session, while Sri Lanka will aim to extend their lead through Dinusha and Rathnayake.
