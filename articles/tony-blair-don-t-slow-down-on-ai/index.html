Tony Blair: Don’t slow down on AI

TheTools · 22.09.2026 14:30 · source · permalink

Pausing development risks ‘profound and compounding disadvantages’ for the West, warns ex-PM

Sir Tony Blair has urged the West not to slow down the pace of AI after Silicon Valley chiefs warned about the existential threat posed by the technology.

The former prime minister, a keen advocate of AI, said pausing development risked “profound and compounding disadvantages” for society which were just as serious as the menace posed by highly intelligent bots.

A report for the Tony Blair Institute (TBI), the former Labour leader’s think tank, published on Monday, said calls to slow down AI were part of a brewing “techlash” in the US and UK which should be resisted.

“Intensifying concerns about AI safety must not stop governments keeping pace with this revolution,” said the report.

Sir Tony’s intervention comes amid calls to curb the aggressive growth of AI driven by safety concerns.

The King last week warned about the “existential dangers” of AI “falling into the wrong hands” and being used with catastrophic consequences.

Labour is also set to vote on the creation of a new, powerful AI regulator when the party meets at its annual conference in Liverpool next week.

A series of incidents where AI bots escaped their testing environments and went on a series of hacking attacks have also prompted alarm.

Google, OpenAI, the developer of ChatGPT, Claude-maker Anthropic and Meta, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, have all admitted to cases where their AI tools went “rogue” and disobeyed their human controllers.

The safety fears prompted Dario Amodei, the chief executive of Claude-developer Anthropic, to call for a pause in the development of AI, leading to similar calls from Sam Altman, the chief executive of OpenAI, and Elon Musk.

However, some critics have argued AI labs are lobbying to bring in strict regulations for the technology to prevent new, cheaper rivals, in particular Chinese AI bots, from challenging them.

Donald Trump, the US president, also labelled concerns as a “sick conspiracy” that would allow China to gain a lead.

‘Significantly more to do’

Writing in a report published on Monday, Sir Tony admitted that there were significant risks from AI, from cyber attacks to biological threats, but that the only “meaningful path” forward for governments was to “harness AI” to keep up with the pace of change.

The think tank insisted that its “objective is not to replace existing institutions or transfer political authority to machines”, insisting policy decisions should ultimately be made by humans.

Keegan McBride, from the TBI, said the UK was falling behind rival nations and there was “significantly more to do” to bring No 10 and Whitehall up to speed in the AI race.

The advances in AI were “real, and leaders will have to get a grip on this”, he said. “There is no putting the genie back in the bottle,” Mr McBride added. 

In a separate report last week, Elizabeth Seger, a TBI adviser, said that countries should neither “slam the brakes” on AI nor let it “run wild”.

While Silicon Valley labs have warned about the potential threats from the technology, there are few signs they are practically slowing its advances.

Despite warnings from its own engineers that AI could lead to human extinction in just a few years, this weekend it emerged that Anthropic was setting up a so-called “wet lab”, a biological testing centre.

This would see Claude AI given direct control of robotic machines to run biological experiments on diseases and potential drugs, despite the risk of lab leaks.

Just weeks ago, Anthropic had warned that biological misuse was “one of the most serious risks of frontier AI” that could have “catastrophic consequences”.

One incident saw a swarm of hundreds of intelligent OpenAI agents break out of a secure system before co-ordinating a mass hacking attack. It has been billed as a wake-up call by AI leaders. 

Fears are also growing on Wall Street. Jane Fraser, the UK-born chief executive of Citi, said there was a “tsunami” of work going on to build up cyber defences from super-intelligent AI hackers.

She said it was “not a good day” when Anthropic introduced its Mythos model, which increased the cyber risk to corporations.
