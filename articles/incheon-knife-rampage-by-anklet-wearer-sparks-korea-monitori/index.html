Incheon knife rampage by anklet wearer sparks Korea monitoring overhaul push

TheTools · 14.10.2026 02:38 · source · permalink

Isn't it a problem in itself that someone even wearing an electronic anklet was able to walk around like that?

After the knife rampage at a large shopping complex in Incheon on the 13th, similar criticism followed on social media (SNS) and online communities. A man in his 50s with a prior conviction for attempted murder swung a weapon while wearing an electronic location tracking device (electronic anklet), injuring nine people.

The man showed warning signs, including four violations of a curfew and a recent booking on suspicion of possessing a weapon. Despite repeated warning signals, the crime was not prevented, reigniting debate over the effectiveness of the electronic monitoring system.

◇ Knife rampage an hour after meeting probation officer… Were warning signs missed

According to police on the 14th, suspect A, 59, was arrested on charges of swinging a weapon at random people in a large shopping mall building in Guwol-dong, Namdong District, Incheon, the previous day, injuring nine. One of the injured was transported to a hospital in cardiac arrest and had not regained consciousness as of the morning.

A was convicted of attempted murder for stabbing his ex-wife with a weapon in 2015 and was sentenced to four years in prison. He was released in 2019 and, by court order, had to wear an electronic anklet until 2029. However, in 2021 he violated curfew four times and was fined, and last month he was booked by police on suspicion of possessing a weapon.

It was found that he met a probation officer even on the day of the crime. A received psychological counseling and met with a probation officer in the morning, and they had lunch together until about 12:30 p.m. He then carried out the knife rampage at about 1:40 p.m. It was about an hour and 10 minutes after parting with the probation officer.

As the incident became known, online communities and social media (SNS) saw continued criticism such as, "An attempted murderer should be in prison—what good is an anklet," and "Why put on a useless electronic anklet." A business owner in the complex said at the scene, "He was wearing an electronic anklet—how can he be walking around like this?" adding, "I'm still so shocked my hands are shaking."

◇ Electronic monitoring subjects surpass 5,000… Only 234 dedicated personnel

There have been reoffenses by electronic anklet wearers in the past. In 2021, repeat offender Kang Yoon-seong, with 14 prior convictions, killed two women before and after damaging his electronic anklet and fleeing. In Aug. last year in Daegu, a wearer with a prior sex assault conviction was arrested for indecent exposure after walking around downtown naked.

According to materials submitted by the Ministry of Justice to lawmaker Kim Han-kyu of the Democratic Party of Korea, a member of The National Assembly's Legislation and Judiciary Committee, as of July this year there were 5,273 subjects of electronic monitoring, up 9.2% from 4,827 last year. The figure surpassed 5,000 for the first time.

From January to July this year, there were 599 violations of compliance requirements by electronic monitoring subjects. Curfew violations accounted for most of them at 590, with four violations of restraining orders and five violations of access bans.

Meanwhile, as of Aug. this year, there were only 234 personnel dedicated to on-site guidance and supervision. Ministry of Justice data show that as of July this year, each staff member managed an average of 20.4 people, up from 17.1 in 2022. There were also 14,363 on-site dispatches last year in response to electronic device alarms and the like.

Electronic monitoring is a system that identifies a criminal's location and movement path and supervises compliance with requirements such as curfew. But it is difficult to physically block weapon possession or sudden crimes. In this case, a key question is whether A's repeated violations and recent suspicion of weapon possession were properly reflected in management and supervision.

◇ President Lee orders "special measures"… Full review of electronic monitoring system

The incident is prompting calls to strengthen systems that identify and respond in advance to the risk of reoffending by those under electronic monitoring. In particular, a key issue is whether appropriate measures were taken during management and supervision despite A showing warning signs, including being booked on suspicion of weapon possession.

President Lee Jae-myung visited the Incheon Metropolitan Police Agency the previous day and said, "Devise special measures against violent crimes that threaten the public's peaceful daily life."

In response, the Ministry of Justice decided to conduct a full review of the electronic monitoring system and push for institutional improvements. The Korean National Police Agency also plans to strengthen crackdowns on weapon possession in public places and on online posts predicting crimes, and to concentrate police forces in crowded areas.
