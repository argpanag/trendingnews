MAGA admits midterms panic over heading to polls without 'our captain'

TheTools · 11.09.2026 17:11 · source · permalink

A Republican attendee at the Republican National Committee's midterm convention appeared to spark concern for the upcoming elections on November 3 while remembering conservative commentator Charlie Kirk.

"This time of the year is tough for conservatives, because it's not just the remembrance of 9/11, but also the loss of Charlie Kirk, and I'm not over yet, I'm not over it by a long shot," Chris Johnson, a Louisiana Republican, said. "it is something that will be instilled in my memory, along with 9/11 for the rest of my life, as two dates where I felt that the global evils got the better of the American people."

"I think 9/11 is a tragedy and I do think that ongoing foreign policy and wars in the Middle East are not as productive," he added. "I'm not a big fan of the forever wars, I do hope that the Iran war comes to a swift conclusion... But also in the remembrance of Charlie Kirk, that's a day that I think we're still feeling the effects of now because it is the midterms and we needed our captain, and he's not here."

Kirk played a key role in Donald Trump’s 2024 victory by mobilizing young conservative voters through Turning Point Action, running a multimillion-dollar grassroots get-out-the-vote operation in battleground states and helping bring younger voters toward Trump.

It comes as the nation grieves the thousands of lives lost 25 years ago during the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks on the World Trade Center, Republicans at their midterm convention in Dallas paid tribute to those who perished.

On Day 2, a short memorial was held for the victims of the terrorist attacks. The crowd observed silence as an honor guard conducted a solemn bell salute on the eve of the 25th anniversary.

Yet at the venue, participants and candidates expressed that the 9/11 attacks weighed heavily on their minds despite the otherwise celebratory atmosphere for Republicans.

"I remember, I was standing at a Starbucks in Franklin, Tennessee, and someone walked in and said 'a plane just hit the World Trade Center.' We walked down into our office, and we watched that second plane fly into the building. We went to the airport that night, rented a car, and drove back from Nashville to Dallas. We did not see a single car on that road," Nate Sheets, Republican candidate for agriculture commissioner in Texas, said.

"So to see what happened the next several weeks in America, where we saw American resilience and patriotism, churches were filled, people were patriotic, and we have lost that," he added. "We need to gather that back. So, as we look back on this 25th anniversary, when we remember those people who were murdered by Islamic terrorists who came over... we need to remember that to make sure that the past is not repeated into the future."

Others voiced appreciation for being alongside fellow conservatives during what remains a particularly challenging period of the year. Some believe the day has taken on an even deeper solemnity following the assassination of conservative influencer Charlie Kirk, who was killed on Sept.10, 2025, during an event in Utah.

Abdul El-Sayed's campaign thrust into the spotlight ahead of 9/11 anniversary

One Democrat who repeatedly dominated the conversation on Thursday, the eve of the anniversary, was Michigan Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed. Republicans moved to portray him as a far-left radical, focusing on his progressive platform and his stance against Israel.

They also drew connections between him and progressive streamer Hasan Piker, a pro-Palestinian activist who once claimed that the U.S. "deserved 9/11," remarks he subsequently described as "inappropriate," stating he had been attempting to make a nuanced point regarding U.S. foreign policy.

El-Sayed has sought to separate himself from the streamer, yet that did little to deter his rival, Mike Rogers, from bringing up those comments once more.

"I never imagined that 25 years after 9/11, I would be running against somebody that campaigns with Hasan Piker, who thinks America deserved it," Rogers, a Republican congressman, said.

"El-Sayed's biggest booster, a man named Hasan Piker, he said, and I quote: 'America deserved 9/11.' Well, ladies and gentlemen, my mom has a saying: 'You are who you hang with,' and let me tell you, Hasan Piker is who Abdul El-Sayed hangs with," Michigan Rep. Lisa McClain said.

Vice President JD Vance also dedicated several minutes of his address to criticizing El-Sayed, taking aim at him for questioning whether Vance's conservative grandfather would have approved of his wife, Usha, an Indian woman, had they ever met. Notably, however, references to 9/11 from both the vice president and subsequently the president were remarkably brief.
