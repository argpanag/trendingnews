United Rugby Championship Round 5: All You Need to Know

TheTools · 29.10.2026 10:54 · source · permalink

The final round of BKT United Rugby Championship games before the international break, and one last chance for Ireland's provinces to get a bump up the table before a few weeks off.

Once again, an Irish derby headlines the weekend action from our point of view, with defending champions Leinster away to Connacht on Friday night (7.45pm), although both sides will be heavily impact by their Ireland call-ups.

On Saturday, Ulster face a daunting challenge as they seek to pick up a first win of the season, with Richie Murphy's side away to the Stormers in the early game (12.45pm).

On Saturday evening, Munster's tough run of fixtures to start the campaign concludes with a home tie against the Sharks at Thomond Park (5.30pm).

ONLINE

We'll have live blogs, score updates, reports and reaction from across the weekend on RTÉ.ie/sport and the RTÉ News app.

TV

Connacht v Leinster (Friday, 7.45pm) is live on TG4 and Premier Sports.

Stormers v Ulster (Saturday, 12.45pm), Munster v Sharks (Saturday, 5.30pm) and all other games can be watched on Premier Sports and streamed on URC.tv.

WEATHER

Conditions in Galway should be reasonable for the meeting of Connacht and Leinster on Friday night, with a small bit of rain - if any - forecast. Temperatures will be around 14C, with moderate to fresh winds.

It's expected to be warm and humid in Cape Town, where Ulster are taking on Stormers on Saturday afternoon, with temperatures around 21C.

It should be a great evening for rugby in Limerick on Saturday for Munster v Sharks, with a dry night forecast, and only a light breeze in temperatures of 13C.

Listen to the RTÉ Rugby podcast on Apple Podcasts, Spotify or wherever you get your podcasts, and email us at sportpodcasts@rte.ie

With Ireland's Nations Championship camp already up and running, both sides will be heavily depleted for this interpro, with Connacht down eight players, and Leinster without a whopping 17 of their squad.

Leo Cullen's side have been utterly dominant in this fixture, winning their last 13 games against Connacht, while they haven't lost at the Sportsground since April 2018.

Both teams have started the season with two wins and two defeats, although Leinster's three extra bonus-points have them two spots ahead of Stuart Lancaster's side in the table.

Connacht have won their last two interpros, beating Ulster and Munster in the second half of last season, but Leinster's win against Munster last week stretches their run of consecutive derby victories to six.

Leinster are also one of just two teams to record wins against Connacht at the Dexcom since its renovation, with Stormers the only other side to claim a win in Galway since the start of the year.

While Ulster are yet to win so far this season, their two draws and five bonus-points mean they are still very much in touch with the top half of the URC table.

This will be their fourth visit to Cape Town to face the Stormers, and the province are yet to pick off a win, with a dramatic 17-15 defeat in the 2022 semi-final among them.

The Stormers have made an impressive start to the season, with four wins from five in all competitions, and their only defeat coming away to league leaders Edinburgh in Round 2.

Slow starts have plagued Ulster's season so far, with Richie Murphy's side falling 34-5, 26-5 and 21-0 down in three of their four games to date.

The Stormers have lost just one of their last eight home games in all competitions, while Ulster come into this game without nine of their players due to Ireland's Nations Championship camp.

With one win, one draw and two defeats on their card this season, Saturday's meeting with the Sharks can have a major swing on how Munster are feeling after their first block of games.

A win on Saturday and they would be well-placed in the league after a difficult first five fixtures, ahead of very winnable ties with Zebre and Scarlets. Conversely, a defeat would have to be stewed on for a full month.

Munster are without Craig Casey and Tadhg Beirne in addition to several injuries, mainly across the backline, while the Sharks, who have won three of four games so far, can call on many of their Springbok stars this week.

The Sharks were 45-0 winners when these sides met in Durban last season, which was the low-point of Munster's campaign, but the South Africans have often struggled away from home.

Their win against Cardiff last week was their first URC win outside South Africa since April 2025 against Ulster, which just so happens to be their only win in 12 games in Ireland since joining the league in 2021.
