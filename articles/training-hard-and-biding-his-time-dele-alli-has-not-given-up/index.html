Training hard and biding his time, Dele Alli has not given up on football yet

TheTools · 20.11.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

The 30-year-old former England star combines Twitch streaming with working to return to the top as a player

Consider the training regime Dele Alli has followed since he left Como 18 months ago and it becomes clear why he takes exception to suggestions he has given up on football in favour of becoming an internet personality.

He trains five or six days a week with Connor Peters, an elite performance coach who has worked with players including Trevoh Chalobah. They spend four days on the pitch and days in between in the gym. A typical day begins at 9am with an hour of activation and stretching before Dele heads to non-league Windsor & Eton at 11am, where he trains on the pitches and uses the facilities. Then it’s back home to use his own oxygen chamber and sauna for a recovery session.

“We’ll do maybe two days on, one day off, and then three days on, one day off,” says Peters. “We’ve been doing that for just over a year now. It’s full-on. It’s pretty much as if you were at a club.”

Dele rose as a teenager to a level of stardom few experience and, used to media attention, doesn’t mind that his absence from football draws scrutiny. But he disagrees with the suggestion that choosing to stream on Twitch is a sign he has lost interest in the game that made him wealthy enough to never need to work again.

To a modest Twitch audience, he sometimes plays video games, sometimes just talks. He has a knack for creating viral content, sharing a phone call with Harry Kane and playing a game of EA Sports FC with IShowSpeed, one of the most famous online influencers. When he lost that game – and a bet – he wore an Arsenal shirt, apologising to Spurs fans before burning it. When TalkSport pundits claimed Dele was wasting his potential by becoming a streamer, Dele responded in a live stream, pointing out he was recording himself in his spare time and if he was out at nightclubs, it would be worse. Even then he was polite.

“When people see him online streaming or whatever he does in his private time, the initial thought is: ‘He’s done with football, then,’” Peters says. “He’s been training for a year and a half by himself. If he was done with football he wouldn’t be doing that. His actions show that the hunger is still there. A lot of people would come out of the game and then stop and lose the hunger for it, but he’s still chipping away every day, trying to get back and get to somewhere that he’s happy with.”

Dele broke through at MK Dons as a slight but devilish defensive midfielder, dominating League One midfields full of hardened professionals, before being scouted by Tottenham. Mauricio Pochettino attended MK Dons’ 4-0 League Cup win against Manchester United in 2014 and saw enough to leave early and convince Daniel Levy to pay £5m.

By the time Dele turned 21, his statistics were superior to Cristiano Ronaldo’s at the same age: 26 goals and 14 assists to Ronaldo’s 14 and 13. His 40 goal involvements equalled Frank Lampard, Steven Gerrard and David Beckham combined at the same age.

“He’s scored more in his first 50 league games than Steven Gerrard, Wayne Rooney and Frank Lampard,” Pochettino wrote in his book Brave New World. “He may go on to be better than all of them because he has the quality; now he just needs consistency.”

But there were, perhaps, warning signs of what would happen at Spurs. One source who worked closely with Dele describes him as a “cheeky chappy”; charming and universally popular.

He wasn’t one to miss training, but there were concerns he wasn’t leading the pristine lifestyle of many elite players, and tabloid stories about his private life trailed him. Pochettino was concerned when he saw Dele’s WhatsApp photo, a cartoon of a boy surrounded by people wanting a piece of him, believing he needed support from the right people. John McDermott, the head of coaching and development, said: “When the trough is full, the pigs come from all over to feed.”

When Dele voluntarily liquidated one of his companies this month, documents showed an estimated surplus after costs of £7.6m, suggesting the 30-year-old has not squandered the millions he earned at his peak in wages and sponsorship agreements, including a five-year deal with Adidas. He also owns a property portfolio worth more than £1.5m.

In the early days Pochettino warned Dele about the danger of not giving maximum effort in matches and training, a sentiment later shared by José Mourinho when he became Spurs manager. “I think one day you will regret, if you don’t reach what you can reach,” Mourinho told Dele in his office as Amazon Prime’s cameras rolled.

Although Dele formed a formidable relationship with Kane on the pitch, and was twice named the Professional Footballers’ Association’s men’s young player of the year while playing behind the striker, he was closer to Eric Dier off it. And perhaps it was those anchors – Pochettino and Dier – that Dele lost later in his career.

His time with Pochettino was fondly remembered. For the Argentinian’s 50th birthday celebrations, Dele recorded a heartfelt video for his former manager. “The message was beautiful,” says one party-goer.

In almost five years since leaving Spurs, he has made one league start at Everton and 10 at Besiktas, before a loan at Como, where Cesc Fàbregas tried to rediscover the player he once was. He came off the bench once and was sent off nine minutes later.

Dele, according to those close to him, is biding his time to get his next move right. He will be 31 in April and wants a suitable contract. He has spoken to a host of clubs, with interest from the Premier League and the Championship, and from the Middle East, where he has been tempted by the riches of the Saudi Pro League, the United Arab Emirates and Qatar. There has also been interest from Major League Soccer and he has held talks with clubs in Brazil and Japan. It is believed he will join a club this season.

In preparation, Peters is also guiding Dele’s nutrition, though he does not require much guidance. “Del’s a weird one, because he doesn’t snack, he doesn’t eat unhealthily, he doesn’t have a Sprite or a Coke or whatever. He’s never really been that kind of guy. All he does is drink coconut water, and he’ll have a small lunch, or his cheat day will be a Nando’s or something.”

Happiness is his priority. He spoke candidly on Gary Neville’s The Overlap in 2023 about his struggles with mental health and an addiction to sleeping pills after revealing abuse as a child. “Because he’s had such a long time out, he’s like: ‘What’s another few months if it means that I’m going to find something I’m actually happy with?’” Peters says. The aim is to “carry on for as long as possible”.

For the time being, Dele’s days are simple. When not training, he goes for coffee or a swim, and he has taken up padel. “He’s very much a person that’s happy with the little things,” Peters says. “He’s always the person that’s positive, the one that, if the group’s down or whatever, is going to pick everyone up. He’s happy with what he’s doing. His main happiness is playing football, and that’s what he wants to do.”
