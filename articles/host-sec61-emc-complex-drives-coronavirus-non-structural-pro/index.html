Host Sec61-EMC Complex Drives Coronavirus Non-Structural Protein Synthesis

TheTools · 26.11.2026 00:05 · source · permalink

A laboratory study published in PLOS Pathogens has revealed that severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) co-opts host endoplasmic reticulum translation machinery to synthesize its non-structural viral proteins. Investigating human cell infection models, a research team demonstrated that the virus exploits the Sec61 translocon and its associated ER membrane complex (EMC) to translate the polyproteins pp1a and pp1ab. While previous coronavirus models assumed that viral genomic RNA translation occurs exclusively within the cytosol via soluble ribosomes, the new experimental findings show that viral replication depends critically on endoplasmic reticulum-associated co-translational translocation. By applying chemical inhibitors and genetic knockdown tools in cultured human cells, the researchers established that the Sec61-EMC complex physically engages viral non-structural protein 3 (NSP3) during active translation, facilitating membrane insertion of viral replication components. Disrupting this host-mediated pathway suppressed non-structural protein abundance, impaired viral genome replication, and reduced the release of infectious viral progeny.

The genomic RNA of SARS-CoV-2 encodes two large overlapping polyproteins, pp1a and pp1ab, which are cleaved into 16 individual non-structural proteins that govern pathogenesis and viral replication. Among these, non-structural proteins 3, 4, and 6 are multi-pass transmembrane proteins that remodel the host endoplasmic reticulum into double-membrane vesicles, specialized protective structures where viral RNA replication occurs. Additionally, non-structural proteins 7, 8, and 12 assemble into the core viral replication-transcription complex that synthesizes new viral genomes and subgenomic transcripts. Despite the essential functions of these non-structural proteins in infection, the molecular mechanism by which the host cell synthesizes and inserts these hydrophobic multi-pass proteins into cellular membranes remained unresolved. The authors noted that synthesizing multi-pass membrane proteins entirely in the cytosol without engaging membrane-associated translocation channels presented a biochemical paradox, motivating an empirical search for host endoplasmic reticulum machinery capable of handling the viral polyprotein during early biogenesis.

To determine whether host translocation machinery physically associates with viral components during live infection, investigators conducted immunoprecipitation experiments coupled with mass spectrometry in human Huh7 liver cells stably expressing the ACE2 receptor. Cells expressing FLAG-tagged Sec61β, an integral subunit of the Sec61 translocation channel, were infected with the SARS-CoV-2 USA-WA1/2020 strain for 14 hours. Mass spectrometry analysis of eluted protein complexes identified specific peptide sequences belonging to viral non-structural proteins 3, 6, 8, and 14, whereas control cells expressing cytosolic green fluorescent protein showed no viral peptide binding. In parallel, reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) demonstrated that viral genomic RNA specifically coprecipitated with the Sec61 complex. When infected cells were treated with the Sec61 inhibitors CK147 or Apratoxin S4, which stabilize ribonucleoprotein-translocon intermediates, viral RNA recovery increased by 35-fold and 70-fold, respectively. Conversely, exposing cells to puromycin, an inhibitor inducing premature chain termination and ribosomal disassembly, completely eliminated the RNA-translocon interaction, confirming that viral RNA docks to Sec61 indirectly through actively translating ribosomes.

Having observed physical association between the translocon and the viral translation complex, the team evaluated whether Sec61 activity is functionally required for non-structural protein production during synchronized infection. Huh7-ACE2 cells were incubated with viral inocula at a multiplicity of infection of 2 at 4 degrees Celsius for one hour to synchronize surface binding before warming to 37 degrees Celsius to permit viral internalization. Infected cells were treated with dimethyl sulfoxide control, 10 micromolar CK147, or 50 nanomolar Apratoxin S4, and cellular lysates were collected at six-hour intervals across a 24-hour time course. Immunoblot analysis revealed that viral non-structural proteins became detectable by 12 hours post-infection and accumulated abundantly by 18 hours in untreated cells. Under Sec61 inhibition, protein levels of NSP1, NSP2, NSP3, NSP4, NSP8, and NSP15 were significantly reduced at 18 and 24 hours post-infection. Simultaneously, viral RNA quantification demonstrated that Sec61 inhibition severely suppressed viral genome replication, while cytotoxicity assays and measurements of viral RNA at two hours confirmed that inhibitors did not cause cellular toxicity or impair entry.

Because non-structural protein 3 is the first transmembrane protein encoded within the polyprotein, researchers investigated whether Sec61 is directly responsible for translating this single polypeptide in isolated expression systems. In HEK293T cells transfected with an amino-terminally tagged 2xStrep-NSP3 construct, metabolic pulse-chase labeling using radioactive sulfur-35 methionine and cysteine demonstrated that a 20-minute pulse yielded significantly lower quantities of newly synthesized NSP3 when Sec61 was chemically inhibited. In contrast, synthesis of a control cytosolic green fluorescent protein remained entirely unaffected. Using an orthogonal cycloheximide shutoff-recovery assay, in which translation was blocked for 12 hours to deplete steady-state protein pools before drug washout, newly synthesized 2xStrep-NSP3 recovered to roughly 40 percent of baseline in vehicle-treated cells, whereas recovery remained below 10 percent under Sec61 inhibition. Control experiments with isolated cytosolic FLAG-tagged NSP2 showed that its de novo synthesis was independent of Sec61. Furthermore, immunoblotting revealed accumulation of a high-molecular-weight protein species exceeding 260 kilodaltons, representing an uncleaved NSP1-NSP2-NSP3 intermediate, indicating that Sec61 inhibition impairs downstream polyprotein processing.

The authors next evaluated the role of the ER membrane complex, a multi-subunit transmembrane chaperone that interacts with Sec61 to assist insertion of unstable, marginally hydrophobic transmembrane domains. Using small interfering RNA (siRNA), researchers silenced core complex components EMC5 and EMC6, which simultaneously depleted subunits EMC1 and EMC3, or selectively targeted EMC1 alone in human host cells. Cell viability remained intact under both depletion conditions based on colorimetric metabolic assays. Measuring infectious viral production using median tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50) assays on Vero E6 cells revealed that EMC depletion caused robust reductions in secreted infectious virus titers at 48 hours post-infection. This antiviral effect occurred following infection with both the ancestral USA-WA1/2020 strain and the SARS-CoV-2 Delta variant (B.1.617.2). Control experiments confirmed that viral internalization remained normal at two hours post-infection in EMC-depleted cells, demonstrating that the ER membrane complex operates during post-entry viral gene expression rather than during attachment or cell penetration.

Consistent with findings from Sec61 inhibition, silencing the ER membrane complex caused severe deficits in non-structural protein abundance and downstream genome replication. In synchronized infection experiments, Huh7-ACE2 cells depleted of EMC5 and EMC6 showed substantial decreases in the levels of NSP1, NSP2, NSP3, NSP4, NSP8, and NSP15 across 12, 18, and 24 hours post-infection relative to scrambled siRNA controls. Because active coronavirus RNA synthesis produces double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) intermediates inside double-membrane vesicles, the team tracked replication organelle formation using confocal immunofluorescence with an anti-dsRNA monoclonal antibody. Depletion of EMC1 or EMC5/6 produced a marked decrease in the percentage of cells positive for double-stranded RNA puncta at 24 hours post-infection. Transfecting cells with an siRNA-resistant EMC1-FLAG construct successfully rescued dsRNA intermediate formation, ruling out off-target effects. Corroborating the microscopy data, RT-qPCR measurements performed at 72 hours post-infection showed significant reductions in total viral RNA levels following EMC knockdown, demonstrating that genome replication strictly requires functional host chaperone machinery.

Physical binding analyses further demonstrated that the ER membrane complex directly engages viral non-structural protein 3 during protein synthesis. Immunoprecipitation of endogenous EMC4 from SARS-CoV-2-infected cellular lysates recovered native NSP3, confirming that the two proteins interact during authentic infection. To determine whether NSP3 simultaneously associates with both Sec61 and the EMC, researchers developed a two-step sequential immunoprecipitation protocol in cells expressing FLAG-Sec61β and 2xStrep-NSP3. Primary elution of the translocon complex with FLAG peptide followed by secondary immunoprecipitation against EMC4 specifically captured 2xStrep-NSP3, but not the endogenous ER control protein DNAJB14. This interaction was sensitive to translational activity, as treating cells with puromycin for one hour disrupted association between EMC4 and 2xStrep-NSP3 without disrupting stable binding between EMC4 and other EMC subunits. Metabolic pulse-chase labeling confirmed that newly synthesized 2xStrep-NSP3 dropped significantly in EMC-depleted cells, whereas control FLAG-Erlin1 synthesis remained stable. Furthermore, treating cells with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 or the lysosomal inhibitor bafilomycin A1 failed to restore NSP3 levels, establishing that reduced protein yield stemmed from impaired synthesis rather than accelerated degradation.

To identify the specific molecular determinant governing EMC dependency, investigators analyzed the primary structure and hydropathy profiles of the four transmembrane domains of NSP3. Hydropathy plots and interfacial free-energy algorithms identified two weakly hydrophobic regions: one within the luminal ecto domain between transmembrane domains 1 and 2, and another within the helix domain connecting transmembrane domains 3 and 4. Using a truncated carboxy-terminal construct containing all four transmembrane segments (NSP3C-FLAG), the team created deletion mutants lacking either the ecto region (NSP3CΔecto-FLAG) or the helix region (NSP3CΔhelix-FLAG). While expression of both parental NSP3C and the ecto-deletion mutant remained highly sensitive to EMC knockdown, deletion of the helix domain rendered protein expression largely resistant to EMC depletion. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments verified that deleting the helix domain abolished physical binding between the viral polypeptide and endogenous EMC4. Crucially, the helix-deletion mutant retained sensitivity to Sec61 inhibition in cycloheximide recovery assays, colocalized with the endoplasmic reticulum marker BAP31, and remained in the insoluble membrane pellet during alkali extraction, demonstrating that resistance to EMC knockdown did not arise from loss of ER targeting or gross membrane insertion failure.

Together, these experimental findings delineate a model wherein SARS-CoV-2 relies on coordinated endoplasmic reticulum machinery to translate and assemble its non-structural replication complex. Following viral entry and release of genomic RNA into the host cytoplasm, ribosomes initiate translation of the polyprotein. As initial cytosolic portions of NSP1, NSP2, and NSP3 emerge, the first transmembrane domain of NSP3 directs the translating ribosome-nascent chain complex to the Sec61 translocon. As translation proceeds across the translocon lateral gate, the marginally hydrophobic helix domain of NSP3 emerges and engages the ER membrane complex, which acts as a chaperone to ensure stable integration into the ER membrane. The researchers noted that while sequence analysis shows conservation of this helix domain across multiple variants including Delta and Omicron, the study relied primarily on transformed human cell cultures, ectopic expression vectors, and chemical inhibitors. The authors emphasized that future work will be required to explore whether additional non-structural proteins, such as NSP6, contribute secondary determinants that guide the viral replication complex toward ER-associated biogenesis.
