Ranulph Fiennes case dismissed nine times by watchdog

TheTools · 28.09.2026 21:41 · source · permalink

Explorer’s friends asked Office of the Public Guardian to investigate ‘missing’ 82-year-old’s safety

A care watchdog refused nine times to investigate the plight of Sir Ranulph Fiennes, despite increasingly desperate pleas from his family and friends.

The Office of the Public Guardian (OPG) told them it could not look into the care arrangements of Sir Ranulph, 82, even though it is the body responsible for policing legal guardians.

Joseph Fiennes, the actor and a cousin of Sir Ranulph, is among those who wrote to the OPG with concerns about him being cut off from his family and friends by his second wife.

MPs have urged the OPG to look into the explorer’s welfare, saying Sir Ranulph’s case “raises serious concerns” and has cast light on the wider risk that guardians who abuse their legal powers are not being held accountable.

However, the watchdog has said the case is outside its remit.

Concerned family and friends have also contacted three police forces, three social services departments, the NHS and the Care Quality Commission by to no avail.

In total, The Telegraph is aware of 29 separate occasions on which various authorities were asked to look into Sir Ranulph’s wellbeing.

The explorer’s wife, Louise Millington-Cotes, 59, who is his legal guardian, has been moving him between care homes under a false name. His current whereabouts is unknown.

Joseph Fiennes, who found international fame with his starring role in the film Shakespeare in Love, told The Telegraph: “It is terrible that petitions for Ran have gone unanswered.

“If that happens to Ran I can’t bear to think how many others that has happened to and is happening to. It throws up vital questions, including questions for care homes and legal services.”

In May last year, five of Sir Ranulph’s friends, including Mike Kobold, a German entrepreneur, jointly wrote to the OPG with what they described as a “high-risk safeguarding referral”.

They told the OPG that Mrs Millington-Cotes had “completely isolated him from his friends, extended family and all social contact for more than seven months”. They added that “no one can even confirm that Sir Ranulph is alive, or what physical or mental condition he is in”.

The OPG replied that the complaint did not fall within its remit and suggested the friends contacted the Court of Protection or the police.

Jill Bowring, the wife of Sir Ranulph’s long-standing friend and business partner Anton Bowring, wrote to the OPG twice in May last year, and Mr Kobold wrote again in July last year.

In December, a female friend of Sir Ranulph wrote to the OPG, following up with a phone call on Jan 14, this year. Also on Jan 14, a male friend of Sir Ranulph separately contacted the OPG.

On Jan 6, Joseph Fiennes wrote to the OPG setting out his concerns, then on March 11 the owner of a care home in Wales, where Sir Ranulph had been placed by his wife for seven weeks, wrote to the OPG to record a list of safeguarding concerns he had about him.

They included irregularities with the way his medicines were being supplied by Mrs Millington-Cotes, questions about the validity of a “do not resuscitate” form she supplied and the disruptive nature of repeatedly moving him between care homes.

Social services: ‘No evidence of coercion’

On one occasion, the OPG said it had passed on concerns to a social services department in London, which visited Sir Ranulph in a care home and said it had been satisfied that “there was no evidence of coercion or controlling behaviour”.

However, the friend complained that social services had spoken to Sir Ranulph with care home staff present, meaning he might have felt unable to speak freely and in complete confidence.

In May last year, the King tried to contact his friend Sir Ranulph after hearing about his situation, but Mrs Millington-Cotes rebuffed his approach.

Mr Bowring said: “I find it deeply distressing that, with all the notification the OPG has received, nothing seems to have been done. We have to question whether the OPG is fit for purpose and, if it isn’t, that needs to be urgently addressed.”

The OPG’s website encourages people to report concerns if they think a guardian is “not acting in the person’s best interests, using money or property in the wrong way, not doing their job properly, abusing their position [or] not following the legal rules of their role”.

Friends of Sir Ranulph have written to the OPG saying they think that, by preventing her husband from being visited by family and friends, Mrs Millington-Cotes is not acting in his best interests. The OPG has told them it cannot investigate because the case falls outside its remit.

In the past two years, the Met Police, Cheshire Constabulary and West Mercia Police have been asked to investigate whether Sir Ranulph is safe and well, with 12 separate reports made in total.

Family and friends have also contacted the NHS Online, Cheshire Social Services, Hammersmith and Fulham council, and social services departments in Wales and Shropshire, on eight occasions.

Mr Kobold said: “Every single part of the British system has failed Ran.”

Friends of Sir Ranulph who have called on the OPG to investigate include Ben Fogle, Bear Grylls, Dame Joanna Lumley, Monty Don, Sir Robin Knox-Johnston and John Simpson.

Sir Ranulph ‘living in appropriate setting’

Alex Millington-Cotes, Sir Ranulph’s stepson, has set up a GoFundMe page to raise money for a legal challenge to his mother’s power of attorney through the Court of Protection.

Det Ch Supt Claire Jesson, of Cheshire Police, said: “Police and partner agencies are aware of the concerns raised and are satisfied that Sir Ranulph Fiennes is living in an appropriate setting with suitable care and professional oversight.

“No immediate safeguarding concerns have been identified at this time. Any new information received will be assessed and considered through the appropriate multi-agency arrangements.

“As this is a matter involving an individual’s care and welfare, it would not be appropriate to comment further on personal circumstances. We would ask that privacy is respected and unnecessary speculation is avoided.”

An OPG spokesman said: “We have a strict duty of confidentiality to all individuals who have made a Lasting Power of Attorney, and it would be deeply inappropriate to comment on a specific case.

“We take all safeguarding concerns seriously and investigate thoroughly where there is the evidence and legal basis to do so.”
