Ford Mustang Ute Revealed As One-Off Falcon Ute Successor

TheTools · 08.10.2026 23:43 · source · permalink

A one-off Ford Mustang ute built by the Blue Oval’s experimental vehicles team is now on display, but is sadly missing from showrooms.

One of Ford’s biggest secrets has been unveiled, with a one-off Mustang Ute concept going on display at its Heritage Fleet display in the US.

Announced by Ford Heritage Fleet Manager Matt Korczyk, the Mustang Ute was built by Ford’s Experimental Vehicle Building (EVB) team in Dearborn, Michigan, appearing to be based on the previous-generation S550 coupe.

Like the Ford Falcon ute which was sold in Australia for 55 years, it’s underpinned by a monocoque chassis, rather than the body-on-frame design of the utes which now dominate the local market, such as the Ranger.

“This concept was built by the team in the Ford EVB Metal Shop, as a way to train apprentices by allowing them to get real world, hands-on experience by way of building a concept car,” Korczyk said in a social media post on Instagram.

“The team took a normal Mustang, and turned it into a hand-crafted, one-off concept ‘Ute.’

“Hundreds of hours went into this build, and it serves as a great reminder of how skilled trades of all kinds are integral to the Company. It also showcases the raw talent and skill of not only the apprentices who honed their craft on this vehicle, but also the talent of those who helped teach them.

“Until now, this concept has never been shown outside of the walls of Ford EVB (Experimental Vehicles Building)!”

Sadly, there’s little to no chance we’ll ever see a Ford Mustang ute in showrooms, however that doesn’t mean that the brand isn’t willing to branch out from its pony car’s two-door roots.

Ford has strongly hinted – and shown to dealers – that it’ll build a Mustang-based sedan in the near future, with a report by Automotive News in August last year saying it launch as soon as early 2028, at least in the US.

A Mustang sedan was reportedly previewed to North American dealers last year, and prior to this Ford CEO Jim Farley told media outlets it could launch a four-door version of the pony car, as long as it has “all the performance and attitude of the original.”

Fuel was added to the fire of speculation when, speaking to Automotive News at the Detroit motor show, Farley refused to rule out a return to the sedan segment, saying “never say never”, before adding a caveat.

“The sedan market is very vibrant. It’s not that there isn’t a market there. It’s just we couldn’t find a way to compete and be profitable. Well, we may find a way to do that.”

The sedan could potentially adopt the Mach 4 nameplate - similar to the previous Mustang Mach 1 performance flagship, as well as the Mustang Mach-E electric SUV. Earlier this year the Mach 4 nameplate was trademarked in the US and Australia.
