Bruce Willis' heartbreaking decline revealed

TheTools · 08.11.2026 22:25 · source · permalink

In the months before Bruce Willis was diagnosed with frontotemporal dementia (FTD), a degenerative and incurable brain disease that affects language, behavior and personality, the Sixth Sense star wasn't acting like himself.

Conversations with Willis, 71, just weren't lining up, according to his wife Emma Heming Willis, whom he married in 2009 after less than two years of dating. The proud Jersey boy, whose motto was 'live it up,' started acting withdrawn at home.

The model, 50, initially wondered if something was wrong with his hearing since Willis had suffered partial hearing loss in his left ear after firing a gun without earplugs during the filming of the 1988 blockbuster Die Hard.

So in March 2022, when Willis was diagnosed with aphasia, a language disorder that affects your ability to speak and understand what others say, it was a shock to his family and to his fans who had come to know the actor as brave, fearless and tough – just like the characters he portrayed on the big screen.

His family announced his retirement from acting right before his condition had progressed, as doctors gave the heartbreaking news that he would never be the same again.

Four years after his FTD diagnosis – and one year since the Pulp Fiction actor was last seen in public – Willis 'has declined a lot mentally' and is 'living in his own world,' according to a source who told the Daily Mail that the Hollywood legend is unaware of his own mental decline.

'He knows his life is different, but he doesn't think it is that different,' the source said.

'He doesn't talk about it or even complain much. He senses something is off, but he doesn't have a full grasp of it.'

His personality has changed quite a bit amid his health struggles, the source claimed. He is now 'Mr Mellow,' becoming more 'at ease' than he was in his younger years 'when he had a lot of energy and talked a ton.'

'Sometimes he looks like a baby who is staring at an object with curiosity, just taking it in,' the source said. 'But if you look at him, he appears like a healthy middle-aged man.'

Even before the family had answers from doctors, they noticed the man who was once the life of the party no longer seemed interested in engaging with his five daughters – his two young girls with Heming Willis, and three adult children from his 13-year marriage with ex-wife, actress Demi Moore – during their once conversation-filled weekly family dinners.

Doctors eventually gave a more specific diagnosis: primary progressive aphasia, a type of FTD that causes a gradual decline in the areas of the brain linked to personality and language abilities and has a life expectancy of between eight and 12 years after the start of symptoms.

In February 2023, three months after Willis' diagnosis, the actor's family thanked fans for their support as Willis dealt with the debilitating disease that affects an estimated 50,000 to 60,000 people in the US.

'Your continued compassion, understanding, and respect will enable us to help Bruce live as full a life as possible,' the family wrote.

But there were many in the Unbreakable star's professional orbit who sensed something had been off with him long before his diagnosis became known to the public.

When it was announced the previous year that Willis was stepping away from acting after four decades in the business, nearly two dozen former coworkers of the one-time action star told the Los Angeles Times that he had been exhibiting serious signs of mental decline for years.

Despite making 25 films between 2020 and 2023, many of which were direct-to-video releases, sources recalled that Willis was allegedly often unable to remember dialogue and traveled with an actor who fed him his lines through an earpiece.

Sources even questioned whether the star was fully aware of his surroundings while shooting. Former colleagues describe one alleged incident on the set of the 2020 movie Hard Kill in which he fired a prop gun on the wrong cue. No one was hurt, but sources said the event left co-stars and crew shaken.

So why did it take so long for someone to step in and help the Unbreakable star?

FTD is a difficult disease to diagnose, according to the Mayo Clinic. There is no single test for it, and symptoms often overlap with other ailments, including liver and kidney disease, making it difficult to catch early.

Heming Willis learned this the hard way.

Only after various tests, doctors' consultations and a 'vague' aphasia diagnosis did she finally know what was going on with her husband. Yet having an answer wasn't necessarily the comfort she thought it would be.

Unlike Alzheimer's, the most common form of dementia, patients with FTD don't lose their memory immediately but instead undergo personality changes that come on gradually and worsen over time.

As the disease, which is the most common form of dementia in people under 60, progresses, it causes symptoms that mirror those of late-stage Alzheimer's, including difficulty eating or swallowing, having trouble walking and being vulnerable to infections due to the blood-brain barrier weakening.

While FTD is not fatal on its own, pneumonia is the leading cause of death in those with the disease.

As of now, there is no cure for FTD and no way to slow down or prevent it, but some prescribed drugs and therapies can help to ease symptoms.

Heming has said that knowing there was no treatment or cure 'was one of the most traumatic experiences, feeling completely alone.'

Yet, Heming quickly realized she wouldn't have to face this cruel reality alone while caring for Bruce and their two daughters, Mabel, now 14, and Evelyn, 12. She had the support of Moore, who was married to Willis from 1987 to 2000, and her adult stepdaughters: Rumer, 38, Scout, 35 and Tallulah, 32.

Since their divorce, Moore and Willis have had an amicable co-parenting relationship and friendship. 'It's a funny thing to say,' the Ghost star wrote in her 2019 memoir Inside Out. 'But I'm very proud of our divorce.'

The split 'wasn't easy at first,' she noted. 'But we managed to move the heart of our relationship, the heart of what created our family, into something new that gave the girls a loving, supportive environment with both parents.'

Following his diagnosis, Moore stepped up to support her ex-husband amid his ongoing health battle, often visiting with him and providing updates for fans.

In 2024, Moore said there was a 'great beauty' in meeting Willis where he is now.

'When you're holding on to what was, I think it's a losing game,' she said while appearing at that year's Hamptons International Film Festival. She was focused on 'being able to share whatever we have, for however long we have it.'

Last year, Heming revealed that her husband was living in a nearby one-story home with round-the-clock care, a decision she said was difficult for everyone but beneficial.

'Ultimately, I could get back to being his wife,' she told The Sunday Times. 'And that's such a gift.'

Now, she is reportedly spending 'a mega fortune' on her husband's care, according to our source, who estimated it costs 'more than $30,000 a month.'

'It's a good thing that he is worth a fortune because insurance doesn't cover much,' the source noted.

Indeed, a source close to the family told the Daily Mail last year that Heming was feeling the pressures of managing her husband's estate, which is estimated to be worth $250 million.

'Taking care of Bruce isn't just about keeping up his spirits and making sure he can physically get through the day. It also involves looking after the massive fortune he accrued as an A-list movie star,' the source said then.

Knowing how isolating his disease can be, Heming works hard to make sure her spouse has time to connect with friends and family, according to the insider, who said 'there are people by him all the time that he can interact with.'

'His home is very bright and happy' and there are always 'funny people around him to keep things light,' an insider said.

Plus, 'Bruce has family around all the time' who 'visit frequently.'

Tallulah, his youngest daughter with Moore, is a regular visitor. 'She is amazing and really loves her dad,' the source said.

As for Willis's celebrity pals, they don't come around all that much anymore. 'That is how the business is though,' the source said. 'Once you stop working, all those glam friends forget you.

'In this town, the A-listers are saying, "Bruce who?"'

Willis has mostly stayed out of the spotlight since his diagnosis. But last November, in a rare sighting, he was spotted out and about with his carer, whose hand he held during their seaside walk in Los Angeles to keep himself steady.

Today, he has hobbies that keep him busy and engaged, but they are much different than they once were, according to our insider.

'Bruce doesn't just sit in front of a TV all day,' the source told the Daily Mail. 'His big thrill is getting an ice cream cone or going for a ride in a car. He loves listening to Neil Diamond. Music is great for him.'

Prior to becoming an actor, Willis enjoyed a short-lived music career, playing harmonica in blues bars to get by in the late 80s.

Yet the source also noted that Willis is now struggling with the same speech issues that once plagued him in his youth.

As a child, Willis earned the nickname 'Buck-Buck' because of his stutter, but after joining the drama club at school he learned to manage it.

Back then, 'if he had a tough time saying a word, he would change the word,' the source explained. 'He even did that with movie scripts' as he got older.

However, at this stage in his disease, he can no longer control his stutter.

He 'pauses for a long time, like he is lost in thought,' the source said. 'But he is really trying to find the words.

'He is figuring out how to articulate himself.'

The Daily Mail has reached out to representatives for Willis for comment.

As Willis adjusts to his new normal, his youngest children are also learning to live without the dad they once knew.

In an October 2025 interview with Vogue Australia, Heming said that her girls 'grieve' for their dad. 'He's missing important milestones, that's tough for them.'

While children are resilient, she admitted that she's not sure 'my kids will ever bounce back, but they're learning and so am I.'

But Heming said it's important to cherish 'the moments of connection' she still has with Willis, all too aware they could disappear without any warning.

'Do I think he knows, "Oh, this is Emma, and we've been married for this many years?"' she told the Sunday Times. 'I don't know what that process is for him.'

But 'when he puts his arms around me, it just feels like Bruce,' she said. 'It's not different in that way.

'And that's really, really beautiful and really, really heartbreaking.'
