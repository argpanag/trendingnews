Anthony Davis’ future in Washington faces growing uncertainty

TheTools · 13.11.2026 13:58 · source · permalink

The Anthony Davis situation in Washington is drawing increasing attention around the NBA, with the Wizards facing questions about his long-term future and contract expectations.

Sam Amick of The Athletic reported Friday, November 13, that teams are closely monitoring the 33-year-old forward-center as the trade deadline approaches. Davis is averaging a team-high 25.6 points and 10.5 rebounds through 11 games, but Washington is off to a 4-9 start.

Davis is in the second season of a three-year, $175 million contract. His deal includes a $62.7 million player option for 2027-28, leaving him with one guaranteed season remaining after the current campaign.

According to Amick, Davis’ camp views Washington’s decision to give Trae Young a four-year, $212.9 million contract in June as a major obstacle to securing a maximum or near-maximum extension.

The Wizards also have important decisions ahead involving young players Alex Sarr and Kyshawn George, who become eligible for extensions this summer.

Amick added that David Aldridge and Josh Robbins previously reported there is “almost no chance” Washington will offer Davis a maximum contract.

Davis addressed the contract situation during the Wizards’ media day, saying he expected to play through the season before discussing his deal with the organization “when the season is over.”

The team’s results could also influence his outlook. Washington has not shown enough early progress to suggest it can compete for a championship this season, while Davis continues to produce at an elite level.

His performance against the Detroit Pistons on Thursday illustrated the contrast. Davis recorded 30 points, 15 rebounds and four assists, but the Wizards suffered a double-overtime defeat.

The production could strengthen his appeal to potential trade suitors. Davis is shooting 52.4% from the field and averaging 25.6 points, 10.5 rebounds and 2.0 assists in 34.1 minutes per game.

However, his injury history and next-contract expectations could complicate any potential move. Amick noted that Davis’ longtime agent, Klutch Sports CEO Rich Paul, has a history of applying pressure to organizations when necessary.

The central question is whether another team would be willing to make a significant long-term financial commitment to Davis before the trade deadline. Washington must weigh his current production against its rebuilding timeline and the financial commitments it faces to its younger core.
