'Perfect storm': How banks allegedly defrauded of up to $600m

TheTools · 10.09.2026 06:31 · source · permalink

  • By Else Kennedy

  • Topic:Crime

By Else Kennedy

Topic:Crime

The crime ring is accused of making false loan applications. (Supplied: NSW Police)

In short:

NSW police say one of Australia's largest fraud syndicates has fleeced banks of up to $600 million in fraudulent loans.

The criminal group allegedly purchased properties across metropolitan Sydney, but mainly targeted the eastern suburbs.

What's next?

Police are expecting to make more arrests and will now focus on real estate agents and property developers.

Australia's banks have been defrauded of more than $600 million by one of the country's largest fraud syndicates, NSW Police says.

Police yesterday charged three people with allegedly assisting the multi-million dollar fraud and money laundering syndicate, including a Bankstown accounting firm's director and two accountants.

It followed the arrests a week earlier of two women who were charged with offences including six counts of dishonestly obtaining financial advantage by deception, allegedly totalling more than $18 million.

They were granted conditional bail.

An accounting firm director, two employees and two alleged money mules are accused of assisting the syndicate. (Supplied: NSW Police)

Strike force detectives carried out a search at the accounting firm in Sydney's west yesterday afternoon, with help from the NSW Crime Commission, Australian Taxation Office and other authorities.

A 46-year-old man was charged with offences, including 17 counts of making false documents to obtain financial advantage, allegedly totalling $16.2 million in fraudulent loans.

A 34-year-old man was charged with multiple offences, including another 17 counts of making false documents to obtain financial advantage, allegedly totalling $15.6 million in fraudulent loans.

Both men were refused bail.

Accountants and money mules are alleged to have participated in the multi-million dollar fraud ring. (Supplied: NSW Police)

A 28-year-old woman has been charged with offences including 17 counts of making false documents to obtain financial advantage, allegedly totalling $14.5 million in fraudulent loans.

She was granted conditional bail.

The arrests bring the total number of people charged by Strike Force Myddleton to 33.

Set up by the Financial Crimes Squad to investigate a criminal syndicate targeting Sydney automotive financing companies, the strike force originally discovered the group was allegedly using personal information to apply for loans to purchase luxury "ghost cars", which did not exist.

The investigation further found the syndicate's reach extended into alleged large-scale personal, business and home loan fraud against financial institutions.

To date, the NSW Crime Commission has recovered $95 million in assets.

'Big Four issued $500m in fraudulent loans'

Detective Superintendent Gordon Arbinja said the banks were facing "a perfect storm of loss".

"I have been doing this type of investigation for over 20 years," he said.

"This is the largest financial crime that I have ever seen."

Detective Superintendent Arbinja said an estimated $500 million in fraudulent loans were issued by Australia's "Big Four" banks: the Commonwealth Bank, National Australia Bank, ANZ and Westpac.

Smaller financial institutions issued fraudulent loans estimated at $100 million.

Police allege the Bankstown accounting firm was involved in creating fake financial documents to support loan applications.

The NSW Crime Commission has seized $95 million in assets to date. (Supplied: NSW Police)

The syndicate allegedly purchased properties across metropolitan Sydney, but mainly targeted the eastern suburbs.

"The eastern suburbs of Sydney is a very prestigious market where the value of properties is higher than anywhere else in the Sydney property market," Detective Superintendent Arbinja said.

Police allege one of the methods the syndicate used to accumulate funds was to purchase properties at highly inflated prices, then have the vendor return a portion of the funds to the buyer through a legal contract called a deed of rebate.

Detective Superintendent Arbinja said, in all cases, the deed was not visible to the bank, which meant the financial institutions accepted the loan at the inflated rate and people in the syndicate were allegedly able to pocket the extra cash after the rebate.

He said some properties sold at prices between 40 and 50 per cent higher than similar properties in the same street.

Strike Force Myddleton has now laid charges against 33 people. (Supplied: NSW Police)

Police expect to make more arrests and will now focus on real estate agents and property developers.

"It's not an offence to sell your property for a higher rate than it is worth … however I think that the real estate agents could have given better indication to vendors if the property was severely overvalued," Detective Superintendent Arbinja said.

He said it was unlikely the banks would be able to recover the full value of their loans.

"Every property that was purchased was highly overvalued," Detective Superintendent Arbinja said.

"The issue that we have now is, with the property downturn, that overvaluation will be magnified somewhat."

'Failure of oversight'

Niall Coburn, a barrister specialising in financial crime and investment malpractice, said it appeared banks may not have done appropriate due diligence before issuing the fraudulent loans.

"I think the banks probably dropped the ball because they were relying on credible associates to do the right thing," he said.

"There appears to be an acute failure of oversight in allowing this procedure where loans were not … tested."

Niall Coburn says it appears banks "dropped the ball" in overseeing the loans. (Supplied)

He said overvalued properties should have been a red flag to the banks.

"If the valuations are fraudulent, one could only surmise that there was very little oversight by the banks, which is concerning," he said.

A Bendigo Bank spokesperson said it remained committed to ongoing engagement with AUSTRAC, Australia's anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing regulator, and industry partners to help protect customers and the integrity of the financial system.

ANZ, Westpac and Macquarie Bank have been contacted for comment.

NAB, Macquarie Bank and Commonwealth Bank declined to comment as the matter was under police investigation.
