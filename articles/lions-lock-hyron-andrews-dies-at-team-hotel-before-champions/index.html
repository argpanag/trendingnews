Lions lock Hyron Andrews dies at team hotel before Champions Cup game against La Rochelle

SA Rugby said Andrews fell at hotel in tragic accident

Squad will play on Saturday to honour teammate

Tributes have been paid to Johannesburg Lions’ Hyron Andrews after he died in the team hotel on the eve of their Champions Cup game against La Rochelle.

Andrews, 31, who also played for the KZN Sharks and Sale Sharks, could not be revived by emergency services after the alarm was raised at the Atalante-Relais Thalasso hotel on the Île de Ré. A statement from SA Rugby said the lock forward had “passed away tragically” after an accident in which he fell.

“This is terrible and distressing news; our hearts go out to Hyron’s family and the whole Lions rugby community,” said Mark Alexander, president of the South African Rugby Union in a statement released after Andrews’ family had been contacted.

“There are no words to adequately convey our shock and the depth of sympathy we feel for his wife Maighlin, and for his extended family, friends, teammates and loved ones during this time of immense sorrow.

“Hyron was a respected member of the rugby community whose contribution to the game and impact on those around him will be remembered with great fondness.

“His untimely passing is a devastating loss, and we share in the grief of all who knew and loved him.”

The Lions had been preparing to make their debut in the top-tier tournament on Saturday having flown to Europe after a heavy 57-15 defeat by the Bulls, their South African rivals. The squad has chosen to fulfil the fixture in honour of their teammate.

European Professional Club Rugby, the tournament organisers said: “In memory of Hyron, a moment’s silence will be held ahead of every Investec Champions Cup and EPCR Challenge Cup match this weekend.”

Andrews, who was born in Paarl and attended Hoërskool Garsfontein in Pretoria, also represented the Junior Springboks in 2015.

The Lions posted an emotional tribute on Instagram, saying: “It is with broken hearts that we mourn the passing of Hyron Andrews, a valued member of our Lions family. Our deepest condolences are with Maiglin, Hendrix, Emerson and the entire Andrews family during this incredibly difficult time.

“Rest in peace, Hyron. You will always be part of our family.”

Sale reflected on the loss of a “much-loved member” of their squad, who had stayed on past his initial short-term deal through to the end of the 2025-26 campaign, making 45 appearances.

“Hyron was just a brilliant guy and a brilliant team-mate. He was so skilled on the field, but off it, he was always someone you would want to have a beer with,” Sharks’ director of rugby Alex Sanderson told the club’s website.

“He was funny and charismatic in his own unique, dry way and the lads, to a man, loved playing with him and being around him. It was a big move for Hyron and his wife Maighlin to come to England but he truly made a second home in Manchester and it was incredible to see how he and his family embraced the club and area.”

The tragic incident comes on the 10th anniversary of the death of the Munster coach Anthony Foley in a Paris hotel room in 2016. Foley, who was 42, died in his sleep on the eve of a European tie against Racing 92 after a heart condition caused fluid to build up in his lungs. Tributes are due to paid to him before this weekend’s game against Racing in Cork, with the Munster players set to wear commemorative presentation jackets featuring the club crest and the number 8, Foley’s position.
