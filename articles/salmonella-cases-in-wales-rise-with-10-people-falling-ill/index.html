Salmonella cases in Wales rise with 10 people falling ill

TheTools · 07.09.2026 13:01 · source · permalink

Salmonella infections have increased in Wales following a UK-wide outbreak that has resulted in two deaths and left hundreds unwell. Public Health Wales has confirmed there have been two additional cases of salmonella, bringing the total to 10 people across the country.

The outbreak has been potentially connected to eggs imported into the UK from overseas with infections linked to dining at small cafés, restaurants, and takeaway establishments, according to the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA).

As of September 1 the UKHSA has recorded 305 confirmed cases and one death from salmonella associated with the initial salmonella enteritidis cluster.

Two further clusters of the bacteria have been identified with cases rising across all three clusters since June.

There has been a total of 41 confirmed cases and one death linked to the t5.9765 cluster in England.

The genetic similarity of these clusters, combined with food traceability information, suggests these outbreaks may all be linked to imported eggs and are being investigated together, the UKHSA has stated.

The agency maintains that the risk to the general public from salmonella remains low.

Residents across Wales have been advised to inspect their eggs before consumption to minimise the risk of contracting salmonella.

To confirm your egg is safe to eat, look for the Red Lion symbol, along with the eight-digit code.

Most individuals who contract a salmonella infection encounter mild symptoms including diarrhoea, stomach cramps, fever, and occasionally nausea, vomiting, or headache but those with compromised immune systems such as very young children, the elderly, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals can suffer more serious reactions.

More severe symptoms can include bloodstream infections, infection of metastatic sites, sepsis, and multi-organ failure.

The Food Standards Agency provides guidance on the safe storage, handling, and use of eggs in catering settings. It includes an egg safety checklist that states:

  • buy eggs from reputable suppliers
  • do not accept or use damaged or dirty eggs
  • follow good hygiene practices
  • refrigerate eggs where possible
  • do not use eggs beyond their best before date
  • take extra care with raw or lightly-cooked egg dishes
  • always be mindful of who you’re cooking for, especially if there’s a risk your consumers could be vulnerable to salmonella infection
  • use pasteurised eggs, Lion Code, or Laid in Britain assured eggs when serving vulnerable consumers
  • keep pooled liquid egg refrigerated until use, never top up with new eggs, and use on the same day
  • always cook non-hen eggs thoroughly
  • serve egg dishes straightaway or cool them quickly and keep chilled
