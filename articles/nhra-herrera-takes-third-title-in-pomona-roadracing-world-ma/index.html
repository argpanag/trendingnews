NHRA: Herrera Takes Third Title in Pomona - Roadracing World Magazine | Motorcycle Riding, Racing & Tech News

TheTools · 17.11.2026 23:11 · source · permalink

© 2026, Roadracing World Publishing, Inc. From a press release issued By Suzuki Motor USA, LLC

Brea, CA — In a thrilling conclusion to the 2026 Pro Stock  Motorcycle Countdown to the Championship, RevZilla/Motul/Vance & Hines  Suzuki rider Gaige Herrera raced his Suzuki Gen 3 Hayabusa to victory in the  final race of the season and claimed his third world championship by a slim  margin over teammate Richard Gadson at the In-N-Out  Burger NHRA Finals at In-N-Out Burger Pomona Dragstrip in Pomona, CA, on Sunday.

 

Race Highlights

  • Gaige Herrera qualified number one, won his fifth race of the season and clinched his third Pro Stock Motorcycle world championship

  • Richard Gadson qualified second, advanced to the semi-final round and finished second in the championship standings

Gaige Herrera qualified number one, won his fifth race of the season and clinched his third Pro Stock Motorcycle world championship

Richard Gadson qualified second, advanced to the semi-final round and finished second in the championship standings

 

After persistent thunderstorms forced the cancellation of the 2025 NHRA Finals, this year’s finale delivered the exciting winner-take-all showdown race fans were denied last season.

Adding to the drama, all four qualifying sessions carried heightened significance to the championship outcome, as Saturday’s third round also served as the final round of the penultimate race in Las Vegas, which was postponed due to high winds. Qualifying bonus points awarded to the quickest riders in each session provided even more opportunities to shake up the standings.

Herrera took maximum advantage of both situations by winning the delayed Las Vegas race, earning maximum bonus points and qualifying number one for the sixth time this season with a 6.673/201.76 mph pass.

The belated Las Vegas victory came when Herrera defeated Gadson with a 6.701-second/201.13 mph run versus his teammate’s 6.777-second/200.65 mph pass to claim his fourth win of the season and 32nd of his career.

Between earning 20 points for the Vegas win, 16 qualifying bonus points and 10 points as number one qualifier, Herrera shrank his deficit to Gadson to 28 points heading into Sunday’s elimination rounds.

More crucially, with 30 points available for each round in Pomona’s points-and-a-half, that meant Herrera would need to go only one round further than Gadson on Sunday to add a third championship to the titles he won in 2023 and 2024.

As he had done in Las Vegas, Herrera immediately stamped his authority on the field by posting low elapsed time in rounds one and two with victories of 6.686 seconds at 202.48 mph over Alex Laughlin (7.245 seconds/165.80 mph) and 6.721 seconds at 201.94 mph over John Hall (6.845 seconds/197.94 mph).

Needing to match Gadson round-for-round to keep pace, Herrera defeated Ryan Oehler (6.778 seconds/198.44 mph) in the semi-finals with a 6.708-second/201.43 mph pass to advance to his 39th career final round. That round win was enough for Herrera to clinch his third world championship after Gadson failed to advance.

But Herrera wasn’t done winning yet. He completed a clean sweep by defeating Matt Smith (6.735 seconds/201.40 mph) in the final round with a 6.683-second/202.39 mph run to seal his fifth race win of the season and 33rd of his career.

“When I got off the plane, I told myself, ‘I’m here to win and that’s what I’m going to do,’ but I’m not going to lie. My stomach was twisting this morning. I haven’t ever been as nervous on a race day as I was today,” Herrera said.

“After I went out early in the first Countdown race at Martin and then went out in the second round in Dallas, I really didn’t think it was going to be possible,” he said. “I’m not a numbers person but I started counting and putting scenarios together in my head and it was making me think too much about points instead of just riding the bike. I had to go back to my normal approach of not doing the math and just going out there to have fun and remembering how lucky I am to be out here living my dream riding these motorcycles for Vance & Hines and Suzuki.”

Herrera finished the season with 2,735 points, seven final round appearances and five victories in 14 races, including three of the final six races in the Countdown playoffs. His championship-winning margin over Gadson was 32 points.

 

 

“Chasing Richard all year put me in a different mindset compared to my first two championships. You’re much hungrier for something when it feels like you can’t reach it and for me to be able to fully capitalize on this weekend and reach it feels surreal and amazing,” Herrera said. “Richard and I are very competitive people and we both wanted to win, but finishing one and two for Vance & Hines is what it’s all about.”

Reigning world champion and pre-race points leader Richard Gadson qualified second on Saturday with a 6.713-second/200.00 mph run, marking the fourth time this season the Vance & Hines riders locked out the top two spots in the starting order.

Gadson defeated Malcolm Phillips Jr. (6.814 seconds/197.77 mph) in round one with a 6.780-second/200.86 mph run and then kept his title hopes alive in round two with a narrow 6.816-second/197.88 mph victory over Chase Van Sant (6.843-second/196.93 mph).

But the defending champion’s dreams of a repeat title were dashed in the semi-finals, when Gadson couldn’t muster the speed to overcome Matt Smith, falling short with a 6.811-second/198.64 mph run against Smith’s 6.760-second/200.47 mph pass. Herrera’s victory in the other semi-final pairing provided the 30 points he needed to leapfrog over Gadson to win the title.

“I knew coming into this weekend that there was a good possibility of that happening. What Gaige and Andrew Hines are doing together with that motorcycle is historic, so just to be able to battle with him and make him sweat is something I consider a feather in my cap,” Gadson said. “My crew chief Eddie Krawiec has an 8-year-old son named Chase and he always tells me that if you do your best, it’s OK if you lose. I did my best and fought as hard as I could all year. I gave it everything I had. I feel like I overachieved, so I’m not hanging my head at all.”

Gadson wrapped up his runner-up season with 2,703 points, nine final round appearances and three victories.

“As riders and competitors, we empty the tank to compete at this level. The only thing that makes it feel worth it sometimes is to win,” Gadson said. “I did the best I could. It wasn’t quite enough to win the championship this year, but I still had a great season, and I’ll hang my hat on that.”

 

 

RevZilla/Motul/Vance & Hines Suzuki crew chief Andrew Hines praised the collective effort of the riders and crew members who, combined with the speed and performance of the team’s Gen 3 Hayabusas, won eight races this year and their fourth straight championship.

“Gaige’s Hayabusa is in a league of its own and he’s riding it to the fullest of its ability. The night before the race I told him he was going to have to win two races in a row to win this championship, which we haven’t done since the 2024 season when we won 11 races in a row,” Hines said. “That’s astounding to say, but our team has been spreading the wins around between Richard and Gaige, and today he got it done.”

“As a team, we struggled at times through the middle of the season with some red lights and mechanicals but I told them we’ve got to settle in, figure out what our best combinations are for both bikes, and bring that to every race for the rest of the year, and that’s what we did,” Hines said. “There’s a lot of behind-the-scenes preparation that goes into looking unstoppable on the track. That all happens at our shop in Indy. Without all those long hours, stressful nights and great work from the team at the track and the five guys in our engine and machine shop who people don’t see, we couldn’t get it done.”

At the conclusion of the 2026 Pro Stock Motorcycle season, Herrera and Gadson finished first and second in the standings, with 2,735 and 2,703 points, respectively.

The RevZilla/Motul/Vance & Hines Suzuki team returns to action at the opening round of the 2027 NHRA season March 11-14, 2027, at the NHRA Gatornationals at Gainesville Raceway in Gainesville, FL.
