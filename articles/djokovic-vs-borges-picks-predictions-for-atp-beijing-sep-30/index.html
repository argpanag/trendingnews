Djokovic vs Borges Picks & Predictions for ATP Beijing (Sep 30)

TheTools · 29.09.2026 17:21 · source · permalink

By Sascha Paruk in Tennis

Published: September 29, 2026 at 1:21 pm EDT

  • Novak Djokovic and Nuno Borges meet in the first round in Beijing on Wednesday, Sep 30
  • Back Djokovic to win outright based on his elite pressure-point metrics and hard-court Elo edge
  • See all of my top Djokovic vs Borges picks and predictions

Nuno Borges takes on Novak Djokovic in the Round of 32 at ATP Beijing on September 30, with first serve scheduled for 7:00 am ET. Sitting just 11th in the world, the 39-year-old Djokovic enters as a sizable favorite. The 29-year-old Borges played him tough in their only previous encounter last season, but still wound up losing in straight sets.

Below, I will break down the best bets for Djokovic vs Borges, plus the latest odds and full H2H history.

Borges vs Djokovic Picks & Predictions

My first pick is Djokovic on the moneyline, which is offered at a market-best 69¢ (equal to -223 odds) at prediction site Polymarket. The biggest question mark is fitness: Djokovic hasn’t played since losing a five-setter to Mariano Navone at the US Open, his third straight loss. He looked physically ill/unwell throughout. But I’m assuming he wouldn’t be here if he wasn’t physically fit, and this is only a best-of-three.

Match Winner Prediction: Novak Djokovic (-223/69¢ at Polymarket)

According to Tennis Abstract‘s surface-specific Elo ratings, Djokovic holds a hard-court hElo of 1997.1 – fourth-best on tour – compared to just 1773.3 for Borges (42nd), a gap of roughly 224 points that implies approximately a 76% win probability in a best-of-three format. His overall Elo of 2047.2 versus Borges’s 1817.1 tells the same story, and while Djokovic’s peak Elo of 2463.2 is a decade behind him, his current hard-court mark still towers over most of the draw. That kind of edge validates his price and then some.

The 52 week stat comparisons from Tennis Ratio reinforce that separation. Djokovic posts a 70.4% break-point save rate versus 65.7% for Borges and converts 39.2% of his break-point opportunities to the Portuguese player’s 36.8%. But the real story is the efficiency gap: Djokovic’s match efficiency rating sits at a towering 2.555 compared to Borges’s 1.070, meaning the Serbian closes out matches at a dramatically higher rate when he gains an advantage.

His dominance efficiency (1.323 vs. 1.073) and breakpoint prevail ratio (1.424 vs. 1.140) confirm Djokovic squeezes more value from every pressure moment. The pressure-point data underscores this: Djokovic wins 66.6% of pressure points on serve and 38.4% on return, while Borges sits at 66.4% and 34.9%, respectively.

That return-side gap is the key – Djokovic generates more stress in the opponent’s service games and finishes the job more reliably.

In their lone prior meeting at the 2025 ATP Athens quarterfinal, Djokovic secured a 7-6, 6-4 victory. He won 78 total points to just 63 for Borges, out-aced Borges (9-7), and successfully fended off both break points he faced while converting one of his five break chances.

That clutch performance under pressure has been a Djokovic signature his entire legendary career, and the 52-week stats indicate it hasn’t waned in his twilight years.

Recent form further supports this pick. Djokovic opened 2026 by reaching the Australian Open semifinal – beating Sinner in five sets before falling to Alcaraz – and pushed through to the quarterfinals at Wimbledon, where Sinner avenged the Melbourne loss in straight sets.

At the US Open he advanced to the Round of 32 before falling to Navone in five grueling sets, a match that confirmed the 39-year-old still competes at an elite level deep into events. His lone soft spot was a second-round exit at Cincinnati against Tirante, but that appears to be an outlier against a run of deep results.

Meanwhile, Borges has been streaky in 2026: he scored quality wins over Andrey Rublev and Francisco Cerundolo at Cincinnati and reached the fourth round at Wimbledon before Sinner stopped him, but he also suffered early exits at Madrid (lost to Navone), Bastad (lost to Darderi), and Montreal (lost to Darderi again).

On hard courts specifically, Borges has managed a mixed bag – a third-round loss to Tien at the US Open, a third-round loss to Nakashima in Cincinnati after the Rublev and Cerundolo scalps, and a loss to Opelka in Miami – and his 44.4% tiebreak win rate this season against Djokovic’s 66.7% is particularly concerning given their Athens match went to a first-set breaker.

Total Games Pick: Over 22.5 (-113/53¢ at Polymarket)

I am backing Over 22.5 games at -113 for this matchup. Their previous encounter featured 23 total games across two tight sets, proving Borges can hold his serve for long stretches. The Portuguese player matched Djokovic with 37 first-serve points won.

Both players’ 52-week serve profiles point toward competitive service games. Borges wins 84.5% of his service games and lands a first-serve accuracy rate of 65.5% with 74.7% of first-serve points won – numbers that trail Djokovic’s 86.3%, 66.9%, and 75.0% only narrowly.

Neither player carries a high double-fault rate (0.18 per game for Djokovic, 0.17 for Borges), and Borges fires aces at a slightly higher clip (0.55 per game vs. 0.52). These margins suggest Borges will hold serve often enough to extend sets.

Borges fired seven aces in Athens and demonstrated excellent baseline resistance, forcing the first set to a tiebreak despite Djokovic winning an exceptional 37 of his 43 first-serve points.

Because both players hold serve efficiently on hard courts and the return-game win rates are modest – 19.9% for Djokovic and 17.5% for Borges – breaks should be scarce, and I expect another extended battle that clears this total.

Head-to-Head History for Borges/Djokovic

Djokovic and Borges have only met once on the ATP Tour, which provides limited predictive value.

Despite the small sample size, their 2025 Athens clash illustrates the narrow margins that dictate betting outcomes against elite competition. Borges matched Djokovic on first-serve points won, securing 37 each, and surrendered just a single break. However, the Serbian star’s ability to save both break points ultimately decided the straight-sets result.

With Djokovic’s break-point save rate sitting at 70.4% over the past 52 weeks and his tiebreak win rate at 66.7%, the GOAT still has the tools and the veteran savvy to advance.
