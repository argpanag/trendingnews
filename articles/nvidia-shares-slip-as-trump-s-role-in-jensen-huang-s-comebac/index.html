  1. Nvidia shares dip as Trump's role in Huang comeback questioned by US politicianspublished at 17:11 GMT
    17:11 GMT

    Jack Grey
    Live reporter

    Shares in US chip giant Nvidia have dipped today after soaring around 32% on yesterday’s news that Jensen Huang was returning as chief executive.

    Huang had resigned from the firm after admitting to an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, but was reinstated on Monday following involvement from US President Trump.

    Nvidia stock price

    • The firm's share price surged following the announcement, closing on Monday at $165.05 compared to Friday's close of $125.53 - adding nearly $1trn to the firm's market value
    • The company's share price is down around 8% today, but remains well above the level it closed at on Friday

    Huang's Trump-backed return

    • Following Huang's return to Nvidia, the US President said he believed it would not have happened without his involvement, telling US media he had "made the deal"
    • Huang was greeted by employees chanting his name on his return to Nvidia HQ, where he told them he would "be a better CEO the second time"

    Political backlash

    • The decision to reinstate Huang, and Trump's involvement, has provoked political backlash from both sides, with Democratic Senator Chuck Schumer saying Trump had put "financial interests" before national security
    • Meanwhile, Republican Senator Pete Rickett said Huang's behaviour "does not support reinstatement" and that his return "doesn’t absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns"

    US Attorney General Todd Blanche says Nvidia is a "good corporate citizen" when asked about investigations into China obtaining advanced chips from the US tech giant.

  5. Polymarket looking into suspicious bets on Huang's Nvidia returnpublished at 15:37 GMT
    15:37 GMT

    Ben King
    Business reporter

    Prediction market Polymarket has identified a small number of suspicious bets made ahead of the surprise return of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO, the BBC understands.

    The site allows users to bet on news events ranging from sports events to election results. It is illegal for corporate or government insiders to use information they learn through work to profit via the platform.

    Polymarket is looking into a small number of Huang-related transactions, the BBC understands, but that so far no connection between those trades and people with access to inside information had been identified.

    A spokesperson for the site says its "propriety surveillance system", Polygraph, helps identify activity that warrants further review and that the firm works "regularly with law enforcement and relevant authorities".

    Analytics company Bubblemaps says it has identified two “suspicious clusters” of Polymarket accounts, which placed bets on Jensen Huang’s return worth around $20,000 before the news broke, and returned a profit of $350,000.

    The accounts only made bets related to Nvidia, Bubblemaps says, although making well-timed trades does not prove insider knowledge.

    A Polymarket spokesman said they were aware of the Bubblemaps report.

  20. Senior Democrat sceptical of Trump's national security assurancespublished at 10:24 GMT
    10:24 GMT

    A senior Democrat on the US House Intelligence Committee has labelled Jensen Huang's reappointment a "risky act".

    Jim Himes tells the BBC's Newsday programme: "It's impossible to imagine that the board of directors of Nvidia had anywhere close to the amount of time to undertake the investigation that they had promised."

    Following Trump's claims he was instrumental in Huang's return, Himes says: "Under no circumstances should a president of the United States be meddling in a corporate board of directors' decision."
