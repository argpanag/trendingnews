Bruce Willis Turned Down 'Lethal Weapon,' Now Ranked 25th Best Action Movie Ever - AOL

TheTools · 03.09.2026 20:50 · source · permalink

Before Bruce Willis became one of the biggest action heroes in film history, he landed a role in the comedy drama series Moonlighting where he starred alongside Cybill Shepherd.

Willis won the Emmy for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Drama Series for his role in the show in 1987, but launched his action hero career just a year later – thanks to Shepherd’s pregnancy with twins Ariel and Cyrus. The Die Hard legend revealed filming for Moonlighting had to be shut down for 11 weeks, giving him time to film his most iconic role as John McClain.

Some of the biggest names in the business were linked to Die Hard, like Western legend Clint Eastwood, Arnold Schwarzenegger and Burt Reynolds. When recounting the names linked to his most well-known role, Willis revealed he read the script for the Mel Gibson-led series Lethal Weapon around the same time, but it was “too violent.”

“I remember right around that time, the script for Lethal Weapon came across my path and my girlfriend at the time read it and said it was way too violent. Thank God I didn’t do that one!” he said at the time.

‘Lethal Weapon’ Named Among the Best Actions Movies of All Time

StudioBinder rankedLethal Weapon as the No. 25 best action movie of all time – behind Willis’ Die Hard, which came in at No. 2 on the list.

“Lethal Weapon changed what action movies were like in the ‘80s and ’90s. The buddy cop dynamic. The greater reliance on humor. Many tropes you see in some of the best action movies ever that now seem cliched were invented or reinvented with Lethal Weapon, and everyone just struggled to keep up,” StudioBinder wrote.

Lethal Weapon owns an impressive 86% from the audience and 82% from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, but Willis’ action flick did one better, receiving a 94% from both critics and audiences alike.

Both spawned successful franchises with Die Hard taking in nearly $1.5 billion while Lethal Weapon racked up over $950 million.
