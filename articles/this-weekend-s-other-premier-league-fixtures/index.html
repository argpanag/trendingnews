Liverpool stuck in the bottom half

We have looked at the top half of the table so it is only right that we bring up the bottom half too.

Liverpool, while unbeaten, are winless so find themselves 13th ahead of today's game.

And look at Tottenham propping up the table! An awful start to the campaign for them and their head coach Roberto De Zerbi.

An opening-day win for Ipswich has them in a position that they would love to stay in all season.

The top half of the table heading into today

It’s foolish to read too much into the Premier League standings at this early point in the season, but here’s a look at which sides make up the top half of the Premier League table after two matches.

Liverpool fans will be frustrated that their team is nowhere to be seen while it has been an excellent return to the top flight for Hull City who are one of just four teams with a pair of victories.

The last time these sides met

Liverpool were twice winners when these teams faced each other in the 2024-25 Premier League campaign.

Those matches finished 2-0, in their season opener at Portman Road, and 4-1 in the reverse fixture five months later.

In the second of those matches, a brace from Cody Gakpo and goals from Mohamed Salah and Dominik Szoboszlai completed the Liverpool rout on their way to the title, while Jacob Greaves got Ipswich’s only goal in the 90th minute.

Ipswich’s last outing

After opening their season with a victory, Ipswich were on the wrong end of a lopsided scoreline against Manchester United last weekend.

Leif Davis gave them an early lead at Old Trafford but a Bruno Fernandes hat-trick, a Jacob Greaves own goal, and a lob from Bryan Mbeumo set up a 5-2 victory for the hosts, with Chuba Akpom adding a consolation in stoppage time.

Victor Munoz — the star Liverpool didn’t know they had?

Signed for £34million from La Liga side Osasuna in June, Victor Munoz has dragged Liverpool out of the mire in his opening two games for the club.

With his relentless, direct approach, Munoz — part of Spain's World Cup-winning squad — feels almost like a footballer custom-made for Andoni Iraola.

Against Newcastle, it was his last-gasp burst after coming off the bench that forced Lewis Hall into a clumsy challenge, winning the penalty that Dominik Szoboszlai converted to rescue a point.

The irrepressible Munoz started against Nottingham Forest but, until his goal, had endured a frustrating afternoon, completing none of his five attempted dribbles.

Those five attempts were more than any other Liverpool player managed in the game, a reflection of his willingness to keep attacking even when things were not quite coming off.

It was that forward thrust which brought the late equaliser against Forest, receiving Florian Wirtz’s threaded pass before producing a magnificent spin to create the space needed to unleash his shot.

It is a long season, but the early signs suggest Liverpool have pulled off a coup in bringing Munoz to England.

Liverpool’s last outing

Liverpool have twice been left chasing games at the start of this season and last week’s 2-2 draw against Nottingham Forest at Anfield underlined how much they have to improve in their own half.

Dan Ndoye opened the scoring for the visitors before Alexander Isak tapped in Cody Gakpo’s pinpoint cross to level on the hour mark.

A penalty conceded by goalkeeper Alisson allowed Morgan Gibbs-White to restore Forest’s lead, but a thundering late strike from new signing Victor Munoz rescued a point for Liverpool.

Today’s venue

With a capacity of just over 30,000, Portman Road is among the Premier League’s smaller stadiums.

Top-flight football made a welcome return here two weeks ago when Jack Clarke scored a late winner to defeat Sunderland 2-1 in Ipswich's opening game of the season.

And the bigger picture is that Ipswich are unbeaten in their past 18 home games, though 17 of those were in the Championship last season.
