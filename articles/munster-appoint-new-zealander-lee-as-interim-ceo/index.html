Munster appoint New Zealander Lee as interim CEO

TheTools · 07.10.2026 11:00 · source · permalink

Getty

New Zealander Avan Lee has been appointed as interim CEO of Munster Rugby, following the resignation of chief executive Ian Flanagan las week.

Flanagan confirmed last Thursday that he would be stepping down from his position at the end of October, following seven years in the role.

Munster have moved swiftly to find a replacement, with Lee set to fill the role on an interim basis, although he is open to applying for the permanent position.

An experienced rugby executive, Lee previously served as CEO of the Hurricanes from 2015 until the end of 2025, with the Wellington franchise winning a Super Rugby title in 2016, while they were also crowned champions just after his departure earlier this year.

"I am honoured to be appointed interim CEO of Munster Rugby," the New Zealander said.

"This is a province with a proud history, passionate supporters and a strong reputation within the game."

And the new interim chief executive admitted he would be keen to stay in the role beyond this season.

"It's 12 months, is the interim, which is whatever you take from that. But 12 months, from my perspective, my attitude is that it's permanent.

"Once I do a good job, I'm sure that will happen. But I'm not coming over to look at a 12-month job.

"I feel like I'm leaving New Zealand for this opportunity," he added.

Lee has previously been involved in Irish rugby. An out-half and full-back in his playing days, he spent seven years living and working in Ireland, playing club rugby with Clontarf, while he was also based in Dublin during his time working as World Rugby’s general manager of Sevens.

He has also had previous roles in New Zealand Rugby and the Northland provincial union.

"While my professional career has taken me around the world, Ireland has been an important part of my own rugby journey. I spent nearly a decade living here, including playing club rugby, and I developed a deep appreciation for the game's place in Irish communities and the unique connection between rugby clubs and their supporters.

"There are important challenges ahead but I firmly believe there is also tremendous opportunity. I am excited to work alongside our players, coaches, staff, volunteers, partners and supporters as we build on Munster Rugby's strengths and shape a successful future.

"My message to everyone connected with the province is simple: we are stronger when we stand together. If we unite behind a shared ambition and a commitment to excellence, I am confident Munster Rugby can achieve great things in the years ahead," Lee said.
