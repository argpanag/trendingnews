Wet, cool weather settling in, with a near miss from a big nor’easter

TheTools · 21.09.2026 18:29 · source · permalink
  • Capital Weather Live

A damp, chilly stretch settles in through midweek, while a potentially powerful late-week nor’easter currently looks likely to pass just to our northeast.

Periods of rain beginning this afternoon signal the start of a damp and somewhat raw few days. Temperatures in the low 70s Monday before the rain started are as warm as we'll see around here until perhaps the weekend.

We could see showers around into late week, but the bulk of the rain should come from this afternoon through at least some portion of Wednesday. It won't rain every moment, but Tuesday could come close to a washout. There could also be a few rumbles of thunder.

Below are selected totals from the most recent model runs. The range on them in DC is from just over half an inch to 3 inches. The wetter models in the group tend to be somewhat more skilled.

  • NAM model: 3 inches (1.75 to 3.25 inches across the area)
  • Canadian: 2.5 inches (1.75 to 3 inches across the area)
  • European: 2 inches (1.5 to 3 inches across the area)
  • European AI: 1.5 inches (1.25 to 1.75 inches across the area)
  • American GFS AI: 1 inch (0.9 to 1.25 inches across the area)
  • American GFS: 0.6 inch (0.5 to 1.5 inches across the area)

With daytime highs perhaps stuck near 60 to the mid-60s if rain is steady enough, a breeze from the east and northeast gusting to 25 mph or so will add a chill. Overnight lows are mainly in the 50s.

Close but no cigar nor'easter?

We've noted this pattern resembles one you'd look for in winter for the development of a coastal low and wintry storm risk. Such a storm does look to come together late week into the weekend, possibly in an unusually strong way. Right now, its rain appears likely to miss us to the northeast.

As close as Northeast Maryland could get some rain from it under current scenarios, with most of the precipitation relegated to New England. The storm is projected to be close enough to us that we should watch for shifts, but won't have much impact on our rain potential beforehand.

This is on the early side for a big coastal low, although El Niño winters do often produce them earlier than usual, and this El Niño is turning into a true monster. The days ahead could be a preview of coming attractions.
