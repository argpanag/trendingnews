Great scope for US-China cooperation on AI, Xi says

The US and China have great scope for cooperation in AI, Chinese leader Xi Jinping said during his meeting with US President Donald Trump in Washington on Thursday, according to a Chinese readout.

The Chinese leader said both countries should play to their strengths and, instead of “guarding against each other,” they should jointly guard against the misuse of AI.

Xi also remarked on the state of US-China relations. He described the exchange of state visits by the two leaders in such a short period of time — Trump visited China in May — as “unprecedented” in the history of the countries’ bilateral relations.

There will be performances by Christopher Macchio, a tenor who has performed at several of President Trump’s functions, and the United States Marine Corps and Army bands and choruses.

Why Taiwan has long been a sticking point in China's relations with the US

When it comes to US-China relations, there are few issues more thorny than the ultimate future of Taiwan.

During his meeting with US President Donald Trump at the White House this morning, Chinese leader Xi Jinping said he hopes the US will oppose Taiwan’s independence, according to Chinese state media.

Here are some key things to remember.

  • Taiwan is a democratic, self-governing island of 23 million residents.
  • Taiwan has its own military, currency, constitution and democratically elected government. It controls its own borders and issues its own passports. But as of 2026, it is only recognized diplomatically by an ever-diminishing number of small nations and is not a member of the UN.
  • China’s ruling Chinese Communist Party (CCP) views Taiwan as part of its territory, despite having never controlled it — and has long vowed to “reunify” the island with the Chinese mainland, by force if necessary.
  • The US formally switched diplomatic recognition from Taipei to Beijing in 1979 — but has long trod a delicate middle path. Under the “One China” policy, the US acknowledges China’s position that Taiwan is part of China but has never officially recognized the Communist Party’s claim to the island.
  • Washington maintains robust unofficial relations with Taiwan and is bound by law (under the Taiwan Relations Act) to provide Taiwan with defensive weapons. But the US has remained intentionally ambiguous on whether it would intervene militarily in the event of a Chinese attack — a policy known as “strategic ambiguity.”
  • Under Xi, China has become increasingly assertive in foreign policy and grown more authoritarian at home. Its crackdown in Hong Kong has further alienated many people in Taiwan, who fear they could face the same fate were they to be under Beijing’s rule.
  • Tensions between Washington and Beijing have pulled Taipei closer into the orbit of the US, especially under several Taiwan presidents from an independence-leaning party. This has infuriated Beijing, spurring it to apply more pressure on Taiwan and sending cross-strait relations on a downward spiral.

Xi told Trump he hopes US will oppose Taiwan independence, Chinese state media reports

Chinese leader Xi Jinping said during a bilateral meeting with US President Donald Trump that he hopes the US will oppose Taiwan independence, according to Chinese state media.

“China hopes that the United States will uphold the correct position of opposing ‘Taiwan independence,’” state news agency Xinhua reported Xi as saying, adding that China hopes the US handles the “Taiwan question with prudence.”

The US has long held the position that it does not support Taiwan independence. Under what is known as the “one China policy,” the US recognizes the People’s Republic as the sole legitimate government of China and it also acknowledges Beijing’s position that Taiwan is part of China. However, the US has never accepted the Chinese Communist Party’s claim of sovereignty over the island.

Trump and Xi also discussed the US-Iran war during the bilateral meeting, according to Xinhua.

Xi said that China would support the US and Iran returning to the 14-point memorandum of understanding reached in June and that he hoped the two countries would resume negotiations as soon as possible. He also said he hoped they would ultimately come to an agreement on the nuclear issue, Xinhua reported.

The US is Taiwan’s most important arms supplier and security guarantor. China’s government has consistently opposed arms sales to the island democracy, which Beijing claims as part of its territory, despite having never controlled it.

Xi still holds an ace in his deck: rare earths

China’s dominance over the processing of rare earth materials remains a powerful card for leader Xi Jinping during ongoing talks at the White House — and one that Beijing has quietly reminded Washington it holds in recent months.

China accounts for roughly 90% of the global processing of rare earths and their magnets, which are essential for everything from F-35 fighter jets and cars to iPhones. And Beijing did not shy away from leveraging that dominance during last year’s trade war between the world’s two largest economies.

At the height of the US-China trade war in April last year, Beijing imposed a licensing regime on seven types of rare earths and their magnets, restricting their exports. It drastically stepped up controls in October 2025 but postponed the measures for a year as part of its trade truce with the US sealed in South Korea. That truce was due to expire, but was extended until January on Wednesday.

But despite Beijing’s commitment to address Washington’s concerns about rare earth exports following the Trump-Xi summit in May, shipments are now falling at the fastest pace in volume terms since the pandemic, according to a research note by Oxford Economics.

“Beijing appears willing to test the limits of the truce. It has deliberately kept license approvals slow and selective,” Nguyen Hoan Nam, the research firm’s China economist, said in the report earlier this month.

The critical minerals are expected to feature prominently again in the Washington talks between Xi and US President Donald Trump.

“Washington could point to the drop in rare earth exports as evidence of China not holding up its end of the deal,” Nguyen said.

A look at Trump’s praise of Xi and other strongmen

US President Donald Trump has often mixed compliments with hard political stances when describing leaders of geopolitical adversaries, including Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

Here’s what he has said about Xi and other authoritarian leaders:

On Xi: Trump has repeatedly referred to the Chinese leader as “a friend.” During his first term, the president described Xi as a “king” and added: “I get along with him great but I must hit him hard about how they have hurt our country.” Despite a friendly public posture, Trump has expressed frustrations over trade tensions with China. In 2025, he wrote on Truth Social that the Chinese leader was “VERY TOUGH, AND EXTREMELY HARD TO MAKE A DEAL WITH!!!”

On Vladimir Putin: Throughout his 2016 presidential campaign, Trump broke from political orthodoxy in his effusive praise of the Russian president. Trump has also often touted his good relationship with Putin, but called him “absolutely CRAZY” in 2025 after major attacks on Ukraine in the ongoing war.

On Kim Jong Un: In August, Trump worked to curry favor with the North Korean leader, including by cutting planned US-South Korea military exercises and posting a photo of their 2019 meeting. He claimed he “gets along” with the North Korean leader. But during his first term, Trump mocked Kim, calling him “Little Rocket Man” and warning the US would “totally destroy North Korea.” He later praised Kim as “very open” and “honorable” ahead of their 2018 summit in Singapore.

Trump welcomes Xi to Oval with military review and flyover

President Donald Trump welcomed Chinese leader Xi Jinping to the Oval Office with a pair of his favorite ceremonial flourishes: A performance by the US Marine Corps band and an aerial flyover.

In a roughly 15-minute military review held in the paved-over White House Rose Garden, Trump and Xi silently watched performances from a marching band dressed in American colonial outfits, the US Marine Corps silent drill platoon and the Marine Corps band.

A group of Chinese and US officials also attended the review, standing along the walkway outside the Oval Office. Those attendees included Vice President JD Vance and second lady Usha Vance, as well as Trump’s daughter, Ivanka, and a handful of Cabinet secretaries.

Trump, Xi and their respective contingents went into the Oval Office afterward to begin their bilateral meeting.

Trump wants to keep US ahead in the AI race. A look at how big of a threat China is

As the world worries about the possibility of unconstrained artificial intelligence wiping out humanity, the leaders of the United States and China have articulated visions for the era-defining technology that come from almost opposite sides of the spectrum.

Brushing aside the calls from AI scientists and executives from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google to slow the development, US President Donald Trump said earlier this month that what was most important was keeping the US in the lead against China in the intensifying AI superpower race.

China’s leader Xi Jinping later made his own call for more international cooperation. Countries should “encourage open source, openness, collaboration and sharing,” he said in a speech in India to Global South leaders, in which he reiterated a proposal for a global AI governance pact – although he did not give details on what that framework would look like.

The juxtaposition of Trump and Xi’s messages underscores the lack of consensus and a deep trust deficit between the two superpowers in the high-stakes race to dominate AI.

The US appears to be increasingly weaponizing its dominance in the sector, seeing it as a strategic asset to be protected, and exported to friendly countries, while China, lagging in the race, is proposing a more open model.

AI will loom large at the Trump-Xi summit in Washington. At their May meeting in Beijing, the two discussed AI guardrails but produced limited results, and last week the Chinese Ministry of Commerce said the two leaders had agreed to set up an intergovernmental dialogue on AI. But with their broader geopolitical rivalry, experts have low expectations for any meaningful breakthrough.

China will send 2 pandas to a US zoo in coming days, Xi says

During his remarks at the White House, Chinese leader Xi Jinping called the giant panda a historic “envoy of friendship” between China and the US and said that two will arrive at a zoo in Atlanta in the coming days.

Zoo Atlanta announced earlier this year that the pandas, named Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, would be coming to the city. Fu Shuang means “double blessing” and Ping Ping translates to “peace peace,” according to Chinese media.

More background: Beijing’s panda diplomacy with Washington kicked off in 1972, following President Richard Nixon’s historic visit to China. Chairman Mao Zedong’s government gave two giant pandas to the United States in a sign of warming bilateral ties after decades of diplomatic deep freeze.

Agreements between China and the US zoos stipulate Beijing owns the pandas and any of their offspring, and they require payment “for the conservation of giant pandas in China.”

China and US have responsibility to "manage AI for good," Xi says

Chinese President Xi Jinping stressed that both China and the US are leading nations in artificial intelligence, and have the responsibility to ensure it is a force for good, as he spoke alongside President Donald Trump.

“Both China and the United States are leading nations in artificial intelligence,” Xi said in comments made in the White House Grand Foyer.

“We have both the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good, and ensure that the development of AI is always under human control and serves the wellbeing of the people.”

Xi also spoke of China and the US both shouldering a “historic responsibility” of advancing human development and progress.

Trump and Xi are expected to discuss AI as part of their talks, with Trump writing on social media earlier today that AI “will be a big topic of discussion, but I want to leave it exactly where it is.”

Xi says he's in DC to promote China-US friendship and cooperation

Chinese President Xi Jinping said his first trip to the US in over a decade is aimed at promoting friendship and expanding cooperation between the two nations.

“Facing the changes in our world, in our times, and of historical significance today, China and United States, as two major countries, shoulder a historic responsibility of advancing human development and progress,” he continued.

Xi added that he stands ready to work with US President Donald Trump to “steer the giant ship of China-US relationship on a steady course toward the future.”

“The interests of China and the United States are deeply intertwined, and there is plenty of room for us to work together. China-US cooperation may not solve every problem in the world, but without our cooperation, it would be hard to solve many of the world’s problems,” the Chinese leader said.

Who is Peng Liyuan, China's first lady?

Peng Liyuan — the wife of Chinese leader Xi Jinping, who sat alongside US first lady Melania Trump during introductory remarks this morning — is a famous soprano with the People’s Liberation Army and a Chinese folk music singer.

A native of China’s eastern Shandong province, Peng started receiving professional vocal training at the age of 16 and joined a military performing art troupe when she was 20.

Peng, 63, has been married to Xi since 1987. They knew on their first date that they were meant for each other, according to an interview Peng did with Chinese state media.

Peng has regularly joined Xi for his overseas tours since he assumed the office over a decade ago. Her often bold fashion choices contrast starkly with the more subdued clothing of her predecessors while her active role in health and welfare has become part of package of the soft power Beijing wants to project abroad.

Xi and Peng last made trip to the US together in 2015 during the Barack Obama administration. She joined Xi in hosting Donald Trump and his wife Melania at the Forbidden City in November 2017 when Trump visited China for the first time.

Trump touts forthcoming discussions of AI, trade with China as a way to promote peace

President Donald Trump on Thursday, standing at a podium in the Grand Foyer of the White House with Chinese leader Xi Jinping flanking him, said the two will discuss the future of artificial intelligence during Xi’s state visit, portraying AI as a way to promote peace “for decades to come.”

“During this visit, we will also discuss pressing issues concerning security, technology, and superintelligence— SI,” Trump declared, referencing a term for AI that he argues is less threatening. “The decisions we make in these areas today can promote peace and prosperity for decades and decades to come.”

Trump also touted new trade progress between the US and China, saying that he and Xi “have made tremendous strides on the issues facing our two countries.”

Trump referenced his trip to Beijing in May and the creation of a new “Board of Trade,” calling it “a brand new mechanism and something that’s been unbelievably successful.”

The so-called Board of Trade was introduced earlier this year as a way to advance trade of nonsensitive goods between the US and China.

“Our teams have been working to encourage a more balanced trading relationship, including the new market access for American farmers and ranchers,” Trump said.

Trump and Xi to visit the National Archives on Friday

President Donald Trump said Thursday that Chinese leader Xi Jinping will accompany him to the National Archives on Friday, where they will view historical documents including the Declaration of Independence and the US Constitution.

“President Xi and Madam Peng arrive at an important moment for America: the 250th anniversary of our founding,” he said in a speech at the White House. “Tomorrow, we will visit the National Archives to see some of the greatest treasures of that proud history.”

Trump added that the group will also see artifacts of past cultural exchanges between China and the US, calling them a testimony to “the long and cherished relationship of really something that’s so incredible.”

“Just as our ancestors reached across the ocean centuries ago to trade, to learn and to build a better world, our two countries do really the same thing, and we’re very proud of it and the relationship,” he said.
