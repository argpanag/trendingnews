Luis Hernandez Aims for Historic UFC Run in Three Divisions

TheTools · 27.09.2026 01:11 · source · permalink

Cop turned UFC fighter Luis Hernandez wants to make history in the UFC by becoming the first person to compete in 3 different weight classes in the shortest time.

Most fighters in the UFC take months to recover and prepare between bouts, carefully managing their weight cuts within a single division. Then there is Luis Hernandez, a Miami-Dade police deputy turned UFC contender who plans on flipping the conventional octagon playbook upside down.

The Florida native is targeting a streak that no combat sports athlete has ever accomplished: fighting in three different weight divisions in just three months, with an ultimate goal of eventually dropping down to lightweight.

Hernandez is kicking off this ambitious experiment at middleweight, where he takes on Sedriques Dumas at UFC Vegas 121. While stepping in to face Dumas on short notice is a tall task in itself, Hernandez views the 185-pound matchup as merely step one of a broader, record-breaking roadmap.

"Nobody has done 3 fights in 3 different weight classes in 3 months," Hernandez stated ahead of his bout. "I’m gonna handle Dumas and come back at 170. We had Kevin Holland; now we have Luis Hernandez."

Following in the Footsteps of Active Octagon Legends

Referencing Kevin Holland is fitting, given how the fan-favorite built his reputation by taking on all comers on short notice across middleweight and welterweight. However, Hernandez aims to take that wild activity rate a step further by carving out an unprecedented path across middleweight, welterweight, and ultimately lightweight within a 90-day window.

Jumping between weight classes presents brutal logistical and physiological challenges. Dropping down to 185, 170, and eventually 155 pounds requires strict body composition control and rapid recovery cycles that leave virtually no room for error or lingering injuries.

While critics might view the plan as overly reckless, Hernandez's background in law enforcement and combat sports gives him the work ethic to back up the bold claims. If he delivers on his promise against Dumas and makes a quick turnaround at 170 pounds, MMA fans could be watching one of the most remarkable operational streaks in recent octagon history.

Allan Binoy is a combat sports journalist and Staff Writer at MMA Sucka.
