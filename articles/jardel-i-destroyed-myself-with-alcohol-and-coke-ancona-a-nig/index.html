Jardel: "I destroyed myself with alcohol and coke. Ancona? A nightmare. I even applauded the wrong end..."

TheTools · 13.11.2026 05:48 · source · permalink

interview

The former Brazilian forward reflects: "I scored more than anyone, I can't explain Scolari's decision to leave me out of the 2002 World Cup squad. Now I work as an entrepreneur in Fortaleza"

  • Italiano
  • Deutsch

Say the name Mario Jardel and you think of the purest form of the word goal. A Brazilian without too much 'alegria', but with an almost obsessive talent for the only thing that truly interested him: scoring. And plenty of it. For the most nostalgic, he was the top centre-forward of the late nineties and early 2000s, Milan's bogeyman in the Champions League ("Berlusconi wanted me"), with a lifelong dream: to play at a World Cup. Spoiler: he never would. "Even today I can't explain why Scolari didn't call me up for Brazil in 2002. I was among the five best centre-forwards in the world. From that moment on, my life was over: alcohol, depression, cocaine. I almost died from an overdose". The rise and fall of "SuperMario", in the end, unfolded entirely across six years, from '96 to the fateful year of 2002, before a freefall that saw him wander through eight different countries. Today he is a man at peace with himself. And thanks to his wife Sandra, he managed to banish the demons that had swallowed him into a black hole at the age of thirty. In January 2004 he also came close to Italy: Jorge Mendes brought him to the famous Ancona side of 46 players, one of the most surreal teams in Serie A, destined for bankruptcy a few months later ("They didn't even pay my wages"). At 53, Jardel lives in Fortaleza, works as an entrepreneur, and has part of his family in Bologna.

Mario, what do you invest in? 

"Shops. In Fortaleza the property market is very popular and it's a good way to make money. In my free time I enjoy the sun on the beach, although I miss Italy". 

Do you have ties to our country? 

"I have two uncles who have lived in Bologna for over twenty years. Then there's the climate and the food, wow. When I was at Ancona I used to eat until I dropped". 

Misfortune meant you ended up at Ancona in 2003-2004, one of the worst teams in Serie A history with 13 points from 34 matches. What was that experience like?

"I only have bad memories. In six months I didn't receive my wages and I played barely three matches. The squad was completely packed with players, poorly run, and at the end of the year it went bankrupt. I got on well with Sonetti and Galeone, much less so with the fans". 

Why was that? 

"I was 31 years old, I was overweight and someone called me 'Lardel'. Probably because when they unveiled me against Perugia I applauded the wrong end: we had the same colours. Some fans took offence after that gesture".

Who brought you to Italy? 

"Jorge Mendes. He wasn't my agent, but a dear friend. He had contacts in Italy, he put my name forward to some agents and somehow they brought me to Ancona. I was looking for a club where I could play regularly and recover after the nightmare of missing out on the 2002 World Cup squad, but mentally I never recovered". 

Let's look into that. Why didn't Scolari call you up for that World Cup? 

"I'm still asking myself that today. Nobody has ever given me an explanation. Between the late nineties and the early 2000s I was among the five best forwards in the world: at Porto I scored 168 goals in 175 matches in four years, while in the season leading up to the World Cup in Korea and Japan I scored 55 in 42 matches with Sporting Lisbon, winning my second Golden Shoe. I understand that Brazil team had phenomena, but I deserved a call-up". 

How heavily did that disappointment weigh on your life? 

"I fell into depression and started drinking excessively. Playing in a World Cup was the dream of my life. My first wife left me and there were issues regarding the custody of our children. I felt everything was slipping away from me, but the worst was yet to come". 

Go on. 

"In the summer of 2003 I tried cocaine. I was on holiday and I knew I could take it during that period because there were no anti-doping controls. I liked it; it made me feel alive and not depressed. Once I stayed awake for an entire week. Then I had an overdose, I almost died, and a friend who was with me saved my life. That's when I hit rock bottom".

How did you come through it? 

"I spent months talking to psychiatrists. I came through it for good when I met my second wife Sandra: she made me understand the meaning of life, and today I'm happy". 

What do your children think of your story? 

"They are proud of me. Not of what I did, but of how I came through it. I often talk to them about the importance of asking family for help when needed, believing in God, and having plenty of willpower". 

Back to the pitch. Did you ever have the chance to sign for big clubs? 

"I came close to Barcelona and Milan". 

Speaking of Milan, they were one of your favourite victims in the Champions League… 

"Between Galatasaray and Porto I scored four goals in five matches against the Rossoneri. Someone told me that Berlusconi wanted to bring me to Milan because he had a soft spot for goalscorers. That was the period when they called me 'SuperMario'. I didn't sign because of some bureaucratic issues that I was never informed about". 

Who does Jardel see himself in within today's football? 

"Haaland". 

How is Ancelotti viewed in Brazil? 

"Great coach. He hasn't brought out the best in the team yet; he needs time. Many criticise his style of play for not being attacking enough, but currently this Brazil side is much less strong compared to previous ones. New talents are needed". 

Last one. What would you say to the Mario of 25 years ago? 

"To use his head and rely less on instinct".
