Balloon pilot undertakes 'Up'-style stunt with 94 helium balloons

TheTools · 03.10.2026 00:33 · source · permalink

ALBUQUERQUE, N.M. – A pilot embarked on a one-shot Saturday morning flight with 94 helium balloons, no burner and no set destination.

At Balloon Fiesta Park, Jonathan Trappe spent eight months planning the launch. His team had 30 volunteers arrive at 2 a.m. to help inflate the balloons.

Before the balloons lifted, Trappe said the launch takes a lot of helium and preparation on the field.

“It is a high stakes moment. it’s very exciting, but it’s also very serious because we’re talking about manned flight under a cluster of helium-filled balloons,” said Jonathan Trappe.

Trappe said he will use one balloon less than the first gas-cluster flight in 1937.

Unlike a traditional hot air balloon, this flight has no burner to control altitude. Trappe said he manages the flight with ballast, using one bag at a time.

“If we’re climbing and we don’t want to climb, we can arrest a climb by popping a cell, that’ll get us descending,” said Trappe. “If we’re descending, we don’t want to come down, you drop ballast, sand or water.”

Trappe said sand matters so much that his crew has thrown other items overboard to save it for later in a flight.

“It’s an interesting sport where sand becomes precious and food is expendable. We’ve literally thrown over food to keep the sand, because the sand’s a little bit easier at landing,” said Trappe.

It’s unknown at the moment where Trappe will land.

“That’s part of the beauty of this flight is it’s destinations unknown. we don’t know where we’re going to land until we’re in flight and we choose a safe place to land,” said Trappe.

Trappe said his team gave itself the full Balloon Fiesta window to wait for safe conditions.

Trappe took off just before 9 a.m. on Saturday morning.
