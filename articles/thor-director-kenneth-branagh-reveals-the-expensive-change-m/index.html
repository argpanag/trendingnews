  • Kenneth Branagh revealed that Marvel considered changing the visual presentation of Thor by removing Dutch angles due to cost concerns.
  • The director intentionally chose Dutch angles to give Thor a heightened visual style that reflected the fantastical world and comic-book origins of the story.
  • Branagh's original visual identity for Thor, including the distinctive canted shots, remained intact in the 2011 film, starring Chris Hemsworth and Tom Hiddleston.

Kenneth Branagh has revealed that Marvel nearly altered the visual presentation of Thor. The 2011 MCU film is known for its frequent use of Dutch angles, which give the shots a noticeable tilt. Marvel considered "horizontalizing" the footage, but ultimately dropped the plan because of the cost.

Branagh, who directed Thor, recently discussed the conversation on the On Film…With Kevin McCarthy podcast. "I know there was a conversation at some point in some part of the Marvel universe where they had a conversation about, 'What would it cost to horizontalize this movie?'" the director recalled. He explained that the "cash price for that process was so prohibitive," so the studio left the shots intact.

The Dutch angles were an intentional choice. The director wanted Thor to have a heightened visual style that reflected the story's fantastical world and comic-book origins. Branagh described the approach as being about "visual impact." He said, "It was just what would hit you in a world, in a story about gods."

The decision allowed Thor to retain Branagh's original visual identity when it was released in 2011. The film starred Chris Hemsworth as Thor and Tom Hiddleston as Loki, with the characters going on to become major figures in the Marvel films. The canted shots remain among the most distinctive visual choices of the early MCU.

Kenneth Branagh & Ryan Reynolds team up for Apple TV's Mayday

Branagh is currently starring opposite Ryan Reynolds in Mayday, Apple's new action comedy. Directed by John Francis Daley and Jonathan Goldstein, the film marks the actors' first time working together. Reynolds plays U.S. Navy pilot Lieutenant Troy 'Assassin' Kelly, while Branagh portrays Nikolai Ustinov, a gruff ex-KGB agent.

Set during the Cold War, Mayday follows Troy after a top-secret mission in Russian territory goes wrong and leaves him stranded behind enemy lines. Nikolai discovers him, leading the unlikely pair to work together to help Troy escape.

Mayday began streaming on Apple TV on September 4, 2026.
