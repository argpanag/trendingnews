Bates dedicates historic Bathurst lap to late mum after stunning qualifying

Zach Bates was the fastest during Friday's qualifying session at Mount Panorama. (Getty Images: Daniel Kalisz)

In short:

Zach Bates has set the fastest ever lap time at Bathurst in the current era of Supercars, topping Friday's qualifying session at Mount Panorama.

Two entrants missed out on qualifying after damage to their cars from crashes earlier on Friday could not be fixed in time for the session.

What's next?

ABC Sport will have a live blog of the Bathurst 1000 from lights out to the chequered flag on Sunday, October 11.

Zach Bates has dedicated his historic Bathurst lap to his late mother after becoming the first driver in the current era to break the 2:04 barrier at Mount Panorama.

The 22-year-old from Canberra was a class above the field during Friday's qualifying session, topping the time sheets in his Matt Stone Racing Chevrolet with a 2:03.938, the first and only driver to break the 2:04 barrier in the Gen3 era which began in 2023.

Bates, who was Craig Lowndes's co-driver in the Team 18 entry last year, will head into tomorrow's shootout as the favourite and will be the last driver on track.

Bates dedicated the moment to his mother, who died in 2025.

"I can't believe it; I am speechless," he said.

"Whole credit to the crew behind me. The boys and girls have been working so hard.

"This is for my mum who passed last year. We've had a pretty average year, the whole family. So this is definitely a highlight."

The top 10 from Friday's qualifying session will battle on Saturday for pole position in the Top 10 Shootout. 

Brodie Kostecki will have a chance to set a new Bathurst record tomorrow in the shootout, striving to become the first driver to secure four consecutive pole positions at Mount Panorama. 

Two cars miss qualifying after crashes in practice

Ryan Wood crashes during the third practice session of the 2026 Bathurst 1000 at Mount Panorama. (Supplied: Supercars)

Ryan Wood/Jaxon Evans (number 2 Walkinshaw TWG Racing, Toyota) and Rylan Gray/Tony D'Alberto (Dick Johnson Racing, Ford) will start from the back of the field on Sunday after missing out on Friday's qualifying session.

Both entrants suffered crashes during practice earlier on Friday, with the damage unable to be fixed in time for the following practice session or qualifying.

Wood clipped the inside wall on the entry to Forrest's Elbow, which sent his Toyota into the outside barrier with force.

The force of the impact was reportedly recorded at 42 G-forces, with the Kiwi later cleared by medics at Bathurst.

"The boys will work pretty hard and that's what I feel stink about. It's given them so much work that they really didn't need," he said.

"Kudos to our team — they do everything by the book. To have a 42G impact like that and be able to walk away is pretty good."

Wood qualified fourth for last year's Bathurst 1000, but watched from the sidelines on Friday. 

D'Alberto crashed in the final minutes of the session at The Dipper, with his number 38 Dick Johnson Racing entry taken back to the garage on the back of a tow truck.
