Storm begin hunt for Bellamy’s successor as retirement confirmed

TheTools · 22.11.2026 03:11 · source · permalink
  • Sport
  • NRL
  • NRL

November 22, 2026 - 3.11am

Storm coach Craig Bellamy will step into retirement at the end of the 2027 season.

There was widespread speculation around when Bellamy would call time on his career with the Storm, as he prepares about to enter his 25th year with the team.

Craig Bellamy will call time on his coaching career at the end of next season. Getty Images

Bellamy had long said he would stay in top job until he felt he could no longer have a positive impact; thus he began to consider what his future would look like.

With five premierships – two of which were stripped – under his belt, the 67-year-old felt the best time to walk away would be at the end of next season.

In a statement released by the Storm, Bellamy made clear he is still determined “to make the most of the opportunity” he has to win another premiership.

“I still have huge passion for the job and I’m excited about what we can achieve together in 2027,” he said.

“While 2027 will be my final season as senior coach, I’m determined to make the most of the opportunity and give everything I can to our club and our players.

“We’ve got a great group of players coming through, along with an outstanding coaching and performance team. I’m looking forward to continuing to work with them, helping develop our players and making sure we keep improving as a club.”

The Storm confirmed the club will host a series of events in 2027 to celebrate the incredible coaching career and legacy Bellamy leaves behind.

“Craig is one of the greatest coaches in the history of rugby league, and we’re proud of everything he has achieved in the game,” chairman Matt Tripp added.

“While we would obviously love to have Craig coaching our team forever, we understand how significant this decision is and are incredibly grateful that we will have another season with him leading our club.

“His record speaks for itself, but what continues to impress us is the passion, energy, and commitment he brings to the role every single day.”

The retirement announcement comes less than eight months after Bellamy announced he had been diagnosed with a neurodegenerative disorder.

He is one of four coaches in the history of the NRL to surpass 600 games in charge of a first-grade team. Bellamy also holds the record for most consecutive games coached at a single club as well as the best win-loss record of any current coach.

The Storm are set to begin the process of appointing Bellamy’s successor soon.
