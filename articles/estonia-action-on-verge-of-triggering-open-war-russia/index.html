Estonia action on 'verge of triggering open war' - Russia

TheTools · 23.11.2026 16:27 · source · permalink

Getty

Russia's Foreign Ministry accused Estonia, a NATO and European Union member state, of a range of hostile actions "on the ⁠verge of triggering open war", ratcheting up its rhetoric after Estonia announced the closure of their land border.

Estonia plans to close border crossings with Russia from 1 December for at least six months after accusing Russian security services of staging an attempted arson attack on an Estonian defence company.

Russia denied involvement in the attack.

The Russian ministry set out its accusations in a statement after summoning Estonia's charge d'affaires in Moscow to issue a formal protest over Tallinn's decision to close the border.

"The Estonian diplomat was once again told ‌that such provocative and extremely dangerous ⁠actions are on the verge of triggering open war with Russia," the Russian ministry said.

Among a list of accusations, it said Estonia had allowed Ukraine to use its airspace "on the quiet" to attack Russian targets.

It also accused Estonia of trying to obstruct the movement of Russian cargo ships in the Baltic Sea, banning the transit of Russian ‌and Belarusian grain, seeking a ban on the Russian language and threatening to close the Russian Orthodox Church.

"The Russian side reserves the right to take ⁠comprehensive political and diplomatic measures aimed at protecting Russia's national interests and the legitimate rights of ‌its citizens," the ministry said.

In a statement, Estonia's Foreign Ministry did not respond to ⁠all of ‌the allegations but denied that it allows its territory to be used for Ukrainian military operations.

"While we generally do not comment on or engage with Russian propaganda narratives, in this case we can reiterate that Estonia has never allowed, and ⁠will never allow, its territory to be used for military operations by another country," it said.

"Other countries ⁠in our region have also made this position clear," the ministry said.

Russia has long accused Estonia and other NATO states in the Baltic region of allowing Ukrainian drones to use their airspace.

Ukrainian drones have frequently entered NATO airspace, which Kyiv says is a result of Russian signal jamming.

NATO countries have repeatedly denied intentionally allowing drones to use their airspace, and blamed Russia for the incursions, ‌for having launched the war with Ukraine in 2022.

Finland summons Russian envoy, condemns attacks in Ukraine

Meanwhile, Finland said it had summoned the Russian ambassador, days after a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating Finnish airspace, and condemned Moscow's strikes on civilian targets in Ukraine.

"We strongly condemn Russia's illegal war of aggression and its escalating strikes against civilian targets in Ukraine. We stand in solidarity with Denmark, Poland and our other European partners," the foreign ministry said on X.

It added that Russia's "dangerous and reckless actions in Europe" were "unacceptable".

The move comes after a Russian cargo ship, identified as the Sparta II, was hit by a Ukrainian drone attack in international waters in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, causing a fire and forcing the evacuation of the crew, one of whom suffered minor injuries.

Just hours after the Sparta II was hit, a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating the Nordic country's airspace.

Finnish authorities, with help from Estonian and Russian rescue vessels, put out the fire as the vessel had drifted into Finnish waters and was anchored about 90 kilometres east of the capital Helsinki.

It was towed from Finnish waters to Russia yesterday evening.

"Ukraine has the right to defend itself against Russia's illegal aggression," the foreign ministry added in its statement.
