After saying that his deportation policy was “too tough” and offering to strike an immigration deal with Democrats in an interview on 60 Minutes broadcast yesterday, Donald Trump today said “we’re not going to have amnesty.”

“Somebody suggested, will there be amnesty? No, but— they came in. They knew the law,” Trump told reporters during a press conference today. He said his administration’s deportation policy was “very strong” and that he had not spoken with incoming chief of staff Stephen Miller about any changes.

“My policy hasn’t changed,” he said.

The Supreme Court will allow the Trump administration to resume border wall construction in the Big Bend region of Texas. The majority paused a lower court’s decision as the case plays out. The Trump administration had filed an emergency appeal, saying there are illegal border crossings and drug seizures in the region.

“This horrific decision clears the way for contractors to bulldoze pristine desert and dynamite canyon walls before the courts have even decided whether this destruction is legal,” Laiken Jordahl, national public lands advocate at the Center for Biological Diversity, one of the groups that sued to stop the construction, told the Associated Press.

The man accused of assassinating Charlie Kirk will stand trial in June 2028, a Utah judge ruled today.

District Judge Tony Graf allocated 105 days for the trial, with jury selection slated to begin on 21 June 2028. Graf said the 2028 date balances the defense’s right to prepare with the Kirk family’s desire for a speedy resolution.

Attorneys for defendant Tyler Robinson, 23, had proposed a trial starting in 2029. Robinson could be eligible for the death penalty if convicted.

In a statement, the Kirk family called the hearing date a “delay” and said that “every additional day without justice prolongs our family’s pain.”

Josh Hawley, the Republican senator from Missouri, called for “a time of fasting and prayer for the nation” after Republicans’ losses in the midterms.

In a social media post, Hawley wrote, “What happened last Tuesday was shocking and historic. It calls for a historic response. Christians need to gather and seek the Lord. I’m going to host a time of fasting and prayer for the nation on Tuesday, December 15 from 12 to 1 at the Capitol. Meet me there!”

During his press conference today, Donald Trump alternately claimed that the Iran war has already been won and is going to be solved soon.

Speaking about his recent meeting with the prime minister of Qatar, Trump told reporters he believes the war “militarily is effectively over.”

Later he added that Pete Hegseth is “winning” the war and that there is “positive momentum” toward resolving the conflict.

Elizabeth Warren, the Democratic senator from Massachusetts, and Jim Banks, the Republican senator from Indiana, who sit on the Senate Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee released a statement today into NVIDIA’s continued export of AI chips.

“In March, bipartisan members of Congress urged the Department of Commerce to suspend or reconsider NVIDIA’s export licenses to China and conduct a full national security review. Since then, NVIDIA has reportedly received approvals to export hundreds of thousands of advanced AI chips to China, including to companies tied to China’s military, all while attempting to influence the debate over chip controls in Washington, D.C.

“NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang’s confirmation of a relationship with a suspected spy for China’s intelligence service makes these national security concerns even more urgent and raises important questions NVIDIA must answer about the extent to which these activities were related.”

They added that Congress should pass the AI Overwatch Act and called on the commerce department to “close export control loopholes and ensure that advanced AI chips are not going to China’s military.”

Trump defends Hegseth, bashes Massie

Donald Trump defended defense secretary Pete Hegseth, who is facing articles of impeachment introduced by Republican senator Thomas Massie, saying Hegseth “won two wars”.

“He won the war in Venezuela,” Trump said of Hegseth, adding that the United States’ military raid to capture former president Nicolás Maduro “was flawless”.

“He’s winning the war in Iran,” Trump added.

Meanwhile, the president called Massie “a stone cold loser” and “sick”.

Donald Trump said he spoke to Jensen Huang, the Nvidia CEO and founder, today “just to see if he’s feeling all right.”

The president added Huang denied knowing Alice Huang was a suspected Chinese government spy. “I really believe him,” Trump said.

Donald Trump was joined by Dana White, the CEO of UFC, at his press conference today.

“He loves the idea and I love the idea, the only thing is we have other things we have to think about,” Trump said, of his remarks – which he later called a joke – over the weekend that White should be his next press secretary.

“I have zero political aspirations,” said White, who briefly took the podium and answered reporters’ questions.

Donald Trump said that he had an “excellent conversation” earlier today with the prime minister of Qatar, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, during a press conference.

Trump read a statement similar to an earlier social media post, where he wrote that “Most importantly, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, and others, would prefer the status quo, in other words, a continuation of the Greatest Naval Blockade in History, and the new Operation Iron Gate — Therefore, Land and Sea.”

“Israel and the entire Middle East are protected from Iranian danger and threats,” Trump said. “And we’re seeing momentum toward the end of this conflict. I think it’s going to be ending fairly soon.”

Marco Rubio, the secretary of state, defended Pete Hegseth in comments to reporters after Thomas Massie introduced articles of impeachment against Hegseth.

“I think he’s done a great job. I’ve worked with him every single day on many of these topics, I’ve seen the work he does, the professionalism that he brings to it,” Rubio said.

He added, “I think it’s unfair” that a Republican introduced the articles of impeachment.

A press conference by US attorney general Todd Blanche interrupted efforts to count ballots in Jackson, Mississippi. The press conference comes as the state of Mississippi is in embroiled in a tight Senate race with a razor thin margin. Democrat Scott Colom leads incumbent Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith – if he wins, Colom will be the first Democratic senator Mississippi has sent to the Senate in decades and the first Black senator the state has sent since the Reconstruction era.

Dozens of police officers idled outside buildings in downtown Jackson while Blanche spoke inside the Jackson City Hall. Elsewhere, officers hovered on rooftops, patrolling local streets and circling in police cars. Many streets around the Hinds County Circuit Clerk office, where votes are still being counted, are blocked off. Officers outside the Jackson City Hall would not let the two dozen people who waited outside the building in

Across the street, dozens of protesters chanted, “Don’t interrupt our vote!”

Ahead of the start time, officers refused to let dozens of people who waited outside the butte Jackson City Hall in, saying that the building was at capacity.

Blanche is ostensibly in town to announce that Jackson has been selected as one of four jurisdictions to federal funding through the Trump administration’s Model Cities Initiative, a controversial program that other southern cities, including New Orleans and Charlotte rejected due to concerns around civil rights violations.

Thomas Massie floated Florida governor Ron DeSantis as a potential replacement for defense secretary Pete Hegseth during a press conference on the eight articles of impeachment he introduced against Hegseth today.

“I will name a name because I suspect he doesn’t want the job,” Massie said, after initially deferring to name a contender because “if I named a name, they would probably never put that person in there.”

In a press conference full of sharp remarks, Massie said he was advocating for the impeachment in his final weeks in Congress because “the time for waiting around and being polite and introducing bills and waiting six years to try to get them inside of some other bill, that time is over for me.”

“I am trying to force a vote here on this issue because I think it’s at the heart and soul of our republic, who has the war powers,” he added.

Here's a recap of the day so far

  • Republican Congressman Thomas Massie has introduced eight articles of impeachment against Pete Hegseth, refiling a resolution in the hope of ousting the defense secretary. The House has two days to take it up. This could result in a vote on the floor, a formal investigation or the measure could be tabled. All require a simple majority (218 votes) in the House.

  • Massie, the outgoing representative from Kentucky, accused Hegseth of illegally waging war in Iran without congressional authorization and abusing his powers. The articles also focuses on Hegseth’s move to strike several boats in the Caribbean that have killed over 200 people throughout the campaign. The resolution also points to the defense secretary’s targeted Democratic Senator Mark Kelly, a former Navy captain, for telling troops to resist unlawful orders. Hegseth formally censured Kelly, accused him of seditious statements, and moved to reduce his retired military rank and pension. A federal judge later blocked the Pentagon’s effort.

  • While Democrats are expected to back the impeachment effort, some Republicans have also expressed some support, even though they hold a narrow majority. A reminder that for the removal of an impeached official, there needs to be 67 votes in the upper chamber. Speaking to reporters, Massie insisted that his articles of impeachment are “not symbolic”. “He is not doing a good job. In addition to breaking the law and violating the constitution, he’s not doing a good job,” Massie said. “If he stays in power, and Republicans reject this – what message do you think he sends to the American public after last week’s election?”

  • Democratic Senator Chuck Schumer responded to Trump’s statement, saying that the president’s “return to election denialism was inevitable”. Jeffries dismisses Stephen Miller as a 'stone cold racist', rejects possibility of working together in new Congress

Could Hakeem Jeffries, as the likely next speaker of the House, work with Stephen Miller, the architect of Donald Trump’s mass deportation campaign who last week named White House chief of staff?

“No”, Jeffries replied, when asked about the possibility of collaboration at his press conference today.

“Stephen Miller is a stone cold racist. His track record speaks for itself, and I’ve got zero interest in working with that kind of individual,” Jeffries added.

It’s not the first time the top House Democrat has condemned Miller, but this language is Jeffries’s strongest yet. Needless to say, this does not bode well for the possibility of bipartisan collaboration in the next Congress on issues like immigration reform.
