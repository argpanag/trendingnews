'Positive conversations' on Drumcree stand-off - Bryant

TheTools · 27.09.2026 23:01 · source · permalink

The Northern Ireland Secretary Chris Bryant said he was grateful for "all of the positive conversations" on the stand-off in Drumcree.

Mr Bryant said "constructive dialogue" is the "route to a solution".

"I continue to hope and work for progress in further talks tomorrow," he added.

Mr Bryant held crunch talks today aimed at resolving deepening tensions around the contentious loyal parade in Portadown.

He described the meetings with the Orange Order, nationalist residents of Garvaghy Road, and political parties as "very positive" and said he was "exploring every option to see whether there's a solution".

Earlier, Mr Bryant told reporters at Hillsborough: "It’s been positive so far today and sometimes people showing a bit of ankle, and I think an awful lot of people of good faith wanting to get to a good outcome.

"It’s not quite clear what that will look like yet, but kind of, a bit through the mist, I can see maybe what that might look like if I can persuade everybody to sign up."

The Orange Order described its meeting with Mr Bryant as "very constructive".

Asked whether the Parades Commission's determination last Friday that the Drumcree parade could go down the Garvaghy Road was still live, Portadown District Master Nigel Dawson said: "I spoke to the chief constable this morning shortly after seven o’clock, and the determination is still a live document."

However, later asked if he believed the determination around the parade was still live, the Northern Ireland Secretary said he could not answer the question as he was "not a lawyer".

Meanwhile, the Garvaghy Road Residents’ Coalition told Mr Bryant that it would be open to a mediation process to resolve the parade dispute.

Speaking alongside solicitor Darragh Mackin, Breandán Mac Cionnaith from the coalition said he wanted an international mediator to oversee the process.

"We haven’t put conditions on it because we explained to the Secretary of State himself why there should be an international mediator."

Asked what his message to the Portadown Orangemen was, he said: "Why would you want to walk through an area that you know would cause great offence, which would cause great anger, cause great annoyance to many people, when you have an alternative route that is acceptable to everyone but you refuse to accept yourselves?

"We are up for it, if the mediation comes along and the right mediator is there, the right mediator with experience of looking at conflicts that have been caused by similar to our own in Ireland, as a result of occupation."

Some Orange Order members walk down Drumcree hill this morning

Mr Bryant also met people from Sinn Féin and the DUP as the political fallout from the dispute widened.

Michelle O'Neill, Northern Ireland’s First Minister, rejected claims she showed "gross contempt" for the Assembly after not showing up to be questioned about taking part in the Garvaghy Road protest.

Speaking after meeting Mr Bryant, she accused political opponents of seeking an "opportunity" to criticise her amid a deepening dispute around the parade in Portadown and defended her presence at the protest as related to her role as deputy leader of Sinn Féin.

She said: "I’m very confident and proud to stand with the community in Garvaghy, and I will continue to do so."

Earlier, the DUP vowed to take action against Sinn Féin after senior members, including the First Minister, took part in an unlawful protest against the loyal order parade.

DUP leader Gavin Robinson had been facing calls from unionist rivals to collapse the powersharing institutions - but he said he would not be "reckless".

Mr Bryant also said there had been no requests from the Police Service of Northern Ireland to call off the parade under section nine of the Public Processions (Northern Ireland) Act 1998.

He said: "I believe in the rule of law and I believe that the rule of law should be upheld."

He added: "I’m trying to find a solution that really works for everybody so that everybody can hold their head up high – and it’s tantalisingly just a bit far away at the moment, but we’re working on it."

PSNI Chief Constable says priority to 'keep everyone safe'

A ruling by the Parades Commission permitting the Orange Order march from Drumcree Church to Portadown town centre via the Garvaghy Road was upheld in an "unprecedented" late-night sitting of the High Court in Belfast in the early hours of yesterday morning.

On Friday, the commission had reversed its long-running determination and granted permission for the parade - a decision welcomed by unionist parties and met with outrage from nationalist and republican parties.

Permission was granted for a parade subject to strict restrictions, including a limit of 35 participants and an 8am start time.

However, thousands of people gathered on the Garvaghy Road to prevent the parade from passing along this route.

The protest was organised after Orangemen managed to overturn a 28-year-long ban on them parading down the predominantly nationalist road.

The PSNI said today that using force to remove protesters from the Garvaghy Road would have been disproportionate.

Assistant Chief Constable Ryan Henderson said the march could not proceed because of an unlawful, unnotified protest and repeated efforts to persuade people to leave the road were unsuccessful.

He said that removing an estimated 1,000 protesters, including women, children and older people, would have required significant force, risking serious injuries and disorder.

A dedicated inquiry team is reviewing evidence and anyone found to have broken the law will be investigated.

The PSNI is maintaining a low-key presence near Drumcree Church where a number of Orange Order members stayed overnight in the expectation of completing their march through the town.

The Grand Orange Lodge of Ireland (GOLI) today insisted it is the duty of police to "uphold the law".

In a statement, it said no-one should be "considered above the law" - while local residents accused the order of "pulling a stunt".

Taoiseach Micheál Martin urged calm as he expressed concern about the situation.

Mr Martin described the decision to allow the parade to go ahead for the first time in nearly three decades as "incomprehensible and wrong", and expressed concern that it might impact the Stormont Executive.

He said he did not believe "he collapse of the institutions is in the best interests of the people of Northern Ireland", saying it would be Mr Martin a "very retrograde step".

Asked about Sinn Féin calls for Mr Bryant to overturn the Parades Commission decision, Mr Martin said: "I would say to Sinn Féin and to everybody else: Time to dial back now and time to engage in dialogue, and to work this out in a sensible, community-based way."

Mr Martin said no-one wants to go back to the divisions of the past and the "trauma" experienced at the Garvagh Road.

"Over the last number of decades, community relations in this area has improved immensely and very significant work has been put in by residents' groups, by community groups, to develop community relationships, put them on a much more sustainable level.

"What’s happening over the last number of days threatens to undermine all of that and I think we have to stand back, have a clear vision as to the future – the future has to be one of engagement, of dialogue, working through these problems as we did in the past, and to avoid the tensions and the trauma and the divisions of the past."

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham appealed for "calm on all sides", saying there have been "big gains made in Northern Ireland, and we don't want to see things go backwards".

The leader of the SDLP said she told Mr Bryant last night that the situation in Portadown has to be de-escalated and a political process is needed to "get the dispute off the street".

Speaking on RTÉ's Morning Ireland programme, Claire Hanna added that she "coloured in" the depth of feeling locally for Mr Bryant.

"We also coloured in for him, very much, the depth of feeling around this, around the fact that most people live in a state of love and grace and thousands of parades go off all throughout the year but this one should not have been given permission.

"The Commission has refused permission for this particular parade for decades because of the depth of violence associated with it during the 1990s," she explained.
