Fernandes calls Ronaldo ‘greatest symbol’ of Portugal amid national team dispute

TheTools · 04.10.2026 18:36 · source · permalink

Reporting the Changing World

Anadolu Ajansı © 2026

Cristiano Ronaldo will always be the “greatest symbol” of Portugal’s national team and country regardless of the controversy surrounding his departure from the squad, Bruno Fernandes said Saturday.

Fernandes, who played alongside Ronaldo at Manchester United, urged unity amid uncertainty over the 41-year-old captain’s international future.

“I had a conversation with Cris. What I told him is that the most important thing, regardless of what happens, is that he will never cease to be the greatest symbol of our national team and country, for everything he has done in Portugal and around the world,” Fernandes told reporters ahead of Sunday’s UEFA Nations League match against Norway.

“He has a reserved place not only among the best of all time, but also in the hearts of the Portuguese people.”

Fernandes also dismissed suggestions of divisions within the Portugal squad and called for the dispute to be resolved internally.

“We cannot divide ourselves; the national team is one, and all parties should resolve their issues behind closed doors. I think that’s how it will be, and that everything will end well on both sides.”

“We are out of the matter; we spend a lot of time playing cards and are outside the controversy surrounding this issue, but I ask that the country not be divided, not make choices, and accept all sides.”

“Cris will always be the greatest icon of our national team, of world football, and deserves the respect of all Portuguese people, regardless of who likes him more or less.”

Ronaldo is Portugal’s all-time leading scorer with 146 goals and its most-capped player with 234 appearances. He helped the country win Euro 2016 and two UEFA Nations League titles.

Ronaldo announced Wednesday that he had left Portugal’s training camp in Copenhagen after head coach Jorge Jesus said the forward would not start against Denmark.

The five-time Ballon d’Or winner had been an unused substitute in Portugal’s 2-1 victory over Norway in Oslo last Sunday.

“Following the national team coach’s press conference, and after having a conversation with the Portuguese Football Federation president, I decided to leave the national team’s training camp,” Ronaldo wrote on Instagram.

He said he would explain the reasons behind his departure at a later date.

Portugal went on to beat Denmark 4-2 without Ronaldo on Thursday, maintaining their perfect start in Nations League Group A4.

Jesus sought to shift attention back to Sunday’s home match against Norway, while acknowledging Ronaldo’s importance to Portuguese football.

“The focus has to be on the national team above all else,” he said.

“Regarding Norway, what we have to do well is for our people to also be in tune with the flag and the objective.”

“Ronaldo is a symbol of the national team and of Portugal. After the game, there is plenty of time to talk about that.”

Portugal will host Norway at the Estadio do Dragao in Porto on Sunday.
