BBC forced to apologise after airing huge error in celeb death announcement - AOL

TheTools · 08.10.2026 07:14 · source · permalink

BBC Radio 2 was forced to apologise after paying tribute to the death of composer George Fenton with a song credited to George Benson. Following the mix-up, which arose during Monday's instalment of The Good Groove, BBC Radio 2 issued a statement to Radio Today, clarifying: "This was a production error, for which we apologise." A mistake that was noted by composer David Arnold, known for his work on five of the James Bond films, the Grammy Award winner fumed: "Radio 2 announced the passing of George Fenton on the 11pm news tonight, immediately followed by the presenter playing 'Nature Boy' by George Benson in tribute to 'George Benson'.

Arnold added that Fenton was "such a great person as well as a genius composer" and stated that he "deserved better than that from the company he gave voice to musically on so many brilliant occasions".

Likewise, listeners noted their disappointment on social media. "Really? Do better, folks. Does no one check things anymore?" wrote one. "BBC Verify obviously out for lunch," quipped a second.

An Oscar-nominated composer who worked on over 100 films, Fenton famously provided scores for Gandhi, Dangerous Liaisons, Groundhog Day, You've Got Mail, Sweet Home Alabama, and Hitch. According to a statement released by his family, the 76-year-old's death was "unexpected". They added that he was survived by several family members and "a life of music".

Following the news of his passing, tributes have flooded in for Fenton. Among them, Hollywood icon Anthony Hopkins penned a heartfelt tribute on Instagram. "George possessed an extraordinary musical gift, but what I will remember just as deeply was his generosity," the actor began.

"He could have spoken to me as the accomplished composer he was. Instead, he spoke to me as a friend. He encouraged rather than instructed. He gave me confidence when I had very little of my own. I have never forgotten that kindness, and I never will. George, my dear friend, thank you for believing in the music before I had the courage to believe in it myself."

The National Theatre has also paid tribute to Fenton. A statement released on Instagram read: "We are deeply saddened to learn of the death of composer George Fenton.

"George was a longtime collaborator with the National Theatre, having composed music for seven productions during his prestigious career, beginning with A Fair Quarrel directed by William Gaskill in 1979."

"Acclaimed for his majestic and narrative-driven orchestral scores spanning film, television and theatre, George will be hugely missed not only for his extraordinary music but the warmth and artistic teamwork he brought to every project, making him such a joy to work with. Our thoughts go out to George's family and friends at this sad time."
