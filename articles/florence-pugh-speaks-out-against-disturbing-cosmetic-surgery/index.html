Florence Pugh speaks out against ‘disturbing’ cosmetic surgery speculation

TheTools · 18.11.2026 22:52 · source · permalink

Actor criticises ‘stressful’ online conjecture in lengthy statement after photos of her at the Governors awards went viral, denying she had ever been on Ozempic or had surgery

Florence Pugh has said she is “disturbed” by a flurry of “stressful” online speculation about her appearance after images of her at the Governors awards this week went viral.

The 30-year-old actor wrote a lengthy statement to her 11 million Instagram followers on Wednesday in which she denied speculation that she had undergone cosmetic surgery or used weight-loss injections.

“I find it so stressful waking up and have thousands of people talking about you and your body in ways that you can’t even comprehend,” she wrote.

“I woke up the other morning and quite a lot of you were discussing my face and body in such a disturbing way. I learnt online that I had been on Ozempic? I learnt that I’d had upper bleph[aroplasty]? Lower bleph? Filler? Face lipo[suction]? Buccal fat remover? Chin surgery? Nose surgery?!”

The Dune, Thunderbolts and Oppenheimer actor continued: “Yes, I’m disturbed. For women who now think that I’m someone I’m not. For having my body dissected and pointed at again.

“I have spent my whole career defending and protecting bodies that just grow the way they grow. I was subjected to fat shaming in the business from the moment I started and I have loved every time I get to tell someone they are being inappropriate about my weight, and I’m not changing that now just because you’ve all said so.”

Pugh, who is known for speaking out about beauty standards and scrutiny of women’s bodies in Hollywood, insisted she had not undergone cosmetic procedures.

“I have never been on Ozempic, I have NEVER done any face surgery. And don’t EVER say that I didn’t make my own nose,” she wrote. “I’ve always had that beautiful ski slope. Even as a baby!”

Pugh said she had made a series of lifestyle changes since turning 30, including “chang[ing] my relationship with alcohol and to be able to do at least one pull-up in the gym.”

“It’s funny to see you all use reference images from times where I was really mentally unhappy and abusing my body, and put it next to the image from the Governors awards the other night where I am happy and truly respecting my body,” she wrote.

“For the first time in my ENTIRE career, I have had the opportunity to be rested, to sleep, to wake up and work out and to eat regularly and healthily.”

Pugh said she had worked hard to improve her physical and mental wellbeing, adding: “I’m looking after myself, and that’s literally it.”

Reflecting on the criticism she has faced throughout her career, she said: “I’ve always been aware that seeing a confident woman be happy in her skin is triggering to a lot of people and for a long time just accepted that I will receive commentary like that.

“But now this…? I’m asking us all to just be mindful. We are real people, living real lives.”

Pugh also claimed she had sued several publications earlier this year, claiming they published AI-generated images appearing to show her receiving cosmetic injections that were presented as real photographs.

“I was fucking horrified,” she wrote, claiming the articles had damaged her reputation.

Pugh concluded by urging followers to reflect on their attitudes towards physical appearance and the pressures of changing beauty standards.

“But please be gentle to yourselves and others when you go in on people’s physical appearance,” she wrote. “I’m not angry at you, I’m angry at the situation that’s being projected onto us.

“It feels inescapable, but part of changing the tide is on you as much as us in the public eye.”

In 2022, Pugh hit back at “vulgar” social media users who criticised her body after she posted photographs of herself wearing a sheer dress designed by Valentino’s creative director Pierpaolo Piccioli to one of the brand’s shows.

“What happened to you to be so content on being so loudly upset by the size of my boobs and body?” Pugh wrote at the time.
