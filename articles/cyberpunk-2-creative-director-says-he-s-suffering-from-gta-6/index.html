Cyberpunk 2 creative director says he's suffering from 'GTA 6-induced-open-world-game-dev-depression,' jokes that 'Cyberpunk 2 is cancelled'

It's been a big week for videogames—or rather, videogame. Grand Theft Auto 6 is enjoying what may very well be the single greatest media launch in history, securing over $3.7 billion in launch sales. But as Rockstar enjoys its acclaim, other developers—like Cyberpunk 2 creative director Igor Sarzynski—are professing some anxiety about releasing their own games in a post-GTA 6 world.

"GTA 6-induced-open-world-game-dev-depression is real. Probably the single greatest artistic achievement in history, I mean it. Scale, density, coherence, thoughtfulness of it all, just a technological and creative miracle," Sarzynski said on Bluesky over the weekend, joking that "Cyberpunk 2 is cancelled."

That news came as a surprise to Cyberpunk 2 lead writer Anna Megill, who replied with a baffled "But wait—"

Sarzynski was, of course, just goofing. But I'm sympathetic to any game dev who might be feeling an amount of apprehension about being measured against GTA 6's success, particularly when there's a genre overlap.

In Cyberpunk 2's case, however, CD Projekt is very much in its own lane: Cyberpunk is a different thematic setting, with entirely different systems and design goals. I'd hope that any reasonable person would think comparing the two is like comparing apples and oranges—particularly when the oranges reportedly had a production budget upwards of $1 billion.

In a follow-up post, Sarzynski acknowledged that each project is doing its own thing, while noting that GTA 6's example will benefit other game developers by serving as a source of creative inspiration.

"Seriously though, I think we're doing something of our own, with a different unique flavor," he said. "GTA is impressive in a way all great art is: It expands what is possible, a captivating vision flawlessly executed."
