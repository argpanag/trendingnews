Gauff Shakes off Back Issue and Shakes Down Kostyuk at WTA Finals

TheTools · 12.11.2026 23:36 · source · permalink

Coco Gauff won the final six games against Marta Kostyuk to keep her semifinal hopes alive at the WTA Finals.

Coco Gauff – count her out at your own peril.

The 22-year-old American came through a dramatic battle that kept fans on the edge of their seats on Thursday at the Indian Wells Tennis Garden, emerging with a roller-coaster 6-4, 7-5 victory over Ukraine’s Marta Kostyuk to keep her hopes of reaching the semifinals alive.

“I’m glad that I was able to close that out, but definitely I was looking into the third at 5-1,” Gauff said after completing her fifth career victory over Kostyuk in dramatic fashion. “I’m not going to lie.”

A topsy turvy affair started with a flip-floppy feel that never ceased.

After an early trade of breaks in the opening set, two of the quickest, most athletic players on the WTA Tour put their collective athleticism on display as Gauff blanketed the court and finished a cat-and-mouse point with a smash into the open court to earn a break point with Kostyuk serving at 2-3. The American converted on the next point.

Gauff won a similar point to save a break point in the next game, tagging a backhand winner past Kostyuk from midcourt to stretch the lead to 5-2.

Kostyuk, who had her right wrist examined during a changeover after the seventh game, returned to the court after having the wrist taped. She struggled with her forehand in the ensuing game but managed to hold to force Gauff to serve for the set.

It didn’t go well for Gauff, as an ugly double fault – the serve missing by about 10 feet, wide and into the net – gave Kostyuk a break point.

The Ukrainian, in the midst of a career year, capitalized to get back on serve, then had two game points while serving at 4-5, but Gauff held firm and eventually claimed the set with a textbook forehand volley.

The pair’s seventh all-time clash was far from over, however.

Kostyuk, playing for her first WTA Finals win, raced out to a double-break 3-0 lead, and now it was Gauff’s turn to need medical attention. She looked distraught during a medical timeout for her lower back and returned to the court with a mountain to climb.

“It just feels weird,” Gauff later said of the injury. “I’ve been, like, fine movement-wise. It was just only on the serve that I was struggling a little bit.

“But I think I was just focusing more on taking a little bit off of it and just really trying to use my arm more so than rotation, which is kind of the opposite of what we’ve been working on, but it’s what I needed to do today.”

The 3-0 lead became 5-1, but Gauff, now feeling better, began her ascent. Saving three set points while serving at 5-1 proved to be the catalyst, and the breaks – for 5-3 and 5-5 – came quickly.

“Honestly, I don’t even know how I got to 6-5,” Gauff said after the match at the Tennis Channel desk. “It felt like a blink. It really felt like a blink.”

After holding for 6-5, the mood of the encounter had completely shifted once again. Kostyuk, out of sync and frustrated, yielded a third consecutive break, sending Gauff to the finish line with a hard-fought victory that was a testament to the no-quit competitive mindset that has made her the champion she is today.

“Down 1-5, I feel like that’s where Coco’s at her best,” said Madison Keys, who commentated the match for Tennis Channel. “She finds her form, she’s able to relax a little bit, find her rhythm again. On the other side of the net, you know once Coco starts getting herself back in those sets, you have so much pressure to close it out.”

Kostyuk goes home with seven consecutive losses after an 0-3 run at the WTA Finals, while Gauff must await the result of Thursday’s second singles match to learn her fate. If Linda Noskova defeats Jessica Pegula, Gauff will qualify for the semifinals. If not, Pegula takes the spot, and Gauff will be done despite her impressive 2-1 record in her three round-robin matches.

The 2024 WTA Finals champion, making her fifth consecutive appearance at the event, is bidding to become the youngest player to reach her third semifinal at the WTA Finals (2023, 2024 and 2026) since Maria Sharapova (2004, 2005 and 2006).

Already, she has become the youngest player to appear at the WTA Finals for five consecutive editions since Martina Hingis (1996-2000) and the youngest American to do so since Lindsay Davenport (1994-1998).
