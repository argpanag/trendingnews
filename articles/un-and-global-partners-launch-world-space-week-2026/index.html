UN and Global Partners Launch World Space Week 2026

TheTools · 06.10.2026 14:07 · source · permalink

The United Nations, global educational institutions, and national legislatures launched World Space Week 2026 on October 4, centering on the theme Rocket Revolution to address democratized orbital access and commercial space governance through October 10.

Established by the UN General Assembly in 1999, the annual observance commemorates the October 4, 1957 launch of Sputnik 1 and the October 10, 1967 entry into force of the Outer Space Treaty. This year's program spans over 50,000 events across more than 100 countries, driven by lower launch costs and reusable rocket developments that experts project will build a $1 trillion global space economy by 2030.

United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA) Director Aarti Holla-Maini highlighted the expanding participation of developing nations and private entities in orbital operations.

"Now more countries, institutions and communities than ever have the possibility to use space for scientific purposes, for Earth observation, communications and the search for solutions to the challenges we face down here," said Aarti Holla-Maini, Director of UNOOSA. "That is the whole rocket revolution in today's world."

Holla-Maini emphasized that expanded access requires international frameworks through bodies like the Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS) to manage space debris, satellite traffic, and lunar coordination.

"A greater access to space brings greater responsibility," said Aarti Holla-Maini, Director of UNOOSA. "As a growing number of actors reaches orbit, international cooperation becomes more important, and no less necessary."

In Mexico, federal deputies hosted national celebrations at the Palacio Legislativo de San Lazaro, calling for domestic technological investment and equal access in STEM fields.

"Outside this room there are still those who think that space is something very distant, a matter for astronauts or other countries, but those who accompany us today know that space is already here," said María Teresa Ealy Díaz, Deputy of the Mexican Federal Parliament. "It is in the experimental rockets designed and launched by Mexican teams, in the classrooms and laboratories of our universities, in the planetary sciences currently researched from Latin America and in space law that will have to give certainty to everything that is coming."

Ealy Díaz stressed the urgency of creating tangible domestic opportunities for young scientists rather than relying on imported technology.

"The question is very simple: Is Mexico going to limit itself to consuming the technology that other countries develop, or are we going to be able to research it, design it, and also build it ourselves?" said María Teresa Ealy Díaz, Deputy of the Mexican Federal Parliament. "I want a Mexico that also builds it, and to achieve it we have something fundamental, Mexican talent."

Herminia López Santiago, Secretary of the Science, Technology, and Innovation Commission, recognized student participants from UNAM and IPN presenting advanced aerospace projects.

"My highest recognition and, above all, my congratulations to all the work and projects they are building, which will one day represent Mexico," said Herminia López Santiago, Secretary of the Science, Technology, and Innovation Commission.

Denzel Carrillo, President of the Network for Knowledge Generation and Open Innovation (Regenia), noted that institutional bridges are necessary to help young researchers overcome industry barriers.

"Regenia was born to be a bridge between all these actors from industry, government, academia, and the social sector, so that we can create these spaces," said Denzel Carrillo, President of Regenia.

Educational centers and regional groups are hosting parallel events during the week. Mexico's UNAM UNIVERUM science museum scheduled interactive exhibits, solar observations, and workshops through October 10. In Aguascalientes, the general public was invited to astronomical sessions and lectures at Secondary General School Number 6.

Astronomer Jennifer Alfaro explained the historic significance of the event dates during her local announcement.

"These dates were chosen mainly because on October 4, 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, an event that marked the beginning of the space age," said Jennifer Alfaro, Astronomer at the Astronomical Society of Aguascalientes. "And on October 10, 1967, the Outer Space Treaty came into force, which is one of the main international agreements establishing principles for countries' activities in space."

Alfaro stated that her educational presentations will focus on launch technology and historical engineering milestones.

"In my lecture we are going to address the evolution of the space rocket," said Jennifer Alfaro, Astronomer at the Astronomical Society of Aguascalientes. "We will understand how this invention was so important for the advancement of all humanity, what we have achieved with it, and what would have happened if a rocket had never been invented."

Activities across participating global venues will conclude on October 10 with synchronized telescope observations and space policy forums.
