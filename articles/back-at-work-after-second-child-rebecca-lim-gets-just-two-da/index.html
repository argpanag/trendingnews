Back at work after second child, Rebecca Lim gets just two days off a month and wanted to quit new drama before filming

TheTools · 25.11.2026 02:06 · source · permalink

AI-translated article with transliterated names. Read original Chinese article here.

Back in actress mode, Rebecca Lim jokes that she will need another round of postnatal confinement after filming Back to Back (《背对背》)!

Rebecca Lim and her husband Matthew Webster, who is not in show business, married in 2022. They welcomed a son in 2024 and a daughter in May this year. Since returning to work in September this year, she has hosted President's Star Charity 2026 (《总统挑战之夜2026》) and served as an ambassador for the 37th Singapore International Film Festival (SGIFF). She has also finally returned to Channel 8 with Back to Back (《背对背》), her first Mandarin drama in four years since Soul Doctor (《灵医》), which aired in 2022.

Back to Back (《背对背》) follows Fang Ling (Rebecca Lim), who loses control of her emotions after discovering that her husband Wu Yanyu (Pierre Png) is having an affair with the daughter she had out of wedlock and was forced to abandon in her youth. In her dazed state, she may have killed him. As she unravels under the strain of covering up the crime and having her dark past exposed, she discovers that Ricky (Romeo Tan), the only friend she trusts, may be her former stepbrother — and that it may all be a revenge plot targeting her...

An emotionally demanding role

Speaking to Lianhe Zaobao on the set of Back to Back on 24 November, Lim said her character's extreme emotional swings made the role more mentally and emotionally demanding than physically tiring. “Fang Ling is completely different from me in real life. She is trapped in the past and cannot move on, whereas I accept my past. I believe we have to travel those roads and see different things in life before we learn to cherish the people and things before us. Unfortunately, she has no one around to give her a helping hand, so I feel a lot of compassion for her.”

She said the drama has many intense scenes, including plenty of crying. “I joked with Romeo that I would need another round of confinement to build myself back up after this drama, because I have used up all the nourishment I took in during confinement. Ha ha!”

On returning to a drama set, Lim admitted that she lacked confidence during the early preparations. “I didn't know if I could do it, and I was afraid I wouldn't do it well. Every day, I thought, ‘I probably can't do this any more.’ Two weeks before filming began, I called her,” she said, referring to her manager beside her. “I asked if I could pull out of the drama, because I felt I couldn't get back into the routine of filming every day. Before becoming a mother, I was used to work being my life. But after becoming a mother, family became my priority, so getting back into that mindset was the biggest challenge.” What did her manager say? Lim replied with a laugh: “Three words: ‘It's too late.’ So I thought, OK, I'll grit my teeth... Ha ha!”

Setting aside her warmth

Another challenge was setting aside her warmth. Lim said she attended classes to prepare for filming. Her instructor's assessment was that, since becoming a mother, she no longer projected the same sense of a strong woman. “He said I was now too gentle and warm — a Rebecca Lim he had never seen before. So for this drama, I had to set aside who I am at this stage of my life and rediscover my more forceful side.”

Fortunately, she found her familiar rhythm again after about two weeks. “When I'm filming, I put away that sunny side of myself as a mother. I'm not worried about losing my sense of happiness, because when filming ends and I go home to the people I love, that happiness comes naturally.”

She added: “What I ask of myself is to give a truthful performance without overacting. I told the director very directly: don't worry about hurting my feelings. If I'm not acting well, please tell me straight away, because I can do better there and then.”

Twenty years on, “like a little star”

Since returning to work in September, Lim has barely stopped, with only about two days off a month — and just one this month. She likened returning to work to restarting an engine: finding her rhythm again has not been easy. At the same time, these opportunities have been a source of encouragement. “I haven't appeared before audiences for so long, yet people are still willing to approach me and work with me. That's encouraging, and perhaps I need that encouragement at this point.”

Besides turning 40, Lim also marks her 20th year as an actress in 2026. Joking that hearing the number made her “not want to face reality”, she reflected: “I feel very fortunate because it has been such a wonderful journey. I've been able to make acting my career. It has supported me, and I've gradually fallen in love with it too. That brings me great happiness.”

She described herself as a little star in the sky: “Sometimes it shines brightly, and sometimes it is hidden by dark clouds, but it is always there.” She explained: “I always tell the people around me that I'm not the prettiest, the tallest or the most talented, yet so many good opportunities have come my way. So I cherish every opportunity and the experience it brings. Just like now: even after becoming a mother of two, people still ask me to act. I really treasure that, so I will give it my all and hope to do my best.”

The 20-episode Back to Back, executive-produced by Wong Kuang Yong with a story by Lin Jinlan and Peng Kaiyi, is expected to air in Channel 8's 9pm slot in May 2027.
