David Hearn lawyers say DOJ watchdog won’t probe failed prosecution — but Interior IG is considering

TheTools · 21.10.2026 13:00 · source · permalink

The watchdog for the Department of Justice is declining to investigate the prosecution of former Olympian David Hearn, whom the DOJ wrongly accused of vandalizing the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, according to his lawyers.

Hearn’s legal team had asked inspectors general for both the Justice Department and the Department of the Interior to probe what it called an abuse of government, but the DOJ’s watchdog informed the team on Oct. 8 that it would not open an investigation, saying the inspector general lacked authority to do so. That letter reportedly arrived two days after Hearn’s lawyers first asked for the independent probe.

In a letter obtained by MS NOW, lawyers representing Hearn responded to that rejection on Wednesday, writing that the Justice Department’s Office of the Inspector General was incorrect and did have “jurisdiction to conduct both administrative and criminal investigations.”

“The DOJ OIG has plenary statutory jurisdiction to investigate potential crimes committed by DOJ personnel,” the letter says. “The allegations in our October 6 Letter point to facts that warrant an investigation into numerous potential crimes, including witness tampering, subornation of perjury, obstruction of justice, and criminal civil rights violations.”

Hearn’s lawyers also warned of potential consequences if there were no independent investigation.

“Given the substantial public attention devoted to this matter, and the significant questions raised concerning the conduct of federal officials, declining independent review would risk undermining public confidence in the Department’s ability to police its own conduct,” the letter said. “Such a result would be inconsistent with the purposes for which Congress established the OIG and would be a betrayal of its mission.”

The Department of Justice and its inspector general did not immediately respond to MS NOW’s request for comment.

The Office of the Inspector General for the Department of the Interior told MS NOW on Tuesday that it does not confirm or deny ongoing investigations. But in a letter obtained by MS NOW, the watchdog office told Hearn’s lawyers it was considering their request.

Earlier this month, lawyers representing Hearn sent a letter to the inspector general for the Department of Justice, saying the former Olympian had suffered a “nightmare” when he was arrested and U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro’s office subsequently charged him with vandalizing the reflecting pool’s liner.

Hearn’s lawyers allege that the government had “simply ignored” exculpatory evidence for weeks. They asked the watchdog to investigate why prosecutors “rushed to indict” Hearn under what they said was political pressure from President Donald Trump.

“From June 19 to the present, gross incompetence, negligence, and misconduct have been committed by personnel in both agencies,” the lawyers’ letter said. “A critical purpose of the requested investigations is to collect the facts … to hold accountable the individuals and groups who failed Mr. Hearn, the criminal justice system and the public.

In the same letter, lawyers for Hearn had also asked the inspector general for the Department of the Interior to investigate the decision to charge Hearn.

The letter accused the department of backing the accusations against Hearn when internal documents showed that the damage was caused by a “rushed and botched installation” by the contractor, as Pirro would later concede.

Lawyers for Hearn have asked the department’s inspector general to determine whether Interior Secretary Doug Burgum knew his public statements attributing the damage to vandalism were false or whether he was left in the dark by his staff. They also asked investigators to hold agency personnel accountable for these false claims.

At the time, John Bell, the director of external affairs for that department’s inspector general, told MS NOW, “As with all submissions to our office, we will review it carefully to determine the appropriate course of action.”
