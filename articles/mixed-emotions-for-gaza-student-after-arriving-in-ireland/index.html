Mixed emotions for Gaza student after arriving in Ireland

TheTools · 25.11.2026 18:52 · source · permalink

"I couldn't process my emotions. I didn’t know what I was feeling. I felt empty inside."

This is how Raghad Abdalrahim responded when asked how she felt as she made her long journey out of Gaza, across Israel and into Jordan earlier this week.

Raghad is among 14 students from Gaza who arrived in Dublin this morning, having left Gaza in the very early hours of Monday morning.

The journey for Raghad and the others began even earlier because they had to travel across the war-torn strip to a meeting point in the south to be there for 3am on Monday.

Travelling on the dangerous journey from northern Gaza, Raghad was accompanied by her brother.

"When we got on the buses we were crying because we were leaving our families behind. We were in shock that this is happening," she said.

Gaza student Raghad Abdalrahim on her hopes for her new life in Ireland

Speaking from a health facility in Dublin, where the students have undergone medical checks and are now due to get a long-awaited sleep, it was clear that Raghad was still trying to absorb everything she has gone through.

"After leaving Gaza and crossing the occupied Palestine, I felt relieved, I felt happy," she said.

Raghad refers to Israel as occupied Palestine.

"I am Palestinian and this was the first time in my life to see occupied Palestine," she said.

"I was looking out the window the entire time.

"In Gaza, we were not living, and seeing the roads, seeing the cities, seeing the lights, that there is electricity. We don't have electricity in Gaza.

"To see [the city of] Amman full of lights, it was hard for me to see that the rest of the world is living normally. It was hard for me to process."

Raghad described how she was "full of hope" when she first received news of her fully funded scholarship to study a degree in business at UCD and applied for a study visa.

But, in July, her visa was rejected and she was informed that she would not be part of an evacuation that was due to take place that month.

"When I read the message that I would not be evacuated I felt devastated. I completely lost hope because I didn't know that we could appeal," she said.

Her devastation was followed by depression.

Raghad believed that an appeal was not an option, because the letter informed her that any appeal had to be submitted by post.

"We don't have a postal system functioning in Gaza," she said.

Raghad is among around 60 Gaza students whose visa rejections were taken up by a number of legal firms here.

They brought proceedings in the High Court which ended in an instruction from the court to Minister for Justice Jim O'Callaghan to reconsider their cases.

Last week, the minister reversed the visa rejections on appeal.

"The past month alone was one of the hardest months [for me]," Raghad said.

"For so many nights I couldn't sleep fearing that maybe my visa would not get approved," she added.

When she received the news of her successful appeal, and that her evacuation was imminent again Raghad's emotions were mixed.

"I felt scared because I wasn't expecting it. I felt kind of lost and scared but also happy and relieved," she said.

"But I couldn't ignore the fact that I would have to leave my family.

In the days that followed, Raghad described how she tried to not make eye contact with her family because she could not face the emotion that would follow.

"I couldn't see them crying because I couldn't bear facing that feeling.

"In front of my family I acted excited but deep inside I felt guilt, because I'm going to be leaving and living a happy life and leaving them behind in Gaza to live the devastating reality."

Raghad's goal now is to complete her business degree and go on to set up supports for children who are experiencing psychological trauma as a result of war.

"I love children and seeing them suffer during the war and during the genocide was really heart-breaking for me. I know how much they suffer from psychological trauma in their lives," she said.

"There are so few institutions that help these children recover and I want to learn how to build an institution from scratch. After leaving Gaza I am more determined to do this.

"God didn't give me this empathy for children for no reason and I'm going to make the most of it and work as hard as I can."

To the Irish people who supported the students, she said she wanted to say "thank you".

She described the Department of Foreign Affairs staff who accompanied the students for much of their journey this week as "amazing".

"They all supported us. They were super-caring and super-fun, making sure that we felt comfortable and didn't feel pressured," she said.

Raghad listed them by name.

"I want to say thank you to them and to all the Irish people who helped us", she said, referring especially to the lawyers who took on the students' cases."

"But there are still students waiting for their evacuation," she said.

Those students have received letters today informing them of an evacuation on 21 December.

Before Raghad finished the interview and went to try to sleep, she said: "I am still trying to figure out how I am feeling and trying to understand myself and how I will get used now to my new life."
