Bees sense chance to sting Blues’ backline

Chelsea have not won this west London derby since Brentford’s first Premier League season. Each visit since October 2021’s 1-0 win, Ben Chilwell the scorer, has been a draw. This season has continued the divergence between the clubs’ respective approaches, Brentford’s solidity and continuity contrasting with Chelsea attempting their latest reboot. Kevin Schade, after two well-taken goals at Bournemouth, will fancy facing a defence not yet attuned to Xabi Alonso’s configurations. Only one team has won a Premier League title with a defensive trio: Chelsea’s last title team, in 2016-17 under Antonio Conte. Alonso’s attack has been rather more successful, presenting another challenge for Jannik Schuster. Replacing Nathan Collins, the 20-year-old central defender had a tough examination from Bournemouth’s Evanilson on his first Premier League start, emerging with credit. For Chelsea, João Pedro has three goals and three assists, but doubts surrounding his fitness could lead to Danny Welbeck’s first league start. John Brewin

Spurs and Villa meet in desperation derby

What happens when a very stoppable force meets an extremely movable object? We will be tuning into events at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on Saturday to find out as 19th-placed Aston Villa arrive in north London. The teams have scored one goal between them in the league and are desperately hoping for a first Premier League win. The return of Pedro Porro is big for Roberto De Zerbi and that should bring the Archie Gray Experiment at right-back to a merciful end. No Premier League starting XI feels less settled than Spurs – most players look as if they are a mistake or two from being dropped – which has culminated in a side that seems governed by fear and continuously prefers to take the safe option. After coming off the bench against Everton and Liverpool in the past week, the injury-plagued James Maddison will be itching to start and take some sort of initiative for Tottenham. Michael Butler

Hürzeler’s scoring express meets champions

When Fabian Hürzeler spoke about Brighton’s success being founded on them being “well structured” after their stirring comeback against Manchester United at Old Trafford on Wednesday, he could have been referring to Malick Yalcouyé. The 20-year-old Ivorian moved to the south coast in 2024 after spending just a few months with IFK Gothenburg in Sweden but had to wait until this season to make his senior debut for Brighton after being sent on loan to Sturm Graz in Austria and then Swansea last season. With Jack Hinshelwood sidelined by injury until after the international break, Yalcouyé has taken his chance in the No 10 role and already found the net twice in the Premier League as Brighton have made a brilliant start. Arsenal are up next for the division’s top scorers – who have 13 goals from their opening four matches – and Mikel Arteta should be wary of the threat posed by Yalcouyé and co as the champions prepare for a fourth away game in succession. Ed Aarons

Brighton v Arsenal, Saturday 3pm

Mykolenko form a boost for Moyes

Vitalii Mykolenko’s early-season improvement has gone largely unheralded – and it probably always will be among his detractors – but at a time when David Moyes needs experienced players to step up and defy the constraints of a small squad, the Everton left-back has produced. Having signed a new three-year contract in the summer, and seemingly bulked up, the Ukraine international has kept his critics quiet with several impressive displays for a defence that has four clean sheets in six matches. There remains a problematic lack of cover at left-back after the decision to loan Adam Aznou to Málaga for the season but Mykolenko, the better defender, has eased the concerns that surrounded his position before the new campaign. Ipswich’s new forward recruits, Emersonn and Zian Flemming, will test Everton’s solidity but Moyes’s side will have made an encouraging start should they sign off with a win before the extended international break. Andy Hunter

  • Everton v Ipswich, Saturday 3pm

Everton v Ipswich, Saturday 3pm

Jaissle refuses to delete Newcastle blueprint

As befits a lover of direct football, Matthias Jaissle likes to get straight to the point. On Thursday he was asked whether, in the light of Monday night’s 4-1 thrashing at Leeds and a raft of injuries, he was tempted to perhaps switch to a back five or modify his beloved “vertical” approach when Hull visit Tyneside on Saturday. The reply proved unequivocal. “No,” said a manager unafraid to answer questions with a single word. Instead Jaissle expects his young team to retain faith in a wider vision as Newcastle – who signed eight players aged 24 and under this summer – adapt to life after Bruno Guimarães, Sandro Tonali and Anthony Gordon. “It’s a brave journey the club is going on and I’m fully committed in that,” the German said. “If there’s one setback, we shouldn’t delete our journey or our vision. We’re fully committed. We’re fully aligned. We anticipated that something like that against Leeds would happen. It’s on us to respond. But while still believing. We don’t change.” Louise Taylor

Delap hopes to impress as Wood regent

Many scoffed at the £45m that was paid for Liam Delap in the summer but Nottingham Forest feels like the perfect place for the striker to regain his form. Forest previously thrived with the now injured Chris Wood as their arrowhead and Delap is something of a younger regent for the New Zealander – powerful, instinctive and willing to bring others into play. During Saturday’s win at Villa, Morgan Gibbs-White and Delap looked as if they already have an understanding and both will be keen to capitalise against a Coventry side that have conceded eight goals in the past two games. After a nervy campaign last season, Forest feel back in the ascendancy and will have been delighted that plans to increase the City Ground’s capacity from 31,000 to 52,500, at an estimated cost of £790m, were approved by the local council on Wednesday. MB

Iraola faces test on south-coast return

After watching Bournemouth make their European bow from afar in San Sebastián, a city Andoni Iraola knows inside out, the Basque head coach will return to his previous club on Sunday. Iraola – whose assistants Tommy Elphick and Shaun Cooper played for Bournemouth, the former captaining the club into the Premier League in 2015 – led the Dorset club into the Europa League by recording their best-ever finish, sixth. His body of work on the south coast earned him the Liverpool opportunity and a warm welcome is a guarantee. Despite the Cherries’ slow start under Iraola’s successor, Marco Rose, they will pose tricky opposition. The German spent a couple of hours in Iraola’s company before officially starting in June and both will be braced for an all-action contest. Eddie Howe failed to beat Bournemouth in the eight league games he managed against them with Newcastle and Iraola and his staff will expect a tough test. Ben Fisher

Calvert-Lewin: man on a mission

Dominic Calvert-Lewin will be a man on a mission against Crystal Palace at Elland Road on Saturday. Daniel Farke’s centre-forward hopes to become the first Leeds player to score in six consecutive home league games since Arthur Hydes back in 1932. Managers often, quite rightly, say players matter more than formations but, in Calvert-Lewin’s case, Farke’s switch to 3-5-2 late last November proved a road to Damascus moment. Until then he had struggled as the team’s lone striker but, once he was paired with Noah Okafor or Lukas Nmecha, the goals started to flow. Granted, Leeds sometimes play 3-4-3 or, occasionally, revert to a back four but Calvert-Lewin has emphasised the importance of attacking partnerships. After collecting eight points from their opening four games, Farke’s team have registered the best beginning to a top-tier campaign of any Leeds team since David O’Leary’s class of 2001-02. Will it continue at Palace’s expense? Is Hydes’ record under threat? Might 3-5-2 be the answer to ending more strikers’ goal droughts? Louise Taylor

  • Leeds v Crystal Palace, Sunday 2pm

Leeds v Crystal Palace, Sunday 2pm

Le Bris plots party crash in Manchester

Manchester City’s maximum 12 points from their first four Premier League games featured 1-0 wins (over Coventry and Manchester United), a 2-1 victory (Bournemouth), and a 4-1 success (Crystal Palace). Conceding only two goals while scoring eight is an impressive record that is only marginally bettered by the champions, Arsenal (one conceded, eight scored in their 12-point tally), and it seems a rosy augury that Enzo Maresca’s team will be genuine title contenders this season. Régis Le Bris, though, plots to halt City’s perfect start, and can draw on guiding Sunderland to a share of the points last season against the FA Cup holders, though that was at the Stadium of Light. In the corresponding fixture at the Etihad Stadium, City cruised to a 3-0 win. Jamie Jackson

  • Manchester City v Sunderland, Sunday 2pm

Manchester City v Sunderland, Sunday 2pm

Bobb needs to provide more end product

Oscar Bobb came off the bench against West Ham and fired Fulham into the fourth round of the Carabao Cup with a clinical late winner at the London Stadium on Tuesday night. It was a fine piece of individual play from the Norway winger but he needs to do it more often. The goal was Bobb’s first since joining Fulham last January and a player of his ability should be producing more of an end product. The 23-year-old is a clever dribbler and looked ready to establish himself at Manchester City before a serious injury curtailed his development. The talent is clear. Bobb had a good World Cup and should be full of confidence when Fulham, still seeking their first win in the league this season, host Manchester United. Jacob Steinberg
