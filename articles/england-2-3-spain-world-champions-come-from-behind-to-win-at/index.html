  7. 'Spells were very good, but result doesn't prove it'published at 22:31 BST 26 September
    22:31 BST 26 September

    FT: England 2-3 Spain

    England

    England boss Thomas Tuchel, speaking to ITV Sport: "Terrible start, the worst start possible.

    "It makes it so, so difficult to be behind against this type of team. After 10-12 minutes, I think we were the better team. We dominated in the opponent's half.

    "We created and scored two goals, our best 15 minutes after the whistle. We couldn't convert the penalty - I think then maybe Spain would have believed that their long run of unbeaten games ends here.

    "Another big, big mistake for 2-2. The third goal, was the only goal they really scored. Just is what it is. We have to keep going and learn from it. Take what is positive and take the points back to the Czech Republic."

    On Anthony Gordon's goal being what England should embody: "No, the Anthony Gordon goal was a counter-attack.

    "The top transition goal we scored, we scored amazing goals and could have created more if we were a bit more clinical in the last 20 metres.

    "We had an incredible amount of ball wins. We couldn't convert enough into chances and goals, otherwise we would have won the match."

    On Gordon's performance: "Good performance. With ups and downs. Like the team, it is a team sport."

    On the result: "Always, but there will always be moments when you suffer like this. It matters that you take your chances. Spells of the game were very good, but the result doesn't prove it today."

  12. 'We just need to not make mistakes at the highest level'published at 22:14 BST 26 September
    22:14 BST 26 September

    FT: England 2-3 Spain

    England

    England forward Anthony Gordon speaking to BBC Radio 5 Live: "Good game, one I thought we were in control of. The penalty is a massive moment. If that goes in we are in a much better position than when it doesn't but obviously H [Harry Kane] slips and there is nothing you can do about a moment like that. I think all of their goals came from us really and at that level you can't afford to do that. It was a bit of a rollercoaster in the match. It must have been a great game to watch for the fans, it was a great game to play in.

    "They're the best in the world for a reason. When it went to 3-2 you see their ability to really control possession. I think against someone like that you have really got to go for the throat which we did. The penalty was a massive momentum shift. We just lost to the best in the world. The gap is not big in my mind and I think tonight proved that. We just need to not make mistakes at the highest level.

    On being substituted: "I'm always disappointed when I come off only just because I want to score more goals and be part of every part of the game. I want to get more assists and excite people who are watching me. Tonight I felt like it was maybe the right decision because I felt a bit tired. I'd done a lot of long distance running at high speed so tonight I have no complaints with the manager taking me off."

21:51 BST 26 September

FT: England 2-3 Spain

Paul Robinson
Former England goalkeeper on BBC Radio 5 Live at Wembley

There's two sides to that England performance.

There is the side who were susceptible defensively but saying that two of Spain's goals came from England errors that could have been avoided.

Thomas Tuchel will take a lot of heart from the way that England got themselves back into it.

Tuchel will take from this that his team has got heart and fight. But once again we are talking about a 90 minute performance.

They were good and probably the better team for large spells of that game but not over the whole game.

Jude Bellingham was brought down in the penalty area in the second half, but Harry Kane missed the resultant spot-kick

England's Nations League campaign got off to a losing start as they were beaten by World Cup holders Spain in an enthralling encounter at Wembley.

Spain struck inside two minutes when Lamine Yamal was the beneficiary of Marc Guehi's error and slotted past James Trafford.

England then managed to survive several close calls before Anthony Gordon and Harry Kane scored in quick succession to give them an unlikely lead before half-time.

However, Kane crucially missed a penalty early in the second half - after Jude Bellingham was brought down in the box - which left the door open for the visitors.

Substitute Alex Baena took advantage of Nico O'Reilly's mistake to equalise before Mikel Oyarzabal concluded a clinical move with a beautifully dinked finish for the winner.

Victory extends Spain's unbeaten run to 39 games, but England must learn lessons from this defeat.

Thomas Tuchel's side need to adopt a more aggressive approach and cut out individual errors, which are always going to be punished against a side of Spain's quality.

The world champions went ahead early on, the result of a blunder by Guehi, who inexplicably turned into trouble - and to make matters worse straight into Lamine Yamal - who beat Trafford, deputising for Jordan Pickford, with ease.

England were living on the edge and Spain could have been out of sight as Ferran Torres saw a goal ruled out for offside, forced a fine save from Trafford, then rolled a finish wide when clean through.

When England finally adopted a more direct approach, it reaped rewards as they turned the game on its head to lead before the break.

The excellent Gordon raced clear to beat Spain goalkeeper Unai Simon with a cool finish after 36 minutes before Kane added England's second four minutes later as his header was deflected in off Marc Cucurella.

England suddenly had Spain in retreat, but wasted a huge opportunity to extend their advantage after 55 minutes when a penalty was awarded for Rodri's foul on Bellingham after a Video Assistant Referee intervention.

Kane, normally so reliable, slipped as he went to take his kick, actually touching the ball twice before hitting the bar.

Once again, it was an error that undermined England as Spain equalised on the hour. O'Reilly was the culprit on this occasion, losing possession before substitute Baena beat Trafford low down from the edge of the area.

Spain had regained their composure and also regained the lead with 16 minutes left, a slick move finished by Oyarzabal - who scored the winner against England in the Euro 2024 final - lifting a delicate finish over Trafford.

England pay price for mistakes

England suffered from making the kind of basic mistakes that always carry the danger of being punished at international level - especially when the opposition are the best team in world football.

Guehi's gift to Lamine Yamal set England back almost instantly, and it was only Spain's wastefulness in front of goal that prevented the game being over as a contest before the break.

England showed determination and a more positive approach to go in front, but were again the architects of their own downfall to sabotage their chances of victory - Kane uncharacteristically missed from the spot and O'Reilly squandered possession before Baena's equaliser.

Spain then took control of the game and even a late rally was not enough to earn England a point.

Gordon a shining light

Gordon's goal against Argentina in the summer had England dreaming of their first men's World Cup final since 1966 - before Tuchel's side slumped to an agonising defeat.

And the winger, now at Barcelona, was the stand-out performer for the Three Lions again on Saturday with an electric display.

His finish for the equaliser was high quality - exchanging passes with Bellingham then coolly sliding a finish past Simon having never looked likely to miss.

Bellingham was also excellent while keeper Trafford, given his opportunity for two games before first-choice Pickford returns, did his cause no harm with a fine first-half save from Torres.

Trafford had no chance with any of Spain's goals. and will have created a good impression on Tuchel as he seeks to increase competition for Pickford.

The Three Lions next travel to face the Czech Republic on Tuesday (19:45 BST) while Spain host Croatia at the same time.
