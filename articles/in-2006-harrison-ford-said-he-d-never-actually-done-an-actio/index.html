In 2006, Harrison Ford said he’d “never actually done” an action movie. Rarely has an actor been more wrong

TheTools · 01.11.2026 16:45 · source · permalink

When you hear the name Harrison Ford, what are the first two genres that come to mind? Nobody really needs to answer that, because it’s obviously sci-fi and action.

The former, even more obviously, because of Star Wars and Blade Runner, which made him one of the medium’s definitive big-screen icons. He may have hated playing Rick Deckard and eventually got over his aversion to Han Solo staying alive, but those films are classics for a reason.

As for action, take your pick. His most seminal contribution to the genre can’t be anything other than the Indiana Jones franchise, and while it was inspired by old serials and was more adventure-driven than a conventional actioner, you can’t have set pieces like the rolling boulder, the mine-cart chase, and the motorcycle/sidecar showdown across the first three flicks and not call them action movies.

Whether he wants to admit it or not, and he clearly doesn’t, Ford has been in plenty of them. Indy by himself counts for five, but when it was brought to his attention in a factually accurate manner that he is, in fact, known as one of cinema’s greatest action heroes, he didn’t seem to understand why.

“I’ve never actually done action films,” he claimed in 2006 when promoting the terrible Firewall, which wasn’t an action film, at least. “I do thrillers that have action in them. I do films like Indiana Jones, which I think of as kind of a fantasy comedy, but Indiana Jones is as close as I’ve come to doing an action film.”

He has done a lot of thrillers that have action in them; you have to give him that, but the clue’s in one of the two words he used to describe them. Clear and Present Danger, Patriot Games, and The Fugitive are all action thrillers, and very good ones, while Hollywood Homicide is an action comedy, albeit a shite one.

“I think of films like Lethal Weapon as action films,” the star insisted. “And I’ve never actually done one like that.” You kind of had, mate, because there’s no such thing as an odd-couple caper with gags, set pieces, and shootouts that exists without Richard Donner’s influential classic, even if the aforementioned Hollywood Homicide was one of the worst possible spiritual successors.

Even if he’s cut the most amount of slack, for talking’s sake, explain Air Force One: that’s nothing but an action flick, to the point that the easiest way to describe it is as Die Hard on the president’s plane. It’s not a thriller, it’s not a comedy, it’s not a fantasy, and it’s not an adventure, so, on at least one count, but definitely several more, he’s talking mince.

Maybe he’s reversed his position in the last two decades, because what else could you call The Expendables 3? However, rarely has an actor been more wrong than when Harrison Ford said he’d never made an action movie, seeing as he’s made a lot of them, and many of them are classics.
