History beckons for Waller as Birdman, Autumn Glow win

TheTools · 07.11.2026 07:52 · source · permalink

Chris Waller is on the brink of racing history after Birdman and Autumn Glow closed out the Melbourne Cup carnival with eye-catching wins.

The Sydney-based trainer claimed two of Saturday's three $3 million Group 1 features at Flemington.

Birdman ($5) turned the tables on Cox Plate champion Cosmic Crusader ($3.30) in the Champions Stakes, while star stablemate Autumn Glow ($1.18) battled to victory in the Champions Mile.

It gave Waller four of the eight Group 1 titles across the carnival after chalking up the Victoria Derby-Empire Rose Stakes double a week ago.

His Group 1 win tally for 2026 stands at 27, one shy of Aidan O'Brien's calendar year world record of 28.

Ben Melham's ride on Birdman in the 2000m main event was a tactical masterclass, coming from dead last to reel in the field.

"In the Cox Plate, he burst through and thought he was the winner, but he couldn't see the horses out wide with his winkers on," Melham said.

"I got onto the back of Cosmic Crusader ... and I was happy just to smoke the pipe and drag it out for as long as I could."

Waller was made to sweat as stablemate Lindermann ($16) took up the fight to Autumn Glow in the Champions Mile, with Buckaroo ($15) rounding out the places. 

Jockey James McDonald said the star miler, who has won 15 of her 16 races, got a little "lost at sea" while running in the Melbourne direction for the first time.

"Even when I straightened and gave her a click, she took about 50m to get going," he said of his ride.

"But boy once she opened the shoulders up, she was never going to get rolled."

Waller, who saddled wonder mare Winx across her 33-race winning streak, said Autumn Glow was as good as any horse he had trained.

"She could beat Winx on her day and Winx could probably beat her up on Winx's best day pretty easy, in fact, especially over 2000m," he said.

Earlier, Point Barrow ($19) upstaged Everest star Tempted and five-time Group 1 winner Joliestar to claim the $3 million Champions Sprint.

Mark Zahra steered the Anthony and Sam Freedman-trained mare to her first Group 1 win, beating Plaintiff and Sky Bird in a blanket finish.

It was the third time Zahra has won the sprint race after previous triumphs with Giga Kick in 2025 and Santa Ana Lane in 2018.

The star jockey said he didn't like the ground out wide so went back to the inside after following Tempted, who ended Hong Kong star Ka Ying Rising's 21-race winning streak in the $20 million Everest.

"I always say the fresh horse on the scene that hasn't been to the Everest, they're always the ones to beat, and she got the chips," he said.
