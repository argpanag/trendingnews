Star sobs on fellow American’s shoulder after epic

Incredible scenes in New York as Alex Michelsen sobs on Frances Tiafoe’s shoulder after an epic all-American quarter-final.

Tiafoe, the 11th seed, won 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6 (6).

He recovered from two sets and a break down to reach the semi-finals for a third time in a thrilling fightback.

Tiafoe appeared to have lost all the magic of his early campaign before wresting the momentum in the third set and clinching it with a forehand winner.

He will next play either eighth seed Ben Shelton or defending champion Carlos Alcaraz.

Tiafoe thrived in his beloved Louis Armstrong Stadium this week but struggled to hit his stride on the Arthur Ashe court, sending four winners versus his opponent's 15 in the opening set, before handing over the critical break on set point.

Alex Michelsen and Frances Tiafoe of the United States embrace.  Getty Images

Michelsen did some of his best work running up on the net in the second set, where he got the decisive break with a fine backhand strike, and he sent another shot down the line to break in the opening game of the third.

But Tiafoe dug in his heels, levelling it in the 10th game as Michelsen lost his poise, and the veteran slammed his foot on the gas with another break on set point before winning the first three games of the penultimate set.

The two brought their best tennis to a nerve-searing final set, where the crowd sprang to their feet as Tiafoe sealed the win before embracing an emotional Michelsen after closing out the four-hour and 38-minute contest.

Superstar reveals her home club has been bombed

Ukrainian tennis star Elina Svitolina has announced that a Russian attack has hit the padel club she owns in Kyiv.

“As of today, 776 Ukrainian athletes and sports officials have been killed as a result of Russian aggression,” Svitolina said on Instagram.

“942 sports facilities across Ukraine have been damaged or completely destroyed. Today, my club, SVITO Padel Club, became one of them.

“Ukrainian athletes and our young generation do not have the opportunity to train and prepare for international competitions on equal terms with athletes from other countries.

“With this message, I want to reach out to our international partners and friends who support and cheer for us, Ukrainian athletes, on the international stage, and ask for your support and concrete actions that can help stop the aggressor.”

Noskova plans to ‘get drunk and forget’

Linda Noskova plans to drown her sorrows and enjoy New York after the Wimbledon champion came agonisingly close to a stunning upset against world number one Aryna Sabalenka in the US Open quarter-finals.

The Czech led by a break in the deciding set and held an advantage in the final tiebreak before eventually falling 7-6(1) 3-6 7-6(10-7) to the two-time defending champion in a gruelling three-hour battle at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

Rather than mope about the defeat, the 21-year-old has decided to make the most of the Big Apple before heading home.

Linda Noskova of Czechia. Getty Images

“I’m going to stay for, like, three more days. I’m going to get drunk and forget about this, I guess,” Noskova told reporters with a laugh.

“I’m old enough to do that, thankfully. I love New York. I have so many places I want to go. I have a lot of great friends here. They will show me around and, you know, I will forget about it hopefully in the next couple of hours.”

There was plenty worth forgetting after Noskova repeatedly pushed top seed Sabalenka to the limit, only to watch the Belarusian fight back in the third set and prevail in the tiebreak.

Michelsen melting down after blowing lead

American young gun Alex Michelsen was on the verge of upsetting countryman Frances Tiafoe in the quarter finals.

The 22-year-old was absolutely cruising, winning the first two sets with relative ease and then only narrowly losing the third 7-5 to the more experienced Tiafoe.

But Michelsen is starting to melt down after being broken early in the fourth set.

During his service game he turns around and sprays a fan sitting behind him.

A couple of points later he is throwing his racquet on the ground and yelling at his box.

“First real visible signs of irritation from Michelsen,” Jim Courier says on the broadcast.

“He has been pretty buttoned up, with only positive emotion coming out of him, until now.”

This would be a stunning comeback from No.11 seed Tiafoe if he can pull off victory.

Sabalenka’s odd response to losing No.1 spot

Aryna Sabalenka has been the women’s world No.1 for an astonishing 99 weeks straight.

In fact since Australia’s own Ash Barty forfeited the position by retiring in 2022, only Sabalenka and Iga Swiatek have been No.1 in the women’s game.

But Sabalenka is on the verge of losing the title to Elena Rybakina.

Rybakina only has to beat Zheng Qinwen in their semi final to become world No.1 for the first time.

Sabalenka was asked about the situation this morning and claims she doesn’t care if she loses the No.1 spot.

“Yes, I’ll be more than happy,” she said,

“Whatever, I don’t mind.”
