Leclerc and Hamilton address aftermath of Las Vegas clash

TheTools · 28.11.2026 10:05 · source · permalink

After contact limited them to the lower end of the points in Las Vegas, Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton have insisted they’re working towards a solution to avoid further intra-team incidents.

Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton have opened up on the mood within Ferrari after they clashed during the Las Vegas Grand Prix, with the former admitting that “we’re all disappointed” despite ongoing efforts to improve their on-track relationship.

Following incidents in Italy and Mexico, the pair made contact when Leclerc attempted a pass on his team mate in Las Vegas, resulting in a 10-second time penalty that dropped the Monegasque to P10 in the final classification.

An unrelated penalty for Hamilton contributed to his own P7 finish and rounded out a frustrating weekend for the Scuderia, especially as both had made significant progress after being eliminated in the second stage of Qualifying.

Although Team Principal Fred Vasseur previously confirmed that he had held talks to address how they race one another, he lamented their "lack of respect” in the “unacceptable” episode – a comment Leclerc readily agreed with.

“Fred had already talked on the radio, telling me he wanted to speak to me before [I went] to the media, so I obviously knew where Fred was standing,” Leclerc explained during media duties in Qatar. “As a driver, you’ve got to accept that.

“As a team, we cannot afford to clash between team mates. I think that is very clear for everyone. For the clash in particular in Las Vegas, I took responsibility. I think there’s tension around that we just need to dissipate and make sure that we look at more positive days in the future. That’s all I’m looking for.

“At the end of the day, we are two very strong drivers that want to finish one ahead of the other. Unfortunately, whether it’s here or Monza or Mexico, there have been a bit too many events lately. We need to find a solution as a team to make sure this kind of thing doesn’t happen again.”

He also admitted that he and Hamilton had already spoken ahead of Round 21 in Qatar, but was reluctant to disclose what “solutions” had been proposed within the team.

“We have a very good relationship outside of the car with Lewis,” he said. “I wouldn’t say the atmosphere is good because obviously we are all disappointed with the situation we find ourselves in.

“At the same time, we’re all grown up and responsible so we’ve got to find a solution. Surely there’s not a super happy feeling at the moment. We are the ones that need to find a solution in order to make that better.

“The only thing that I don’t like seeing is people questioning my integrity to the team, my love for the team. I’ve been in Ferrari since 2019. I think there is nothing clearer than the love I have for the team. I’ll do absolutely everything in order to bring Ferrari back to the top.

“Of course mistakes happen along the way and always will. We just try to minimise them, particularly between team mates. I think it’s more the question mark of that that I have seen going around that hurts me more than anything else. The rest is a lot of noise and it’s not the most enjoyable time, but it’s part of the game and I have to accept that.”

While the titles have already been won by Mercedes and Kimi Antonelli respectively, P2 in the Constructors’ Championship is still to play for. Ferrari currently hold a healthy advantage of 77 points over Red Bull, but as Max Verstappen has clinched four victories in six races, they’re still targeting a clean end to the season.

Hamilton – who has experience with intra-team battles, including an unforgettable one with his Mercedes team mate Nico Rosberg – asserted that securing second place is his main priority in the remaining races, with conversations around the rules of engagement a task for the winter break.

He said: “It's interesting, you work with different people along the way and I had great battles with many different team mates in the past and we always figured things out and ended up moving forward in a positive way, which I've no doubt will be the same with Charles.

“We learn the most when we go through these difficult, challenging phases and times and so naturally through those we try and look to see how we can do things better moving forwards. That's just normal, that's what we do as a team to continue to try and elevate.

“You've got to continuously be evolving and I think sitting and having good, open conversations is only positive for what we're trying to build. I still believe entirely in what we're working towards and the amazing job I think everyone back at the factory has done this year.

“We're going to focus on making sure we maximise these next two weekends because it's key to making sure we get second in the constructors' for the team, and then we'll go away and we'll make improvements on processes, on how we engage and communicate and continue to build on that.”

Asked what Ferrari need to do to avoid similar crises, Hamilton added: “I don't have the answer for that. It's not my job, it's not my role, I'm not going to put together what should happen.

“We'll continue to just have good conversations, I think that's the most important thing. It's for Fred to decide the pathway moving forwards as our leader, and I think he can do that.

“We're still free to race and I think that's the ultimate goal being here. We want to race. But of course we don't want what happened last week to ever happen and so we communicate.

“We do enjoy racing together and we do have a really good relationship. It's obviously intense in the background. On the outside it seems a lot more intense than it really is inside.

“I personally don't feel tension. I love racing and I think it's more built up probably outside than I think inside. I think our goal is to get P2 in the constructors' for the team, so we have to work really well together as a team to deliver those results.”
