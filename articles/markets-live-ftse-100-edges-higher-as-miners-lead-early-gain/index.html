Last updated: 03:26 04 Nov 2026 EST, First published: 02:00 04 Nov 2026 EST

 

  • FTSE 100 up 18 points to 10,656
  • Miners lead early gains
  • Defence stocks under pressure
  • Copper remains above US$14,000

8.15 am: FTSE 100 opens higher

The FTSE 100 edged 18 points, or 0.2%, higher to 10,656 shortly after the open, recovering part of Tuesday’s decline.

Mining stocks led early gains, with Fresnillo up 4%, Endeavour Mining up 3.2% and Antofagasta up 2.9%.

Metals prices remained elevated despite some overnight weakness, with three-month copper still around US$14,000 a tonne on the London Metal Exchange.

HSBC gained 3%, while Lion Finance advanced 3.8%.

Defence stocks were among the main fallers, with BAE Systems down 4.6% and Babcock International 2.4% lower, while Weir fell 4.5%.

 

7.00 am: FTSE set for another weak start

The FTSE 100 is set to open sharply lower on Wednesday, with futures trading around 10,545, pointing to a fall of roughly 90 points from Tuesday’s close of 10,638.

London is taking its cue from a heavy sell-off across Asia, where Japan’s Nikkei 225 tumbled 3.6%, and South Korea’s Kospi fell more than 2% as investors cut exposure to technology and semiconductor stocks.

Wall Street provided a mixed lead overnight, with the S&P 500 rising 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite gaining 0.8%, while the Dow Jones fell 0.4%.

Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 was down around 0.6% at 8,832, with financial and healthcare stocks among the main drags after the Reserve Bank of Australia raised the cash rate to 4.85% on Tuesday.

The broader risk-off mood has also spread into commodities, with copper down around 1.6% and silver nearly 1% lower.

Gold was slightly firmer near $3,947 an ounce, while Brent crude slipped towards $103 a barrel.

Bitcoin has slipped below $77,000 as cryptocurrency markets remain under pressure from the fallout of the record Binance hack, heavy liquidations and renewed caution across risk assets.

The FTSE 100 fell 1.1% on Tuesday to 10,638, leaving London facing a second consecutive session of selling pressure.
