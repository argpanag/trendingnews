Lakers Weigh Trading Cameron Carr for Frontcourt Help

TheTools · 24.11.2026 04:06 · source · permalink

The Los Angeles Lakers are weighing whether to trade rookie Cameron Carr to strengthen their frontcourt alongside Luka Doncic as the franchise explores roster improvements ahead of the NBA trade deadline. Published reports on November 23, 2026, indicate that the 22-year-old guard could become a valuable trade asset as the team searches for another center and better defensive rebounding.

According to Bleacher Report's Zach Buckley, rival teams have shown little interest in exchanging useful players for the Lakers' available future second-round draft picks. An NBA executive told Sean Deveney of Heavy.com that Carr could attract interest if Los Angeles makes him available in negotiations for frontcourt reinforcements.

The Lakers selected Carr with the 24th overall pick in the 2026 NBA Draft after trading one position ahead to acquire him from the New York Knicks. His potential as a versatile perimeter defender and three-point shooter had generated considerable attention before the draft.

However, opportunities have been limited during his rookie season. Heavy.com reported on November 23 that Carr was averaging 5.3 minutes per game, had appeared in only seven games and had already been assigned to the G League.

Los Angeles general manager Rob Pelinka has indicated that the organization intends to pursue improvements before the trade deadline while maintaining its competitive ambitions.

"We'll be active at the trade deadline to shore up things that we see. We think this team has a real chance to make a run, so we want to give the group as much talent as we can," Pelinka said.

The Lakers' immediate priorities include finding dependable support for starting center Walker Kessler. Veteran Kevon Looney has moved further down the rotation, leaving the team without a reliable backup option, according to Heavy.com.

Defensive rebounding presents another significant concern. Bleacher Report identified Los Angeles as ranking 29th in defensive rebounding percentage, highlighting an area that additional frontcourt personnel could address.

Trading Carr would nevertheless mean giving up a young prospect whose development could benefit the franchise over the longer term. His athleticism, defensive versatility and potential offensive contributions make him an attractive developmental asset, although his limited playing time has restricted his immediate impact.

Sean Deveney previously outlined the possibility of using Carr to pursue a more substantial roster upgrade, particularly with the Lakers evaluating their championship prospects in the Western Conference.

"But the Lakers do have an asset with value on hand right now: rookie Cameron Carr. He got his first real minutes last week, but on a roster loaded with wings, he is not likely to get any time this season. It would be absolutely shortsighted to deal away a 21-year-old first-round pick, but if the Lakers think they can contend in a West that has seen the Thunder hobbled, maybe they should be shortsighted. The Lakers do not have a first-round pick to trade, but they do have Carr," Deveney wrote.

Los Angeles also has less aggressive alternatives. The November 20 report identified packaging remaining second-round selections with salary-matching players as another possible approach to acquiring a backup center.

Potential frontcourt targets mentioned in that report included Jalen Smith of the Chicago Bulls, Karlo Matkovic of the New Orleans Pelicans and Daniel Gafford of the Dallas Mavericks. The Athletic's Dan Woike had also reported that the Lakers were monitoring opportunities to add another big man.

Despite the speculation, no agreement involving Carr has been confirmed. The Lakers' front office must weigh the immediate benefits of adding experienced frontcourt support against the long-term potential of its 2026 first-round selection.
