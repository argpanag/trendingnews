14 Years Later, Bruce Willis' 'Mind-Bending' Sci-Fi Is Still the Best Time Travel Movie Ever

TheTools · 04.09.2026 20:00 · source · permalink

Time travel is a notoriously tricky concept to pull off convincingly, but Rian Johnson solved that issue with one of the best sci-fi movies of its time. His 2012 sci-fi drama, Looper, starring Bruce Willis, is the best time travel movie of all time, and that is not an easy task.

Films like Back to the Future and The Terminator play with the rules of time travel, but Looper defines them. Before his lucrative Knives Out franchise, Johnson proved he had more than a handle on the sci-fi genre. Looper is clever, humorous, and more fulfilling than any time travel movie that came before.

Looper Solves a Common Time Travel Issue

The biggest issues with time travel movies are the likelihood of time paradoxes. Mike & Nick & Nick & Alice is one of the most engaging time travel experiences in recent memory, but it falters when it comes to the execution of the genre. Looper cleverly does away with the potential for inconsistencies by making a world that is extremely streamlined.

The rules of Looper are fairly straightforward for a lore-heavy time travel movie. 30 years into the future, time travel has been invented but is quickly outlawed. The only people who harness the technology are mobsters who send targets back in time to the present, where they can be executed. This leaves no paper trail since, in the present, time travel doesn’t exist yet.

These assassins are called Loopers, and Joe, played by Joseph Gordon-Levitt, is one of the smarter ones. This all changes when he has to close his loop by killing his future self. Bruce Willis plays future Joe in a time travel movie that triumphs over all others. This succinct concept alleviates any confusion about the rules of time travel pretty explicitly.

Rian Johnson sticks to the basics that the future can be changed. Old Joe wants to do this by traveling back in time and killing the mob boss known as the Rainmaker. With him dead in the past, there is no one to close the loop, and Joe can protect his future wife from her grisly fate.

What exposition exists in the story is minimal, as the narrative is largely character-focused. This is classic Johnson territory, who never drops the ball when it comes to story. He sets up a near-immaculate sci-fi drama and accomplishes the near-impossible task of making a perfect time travel film.

The Perfect Time Travel Story Is About History Repeating Itself

Like most Rian Johnson stories, Looper is carefully crafted to create an emotional throughline that works on virtually every level. The narrative doesn’t just work as one of the best time travel movies of all time, but also operates on a higher story level.

Looper does this by showing how many loops occur in the story. It demonstrates this through many themes, like child abandonment and violence. Young Joe laments a mother he never knew who gave him up to pay for drugs. While at first he can’t conceive of making that choice, he ultimately does and becomes a drug addict just like her.

History repeats itself when Young Joe meets Cid, who has early childhood abandonment issues himself. The biggest loop in the time travel story is the cycle of violence. Looper considers violence to be a never-ending circle, and demonstrates this quite literally through time travel.

Older Joe is intent on killing Cid before he becomes the Rainmaker and can start closing loops. However, it is Joe’s action in the first place that radicalizes Cid and turns him into a monster. Older Joe is the one who potentially kills Cid’s mother and starts a reign of terror.

The only way to truly save all the innocents caught in the crossfire is by ensuring Cid never becomes the Rainmaker. With Sarah in his life, she would raise him to be good, so he would never make those choices. Every time travel movie needs a level of irony, and Looper does it best.

Young Joe closes this loop by the end of the story, but not in the way that he envisioned. Though he was tasked with killing his older self, he realizes that the only way to save Cid is to ensure that Old Joe doesn’t kill Sarah. The answer is obvious, and Young Joe makes it quickly.

He closes the loop by taking his own life, ensuring that Old Joe ceases to exist. That is the way to end a time travel movie, and Johnson pulls it off seamlessly.

Looper Ends Before Any Inconsistencies Can Arise

Time travel movies get into trouble when there are too many questions being asked. Avengers: Endgame and Back to the Future fix this by making alternate timelines, but the smoothest time travel stories accomplish their goals by making the simplest decisions.

Rian Johnson doesn’t attempt to save Joe or create a world where everyone gets what they want. The best way to end the story is the easiest, which is taking Joe out of the equation. He is where the trouble all starts.

Older Joe needs to save his wife from dying in the future, so he decides to kill anyone who threatens that possibility. He refuses any and all solutions where he can’t have it all. He insists on staying with her even though it endangers her life, instead choosing to literally kill children.

He is the thorn in the side of the movie, and closing the loop is the only way everyone else makes it out alive. With Young Joe dead, his future wife never meets him and never has the chance to die. Cid never has the chance to become the Rainmaker.

This is the simplest way to conclude the story, which ends right when it needs to. There are no attempts to make a happy ending. Once Joe has died, Looper doesn’t stick around to risk any time paradoxes. There may be many questions that come with Joe’s death, but it is the best way to end the movie.

It closes the loop, so to speak, so that there are no time paradoxes. There is only the story of a man who made his life meaningful for one family, even if he couldn’t do it for himself.
