Playoff projections: Local state title hopes lie with Seminole, DeLand, Bishop Moore, Jones, TFA

TheTools · 05.11.2026 20:54 · source · permalink

The final Florida high school football regular-season MaxPreps rankings will not be released until after the Florida High School Athletics Association announces its postseason playoff brackets at 5 p.m., Friday (YouTube), so we have taken on the task of trying to figure out the much-maligned MaxPreps algorithm that determines who makes the playoffs and the seeds.

The best hopes for winning a state title among teams from the Orlando area appear to be with the five teams that top the Sentinel’s Super 16 Coaches Poll. While all of the 30 area teams expected to gain playoff berths are eyeing the top prize, Orlando’s best bets are undefeated Seminole (10-0) or No. 1 DeLand (9-1) in 6A, Bishop Moore (8-2) and Jones (7-3) in 4A and The First Academy (7-3) in 2A. Oviedo (7-3) and Evans (7-3) are the best local bets in 5A.

Jones has made back-to-back 4A state championship games and Bishop Moore reached a 3A semifinal last season. TFA is entering it’s first postseason since being banned for two years by the FHSAA. DeLand made it to the state semifinals three seasons ago.

Among the best in the new Independent Football League are Innovation and Windermere, the favorites in the large-school Pool A, while Mount Dora Christian, Orangewood Christian and Central Florida Christian have high hopes in Pool B. MDCA made it the the Sunshine State Athletic Association championship last season.

This the first year the FHSAA has gone solely with MaxPreps rankings for its playoff selections, as requested by a majority of state coaches. However, having gone through this first season, many are now baffled as to how the algorithm works.

In the forefront of the controversy are a pair of sub-.500 Orlando-area teams: Lake Mary, which has only won two games, and Osceola-Kissimmee, which has won three.

The latest MaxPreps rankings have both teams projected to easily qualify in their respective classes, with Lake Mary (2-8) looking like the No. 4 seed in Class 6A Region 1, and Osceola (3-7) the No. 3 seed in 5A Region 2.

Most of the playoff positions appear to be set going into Friday’s 5 p.m. FHSAA YouTube channel reveal show, but there will still be some Orlando-area coaches and players on the edges of their seats as the playoff spots are unveiled.

The controversy in 6A sits with Lake Brantley and Hagerty and the battle for the final spot in Region 1. The top seven appear to be locked in, but the Brantley-Hagerty scenario is mesmerizing. If the decision was based on head-to-head matchups, Brantley would have clinched the final spot after blowing out Hagerty on Friday 56-0. But this is the computer algorithm era, and it’s not that simple.

Lake Brantley was sitting 0.29 decimal points ahead of Hagerty according to the last MaxPreps ratings. Hagerty, however, gained another victory Tuesday to move to 6-4, while Brantley’s season was complete at 5-5. The question: Was Hagerty’s 20-15 victory over 4A New Smyrna Beach enough to vault the Huskies past the Patriots?

Two teams Lake Brantley played gained victories this week, including Hagerty, so that should help the Patriots. But will it be enough? Timber Creek’s loss to Apopka will work against Hagerty. The combined rating numbers for Hagerty’s opponents are 34 points higher than the combined number for Lake Brantley’s opponents, however, that was already true last week when Brantley had the edge.

We are going to go out on a limb here and side in favor of logic, although we know that’s not part of the computer algorithm’s process. We could be wrong, but we foresee Lake Brantley as the No. 8 seed in 6A Region 2.

One other team that appeared to be on the outside last week, Lake Howell (6-4), snuck back into contention after a victory over a decent St. Cloud squad. The Silverhawks added a forfeiture over Viera, so we are thinking Lake Howell will have enough computer points to keep Auburndale from knocking them out of their first playoff appearance in 30 years. Lake Howell looks to be the No. 7 seed in 5A-2.

Also in 5A, Evans (7-3) sits in a favorable position, but only by 0.37 decimal points ahead of Pace (7-2). At stake is the rights to play at home. The two teams will face a rematch of  last year’s first-round game and both are district champions, but one of them will have to make the six-hour road trip.

Evans had to travel deep into the panhandle last year and lost to Pace 20-6. Trojans coach Karlos Odum made a move to prepare his season by scheduling a Kickoff Classic game at Wakulla this season. But he is obviously hoping he doesn’t have to make that Interstate 10 trip again. The Wakulla game was canceled due to weather, but his team knows what it’s like to make a long bus ride. We think Evans will get the No. 4 seed and stay home.

There is an issue in the MaxPreps rankings in the Independent Football League. MaxPreps has The Master’s Academy (3-7) of Oviedo listed among its SSAA rankings, and we missed it the first time around, but the Eagles actually hold the eighth position in the small-school Pool B North Division. Master’s will possibly face No. 1 seed Mount Dora Christian, which we have moved to the top spot after Jay lost to Marianna on Wednesday night.

Should MDCA remain in the No. 2 spot, where the Bulldogs were as of Sunday’s MaxPreps update, Master’s will have to make the 6-plus hour drive to the Panhandle to face Jay in the first round. If Jay drops to No. 2, that will mean No. 7 Geneva School makes the Panhandle roadtrip.

Master’s is one of seven Independent teams with losing records projected as playoff qualifiers, among which is also Oak Ridge (4-6) and Pine Ridge (1-8). Trinity Prep, which will likely be left out of the mix, is 5-4 but sits 10th, behind Milton Central (2-4), which played only six games due to weather issues in the Panhandle.

In Pool B South, Lake Highland is hoping it can gain enough points in the final rankings to move up two spots from No. 10 and slide into the playoffs. But the final spot is looking more like a battle between St. John Paul II of Boca Raton and Pine Crest of Fort Lauderdale.

We are projecting to have 19 area teams in five classes in the FHSAA state series brackets, with no representatives in 3A. There are 12 area teams projected to take part in the Independent playoffs, which are split into two different tournaments, with Pool A for for large schools, and Pool B for small schools.

Higher seeds will be on the road in some cases because district champions are guaranteed home games (first round only) unless they are playing higher-seeded district foes.
