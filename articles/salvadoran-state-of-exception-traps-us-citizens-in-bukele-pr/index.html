Salvadoran State of Exception Traps US Citizens in Bukele Prisons

TheTools · 22.09.2026 10:06 · source · permalink

Multiple US citizens visiting El Salvador have been arrested and held in local prisons without access to legal defense or trials under President Nayib Bukele's ongoing state of exception targeting street gangs.

Family members confirm that detainees were targeted based on body tattoos or alleged nervous behavior, with communication restricted entirely to occasional updates provided through the US embassy in San Salvador.

Construction worker José Martínez, 50, was detained in Ahuachapán while showing his wife, Patricia González, the country he left at age 14. Police questioned a three-dot hand tattoo before arresting him.

"In the United States it is not prohibited," said Martínez.

"In the United States no, but here yes," said the police officer.

Police armed with long rifles searched the couple, took their money, and denied requests to retrieve their US passports from home before accusing Martínez of terrorism at the station.

"If I were Salvadoran, I would also be detained," said González from her home in Compton, California.

"His only mistake was going to his country, a place he missed so much," said González.

"I told him: 'May God bless you.' He gave me a hug and a kiss. It was the last one," said González.

Martínez has been held at the Santa Ana prison for nearly 10 months without contact, joining other naturalized and native-born American citizens swept up in the security dragnet.

Among them is 31-year-old construction worker and rap composer Nelson Vladimir Hernández Tovar, who disappeared into police custody during a December 2022 trip to visit his girlfriend after being labeled suspicious.

"My son was captured by the police and says they saw him as suspicious, that he was nervous. Now they say he is part of a gang," said Carlos Hernández, the father of Nelson.

"When he went, first my fear was because of the gangs, that they would do something to him. Well now it is not the gangs, now it is the government itself with the police, which has the authority to do and undo," said Hernández.

Similarly, 33-year-old Los Angeles-native Walter Josué Huete Alvarado remains imprisoned after traveling to El Salvador for a consular errand regarding his daughter, subsequently facing allegations of MS-13 membership.

The state of exception emergency measure has extended past four years, leading to over 90,000 arrests representing roughly two percent of the nation's population.
