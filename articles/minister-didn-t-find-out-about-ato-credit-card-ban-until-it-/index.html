Almost 80,000 older Australians expected to ditch private health over change

S

By Stephanie Dalzell

By Stephanie Dalzell

Nearly 80,000 older Australians are expected to dump their private health cover by the end of the decade as a result of the federal government's controversial proposed changes.

The latest estimate is nearly double the 44,000 figure repeatedly cited by the federal government in defence of the policy.

The proposal would wind back the rebate Australians aged 65 and over receive on their private health cover, banking about $3 billion to spend elsewhere in the aged care system.

However, health department analysis released under Freedom of Information (FOI) laws reveals that while 44,000 Australians are expected to drop their cover in 2028-29, that is forecast to rise to a cumulative total of 78,000 by the following year.

The Coalition says Health Minister Mark Butler has consistently used the smaller figure to defend the policy.

"Either he didn't read his own briefs, or he's been deliberately misleading older Australians," Shadow Health Minister Anne Ruston said.

"Either he didn't read his own briefs, or he's been deliberately misleading older Australians," Shadow Health Minister Anne Ruston said.

A federal government spokesperson said the changes were about "making the private health insurance rebates fairer" and the overall number of people with private health insurance would continue to increase.

By Jane Norman

Trade Minister Don Farrell has issued a rallying cry to the business sector to back Australia's free trade agreement (FTA) with the European Union, as the Coalition prepares to oppose the landmark deal in federal parliament.

The FTA was signed in 2026 after eight long years of negotiations, allowing 98 per cent of Australia's export to enter the market of 450 million people duty free.
