Should You Use Apple Upgrade to Pay for the iPhone Duo? Here's the Real Cost

TheTools · 14.10.2026 16:30 · source · permalink

The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max have been out for nearly a month, and now it's time for Apple's first foldable, the iPhone Duo. Apple's iPhone Duo will launch on October 23, with preorders starting on October 16.

The Duo will start at $1,999, and if you can't afford to buy it outright, you have options. You can trade in your old device to shave some money off of your bill, finance with your carrier, or lease a device through Apple's newly launched Upgrade program.

Here's how much the iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro, and iPhone 18 Pro Max cost through Apple Upgrade, and whether or not this leasing option makes sense for you.

How Much Is the iPhone Duo With Apple Upgrade?

The iPhone Duo will retail for between $1,999 and $3,199. Apple Upgrade plans for the iPhone Duo will start at $57.99 per month for a 24-month lease and $82.99 for a 12-month lease.

How Much Are the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max With Apple Upgrade?

If you're looking to get an iPhone 18 model, the Pro starts at a retail price of $1,199, while the Pro Max starts at $1,299. If you want to lease the iPhone 18 Pro through Apple Upgrade, prices will start at $34.99 per month for a 24-month lease and $49.99 per month for a 12-month lease. For the Pro Max, you'll pay $37.99 per month for 24 months or $53.99 per month over 12 months.

How Does Apple Upgrade Work?

In partnership with "buy now, pay later" (BNPL) platform Klarna, Apple Upgrade is a new leasing program that launched on July 28, which lets you pay a monthly fee for select iPhones and Apple Watches for 24 months and MacBooks and iPads for 36 months. You'll then have the option to return the device, pay off the balance and keep it, or upgrade to a new device.

Eligibility will be based on a credit check. For an iPhone, it'll need to be connected to AT&T, T-Mobile, or Verizon when you enroll.

This new service replaces Apple's iPhone Upgrade program. If you're currently a member of the iPhone Upgrade Program, your loan agreement with Citizens One remains active, and you’ll continue making your monthly payments until your 24-month term is complete, when "the iPhone will be yours to keep."

Does Apple Upgrade Charge Interest or Fees?

Neither Apple nor Klarna mentions any interest payments. Apple's current iPhone Upgrade Program is a zero-interest installment loan, and so was Apple Pay Later, which the company ditched in 2024.

Apple says there are no fees on a monthly lease payment, but notes that you could incur a damage fee at the end of your lease term if you don't take care of the device. There are also fees if you upgrade or terminate a lease early.

Plus, if you don't decide what to do at the end of your lease, Klarna will start an "automatic extension period" for up to 6 months. "During this extension, your monthly payment may increase because promotional credits (such as trade-in credits) only apply to the original lease term. If you still make no decision by the end of the extension, Klarna will charge a one-time payment, otherwise known as the Purchase Option Fee, to pay off the device. You will be notified 1 month in advance if your next payment will be the final payment."

There were reports that Apple would restrict access to certain apps if payments were late, but sources tell PCMag that's not the case. "If a payment cannot be collected, Klarna will retry," Klarna says. "If that also fails, Klarna will attempt to charge a backup payment method on file. There are no late fees for this program. However, missed payments can eventually be sent to debt collection after multiple failed attempts, and missed payments may be reflected in your credit report."

Should You Use Apple Upgrade to Lease a New iPhone or Mac?

There are some scenarios where leasing an Apple product makes sense. Maybe you need a MacBook for grad school, but don't plan to keep it after you graduate. Or, perhaps you're already upgrading to the newest iPhone every year, and leasing will save you money. But even if the math works in your favor, make sure you understand all of the risks before you sign up.

Which Products Can You Lease Through Apple Upgrade?

While Apple Upgrade is available for most newer Apple devices, some older and budget versions are not available. You cannot use Apple Upgrade for the iPhone 16, iPhone 16 Plus, Apple Watch SE, MacBook Neo, Mac mini, iPad (A16), or Studio Display.

You can currently lease the following phones, MacBooks, iPads, and Apple Watches with Apple Upgrade:

iPhones:

  • iPhone Duo
  • iPhone 18 Pro
  • iPhone 18 Pro Max
  • iPhone Air
  • iPhone 17
  • iPhone 17 e

Macs:

  • MacBook Air
  • MacBook Pro
  • iMac
  • Mac Studio

iPads:

  • iPad Pro
  • iPad Air
  • iPad Mini

Apple Watches:

  • Apple Watch Series 12
  • Apple Watch Ultra 4
  • Apple Watch Hermès Series 12
  • Apple Watch Hermès Ultra 4

How Much Does It Cost to Lease Other Apple Products?

The main draw of Apple Upgrade is its low prices. Here are the starting prices by Apple device:

  • iPad - $11.99 per month
  • iPhone - $17.99 per month
  • Apple Watch - $11.99 per month
  • MacBook - $24.99 per month

These are just starting prices and will vary by product (and possibly your credit score). Leasing an iPhone 17, which costs $899 to buy, starts at $25.99 per month to lease for 24 months and $36.99 to lease for 12 months. However, Apple says you can trade in your old device to lower your lease payments.

For comparison, Apple's now-defunct iPhone Upgrade plan charges $42.41 per month for an iPhone 17 with 256GB of storage over a 24-month term.

What's the Catch?

We're glad you asked. There are a few big risks you should understand before you sign up for Apple Upgrade.

Apple Upgrade Does Not Include AppleCare+

Apple Upgrade will not include AppleCare+, which protects your device against certain accidental damage. You'll have to add and pay for AppleCare+ Theft and Loss coverage yourself within 60 days of your lease starting. Without that protection included, you're on the hook for any damage. "You may incur damage fees if the device is...not returned in the condition required by the lease," Apple notes. If you don't have AppleCare+ Theft and Loss coverage, and the device is lost or stolen, you'll have to pay an early termination fee or the purchase option fee. If you do nothing, you'll continue to be charged the monthly lease fee.

You May Get Trapped in a Cycle of Monthly Payments

Much like streaming services require you to pay every month to continue to access your favorite movies, TV shows, and music, Apple Upgrade could keep you hooked on monthly payments for decades. Unlike financing an iPhone or MacBook, Apple Upgrade leases don't cover the full cost of the device, so when the end of your lease term arrives, you'll be on the hook for a large balloon payment, the Purchase Option Fee, if you want to buy your device outright.

As an example, let's look at the cost of leasing the iPhone Duo (which starts at $1,999). Lease prices start at $57.99, so if you leased this phone for 24 months, you'd have paid $1,391.76 after 24 months. So, you'd need to pay at least another $607.24 at one time in order to purchase and keep the phone.

If you can't afford to make this large lump-sum payment on time, the easiest solution is to lease another device, essentially locking you into another couple of years of monthly payments. Suddenly, you're trapped in an endless cycle of monthly fees for a device you don't even own.
