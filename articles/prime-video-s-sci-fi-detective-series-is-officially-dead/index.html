Prime Video's Sci-Fi Detective Series Is Officially Dead

TheTools · 04.09.2026 21:20 · source · permalink

Few genres have thrived on Prime Video quite like the crime thriller. From Reacher to Cross and the extended Bosch universe, many of the biggest recent success stories for the platform lean on the grounded and gritty end of entertainment, being driven by their complicated, proficient, and often deadly lead investigators. One of the streamer's latest entries into the genre, however, came not from a bestselling detective novel, but the pages of Marvel Comics — Spider-Noir. Premiering at the end of May on Amazon's flagship service and its sister streamer, MGM+, the series featured Nicolas Cage in his first-ever series regular role on television, playing the titular superhero crime-solver in live-action after last voicing another version of the character in Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse.

While it launched as a massive success, earning 11 Emmy nominations for its distinct noir style and plenty of acclaim from both critics and audiences, the office of Ben Reilly is now being closed for good. Prime Video has officially cancelled Spider-Noir after just one season, meaning the troubled Spider's story will end with just eight episodes. It's a bit of a shock considering it wasn't just the most-nominated Marvel television series, not named WandaVision, but it debuted with 2.6 billion viewing minutes in its first six weeks. With an unceremonious end, it's not the beginning anyone expected from Amazon's partnership with Sony Pictures to develop Marvel-related projects.

Adapted for television by Oren Uziel and co-showrun by Uziel and Steve Lightfoot, Spider-Noir took place in Great Depression-era New York, where Reilly, an aging, down-on-his-luck private investigator who has abandoned his old life as the city's only superhero following a crushing tragedy. When an extraordinary case comes across his desk that he can't ignore, however, he's forced to wrestle with his past as The Spider and swing back into action to stop the criminals who have taken over the streets, like Silvermane, Sandman, and Megawatt. Cage was accompanied by New Girl star Lamorne Morris as Ben's best friend, Robbie Robertson, and Li Jun Li as Cat Hardy, alongside Karen Rodriguez, Abraham Popoola, Jack Huston, and Brendan Gleeson.

'Spider-Noir's Cancellation Is a Damper on a Big Year for Spider-Man

While Season 1 wrapped things up relatively nicely, with Ben completing his transformation from broken hero to embracing the role of The Spider alongside his friends, just about everyone was interested in doing more Spider-Noir. Executive producers and Spider-Verse duo Christopher Miller and Phil Lord were open to it, and both Cage and Uziel were thinking of ideas for where to take the series and its characters next. The general belief was that there was room for another knock at the door and another case, but instead, the attention will turn to whatever comes next from Sony and Amazon's partnership, which is expected to be announced soon. It's a bit of an unexpected low amid a year of highs for Spider-Man. The web-slinger recently enjoyed a roaring return to the MCU with Spider-Man: Brand New Day to the tune of over $2.34 billion and, in January, Your Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man will return to Disney+ complete with some fan favorites, like Venom.
