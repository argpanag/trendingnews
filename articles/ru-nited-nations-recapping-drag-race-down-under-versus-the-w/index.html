Francis Exequiel AmpilFeatures and Lifestyle2 days ago

They say, at rock bottom, there’s no way but up. 

On Drag Race’s newest international All Star season, however, to be on top, you must first conquer down under.

Released July 24, 2026, Drag Race Down Under Versus the World brings together global glamazons going head to head, toe to toe, and wig to wig, on the way to the title of Queen of the Mother Tucking World and a cash prize of 50,000 AUD, equivalent to ₱2,145,922.00. A spin-off of the formerly RuPaul-hosted Drag Race Down Under, DUVTW is the latest iteration of international All Star seasons, joining “UK Versus the World,” “Canada Versus the World,” “Latina Royale,” and, of course, the Philippines-hosted “Slaysian Royale.”

Among Filipinos, the new series cements itself as a must-watch as the country’s next representative—after Marina Summers, Eva Le Queen, Minty Fresh, and the Filipinas of Slaysian Royale—enters the global spotlight.

M1ss Jade So utters, “I’m calling all the goddesses with me.” The line opens not only the episode but the entire season. And as this premiere shows, the queens who answered Down Under host and franchise icon Michelle Visage’s call are nothing short of worship-worthy.

Guess who’s back in the house

Down Under season 1’s Coco Jumbo—the so-called queen of Sydney—crashes the party first in a black-and-yellow patterned number and hair so high it could touch the heavens. A lip sync assassin, she famously eliminated Art Simone in their original season’s Snatch Game episode in a blow-out performance. Though initially without much to play off of, the more queens join her, the more Coco shows her personality in full force.

Angel of the north Michael Marouli follows her in black-and-gold spikes and even higher hair. Michael racked up a trio of wins—among them, one of the finest roast performances in Drag Race herstory—and finished as second place overall on season 5 of the UK franchise, where she lost the crown to Ginger Johnson.

Third in is Flor: red-lipped, ponytailed, and in a white party-ready gown that hits the floor, no pun intended. Representing Latinos, Kiwis, Aussies, and now apparently the English, she placed third on Down Under season 3, where she won the makeover challenge (with a dog, mind you) and the title of fan favorite. Two years later, she enters a villain era as she stirs the pot with Coco Jumbo.

Next, all the way from Atlanta, Georgia, comes RuPaul’s Drag Race season 2 OG RuGirl, Nicole Paige Brooks: lipstick on her teeth, draped in a hot-pink cloak with a matching shimmering gown. Still winless across her two seasons, the Southern queen hits the southern hemisphere after snatching the attention of old and new fans on All Stars 10, when she feuded with purported drag family Mistress Isabelle Brooks, all with old-school wit and chronically un-online charm.

“That’s a man,” Flor jokes as her Australasian sister, Vybe, struts through the doors next. Finishing runner-up to Lazy Susan on Down Under season 4, she cleared the comedy and choreography challenges with ease. Here, she reunites with frequent co-worker Coco Jumbo when she dives into a delightful bit (“Steve?” “Peter?” “Steve, [it’s] Peter from school!”).

There is also Estrella Xtravaganza who represents one of this season’s two non-English-speaking series was dressed in what can only be described as a ladybug by way of Leigh Bowery: a red-and-black polka dot number with extreme shoulders and hips, plus a prop bag that confuses the girls on what it is supposed to resemble—earning statements “What is that purse? A foot?” and “Oh, I think it’s a—it’s a ham.”

Estrella was a runner-up on Drag Race Espana season 2, where she won two challenges and the title of Miss Hungriest Queen. Upon entering the Werkroom, she immediately forms a connection with fellow Spanish-speaking Flor, who is elated at the presence of another Latina queen in the competition, and fawns at the feet of NPB, who… does not understand her.

Down Under season 4’s Nikita Iman enters next, ready to showcase Polynesian beauty on her second run of Drag Race in a blonde pixie cut and gold chain-link dress. Her season 4 sister Vybe asks if she made her look that morning. Eliminated before the finale, the fashion-heavy Nikita contrasts with the comedy-driven Vybe, who says she is “still healing” from their original run together.

Coming out of retirement for her second All Stars season is LaLa Ri, bringing his two seasons’ worth of experience from the flagship franchise’s season 13 and All Stars 8 in a tattered, leathery look, complete with train. Miss Congeniality, Queen of the Fame Games, and Golden Boot awardee, LaLa took a break from knucking and bucking in gigs around the world to focus on a creative career in a non-drag capacity, but could not resist the roar of the crowds (and the bag).

Then comes the moment we’ve been waiting for, announcing her entrance to the earthlings in her company with a greeting that can simply be described as her: “Maburat!” Out of her dollification era, Drag Race Philippines season 2’s M1ss Jade So enters as a dakesang goddessa. In an Eman Rinosa original, she reps not only the designer behind various Mamwa Pao looks from her franchise but the Filipina trans community in a revealing blue, pink, and white attire with terno sleeves. 

From the planet Vortexa, M1ss Jade So is a breakout name from the Philippine franchise, known for her out-of-this-world personality, sickening looks, and her tendency to eliminate herself. She gathers the girls to hold hands and hum together, spooking LaLa, who’s afraid of any sort of ritual.

Her appearance this season comes at a time of healing and reconnection after an alarming incident took place on her official livestream; Jade has since returned to social media to reassure fans of her recovery, saying, “I am taking serious steps to fully recover, so that I can proudly say and embody, ‘I am strong coming back to drag, music, [and] fashion.’”

The last Down Under queen to enter—teal-haired, black and silver fringe, and wrapped in fishnets—is also its first. Art Simone walks in reputation-first as the original and perhaps the most prominent Australiasian drag personality to compete on Down Under season 1. Thus, audiences were gagged when she was eliminated in only episode 2 at Snatch Game, and in a bitter exit nonetheless. She’d soon pull a surprise RuTurn, popping out of a pile of trash two episodes later, making it all the way to the finale. A third-time returnee, and technically an early out at that, her presence already triggers eliminator Coco Jumbo.

Under a sense of false security, the queens prematurely christen themselves as the ten contestants of the newest Versus the World season, only to have their faces cracked. Two-time first runner-up, Emmy-award winning makeup artist, and Drag Race institution Raven enters in full white cat couture, complete with ears to the awe and distress of her competitors. Immediately, they ask her for an alliance, who’s doing Ru’s makeup, and if she’s actually competing, the last question having Raven answer in the affirmative.

“No, you’re not,” jabs back Nicole, who was eliminated by Raven in their original season all the way back in 2010. She also invokes nepotism, given Raven’s herstory as a member of production since the ninth season of the American series, solidifying Nicole’s reliability to give us a good ol’ soundbite.

Her return to the competition drew hasty claims from fans of her place as a mole for the judges, a fake-out, and everything under the sun except a contestant. By the ending part of the episode, her appearance this season proves that she may be everything fans hoped for—and everything her competitors will have to overcome.

D.I.Y. divas

With the eleven queens assembled, the sirens blare, heralding Drag Race icon and RuPaul’s right-hand woman Michelle Visage down the Werkroom steps in her second season as host.

Their first challenge sends them back to basics. The queens have a pass-around party with a series of boxes and have to create an outfit with the fabrics within. The Werkroom drama is light and airy; though several queens are familiar with some, the international format has them gauge and get to know the more foreign competitors—one of the most satisfying symptoms of the Versus the World series.

These international, inter-franchise interactions are some of the stand-out moments before the runway. The undoubted beauties of the season, Flor and Jade, trade playful banter when the former says “Filipino drag is just another level.” Jade expresses her genuine gratitude in response when Flor replies, “I wasn’t talking about you.”. Minutes later, Coco Jumbo pays homage to Season 17’s Lexi Love, saying“Who spray painted all over my outfit?!” to the laughter of her fellow queens before a quick moment of vulnerability the next day. Lastly, amid claims of a choppy flow throughout the episode, the editors make sure to instill the amount of work it takes to be a queen with a quick shot of Art applying make-up to her… nostril. Drag at its finest.

The first category of the season has the contestants flaunt their D.I.Y designs on the new and improved runway. Called safe are Art Simone and her elegant gown in Michelle’s favorite color — leopard print; Coco Jumbo in a floor-length drapey dress in metallic pistachio lycra, which looks closer to dubai chewy chocolate green; Nikita Iman in a standard crushed velvet mermaid gown in red; Nicole Paige Brooks in a Mad Max-meets-showgirl hot glue gun couture, made out of gray faux fur that she compares to “bathroom rug […] material” (and she’s not wrong); and Raven in stretchable denim pants and a ruffled top, complete with a wig furnished out of the same material.

These five queens are sent back to the Werkroom, but the case for both Nicole and Raven to be at the top of this challenge is strong. For Nicole, the reality of making at least a legible, much less actually fashionable, outfit seemed impossible. For Raven, her original goal was to simply make something tootable. But she is seemingly called out by Michelle for playing it safe, given her experience and expertise. The simplicity and construction prowess her final look requires, however, deserves more flowers than what is actually given. But you never know what happens down under.

The top queens for the episode are PH’s M1ss Jade So, UK’s Michael Marouli, and Down Under’s Vybe. In black and white mod cotton, Vybe satisfies a self-fulfilling prophecy and creates a cute mod dress, though Nicole and Raven had arguably stronger looks. Michael Marouli impresses with a gothic tiger print gown in velvet. Being known mostly for personality and comedy, a top placement for a design challenge makes Marouli a more versatile player than ever before.

The absolute stand-out, though, is M1ss Jade So and her two gold front teeth. Her metallic red lycra is manipulated into her very own lava gown, slinking around her torso and adorned with gold pearls that “came from [her] womb.” Her look is complete with a cigarette holder she dips into her behind and pops into her mouth. 

 “Now, she’s smoking crack,” says underrated judge Rhys Nicholson, one of the more clever judges’ commentary in recent memory.

As for the bottom queens, Estrella in bright orange hair and sporty head-to-toe chartreuse tricot bodysuit and dress—infamously Michelle’s least favorite color — is critiqued for over-accessorizing, getting told she could lose a bangle or two, which she responds to in a funny bit by… discarding her bag instead.

In a floral sequin mini dress that recalls one of his previous design looks, LaLa is commended for personality and presentation, which is dragged down by a certain peekaboo moment that shows his down under.

Flor’s look is a corset and skirt with horizontal slits that reveal her legs in holographic rainbow plisse satin, purple-blue feather flourishing her wig, and blocky shades. The judges point out a lack of polish and cohesion regarding her intended reference, one the writer of this article did not understand was supposed to be of an insect of any sort until she pointed it out. No way that feather is supposed to be antennae at all, Flor!

In the end, Vybe and Estrella are called safe, Flor and LaLa the bottom two at risk of sashaying away, and Michael and Jade the top 2 who earn a Down Under badge and the chance to lip sync for their legacy.

Jade’s win marks the fourth time the Philippines representative in an international franchise—not counting Slaysian Royale—won their first competitive episode, after Marina, Eva, and Khianna also placed top 2 in their All Stars seasons’ respective first episodes.

After a lip-sync battle with stunts from Michael and reveals from Jade to ‘Melodrama’ by guest judge Beex, Michelle eventually grants the win to Michael, a decision already being debated online. But, in the end, the responsibility to eliminate one of the girls is all for naught when Michelle announces no one shall be eliminated for the premiere. 

A dance party off the runway ends the episode—but not before Michelle receives a mysterious call. “How long will it take you to get here?” she asks, an ominous warning that the land down under may be turned upside down in the episodes to come.

Queens at werk

A dark cloud may have hovered over the season’s premiere in light of M1ss Jade So’s livestream the same week. But, as she said, there is life after downfall.

As much as Drag Race saturates LGBTQIA+ culture and media, it is especially showcases like this, bringing together queer expression from all over the world for the sake of art, that remind people—in Jade’s words—the “purpose to live, to preach, and to share […] unique knowledge to the world is something worth to behold and believe for.”

It is queens like Jade and the ten competitors alongside her that truly does give our LGBTQIA+ seniors, baby gays, and everyone in between something to cherish in distressing sociopolitical times, no matter where in the world they’re watching.

Though DU has been bogged down by controversial contestants, claims of low production value, and behind-the-scenes shenanigans, the newest “Versus the World” series defied all expectations with a truly impressive cast of veterans, fan favorites, and underdogs, an even more impressive first episode showing, and the potential to become one of the breakout seasons of the year. 

In the echelon of Drag Races all over the world, this 2026, Down Under may just come out on top.
