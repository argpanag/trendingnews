Ingram announces retirement after 5 NHL seasons | NHL.com

TheTools · 12.09.2026 09:11 · source · permalink

Goalie, 29, played 32 games for Oilers last season, won Masterton Trophy in 2023-24

© John Cordes/Icon Sportswire via Getty Images

Connor Ingram announced his retirement from hockey on Friday after five NHL seasons.

The 29-year-old wrote in an Instagram post, "Don't be sad it's over, be happy it happened. Thanks hockey, I owe ya."

Ingram’s agent Blake Evans confirmed to NHL.com that the goalie is retiring.

Ingram was 16-10-3 with a 2.60 goals-against average, .899 save percentage and two shutouts in 32 games (30 starts) for the Edmonton Oilers last season. He then went 2-3 with a 3.86 GAA and an .876 save percentage in five Stanley Cup Playoff games.

A third-round pick (No. 88) by the Tampa Bay Lightning in the 2016 NHL Draft, Ingram was 55-54-18 with a 3.01 GAA, .901 save percentage and nine shutouts in 134 regular-season games (129 starts) for the Oilers, Nashville Predators, Arizona Coyotes and Utah Hockey Club. He was 2-6 with a 3.76 GAA and .895 save percentage in nine playoff games (eight starts).

Ingram nearly retired due to obsessive compulsive disorder and lingering depression before seeking help from the NHL/NHLPA Player Assistance program Jan. 15, 2021.

After resuming his career, he won the Bill Masterton Memorial Trophy in 2023-24 while with Arizona. The award is presented annually to the NHL player who best exemplifies the qualities of perseverance, sportsmanship and dedication to hockey as voted on by members of the Professional Hockey Writers Association.

Ingram went 23-21-3 with a 2.91 GAA, .907 save percentage and six shutouts in 50 games (48 starts) that season, setting career highs in wins, shutouts, games played and starts, and tying his career high in save percentage.

This offseason, the Oilers did not re-sign Ingram, who was an unrestricted free agent. They signed goalie Frederik Andersen to a one-year, $2.8 million contract July 1 after he helped the Carolina Hurricanes win the Stanley Cup last season, but he is expected to miss training camp due to injury.

Edmonton also has goalies Tristan Jarry and Devon Levi on the roster.

NHL.com staff writer Derek Van Diest contributed to this report
