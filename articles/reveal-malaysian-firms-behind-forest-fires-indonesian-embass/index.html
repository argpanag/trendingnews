Reveal Malaysian firms behind forest fires, Indonesian embassy told

TheTools · 04.09.2026 04:34 · source · permalink

Civil society groups demand transparency over open burning and call for Putrajaya to enact the long-delayed Transboundary Haze Act.

Greenpeace Malaysia’s interim country representative, Heng Kiah Chun, said clarity was needed over who is to blame for the haze.

Heng said the Indonesian authorities are pointing fingers at Malaysian and Singaporean companies, while these firms are claiming that they were carrying environmentally friendly policies.

“Transparency is required so responsible companies can clear their names and non-compliant ones can be held accountable,” he said after submitting a memorandum to an official from the Indonesian embassy here today.

Both Heng and Brendon Gan, representative of student advocacy group Himpunan Advocacy Rakyat Malaysia (Haram), also urged Putrajaya to enact a standalone Transboundary Haze Act.

Although Malaysia was the first signatory to adopt the 2002 Asean Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution, they said, the country lacks extraterritorial legislation empowering its courts to prosecute local companies operating abroad.

Gan called on Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim to coordinate directly with Indonesian president Prabowo Subianto to share data, analysis, and lists of companies to jointly tackle the environmental crisis.

He also said progressive youth and civil society movements will hold elected leaders accountable if they fail to show the political will to address transboundary haze.

Earlier, about 20 people gathered outside the embassy, holding placards reading “Can’t breathe”, “Belum merdeka dari jerebu”, and “Name them, show the evidence, take action”, while chanting “Tolak, tolak asap!” and “Tolak, tolak jerebu!”.

The demonstration was in response to remarks by Indonesian lawmaker Marwan Dasopang, who urged Malaysia and Singapore to help prevent Kalimantan’s forest fires rather than merely complaining, pointing out that companies from both nations operate palm oil plantations in the region.

The air pollutant index in Serian, Sarawak, stood at a hazardous level of 517 at 10.45am today. According to the environment department, the API readings were between moderate and hazardous in East Malaysia, while in Peninsular Malaysia it ranged between good and unhealthy.
