Premier attending Canada Investment Summit with world's largest investment firms gathering to hear pitches

Article content

Premier Susan Holt is pushing eight New Brunswick projects in Toronto next week in front of some of the world’s largest investment firms as part of the federal government’s massive effort to attract billions in new capital spending into Canada.

That list is headlined by a call for investments in new nuclear development, as well as port expansion, but also for a financial backer in the multibillion-dollar rehabilitation of the Mactaquac Dam.

Among the eight, there’s also the expansion of an existing quarry in Dalhousie.

The controversial proposed Lorneville data centre also makes the list, as well as a new Atlantic e-methanol production project.

It adds up to $30 billion in capital investment opportunities in New Brunswick, according to the premier’s office.

The invitation-only Canada Investment Summit is set to welcome executives from nearly 100 global investment organizations from 28 countries – including Canada – that collectively manage almost $120 trillion.

The Four Seasons Hotel in downtown Toronto will host the two-day summit on Monday and Tuesday.

Holt will attend.

So will Natural Resources Minister John Herron.

That’s as Prime Minister Mark Carney has set the ambitious goal of “catalyzing” $1 trillion in investment over the next five years.

“New Brunswick has the people, resources, infrastructure and opportunities investors are looking for,” Holt said in a statement. “We are going to Toronto to attract new investment, build relationships and show investors that New Brunswick is ready to work with them to get projects moving and create good-paying jobs for New Brunswickers.”

Canada plans to show some of the world’s biggest investors a pitchbook laying out tens of billions of dollars worth of projects spanning pipeline projects, liquefied natural gas plants, digital technology opportunities, ports, and dozens of mines.

The provinces were asked to submit a list of projects for potential investment.

A final book of 167 projects has now been circulated to those invited to be in attendance, the document detailing each opportunity’s current stage, the capital expenditure needed, and then the kind of investment being sought to help it get built.

Eight are from New Brunswick, according to the premier’s office.

They include:

  • Belledune port expansion, with capital expenditures of US$250 million in a first phase and another US$1.24 billion in a second phase,
  • The Dalhousie pozzolan project, with a US$432.3 million capital spend,
  • Lorneville data centre, needing US$3.9 billion in capital,
  • Mactaquac lifetime achievement project, with a needed US$5.1 billion to US$6.6 billion in capital,
  • New nuclear development,
  • The Saint John trade corridor,
  • Sisson mine project, and a needed US$1.3 billion capital spend,
  • Atlantic e-methanol production project, with more than US$1 billion in capital expenditure forecasted.

Several of the projects on the list don’t come as a surprise.

The Port of Belledune has filed a $750-million nation building project pitch to the federal government’s major projects office for consideration, a proposal that would bring in and berth of defence ships for turnaround, as well as become a hub for mineral transshipment.

NB Power has signed an agreement to complete the design and cost estimates for the rehabilitation of the Mactaquac Dam, with an expected price tag between $7.6 and $9 billion.

But others on the list arguably stick out.

The province’s decision on the 390 MW data centre in Saint John’s Spruce Lake Industrial Park is likely delayed until it can be informed by the Holt government’s policy framework on the building of those facilities in the province, which it is currently developing.

Meanwhile, the idea of an e-methanol production project appears new.

Brunswick News has learned it’s a project headed by Elemental Clean Fuels, a company with offices in Calgary and Oyster Bay, NY, pitching to produce low-carbon marine and aviation fuels using green hydrogen and clean energy created from waste from pulp and paper mills.

The company didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

Meanwhile, five different federal ministers, including New Brunswick MP and Canada-U.S. Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc, are slated to host investment sessions at the summit.

Carney will also deliver a keynote address.

Holt’s office said the premier will participate in the summit on both Monday and Tuesday, while using the event “as the anchor for a broader investment-attraction initiative across the Greater Toronto Area.”

As part of its market diversification efforts, Opportunities NB will also hold meetings in Toronto on sector-focused business development with both existing clients and prospective investors, according to the premier’s office.

Talks will focus on priority sectors, including mining, cybersecurity, defence, financial services.

The goal is to generate new leads, advance strategic opportunities and attract investment that supports jobs and long-term economic growth, the office added.
