Registered Nurses Can Earn $100K+ in These States

TheTools · 17.09.2026 19:30 · source · permalink

The median registered nurse (RN) earns $97,550 annually, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). That’s nearly double the $50,980 salary of the average earner across all occupations.

Benjamin Franklin said to do well by doing good. In one-seventh of the country, RNs can earn their way into the upper-middle class by helping to care for the sick and heal the injured.

However, depending on where they live, these specialized healthcare workers can earn well over $100,000 per year. A new SmartAsset analysis identified seven states where RNs earn the equivalent of six-figure incomes when adjusted for the local cost of living.

What Do RNs Do?

According to the BLS, RNs must manage many responsibilities to earn their sizable salaries, including:

  • Assess patient conditions

  • Analyze patient medical histories

  • Provide and coordinate patient care

  • Administer medicines

  • Observe and analyze patient status

  • Educate patients and the public

  • Offer emotional support and advice

What Do RNs Earn?

Location isn’t the only factor that influences pay. The BLS notes that some RNs make higher salaries than others, depending on their specialty and place of practice.

  • Educational services: $78,620

  • Nursing and residential care facilities: $84,300

  • Ambulatory healthcare services: $91,230

  • Hospitals: $100,220

  • Government (excluding public hospitals): $110,780

7 States Where RNs Earn Six Figures

Since a given wage can go much farther in an inexpensive Midwestern town than in an exorbitantly pricey coastal big city, the study adjusted each of the 50 states’ typical RN salaries, according to local living costs, to express them in real, nationally comparable dollars.

The middle-earning RN enjoys a six-figure adjusted salary in the following seven states, listed alphabetically. However, it’s important to note that in several notoriously expensive states, the typical RN’s salary is adjusted downward — by five figures, in some cases — because the cost of living stretches it further than it would in the country as a whole. 

Interestingly, RNs in some of the most expensive states — such as Massachusetts, Connecticut and New York — earn actual salaries over $100,000. However, they didn’t make the list because the cost of living in their states is so high that their adjusted real earnings fall into five-figure territory.
