Netflix Reveals Series & Movie Book Adaptation Lineup For 2027

TheTools · 16.11.2026 19:00 · source · permalink

The streamer confirmed its slate of upcoming book adaptations and detailed how book sales jump following Netflix releases.

Picture Credits: Netflix / Getty Images

Netflix has used its first trip to the Texas Book Festival to lay out its slate of upcoming book adaptations, including a brand-new Harlan Coben series and a release date for 3 Body Problem season 2.

Book adaptations have long been Netflix’s bread and butter, and the streamer now has the numbers to back that up. According to new figures released today, adaptations have generated over 13 billion views and nearly 20% of all viewing hours on the service since 2025. A book adaptation has also featured in the global top 10s for 98% of 2026 so far.

It follows Netflix’s book festival in Paris last month and comes after the streamer optioned over 25 more books for development over the past year. Below, we’ve rounded up everything Netflix confirmed for 2027 and beyond (plus a few more titles we’ve been tracking!).

AUSTIN, TEXAS – NOVEMBER 15: A view of books on display at the Netflix Reading Lounge at The 2026 Texas Book Festival on November 15, 2026 in Austin, Texas. (Photo by Chris Saucedo/Getty Images for Netflix)

What’s New: The Big Announcements

Harlan Coben’s The Innocent gets a limited series

Netflix is billing this as its 15th Harlan Coben adaptation. It comes from Robert Hull, Bryan Wynbrandt and Steven Lilien, the showrunners behind I Will Find You, which finished its run at #5 on the Most Popular list with 120.8 million views. The trio has also signed an overall deal to create new series exclusively for Netflix through StoryMath.

“We’re excited to bring our signature brand of twisty and propulsive storytelling to The Innocent and the many new stories we look forward to telling at Netflix,” the three said in a statement.

Cole Galvin, Netflix’s VP of Drama Series, added: “Our partnership with Harlan Coben is one of the greatest creative relationships at Netflix, and The Innocent marks our 15th adaptation together.”

Netflix says Coben’s shows have made the weekly Global Top 10 41 times since 2021, reached the top 10 in 93 countries, and drawn over 450 million views since 2023. Fool Me Once alone accounts for over 98 million of those.

3 Body Problem season 2 arrives January 14, 2027

Season 2 lands on Thursday, January 14, with a teaser due tomorrow (November 17). That January date marks a slip from the late-2026 window previously on the books. Viewers also get a shorter run this time: six episodes rather than eight.

Season 1 spent seven weeks in the Global Top 10, hitting #1 in 44 countries with 46.6 million views. Netflix teases a space fleet on a centuries-long mission and “a secret locked inside of one man’s mind” for the new season.

Upcoming Book Adaptations on Netflix

New Series Based on Books

  • The Age of Innocence – Based on Edith Wharton’s novel. February 2027 is the release window for this eight-parter from showrunner Emma Frost.
  • The God of the Woods – Based on the novel by Liz Moore. Eight episodes are on the way with Maya Hawke in the lead, and the shoot was close to finished as of last month.
  • Icebreaker – Based on the novel by Hannah Grace. Cameras were due to roll in October with Daisy Jelley and Connor Simos as the leads.
  • The Innocent – Limited series based on Harlan Coben’s novel, announced today.
  • I Suck at Girls – Based on Justin Halpern’s autobiography. Los Angeles hosted the shoot for this comedy, which Halpern showruns with Patrick Schumacker.
  • Kennedy – Based on Fredrik Logevall’s book. The patriarch role of Joe Kennedy Sr. belongs to Michael Fassbender.
  • Myron Bolitar – Based on Harlan Coben’s 12-book series, now in production in New York with David E. Kelley and Kyle Long co-showrunning. The lead trio is Colin Woodell, KJ Apa and Diane Guerrero.

  • Better Than the Movies – Based on the novel by Lynn Painter. A Chicago shoot is in the can for Julia Hart’s rom-com, where Beatrice Kitsos and David Iacono share top billing.

The “Netflix Effect”: What Adaptations Do for Book Sales

Netflix says titles see an average 10x lift in US book sales in the 30 days after their adaptation premieres. Here’s how this year’s biggest adaptations stack up, per Netflix and the publishers:

The publishers have been effusive. Miriam Parker of Ecco/HarperCollins says Remarkably Bright Creatures has spent 23 weeks on the New York Times Paperback Bestseller list, “all as a direct result of the promotion of the film by Netflix.”

Rachel Chou of Celadon Books says The Whisper Man “has been a backlist staple” since 2019, and that the film “helped bring it back into the spotlight.” Spotify adds that listening to Alex North’s other audiobooks rose 100%.

On A Good Girl’s Guide to Murder, Delacorte Press executive editor Kelsey Horton said: “Season 1’s release sent it back to #1 on the series list. Season 2 brought it back onto the list for months after it had dropped off. With season 3, we expect the series to continue to sell and sell.”

The Hunting Wives may be the most dramatic example. Berkley went back to press twice on the trade paperback in a single week and saw a 5,000% year-over-year increase across all formats. The show has drawn over 35 million views since July 2025, and a new tie-in cover goes on sale tomorrow.

Then there’s Enola Holmes. According to the author’s literary agent, Jennifer Weltz, the series has grown from six books in nine territories to ten books in 41 territories and 38 languages since the movie deal. “Every time there is a new ENOLA film, we again see a spike in book sales!” she said.

It isn’t just a US and UK phenomenon either. Adaptations in this year’s Global Top 10 came from over 25 countries:

  • The Leopard (Italy) – Sales up more than 1,180% in the week after launch, per NielsenIQ.
  • Detective Hole (Norway) – ARK bookstore sales up more than 1,000% over Easter versus the prior year.
  • Museum of Innocence (Türkiye) – A 406% increase in book sales on Hepsiburada.

“What I love most is that these shows send people back to the page,” Galvin said at the festival. “It’s a reminder that readers and viewers aren’t separate audiences, and I’m grateful to the authors who trust us with their stories.”

He also pointed to the growth of what Netflix calls “comfort and heartland” titles such as Virgin River and Little House on the Prairie, which have “drawn more than a billion views on Netflix since 2023” across more than 90 countries.
