What it's worth right now

The final-year manual Hellcats have become genuine collector pieces, with strong recent results. According to Classic.com and recent sales, the third-generation Jailbreak carries a market benchmark close to $95,000, with near-new manual Widebody cars generally trading from the mid $80,000s past $100,000, though some have stalled below reserve in the mid $70,000s.

Fair value for a delivery-mile manual example like this sits close to $85,000 to $105,000, most likely close to $90,000 to $100,000, with the extraordinary mileage and rare manual supporting the figure. At $65,000 with five days remaining, the bidding should climb into that range.

What makes the Jailbreak unique

The Challenger SRT Hellcat became a modern legend by pairing outrageous supercharged power with everyday muscle-car usability, and the Jailbreak edition took the personalization further, allowing buyers to mix and match colors, trims, and options in combinations the standard cars did not offer. As a Last Call model for 2023, this car marks the end of an era, the final year of the supercharged Hemi Challenger before Dodge moved on from the naturally aspirated and supercharged V8s that defined the brand for a generation. That finality, combined with the Widebody bodywork and Jailbreak-specific configuration, makes it a genuine collector piece.

2023 Dodge Challenger SRT Hellcat Widebody JailbreakBring a Trailer

The six-speed manual is the rarest and most special part of the equation. Dodge paused manual Hellcat production during this era over emissions and durability concerns, making the final-year manual cars scarce, and enthusiasts who want a stick-shift Hellcat want one badly. That rarity, the ferocious supercharged Hemi, and the final-year significance are exactly what make this Widebody Jailbreak so collectible.

My take

This is a car to buy and keep, and on those terms it is a genuinely special final-year collectible. The combination of the widebody, the Jailbreak configuration, the rare six-speed manual, and just 28 delivery miles is as good as it gets for the end of the supercharged Hemi era, and the factory wrapping underscores just how untouched it is. For the collector who wants a definitive send-off Hellcat, this is a compelling opportunity.

Fair value sits close to $85,000 to $105,000, most likely close to $90,000 to $100,000, reflecting the delivery mileage and rare manual against a speculative, sometimes-soft market. Before bidding, confirm the private-party transaction and title process, and understand that driving it undermines the value proposition. Handle that, and this is a definitive, preserved example of one of America's last great supercharged muscle cars.
