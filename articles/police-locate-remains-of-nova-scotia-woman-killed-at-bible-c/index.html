Police locate remains of Nova Scotia woman killed at bible college in 2024 | CBC News

TheTools · 19.09.2026 04:28 · source · permalink

RCMP say remains of Esther Jones found Sept. 9 in wooded area at Cloud Lake, N.S.

Esther Jones's niece speaks out after remains found

The niece of a 55-year-old woman who was killed at a bible college in Nova Scotia’s Annapolis Valley two years ago says she is left with mixed emotions following the recent discovery of her aunt's remains.

RCMP announced in a news release Friday that members of the major crime unit located the remains of Esther Jones on Sept. 9 in a wooded area at Cloud Lake, about 12 kilometres south of where she was killed.

Esther Uhlman, who was named after her aunt, said she has a large family and everyone has reacted to the news differently.

"I'm very glad to know where her remains were and to know that they have been recovered, but it doesn't change anything," Uhlman said in an inteview Friday from Ottawa.

"Finding a body that she no longer is in it doesn't make it better.... I don't believe that it provides closure, not for me personally."

Earlier this month, a Nova Scotia man pleaded guilty to second-degree murder in Jones's death.

An agreed statement of facts from Nova Scotia Supreme Court in Kentville said Dale Toole admitted to entering the Kingston Bible College in Kingston, N.S. — where Jones leased a room and operated a music studio — on Aug. 31, 2024, and causing her death.

He then "removed her body from the college and disposed of it."

The statement does not specify a motive for the killing. It said Toole indicated he did not know Jones personally but "knew of her by virtue of living in a small town."

Toole was originally charged with first-degree murder in September 2024, after being located by American authorities at an airport in Texas while attempting to flee to Mexico.

Uhlman said Toole is a member of her extended family, being her father's ex-brother-in-law. Despite that, Toole did not know Jones personally, she said.

The 22-year-old woman said police have not informed the family about how Jones died, a possible motive for the killing, exactly where her remains were found or how they came to know the remains were there.

Uhlman said the Cloud Lake Wilderness Area is somewhere she is quite familiar with, having camped there throughout her childhood.

"The fact that he left her remains there is very unfortunate because a place that was kind of an enjoyable, comforting place will kind of be a little dark now," said Uhlman.

RCMP declined an interview request on Friday, but in the news release, Cpl. Mike Robinson said the Mounties are grateful for the family and community support throughout the investigation.

“The dedication and perseverance of the investigative team, together with the support of our policing partners, were instrumental in advancing this investigation,” said Robinson.

"We hope this recovery provides answers to outstanding questions and helps bring a measure of closure and healing to those closest to Esther.”

Second-degree murder carries with it an automatic life sentence, however a judge sets how long Toole will have to serve before he can start applying for parole, which ranges from 10 to 25 years.

Toole is scheduled to return to court in Kentville on Oct. 16 for a status update and to set a date for sentencing.

Uhlman said her aunt was a compassionate person who always helped others. She loved animals and was passionate about her faith and music, making her career as a piano teacher.

She said she carries her aunt's kindness into her own life, but struggles to cope with what happened.

"I would like to highlight to people that have never experienced this how much of a life sentence it is and how much you just don't get to move past it," she said.

"It's not like a normal loss. You never get to really move on from it because you constantly have to deal with it like in the court system and every day.

"Just remember how important it is to value the people around you. You never know when they're going to be gone."
