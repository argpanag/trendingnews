Power eyeing up Ryder Cup tilt after injury nightmare

TheTools · 31.10.2026 18:05 · source · permalink

Getty

Seamus Power is confident that the best is yet to come in his career after he secured his full PGA Tour card for 2027 with a runners-up finish last week at the Bermuda Championship.

Power had been at risk of facing another campaign on a 'conditional card' after missing a number of cuts on the stateside tour, as well as his home event, the Irish Open, in early September.

Injuries had played their part in his fall off from his best, with the 39-year-old fearful at one stage that his career might be coming to an end.

But the Waterford native says that he is growing in confidence all the time and he reckons he can get back to the sort of form that saw him reach a career high of 28 in the world rankings back in 2022.

"I feel like I'm like a better player than I was then," he told Greg Allen on RTÉ's Saturday Sport.

"I know more about my game. Even the adversity of the last couple of years, I feel like it will stand to me again once I get going.

"So I do think I can get back to that."

The injury set back was something that began brewing early in 2023 when Power noticed that something was wrong with the left side of his body.

He put it down to a long flight to the Middle East, but when symptoms didn't clear, there was the added frustration of being unable to find out exactly what was wrong with him.

It all came to a head at the Arnold Palmer Invitational, which was played at Bay Hill. Power ended up making the cut, but finishing in last place on the Sunday.

"I haven't felt this good in a long time now. The injury was just so strange, nobody could really get an answer," Power continued.

"A few months later, I tweaked something in Bay Hill, but I finally got to the bottom of it.

"My current guy was able to show me what happened and how it happened and how to build a buffer so I can get further and further away from that injury."

The injury centred on what Power calls the "relationship between the left hip and lower right back", and the inconsistent nature of it left him unable to predict his form from day to day.

"It was just very, very annoying," he says."It wasn't as if I was walking around in pain. I would go to the golf course and one day a ball speed could be 178. Then I would go out the next day and it could be 165 and I would feel almost the same, so it was very, very unusual.

"Sometimes it would seize up and it wouldn't really want to work at all. I would get an MRI and it really wasn't much showing up on that.

"It was just very debilitating in terms of trying to play golf.

"I'm long ways down in driving distance stats, but even recently, I'm starting to see that pick back up again."

That second place finish in Bermuda, where he only lost in a play-off, could be a catalyst for Power as he looks ahead to 2027.

It's a big year, with the Ryder Cup set to come to county Limerick's Adare Manor.

"The second place gets me back heading in the right direction, and that's what I needed," Power says.

"The last couple of years, I just felt like momentum was not with me and I couldn't quite get out of my own way.

"All of a sudden, the clarity and the general feel about my own game, it's through the roof. It's a long ways ahead of where it's been the last couple of years, where I definitely was a bit more down about it because of the unknowns.

"It was the worry that this could be a career ending situation with the body.

"To get that cleared up was the first big step and just really made massive strides in my game."I still think there's going to be some good golf left in 2026 but then I really can't wait already for 2027.

"(To) get back into some of these big tournaments, get back into Majors and show them, show myself and others that my game is back.

"I'll be back amongst some of the best players in the world again.

"I fully believe, deep down, that my game is good enough and I could play on hard golf courses, I could challenge and win a major championship.

"I still have aspirations to try to play a Ryder Cup, all these things that obviously I haven't done.

"I'm not going to stop until I feel like I've given everything towards all those goals."
