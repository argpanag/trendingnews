Here's what $5,000 would be worth today

Since its inception, the Invesco QQQ ETF has returned approximately 10.6% annually with dividends reinvested. That may not sound like an impressive number, but keep in mind the fund lost more than 80% of its value during the tech collapse within the first few years of its existence.

The past decade, of course, has been incredible. It's capitalized on the artificial intelligence (AI) rally and unprecedented liquidity in response to the COVID pandemic. Over the past 10 years, the fund has returned nearly 21% per year.

If you consider this altogether, a $5,000 investment made on March 9, 1999 when the fund launched and held through the market close of November 4, 2026 would have grown to $79,030.

That's roughly 16 times the original investment.

But it's important to point out that you would have needed to ride out multiple severe bear markets to capture these returns. That includes the tech bubble, the 2008 financial crisis, the COVID pandemic, and the 2022 bear market. History shows what long-term patience can accomplish, even when you start at the worst time possible.
