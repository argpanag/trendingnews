Rashid Khan’s Return to Test Cricket Delayed as Fitness Concerns Persist

TheTools · 07.11.2026 09:47 · source · permalink

New Delhi, 05 November (H.S.): Afghanistan's star leg-spinner Rashid Khan is still not fully fit to return to Test cricket, with concerns over his back injury and overall fitness preventing his comeback to the longest format. The Afghanistan medical team has advised him against playing Test matches for the time being. Captain Rahmat Shah made it clear ahead of the two-Test series against Zimbabwe that Rashid would be considered for selection only after achieving full fitness.

Rashid has played just six Test matches in his career, although he has featured in more than 245 matches across the two limited-overs formats — One-Day Internationals and T20Is. He has remained away from Test cricket in recent times because of recurring back problems and fitness concerns. However, he continues to be a key member of Afghanistan's white-ball teams.

According to the ICC website, Rahmat Shah said the team's medical staff and physiotherapists had advised against Rashid playing Test cricket at present, as he was not yet physically prepared for the demands of the format.

We cannot afford to risk losing Rashid for limited-overs cricket. He is an extremely important player for us, and I wish him a speedy recovery. Rashid himself is keen to play Test cricket. However, if his body does not permit it and the doctors and medical team do not give him the clearance, we will not take any risks, Rahmat said.

The Afghanistan captain also highlighted the physical demands placed on bowlers in Test cricket. He pointed out that Rashid had a history of back problems, making careful workload management essential.

Rahmat said Rashid was Afghanistan's frontline spinner and that, if included in the Test side, he would inevitably be required to bowl extended spells, particularly when the opposition built long partnerships.

He further explained that the workload in Test cricket was considerably greater than in shorter formats. While a T20 match lasts around three hours and a bowler is required to deliver only four overs, Test cricket places significantly greater demands on a player's stamina and physical endurance.

According to Rashid's physiotherapist, managing his workload is particularly important given his previous back injury.

However, Rahmat Shah made it clear that Rashid remains an integral part of Afghanistan's long-term plans for Test cricket. He said he had personally discussed the matter with Rashid and the Afghanistan Cricket Board (ACB), adding that the leg-spinner would certainly return to the Test squad once he was completely fit and 100 per cent ready to play.

Afghanistan are scheduled to play a two-Test series against Zimbabwe. The opening Test began on November 5 in Sharjah, with the second match scheduled to be played at the same venue from November 13.
