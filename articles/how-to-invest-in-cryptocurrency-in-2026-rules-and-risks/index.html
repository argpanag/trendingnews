How to invest in cryptocurrency in 2026: rules and risks

TheTools · 05.09.2026 05:27 · source · permalink

Starting September 1, 2026, investing in cryptocurrency in Russia will become easier: such assets can be bought and sold through licensed intermediaries, and businesses will be able to use digital currency for settlements with foreign counterparties.

We analyzed what changes for private investors and companies, why increased access to the crypto market does not eliminate risks, and what role cryptocurrency can play in a long-term portfolio.

What Rules for Cryptocurrency Will Take Effect on September 1, 2026

The crypto market will have a more transparent and regulated infrastructure. It will include existing financial organizations as well as new participants: crypto exchanges and digital depositories.

Under the new rules, both qualified and non-qualified investors will be able to make cryptocurrency transactions. There is a restriction for non-qualified investors: they will be able to buy only the most liquid cryptocurrencies and no more than 300,000 ₽ per year through a single intermediary.

Investors will also be able to exchange cryptocurrency for securities and other digital instruments within the framework of Russian legislation. This brings the crypto market closer to the traditional financial infrastructure, although it still remains a separate and riskier segment.

Crypto exchanges will handle the purchase and sale of digital currency, while digital depositories will account for rights to such assets. Transactions can be conducted through brokers and asset managers, including on organized trading platforms.

There is a transition period for market participants until July 1, 2027. During this time, they need to obtain licenses and set up internal processes.

Cryptocurrency is moving into a more legal field and gaining a clear status as property. This is important not only formally: if an asset is stolen or a dispute arises, the owner has more grounds to defend their rights.

The method of accessing cryptocurrency will also change. Currently, many people buy it through external platforms, P2P, or other complicated schemes. After the infrastructure is launched, the operation may become similar to buying a security: through a bank app, broker, or another licensed intermediary.

Should You Invest in Cryptocurrency Now

Short answer: yes, but only cautiously. Cryptocurrency investments will become more accessible, but volatility will not disappear. You can make money on cryptocurrency if you choose the right asset, buy it before a rise, and lock in profits in time. But this only works with a clear strategy: long-term holding of liquid coins, active trading on price fluctuations, or using yield instruments like staking and liquidity pools—if the investor understands where the risk lies.

The outcome is most affected by the entry point, asset selection, liquidity, investment horizon, and discipline. The same asset can yield a profit with a calm long-term approach and result in a loss if bought at the peak of hype.

Sofya Donets believes that the Russian crypto market will become more mainstream: when an asset appears in a clear infrastructure and is available “at the click of a button,” people who previously did not want to deal with foreign exchanges, wallets, and complicated purchase schemes start to take interest.

But easier access does not mean automatic price growth. Domestic quotes will reflect global trends. The market may be influenced by decisions of major central banks on interest rates, news about cryptocurrency regulation in the US, demand dynamics for risky assets, and the overall situation in the world economy.

The regulatory environment is also important for cryptocurrencies. Usually, the market reacts positively to clear rules, but there is a flip side to this process.

  • Increased trust: clear rules help attract more participants.
  • Reduced uncertainty: it is easier for investors to understand which transactions are allowed and how to protect their rights.
  • Lower gray premium: the more actively governments bring cryptocurrency into the legal field, the less attractive it becomes to operate outside traditional financial controls.

How Large and Small Crypto Assets Differ

Bitcoin and Ethereum: higher stability due to recognition, capitalization, and developed infrastructure; growth potential is usually more moderate; risk is lower than with small coins, but still remains high.

Ripple, Dogecoin, and other notable projects: stability depends on demand, reputation, and market interest; growth potential may be higher; risk of sharp declines is also higher.

Small coins and new tokens: lower stability, potential for speculative growth can be sharp, but the risk of going to zero is much higher.

What Share Can Cryptocurrency Occupy in a Portfolio

Cryptocurrency is a high-risk asset. It should not be seen as a replacement for a deposit, the main bet of a portfolio, or a full alternative to currency diversification.

It is better to assess the main risks before buying, not after a sharp drop.

  • Volatility: the price can quickly change by tens of percent.
  • Regulatory risk: rules for cryptocurrencies may change in Russia and other countries.
  • Technological risk: network, smart contract, or service errors can lead to losses.
  • Risk of losing access: if an investor loses keys or access to a wallet, it may be impossible to recover assets.
  • Fraud: dubious schemes often appear around popular coins, staking, liquidity pools, and loans.
  • Market manipulation: in illiquid tokens, it is easier to pump or crash the price.

Simple discipline helps reduce risks: do not invest a significant part of your capital, choose liquid assets, do not use unclear yield schemes, store access securely, and understand in advance under what conditions you need to close a position.

It is better to add cryptocurrency to your portfolio only after independent verification: understand why you chose this asset, what share it occupies, and what will happen if the price drops sharply.

The main difficulty of cryptocurrency is that it does not have a clear current income. A stock gives a share in a business and potential dividends, a bond—a coupon, a deposit—a known interest rate in advance. Even gold, although it does not bring interest income, is based on a very long global consensus: for centuries it has been perceived as a safe-haven asset.

Cryptocurrency is structured differently. Bitcoin is based on the idea of limited supply, trust in mathematics, cryptography, and independence from central banks. Ethereum and other networks rely on the technological logic of blockchain. But determining the fair value of such assets is much harder than valuing a business, a debt security, or a familiar financial instrument.

Therefore, it is important to consider cryptocurrency separately from conservative investments. It is not a bank transaction with a predictable result and not an instrument with fixed income. In accounting terms, such an asset can be recorded as property, but for an investor, this does not eliminate the risk of sharp price fluctuations.

How Much Money Do You Need to Start With Cryptocurrency

For a start, a clear risk limit is more important than a large sum. For a beginner, it is more reasonable to start with such a share of capital that losing it will not ruin the financial plan or force the sale of assets in panic.

For non-qualified investors, the new rules will impose a limit: no more than 300,000 ₽ per year through a single intermediary and only the most liquid cryptocurrencies. The actual purchase amount will also be affected by intermediary commissions, minimum lots, the spread between buy and sell, and storage costs if the investor chooses a separate wallet or depository accounting.

What Cryptocurrency Investment Strategies Exist

Buy and hold: the investor chooses liquid assets, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, and holds them as a small part of a long-term portfolio.

Active trading: the investor tries to profit from price fluctuations. This approach requires understanding volatility, liquidity, and the risk of quick losses.

Staking: the investor receives income for participating in the network or through an intermediary, but market risk is supplemented by the risk of the specific service’s terms.

Liquidity pools and loans: assets are transferred into schemes where returns depend on demand, platform conditions, and counterparties. Here it is especially important to understand who pays the income and from what source.

Investments through funds, asset managers, or derivatives: access may be easier, but the investor depends not only on the cryptocurrency price but also on the rules of the product itself.

What to Check Before Buying Cryptocurrency

You should only turn to cryptocurrency after understanding basic investment instruments: deposits, bonds, stocks, the securities market, and risk allocation principles. If an investor does not understand how diversification works, crypto may add chaos rather than opportunities to the portfolio.

It is more reasonable to start with the most liquid and understandable assets. For most investors, these are Bitcoin and Ethereum. They have higher recognition, more liquidity, and a more stable infrastructure around them.

When choosing a coin, you should look not only at past price growth but also at a set of basic characteristics.

  • Liquidity: how easy it is to buy or sell an asset without significantly affecting the price.
  • Capitalization: how large and stable the project appears compared to others.
  • Reputation: how long the asset has existed and whether there have been major scandals around it.
  • Technology: what problem the network solves and whether it has a clear application logic.
  • Team and ecosystem: who is developing the project and whether there are users, developers, and services around it.
  • Project history: how the asset has gone through periods of decline, crises, and regulatory changes.

Independent analysis is best started with simple questions: why does this coin exist, who uses it, where can it be sold, what can support demand, and what can crash the price.

Small coins, memecoins, and new tokens are best considered only as speculative ideas. It is harder to assess real value, the risk of manipulation is higher, liquidity is lower, and there is a greater chance that the investor will not be able to exit the position on acceptable terms.

You should not chase returns in crypto staking, liquidity pools, and loans if it is unclear who is taking on the obligations and where the risk lies. Such schemes often add counterparty risk to the volatility of cryptocurrency: the asset may fall in price, and the intermediary may not return the money.

Investors should save documents and transaction history in advance. The emergence of regulated infrastructure does not eliminate tax reporting issues. If the origin of income cannot be explained, the problem will not disappear by itself.

  • Save documents for transactions.
  • Record dates and amounts of transactions.
  • Store information about where assets are held.
  • Prepare explanations for the tax authorities.

It is important not to confuse the legality of cryptocurrency with the reliability of a particular transaction. According to the Central Bank, in 2026, more than 74% of financial pyramids used cryptocurrencies to attract money. The more popular the topic, the more active scammers become around it.

It is also important to remember about complex products. A derivative financial instrument tied to cryptocurrency can be even riskier than directly buying a coin: the investor depends not only on the price of the underlying asset but also on the terms of the instrument itself.

Where to Buy and How to Store Cryptocurrency

After the new infrastructure is launched, cryptocurrency can be bought and sold through licensed intermediaries: brokers, asset managers, crypto exchanges, and other organizations operating under Russian law. Currently, many use external platforms, P2P, and exchanges, but such methods require more attention to the reliability of the counterparty and transaction documents.

Storage can also be organized in different ways.

  • With a licensed intermediary or in a digital depository: easier for rights accounting and documentation, but the investor depends on the intermediary’s infrastructure.
  • On an exchange wallet: convenient for frequent transactions, but there is a risk of platform problems or access being blocked.
  • In a software wallet: more control over assets, but all responsibility for keys and security lies with the owner.
  • On a hardware wallet: suitable for long-term storage and reduces the risk of online hacking, but losing the device or seed phrase can lead to loss of access.

How Cryptocurrency Can Be Useful for Businesses and Individuals

Cryptocurrency is gaining practical use in cross-border settlements. From September 1, companies and sole proprietors will be able to use digital currency under foreign trade contracts with foreign partners: it can be used to pay for imports and receive payment for exports.

For businesses, this is an additional payment channel when international payments are restricted. Here, cryptocurrency becomes a way to conduct a transaction with a foreign counterparty if the usual payment route is unavailable or difficult.

But within the country, crypto does not become a means of payment. Russian businesses will not be able to legally accept Bitcoin for goods from Russian clients or pay employees in cryptocurrency.

The logic is the same for individuals. Russians will be able to transfer cryptocurrency abroad—to their own wallets opened outside Russia or to foreign companies to pay for goods and services. But it is not allowed to pay for purchases within Russia using cryptocurrency.

Main Points for Investors

Cryptocurrency is becoming more accessible and understandable from a legal perspective, but it is not turning into a risk-free asset. Its price is influenced by global trends, regulation, inflation, demand for risk, and investor sentiment.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Investing in Cryptocurrency

Advantages: access to a new market, additional diversification, a clearer legal framework, and the possibility of cross-border settlements for businesses.

Disadvantages: high volatility, complex assessment of fair value, regulatory and technological risks, fraud, and the risk of losing access to the asset.

For a long-term portfolio, crypto can be a small addition—a guideline can be a share of about 1–1.5%. A higher share is suitable only for those who consciously choose active trading and understand that they may lose a significant part of their investment.

A reasonable approach is to start with liquid assets, not chase promised returns, keep documents for transactions, and remember: the new infrastructure reduces some legal and technical complexities but does not eliminate market risk.
