Here's the latest on the AL playoff picture

TheTools · 24.09.2026 03:51 · source · permalink

The 2026 postseason picture is clarifying with five days remaining in the regular season, but three persistent questions loom over the AL side of the bracket.

  1. Who will win the AL Central?
  2. Who will win the AL West?
  3. Who will take the third and final AL Wild Card spot?

Essentially, we have four teams (the Guardians, White Sox, Astros and Rangers) battling for the final three AL postseason berths. Let’s break down each of the above questions, one by one, heading into Wednesday’s action.

The race for the AL Central (and a first-round bye)

AL Central standings

  1. Guardians: 82-75 (.522) -- 2 at BOS, 3 at KC
  2. White Sox: 81-76 (.516) -- 2 at KC, 3 vs. COL

It’s been an incredible, ahead-of-schedule leap into contention for the White Sox, who lost 101 games in 2023, a Modern Era-record 121 in 2024 and 102 in 2025. They have led this division for much of the season and by as many as 5 1/2 games in mid-August. The Guardians, however, moved into sole possession of first place last Friday and have remained there since. 

Yet it’s important to note that the White Sox eked out the season series against the Guardians, winning seven of 13 games, by a combined score of 58-57. That gives the White Sox the tiebreaker between the two teams, meaning that if they finish with identical records, Chicago would take the division crown. (All ties in the standings are now resolved with math, rather than with tiebreaker games.)

A division title is always a significant prize, but in this case, there’s a big bonus. The way the bracket is shaping up, the AL Central champion is highly likely to also capture the No. 2 AL seed and therefore receive a bye into the Division Series. (Only division champs are eligible for a top-three seed, so even though the Yankees and Red Sox have the second- and third-best AL records, they can’t be seeded higher than fourth and fifth.)

The Guardians entered Wednesday four games ahead of the Astros and Rangers, who are battling for the AL West title (though Cleveland would lose a tiebreaker to both). The White Sox are three games ahead of both (winning a tiebreaker against the Rangers but losing one to the Astros).

The race for the AL West

AL West standings

  1. Astros 78-79 (.497) -- 1 at SEA, 4 at ATH
  2. Rangers 78-79 (.497) -- 2 vs. NYM, 3 at MIN

In the Divisional Era (since 1969), no team has ever won its division without having a winning record. (The closest was the 2005 Padres, who went 82-80.) But it could happen in the AL West in 2026, as both the Astros and Rangers sit one game under .500.

Because the Astros dominated the season series between the teams -- they went 9-4 with a plus-23 run differential -- they have the inside track on what would be their eighth division title in a 10-season span. The Rangers haven’t finished first since 2016; they were a Wild Card team when they won the World Series in 2023. While Houston has mostly been on top since the beginning of August, Texas briefly took a one-game lead on its intrastate rival over the weekend before an Astros win and Rangers loss on Monday brought them back even.

The race for the final Wild Card spot

AL Wild Card standings

  1. Yankees 90-67 (.573) -- Clinched playoff spot
  2. Red Sox 84-73 (.535) -- Clinched playoff spot
  3. White Sox 81-76 (.516)
  4. Rangers 78-79 (.497)

The Yankees are locked in to the top AL Wild Card, the No. 4 seed and home field in a matchup against the No. 5 seed. That is likely to be the Red Sox, especially since they hold the tiebreaker over the White Sox, in addition to having a three-game lead on Chicago. However, that outcome looks less assured than it did recently, with the Red Sox losing three straight and eight of 12 and closing the season against two tough opponents with something to play for, in the Guardians and Cubs. (Bad weather could also complicate that Cubs series at Fenway Park.)

Similarly, it’s probable (but not certain) that the second-place finisher in the AL Central will be the last Wild Card team, facing the No. 3 seed in the opening round. The Orioles are the only other team still in contention, but they entered Wednesday one loss or one White Sox win away from being eliminated. And even the second-place AL West team would have a hard time jumping into that spot. Let’s run through one extreme scenario to illustrate that.

• Astros and Rangers both go 4-1, finish 82-80 
• Guardians and White Sox go 1-4, finish 83-79 and 82-80, respectively

In this situation, the Astros win the AL West via tiebreaker. The Guardians finish one game ahead of the White Sox for the AL Central crown. And the White Sox take the third AL Wild Card spot via a tiebreaker with the Rangers. The Guardians would get a first-round bye, the No. 3 seed Astros would face the No. 6 seed White Sox in the Wild Card Series, and the Rangers would head home for the offseason.

Wednesday’s clinch scenarios 

• The Guardians can clinch a postseason berth with a win at Boston AND a loss by either the Rangers (vs. Mets) or the Astros (at Mariners).

• The White Sox can clinch a postseason berth with a win at Kansas City AND a loss by the Rangers (vs. Mets).

• The eventual AL Central Champion can clinch a bye in the Wild Card Series and home-field advantage in the Division Series with a win by the Guardians (at Red Sox) AND a loss by the Astros (at Mariners) AND a loss by the Rangers (vs. Mets).
