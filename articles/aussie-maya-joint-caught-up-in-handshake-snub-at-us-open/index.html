Aussie Maya Joint caught up in handshake snub at US Open

TheTools · 07.09.2026 21:17 · source · permalink

  • Topic:Tennis

Topic:Tennis

Maya Joint (left) was in the centre of a US Open controversy when her doubles partner Hao-Ching Chan (right) refused to shake an opponent's hand. (AP: Adam Hunger)

In short:

Australian Maya Joint has had her US Open end on a sour note, losing her third round women's doubles match, then her playing partner refusing to shake an opponent's hand.

Mirra Andreeva, Qinwen Zheng and Elena Rybakina have progressed to the US Open quarterfinals with wins over Anastasia Potapova, Iga Świątek and Naomi Osaka respectively.

2023 champion Coco Gauff beat Iva Jovic to secure the last women's singles quarterfinal spot.

Maya Joint's US Open has come to an end with defeat in the third round of the women's doubles in New York, but the Aussie's exit came with an unexpected controversy.

Joint and partner Hao-Ching Chan, who eliminated Serena and Venus Williams in the opening round of the grand slam, lost their third round match in straight sets, 6-4, 6-4, to Su-wei Hsieh and Jelena Ostapenko.

But the controversy came after match point, when Chan and Hsieh, both from Taiwan, did not shake hands at the end.

When match point was won, Ostapenko screamed towards Chan's coaching box before celebrating with her teammate. Despite Ostapenko's boisterous and vocal act following the victory, Chan had no issue shaking her hand at the net.

But once that was done, Chan walked off the court without looking at Hsieh.

The pair reportedly have a decade-long feud that stems from Hsieh claiming the Chinese Taipei Tennis Association gave greater resources and funding to Chan.

The loss brings an end to an eventful grand slam for Joint.

She advanced to the second round of the women's singles draw after defeating Liudmila Samsonova in the first round, but fell to ninth-seeded Ukrainian Elina Svitolina in a competitive three-set match.

In the women's doubles, Joint and Chan staged a huge comeback in a 10-point tiebreaker to defeat the Williams sisters in front of a packed crowd at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

Joint and Chan would then defeat the team of Tamara Korpatsch and Nicole Fossa Huergo in the second round.

Mixed emotions as Andreeva embraces injured idol after US Open win

US Open fans have witnessed a display of sportsmanship as Mirra Andreeva tended to and consoled an injured Anastasia Potapova as the fifth seed won their fourth-round match.

Potapova jarred her knee as she lost the first point of what turned out to be their final game on Louis Armstrong Stadium, prompting Andreeva to rush to the other end of the court to check on her.

She helped the 24th seed up and Potapova was able to limp to her bench, where Andreeva asked if someone could bring her ice.

The French Open champion finished off the next three points for a 5-7, 6-4, 6-3 victory that sent her to the quarterfinals in Flushing Meadows for the first time, jogging around the net again for a warm embrace.

"It's not the perfect end to the match," 19-year-old Andreeva said.

Potapova appeared to be tearing up as Andreeva came around the net for a long embrace after the final point.

Potapova represents Austria but was born in Russia and still played under that flag when she won the junior Wimbledon championship in 2016.

"For me, Nastya, she was always like a prodigy," Andreeva said of her 25-year-old opponent.

"She came on tour early. She also had a lot of wins. I also remember we were all watching and cheering for her when she won juniors Wimbledon title. I remember watching that match.

"For me, she was always, like, a little star that I was kind of always looking at."

Potapova (right) appeared to be tearing up as the pair embraced after match point. (Getty Images: Pete Staples/USTA)

Potapova won their first meeting on tour in 2022 but Andreeva has won the past four, preventing Potapova from reaching her first grand slam quarterfinal.

"First, she just beat me. So after, I just kind of knew that she's a tough player to play, but at the same time, she was so nice," Andreeva said.

"Then since then we would practice together, and just like that, the more you see each other, the more you spend time together, then you just talk about everything, discuss everything. Then, just like that, I feel like we have a good connection off the court."

Elsewhere, Qinwen Zheng mounted another extraordinary fightback to upset six-time major winner Iga Świątek and reach the quarterfinals.

Qinwen Zheng is the first qualifier since 2021 to make it this far in the women's draw. (Getty Images: Al Bello)

Zheng has become Flushing ‌Meadows' most entertaining escape artist this year, coming back from a 0-5 deficit in the deciding set against third-round foe Madison Keys.

She let the first five games slip through her fingers in 20 minutes against 2022 champion Świątek before digging in, and she collapsed to the court with her hands ⁠in her face after sealing the win on the fourth match ‌point.

China's Olympic champion is the first qualifier ​since Emma Raducanu in 2021 ‌to reach the quarterfinals of the women's draw, as she battles back from a chronic elbow injury that had stunted her career.

Two-time winner Naomi Osaka was summarily swept aside by second seed Elena Rybakina, who won 6-1, 6-4 in just over an hour.

Coco Gauff, the 2023 champion, beat 18-year-old Iva Jovic 6-1, 6-4 in an ​all-American clash to decide the final women's quarterfinalist.

Gauff will face Andreeva, and Rybakina will take on Zheng. On the other side of the draw, third seed Jessica Pegula faces Emma Navarro, while world number one Aryna Sabalenka is up against Wimbledon champions Linda Noskova.
