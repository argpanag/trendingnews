Ranking The Hunger Games franchise

The Hunger Games franchise started 14 years ago and we are now on the sixth instalment, Sunrise on the Reaping.

Despite being a huge fan of the books and films, I hadn’t actually seen the original franchise for a while, so in the run-up to Sunrise, I rewatched them to refresh my memory. This ranking is based on that rewatch marathon.

Here is my ranking from favourite to least favourite. Where does SOTR place?

The Hunger Games: Catching Fire

This is widely regarded as the best one and I have to agree with the masses. It’s so action-packed, so much happens, the arena is unusual and exciting, and the end of the games is flipping cool. There are many great side characters, such as Finnick, Beetee and Johanna. It shows the flames of dissent and defiance growing in the districts, and the ending reveal of District 13 – and that Haymitch and Plutarch are leading the rebellion – is a major bombshell. It doesn’t get better than that!

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping

Yes, I truly think the latest one comes second. I really enjoyed it. It’s a great adaptation of the book; Joseph Zada is a star and a perfect young Haymitch, and it’s so fun to see younger versions of beloved characters. I’ve read a lot of criticism about the games – they lack tension, we don’t see enough of the other characters, and there’s not much person-on-person violence. I see where people are coming from, and I’ll readily admit the film loses something by sanitising the violence in the book and cutting some story beats from the games. But I loved the film so much before and after that I could forgive this. I was very happy with most of the casting (especially Glenn Close, Elle Fanning and Jesse Plemons) but wasn’t convinced by Kieran Culkin and Ralph Fiennes as the younger versions of their characters. The epilogue has also been overhyped – it’s short, unnecessary and pure fan service.

The Hunger Games

Where it all began. It looks very different; it’s the only film not directed by Francis Lawrence, and it clearly had a smaller budget. The arena feels basic now in retrospect – considering what the others have become – but the woodland setting seemed fine at the time. You actually get to know some of the other tributes and there’s a genuine sense of threat inside the arena. This has to come near the top because it’s the foundation for everything and our introduction to Katniss, Peeta and co. You need to see this to understand the format of the games and the historical reason for them because this context gets more and more abridged as the films progress.

The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 2

This was the thrilling showdown; what the original franchise has been building towards, and it did not disappoint. It’s so exciting seeing Katniss and the District 13 rebels in the Capitol, edging ever closer to President Snow, dodging traps and scary mutts (the underground sequence is scary!) The seeds of dissent and defiance throughout the franchise finally come to a head and they stop the games once and for all. It’s a shame this was split into two films, but I’ll talk about that more in a moment.

The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes

This prequel, set during the 10th Hunger Games, is really interesting because it’s told from the mentor’s perspective rather than a tribute’s. It also shows the humble beginnings of the tournament, before it became a huge event watched across Panem. There’s no escort, no chariot parade, no training sessions and tributes are held in a zoo like caged animals! I paid more attention to the differences this time around. Rachel Zegler and Tom Blyth are excellent leads, and Viola Davis is the scariest Head Gamemaker of the franchise as Dr. Volumina Gaul. The evolution of Coriolanus is intriguing – how he goes from a kindhearted person to a killer –  but the games aren’t that exciting, and it goes on too long afterwards (my biggest complaint about the book).

The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 1

I’d only seen this film once because I found it really boring the first time. Not enough happens; it’s just setting up part two and doesn’t have anything hugely exciting in it, except Peeta’s return. Most of the good stuff in this book appears in the second half. I don’t think the story warranted two films, but splitting the final book into two was the done thing at the time (Harry Potter, Twilight etc). The combined film would have been three+ hours long, but it would have been a banger. I still had these thoughts on my recent viewing, but it’s more interesting once you’ve seen Sunrise and can appreciate the full Haymitch and Plutarch throughline. I liked watching their moves and countermoves (to quote Plutarch) more this time around.

What do you think? How would you rank the franchise?

PS. I reserve the right to change this ranking whenever I next do a rewatch marathon. This is how I feel about them this time around. And I was really torn about where to place the 2012 original!
